Sheepish Man United stars roll into Carrington after 4-0 trouncing at Liverpool as Mauricio Pochettino and Co launch FA appeal against Leny Yoro's 'painful' red card

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

By OLLIE LEWIS, SPORTS DIGITAL EDITOR and CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER

Published: 05:58 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 10:41 EST, 23 November 2026

Mauricio Pochettino hauled his beleaguered Man United stars back into Carrington early on Monday morning after the new manager's chastening Anfield bow on Sunday.

Pochettino, who replaced Michael Carrick last week, was served a sobering reality check at the hands of United's fierce rivals Liverpool, with the Red Devils on the receiving end of a 4-0 trouncing on Merseyside.

The Argentine bemoaned the impact of what he described as a harsh red card against defender Leny Yoro in the opening eight minutes of the contest, but admitted he has plenty to do to turn things around - with United stuck in 19th in the Premier League table.

And Pochettino had his players check in for training early on Monday morning, with a downcast Bruno Fernandes and his team-mates spotted arriving at around 8.30am - roughly an hour earlier than under Carrick.

Under Carrick's stewardship, players were given the day after a game off, and it remains to be seen whether Pochettino will do away with that approach in favour of a recovery day at the club's training ground - a similar approach to that taken by former United boss Ruben Amorim.

Matthijs de Ligt cut a contemplative figure as he drove through the gates at Carrington

News also came on Monday that Man United are appealing to the FA over Yoro’s controversial red card.

Yoro was sent off in the eighth minute for a challenge on Alexander Isak on the edge of the penalty box. The Frenchman was dismissed by referee Sam Barrott, and Dominik Szoboszlai scored Liverpool’s first goal from the free kick as 10-man United were thrashed 4-0.

However, United insist that Yoro did not deny Isak a clear goalscoring opportunity because goalkeeper Senne Lammens would have reached the ball first.

The club have notified the FA of their intention to appeal in the hope of having Yoro’s one-match ban overturned so he can play against Brentford at Old Trafford on Saturday.

In September, Pro Ref boss Howard Webb apologised to United when Erling Haaland’s winning goal for Manchester City in the Old Trafford derby was allowed to stand after VAR incorrectly ruled that teammate Enzo Fernandez was not interfering with play as he tried to head the ball in an offside position.

Last season, Webb acknowledged that the match officials got it wrong when they decided not to send off Brentford captain Nathan Collins for hauling back Bryan Mbeumo inside the box as United lost 3-1 at the Gtech Community Stadium.

New United head coach Pochettino blamed Yoro’s red card for the heavy defeat in his first game in charge, and was unhappy that Barrott took no action over a similar incident involving Jeremy Jacquet and Mbeumo in the Liverpool penalty area moments earlier.

'So painful, so disappointed,' said the Argentine. 'It's not OK to start a game in the Premier League with this situation. The game changed completely after eight minutes. Now we can explain everything, but the damage is done – it's so painful.

'It's clear that after eight minutes the plan was in the bin. Do you think that is easy? Even the best team in the world playing in this situation concedes four goals or maybe more.

'We are not going to lose more than one game how we lost today. I take the responsibility, and we keep pushing.

'We feel pain for this defeat, but we need to keep our heads up and translate that anger into energy to start to work from tomorrow for the next game.

'We are in a situation that this is very complicated. It's difficult, yes, but I think we fight, we made the effort, and I know how they feel now in the dressing-room.

'The players are feeling the pain of the result. I told them to be calm: it was an unfair situation that happened. We need to accept that reality. We need to try to increase our focus, concentration and make better decisions on the field.

'We also need to be critical on ourselves but some situations happened in the game that we are not happy.'

But more critical were former Red Devils Roy Keane and Gary Neville, both of whom were pushed to extremes assessing the match after the final whistle.

Lisandro Martinez was among the Man United defenders that Gary Neville did not feel were up to the task at Anfield

But incoming manager Pochettino was keen to place the majority of blame on the harsh red card doled out to Leny Yoro

Neville was driven to slapping his face in the Sky Sports studio to demonstrate Man United's defenders' lack of hunger and poise.

'I never see any of them on their toes, alert, looking like they've got something fixed in here', he said before pointing to his head.

'I never see any of them look like that,' he added. 'They look like that -'

Neville then stared into the middle distance for a few moments to share the blankness he sees on the faces of the back line, before spitting: 'Wake up!'

The Overlap broadcaster slapped himself a number of times while repeating 'wake up!' and adding: 'You play for Man United'.

Keane was pushed to the brink of swearing in his snap description of the team's performance, adding that he thought Neville's verdict - that the 4-0 scoreline might have been worse under the circumstances - took players off the hook.

'If I hear that one more time from the lads in the commentary that 4-0 is not too bad when you're down to 10 men - has a team ever got a result with 10 men before?' Keane said immediately after the final whistle. 'It can be done.

'I thought United were desperate. I thought they were so bad and everyone and Nev, who played for Man United saying 4-0 ain't bad.'

'(The red card) doesn't help but you're telling me they couldn't have done better? They lost 4-0 with 10 men, that's not too bad? Has a team ever got a result with 10 men? It can be done. At least show a bit more fight, a bit more quality - I can't believe what I'm hearing from people.

'You played for the club, saying 4-0 isn't too bad. Wow. Fans are applauding. Applauding what? Performing rubbish. Liverpool were excellent, but how easy did United make it for them? I can't believe what I'm watching sometimes.

'This team two years ago finished 15th. The last year or two, we're talking. We're blaming the board, blaming ownership, blaming managers.

'When do the players take responsibility for some decision-making? Liverpool probably should have scored eight, probably eight or nine, unbelievable chances they had. And everyone's coming off the pitch saying 4 is not too bad? I feel like swearing.'
