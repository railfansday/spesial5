One Shot, One Goal, Three Points: West Ham’s Season in a Nutshell

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

After another disastrous result last night, I was left scratching my head wondering what can actually be done to solve the problems facing West Ham. If results continue along this trajectory, the Hammers could find themselves looking nervously over their shoulders at the bottom end of the Championship table rather than fighting for promotion. At this rate, even a play-off spot feels optimistic.

I think 50 points might be enough to survive this league!

Earlier this week I wrote about the statistics, and they tell the real story behind West Ham’s struggles.

Shot after shot, chance after chance, attack after attack, and still the ball refuses to go in. Remarkably, despite their wastefulness, the Hammers remain the division’s top scorers. In the last two matches alone, they have struck the woodwork five times.

Against Portsmouth, West Ham registered 19 shots with six on target and failed to score.

When you add that to the wider picture, the numbers become even more alarming. Across the last six matches, West Ham have taken 130 shots, with 41 on target, and scored only eight goals.

At the opposite end of the pitch, defensive frailties and questionable goalkeeping continue to cost the team. Herrick was barely tested at Fratton Park, but when he was called into action, he was beaten. The goal will almost certainly be recorded as a goalkeeping error. Portsmouth created virtually nothing, yet their only shot on target produced the winning goal.

You can only work with the players available, but it’s difficult to understand how Areola has suffered multiple injuries this season despite not playing a competitive match. Entering the final year of his contract, albeit with a club option for an additional season, he is reportedly earning around £60,000 per week.

These are issues that might be understandable in the Premier League. This is the Championship. These players, including the youngsters, should be capable of performing consistently at this level.

Heads – down, negativity growing…time to stop the rot…

The negativity around the club is beginning to grow.

Nuno had to go, and he did. His departure gave Parker an opportunity to reintegrate certain players and rebuild fitness levels through competitive football. To be fair, time has not always been on the new manager’s side. However, following the international break, he has now had a chance to work with the squad, implement his ideas and stamp his authority on the team.

The last two matches have to fall at his door. One point from six is simply not good enough. Extend that across his first four games and the record reads two defeats and two draws.

The much-talked-about new manager bounce? It never arrived.

You can see the frustration written all over the players’ faces. The supporters are becoming restless too, and I include myself in that group. I predicted a narrow 1-0 victory for West Ham at Portsmouth, but deep down I feared a repeat of the Preston performance. Unfortunately, those fears proved worse as at least we got a point on Saturday!

Against Preston, West Ham enjoyed 80% possession and recorded 53 touches inside the opposition penalty area. Preston registered just ten.

At Fratton Park, the Hammers dominated possession with 63% of the ball and managed 29 touches in Portsmouth’s box compared to just eight for the home side.

At this point, I’m running out of explanations. The statistics suggest West Ham should be winning games comfortably, but football is decided by goals, not data. Right now, the Hammers can’t convert their dominance into results, and until that changes, the gap between expectation and reality will continue to grow. I’m lost for words. I really am!

I have been a season ticket holder since the late eighties, so experienced the highs and lows of being a West Ham supporter. I previously wrote for OLAS and have contributed to a number of football publications in the past.
