You can get by without a washing machine, especially if you live near a laundromat, though you can’t beat the luxury of having one in your basement.

Many of today’s high-end washers have price tags in the thousands, but if you’re shopping on a budget, the good news is that you can find cheap washing machines that cost much less and work just as well.

Whether you want a full-size washing machine to handle your family’s oversized loads or a more compact model that can be tucked in a closet, there are a number of highly rated appliances well under $1,000. Some even have high-end features that are generally reserved for more expensive machines, such as a built-in water faucet or sanitization cycle, which helps make laundry day quicker and easier than ever.

When shopping for a cheap washing machine, you’ll need to decide whether you want a full-size, portable, or manual model. Most of the full-size options you’ll find under $1,000 are top-loading washers, and they offer a large capacity as well as more settings and cycles. However, there are a few select front-loading models in this price range, as well.

For those with limited space, portable washing machines have a more compact footprint and can easily be stored in a closet. However, these machines typically require a faucet connection for operation and don’t always wring out your clothes, making them trickier to dry.

Finally, manual washing machines are the most affordable, but they require a bit of elbow grease for operation. You usually have to crank or agitate the machine by hand, but these models don’t require electricity, making them ideal for camping or off-the-grid living.

The capacity of washing machines is generally measured in cubic feet. Most standard-size appliances have a capacity of 3.5 to 5 cubic feet and are suitable for average households. For reference, a washer with a capacity of 4.5 cubic feet can wash a king-size comforter. If you have a small household, you’ll likely be fine with a smaller-capacity washing machine. A compact machine with 2.3 cubic feet can wash about 6 towels.

Cheap washing machines often have fewer wash cycles than more expensive models. In general, you’ll find most affordable washers have between eight and 12 wash cycles, including standard ones like quick or light wash, delicates, and heavy-duty. There are also several temperature settings on most washers, and some allow you to select the water level and soil level as well. These basics are generally sufficient for most loads of laundry, but if you want more advanced settings—such as sanitization or fabric-specific cycles—you may need to spend a little extra.

To help save money on operation costs, you may want to look for a high-efficiency washing machine, frequently abbreviated as HE. These models use 20 to 66 percent less water, saving around 6,000 gallons of water per year for an average family. You may also want to look for an Energy Star-certified appliance, which uses 25 percent less energy than standard models.

Amy Chernoff, VP of Marketing and Appliance Expert at AJ Madison, says that budget washing machines will still get the job done. The differences are in everything else—build quality, noise, fabric care, and how long the machine holds up under heavy use.

The trade-offs at lower price points are less about cleaning power and more about small details. “You are usually giving up fabric care refinement and overall build quality rather than basic cleaning performance,” Chernoff says.

That means simpler drum movement, fewer specialty cycles, and suspension systems that don’t dampen vibration as well. Over the years, a pricier washer tends to be gentler on your clothes, but for most people washing everyday loads, a more affordable model gets the job done.

A cheap washer doesn’t automatically mean higher utility bills. “Many affordable modern models are still quite efficient, especially if they carry an Energy Star certification,” Chernoff says. Some of the machines on this list are Energy Star certified, so they should be just as efficient as more expensive models.

A single person or couple can usually get by with 3.5 to 4.5 cubic feet. Families should look at 4.5 cubic feet or larger, which offers enough room for bedding, towels, and several days of clothes in one load.

“Choosing a slightly larger machine than you think you need can reduce the number of loads per week and make laundry more convenient over time,” Chernoff says. Every washer on this list falls between 4.2 and 5.0 cubic feet, so even the smallest option here handles a full week of laundry for two people without cramming everything in.
