Woman Faced A Felony Charge For Stealing A Rental Car—Then Won A Seat In Congress And Prosecutors Dropped The Case

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

This year was such a wave election that in some cases candidate quality didn’t matter at all. It was enough to win just not to be a Republican. Shortly before the election, Democratic congressional candidate LaKesha Womack pulled into a high school parking lot in North Carolina for a Sunday rally. A sheriff’s deputy pulled up behind her. He handed her a felony summons for stealing a Toyota Corolla she’d rented from Budget nearly seven years earlier. The incumbent congressman she was running against turned the rental into a television ad. She won anyway. And on Tuesday, after Budget got paid for the six weeks its car was overdue, the district attorney dropped the charge for good. It’s good to get elected to Congress. Budget Car Rental Got The Corolla Back In 2019 Womack picked up the car in Charlotte on August 27, 2019, and renewed the rental twice. Her last extension began October 7, 2019. According to the police report the vehicle was due back at noon on October 23. On November 26 an officer reported that police couldn’t find her and put out an all points bulletin. Police recovered the Corolla and returned it to Budget on December 4, 42 days late. Budget sold the vehicle the following year. Womack has a number of excuses for never returning the car. She says a diabetic seizure and long hospitalization had wiped her out financially. (It’s not clear why that kept her from returning it.) Her son went into foster care while she was in the hospital (again, unclear why that kept her from returning it) and the two of them wound up living in a hotel (hotels have parking, but the hotel didn’t force her to keep the car). She’d expected the company to come get the car. She says she had nowhere reliable to receive mail (when you rent a car you know you’re supposed to give it back, even without a mailed notice) and she thought the matter was settled (exactly how it would have been is unclear). On January 14, 2020, a court issued a summons for felony failure to return the car. Under North Carolina law, willfully keeping a rental vehicle past its deadline is a felony if the vehicle is worth more than $4,000. The car was valued at $20,000. Womack says she first heard about the case on Friday, October 9, 2026. A local reporter posted the records.
