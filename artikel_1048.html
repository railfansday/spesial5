Japan Football Association (JFA) honorary president Kozo Tashima has officially submitted his candidacy for the Fifa presidency after securing the support of five national federations, signifying a rupture in Asia’s backing of Gianni Infantino.

Nominations for the Fifa presidential election close at 10:59pm GMT on Wednesday, four months before the 77th Fifa Congress in Rabat on 18 March, where the vote ⁠will take place.

Infantino is seeking re-election for a fourth term as head of global football’s ruling body, with any challenging candidates needing at least five member associations to nominate them.

They should also have played an active role in football for two of the last five years as a player or official within Fifa, a confederation or a national association.

Tashima is a former Japan international ⁠and youth coach and the first Japanese candidate to stand for Fifa president. He ​has ⁠served on the Fifa Council since 2015.

“The JFA submitted to Fifa a letter nominating me as a candidate in the Fifa presidential election," Tashima said in a statement shared by the JFA.

"I would like to once again express my gratitude to President (Tsuneyasu) Miyamoto and all the members of the board. I would also like to sincerely thank the football associations of many countries who gave me the necessary support for this nomination.

"With a strong sense of mission ‌and determination, I will take on this challenge so that Fifa can become a healthier and more trusted organization and contribute to the further development of football around the world."

He did not disclose which associations' nominations he had received, but told reporters at the Asian Football Confederation's (AFC) annual awards ceremony in Thailand on Wednesday he would later reveal which nations had backed him.

Asked about his ‌objectives, the 68-year-old said: "First and foremost, I believe it is essential to establish proper governance and ensure that transparency is firmly maintained.

"We must get that right first. Then, on top of that, various policies can be put forward."

Candidates must pass formal eligibility and integrity checks before standing for election, and Fifa’s general secretariat must notify member associations of the proposed candidates for president at least one ⁠month before the Congress.

Infantino has faced opposition to his re-election over his aborted attempt to bring private investors into a new Fifa commercial entity holding rights linked to competitions including the World Cup.

The Swiss later apologised to the Fifa Council for the way the proposal had been handled.

Other challengers include former Switzerland captain Ramon Vega and French Football Federation president Philippe Diallo.

Tashima said he was aware that he was "not necessarily in an advantageous position" heading into the election.

"From here, I need to think carefully about what strategy to adopt, what kind of manifesto to create, and how to make my case," he added.

Diallo, who was drafted in at the last minute as European governing body Uefa’s candidate, said at a press conference on Tuesday he would reach out to Tashima to "unite our forces".

Tashima said he had not yet spoken with Diallo.

"I have spoken indirectly with people around him, but we have not discussed specifically what we would do," he added. "Mr. Diallo only recently made his announcement, so I would like to speak with ‌him at least once going forward."

Asia's regional confederation ⁠had already said it was backing Infantino before Tashima announced his intention to run earlier this month.

AFC head Sheikh Salman bin Ibrahim Al-Khalifa publicly endorsed the Swiss administrator at their extraordinary congress in Tokyo in October, just two months after describing his plan to sell a stake in Fifa tournaments as a "breach of trust".

Asked about ‌his position once again on Wednesday, Sheikh Salman ​told reporters: "Well, we already made our stance.

"We mentioned that, whether it's during the AFC Executive Committee meeting in Vancouver (in April), or during the congress in Tokyo. It's as simple as that.

“I prefer that you talk to the member association more, hear it from them."
