Stop making this common swing mistake for more consistent contact

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

A major key to consistency with the long game is learning to turn through the shot rather than slide through it. When you rotate properly through impact, you encourage the clubface to remain more stable through the hitting zone, which reduces the need to "save" the shot at the last second with your hands.

Sliding, on the other hand, often leads to excessive clubhead manipulation, as golfers try to compensate by slapping at the ball with their hands through impact. This can make it much more difficult to control both the direction and quality of your strike.

A great way to develop a better turning motion is with a simple alignment-stick drill. Check out below to see how it works.

At address, place an alignment stick in the ground just outside your lead leg, positioned so that it serves as an obstacle during your downswing and follow-through. Then, take a series of swings without letting your body run into the stick. The goal is to create rotation through the hitting zone rather than allowing your lower body to slide laterally toward the target.

As you work through the drill, I like to feel as if I'm slamming my right shoulder directly toward the golf ball. This encourages your upper body to stay engaged and helps you maintain more rotation through impact. Many good players describe this sensation as covering the ball through the hitting zone. It's a useful feel because it encourages you to keep turning rather than getting stuck and relying on your hands to square the clubface.

Another way to reinforce this movement is to practice hitting punch shots. By shortening your finish and taking some of the wrist and hand action out of the swing, you'll encourage your body to do more of the work. Think of it as temporarily taking your hands out of the equation.

If you reduce the amount of manipulation with your hands, your body will have to activate and rotate properly to create speed and deliver the club consistently. Once you become comfortable with that feeling on shorter shots, you can gradually transfer it to your full swing.

You can take this drill a step further by using a training aid like the Downshift Mini underneath the outside half of your lead foot. This provides additional feedback and helps prevent you from sliding past the ball during the downswing. The concept is closely related to what modern golf instruction often calls braking: learning to stabilize and use your lead side so your body can rotate through impact instead of continuing to drift toward the target.

The goal isn't to stop your lower body from moving altogether. It's to learn how to manage that movement so you can use the ground effectively and create a more efficient rotation through the ball. When you combine that feeling with a stable clubface and less excessive hand action, you'll have a much better chance of producing consistent contact.

Spend some time working on these feels in practice before taking them to the course. You'll be amazed at how much more crisply and accurately you strike the ball when you learn to turn through impact instead of sliding.
