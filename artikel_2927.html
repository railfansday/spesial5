Record Book: Josh Allen has Jim Kelly in his sights

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

We're going to need a fresh pencil, a good eraser, and maybe a little more room in the record book. Josh Allen keeps giving us names to cross out and new numbers to write in their place.

Allen moved past Peyton Manning on the NFL's all-time touchdown list and tied Eric Dickerson on the career rushing touchdown list. And while we've been keeping a close eye on both of those records for weeks, seeing Allen check them off in the same game gave us a little extra work to do.

Of course, we're not complaining. Crossing names off our lists and moving Allen up a few spots is pretty much the whole point of this exercise. Besides, he's not the only one keeping us busy. There are still plenty of records within reach for the Bills, and with another week of football ahead, it's time to see which pages we might need to rewrite next.

With another week in the books and a new set of milestones within reach, here's where things stand heading into Week 11.

_____________________________________________________________________________

Josh Allen moves into the NFL's top 50 in career passing yards

This one shouldn't take long. Josh Allen needs just 20 passing yards against Miami to move past Hall of Fame quarterback Kurt Warner (32,344) and into the NFL's all-time top 50. Allen enters Week 11 with 32,325 career passing yards, so we're probably looking at a first-quarter update to the record book.

And there's a good chance he won't stop there. Cam Newton sits just ahead of Warner with 32,382 career passing yards. Allen needs 58 yards to pass both quarterbacks and move up another spot on the leaderboard.

Unless something goes very wrong against Miami, Allen should be leapfrogging a couple of NFL legends before halftime.

_____________________________________________________________________________

Josh Allen moves up the NFL's all-time rushing touchdown list

Allen didn't waste any time making changes to this list last week. With two rushing touchdowns against the Jets, he reached 90 for his career, tying Eric Dickerson and Curtis Martin on the NFL's all-time rushing touchdown list.

Now he's just one away from moving up again. Another rushing touchdown against Miami would put Allen at 91, moving him past Dickerson and Martin and into a tie with Jerome Bettis and Franco Harris for 12th all-time. Not bad company for a quarterback.

Josh Allen climbs the all-time passing touchdown list

Allen has another opportunity to move up the NFL's passing touchdown leaderboard this week, and he won't need a career day to do it. He enters Week 11 with 233 career passing touchdowns, just one behind Donovan McNabb (234).

One touchdown pass would tie McNabb for 38th all-time, while two would move Allen ahead of him. Three would bring Allen to 236, tying George Blanda for 37th. We're not saying Allen is going to throw three touchdown passes against Miami. But we're not not saying it, either.

James Cook takes sole possession of second place

For eight straight games, James Cook has rushed for at least 75 yards and scored a touchdown. That's a pretty impressive streak on its own, but it gets even better when we look at where it puts him in NFL history.

Cook is currently tied with John Riggins, George Rogers, and LaDainian Tomlinson for the second-longest streak in league history. If he reaches both marks again against Miami, he could put those names behind his.

And then there's Terry Allen, who holds the NFL record at 10 straight games. We're not quite there yet, but Cook could be knocking on that door before November is over.

At this point, Greg Rousseau's name might as well have a permanent spot in this chapter. We've been waiting a few weeks to move his single-season sack record from the watch list to the history books, but that ninth sack is proving a little stubborn.

Rousseau enters Week 11 with eight sacks, matching his previous career high. One more against Miami would give him a new personal best, and we'd finally get to retire this particular entry.

But don't worry, Groot. We've got another spot for you later in the record book. You're not getting out of here that easily.

_____________________________________________________________________________

Bills defense closes in on a franchise record

The Bills' defense has gone 33 consecutive opponent drives without allowing a touchdown. That's a lot of possessions ending without six points on the scoreboard, and it's the longest such streak for Buffalo since 1999.

That 1999 defense put together 38 straight drives without surrendering a touchdown, leaving this year's group just five away from matching the franchise mark. Six more would give the current defense the record outright.

Of course, if the Bills are going to break a franchise record, why stop at 39? They've already kept three straight opponents out of the end zone for a full four quarters. What's one more?

Dawson Knox moves up the Bills' receiving touchdown list

Dawson Knox is becoming another familiar face in our weekly record watch. We've been waiting for him to move up the Bills' receiving touchdown leaderboard for a few weeks now, and we're still holding his place.

Knox enters Week 11 with 28 career receiving touchdowns, tied with Stevie Johnson and Gabe Davis. One more would move him past both former Bills receivers and into a tie with Jerry Butler at 29. Two touchdowns against Miami would put him at 30 and move him ahead of Butler, too.

We've already made room for Knox a little higher on the list. Now we just need him to give us a reason to write his name there.

_____________________________________________________________________________

Josh Allen chases Jim Kelly's franchise passing touchdown record

We've already got Allen moving up the NFL's all-time passing touchdown list in Chapter 2, but this is where things get a little crazy.

Allen needs four touchdown passes against Miami to tie Jim Kelly's franchise record of 237, or five to break it. Six would also tie Len Dawson (239) on the NFL's all-time list.

Five touchdown passes in one afternoon? That's asking a lot, even of Josh Allen. But we've seen him do some pretty ridiculous things on a football field, so we're not ruling anything out – and that's the point of this Superhuman chapter anyway, isn't it?

Greg Rousseau chases Mario Williams on the Bills' sack list

Remember when we told Groot he wasn't getting out of our record book that easily? Well, here we are.

Rousseau enters Week 11 with 40 career sacks, sitting three behind Mario Williams (43) on the Bills' all-time list.

A three-sack game is a pretty tall order, even for Rousseau. But if he's going to pick a week to finally break that single-season personal best we've been tracking, he might as well make it one that sends us scrambling to update two chapters at once.

_____________________________________________________________________________

Some of these milestones could be checked off before halftime Sunday. Others might keep us reaching for that pencil for another week or two. Either way, there's no shortage of history within reach for Buffalo.

And while we've been keeping track of what individual players are accomplishing, there's one more number worth watching. A win over Miami would give the Bills their first 9–1 start since 1991.

That's a pretty good reminder that, for all the individual records being chased, the most important number on Sunday will still be the one in the win column. Let's hope we're adding another one.
