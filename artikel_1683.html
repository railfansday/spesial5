How Arsenal have evolved in 2026-27

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

There was a lot of speculation as to how Arsenal might change this season having flung a 22-year-old monkey (how long do monkeys live for?!) from their back in May by finally winning a league title. Would lifting the Premier League trophy lead to a new era of more expressive, urbane football?

I always had my doubts, not least because your style of play is always defined by your talent. Most of Arsenal’s elite talent is behind the ball. Arteta didn’t build Arsenal from the defence first, in my view, because he felt specifically that was the best way. It was just the area of the team that required the most attention when he was appointed.

In attacking areas, he had talent, even if it wasn’t balanced. Aubameyang, Lacazette and Pepe with Saka and Martinelli coming to the boil was a good collection of talent, even if the pieces didn’t always fit neatly together. The list of defenders he inherited included Mustafi, Sokratis, Holding, Chambers, Kolasinac and Mari. That’s why he went defence first, broadly speaking.

The moves the club have tried to make in recent seasons, especially this summer, shows you that Arteta wants his attack to be sharper than it is. For reasons I won’t relitigate here, the club were not able to add a number of high end attacking targets. Which means once again, Arsenal are reliant on Arteta’s coaching talents to squeeze more juice from the orange. Here are three things that have jumped out at me this season.

Arsenal spent a lot of money on Bruno Guimaraes this season and I will come onto some of the reasons why in the next section. But that signing has given Arteta a deep well of central options, with Rice, Bruno, Lewis Skelly, Odegaard, Merino and Zubimendi part of this midfield menagerie.

Arsenal use these options interchangeably and often according to game state. Art de Roche of the Athletic went into how Arteta has used the divergent profiles of Myles Lewis Skelly and Bruno Guimaraes so far in this campaign. In home games against Leeds and Everton, Lewis Skelly got the nod from the start.

In away games at Nottingham Forest and Liverpool, Bruno’s more robust approach was favoured. The pair have made significant impacts from the bench too. When the 1-0 victory at Anfield had become a little too ‘basketball’, Lewis Skelly came on and provided calm assurance in possession.

When the Gunners needed a little more belligerence away at Sunderland, Bruno came on and larruped the ball into the top corner from 25 yards. However, Arteta didn’t part with a significant part of his summer budget for a 28-year-old (he turns 29 next week but, for now, he remains 28 because that’s how it works) to be a high level rotation option.

Against Hull City last weekend, Bruno was selected and he played deeper than Declan Rice and Martin Odegaard. As Adrian Clarke pointed out in his match analysis piece, Bruno passed the ball to Odegaard 25 times in that match and the pair exchanged 35 passes in total. The idea was clear, to use Bruno’s punchiness in possession a little lower to feed Odegaard.

Against Everton, Mikel Merino was selected in midfield and Lewis Skelly started at left-back. I think this was about more than mere rotation. Arteta always highly respects the aerial capabilities of David Moyes’ Everton team, which makes sense since he played in a David Moyes Everton team for such a long time.

In 2023-24, Arteta selected Fabio Vieira ahead of Kai Havertz in midfield at Goodison Park because he wanted to keep the ball on the floor (Arsenal won that game 1-0 via a short corner routine because they did not see the logic of aerial corners against a team like Everton).

Last season at the Hill Dickinson Arsenal enjoyed a season high for passes attempted and completed because they wanted to keep the ball on the floor and avoid transitions. Arteta has a carousel available to him in midfield and a different club in the bag depending on what is called for on the day.

Per Opta, Arsenal have been caught offside 35 times so far this season, at least 11 more times than any other team. That rate of 3.5 offsides per game is the highest of any Premier League team since 2009. Kai Havertz has been flagged 15 times now, the next highest offender in the league is Dominic Calvert-Lewin with eight. Opta provide a deeper dive on those numbers here.

So what does this tell us, aside from the fact that Havertz needs to work on his timing? It means that Arsenal are far more willing to go in behind and they are willing to test the opposition backline earlier in moves. This was a big part of the reason they signed Bruno in the first place, Arteta wants to play with the risk reward dial with the ball.

Havertz also hasn’t been quite good enough to finish some of those one v one situations when he has got in behind the opponent’s back line. He fluffed his lines against Hull last weekend and in the 3-0 defeat at Brighton, he blasted his effort straight at the Brighton keeper when put through one v one.

But here, I will express my usual caveat, we need to normalise strikers missing chances and focus more on process than outcome. Most chances are missed, you want to worry when your striker is getting few shots. There have also been nascent signs that Arsenal have been using Gyokeres well in some of these situations.

That Havertz chance at the Amex was created by Gyokeres taking one touch with his back to goal and hooking the ball past the committed defensive line. Gyokeres did much the same for Madueke in Prague. It seems like Arsenal have worked on getting Gyokeres to use his physicality to initially retain the ball- something he badly struggled with last season- but creating situations where he can release it quickly.

Arsenal’s out of possession prowess is one thing that has been a constant from last season. It is Arsenal’s superpower, so even when they can only manage one goal, it is usually enough to take maximum points. To retain their superpower, Arsenal changed to stay the same, if that makes sense.

The signing of Ezri Konsa from Aston Villa was questioned by many but his presence has made the absence of a world class centre-back like William Saliba a sidebar, which is quite a feat. Arsenal ostensibly took William Saliba’s pizza chart and looked for the most Saliba like defender they could find and bought Konsa.

The loss of Saliba ought to have offered an enormous threat to Arsenal’s defensive stability. Arsenal’s tactical organisation out of possession is elite but so too are the individuals that helm it. Raya, Saliba, Gabriel and Rice are among the best players in the world in their respective positions.

Taking out one of those pillars ought to have derailed Arsenal’s defensive stability and it hasn’t because Konsa has the quality and poise to retain the team’s biggest strength. His pass completion stats are Saliba-esque and the quality of not giving the ball away needlessly is a significant part of the Gunners’ meanness.

How much Arsenal could improve in attack without significant additions was an unknown so Arteta could not afford to sacrifice the team’s defensive ceiling while he plays with those levers. The addition of Konsa was a personnel change that allowed Arsenal to remain the same.
