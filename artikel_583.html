Qatar Is Forging Ahead With Film and TV Industry Plan, Undeterred by Regional Turbulence

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The tiny Arab state rich in oil and gas is laying the groundwork to secure a spot on the global entertainment map

Undeterred by regional turbulence, Qatar is forging ahead with its plan to build a film and TV industry that intends to put the tiny Arab state rich in oil and gas on the global entertainment map.

That is the main takeaway of the recently concluded second edition of the Doha Industry Days, held Nov. 19-21 during the Doha Film Festival, attended by some 450 prominent executives from 38 countries. They included a robust contingent of Hollywood honchos, comprising Candle Media co-founder Kevin Mayer; Neon CEO and founder Tom Quinn; Mike Larocca, co-founder of Department M; Miramax boss Jon Glickman; Steven Paul, CEO of SP Media; and AGC Studios CEO Stuart Ford, to name a few. Most of them, in different guises, also serve as advisors to Hassan Al Thawadi, the lawyer who oversaw the 2022 FIFA World Cup and now chairs the Qatar Film Committee, the official body tasked with driving growth of the country’s entertainment industry.

Talents on hand included “Squid Game” creator Hwang Dong-hyuk, “Avengers” director Joe Russo, Omar Sy and Noomi Rapace.

The Doha event took place despite regional instability caused by the U.S.-Iran-Israel war. In September Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea Film Festival canceled its sixth edition that was scheduled to take place from Dec. 3 to 12 amid sharp escalation in its conflict with Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi rebels, who had targeted several Saudi cities.

“Obviously, you know the journey has not been easy,” Al Thawadi said in his concluding remarks, alluding to the impact on Qatar of the ongoing regional conflict involving the U.S., Israel and Iran. The conflict has delayed plans to shoot a Doha-set contemporary adaptation of William Shakespeare’s “Othello” that Oyelowo will direct and star in alongside Rachel Brosnahan and Cynthia Erivo, being produced by Barbara Broccoli that was announced during the Doha Industry Days last year.

“But most of you are engaging with us; helping us build what we want to do,” he added. “And what we’ve got here, it’s a common vision, right? It’s not my vision; it’s not the Film Committee’s vision. It is all our collective vision,” Al Thawadi underlined.

Al Thawadi went on to vow that next year’s edition of the Doha Industry Days “will be a lot bigger” and that, in the interim, there will more announcements, besides those that were made on the spot.

Those announcements included concrete plans for a Neon-backed biopic titled “Caesar” about Syrian military officer Farid Al-Madhhan who blew the whistle on torture and killings perpetrated by the regime of Syrian president Bashar Assad; and two other still untitled biopics, one on Egyptian music star Mohamed Mounir, known as El King; the other on controversial Lebanese pop star Fadel Shaker.

During the event Qatar’s Film Committee also signed agreements with Egypt and Jordan, expanding the regional reach of the Qatar Screen Production Incentive, launched last year, which offers up to 50% in cash. The partnerships connect Qatar’s production incentives with cross-border collaboration, creating new opportunities to produce films across the Arab world.

“The industry days here a year ago were something of a launch pad, where they laid out some broad parameters,” Ford told Variety during a wide-ranging interview in Doha. “And we’ve seen in the last 12 months, Hassan Al Thawadi and his team have started to put building blocks in place,” he said.

“I think there is a very defined strategy,” Ford added, noting that Qatar has the benefit of starting to build an industry after the UAE and Saudi Arabia. “They are also coming to the industry at a time of great change globally, which creates huge opportunity for them to earn a place at the table,” the U.S. exec who has been long been active in the region went on to point out.

“And I have nothing but admiration for the way that Qatar has, rather than just following tried and tested approaches of getting into the business, very much shaped a strategy around what Qatar is,” Ford continued.

“We’re seeing only the early stages of it,” Ford observed. “But this is a country of a couple of million people that, perhaps against most people’s expectations, hosted and put on an outstandingly well-organized World Cup.”

“So, in many ways, they’ve proven that they can execute on a plan, and I think you know they’re very likely to continue to make inroads into the industry in the next few years.”
