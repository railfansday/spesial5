East Carolina throws its hat back into title race with 10-6 win at Army

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The American Conference championship race saw a whirlwind of upsets in Saturday's noon slate, and implications of a wild afternoon will carry into the final week of the regular season.

North Texas squandered a 10-point fourth-quarter advantage in a loss to 5-5 Tulane. Memphis suffered a similar fate, surrendering a 10-point fourth quarter lead to 3-6 Navy. Those upsets kept the door ajar for East Carolina to climb back into the championship picture, and the Pirates seized their opportunity.

ECU stormed into Michie Stadium and escaped with a 10-6 victory over Army in the second-lowest-scoring game of the 2026 season. The pivotal road win also extended the Pirates' historical dominance in the series, improving their all-time record against the Black Knights to 10-1.

East Carolina's defense had been its defining feature all season, and the unit delivered another masterclass in West Point. The Pirates secured their fourth straight win while holding a fourth consecutive opponent to 17 points or fewer. During that stretch, ECU has surrendered just 12.0 points per game. It was the second time this season the defense held a conference foe without a touchdown, while Army failed to reach double-digits for the first time since last season's 28-6 defeat at East Carolina.

While the defense has dominated, ECU rattled off these four straight victories without eclipsing 20 points in any of them. Saturday's contest was no exception as the Pirates came up emptyhanded on each of their first six possessions before Andrew Conrad sunk a 41-yard field goal at the 1:14 mark of the third quarter, knotting the game at 3-3.

Army responded with a field goal of its own, but East Carolina subsequently answered with an Army-esque drive. The Pirates beat the Black Knights at their own game, stringing together a 16-play, 85-yard drive in the fourth quarter that consumed 8:21 of clock. TJ Engleman Jr. carried much of the workload on the series in his 113-yard rushing performance, marking his fifth 100-yard outing in his last seven games.

While Engleman got ECU on the doorstep of the goal line, a trick play ultimately defined the drive. Wide receiver Jason Dickerson fielded a reverse, but he was quickly met by Army defenders well behind the line of scrimmage. Dickerson kept his composure through pressure, juked his way out of trouble, and lobbed a jump pass to an open Landon Sides in the end zone.

Sides, who led all receivers with seven receptions and 74 yards, scored the game's lone touchdown with 4:24 remaining to give East Carolina a 10-6 lead. The defense took care of the rest. Zyeir Gamble intercepted Cale Hellums on fourth down 1:22 left on the clock. While Army still held all three timeouts, Engleman effectively slammed the door on the Black Knights' comeback hopes. On 3rd-and-8, the running back broke loose for a 23-yard gain, allowing the Pirates to enter victory formation.

Army (5-5, 4-3 American) completed its 13th one-score game across its last 16 FBS matchups, falling to 6-7 in such games, including 3-4 this season. With only one more game prior to the release of the bowl schedule, Jeff Monken's faces a must-win scenario at Rice. A victory would ensure a third consecutive bowl appearance, a feat the program has only accomplished one other time before, from 2016-18.

East Carolina (7-4, 5-2 American) strengthened its résumé and kept its championship hopes alive. The Pirates improved to 21-9 overall and 15-5 in conference games since promoting former defensive coordinator Blake Harrell to head coach in October 2024. Despite holding membership in the league since 2014, they still have never qualified for the American Conference Championship Game. However, an opportunity to end that drought presents itself during the final week of the regular season.

ECU is currently in a three-way tie with Memphis and North Texas for second place in the conference standings, only trailing UTSA. The Pirates can clinch the title game with a Week 13 home victory over Florida Atlantic, as long as Temple also upsets Memphis and UAB upsets North Texas. But if North Texas loses and ECU is only tied with Memphis, the Pirates lose the head-to-head tiebreaker to the Tigers. As long as ECU remains tied with North Texas — regardless of Memphis' result — computer rankings would ultimately determine who advances.
