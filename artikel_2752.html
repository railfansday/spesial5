Gameday column: Bills and the current state of strength of schedule

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It's like each week there's arisen a new narrative about this Buffalo Bills team.

Leading into Week 11 against the Dolphins, it was all about strength of schedule… probably because of Buffalo's competition — or lack thereof — since the bye week.

I'll be the first to mention that teams are not always what their record says they are.

But what these Bills are doing is subtly different from previous Bills teams during the Josh Allen era.

What has remained the same is Buffalo's regular-season dominance.

Since 2020, the Bills have been the NFL's best team in the regular season. They have the most wins and the highest point differential by an insane margin.

But like I wrote a week ago, the Sean McDermott clubs always had an uncharacteristic stumble or two during said regular season that kept them away out of the No. 1 seat in the AFC playoffs.

Of course today, against the Dolphins, represents one of those "stumble" possibilities with the colossal clash on Thanksgiving against the Chiefs looming. But that's the not the crux of this column.

Are the Bills the best 8-1 team in NFL history? No. Are they the best 8-1 team in the past decade. No.

But they've done exactly what a team should do against the competition level it has faced.

Regarding the aforementioned point differential, it's my absolute favorite measure of team quality… because it's straightforward and pretty darn predictive.

How's this for point differential's stickiness — 11 of the past 12 Super Bowl winners finished the regular season in the Top 6 in point differential. Insane, right? From 2004 – 2025, 17 of 22 of the clubs that hoisted the Lombardi Trophy at season's end were a Top 6 point-differential team during the regular season.

And this Bills team enters this AFC East game against the Dolphins second in point differential, at +88.

If Buffalo had been squeaking by lesser opponents regularly, I'd be the first to write their 8-1 feels phony.

And teams can only play the opponents on their schedule.

Among the nine teams at 7-2 or better right now, only the Broncos (.583) and Vikings (.518) have faced a cumulative opponent win percentage above .500. The Bills are third on that list at .480, as their opponents have a combined record of 40-47. The combined record of Chiefs' opponents to date is 34-53 (.391). They're currently in last in that metric.

(another shoutout to Jeremy White from WGR550 who noticed this earlier this week.)

Oh, and as for the strength-of-schedule element that legitimately matters come playoff-seeding time — the Bills currently have the second-best strength of victory at .455, behind only the Broncos, the sole AFC club with a strength of victory over .500.

On this note, the first half of the 2026 NFL season has shown a more sizable gap between the great teams and the noticeably bad ones.

Ahead of Week 11, nine teams with seven or more wins is the most in any season in the NFL since at least 2001. Eight clubs with seven or more losses is tied for the second-most in that time frame.

And, heck, the Bills are 16.5-point favorites at home in the division. The Chiefs are 15.5-point favorites at home against the Cardinals. The Broncos are 10.5-point favorites against the Raiders at home. And the Jaguars are 6-point favorites on the road against the 5-3-1 Giants. Vegas thinks the dichotomy will continue.

The Bills can't focus on proving themselves today against the Dolphins. And they don't have to be concerned about that. They need to stack another win to get to 9-1 before their lengthy test of the holiday season that fittingly begins on Thanksgiving and ends on Christmas, a five-game stretch against the Chiefs, Patriots, Packers, Bears, and Broncos.

We'll know who this Bills team is by then.

But entering that stretch with the AFC's best record possible needs to be the emphasis of today's goal. The quality of opponent is irrelevant.
