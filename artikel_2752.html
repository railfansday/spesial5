'We're not going to talk about schematics': Cal PR blocks QB from answering question

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Tosh Lupoi's debut season in Berkeley has supplied plenty of oddities, but even by those standards, Saturday's big game was a two-part spectacle, with a perplexing quarterback plan on the field followed by an even more perplexing intervention at the podium.

Long before the postgame theatrics, Cal's offense had onlookers scratching their heads throughout a 44-28 loss to Stanford. Star quarterback Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele was calling plays in the huddle before trotting out to line up at receiver, running back Adam Mohammed was taking direct snaps, and third-string quarterback Dominic Ingrassia was frequently spelling the sophomore under center.

When the broadcast asked Lupoi to explain the strategy, he offered about as little as a head coach possibly could.

"We're just doing everything we can to win the game," Lupoi said.

Lupoi was considerably more expansive after the game, but not necessarily any clearer. Over several meandering minutes, the first-year coach explained that Sagapolutele had been hobbled by a high-low hit in Cal's previous game, couldn't practice early in the week, and threw a grand total of two passes on Wednesday, when what Lupoi called "the monsoon" rolled through Berkeley.

By Thursday, Sagapolutele was at roughly 50%, so the staff settled on a contingency plan that kept him out of harm's way and only opened up the passing game if the weather allowed it. That's why Sagapolutele threw more once the rain eased up in the second half.

"The guy's an absolute warrior, so I'm thankful, didn't want to put him in any harm's way," Lupoi said.

When a reporter pointed out that Sagapolutele apparently wasn't healthy enough to start the game but was fine to play once the rain let up, Lupoi insisted his quarterback did, in fact, start, noting he was on the field for the first play. That was technically true, provided you count lining up at receiver.

That made Ingrassia the natural candidate to shed some light on how the arrangement looked from inside the huddle. He never got the opportunity, however, as a Cal staffer cut in after a reporter asked about Sagapolutele's role in the offense.

"We're not going to talk about schematics, next question," the staffer said.

That's a curious line to draw in the sand, considering Lupoi had just spent several minutes at that same podium describing the injury, the waterlogged practice week, and the weather-dependent game plan in exhaustive, if somewhat rambling, detail. Whatever schematic secrets Cal was so intent on guarding, its head coach had already spilled most of them, and anyone who watched Cal's offense for four quarters had seen the rest.

Somebody in Cal's communications department might want to brush up on the Streisand effect.
