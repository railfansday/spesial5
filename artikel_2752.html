Celtics will be without star player vs Toronto Raptors

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

TORONTO — The Celtics and Raptors will each miss key players as they meet on Wednesday night.

For the Celtics, Paul George is out with an illness; the star is just a day removed from a 36-point performance against his former squad, the Philadelphia 76ers. Everyone else on the Celtics will play on the second night of a road back-to-back; the team flew from Philadelphia to Toronto late Tuesday night, and Mitchell Robinson and Jayson Tatum, both of whom have gotten one rest game this season, are among them.

George has been the Celtics' second-leading scorer so far this season; he's averaging 22 points, 5.2 rebounds, 3.8 assists, and 2.1 steals per game. He's also been the team's most efficient scorer, shooting 48.8% from the field and 44% from three. The Celtics are 0-1 without him so far this season; they lost by 16 to the Houston Rockets last Monday.

The Raptors could be significantly shorthanded; Scottie Barnes (left groin strain) and Kawhi Leonard (left knee injury management) are both out, while Trayce Jackson-Davis (illness), Colin Murray-Boyles (illness), and Immanuel Quickley (right hamstring injury management) are all questionable.

After blowing out the 76ers on Tuesday night, the Celtics lead the Eastern Conference at 9-2. The Raptors have the 6th-best record in the East at 5-5. The Celtics have won four straight; the Raptors are coming off a loss to the Memphis Grizzlies.

The Celtics are in the middle of their third back-to-back of the season, and Jayson Tatum has played in every single back-to-back game so far.

But last week in Charlotte, he said he's receptive to conversations around his rest management.

"It's my 10th year, so I understand the balance of wanting to play every game, and rest, and back-to-backs, three-in-fours, five-in-sevens," Tatum said. "It's always a conversation. We talk about it. We have a plan. I don't really like sitting out on away games, but we just kind of figure it out as we go."

Tatum said he does not like missing road games, and that has factored into his decision to play on the second nights of multiple back-to-backs this season.

"[That's] part of it, and just kind of the mental aspect of it," he said. "I used to always play back-to-backs, and obviously health is most important, and how I'm feeling physically. But it was an opportunity for me to test myself out, to see how I felt playing the back-to-backs, traveling, getting in at a certain time, and see how I felt after that. I kind of just really wanted to go through that for myself, and I felt really good."
