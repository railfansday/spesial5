Knicks rally to beat Cavaliers behind unlikely hero, expanded lineup to keep slim NBA Cup hopes alive

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Mike Brown coached Wednesday's NBA Cup contest like a preseason game. He used a 12-man rotation, throwing out Tyler Nickel for his NBA debut and giving important minutes to sparsely used Pacôme Dadiet.

Brown, as he noted before tipoff, sees the scheduling benefits of being eliminated from Adam Silver's beloved tournament.

But it didn't matter. The Knicks (12-6), winners of six straight overall, surged in the fourth quarter behind unlikely hero Jose Alvarado.

Mikal Bridges celebrates after hitting a 3-pointer during the Knicks' 121-102 win over the Cavaliers on Nov. 25, 2026, at Madison Square Garden. Jason Szenes for NY Post

They avoided Cup elimination — at least for a couple of days — with a 121-102 win over the collapsing Cavaliers, who blew an 11-point lead late in the third quarter.

The Knicks (1-2 in group play) remain unlikely to advance. To win the group, they'd need a victory over the Heat on Friday by at least seven points and a Sixers loss to the Pacers. Advancing as the wild card is more complicated and unlikely.

But the defending Cup champs aren't technically eliminated as of Thanksgiving.

They were short-handed Wednesday without OG Anunoby (calf strain) and Miles McBride (left adductor tightness). They lost backup center Andre Drummond to a head injury in the first half.

Then they punished the Cavs (9-10) by winning the fourth quarter 39-17 with Alvarado scoring 16 of his 21 points in the final period and Brown going deep into his bench.

"I looked at the bench in the first quarter and realized, 'Wow, we played like 10 people in that first quarter,' " said Josh Hart, who had 15 points and 11 assists.

Jose Alvarado celebrates after hitting a second 3-pointer in the fourth quarter of the Knicks' win over the Cavaliers. Jason Szenes for NY Post

Mikal Bridges finished with an efficient 19 points. Karl-Anthony Towns extended his double-double streak to 15 (barely) with 12 points and 10 boards.

If and when Brown's squad is out of the tournament, the coach is looking at elimination as an NBA Cup half full. Losing translates to an easier schedule for the Knicks, which wasn't lost on the coach before Wednesday's comeback win.

Brown knows because the NBA shared the possibilities. Advancement potentially brings another three road games — including the final in Indiana.

Josh Hart reacts during the second quarter of the Knicks' win over the Cavaliers at Madison Square Garden. Jason Szenes for NY Post

And elimination means two games — including one at home — over a nine-day stretch.

"You look at it and you're like, 'Whoa, hell, that's a lot of days off if you don't advance,' " Brown said. "And so, again, you wanna compete, you wanna win, but if you don't … there's an opportunity if you got some guys banged up to get rest and or to work on some things in between the games that you have if you're not involved in the Cup."

Silver won't like that answer from the defending NBA Cup champs. But Brown was speaking from experience.

The Knicks added a long road trip to their schedule last season because of the NBA Cup, then an 83rd game with the final in Las Vegas.

The results were phenomenal for the Knicks. They won the Cup, earned the prize money (each player won over $500,000) and months later captured the big NBA championship.

But the Knicks, as an organization, didn't fully embrace Silver's tournament. It took them nearly a full year to hang the banner.

Then Wednesday, the coach of the Cup champs was openly espousing the virtues of being eliminated.

Mikal Bridges rises up for a layup during the second quarter of the Knicks' win over the Cavaliers at Madison Square Garden. Jason Szenes for NY Post

No crying over a spilled NBA Cup. For now, though, the Cup is still on the table.

"When I talk about the Cup, it adds a level of stress maybe on your mind and your body because the games are intense as you go along and you're playing more games in a short amount of time," Brown said. "I remember last year it was like, 'Game, Game, Game.' And then you win the Cup and you celebrate it that night and you hop on a plane and go play in Indiana the next night. And so it's great. You like the competition and the setup but it adds a lot of stress to you physically and mentally. I'm saying this because especially with the extended season we had last year and how taxing the games were for our playoff run mentally and physically, I'm trying to be smart."
