Documents tie banned Nvidia AI chips to Alice Huang-linked Shanghai data center

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Filings list servers worth 485 million yuan ($73 million) as collateral, including units with restricted Nvidia chips

A Chinese company that Alice Huang founded obtained Nvidia $NVDA AI chips that had been smuggled and then put into service at a Shanghai data center connected to Megaspeed International, according to documents reviewed by Bloomberg.

Alice Huang founded Megaspeed in 2023, according to Bloomberg, and U.S. authorities are investigating the company over suspicions that it illegally shipped Nvidia chips into China. Megaspeed has denied the allegations. It showed investors an image of the facility, known as the Yangtze River Delta Project, in presentation materials from 2024, Bloomberg said.

Some of the servers were sold to Aperia Cloud Services, a Singapore-based Nvidia Cloud Partner, according to Bloomberg. Aperia acquired 22 Dell $DELL Technologies servers that were fitted with Nvidia H800 chips, a model Washington placed off-limits for China sales in 2023. Dell said in a statement that it "stopped all sales and support to Aperia International in February 2025 due to unauthorized diversion concerns."

Singapore detained Aperia's CEO that month, accusing him of cheating server suppliers, according to Bloomberg. He has pleaded not guilty and awaits trial. Aperia has denied wrongdoing, and Singapore prosecutors charged a fifth person in the case on Friday.

A unit of Shanghai Longteng Intelligent Computing, which Alice Huang established 10 years ago, recorded the Dell servers in its filings among a batch of 193 servers located in Shanghai. The company put them up to secure financing in January 2025, according to Bloomberg. The hardware had a combined value of 485 million yuan ($73 million) and included 52 Super Micro Computer servers fitted with H100 or H200 processors from Nvidia. Neither chip can be sold to China unless the seller holds a U.S. license.

Super Micro said the servers in question were sold lawfully under U.S. export control rules and that it is not affiliated with the facility. The filings also name 119 Gigabyte Technology servers that backed those same loans, but they do not identify the chips inside.

The filings gave no detail on how the chips were acquired or on whether Alice Huang had a direct hand in it. She stepped away from her ownership stake in Shanghai Longteng during 2017. Huang Yu took over as shareholder and stayed on as the majority owner through January 2026, according to Bloomberg, which cited official company registry data. That period covers the time the servers were offered as collateral.

Kang Wei, a longtime business associate of Alice Huang, is president of the data center, according to Bloomberg. Kang holds a senior executive role at Shanghai Hexi, the private equity firm that funded the project and previously had her on its payroll.

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, a disclosure that pushed him out of the company, according to Bloomberg. In May 2025, Jensen Huang said there was "no evidence" of chip diversion. Nvidia declined to comment.
