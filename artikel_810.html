The English Lamine Yamal? Why Williams-Barnett is getting Spurs fans excited

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Sky Sports' Callum Bishop takes a look into Tottenham's next big hope Luca Williams-Barnett after the teenager signed a new long-term deal at the club; watch Sunderland vs Tottenham live on Sky Sports Main Event on Super Sunday; kick-off 2.05pm

To earn comparisons to Lamine Yamal after just three Premier League appearances might seem a little far-fetched, but Roberto De Zerbi showed no hesitation.

So highly thought of is Luca Williams-Barnett at Tottenham that the remarks made following his stellar performance in the 2-0 win over Ipswich weren't all that shocking to hear.

"Luca is a special player. I'm not surprised because his talent is bigger than you think," De Zerbi said before simply saying, "yes, I think so," when asked if he could reach Lamine Yamal's level.

The 18-year-old wouldn't even be halfway through his second year of A-levels, but his talent hasn't been a secret. The likes of Bayern Munich and Manchester United had already been linked with the starlet, so the news of a long-term contract at Spurs is one that brings unbridled joy among the fanbase.

An England U19 international, Williams-Barnett made plenty of headlines in his first start. Most notably, he became Tottenham's third-youngest Premier League goalscorer at 18 years and 51 days, only four days older than Gareth Bale, who is second on the list. That name certainly rings a bell.

The winger was also Spurs' sixth-youngest Premier League starter, and it's not just with his club that he's making history.

Just three other players have scored on their first Premier League start at a younger age, while he is only bettered by future Ballon d'Or winner Michael Owen as the youngest player to score and grab an assist in the same game.

The records are a great indicator of the talent that the youngster has. Famous Spurs wonderkids such as Harry Kane, Dele Alli and Glenn Hoddle had yet to make an impact at that age.

But what is it about his game that is really making people stand up and take notice?

De Zerbi said after the Ipswich victory that he asked Williams-Barnett "to play like he plays at the park with his friends, with the same freedom".

It's the sort of cliché that you often hear levelled at emerging talents. Where they often lack in experience and nuance, they make up for in fearlessness and often youthful abandon.

De Zerbi's request was never going to be a challenge for the teenager, though. That trait is what had already earned him admirers from those in the know.

"He deserves the opportunity to play. He's got the swagger, he's got the ability to play in that team," was Tim Sherwood's assessment before he had even kicked a ball this season.

While the swagger can't be denied, his trademark on the pitch is rather straightforward. Williams-Barnett's tendency is to slow the play down and keep the ball close to his feet before showing explosive acceleration to catch his marker off guard.

Though it isn't necessarily the most groundbreaking piece of skill, the youngster possesses an abundance of composure that sees him capable of going on the inside or outside. Something that Anan Khalaili found out the hard way during the youngster's cameo against Crystal Palace.

It's the sort of foundational skill that gave the likes of Ronaldinho and Neymar a platform to develop into the greatest flair players of their generation. Funny that Williams-Barnett should bear some resemblance considering the Brazilian duo appear to play a role in his pre-match routine.

"I've seen today in the bus from the training ground to the stadium, because he was very close with me, while we were coming in the stadium, he was watching on YouTube Ronaldinho, [Eden] Hazard, Neymar," De Zerbi disclosed after the Ipswich win.

He is clearly taking inspiration from what he has seen from these idols. If we are being critical of those superstars, the one thing the youngster might want to avoid adopting from them is their desire off the ball, as his head coach has already challenged him to.

"Work hard without the ball, like [Ousmane] Dembele, like Raphinha, like [Lamine] Yamal, because football is going in a different way and the first defenders are the attackers. Even if they are Ballon d'Or winners, or any players with talent, they have to work on the pitch."

To that degree, the gauntlet has been set by De Zerbi. But there is no doubting that Williams-Barnett has the backing of those at Spurs to go and make himself a huge success.

"What stands out most for me is his courage and bravery because as a young lad coming into Spurs first team and performing like that is not easy. He will have a very big future," said team-mate Omar Marmoush.

It has been over a decade since the club had a youth product that has captured the imagination in quite this way. Kane is the most obvious example, but not even he immediately made fans pay attention to this extent.

As is the case with every young player, patience is a virtue. But with a new contract, De Zerbi's blessing and a start to Premier League life that could hardly go any better, the sky is the limit for Williams-Barnett.

It is far too early to say exactly how far he goes. But if he shoots for the moon and misses, he will still become one of the stars.
