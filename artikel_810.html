Inside the rise of 'humble' Nyoni and why Iraola trusts him

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Trey Nyoni scored his first Liverpool goal earlier this month

Trey Nyoni knew this was the season when opportunity beckoned for him at Liverpool.

With Curtis Jones leaving for Inter Milan and the club deciding not to buy another midfielder, Nyoni saw the prospect of regular first-team action if he impressed new head coach Andoni Iraola in pre-season.

First impressions matter. So on the day he returned from a holiday in Morocco in June, Nyoni went from the airport to a personal training session in Leicester - where the 19-year-old grew up and where his family are still based.

Nyoni sought the help of a private football coaching operation as he targeted off-season gains before joining up again with Liverpool.

Coach Steve Stavrou and his son Emillio have also worked with players including Harvey Barnes and Haji Wright. Nyoni asked to work on line-breaking passes, getting away from pressure and improving his finishing.

"We merely supplemented the ability he's already got. He's a quiet and humble lad but knows his ability on the pitch and plays with such a confidence," Emillio Stavrou tells BBC Sport.

Under Iraola, Nyoni has thrived after a stellar pre-season, making 13 appearances already, just one shy of his total appearances last season.

Earlier this month, he scored his first goal for the club in Istanbul.

Liverpool ultimately lost to Fenerbahce in the Champions League that night and Nyoni's belief that "the night has been ruined" showed his inner competitiveness.

His first Premier League start came against last season's champions Arsenal on 1 November.

Nyoni fouled Arsenal's captain early on, leading one source to remark: "He's a nice kid but you saw that steel too with the way he went into Martin Odegaard."

Perhaps the most significant praise came from Liverpool legend Steven Gerrard, who said he couldn't see why Iraola was taking Nyoni off just before the hour mark in that game.

Iraola was managing Nyoni's workload, with the midfielder playing 85 minutes three days later in Turkey.

By now, it is clear that Iraola is unafraid to use him.

"He has to continue in the same place he has been since pre-season: being ready for every game. He is playing all the games lately - so we trust him," said Iraola.

That speaks volumes of Nyoni's rise since joining from Leicester City in 2023 and making his Liverpool debut under Jurgen Klopp as a 16-year-old.

Nyoni, whose parents have Zimbabwean heritage and support Liverpool, is in advanced talks over a new deal.

Having made his England Under-21 debut last week, this is turning into a breakout season with club and country.

Iraola saw enough over pre-season to know the teenager would play a prominent part in the new campaign. He only got 237 minutes last season under Arne Slot but has already played 619 minutes this time around.

Slot saw the potential of Nyoni, who trained regularly with the first team. But, given Liverpool's struggles in 2025-26, the Dutchman was reluctant to experiment.

Under Iraola, Nyoni is finding his feet in a deeper midfield role than he had been used to, seeing more of the ball.

"I've been playing like a number six. I played there when I was young, but it's a new position for me. I've had to learn a lot in a short space of time," Nyoni told The Inside Scoop., external

The time he spent before pre-season impressed on the coaches there what a talent Nyoni possessed.

"In the drills, he'd make everything game realistic. Everything was with intent and you see a lot of what he does is with disguise, particularly with his passes," Emillio Stavrou recalls.

With two games a week now the norm for Liverpool, Iraola will continue to rotate his team.

But seeing who he names in midfield against Manchester United on Sunday will be interesting because Nyoni is showing that he can fight for a position alongside the likes of Dominik Szoboszlai, Ryan Gravenberch and Alexis Mac Allister.

Inevitably there is work to be done, particularly in terms of endurance and physicality, in order to cope with the rigours of playing consistently at the top level, but it is so far, so good for Nyoni.

In a similar manner to Ngumoha's emergence last season, Liverpool were keen not to block Nyoni's pathway, and under Iraola he has been given the platform to thrive.

Liverpool are still only an injury away from looking short of options, so getting another midfielder remains a priority for next summer.

Monaco's Lamine Camara and Bournemouth's Alex Scott are two players of interest.

In the here and now, Nyoni needed this opening. He has come out of his shell and feels a lot more like a part of the squad. That quiet nature is slowly changing and that was visible in an interview he did while on England duty.

Asked how Liverpool are doing by one of the hosts, who supports Manchester United, Nyoni simply responded: "Better than United."

Deep down, there is a young man that backs himself and by all accounts, loves football. Neymar is his favourite player.

"He's obsessed with training and obsessed with getting better. The first session we had with him after he'd just got off the plane tells you about him," adds Emillio Stavrou.

Nyoni told Stavrou's little brother in the summer that he backed himself to play regularly for Liverpool.
