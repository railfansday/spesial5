Hirst key for Stoke, injuries derail Millwall - Sam Parkin's EFL takeaways

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

After the last international break of the year, Final Score pundit and former Swindon Town, Luton Town and Northampton Town striker selects his takeaways from the Championship, League One and League Two.

Stoke City are the Championship's form team, having taken 32 points from the last 39, but can they stay the distance in the promotion race?

Parkin: "I think everyone, probably me included, is really optimistic about their chances of this being the season they get it right.

"We're a fair chunk into the season now and I thought today's result was really important because I believe this was their first win over anyone in the top eight. It felt like a bit of a statement win because Birmingham, apart from their defeat at Cardiff, have been really competitive in more or less every game.

"They've been able to just come out the right side of the scoreline and they've had to rely on their goalkeeper [Viktor] Johansson, who I think is one of the top goalkeepers at this level.

"I think there was an early shift in formation, going to a back three in the early part of the campaign, and I think that's proved quite important. They're keeping clean sheets and they've got a goalscorer in George Hirst.

"I read a lot about Stoke City fans saying they're still probably short on forwards who are going to get them the goals. I know that [Jack] McGlynn has chipped in with a really impressive number from midfield, but for Hirst to get that big winning goal, that will have done his confidence the world of good.

"He's been promoted previously with Ipswich and there's no reason why he can't be Stoke's main man up front."

Millwall have slumped to 16th after fighting for promotion in the last few seasons and they have lost five of their last seven league games.

Parkin: "They've had to deal with a horrendous injury crisis. It was at a level that I was struggling to think of a comparable club in the early part of the season. They were just having players go down week after week.

"There was one game in particular I covered against Wrexham and they had the walking wounded (out there), so I think they're still recovering from that.

"They've got some of the main players in [Casper] De Norre back fit now and available, but I think [Josh] Coburn was unavailable again today, and [Mihailo] Ivanovic is just making his way back - I would say they are three of their more important players.

"Take away [Jake] Cooper at the back, who's been a mainstay for a number of years, and I think that trio I've just mentioned are so fundamental to their chances this season.

"They're still playing catch up to a degree, but Alex Neil has not pulled any punches in some of his interviews and he'll be disappointed.

"On the flip side of that, QPR are in a really good place at the moment, and are a much tougher proposition than maybe they would have been over the last couple of seasons.

"I think Millwall will improve over time. I don't see them emulating what they did last season, but I think they can be optimistic they can get some better results once everyone's back available."

Lincoln City management duo Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw

Lincoln City are finding life in the Championship tough after romping to the League One title last season and they have lost seven of their last nine league games.

Parkin: "It's been a downward spiral and the home form's a bit of an issue. They just have the one win over Burnley there - and Burnley were in dire straits when they came.

"They've lost four in a row so that will be a little bit of a concern because they found a formula on the road in the early part of the season. But, yeah, you've got to do better in your home form.

"I still trust they're going to be competitive and the majority of that squad, Jack Moylan apart, is still there from last season. You don't get over 100 points and then lose everything.

"That's always going to count for something when you've got the nucleus of that squad still together, even if it is in a higher division. So I think the spirit will remain amongst that group, despite the recent defeats.

"They've got to start taking points off the better teams in the division as well. All their victories have come against teams who are in the lower part of the table, like Preston and Burnley.

"They won at Bolton and at Bristol City, so they're not really mixing it with the teams in the top half of the table at the moment.

"But I think the bottom line is the management duo would have absolutely taken being five points above 22nd at this stage of the season, so they've given themselves a good opportunity to stay in the division."

Barnsley are four points from safety at the bottom of League One after Friday's defeat by Sheffield Wednesday in the South Yorkshire derby and are on course for a first return to the fourth tier since 1979.

Parkin: "I think on last night's evidence, they're a better team than the league table suggests. That's an absolute certainty in my mind.

"My one concern is where are the goals going to come from? For all their dangerous play, which is incredibly aggressive and energetic, with and without the ball, where are the goals?

"They throw caution to the wind and there's a real energy and youth about the side, which I actually really admired in defeat.

"It's just translating all that into goals and that was obviously one part of it against Wednesday, and giving away bad goals - they've conceded the most set pieces now in the division.

"They conceded in that manner again, and because they play such a high line, they (also) get exposed by teams on the counter. Maybe that's a little bit of naivety. Maybe that's a little bit lack of experience, which they can go some way to mending in January when the transfer window opens.

"But they may be adrift at the bottom if they don't turn things around soon. In short, I think they're a better team than a relegated team. But the most important thing in football, as we know, is putting the ball in the back of the net and that clearly is a problem for them at the moment."

Crawley Town's Dylan Morgan celebrates scoring against Cheltenham Town

Crawley Town boosted their chances of staying in the EFL when they recorded their first home league win of the season over Fleetwood Town and they have generated some momentum in their last few matches.

Parkin: "Former Leeds and Sheffield United manager Kevin Blackwell is in charge now till the end of the season. I was injured for the majority of his spell at Luton Town when he was my manager, but I'm kind of well versed in his methods and he will make them really hard to beat.

"That's always the first thing you want when you take over a struggling side, you need to stop shipping goals. You need to get them organised. You need to work on set-pieces and be a threat on that front. He will do all those things.

"I think the three results in the league, two wins and a draw, have showed you that his methods have already come to fruition. His approach will be very different to probably what the players were being asked to do under Scott Lindsey.

"It'll be a different kind of football. They'll play long at times, they'll want to play in the opposition's half. I think in terms of a plan to get survival, he could very well be the man to supply that.

"They've chosen him after he was initially brought in as interim manager because the performances have improved across the last few weeks, despite the embarrassment of the FA Cup defeat to Wimborne.

"He's made them competitive very quickly and I think a lot of teams are going to be in for a real game when they face them."

Sam Parkin was speaking to BBC Sport England's David Anderson
