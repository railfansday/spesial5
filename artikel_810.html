Travis Scott and Don Toliver Sued Over Sample on Hit JACKBOYS Track ‘What To Do?’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A music producer says he deserves master royalties for providing a beat sampled in the 2019 song.

Travis Scott, Don Toliver and other collaborators in the hip-hop collective JACKBOYS are facing a copyright lawsuit over their 2019 track “What To Do?”.

The JACKBOYS team was sued on Tuesday (Nov. 17) by Niv Kalisky, an Israeli music producer who goes by “Early Yellow.” The lawsuit, obtained and first reported by Billboard, alleges that Kalisky hasn’t gotten the master points he deserves for creating a sample used by the Scott-led collective of artists from his label, Cactus Jack Records.

“What To Do?” was one of seven tracks on the group’s self-titled 2019 album JACKBOYS, which debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 and was followed up last summer by another chart-topping set, JACKBOYS 2. The song itself charted at No. 56 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 20 on Billboard‘s Hot Rap Songs chart.

Kalisky is credited as a songwriter and additional producer on “What To Do?”, meaning he has received publishing income for the composition. But the lawsuit claims he’s also entitled to master royalties for providing the track’s producers with a sample beat that was later mixed into the final sound recording.

According to the complaint, Kalisky’s lawyers sent multiple royalty requests over the years to both Cactus Jack and its distribution partner, Sony’s Epic Records, but without any success. Kalisky’s team also submitted takedown requests for the song to Spotify, Apple Music and other digital streaming services, but Sony allegedly canceled them out with counter-notices to the streamers.

“It is clear that the Defendants had actual knowledge that the Plaintiff had received zero compensation for the usage of the sound recording contained in the registered sample,” reads the lawsuit. “In fact, in response to the Plaintiff’s several written requests for payment, income accounting statements, and proper sound recording royalties, the Defendants continued to tell the Plaintiff that they would resolve the matter, yet the Defendants never attempted to make good faith or any efforts at all on its repeated promises to do just that.”

Kalisky is now leveling copyright infringement claims against Cactus Jack, Sony, Scott, Toliver and fellow JACKBOYS member Sheck Wes, as well as various producers who worked on “What To Do?”. He wants to be awarded a portion of the “substantial” recording revenue generated by the track, including through album sales, streaming and synch licensing.

Kalisky’s lawyers, Justin M. Jacobson and Daniel Cruz Hernandez, told Billboard on Tuesday, “Producers are becoming more aware of their rights, and today our client chose to stand up for what he deserves.”

Reps for the various defendants did not immediately return a request for comment on the lawsuit.
