SpaceX and Tesla have named five directors to guide their merger talks

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The directors lead two special committees negotiating a possible all-stock deal, with three banks advising the companies

SpaceX and Tesla $TSLA have appointed five directors to lead the special committees negotiating a possible merger, according to the Financial Times. Three of the directors come from Tesla and two from SpaceX.

The SpaceX directors are Roelof Botha and Randy Glein. Tesla is represented by Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung. The five are viewed as having no financial stake in the outcome, which qualifies them to steer the talks, according to the Financial Times.

The committees have brought on advisers to build the financial models each side will lean on when setting its bargaining stance for a possible all-stock merger, according to the Financial Times. Morgan Stanley $MS and Goldman Sachs $GS are advising SpaceX, and Evercore is advising Tesla.

The bankers are examining the areas where the two companies' businesses overlap, such as artificial intelligence models, semiconductor design and data center operations, according to the Financial Times. After the financial terms are worked out, the companies would move on to questions of governance, among them whether to name a chairperson, who would serve as CFO and which directors would keep their seats. The people cited by the Financial Times said the transaction might be wrapped up before the year closes.

A combination faces regulatory trouble because SpaceX holds U.S. defense contracts and Tesla depends on China, according to Reuters. Tesla and SpaceX did not immediately respond to requests for comment from Reuters and Benzinga.

The companies had been making headway in merger talks earlier this month, and an announcement could come as soon as December, according to Bloomberg.

Elon Musk disclosed in October, on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call, that the boards had formed special committees of independent directors to examine a combination. "Because I'm a conflicted party, I can't actually be in the discussions of the special committees," Musk said. He said a merger would be a good idea but that shareholders have to support it, and that a deal would need the backing of 50% of all Tesla shareholders, not just a majority of votes cast.
