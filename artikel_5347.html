Saylor's Strategy Spends $228.1 Million Buying Back Shares, 7x More than Bitcoin

Strategy, formerly MicroStrategy, spent $228.1 million buying back its STRC preferred stock between Nov. 9 and Nov. 15, according to the company's new filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). Over the same period, Strategy bought 485 BTC for $33.4 million, so the company spent almost seven times more on STRC than on Bitcoin.

"Strategy has acquired 485 BTC and repurchased $228M of STRC. As of 11/15/26, we hold 850,849 BTC and $4.9B of USD Assets," Michael Saylor wrote on X.

The filing shows that Strategy sold 2.09 million shares of its Class A common stock and raised $244.7 million. The company used $206.3 million of that amount for STRC buybacks and added another $21.8 million from interest earned on its dollar reserves.

The 485 BTC were bought at an average price of $68,887 per coin. The remaining $5 million from the share sales stayed on the books as free cash.

STRC, also known as Stretch, is a perpetual preferred stock with a variable monthly dividend, and Strategy launched it in July 2025.

When the price of Bitcoin slipped below $65,000, STRC fell to $99.08. Strategy aims to keep STRC close to its $100 par value, and the company has another $332.2 million set aside for buybacks. The board of Strategy also approved a December STRC dividend at an annual rate of 12%, or $1.00 per share.

"USD Duration is 3.4 yrs and STRC's BTC Credit tightened to 67 bps (-3 bps), assuming 10% BTC ARR, 40% BTC Vol, and a BTC price of $68,800," Saylor added.

As of Nov. 15, Strategy held 850,849 BTC, bought for a total of $64.2 billion at an average price of $75,452 per coin. On Monday, the price of Bitcoin stands at $63,941, and Strategy's holdings are worth around $54.4 billion.

The unrealized loss of the company on its Bitcoin holdings stands at around $9.8 billion. On Sunday, when the price of Bitcoin fell to $62,029 on Bitfinex, the loss was above $11 billion.

Strategy also holds $4.9 billion in dollar assets, and $4.75 billion of that amount is in its dedicated USD reserve.
