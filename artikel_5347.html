U.S. charges Russian man with stalking Palantir workers over Ukraine work

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Prosecutors say Igor D'iu was recruited over Telegram to surveil employees of a Palo Alto company because of its help to Ukraine

The Justice Department announced charges on Wednesday against Russian national Igor D'iu, accusing him of acting as an agent of Russia and stalking employees of a Silicon Valley technology and defense company. A federal grand jury indicted him on four counts: conspiracy to act as a foreign agent, acting as a foreign agent without notice to the Attorney General, interstate stalking and fraud in connection with identification documents.

The department did not name the company, which has an office in Palo Alto, California. The company was Palantir $PLTR Technologies, Bloomberg revealed. According to CBS News, context in the charging documents hints that the company may be Palantir. Palantir did not immediately respond to requests for comment, according to both outlets.

"The allegations in this case describe a state-sponsored operation to target and harass employees of an American technology and defense company because of that company's assistance to Ukraine in its war with Russia," U.S. Attorney Craig H. Missakian of the Northern District of California said in a statement.

According to the indictment and a supporting affidavit, co-conspirators recruited D'iu through Telegram in 2026. They told him the work was for "the Motherland" and tasked him with collecting intelligence on the company, citing its supply of weapons used against Russia in Ukraine. He traveled from Istanbul to Mexico City in June, where he was connected with a person who helped him cross the U.S. border, prosecutors say.

The co-conspirators told him to buy laptops, phones, networking equipment and remote access software, the affidavit says. They directed him to photograph employees' laptop screens at a coffee shop near the Palo Alto office and to place a laptop near the company so they could access it remotely.

Prosecutors say D'iu searched for employees' home addresses, including that of a senior executive. On or about Sept. 3, he followed a female employee for close to 30 miles from Palo Alto toward San Francisco, running a red light to stay behind her. She called 911 and drove to a San Francisco police station, and D'iu ended the pursuit, according to the Justice Department.

Investigators intercepted two packages from overseas containing a falsified California ID card, an altered U.S. passport and a Social Security card in the name "Hugo Landry Johns," the affidavit says. FBI agents searched D'iu and his East Palo Alto residence in October. They recovered about $18,000 in cash, two laptops, two iPhones, two Russian passports and a notebook of 10 addresses, many matching employees' residences. The notebook also held profiles of at least three employees.

The FBI also uncovered exchanges in which co-conspirators shared pictures and addresses for at least four employees and instructed D'iu to pretend to be a food courier, according to CBS News.

The U.S. Attorney's Office said D'iu was arrested on Oct. 30. He first appeared in court on Nov. 2 and is still being held by federal authorities. His next appearance is scheduled for Nov. 24.

If convicted, D'iu faces up to 10 years in prison on the foreign-agent count without notice to the Attorney General, up to five years each on the conspiracy and stalking counts, and up to one year on the fraud count. An indictment alleges crimes, and defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.
