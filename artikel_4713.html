The Part of Your Holiday Hangover You Can’t See

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

FOUR GLASSES OF wine? Your liver drinks them, too. Extra gravy? Your liver probably would have liked for you to stop at the third scoop, but you’re in this together. Nearly every bite you eat and every drink you down ends up getting managed by the same organ in your body: your liver.

The liver is a workhorse that plays a role in over 500 bodily functions, which include filtering toxins, regulating blood sugar, processing cholesterol, and more. But come the holidays, all the food and festive drinking can put your body’s largest organ to the test. Here’s how the wreckage happens and how to avoid accumulating long-term damage.

WHEN YOU HAVE a drink or two, your liver kicks into gear and performs triage: It transforms alcohol into harmless water and carbon dioxide, which you then pee out or exhale. The liver also holds on to anything it can use, such as the antioxidants in wine or the carbs in beer. But that doesn’t mean alcohol is nutritious. Metabolizing alcohol also creates fatty acids along the way, which get stored as triglycerides—a kind of cholesterol—until your body can use them up. Typically, if you eat and drink moderately, your body processes them just fine. Until you...

THE MORE YOU drink, the more your liver shuffles those triglycerides into the “burn later” pile...until the pile is so big that your liver can’t get to the bottom of it.

“If you binge, then binge again, then binge again, your liver gets shocked each time and can’t recover,” says Christopher Koh, MD, clinical director at the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases.

You may not feel the damage on your liver after the holiday or the next one. But over time, the holidays may overwhelm the organ. This can look like increased inflammation that eventually kills off liver cells, leaving scar tissue in their place (and can lead to cirrhosis, a problem in which 25- to 34-year-olds have the highest risk of death compared with other age groups). Not good. Experts say just three get-togethers a week through the holiday season can initiate inflammation if each outing involves more than five servings of alcohol in two hours. Not to mention when...

YOU DON’T EVEN have to drink alcohol to mess up your liver. Seasonal foods (you know the sides that are delicious but are high in saturated fat or refined carbs, along with processed red meat) combined with holiday-associated weight gain may increase the risk of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly known as nonalcoholic fatty liver disease.

“It’s estimated that up to 30 percent of the US population has [MASLD], and there aren’t symptoms, so people usually don’t know and aren’t doing anything to reverse it,” says Mazen Noureddin, MD, hepatologist at Houston Methodist.

As obesity continues to rise, researchers estimate MASLD rates will rise to over 41 percent of the US population in 2050. That fat is wreaking the same havoc alcohol does—causing inflammation and eventual scarring—without the libations. MASLD may develop in as little as six weeks if you really overdo it.

YOU DON’T NEED to buy an activated-charcoal smoothie or a beet-juice detox cleanse to do it. Your liver can regenerate and naturally get itself back in working order if you move the heck out of its way. A month or so without a drop of alcohol (no beer during the game, no wine during date night) can reduce the fat storage in the liver and help tame inflammation. You can also undo some of the fat that causes MASLD by cleaning up your diet and cutting portion sizes: Losing 5 percent of your weight can reduce up to 25 percent of liver fat.
