Ex-PGMOL Chief Drops Verdict on Leny Yoro Red Card & Man Utd Appeal Hopes

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

All eyes were on Anfield as Manchester United travelled to Liverpool for Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge. The affair, however, was as good as over after just 10 minutes.

With Alexander Isak running through on goal, Lenny Yoro clipped the striker's heels. As the last man, the Man Utd defender was consequently sent off for Denying an Obvious Goal-Scoring Opportunity (DOGSO).

Dominik Szoboszlai scored the resulting free-kick, and Liverpool didn't look back from there. Further goals from Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz sealed a comprehensive 4-0 win.

Even so, the controversial red card has sparked plenty of debate in the post-match analysis.

The main argument against the decision made by referee Sam Barrott was that Isak's touch was so heavy that he wouldn't have actually been able to reach the ball before Senne Lammens. While Yoro clearly clipped the Liverpool forward, seeing as the chance was probably gone, it wasn't necessarily DOGSO.

Former PGMOL chief Keith Hackett made this point on social media. He explained:

"Judge the foul - yes it was a foul. Now apply the DOGSO Criteria. Result should be yellow, NOT red."

Speaking to Football Insider, Hackett then claimed that Man Utd have a good chance of winning an appeal, now that the Red Devils have officially notified the FA of their decision. He explained:

"These decisions tend to be subjective. I do like this referee. I think he's a very good referee. And he's faced with that incident. He's got to make a decision on what he sees. I'd have gone, 'Spot on'. Then I started looking at the replays. And that's when doubt comes in.

"It's patently evident to me that the forward has got little chance with a poor touch of gaining control again, and the goalkeeper's going to gain possession.

"And you know when you're dismissing a player, you can't have an element of doubt, and therefore, for me I've gone, 'That's yellow, not red'.

"Therefore, if Manchester United appeal, and their appeal is based on law and they have the technology to be able to show accurately the speed at which the player was travelling, the attacker, at the point of the foul, and the speed of the ball...That gives grounds for Manchester United to appeal."

Mauricio Pochettino watched his side go down to ten men after just eight minutes.

Despite Hackett's insistence that Yoro was unfortunate to be sent off. Not everyone agrees. Speaking exclusively to GIVEMESPORT, former Premier League referee Graham Barber thought the right decision was made. He explained:

"I think it was a red card. I think everybody in the stadium and most pundits and when I first saw it, I thought it was a red card. and I thought it was, in fact, quite an easy decision."

When asked if Isak's loose touch was enough to overturn the DOGSO red card, Barber didn't feel as though it was clear enough. He said: "The consideration that the referee has to make, he probably has to make these decisions in a millisecond, so I think people need to understand how quickly that has to be computed through his head.

"The distance between the offence and the goal is one of the things he has to consider. He obviously looks at the general direction of play, and then also whether there are the defenders around him or not, but the one that comes into question is the likelihood of Isak winning the ball.

"Who is to say whether Isak would have got the ball or not? He was running at pace, at real pace at that time. Can you overturn a red card based on that? Highly unlikely, and that's why I don't think VAR was brought into the case because no one will ever know unless a red card was never issued, if the foul was never committed, if he would have got onto the ball.

"In that instance alone, with that particular part of the law, I think the decision was correct."
