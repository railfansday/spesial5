Both trackers help you better understand sleep, glucose, activity, diet, stress, and the fertility cycle, and the app experience for members is pretty universal. So, with that in mind, should you upgrade? Here's what you need to know.

First, let's look at price. The Oura Ring 4 will still be your cheapest option, but you can access the Oura Ring 5 in the black shade for $299, which is incredibly affordable.

In terms of durability and looks, I've worn both, and the 5 is definitely more scratch-resistant. It's much more sleek and lightweight in design with a smooth interior that sits nicely on your finger, and I even sized down when moving from the Ring 4 to Ring 5. It's more discreet and much more comfortable and attractive.

The Ring 5 is also more accurate with detecting my activity and the types of activity I'm doing, meaning I'm less often going into the app and editing activities. The Oura Ring 5 uses different sensor technology to the Oura Ring 4, and they work better across different skin tones. 12% more accurate overnight HRV than the Oura Ring 4, in fact.

The Oura Ring 5 also has better battery life, lasting 6 to 9 days on a single charge (compared to the Oura Ring 4's 5 to 8 days). That said, I'd take this information with a pinch of salt, because I've found the battery life declines over time with both, and there's currently no recycling system in place for when your ring runs out of juice completely.

In terms of the app, every member has the same experience. For example, both models provide the same top-notch sleep metrics, including sleep stages (REM sleep, deep sleep, sleep latency) and a sleep score. You can track meals and glucose, stress resilience, activity and steps, menstrual cycle, HRV and heart rate, readiness, and even check Symptom Radar, which detects signs of physical strain on the body over time.

Long-term trends are accessible in the app, including GLP-1 insights and health panels.
