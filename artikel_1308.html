Portuguese Media React to Cristiano Ronaldo's International Retirement

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

At the ripe old age of 41, Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from international football with Portugal. The iconic forward departs having scored a record 146 goals in 234 caps, most notably winning Euro 2016.

In an emotional farewell message, he said: "To the Portuguese people, the day has come to return the number 7 jersey of our national team. I never felt it was exclusively mine. But for 23 years I had the privilege and responsibility of wearing it, defending it, and taking it with me to stadiums all over the world. Today the moment has come to hand it over to the future. It wasn't an easy decision, because there are decisions that aren't made only with the head. They are also made with the heart."

Of course, Ronaldo departs as one of the greatest football players of all time, but he also leaves under a bit of a cloud. His final appearances with the national team before making the decision to retire were rather controversial. Indeed, the Al-Nassr striker walked away from the camp after falling out with manager Jorge Jesus over game time.

Many have felt Ronaldo should have retired years ago, and that he was actually holding the team back due to his diminishing quality. With that in mind, it's interesting to see how those in Portugal have reacted to the big news.

Portuguese boss Jesus has naturally given his thoughts on Ronaldo's retirement. He distanced himself from the decision and praised the player's career, stating:

"This is all his decision. We already had the opportunity to talk about this before the first call-up. I asked him to stay, to stay with me because I needed him to be by my side, but I have to accept his decision"

Jesus added: "He is a world reference, a reference for Portugal, a reference for the National Team. He is a player who, throughout these years, has given a lot to the National Team. He is a symbol for Portuguese youth. Most of his fans are young. I had the opportunity to share the experience of working with him for a year at Al Nassr . Therefore, he will always be a reference for our National Team, for our people, for our history."

With Ronaldo's falling out with Jesus in mind, media outlet O Jogo compared the player's exit to Lionel Messi, noting how the Argentine left international football in much happier circumstances: "Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi marked an era in world football, starring in one of the greatest rivalries in the history of the sport for about two decades. However, the end of their respective international careers is proving to be very different: while the Argentinian had a farewell in a festive and celebratory atmosphere, the Portuguese ended his connection with the National Team without fans anticipating that they would never see him again in the national jersey."

Duarte Gomes, the former referee and former technical director of refereeing at the Portuguese Football Federation (FPF), called it a 'shame' that things ended on such bad terms, noting:

"Cristiano Ronaldo will always be a unique figure in the history of Portuguese and world football. His legacy, his career, and everything he offered to the country deserved a different ending. Hardly anyone could have imagined that such an extraordinary connection could end like this. Not even him.

"What saddens me most is not the decision – inevitable in the short term – but the certainty that it should never have happened this way. This outcome is the culmination of a succession of thoughtless, avoidable, and poorly managed episodes, where a glaring inability of everyone – I repeat, everyone – to find a different solution stood out. Regardless of the underlying reasons that may have guided some and others (now irrelevant), one conclusion seems clear to me: somewhere in the process, an adult was missing from the room. A shame."

It's worth noting that the FPF are now planning a final game for Ronaldo to celebrate his career, as they 'refuse to let this farewell take place without due tribute.'

For the most part, however, media outlets across the country were keen to celebrate Ronaldo's special career, one of the most decorated in football history. A Bola, for example, spoke in fond terms of the departing legend, writing:

"Cristiano Ronaldo's farewell to the National Team marks the end of the most golden and impactful era in the history of Portuguese football. Twenty-three years of pure devotion, impossible goals, unprecedented titles, and global records transformed Portugal's status in the world. From his hopeful debut in Chaves in 2003 to his abrupt retirement in Copenhagen, the eternal captain bids farewell with the weight of legend."

Speaking to A Bola in a separate article, former Portugal manager Luiz Felipe Scolari – who gave Ronaldo his international debut in 2003 – insisted that this was the right moment to step away from the national team. He said: "Ronaldo, you did well. You're calm, you made the right decision," before adding: "You were one of the athletes that Portugal will never forget. And I, as your friend and as someone who admires you, will never forget you either, for everything you did with me and with your teammates."

Adding to that, as quoted in O Jogo, former Portugal and Manchester United teammate Nani said: "Congratulations, brother, on this beautiful journey! You inspired us all and made us believe that, as Portuguese people, we could be the best in the world. You made us feel proud of ourselves, as a people and as a team."

"You are and will always be a role model for all of us who follow you and who have shared wonderful moments with you, both on and off the field. The national team is the pinnacle of our careers, and I am incredibly proud to have been a part of your story. For me, you are the best of all time, not only for the player you were and everything you gave to our national team, but also for what you represented to all of us. Even when we often disagreed, we were always growing, improving, and learning. And we owe that to you as well. For all of this, I can only say: thank you very much, Captain! What you did for Portugal and for all of us will never be forgotten."
