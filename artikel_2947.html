Investors give a thumbs down to Tata Steel-APL Apollo $1.67 billion deal, plunging shares

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Tata Steel, one of the world's leading steel producers, on Monday said it will buy a 26.4% stake in India's largest structural steel pipe and tube maker, APL Apollo, for 155.1 billion rupees ($1.67 billion).

The acquisition will boost Tata Steel's downstream integration to around 53% from 35%, the company said in a stock exchange filing. That will lead to more value addition, improved product mix, and greater exposure to higher-margin downstream segments, it said.

Investors reacted negatively to the deal, with shares of Tata Steel sliding over 9% on Monday, while APL Apollo's stock crashed more than 19%.

While the deal is likely to be positive in the long term, the complex structure and lack of clarity have rattled investors, Deven Choksey, founder and managing director of wealth management firm DRChoksey FinServ, told CNBC.

As per Tata Steel, before acquiring APL Apollo, it will sell its steel tubes business and Tata Steel Colors to APL Apollo for 280 billion rupees. "APL Apollo will initially fund the acquisition through a mix of acquisition financing and subsequently replace the debt with long-term capitalization," Tata Steel said.

"The deal is appearing very complex," said Choksey, adding that minority investors have not been given any clarity or taken into consideration while signing this deal.

Tata Steel will hold an investor call, post-market hours on Monday.

APL Apollo said after the deal it plans to more than double the downstream capacity to 15.5 million tons from the current 7.5 million tons, making it a leading global downstream steel firm.

This capacity addition will be completed by 2035 and would include around 9 million tons per annum of steel tubes and around 6.5 million tons per annum of color-coated products, as per Tata Steel.

Tata Steel will acquire a stake from existing shareholders of APL Apollo for 2,100 rupees per share and will ​also make an open offer to acquire an additional 26% ‌stake for 2,301.40 rupees a piece.

If the open offer is successful, Tata Steel would get a controlling stake of 52.38% in APL Apollo. World's second-largest steel maker ArcelorMittal was also reportedly in the fray to acquire APL Apollo.

The acquisition of APL Apollo would "create a downstream powerhouse for the future" that will serve the growing Indian market, T V Narendran, chief executive and managing director of Tata Steel, said in the release.

Tata Steel, which has an annual crude steel capacity of over 35 million tons per annum, is one of the world's most geographically diversified steel producers with presence across India, Europe and Thailand.
