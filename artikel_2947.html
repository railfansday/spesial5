Celsius vs. Kraft Heinz: One Is Chasing Growth. The Other Is Chasing a Turnaround.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Celsius's revenue nearly doubled in 2025, but its reliance on PepsiCo for almost half of sales adds a risk Kraft Heinz largely avoids.

Choosing between rapid expansion and steady value is a classic investor dilemma. This comparison examines whether Celsius (CELH +1.66%) or Kraft Heinz (KHC +2.57%) is the better long-term addition to your holdings.

Celsius is a fast-moving player in the energy drink space, while Kraft Heinz manages a vast empire of legacy food products. Comparing these two involves weighing the potential of a high-octane growth story against the relative stability of a global consumer giant.

Celsius develops and markets functional drinks designed to appeal to active lifestyles through brands like Celsius, Alani Nu, and Rockstar. The company operates in the competitive world of beverage stocks, focusing on fitness-oriented products. Its distribution is heavily tied to a partnership with PepsiCo, which acts as its primary distributor in the U.S. and Canada. Sales to this partner account for roughly 43.2% of total net revenue, and customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In FY 2025, revenue reached nearly $2.5 billion, representing growth of approximately 85.5% compared to the previous year. This massive surge was supported by expanded distribution and strong consumer demand for its core energy drink lines. The company reported net income of close to $108 million for the period. While profitable, the bottom line saw a decrease from the prior year as the company invested in its three-brand energy strategy and global expansion.

Kraft Heinz maintains a portfolio of iconic household brands, including Heinz, Kraft, Oscar Mayer, and Philadelphia. It serves a diverse global customer base ranging from independent grocery stores to massive mass-market retailers like Walmart (WMT +1.51%). In its latest annual report, filed in early 2026, the company noted that its five largest North American customers account for nearly 46% of segment net sales. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In FY 2025, revenue reached close to $24.9 billion, which was a decrease of approximately 3.5% compared to the prior year. The company reported a net loss of nearly $5.8 billion for the fiscal period. This significant loss was primarily driven by roughly $9.3 billion in impairment losses related to goodwill and intangible assets. These non-cash charges reflect a lower estimated value for certain business segments compared to previous accounting periods.

Celsius faces significant risks due to its heavy reliance on a single distribution partner for nearly half of its revenue. The company is also in the process of integrating recent acquisitions like Alani Nu and Rockstar, which carries risks of brand cannibalization or cultural friction. Furthermore, Celsius is navigating multiple securities lawsuits and increasing regulatory scrutiny regarding the marketing claims of energy drinks. Competition from massive rivals like Monster Beverage (MNST +1.95%) and Coca-Cola (KO +1.86%) remains a constant threat to market share.

Kraft Heinz deals with the increasing purchasing power of consolidated retailers who often demand lower pricing and more favorable terms. The company still carries roughly $59.7 billion in goodwill and intangible assets, meaning further impairment charges could impact future earnings. Its recent decision to divest its North American coffee business also creates execution uncertainty and potential one-time costs. Finally, the company faces intense competition from private-label products and branded rivals like Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP +2.60%) in an inflationary environment.

Kraft Heinz is currently the more affordable option based on both earnings and sales multiples, while Celsius commands a premium for its high-growth trajectory.

While each stock is broadly in the food and beverage industry, they are very different investments. Instead of an apples-to-apples comparison, an investor is looking more at their investment goals. Celsius would work better for those looking for a high-growth stock, while Kraft Heinz works better for a more value-oriented investor seeking dividend payouts.

In the third quarter of 2026, Celsius grew revenue 17% from the prior-year period to $847 million, and international revenue grew 78%. That international sales growth shows that even if sales within the United States start to slow, it still has tremendous revenue growth opportunities globally. Speaking of slowing, something to watch out for is sales of its legacy brand. For the third quarter, its Celsius brand revenue fell 6%. Also, gross margin is starting to decline, from 51.6% in the prior-year period to 47.7% for the first nine months of the year. The opportunity with Celsius is that it can continue to scale its brand. Still, the challenge is that investors are paying for future execution expectations while the company's legacy brand is declining in sales and gross margin.

Kraft Heinz is working on a turnaround, as evidenced by its recent third-quarter earnings report. Organic net sales dropped 1.1%, adjusted earnings per share fell 13.1%, and adjusted operating income dipped 14.6%. To help fuel that turnaround faster, its marketing costs are increasing to 6% of net sales, but the biggest risk in owning Kraft is if that turnaround never materializes. Still, while shareholders wait for the turnaround to take clearer shape, they at least receive a dividend that yields 6.3%.
