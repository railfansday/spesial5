Do you feel better, worse about Detroit Lions after loss to Bears?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

It's never good to lose to a division rival. It's even harder to swallow the loss when it feels like self-inflicted wounds turned a likely win into a painful loss.

That's what Detroit Lions fans are going through this Black Friday, as they try to cope with the Lions blowing a double-digit lead in the fourth quarter to lose a pivotal game to the Chicago Bears. But after the sting of the loss wears off, we'll have to assess the team and what Thursday's loss means for the season.

Maybe you're still not over it. Maybe you're still seething over certain plays or play calls. But for now, let's assess where things are at. Today's Question of the Day is:

My answer: Thursday's game confirmed a lot of good things I knew about the Lions. Their run game is back, their offensive line is playing their best football, and this team's offense will keep them alive in just about any game. Jahmyr Gibbs is an insane weapon to have, and when Jared Goff is dealing, no one can stop them.

Remember that going into this game, the Bears were considered one of the best defenses in football. Detroit just hung 549 yards and 35 points on them—and it probably should have been even more. For three straight weeks now, the Lions have played decent-to-great defenses, and they've hung at least 30 on each. That has to make you feel pretty good right now.

Unfortunately, Thursday also presented the first real test of this defense in months, and they failed spectacularly. All of the problems that we saw earlier in the season—missed assignments, poor run defense, failure to capitalize on opportunities to get off the field—re-emerged. It begs the question as to whether all of Detroit's defensive improvements over the past couple of months were real or the result of playing poor offenses.

Particularly concerning is the play of the linebacking corps, where Detroit doesn't seem to know who they can depend upon. With Jack Campbell out, there is no one linebacker that is the defense's rock. Derrick Barnes barely played in the first half, Malcolm Rodriguez was a huge liability for most of the day, K.J. Britt's magic seems to have worn off, and even Trevor Nowaske struggled.

Additionally, while Derrick Moore had a fantastic first half, Detroit's pass rush disappeared in the second. Aidan Hutchinson's presence was certainly missed constantly in the final two quarters when the Lions defense just needed one more play.

The good news is that Detroit's defense was also missing a few key pieces that they'll likely get back soon. Defensive end DJ Wonnum and safety Chuck Clark would've made a difference in this game. Detroit's nickel and dime looks with Clark, Brian Branch, and Kerby Joseph at safety could alleviate some of the linebacker issues. And Wonnum can help with pass rush.

So, ultimately, I feel a little worse. The defensive problems aren't likely to completely go away soon. But with how the offense is playing right now, I still believe everything can be on the table for this team.
