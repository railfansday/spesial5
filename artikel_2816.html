Nets’ jumbo lineup proving to be intriguing wrinkle — even with mixed results

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

It was deadly in New Orleans. It misfired the next game in Memphis — albeit with lesser ammo. But the Nets have it all the same.

Coach Jordi Fernández will likely use his historically big lineup less as Brooklyn's young guards eventually command more playing time. But he has the supersized wrinkle in his arsenal, ready to go to whenever he needs.

"Yeah, I think it helps," Fernández told The Post. "I mean, you think about Danny [Wolf] at the quote-unquote two, but obviously his versatility of shooting, being able to playmake, also a big body defensively that can rebound: All those things are important. Let's see how we keep evolving."

In each of the past two games, the Nets started five players all 6-foot-9 or taller.

No NBA team had started such a lineup in 37 seasons, when the Lakers fielded A.C. Green (6-foot-9), Magic Johnson (6-9), Mychal Thompson (6-10), Orlando Woolridge (6-9) and James Worthy (6-9) back on April 21, 1990.

Danny Wolf #2 of the Brooklyn Nets dribbles during a game against the Memphis Grizzlies at FedExForum in Memphis, Tenn., Saturday, Nov. 14, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

For Brooklyn, it provided intriguing — albeit mixed — results.

"It just speaks to the type of players we have," center Day'Ron Sharpe told The Post. "For us to have five 6-9 or higher on the court, speaks to the ability to switch one through five or switch one through four. And it gives a size for rebounding, which has been a big key for us.

"So even if I'm out there on the perimeter, you've got Danny, you've got Michael [Porter Jr.], you've got Julius [Randle], you've got Egor [Dëmin], who all could come back here and rebound. We've just got to keep buying into it."

In the past two games the Nets started Sharpe, Randle, Dëmin and Wolf, the latter's rare combo of size and ballhandling making him a 6-11 off-guard. They split the games, winning in New Orleans and falling Saturday in Memphis. The difference was leading scorer Porter missing the Grizzlies tilt, replaced in the starting lineup by Noah Clowney.

"Yeah, last year, we started off very small. Then, we transitioned over time to a bigger lineup and more length," Porter, who'll be back Monday vs. the 76ers, told The Post.

Julius Randle (30) of the Brooklyn Nets looks on during a game against the Memphis Grizzlies at FedExForum in Memphis, Tenn., Saturday, Nov. 14, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

"It just helps out for sure in different aspects of the game. Danny's a great piece to connect players; he can rebound, he can push the ball, he can dribble the ball and just facilitate, he has a high IQ. So he's helped on the boards as well. And obviously having Egor back was big for us."

With the 6-foot-9 Dëmin returning from a bone bruise — and 6-foot-4 pure point guards Nolan Traore (sore calf) and Mikel Brown Jr. (illness) sidelined against New Orleans — the supersize starting lineup powered a 145-111 victory.

The starters logged a dozen minutes together and were a plus-18. All scored in double figures and were at least a plus-17, led by Randle's plus-38 — tied for the best in Nets history for a road game.

Michael Porter Jr., left, Terance Mann and Moritz Wagner react during a game against the New Orleans Pelicans on Wednesday, Nov. 11, 2026, in New Orleans. Getty Images

But with Porter out against the Grizzlies, Fernández subbed Clowney in his place. That version was nowhere near as effective in a 129-110 loss, the Jumbo Lineup a minus-2 in 13 minutes. Every starter was a minus-7 or worse, except Sharpe (plus-4).

Granted, the competition was a lot tougher, the Pelicans 4-10 and losers of six straight while the Grizzlies are 9-4. Just as important was Porter, whose absence is expected to just be for one game.

Fernández himself has admitted Dëmin is best deployed off the ball, which means he'd eventually go back to two-guard and relegate Wolf back to the bench. The point guard spot would then be manned by either Traore or eventually Brown, the sixth overall pick and heir apparent once he's off his current minutes restriction.

But with Porter back, the Jumbo Lineup remains dangerous.

"Yeah, for sure. Obviously, it's a unique lineup," Wolf said. "And there's definitely some things that I've done well and a lot of things I can continue to work on.

"It also gives us a unique look defensively and on the glass. We've just got to continue to rebound at a high level with so much size out there."
