91-Year-Old Athlete, Who Started Competing in Track and Field at 60, Is Still Breaking World Records: ‘Never Too Late’

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Florence Meiler started her track-and-field career 31 years ago

Now 91, the Vermont athlete is breaking world records, including in the 2,000-meter steeplechase, 80-meter hurdles, pole vault, high jump, triple jump and pentathlon

During motivational speeches, Meiler tells fellow senior citizens that it's "never too late" to take up a sport or try new things

Three decades ago, a then-60-year-old Florence Meiler was recruited by her best friend to try track and field.

For 20 years, she and that friend, Barbara Jordan, trained and competed together, breaking world records at track-and-field events, as documented in an Oct. 30 Aging Untold talk show segment.

But 10 years ago, their time as athletic partners was cut short when Jordan was diagnosed with cancer and forced to stop sports altogether. Then, in 2020, Jordan died at age 85 after a five-year cancer journey.

Now 91, Meiler still thinks about her late best friend as she continues to break world records in her age group, 31 years after starting the sport.

"I miss her so much," the Shelburne, Vt., athlete told Aging Untold.

In a 2020 video profile aired as part of the Olympic Channel's Body+ series, Meiler elaborated on her longtime friendship.

"Training together was fantastic because we would challenge each other and we would criticize each other," the master athlete explained.

Pole vaults on a track field.Credit: Isaiah Vazquez/NCAA Photos via Getty

Meiler continued, "It's really a struggle for me to train by myself. It is a real struggle, but I have a determination, and I put Barb into my mind, and I've got to do this for Barb and I've got to do this for the state of Vermont."

That determination has since led her to break several world records in her 90–94 age group.

According to World Masters Athletics, Meiler holds world records for the 2,000-meter steeplechase (20:54.78), 80-meter hurdles (34.55), pole vault (1.30), high jump (0.92), triple jump (4.55) and pentathlon (2218).

"I really feel really warm inside because I've had so many athletes come up to me and say, 'What an inspiration you have been to me.' That's a wonderful feeling," she told Aging Untold.

As for her motto, Meiler told the Olympic Channel it's three simple words: "Never too late."
