Lakeland football stuns Chaminade to clinch top spot in Open Division

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Dreadnaughts rallied from a 21-0 first half deficit to beat Chaminade-Madonna 28-27 on the road. Lakeland's win coupled with St. Thomas Aquinas' win at Venice means the Dreadnaughts have clinched the top seed in Pool A and will play in the state semifinals against the No. 2 seed from Pool B.

All this from a team that still hasn't played a home game in the Open Division.

Considering the distance it had had to travel and the quality of the opponents it played in the first two weeks, no team has a more challenging first two weeks of the Open Division that Lakeland.

All the Dreadnaughts have done is go 2-0 and return home conquering heroes.

Here are some takeaways from the second week of Open Division play.

OK, so it's only been two weeks, and the two biggest rounds of the tournament remain, but Hendrix has been the single most dangerous player so far.

The senior scored three rushing touchdowns, including the go-ahead score on the final play of the third quarter.

His decision making, ability to make defenders miss, speed and acceleration have been the difference against both Aquinas and Chaminade. The Lions were so keyed on Hendrix that it opened lanes for the Dreadnaughts final score by Ahnarri Cooper.

Chaminade scored a touchdown in the final minute to make it a one-score game. Coach Dameon Jones opted to go for two and take the lead, but the snap rolled back to the quarterback and was doomed from the start. But there were other issues for Chaminade.

The Lions were outstanding in the first half, with junior quarterback Brady Quinn throwing a pair of touchdown passes. Chaminade led 21-8 at the half. And then it was almost all Lakeland in the second half.

This played out very similar to Chaminade's 21-20 loss to St. Thomas Aquinas on Oct. 2. Great first half, followed by a relatively quiet second half. It was a similar script in Chaminade's 20-7 win at Cardinal Newman on Oct. 9. Outstanding start, quiet finish.

Now, Chaminade has to win at Aquinas or the Lions season will be over next week.

The Raiders showed no ill effects after suffering its first loss in more than 14 months.

Pitt running back commit Tyler Reid scored two rushing touchdowns, including a spectacular 66-yard run in the first quarter, as Aquinas cruised to a 38-16 win. The win sets up the Aquinas-Chaminade showdown next week, while Venice is mathematically eliminated.

Aquinas' offensive line did a good job of opening holes throughout the game. The Raiders jumped out to a 14-0 lead and never trailed. Venice made it a one-score game late in the second quarter, but the Raiders retook momentum after the half.

Columbus made its longest road trip of the season and played in less-than-ideal conditions but handed Raines a 28-6 loss in Jacksonville.

After allowing a touchdown in the first quarter, the Explorers defense was dominant and shut out the Vikings the rest of the way. Columbus has allowed just nine points — none in the second half — in their first two Open Division games.

Columbus is in the driver's seat in Pool B and could clinch a spot in the semifinals on Saturday if West Boca beats Miami Central.

It was a valiant effort for Raines' shorthanded defense, but the Vikings were unable to get things going offensively.

Miami Central (0-1 vs. West Boca (1-0) at Wellington High School, 7 p.m.

If West Boca wins, both West Boca and Columbus will clinch spots in the state semifinals.

Chaminade-Madonna (1-1) at St. Thomas Aquinas (1-1), 7:30 p.m.

The winner will be the No. 2 seed in Pool A and advance to the state semifinals.

Venice (0-2) at Lakeland (2-0), 7:30 p.m.

West Boca (1-0) at Columbus (2-0), 7:30 p.m.
