Pick a small-business card by where most of your company’s spending goes. Put scattered costs on a no-fee card paying 2% on everything, and big monthly bills for shipping and travel on the Ink Business Preferred, which earns more there even after its $95 fee. Pay a higher fee only for perks you’ll use.

Of the small businesses with employees that regularly use a credit card, 8% pay with a personal card alone and 34% mix personal and business cards, according to the Federal Reserve Banks’ Small Business Credit Survey. A card of the business’s own keeps its costs on one statement, where they’re easier to track and to deduct at tax time, and it pays rewards on spending the business does anyway.

Each card below is judged by what a business putting $5,000 a month on it, in the categories where it pays most, would earn in a year after the annual fee. Points and miles are valued at a cent each, what Chase, American Express, Capital One, and Delta pay for them in cash or toward travel. An LLC or corporation with cash in the bank can get an EIN-only corporate card, approved on its federal tax ID, cash, and revenue, so the owner signs no personal guarantee. Sole proprietors who haven’t formed a company can choose among no-fee business cards for freelancers.
