FAA brought first digital air traffic control towers online in the U.S.

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Digital towers are operating at Bartow Executive Airport in Florida and Kenai Municipal Airport in Alaska, with five more planned

The Federal Aviation Administration announced that its first digital air traffic control towers are operational at airports in Florida and Alaska.

The systems are running at Bartow Executive Airport in Florida and Kenai Municipal Airport in Alaska, the agency said. Cameras and digital displays let controllers keep the whole airfield in sight without interruption, covering the full 360 degrees around it, from the runways and taxiways to the nearby skies. Controllers continue to manage the airspace, and the technology adds information to support their existing work, the FAA said.

"Our air traffic controllers continue to do the critical job of keeping aircraft safely separated, and we're giving them better technology to do that," FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said in a statement.

Five non-towered airports will get digital towers with funding from President Donald Trump's Working Families Tax Cut Act, the FAA said. They are Winter Haven Regional Airport and Venice Municipal Airport in Florida, Montgomery County Airpark in Maryland, Johnston Regional Airport in North Carolina, and Durango-La Plata County Airport in Colorado.

The FAA said it conducted years of testing at the William J. Hughes Technical Center in Atlantic City, New Jersey, and other locations. The tests were meant to develop standards and confirm that digital tower systems meet the agency's safety, accuracy and reliability requirements. The agency selected the Frequentis system for deployment.

Bedford is scheduled to testify on Tuesday before the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee about the $12.5 billion push to overhaul how the nation controls air traffic, according to Reuters. He is expected to address how far the work has come and what obstacles remain in retiring outdated equipment, including paper flight strips, floppy disks and copper telecom lines.

In July 2025, lawmakers authorized $12.5 billion spread across five years to overhaul air traffic control, and the Trump administration is seeking an additional $30 billion, according to Reuters.

In September, the FAA began using an AI-powered air traffic management tool called SMART at Ronald Reagan Washington National, Dulles International and Baltimore/Washington International Thurgood Marshall airports. The Transportation Department said at the time that the $12.5 billion in infrastructure upgrades is on track for completion by the end of 2028.
