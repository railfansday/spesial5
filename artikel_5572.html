Chinese AI firms are rushing to lock in overseas Nvidia capacity before possible U.S. crackdown

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A House-passed bill would give the Commerce Department authority over cloud access to restricted chips, which Chinese firms rent abroad

Some Chinese AI companies have contacted overseas data-center operators about reserving Nvidia $NVDA chip capacity, according to Reuters, amid concern that the controversy around Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang could lead Washington to restrict cloud-based access to advanced processors.

U.S. export controls keep Nvidia's top-end AI processors out of China, yet Chinese firms can still lease them in data centers located abroad. One of China's large cloud providers has held internal talks about reserving capacity abroad, according to Reuters. Smaller AI developers are mulling comparable moves, such as reaching out to compute-rental firms in Southeast Asia.

The companies worry the episode will build support for the Remote Access Security Act, or RASA, a bill that would empower the Commerce Department to oversee how restricted technology is accessed through the cloud, according to Reuters. The House approved the measure in January on a 369-22 vote, but the Senate has yet to act on it.

Jensen Huang acknowledged on Sunday, Nov. 8, that he had cheated on his wife. The woman in question was Alice Huang, who previously worked as an executive at Megaspeed, a cloud company based in Singapore, and U.S. authorities determined she was operating as an intelligence asset for China, according to The New York Times. The two Huangs are unrelated, and China has denied the allegation, according to Reuters.

Megaspeed has rejected claims that it illegally sent chips to China, according to Reuters, following U.S. investigators' scrutiny of whether it aided Chinese clients in getting hold of export-controlled Nvidia chips. The company said Thursday that it had assisted inspectors from the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security and engaged a U.S. law firm to catalog its graphics processing units.

Shares of Alibaba, Z.ai and MiniMax fell in Hong Kong on Thursday. Alibaba shed up to 7.1% at its low point, and the other two each lost 7% or more.

Traders and intermediaries in China’s gray market have started stockpiling restricted Nvidia chips, according to Reuters. The Commerce Department is finalizing a rule meant to stop Chinese AI firms from getting at advanced chips via overseas data centers, according to CNBC, which cited The Information. Lawmakers are pushing to add chip-control bills to this year’s National Defense Authorization Act, CNBC said.

Chinese AI firms including Moonshot AI, ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have been reported to have obtained access to restricted Nvidia chips through data centers in Thailand, Malaysia, Japan and elsewhere in Asia. U.S. export controls cover the physical transfer of chips but not remote access to them, said Cassia King, a senior researcher in the Compute Policy group at the Institute for AI Policy and Strategy.

RASA alone would fall short of closing the loophole, said Michelle Nie, a visiting fellow in technology and national security at the Center for a New American Security. The bill would establish legal authority to regulate remote access, she said, but a separate rulemaking process would be required before that authority could be used on advanced chips.
