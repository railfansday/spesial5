I watched Celtic leave it late in Portugal and noted gamble pay off, emotional homecoming and serious issue

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Unconvincing Celtic kept their Europa League qualification hopes alive with a hard-fought and historic 2-1 victory over Portuguese second-tier side Torreense.

Martin O’Neill’s men took an early lead through a Javi Vazquez own goal after nine minutes, but Dany Jean restored parity for the hosts before half-time in Leiria.

The visitors struggled to find a breakthrough after the interval, until substitute Camilo Duran rose unmarked to head home James Forrest’s cross in the 84th minute.

The late winner secured a second consecutive victory in the competition for Celtic, moving them on to six points from five matches and up to 25th in the league-phase standings after keeping their hopes of reaching the knockout stages alive.

It was also the Scottish champions’ victory win on Portuguese soil since 2003.

Lewis Anderson watched the action unfold for The Scotsman and picks out his four main takeaways from the game.

Celtic may have made unnecessarily hard work of beating a Portuguese second division outfit currently sitting 13th in their league, but Martin O’Neill will care little about the aesthetics after securing three precious points.

Although Celtic enjoyed plenty of possession after the interval, their use of the ball was frequently pedestrian, with too many passes going sideways or backwards rather than putting Torreense under meaningful pressure. There were spells when Celtic appeared destined to settle for a frustrating draw, but ultimately they have given themselves a genuine opportunity to reach the play-off round and, at this stage of their European campaign, that is what matters most.

Once again, Camilo Duran proved to be the difference-maker. O’Neill's decision to keep the in-form striker in reserve paid dividends, with the substitute delivering the decisive moment when Celtic needed a spark. The Colombian’s back-post header from fellow replacement James Forrest’s pinpoint delivery was precisely the injection of quality that had been missing for much of the evening.

When the ball arrived at his feet, his first touch was outstanding, allowing him to open his body and switch the play. He then demonstrated the instincts of a natural goal scorer, occupying defenders before finding the space to deliver the decisive finish. Duran may have had only 12 touches, but the one that mattered most was emphatic. He is becoming Celtic's go-to man in crucial moments, and delivered once more when it counted.

Although Jota did not manage to get his name on the scoresheet or provide an assist, his performance in Portugal offered plenty of telltale signs that he is rediscovering the devastating form that once made him such an exciting attacking presence for Celtic. The winger looked particularly at home on his return to familiar surroundings, relishing the chance to perform in front of friends and family after an arduous period on the sidelines.

Making his first European appearance since Celtic’s Champions League disappointment against Bayern Munich in February 2025, the 27-year-old showed few signs of rustiness despite two injury-disrupted seasons. He was a constant menace to Torreense down the left flank, using his pace, close control and willingness to take on defenders to cause problems throughout his time on the pitch.

His influence was particularly evident during the opening period, creating two opportunities, testing the goalkeeper twice and delivering a succession of enticing crosses that deserved more decisive finishing from his team-mates. One such delivery contributed to Celtic's opening goal, when Vazquez inadvertently turned the ball into his own net.

Given the length of his absence and the physical demands of returning to elite-level football, there had been understandable questions over whether Jota could recapture the explosive qualities that previously made him a favourite among the Celtic support. His latest display provided a really encouraging response. There is still work to do before he reaches his previous heights, but the signs are increasingly promising.

This success has breathed fresh life into Celtic's European campaign, but O’Neill will be under no illusions that there is considerable scope for improvement if his side are to make further progress. Individual contributions may have rescued the evening in Portugal, yet they should not disguise some worrying collective shortcomings, particularly the vulnerability of the defence and the lack of composure when chances presented themselves in the final third.

The format offers Celtic a route into the knockout stages, with the top eight in the league phase advancing automatically to the last 16, while those finishing between ninth and 24th must negotiate a two-legged play-off. There remains little margin for error, though, with three fixtures remaining. Celtic still have Omonoia, Marseille and current league-phase leaders Union Saint-Gilloise to negotiate, and will almost certainly require further points if they are to extend their European journey.

There is, however, reason for optimism. The win over Torreense means Celtic have now recorded successive wins in a major European competition for the first time since defeating Ferencvaros home and away during the 2021-22 group stage of this tournament. It is a positive milestone for O’Neill's side and another indication that, despite their problems, Celtic are beginning to build some momentum at a crucial stage.

Torreense may have been unfancied on paper, but they demonstrated they were no pushovers and gave Celtic a considerably sterner test than their second-tier status might have suggested. The Portuguese outfit were organised, and disciplined, refusing to be overawed by the occasion or the gulf in resources between the two clubs.

João Nuno’s side pressed aggressively, competed well for second balls and showed enough attacking ambition to expose some of Celtic's defensive vulnerabilities. Their equaliser was reward for an approach that refused to crumble after falling behind, while their resistance after the interval threatened to frustrate the visitors further.

Celtic's superior individual quality eventually told, but Torreense deserve credit for making them dig deep for the result. This was no routine European assignment. For all the criticism Celtic will face over aspects of their performance, Torreense should not be dismissed as cannon fodder. They showed spirit and sufficient quality to suggest their lowly league position told only part of the story.
