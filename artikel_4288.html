When adding new content to an early access title, developers often need to strike a balance between addressing player feedback and slotting in planned additions. When done properly, adding both can not only move a game toward its final form, but also squash bugs and hiccups that they didn’t even notice without a wide player base to catch them.

MENACE is one such indie early access darling that’s been an absolute gem to follow, providing XCOM-style combat with MechWarrior flair, a beautiful fusion of grounded, high-stakes combat.

The newest Honkai title by HoYoverse is approaching its final beta test, giving players a larger taste of its auto-battler combat.

In their most recent update, the team at Overhype Studio has implemented a mountain of fixes, as well as an XCOM Long War-style extended campaign, slowing down progression to force players into a proper test of endurance on their path to victory.

MENACE has defined itself through gritty combat and long-form tactical planning, making it a perfect follow-up from the team that developed Battle Brothers. In addition to the new campaign options, the update adds two more squadmates to join the crew, Kubra Oztok and Clay Fleetwood.

While Kubra is a super solid frontliner, with the ability to deal great mental and physical damage to enemy units, Clay is a very funny special case, being a simulation expert with no actual physical combat experience.

Rather than having a normal HP value, Clay is a long-range powerhouse who will flee instantly upon taking any form of damage. He’ll also hightail it if the battle is going poorly enough, making him a ‘win more’ addition to the team more than anything.

MENACE keeps up a great tone of realistic action and drama by balancing it with genuinely funny bits, a hard duo act to perform but one that pays off immensely. In the same way that horror and comedy are shockingly intertwined, allowing players to come up for air with a good joke or two makes it easier to take the whole setting seriously.

All this new content comes with a facelift and small additions to make the game look and run better, including an internal error log to help diagnose hiccups or crashes, even if they stem from mods. Now that’s an addition I’d kill for in a lot of heavily moddable games, and by prepping it this early, MENACE is making the world much easier for vanilla and modded fans alike.

MENACE is a title that really feels like it has a bright future thanks to the great work from the team behind it and the incredibly passionate community it has already established. When a blog post ends with a series of community-made guides being shouted out by the devs, it really feels like the title is going places.

Thanks to early access, MENACE has opened one of the most effective developer/community pipelines I’ve ever seen, avoiding the age-old scramble of launching a game and pivoting to fix community complaints after launch by stomping them out now and encouraging the hunt for more. With a strong Very Positive on Steam and more reviews flying in every day, the players clearly like what they see.

As outwardly popular as Mechs are, it's hard to find titles that really sell their weight. While Gundam-style humanoid dash machines are always fun, getting a long-form game focusing on the big industrial stomper kind of mech is a real diamond in the rough. MENACE is a title that knows what it wants to be, is willing to shift for player feedback, and is focused on how it wants to expand despite its incredibly solid current offerings. As long as the team stays on course, it's sure to be a hit.
