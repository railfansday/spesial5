Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: Bad day after Really Bad Day

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Not gonna lie, it's tough to see days like today -- and more accurately, weeks like this week. But I agree with EnjoyIt that it's a good thing to look, to watch it go down and see your accounts go down -- rather than avoiding it. It just reinforces the idea that this is all a normal part of how markets and their cycles work -- and after a couple of decades of this one really does acclimate to it.

As per my (informal) IPS, i continued to purchase VTI yesterday and today as things ticked downward, above/beyond my usual automated scheduled purchases. The extra funds do not come from 'dry powder' (a concept in which I don't believe), but rather from funds that were allocated for "general spend" which had yet to be utilized.... and so put them to use on these down days. Naturally, every single tranche continued to drop in value after it was bought Such is life.

But it's all good, because we all invest in the markets as BH's because we believe over time the markets go up. And when it goes back up, the rising tide lifts all boats. It sure is wild to see the total paper losses as compared to the ATH from August 13th -- multiple years of annual expenses, *poof*, vaporized. I don't deny that it helps that I've not yet retired, and thus still have Megacorp income flowing in to snap up the cheaper shares. If this were all happening next summer, post-retirement, well... I'd still not sweat it because I'd only retire when I felt I could reasonably endure a 50% drop, and I am pretty much there. But I won't pretend that it wouldn't be at least a bit more unsettling with the income stream gone, pondering SORR.

The other thing I continue to find is that it really bothers me more to miss out on gains than it does to deal with losses. I did dial down the exposure a bit earlier this year, going from 93/7 down to 88/12 -- but I think we all know that isn't really moving the needle much. I do appreciate that many here will see "88/12 less than a year from retirement" and consider that nuts... but I'm still investing for the long haul, in anticipation of a 40-45 year retirement. 40/60 or even 50/50 just isn't in my nature. I'm more an LMP/RP guy at this stage, anyway. X years of expenses in FI (TIPs, CDs, MMF) and pretty much everything else in equities.
