Planning Your First Trip to New Zealand? Here's Where to Go

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Living in Sydney, I’m guilty of thinking of New Zealand as somewhere I can always go next month. It’s close enough for a skiing trip, which is usually my first excuse to book. I love Queenstown for that, but what I’ve particularly enjoyed discovering on my travels is how naturally New Zealand brings together its spectacular outdoors and a tradition of luxury hospitality, in every season.

Aotearoa is easy to admire from a car window, but I’ve found that the places I remember most are those where I’ve stopped and spent a little more time. Wellington is overdue for another visit, and Fiordland is somewhere I'd like to explore properly. Then there are the lodges, each with its own character and reasons to return: Huka Lodge for its history, Cabot Lodgefor its working-farm roots, and many others worth discovering.

Below are the five places I’d build a trip around, leaving enough room in the itinerary to linger a little longer wherever I find myself wanting to stay.

I’d start with Queenstown if you’re coming for the mountains, with time allotted for Arrowtown and a few days in Fiordland National Park. For the North Island, Auckland is a useful starting point before heading towards Rotorua for its geothermal landscapes and Māori culture, then Lake Taupō. If food is your priority, I’d make Wellington part of the trip. My main advice is to choose a few places you’re interested in and give each at least a few days.

It can be, although a luxury lodge and a helicopter aren’t compulsory parts of an itinerary. I’d spend on a stay I really wanted, then balance it with somewhere simpler, ideally with a kitchen so you can cook some of your own meals. Skiing and guided activities can take a sizable bite out of the budget, so decide which matter most before booking everything else. If you’re considering a camper van, compare the total hire cost, including fuel and campsite fees, with a car and accommodation. I’d also leave room on the budget for eating out and wine tasting.

Seven days is sufficient for a trip, but I wouldn’t try to cover both islands in that time. On the South Island, you could spend the week around Queenstown and Arrowtown, adding Te Anau if Milford Sound is a priority. Or drive from Christchurch to Lake Tekapo and spend time in the Mackenzie region. On the North Island, Rotorua and Taupō work well together.

I always prefer to spend an extra night somewhere than keep repacking. Leave some flexibility, too: if you’ve come for stargazing and the sky clouds over, you’ll appreciate having another evening available.

Arrowtown, 20 minutes from Queenstown, is an idyllic place to visit.

Queenstown is great for the skiing, but I’d make time for nearby Arrowtown even if it meant losing a day on the slopes. I love the old cottages and shopfronts, and how much of its gold-mining history is still visible. The partially restored Chinese miners’ settlement beside Bush Creek is worth walking through before lunch.

Food is another reason to stay. At Aosta, northern Italian cooking makes good use of Central Otago produce. Nearby Ayrburn occupies a former 150-acre farm, with restaurants spread across its restored buildings. Billy’s, in the original homestead, serves modern Chinese food. I’ve visited both Arrowtown and Ayrburn, and would happily organize another Queenstown trip around eating here, with some skiing fitted in.

Where to stay: Arrowtown House is a small boutique hotel in town, making walking to dinner easy. Millbrook Resort, nearby, has more of a resort feel, with apartments as well as rooms and a spa for sore ski legs.

I still think about the water outside Huka Lodge. The Waikato River is this extraordinary emerald color, and I could have spent much of my stay watching it. Huka also got me interested in New Zealand’s lodge history: it began as a fishermen’s camp in 1924. There’s something appealing about discovering that a place has been welcoming people for the same reason for more than a century.

You don’t have to stay there to spend time beside the river. Follow the riverside walks towards Huka Falls, where the water forces its way through a narrow rock channel. I’d also get out on Lake Taupō, with a boat or kayak trip to the Ngātoroirangi Mine Bay Māori rock carvings. Created by Matahi Whakataka-Brightwell and his team, they’re a striking example of contemporary Māori art.

Where to stay: Huka Lodge has a certain stateliness, with its manicured grounds beside the Waikato River and a history of royal guests. I have dreams of running away to Poronui Safari Camp, in the wider Taupō region. Its two canvas tents sit beside the Mohaka River, with hot showers and food supplied by the kitchen. It’s booked exclusively for up to four people.

The Cairns Alpine Resort’s Alpine Lodges on crystalline Lake Tekapo

I visited Dark Sky Project while reporting on quiet destinations for Traveler and loved having someone explain what I was looking at. The indoor experience brings Māori astronomy and science together, and houses an enormous restored 19th-century telescope. It gives you a way into the subject even if your astronomical knowledge is limited. For nighttime stargazing, book the Summit Experience at Mount John Observatory, within the Aoraki Mackenzie International Dark Sky Reserve. I’d arrange it early in the stay, leaving another night available if the weather doesn’t cooperate.

There’s plenty to look at during the day, too. Lake Tekapo gets its milky turquoise color from fine glacial rock flour floating in the water. It’s a good place to spend a few days on a South Island drive from Christchurch, with time for the Southern Alps and a separate excursion into Aoraki/Mount Cook National Park.

Where to stay: The Cairns Alpine Resort’s Alpine Lodges take their inspiration from musterers’ huts. Inside, there are two bedrooms and a kitchen, with timber-lined rooms and a wood-burning fireplace. After a late night stargazing on Mount John, you can have a lie-in and cook breakfast whenever you’re ready.

The city of Wellington, a great place to visit for good eating and galleries.

New Zealand received its first Michelin restaurant selection in 2026, although you hardly need the guide to eat well here. For me, Wellington would be a few days of galleries and restaurant bookings, with enough time between them to walk around and see what’s changed since my last visit some years ago City Gallery’s reopening has moved it up my list and is the sort of thing I’d build a weekend around. I’d leave plenty of time for eating, though.

Puffin is on my list for a glass of natural wine. I’m also keeping an eye on Rita, a small restaurant in an Aro Valley cottage known for its changing set dinners. The team is also opening a daytime deli called Super Rita, expected to open in early next year.

Where to stay: The Intrepid Hotel is the one I’d book: 18 rooms in Te Aro, near Cuba Street, with Puffin downstairs. I like the idea of a nightcap in a locals-filled wine bar and a short walk upstairs to bed.

The Cabot Lodge, a working farm, is an intriguing stay in Fiordland

Milford Sound and Doubtful Sound are still on my list, and I’d like to give Fiordland a trip of its own. I’d stay around Te Anau rather than make the long journey out and back from Queenstown. There are glowworm caves across Lake Te Anau, while nearby Manapouri is the starting point for the most direct land-and-water route to Doubtful Sound: a boat across Lake Manapouri, followed by a coach over Wilmot Pass to the fjord.

I also have a shopping recommendation for the journey. At The Key, on the approach to Te Anau, Davaar sells knitwear (that has a cult following) made from wool grown on the family’s working sheep station, Davaar Station. Founder Kate Macdonald is the fifth generation, and her sister Anna runs the food caravan next door. I bought a jumper a few years ago when she had a pop-up in Arrowtown that I still love. It’s exactly the sort of place I want to find when traveling: a homegrown product with a family story; make the best souvenirs.

Where to stay: Cabot Lodge interests me because it’s on a working farm overlooking Lake Manapouri, and near Doubtful Sound departures. For a night at Milford itself, Milford Sound Lodge has chalets overlooking the river and rainforest. I’d decide which sound I wanted to spend more time exploring before choosing where to stay.
