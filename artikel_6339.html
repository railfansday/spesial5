The 7 Best Fireproof Home Safes Protect Your Valuables from Extreme Heat

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Your standard safe may stop burglars, but can it also withstand fires and floods?

A good home safe will protect your most important items from theft and damage, but if you want one that will also offer protection from heat, direct flames, and even water (in some cases), you need a fireproof home safe. This protection isn’t just limited to non-flammable items like jewelry or firearms, either. High-quality fireproof safes keep cash, important documents, photographs, and passports safe, too.

To go one step further, home safes rated as fireproof will protect your belongings from house fires and sprinkler systems, but if you're looking for protection from natural disasters, there are solutions out there. A safe rated for extreme temperatures will safeguard your things in the event of a wildfire, and those with airtight seals should prevent water from seeping in even during a flash flood.

We’ve reviewed the best fireproof home safes below with picks that cover your needs regardless of what you’re storing or how much space you need.

We made our choices on this list based on extensive research, looking closely at key features, brand reputation, and of course, each model’s temperature and fireproof rating. We also looked through customer reviews, making note of how well their safes held up in real-world situations. Beyond this, we took into account recommendations from professional firefighters. Because of these factors, you won’t see a wide variety of brands on this list.

In previous versions of this article, we featured fire safes and chests that were tested out by our team. While these models have since been discontinued or are out of stock, we used this experience to inform new choices for this list. All the safes on this list are “true” fireproof options, meaning that the safe itself will hold up, not just an included fireproof envelope. Each safe we selected is rated to withstand temperatures of at least 1,200 degrees Fahrenheit.

To withstand high temperatures, choose a safe that is completely fireproof rather than a security safe with a fire-resistant envelope. Unless all of your valuables fit comfortably in a fire-resistant envelope, the risk of damage to other items in the safe is far too high. Even in the envelope, your items will not be protected to the same degree as a completely fireproof safe.

For advanced protection, fireproof safes should withstand temperatures of at least 1,150 F for 30 minutes or more. In the case of wildfire risk, a fireproof safe that protects at temperatures up to 1,800 F is your best protection. Keep in mind, though, that wildfires get as hot as 2,000 degrees, which would melt most of the safes included here. Any safe you purchase should have fireproof ratings certified by either the Underwriter’s Laboratory (UL) or the inspection firm Intertek, designated by an “ETL” mark.

There are several different types of locking mechanisms available depending on the level of security you desire. Among our recommendations, you’ll find models with traditional keys, combination locks, and electronic keypads. Some safes also come with biometric locks that only unlock to your specific fingerprint, which may offer an additional layer of security.

The size of the valuables you need to store and the available space in your home will dictate the ideal dimensions for your fireproof safe. Look for a deeper safe if you need to protect a large cache of valuables or a variety of different-sized items.

Some smaller safes, like the Honeywell Small Safe Box Chest, are shaped like briefcases and work best for holding important documents. Similarly, if you are looking to store jewelry, look for deeper safes equipped with drawers or trays to better organize your items.

For a fireproof safe you can transport, look for a fireproof chest or document box that weighs 30 pounds or less. These safes are also light enough to be placed on a shelf. Heavier fireproof home security safes (upwards of 75 pounds and designed to deter thieves) usually specify and provide hardware for floor mounting.

Many fireproof safes that are water-resistant or waterproof may only be so under certain conditions, such as standing water of 5 to 8 inches, and possibly for only a certain duration of time. This ensures added protection of the safe’s contents when sprinklers go off in an apartment complex, or if the safe is in the direct line of heavy-duty hoses. Some fireproof security boxes or chests offer waterproof ratings anywhere from 8 to 72 hours spent submerged, which are optimal for homeowners who may face flood risks.

Fireproof rating is easily the most important feature to consider. An independent testing agency must rate the safe, and it should be able to withstand temperatures of at least 1,200 F. However, we recommend much higher for those who are worried about wildfires.

Beware of home security safes that companies list as fireproof, but aren't rated by a testing agency. Many of these safes do not have fireproof bodies and instead just include a small fire-resistant envelope to store sensitive items in.

Once the temperature on the safe interior gets above 350 F, any paper inside (including all your hard-earned cash) is at risk of scorching. Even rooms in a burning house that aren’t on fire can get above 600 degrees F at eye level, which is definitely hot enough to soften steel.

There are many things you can and should keep in a fireproof safe, but if it you need to keep the items chilled or temperature controlled, it shouldn’t go in the safe long term. Avoid storing volatile chemicals or batteries in a safe as they can deteriorate or spill and damage other valuables.

You might assume most safes are lock it and forget it, but they do require some maintenance. You should “air out” your safe to avoid moisture build-up every few weeks to a few months depending on the climate where you live and the contents of your safe. You should also replace the batteries in battery-powered safe locks once a year.

Avoid installing a safe in the master bedroom, where security experts indicate thieves are most likely to check for valuables first. Fireproof safes are heavy, so it’s preferable to store them on the floor even though it might make it harder for you to access.

In fact, large standing safes often have mounting hardware included so you can bolt them into the floor of your basement or garage for additional security and stability.
