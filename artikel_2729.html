Are big leads safe with the Celtics now? They seriously might be.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Celtics fans felt like they were watching the same horror movie play out for the upteenth time when Boston blew a 22-point lead to the Detroit Pistons on Opening Say. The classic "they can't hold a lead" and "the Celtics are choke artists" takes flooded timelines throughout the community.

Frankly, those reactions were valid. This particular Cs regime has had its fair share of disappointing collapses in meaningful games. Dropping what should've been a surefire win against another top Eastern Conference opponent isn't the way anyone wanted to start this new season with a refurbished core.

Fast forward about a month, and Sunday's complete and utter beatdown of Cooper Flagg and the Dallas Mavericks was a reminder that it's #DifferentHere.

Boston jumped out to a quick 5-0 lead. Then it became 14-4. Then 31-6.

The Celtics' eventual 42-9 lead at the end of the first quarter set a record for the largest margin to open a game in franchise history.

A record-setting lead like that would be nearly impossible to blow. So it's not all that shocking that the Cs walked out with a win. The stability they played with, though, that was impressive. Boston led by many as 41, and Dallas never managed to get themselves within 25 points after falling behind early.

This was a true wire-to-wire display of dominance from the first-place Celtics. That dominance, though, isn't the expectation.

"It's not about hanging onto leads," head coach Joe Mazzulla told reporters postgame. "That's a passive mindset. It's not about building on them. It's understanding the ebbs and flows of the game and constantly answering the questions: Are we playing well? Having a good process? Or are we not playing well? And that's something you learn over the course of the season."

Fans don't really feel the same as Mazzulla does about leads. They expect a win once the Cs distance themselves from opponents. There's no debate as to whose opinion should matter more, but it's funny how different the viewpoints are.

Nonetheless, Boston answered those questions well on Sunday. The 16-point swing from being up 41 to 25 doesn't feel all that drastic when you've got such a luxurious cushion. It's still 16 points. If they were up 16 and allowed an opponent to tie, we'd all be talking like the sky is falling.

Even Dallas had peak momentum (if you could even call it that), the Celtics never panicked and maintained full control.

That's been the story ever since the start of the season. Sunday's win was the sixth straight for the Cs, who have won 11 of 12 since losing to the Pistons.

As the NBA's leader in point-differential, they've built their fair share of big leads. They've led by 12 or more in nine of their 11 wins.

In that same breath, you haven't heard much about them letting go of the rope. Boston's victories over the Chicago Bulls (twice), Milwaukee Bucks (the game in Milwaukee), and Charlotte Hornets all followed similar scripts to Dallas' demise.

These — for what it's worth — are all teams that you'd expect the Cs to keep down once they got them on the mat. The team did their part in keeping the fans happy in these spots.

In the other four big-lead spots, they met their coach's more realistic expectations. They managed to stay the course and remain steady when faced with the ebbs and flows of a typical NBA game.

This feels like their more important display of character. It's the fact that they're able to bend without breaking — something I vividly remember writing about it when they won the title in 2024.

It helps that their recent win over the Toronto Raptors brought Boston's "clutch" record to 5-1 this season. The Celtics' have actually been excellent in the final minutes of nailbiters. The've scored the ball well and made things difficult for opponents. Their 39.5 net rating is fourth-best in the NBA, as is their 5.7% turnover rate.

The sample size is exceptionally small, but these numbers carry the simple message that the Celtics lock in when the time calls for it. Even beyond the final five minutes of games, it's not hard to notice this. It's why they've held of big charges from the teams who actually manage to erase the large leads.

Frankly, there's no way I would've believed last season's team would stave off a surging Philadelphia 76ers team on the road like this group did last week.

Not only did they stop Philly's momentum, but they turned that close third-quarter battle into an absolute joke in the fourth quarter.

It's extremely early and the sample size is, again, small, but this Celtics team might be trying to tell us that they've got something special in there.
