Arch Manning throws game-winning TD pass with 3 seconds left to crush No. 21 LSU's playoff hopes in No. 6 Texas' 22-21 win

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

No. 21 LSU's College Football Playoff hopes got dashed by Arch Manning and Raleek Brown.

No. 6 Texas, meanwhile, is almost assuredly in the 12-team field.

Manning hit Brown on a swing pass with three seconds left for a 4-yard touchdown to give the No. 6 Longhorns a 22-21 win over the No. 21 Tigers.

The TD pass came after Manning ran for a first down inside the LSU 5-yard line with nine seconds to go. The Longhorns had one timeout remaining. Instead of spiking it and saving the timeout to potentially have the ability for a run play near the goal line, Texas used that final timeout after Manning's run.

After an incomplete pass on first down, the Longhorns motioned Brown out into the flat as quickly as they could to get him into space.

"I thought our guys had great poise and composure in that final drive to execute, showed a lot of guts by Arch for sure," Texas coach Steve Sarkisian told ESPN after the game.

Manning came through on the final drive after a night of struggles. He threw three interceptions and had one of his worst games of the season as the LSU defense was consistently able to pressure him in the pocket. A pick by DJ Pickett with less than 10 minutes to go could have been the nail in the coffin for the Longhorns.

But Texas forced a three-and-out from the LSU offense and got the ball back with three minutes to go for the game-winning drive.

Manning wasn't the only quarterback who threw three interceptions Saturday night at Tiger Stadium. LSU QB Sam Leavitt also threw three picks as the Tigers squandered numerous scoring opportunities for the second straight week.

Leavitt threw two red zone interceptions on Saturday night. Both were to Texas defensive back Jelani McDonald. The first came in the second quarter, when LSU got to the Texas 12-yard line. The second came with the Tigers leading 14-10 in the third quarter and at the Longhorns' 16-yard line.

Throw in a missed 38-yard field goal at the end of the second quarter and you can generously argue that LSU left 17 points on the table with the two picks and the missed kick. Heck, even if LSU got six points out of those three drives, it's a totally different ballgame.

Instead, the Tigers are set to drop out of the CFP rankings on Tuesday night. That's not at all what was supposed to happen when Kiffin was dramatically brought over from Ole Miss nearly a year ago.

Kiffin's hire came with immediate playoff expectations. He added players like Leavitt, offensive tackle Jordan Seaton, edge rusher Princewill Unmanmielen and others through the transfer portal thanks to a healthy NIL budget.

The Tigers were team built to win right away.

Instead, Leavitt has thrown 12 interceptions and LSU is having a hard time beating ranked opponents. You're not going to succeed in the SEC if you can't do that.

The Tigers are now 2-3 against teams ranked in the AP Top 25 in 2026. The two wins are against Texas A&M, a team that's now 4-6 and spiraling, and against Mississippi State. The Bulldogs were ranked No. 16 at the time but didn't have Kamario Taylor as he missed the game due to the toe injury that has sidelined him for the rest of the season.

Saturday night's loss is also the second straight for the Tigers at home. You have to go all the way back to 1994 — a year where LSU finished 4-7 — to find the last time the Tigers lost home games in consecutive weeks.
