‘I Appreciate the Wake-Up Call’ — ESPN’s Stephen A. Smith Sounds Off on Suspension Rumor Tied to Controversial Lakers Take

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Over the weekend, Stephen A. Smith denied a rumor that ESPN suspended him indefinitely. The false report is the latest twist in a week of fallout from his comments about the Los Angeles Lakers.

The latest wave of controversy erupted after a "First Take" segment days earlier, when Smith revisited a point he had previously made about the Lakers.

The ESPN host sized up the team's title chances and brought the race of its top three players into the conversation, referring to Luka Dončić, Austin Reaves, and Walker Kessler as "these white brothers."

The post Smith responded to claimed ESPN suspended him indefinitely over his Lakers remarks and removed him from the network's sports coverage. He did not mince words in shooting it down.

"Good Lord. For the record, this is a flat-out lie… I'm neither suspended nor did I do a [expletive] thing to BE suspended," Smith wrote.

The ESPN host then closed on a pointed note: "But hey, I appreciate the wake-up call. When ya mow the grass, the snakes come out."

With the Lakers off to a strong start, Smith previously gave their three leading players their due before turning to his doubts about a title run:

"Luka Dončić is just all-world. Austin Reaves is showing you how he can ball. Walker Kessler can play … I got it. But I'm telling you that I ain't ever seen what they assembled… deliver an NBA championship in my lifetime, and I don't think I'm gonna see it this year."

He followed up by saying, "These white brothers can play," and asking, "Y'all gonna let them win a championship?"

ESPN colleague Jay Williams and sports commentator Jeff Pearlman were among those who criticized the comments, while Smith used Friday's edition of the show to stand by them.

Williams took direct aim at that closing question in a post of his own: "We spent generations fighting to ensure athletes were judged by their talent, not the color of their skin… Now we're openly questioning whether Black players should allow white players to win?"

Pearlman went further in a video posted before Smith's denial, saying, "He doesn't see it yet, but I 100% do. We are witnessing a career dissolve in front of us. And what you are seeing right now is Stephen A. Smith, the backlash to his Trump interview, insane."

Smith answered the criticism head-on during Friday's "First Take" episode, singling out longtime commentator Clay Travis, who had also taken issue with the remarks:

"We all know what statements I made about the Lakers that have taken on a life of their own and been controversial. You got guys like Clay Travis opening his mouth, talking about it… I said it. I meant it. And I ain't moving because I didn't expect these Lakers to be what they are. These boys can play."

Smith's prediction that this group won't win a title this season will take months to settle. In the meantime, the Lakers' next chance to build on their hot 14-3 start comes Monday at home against the Utah Jazz.
