Aaron Rodgers got no support from the Thanksgiving broadcasts

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The day after former Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers went on TV and aired dirty laundry from the Pittsburgh locker room, none of the former quarterbacks (or other former players) who appear on TV supported him.

Across the board, the reaction from the NFL’s analysts and broadcasters was negative.

Former Steelers quarterback and Fox analyst Terry Bradshaw went off. Fox’s Michael Strahan agreed that Rodgers shouldn’t have gone public with his complaints — calling him a “narcissist” and a “rich jerk” and a “rich bad teammate.”

Fox’s Howie Long said that the moment “defines who [Rodgers] is.”

“He’s a guy that I think picked the wrong sport,” Long said. “He probably should have played tennis.”

Fox’s Rob Gronkowski said that, even if a contender loses its starting quarterback, there’s “too much smoke” to justify bringing Rodgers in.

The CBS pregame crew offered similar views. Nate Burleson said Rodgers “didn’t handle this right.” Bill Cowher said he hates to see a Hall of Famer go out this way. Russell Wilson said, “It’s about Aaron Rodgers’s legacy. . . . If I’m his family members, if I’m somebody close to him, I’m his significant other, whatever it may be. ‘Is this how you want to go out?’ After all the success he’s had, is this how you want to go out? . . . This is a disappointment.”

During the Eagles-Cowboys game, Fox’s Tom Brady criticized Rodgers for violating the “sanctity” of the locker room.

The irony, if I’m using the term correctly (I never know), is that Rodgers has previously railed about leaks from the locker room.

“I think it’s chickenshit at its core, and I think it has no place in a winning organization,” Rodgers said about the Jets.

“One thing I really love — and it’s kind of the antithesis of where I was — is there’s not really any leaks in the boat,” Rodgers told reporters during his first year with the Steelers. “Every year, you have difficulties and adversity, both on the field and off the field, and to go through a season like this, and to be able to focus on football and not have a lot of other little bullshit out there has been really nice.”

Rodgers didn’t leak anything. He said it directly, bluntly, unapologetically. He took the team’s focus off of football, at a time when the team was otherwise trying to fight through “difficulties and adversity.”

Again, he isn’t a whistleblower. He’s a troublemaker. He didn’t get his way, so he set the house on fire as he walked out the front door.

As one PFT reader put it, ultimatums and “blackmail” are close cousins. For Rodgers, it wasn’t just “let me call the plays or cut me.” It was, whether he said it this way or not, “let me call the plays or I’ll tell the world all the stuff you don’t want the world to know.”
