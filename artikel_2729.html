10 Bears Takes: Superman is back, a 9-2 start, and more on the last-second win

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Thanksgiving opener might have started a bit slowly, but the second half gave football fans everything and more to kick off an action-packed Turkey Day. Coming into the game, the Chicago Bears had not trailed in five complete games. That streak ended early, and they ultimately flipped the script, leading for only three seconds. In the end, quarterback Caleb Williams put on his Superman cape and led the Bears to another comeback win. At 9-2, Chicago is in the driver's seat for a playoff spot and controls their own destiny when it comes to winning the division. That said, there was plenty to dissect on Thursday, including a key injury and some concerning trends. We'll dive into all of that and more in our Week 12 installment of 10 Bears Takes.

1. With their win on Thursday afternoon, Ben Johnson becomes the first head coach to have back-to-back winning seasons since Lovie Smith, exactly two decades ago.

Just as important for team morale, Johnson finally got the monkey off his back while earning his first win against his former team. Unlike last season, 2026 has been a much smoother ride for the Bears, with more leads and more wins. Outside of them blowing a big lead against Tampa Bay, quarterback Caleb Williams has not had many chances to lead fourth-quarter comebacks.

Thursday's game didn't start promising, and every time it felt like the Bears were about to recapture momentum, their offense would stall, or their defense couldn't get a stop. Frankly, there were many times that it felt like Chicago was down and out of the game. Instead of crumbling and allowing the game to get out of hand, they kept swinging back, and with the game on the line on a third-and-goal from the Lions' six-yard line, Williams found Rome Odunze in the back of the end zone for the game-winning touchdown with just three seconds to go.

Since taking over as the team's head coach last season, Johnson is now 20-8 in just over a season and a half in Chicago. His .714 win percentage currently ranks 1st of all 19 head coaches in franchise history. Thursday's win allowed him to overtake Ralph Jones' position at the top (.706). Despite that, he's won just three divisional games over 10 tries. Not only had Johnson failed to beat Detroit in his first two attempts, but Williams also came into Thursday with a 0-4 record, despite two close games that didn't go his way.

Through his first 28 games as the team's head coach, Johnson has done a great job of breaking down old barriers that have held them back since the Mike Ditka days. The latest of which was clinching back-to-back winning seasons for the first time in exactly 20 years. Even more impressive is the fact that wins didn't start coming in bunches for Lovie and company until his second year as the team's head coach.

The job is still far from complete, and their divisional record needs to keep improving, but considering the Bears were close to a 50-50 bet even to make the playoffs, Year 2 under Johnson has gone about as well as possible. It's also worth noting that their current six-game winning streak is the longest they've experienced since they opened up the season at 6-0 in 2006. Like it or not, there are many parallels between what the Bears are doing now and the 2005-2006 team led by Smith.

2. The loss of tight end Colston Loveland is a tough one, especially after Cole Kmet went down earlier in the year.

While the initial hope (via David J. Chao) is that it's an MCL sprain that might cost him a short-term stint on Injured Reserve, the ultimate hope (and goal) is that he'll be back for the end of the season and into the playoffs. Even if he beats that timeline and returns after four games on IR, it's a big blow for a team that has already lost Kmet for the year to a torn ACL.

Loveland has had an up-and-down sophomore season, but his target share and overall involvement in the offense have picked up in recent weeks. Holding onto the ball has been an issue at times this year, but even when he wasn't a factor in the receiving game, he was the team's best blocker with Kmet on the shelf.

At least in the short term, the Bears will look to third-round rookie Sam Roush and journeyman tight end Stephen Carlson. Unfortunately for Chicago, the trade deadline has already passed, so any help that might be coming from an outside source will have to be in the form of a street free agent. It's possible that the team could elevate Quadir Ismail before next weekend's game, but one thing is for sure: What was once a strength is going to be something that Johnson and his coaching staff are going to have to scheme around.

Speaking of scheming around, it seems safe to assume that the Bears will feature more 11 personnel. Luther Burden and Odunze have taken turns being the featured receiver in this offense, and with veteran Kalif Raymond and third-round rookie Zavion Thomas ready to step into bigger roles, there should be confidence that Johnson can keep this offense moving in the right direction. This could also open up more opportunities for former undrafted free agent Jahdae Walker, although his "drop" on Thursday in a big spot was not his best moment.

We should find out more in the coming days on the long-term prognosis of Loveland, but assuming that Loveland isn't lost for the year, the offensive staff will need to get creative by reimagining their passing offense.

3. Despite two short weeks already behind them, the Bears will still have two more over their final six games of the season.

When the Bears are good, the NFL loves it. Last year was somewhat of a test run for Ben Johnson and company, but now that they have proven themselves as a good, fun team to watch, the NFL has maximized opportunities to get them in standalone games. That has led to multiple short (and long) weeks. Thanksgiving marked the second time they've played on Thursday this season, and even if they don't get that short a week again to finish out the season, they'll play a Saturday game against Buffalo, followed by a Christmas Day game the following week.

Some might see this as a negative, and to a certain extent, it is. Luckily, the Bears had a relatively late bye, and despite a short week heading into Detroit, they'll have another mini bye to make more adjustments for the final six games of the season. That said, playing back-to-back games with one less day of rest is not ideal, especially with a divisional race that is shaping up to go all the way down to the wire.

The good news is that Ben Johnson is now 7-2 coming off short weeks, so his track record for having his team prepared is extremely promising. That said, facing the Bills and Packers on short weeks isn't ideal. It is worth mentioning that both of their opponents will be experiencing the same shortened preparation period.

With just six games remaining in the regular season and hopes of a deep playoff run into late January or early February, the Bears are just a few short weeks away from some added adversity that fans should have confidence they'll be prepared for. Moving forward into next season and beyond, hopefully they won't draw the short end of the stick when it comes to abbreviated weeks quite as often.

4. Over the last few weeks, the offense had taken a noticeable step back. Between poor offensive line play and a gun-shy quarterback, the second half was a welcome development.

When Williams first returned, the offense looked unstoppable. The running game wasn't nearly as effective, but the passing game attacked defenses from multiple directions. Their 34-point performance against an elite Seattle defense felt like a statement, and after they jumped out to a 17-point lead against the Buccaneers, it felt like this group was finally reaching their potential. Four interceptions later, the Tampa Bay game took a turn that felt like a minor speed bump.

Coming out of their bye week, the Saints looked like a great matchup on paper for the rushing attack and overall offense to get back on track. After jumping out to a 16-point lead, the offense went stagnant. Even after pulling out a relatively comfortable win against New Orleans, this group's struggle was becoming more noticeable.

Thursday started in promising fashion until Loveland's fumble. The team's streak of scoring in each quarter ended before tacking on 10 points heading into halftime. In roughly nine quarters of football, the passing offense stopped being as effective, their two-headed monster at running back weren't producing consistently, and the offensive line looked like a completely different unit.

Going into the half down 19-10 facing one of the league's best offenses, it felt like the Bears were at a mid-season crossroads. Although Detroit's defense had been playing much better football as of late, they were missing their two best players, in addition to D.J. Wonnum and Chuck Clark. In many ways, there was no real excuse for why the Bears were not moving the ball.

Then the second half came. Sure, there were still some bumps along the way, but the offense turned things on like we had not seen since Williams had first returned from a three-game absence. Not only did the offense hang 29 points over the final 30 minutes of play, but it also totaled 319 yards. Williams looked more confident and decisive, and the run game was effective enough to keep the chains moving. There's no question that this offense is going to have to adjust with the loss of Loveland, but they proved that they can put up four touchdowns in a hurry.

With the Jaguars coming into Soldier Field next Sunday, the road won't get any easier for this unit. That said, they built plenty of momentum, even on a short week, that should carry over into Week 13. Bears fans must hope this gets Williams back on track, and with some luck, maybe they have started to figure things out in the run game. We'll see if changes are made elsewhere, but we'll talk about that more in a few.

5. This game should have been won and lost in the trenches, and despite having the better matchup on both sides of the ball, the Bears simply didn't do much winning.

Simply put, the Bears got the hell beat out of them on both sides of the trenches on Thursday.

We'll start on the offensive side of the ball, where Williams was under constant duress and sacked four times and hit six. The run game was inconsistent again, though better than it was against the Saints last Sunday. More concerning is how poorly Theo Benedet and Garrett Bradbury played. Benedet has done an impressive job taking over for Braxton Jones, but against the Lions, he struggled most of the day. It's easier to excuse one bad performance from him, especially given his shoulder issue that he's currently playing through. For Bradbury, the story is much different. When the team traded for him, I admittedly had some reservations. He's never been good, and even in New England, he had plenty of holes in his game. In 2026, he's put a lot more bad than good on tape through 11 games. At some point soon, the coaching staff needs to have some honest conversations. It's clear that Johnson doesn't like the idea of handing the reins over to a rookie center, but considering their stark differences in both strength and athleticism, it must be strongly considered. Of all the development for the offense so far, the offensive line heading in the wrong direction is the most concerning. After all, Detroit was without both Aidan Hutchinson and Wonnum.

On the defensive side of the ball, this was the first time that the defensive line has struggled since the acquisition of Jeffery Simmons. In the box score, the Bears were credited with a sack, but it wasn't credited to a single player. Simmons was consistently double-teamed, and outside of a few moments here and there, the defense was struggling to get home with four. In addition to a nonexistent pass rush, the trenches were gashed in the run game. Gibbs is a great player, but any time a defense gives up 200-plus yards to one player, it's an issue. The former Alabama product averaged 9.8 yards per carry, including a 70-yarder to the house in the second half. The Lions' offense is vastly improved from what it was a year ago, but there's no excuse for the Bears to struggle as much as they did.

Despite getting dominated on both sides of the ball, Chicago still found a way to keep the game within reach and ultimately won. Ultimately, it's a great sign for any team when they can win games against top teams, even when they are far from their best. It's one thing to do it against the likes of the Buccaneers and Saints. It's another to do it against a team that was 8-2 coming into the game and dominating teams at home. Things will need to be cleaned up before next Sunday if they expect to beat a high-powered Jacksonville team, but if you'd told me they played this poorly on both sides of the trenches, I would have expected a blowout loss. Instead, they came away with a thrilling, last-second win, and that's something to be thankful for.

6. Over the last five games, Rome Odunze has become the team's most important pass catcher– More on a nice stretch, including a pair of touchdowns on Thursday in Detroit.

When the team took Odunze with the No. 9 overall pick in the 2024 NFL draft, they were expecting a game-changing receiver that would be able to complement D.J. Moore and become a star in his own right. Because he was taken in the same draft as Williams, he hasn't garnered the same focus that a typical first-round pick would.

After a quality rookie year, Odunze started hot last year before a fractured foot slowed him down over the second half of the season. The bigger issue for Odunze has been his failure to win 50-50 balls consistently, and his overall consistency in catching the ball wasn't there. He has experienced more of those inconsistencies this year, but there's no denying his ability to get open. What's even more confounding is that while he struggles with some routine catches, he tends to make the difficult catches look easy. We've seen plenty of examples of that exact situation playing out over the last few games.

Over the last five games, Odunze has led the team in both receptions (22) and yards (348), and touchdowns (3). While Burden still has more burst, twitch, and the ability to make defenders miss in open space, Odunze has been running the deeper, more technically savvy routes.

When Johnson was hired, many questioned how Odunze would fit into the offense, especially compared to what Detroit had. His game doesn't resemble Amon-Ra St. Brown's or Jameson Williams ', but it's clear that the offensive coaching staff trusts him and, by proxy, he's been their WR1. With Loveland out for the foreseeable future, it will be interesting to see how his role changes, if at all. Johnson will have to rely more on 11 personnel, which means Burden will be on the field more, and players like Thomas and Walker will also get more opportunities.

While it's fair to say that Odunze still has a ways to go before fully "justifying" his draft position, it's clear what the vision is for him, and in recent weeks, he's becoming that player. We'll see if he takes on a bigger role as the team shifts to a more wide-receiver-focused passing attack.

7. Thursday afternoon was not a good look for the Bears defense, but with some better health expected soon, this unit still has plenty of room to grow.

Coming into Thursday, the Bears defense had transformed with the addition of Simmons. They gave up fewer than 20 points twice and shut down opposing offenses for most of those games. Then came the Lions offense, and to put it lightly, it was not pretty. The defense was credited with a sack, although it wasn't credited to a player. They had five tackles for loss, but gave up over 500 total yards of offense, and Gibbs went for 200 yards. Outside of forcing a handful of false starts, Simmons was mainly held in check. The defense struggled to get home with four and could not keep up in coverage against the likes of St. Brown and Williams. Outside of their performance against the Panthers in Week 1, I would (safely) argue that this was their worst performance of the year.

It's easy to look at this performance in a vacuum and draw too many conclusions. Reality is, almost every defense in the league has had a bad game (or two) through the first 10-plus games of the season. On the plus side, giving up 35 would be a pretty solid day at the office for a team like the Dallas Cowboys.

While it might be easy to forget, Detroit's defense also came into this game with plenty of good football under the belt as of late. In fact, their numbers were slightly better than the Bears since Week 5. Yet, in the end, the Lions had more sacks, forced one more takeaway, and still gave up 39 points and lost the game. Sometimes, these weird shootouts happen in divisional games, the same way that 9-3 type of games happen, as they did in Week 2 with the Bears and Vikings.

It's best not to draw too many conclusions from either team following this game. Instead, how they respond will be worth monitoring. The Bears will host the Jaguars next Sunday, and that will be another tough matchup. On paper, the two teams are pretty evenly matched. Sometimes the healthier team can make the difference, and that's what Chicago will be hoping to have heading into Week 13. It was reported that Kyler Gordon has a separated shoulder, and now that their stretch of two games in four days is out of the way, it's possible that Gordon can return, even in a limited role, against Jacksonville.

With any luck, Coby Bryant's window will open over the next few weeks, but that has yet to be confirmed. There's still plenty of ambiguity surrounding his status, but it sounded like he was at least trending in the right direction. Bryant's inclusion in this defense would be big for many reasons, especially as they continue to struggle in the deep passing game. With any luck, they'll be back to better health over the next few weeks, which should help them round into form before the playoffs start in mid-January.

The one bummer of the week is that the Chicago Tribune's Brad Biggs reported that Dayo Odeyingbo had surgery and will not return this season. It was originally believed that he would miss a few games and be back, so that's an unfortunate development, and it might be the last we've seen of him in a Bears' uniform.

8. The Aaron Rodgers saga was appointment television in what might have been his final chance in his 22-year NFL career.

Late on Tuesday night, news broke that the Pittsburgh Steelers were benching Rodgers in favor of 32-year-old career backup Mason Rudolph. Simply put, it came out of nowhere, but it also shook the NFL landscape. The theatrics surrounding the soon-to-be first-ballot Hall of Fame quarterback have been nothing short of tiring since his final few years in Green Bay. His two-year stint in New York was about as disastrous as expected, but in some ways, closing out his NFL career by being benched and subsequently released felt right on the nose.

Many expected Rodgers to fire back in some way, shape, or form, which is why it didn't come as a surprise when he decided to take his dog-and-pony show onto the Pat McAfee show in front of a national audience. While it's always difficult to predict exactly what to expect from Rodgers' hits on the McAfee show, his 40-plus minute telling of his side of the story might have been the craziest thing we've seen when it comes to a player publicly airing out his grievances with his (now former) head coach.

To fully understand the relationship dynamic between Rodgers and Mike McCarthy, you'd have to go back a decade and a half to their time together in Green Bay. It came as a mild surprise to most when the Steelers hired McCarthy, but it was even more surprising that Rodgers wanted to run it back with his former head coach. After all, Rodgers was a primary reason that McCarthy was eventually ousted in Packers land following the 2018 season.

To be fair, Rodgers got to get away with a lot for most of his career, primarily because he's one of the best quarterbacks ever to play the game. It's a lot easier to accept when he was winning MVPs and was a regular playoff presence. Over the last four seasons, his age has started to show, and his production has drastically declined. Last year's production was nothing to write home about, and despite a strong start to the season by the Steelers, Rodgers had taken another step back to start this season.

History has been unkind to most NFL head coaches on their third team, and coupled with a 43-year-old quarterback who isn't afraid to share his opinions (both on and off the field), the remarriage felt like a bad idea from the start. Despite their impressive start, the cracks began to show during their recent two-game losing streak. Benching Rodgers outright would have made sense, had it not been announced at 11 pm local time on a random Tuesday night by the Steelers' PR. Instead, the timing of the decision and announcement immediately raised eyebrows among onlookers.

Rodgers spent over 40 minutes telling his side of the story and, to be quite honest, took over the interview. There were several times, especially toward the end, when McAfee tried to wrap up the conversation and get to a commercial break, but Rodgers simply did not take the hint. The truth of Rodgers' allegations probably lies somewhere in the middle, but they were far too detailed just to be flat-out made up. The bigger issue most people had was that he took to a very public forum to do so, without taking accountability.

The long-term ramifications for the Steelers are not yet known. The organization has publicly stated its support for McCarthy. Still, if they continue to sputter down the stretch and miss the playoffs, the optics of keeping McCarthy for another year could be disastrous. What will be interesting to monitor over the next few weeks is how strong the locker room bond between players and coaches in Pittsburgh truly is. Rodgers repeatedly claimed that everyone on the team felt the same as he did, but as we've learned, that's not always the case.

As for Rodgers, it's hard to envision any team looking at how much drama he's created over the last six-plus years and believing that his play would be worth the risk of adding him. Some have speculated that maybe the Patriots and Ravens could make sense, but there's already enough strife in New England, and the Ravens are a few weeks removed from trading for Jacoby Brissett. The short-term gain is highly unlikely to be worth the potential long-term issues that come with adding a player like Rodgers. If that turns out to be the case, it will be a fitting end to a career that has taken on many similarities to that of Brett Favre.

Either way, the drama-filled 24 hours that were brought on by Rodgers' benching, his very public segment on McAfee's show, and his immediate release shortly after did a great job of bridging a slow Tuesday and Wednesday morning into Netflix's "Thanksgiving Eve" game that night. For Bears fans, watching the Rodgers situation implode, and a few hours later, the Packers lose was quite the appetizer to the team's Thanksgiving matchup in Detroit.

9. NFC North Lookaround: The Packers lose a critical game on "Thanksgiving Eve," and the Lions and Bears kicked off a Thanksgiving trio with a banger.

Thanksgiving week is always a fun one for both college and the NFL. This year, the NFL added a "Thanksgiving Eve" game, featuring two teams on the edge of the NFC playoff picture that both needed a win. Coming into the game, the Packers' playoff chances pivoted pretty drastically depending on whether they won or lost. With a win, their chances would hang right around 50%. With a loss, they would drop to 25% or less. Despite a close first half, the Rams dominated the second half on the back of a strong rushing attack. Packers quarterback Jordan Love struggled most of the night, throwing five different passes that all could or should have been intercepted. In the end, he failed to eclipse 200 passing yards and threw two interceptions. Even with a healthy Micah Parsons making his debut against a severely hampered Rams offense, Green Bay could not get the job done. Depending on the results of Seattle's game in San Francisco on Sunday afternoon, Green Bay will either be sitting at eight or ninth in the NFC's playoff picture. After two straight losses, the Packers' season is on life support and will require drastic measures to get back into the mix.

Chicago and Detroit kicked off a three-game slate on Thanksgiving in Detroit, and it did not disappoint. Honestly, all three games provided plenty of entertainment. For now, we'll focus on the barnburner in Detroit. For the majority of the game, the Lions looked like the better team. Not only did they snap the Bears' five-game streak of never trailing in a game, but they hung 35 points and 549 yards. In fact, Chicago only led for three seconds of a 60-minute game. Running back Jahmyr Gibbs was incredible, totaling 205 rushing yards, three rushing touchdowns, and 252 total yards. In terms of consistency, the Lions set the pace early and, until the final few drives, dictated it. Both defenses have taken noticeable strides since their early-season struggles, but on Thursday afternoon, the game turned into a second-half shootout. This was a tough loss for the Lions and snapped a six-game losing streak, but they are still very much in the division race due to a favorable remaining six games and a 3-1 divisional record.

The Vikings were the only team in the division without a Thanksgiving-timed game. They'll host a struggling three-win Falcons team, which appears to be falling apart the longer the season goes on. Minnesota will look to get back on track and build more continuity on offense with quarterback Kirk Cousins. Looking at the matchups, Atlanta's primary hope of winning the game will be on the shoulders of Bijan Robinson and their rushing attack. Michael Penix might be a sitting duck in the pocket, and even though the Falcons have a good offensive line, this should be a bounceback game for Brian Flores and his unit.

10. Week 13 Look Ahead: A well-rounded Jacksonville Jaguars team that is in the driver's seat for a second-straight AFC South title comes into Soldier Field for a potential Super Bowl preview.

Week 11 presented a speed bump for some of the AFC's top contenders, including both the Bengals on Monday Night Football and the Jacksonville Jaguars a day before that. Heading into Sunday's action, Jacksonville's +120 point differential ranks as the best in the NFL. Every team slips up throughout the year, and as Bears fans know, sometimes being heavily favored in a game can be a bad omen. That's exactly what happened to the Jaguars last Sunday against the New York Giants, but make no mistake: This is one of the best teams in the league and will be a tough matchup for the Bears (or any team they face).

What makes the Jaguars so tough is that they are one of the league's more well-rounded teams. Their offense is performing at a high level, and quarterback Trevor Lawrence appears to be "fixed." The offensive line has some holes, but their passing and rushing attacks have plenty of talent and production. Defensively, their impressive turnaround has continued into Year 2 under defensive coordinator Anthony Campanile. Heading into Sunday, their defense ranks 1st in EPA/play, 5th in defensive DVOA, 9th in yards per game, and allows 16.7 points per game.

Outside of matchups against the Vikings, Seahawks, and Buccaneers, it's easy to argue that this is one of the best defenses they'll see all season. Offensively, they are right on par with the Bears, too.

On paper, this is a pretty even matchup. Sure, Jacksonville has beaten up on some bad teams, but you don't get to a +120 point differential by accident. This will be a chess match on both sides of the ball, and the teams are built pretty alike in many ways. Once the game is finished, I suspect many Bears fans will follow the Jaguars more closely because, in many ways, they are the Bears of the AFC. Even though the game was not flexed out of the early Sunday time slot, fans should expect one of Fox's top crews and plenty of national coverage leading up to the game.
