Lions vs. Bucs snap counts: Derrick Moore levels up; return of 3-safety sets

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Detroit Lions (8-2) beat the Tampa Bay Buccaneers in Week 11, extending their win streak to six games and remaining atop the NFC North. This is the second time in the last four weeks that the No. 1 Defense in DVOA entered Ford Field and failed to slow a Lions offense firing on all cylinders. While the game ended 31-21, it wasn't as close as the final score suggests, as the Lions defense continued to play complementary football and was willing to give up late-game points to drain the clock and secure a victory.

Let's look at the snap counts from this game and see what they mean for the depth chart hierarchy and what we can ascertain moving forward.

Another top defense and another stellar day from Goff. He completed 20 of 28 for 279 yards and two touchdowns, navigated the pocket at an MVP level, gained 12 yards on the ground, including converting three first downs, and even got the Lions into victory formation.

"He should be in the MVP conversation, " Lions center Juice Scruggs said of Goff post-game. "He's been playing light-out all year, and I don't think he gets enough respect in this league. He's very underrated, and I think people are just starting to see how good he is."

Sione Vaki: 13 (22%) — 12 special teams snaps (48%)

Gibbs has hovered around the 75% snap range for a while, but his touches are the number to watch. He had 27 in this game, which isn't far from his "plan" for the season, but they'll likely need to keep trying to find ways to take pressure off him as we head into the final seven games of the regular season.

Vaki continues to roll as RB2, while Saylors found the field in victory formation.

For the first time since the early parts of the season, St. Brown saw more snaps than Williams. It's not a major change, but when everything else played out close to what we have seen over the last two months, it's the only notable shift.

Collectively, this group all saw their snaps level up, as it was clear the Lions wanted the tight ends involved in this game, and they produced 122 yards on six receptions in this game.

"Yeah, it's pretty good. Yeah, (Tyler Conklin) Conk's added that little layer for us this year where he is a third tight end, but he can run routes, he can get out of the backfield and do some things, and it was good to get him that one, off schedule," Goff said of having three tight ends at his disposal. "And then Brock (Wright) and Sam (LaPorta) have just been so reliable. The seams were open today, and we hit them on a few of those. Thought Sam played so well, and he's been deserving of getting more touches like that, and it was good to see him get the ball a little bit."

LG Christian Mahogany: 58 (100%) — 6 (24%)

The Lions offensive line is in rhythm, and against the NFL's second-highest blitzing team, they allowed only eight pressures and one sack, according to PFF.

With the emergence of 13 personnel (three-tight-end sets), the Lions have almost entirely moved away from "Jumbo" (six-offensive-lineman formations).

3T Skyler Gill-Howard: 28 (41%) — 3 (12%) — 3 offensive snaps (5%)

With the Bucs rushing attack being their greatest offensive asset, we saw a bit more Tyleik Williams in this game opposite McNeill. Gill-Howard continues to see goal-line snaps on offense as an extra blocker, but it looked like he may have injured his leg on his final blocking attempt and needed to turn down post-game interview requests from Pride of Detroit to get treatment. On a short week, this will be worth keeping a close eye on.

Rush End Derrick Moore: 55 (80%) — 4 (16%)

With Aidan Hutchinson on injured reserve, speculation ran high about how the Lions would replace his snaps, but in their first game without him, the answer was simpler than expected: increase Derrick Moore's playing time.

Wonnum, Lacy, and O'Neill saw few changes to their snap counts, but Moore's snaps increased significantly. Over the Lions' first six games, Moore never saw more than 30% of defensive snaps, but that number climbed to 42% in Week 8, 58% in Week 9, 68% in Week 10—as a game replacement for Hutchinson—and 80% now that he's a starter. He rewarded coaches with a half-sack, a team-leading seven pressures (per NextGen Stats), and a 19.4% pressure rate.

Fan favorite Hassanein finally got to play in his first NFL game, and while he was quiet in his 12 snaps, it's a valuable first step for the developing defensive end.

MIKE/WILL Malcolm Rodriguez: 55 (80%) — 13 (52%)

SAM/MIKE Derrick Barnes: 53 (77%) — 7 (28%)

OLB/PR Trevor Nowaske: 22 (32%) — 18 (72%)

The Lions linebacker carousel continues to rotate weekly. After losing Jack Campbell, the Lions leaned heavily on Barnes and Rodriguez in their first game without their leader. The following week, Barnes' snaps stayed high, while Rodriguez stepped back and made way for Nowaske and Britt. This week, Rodriguez jumped Barnes in snaps for the first time this season, while Britt jumped Nowaske by a few reps.

While there has been little consistency beyond Barnes playing at least 75% of snaps, the rotation is working. Rodriguez secured a sack, Barnes was key in run defense, Britt made an important fourth-down stop early in the game, and Nowaske delivered a delicious sack to help close things out.

CB Ennis Rakestraw: DNP (Inactive – Injury)

With Kerby Joseph's return at safety, the Lions shifted their secondary usage, keeping McCreary and Ya-Sin almost exclusively on the outside and letting the safeties handle the middle of the field. In base sets, the Lions leaned on Branch and Clark—now wearing the green communication dot and the "C" on his jersey—and in subpackage sets, Kerby took Branch's deep role, while Branch shifted into the slot/box.

"I think it actually slowed it down for me even more, just calmed the situation down," Clark said of the returns of Joseph and Branch. "Two guys that have been in this system. I know it was some things out there on the field that we just talking about and it just naturally flow. Alright, cool. It felt good. Like I said, I kind of said this the other day, looking at the tandem of them to do all them from the outside being on different teams like, OK, them boys, they hooping. And actually be in the mix with them, it felt good."

Bates missed from 53 yards on the Lions' opening drive but was clean the rest of the way, connecting from 56 in the second quarter and on all four extra-point attempts.
