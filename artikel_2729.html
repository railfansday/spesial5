Flour Bluff volleyball seniors leave mark after second state title match

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Flour Bluff and Highland Park came into this week as the last two UIL state champions in Class 5A Division I volleyball in the state, and both showed their championship meddle in the 2026 title game.

Pushed to the brink in each of the first two sets, the Scots found a way to erase multiple set points and will themselves to a 2-0 lead.

But like any team with championship pedigree, the Hornets showed the grit that allowed them to earn their way to the Curtis Culwell Center for the second time in three years, battling back to force a decisive fifth set, before the Scots emerged with a 27-25, 28-26, 22-25, 20-25, 15-12 win.

FLOUR BLUFF VOLLEYBALL: Flour Bluff volleyball falls in five sets to Highland Park in UIL title game

The Hornets and nine seniors saw their aim of a second state title in three years fall a few points short, ending as the state runner up after another dominant season.

Flour Bluff (37-11) had won 60 straight sets going into the championship match as part of a 20-game win streak, including sweeps in both the regional final against Sharyland and state semifinal against Georgetown.

The Scots, which claimed back-to-back state titles with the win, had a height advantage with three players taller than 6-foot-2, but the Hornets controlled the majority of all five sets. Highland Park needed late surges in all three sets they claimed, to hoist the trophy.

"We dug deep," Flour Bluff head coach Kara Wallace said. "A lot of people would probably say that team was better than us, but we came in and showed them what South Texas was about. I don't think there is anything for them to hang their head over. They gave them an amazing game.

"How can you be mad if you go five sets with anyone? I'm super proud of the leadership that I had from them. They stepped up and they fought back after we could have keeled over after those first two sets."

Flour Bluff led 24-21 in the first set only to see the Scots rattled off a 6-1 run and then had another lead at 24-23 in the second before Highland Park surged late.

Kristina Rodriguez helped the Hornets late in the third to close out the set, while Nya White and Emily Eggleston had strong fourth sets as Flour Bluff pushed the match to five.

"I told them to trust each other, believe in each other, have heart and have faith," said Wallace of her message during the changeover after the second set. "That is what got us here — working as a family and a team.

"We figured out what was working for us, shook off some of the nerves and we were ready to settle in. We had a lot of kids step up in places we really needed."

White, a Texas State signee, led all players with 20 kills in the championship match while Alyssa Thornton (UT Tyler commit) recorded 10 kills. Rodriguez (St. Mary's) excelled in multiple areas with nine kills, 25 assists and 12 digs.

Rodriguez explained how tight-knit the team is and how much representing Flour Bluff has been for her.

"A lot of us have played multiple seasons together for years," Rodriguez said. "When we wear Flour Bluff across our chest, it is not just the school, it is the community, our teammates and our sisters along with us."

Wallace said it will be challenging to replace a large, highly successful senior group, but the mark they have left will last.

"They've been with me a long time. Some of these kids have played with me for four years straight," Wallace said. "It is going to be big shoes to fill. They are leaving behind a huge legacy and tradition. These kids have been able to go to state twice in three years, which is incredible for us.

"I am going to miss them a lot. They are like my kids. My daughters. We laugh. We cry. I crash out on them but then we come back together and its ok. We love each other. We have a lot to fill for next year, for sure."
