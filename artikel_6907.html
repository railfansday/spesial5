Why Don’t You … Take Fashion Inspiration from the World’s Great Performers?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Diana Vreeland had a singular take on not only fashion but life. Her eccentricity has become folklore and was best exemplified in an illustrated column of irreverent advice for chic living she called “Why Don’t You?” Resurrected and reimagined for modern life, the column, now written by Bazaar’s executive digital director, Lynette Nylander, will dispense tips on how to live stylishly, decadently, and daringly.

Here’s a thought: Don’t get dressed. Mount a production. Put on a show. Go on tour. And if the reviews are lukewarm, don’t change the costume; blame the critic! For the performance issue, Lynette Nylander takes cues from the stage’s most stylish to arm you with tips for stepping out.

that the “effortless” look took 40 minutes. The “I just threw it on” leather jacket, the smudged eye makeup, the shirt that looks slept in: All of it is stagecraft. Miley Cyrus’s new rock-star look personifies this. She’s not someone to shy away from an “era,” and this one is all tousled hair, loads of lashes, and leather and lace from the likes of Tom Ford, Roberto Cavalli, and Saint Laurent. Effortless? No. Fabulous? Absolutely!

some noise. Taffeta rustles, silk whispers, embellishments jangle, and leather creaks with intent. Performance is not only visual. The swish of a skirt on a staircase is part of the entrance, and the weight of crepe hugging the hips is part of the walk. The late, great Tina Turner was a master of clothes that created a symphony as she writhed around the stage; often, they were the creations of the late, great Bob Mackie. Björk went viral earlier this year at the Venice Biennale in a neon-pink Bottega Veneta dress made of fiberglass shards that jingle-jangled together as she fist-pumped. The lesson? Dress for the ear as well as the eye.

the precision of a uniform. The chef’s whites, the lawyer’s suit, the professor’s tweeds, the tailored black of the gallery assistant: Each is a costume designed with a very particular audience in mind. Even people who wear the same thing every day are making a statement, and the strongest of them curate that repetition with real rigor. Consistency, done well, is a kind of theater. Just don’t make it boring! Consider Steve Jobs, who famously wore only Issey Miyake turtlenecks and Levi’s. Truly the only tech titan to ever have taste!

the art of quick change. For Céline Dion’s fall residency at the Plénitude Arena in Paris, underneath the spotlight wasn’t the only site where a performance took place. Like all true divas, Dion knows that every moment of every day is an opportunity to perform. Outside of the Royal Monceau hotel, her home in Paris, where her faithful fans lined up to greet her, she treated onlookers to a dance, a song, an air-kiss, and always a fashion show: off-the-runway looks from Jacquemus, Loewe, and Phoebe Philo. As they say, all the world’s a stage.

the ordinary garment its moment. Not every act needs sequins. A perfect white tee, a pair of jeans that have taken on the shape of a life, a forever leather jacket worn with confidence: these are the understudies who steal the show. Performance in fashion is not always about drama. Sometimes it is the quiet authority of a simple thing worn exactly right. A disciple of this way of dressing? Kim Gordon, whose effortless style, both onstage and offstage, has made her your favorite celebrity’s favorite celebrity for the past 40 years.
