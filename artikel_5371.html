ZetaChain's 55M ZETA Burn Isn't a One-Time Event: What Proposal 2 Really Does

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

ZetaChain's Governance Proposal 2 would halt its L1 around Dec 9, mint ZETA 1:1 on Solana and burn about 55M ZETA in monthly installments. Voting closes Nov 7.

Headlines are calling it a 55 million ZETA burn. Read the proposal's own schedule, though, and nothing close to 55 million disappears on the day the chain halts. The figure is real, but it arrives in twelve installments over 2027.

ZetaChain published Governance Proposal 2 on November 4, and its announcement post lays out the mechanics. About 55 million ZETA of undistributed validator emissions will be burned monthly through 2027, following the schedule on which they would have been emitted. The roadmap in the same post puts the pace at twelve monthly burns of roughly 4.6 million ZETA each, from January to December 2027.

That detail changes how the number should be read. Most of the early flash coverage collapsed it into a single burn, which implies an immediate supply shock. The staged schedule tells a different story. These tokens were never going to circulate, because they are validator rewards for an L1 that is about to stop. Burning them on the original emission timetable keeps the published release schedule intact and introduces no new mechanism, as ZetaChain's FAQ puts it.

Governance Proposal 2 builds on Proposal 68, which approved the move to Solana in September with 99.4% in favor. Proposal 2 supplies the mechanics. Voting runs 72 hours and closes on November 7, and ZetaChain says nothing moves unless it passes.

If it does pass, every ZETA balance is recorded at snapshot block 20,755,000, expected around December 9. The ZetaChain L1 halts at that same height and isn't restarted. Claims open the same day through the ZetaHub migration portal, and ZetaChain's new private AI app, ZetaLabs, launches alongside it.

The proposal also pairs the migration with a new whitepaper, which ZetaChain calls the document of record for the token after the move.

The snapshot goes well beyond a simple wallet count. It records liquid, staked, unbonding and unclaimed-reward balances. Staked ZETA is credited automatically, so nobody has to unstake, and exchange balances convert without any action from holders. Staking rewards stop at the snapshot height.

Holders who want to claim directly face a short process. They connect the wallet that held ZETA, sign a message naming a Solana wallet, and receive the full balance shortly afterward. The claim costs no fee and requires no SOL. According to the post, the portal asks only for a signature, never for a transaction or a seed phrase. A minimum of 10 ZETA at the snapshot is needed to claim.

Two edge cases deserve attention. First, ZETA wrapped or sitting in pools and contracts can't be claimed by a key, so holders need to unwrap and exit before the snapshot. Second, assets still held as ZRC-20 tokens at the snapshot can be claimed back on their original chain through a separate bridged-assets page, with a $5 minimum per token. Both are the kind of detail that catches people out when a deadline arrives.

On Solana, ZETA becomes a native SPL token under the same ticker, with 9 decimals and no freeze authority. The full snapshot total is minted before the portal opens, and then the mint authority is revoked, so no one can ever mint more. No third-party bridge is involved.

That sequence is the strongest holder protection in the plan, and it is also the part that can be checked in public. The supply stays capped at 2.1 billion less whatever has been burned, and ZetaChain says it will publish the SPL mint address and the migration program address before December 9. Once those exist, anyone can compare the minted total against the snapshot and confirm that the authority was actually revoked. That confirmation deserves the most scrutiny.

Meanwhile, the portal and its program are under independent review by Hacken, and ZetaChain says the portal opens only after the review is complete and the report is published. The ERC-20 version of ZETA on Ethereum is unaffected by the proposal. Purchaser and core contributor vesting completes on January 1, 2027, and ZetaChain says no newly released ZETA will be sold in 2027.

The biggest practical risk is inattention. Most holders don't need to do anything until December 9, but exchange users depend on each exchange's own conversion window, and anyone holding ZETA through a contract or pool has homework to do beforehand.

Scams add another layer of risk. Any migration with a claim portal invites imitators, and ZetaChain tells holders to trust only its official domains and social accounts. A simple rule of thumb helps here: a site that asks for a seed phrase should be closed immediately, since the legitimate flow only needs a message signature.

On the technical side, claims are checked against the published snapshot rather than the live chain, so they continue to work after the halt. ZetaChain says it will announce any change to the process in advance.

The vote decides the terms, not the direction. If Proposal 2 fails, the direction approved in Proposal 68 stands and ZetaChain returns with a revised proposal. Nothing moves on these terms.

If it passes, the sequence is clear. The security report, mint address and migration program address arrive before December 9. The snapshot, halt, mint and portal opening follow on the day, and the monthly burns run through 2027. ZetaLabs then adds a Premium tier in the first quarter of 2027, with ZETA as the token for access and payment.

Taken together, this looks more like a retirement plan for an L1 than a dramatic supply event. The burn is gradual, the mint is capped, and the claim process is simple on paper. Execution is what will matter most: the audit report, the published addresses and the clean revocation of mint authority. Those are the items to watch between now and December 9.
