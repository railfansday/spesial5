Chanel’s CEO Leena Nair makes the case for human made ‘beauty and creation.’ In an era of AI saturation, her $19 billion-a-year business depends on it

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The chief executive of one of the world’s leading fashion brands has said that the intense and often slow process of craftswomen and men making products by hand has become a vital skill in a world of online “instant gratification.”

Leena Nair, the CEO of Chanel, said that business leaders are living through an “unprecedented” period of transformation and fragility, adding that it was time to prioritize human ingenuity and talent.

“Macro-economic shifts, huge leaps in AI and technology and geopolitical instability, these are challenges affecting all sectors,” she told the annual gala dinner of the French Chamber of Great Britain, which brings together U.K. and French business executives, policy makers, and cultural leaders.

“And in this context of disruption, the power of creation and beauty has never been more vital or more precious. Creation and beauty have been vital sources of inspiration and joy for thousands of years. They’re even more precious today in a world of constant instant gratification, constant overstimulation, and infinite touch points.”

Nair, who has been Chanel CEO since 2022, saw the luxury brand return to growth last year, led by an increase in sales in America. Global revenues rose by 1.8% compared with 2024 to $19.3 billion, while operating profits rose 5.2% to $4.7 billion.

Nair said there was a growing body of scientific evidence that revealed that exposure to art and human-led creative work led to higher levels of happiness and performance.

“This growing body of academic research shows how encounters with beauty correlate directly to a greater sense of human flourishing at work and in life,” she said. “In recent years, we see growing scientific evidence of the improving impact that arts and culture has on physical and mental well-being.”

“In this context of disruption, the power of creation and beauty has never been more vital or more precious”

Over 95% of Chanel’s customers are women, Nair said, adding that “beauty” is a very important driver of how people respond to the world around them. She said that one customer who had been through surgery said that buying crafted products was part of her recovery.

“Surgeons tell me this all the time, that when surgery is over they know the women are back to normal when they ask to put on their makeup for the first time,” she said.

Many leaders of luxury brands often feel ignored when it comes to policy debates about economic growth. In the U.K., the end of foreign visitors being able to shop without incurring the national sales tax has caused controversy. In the U.S., new tariffs targeting luxury goods have unsettled markets.

“Luxury is an economic powerhouse, it’s a cultural powerhouse and an emotional powerhouse,” Nair said. “To put that scale into context, European high-end luxury brands generate a trillion dollars in business every year. That’s 5% of Europe’s GDP. “It’s not a niche sector. More than 2 million people work in the sector across Europe.”

Chanel works with the King’s Foundation, King Charles III’s organization that supports the retention and development of traditional skills.

“It looks simple, but to do a pleated skirt takes eight hours of pushing a cardboard mold back and forth by two talented artisans doing it the right way,” Nair said, arguing that Chanel continues to invest in “skills that will otherwise be lost, working with extraordinary artisans and partners, many of whom we have worked with for generations.”
