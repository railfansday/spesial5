Columbia University's Japanese Culture Center Rebuilds Website With Digital Silk to Open Three Decades of Lectures to the Public

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Donald Keene Center, a Columbia University institute, had Digital Silk rebuild its site as a mobile-friendly archive in English and Japanese.

NEW YORK, Nov. 26, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The Donald Keene Center of Japanese Culture at Columbia University has launched a new bilingual website with Digital Silk. The site turns more than 30 years of lectures, events and programs into an archive that students and the public can browse on any device.

The project addresses a problem shared by many university centers, museums and libraries. Their history is often their strongest proof of authority, yet it sits on aging websites where few people can find it.

A Legacy Hidden on a Desktop-Era SiteThe Center was named for Donald Keene, the late Columbia scholar and translator of Japanese literature. For more than three decades, it has hosted lectures, book talks, symposia and programs such as the Sen Lectures and a translation prize.

That record had piled up on a website built for desktop browsing, with no clear way to explore it. On a phone, the site was difficult to use. The old site also reinforced a narrow reputation. The public mainly saw the Center as a literary institution, while much of its programming reached into film, the arts and culture.

Letting the Scholarship LeadDigital Silk started with little more than a logo, Columbia's brand guidelines and a request for a conservative academic look. Rather than add visual effects, the team let the scholarship lead. The design uses a clear editorial hierarchy and a few quiet video moments, so the site reads as scholarly but not cold.

The site is now organized around how visitors explore: About, Programs, Events and Lectures, the Archive and News. Major programs, including the Sen Lectures, the translation prize and Columbia student support, each have a dedicated home. Visitors can filter events by lectures, book talks, workshops and symposia.

Digital Silk migrated more than 30 years of historical material into a dedicated archive of lectures, events and translated works. Consistent page templates for programs, lectures, events and resources let the Center present its film and arts programming as clearly as its literary work.

The Japanese version is a full site, not a translated summary. Typography and layouts were designed for both scripts, and language signals tell search engines which version to show each audience. Contact and newsletter forms give the Center a direct line to its audience, and search optimization on the main pages prepares the site for future discoverability.

"Academic institutions often assume a modern website means more motion and more marketing. For the Keene Center, it meant the opposite," said Ana Margarida Meira, VP at Digital Silk. "Thirty years of lectures was the most persuasive thing they had. The work was to make that record easy to find, in both languages, for a team of essentially one person."

For other university centers and cultural nonprofits, the project serves as a reminder that before a redesign adds new features, it should make existing scholarship visible, organized and readable on a phone.
