I Hike With a Flip Phone. Here’s the Tiny Camera I’m Bringing Along.

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Over the past few years, I’ve learned I’m much happier when I don’t have my smartphone on me—I’m not sure the human mind evolved to have 24-hour access to all of the Internet’s information on demand. In an effort to disconnect and be more present, I took a page from the Gen Z playbook and started carrying a flip phone instead. It’s been great—except for the camera.

A while ago, I started testing a potential solution in the Life Camera S168, a thumb-size digital camera built to compete with the Kodak Charmera. How did it actually perform on the trail? Check out this video to see what kind of photos and footage this $30 gadget is capable of turning out and read our full review for more.

A while ago, I swapped out my smartphone for a flip phone. I was getting tired of how distracted I was—doomscrolling, checking my emails—and I wanted to simplify things. This has really done the trick. On the trail it’s pretty much everything I need. It can send messages, make calls, if I need to get a hold of 911 in an emergency, it can do that. It has an MP3 player, so I can listen to podcasts at night, and it even has a rudimentary mapping app, although I’m not sure I’d trust it, I always bring a paper map and compass as backup.

However, it does have one big shortcoming: the camera on this thing really sucks. The pictures are grainy, blurry, and usually blown out. It’s not how I want to remember my trips. I often find myself hiking with my smartphone just so I can get better pictures and video. I’ve been looking for a solution, since that kind of defeats the purpose of ditching the smartphone, and I think I’ve found one.

This is the Life Camera S168. It’s part of a crop of new thumb-sized digital cameras that are designed to appeal to the retro crowd, they’re kind of like old digital cameras or old film cameras, you know, the disposable you used to take to summer camp with you. The most familiar one is the Kodak Charmera, you may have seen that in stores.

However, according to a lot of online reviews, the Life Camera’s photos and video quality is significantly better, and it has, from what I’ve seen, a fairly decent camera, you’re not going to confuse it with an iPhone or anything, but it may be enough for my purposes.

So, is the Life Camera a gimmick or a game changer? Well, that depends on you. If you’re like most hikers and you have your phone in your pocket most of the time, it’s definitely a gimmick. Your phone can do anything the Life Camera can do, and probably in better resolution.

If, however, you’re part of the small subset of hikers like me, who find they’re happier when their phone is off and in their pack, or if they’re just carrying a flip phone, you might give the Life Camera a try. No, the resolution isn’t anything to write home about, but it can capture your life on the trail in adequate detail, and if you don’t mind that retro vibe, it’s kind of fun. I’ll be carrying one on my next hike.
