Nearly half of U.S. weight-loss drug users are changing how they take them, CDC finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Skipping doses was the most common change at 28%, and 38% of users who made changes cited cost, according to the CDC

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention released a report on Thursday showing that 44% of U.S. adults taking GLP-1 medications such as Wegovy and Zepbound changed their use of the drugs from the way they were prescribed.

Skipping doses topped the list of changes, with 28% of users saying they did it. Among users who made changes, cost was the reason for 38% and availability for 36%.

The data come from the CDC’s Rapid Surveys System, which collected responses from 8,118 U.S. adults in July and August 2025. Overall, an estimated 9.8% of adults said they had used a GLP-1 drug at some point over the past year. The 976 users in the sample received follow-up questions about their use.

Younger adults reported the most changes. Modification rates were 54% among users ages 18 to 39 and 30% among users 65 and older. Delayed prescription fills were reported by 37% of the younger group and 6% of the older group, and 58% of younger users who made changes cited cost, compared with 20% of older users.

Compounded formulations were used by 22.6% of GLP-1 users, and another 8.8% could not say if theirs was one. The Food and Drug Administration does not vet compounded GLP-1 medications for how safe, effective or well made they are before they go to market. Modification rates were 62% among compounded-drug users and 36% among other users.

About 12% of users reported reducing or stopping their medication because of side effects or symptoms. The CDC said more evidence on the safety and effectiveness of these real-world practices could help guide clinical guidelines.

The report noted limitations. The two survey panels had cumulative response rates of 13.3% and 3.8%, and the findings rely on self-reported answers.

The survey was conducted before Eli Lilly $LLY and Novo Nordisk intensified their push to make the drugs easier to get and to take on lower-priced compounded versions, according to Bloomberg. The federal government has also taken steps to broaden access, with Medicare patients among those covered.

Cost has been a barrier for some time. Without insurance or discounts, FDA-approved weight-loss drugs carry a sticker price that can top $1,000 a month, which helped drive demand for compounded versions during shortages of approved products.

The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge program, which began July 1, offers eligible beneficiaries weight-loss drugs for $50 a month.
