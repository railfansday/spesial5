SpaceX sold $32 billion in AI chip debt, with most at yields above 8%

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The sale is part of a more than $40 billion financing that funds Nvidia chips SpaceX will lease for about five years

SpaceX sold $32 billion of debt tied to Nvidia $NVDA chips on Thursday, with most of it priced at yields above 8%, according to Bloomberg. The debt was sold in three parts and will fund the company's access to AI chips.

The largest piece was Class A3 notes totaling roughly $25.2 billion, which were priced to yield 8.259%, according to Bloomberg. The average yield on U.S. investment-grade bonds is 5.95%, according to Bloomberg index data. Roughly $6 billion of the A3 notes was bought by institutional buyers on Thursday, while earlier placements accounted for the remainder.

The money will pay for Nvidia chips bought by Saturn Funding Trust, a special-purpose vehicle managed by an Apollo Global Management entity, according to Bloomberg. SpaceX will rent the hardware under a lease lasting roughly five years. The deal is structured so the debt stays off SpaceX's books, and it forms one piece of a financing worth over $40 billion that additionally involves bank loans.

Goldman Sachs $GS and Apollo ran the transaction, according to Bloomberg. Spokespeople for the firms and SpaceX did not reply when Bloomberg asked for comment.

Saturn Funding Trust purchases the chips, yet SpaceX remains obligated to keep paying on the lease if the hardware breaks down or a force majeure event such as a natural disaster strikes, according to Bloomberg. The structure should allow SpaceX to hold on to its investment-grade credit ratings.

The debt package will cover roughly $47.3 billion in GPU equipment, primarily the newest Rubin chips from Nvidia, according to Bloomberg. Delivery is expected to start in January. Accounts at BlackRock $BLK, Goldman Sachs Asset Management and KKR numbered among the cornerstone buyers.

Davis Hebert, head of technology, media and telecom at CreditSights, said the pricing was deliberately generous so that more conventional buyers would take part, according to Bloomberg. He said clients have been skeptical of the deal after losses on SpaceX's unsecured bonds earlier this year.

Those bonds were part of a $25 billion high-grade offering priced days after SpaceX’s initial public offering in June, which raised $86.2 billion including the underwriters’ option, according to Bloomberg.

SpaceX is not the only AI company turning to chip-backed financing. CoreWeave was seeking an investment-grade loan of at least $4 billion last week, backed by chips and related contracts. Early discussions pointed to a yield of 7% to 7.5%.
