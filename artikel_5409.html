Nvidia is stopping AI chip shipments to China after Jensen Huang's exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Interim CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia sold $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper chips to China last quarter and has none in guidance

Samuel Boivin / NurPhoto via Getty Images

Nvidia $NVDA has stopped shipping licensed Hopper data center chips to China, interim CEO Colette Kress said on an earnings call on Tuesday. The halt comes days after co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO.

Nvidia booked $2.4 billion in China sales of licensed Hopper-based data center hardware during the fiscal third quarter, which ended Oct. 25. Kress said the company's forward guidance includes no such products. "Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China," she said.

Kress gave no reason for the end of the shipments and did not indicate whether the pause would last, according to Reuters. The Hopper family counts the H200 among its chips, a processor that powers both the development and the operation of cutting-edge AI systems.

Huang had pushed harder than anyone at the company for selling chips into the Chinese market, according to Bloomberg. After meeting Chinese President Xi Jinping at a summit last year, President Donald Trump declared that vetted buyers in China could purchase the H200. The arrangement routed eligible chips through the U.S. on their way to China, with a 25% tariff imposed on them when imported into the U.S. Washington cleared 10 Chinese firms to buy the H200, among them Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance, according to Reuters. Beijing has restricted H200 imports.

On Sunday, the Trump administration issued rules that limit sales of advanced U.S. chips to data centers that plan to give Chinese companies remote access. Kress said Nvidia's pile of unfilled orders for high-end products such as Vera Rubin means the rules would not touch its top-line revenue.

Nvidia announced that its board has created a committee whose remit covers export restrictions and national security. Board Chair Harvey Jones said on the call that the board recognizes it is "living in a world" where "national security dynamics are making major changes in our marketplace." The next CEO will need to "navigate those waters very carefully," Jones said.

Huang, who said Tuesday he would be honored to return as CEO if the board decides it serves shareholders, employees, the company and America, resigned on Nov. 12 after admitting to an affair. The woman is a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer. U.S. intelligence officials suspect she is an agent of Beijing's spy agencies. Beijing has rejected any suggestion that it targeted Huang for espionage.

Jones said the board has hired Heidrick & Struggles to run the search for a permanent CEO and does not intend to reinstate Huang. Kress is serving as interim CEO while keeping her role as chief financial officer.
