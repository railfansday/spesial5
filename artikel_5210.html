Bitcoin mining industry rocked by AI-linked breach as hackers divert up to 13 EH/s of computing power

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Luxor confirmed attackers compromised its Commander platform, while operators reported thousands of miners redirected to an unfamiliar pool.

Bitcoin mining operators faced a suspected large-scale hijacking over the weekend after thousands of machines were allegedly redirected to the OCEAN mining pool without authorization.

On Nov. 15, mining infrastructure provider CoreX Hosting raised the alarm that mining equipment was being redirected to OCEAN and that rewards were being assigned to a single Bitcoin address.

BTC enthusiast GalaxyMind estimated that the attack affected approximately 64,000 Bitcoin mining machines, redirecting 13 exahashes per second (EH/s) of computing power since around 8 p.m. Central Time on Nov. 14.

Blockchain security firm CoinSpect added that the redirected computing power represented approximately 1.5% of Bitcoin's global hashrate.

The incident threatened miners' earnings because the unauthorized changes directed rewards away from their intended recipients while their equipment continued consuming electricity and processing transactions.

CoreX subsequently reported that it was restoring affected miners to their original configurations and believed it had isolated the program responsible for the unauthorized changes.

The company later declared the incident over after the computing power associated with the suspicious address reportedly fell to approximately 100 petahashes per second (PH/s), down from the previously reported 13 EH/s.

Still, blockchain records show that the Bitcoin address identified in CoreX's original warning had received mining rewards. Data from OCEAN shows three payments totaling 0.24308905 BTC between Nov. 15 and Nov. 16.

While the central cause of the attack remains unknown, Bitcoin mining technology company Luxor confirmed that its Commander mining-management platform suffered a security breach that allowed unauthorized changes to customers' mining configurations.

“Last night our internal monitoring alerted us to an unauthorized redirect of hashrate through Commander…over a dozen client accounts. Affected machines were pointed to a different pool for a limited window. No data was leaked or exfiltrated from our systems.”

The firm's engineering team patched Commander within two hours and began restoring affected machines to their original mining pools. It also promised full compensation for the redirected computing power.

Luxor CEO Nick Hansen attributed the compromise to a “sophisticated AI-enabled vector,” saying the attack began around 6 p.m. Pacific time on Nov. 14.

Hansen apologized to affected customers and warned that cryptocurrency companies were facing an increasingly aggressive wave of AI-powered attacks. He said:

“At this moment, anyone running any sort of substantial business, especially in crypto, is inundated with AI-enabled attacks. Abliterated models are running 24/7 against your entire surface, looking for a little crack, which they then use to (almost instantly) expand. This does not seem to be slowing down; rather, it's speeding up as models get better and more attackers enter the arena.”

He revealed that Luxor had acquired dedicated GPUs to run its own modified AI models for defensive security testing and had suspended much of its new feature development to harden its infrastructure.

Luxor has not disclosed the precise method used to compromise Commander or confirmed GalaxyMind's estimates of 64,000 affected machines and 13 EH/s.

The company has promised a full post-mortem, which could establish how the attackers gained access, the actual extent of the disruption, and whether the reported computing power was entirely linked to its compromised platform.
