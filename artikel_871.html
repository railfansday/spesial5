The Spygate reunion that proves Southampton and Tonda Eckert got off lightly

Southampton face Middlesbrough for the first time since the ‘Spygate’ scandal - but with Tonda Eckert still in a job and Saints flying high, Will Castle questions whether justice was really done

When Southampton were kicked out of the Championship play-off final four days before football’s richest game, there was a bubbling dread that things were about to spiral.

‘Spygate’ had rocked English football to its core. It started with an intern, camera in hand, failing to adequately hide behind a tree outside Middlesbrough’s training ground, birthing the cartoonish image that will forever be the thumbnail of this scandal. What subsequently unravelled was far more sinister; a pattern of cheating by Saints that was driven by those at the very top. As the club was put under the spotlight, they also admitted to espionage against Ipswich and Oxford before being caught red-handed spying on their play-off semi-final opponents.

Tonda Eckert wasn’t simply complicit in sending staff out to spy on rivals; it was initially stated that he spearheaded the plot. Findings of the League Arbitration Panel investigating the scandal laid bare of the Saints boss’ influence in “a contrived and determined plan from top down to gain a competitive advantage”, which left junior staff feeling “compelled to do what they were very uncomfortable doing because they considered their jobs would otherwise be at risk”.

“Deplorable” was the word used to describe this scheme, and it resulted in an unprecedented punishment. Saints were expelled from the play-off final and stripped of their shot at promotion back to the Premier League, along with the £200m in riches that come with it. They were also handed a four-point deduction for the start of the 2026/27 Championship season. The club called this "manifestly disproportionate to every previous sanction in the history of the English game". But it felt like this was only the start.

Eckert, who at just 33 had been building a reputation as one of English football’s most promising young managers, was at serious risk of seeing his rise completely derailed. The FA swiftly opened an investigation into the German and a lengthy ban from football was on the table for bringing the game into disrepute. Had he been slapped with a months-long suspension, Southampton - who wanted to stand by their manager - would have been forced into unwanted turmoil in the dugout.

Instead, Eckert - who took responsibility and admitted the misconduct charges - escaped serious punishment. The FA’s findings were not as harsh as the EFL’s, stating that while he authorised said spying, there was less of a culture of pressure. The contradiction raised eyebrows. He was given a six-week suspended ban and a £28,000 fine.

Now, two months on from this verdict and six months since ‘Spygate’ erupted, Eckert will lead his side into their first post-scandal meeting with Middlesbrough. He’ll do so having just signed a new contract.

There is a fair argument to say Eckert, as an individual responsible for his own behaviour, got off lightly. In any other job, suspicions of such an abuse of power could result in the sack. Southampton chose to back him, which is their prerogative - Eckert has the best win rate of any Saints manager since the 1890s, so it’s no surprise they wanted to avoid losing the German. But both club and manager are fortunate that the FA opted against a full measure, which would have effectively taken his future out of their hands.

Still at the helm, Eckert has been empowered to oversee a recovery at St Mary’s, to the point where the shockwaves of ‘Spygate’ are no longer really being felt. The four-point deduction was swiftly made up at the start of the season and the club are now seven points off the top and very much in the promotion hunt. Their formidable home form sets them apart, unbeaten in 18 on their own turf.

Tipped for a season of chaos in the summer, they welcome league leaders Boro almost back on their perch by November. The visitors might feel a tinge of injustice at just how quickly they’ve managed to return to this point, especially with Eckert still pulling the strings.

Eckert hinted that hostilities between him and Boro boss Kim Hellberg had been quelled in his pre-match press conference on Thursday. The Swede said he had sleepless nights due to the “disgraceful” actions of his opposition, but Eckert stated the pair spoke on the phone earlier this year and that both will go into Friday’s clash with only the end result in mind. "We've had a conversation on the phone and we saw each other at the EFL day before the season,” he said. “We are two managers who will try to win a game and that's it."

Boro are out for redemption this term. Their last-minute ticket to the play-off final went in vain when hearts were broken by Ollie McBurnie at Wembley. They look well-placed to go one better this season, though, fuelled by a fantastic summer transfer window which saw them bring in, among others, the league’s current top scorer Will Lankshear from Tottenham.

But for all their sins, Saints still harbour the feeling that they emerged from this scandal wronged by their sanction; akin to a poor man’s Weimar Germany after World War One. Before Eckert learned his fate at the hands of the FA, chief executive Phil Parsons claimed the club had “taken enough” punishment, chiefly their play-off expulsion. He complained about the legal fees and the money being lost by local businesses, almost as if to drive pity for the club. And while he stated: “We don’t want to be seen as a victim of this,” that is the narrative that is held in parts of Southampton.

Ultimately, however, Saints were let off the hook to the extent where the club could keep Eckert and rejuvenate before the New Year. Any victim sentiment is borderline deluded, and that’s evidenced by the state of play going into a ‘Spygate’ reunion. This is a promotion six-pointer; after all that’s happened, Southampton should count themselves lucky to be in such a promising position.
