Ready Capital (RC) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Management outlines its path back to breakeven as legacy loan resolutions continue.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Ready Capital Corporation (RC -20.40%) substantially completed its balance sheet repositioning and liquidity plan, reducing corporate debt and asset-level financing while shifting its focus toward resolving legacy commercial real estate assets and rebuilding earnings. Management reported that the company generated $2.3 billion in liquidity year-to-date and is prioritizing capital-light business growth, specifically within its SBA 7(a) and working capital lending platforms. The company stated its intention to reach breakeven distributable earnings by early 2027 by increasing origination volumes and reducing operating expenses through noncore business divestitures and staffing adjustments.

Thomas Capasse: Thank you, Andrew. Good morning, everyone, and thank you for joining today's call. In the third quarter, we substantially completed the balance sheet repositioning and liquidity plan we laid out in the fourth quarter of 2025. After the final November payment we expect to make at maturity, we will have repaid $649 million of corporate debt. Along the way, we reduced secured asset level financing by $1.7 billion and resolved through runoff or sale $2.7 billion of legacy CRE loans, predominantly from the 2021 and '22 vintages. While the plan was achieved within the parameters we set, it came with a cost.

Generating $2.3 billion of liquidity reduced book value per share year-to-date by $2.35 to $6.44 at September 30 and required us to reduce the dividend to $0.01 per share to preserve liquidity. Looking forward, our focus will shift from the liquidity plan to continued resolution of the remaining legacy CRE loan book and rebuilding earnings. Our near-term target is to bring distributable earnings and cash flow from operations closer to breakeven. Quarter-over-quarter, distributable earnings, excluding realized losses improved $0.03 per share to a loss of $0.21 per share. To close the gap, we are prioritizing 4 action items. First, growing revenue in our capital-light businesses led by our SBA 7(a) platform.

The June securitization created funding capacity for $500 million of originations, which we believe will allow us to ramp toward our immediate annual target of $1 billion with a long-term target of $1.5 billion. In the third quarter, originations rose 33% to $109 million and gain on sale revenue grew 56% to $6.1 million. We expect the incremental revenue from increasing volume to our immediate target level will be approximately $8 million per quarter. Our current 7(a) pipeline is $150 million. A natural complement to our secured 7(a) is unsecured conventional working capital lending, smaller balance, shorter duration loans that we originate and sell on a servicing retained basis, requiring little balance sheet capital.

Our fintech iBusiness has originated this product for 10 years, and there's strong demand from private credit investors. Gain on sale income plus origination fees from this product rose 23% to $3.2 million quarter-over-quarter on $61 million of originations. We believe we can scale this production approximately 2x in the new year, which would add $3 million in incremental quarterly revenue. Together, we expect these businesses to be our greatest growth opportunity in 2027. Second, improving the earnings contribution on our legacy CRE portfolio. With our 2026 repositioning plan executed, our asset management team is pivoting from selling loans at discounts to generate liquidity to stabilizing nonperforming loans and REO.

In sum, our asset managers are able to employ more flexible asset management strategies to improve NOI and value at exit. As assets stabilize, they generally move from a carrying cost to a source of income or resolution, which reduces the earnings drag from nonperforming assets. Third, we continue to make progress at the Ritz-Carlton Portland. Hotel NOI, net of replacement reserves improved quarter-over-quarter to $1.6 million, driven by increased occupancy and strong high season performance. Trailing 12-month RevPAR improved 35% year-over-year to $271. Condominium sales were slower in the quarter with 52 units or 39% of the buildings sold. We have an additional 2 units under contract. And fourth, divesting noncore businesses and reducing operating expenses.

We expect to announce specific noncore business divestitures in succeeding quarters and with respect to operating expenses, we anticipate reducing spending in nonrevenue-producing businesses and rightsizing staff to match a smaller balance sheet. We believe we can materially reduce our operating expenses without impacting operations or revenue-producing objectives. Collectively, we expect these 4 actions will bring distributable earnings, excluding realized losses closer to breakeven with future earnings growth beyond these levels determined by the turnover of the legacy CRE loan portfolio, which I'll now discuss. The CRE loan portfolio is now $1.95 billion and is segmented into 3 parts based on asset management strategy.

First, performing loans, which comprise 98 positions with a carrying value of $1.1 billion, a weighted average LTV of 83% and a weighted average coupon of 7.9%. The levered yield of this portfolio is 11.5%. This group is 83% multifamily. Within it, we expect that 29 loans totaling $391 million will pay off at par in 2027, generating approximately $97 million of net liquidity. Second, we expect 14 assets with a carrying value of $318 million to be resolved by the end of 2027, generating approximately $109 million of net liquidity.

This portfolio generated $0.5 million of net income in the third quarter, and we believe that full reinvestment of the equity at market yields would generate improvement of $3 million in net income per quarter. Third, nonperforming loans with longer-term workouts comprised 28 assets with a carrying value of $515 million. For this population, we believe value is best maximized by stabilizing operations before exit rather than selling at today's prices. The current quarterly earnings drag associated with this portfolio is $1.7 million. The portfolio is 93% multifamily and has an average carrying value of $18.4 million.

In addition to the nonperforming loans identified for long-term hold, there are 5 REO assets totaling approximately $67 million in carrying value, which we believe will also benefit from a longer-term hold strategy, allowing sufficient time to stabilize operations and for market recovery. While we undertook asset sales early in the credit cycle, which reduced the relative size of the nonperforming loan book and have active management plans in place for all 28 remaining nonperforming assets, the recent increase in the 10-year treasury has created another leg down in multifamily transaction volume and prices, potentially extending realizations. Additionally, we believe that continued focus on increasing liquidity and reducing leverage remains paramount under current market conditions.

This framework makes the path forward measurable. The performing book supports current income, the resolution segment drives near-term liquidity and earnings recovery and the workout segment is where patient asset management can recover value over time.
