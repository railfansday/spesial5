Magic: The Gathering Boss Reveals the Next Marvel Set’s Surprising Origin Story

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Aaron Forsythe said Magic’s original narrative got ‘total buy-in’ from Marvel

Aaron Forsythe said Magic’s original narrative got ‘total buy-in’ from Marvel

Magic: The Gathering has already released two Universes Beyond sets based on Marvel Comics, with Marvel’s Spider-Man in 2025 and Marvel Super Heroes in 2026. The game’s designers are taking a very different approach to their third tie-in set, Darkhold Destiny, which will launch in 2027. Rather than simply making cards based on iconic characters and comic book arcs, Wizards of the Coast designers worked with Marvel to create an entirely new storyline designed with the game’s mechanics in mind. Magic’s vice president of design Aaron Forsythe told Polygon at MagicCon Atlanta that the inspiration for the new approach came from an unlikely source.

“My daughter was actually playing the Guardians of the Galaxy game on Xbox, which has a cool story,” Forsythe says. “It’s not from a comic, it’s not from a movie. They just let the game studio make their own story that fit the medium they were trying to use. It seems like they should let us do that.”

Forsythe says he received “total buy-in” from Marvel as soon as he brought up the idea.

“I think a lot of that was the trust we had built up working on the first two sets and the Secret Lair,” he said. “They saw that we aren't just a licensing team that wants to put the Hulk on a T-shirt. We create our own worlds and stories, and as creators, they could really appreciate the work we did and gave us the green light right away.”

Development on Darkhold Destiny actually began before Marvel’s Spider-Man launched — Forsythe said all Universes Beyond sets take four years to develop, as opposed to the typical three-year timeline for in-universe sets, because they need to get so much feedback from licensing partners. But even without player feedback, Forsythe says he realized something was missing from Spider-Man and Marvel Super Heroes that hadn’t been a problem in other Universes Beyond sets.

“The Marvel sets in particular are not driven by any real narrative,” he says. “We have some saga cards that talk about the death of Gwen Stacy, which we know people know. The Lord of the Rings has a linear story. Everybody knows how that story plays out. In Final Fantasy, there were 16 mostly linear stories woven together in a way that everyone who's touched those games has experienced. Marvel, with 60 years of stuff and us not using the movies — which are the stories people mostly know nowadays — we’re just picking moments in time, characters, and different sagas and whatnot. As a whole, it did not feel like it was telling much of a story.”

Magic’s designers created an original character, Fraxi, Scion of Yggdrasil, who will appear in Darkhold Destiny and a tie-in comic alongside Marvel favorites like Thor and Doctor Strange. The goal is to satisfy Magic players who want a story while still bringing in Marvel fans who will become committed players.

“I'm not going to say there aren't any tourist fans that just want buy their Spider-Man cards and go home,” Forsythe says. “But mostly once you dip your toes in, once you start building a deck, once you see your friend's Commander deck, once you go online and start reading about all the stuff that's out there, once you go to a store or a tournament or a convention, sure Spider-Man brought you in, but this aperture opens up into this vast, incredible world.”

Forsythe says Darkhold Destiny and the two other still unannounced Universes Beyond sets releasing next year will incorporate feedback they’ve been getting from players about the game’s various crossovers. He hopes these improvements will encourage even the most skeptical Magic players to give Universes Beyond a chance as the licensed sets have also helped bring lapsed players back to the hobby.

“There are some parts of the audience that are doubling down on the Magic IP stuff, being holdouts against UB like, ‘We'll show them! We'll all buy Reality Fracture!’” Forsythe says. “I would just say that UB is both getting new fans, and it's reactivated a lot of people too. I've heard 25 times in the last two days of some IP reactivating somebody like, ‘I'd forgotten about Magic, but it was fun and this thing you did got me back in. And now I'm here.'”
