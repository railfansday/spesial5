Black Friday is here once again, and if you've been waiting for the prices to drop before buying your next telescope, smart telescope, binoculars or star projector — this could be the time to pull the trigger.

But as we all know by now, retailers use these sales as a sneaky way to inflate their prices before the sale starts, so in many instances, their "sale" price is actually the same as it was before they put the price up. So how can you be sure that the price you're seeing is truly a deal?

Well, we keep track of prices throughout the year, and use tools to check when they fluctuate. We know whether something is a genuine deal, and, crucially, whether the product is actually worth buying in the first place.

We've searched the web for the best genuine deals on the telescopes, smart telescopes, binoculars and star projectors that we have actually tested ourselves, so you can be confident you're actually saving money and not getting played by capitalism.

Telescopes give you breathtaking views of the moon and planets.

A great beginner telescope that the whole family can enjoy. This 4.5-inch Newtonian reflector comes with a 20mm eyepiece for scanning across the sky and a 10mm eyepiece for detailed views of the moon or spotting Saturn's rings.

The EQ mount requires a bit of tinkering, but it's a useful skill to have once you've figured it out, as it makes tracking objects across the sky easier than an Alt-Az mount.

This model usually retails for $319.99, so a $40 saving on a telescope of this quality is pretty good.

We've reviewed a smaller telescope in the AstroMaster range, the Celestron AstroMaster LT 70AZ, and our optics writer owns the Celestron AstroMaster 130EQ, which has a slightly larger aperture.

The Celestron StarSense Explorer range is great for anyone who wants to start stargazing but doesn't yet have any knowledge of the night sky, and this model in particular is a good option if you want to invest in something you can grow with.

The setup is a straightforward Alt-Azimuth mount, and you can use the smartphone dock to navigate your way around the sky with the guidance of the StarSense app.

It has a 5.1-inch (130mm) aperture and comes with 25mm and 10mm eyepieces, giving you beautiful views of the moon, Saturn's rings, Jupiter's cloud bands and wide views of some deep-sky objects like the Andromeda Galaxy and the Orion Nebula.

The price tends to fluctuate throughout the year, although on average it sits between $420 and $450. However, it's been the full $499.95 since September, so this price is definitely worth taking advantage of.

Although it's very close in aperture to the StarSense DX 130AZ we listed above, the differences in optical design and focal length offer a different observing experience.

This model is the better choice if you want higher-magnification observations of the Moon and planets, thanks to its 1,250mm focal length. It also has a much shorter optical tube, so it's more compact and easier to transport. The Schmidt-Cassegrain design is more complex to manufacture than a Newtonian reflector, hence the difference in price between the two.

It was $579.99 throughout most of the year, rising to $589.99 in August, which it has been ever since.

Smart telescopes will locate, track and stack images for you, making astrophotography easier than ever.

Granted, it's not the biggest saving ever, and it isn't listed specifically as a Black Friday deal, but you can save $35 on the ZWO Seestar S30 Pro at Amazon. This is the cheapest we've seen it since its release, and it typically stays at the $699 RRP.

It's an incredible way to get into astrophotography, even for complete beginners. It's an all-in-one system that automatically locates, tracks and stacks images of stunning deep-space objects for you. It's compact and portable, with a quick and easy setup and produces beautiful 4K images.

The smallest and most affordable smart telescope on the market is now even more affordable (but still the same size).

It only produces 2K images, which is still plenty for sharing with friends and family or on social media. In practice, it just means you'll be more limited when it comes to cropping.

It's been $419 since August, and this Black Friday deal is the cheapest we've seen it since its release.

If you're a more advanced user and have been waiting to upgrade, this is one of the best chances you'll get. You can now save a whopping $1,000 on the Unistellar eVscope 2 right now.

It has an aperture of 114mm and a 450mm focal length, making it ideal for deep-sky imaging of even faint and small objects. And the electronic Nikon-produced eyepiece adds an immersive element to the experience, rather than viewing everything on your screen.

Another fantastic saving from Unistellar, and the Odyssey Pro doesn't have as much reach as the eVscope 2, meaning it works better on wider objects.

The Odyssey Pro has an 85mm aperture with a 320mm focal length, which is still plenty to get stunning views of galaxies and nebulas. It also has a Nikon eyepiece that shows you how your image is doing as it's stacking — which is beneficial if you're not connected to the app.

Vaonis describes the Vespera 3 as the "most affordable premium smart telescope on the market," and judging from its specs, we'd be inclined to agree.

It has a new apochromatic quadruplet design that eliminates chromatic aberration and field curvature across the entire frame, excellent clarity even under city skies, a massive 11 hours of battery life and built-in multi-night capture.

It has a 50mm aperture and 245mm focal length, with a native image resolution of 8.3MP, or up to 24MP with covalENS mosaic mode.

If you liked the sound of the Vespera 3 but wanted something a little more powerful, the Vespera Pro 2 is $500 off for Black Friday.

It offers a higher resolution of 12.5MP native and 50MP mosaic, has more internal storage (225GB vs 115GB), and comes with a carbon fiber tripod (as opposed to aluminum) and a hard case, which are perhaps more suited for traveling to dark skies.

We've been testing it out over the past few weeks and are incredibly impressed. The images we've taken of the moon, for example, are breathtaking. The Vespera Pro 2 is currently the highest resolution smart telescope on the market.

Big astronomy binoculars are more portable than a telescope, making them ideal for camping trips and spontaneous stargazing outings.

If you're looking for a pair of binoculars you can take anywhere, with excellent optical quality and edge-to-edge sharpness, the Celestron Regal ED 10x42 is an excellent investment.

The ED glass eliminates chromatic aberration, and it has field-flattening lenses to correct curvature, resulting in sharp, bright and detailed images with basically no defects. Perfect for observing the moon and stars with excellent clarity.

We have seen them cheaper at the start of the year, but over the summer they've been $379, so this price is worth taking advantage of.

This variant in particular is ideal for birdwatching, thanks to the 8x magnification, and the ED glass is ideal for following birds in flight. Plus, you can enjoy views of the moon and get a closer look at the stars.
