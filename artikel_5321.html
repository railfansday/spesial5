The FCC gave SpaceX approval for up to 1 million AI data centers in space

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The approval is granted in part with conditions, and SpaceX must file more orbital debris plans before deploying satellites above 600 km

The Federal Communications Commission authorized SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million satellites that would operate as data centers in space, according to an order the agency released on Wednesday.

The FCC's Space Bureau granted SpaceX's application in part and deferred part of it, with conditions. The authority covers radio communications for the constellation, called Starmind, that serve as a fallback and in emergencies to carry telemetry and to track and command the satellites, in the 18.8-19.3 GHz and 28.6-29.1 GHz bands. SpaceX must operate in those bands on an unprotected, non-interference basis. The satellites will rely on optical links for their primary communications, the order said.

The constellation would sit in orbital shells at altitudes from 500 to 1,002 kilometers (311 to 623 miles). The FCC deferred SpaceX's request for a waiver of the rule requiring satellites that end their missions below 2,000 kilometers (1,243 miles) to be disposed of through atmospheric re-entry. Before deploying any satellites that operate above 600 kilometers (373 miles), SpaceX must file an application to modify the authorization with a final orbital debris mitigation plan.

The grant sets deployment milestones. SpaceX must deploy one satellite by Nov. 25, 2033, 10% of the authorized satellites by Nov. 25, 2035, and 50% by Nov. 25, 2038. It must launch the remainder by Nov. 25, 2040, or the authorization becomes null and void. The license term is 15 years and begins when SpaceX certifies that the first Starmind satellite is in orbit and operating under the authorization's terms.

Three parties filed petitions to deny the application: Kuiper Systems, which operates as Amazon $AMZN Leo; Viasat; and the American Astronomical Society. The bureau said it dismissed environmental and optical astronomy concerns as matters outside the Commission's authority.

The proposal prompted a comment tally from the public north of 1,500, according to Bloomberg. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said the commission was, for the first time, approving orbital data centers.

SpaceX had revealed the design of its first orbital data center satellite, called AI1, in a video posted to X $TWTR in June, ahead of its IPO. The orbital compute initiative was central to the company's pitch to investors.

The FCC is weighing other proposals for data centers in orbit and plans to rule on every one, according to The Wall Street Journal. Blue Origin has also applied to the FCC to put its own data centers into orbit and is still waiting for a decision, according to Bloomberg.
