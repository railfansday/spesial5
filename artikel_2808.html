The longest single-opponent winning streaks in Knicks history

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

On Tuesday night, the Knicks outlasted the Raptors for the second time in three nights to extend a staggering 15-game winning streak against their Canadian rivals that dates back to January 2023.

Beyond the fact that it means that old friends RJ Barrett and Immanuel Quickley have never beaten their old team, it also extends one of the longest streaks in program history against a single opponent.

As mentioned on last night's broadcast, it's hard to believe such a dominant stretch existed against a longtime rival that has been the Eastern Conference's banner of success for generations.

Simply, the sport is cyclical, and the few times the Celtics were in the doldrums, the Knicks were contending for titles. Through the many dark ages of Knicks history, the Celtics dominated. It's why they've only faced off in the postseason three times in the last 35 years.

From 1965-67, the Celtics won 21 in a row against the Knicks in the heart of the Bill Russell era. It took 25 years to return the favor.

On January 10, 1993, the Celtics outlasted the Knicks at TD Garden in a 100-97 win. They wouldn't win another game in this rivalry for over five years.

The Celtics won 48 games in 1992-93, but quickly fell into the East's cellar. They wouldn't win 40 games again in a season until 2001-02, while at the same time, Patrick Ewing and the Knicks had only the Bulls and Pacers competing with them for spots in the NBA Finals yearly.

Ewing would score 65 combined over the next two meetings in April 1993 before scoring 30 again on Opening Night that October in a three-point win in Boston. He routinely owned the Celtics during this time, reaching a crescendo with a 36-14 effort in March 1994.

As the Celtics got worse, the Knicks took advantage of them more and more. A 35-point blowout in February 1994 still stands as one of the biggest blowouts in the rivalry's history. Hubert Davis nailed five threes and scored 25 points in a 90-83 win the following month.

It was always a different enemy that flummoxed the Celtics. Charles Smith scoring 25, Anthony Mason scoring 21, and John Starks and Ewing's consistency. Even as Pat Riley turned into Jeff Van Gundy, the Knicks never lost a step.

The streak had reached 17 by January 28, 1997, when the pitiful 9-30 Celtics put the greatest scare into the contending Knicks yet. An eight-point lead in the final minutes dwindled to a tie on a Todd Day three in the final minute before free throws by Antoine Walker gave Boston the lead in the final minute.

Just when the streak appeared over, Chris Childs called game with eight seconds left.

Eventually, the streak would finally end on January 26, 1998, when the Knicks were held to just 85 points behind a 25-point gem by Chauncey Billups. Even this triumphant victory came with an asterisk, as Ewing missed the game due to wrist surgery. Huh.

The Knicks own the Cavs in the playoffs, but did you know there was a stretch they owned them like this?

In five all-time playoff meetings, the Knicks are 16-2 against the Cavs, but once took things to another level in the early 1980s.

Unlike the Celtics, this is pretty peculiar. The Knicks were an average team during this streak, but took advantage of one of the worst eras of Cavs basketball under Ted Stepien.

Quite precisely, this streak can be known as the "Stepien Streak", as while he was owner from April 1980 to his sale in April 1983, the Cavs went 1-16 against the Knicks, with the lone win coming just two weeks before he sold the team in late March of 1983.

Highlights of the streak include Mike Glenn's 29-point game in November 1980 (second-highest scoring game of his career), Ray Williams' 37-point gem in an overtime win two weeks later, and perennially playing in front of 4,000 fans at an empty Richfield Coliseum. The NBA was totally different in the 80s.

Back in the day, there weren't many teams. As such, you get stuff like this.

Over a span of 400 days from February 6, 1952, to March 11, 1953, the Knicks played the Baltimore Bullets 17 times and won every single one of them.

The 1950s Knicks can sometimes be referred to as the Silver Era of Knicks basketball due to the team making multiple Finals appearances before Russell came and made the East his own, so it's no surprise they were dominant. But like this?

The lore of 1950s NBA is quite fascinating. These teams played each other three times in six days in February 1952 when the streak began, but played the games in three different places. The Bullets won 82-81 in the Baltimore Coliseum on February 4, the Knicks won 73-70 at the 69th Regiment Armory on February 6, and the two teams met in Philadelphia on February 8 in a 20-point blowout win for Ray Lumpp and the Knicks.

On March 5, the teams participated in a doubleheader at the Boston Garden, preceding a game between the Bucks and Celtics later that night.

In 1952-53, when the Knicks went 10-0 against the Bullets en route to a Finals appearance, they played one game in Philly, two games in Boston (one of which went to triple overtime), and even played in Trenton, New Jersey, on March 5, 1953.

Finally, the Bullets snapped the skid on November 14, 1953, behind 19 points by Max Zaslofsky in Baltimore.

We've seen a new standard of excellence for the Knicks since the turn of the decade, They have three of these streaks in the last six years!

Back when the Pistons weren't the annoying playoff contenders we know them as now, they were pretty damn awful. For years, it was a running joke that the COVID-19 pandemic was younger than the last Pistons win over the Knicks.

The streak began on February 8, 2020, when the Knicks overcame an early double-digit deficit to outlast the Pistons in Detroit in a battle of two lowly teams that wouldn't even be invited to the bubble. Another win on March 8 would mark the team's final home game with fans for another year due to the pandemic.

As the Knicks ascended, the Pistons stayed bad. On April 4, 2021, the Knicks eviscerated them by a 125-81 margin, the second-biggest blowout in the over 430 games in this matchup.

Even as the meltdown in 2021-22 ensued, the orange and blue survived a scare that March when Marvin Bagley III dropped 27, only for Alec Burks to strip Cade Cunningham in the final seconds to hold on.

The most memorable game of the streak came at the end. Facing the 8-48 Pistons at home in late February, the Knicks came out sleepy and watched as Cunningham nearly dragged his hapless team to the finish line to end the skid at 14, but shenanigans ensued with Donte DiVincenzo to somehow pull it off with a Josh Hart and-1.

The streak ended on December 7, 2024. We didn't know it at the time, but the Pistons had finally emerged as threats, and they'd start their own six-game regular-season winning streak against the Knicks that lasted until three weeks ago.

The last time the Raptors beat the Knicks? January 22, 2023.

The last time the Nets beat the Knicks? January 28, 2023.

The next opportunity for both to beat the Knicks? Brooklyn gets another chance on December 27, while Toronto next gets a crack on January 20. It'll be basically four years for both since their last win.

These two streaks are tied for the fourth-longest active streaks in the NBA.

The three longer? OKC over Memphis, Cleveland over Washington, and the Clippers over the Hornets, all at 17. If you thought four years was a lot, it's been almost 10 since the Hornets beat the Clippers!

These two streaks are very different, but also the same. Both teams have been worse than the Knicks this entire time, sometimes considerably worse, and have yielded some ugly blowouts.

There have also been plenty of scares. There's the Brunson game-winner in the NBA Cup, the KAT heroics in The Six, the times that Brooklyn puts up a fight but folds late at Barclays Center, and the multiple double-digit comebacks we saw this week.

Either way, those are two Atlantic Division rivals that can't beat the Knicks. It also comes directly after both dominated the Knicks for prolonged periods.

Remember Nic Claxton's comments? Well, at the time, the Knicks were 0-9 against the Nets from 2020-23.

The Raptors went a blistering 23-5 against the Knicks from January 2016 to November 2023.

Ain't no fun when the rabbit's got the gun.
