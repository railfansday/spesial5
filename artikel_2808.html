Good, bad and ugly from Bears' 39-35 win over Lions on Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

It felt like 2025 all over again at the end of the Chicago Bears' game against the Detroit Lions. They may have allowed the Lions to put up 549 yards, including over 200 yards to Jahmyr Gibbs, but the Bears still managed to pull out the 39-35 win with a last-second touchdown from quarterback Caleb Williams to Rome Odunze and take control of the NFC North.

Williams threw for a season-high of 345 passing yards, which is also the second-most in his NFL career. His two touchdown passes in this game also marked the fifth time this year Williams has thrown for multiple touchdowns in a game. Chicago's offensive line had some difficulties against Detroit's pass rush, allowing Williams to get sacked four times, which ties his season-high. However, the Bears' offense may have suffered a costly loss after tight end Colston Loveland had to be carted to the locker room with a knee injury.

Here is a look at the good, bad and ugly from Chicago's win against the Lions, giving them their sixth straight win and sole possession of the NFC North with a 9-2 record.

Run game: After having a fumble last week in the Bears' win against the New Orleans Saints, he bounced back in a huge way in this game with 65 rushing yards, a career-high of 97 receiving yards and a touchdown. Swift actually finished the game as Chicago's leading receiver for the first time this year. Kyle Monangai also had two rushing touchdowns, despite only having eight carries. The performance of Salvon Ahmed should not go unnoticed either. His 46-yard run led to the Bears' first points of the game in the second quarter. Their 6.1 yards per carry was also the highest for Chicago since their season-opening victory against the Carolina Panthers. Especially after the Bears' run game struggles recently, it was good to see things get back on track in this game.

Rome Odunze: For most of the season, Odunze has taken a backseat to Luther Burden III, and even Kalif Raymond at times. However, he is getting hot at the right time for Chicago, with three touchdowns in the past three games, including two fourth-quarter touchdowns in this game. In fact, his game-winning touchdown in this game was the first game-winning touchdown in Odunze's NFL career. Between Odunze and Burden, as well as Chicago's dynamic running back room, they have an incredibly dangerous offense that has the potential to cause chaos in the postseason.

Defensive line: Ever since Dan Campbell became Detroit's head coach, they have been known for their elite offensive line play. Still, the Bears were only able to get one sack the entire game. With how Jeffery Simmons, Montez Sweat and numerous other defenders for the Bears have been playing, it was shocking to see Chicago struggle to get pressure on Goff at times throughout the game. That is something the Bears need to work on before their rematch in Week 17.

Luther Burden III: Especially after Loveland went down, I thought Burden would be more involved in the offense. However, despite being targeted seven times, he finished the game with just three receptions for 44 yards. In the past two games since the bye week, Burden seems to be less of a factor in the offense despite being Chicago's leading receiver. Particularly if Loveland's injury is serious, the Bears need Odunze and Burden to step up the rest of this season.

Jahmyr Gibbs: Going into this game, it was no mystery that it would be hard to contain Jahmyr Gibbs. But Chicago allowed him to have his best game of the season and one of the best games of his career, with a season-high in rushing yards (205) and tying his career-high in rushing touchdowns (three). This was also just the second time in his career Gibbs has rushed for over 200 yards. His 70-yard touchdown in the fourth quarter was also the second-longest touchdown of his career and his longest score since October of last season. Needless to say, the Bears could have done a lot better to contain Gibbs and they will have to improve when the Lions compare to Soldier Field in Week 17.

Amon-Ra St. Brown: Not only did Chicago's defense allow Gibbs to go off, but they also allowed St. Brown to go off with seven receptions for 146 yards. The Bears at least kept him out of the end zone, partially because Gibbs was getting all the touchdowns. Still, Chicago's defense needs to do better when going against elite receivers, especially with another matchup against St. Brown, Christian Watson with the Green Bay Packers and Justin Jefferson with the Minnesota Vikings this year.
