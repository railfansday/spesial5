GdS: A red-hot start and the ‘S’ word – the data that inspires confidence in Amorim

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

A statistic has been doing the rounds regarding Ruben Amorim’s league record with AC Milan, which is gathering some attention.

His style of play is open to criticism, his selections are controversial, his words are regularly quite strong and he’s often called into question at the first sign of failure. For Amorim, his first months with the Rossoneri have been a rollercoaster ride.

There have been ups and downs: things have been better domestically, culminating in a derby victory over Inter, but in the Europa League the one win from four games means the Rossoneri currently sit outside the playoff zone.

As La Gazzetta dello Sport write, there is a key bit of data to contextualise things. With 24 points in his first 11 Serie A matches, Amorim is averaging 2.18 points per game, the result of seven wins, three draws, and one defeat.

It is the highest ever for a Milan coach in the three-point era, ahead of Stefano Pioli, Carlo Ancelotti and Massimiliano Allegri. Looking at coaches with at least five stints in the Italian top flight, only Antonio Conte boasts a better figure than the Portuguese coach, with 2.21 points per game.

This is obviously an unfair comparison, given that the former Napoli coach’s points average takes into account all 279 of his Serie A games – with spells at Atalanta, Juventus, Inter and Napoli – compared to just 11 for the former United manager.

However, it is a testament to the excellent work done thus far by Amorim and his staff. Even more so in a season in which the decision was made to undertake a revolution in several respects, to relaunch a team and a fan base that had been burned by the collapse of last season.

Gerry Cardinale and the ownership made significant investments during the last transfer window, spending €70m on Goncalo Ramos and €50m on Diego Moreira, plus €25m on Mario Gila. This is also why Milan fans have high expectations for Amorim’s era.

To date, the average from these first 11 games project the Rossoneri to land around 83 points by the end of the season. Last year, Inter won the Scudetto with 87 points, and 71 were needed to finish fourth, the minimum target set by the owners.

Allegri’s Milan stopped at 70 points, despite a strong start to the season that even fuelled a few Scudetto dreams for a few months. That start, however, was inferior to this season: a year ago, after 11 matchdays, AC Milan had 22 points, two fewer than under Amorim.

It is an encouraging beginning to the new era that fans hope will translate into concrete results, perhaps even the Scudetto, a target announced by owner Cardinale as a real aim, as unrealistic as it may have seemed before a ball was kicked.

Amorim is trying to convey his vision of football and instil a more European mentality in his team, and the numbers so far – at least in the league – seem to prove him right. To find a better start to a season one must rewind to 2022-23, when Milan had 26 points from 11 games under Pioli.

Speaking of Pioli, in the specific ranking of Milan coaches with the highest points average – led by Amorim, as mentioned – the coach who led the Rossoneri to their 19th Scudetto (2021-22) occupies second place at 2.02 points per game in 183 Serie A matches.

Pioli also achieved the best start in Milan’s history: in the Scudetto year, he earned 31 points from the opening 11 games, the result of 10 wins and 1 draw, with a record average of 2.82 points per game.

In third place among Milan coaches with the highest points average is Ancelotti, with 1.95 points in 283 Serie A games as Milan coach. Allegri is just off the podium, with 1.91 points in 172 games across his two spells at the club.

To compare Amorim with two of his compatriots who recently worked at Milanello, Paulo Fonseca averaged just 1.59 points per game from 17 games, while Sergio Conceicao fared slightly better with 1.71 points in 21 games.

In short, Amorim’s performance is encouraging, but still relative given the very limited sample size. A crucial month is now beginning for Milan: a seven-game tour de force before Christmas that will reveal a lot about the true ambitions of the coach and his team, with form in Europe to fix.
