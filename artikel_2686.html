JJ Redick not pleased after Lakers nearly blow 31-point lead to Suns

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

It was a "disservice" for the Lakers to nearly blow what appeared to be an insurmountable 31-point lead. He said that playing "to our standards" is what's expected from the Lakers — and seeing a huge lead sliced to five points didn't reflect that.

Yes, the Lakers are tied for the best record in the NBA at 12-2. Yes, they have won eight straight games. And yes, his team has one of the best offensive engines in the league in Luka Doncic.

Even with all that and a 131-123 win over the Phoenix Suns, Lakers coach JJ Redick wasn't pleased with what he saw in the fourth quarter Sunday.

"We're working too hard and we're playing too hard to build these leads," Redick said. "It's a disservice to each other to take your foot off the gas. You have to play for 48 minutes and we're going to need our defense to win us games. ... Gotta be better in the fourth quarter."

Behind Doncic's sizzling first quarter that led to 43 points, the Lakers opened a 101-70 lead with 1:47 left in the third quarter.

Then came a letdown by the Lakers amid an onslaught from the Suns.

The Suns scored 43 points in the fourth, shot 50% from the field and pulled to within 126-121 with 1:05 left on a layup by Collin Gillespie, who had 13 of his 26 points in the final frame.

Matisse Thybulle of the Lakers blocks the shot of Devin Booker in the first half Sunday. (Kelsey Grant / Getty Images)

The Lakers have been down this road before, losing to Golden State after blowing a 25-point lead.

"You go up 30, it's easy to be like, 'You know, the game is over. They're not going to come back.' But it's very easy in the NBA for teams to come back and make runs because this is the game of runs," Jake LaRavia said. "So again, we just have to do a better job at staying in it for the full 48 minutes and being able to complete a full game."

It took two big plays from Quentin Grimes for the Lakers to not blow the game.

Grimes collected an offensive rebound after LaRavia missed a three-pointer and then pulled up and banked in a three-pointer for a 129-121 Lakers lead with 33.7 seconds left.

Grimes had 22 points on seven-for-10 shooting, going five for six on three-pointers. After missing the previous two games with a right finger injury, he saved the Lakers.

He was asked if he called "bank" on his final three.

"I called game. I called game. I called game," said Grimes, smiling. "No, I mean, the bank ain't open on Sunday. I called game. Trying to get out of here and get back to L.A."

Doncic had 20 points in the first quarter, going five for 10 from the field. He leads the NBA in first-quarter scoring (13.6) and in scoring per game (38.6). It was just another masterpiece for Doncic, who was serenaded with "MVP, MVP" chants twice from some fans.

"It was very nice," Doncic said. "I think everybody can see my smile at the free-throw line. And so, that made me happy, honestly."

The Lakers entered the game with three days off after road wins over Detroit, Atlanta and Milwaukee.

"I don't think we should put a ceiling on this group," Redick said. "This group is committed to getting better every single day, recognizing that this is a process to become a high-level team, and that's what I want from this group ... hold each other to that standard every single time.

"And, honestly, that's the frustrating part at times because you see how good we can be. There's no reason to ever play less than that standard."

Lakers guard Austin Reaves missed the game with a left heel contusion/bone bruise.
