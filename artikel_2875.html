Why Leonard Moore seems the right fit to cover Miami WR Malachi Toney

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

SOUTH BEND —Leonard Moore, All-America cornerback for Notre Dame football, knew the question was coming.

When the lanky junior sat down with the assembled media on Tuesday night, Nov. 3, after practice, he opened with a playful comment.

Odds are, when the 11th-ranked Irish (CFP) meet top-ranked Miami on Saturday night at Notre Dame Stadium, Moore will be asked to cover Hurricanes slot receiver Malachi Toney for much of the game.

That's how it was last season against USC's Makai Lemon, now in the NFL with the Philadelphia Eagles. Moore rose to the moment then, limiting the future first-round draft pick to four catches, 76 receiving yards and no touchdowns in a 34-24 Irish win.

Lemon, listed at 5-foot-11 and 195 pounds, finished 2025 with 79 catches for 1,156 yards and 11 scores in 12 games.

Toney, a sophomore who goes 5-11 and 193 pounds, has nearly matched those totals in 2026 through eight games. The lightly recruited product of Liberty City, FL and American Heritage High School enters Saturday with 73 catches for 1,027 yards and 13 touchdowns.

Forging an instant connection with transfer quarterback Darian Mensah, formerly of Duke and Tulane, Toney ranks second at the FBS level in receptions and third in receiving yards. He also has rushed for 61 yards on four carries and returned six punts for an 11.5-yard average.

"Real explosive dude," Moore said. "He's very shifty and he's super good after the catch. That's something that's given people a lot of trouble, so that stands out about him."

Toney has been targeted 83 times this season and hauled in all but 10 of those passes, giving him an astonishing catch rate of 88%. He has forced 18 missed tackles, recorded 49 first downs and piled up nearly half his total receiving yards (497) after the catch.

"The offense runs through No. 1, through Toney," Moore said. "Similar offense (to last year) but they're giving Toney the ball a lot more this year than they were at the beginning of last year."

Having reclassified from the 2026 recruiting cycle, Toney was somewhat of a mystery man heading into Miami's 2025 season-opening win over Notre Dame. He finished with six catches for 82 yards, including a 28-yard touchdown that opened the scoring, and just kept getting better as the year wore.

The Hurricanes lost the CFP title game on their home field to Indiana, but Toney finished the season with 109 catches for 1,211 yards and 10 touchdowns. Another score and 113 rushing yards rounded out the package.

At 6-2 and 197 pounds, Moore isn't an exact match for the physicality of Toney. As with the Lemon assignment, however, he used his long levers and advanced film study to beat the Trojans star to the spot more often than not.

"It's hard to do what Leonard did versus USC," Notre Dame coach Marcus Freeman said this week. "To be a boundary corner and all of a sudden at times go play nickel. But he has that skill set. He is as gifted in his toolbox as I've been around. His toolbox is extremely large."

Widely projected as a Top 10 NFL Draft pick next spring, Moore has sat out two of the past three games following a concussion suffered in the final minutes of an Oct. 3 win at North Carolina.

He said he was "a little dinged up" during the loss at BYU on Oct. 17, but he was held out as a precaution last week against Navy and everything is good now.

And while starting nickelback Christian Gray (6-foot, 196 pounds) figures to see Toney as well, Moore's soaring talent and matching intellect suggest fans will get the matchup they crave.

"I know the game of football," Moore said, "so my IQ and obviously my athleticism allows me to play with some of those guys in the slot and be versatile in certain ways, especially just in man-to-man coverage. And my length allows me to disrupt routes."

Shannon Dawson, Miami's fourth-year offensive coordinator, raved about Moore this week to reporters in Coral Gables. Dawson called Moore "a pretty elite player" with nary a weakness.

"You don't get a lot of separation on him," Dawson said. "In man coverage, he's sticky. He gets in your hip and he's just an elite man cover guy. They'll attach him to people. I don't know what their specific game plan is, but I would venture to guess that's part of it."

Miami's next three leading receivers — Duke/Harvard transfer wideout Cooper Barkate, sophomore wideout Daylyn Upshaw and junior tight end Elija Lofton — have combined for 70 catches totaling 871 yards and eight touchdowns.

No other Hurricanes receiver has more than nine catches this season. That's another reason Moore vs. Toney makes sense.

"(Moore) is as good as there is out there," Dawson said. "When he attaches, he's long. He's got long arms and he can run and he has great body control. I think one theme with all the players that are in that circle is their body control.

"Being able to change direction and start very quickly in another direction is something that just makes them a different-type player. Why that is, I don't know. Genetics? Probably had parents that were very similar, I would imagine."
