How One Live-Wire Stylist Puts His Meta Glasses to Work on Set

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

When Meta announced the latest generation of its genre-defining smart glasses, the response from the fashion industry was, well, kind of tepid. Wearable technology has a shaky track record, after all, and we’ve been down a similar road before. (Google Glass, we hardly knew thee.) To win over folks more fluent in Ai Weiwei than AI, Meta would have to fulfill a much trickier mandate than simply developing a groundbreaking product: it’d have to develop a product that looks good while doing the ground breaking.

So when I got the chance to put Meta Glasses to the test during a typically frenetic day on set shooting for GQ, I didn’t hold back. It helps, obviously, that they actually look cool—especially the limited-edition pair I requested, a collaboration with the chart-topping Blackpink member LISA. Now, though, I’m convinced of their genuine, potentially game-changing utility, too. Here’s how I put the Meta Glasses by LISA through the paces of my chaotic 9-to-5, and emerged all the less stressed for it.

On shoot days, I make a point of arriving on set an hour before our model so I can lock in our looks with minimal distraction. The clothes on the racks, though, tend to play second fiddle to the clothes on me, at least when it comes to my AM fit check. (Like a true sicko, I wore a fluorescent Prada jacket to match the pink tint on my special-edition, LISA-designed Meta Glasses.)

Without fail, every shoot I’ve been on comes together very dynamically—read: absolutely last minute. So even though we boarded each look weeks ago, and styled them all out yesterday afternoon, no fewer than 15-ish items arrived that morning. To sort through them, I tell Meta to put on the new Sophia Stel album so I can lock in and land on the right roster of looks.

The looks have been landed on—it’s time to present them to the team. GQ commerce director Michael Nolledo—the magnanimous Miranda Priestly of the entire operation, if you will—is off-site for the day, so I tell Meta to video call him for approval. He can now see what I see, and I stay totally hands-free.

During the run through, I describe each look down to its socks, clarifying the logic behind my styling decisions with near-comical specificity. (Yes, that minuscule sliver of waffle thermal peeking out from beneath that fleece is, in fact, transformative and entirely nonnegotiable!) I tend to love this part of the day because the looks always end up in a sometimes-different-always-better place once the braintrust has weighed in—and tempered some of my, uh, kookier impulses.

It doesn’t matter how nice the clothes look hanging limply on the rack: they don’t really come to life (or die in front of your very eyes) until you get them on the guy. Luckily, our guy for the day is Gabriel, whose easy, unaffected chill makes everything we put him in look effortlessly incredible.

As we noodle on a few minor alternations (bringing in a suit jacket just-so; adding hefty cuffs to a pair of appropriately hefty trousers), I have Meta snap a photo of each look in its final form. On my command, Meta then sends those photos to Michael and GQ senior editor Avidan Grossman—my closest collaborator on set and our in-house tailoring geek—who has final say on all things pleated pants, dimpled ties, and more. Once Meta beams out the fitting photos, it’s speak now or forever hold your peace.

The next six hours are a frenetic blur, set to a fittingly chaotic blend of ‘80s pop hits and Tate McRae (it’s all about balance, really). Beth, our prodigiously talented photographer for the day, becomes my partner, as we both try to ensure Gabriel ignores the camera as much as possible. The best photos happen when the guy forgets he’s being photographed—like we’re capturing a genuine moment of relaxed solitude.

Problem is, when I’m laser-focused on all that, I hate pulling out my phone to capture content of my own. So the option to have Meta snap a fast photo or record a snippet of video is kind of a godsend.

We made it, folks. Three green teas, four minor hand injuries, and 18 or so looks later, it’s time for the fun part: reviewing the photos, and duking it out over which of them will make the final cut. There really is no greater satisfaction than seeing a slapdash deck of product flats transform into an all-killer, no-filler album of actual outfits.

Because I have no patience (and am beaming with so much pride it's nearly blinding), I have Meta snap a few shots of my favorite moments and then post them to my IG story. The official GQ story won’t run for a few weeks, so I like to think of my story as an unofficial teaser of sorts. We pack up and roll our racks out of the studio and back into the fashion closet, where I promptly ask Meta for directions to the closest bar.
