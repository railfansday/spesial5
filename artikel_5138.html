Bitcoin fell to $62,000 before recovering to about $65,360, a rebound of roughly $3,000. That bounce followed a 16% drop over three trading days, one of the steepest short-term falls of the year.

Measured from the recent high near $87,000, Bitcoin has now lost almost 30%. The token was trading around $81,000 as recently as November 5.

Soloway also pointed to the upcoming Anthropic IPO as a possible drain on market liquidity. According to Soloway, no major news has come out to suggest that anything fundamental has changed for crypto.

The $66,000 to $67,000 zone had been expected to act as support. Bitcoin spent weeks moving sideways in that range earlier this year, and the heavy trading there built up a cluster of buyers and sellers. Levels like that often slow a decline.

Fear pushed the price through the zone and down to $62,000. Prices rarely reverse at an exact level in a panic, and they often overshoot support before finding a bottom. A drop of about 5% below a major level is within the normal range for a sell-off driven by fear.

The crash has once again punished heavily leveraged traders. Soloway warned that traders using 100x or higher leverage are the most exposed in moves like this. Exchanges and large players tend to push prices into areas where clusters of leveraged positions sit, forcing liquidations.

The overall tone in the market is now one of fear, and extreme fear has often lined up with short-term bounces in the past. The near-term path depends on whether Bitcoin can hold above $62,000 and push back through $66,000 to $67,000.

A recovery toward $70,000 to $75,500 would count as a relief rally inside a larger downtrend. Any longer-term move toward $80,000 to $90,000 or higher remains uncertain and would likely depend on how Nvidia and the broader AI trade hold up.
