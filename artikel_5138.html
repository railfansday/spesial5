Microsoft’s “Code Red” is its top-level internal alarm, now used to rebuild how the company prevents and recovers from major service failures after the 24-hour outage on November 12 and 13. “Code Red means that this is the highest priority work for our team,” M365 Core President Jason Henderson told staff in a memo reviewed by Reuters. The plan has five parts, focused on reducing pressure on overloaded systems, splitting traffic by region and speeding up recovery. Microsoft wants any future global incident resolved within an hour and plans to use AI agents to help engineers read system health data faster. Henderson will meet the team running Microsoft 365’s core infrastructure daily and hold weekly reviews. He also flagged that one urgent fix recently took five to six days to reach all servers worldwide, against a goal of one hour. Copilot chief Charles Lamanna said the component behind the outage had logged about a decade’s worth of tolerated failures in a short burst. Microsoft has blamed a software bug hidden since July, which its tests missed because lab machines used weaker hardware with fewer cores.
