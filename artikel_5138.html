Bitcoin’s $2.58B ETF outflows deepen November sell-off – Is $60K next?

Bitcoin’s [BTC] price slide is accelerating, with the king coin falling below $63,000 amid a new wave of ETF outflows and a broader rotation of capital into the AI sector.

The drop extends a brutal month that has seen roughly $2.58 billion flee U.S. Spot Bitcoin ETFs since the start of November.

On the 19th of November, Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded a net outflow of $306.50 million, with BlackRock’s IBIT accounting for $209.5 million, roughly 87% of the day’s redemptions.

The price action followed the outflows, as Bitcoin traded near $63,071 at the time of writing, down nearly 3% on the day and hovering at its lowest levels since mid-August. Ethereum fared worse, though, dropping below $1,800 at press time.

Another massive factor behind Bitcoin’s fall was noted by the X account Why Markets Move. It stated that currently, investors are seeing more potential in the AI boom and the upcoming Anthropic IPO, compared to crypto.

For the uninitiated, Anthropic is currently targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion in its Nasdaq listing, with investors such as Nvidia and Microsoft. This is undoubtedly good news for AI, but not such much for digital assets.

Raj Kamal, founder and CEO of TransFi, told AMBCrypto how policy shifts and macro conditions can crush crypto sentiment. He said,

I think the market read the result as a short-term negative for crypto. Much of the rise in bitcoin and in stablecoin adoption came after the 2024 US election, and investors now expect closer scrutiny of consumer protection, anti-money-laundering rules and the crypto interests of President Trump’s family.

Kamal also outlined how ETF redemptions became another salient point for Bitcoin’s fall.

U.S. Spot bitcoin ETFs recorded about $1.91 billion of net outflows over seven straight sessions from 5 to 13 November. Institutional money began to leave as the price fell, and once Bitcoin went below $70,000, leveraged positions were flushed out.

His outlook was playing out in real life, with November’s cumulative ETF outflows now exceeding $2.5 billion until press time. The CEO ended with a warning.

I don’t think that flushing out is over yet.

On a similar note, stablecoin supply has plunged by roughly $14.28 billion over the past thirty days, signaling a broader drain of dollar liquidity from the crypto ecosystem.

The AI rotation has to simmer down, and regulatory clarity needs to improve for Bitcoin, otherwise its downward spiral will not stop. Therefore, traders have to now watch the $62,000 support level, but a sustained break below will lead BTC to $60,000.
