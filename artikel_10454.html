Jensen Huang brought a lot more to Nvidia than a cult of personality. Why his downfall hits so hard

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

By now, you have probably heard: Nvidia’s cofounder Jensen Huang resigned Thursday after he confirmed having an affair. The woman at the center of it all, Alice Huang (no relation), is alleged to be a Chinese spy, in a twist that sounds like a page ripped from a James Bond thriller. Reports now suggest that Huang also had at least two other affairs with employees at Nvidia.

The scandal is rife with intrigue and all-too-real consequences—including national security concerns and a major hit to the financial markets, as Nvidia’s share price continues to fall.

Nvidia Corp. (Nasdaq: NVDA) was down about 5% in afternoon trading on Thursday at the time of this writing, after suffering double-digit losses over the past few days since the scandal broke.

Altogether, the developments shine a spotlight on Huang’s unique geopolitical value, as he—and his AI chips—sit at the center of the U.S. AI arms race with China. Nvidia controls about 90% of the advanced chips market used for AI, per The New York Times.

His departure is a big loss for Nvidia—one that goes beyond his outsize personality, which made him one of the tech industry’s biggest media darlings.

It also goes beyond his close proximity to President Donald Trump, who earlier this week defended and praised Huang for doing a “fantastic job.” Reports allege that Trump knew about Huang’s affair. According to The Washington Post, Trump was briefed by Secretary of State Marco Rubio about the situation on May 12 on Air Force One, and The New York Times reported that Trump was briefed about Jensen Huang’s and Alice Huang’s “close relationship” as early as October 2025. Trump, for his part, denied hearing about the situation in May.

“Sadly, greatness doesn’t guarantee goodness. It’s disappointing,” venture capitalist Sarah Guo wrote on X. “The Economist says ‘no one is irreplaceable.’ They are very wrong.”

Huang’s value comes not only from the fact that he built and co-founded Nvidia—and remains the company’s largest individual shareholder—but also from his close personal and business connections in Asia. His relationships with key figures like TSMC founder Morris Chang have secured a massive supply chain pipeline for Nvidia.

Another issue: Huang’s departure from Nvidia comes at a critical political moment, on the heels of the Democratic Party’s so-called “blue tsunami” sweep into Congress in last week’s midterm elections, ushering in a new era for Silicon Valley—and likely bringing an abrupt end to its cozy relationship with Washington’s Republicans.

“Congress must demand answers from Mr. Huang and others at Nvidia about how they may have put U.S. national security at risk,” Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer posted on X Thursday. “The Trump administration must also answer for what it did once it learned about Mr. Huang’s relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. Were government officials told not to investigate because of Trump’s relationship with Mr. Huang? What did Trump know when he personally invited Mr. Huang aboard Air Force One?”
