Jeff Bezos praised Raising Cane’s. Its founder built a $22 billion fortune by breaking 3 fast-food rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Raising Cane’s isn’t following the standard fast-food playbook.

From its limited menu to the employee dress code, founder Todd Graves is charting his own path in a competitive industry. Now, one of the world’s richest men is pointing to his chicken finger empire as a prime example of the power of entrepreneurship.

In a recent Fox News interview, Jeff Bezos pushed back on the notion that building a successful business and becoming a billionaire made someone a villain.

Instead, he pointed to entrepreneurs who expanded their companies and created thousands of jobs. For the Amazon founder, Graves is a prime example of that.

“There’s nothing wrong with making unbelievably good chicken that people love,” he said. “And it doesn’t make him a bad person.”

However, although Graves and Bezos are both billionaires, the former followed a different playbook than the latter.

In 1996, Graves, who was 24, opened the first Raising Cane’s in Baton Rouge, Louisiana. He used loans from friends, family, and the Small Business Administration, as well as savings from his jobs as a boilermaker and an Alaskan salmon fisherman.

Three decades later, the ethos of the fast-food chain remains the same: a simple menu centered on chicken fingers, fries, coleslaw, Texas toast, and Cane’s Sauce.

Even now, the smallest change, such as the addition of a sandwich combo, can create anxiety and stress.

“To tell you the truth, man, I kind of wish we didn’t even have it on the menu. It’s like 3 or 4 percent of sales. And it slows us down,” Graves previously told Inc. “I wish we didn’t have it, but I won’t take it away now.”

This small admission reflects the chicken chain’s broader philosophy. While Bezos launched a publicly traded giant, Graves has focused on keeping Raising Cane’s limited in scope and tightly controlled.

Keeping the company private

The biggest difference between the two billionaires may be their views on taking a company public.

Forbes estimates Graves currently owns about 92 percent of the company, with a net worth of approximately $22 billion. The founder has said Raising Cane’s has no plans to go public, a choice he has tied to preserving the chain’s culture.

A key executive has echoed the same sentiment. “We have no plans on that,” AJ Kumaran, the company’s co-CEO and COO, told Restaurant Business. “We are privately held because we want to remain that way.”

By comparison, Bezos took Amazon public just three years after its founding. It debuted on the stock market in May 1997 at $18 a share, raising $54 million to fuel its growth.

Focusing on employees

However, even though Graves is committed to retaining control of the business, he takes a more relaxed approach with his employees.

At Raising Cane’s, crew members wear casual uniforms and music has been allowed in the kitchen since the early days. It is a deliberately relaxed approach that stands apart from Amazon’s more regimented structure.

A commitment to charity

Yet, even amid their differing approaches to business, both Bezos and Graves are committed to charitable giving.

Bezos’s Earth Fund, backed by a $10 billion pledge, has distributed about $2.4 billion to date. His Day 1 Families Fund has donated more than $850 million to nonprofits addressing homelessness.

After the Texas floods in 2025, Graves donated $1 million to the Red Cross. In August, Graves told Dallas Weekly that the company had given more than $165 million to charitable causes over its first 30 years, something he hopes will continue.

Raising Cane’s didn’t immediately respond to a request from Inc. for comment.
