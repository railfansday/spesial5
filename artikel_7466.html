Twitch and Amazon want to turn “git gud” into “u good?” for men’s mental health

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Online multiplayer gamers face genuine psychological warfare. Don’t believe me? Log on to your local Apex Legends, Call of Duty, or League of Legends lobby and let the “git guds” (and, well, lots of other stuff we’d rather not print) roll in.

But Twitch, its parent Amazon, and a new organization called GamerCounsel are hoping to change “git gud” into “u gud?”

Timed for Movember, an annual monthlong recognition of the need for more thorough and compassionate healthcare for men, the Squad Support campaign taps Australian Twitch streamers Bajo, Crayator, Jack Buzza, and Midbeast to promote checking in our your gamer buds and–when needed–chatting with licensed professionals about what’s on your mind.

The licensed experts Squad Support recommends are, of course, those from GamerCounsel. The service launched in 2026, and says it brings “professional counselling and psychotherapy into gaming and interactive online spaces.”

Just like an IRL mental health office, GamerCounsel intakes clients and matches them with qualified therapists. The twist here is that therapists aren’t sitting behind desks and clients aren’t lying on couches.

Instead, they’re both squadding up in the same video game.

GamerCounsel therapists and clients (in both one-on-one and group settings) meet remotely over platforms like Discord and work through an appointment while button-mashing together. The org’s website recommends games like Minecraft, Roblox, Fortnite, Rocket League, Stardew Valley, Among Us, Animal Crossing, Mario Kart, Fall Guys, and Destiny, but says its services extend to the thousands of other games on Steam, too–so clients don’t seem super limited in what they can pick.

According to ContentGrip, GamerCounsel is allotting 500 hours of support time to people who see the Squad Support campaign and want to access its gaming-themed help.

A graphic for the campaign illustrates the constant mixed messages male gamers might want to work through with a therapist: “Git gud. But ask for help. Be a man. But don’t be masculine. Share your feelings. But don’t give the ick. Make the first move. But don’t be a creep. Be romantic. But don’t simp. Be the provider. But be present. Be strong. Be vulnerable. Toughen up. Open up. Green flag. Red flag. Red pill. Blue pill. Say less. Say more.”

The Squad Support campaign is going out through Amazon Ads, and includes Twitch components like a new “U GUD?” emote, interactive polls, fundraising tools, and use of Twitch’s Stream Together feature, which Bajo, Crayator, Jack Buzza, Midbeast, and other creators can use to host Movember experts on their streams.

“Gen Z and young millennials are connecting through gaming, and Squad Support meets them where they are. Our first collaboration with Movember brings together a shared ambition to reach young people through the communities and experiences they already value,” Willie Pang, Managing Director of Amazon Ads Australia and New Zealand, said in a statement. “By combining Twitch creators, engaged communities and interactive experiences, Amazon Ads, through Twitch’s Brand Partnership Studio, is demonstrating how brands can go beyond traditional advertising to show up in culture and build meaningful connections with audiences that are notoriously difficult to reach and engage.”

Folks who want to access Squad Support’s free help from GamerCounsel must be 18+ and located in Australia. The org asks that people submit applications, and it will accommodate as many as possible into solo and group therapy sessions.

Here at Tubefilter, we’ve talked many times about creators’ need for mental health support. But this need extends beyond individual communities. Everyone needs more mental health support–especially people who spend their lives being told they shouldn’t.
