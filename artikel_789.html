Taylor says he is quitting darts for good after comeback

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Phil Taylor retired from professional darts in 2018; he came out of retirement for this week's MODUS Super Series International Pairs, teaming up with friend Adrian Lewis; the Pair finished second-bottom in group C and were knocked out of the event

Phil Taylor says he is quitting competitive darts for good and has no plans to come out of retirement again.

The 16-time world champion was lured back to the oche for this year's MODUS Super Series International Pairs Tournament in Portsmouth alongside his long-time friend Adrian Lewis.

The English duo lost all five of their matches on Thursday but rebounded on Friday, winning three and losing two matches, to finish second-bottom in the Group C standings.

Taylor, 67, retired from the competitive circuit in 2018, five years after winning his final world crown, and when asked if he would make another comeback, the 14-time PDC World Championship winner told Pluto TV: "[This is] proper retirement."

A heartwarming moment unfolded after Taylor and Lewis were beaten 4-3 in their final group match against Scotland, as each of the other nations arrived to give Taylor a guard of honour, as he walked away from the oche.

"I mean I'm 67, I get my pension next February," Taylor said, when asked if this would be his last competitive appearance. "I'm a pensioner now, officially!"

"I'll still do personal appearances, more having a chat with people, talking about my career, that kind of thing."

Taylor and Lewis, who teamed up to win four World Cup of Darts titles between 2012 and 2016, finished Thursday at the bottom of Group C. Friday started poorly for the Englishmen, losing to Denmark's Ivan Springborg-Poulsen and Andreas Toft Jørgensen 4-2.

Speaking on his retirement U-turn this week, Taylor said: "I was dreading it to be honest with you because I know I wasn't playing like I can do, but now I've been here and it's all over, I've loved it."

The pair hit back on Friday with a 4-3 win against Germany, then beat Switzerland and Sweden, both 4-2.

The Englishmen would tie Scotland at three legs apiece in his final competitive match, before Taylor missed a checkout for 48 for victory, in what would have been a fairy-tale finish.

"Looking back now, it's like it never happened," Taylor said, reflecting on his glittering career.

"I've said to other people who have been on stage and retired: 'How do you feel now?'

"'It's like you've never been there'. It's ever so weird. And you come up on YouTube and you just go: "No, I know. I know I was there, but I can't remember it."

"It's very, very strange because, mentally-wise, you move on. That was your former life and now my life is different."

Instead of flying around the world competing, Taylor says retirement has allowed him to spend more time with his grandchildren.

"I've got 11 grandchildren," Taylor said. "So I spend a lot of time with them now.

"[I do] things I've never done before. Go to bed when I want; I go to bed early. For me, 9pm would be like, I'd be asleep two hours before 9pm, and it's great.

"I love getting up early as well. We can do all the chores. We can go shopping and get everything done, and it isn't even 11am. It's great!

"People say I'm the GOAT [Greatest of All Time]-I'm an old goat now!"
