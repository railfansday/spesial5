How Is El-Sayed So in Sync with the Muslim Brotherhood?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Michigan Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed campaigns at the Meanwhile Bar in Grand Rapids, Mich., October 8, 2026.

In the week prior to Election Day, I opined that Abdul El-Sayed is the Gerry Adams of the Muslim Brotherhood. Just as Adams — laughably at times — denied connections to the Irish Republican Army, El-Sayed, a hard-left Democrat who was dismayingly elected earlier this month to represent Michigan in the U.S. Senate, implausibly denies ties to the Muslim Brotherhood.

In the piece linked above, I laid out some of his extensive connections (including his leadership posts in the University of Michigan chapter of the Muslim Students Association — the MSA having been established in the mid-Sixties as the Brotherhood’s building-block organization in North America. I also noted that, in America, the Brotherhood operates as an ideological movement and through front groups — not as a regimented organization with membership cards — precisely to enable its activists to disavow membership. That’s expedient because (a) the Brotherhood has been banned as a terrorist organization in several countries, (b) the Brotherhood’s branches in Egypt, Lebanon, and Jordan have been designated as foreign terrorist organizations by the U.S. government, and (c) the Brotherhood’s highest priority in America since the 1980s has been support for Hamas (the Brotherhood’s Palestinian branch), which has been a designated foreign terrorist organization in our country for nearly 30 years.
