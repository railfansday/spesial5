Give Boxed Cake Mix A British-Style Upgrade With Cream And One Jarred Ingredient

Anyone who believes British cuisine falls short has likely never experienced a British cake, with notable example being a traditional Victoria sponge. Much like a classic cup of tea, this dessert represents the essence of British baking, offering elegance through simple ingredients. Even novice bakers can easily prepare one from scratch, but using a boxed cake mix offers a far more convenient alternative. Upgrading a simple store-bought mix into an authentic Victoria sponge requires just one key addition to your pantry: a good jar of strawberry or raspberry jam.

Jam is at the heart of a Victoria sponge. The cake consists of two light, fluffy sponge layers sandwiched together with jam and whipped cream. Early versions of this cake, which trace back to Victorian-era afternoon tea culture heavily championed by Queen Victoria, were most commonly filled with just raspberry jam, no cream. Today, however, many bakers include a layer of whipped cream or vanilla buttercream alongside the jam, although traditionalists still debate the "proper" filling.

Adding jam to a Victoria sponge sounds simple enough, but there is an art to it. Before assembling the cake, make sure that each sponge layer has cooled completely. This typically takes about 20 to 30 minutes after removing the layers from the oven. If you assemble the cake while it is still warm, the jam (and cream) will melt and run, ruining the distinctive layers that give the Victoria sponge its elegant charm. It is also important to use the right amount of jam — too little will be unnoticeable, while too much will ooze out. A helpful tip is to leave a small border around the outer edge of the bottom sponge so that when the top layer is added, the jam spreads outward without spilling over.

The Victoria sponge might be an old-school recipe, but there's always room for improvement. While we'd advocate for experimentation, a Victoria sponge is almost always a golden-yellow sponge. There's no going wrong with a chocolate boxed cake mix, but if you want to keep things traditional (and avoid the wrath of the Brits), it's best to toss a yellow cake mix into your cart. To make life easier, we've already ranked Betty Crocker's Super Moist Yellow Cake Mix as one of the best you can buy.

Although a Victoria sponge might sound simple, there are, pardon the pun, levels to this cake. Assembling the sponge is easy enough, but the jam and cream are where you can really let your baking skills speak for themselves. Instead of jarred jam, you could make your own. Sure, it's not as convenient, but your taste buds will thank you. Simply combine diced raspberries or strawberries with lemon juice and sugar, leave it for an hour, then simmer over medium-low heat until it reaches your desired consistency — or consider making a jarringly beautiful ruby red jam. Let it cool completely before spreading, so it doesn't seep into the sponge. Homemade jam will completely elevate your boxed cake mix, and it certainly won't go unnoticed by guests.

The cream also offers plenty of room for improvement. The standard approach is a thick layer of homemade whipped cream over the bottom layer, but there are some easy (and more flavourful) upgrades. Whip a few spoonfuls of mascarpone into the cream for a richer, tangier filling, or scrape in some vanilla bean paste, lemon zest, or a splash of elderflower cordial for something brighter. For a nod to a classic British cream tea, swap in clotted cream, which will pair perfectly with the jam. To make clotted cream (because you won't find it in most stores), simmer about three cups of heavy cream with an ounce of unsalted butter until it reduces by half. Chill it overnight, and Bob's your uncle.
