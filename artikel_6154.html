Democrats And Republicans Both Spent More Campaign Money On American Airlines Than Any Other Airline—Here’s Why

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

American Airlines collected the most money that campaigns spent on airfare in the latest election cycle, with Democrats and Republicans both putting it first. Alaska Airlines beat Southwest by just $271.57, and one Senate campaign supplied nearly half of the political spending on Alaska. National Journal examined campaign disbursements for July through September. His ticket spend tally comes to just under $484,000. These are campaign committee expenses, which can include staff travel, not just the flights taken by candidates themselves. American’s Washington National Hub Is A Big Driver American dominates Washington National, the airport most convenient to Capitol Hill. United has its hub at Dulles, plus a network connecting the country’s major business and fundraising markets. I’ve written about the politics of National’s flight restrictions. Congress limits access to the airport, and American’s large slot holdings make its position difficult to challenge. Lawmakers are customers of the airlines whose access they help determine. Southwest’s large Baltimore operation is less convenient. Southwest serves National, too, but with a smaller operation out of the dilapidated banjo terminal. Delta’s third place makes sense from the other end of the trip. A Michigan campaign, such as Mike Rogers’, has an obvious reason to use Delta’s Detroit hub, and the same applies to Atlanta and Minneapolis travel. And Delta also has hubs at New York JFK and LaGuardia. There’s money in New York. One Campaign Put Alaska Airlines Ahead Of Southwest Mary Peltola’s campaign spent just under $40,000 on air, more than twice the second place campaign. She was running for Senate in Alaska. Getting around the state requires flying in a way that campaigning in many states doesn’t. Her campaign pointed out in October that more than 80% of Alaska’s communities aren’t connected to the road system. About $24,750 of her campaign’s spending went to Alaska Airlines (63%). Another $562 went to Sterling Airways, which operates Aleutian Airways. Peltola accounted for about 49% of all the Alaska Airlines spending.
