Jeep Wrangler Scrambler SRT Concept Previews the Future by Pulling from the Past

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Remember the CJ-8 Scrambler? This one is like that, but with an independent front suspension and a trophy truck–inspired rear axle.

CORRECTION 11/3/26: This story has been updated to correct details about the origins of the original CJ-8 Scrambler.

Long before the Jeep Gladiator pickup truck, the Scrambler from the 1980s was the brand’s first cargo-bed-toting model. Now, Jeep has plans to resurrect the Scrambler as a production vehicle, with a new SRT concept revealed at this year’s SEMA show providing a preview.

Unlike the original CJ-8 Scrambler, which was based on a stretched CJ-7 chassis, the new Scrambler SRT concept is built on a Wrangler foundation. For the bits under the bodywork, Jeep worked with a Dakar-winning off-road driver to develop the Scrambler SRT’s suspension, which, for the first time on a Wrangler-branded vehicle, has an independent front setup. The high-travel front suspension pairs with a trophy truck–inspired rear axle, which builds on the design Jeep first showcased on the race-winning Gladiator Striker.

The new Scrambler’s front end is instantly recognizable as a Jeep, though it looks a bit different from the rest of the brand’s lineup. For starters, the grille takes on a slightly retro style and sports a bit of an overbite. Then there’s the prominent hood scoop atop the engine cover and oversized fender flares, both of which are fitting for a truck wearing SRT badges. The concept is finished in a suitably loud Punk’n orange paint that gives Toyota’s Solar Octane paint a run for its money.

The goal with the Scrambler SRT, according to Tim Kuniskis, who now fills the role of head of American Brands, SRT Performance and Direct Connection, Stellantis North America, was to find out what happens when “SRT stops treating the trail as the limit and builds the ultimate open-air desert weapon.”

To that end, Jeep decided that the Scrambler SRT concept needed a V-8 under the hood. Unfortunately, we don’t know exactly which V-8 the truck is equipped with. Given the SRT badges on the sides, though, we think it’s a safe bet that the concept is rocking either the supercharged 6.2-liter Hellcat engine or the 6.4-liter Hemi 392. Either engine would provide suitable performance, but, knowing Kuniskis, the Hellcat is the likelier choice.

The Scrambler SRT concept is the first public appearance of the Scrambler name since Jeep confirmed plans for the model to return at its investor relations day back in May. When it does arrive, there will likely be a full lineup of Scramblers, with an SRT version sitting at the top of the line. The official timeline is still up in the air, as are official style and mechanical details, but the Scrambler SRT concept gives us a great preview of future production models, which we think could arrive in 2028.
