Eva Mendes and Ryan Gosling are one of those celebrity couples that keep their heads down and their private lives just that; private. So sue us, then, that we get excited when they offer us a sneak-peek into their world... especially when it comes with a Christmassy twist!

Now, we usually think of a Christmas tree as being larger than life, and twice, especially when it's in the home of a celebrity. Honestly, though, most stars seem to be taking a laidback approach to their festive decor of late, whether it's the Beckhams' bow-filled Christmas tree, Sarah Jessica Parker's floral Christmas tree, or even Michelle Obama's twig tree.

Eva and Ryan have opted for a similarly unfussy approach; the couple's kitchen island is full of miniature Christmas trees. Check it out:

And just like that, they've proven you don’t need a large traditional evergreen tree to make your house look stylish during the holidays. In fact, if you decide to follow in Eva Mendes and Ryan Gosling's footsteps, you might not need to buy much at all.

All you have to do is take a simple stroll around your garden with a pair of pruners and a small pot to get the same look; fortunately, some experts told me exactly how to do it.

“The best mini Christmas trees start with a walk around your own garden,” says Nick Alan, founder of Palma Botanical Co., who has been growing plants for 25 years.

His picks? “Conifer tips like cedar, cypress, juniper and fir hold their needles longest. Boxwood and rosemary fill gaps and add scent.”

For clean cuts, Nick Alan recommends these Fiskars SoftGrip bypass pruners.

Use gentle hands with the shrub, though. “Harvest on a cool morning with clean pruners, cutting just above a side shoot so the plant regrows, and take no more than a third of any shrub,” says Alan.

Nadi Melam, founder of Florals Memories, has a simple pot method for getting the Eva Mendes and Ryan Gosling look this Christmas.

“Fill a 4-inch pot with damp sand (this will help hold your stems upright and will allow you to leave the tree in its pot as it dries). Remove the needles from the bottom 2 inches of each stem (you don’t want any foliage to rot in the sand!).”

Once this is done, she says you should “arrange the stems from tallest in the middle to shortest as you work your way out (creating a cone shape).” Remember, though, that cut foliage won’t last forever. “Mist every few days and keep it away from the stove, dishwasher vent, and sunny windows. Expect two to three weeks, swapping tired sprigs,” says Alan.

A living tree means you don’t have the extra work of worrying about sprigs. “For a kitchen island display like Eva Mendes and Ryan Gosling's, I like a living miniature tree best, because it does two jobs at once: it looks festive, and it stays useful after Christmas,” says Morgan Brooks, founder and editor of Plant Style Home.

Brooks says rosemary is the practical pick for a kitchen: “Rosemary is the most practical in a kitchen because you can pick sprigs for cooking, and it smells wonderful near the stove.”

For care, Brooks says: “I keep young trees in cool spots away from radiators and water the compost so it stays lightly damp but never wet.” Alan adds: “Give it your brightest window, water when the top inch is dry, and plant it out afterward in zone 8 and warmer.”

Your kitchen island may be a great spot for trees, but don’t forget it’s also a workspace for cooking. Keep your trees all to one side and never let them near heat sources, especially the dry foliage.

Roberta Sabbatini, founder of ItalianTouch Events, says that “a little greenery with one beautiful ribbon often looks more considered than a crowded arrangement.”

For the layout, Melam says: “I love displaying mini trees in odd-numbered clusters of varying heights. Negative space is key! I’d keep the tallest stem 10-12 inches so as not to compete with faucets, etc.”

A tray helps too: “Displaying them on a tray or wooden slab can help tie them together & unify the display (as opposed to them looking like random pots on a counter).” On a budget, Alan says: “Pine cones, dried orange slices and ribbon dress them cheaply.”

Alan has one note of advice for people with pets or small children: “Skip holly, yew and mistletoe around pets and kids, since all are toxic.”

While Eva Mendes and Ryan Gosling have both played a variety of characters throughout their film careers, we really do think that “people with a stunning Christmas kitchen island” is one we really fancy having a go at, too.
