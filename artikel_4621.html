Leclerc Given FIA Minimum Penalty for Leaving the Track and Taking the Lead at the Mexico City GP

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Neither his teammate nor Max Verstappen is happy that the FIA chose not to give Charles Leclerc a 10-second penalty for his opening-lap move.

At the start of the Mexico City Grand Prix, Charles Leclerc moved up from third to challenge his teammate Lewis Hamilton for the lead. And as has often been the case between the two Ferrari drivers this year, the battle could’ve gone cleaner.

Charles Leclerc took the lead by leaving the track in turn four, pushing Hamilton off his line and sending the pole-sitter tumbling back to third, with Max Verstappen taking advantage.

Quickly, Leclerc pulled out to a one-second lead over Verstappen. Leclerc had until turn 12 to give back the position, but with Verstappen down, he would drop from the lead to third.

Leclerc stayed in the lead, and the Stewards investigated his pass; recently, the FIA has had a stern talk with drivers and said that nearly every advantage gained by leaving the track that the driver did not reverse in time would result in a ten-second penalty.

Leclerc received a 5-second penalty, which was relayed over the radio to the drivers once he was 3.6 seconds clear of Verstappen and 5.1 seconds clear of his teammate Hamilton.

The mitigating circumstances for the league minimum of 5 seconds seem to be the nature of the opening turns at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez.

“Ha, I thought all the new penalties were a ten?” Verstappen replied over the radio to the news.

Neither Hamilton nor Leclerc ended up winning the race, with Hamilton finishing second and Leclerc fourth.

With his podium finish, Hamilton was asked about missing out on the win because of his teammate’s move and unwillingness to give back the position which ultimately led to a Ferrari loss.

“I mean, in the drivers' briefing we discussed that as soon as you go off in turn one, you have to give the position back, but obviously.” Hamilton referred to Leclerc’s 5-second penalty, “Last year I got a 10-second penalty for that same move that he did. But ultimately, it was that that cost us the win today. And I think we have to have some serious talks behind the scenes for sure.”

Post-race in the press conference, Hamilton expanded on his issue with Leclerc’s first-lap move and his subsequent move on the red-flag restart, saying that it has more to do with mentality than age.

“The guy’s 28 years old, so I don’t think it’s an age thing. I think it’s a mentality thing. It’s like do-or-die: either I win, or none of us do, and it shouldn’t be that. It should be us getting that one-two.”

With Hamilton in second place in the WDC challenge ahead of a Mercedes, his bitterness towards teamwork has grown over the season.
