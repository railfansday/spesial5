Netflix’s ‘Black Doves’ Has the Perfect Solution for James Bond’s Biggest Problem

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

There are more than a few shows about espionage and spycraft on television right now, and the genre is about to get a major shot of adrenaline on the big screen with the confirmation that Robert Pattinson has been cast as the newest James Bond. Black Doves stands out among other secret agent shows because it portrays more of the characters than just their relationship to government conspiracies and world-ending threats. Being an assassin is a dangerous line of work, but it's also a profession both Helen (Keira Knightley) and Sam (Ben Whishaw) have fallen into and now have no way to escape. Although there are certainly threats that could potentially thrust all of Great Britain — and potentially the entire world — into chaos, the emphasis on grounded characterization is what makes Black Doves such an exhilarating series.

The titular “Black Doves” is an organization of high-ranking assassins who perform covert missions with global implications, and one that Helen is still working for while her husband, Wallace Webb (Andrew Buchan), becomes the Secretary of State for Defense. While Helen struggles to be more open with her loved ones, Sam has the opposite problem; he has never been able to find someone he can have a permanent relationship with, and often chooses isolation to avoid being further devastated. Black Doves shows how spies’ advanced training and perceptiveness come at the cost of other aspects of life, making “normalcy” a foreign concept. It’s among the most action-packed and twisty shows on television right now, but the solid writing provides just as much reason to care for the characters on screen.

Helen is an atypical protagonist for an action show because Black Doves highlights a mother in the middle of a struggling marriage that does not provide an easy solution to her problems. Although she was initially assigned to seduce Wallace and gain information through him for the Black Doves, she develops a genuine attraction to him that ultimately turns into love. The guardedness that Helen shows, leading to her affair with Jason (Andrew Koji) in Season 1, isn't a sign that she is unhappy with Wallace, but that she can't fully open up to someone she can't be honest with. This makes for a fascinating character arc, as Helen is clearly an expert in her field but is made to feel guilty about what she is best at.

Espionage meets the unground world of crime.

Whishaw gives Sam a gritty, worn-down persona that reflects his efforts to bury his emotions and avoid any commitment. Although, in theory, not being tied down to a single person would make Sam less vulnerable to threats, Whishaw is able to show the draining impact this has as his character is forced to give up time with those who might make him happy; Season 2 even introduces a heartbreaking twist when Sam’s duties get in the way of his burgeoning romance with Jerry (Samuel Barnett). This leads to one of the show's most powerful moments, where Sam admits his vulnerabilities to Helen, revealing the strong connection between them, forged by the shared secrets they are forced to keep.

Black Doves goes beyond the clichés of a typical spy story because it provides context and depth to one of its most mysterious characters. Season 1 introduces Elizabeth Reed (Sarah Lancashire) as the most ruthless, brilliant mastermind in the Black Doves organization who serves as a handler for Helen, positioning her in a way similar to Bond's MI6 chief M. Season 2 complicates the character by examining her backstory through an undercover operation in East Berlin that went south. It’s a reminder that there are no clean getaways for those involved in espionage who think they can make a clean break, and the complex dynamic that emerges between Reed and her daughter Vita (Bailey Fines) in the aftermath explores the generational cost of giving up one’s life to duty. Black Doves is able to expose the faults in even its heroic characters; all of them have committed terrible acts in their line of work, and it is up to the audience to determine whether the ends justify the means.

Although its trio of stars makes Black Doves one of the best-acted shows on the air, the ensemble cast is full of great performances that offer more depth to the characters. Season 2 develops an earnest and emotional bond between the assassins Williams (Ella Lily Hyland) and Eleanor (Gabrielle Creevy); while they easily could have been used for little more than comic relief, Black Doves makes a thoughtful point about generations' different priorities by showing how sharply this pair contrasts with Helen and Sam. Just because someone possesses incredible skills doesn’t mean that they aren't also human. Not only is the ability to graft emotional honesty within a series that is so frequently absurd (in the best way) one of Black Doves' biggest achievements, but it's also a storytelling strength that the upcoming James Bond reboot should take a page from.
