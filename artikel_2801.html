While Jayden was doing his thing on offense, another great story of our season continued Thursday night: Sonny Styles is making an increasingly legitimate run at Defensive Rookie of the Year. His top competition will likely come from his college teammate and fellow NFC East linebacker, Arvell Reese. Styles edged Reese on the stat sheet with an extra solo tackle and a tackle for loss that Arvell wasn't able to get. What stands out more than the stats, though, is the tape of that tackle for loss. After positioning his entire team for the play he seemed to know was coming, Styles beat every member of the Giants offense to the spot and brought down J.J. Styles continues to impress, displaying the speed, intelligence and tackling ability we sorely need in the middle of our defense. Jayden Daniels could be a matchup problem.

Do you remember when I said the Bengals' secondary doesn't have too tough a matchup on Monday Night Football? Well, even the worst receiving corps in the NFL can figure out a way to get open if the quarterback is able to extend the play. No corner can stay on a receiver's hip for more than a few seconds. If the Bengals' defensive line can't put pressure on Daniels, it could be a big problem for the secondary.

Daniels is one of the most athletic quarterbacks the Bengals will face all season long. If he gets out of the pocket, not only could he punish the Bengals with his legs, but he can buy time to find a receiver downfield. He's perfectly capable of making any throw required of him, and if the Bengals can't keep him in a pocket they can close around him, the secondary could have a long day.
