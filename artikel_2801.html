Mets reportedly sign Tarik Skubal for nine years, $450 million: Breaking down the record-setting deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

For the second offseason in the last three years, the New York Mets have landed the biggest player on the market, reportedly inking Tarik Skubal to a record-setting, nine-year, $450 million contract, according to ESPN's Jeff Passan.

The contract sets a new record for total guaranteed money and average annual value (AAV) for a pitching-only contract. That has previously been set by Yoshinobu Yamamoto's 12-year, $325 million contract with the Dodgers. There are no opt-outs in the contract, and Skubal has a full no-trade clause, which means there's a strong likelihood that the 30-year-old left-hander will end his career with the Mets.

As The Athletic's Jayson Stark noted, "The Mets now employ the highest-paid position player (Juan Soto) and pitcher (Tarik Shubal). They are on the hook for $907.3 million, just for those two players, for the next nine seasons." That's an interesting juxtaposition with the ongoing dispute between the owners and the MLB Players Association about imposing a salary cap, and Mets owner Steve Cohen making this deal doesn't seem like a person who is worried about having to adhere to a cap in the near future.

With Skubal now off the board, the free agent market has now been set for other starting pitchers like Freddy Peralta, Michael King, Trevor Rogers, Kevin Gausman, and others. But what does this mean for the Mets' rotation?

Obviously, Skubal will be positioned as the ace for a franchise that desperately needed one. In 2026, the Mets' starters were ninth in strikeout rate (23.3%), 22nd in WHIP (1.33), 23rd in ERA (4.47), 24th in walk rate (9.1%), and just 26th in wins (38). They needed an anchor, and they got one.

Skubal pitched to a 2.72 ERA, 0.96 WHIP, and 186/27 K/BB ratio in 158 2/3 innings despite having to miss six weeks following elbow surgery. That procedure, which was done with NanoScope technology, allowed Skubal to return faster than is typical when a pitcher needs to have loose bodies removed from their elbows. It also didn't seem to have any impact on his performance, as Skubal pitched to a 2.36 ERA over his final 16 starts. In fact, in 10 starts with the Dodgers after the trade deadline, Skubal had a 2.61 ERA, 1.05 WHIP, and a 70/13 K/BB ratio in 62 innings.

There are some long-term health concerns with Skubal, which need to be taken into account given the length of this deal. He had Tommy John surgery back in 2016 before his professional career began. He then had flexor tendon surgery in September of 2022 and then had this elbow procedure in May of this past season. The left-hander has pitched to a 2.47 ERA since returning from that flexor tendon surgery in 2023, so the previous injuries have not impacted his performance, but you do have to wonder how many more years the Mets will get at peak production.

With Tarik Skubal in New York, the Mets can now slide Nolan McLean down to the number two spot in their rotation. Christian Scott seems locked into another spot after his strong 2026 season, but Zac Thornton, Sean Manaea, Kodai Senga, Tylor Megill, Jonah Tong are all options for the final two spots. Which also means we should not rule out the Mets adding another starting pitcher in free agency.

McLean is a talented young pitcher and had a good season with a 3.16 ERA, 1.17 WHIP, and 194/70 K/BB ratio. The 25-year-old is a future ace, but it's hard to rely on him as the ace of a potential World Series contender in 2027. Last year was his first full MLB season and his only season in professional baseball with over 113 innings pitched. McLean has also struggled to find a secondary pitch he can consistently rely on for whiffs against righties, and he posted a below-average 9.9% swinging strike rate against right-handed hitters in 2026. The addition of Skubal gives him continued time to develop without having to carry a rotation.

Scott is another arm that will benefit from a veteran like Skubal. Scott pitched 110 innings in 2026 after missing all of 2025 while recovering from Tommy John surgery. The 27-year-old registered a 3.85 ERA, 1.27 WHIP, and 134/47 K/BB. He posted a solid 12.1% swinging strike rate and has continued to tweak his arsenal, adding in a cutter to help him handle left-handed hitters.

Skubal, who has an elite changeup, could also be a big help to Thornton, who had a 3.42 ERA and 1.14 WHIP in 15 starts for the Mets in 2026 but lacks a changeup that can be a real weapon against right-handed batters. With Skubal in the mix, the Mets could also feel better about keeping Senga in the bullpen, where he posted a 4.85 ERA but a 4.16 xFIP and a nearly 28% strikeout rate.

All in all, the move clearly positions the Mets to be one of the favorites in the National League in 2027, but don't assume that it also means they're done adding to their rotation this offseason.
