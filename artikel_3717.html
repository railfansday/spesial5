Dark fantasy and sudoku fuse together in unholy matrimony in Dungoku, a dungeon crawling twist on the classic puzzle game

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

My only question is, is it a soft g like in dungeon, or a hard g like that guy from Dragon Ball?

Maths is not my strong suit, I make no secret of this, so when I come face to face with something like sudoku I will admit to recoiling in fear at having to confront this issue. Numbers don't make sense to me at the best of times, and now I have to figure out the specific placements of them whereby each number can only be used once per section, and each column and row have to all contain numbers one through nine? Terrifying! No, thanks, I'd much rather do the exact same thing but with, I don't know, skeletons and ghouls and the like. Which, conveniently, is more or less what Dungoku offers, a genre mashup of dungeon crawling and sudoku with a retro dark fantasy vibe.

Okay, it's not exactly the same. You don't crawl the dungeons in the typical fashion here as it leans heavily towards the puzzle side of things. Maps come in different, grid-based shapes and sizes, but to start they are mostly void of enemies. It's predominantly up to you to place enemies, or sometimes chests, in specific squares, all of which have their own rules that you must follow, with a set number that need to be placed on the map.

For example, one enemy may have a requirement that it is adjacent to a zombie in the same room. Another might need to be placed in a dead end. Others, like an owl creature, can only share a room with itself or other animals. So, it's different from sudoku in that each puzzle features changing enemies that have to be placed, as opposed to always needing to follow the same basic rules. It seems like a clever spin on the classic puzzle! Plus there's loot to be acquired that improves your stats and thereby your chance of survival (making a mistake in placement results in, obviously, getting hurt; this is still a dungeon crawler!).

In total there's apparently over 100 "hand-crafted dungeons to explore," and more than 20 monsters to take on, so it sounds like there's some good potential for interesting puzzle combinations. I also like the specific mention of "human composed music," certainly makes it sound like AI wasn't involved, and lord almighty I hope that to be true! No release date attached to this one just yet, but you can wishlist it on Steam in the meantime.
