James Bond Fans Already Complaining About Robert Pattinson: “Not Alpha Male”

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

As is the ritual, the new Bond choice is getting gripes from franchise fans: "I've seen sexier potatoes."

There are many great reasons for casting Robert Pattinson as James Bond.

But in the wake of Amazon-MGM Studios announcing the Batman actor as the seventh 007, plenty of fans are griping online about the choice.

Such complaints are to be expected, of course. Any actor cast as Bond is going to get bashed. Even Daniel Craig was roundly criticized by fans when producers tapped the character actor as Bond back in 2005 (“he isn’t handsome enough,” “Bond can’t be blonde,” etc.) Our visual concept of Bond is entirely formed by the other six actors who have played the role, so anybody new is going to be tough to imagine — at least, at first. That said, the Pattinson complaints are interesting, perhaps even valuable, as they show producers what they’re up against in terms of preconceived notions about their casting choice.

The comment section of the British tabloid Daily Mail has been a hive of Pattinson bashing all morning.

Wrote one: “He lacks the magnetic charm, polished elegance, rugged confidence, seductive sophistication, and effortless swagger that define James Bond.” Similarly penned another: “He doesn’t stand out as having the animal masculinity or charisma of a Bond character.” Another opined: “He doesn’t come across as Alpha male, ladies man, action type.” Snarked another: “I’ve seen sexier potatoes.”

Others felt that Pattinson has had too many iconic roles and expressed frustration that the same actors seem to be cast in so many projects.

“You can’t have the same actor playing Batman and James Bond at the same time,” wrote one. “The Broccoli family would have never made this decision.” Or as one fan elaborated on Amazon MGM Studios’ Instagram post: “If you want to make a James Bond truly immortal, you have to cast someone who isn’t already strongly associated with other iconic movie characters.”

Or wrote one Redditor: “I’m not fond of this choice. I’d much rather see him continue his very interesting career as he has been excellent in so many roles & then have a newly discovered talent or up & comer to play Bond.”

One influencer on X called the casting a “disappointingly unimaginative choice … Do we really have such a shortage of actors that we need the same handful of guys playing every iconic character? … This feels like a lazy, risk-averse pick designed to hedge Amazon’s bets by casting an established star.”

A recurring comment is that Pattinson would have been better as the next film’s villain, though this probably has some recency bias coming off his heel turn in The Odyssey and his creepy take on Chris Hanson in Primetime. “He doesn’t feel like Bond. He’d have a made a fantastic Bond villain though,” one fan wrote on Reddit. But as another countered: “The fact people say he would make a good Bond villain is one of the main reasons I’m excited for him to instead be Bond.”

Of course, plenty (if not most) reactions online have been positive, and even some of his detractors begrudgingly admit that Pattinson is a terrific actor who will probably crush the role.

The truth is there’s too much at stake — and too many talented people behind the scenes in a Bond production — for any actor cast in the role to outright fail. Any of the actors who were under consideration could have been made to work, and Pattinson, one suspects, will have less of a hill to climb than others given his star power and franchise experience. A week or two after the opening of Bond 26, Pattinson will have evolved from a decision made by producers and a studio to simply being Bond (James Bond).
