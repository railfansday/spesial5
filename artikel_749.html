Sarah Has ‘One Month’ to Leave Her Parents’ Home. She’s 45. (Exclusive Clip, Director Chat)

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Writer-director-producer Maya Vitkova-Kosev, known for collaborations with Cristian Mungiu and Radu Jude, discusses her Tallinn premiere in which madness and sadness collide.

Artist Sarah, 45, is in a pickle. With her parents’ 50th wedding anniversary approaching, they give her a shocking ultimatum: After a lifetime of being protected and supported, Sarah has one month to (finally) leave the family home.

The question is: Can Sarah suddenly become independent while navigating dating, reconnecting with ex-boyfriends and complicated feelings about motherhood? Oh yeah, there is also a rather unusual condition her parents mention. If she ever wants to return to living with her parents, she must have a baby – to carry on the family line. Welcome to the quirky world of One Month, the new feature from Bulgarian writer-director-producer Maya Vitkova-Kosev (Viktoria), who is also known for her collaborations with Cristian Mungiu, Radu Jude and Cristi Puiu. Madness and sadness collide in a very different cinematic experience.

One Month stars Miroslava Gogovska, Mariana Krumova, Marin Yanev, Plamen Velikov and Yordan Rasin. The cinematographer is Krum Rodriguez, with editing by Bernat Aragones.

Ahead of its world premiere in the Critics’ Picks Competition of the 30th edition of the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival (PÖFF) in Estonia on Wednesday, Vitkova-Kosev talked to THR about her inspiration for One Month during the COVID pandemic, her cast and returning to the mother-daughter relationship.

But first, watch a clip from the film, which THR can exclusively premiere, for a taste of the wackier side of life presented in the film. Warning: you may never dance again!

After watching this wild dance scene, you may wonder how it came together. “Only six or seven of the dancing people are actors. All the rest were extras,” the filmmaker shares. “We knew the piece of music right from the start. And when we started filming, the entire crew was standing at the monitors, laughing. The actors and extras didn’t have any other direction than just to go crazy. So some of them started undressing, as you saw. One of them poured wine over her head. Unfortunately, we had to cut many parts out because otherwise the scene would have become a mastodon in the film.”

No, the premise of the film is not inspired by Vitkova-Kosev’s life! “The idea for the film came totally out of the blue,” she recalls. “It was just a scene that [hit me] at the beginning of the [COVID] pandemic. It was actually one month after we were locked down in Barcelona [where she lives]. I had this idea about a woman who’s been living with her parents forever, and suddenly they have a conversation letting her know that she has a month to go. And I started laughing. Because it was a little bit gloomy at that time, I really needed to entertain myself, so I sat down and wrote it.”

The process of making the film took longer than originally thought, “because I got pregnant, I gave birth, I was breastfeeding my kid, and so on,” the filmmaker quips.

The casting was, of course, key to getting the tonal mix of comedy, drama, loneliness, lack of direction and madness right. Vitkova-Kosev knew Gogovska from an experimental theater group. “I always wanted to do something with her,” she tells THR. “I did a short film a couple of months before we shot One Month, and I invited her. She had a totally different role in the short film, but I wanted to see whether she could bring this sense of humor with a bit of madness, and with drama behind it. And yes, it was clear for me that she was the person.”

You may not recognize the rest of the cast, but some people in Bulgaria still will. “All the older guys are stars, the old stars of Bulgaria,” the director explains. “Some of them had maybe even been forgotten.”

One key theme in Vitkova-Kosev’s new film, and a recurring topic in her work, is a key familial relationship. “This was not an intentional thing,” she tells THR. “But I discovered that it is a mother-daughter story again. In Viktoria, that was intentionally there with three generations of mothers and daughters. Here, I thought this was a totally different thing – I’m just making a funny film.”

In fact, various comedic elements ended up on the cutting-room floor. “Honestly, the film was so much funnier,” Vitkova-Kosev shares. “But my editor Bernat and I aimed to make Sarah’s journey clear. So that meant killing a lot of darlings on the way to make her grow up, to set her free.”

As you can see in the exclusive clip that THR could premiere above, music is a key part of One Month and Sarah’s journey. Among the songs you can hear in the movie are the Bee Gees’ “Stayin’ Alive,” Eagles of Death Metal’s version of George Michael’s “Careless Whisper,” Muse’s “Can’t Take My Eyes Off You” and The Cranberries’ “No Need to Argue.”

“Maybe the hardest thing for this film was getting the music,” Vitkova-Kosev says. “It took me a year and a half. I did it on my own. I didn’t hire anybody to do that. I was in contact with the companies, and the first thing that they had to understand was that we had limitations in the budget. So basically, my choice as a producer was to not pay myself for my work as a screenwriter, director, and producer, but pay for the music. I feel music is important and something that will stay.”
