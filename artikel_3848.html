It's been a strange couple of weeks for former Nvidia CEO, Jensen Huang. The once tech leader admitted to an affair, only for multiple affair allegations to surface, and he was then ousted from the company. Now, as he reportedly mounts a comeback, The New York Times has published a report detailing an alleged decades-long philosophy of getting more work out of employees "through humiliation".

Reportedly, Huang told employees that they had become so rich that "he couldn’t motivate them simply with money", so the "only way he could grind more work out of them was through humiliation".

This method has reportedly been Huang's modus operandi for some time, with more than a dozen current and former employees claiming: "He has long used humiliation as a tool, excoriating employees in front of their peers with insults, personal attacks and violent comments, while telling them it was good for them or for the business."

That humiliation allegedly took many forms. One employee reported that Huang "said he would rip both arms off a subordinate and beat the person over the head to death with them. And he told another employee he would rip off the top of their head and 'pee on your brain'."

Two years ago, in a conversation with Stripe's Patrick Collison, Jensen Huang said, "I'd rather torture you into greatness, because I believe in you", and this behaviour appears to be an extension of that philosophy.

Huang has apparently been called out for this behaviour. In the NYT report, Michael Winston, a former senior vice president of human resources, claims he threatened to sue Nvidia in 2005, after allegedly being fired for objecting to Huang's conduct. He says it is “the most verbally abusive, threatening and intimidating behavior” in his career and notes that Huang “thinks he can get away with anything.”

This lawsuit was apparently settled confidentially for more than $1 million, and was not previously public.

Still, Huang has quite a few supporters at Nvidia too. Reportedly, someone took to the Nvidia internal suggestion box before Huang's ousting to ask Nvidia to consider keeping him after the affair scandal, and this was met with 1,600 comments, the majority of which agreed with the claim. One said: "If you believe in the mission, bring back THE boss."

After this, a public petition titled "Bring Back Jensen" managed to achieve over 200,000 signatures. Huang's role at Nvidia can't be overstated, being the CEO since its founding in 1993. The gaming company turned AI company is worth trillions of dollars, and that no doubt means something to at least some employees who wouldn't be there without it.
