Husband on trial over wife’s disappearance admits to dismembering her body

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Naresh Bhatt says he discovered his wife dead from suicide, and decided to get rid of the body, fearing accusations that he was responsible for murdering her.

MANASSAS, Va. (CN) â Naresh Bhatt shocked a Virginia courtroom Thursday when he testified that he had cut his wife’s body up after finding her dead from what he believed to be suicide.

Bhatt, 39, is on trial over the 2024 disappearance and presumptive murder of his wife Mamta Kafle Bhatt, 28. He has pleaded not guilty but admitted during testimony to charges of defiling a body and concealing a body. He maintains he did not kill his wife.

Mamta Bhatt’s family members and friends, wide-eyed, sat in a row behind prosecutors. Spectators in the packed courtroom gasped at his admissions, which contrasted with his previous claims he had no knowledge of his missing wifeâs whereabouts. Prosecutors say he previously gave shifting accounts of his wife’s whereabouts after her disappearance.

Mamta Bhatt’s remains have not been found, leading prosecutors to lean on circumstantial evidence such as an increase in their home’s water usage and Naresh Bhatt’s web searches.

During his second day on the stand, Naresh Bhatt said that on July 30, 2024, he discovered his wife unresponsive and next to a poisonous substance in a pool of vomit.

Bhatt said he attempted medical interventions himself, pulling the body into the bathroom and pouring water into his wife’s mouth. Instead of dialing 911, he said he panicked and decided to get rid of the body.

“Why didn’t you call literally anyone else for help?” asked Tracey Lenox, his attorney and chief public defender for Prince William County. “Were you concerned about how it would look?”

“I was concerned about everything,” he said.

Lenox then asked if he was afraid he would be blamed for driving her to suicide.

Prosecutors have characterized Naresh Bhatt as abusive. He testified Wednesday that his wife’s demeanor had changed in the months following the birth of their child.

Naresh Bhatt has maintained that he never physically abused his wife. During cross-examination, Deputy Commonwealthâs Attorney Matthew Sweet derided this, pointing out that Bhatt chopped her up.

“She loved you, always,” the prosecutor said.

Naresh Bhatt testified that after unsuccessfully trying to rouse his wife, he took his infant daughter to a babysitter’s house and returned home, where he considered hanging himself. Instead, he went to Home Depot, “to find anything to hide a body.”

He said he considered dumping his wife’s corpse in the Potomac River but then decided to chop the body up, “just to make it a smaller size so it fit in the can,” he said.

It was difficult. “I was trying to lower the body apart,” he testified. “Like, separate the legs from the body.”

Mentally and physically, he felt powerless, he added, explaining that initially, he did not have the strength to continue.

Later, he got back to work, partially using knives but primarily depending upon a saw, in which prosecutors claimed to have found blood.

Lenox asked if Naresh Bhatt finished cutting the body before neighborhood trash pickup came by, and if he knew the body would then be taken away. Naresh Bhatt answered affirmatively.

Bhatt said he did not throw his wife’s body in trash dumpsters around the area, as prosecutors contend. Instead, he only tossed out clothing and trash in the dumpsters, he said. While wiping his eyes on occasion, he mostly spoke without hesitation.

Mamta Bhatt was a nurse, and attendees included a contingent of nurses dressed in scrubs. When Sweet pointed to Bhatt and said, “You’re a liar,” and Bhatt responded, “yes,” applause rippled through the crowd.
