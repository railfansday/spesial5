For more than two decades, artificial intelligence has played a growing role in the practice of radiology. Today, nearly every radiologist uses AI tools, whether that’s to triage the images they interpret, dictate their reports, or detect findings in the dozens of X-rays and CTs they read every day.

But last year, radiology experts and developers at the annual meeting of the Radiological Society of North America began to foreground a new paradigm in AI. Business acquisitions and debates centered on technology, powered by vision-language models, that can do the core work of a radiologist: looking at an image, and drafting a report of the clinical findings within it.

“Last year’s RSNA was mainly focused on whether it’s actually possible that an AI model will write a draft for radiologists that would look like an actual radiology report, and that would be accurate enough to be even considered by a radiologist,” said Michael Braginsky, chief technology officer of Aidoc.
