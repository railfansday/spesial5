Zillow Rental Market Report: Demand for Single-Family Rentals Surges

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Zillow’s latest Rental Market Report shows that demand for renting single-family homes has risen, with consumers noting that they would need a $14,408 pay raise to cover the monthly costs of owning a home, even before saving for a down payment. Nationally, the typical single-family rent is $2,243 a month, compared with $2,603 a month to own a single-family home with 20% down, or $360 more each month.

“For a growing number of households, renting a house is a legitimate financial strategy,” said Kara Ng, senior economist at Zillow. “You get many of the lifestyle benefits, but it takes less income than owning and keeps more flexibility in the monthly budget. That’s the appeal for the deliberate renter.”

The size of the raise depends on the market According to Zillow, the pay raise needed to buy is now the highest since early 2025, more than double the $6,463 it took in February 2026, when mortgage rates briefly fell below 6%. The gap looks very different from market to market. It’s largest in San Jose, where the income needed to buy ($404,925) is more than double the income needed to rent ($190,561), a gap of $214,364. The next largest gap is in San Francisco ($139,353), followed by San Diego ($84,101), New York ($78,208) and Los Angeles ($78,003). The smallest annual gaps are in Pittsburgh ($2,554), Houston ($4,500) and Cincinnati ($4,774). Buffalo is the only market where buying would not require a raise.

Even with such a raise, would-be buyers still face another hurdle: saving a 20% down payment. About 30% of mortgage buyers use a gift or loan from family or friends, and 46% draw on two or more sources, including the use of down payment assistance programs, according to Zillow’s 2026 Consumer Housing Trends Report.

In addition to the monthly cost, households deciding between renting and buying should weigh the upfront costs of buying, the chance to build equity and the opportunity for renters to invest their down payment instead. Zillow’s rent vs. buy research puts the national break-even timeline—the number of years a household would need to own before buying comes out ahead—at 7.8 years with 20% down.

Rental demand keeps climbing As renting a house becomes an increasingly better value, more shoppers are turning to rentals. Rental demand is up 22.1% year over year nationwide, according to the Zillow Observed Renter Demand Index (ZORDI). Demand for single-family rentals is up 24.3%, roughly double the 12.1% rise for multifamily rentals.

Rental demand surges in the Bay Area and the Sun Belt Demand rose the most in San Jose, where ZORDI is up 78.9% from a year ago, followed by San Francisco (60.5%), Austin (59.6%), Miami (51.5%) and Jacksonville (47.9%). San Jose and San Francisco are also among the markets where buying is furthest out of reach, and where rents are rising quickly, up 8.9% and 12.2% respectively. In Austin, Miami and Jacksonville, rent growth is more moderate, at 1.1%, 2.1% and 2.6%.

Not every market is following the national trend. ZORDI is down year over year in Providence (-4.3%), Richmond (-1.9%), Baltimore (-1.7%), Hartford (-1.3%) and Washington, D.C. (-0.9%).
