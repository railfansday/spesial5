Michael Burry warned Jensen Huang affair could invite antitrust crackdown on Nvidia

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The investor said the affair could revive U.S. scrutiny of Nvidia's China chip trade and the Justice Department's antitrust probe

Michael Burry attends the "The Big Short" New York premiere at Ziegfeld Theater on November 23, 2015 in New York City. (Jim Spellman/WireImage)

Michael Burry warned that the scandal involving Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang could reopen examination of how Nvidia sells its top-end chips into China, as well as the Justice Department's antitrust probe, according to Benzinga. The investor made the argument in a Substack post.

Nvidia’s board has ordered an independent review of Jensen Huang’s ties to a businesswoman suspected by intelligence agencies of spying for China, according to Benzinga. Huang is said to have had an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed. Investors sent Nvidia stock lower after the news.

Burry thinks the affair is unlikely to hurt the underlying profitability of Nvidia's operations, according to Benzinga. He wrote that he had not seen the news coming and described himself as "a bit shocked" by the rush of shareholders to exit the shares.

Burry said Nvidia's dealmaking is the real exposure. Nvidia's "mafia-like behavior investing in and making requirements of many players in the AI space has, to me, long seemed ripe for anti-trust prosecution," he wrote, according to Benzinga.

"There is a lot there if Justice and Commerce and various enforcement agencies stop ignoring and start investigating," Burry said, according to Benzinga. Until recently, he had no idea what would trigger that change, and the Huang news altered his view. "The mighty are only mighty until they have fallen. The fallen attract all manner of opportunists, both righteous and repugnant," he wrote.

The Justice Department has been examining whether Nvidia attempted to sidestep antitrust review of its licensing agreement with the AI chip startup Groq, The New York Times reported in September.

Burry said he has no interest in holding the stock over the long haul. "I believe Nvidia's competitive advantage will shrink over the next five years," he wrote, adding that the scandal makes it more likely the decline will arrive sooner. He holds puts on the VanEck Semiconductor ETF, the Nasdaq $NDAQ-100 index, Oracle $ORCL and Palantir $PLTR Technologies, among other names, according to Benzinga.

Burry has maintained a short position in Nvidia this year. In August, he said all of his short positions were profitable except his bet against Nvidia. Earlier this year, he added to that bet by buying January 2027 puts on Nvidia with a $115 strike price at $3.30 per contract.
