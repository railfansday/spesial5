James Farrior reveals the mistake that nearly ruined Super Bowl XLIII

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The final minutes of Super Bowl XLIII remain etched in Pittsburgh Steelers lore for James Harrison's 100-yard pick-six and Santonio Holmes' toe-tap catch. Yet according to former defensive captain James Farrior, the 2008 defense nearly unraveled late in the fourth quarter before sealing the championship.

Speaking on former teammate Cameron Heyward's "Not Just Football with Cam Heyward" podcast, Farrior recalled how Pittsburgh's defense splintered on the sideline after Larry Fitzgerald's go-ahead touchdown, with position groups pointing fingers while waiting for the offense to respond.

"The linebackers in one group, the defensive line in another group, and the DBs are over in another group, and we're all at each other, talk blame," Farrior said. "We're all blaming all the other guys."

Even after Holmes hauled in Ben Roethlisberger's game-winning pass, Farrior held back his celebration because he doubted the receiver stayed in bounds. Once the touchdown stood, Farrior immediately warned Harrison to get deep on Arizona's final possession, only to watch Harrison get beaten deep on the very next snap.

"And the first damn play, quarterback drop, Kurt [Warner] drops back, Deebo doesn't get deep, and they throw the ball over his damn head," Farrior recalled. "I was so mad, man. I was like, 'Oh, man. They got a chance now.'"

Moments later, LaMarr Woodley forced a strip-sack, and Brett Keisel recovered the loose ball. Thinking the game was over, Farrior ripped off his helmet and sprinted around celebrating, until an official delivered a jarring warning.

"The ref's like, 'We got to look at that call. But if that's not a fumble, you're going to get a 15-yard penalty for taking your helmet off,'" Farrior said. "I was praying to God that that was a fumble."

When replay confirmed the fumble, Farrior's relief and his celebratory words to the referee capped one of the wildest finishes in Super Bowl history.
