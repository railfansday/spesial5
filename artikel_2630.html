Marlins Hand Out 1,000 Meals To Miami Families

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

On Friday at loanDepot Park, 1,000 South Florida families received holiday meals distributed by two hundred volunteers. Marlins president of baseball operations Peter Bendix brought his 7-month-old son to the event, according to MLB.com. The baby wore a turkey onesie while his father helped organize the distribution. This annual event marks the 18th year the team has fed local families before Thanksgiving.

The team did not just drop off boxes and leave. They set up stations where families could grab turkeys, beans, rice, and stuffing mix. Volunteers also handed out pasta, tomato sauce, canned vegetables, and even protein shakes. Marlins president of business Caroline O'Connor stood at the turkey station to help. She noted that the event grows larger every single year because the team gets closer to the people.

The financial support went beyond just food on a plate. Feeding South Florida was presented with a check for $48,890 as part of the initiative. This money helps the charity buy more food for the region throughout the year. About 150 Marlins employees and 50 workers from Amazon joined forces to make it happen. The group also gave out 400 extra turkeys around Miami-Dade County earlier in the week.

First-time dad Bendix said the event matters deeply to his own family. "It's such a great family event, and it's such a great thing that we do as the Marlins to help families in the local community," Bendix said. He added that providing for the community is a huge part of their family life. "Having our own family is something that's really important to us, and being able to help provide for the community is a huge part of that."

Local leaders showed up to thank the volunteers and see the work firsthand. Miami-Dade County mayor Daniella Levine Cava and City of Miami Commissioner Rolando Escalona attended the event. Escalona said the food distribution helps families who need it most. "This goes a very long way," Escalona said, "so I'm extremely grateful for this event."

The partnership received praise from Giovanni Castro, who leads Amazon's state and local public policy efforts. He said the team shows they are more than a ballclub. "For three years, Amazon has been a proud partner with the Marlins, and every time this team shows they're more than a ballclub. They're a neighbor who shows up for all of South Florida." The story was excerpted from Christina De Nicola's Marlins Beat newsletter.
