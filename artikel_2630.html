Bears Making Super Bowl Push Amid Uncertain Future

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

CHICAGO—These are highly consequential times for the Bears, as they make a potential Super Bowl push in a season that's playing out against the backdrop of their new stadium deliberations and a prospective mega-deal for third-year quarterback Caleb Williams this offseason.

Those elements came together on a gray, occasionally rainy, but mild Sunday at Soldier Field. The Bears may be working on finding their next home, but they're also still busy trying to generate a few lasting memories in their venerable, old one. They extended their winning streak to five games and remained in the thick of the Super Bowl-contending mix in the NFC by beating the Saints, 21–13.

This is a flagship NFL franchise. But the Bears' on-field glories are ever-more-distant memories. They last reached the Super Bowl in the 2006 season, losing the big game then to Peyton Manning and the Colts with a team that had Lovie Smith as its coach and Rex Grossman as its quarterback. The Bears last won the Super Bowl after the 1985 season with the memorably dominant team of Mike Ditka, Walter Payton, Jim McMahon, an all-time-great defense, and the "Super Bowl Shuffle" bravado.

"We're due for one," an Uber driver said Sunday while en route to Soldier Field.

The parking lots by the stadium were relatively filled even more than three hours before the noon local time kickoff. Fans displayed huge Bears flags above their tailgating locations. It was a relatively warm weekend, with people still wearing shorts Saturday night with temperatures in the 60s on the day of the Magnificent Mile Lights Festival and holiday parade. The members of the Chicago Bears Drumline practiced on the field a few hours ahead of Sunday's kickoff.

Chants of "Let's go Bears! Let's go Bears!" rang out from the crowd of 59,911 during the fourth quarter. Practically no one, it seemed, exited early to beat the postgame traffic.

The number of such Sunday afternoon experiences at Soldier Field is beginning to dwindle, but the site of the Bears' next stadium remains undetermined. The fall veto session for the Illinois legislature runs through Dec. 3. Legislators are finalizing a new stadium proposal designed to keep the team in the state, rather than having the Bears leave for a competing stadium proposal in northwest Indiana that's legislatively approved.

"We expect productive dialogue with both leadership teams in the coming weeks," the Bears said earlier this month. "We have a responsibility and obligation to explore every option available to the club."

The newest legislation in Illinois includes the creation of a municipal stadium authority that would own a new domed stadium. The Bears would fund the stadium construction. About $855 million in public money would go toward infrastructure costs, such as new roads around the venue.

A meeting last month involving NFL commissioner Roger Goodell and Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker helped to reopen the conversations. That meeting occurred as the Bears contemplated a potential move to Hammond, Indiana. State leaders there already have approved a funding package for a new stadium. Bears president Kevin Warren said in August that Hammond was the team's "sole focus" in its stadium site search.

The franchise's stadium deliberations in Illinois have focused on land that it owns in suburban Arlington Heights.

There also is pending business to be conducted with Williams, the quarterback chosen first overall in the 2024 NFL Draft out of USC. He becomes eligible for a contract extension this offseason, under the provisions of the NFL's rookie compensation system. Some observers wonder whether he and other members of the celebrated 2024 draft class of quarterbacks will get top-of-the-market money.

That could mean a deal approaching or even surpassing an average of $70 million per season for Williams, who does not have an NFL Players Association-certified agent but has a representation team led by his father Carl.

"Back up the Brinks truck," broadcaster and former NFL safety Ryan Clark told Front Office Sports editor-in-chief Daniel Roberts last week. " … Let's give him his money."

For now, the Bears have the luxury of trying to construct a Super Bowl team around a quarterback still on his comparatively inexpensive rookie contract. Williams originally signed a four-year deal with the Bears worth a guaranteed $39.49 million. He is making about $4.3 million in salary and bonuses this season while counting approximately $10.7 million against the Bears' salary cap, including the prorated amount of his original signing bonus. He is set to make about $6 million next season and count approximately $12.5 million against the cap.

NFL success is all about having a capable coach-and-quarterback combination. Williams and the Bears thrived last season after the franchise's hiring of Ben Johnson as its coach. The Bears are paying Johnson an estimated $13 million per season, a considerable amount for a first-time NFL head coach but not quite at the top of a pay scale that's now around $20 million annually for some coaches.

Johnson and Williams got the Bears to the divisional round of last season's NFC playoffs. They could go even further this season. They added star defensive lineman Jeffery Simmons ahead of this month's NFL's trade deadline. Sunday's win improved the Bears' record to 8–2, keeping them in a virtual tie for first place in the ultra-competitive NFC North division race.

"We win in multiple ways," Williams said after the game. "And that's good for us as a team to know that when we get in these situations throughout the year and late this year … we know that we can win in multiple ways."

"He's a momentum-changer," Williams said. "He swings momentum in our favor a lot of times."

A Super Bowl appearance in February in Los Angeles is far from certain. But it clearly is possible. Next, the Bears have a quick turnaround for a big Thanksgiving Day matchup with the Lions in Detroit.

"Every game counts as one," Johnson said. "But this one, because it's a divisional one, you can say it's one and a half. You know, it's a big one. … We'll be able to catch up on rest after Thursday. But the next little bit is gonna be a race to get ready for this next one."
