High School Football: 5 things to know before regional finals begin

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It's time to settle which area teams are the best of the best.

The Ohio High School Athletic Association football postseason continues Friday, Nov. 20, with regional final games across the state.

This week's games are filled with teams familiar with one another as four rematches from the regular season take place. A few have been anticipated for weeks.

Some playoff heavyweights are looking to maintain their spot at the top of the hill, while other want to make history for their programs.

Here are five things to know before the regional finals begin:

It's the second time in three seasons two Greater Western Ohio Conference teams square off for a chance at a regional title. This game is going to produce fireworks one way or another in Piqua.

Wayne (8-4) also played in the last all-GWOC final in 2024 falling short against Centerville. The Warriors have advanced to this round for a third consecutive season and are trying to get over the hump and back to state for the first time since consecutive title games appearances in 2014 and 2015.

The kings of the mountain in the GWOC are trying to regain the same title in the Region 2. Springfield (10-2) had won five consecutive regional titles before suffering a pair of early playoff exits the past two seasons.

Springfield already defeated Wayne by 31 points in the first meeting on Oct. 2. The feeling is it would be surprising to get another lopsided game this time around. Springfield certainly has a strong defense, led by Southwest District D-I Defensive Player of the Year Jahmiere Daniels-Portis, and the Wildcats have leaned on that group in some of its biggest games. Wayne's dynamic combo of quarterback Kye Graham and receiver Jamier Brown create too many matchup issues to be held down for long.

Will fireworks come on offense or defense? That could determine which side gets into the final four.

Aug. 21 turned out to be a bad weather night. Rain and lightning delayed many games during the opening week of the season. Bellbrook and Tippecanoe's anticipated matchup were one of those affected. This Friday's rematch should have no outside influences to create excuses for not being ready to play in one of the biggest games across the state.

The unbeaten run this season began for Bellbrook (12-0) at Tipp's City Park. Head coach Jeff Jenkins said he felt his team bonded that evening by turning a one-point fourth quarter deficit into a 13-point win. The result assuredly is bugging Tippecanoe (11-1) as its only blemish.

Both sides have mostly dismissed the competition quickly on their remaining schedules. Those roads to the regional final now lead them to crossing paths again. Two hard-nosed programs that don't like to give up an inch will play with the stakes bigger than ever.

Enjoy this Region 12 game between two of the top D-III programs as it's now unsure when the two will meet again. After opening the season against one another for the past six years, both have scheduled new Week 1 opponents beginning next year.

Every year the teams from the Midwest Athletic Conference earn their reputation for being one of the best leagues in the state. This year has been no different.

The only two teams in the state to win three road playoff games? Coldwater and St. Henry. Seven total MAC teams qualified for the postseason. The only teams from that group that have been eliminated were beaten by fellow MAC teams. The average margin of victory for MAC teams playing opponents from other leagues this postseason is 29.4 points.

The league is guaranteed to lose one more team this week as Marion Local (12-0) and New Bremen (8-5) meet again for the Region 28 title. St. Henry (10-3) has rounded into form to try and repeat its state title run. The Redskins get a Deer Park team in the Region 24 final that is on the most historic run in program history.

Coldwater (9-4) may have the biggest test. Its Region 18 game against Archbold will actually be its shortest drive this postseason playing in Findlay. The Cavaliers, however, are seen as 18-point underdogs at Fantastic50.net. Coldwater beat Wooster Triway by 23 points in a Region 18 quarterfinal game when estimated to be a 15-point underdog, so it wouldn't be surprising if they overcome the odds again. After all, they're a MAC team.

Many familiar names fill out the area list of teams playing for regional titles this week. Five teams were here a year ago: Coldwater, Marion Local, St. Henry, Tippecanoe and Wayne.

Bellbrook advanced to a regional final for the third time in six seasons and is still looking for its breakthrough first title. Springfield as previously noted are regulars. Waynesville (10-2) is back for a second time in four years.

Only three of this week's competitors don't have a senior class that have experienced the thrill of playing for a chance to reach state. New Bremen last got here in 2022, but the other two have not gotten this far this decade.

Fenwick (8-5) last played for a regional title in 2015. Urbana (10-3) is the true new kid of the block. The Hillclimbers were last on this stage in 2002 and are seeking their first title since 1988.

Neutral doesn't always mean equal. The OHSAA has mentioned that neutral site game selections would like to be of equal distance for the two teams playing, but it isn't the end-all, be-all determination of where games take place. Other factors, such as schools still playing and others willing to host games play roles as well, as examples, are taken into consideration.

This grouping of game sites for area teams should satisfy the crowd that yearns for similar drives for both teams.

Google Maps shows Urbana and London both drive exactly 26.3 miles from its high schools to reach Marysville's IMPACT stadium. Marion Local and New Bremen being practically next to one another makes it easy for those two to have similar travel plans and Sidney Memorial Stadium makes it a 30-minute drive for both.

The St. Henry and Deer Park matchup is the type of separation people think of when it comes to finding an equal drive and Northmont's Premier Health Stadium meets those expectations. St. Henry will take close to an hour to arrive, and Deer Park needs only a few minutes more.

The largest disparity will be Bellbrook and Tippecanoe. The Golden Eagles have a 14-minute drive across the back roads of Greene County over to Xenia, and the Red Devils need roughly 40 minutes starting on I-75.
