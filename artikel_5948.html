Jeffries puts immigration bill high on Democratic priority list

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries is signaling Democrats will move quickly on immigration legislation when the party reclaims the House in January, while acknowledging it still has work to do in shaping its healthcare agenda.

Jeffries, who is expected to secure his spot as the next speaker during a closed-door vote of the Democratic caucus Wednesday, identified immigration and health care as among the top priorities for the incoming majority during a Tuesday news conference.

“One of the things that we need to do is to move the Dream and Promise Act for DACA recipients and TPS recipients,” Jeffries said, referring to a Democratic bill introduced last year. “We should actually move that bill immediately.”

The proposal aims to create a path to citizenship for certain Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals recipients and Temporary Protected Status holders. Similar legislation has been a top priority for Democrats in both chambers for more than a decade, with Democrats unable to advance this bill in the House while Republicans have held the majority.

Jeffries also reiterated his calls to rein in Immigration and Customs Enforcement and redirect funding allocated to the agency through Republicans' budget law known as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, while expressing a desire for bipartisan cooperation on immigration more broadly.

“We think the border should be safe and strong and secure, and we think our immigration system overall is broken,” Jeffries said. “We need to fix it in a comprehensive way, and certainly, it's our hope that a handful of Republicans will be willing to join us, so we can fix it in a bipartisan way.”

On healthcare, however, Jeffries offered fewer specifics, suggesting Democrats still have decisions to make before taking control of the chamber. Democrats broadly agree on the need to restore subsidies for plans purchased through health insurance markets that expired at the end of 2025. But the question of what additional steps the party should pursue with the goal of expanding coverage and lowering prices is likely to be a major test of Democratic leadership’s ability to find consensus among its robust new majority.

When asked how far Democrats want to lower the Medicare eligibility age, Jeffries responded, “that's all part of an ongoing conversation.”

The New York Democrat outlined several broader healthcare priorities, including reversing Medicaid cuts enacted under President Donald Trump, extending Affordable Care Act tax credits, all of which Democrats have vowed to do over the last year.

“Out of the gate, what we clearly have to do is fix our broken healthcare system in terms of the damage that's been done by Donald Trump and MAGA extremists over the last two years, and that clearly involves reversing the Medicaid cuts and extending the Affordable Care Act tax credits, and beginning to engage in supporting medical research, particularly things like research for pediatric cancer,” Jeffries added.

Democrats established a healthcare working group earlier this Congress, led by Reps. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, D-N.Y. and Terri Sewell, D-Ala., to gather perspectives and develop policy proposals as the party works toward a broader agenda.

Without an announced challenger, Jeffries has expressed confidence that he’ll secure the top Democratic spot in the House, brushing aside questions about incoming members who have said they remain “undecided” ahead of Wednesday’s elections. Jeffries, who was introduced as “Speaker Jeffries” on election night, said last week he was “very confident we’ll get there” and confirmed that the caucus will hold a recorded vote in the contest.

Rep. Nancy Pelosi, D-Calif., was nominated for speaker by acclamation ahead of her first election to the position in 2007. But Jeffries’ allies portrayed his decision to go forward with a recorded vote as a show of strength after a group of incoming progressives, many aligned with the Democratic Socialists of America, had floated forcing a recorded vote in the internal leadership race.

Jeffries said Tuesday the approach mirrors the caucus’ 2018 transition from minority to majority, giving members an opportunity to weigh in on who is best positioned to lead based on “track record, experience and vision.”

This report was corrected to reflect Rep. Nancy Pelosi’s status as a current member.
