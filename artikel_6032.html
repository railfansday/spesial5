Democrats flip House as Republicans confront ‘blue tsunami’

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Democrats seized control of the House on Tuesday by safeguarding vulnerable incumbents and flipping districts in both battleground and deep-red terrain, delivering a sweeping repudiation of Donald Trump and Republicans that threatens to stall the president’s remaining agenda.

The Associated Press call came at 1:26 a.m. Eastern time Wednesday, hours after Democrats were projected to win a majority in the Senate, with a string of victories in deeply conservative states.

“The American people have spoken and made clear they’ve had enough of the extremism, enough of the corruption,’’ House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, who is poised to become the first Black speaker, told cheering supporters in Washington. “They want a Congress that’s going to deliver for the American people, and that is exactly what House Democrats will do.”

Recent polls had forecast a strong Democratic showing amid rising discontent with the president’s policies and economic anxiety driven by rising prices and the Iran war. But the magnitude of the Democratic majority surprised even the most optimistic Democratic partisans.

The “blue tsunami” marked an extraordinary turnaround for a party that suffered withering losses in 2024, when Trump returned to the White House and Republicans won a governing trifecta in Washington.

Republicans had hoped that a mid-decade redistricting push driven by Trump would help them keep their majority by flipping House seats in Texas, North Carolina, Florida and Ohio, among other states. Instead, many of those districts remained in Democrats’ hands.

Trump put himself front and center of the midterm campaign, imploring voters to “pretend that I’m on the ballot.” On Tuesday, he posted on social media that he “wasn’t on the ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE!”

But in a later interview with CBS News, Trump congratulated Democrats on their rout. “My message is, congratulations to the Democrats, and I look forward to working with you for the good of our country,” he said.

Among the night’s Democratic pickups were seats in rural and Republican-leaning districts. Farmer Jamie Ager defeated Republican state Rep. Jennifer Balkcom to flip a Western North Carolina seat that was left open after Republican Chuck Edwards announced his retirement after facing allegations of sexual harassment.

In Kentucky’s 6th District, Democrat Zach Dembo, a Navy veteran and former federal prosecutor, defeated former state Sen. Ralph Alvarado for an open seat anchored in Lexington.

Democrats also swept perennial battleground districts, flipping seats in states such as New Jersey, Colorado and Wisconsin. And in Pennsylvania, Democrats were projected to flip all four seats they were targeting, including the Philadelphia-area district of GOP Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick.

Anger over Trump’s tariff policy drove discontent in farm country, with Democrats ousting Republican Reps. Zach Nunn and Mariannette Miller-Meeks in Iowa and flipping the seat of Senate hopeful Ashley Hinson, who lost her bid to succeed retiring Sen. Joni Ernst.

Meanwhile, Latino voters, who were credited with helping propel Trump back into the White House in 2024, bolstered Democratic successes in Texas, scuttling GOP hopes of making good on the party’s mid-decade redistricting gambit.

Texas Republicans believed Latino voters would stick with the party this year when they redrew the state’s congressional map to create up to five pickup opportunities. But anger over the president’s immigration policies, coupled with economic anxiety, has driven Trump’s approval numbers among Latinos to historic lows.

In South Texas, a pair of Democratic incumbents — Reps. Vicente Gonzalez and Henry Cuellar — easily held on to their redrawn seats, while former Bexar County sheriff’s deputy Johnny Garcia prevailed over Air Force veteran Carlos De La Cruz in a district newly redrawn to elect a Republican. Democratic attorney and educator Katy Padilla Stout flipped the open 23rd District, which stretches across the state’s border with Mexico, and Tejano singer Bobby Pulido routed GOP Rep. Monica De La Cruz in another South Texas race.

The strength of the Democrats’ performance was evident in several under-the-radar races the party won in traditionally red districts, including Missouri’s 2nd District, where Republican Rep. Ann Wagner was ousted, and Indiana’s 5th, where Rep. Victoria Spartz lost reelection.

Speaker Mike Johnson, who crisscrossed the country to boost Republican candidates in the final weeks of the campaign, noted Tuesday night that history wasn’t on the GOP side: The president’s party almost always loses seats in a midterm election.

“From the first day of this election, we knew that to keep the House and sustain our majority we would have to defy more than a century of midterm history,” Johnson said in Shreveport, La. “It looks like history will be kept tonight.”

The new Democratic majority is expected to dedicate resources to investigating the activities of the Trump administration as well as the president.

The likely new Democratic chairman of the House Judiciary Committee is Maryland’s Jamie Raskin, while the Oversight and Government Reform panel is likely to be led by California Rep. Robert Garcia. Raskin and Garcia have already been busy making oversight requests of the administration.

For instance, the two lawmakers joined on a letter last week calling for the retention of conversations between the president and AI chatbots on matters of policy.

“The President and his advisors’ use of AI exposes the country to malign influence from unknown and unaccountable third parties. We already have ample evidence that AI chatbots can be manipulated to suit the commercial, political, or personal agendas of their designers​,” Raskin and Garcia wrote.

Beyond the oversight, Jeffries outlined last month some of his legislative priorities for the next Congress.

“I think we need to put money in the pockets of everyday Americans by doing things like expanding the Child Tax Credit, as well as the Earned Income Tax Credit​,” Jeffries told News12. “And, of course, we also need to end this war because the war has dramatically increased gas prices, fertilizer prices and diesel costs.​”

That was a reference to the military campaign against Iran, which appears likely to ratchet back up after the midterms.

I​n a pre-election interview with CNN, Jeffries said priorities that unite the Democratic Caucus include action on stopping the president’s sweeping application of tariffs, as well as on reversing cuts to Medicaid and extending Affordable Care Act tax credits.

Mary Ellen McIntire contributed to this report.
