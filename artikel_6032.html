GOP congressman-elect makes unusual Trump request, ejects reporter during profanity-laced victory speech

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Trump loyalist, Republican congressman-elect and Sticker Mule CEO, Anthony Constantino, celebrated his defeat of Democrat Blake Gendebien in New York’s 21st Congressional District during a profanity-laced election-night address.

Anthony Constantino, a former boxer and the CEO of the printing company Sticker Mule, delivered a profanity-laden victory speech after his election to New York’s 21st Congressional District, kicking a reporter out of the room and calling on President Donald Trump to pardon Sean "Diddy" Combs.

"I think President Trump should consider freeing Diddy," Constantino said, referring to the incarcerated producer.

"A historic New Yorker that did great things for New York State. I think we should consider it."

Constantino’s brazen speech contrasts sharply with the demeanor of the district’s outgoing incumbent, Rep. Elise Stefanik, R-N.Y., the former No. 3 Republican in the House of Representatives. It also raises questions about whether Constantino earnestly hopes to see the administration pardon Combs, who was convicted of prostitution across state lines.

"Where’s my lawyer at? I have a very good lawyer. Listen, I’m fighting for New York. I want to bring excitement back to New York. I want to bring jobs back to New York," Constantino said moments before his request.

During his 31-minute speech, Constantino spoke about political allies, enemies, the media, power struggles and his vision for the country. He even had a rapper friend perform a freestyle tribute to his congressional election.

At points, Constantino adopted an openly combative tone — especially with respect to members of the press.

"Listen to me, scumbags. Telling people the truth is the right thing to do. I had to listen to you a--holes say ‘dairy farmer Blake Gendebien,’" Constantino said, referring to news coverage of his Democratic challenger.

"He’s not a dairy farmer; his parents were dairy farmers … but you wouldn’t say that because you’re a bunch of f---ing a--holes," he added.

At one point, Constantino singled out a pair of reporters in the crowd.

"Look at that a--hole right there. Him. Look at that guy. Take a picture of him … and Kay Lisa, another f---ing a--hole."

Constantino then ordered one of the reporters removed from the room, describing him as an "angry motherf---er."

U.S. Rep-elect Anthony Constantino gives a victory speech after defeating Blake Gendebien in the race for the 21st Congressional District on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, in Amsterdam, New York. (Jim Franco/Albany Times Union via Getty Images)

"Actually, do me a favor. This guy is so — he’s looking at me. Kick him out. Just kick him out. Get him out of here. You’re just a rude person. If you want to be tough on me, get out. Go right now. I’m not playing games with you. You deleted two of my interviews … bye bye," Constantino said.

Stefanik, a former GOP conference chair and Trump’s first pick to become U.S. ambassador to the United Nations, was considered by many a rising star within the Republican Party.

Although Stefanik began the confirmation process for the nomination, Trump ultimately rescinded his pick, calling on Stefanik to remain in the House, where Republicans held a razor-thin majority. A few months later, Stefanik announced that she would not pursue reelection or a bid for governor, citing a desire to focus on her family and avoid a primary contest.

Notably, Stefanik endorsed Constantino after the GOP primary for New York’s 21st Congressional District.

"In the coming months, I know Anthony will continue to work hard to earn your support. He will put the people first and will be a strong representative in Washington for the incredible hardworking families in our great district," Stefanik said in a post to social media.

Constantino also secured backing from President Donald Trump.

Constantino made headlines in 2024 when he put up a giant 100-foot "VOTE FOR TRUMP" sign atop his company building.

City officials, including Democratic Mayor Michael Cinquanti, argued the sign violated code because it "presents a dangerous distraction and impacts traffic flow," especially at night when it is illuminated.

After a legal battle, courts ruled that Constantino had a right to keep the sign in place, a decision he called an "epic win for free speech."

Last Tuesday, Constantino narrowly won his race in a 50.9% to 48.8% victory over Gendebien.

During his campaign, Constantino stressed his alignment with Trump, highlighting his record as a business owner, promised to bring reliable cell phone service to northern New York and reduce utility costs, according to his website.

Having secured his congressional race, Constantino will be sworn into office on Jan. 3, 2027, alongside the rest of the 120th Congress.
