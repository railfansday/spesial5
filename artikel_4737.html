Personal computers became appliances in 1977. The internet became a public utility in 1995. The iPhone debuted in 2007. These events meant that life increasingly revolved around screen time, today more than ever.

Tech has replaced a good deal of face-to-face human interaction, causing what some psychologists call “an epidemic of loneliness.” The issue became a public health problem in 2020 when the COVID pandemic enforced extended social isolation to a degree never experienced by those spared solitary confinement. Many studies agree that chronic loneliness aggravates stress, contributes to depression, weakens immunity, dramatically increases risk of heart disease and stroke, and in the elderly, accelerates cognitive decline. But loneliness is only one dimension of the harm inflicted by social isolation. Another is the loss of gentle, affectionate, massage-like touch.

Of course, gentle, loving touch is fundamental to partner sex. Some sexologists call sex “whole-body massage that eventually extends to the genitals.”

With touch deprivation, these compounds don’t get released, and people can’t experience their benefits. When infants don’t receive regular loving touch, when they aren’t cuddled, their little bodies may shut down from what pediatricians call “failure to thrive,” a condition that may prove fatal. Beyond infancy, touch starvation isn’t fatal, but it drives some people quietly crazy.

A great deal of research shows the benefits of gentle touch, and by extension, the harm suffered from touch starvation. The largest and most ambitious study was conducted by German and Dutch researchers who reviewed several decades of touch/massage research involving some 13,000 men, women, and children (Packheiser, 2024). Most of the studies compared subjects who received gentle, massage-style touch for around 20 minutes with those who did not. Some compared people who touched things like kittens or stuffed animals with those who did not. Both touch recipients—and providers—experienced what the researchers called “massive” benefits: “The evidence is clear that touch is beneficial across a large number of physical and mental health conditions, for all ages, and for both healthy individuals and those with diagnosed conditions.”

For all age groups but one, it didn’t matter who administered the massages. The exception was newborns born prematurely. Compared with massages administered by medical personnel, for reasons that remain unclear, those infants benefited more from being touched by their parents.

Massage produced significant benefits for everyone, but the greatest life enhancement accrued to those with diagnosed physical or mental health conditions. The researchers encouraged physicians and mental health professionals to incorporate massage routinely into clinical care.

Vision, hearing, smell, and taste all deteriorate with age. Touch does not. The elderly enjoy as much pleasure from gentle touch as young people.

While gentle touch helps everyone, compared with men, women experience greater benefit. So, it should come as no surprise that women crave the gentle touch and massage of erotic foreplay, often a great deal more of it than many men provide.

The word “foreplay" is problematic. It implies things that happen before the main event, intercourse. Actually, gentle, leisurely, playful mutual massage fits neatly into lovemaking not only before intercourse, but also during and after. Many sexologists prefer the term “loveplay.”

If you’re a heterosexual man, you’re likely to enjoy better sex and get better reviews as a lover if you spend at least 20 minutes kissing, hugging, and gently massaging your partner from head to toe first.

Many sex therapists suggest that couples trade foot massages before getting into bed. Foot massage feels marvelous. But in the review of touch, foot massage did not produce the greatest benefits. That distinction went to massage of the face and scalp. Try five to 10 minutes of massage above the shoulders before you touch much below them. If men do this, by the time the action moves south, their bodies are more likely to function as they wish, and their partners are more likely to display the sexual responsiveness men want.
