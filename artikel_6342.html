Airport lounges can serve as oases from a busy, crowded or outdated terminal. Some lounges participate in membership networks like Priority Pass, while others, like the United Club lounge, are operated by individual airlines.

If you’re a loyal United Airlines flyer or frequent one of the carrier’s largest hubs, visiting a United Club lounge is probably already on your radar. However, the airline made some major access changes in 2025.

Here's what you need to know about accessing United's lounges.

The Chicago-based airline operates a network of standard lounges known as United Clubs, as well as more exclusive Polaris lounges for eligible premium-cabin travelers on select long-haul international, transcontinental and Hawaii routes. Both offer complimentary food, drinks and Wi-Fi, while Polaris lounges feature additional amenities, such as sit-down dining, relaxation areas and shower suites.

So, how can travelers gain access to United's network of lounges?

You'll need a same-day boarding pass to access United lounges. This can be a same-day boarding pass for a United-operated flight or a flight operated by a Star Alliance partner airline, like Lufthansa or Swiss.

United now has two tiers of paid membership for entry to the Club.

This membership offers the primary member unlimited access to all 45-plus United Club locations. There are no discounts available for United Premier members. Individual members can pay to bring guests into the lounge on a one-off basis; a one-time pass costs $59 per guest and is subject to availability.

Individual members do not have access to Star Alliance partner lounges.

Annual fee: Varies depending on Premier status

All Access members can enjoy unlimited United Club access for the primary member and up to two adult guests, or one adult and any dependents under 18.

This membership also includes access to Star Alliance partner lounges and other select partner locations, subject to applicable access policies.

Lifetime United Club members have been converted to All Access memberships.

Unlike a paid Individual membership, a United Club membership earned via a cobranded credit card offers a unique access policy that includes entry for primary cardmembers, one adult guest and any dependents under 18 years old.

Cardmembers can unlock an All Access membership, which increases the guest allowance and provides access to participating Star Alliance partner lounges, by earning Premier Gold status or higher, or by spending $50,000 on eligible purchases with their United Club Card or United Club Business Card in a calendar year.

Since the annual fee on both the United Club Card and the United Club Business Card is $695, having a credit card to enter the lounge remains a more attractive option than outright buying a membership.

Additionally, the United Club Card and the United Club Business Card offer four one-time passes on each account anniversary. A pass can be used by one person accompanying the primary cardholder or an authorized user into the lounge.

If you’d prefer to avoid the hefty annual fee, you can opt for the entry-level United Explorer Card or the United Business Card, both of which include two single-use United Club passes each year. Note that these day passes don’t include guest access, nor can they be transferred to friends or family who aren't authorized users or accompanying a cardmember into the lounge.

Certain MileagePlus Premier and Star Alliance Gold members can access United Clubs when traveling on eligible flights.

United Premier Gold (and higher) members can access United Clubs when traveling internationally on United or another Star Alliance carrier, regardless of their class of service. They can also bring along one guest who's traveling on the same flight.

Travelers who earn Star Alliance Gold status through another member airline can access United Clubs when flying on any eligible Star Alliance flight, including domestic United flights. Again, guests must be traveling on the same flight as the elite member.

United’s invite-only Global Services members can enjoy lounge access on all United-operated flights, including those operating exclusively within the U.S. Note that this new perk only covers the Global Services member — you’ll only be able to bring a guest into the lounge with an All Access membership, eligible cobranded credit card or when flying internationally.

Travelers flying in certain premium cabins on United or its Star Alliance partners can also access United Clubs. However, eligibility and guest privileges vary by airline, route and cabin class, as outlined below.

If you don’t travel enough to justify an annual membership, you can also purchase a one-time United Club pass for $59 per person at United Club locations or through the United mobile app. Just note that day passes don’t guarantee entry when lounge is at capacity.

Travelers using a one-time pass can enter a United Club only up to three hours before their scheduled departure time. (This exclusively applies to departure flights, and there is no time limit for entry on connecting flights.) These one-time passes are not accepted at grab-and-go locations, United Polaris lounges, United Arrivals Lounges or partner lounges.

In addition to United Clubs and Polaris Lounges, the carrier operates a grab-and-go lounge concept called United Club Fly.

The first such space opened at Denver International Airport (DEN) in November 2022, and a second one opened at George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) in 2025. Eligible travelers can enter Club Fly by scanning their boarding pass at the self-service entry gate.

Once inside, you can head to the snack bar that’s stocked with packaged and prepared foods. You can also order a coffee from the barista.

Given Club Fly’s focus on efficiency, the access policies are a bit different. Eligible United Club members and travelers flying in international premium cabins are eligible to enter, but they cannot bring any guests.
