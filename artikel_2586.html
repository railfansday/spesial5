3 Young NBA Teams That Could Benefit From Trading For Dejounte Murray

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The trade buzz surrounding the New Orleans Pelicans only a month into the 2026-27 season is significant. While much of the chatter has been revolving around Trey Murphy III in light of the latest updates concerning his asking price, veteran guard Dejounte Murray has also commanded considerable attention.

Despite his earlier comments, which reflected his commitment to New Orleans, former All-Star guard Dejounte Murray recently requested a trade from the Pelicans in light of their poor start. To that end, moving Murray also seems to be in the team's best interests. Unfortunately, the circumstances aren't as promising.

According to some reports, Murray's trade value is quite low, with teams demanding draft compensation to acquire him. While somewhat understandable, Warriors forward Draymond Green didn't necessarily see things the same way, as he defended Dejounte Murray by highlighting the impact he could have on a young team. As he discussed the events leading up to Murray's trade request on "The Draymond Green Show," he stated:

"Then obviously, you know, I saw the reports and, like, 'Oh, they drafted Jeremiah Fears and Dejounte Murray wasn't happy about that.' That's not something I believe because I've seen Dejounte Murray play the two."

"He played the two with Trae Young. He has been playing the two for quite a while now in his career at times. And so, to act like he'd be happy or jealous about a Jeremiah Fears. I can't foresee that. That's something that I would have to hear come out of the mouth of Dejounte Murray."

"I don't even like how that's painting him as if he's a guy who's going to have a problem with being a vet, as if he's a guy that if you got a young point guard, you can't bring him in. If you got a young point guard, Dejounte Murray is the guy you actually want to bring in."

When speaking about Murray's dynamic with young point guards, Green highlighted how the guard's lifestyle choices position him as a great influence for younger players. Despite this, the 30-year-old is being painted in a negative light.

Theoretically, Green's theory about Murray holds, especially for young teams. When factoring in the rumors linking him with the Phoenix Suns, this makes even more sense.

But even though the fit is promising, given the Suns' latest demands for a first-round pick and a first-round pick swap to be packaged with Murray, there is enough evidence to suggest that this would not be a good move. On that note, we look at three young teams that could benefit from trading for him.

Charlotte Hornets Receive: Dejounte Murray

New Orleans Pelicans Receive: Grayson Allen, Grant Williams, 2028 first-round pick (top-14 protected)

Among the first teams that could truly benefit from acquiring another veteran guard are the Charlotte Hornets, who embraced a rebuild around their young core this offseason.

On paper, the Hornets' guard depth seems quite solid, with Coby White and Dennis Schroder shoring up the point guard rotation. Even so, given Schroder's recent injury and Charlotte's desperate need for a reliable backup, pursuing a player like Murray, who is averaging 13.4 points, 4.6 rebounds, 4.4 assists, and 1.6 steals per game, would seem worthwhile.

With a salary of $32.7 million this season, formulating a trade package for Dejounte Murray would require the Hornets to package Grayson Allen ($18.1 million) and Grant Williams ($14.2 million, expiring) to match salaries. Theoretically, this would allow New Orleans not only to shed salary but also gain future cap flexibility. But there is a way to make this trade even more enticing for the Pelicans.

The Hornets are in possession of a $40.7 million trade exception after parting ways with LaMelo Ball this offseason. By using this to absorb Murray's salary, Charlotte could effectively get this deal done without moving any player assets, though it may require them to send more draft capital. With the opportunity to gain multiple draft picks for no incoming salary, the Pelicans are more likely to entertain this offer.

Brooklyn Nets Receive: Dejounte Murray, 2028 first-round pick swap

New Orleans Pelicans Receive: Terance Mann, Keon Ellis, 2028 second-round pick (ATL)

Unlike the Hornets, the Nets aren't necessarily a team with noteworthy point guard depth. Although the backcourt duo of Egor Demin and Mikel Brown Jr. is promising, they are clearly a work in progress. In accordance with Draymond Green's statement, this could be the perfect place for a player like Murray.

At 30, Murray can play the role of mentor to both Demin and Brown, aiding the development of the Nets' backcourt of the future. In the short-term, the former All-Star guard can also help boost the team's overall roster strength by serving as a connecting piece between Michael Porter Jr. and Julius Randle.

Given the Nets' 3-11 record, adding a player like Murray could help make them a more competitive team. In light of their latest loss, the franchise may see more appeal in this approach.

To acquire Dejounte Murray, Brooklyn could package Terance Mann ($15.0 million) and Keon Ellis ($9.0 million) with a second-round pick. This would also give them enough cap space to absorb Murray's cap hit without giving up too much of their potential cap flexibility next summer once Porter Jr.'s $40.8 million contract expires.

Oklahoma City Thunder Receive: Dejounte Murray, 2028 first-round pick swap

New Orleans Pelicans Receive: Nikola Topic, 2029 first-round pick (top-10 protected)

An unlikely but intriguing landing spot for Dejounte Murray in a trade scenario would be the Oklahoma City Thunder. While they aren't a "young" team by definition, the Thunder's core is on the younger side. Additionally, the Thunder's need to replace some of the injured players on the roster could make this move seem even more appealing.

Theoretically, a player like Dejounte Murray would serve as a replacement for Cason Wallace. While he lacks Wallace's perimeter shooting ability, shown by his 29.6% shooting from beyond the arc this season, Murray's defensive effort and offensive upside could see him emerge as a contributor for the team.

On the face of it, acquiring Murray poses a financial challenge due to OKC's current cap situation. Although this is concerning, the Thunder possess roughly $29.0 million in trade exceptions following their offseason moves. Hence, by packaging Nikola Topic ($5.4 million) and a protected first-round pick, OKC would not only add a reliable veteran guard, but it would also shed approximately $1.7 million in salary.

To some extent, this move may be unlikely, primarily because the Thunder already acquired Nique Clifford. Nonetheless, given what Murray brings to the table, there is reason to believe he could be a valuable addition to the team's bench.
