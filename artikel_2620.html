Waterford heading to boys soccer CIAC final for first time since 1987

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

DEEP RIVER - Waterford boys soccer coach Jeff Emerich was grooving when he heard "Jump" by Kris Kross hip-hopping over the Valley Regional PA at halftime of Tuesday night's CIAC Class M semifinal between his No. 1-seeded Lancers and No. 5 Daniel Hand.

Emerich knew, too, that if his boys executed a little more criss-cross on the pitch, they'd be jumping Waterford boys soccer into a state final for the first time since 1987, about five years before Chris "Mac Daddy" Kelly and Chris "Daddy Mac" Smith arrived on the scene.

Yeah, it all goes back a ways, and it's funny what stands the test of time.

This Waterford boys soccer team of 2026 is proving to be one for the ages. With a 2-0 victory over Hand on Tuesday night, in a game that showcased the breadth of their remarkable skill set, the Lancers are one step away from the first-ever boys soccer championship in Waterford history.

Brady Wiseman scored off a left-side cross from Brayden Michonski two minutes into the second half and Jaydev Villa, set up by an inexorable upfield rush by Yaman Qarqour, added insurance with 20:35 to play as Waterford improved to 19-1-1 and advances to this weekend's state championship at Trinity Health Stadium in Hartford to face No. 2 Weston, which dethroned reigning champ Tolland 1-0 in Tuesday's other Class M semi.

"It's been since 1987 since the last time we made it in, so this is our first chance to go up and compete," Emerich said. "We've never won a state title, and it's been 39 years since we've seen a chance, so we are very humbled that we are able to do this, and we continue to work, and that's the thing that makes these boys special."

The Lancers got an emotional boost on Monday when they learned goalie Luke Wilson was cleared from a concussion suffered in the ECC tournament semifinals against Bacon Academy. Wilson set the tone Tuesday night by charging out and sliding to break up a Hand scoring chance in the opening minute for the first of his 16 saves.

"I didn't even know if I was going to be able to play this game, especially last week, watching when we played Lewis Mills (in the quarterfinals), I was still nervous," Wilson said. "All I wanted to do was get back on the field."

Wilson wore a foam helmet for the first time Tuesday night. The rest was very much the same look for a Waterford team that has now scored 94 goals and allowed just 13 on the season.

The first half was fairly even, with both teams generating scoring chances. In the second half, the pitch decidedly tilted Waterford's way. Once Wiseman converted that cross from Michonski, a stiff challenge for Hand grew steeper. The Tigers, the last SCC team in the boys soccer state tourney, ended their season at 16-5-1.

"I thought our team came out and executed our game plan in the first half really, really well," said Hand coach Mike Shirley. "It was always going to be a game decided by one-versus-one moments, and we knew we had to defend them on the edges, and it was just about, 'Can we find our opportunities and put them in the back of the net before they can?'"

The Tigers could not, and with it passed their chance to return to the state finals for the first time since the grand slam championship run of 2016-2019.

Dates and times for all state soccer finals will be announced later this week.

Luke Wilson. Cleared from a concussion just one day before the game, Waterford's senior goalie opened the night with a sliding, charging stop to thwart a Hand scoring chance and punctuated the victory punching out a final shot in the final minute. Wilson had 16 saves in all.

"Kudos to Waterford because they have some extremely talented players, and they also played a quality game " - Hand coach Mike Shirley.
