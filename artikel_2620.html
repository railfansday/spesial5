Brian Schottenheimer has been unable to conquer this team problem

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Penalties seem as old as time with the Dallas Cowboys. From 2020 to 2024, under Mike McCarthy, the Cowboys ranked first in the NFL with 570 penalties. It became a consistent talking point every Sunday with McCarthy at the helm and has continued under Brian Schottenheimer.

Penalties aren't always the defining trait that makes a bad team. The Houston Texans are second with 195 penalties since 2025, and the Denver Broncos are tied for third with 193. The combined record for both of those teams is 36-16. It's the type of penalties the Cowboys have been getting—emblematic of what the last few years under Schottenheimer have felt like.

On defense, it's the holding calls or pass interference calls on a critical third-and-long that keep them on the field and the drive moving for the opposing offense. Dallas is tied with the Green Bay Packers for the NFL lead in automatic first downs (9) given via penalty on third down. The Cowboys also lead all teams with 22 penalties resulting in an automatic first down for opponents.

The most defining penalty of the season—and maybe the culture of the entire organization—came versus the Indianapolis Colts, when DeMarvion Overshown ran out on the field as an inactive player. The Cowboys still led 20-17 at that point, but the 15-yard penalty set them back, and the offense ended up punting back to Indy, who tied the game on the next possession.

Then, late in the fourth quarter against the San Francisco 49ers, George Pickens got a taunting penalty as the offense is trying to score quickly and avoid wasting time off the clock. Instead of being at the 49ers' 19-yard line, they got moved back to the 28. It took another six plays and a precious 42 seconds off the clock.

Again, there are many reasons why the Cowboys sit at a 4-6 record. Penalties aren't the only reason they are on the outside looking in as the playoff race starts to take shape. However, penalties signal an undisciplined team, and being the league leader over the last two seasons is starting to negatively define the culture Schottenheimer is building in Dallas.
