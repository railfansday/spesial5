Jimmy Carter’s Family Farm Just Sold for $8.5 Million

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Georgia property will become part of a nearly 7,000-acre wildlife management area, with public access possible as early as 2027.

President Jimmy Carter’s family farmland near Plains, Georgia, was just sold to the Conservation Fund as part of an approximately $8.5 million acquisition that will protect nearly 2,500 acres of fields and woodland. Announced November 9, the deal sets the stage for the land’s eventual transfer to the state of Georgia, with public access potentially beginning as early as 2027.

The purchase encompasses approximately 1,600 acres owned by the Carter family, along with an adjoining tract. It does not include Carter’s boyhood home and farm, which are already preserved by the National Park Service as part of Jimmy Carter National Historical Park.

Instead, the newly acquired property is slated to join the nearby Lanahassee Creek Wildlife Management Area, expanding it to nearly 7,000 acres. The land provides habitat for gopher tortoises and other wildlife, while neighboring creeks support rare freshwater mussels. Plans call for the Georgia Department of Natural Resources to take ownership in 2028.

The acquisition also continues restoration work Carter initiated himself. At his request, the family preserved a stand of mature longleaf pine and replanted much of the surrounding acreage with the same native species. Once used to grow crops, including peanuts, the property became a retreat where he and Rosalynn spent time away from public life. The family acquired the land around the turn of the 20th century, according to the Conservation Fund.

“They liked to go and walk the land,” Blake Sullivan, who managed Carter’s farm and timberland for more than four decades, told The Washington Post. “For both of them, it was a place of rest and renewal.”

Carter continued visiting into his final years, arranging to be driven through the property when walking became difficult. Amy Carter, the president’s daughter, told The Post, “I think his very favorite place was to be in the town and on the land where he grew up, and where he had those very deep roots.”
