2028 Scion Kei-01 First Drive: Mite Makes Right

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

We spent a little bit of time driving a short distance in a tiny pickup that’ll be sold in small numbers.

We haven’t timed one, but eyeballing it, the Scion Kei-01 should reach its top speed before the 1250-hp Chevy Corvette ZR1X reaches 100 mph. (You’re welcome, Toyota marketing team.) Of course, that’s because America’s newest and cutest truck is limited to just 25 mph, per the feds’ FMVSS No. 500 regulation that allows the Kei-01 to be sold here. Toyota is assembling and selling these rebadged Daihatsu Hijets in the United States starting next year, and we got to take one for a short drive—which will be most drives in a pickup this small—to see whether it lives up to the hype.

First, we had to fight our instinct to get into the driver’s seat from the left side. The Kei-01 is right-hand drive, like the Hijet sold in Japan, because Toyota wanted to bring it to the United States as quickly and inexpensively as possible. Production models will get a different backup camera to meet federal requirements, but beyond that, the U.S. version is largely unchanged from the Daihatsu sold in Japan.

Every Kei-01 sold here, mainly to fleets, will have an extended cab, which affords extra cargo space for a shotgun or your largest bushel of chewing tobacco. Or both. The fixed steering column doesn’t tilt or telescope, but stovepipe-hat enthusiasts—and it’s been a while since they’ve heard this—have reason to celebrate, as the one thing this truck does big is headroom.

The Kei-01’s “jumbo” cab is why it stands just 2.9 inches lower in height than an F-150, but it’s a lot easier to get into. You barely have to lift a foot to climb in. A portion of the footwell is painted metal, and your feet are ahead of the front wheel wells. The front axle is practically beneath your butt.

Unlike the workaday pickups you can rent from Home Depot and Menards, this truck, with its massive screen and power windows, is an ergonomic miracle compared with single-cab full-size rentals. Remember when steering wheels were only responsible for turning left and right and occasionally honking the horn? The Kei-01’s wheel is like that. Except the horn’s high-pitched beep is too humiliating to use in anger.

Your fingertips on the steering wheel are so close to the front of the vehicle that they could almost be the hood ornament. Which is certainly novel and makes it super easy to nose in close to objects. Though the lack of a crumple zone aft of your knuckles is a concern that flashes through your mind.

Around the little dirt loop, the Kei-01 offers a driving feel that’s akin to a riding lawn mower. The steering takes nearly 4.5 turns lock-to-lock, but the Kei-01 is still stupid-easy to maneuver. There’s a rawness about scooting around in it that makes you feel like you’re getting paid hourly. This workhorse may be at a speed disadvantage compared to most of the electric scooters in our Gear Team’s roundup, but try hauling hay with one of those.

Underneath all that adorable sheetmetal is a 660-cc three-cylinder engine that sounds about as smooth as a Honda Grom. It’s paired with a continuously variable automatic transmission. Despite its goofy proportions, the Kei-01 isn’t as front-heavy as it looks. With the engine mounted near the middle of the truck, this little box on wheels feels balanced. It lacks a dedicated transfer case, but its four-wheel-drive system has an electronic lock mode to add a bit more confidence on hill climbs.

The best part might be the roughly five-and-a-half-foot bed. Its low load floor makes for a perfect bench, and all three bedsides fold down. Lower the tailgate, or either side, or all three. It’s brilliantly simple and quick. There’s no tall bedside to reach over, and you don’t have to climb into the bed to reach stuff. The truck can handle 600 pounds of cargo too. Seriously, why can’t full-size pickup beds offer this kind of flexibility?

We spent only a short time driving the little Kei-01, but even limited to 25 mph, it’s still rather compelling. And, unlike the used kei trucks imported from Japan, the Scion has a factory warranty. The big unknown still hanging over this small truck is the price, which Toyota hasn’t announced yet. When all you need is a little truck, without most of the drawbacks of a big one, the Kei-01 certainly fits.
