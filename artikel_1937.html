Tim Sherwood is wrong, Michael Carrick is perfect for vacant Championship manager's job

Michael Carrick could certainly make a swift return to management after being sacked by Manchester United just a few days ago.

The 45-year-old managed just 15 games in charge as permanent head coach before the hierarchy decided enough was enough at Old Trafford.

Everyone in the crowd is well aware that the real problem lies amongst the owners, rather than those in the dugout, but a switch of leadership appears unlikely.

Nonetheless, Carrick is now without a job once again, and a return to the Championship could be on the cards after the latest developments.

Both Blackburn Rovers and Swansea City are on the search for new managers.

Tony Mowbray has sadly been forced to step down at Blackburn due to health reasons, and Vitor Matos has decided to join Strasbourg in Ligue 1.

And it is the vacant job at the Swansea.com Stadium that could require Carrick's expertise, especially given his experience with Middlesbrough.

Speaking on Sky Sports and being asked if fans will see him return to a dugout soon, Tim Sherwood said: "I don't think we will. It kills you.

"And you have a sick feeling in your stomach, maybe I could have done this better or that better. But I think Michael will be quite content."

Even the bookies think a move to Swansea City would be unlikely, pricing him at 20/1 to take over, while Rob Edwards is priced at 2/1 as the favourite.

But much weirder things have happened, and the Swans will want to keep up their chase in the battle for the Championship title this season.

Swansea have done some great things under Matos in the early stages of the season, and were even leading the way at one point in the campaign.

Results have stagnated, seeing them drop to sixth in the table, but they're still only three points off league leaders Birmingham City.

Carrick's desire to play out from the back, possession-based play and pressing approach would suit the Swans perfectly.

He also knows the division well and is good at developing young and upcoming talents, but the owners would be making a huge decision by going for him.

Carrick is unlikely to come cheap, so the hierarchy need to make sure that going for the former Man United boss is the right call this season.
