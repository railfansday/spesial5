GTA VI didn't break the internet, thanks to 'stupid DNS tricks'

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Multiple CDNs and routing users to nearby download sites kept traffic flowing for everyone

Grand Theft Auto VI may put a dent in global productivity today but the game didn’t break the internet.

For those unfamiliar, GTA VI is the first new instalment of the mega-hit game in 13 years and it is nearly impossible to understate the excitement surrounding its release. Anecdotal evidence suggests fans of the game may today report they suddenly feel unfit to perform paid work.

Developer Rockstar Games allowed Xbox and PlayStation owners to pay for and download the 200GB-plus game on November 10, before play commenced on the 19th.

Millions took advantage of the chance to download the game, creating just the sort of internet traffic event that regional internet registry the Réseaux IP Européens Network Coordination Centre (RIPE NCC) can observe with its RIPE Atlas internet measurement network.

RIPE data scientist Emile Aben, with help from staffers Alun Davies and James Cowie, went looking for GTA-related traffic and found some interesting patterns.

Aben observed that traffic to PlayStations mostly came from content delivery networks operated by AWS and Fastly. Microsoft relied on Fastly for over half of traffic flowing to Xboxes and used Akamai too, but eschewed AWS's CloudFront.

The data scientist observed heavy use of “GeoDNS,” a technique that returns different IP addresses for a single domain request, depending on a user’s location. DNS’s designers didn’t intend that. Indeed, the RIPE trio point out that Paul Vixie, the computer scientist who earned his place in the Internet Hall of Fame for massive contributions to the DNS system, regarded things like GeoDNS as “stupid DNS tricks.”

Whatever you think of GeoDNS, the RIPE researchers found that folks who were likely downloading GTA VI from Los Angeles, Tokyo, and Istanbul were each directed to different sites.

Their probe of a network in Shanghai, China, found “a very distinct long lookup-chain with a hostname that is associated with the Alibaba Cloud in the end that maps to six different IP addresses.”

“This hints at specialised treatment for some countries/regions,” Aben wrote.

He also noted that Internet Exchange Points, the interconnect facilities that network operators use to exchange traffic and shorten paths, had to cope with record traffic during the download period – and seem to have handled the load.

Aben thinks the GTA VI download program offers a useful lesson.

“DNS helps steer each player towards a download server, while multiple CDNs share the work of delivering the files,” he wrote. “That distribution matters beyond gaming. On the open Internet, game downloads share routers and link capacity with all kinds of other traffic, including critical services.”

“Keeping downloads local helps leave capacity on long-distance links free for other uses, making that enormous demand manageable.” ®
