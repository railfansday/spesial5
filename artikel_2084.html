Shock Tom Brady, Chelsea twist emerges after Birmingham City exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The seven-times winning NFL star Tom Brady divested his interest in Birmingham City just a couple of weeks ago, but it's now being reported that he's already set to re-enter English football.

The high-profile involvement of former NFL quarterback Tom Brady was a real headline-grabber, when the Knighthead Capital group, headed by Tom Wagner, took control of Birmingham City in 2023.

Over the three and a half years since then, the Blues have been on quite a rollercoaster ride. They were relegated into League One at the end of their first season in charge, but then bounced back as champions with a record-breaking 111 points.

Last season saw Birmingham finish in 10th place in the Championship, but as clubs return from the November international break they sit at the top of the division ahead of Middlesbrough and Stoke City.

But if Birmingham are going to end this season by returning to the Premier League following an absence of 16 years, they'll be doing so without Brady, who relinquished his shareholding in the club earlier in November. But Brady's interest in investing in football doesn't seem to have gone anywhere, with fresh reporting now suggesting that he could be set to buy a small stake in Premier League giants Chelsea.

The Guardian reports on Friday afternoon that Tom Brady is in negotiations with Chelsea over buying a small stake in the Premier League club. Sources have told them that this is intended to "to pave the way for investing at Stamford Bridge."

Brady is understood to have a good relationship with Behdad Eghbali, whose fund Clearlake Capital own 86.5% of Chelsea after former co-investors Todd Boehly and Mark Walter sold their shares in the London club in September.

When Brady sold up his stake in Birmingham City, the club issued a statement thanking him for his involvement at their club: "Birmingham City Football Club expresses its sincere gratitude to Tom Brady for his involvement with and unwavering support for the Club over the last three years."

The club went on to add, "It was truly amazing to have someone with Tom’s experience and passion associated with the Club, and the Club looks forward to continuing to build on that foundation from the training ground to the Birmingham community."

The NFL icon severed his ties with the Championship leaders on Monday evening

The departure of Tom Brady comes at the start of what could turn out to be the most exciting period in the history of Birmingham City. The club unveiled their plans for a new 62,000-capacity stadium as part of a 'sports quarter' in November 2025, and the club hope to be in their new home for the start of the 2030-31 season.

And things are getting interesting for the team, as well. Going into the international break, a run of six wins in seven games had lifted Chris Davies' team to top spot in the Championship, a symbolic sign of their ongoing progress since Knighthead took ownership of them, just over three years ago.

Chelsea have refused to comment on this story, but the appeal to Brady isn't a huge surprise, given the events of the summer of 2025. When Chelsea won the FIFA Club World Cup in July of that year, he was reported to have joined the Chelsea squad for a private party to celebrate their win.

While involved with Birmingham, Brady was appointed the chair of their new advisory board, and his time there wasn't without controversy. He earned headlines after criticising former manager Wayne Rooney, with the former England star replying to the criticism by saying that, "I don’t think he really understood football."

Brady's involvement at St Andrew's ensured huge publicity for the club at the time of the takeover, but his actual shareholding in the club was only 3.3%. With the team top of the Championship table and the planning and infrastructure work continuing apace for the new stadium, Birmingham City supporters should be able to rest easy in the knowledge that his departure from their club is unlikely to temper the ambition of the club's majority owners.
