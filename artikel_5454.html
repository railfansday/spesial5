Democrats are preparing multiple probes of Trump's Venezuela oil deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

House and Senate committees are laying groundwork to examine the 35% U.S. stake in North American Blue Energy Partners

Congressional Democrats are lining up several investigations of the Trump administration’s agreement with Alejandro Betancourt’s North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP), according to Bloomberg. Democrats on panels including the House Oversight Committee and the Senate Armed Services Committee have started preparing for those inquiries.

The agreement, announced in August, would hand NABEP a century-long concession covering 17 oil fields in Venezuela, while the U.S. would receive a 35% ownership share of the company, according to Bloomberg. The U.S. would be entitled to buy a fifth of what the fields pump, priced at cost.

Rep. Robert Garcia, a California Democrat expected to take the helm of the House Oversight Committee next year, said the aim is to establish the facts. “We want to go in there and find out exactly what happened,” Garcia told Bloomberg. He added that Democrats also want to pass legislation to prevent any abuse in the future.

Sen. Jack Reed, a Rhode Island Democrat and the top Democrat on the Senate Armed Services Committee, said his panel is among those planning investigations. “If we went in there with Maduro as an excuse, but the real goal is to take oil, that is to me wrong,” Reed told Bloomberg. Sen. Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat set to chair the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, said he is concerned about “the potential for corruption.” A spokesperson for Sen. Martin Heinrich, a New Mexico Democrat expected to take over the Senate energy panel next year, said he plans to keep overseeing the deal, according to Bloomberg.

Following their midterm election victories, Democrats in both chambers will have the authority to summon administration officials for testimony, demand records and, if needed, issue subpoenas, according to Bloomberg. Their questions center on the contract's provisions, the compensation the U.S. extended to NABEP for its equity stake and the mechanism for supervising oil revenue. Democrats have also faulted the administration for keeping the text under wraps and questioned Betancourt's role, citing money-laundering and corruption probes that have targeted him in the U.S., Spain and Switzerland. Betancourt denies any wrongdoing and has faced no charges.

House Natural Resources Committee Democrats intend to carry on an inquiry launched earlier in the year, a staffer on the committee told Bloomberg. The Senate Foreign Relations Committee is also expected to launch an investigation.

Four ranking Senate Democrats, including Reed and Heinrich, had challenged the legality of the Pentagon's plan to take a 35% stake in NABEP in a letter to top administration officials. They demanded the full text of the deal before it was finalized. House Democrats sent a similar letter.

White House spokesperson Taylor Rogers defended the deal. "This deal is a major win for both the United States and Venezuela," Rogers said, according to Bloomberg. "If Democrats choose to pursue sham investigations over commonsense victories, they are choosing to work against the American people."

The administration has argued the deal would cut gasoline prices for Americans and give the government a route to restocking the strategic oil reserve, according to Bloomberg. A NABEP spokesperson told Bloomberg taxpayers paid nothing for the U.S. stake and that U.S. citizens must hold most of the seats on NABEP's board.
