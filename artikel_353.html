MAHA PAC spent more on steak, luxury club than candidates, report shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Expert called MAHA’s spending “pretty unusual” for a PAC.

In the wake of the Make America Healthy Again movement’s midterm wipeout, a top leader suggested the heavy losses came down to being outspent by wealthier, more powerful rivals. But a Stat report on MAHA’s own spending in the run-up to the election raises questions about whether the group’s priority was ever winning elections in the first place.

The dive into the financial documents reveals that the MAHA PAC, led by Tony Lyons, spent more on allies, boosters, meals, luxuries, and its affiliated nonprofit than it did on political candidates.

The findings are in stark contrast to an announcement from Lyons in the spring that he was heading a $100 million fundraiser to boost MAHA’s clout in the midterms. In reality, MAHA PAC took in $3.2 million. During the election cycle, the PAC spent more than $10.5 million, with over $7 million coming from money it had collected previously.

According to Stat’s findings, of the $10.5 million the MAHA PAC spent on the midterms, less than a tenth—roughly $1 million—went to messaging on political candidates, which are expenses called “independent expenditures.”

Andrew Mayersohn, a committees researcher at OpenSecrets, called the spending “pretty unusual,” explaining that “Major super PACs spend most of their money on independent expenditures.”

In some cases, the scant political spending was dwarfed by spending on luxuries. For instance, in a key House race in Northern California, MAHA supported Kevin Kiley over his political rival, pediatrician Richard Pan. Despite calling the race “the most direct ideological confrontation” between MAHA and its opposition “anywhere on the 2026 ballot,” the PAC only spent $49,900 to back Kiley. That’s a little less than the $50,100 it spent at Mastro’s Steakhouse, an upscale restaurant that offers $110 porterhouse steaks in Washington, DC. Further, it’s less than a third of the $155,900 the PAC spent at Ned’s Club, a luxury club near the White House that has become a popular gathering spot among Trump officials.

Kiley lost the election to Pan by double digits.

Of the roughly $1 million spent on political candidates, most of it—over $700,000—went to defeating Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-La.). Many other MAHA-endorsed candidates received $50,000 or less. Some got less than $2,000.

Outside of steaks and a private club, the MAHA PAC spent $500,000 on a media company, Merit Street Media, run by Phil McGraw, who hosted a MAHA special featuring Dr. Phil. MAHA leaders used PAC money to cover meals, payroll, and credit card bills. The PAC paid $275,000 to a security firm, Gavin de Becker & Associates, which is led by an ally of anti-vaccine Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., MAHA’s top leader. Tens of thousands more went to LLCs that have little to no online information.

On the other hand, prominent online MAHA influencers also pocketed tens of thousands from the PAC, as did writers for The MAHA Report, a Substack that promotes Kennedy and MAHA interests. In the days after the midterms, as members of the movement were coming to grips with the election losses, The MAHA Report chose to publish a lengthy article defending convicted rapist Russel Brand, a Kennedy ally who became a MAHA fixture.

The biggest chunk of the MAHA PAC’s spending—more than $3 million—went to the nonprofit MAHA Action, which is also run by Lyons. It’s unclear how all that money was spent.
