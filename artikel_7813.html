Second Amendment Roundup: Major New Film "Firearms and the AR-15 Rifle"

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

The mechanics and features of America’s leading rifle are explained for the bench and bar.

"Firearms and the AR-15 Rifle: A Primer for the Bench and Bar" is the title of a new and important film designed to help judges and legal professionals understand the mechanics and features of modern firearms, including the AR-15 semiautomatic rifle. The film is available on YouTube and on Rumble. Following my brief introduction, firearms expert Richard Vasquez takes the center stage.  After 21 years in the Marine Corps as an advanced gunsmith and ordnance officer, Mr. Vasquez served for 14 years in the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF), including time within the Firearms Technology Branch.  He is now a firearms consultant.

This is a critical time to inform the bench and the bar about the operation of the AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles, given the ongoing legal fights over modern semiautomatic firearms including the pending Supreme Court cases of Viramontes v. Cook County and Grant v. Higgins.  This film sticks to the facts about the operations and mechanics of modern firearms without touching upon any of the legal or political debates about the AR-15. It does a particularly good job explaining the various features found on most AR-15 rifles and does a great job explaining the differences between semiautomatic firearms and other types of modern firearms, including fully-automatic machine guns.

The film was written, directed and produced by law professor and Second Amendment scholar Mark W. Smith, who was most recently seen discussing firearms and the American Revolution on the Federalist Society annual convention panel "When in the Course of Human Events" (at 22:30).

The film begins with a historical overview of the development of firearms, from single-shot flintlocks to today's semiautomatics. The film describes the basic components of firearms and rifles, in particular.  It explains what makes gas-operated semiautomatic rifles different from lever action, bolt action and pump action rifles.  The film then goes on to show in great detail how a gas-operated semiautomatic rifle operates.

Spoiler alert:  I suspect that one of the film's biggest contributions to the public discourse on semiautomatic firearms will be its easy-to-understand graphics and animations. Thanks to many colorful images, video clips, animations as well as Vasquez's break downs of modern firearm mechanics and operations, I suspect this film will become a valuable teaching tool for people interested in firearms including judges and legal professionals.

Getting to the technical aspects, Mr. Vasquez explains the AR-15 firing mechanism, beginning with the insertion of a cartridge into the chamber, pulling of the trigger, activation of the sear, release of the bolt and firing pin, striking of the primer, explosion of the powder, discharge of the bullet, bleeding of gas causing the bolt carrier group to retract, elimination of the spent cartridge, and insertion of a new cartridge from the magazine to the chamber, thus beginning a new firing cycle.  Easy to follow diagrams of each process in that order simplify and bring to life what happens each step of the way.

Next, Mr. Vasquez moves onto the outward features typical of the AR-15 platform.  The telescoping stock allows the shoulder stock to be adjusted for different statures, such as two spouses, or seasonal clothing, like an overcoat while hunting.  The pistol grip and straight-line stock reduce recoil and muzzle jump, allowing a second, more accurate shot.  The handguard (barrel shroud) with Picatinny Rail facilitates various attachments like iron sights, scopes, and flashlights.  A threaded barrel can be equipped with a flash suppressor to reduce blindness when firing at night or with a muzzle brake to lesson recoil.

Two aspects of the operational process are worth emphasis.  First, nothing about the AR-15 rifle itself dictates the number of rounds it will fire before needing to reinsert a new magazine.  That depends on the magazine, which may hold 5, 10, 20, or 30 rounds.  Second, while firing a shot automatically loads a new cartridge into the chamber, firing the next shot requires that the trigger be pulled again.  That distinguishes a semiautomatic from a fully automatic, aka a machine gun, which requires only the trigger to be depressed and held down to fire all rounds in the magazine.

It's my belief that "Firearms and the AR-15 Rifle: A Primer for the Bench and Bar" will become the definitive documentary on the mechanics of the AR-15 and modern semiautomatic rifles.

Stephen Halbrook is a Senior Fellow with the Independent Institute. His latest books are America's Rifle: The Case for the AR-15 and The Right to Bear Arms: A Constitutional Right of the People or a Privilege of the Ruling Class? He is also author of The Founders' Second Amendment, Securing Civil Rights, Gun Control in the Third Reich, and Gun Control in Nazi-Occupied France. He has written over 30 law review articles and has testified in Congressional hearings on firearm law bills. He has also litigated extensively in the field, often representing groups such as the NRA, National African American Gun Association, Western States Sheriffs' Association, Congress of Racial Equality, National Association of Criminal Defense Lawyers, and more. He has argued before the U.S. Supreme Court in Castillo v. U.S. (2000), Printz v. U.S. (1997), and U.S. v. Thompson/Center Arms Co. (1992), as well as in front of many other courts.  In D.C. v.
