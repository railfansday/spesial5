SHT Day 14: The Nearly Unfortunate Incident

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Last night was my final night spent in my tent. For the remainder of this year’s SHT adventure I will be sleeping in my camper. I heard coyotes at one point in the early evening, and also a train all night long. It didn’t blare its horn all the time, but I definitely heard the constant hum of the motor.

I knew I had shorter mileage today, with my husband likely arriving later in the afternoon, so I was yet again in no hurry to leave camp. I stretched out and took off around 9:20. The morning was a quiet, lonely hike, passing by Beaver Pond and Fault Line campsites without seeing anyone.

The farther north I go, the more the trees have changed color.

I had planned to unpack my stove and take a legitimate coffee break at Fault Line, but opted instead to just shake up the Trader Joe’s instant coffee with cold water when I filtered up at a stream. I do love a hot coffee on a cold morning, but I can be kinda lazy and I prefer not to stop for long once I’m in a groove. I definitely could have fired up the stove before I left camp, but I already had my breakfast shake. Besides, having this “cold brew” mid-morning was a nice jolt and change from plain water.

The trail did some steep ups and downs through what seemed like prime moose habitat before descending into the town of Beaver Bay. I crossed Lax Lake Road and kept going. I had service and talked to Bill (the husband), who said his ETA would just about match mine at the trailhead outside the next town of Silver Bay, another three or four trail miles for me.

This is one of my favorite pics. It would be top ten if there was a moose down there!

After Lax Lake Road I started to see more people. I passed by the Beaver River campsites, the second of which would have been an amazing spot to stay. Instead, I had L&L here, and stayed just long enough to appreciate its beauty. The latrine seemed private enough, as it was set back in the woods, away from the river and tent spots. Just after I finished my business, I heard an ATV coming closer. Turns out there was a trail, mere feet behind the latrine! I finished in the nick of time!

The driver didn’t seem to notice me, or at least knew the trail well enough to expect someone may be on the throne and look away. This is the second time I’ve noticed a latrine well within sight of passers-by. The first was at East Gooseberry Campsite, where it was I who happened to see an unfortunate person doing their business on the latrine, as it was not very hidden at all. I screeched a quick “I’m so sorry!” as I looked away, feeling terrible for the person caught with their literal pants down. Let’s just say some latrine spots out here are better than others.

Day hikers took my picture on the Beaver River bridge.

The view from near one of the Beaver River campsites.

After the close call, I headed on. The trail crossed a few roads and came very close to the golf course in Silver Bay. It also briefly followed the Gitchi-Gami trail, a well-established and paved trail for non-motorized use. There were a few final steep climbs and descents over rocky cliffs when I suddenly saw Bill on trail! He came up to meet me about a quarter mile from the Penn Boulevard trailhead. We finished the steep downhill together and crossed Penn Boulevard to reach the massive trailhead, where the sweet sight of our truck and camper was waiting for me.

The Gitchi-Gami crossing under the train tracks.

Bill had an electrolyte drink ready for me, and I stretched while we talked trail news. This trailhead, the largest I’ve seen for the SHT so far, is just on the outskirts of Silver Bay. It must be popular!

Now we’re off to Lamb’s Resort, near the town of Schroeder, for the next few days. This campground will serve as our first home base as I continue to tick off trail miles as a series of day hikes. Having completed nearly 100 miles so far, I’m ready for a zero day, a shower, and a bed with a snuggly electric blanket.
