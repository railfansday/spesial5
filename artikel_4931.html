8 Sweat-Wicking Workout Shirts That We Keep Reaching for

These editor-tested tops deliver on comfort, fit, and breathability.

Working up a sweat feels great, but staying drenched in a sweaty shirt post-workout really doesn’t. That’s where moisture-wicking shirts designed to handle your sweat sessions come in. No pit stains or clammy skin here! These workout tops are made with breathable, workout-focused fabrics like nylon, cotton, or polyester that perform as well as they look, even when you’re putting in some serious work in the gym.

I’ve tested tons of moisture-wicking shirts over the years—from long-line sports bras to the best workout tanks, and I called in more than 30 options from my favorite brands and tested them for this story. I tested them during sweaty workouts, walking workouts, and even on errand days. After months of testing, I, along with a few other fitness experts, chose options that checked off all our boxes.

Many got ditched along the way, but these eight moisture-wicking tops made the final cut as the ones that received the highest marks across categories like breathability, water-wicking abilities, fit, sizing options, colors, and more.

One of my favorite things about this shirt is how well it hides sweat. We’ve put this shirt through the wringer—running, yoga, lifting, HIIT—and it always holds up. It never feels clingy, gives you room to move without any added bulk, and has reflective elements for better visibility during late-night runs.

Another big plus: the waist-length cut offers great coverage, so you don’t have to worry about it riding up when you’re bending, stretching, or running. It doesn’t come with built-in support, so just pair it with your favorite sports bra and you’re good to go.

Most technical fabrics have specific washing and drying instructions, and if you want to maximize your workout wardrobe’s function and durability, it’s a good idea to follow those instructions to the letter. Pay special attention to the temperature of the water the instructions call for, whether or not the items can be placed in the dryer and at what temp they can be dried, or whether line drying is advised.

WH fitness and reviews editor Natascha Grief, NASM-CPT opts for line drying and avoids the dryer altogether when possible for any kind of technical fabric; when that’s not an option, she always sets the dryer at the lowest temperature possible.
