Breakfast News: Nvidia’s Governance Moment

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Here’s what worries us most about the unexpected Huang news.

When unexpected news breaks about a leader we’re invested in, we at The Motley Fool try to slow down and separate the person from the business. That’s how we’re approaching this weekend’s news from Nvidia (NVDA -4.27%).

As we know, CEO Jensen Huang has admitted to an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. And U.S. officials have expressed concerns that she was mindfully gathering sensitive information on Nvidia’s technology and strategy for Chinese intelligence.

Nvidia’s board is now investigating. So far, the board says it has no reason to think any national security information or company secrets were compromised.

Meanwhile, Nvidia’s business looks the same as it did on Friday. Many Wall Street firms have not altered their price targets, and Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co still thinks Nvidia leads China by two to three years in artificial intelligence chips.

However, we do expect substantial fallout in Washington.

Huang has spent years lobbying to sell advanced chips in China. Lawmakers from both parties now want a closer look at those sales, and the incoming Congress could move to tighten export rules again.

We’ve always viewed Nvidia’s chance for China sales as upside to our core thesis. Losing that opportunity would not alter our long-term view.

Huang has led Nvidia since 1993. Around 60 executives report to him directly. That structure leaves no obvious successor were he to step aside, even for a short leave. The board has publicly backed Huang, and we think his departure is unlikely, but shareholders will obviously want more clarity on succession now.

All told, we’re staying the course with Nvidia, a stock that our Rule Breakers team recommended at 16 cents per share in 2005 and never sold. Our Hidden Gems team bought NVDA at $5.29 in 2018 and never sold.

But the views across our teams are subject to change (not surprisingly) if the investigation shows Huang shared secrets or let Nvidia’s chips reach restricted buyers. For Fools with a position size in Nvidia greater than 15% of their net worth, we don’t see reasons to react urgently. But you might consider making some incremental sales.

Finally, if Nvidia’s board handles this well, the company should come out of it with stronger governance paired with a public succession plan. Some silver lining to a grey grey cloud.

Team Hidden Gems has a five-year price prediction of $321.42, implying an annualized return of about 12.5%.

Team Rule Breakers has a five-year price prediction of $562.47, implying an annualized return of around 26%.
