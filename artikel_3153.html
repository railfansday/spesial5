Los Angeles schools switch off Google’s AI Overviews on student laptops

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Google says schools that reject AI must give up search, and districts are finding ways around it.

Los Angeles Unified, the second-largest US school district, has quietly disabled AI Overviews in Google search on student devices, AP reported. Google says schools that turn off AI entirely will lose search, and some districts are building workarounds or weighing other search engines.

Los Angeles Unified, the second-largest school district in the US, has switched off AI Overviews in Google search on student devices, the Associated Press reported. It is the largest district yet to work around Google’s refusal to offer schools search without AI.

The district, which has about half a million students, made no formal announcement. Parents noticed the AI summaries had gone days after a school board meeting on November 13, said Bridie Lee of the parents’ group Schools Beyond Screens LA. The district has already stopped issuing Chromebooks to its youngest pupils.

LAUSD said it had “temporarily suspended” the feature to keep students’ experience “age-appropriate and safe.” It said it uses several layers of controls, including third-party filtering tools.

Earlier this month, Google said it would switch off Gemini and search for K-12 students by default, leaving schools to turn each one on. But it won’t offer search without its AI features. Schools that reject AI entirely will lose search, Google’s education chief Chris Phillips told The New York Times.

That has drawn pushback even inside Google. More than 540 employees and contractors have signed a petition asking the company to let schools keep search without AI. In July, Common Sense Media called Google’s AI search an “unacceptable risk” for students.

Other districts are finding their own workarounds. Fairfax County Public Schools in Virginia has built plug-ins that remove AI summaries, according to published reports. Some districts are looking at Bing and DuckDuckGo, which let users turn off AI answers.

“We have choices,” Jack Kelanic, the district’s chief information officer, told a school board committee. “We can change the default search engine literally overnight.”

Instead of an AI-free search, Google is testing a “guided learning” tool that walks students through problems step by step.
