Cycurion (CYCU) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Margins surged, yet a going concern warning still looms.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Cycurion, Inc. (CYCU -6.99%) reported record quarterly revenue and gross profit, driven by the integration of recent acquisitions and a return to organic growth in its core business. Management stated that the company is shifting its strategic focus toward predictive and offensive cybersecurity capabilities, intended to address the widening gap between vulnerability discovery and remediation. The company reported a 40% year-over-year increase in revenue and significant gross margin expansion to 36%. CFO Garcia indicated that the path to profitability relies on growing gross profit faster than operating expenses, supported by a transition toward higher-margin recurring revenue services. The company expects to begin work on a $54.6 million state government contract in early 2027 while continuing to integrate the Digital Ally and Secuvant acquisitions.

Danny White: Thank you, Kevin. Before I walk through the portfolio, I want to quickly set the stage on what's happening in the industry and why we feel strongly about our product strategy. For most of its history, cybersecurity has been a defensive discipline. Organizations put up controls, collect alerts, respond after something gets through, but that model is breaking under the speed of modern attacks. Recently, Rapid7, their 2026 Global Threat Landscape Report found that the median time from a vulnerability's disclosure to confirmed exploitation fell from 8.5 days to five days.

Over that same period, Verizon's 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report found that the median time for organizations to fully remediate the known exploited vulnerabilities rose from 32 days to 43. Attackers are moving in days, but defenders are still moving in weeks. A purely defensive posture cannot close that gap. The market is starting to recognize this. Gartner projects that by 2030, preemptive security will make up half of all IT security spending, and that is up from less than 5% in 2024. The shift is away from detecting and responding and toward anticipating and acting first. That shift is why our portfolio looks the way it does.

Every security leader we talk to asks three questions: Where am I exposed? What is happening in my environment right now? What is most likely to happen next? Most of the industry answers the first two with separate tools that don't talk to each other, and very few answer the third. We built a product for each question, and we built them to work together. That lets our clients move from a defensive posture to an offensive one, thinking like the attacker and acting before the attack. We tested that thesis in the market.

In August, our team went to Black Hat in Las Vegas, the industry's largest gathering of security vendors and practitioners, and what we saw confirmed our direction. Most of the market was still focused on using AI to help security teams close alerts faster, which is still reacting. Very few vendors were combining exposure, live threat activity, and telemetry into one contextual view, and fewer still were using active testing to prove that a fix worked. Those gaps are exactly where we have focused and how Cycurion stands apart. With that context, our product portfolio is built around those three question areas: exposure, active threats, and the predictive view.

First, we have Panoptic, which covers exposure and is in production today. It identifies vulnerabilities across the client's environment, then ranks cyber risk by asset, threat vector, and exploitability, so clients know where to act first. Panoptic serves customers across multiple industries, from organizations with about $1 million in annual revenue to those with greater than $2 billion. We are in late-stage development of Panoptic 2.0, which adds enhancements and integration with our other two products. Our next product is Habit, which covers active threats. We launched Habit on November 4th as our AI-driven security operations center. Habit learns what normal activity looks like for each client and surfaces deviations.

It combines AI-assisted triage with around-the-clock human analysts, offensive testing, and verification that any fix made actually worked. Lastly, Fathom brings the portfolio together as our integrated predictive intelligence platform, targeted for the first quarter of 2027. Fathom will unify exposure and active threat intelligence with telemetry and operations into a single predictive view to equip organizations with actionable information, allowing them to act before attacks become disruptions. We are also deploying the Cycurion Pulse Technology Modernization Assessment through SLG to help public sector clients with the difficult challenge of modernizing their infrastructure and security investments. Our focus now is execution, turning these products into customer adoption and revenue.

That starts with converting the Habit launch into paid pilots and production deployments. Our commercial strategy is built to scale through MSP agreements, channel partnerships, competitive pricing, and cross-product integration that makes each product more valuable alongside the others. The coming months will set the pace. Habit is in the market now. Panoptic 2.0 releases in mid-December. In the first quarter of 2027, Fathom brings them together into that single predictive view. Each milestone stands on its own, and each one makes the next stronger. In an industry that has spent years trying to help defenders respond faster, we are focused on something different, giving organizations the ability to see what comes next and act before it happens.

That is where the market is going, and we intend to be the company that defines it. Cybersecurity has spent decades reacting to the last attack, but Cycurion is building for the next one.
