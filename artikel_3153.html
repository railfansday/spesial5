What These 6 Snack and Beverage Stocks Have in Common

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Coca-Cola and an array of other beverage and snack stocks deliver tasty dividends to income investors.

By Todd Shriber – Nov 22, 2026 at 2:17PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Coca-Cola ›

Investors who have been around the block know that when it comes to reducing risk and tapping into reliable dividends, some of the largest consumer-staples companies by market cap are excellent places to start. That group includes Brown-Forman (BFB -0.66%) (BFA -0.74%), Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%), Hormel Foods (HRL +2.08%), J.M. Smucker (SJM +1.82%), PepsiCo (PEP +1.11%), and Sysco (SYY +1.32%).

These six beverage and snack stocks serve steady diets of delectable dividends. Image source: Getty Images.

For equity income investors, that's an extensive list from which to choose, and it's reasonable to ponder what these companies have in common besides residence in the consumer staples sector and the food and beverage industry.

The answer is easy and important for dividend seekers: All six are at minimum Dividend Aristocrats®, members of the S&P 500 that have raised payouts for at least 25 consecutive years. (Dividend Aristocrats® is a registered trademark of Standard & Poor's Financial Services LLC.)

Let's examine each one from a dividend perspective.

Typically, the minimum age to open a U.S. brokerage account is 18, though some firms allow 13- to 17-year-olds to open an account with a parent or guardian. This means that younger investors can buy shares of Brown-Forman before they can legally enjoy the company's products, including Jack Daniel's and Woodford Reserve, among other well-known liquor brands.

The company, which also makes Chambord liqueur and Diplomático rum, yields 3%, or nearly triple the S&P 500's dividend, and is on a 42-year run of consecutive annual dividend raises. If recent history is an accurate predictor, November could be the month in which Brown-Forman makes it 43 straight years of boosted payouts, so that's something to keep an eye on over the near term.

Something else long-term investors should monitor with Brown-Forman and related stocks is alcohol consumption trends among Gen Zers and millennials. Younger people are consuming slightly less alcohol today than they did during the worst part of the pandemic.

On the more positive side of the ledger for Brown-Forman is evidence suggesting that those consumers are being more selective when they do drink, indicating they are looking to trade up to premium brands, including some from the Brown-Forman stable.

With payout increase streaks of 65 years and 55 years, respectively, soft drink giants Coca-Cola and PepsiCo are Dividend Kings -- companies that have raised dividends in at least 50 straight years. Yet, while the two share dividend growth, industry, and sector, these stocks' trajectories differ significantly. For the decade ended Nov. 13, Coke returned about 230% while PepsiCo gained 92%.

This year, Coca-Cola, though a little frothy on valuation, is regularly printing new all-time highs while beating Pepsi stock by roughly 10 to 1. The latter is being pinched on multiple fronts, including tepid performance in the key North American market and sluggishness in its salty snacks division , a segment to which Coke is less exposed.

Both of these dividends are widely considered safe, but if "safe" is how investors want to play it, Coca-Cola has obvious momentum on its side. At the same time, PepsiCo is a "show me" story that may, in the near term, lack the clarity some market participants demand.

As noted above, there are concerns about the strength of PepsiCo's snacks unit, but those worries aren't uniform across the staples sector.

Just look at J.M. Smucker. In a sign that the fretting about weight-loss drugs hurting snack consumption may be misplaced, shares of this dividend growth star that is the maker of Twinkies are up 41% year to date. That's stellar among packaged-food stocks, and of note to prospective investors is that Smucker raised its full-year earnings guidance in August, saying it's having some success in passing higher costs on to consumers.

Hormel is a different story. While the company's Spam product found its way into post-Election Day discourse, shares of this Dividend King are slightly in the red this year. But there is hope for the maker of Pepperoni Bites and Planters nuts.

Management is known to be prudent with the balance sheet and is aware that the dividend is paramount to its investors. Some experts see Hormel's dividend growth over the next several years coming in at around 3% annually, which could keep pace with or beat inflation.

As for Sysco -- a Dividend King in its own right that distributes snacks to an array of commercial clients -- the stock slipped following the fiscal first-quarter earnings report earlier this month as investors digested the dual specters of soft sales and higher costs.

That dip may be an opportunity because some analysts argue that the market is overreacting to one weak quarter while possibly ignoring Sysco's progress with deployment of artificial intelligence (AI). The food distributor could harness $100 million in 2027 savings from AI adoption, along with the company's ability to scale up its food-service unit. Sysco shares yield 2.9%.
