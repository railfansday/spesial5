Nations League roundup: Devenny’s divine strike lifts Northern Ireland to top tier

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Haaland’s late penalty grabs Norway last-eight spot

Parrott penalties help Republic of Ireland secure playoff

A wonder strike from Justin Devenny earned Northern Ireland top spot in Nations League Group B2 and promotion to the top tier of the competition. The Stoke player fired home on the volley from the edge of the area to earn a 1-1 draw away to Hungary, who would have leapfrogged Michael O’Neill’s side in the group with victory.

Northern Ireland had fallen 1-0 behind after a 35th-minute goal from the striker Barnabas Varga, who slotted home when one-on-one with the goalkeeper Pierce Charles after a defensive mix-up. The goal ended Northern Ireland’s proud record of not having conceded in this campaign and also sent Hungary to the top of the group in the live standings.

But moments after Isaac Price had hit the post for O’Neill’s young outfit, Devenny smashed the ball into the top left-hand corner in the 65th minute to restore Northern Ireland’s one-point advantage as group leaders. As time ticked down, Hungary pushed forward in increasing desperation – with Rajmund Toth firing over late on – but Northern Ireland dug in with the defensive resilience which has been the cornerstone of their campaign.

“It’s a great feeling for us,” said O’Neill, who had to watch the game from the stands as he served a touchline ban. “We set out when we came into this level of the competition to first of all try and stay and we knew to be in the top two positions would help us.

“We knew that would help us potentially with the draw for Euro 2028 [qualification] but to come away unbeaten, to finish top, and to give the performance we’ve given against some very good teams, it’s very encouraging.”

In Wales’s group, Norway snatched a quarter-final berth thanks to Erling Haaland’s 96th-minute penalty against Denmark. It was Haaland’s second and completed a remarkable comeback from 2-0 down as the Danes were pipped for second place in Group A4.

Denmark, who began the day in second place in the standings and needed to avoid defeat to reach the knockout phase, led 2-0 inside 14 minutes through Rasmus Højlund and Pierre-Emile Højbjerg.

But Haaland pulled one back before half-time and, after Martin Ødegaard’s 83rd-minute equaliser had set up a grandstand finish, the Manchester City striker scored from the spot in the sixth minute of added time after the video assistant referee had ruled Joachim Andersen had handled in the area.

The two nations finished level on nine points and although Denmark had a better goal difference, Norway progressed on their head-to-head record after also winning the reverse fixture 3-2.

“It’s one of the crazier football years,” Haaland told TV2. “It’s fun to be a part of finishing it off in style. Ståle [Solbakken, Norway’s coach] said ⁠it before the match: ‘Let’s just get a good finish now to a completely indescribable football year.’ It’s fun to be Norwegian.”

The Republic of Ireland beat 10-man Kosovo 3-0 at the Aviva Stadium to go ahead of the visitors in the standings for Group B3 to secure second place and a spot in the promotion playoffs. The win also propelled the Euro 2028 co-hosts into a coveted pot-two seeding for next month’s qualifying draw for that competition.

Kreshnik Hajrizi’s first-half handball on the line earned the Kosovo defender a red card and allowed Troy Parrott to bury the resulting penalty. The Republic were awarded a second spot-kick early in the second period after a foul by the goalkeeper Arijanet Muric, with Parrott again scoring. Jack Moylan’s header from a Liam Kitching cross made it 3-0.

What had been a largely uneventful encounter took a dramatic twist in the 26th minute, the red card incident sparked by Moylan’s cross, glanced on by Liam Scales to Parrott. His effort – clearly goalbound – was cleared off the line by Hajrizi, who extended his arm like a goalkeeper, and was immediately sent off.
