5 Businesses I Wouldn’t Start in 2027 — and 5 Models I’d Look at Instead

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

You're viewing the US website. Looking for the Site edition?

These are the businesses I would think twice about starting, plus alternatives that offer better leverage, scalability and income potential.

Opinions expressed by Entrepreneur contributors are their own.

Search “best businesses to start” and you’ll see the same ideas on repeat: dropshipping, influencing, a generic marketing agency, something on Amazon. Technically, they can all still work. But “can work” and “where I’d put my time and money today” are two very different things.

After nearly a decade in ecommerce and online business, I’ve learned that the business model matters almost as much as the entrepreneur. A great operator can fight through a difficult model, but why choose one that demands enormous time, has razor-thin margins or depends on constantly finding your next customer?

Heading into 2027, I’m drawn to three things: leverage, recurring revenue and systems. Here are five businesses I’d think twice about starting from scratch, and what I’d consider instead.

A few years ago, the playbook was simple: land a few local clients at $1,000 or $2,000 a month, outsource the work and scale. But everyone got the memo. Generalist agencies now compete with freelancers, offshore teams, in-house hires and increasingly capable AI tools.

Solve one expensive problem for one type of business. Help dental practices follow up with leads who never scheduled. Build AI customer-service workflows for ecommerce brands. Automate reminders and customer reactivation for home-service companies.

The opportunity isn’t selling AI; businesses can access that themselves. It’s knowing what to automate and connecting it to a real outcome. “Social media management” is a vague line item, while recovering thousands of dollars in lost leads is easy to understand. Sell the outcome, not the technology.

The idea that you can open a Seller Central account, list a few products after work and enjoy effortless income is increasingly disconnected from reality. Sourcing, pricing, Buy Box competition, inventory, customer service and account health all demand attention. For someone who wants to become an operator, that’s fine. But many professionals want ecommerce exposure without creating a second full-time job.

For people with more capital than time, a professionally managed ecommerce storefront may be worth a look. The owner funds and owns the store, while an experienced team handles day-to-day operations for a fee or a share of profits, much like a property owner hiring a property manager. These investors aren’t looking for something else to do. They’re looking for something else to own.

It isn’t risk-free. Marketplaces shift, sales fluctuate and projected income is never guaranteed income, so vet who actually operates the store and how profits are calculated before you commit.

The pitch is irresistible: no inventory, your supplier ships the order and you pocket the difference. But acquiring the customer is the hard part. A store selling products that hundreds of other websites also sell has little competitive advantage, and you’re often dependent on paid advertising and on finding products before competitors pile in.

Think beauty, pet products, specialty foods, personal care or hobby consumables. The category matters less than the economics. Ask yourself: If I spend $40 acquiring this customer today, can they be worth $200 to me over the next two years?

An ad stops producing the moment you turn it off. A loyal customer base, an email list and a recognizable brand can become more valuable every year.

Expertise used to be nearly enough: record six hours of video, build a landing page, charge $997. Today, information is abundant, including through AI, and people are reluctant to pay premium prices for it. Information isn’t scarce anymore; real results are.

Build several specific products that solve narrow problems, such as a template, calculator, workflow, database, paid community or small software tool, and create content for the audience they serve. Someone who knows Airbnb could skip the “$997 Masterclass” and offer a profitability calculator, a cleaning-checklist system, guest-message templates and a paid newsletter on market changes. That ecosystem is much harder to replace with a single AI prompt.

Personal brands are powerful, but they can become cages. If revenue disappears when you stop posting or showing your face online, you’ve created income, not necessarily an independent business. You might love posting three videos a day at 25. Will you still want to at 35?

This is the filter I’d apply to almost any opportunity: Can this eventually work without me? It doesn’t need to be hands-off on day one, but there should be a path from founder-dependent to system-dependent. Can someone else handle customer service? Can sales become repeatable? Can procedures be documented? Can customers keep buying while you’re on vacation?

If every important function leads back to you, that’s not freedom. It’s self-employment with a nicer title.

The business I’m watching in 2027 isn’t really an idea. It’s leverage. AI gives one employee leverage. Software gives a small company leverage. Recurring revenue gives customer acquisition leverage. A management team gives an owner leverage. Capital gives an investor leverage. And systems give entrepreneurs perhaps the most valuable form of all: time.

So instead of asking, “What’s the hottest business to start?” ask: What can I build or own today that becomes less dependent on my time as it grows? If you have little capital but plenty of time, an AI automation service may fit. If you understand products and branding, consider a niche consumer brand. If you have expertise but don’t want to become an influencer, digital products can turn it into an asset. And if you have capital but little spare time, a professionally managed business may deserve a closer look.

The best business isn’t always the one that makes the most money today. It may be the one that eventually stops needing you to make every dollar. After all, your time is precious.
