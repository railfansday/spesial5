Magic: The Gathering Designer Reveals the Commander Trend He Wants to See More Of

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Elves and faeries, cats and dogs, and now… cats and zombies?

While at MagicCon: Atlanta 2026, one of the trickier Magic: The Gathering Commander decks I faced was led by Maralen, Fae Ascendant, from Lorwyn Eclipsed. Currently the 40th most popular Commander, according to data from EDHREC, Maralen is a typal Commander, meaning that her abilities emphasize creature types. In this case, she’s all about two kinds of creatures rather than just one. Specifically, this is an elves and faeries Commander. Since her color identity has three colors — black, green, and blue — that gives every Maralen deck access to a wide variety of both creature types to leverage some big payoffs.

Her strategy is fairly simple: Every time an elf or faerie you control enters the battlefield, you exile the top two cards of an opponent’s library and cast a spell of mana cost equal to or less than the number of elves and faeries you control for free. The results are always unpredictable, but how you get the engine going is easy. Play a ton of small faeries and elves and watch as you turn your opponents’ weapons against them. The impish flavor here feels perfect for the queen of the fae in Lorwyn, but the core design philosophy at play is something Magic’s principal designer Gavin Verhey wants to expand upon.

“One thing I’ve pitched for years is this idea of team-up type legends,” Verhey said during a roundtable interview at MagicCon attended by Polygon. “Seeing the success of Maralen is making me want to do a lot more. There’s also my funky cat-zombie team-up in Mystery Booster Commander. That’s just fun to do, right? People love building around creature types. So why not two? These are the sort of things I want to do more of in the future.”

Verhey served as lead designer of the newly released Mystery Booster Commander alongside co-lead Eric Engelhard. The “cat-zombie team-up” he mentions is Olag and Miau, New Friends. An Abzan (white-black-green) legendary creature, Olag and Miau is the marriage of cats and zombies. Each time you cast a cat, you put a +1/+1 counter on a zombie you control. And every time you cast a zombie, you put a +1/+1 counter on one of your cats. Then at the end of your turn, all opponents also lose an amount of life equal to the number of tapped cats and zombies you control.

Commander’s “foundational, iconic heart” is here to stay

As long as you keep attacking with those cats and zombies — or find some other way to tap them — you can easily whittle away at your opponents. The trade-off is that this leaves you potentially defenseless, so it’s a good thing that Abzan has plenty of lifegain options for you to balance things out. Had I come across Olag and Miau during my own Mystery Booster Commander pick-two draft at MagicCon, I definitely would have gone all-in on crafting that deck. Sadly, I did not.

Another example of this kind of dual-typal strategy in your Command Zone that Verhey brought up is Rin and Seri, Inseparable. (Even more popular than Maralen at 38th on EDHREC, by the way.) Whenever you cast a dog, Rin and Seri create a 1/1 cat. Whenever you cast a cat, they create a 1/1 dog. It also has an activated ability that lets you deal damage equal to the number of dogs you have on the battlefield and heal yourself an amount based on the number of cats you control.

Though it’s technically illegal to play in all constructed formats, save for Mystery Booster Commander drafts and constructed events, there’s a ridiculous test card that riffs on this iconic duo — and it had me burst out laughing when I saw it. Rin and Seri, Inseperabler (yes, “Inseperable-r”) makes all of the cats you control gain the dog type and all dogs you control gain the cat type. And it gives them all haste. It’s tantalizing to think about a Commander deck that gets both versions of Rin and Seri on the battlefield. Every cat or dog you control then counts toward both halves of the original Rin and Seri’s activated ability, potentially doubling its potency.

Inseparabler isn’t exactly broken. It would be if its core abilities were blanket effects that made all cats dogs and vice versa rather than just the ones you control, because then every cat or dog you played would generate two 1/1 cat-dogs. My draft deck wound up being Selesnya (green-white), so Inseparabler was relegated to my sideboard. It’s not worth much money, but I am absolutely keeping it. Maybe I’ll go out of my way to buy the original Rin and Seri and build out the deck, perhaps with some way to tutor Inseperabler into play just to see how effectively it might snowball.

Another interesting example of this multi-typal design philosophy popped up in the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles set, in which plenty of cards care about mutants, ninjas, and, turtles. The five-color Heroes in a Half Shell legendary creature from the precon adds +1/+1 counters and draws a card when one or more of these creature types deal combat damage to a player. It’s another interesting experiment with typically disparate creature types, and it’s one that, in retrospect, I’m far more interested in building than I was previously.

If cats can get along with both dogs and zombies, and faeries with elves, then what other fun typal combinations could emerge in Commander? If only the Lord of the Rings set had a Gruul (red-green) Gimli and Legolas Commander that united elves and dwarves, or perhaps Marvel: Darkhold Destiny could deliver the ideal gods and demons Commander? Universes Beyond might be uniquely suited to these kinds of quirky Commanders, but there’s space for it in all of Magic. And I’m very intrigued to see where Gavin Verhey and the rest of Magic’s design team might take it.
