HTA Design unveils "UK's largest council housing development"

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

A collection of brick-clad buildings containing over 450 homes forms Kidbrooke Park Road in London, which architecture studio HTA Design claims is the largest new-build council housing development in the UK.

Built on a formerly disused site in Greenwich, the development contains 452 homes, all of which are social housing.

Construction company Durkan delivered Kidbrooke Park Road for the Royal Borough of Greenwich as part of its council housing programme, Greenwich Builds.

HTA Design said the project “demonstrates what can be achieved when local authorities take an ambitious approach to housing delivery”.

“Our main goal was to demonstrate that council housing delivered at scale does not have to come at the expense of good design,” HTA Design managing partner Simon Bayliss told Dezeen.

“We wanted to create a neighbourhood where the homes are generous and adaptable, where landscape is integral rather than an afterthought, and where streets and public spaces feel active, welcoming and safe,” he continued.

“Together, the two phases have delivered the UK’s largest council housing development and one of the most significant social housing projects completed in recent years. We hope it serves as a benchmark for how future generations of affordable housing can be delivered across London and the UK.”

HTA Design recently completed the second phase of the scheme, which adds eight housing blocks with 330 homes to the three blocks completed in phase one.

Ranging from six to 15 storeys, the housing comprises one-, two-, and three-bedroom apartments and family maisonettes, most of which are dual-aspect.

The buildings at Kidbrooke Park Road have a reinforced concrete frame, clad in brick chosen to reference red-brick buildings in the local area.

Arched entrances, patterned brickwork and exposed concrete elements were designed to add visual interest among the development’s green spaces, which include tree-lined streets, community gardens, wildflower meadows, play areas and gardens designed for rainwater runoff.

“A key challenge was ensuring that two phases delivered separately would ultimately come together as one place,” said Bayliss.

“Through simple buildings and typologies with repetitive architectural components, combined with a landscape strategy that runs throughout the development, we set out to create a neighbourhood with a clear identity that worked within its context.”

Elsewhere in the development are a nursery, community facilities and green roofs. The housing is complete with air-source heat pumps, electric boilers and rooftop photovoltaics.

“We hope residents feel proud to live here,” Bayliss concluded.

“Beyond the homes themselves, the project has been designed to support everyday life, with play spaces, shared green areas, a nursery, community facilities and local amenities all contributing to a strong sense of community,” he said.

“Those are often the things that transform a housing development into a genuine neighbourhood.”

The deputy mayor of London for Housing and Residential Development Tom Copley said the project represents how London is “leading the way nationally on council home building”.

“The new development at Kidbrooke Park Road is the perfect example of what we’re achieving, with 452 council homes forming the largest single entirely new-build council housing development in the country,” he said.

“We recently announced that 60 per cent of the funding from the first round of the mayor’s new Social and Affordable Homes Programme will go to London boroughs, and we look forward to that decision delivering even more new council homes in future.”

Last month, the Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) announced the brick-clad Lion Green Road housing by Mary Duggan Architects with Ruff Architects, which contains a mix of private and socially rented homes, as the winner of this year’s Stirling Prize.

RIBA also awarded its Neave Brown Award for Housing to Rowan Court, a cluster of brick-clad social housing blocks in Seven Sisters designed by Satish Jassal Architects.
