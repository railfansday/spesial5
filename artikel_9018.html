Bringing Hospitality-Inspired Textures into Workplace Interiors

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

"We can really bring a hospitality look into a workplace, which is something that our clients have been asking for, for many years," says Mark Harder, a design director at Gensler. The warmth of wood, the texture of leather, and the tactile surfaces found in hotels and restaurants are also sought in offices, airports, and shared corridors. Bringing these qualities into different settings, however, requires considering how materials will respond to everyday use.

From Fashion References to Interior Finishes

The teams began by looking at elements that interested them in fashion: woven leather, distressed materials, vibrant colors, and the layering of different finishes. Rather than developing each surface in isolation, they considered how colors and textures could work together across furniture, walls, and other interior elements.

The metallic designs feature deliberately irregular textures over a brushed-metal appearance. Incorporating real metal, the finishes recall the creases of a flattened candy wrapper. Adapting Material References to Different Uses

"A lot of times, we are restricted on what we can use based off the natural performance of a product. But with this collection, we were able to expand that to make it more usable across different market sectors," says Angel Gonzales, resource director at Gensler.
