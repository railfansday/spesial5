The Most Comfortable Sneakers for Women, Tested by a Style Writer

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

We’ve put hundreds of sneakers to the test, and On is at the top of my list.

Women's Health may earn commission from the links on this page, but we only feature products we believe in. Why Trust Us?

At this point, I’m pretty sure I qualify as the woman who lived in her shoes. Not in a glamorous Carrie Bradshaw kind of way, but in a professional-sneaker-tester kind of way. I probs have at least 30 pairs crowding my closet and entryways right now, and that’s not even counting the pairs I’ve run into the ground or given away to friends and family (you’re welcome). My top priority is always comfort, and while you might find that a given when it comes to sneakers, I’ve tested my fair share of options that make my feet feel stiff and achy.

So, I present to you the pairs of comfortable shoes—think walking shoes and cross-training shoes—that actually keep me feeling comfortable and supported without slacking on cuteness. From memory foam insoles that mold to your foot's unique shape to running shoes with responsive soles that absorb your stride’s shock, these are the most comfortable sneakers for women, tested by myself and other sneaker enthusiasts at Women’s Health.

The brand ethos of On is to make people feel like they're running on Clouds. It achieves this thanks to its signature Helion Superfoam midsole, which sets itself apart with a construction that blends rigid, stable sections with flexible foam. This combination of materials provides the perfect balance of cushioning and energy return, giving you a bouncy, lightweight feel when your foot hits the pavement. The result is a soft, natural ground feel, rather than an overly bouncy cushioning experience. Fitness contributor Nicolette Accardi says this tech alone is why this shoe is an absolute workhorse when it comes to running (and running fast!). It’s her all-time favorite race shoe—she even ran a half-marathon in it.

According to Accardi, the shoe feels firm but still has enough flexibility that her running gait isn’t impacted. The toebox has a ton of room for movement, but still holds the foot in place so each step feels responsive and energetic. Even when her legs feel like they’re about to give out, these shoes pick her right back up. On’s innovative speedboard, a flexible plastic plate wedged between the footbed and midsole, snaps back into place for a bouncy effect after your feet hit the ground. This means the shoe requires less energy output with each stride, making running feel easier. Plus, the rebound that comes with the underfoot cushioning gives a spring in your step.

I might not run marathons like Accardi, but I love to wear this shoe for walking and casual jogs. The CloudTec system, which uses air pockets in the midsole (the layer between the insole and the outsole), to create the shoe’s cushiony feel, propels me forward with every step, kind of like walking on a trampoline. And while it’s great for getting your steps in, this shoe has that chunky look that’s super on trend right now. I love pairing it with tailored pants and a boxy T-shirt on top for that upgraded activewear vibe.

One thing to note, though, is the sole wears down fast, which we found to be disappointing, especially given that price tag. Accardi says the Clifton 10 has a better tread thanks to its thicker outsole designed for high-friction.

Out of all the sneakers I’ve tried, I find myself wearing the Brooks Ghost Max 3—it’s the newest addition to the Ghost Max series—the most when I want to feel comfortable during walks or runs (yep, it can do both!). That’s because the rocker bottom sole, which has a slight curve to it like a rocking chair, helps propel my foot forward with less energy. Wearing it literally feels like walking on pillows.

With the most cushioning of any shoe on this list (and the most cushioning Brooks offers), it’s perfect for those who need extra support. Fitness contributor Talene Appleton says it has extra plush, foamy cushioning, but even for a max cushioned shoe, it’s incredibly stable thanks to Brooks’ signature Guardrails technology, which is a broad base with two pieces of foam on either side of the heel. It reduces the rolling of the foot both inward and outward, so it cradles your foot in place without messing with your stride. According to Appleton, this combination is great for those with higher arches. After hours of walking, she doesn’t feel an ounce of pain or discomfort. And if you have wide feet or bunions, you’re in luck—this shoe comes in both wide and extra-wide options (huge win!).

During testing, I did notice that there was some wear and tear on the shoe pretty early on (it had only been a few months of use). The material on the upper of the shoe wore out pretty quickly. While this knocked it down a few points in my book, its supreme comfort still kept this kick at the very top of my list. Plus, I love how many fun colors the shoe comes in—because running and walking should be fun, right?!

The Hoka Clifton 11 is a standout favorite among WH editors. After testing nearly every Hoka model, I can attest that the Clifton 11 reigns supreme for walking. I was also a fan of the previous Clifton 10, and the newest version feels very similar—just a smidge lighter and more springy. It is by far the most comfortable model in the series to date, and it’s earned a spot in my shoe rotation for its supportive yet flexible cushioning.

The first major difference I noticed is how light the Clifton 11 feels. Even after wearing it for several hours at a time, it doesn’t feel bulky or clunky. Which makes sense: Hoka did shave nearly half an ounce off the weight compared to the 10. The toe box is slightly narrower, too, but it doesn’t feel tight or uncomfortable. I prefer the more secure fit because it kept my foot from sliding around without feeling cramped. If you like a little more wiggle room for your toes, I would suggest opting for the wide sizing, though.

Despite its lighter weight, it has a generous stack of foam padding throughout the midsole. That weightless yet cushiony feel makes it ideal for my longer walks throughout NYC. The shoe has a mild heel drop (the same as the previous model at 8 mm), which helps distribute pressure evenly across the foot.

I also find that the foam absorbs impact well. My feet—unfortunately plagued by plantar fasciitis—never feel fatigued wearing this shoe. There's technically a rocker shape built into the sole, but it's subtle enough that I barely notice it—instead, my stride feels smooth and natural whether I’m walking or running. I’m also a fan of the durable rubber outsole that gives plenty of traction. I never feel like I’m going to slip while wearing these, even when walking down slippery subway stairs.

So far, the Clifton 11—which was debuted alongside the new Clifton Pro— does a great job of keeping my feet comfy and supported. It’s a great shoe for anyone in need of a comfortable, everyday walking sneaker that can also handle the occasional jog (unlike the Clifton Pro, which is geared more toward high-speed running).

There’s nothing worse than slipping into an uncomfortable shoe after a tough run—that’s where the Alo Recovery Sneaker comes in. Designed to support tired feet, it features a structured sole and high-rebound foam insole that offers arch support and bounces back into place with each step. It makes every step feel stable, cushioned, and balanced. The second I put this pair on after a long run, my feet are instantly relaxed. I can’t say that about most of my other sneakers.

One of the biggest perks of this shoe? The traction is next-level, which is key for a recovery sneaker that’s meant to keep you comfortable and steady all day. Inside, the sock-like lining prevents your foot from sliding around. The thick ankle collar also helps stabilize the foot and provides extra support up the leg, keeping everything locked in place.

Style-wise, this sneaker goes with everything—sweats, shorts, jeans, leggings, you name it. That’s why I crowned it the best overall platform sneaker. After giving it a spin for a few weeks running around Manhattan, walking my dog around Long Island, and wearing it to and from workout classes, it's done the best job at keeping my feet pain-free compared to many other platform sneakers I've worn. The 1.5-inch platform is subtle but adds just the right amount of height to elongate my legs without being so tall that I feel unstable. Oh, and it’s super cute to boot. I’m a big fan of the Natural White colorway, which looks clean and classic, and pairs effortlessly with everything from sweatpants to denim.

Even if you’re not fresh off a run, it works for the office, errands, or a casual lunch. It’s hands down one of the most versatile (and most worn) shoes in my closet. My one con is that it feels a bit heavy and bulky, especially after a long day of wear.

I hadn't tried Asics until this year, and now it's my go-to shoe brand. Not only does its shoe have a streetwear vibe (I'm loving the bright yellow color with sweats and jeans), but they’re also some of the most comfortable shoes I’ve ever worn. In the UB9-S Gel Kayano 20s, the brand uses its signature gel cushioning technology, which features gel units placed in both the heel and forefoot of the shoe to absorb shock and provide a soft landing. This cushioning adapts to your foot’s movement, offering flexibility while reducing impact on your joints. Plus, the form-fitting upper keeps your foot locked in place, making it perfect for long runs, but stylish enough for everyday wear.

I wear this shoe everywhere—on runs, in the gym, and even to the office—and I get compliments virtually every single time I step out in it. The support is unmatched, the traction is solid, and it’s lightweight enough that I never feel weighed down. Plus, it breaks in fast (I wore it right out of the box), so there’s no awkward adjustment period. If you’re looking for a sneaker that can do it all while still looking cool, this is the one for you.

One con to note: It comes in some funky colors, so if you’re not into something bright and fun, I’d recommend looking elsewhere. I also wish it came in a wider version for those with wider feet and bunions.

We’ve been fans of the Chargefeel line for years, with the previous edition—the Chargefeel 2—winning the best cross-training shoe for our 2025 WH Sneaker Awards. Lululemon has since made a few tweaks that make it even better. “Everyone I know who has worked out in the Chargefeel—myself included—has loved its perfect balance of stability and flexibility,” says WH executive health and fitness director Jacqueline Andriakos, NASM-CPT.

Now with a lighter weight and a lower heel-to-toe drop (6 mm compared to 9.5 mm), the shoe doesn’t weigh me down and puts less stress on the ankles and feet. I’m a big fan of the wider base for lifting and squatting because it makes my feet feel locked. The forefoot-hugging band on the inside of the shoe and a tough plastic heel counter add extra security, while the springy EVA foam cushioning makes it a workhorse for walking and running.

And post-workout, it’s a great leisure kick for traveling or running errands. I recently wore this sneaker while traveling, and it kept my feet happy power walking through the airport and standing in long security and customs lines. Andriakos agrees, stating, “The stylish silhouette looks good outside the gym.”

The fit is true to size, and while wide-size options are not available, the toes still have adequate room to spread with each step or lift. However, the wide outsole may feel too bulky during stair climbing or quick, dynamic moves.

Let me introduce you to my new favorite low-profile, fashion sneaker: the Puma Speedcat. It’s been making waves with style girls, and after testing it out for months, I can see why. This shoe pairs so well with any outfit, with a slim, almost ballet-flat silhouette that doesn’t clash with nicer pieces in my wardrobe. I even see celebs like Em Rata sport the Speedcat all the time, and they always make any streetwear ‘fit look so chic and cool.

Like the Samba, it has a pretty flat sole, but I actually find it more cushioned thanks to its EVA midsole, which has a lightweight, cushioned feel and shock-absorbing properties. This means I can walk around for longer without my feet aching. Plus, after wearing it on wet city streets, I’ve noticed it has better traction, which is a huge win.

The only downside? The fit is a little narrow, especially in the toe area. If you have wider feet, I’d definitely recommend sizing up.

Originally designed for indoor soccer, the Adidas Samba has become a cult-favorite sneaker that’s just as popular on the streets as it is on the field. At first glance, the Samba looks pretty flat, so I wasn’t expecting much in terms of comfort. But after logging hundreds of miles walking around the city, I was surprised by how well it supports my feet. The Samba uses a combination of synthetic leather for the upper and a rubber sole with a cushioned midsole for comfort. The cushioning is more subtle compared to high-performance running shoes, but it still provides a soft, supportive feel. Even without a ton of cushioning, I rarely experience discomfort, which is impressive for a sneaker this sleek. It runs narrow, especially for wide feet, and I recommend going a half size down, as it tends to run big—many with wider feet or bunions agree it’s not the best fit.

It’s a go-to for effortlessly cool outfits—you can wear it with jeans, dresses, slacks, or shorts and always look stylish. I own this shoe in two different colorways (it comes in so many different shades), and after wearing it with practically everything in my closet, I can confidently say it adds a sporty, trendy touch to any outfit.

One of Under Armour’s first-ever shoes, this kick is rooted in sport. But it’s gotten some essential upgrades since its launch, and it’s safe to say I’m hooked. Since 2009, it’s been updated with leather upper overlays to keep the foot supported and soft UA HOVR cushioning, made from soft EVA foam and rubber, which is engineered to store and release energy with each step, providing a bouncy, responsive feel that helps propel you forward. Out of all the shoes I’ve tried, this pair feels the most bouncy, which I know is an absolute essential for the runners and walkers out there that like that super thick underfoot feel. It also has a rocker shape to keep the foot in a forward motion for less energy exertion.

The UA Apparition also has a mesh upper for supreme breathability—I’ve worn it during HIIT workouts, runs, and walks and never feel like my feet are constricted or sweaty, but I still feel supported and balanced. With a sleek, minimalistic look, the Apparition pairs well with anything from gym gear to casual streetwear. The rubber outsole provides traction and durability, allowing for smooth transitions during both dynamic workouts and everyday use.

My one con? There are some fun colors available, but I wish there were more options to choose from.
