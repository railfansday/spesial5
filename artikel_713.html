IDFA: ‘The Year of the Ghost’ and ‘Memories of Lightning’ Win Amsterdam Doc Fest Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

'Hazara Town' is also among the documentaries honored at the 39th edition of the Dutch festival.

The Year of the Ghost and Memories of Lightning are the winners of the top awards at the 38th edition of the International Documentary Film Festival Amsterdam (IDFA), unveiled at a ceremony held at the Dutch city’s Eye Filmmuseum.

The Year of the Ghost from director Louis HotHotHot, a co-production between the Netherlands and China, won the IDFA Award for best film in the international competition section, along with two additional honors.

Like in his debut feature Four Journeys, the filmmaker turned his camera on his own family, prompted by the death of his father. “Due to COVID restrictions, the filmmaker cannot make it to China in time to say goodbye,” reads a synopsis. “The jury was astonished by this work of singular artistic vision exploring deep family dynamics and connections, set against the wider societal forces and gender constraints inherited across generations,” the jury said. “Told with bold honesty, a rare attention to detail, cinematic curiosity and love.”

Meanwhile, the IDFA Award for best film in the fest’s Envision Competition, along with another honor, went to Riccardo Giacconi for Memories of Lightning, a France-Italy co-production. The film follows radio journalist Sibilla, who travels to a village in central Italy to make an audio documentary about a shocking murder that took place there in 1982, but the village is now little more than a ghost town. “Still, she tries to capture memories to create a soundscape of the past with her microphone,” reads a synopsis. “Little by little, the story emerges of a horrific murder that is part of her family history. The victim, a 14-year-old girl, was Sibilla’s aunt.”

Said the jury: “A studio, a village, a hill. A device that renders irrelevant the distinction between what is real and unreal, that exercises precise control over what is said and what is left unsaid, that stretches time and hunts down stories. A film that captures the silence of things falling, like distant flashes of lightning that are sure to herald the storm.”

In other honors, the IDFA award for best first feature was bestowed upon Hazara Town by Khadim Dai. It also won another honor and a special mention.

Hazara Town is named after a neighborhood in the Pakistani city of Quetta, which got its name because it is home to so many members of the Hazara ethnic group. Many of them fled Afghanistan, where they have been facing persecution for over a century. But they are not safe in Pakistan either, as becomes clear in Dai’s portrait of his two nieces, their mother and their grandparents.
