2026 CMA Awards Performances, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

As Ella Langley dominated the CMA Awards' winners circle, Billboard looks at the night's performances.

The night belonged to Ella Langley at Wednesday evening’s (Nov. 18) CMA Awards. Propelled by the success of “Choosin’ Texas,” her album Dandelion and a headlining tour, Langley won her first entertainer of the year honor. She also won female vocalist of the year, single, song and music video of the year (all for “Choosin’ Texas”) and picked up album of the year, for Dandelion.

“I have watched these awards since I was a little kid, and the award I cared most about was song of the year and entertainer of the year,” Langley said in accepting the CMA entertainer of the year honor, adding, “[there] hasn’t been a song on the radio or in my ears at all where I wasn’t imagining what that song would sound like in front of a crowd…”

Other winners during the night included Chris Stapleton, Stephen Wilson Jr., Brooks & Dunn, The Red Clay Strays and the song “McArthur,” from HARDY, Morgan Wallen, Eric Church and Tim McGraw. Lainey Wilson, hosting for a third time, opened the show by paying tribute to the late music legend Dolly Parton.

“Dolly gave us hope, Dolly gave us joy, Dolly gave us her heart,” Wilson said.

Wilson was joined at times to host by Luke Bryan, and also Brad Paisley, in a throwback to the decade-plus that Paisley and Carrie Underwood hosted the CMAs. From there, the evening celebrated the CMA Awards’ six decades of helping to showcase artists on one of country music’s biggest stages.

The evening both celebrated newcomers and established artists, while honoring legendary artists, such as attendees Sammy Kershaw, Randy Travis, Clint Black and Bill Anderson. While Langley dominated the awards wins, the night was filled with an array of performances. Taylor Swift made her first CMA Awards performance in 13 years, and turned it into an entertaining spectacle. Langley performed twice (including a collab with Miranda Lambert), while Wilson Jr. teamed with Chris Stapleton, Zach Top welcomed Dierks Bentley to pay tribute to John Anderson, and more artists shone during their time in CMA Awards spotlight.

The evening was filled with top-notch performances, including heartfelt tributes, unique artist pairings, rollicking new song debuts and of course, a few surprises.

Below, Billboard ranks each CMA Awards performance, counting down to the most memorable performance of the evening.

The CMAs aimed to honor Willie Nelson lifetime achievement award recipients Brooks & Dunn with a star-studded performance. After all, the duo has amassed a catalog of influential, enduring country hits like “Red Dirt Road,” “Boot Scootin’ Boogie,” and “Brand New Man,” and are previous CMA entertainer of the year winners, and Country Music Hall of Fame members.  But the multi-artist tribute performance was one of the weakest of the night. Right from the start, those country songs were infused with an unnecessary hard-rock vibe with Halestorm’s version of “Boot Scootin’ Boogie,” and that vibe flowed throughout much of the performance, such as HARDY’s rendition of “Hillbilly Deluxe.” Brothers Osborne, blues-rocker Marcus King, Brad Paisley and Luke Bryan were also part of the tribute, but overall, it just didn’t quite match up to other B&D tribute performances we’ve seen over the years.

Mumford & Sons and Chris Stapleton teamed up for a rendition of their collaboration “Here,” turning in a pleasant and crowd-pleasing performance, even if it wasn’t a standout of the evening.

More than three decades after earning a career-defining hit with “Don’t Take The Girl,” newly-minted Country Music Hall of Fame member Tim McGraw offered his first CMA Awards performance of the song. Seated on a stool, and donning a suit and his signature black cowboy hat, McGraw conveyed the song’s tender, heartbreaking story with a sincere, if slightly too rugged at times, vocal delivery and highlighting the song’s timeless message.

Moroney was nominated for two trophies at the CMA Awards, including song of the year for her song “Beautiful Things.” She brought that tender ballad to the CMAs stage, performing in the middle of the crowd, while seated on a flower-covered window pane setting that rose over the audience during the song, as she does in her headlining concerts. A sweet performance of a heartfelt song.

Vocal group of the year winners The Red Clay Strays turned in a soulful performance of “Revival” from their album Grateful. Lead singer Brandon Coleman’s voice as powerful as usual, while the rest of the group supported his vocals with a refined, jamband vibe.

Texas-born, powerhouse singer-songwriters Kacey Musgraves and Miranda Lambert teamed up to perform “Horses & Divorces,” a pointed track about letting bygones be bygones (from Musgraves’ Middle of Nowhere album). This track simmers with both wit and vulnerability, and their honest deliveries are undeniable. At one point, Musgraves and Lambert shared a shot during the stone-cold country track, sharing a hug as the song concluded.

Luke Combs has become one of country music’s most consistent vocalists and performers, and his CMAs performance was no exception. Donning his signature black shirt and baseball cap, he performed “Miss You Here,” from his album The Way I Am, which earned a nomination for the CMA’s album of the year. His vocals were top-notch and his band was tight-knit, delivering the high-caliber style of performance he’s known for.

Vince Gill opened the show by singing a heartfelt, piano-backed rendition of “I Will Always Love You” to honor late music icon Dolly Parton. He was then joined by Wilson, with Gill adding harmony. Straightforward and very touching, the moment made for a special tribute to the beloved Parton.

Stephen Wilson Jr. and Chris Stapleton teamed up on “I’m A Song,” a look at the power of music in shaping and sustaining memorable moments. Both Wilson Jr. and Stapleton have powerful voices, making for a distinctive vocal pairing.

Ella Langley led the CMA nominations this year with nine nominations and made her way to the winner’s circle many times during the evening. She also turned in one of the evening’s top performances with the fan-favorite “Loving Life Again.” The set was outfitted to look like a dreamy, dusky field, while Langley was accompanied by strings, lending more depth to the song’s peaceful, hopeful feel.

In addition to hosting the CMAs, Wilson debuted new music, with “Just a Woman.” Wilson is known for strong-willed anthems such as “Heart Like A Truck” and “Wild Flowers and Wildhorses,” but this performance marked one of her most soulful, passionate ballads yet. Donning a flowing dress and her signature hat, the newlywed Wilson sang of romantic, passionate and complex love and delivered a soaring vocal that reminded everyone just why she’s a multiple CMA entertainer of the year winner.

Zach Top used his CMA Awards performance to introduce a new song, “Moonshine.” The rollicking track was filled with his signature sizzling guitar licks and retro country flair. The set leaned into the song’s “barn burner” pace, as the massive screen behind Top shone with images of a barn ablaze. Then, he welcomed Dierks Bentley for a tribute to the late country singer John Anderson, with a rendition of Anderson’s 1983 hit “Swingin’.”

Miranda Lambert and Ella Langley each performed twice on the CMAs, this time teaming up with one another for the premiere performance of their collab “Snakeskin Boots,” featured on Lambert’s Crisco album. The two were clearly having fun during their playful, sultry performance and the groovy energy was palpable.

Tucker Wetmore took the show’s energy to a new level, backed by a horn section as he grooved and commanded the stage for a performance of his own song “Who Told You That.” Then, he settled in (momentarily) at a piano and delved into a cover of Hank Williams Jr.’s “All My Rowdy Friends Are Coming Over Tonight.” He brought a Jerry Lee Lewis-style energy as he kicked the piano seat away and pounded the keys, as a ring of fire surrounded him and lit up the piano, too. He far and away turned in one of the evening’s most energetic, memorable performances, showcasing a star-making sense of showmanship.

Thirteen years after her previous CMA Awards performance, one of the world’s biggest music stars returned to her country roots, as Taylor Swift performed “I Knew It, I Knew You” (from Toy Story 5). Her effervescent performance began with Swift walking through the CMA’s backstage area, and greeting artists including Lainey Wilson, Eric Church, Little Big Town and Brad Paisley along the way, before Bill Anderson handed Swift her guitar and she made her way to center stage. Swift’s always entertaining, fun and theatrical approach to performing was a perfect fit for the song, as both the staging and her outfit paid homage to the movie. Again nodding to her country music roots, she dipped into her country catalog by performing “Our Song” (which she performed on the CMA Awards stage in 2007) before returning to “I Knew It, I Knew You,” backed by guitars and horns, making it the most charismatic, memorable performance of the night.
