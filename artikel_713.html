Arsenal showing bluntless in attack - but Man City have problems too

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Arsenal and Man City both slipped up in the Champions League in midweek ahead of their big Premier League meeting this weekend; watch Arsenal vs Man City live on Sky Sports Premier League this Sunday, kick-off 4.30pm

By Sam Blitz at the Emirates Stadium, Adam Bate at the Etihad Stadium

Arsenal face Manchester City in a blockbuster Premier League contest this weekend, but in recent days and months, both teams have issues to overcome.

Arsenal have drawn back-to-back games against Newcastle and Borussia Dortmund, with Mikel Arteta's side throwing away comfortable half-time leads in both matches.

City, meanwhile, come into the game on a run of just one win in their last six Premier League and Champions League matches.

The game could have a huge impact on the Premier League title race, with Arsenal hoping to open up a nine-point lead over Enzo Maresca's side, who in turn could close the gap to the reigning champions to just three points with a win.

Sky Sports reporters Sam Blitz and Adam Bate were at Arsenal and City's draws in midweek and analyse their respective issues...

Sky Sports' Sam Blitz at the Emirates Stadium:

Arsenal have the most 1-0 wins out of any Premier League team since Mikel Arteta took over the club in December 2019. It's all well and good - until teams start making it 1-1.

And in the last two games, they have. And Arsenal only have themselves to blame.

After the Gunners' 1-1 draw with Borussia Dortmund, Arteta was convinced that their recent draws against Newcastle and the Bundesliga leaders are not comparable.

He claims Arsenal played well in this Champions League stalemate - and repeated on several occasions that Dortmund benefited from an "element of luck". There's an element of truth to that - the woodwork was struck three times in the second half.

But Arsenal had thirty shots against Dortmund - the most they've ever had in a Champions League game. Just four of those shots were on target. Seven shots came from within the six-yard box, no goals came from them.

And it's costing them points. Because there has been a pattern in Arsenal's last two matches. Dominate the first half, only take a 1-0 lead, start the second half slowly and then end up drawing the game.

Out of the 2.2 worth of Expected Goals Arsenal missed in the game, 0.96 of that tally came in the first half. From their 13 shots that didn't lead to a goal, they should have taken a bigger advantage.

It invited Dortmund back into the contest when there shouldn't have been one in the first place. Like Newcastle did at the weekend, the Bundesliga leaders sorted their game plan out in the half-time break and came out firing.

Arteta is right that the Newcastle and Dortmund games are different because Arsenal rallied to seize dominance of the game right after the German side's equaliser.

But once again, the conversation sits around the holes in Arsenal's attacking quality, particularly at left wing and up front. After Gabriel Martinelli and Gabriel Jesus left the club this summer without being replaced, Arsenal are still searching for attacking inspiration at left wing, with neither centre-forward in a goalscoring run of form.

Christos Tzolis is still waiting for his first Arsenal goal, Eberechi Eze is lacking consistency and Viktor Gyokeres backed up his early strike and impressive all-round display by creating doubts again by missing a late sitter.

At the start of the season, Gary Neville questioned whether Arsenal had the attacking player capable of delivering the Champions League. The draw to Dortmund backed that claim up.

Arteta struck a calm and positive tone after the Dortmund draw. It was a figure who knows his team have a six-point lead at the top of the Premier League, and will go on to qualify for the knockout stages of the Champions League.

But if these traits continue, it can all slip away quickly in this busy part of the season. If Arsenal don't beat Real Madrid, their top eight spot will be under threat.

Last season, they also benefited hugely from getting a top two spot that meant the second legs of their entire knockout stages were at the Emirates. PSG and Real can open up a bit of a gap on Arsenal in the coming weeks.

And up next, it's Manchester City at the Emirates. Enzo Maresca's side tend to score in most games. A 1-0 lead may not be enough.

Sky Sports' Adam Bate at the Etihad Stadium:

Move the ball quicker, yelled one fan from behind the press box. Press them, screamed a youngster in front. They were not wrong. Manchester City still have the individual quality to entertain the Etihad Stadium but they are also a frustrating watch at present.

City have picked up just one point from their last four Premier League games and while they did defeat RB Leipzig 3-2 in their previous Champions League outing, they let in two more in this draw with Napoli. It is 10 games now without keeping a clean sheet.

"Off the ball, the team was good from my point of view," said Enzo Maresca in the press conference afterwards, presenting a positive spin. "We did not concede a lot. The problem is that they were clinical. We created many chances." Not everyone agreed.

"It is not good enough," City captain Erling Haaland had told TNT Sports just before. "We let them in too easily and were unable to control or attack the game as we want." Tellingly, the striker then added: "When you do not win you start to question everything."

Maresca is being questioned. He has stressed that he is months into his time here not years and it is evident that he is still searching for the solutions. Finding the best role for new signings Elliot Anderson and Enzo Fernandez is something that he is still tweaking.

Against Napoli, it was Jeremy Doku's spark off the bench that helped to bring the first equaliser and Rayan Cherki's class that delivered the second. But it was all the bits in between, with and without the ball, that will concern the Manchester City supporters.

Rasmus Hojlund got the better of Marc Guehi and Gianluigi Donnarumma as Napoli were rewarded for pressing City high up the pitch. Noa Lang converted Leonardo Spinazzola's right-wing cross on the counter-attack - highlighting another weakness.

Maresca turned on his heels and looked to the skies in exasperation at that one because it is a recurring issue for City. They remain susceptible to the breakaways even when he loads the midfield in attempt to prevent it. Losing duels is another theme.

Sluggishness is an easy accusation. Haaland was largely anonymous, unable to get into the game. But that is because City are yet to find a pressing structure that works for them. Even if Haaland does close down, his team-mates are not necessarily following.

As had happened against Fulham at the weekend, Napoli were able to have good possession of the ball, something that was once unheard of for visiting teams at this stadium. That aura was already fading under Pep Guardiola but it is truly absent now.

Maresca is an excellent coach and the structure at Manchester City remains such that he can expect to be given time to figure all this out. But as he himself has previously acknowledged, there is not just one thing that is undermining this team right now.

The loss at Aston Villa was not the same as the home reverse to Brighton. Fulham approached it very differently to Nottingham Forest. Both got results. And now Napoli, in just one game, showed two different ways to undo Maresca's side. Problems to solve.
