Scattered Spider was once called "one of the most dangerous financial criminal groups" by Microsoft cybersecurity researchers. The U.S. has now closed the case against five of the group's members, sending the final defendant to prison for more than half a decade.

British Scattered Spider member Tyler Robert Buchanan has just been sentenced to six years in prison, according to the DOJ. Buchanan, who is 24 years old, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud and aggravated identity theft.

The five Scattered Spider hackers took part in a massive phishing campaign which targeted employees of major technology companies, telecommunications companies, and entertainment companies between September 2021 and April 2023.

During this time, Scattered Spider carried out a massive phishing scheme where they posed as employers or their IT providers, spamming employees with text messages that urged them to log in to phishing websites. Once the victims logged in, Scattered Spider hackers weaponized these stolen credentials in order to access employees' accounts and their employers' computer systems. The hackers took confidential business information and individuals' credentials and private data.

Using this information, Scattered Spider also deployed SIM-swapping attacks which resulted in the hackers stealing at least $11.6 million from cryptocurrency wallets.

A separate breach linked to Scattered Spider disrupted MGM's hotel and casino operations in September 2023. MGM estimated the disruption reduced its quarterly property earnings by about $100 million.

Buchanan received the second-longest sentence of the five hackers. Noah Michael Urban is currently serving 10 years. Joel Martin Evans received a two-year sentence. Ahmed Hossam Eldin Elbadawy and Evans Onyeaka Osiebo were both sentenced to 45 months.
