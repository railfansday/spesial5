Pennsylvania settlement shows where mortgage joint ventures can run into RESPA trouble

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Pennsylvania Attorney General David Sunday reached a proposed settlement with Bright Financial Group, Conquest Mortgage, affiliates, and owner Barry Newhart. The decree includes a $65,000 payment, RESPA compliance and monitoring requirements, and a potential additional $100,000 penalty for violations.  AI Summary

Pennsylvania Attorney General David Sunday has reached a settlement with Bright Financial Group, Conquest Mortgage and several affiliated mortgage businesses and their owner, Barry Newhart, over alleged violations of federal and state consumer protection laws involving mortgage referrals and affiliated business arrangements.

The proposed settlement was announced last Tuesday in U.S. District court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania. According to the filing, the defendants have agreed to pay $65,000 to the state as part of the settlement.

Beyond the financial implications, the settlement also states that the defendants must comply with the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA), which means they are not allowed to be apart of any referral-based kickbacks or compensation arrangements and they must ensure ownership interests and profit distributions for any joint venture are not tied to referral activity. The defendants are also require to establish compliance-monitoring systems, provide sworn compliance reports to Pennsylvania, maintain detailed business and transaction records, disclose information about businesses they own or control, and cooperate with state monitoring, including employee interviews and additional reporting requests.

Notably, the settlement does not result in any of the defendants losing their license and the settlement amount is not tied to any wrongdoing or harm to anyone. However, if the defendants are found to be in violation of the settlement terms, the settlement includes a potential additional $100,000 penalty.

Originally filed in January of 2025, against Newhart, Bright Financial Group LLC, Conquest Mortgage LLC, Flagship Home Loans LLC, Legacy Mortgage Partners LLC, Nittany Home Loans LLC, MCT Financial LLC, Newhart Holdings LLC and Conquest Holdings LLC, the lawsuit alleged violations of Pennsylvania Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law and the federal Consumer Financial Protection Act, based on alleged violations of Section X of RESPA.

The complaint claimed that Newhart  “controlled, directed, supervised or otherwise participated in the actions and practices alleged herein through ownership, management, and operational control over the other Defendants.” According to the complaint, Newhart and the other defendants transferred “things of value,” specifically underpriced, nonvoting stock in Newhart-owned mortgage brokerages to real estate agents and brokers, who in return referred their clients back to the Newhart-owned mortgage brokerages. Additionally, the complaint alleged that the defendants also paid for event tickets and “expensive dinners” for the real estate professionals, which when combined with the profit distributions, “surpassed $500,000 and potentially amounted to close to or more than a million dollars in total.”In the settlement, the defendants do not admit to any wrongdoing, including alleged violations of RESPA or consumer protection statues.

In an emailed statement, Francis Riley, a partner at Saul Ewing, which represented the defendants in this lawsuit, told HousingWire the thanks to this settlement “Conquest, its affiliated Pennsylvania mortgage brokerage operations, and their current and prospective investors may now proceed with confidence that the company’s affiliated business model, capital and ownership structure, and day-to-day operations are in compliance with RESPA and applicable Pennsylvania law.”

“I and the team of Saul Ewing lawyers who defended and assisted in bring this disputed to final resolution — which spanned more than six years — are pleased that companies and their investors, including Mr. Newhart, will not be able to continue their focus on delivering mortgage loan products and services in their communities,” Riley said.

He added that for the broader settlement services industry, this judgment offers “an implicit affirmation of” compliant affiliated business arrangements.

“The resolution also addresses previously less-defined aspects of compliance, particularly the parameters for determining compliant capital contributions, ownership interests, and the redemption of those interests,” Riley said.

However, he noted that the settlement does not create entirely new standards and that the compliance approaches outlined in it “have long been recognized and implemented by compliant affiliated business arrangements.”
