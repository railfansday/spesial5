More Than Half of U.S. Workers Say They've Been Gaslit at Work

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Resumebuilder.com survey finds workplace gaslighting is widespread, eroding trust and confidence among U.S. workers and prompting some to leave their jobs.

GUAYNABO, Puerto Rico, Nov. 26, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- ResumeBuilder.comâ¢, an easy-to-use online resume builder, has released the findings of its new Workplace Gaslighting Report. Among the more than 1,000 U.S. workers surveyed, 53% say they have experienced workplace gaslighting. Workers report that these experiences can affect their trust, confidence, and willingness to speak up, with some ultimately searching for another job or leaving their position.

More Than Half of Workers Say They Have Been Gaslit

Workplace gaslighting can take different forms, from denying what was said to disputing how events unfolded. More than half of workers (53%) say they have experienced behaviors they associate with workplace gaslighting.

"I Never Said That" Is the Most Common Gaslighting Phrase

Gaslighting often shows up in the responses workers hear when they raise concerns or disagree about what happened. 64% say they have had a dismissive or reality-denying phrase directed at them at work.

"When employees repeatedly feel that their experiences are being dismissed or denied, it can be difficult to maintain trust in the people around them," says Stacie Haller, ResumeBuilder.com's Chief Career Advisor. "Over time, employees may become more guarded and less willing to speak openly when they no longer feel that their concerns are being taken seriously."

Coworkers Are the Most Commonly Identified Gaslighters

Gaslighting can come from different people across an organization, from colleagues to managers and customers. Coworkers are the group employees point to most often, at 36%.

Direct managers follow at 18%, while customers/clients and senior leaders each account for 6%. Another 3% say an HR representative was most often involved.

Gaslighting Is Damaging Trust and Silencing Workers

The effects of workplace gaslighting can have a lasting effect, shaping how employees communicate, who they trust, and how they feel about their work.

Workers also believe workplace gaslighting has become more prevalent in recent years. Nearly 6 in 10 (59%) say it is more common than it was five years ago, with 27% calling it much more common and 32% saying it is somewhat more common. Meanwhile, 35% believe its prevalence has stayed about the same, and 6% say it has become less common.

"If gaslighting becomes more common in a given work environment, it can undermine trust and become a retention issue," says Haller. "Employees may ultimately feel that moving on is better than staying in an environment where they no longer feel comfortable or supported."

The findings presented are based on a nationally representative survey commissioned by ResumeBuilder.com and conducted via Pollfish from August 7, 2026. The survey collected responses from 1,002 U.S. workers and examined experiences and perceptions related to gaslighting in the workplace.

Respondents answered different types of questions, including yes/no, frequency-based, multiple-choice, and select-all-that-apply questions. The survey explored specific experiences associated with workplace gaslighting, including having events denied or distorted, being blamed for things respondents did not do, and being told they were "too sensitive" or remembering events incorrectly. Respondents were also asked about who was involved, how these experiences affected them, and whether they believed they had personally experienced workplace gaslighting.
