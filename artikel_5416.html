Bitcoin snapped a seven-week holding above the 50-week moving average (MA) with a weekly close at $74,968. According to the latest Galaxy Research report, BTC settled 0.7% under this key line, which now sits at $75,488.

Alex Thorn, the firm’s head of research, calls it a fakeout. The history is on his side: it's standard Bitcoin mechanics to slip back under the 50-week MA right after a bear-market bottom, and it's what shapes the structure of the four-year cycle. It has happened exactly twice before.

Losing the trendline again right after reclaiming it is how the biggest cryptocurrency has always behaved. Previous cycles played out like this:

The current chart is running the same script. From the $123,524 peak in October 2025, the coin bled 51.8% into the June cycle bottom at $59,517. A rebound then shoved the price back above the 50-week MA at $81,195. Today’s price is 7.7% off that September reclaim level, yet Bitcoin maintains a comfortable 26% cushion above the June bottom.

Thorn isn’t selling certainty. Two cycles is a thin sample, and bottoms only get confirmed in the rearview mirror. The margin for error is wide: in 2012, Bitcoin broke down again from 18% above the line, while in 2015 it held from just 8% above.

“Bitcoin just closed below its 50 week moving average after holding above it for 7 consecutive weeks. Two other times (2011-12 & 2019-20) btc closed below its 50w after reclaiming its post-bear bottom. Neither of those times did the bear make a new lower low (weekly close data),” the analyst notes.

One level kills the bull case for this 4-year cycle: $59,517. Galaxy Research says on-chain metrics, whale positioning and market sentiment all point to a consolidation that is technically ripe for stacking on spot. The technical case stays alive as long as Bitcoin reclaims the 50-week MA and holds it within the next month.
