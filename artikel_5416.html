The Dow falls 337 points as Home Depot weighs and the Nasdaq holds flat before Nvidia’s earnings

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Home Depot stock fell close to 2% after a CEO change, while chip stocks kept the Nasdaq flat before Nvidia reports after the bell

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 337 points, or 0.7%, on Tuesday as Home Depot $HD stock dropped close to 2% ahead of Nvidia $NVDA's quarterly earnings report. The S&P 500 slipped 0.2%, and the Nasdaq $NDAQ Composite was little changed.

Home Depot's decline followed word from the retailer that a new chief executive will be stepping into the role, according to CNBC. The stock's move weighed on the Dow and overshadowed quarterly results that beat Wall Street expectations.

Chipmakers helped the Nasdaq hold steady, leaving it close to unchanged. Marvell Technology stock rose 2%, while Arm Holdings and Broadcom $AVGO stock each gained 1%. Semiconductor names rose on a report that Anthropic planned to set its IPO price higher than the $70 to $78 range currently marketed, according to CNBC.

Nvidia is due to report fiscal third-quarter results after the bell Tuesday, Nov. 17. Its shares are down almost 24% so far in November, on track for the steepest one-month drop since April 2022, according to CNBC. Nvidia stock showed little direction in the opening hours of the session.

Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO last week following reports of two extramarital affairs, one of them with a woman suspected of spying for China, according to CNBC. Colette Kress, the company’s interim CEO, is expected to pull back on Nvidia’s dealmaking, according to The Wall Street Journal. Nvidia is projected to deliver yet another quarter of robust revenue gains.

Chip stocks in Asia declined Tuesday after the Commerce Department unveiled a rule meant to curb exports of chips made by Nvidia and other American firms to China. European stocks also fell.

Sales at U.S. retailers and food service businesses grew 0.2% in October to $748.2 billion, matching forecasts, according to CNBC. Excluding gas and autos, retail sales were up 0.6% compared with September, topping the 0.4% increase Wall Street had forecast. Import prices were also due Tuesday.

The Dow gave up over 700 points on Monday, a 1.4% decline, while the S&P 500 fell 1.9%. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.5%, marking its steepest single-session drop since Nov. 5, according to CNBC.
