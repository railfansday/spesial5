West Ham told to repeat Brighton & Brentford magic for 'niche' Luca Weinhandl transfer

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

West Ham United have had quite a rocky introduction to life back in the Championship, sitting outside the play-offs completely after 15 games.

The Hammers came down to the second tier with some of the best individuals that the division has ever seen, and they've proven their quality going forward, with their 37 goals an EFL best and a couple of six-goal hauls already.

However, there are some concerns about the overall balance of the side and a lack of a strong spine in the squad. Scott Parker has replaced Nuno Espírito Santo to try and fix that, and the international break couldn't have come at a better time to try and implement an identity.

The upcoming January transfer window can help with that, too, with a focus on bringing in players who can make a long-term impact in the side, both in the rest of this season and then in what is hoped to be Premier League campaigns in the future.

With 2026 drawing to a close, fans across the EFL are starting to think about what an ideal transfer window would look like in January. For West Ham, FLW's Hammers fan pundit, Ryan Horn, wants to see the spine of the midfield fixed.

West Ham broke the Championship transfer record by signing midfielder Arne Engels from Celtic for £22 million, but in terms of out-and-out defensive options, they don't have many available.

Tomas Soucek is still on his way back to full fitness, but he turns 32 in February, while Edson Alvarez hasn't been seen all season and, up until Parker's first game in charge at Cardiff City, neither had Soungoutou Magassa.

"Hopefully, by the time January comes, we'll be a lot closer to the top of the table, but there is a midfielder I'd really like us to sign, who'd solve all our issues," Horn said.

"With the future of Magassa and Alvarez in doubt at the moment, and with Soucek only just coming back from injury and getting on a bit in age, our midfield is lacking. There's this young midfielder I really like called Luca Weinhandl, who plays in the Austrian Bundesliga for Sturm Graz."

2009-born Weinhandl has been one of the key parts of Fabio Ingolitsch's side since he took charge on January 1st this year. The 17-year-old was handed his debut by the current boss, and this season, he's been one of their most consistent players.

He's started all but one league game for Sturm Graz, and all but one in their Europa League campaign, too, assisting in their 1-0 win over Marseille last month.

"He's 17, six-foot-three and doing brilliant there," Horn continued. "He'd just be one of those sort of niche signings that clubs such as Brighton and Brentford pick up out of nowhere and turn into £70 million, £80 million players.

"Bournemouth are buying players for good money now, but they, like Brighton, are competing at the top and in Europe, while Brentford have had a lot of good seasons in the Premier League now.

"But it's the modern-day football approach, and one that West Ham need to take. Weinhandl would be that kind of player."

With Weinhandl not turning 18 until mid-January, he has the entirety of his career ahead of him. If he continues on his current path, he could end up playing right at the top of the game.

The midfielder is one of the tougher-tackling engine room men in the Austrian Bundesliga this season, winning the most duels in the division and sitting inside the top 13% of defensive midfielders for tackles won, according to FotMob.

However, he's also right near the top for the number of dribbles he's completed and the number of fouls he's won. He has plenty of strings to his bow already, and could be a vital player in the future of any football club. Horn hopes it's with West Ham.

"He could form a solid spine next to Engels, or Kante and Magassa in front of him and be a key part of it for years to come, and then we don't have to worry about our midfield every single year," the fan pundit continued.

"Last year it was overrun, then we sold Potts, and this year, we have key players who have first-team and Champions League experience not being picked because of off-field issues.

"Having a younger player coming in, under Parker and [Mark] Noble, as well as Soucek, [Jarrod] Bowen and other experienced players, it'd be a dream. He's definitely one I'd sign. I don't think we will, but that's my realistic pick."

While Weinhandl would be a brilliant pickup for any English side looking to grow and develop a young midfielder, the 17-year-old may already have too much potential to be signed by a team in the Championship.

Murmurs in mid-September had the Australian linked with moves to Aston Villa, Brighton and Arsenal, so if he did move to the second-tier, it may be via a loan move after being signed by a side further up the pyramid.

Weinhandl isn't as much of a hidden gem anymore, having been called up to the Austrian senior squad for the first time for the ongoing break. Ralf Rangnick opted not to debut him against the Republic of Ireland, but could do so on Tuesday night against Israel.

The 17-year-old is a Graz native, too, and growing up through the academy to become a club legend there could appeal to him and seeing a possible move from a side like West Ham seem impossible.

He's definitely one to watch, and it wouldn't be a surprise to see him in the Premier League in the future, but for now, the Hammers may find it difficult to tempt this wonderkid over.
