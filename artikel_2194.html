Liam Scales' honest Celtic form verdict after avoiding 'nightmare' vs Kilmarnock

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

It wasn't pretty and it wasn't memorable, but as is usually the case under Martin O'Neill, Celtic got the job done.

The manager couldn't have picked a kinder fixture – at home against the side bottom of the table – for his team to return to winning ways in the Premiership. Yet Celtic made hard work of beating Kilmarnock.

Sam Johnstone produced a crucial double save inside the opening minute to deny Tom Lowery and if Ieuan Owen had connected cleanly with Erik Ring's cross in stoppage time, Celtic would be back in crisis mode. Alas, the hosts held on.

Liam Scales' first-half finish proved decisive on an afternoon permeated with missed chances for both sides.

The slender victory restored Celtic's four-point lead at the Premiership summit going into the international break, although Rangers have a game in hand.

They took care of Besiktas midweek, but O'Neill's reigning champions hadn't won in the league in a fortnight, losing to Falkirk and drawing with Dundee United, underlining the significance of Sunday's win.

"It was definitely important," Scales said. "Two games [in the league] without a win is bad enough, but three would have been a nightmare.

"We knew we needed that win and can go into the break and refresh. Some of the lads can rest and recover and hopefully we’ll come back in this form.

"I do think confidence is a massive part of our group and it was probably hit a little bit. The win against a top side like Besiktas has boosted us a little bit. There’s kind of a better mood around. We just want to keep adding good results."

Celtic's Liam Scales celebrates with his team-mates. (Image: Steve Welsh/PA)

Celtic entered the last international break following three consecutive losses to Rangers, Ferencvaros and Rangers again.

Things felt ominous, with O'Neill publicly considering his future after the second of those derby defeats.

But Celtic rallied and defeated Motherwell in their first game back. Things weren't smooth over the following eight matches, but as players return from injury, Scales feels positive about the future.

"We had Yang back on Sunday, Jota is obviously starting. These guys are getting back to full sharpness and getting minutes," he said. "Hopefully this will help and we can build on it.

"We know we haven’t been good enough between the last international window and this one. We’ve let a few results go and we’re not exactly where we want to be.

"It was just really important to win these last two games before the break and be in a good place for coming back."

Celtic leading the league was rare last season. Only in the dying minutes of the campaign did they return to the Premiership summit after Hearts knocked them off their perch back in September 2025.

"We always want to be on top of the table. We don’t want to be chasing," Scales stated. "We want to be a good arm’s length from the challengers.

"That is the goal all season – to stay top. It’s important to us but obviously there are so many games to play. You go through really busy spells and both teams will be challenged. But we are just happy where we are now.

"It hasn’t been spoken about – but it doesn’t need to be spoken about. We all know the standards at this club that have been set over a number of years.

"It doesn’t need to be discussed where we want to be – because it’s top of the table. Last season wasn’t what we wanted at all and we don’t want a repeat of that."

Scales scored his second goal in four games against Kilmarnock, connecting sweetly with Camilo Duran's looped cross.

The Irishman's effort sailed over opposition goalkeeper Nico Mantl and into the back of the net to break the deadlock in the 26th minute.

"It took an age to come down," Scales reflected. "I wasn’t sure if it was going to land on the roof of the net. I was just trying to guide it on target and luckily enough it went in.

"I don’t really have a target for goals – it’s more about clean sheets for me. But it’s nice. We’ve done a lot of work on set pieces, we’re getting the ball into the box more rather than going short and I suppose you always get a bit excited as a centre half when you go forward for a corner.

"It is nice to score and hopefully I can get a few more between now and the end of the season. I felt we should have killed the game in the second half. We were unlucky not to score.

"When you are only a goal up the atmosphere can get a bit nervy and that can translate onto the pitch. But I don’t think we were troubled too much. It was comfortable but it would have looked better if we’d scored some more."

For goals this season, Scales is now level with Kasper Hogh. The striker thought he'd ended his 16-game scoring drought on Sunday, only for VAR to intervene and disallow the strike for a foul in the build-up by Mika Baur.

Hogh didn't let his frustrations boil over, instead working even harder and offering a useful focal point in attack.

He earned the in-stadium man of the match award and was deservedly applauded off the field in the 71st minute. Scales is certain the goals will come, even if he snatched one from Hogh against Kilmarnock.

"He’s been brilliant. He keeps doing the right things and eventually it will fall for him," the defender added. "He was so unlucky with his goal being ruled out.

"And he told me he was right behind me with the goal I scored! If I’d known that I might have left it!

"It will fall for him. He’s a goal scorer and a good player. His all-round game was brilliant, he held the ball up really well. He’s just been unlucky. But his attitude has been brilliant.

"If he keeps doing the right things it will work out for him. He has an amazing attitude and that’s why I really believe he will be successful."
