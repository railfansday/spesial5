This Football Manager Lost 90 Pounds in 6 Months Thanks to This Swimming and Walking Routine

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The 64-year-old swapped the dugout for the pool and says he feels better than he has in years.

Scottish football manager Steve Evans is “feeling the best I've felt in years and years” after 90 pounds in just six months. After Rotherham dropped him in March last year, Evans decided to focus on improving his health before jumping straight back in at another club.

And it's worked wonders for the 64-year-old, who has meticulously stuck to the plan prescribed to him by health specialists and trainers. It hasn't been easy for Evans, but he's managed to improve his diet and increase his activity levels to a point where he now swims 2.5km every morning, cycles regularly and gets in plenty of steps by walking his dogs daily.

“Here we are six months or so later, six and a half stone down,” he told Sky Sports. “It has been incredibly hard work, but I am feeling the benefit now mentally and physically, feeling fantastic.”

After waking up and taking his dogs on a walk first thing in the morning, Evans then grabs his swimming trunks and heads to the pool at his local gym. While there, he'll swim at least 2.5km, though sometimes further if he's feeling good in the water. Evans also cycles on the exercise bike at the gym to supplement his training, as well as spending some time sitting in the sauna.

In total, Evans spends around two-and-a-half hours in the gym, while he ensures he keeps his diet healthy with lots of protein and a reduced calorie intake. And, if he still has some energy left at the end of the day, the 64-year-old will take his dogs out on another walk in the evening to add a few more steps to his daily total.

Evans couldn't just jump straight into that level of activity, however. He needed to take gradual steps to build up to the 2.5km he now swims everyday, after recognizing that he had started to neglect his health while working as a football manager.

“I came out of Rotherham and within a few days made some family decisions,” he told Sky Sports. “I sat down with the family and, despite the phone ringing from a couple of clubs, I made the decision to get healthy and fitter. It's something I hadn't done for a lot of years. I went to see some serious people and some specialists, a cardiologist and fitness people. We came up with a plan and my family did not think I would be able to do it.”

“I was worried about myself,” he admitted. “My doctor at Royal Papworth gave me a plan. That plan was obviously a substantially reduced intake of calories and a gradual fitness program to then go and work with a fitness coach.”

“It started off with swimming 10 lengths of a pool, which I found really tough at the time, and doing so many walks and so many jogs, to building up to now where I comfortably go and swim 70 to 80 lengths every morning. I had to make sure that I was mentally in tune with that—it's been brilliant and I'm feeling the best I've felt in years and years.”

Having worked so hard to achieve such impressive results, Evans isn't intent on stopping any time soon. Indeed, he's targeting even more weight loss and will look to add some strength and conditioning work in the near future to further contribute to his lifestyle change.

“It's a little bit more weight loss for another two-to-three weeks, and then hopefully by that time it'll be about conditioning, which will be another tough part to go through, but I'm looking forward to the challenge,” he added. “My body internally is actually in really good shape now after all the scans I had last week.”
