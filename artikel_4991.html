DOJ Targets 800 Defendants in Largest-Ever Scam Crackdown, Including Crypto Fraud

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The U.S. Justice Department said Tuesday that its largest-ever crackdown on scammers has produced actions against more than 800 defendants since March, with cryptocurrency investment fraud run out of Southeast Asian scam centers among its top targets.

The department announced the results of an anti-scam surge that followed President Donald Trump’s Executive Order 14390, signed March 6. Since then, prosecutors have acted in more than 400 cases, bringing new charges, extraditions, guilty verdicts and sentences across 85 federal districts.

U.S. agents also helped police in more than 10 countries raid foreign scam call centers and compounds that targeted Americans. Thirty defendants were extradited to face charges in U.S. courts.

“Predatory scams against the American people will not be tolerated,” Attorney General Todd Blanche said. “The unprecedented results of this surge are just the beginning.”

Crypto sits at the center of much of the effort. The State Department said the U.S. and Southeast Asian law enforcement it funds and trains have helped freeze more than $1 billion in stolen cryptocurrency and take down more than 1.4 million fraudulent websites and accounts.

The Justice Department’s Scam Center Strike Force, formed in 2025 to go after Southeast Asian crypto fraud, says its crypto seizure team has restrained about $1.08 billion in cryptocurrency from these schemes. The team is focused on returning stolen funds to victims.

The FBI’s Operation Blackout has restrained nearly $17 billion in illicit funds, seized more than 14,000 digital devices and helped dismantle more than 29 scam compounds across Asia, Africa and the Middle East, the department said. The FBI also helped free and repatriate more than 2,200 trafficked workers forced to run scams inside those compounds.

“The message to these scam syndicates is clear: if you target Americans, justice will find you,” FBI Director Kash Patel said.

In the past month alone, the Criminal Division filed or unsealed charges against more than 30 scam defendants, including those tied to Southeast Asian scam centers running crypto investment fraud schemes. Other cases involved inheritance fraud, jury duty imposters working from Georgia prisons and sweepstakes schemes aimed at elderly Americans.

The Secret Service said it has restrained more than $200 million in illicit funds during the surge. One of its cases identified a crypto investment conspiracy run from scam centers in Cambodia that targeted at least 174 U.S. victims. In October, a Chinese national was extradited from the Netherlands and arraigned in California over his alleged role.

In Moldova, State Department-backed raids in October shut down five call centers and led to the detention of eight suspected coordinators who allegedly ran fake crypto trading platforms. The SEC said that in the last year it charged three purported crypto asset trading platforms and four investment clubs with defrauding retail investors of more than $14 million.

Homeland Security Investigations said its fraud cases have led to the seizure or freezing of more than $120 million, including funds held in bank and cryptocurrency accounts.

The crackdown builds on months of pressure on overseas fraud rings. Trump planned to highlight the push against overseas crypto scams in October, and the U.S. government seized more than $580 million in crypto linked to Southeast Asian scam networks in February. In October 2025, prosecutors moved to seize 127,271 bitcoin tied to a Southeast Asian scam operation.

The stakes are large. Citing Gallup, the department said 10% of U.S. adults experienced a scam last year, directly or through someone in their household, and Americans lost an estimated $68 billion to scams in 2025, including crypto investment scams.

The department noted that an indictment is merely an allegation and that all defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.
