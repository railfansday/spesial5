Moderate To Severe Hot Flashes Due To Menopause Are Worth Treating. Work With Your Doctor To Develop A Plan

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A Bayer-paid spokesperson, OB-GYN, and menopause specialist explains why hot flashes due to menopause happen, how long they can last, and how to prepare for discussions about treatment options with your doctor.

Branded content for Lynkuet® (elinzanetant) 60 mg capsules. Keep reading for Important Safety Information.

For women, menopause is a natural stage of life and is diagnosed when a woman hasn’t had a period for 12 months and can no longer get pregnant. Menopause is not a disease or disorder—it’s a natural part of aging. It’s estimated that over 2 million women in the U.S. enter menopause each year. Almost 50 percent of women in their first year of menopause experience moderate to severe hot flashes.

Diana Bitner, MD, OB-GYN, is a Bayer-paid spokesperson and has been compensated for her time.

But, moderate to severe hot flashes don’t have to be something you simply endure, says Diana Bitner, MD, OB-GYN, a menopause specialist and Bayer-paid spokesperson. There are treatment options available, such as Lynkuet® (elinzanetant), an FDA-approved, hormone-free prescription treatment for the reduction of moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause.

It’s important to know that women who are pregnant should not take Lynkuet. Lynkuet can cause serious side effects, including central nervous system effects and daytime impairment. Don’t drive or do hazardous activities until these effects go away. Serious side effects also include increased liver blood test values, risk of pregnancy loss, and risk of seizure. The common side effects of Lynkuet include headache, fatigue, dizziness, feeling drowsy or sleepy, stomach (abdominal) pain, rash, diarrhea, and muscle spasms. Please refer to the full Important Safety Information below, and the Full Prescribing Information on Lynkuet.com/pi for more information. Women should speak with an HCP to determine if Lynkuet is right for them.

Here, Dr. Bitner shares what she feels women should know about moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause—and how to prepare for and discuss treatment options with your doctor.

This interview has been lightly edited for clarity and concision.

Dr. Bitner: A hot flash is a sensation of warmth in the face, neck, and chest, or sudden intense feelings of heat and sweating. They can happen day and night. Hot flashes are one of the most common symptoms of menopause. They are considered mild when they involve a sensation of heat without sweating, moderate when there is a sensation of heat with sweating but you are able to continue activity, and severe when there’s a sensation of heat with sweating that causes you to be unable to continue activity.

Dr. Bitner: I’ve heard some women say that they think their hot flashes are something to just “get through.” I’ve also had many patients who say that their moderate to severe hot flashes can come on unexpectedly and they were surprised that hot flashes can last several years during menopause. Menopause is a phase that all women will go through at some point in their lives, and many women will experience moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause. On average, moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause last 3.6 years after diagnosis of menopause, though not all women will experience hot flashes for that period of time or even at all. Individual hot flashes can last anywhere from 1 to 5 minutes, and may occur a few times a week or several times per day or night. Understanding this can help women better understand their hot flashes due to menopause and start a conversation with their healthcare provider.

Dr. Bitner: Women may sometimes feel hesitant to bring up their experience with moderate to severe hot flashes with their healthcare provider. But this is a conversation worth having—particularly when moderate to severe hot flashes are their primary concern.

Something I’ve seen in my practice is that many women come in for a routine visit where there’s a lot to cover. They may not always mention their hot flashes during the visit, but that could be because they are trying to manage hot flashes on their own.

Here’s the thing, though: For women in menopause who have moderate to severe hot flashes, there are treatment options available, like Lynkuet, a prescription treament for moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause. While some women are aware of treatment options, it isn’t until they speak with a provider that they realize they don’t need to manage moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause on their own. I want women to know that their hot flashes are worth treating, and I encourage them to talk to their healthcare provider so they can work together on a plan.

Dr. Bitner: It’s helpful to track the frequency and severity of your hot flashes, along with any specific moments hot flashes come on, and if you’re experiencing hot flashes during the day or night. This information may help your doctor determine what treatment options may be right for you.

Dr. Bitner: This is one part of my job that I really appreciate; I believe it’s paramount that women understand what’s happening in their bodies, and I get to serve as a translator of the symptoms occurring and the treatment options available. In the case of moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause, I often start the conversation by reminding women that they are not alone with this symptom. I then review their treatment options, including Lynkuet, a hormone-free prescription treatment for moderate to severe hot flashes due to menopause. In clinical trials, women experiencing moderate to severe hot flashes who took Lynkuet had fewer and less severe hot flashes, day and night, compared to those who took placebo.* Lynkuet also reduced the number of hot flashes in as early as one week.*

*Based on clinical studies measuring efficacy at 4 and 12 weeks. Individual results may vary.

Dr. Bitner: Women experiencing moderate to severe hot flashes should talk with their healthcare provider about available treatment options, including Lynkuet, to understand what may be right for them.

I also encourage women to speak up throughout the process. When patients feel heard and involved in decisions about their care, they’re more likely to find a treatment option that’s right for them.

Dr. Bitner: My message is that while hot flashes are a common symptom of menopause, you don’t have to navigate them alone. Understand your hot flash frequency and severity and talk with a healthcare provider to explore the treatment options that may be right for you.
