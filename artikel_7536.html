Federal judge says he can’t stop Trump’s ICC judge sanctions

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

President Donald Trump imposed the sanctions on judges after the ICC pursued investigations involving U.S. personnel in Afghanistan and Israeli leaders over the war in Gaza.

Trump declared a national emergency in a 2025 executive order with respect to any ICC effort to investigate, arrest, detain or prosecute “any United States person” unless the U.S. formally consents or becomes a state party to the Rome Statute, which established the ICC in 2002 to prosecute war crimes and crimes against humanity.

Judges Kimberly Prost, Solomy Bossa and Reine Alapini-Gansou of the ICC turned to the U.S. courts asking to be removed from the sanctions list, unblock their frozen property and bar Trump from enforcing measures against them. But U.S. District Judge Jesse Furman dismissed all but one of their claims and denied a preliminary injunction, finding “deference here is owed not only to the president but also to Congress.”

“In short, whatever the courtâs own assessment of the circumstances may be, plaintiffsâ allegations do not permit the court to hold that the president acted ultra vires when he declared a national emergency to address what he determined to be an ‘unusual and extraordinary threat, which has its source in whole or substantial part outside the United States,’ to the national security, foreign policy, or economy of the United States,” Furman wrote in a 37-page order.

Each judge was sanctioned over a different decision. Prost, of Canada, and Bossa, of the Republic of Uganda, joined a 2020 appeals ruling that allowed the ICC prosecutor to investigate suspected war crimes and crimes against humanity in Afghanistan. Alapini-Gansou, of the Republic of Benin, sat on the panel that approved arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former Defense Minister Yoav Gallant.

The trio of judges sued under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, U.S. Constitution and Administrative Procedure Act. Furman, a Barack Obama appointee, dismissed the due process and takings clause claims, but preserved the plaintiffs’ APA claim accusing the federal government of arbitrary and capricious sanctions designations. That count is premature, the judge said, since it must be decided on the full administrative record that was before Secretary of State Marco Rubio when he made the decision.

Furman noted there are three other cases in Manhattan federal court involving challenges to Trump’s executive order, but unlike the others, the trio of judges don’t bring First Amendment claims and are challenging the order in its entirety.

Those other, earlier lawsuits filed by U.S. human rights advocates resulted in rulings barring the administration from enforcing the order against Americans providing speech-based services to the ICC.

Trump signed Executive Order 14203 on Feb. 6, 2025, after the ICC pursued investigations involving U.S. personnel in Afghanistan and Israeli leaders over the war in Gaza.
