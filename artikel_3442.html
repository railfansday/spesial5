‘The Rings of Power’ Just Confirmed Our Biggest Theory About the Dark Wizard

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 Episode 3.

After years of speculation, The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power has finally granted fans an answer about the true identity of the notorious Dark Wizard (Ciarán Hinds). Ever since we first encountered this desert ruler, who holds sway over the eastern land of Rhûn, there have been many theories about which Istari he might be. Season 3 finally confirms that this Dark Wizard of the East is none other than one of the famed Blue Wizards sent to Middle-earth before Gandalf (Daniel Weyman) himself.

Season 3's reveal probably won't surprise diehard Tolkien fans, even if the particulars are a bit surprising. Previously, Tolkien wrote that only five Istari made their way to Arda in physical form. Given that viewers have already met Gandalf and know the fates of characters like Saruman the White and Radagast the Brown, that left only the two Blue Wizards. Between them, Tolkien wrote very little, with most of the material coming from Unfinished Tales, but The Rings of Power fills out the story in Season 3's third episode as Gandalf communes with the Secret Fire. "He was once called Pallando," Gandalf hears, "one of two wizards sent to protect these lands, but he wanted more. So, he betrayed and murdered his partner, an act so evil that it laid all of Rhûn to waste." Thus, it is due to Pallando's lust for power that the east was cursed, destined to never be made right.

Although the decision to kill off Alatar (the other Blue Wizard) is certainly shocking, this development (as well as their arrival in the Second Age) isn't beyond the realm of possibility when examining Tolkien's legendarium. In The Peoples of Middle-earth, the Blue Wizards are described as having been sent to Middle-earth likely around the same time as Glorfindel during the Second Age. "Their task was to circumvent Sauron," Tolkien explains, "to bring help to the few tribes of Men that had rebelled..." These tribes were meant to be some of those furthest from the Kingdom of Númenor. Interestingly, Tolkien concludes that, while the Blue Wizards most likely failed in their efforts, they had some hand in reducing Sauron's potential allies by the time of the Third Age. Even so, their failures would have major ramifications, giving the Dark Lord a true hold over that part of the map.

The cast and creators break down the show's most personal storyline yet for Tolkien's most iconic wizard.

"I suspect they were the founders or beginners of secret cults and 'magic' traditions that outlasted the fall of Sauron," Tolkien added in a letter collected in The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien. While there has never been anything written about one wizard killing the other, it's certainly not implausible, especially since neither of them appears in any form during the climactic events of The Lord of the Rings. After all, even Tolkien himself continued to shift and change their backstory, including their names, which he altered to Morinehtar and Rómestámo near the end of his life. Since Tolkien appeared to believe that both wizards failed in their task, Pallando's betrayal could absolutely work within the context of The Rings of Power.

However you slice it, the Dark Wizard's biggest reveal has been a long time coming. The Rings of Power spent way too long teasing the Stranger's true identity as Gandalf over the first two seasons (despite it being quite obvious), and it's clear that the show's creators have no intention of leading fans on any further here. Indeed, intersecting the lives of Gandalf — who, unlike the Blue Wizards, really shouldn't be around in the Second Age — and Pallando is certainly an interesting direction. His new divinely-inspired mission to redeem his wizard brother is a compelling idea when seen in light of Saruman's eventual corruption during the Third Age. As the Secret Fire tells the Grey Wizard, "Only together can you prevent the armies of Rhûn from joining Sauron."

Whether Gandalf succeeds or not, The Rings of Power continues to dive deeper into Tolkien lore this season, and it's about time. Some page-to-screen translations may leave a bit to be desired (Tom Bombadil should be much jollier), but the route the show is pursuing with the Istari seems to be the right — or at the very least, fascinating — direction. Would it have perhaps been more lore-accurate for Gandalf to be a Blue Wizard? Sure, but it's great to see the show finally exploring one of these lesser-known sorcerers. With the Harfoots out of the story, this reveal comes at just the right time to offer more hope for the future of Middle-earth.
