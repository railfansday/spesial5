A conserved flowering-time module underlies double-season cropping

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Adapting crops to intensive cultivation systems under climate change requires varieties that combine yield potential with optimized flowering and maturation1,2. In central and eastern China, barley has long been cultivated in a double-season cropping system, in which early flowering and maturation are critical for escaping presummer terminal heat stress and enabling flexible rotation with summer crops3,4. Here we trace the genomic footprints of 80 years of modern breeding in China using 505 representative cultivars and identify a flowering-time module defined by a single amino acid substitution in PHYTOCHROME C in combination with a winter-type VERNALIZATION 1 haplotype that underpins this adaptation. The derived allele accelerates heading and maturity, and its frequency has risen sharply in modern autumn-sown varieties. Using base editing in barley and wheat, we confirm causality and demonstrate that this flowering-time combination module, PhyC–VRN1, is functionally conserved across Triticeae. Our results identify and experimentally validate a breeding-relevant flowering-time module that has been selected during modern breeding to enable intensive crop rotations, as well as demonstrate how precision genome editing can be used to reconstruct such adaptive modules for climate-resilient crop design.

The photoreceptor PhyC functions as an upstream flowering regulator in grass species, promoting the expression of the photoperiodic responser PHOTOPERIODIC 1 (PPD1) and the flowering inducer FT1 (VERNALIZATION 3)21,22,23. The ancient haplotype PhyC-l, in combination with the winter-type vrn-H1 allele that requires vernalization, delays the transition from vegetative to reproductive growth, thereby preventing flowering (2nd panel, blue). In contrast, the spring-type Vrn-H1 variant, which carries a truncation in the first intron of VRN-H1 and eliminates the need of vernalization, enable flowering without cold exposure (1st panel, yellow). This allele in conjugation with PhyC-l is commonly adapted to spring-sown regimes in high-latitude or high-altitude regions. The East Asian-specific PhyC-e variant, which carries an F380S substitution at the PhyC locus and is consistently associated with winter-type vrn-H1 allele, promotes early flowering and supports adequate biomass accumulation (3rd panel, pink). The PhyC-e + vrn-H1 module allows barley to escape terminal heat stress, safeguard final yield, and fit to double-season cropping systems by leaving sufficient time for subsequent crops. This module has been widely selected in China’s Yangtze River basin. Notably, the haplotype combination PhyC-e + Vrn-H1 was not detected in barley natural germplasm (4th panel, dashed line). In this study, this novel module was generated either by base editing or by linkage breaking in the near-isogenic lines (NILs). It resulted in extremely early flowering and maturity, significantly reduced biomass, and undetected grain yield. This module holds potential for application in regions with extremely short growing seasons, such as those at very high latitudes or altitudes and at subtropical zones.
