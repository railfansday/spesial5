Techie turned his too-easy job interview into a trap

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

With the flick of a switch, he smoked out competent PC maintenance skills

Returning to work after the weekend can feel like voluntarily walking into a trap. To help readers feel a little freer, The Register therefore uses Monday mornings to serve up a new installment of "Who, Me?" It's the column in which we share your stories of mistakes, misjudgments, and occasional misanthropy.

This week, meet a reader we'll Regomize as "Marshall," who told us about a job interview he endured about 20 years ago.

The interview included a practical component he described as "Here's a PC, we've broken a few things in it, get it going."

Marshall quickly spotted some mundane faults, such as a loose PCI card and unplugged internal cables.

He felt the test was too easy. "One of the things I always checked was whether the power supply 110/230 switch was set for the UK," he told The Register.

Getting the PC running earned Marshall a job offer, but he couldn't resist suggesting a tougher test.

"I said to my future manager: 'Would it be a good thing to set the power supply to the wrong voltage as part of the interview test?' He said that wasn't fair."

Marshall stayed in the job for two years. A couple of months after he left, his former manager sent him a short email.

Marshall asked another former colleague what might have prompted that email.

"It turned out somebody – I don't know if it was an interview candidate – had blown a power supply by not checking the voltage it was set to."

"It wasn't anything to do with me," Marshall insisted. "No idea who it was."
