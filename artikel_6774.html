On the Couch with Valentina De Santis

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The third-generation Lake Como hotelier shares her favorite places to travel, shop, and dine—both in Italy and abroad.

If Eloise had the Plaza, Valentina De Santis had the Grand Hotel Tremezzo, the luxury Lake Como resort her family has owned and operated for three generations. A Como native who spent her early career consulting at Bain & Company, she began working at the property in an official capacity in 2010, the year of its centenary. “Unofficially,” she tells us, “I clocked in as a child, running through the corridors and convinced the whole hotel depended on me.”

In 2016, De Santis took over as CEO and, by 2022, she and her parents had bought a nearby 18th-century villa and transformed it into another five-star hotel, Villa Passalacqua. As De Santis’s portfolio continues to expand, she finds comfort in working with her family and the team that’s known her for her whole life: “People who grow with you, believe in what you are building, and care about it as much as you do, that’s everything.”

In our On the Couch survey, we asked De Santis about all the places, people, and pieces that inspire her. She also divulges a few of her best travel recommendations—both in Como and beyond.

Describe your studio in three words: Full. Of. Chaos.

Greatest Influences: My parents, my first and forever creative directors.

Favorite Eras of Design: The Belle Époque, when more was more and every room seemed to have a little champagne in its veins.

Favorite Sofa: A Daisy curved Italian midcentury sofa from the 1950s. It's beautifully sculptural, it's pink. It's Gio Ponti.

Maverick design style trick: Break one rule. If everything in a room is behaving, something is missing.

Most beloved buildings: Villa Necchi Campiglio in Milan. When I lived in the city, it was right on my doorstep, so I was there all the time. And always, always a little hello to the pink flamingos next door!

Architect you admire: Not the big name you’d expect, but the one closest to my heart, Ico Parisi. He made a real difference in my family’s story. He taught my parents how to look at art, and how to love it, and I grew up seeing the world through that same eye.

Destination Museum: The Peggy Guggenheim Collection in Venice and the Fondation Maeght in Saint-Paul-de-Vence. Before they were museums, they were the homes of great collectors, and it shows. You half expect the host to walk in and offer you a drink. With Casabianca, we hope to bring the same magic to my parents’ collection. Peggy, I hope you approve.

Go-to galleries: Lia Rumma in Milan. It has a strong point of view and never plays it safe. It is where we found one of Anselm Kiefer’s Women of Antiquity, which now lives at Casabianca. I’ll never forget my mother’s face the day we saw her; that was when we knew she was coming home with us.

Favorite Artist: Mark Rothko. My wildest dream is to have one in my living room, one of those enormous canvases where color doesn’t just sit on the surface; it explodes, then slowly pulls you in.

Vintage Piece of Your Dreams: An original Margherita by Franco Albini for Bonacina. Yes, I know, more from the ’50s! I clearly have a type. I love how sculptural it is, while the woven rattan keeps it feeling warm and relaxed.

Favorite getaway: Puglia. I have spent so many summers there with my family that it feels like a second home.

Hotels to Check Into: The Amalfi Coast is hard to beat. In my opinion, some of the best hotels in the world are concentrated in just a few miles, each one an incredible house. On top of that, they are all dear friends, so I would love to check into every one of them. But my favorite memory? Lemon linguine on the terrace of the Santa Caterina.

Best Shop for Gifts: Chez Dédé in Rome. Daria and Andrea are dear friends, and the co-authors of our Passalacqua book, and everything in their shop seems to come with a story. My favorite is their Lettera d’Amore necklace, a love letter you can actually wear.

Best Host/Hostess Gift: Something made with your own hands, or close enough! Olive oil from your grove, homemade biscuits, chocolate-dipped orange peel. Add a handwritten note, and it beats anything you could ever buy.

Entertaining Tips: Always have more food than you need. Italian mothers have taught me this much.

Style Icons: Lady Di. I’ve had a crush on her since I was a little girl, and honestly, I never got over it.

Fashion Designers: JJ Martin (La DoubleJ) for the extraordinary way JJ brings color, print, and joy into fashion. Emilia Wickstead for her beautiful, feminine silhouettes.

Style Tip: Go tone-on-tone. My friend Patrizia, who designs Moresi's collections, taught me there's nothing chicer than a head-to-toe single-color look.

Required Reading: Va' dove ti porta il cuore by Susanna Tamaro. It is the book I have turned to whenever I have had a big decision to make, and somehow it always brings me back to the same place: follow your heart.

Cocktail of Choice: A Bellini. It is hard to improve on a classic.

Favorite Restaurant: La Tirlindana on the lake for old-world magic. In Milan, I stay loyal to the classics: Da Giacomo and Savini never miss.

Must-Have Accessory: A great pair of unscratchable sunglasses. I live by a lake, so they are both a fashion accessory and a survival tool.

Watch to Wear: A Cartier Tank Louis Cartier in yellow gold. It has been chic for so long that it has nothing left to prove.

Favorite Jeweler: Felice Gabaglio in Como. I have known Marina and her family for as long as I can remember. My father used to take me there to choose jewelry for my mother, and I was always so excited to discover those little treasures with him. It is still where I go when I want something truly special. And now Marina’s daughters have launched JAM, a new contemporary line of their own. So now I have another very good reason to stop by.
