Delta Reserve Credit Card Review: Benefits Worth It For Delta Loyalists?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

American Express and Delta have had a close relationship for many years. As part of that, they issue seven co-branded Delta Amex cards. The Delta SkyMiles® Reserve American Express Card is the most premium of these cards, and in this post, I’d like to take an in-depth look at the product. The Delta Reserve Card is worth it if you’re a frequent flyer with Delta, or if you want to earn elite status through credit card spending. Let’s take a look at all the details of the card.

Let’s look at the Delta Reserve Card‘s welcome bonus offer, eligibility rules, and fees. All of these are probably top of mind if you’re considering applying for this card.

The Delta Reserve Card currently has the following welcome offer: You may be eligible for as high as 125,000 Bonus Miles after you spend $5,000 in eligible purchases on your new Card in your first 6 months of Card Membership. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Delta SkyMiles to be worth 1.1 cents each. That means to me, the 125,000-mile bonus is worth $1,375. While Delta SkyMiles is far from my favorite mileage currency, it does have quite a high floor value, so there are good ways to redeem Delta SkyMiles.

The welcome bonus offer on the Delta Reserve Card is “once in a lifetime,” so it’s not available to you if you currently have the card, or if you’ve had the card in the past. However, eligibility for the Delta Reserve Card (including the welcome offer) is unrelated to whether you have any other personal card in the portfolio. You’re also eligible for the personal card if you already have the business card, and vice versa.

You can read more about Amex’s general application restrictions here.

The Delta Reserve Card has a $650 annual fee (Rates & Fees). Obviously that’s on the high side, though you also get quite a few perks. You can add authorized users for $175 each, and authorized users get some card benefits, including airport lounge access and inflight savings on Delta.

If you spend on the Delta Reserve Card, the good news is that there are no foreign transaction fees (Rates & Fees), so you can efficiently use the card abroad.

While Delta SkyMiles is far from my favorite mileage currency, it does have a high floor value, so there are good ways to redeem Delta SkyMiles.

Delta One (business class) awards can be a great option, especially on roundtrip awards rather than one way itineraries. Using SkyMiles for shorter hops on Delta can be great, too. I’ve seen award flights between New York (LGA) and Orlando (MCO) for less than 20,000 miles roundtrip.

Partner award flights can be an especially good option if you focus on flights that don’t touch the United States, since flights that do are more expensive and get you less value for your miles.

You can redeem SkyMiles for flights on SkyTeam partners, like Air France, China Airlines, and Korean Air. You can also redeem SkyMiles on some airlines that aren’t part of SkyTeam but still partner with Delta, like LATAM and WestJet.

One of the primary things that sets the Delta Reserve Credit Card apart from other Delta Amex cards is the lounge access perks that it offers. The card offers Delta Sky Club® access, Centurion Lounge access, and even several Sky Club passes. How does that work?

The Delta Reserve Card offers 15 Delta Sky Club visits each Medallion year for the primary cardmember. Each visit is defined as all entries within a 24-hour period, including at different airports. In other words, if you have a connecting itinerary, you can visit multiple Sky Clubs at different airports while using only one visit allotment.

It’s possible to unlock unlimited Sky Club visits after spending $75,000 in purchases on your card in a calendar year. Once all 15 visits have been used, eligible cardmembers may purchase additional Sky Club visits at a per visit rate of $50 per person using the card.

In addition to the primary cardmember getting Sky Club access when flying Delta the same day, they also receive four Sky Club guest passes annually.

These can be used by the primary cardmember to guest others into the lounge, allowing you to skip the $50 guesting fee four times annually.

The Delta Reserve Card also offers complimentary Centurion Lounge access when traveling on a same-day Delta flight booked with the card. The primary cardmember is entitled to access, and bringing additional guests costs $50 each.

Centurion Lounges, for the most part, offer a significantly improved food and beverage offering compared to most domestic airline lounges in the United States, so this is a nice option to have.

The Delta Reserve Credit Card has a good bonus, though if I were just looking for the best welcome bonus offer, I would go for the Gold or Platinum version of the card, since the cards have significantly lower annual fees.

The reason you want to get the Reserve version of the card is because of the valuable long term perks that it offers. Above I’ve talked about how the Reserve Card can help you earn status and can help you access lounges, though there are lots of perks beyond that. Let’s take a look at some of those benefits.

Every year upon renewal of the Delta Reserve Card, you receive a roundtrip companion certificate valid within the United States (including Alaska and Hawaii), and to Mexico, the Caribbean, and Central America. This can be used for fares ranging from first class to economy, so the flexibility offered by that is pretty awesome, and for many will more than justify the annual fee.

You just have to pay the government-imposed taxes and fees for the companion. There are some things to be aware of when redeeming the Delta companion certificate, including:

Just for having the Delta Reserve Card, you receive a $200 Delta Stays credit annually. The way this works, you receive up to $200 in statement credits per calendar year when you use your card to pay for a new Delta Stays prepaid hotel or vacation rental booking on the Delta Stays platform.

While I’m not usually a fan of using airline portals to book hotel stays and vacation rentals, this could prove worthwhile, as you can use it for a hotel stay costing right around $200, if you wanted to. If used right, the value here is considerable.

Just for having the Delta Reserve Card, you receive up to $240 in Resy credits annually (Enrollment required). The way this works, you can receive up to $20 in statement credits each month after using your card on eligible purchases at U.S. restaurants that offer reservations on Resy.com and the Resy app, purchases made directly on Resy.com or in the Resy app, and purchases made via Resy Pay.

Just for having the Delta Reserve Card, you receive up to $120 in rideshare credits annually (Enrollment required). The way this works, you can receive up to $10 in statement credits each month after using your card on U.S. rideshare purchases with select providers, including Uber and Lyft.

Just for having the Delta Reserve Card, you get a first checked bag free. With the ever-increasing cost of a checked bag with Delta, this can really add up:

Depending on how many bags you’re checking, a few trips with a companion could pay for the card’s annual fee.

You can receive priority boarding just for having the Delta Reserve. This helps maximize your odds of finding space for your carry-on and getting to your seat sooner. To receive this benefit, you must ensure the primary cardmember’s SkyMiles number is linked to the reservation.

If you have the Delta Reserve Amex then you can save 20% in the form of a statement credit for eligible pre-purchased meals, and in-flight purchases of food, alcoholic beverages, and audio headsets, on Delta flights. Allow 8-12 weeks for the statement credit to post.

Delta has a Pay With Miles feature, where if you have a Delta co-branded credit card you can redeem miles for one cent each toward a Delta flight purchase (in increments of 5,000 miles per $50). That’s not the best deal in the world, though this is a feature that’s exclusively available to those with an eligible card.

Some people find this to be useful since they can redeem their miles toward flights, and then they still earn MQDs for taking those flights.

With the TakeOff 15 perk (exclusively for those with select Delta Amex cards), you can save 15% on the cost of a SkyMiles award ticket when traveling exclusively on Delta. Delta SkyMiles award pricing is dynamic, and generally you’ll find that each mile gets you a bit over a cent toward the cost of a ticket.

Saving 15% on these awards is valuable, and means that you’ll generally get somewhere around 1.2-1.3 cents of value per mile (or sometimes even more). In many cases this will end up being a better value than using the Pay With Miles option, so it’s a real perk of the card.

Having the Delta Reserve Card gets you priority for complimentary upgrades, assuming you have Medallion status. That’s because the card acts as a tiebreaker.

If you have the Reserve Card you get upgrade priority over other Medallion members within the same Medallion level and fare class. Given how competitive upgrades are, this can make a material difference in terms of whether you clear or not.

The Delta Reserve Card offers complimentary Delta First and Delta Comfort upgrades on eligible flights for non-elite members. You will be toward the bottom of the upgrade list, but there are lots of situations where this could help you score a good extra legroom seat, or in some cases even a first class seat.

Those with the Delta Reserve Card receive a Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credit. This benefit applies once every four years. This can save you big time both at security and immigration, and is something I highly recommend signing up for if eligible.

One of the great features of Amex cards is access to Amex Offers, which offers savings or bonus points on purchases with all kinds of retailers. There’s huge value to be had in holding multiple Amex cards so that you can get these offers on multiple cards and enjoy a variety of offers across cards.

Amex Offers could save you hundreds of dollars per year. You can access these offers by logging into your account and scrolling down to the bottom of your account summary page.

The Delta Reserve Credit Card has a steep annual fee, but the card also offers great benefits. The card offers perks ranging from lounge access, to a companion certificate, to up to two free checked bags, to upgrade priority, to all kinds of credits, to the ability to spend your way to elite status.

This card isn’t for the casual Delta flyer, but for someone who flies Delta moderately or often, I’d say this card is well worth considering.

If the Delta Reserve Amex sounds interesting to you but you are instead looking for a card for your small business, consider the Delta SkyMiles® Reserve Business American Express Card.

Most of the benefits of the card are the same. One significant difference is that the business card earns 1.5x miles on all purchases after spending $150,000 annually, which could be useful for big spenders. Furthermore, the business card offers a $250 annual Delta Stays credit, rather than a $200 annual credit.
