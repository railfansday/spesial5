Google Flights Explore is one of the best-kept secrets when it comes to finding cheap flights. You may have a basic familiarity with Google Flights in general, but once you know how to use Explore to find deals, it’s a whole other game.

It can unlock hundreds of dollars in savings for your next dream vacation.

Let’s dig deeper into the tool and how you can use it to plan your trips.

Google Flights Explore is a feature nested within Google Flights. You can gain access to it either by clicking the “Explore” button on the toolbar in Google Flights or by going directly to the Explore page.

Google preselects the location closest to where you’ve logged in (in my case, New York City). Then, it shows you the cheapest flights worldwide.

An interactive map lets you indulge your wanderlust. Whether you want to visit a nearby national park or see the Australian outback, the world is available at the click of a mouse.

If you know you want to get away on specific dates but don’t have a specific destination in mind, use Google’s open-ended search functionality to find flight deals within your window of availability.

Using Google Flights Explore is not complicated. Once you play with it a bit, you'll figure it out easily.

You can set your home base pretty much wherever you want. I mentioned that my search defaults to New York City, but for the sake of the examples below, I chose to search from Houston.

Google displays a highlights bar with a few popular destinations on the left side and an interactive map on the right. Clicking any of the cities pulls up the dates and prices; in this example, Google tells me that I can fly round-trip to Orlando for as low as $60 or to New York City for just $109. You’ll also see the lowest price Google finds for hotels on the left-side bar, next to the bed icons.

As you zoom out, the whole world opens up. Google tells me that, in this case, I can fly to Honolulu for $388 or to Tokyo for $895.

You can also move the map around to zoom in or out on a particular region that may strike your fancy.

But let’s say I had my eye on a trip farther afield — perhaps Paris. I can zoom in on Europe, and when I click the Paris pin, Google will show me flight options I can click to book on the traditional Google Flights page.

In this case, Google Flights Explore says that Paris is pricey from Houston at a whopping $987. That might have me looking at a getaway in London instead, where flights are as low as $692.

You also see that prices are currently “typical,” so there aren’t any screaming deals right now. I might hold off on booking in this case, but you see what a powerful tool this can be.

Google Flights Explore defaults to one-week trips within a six-month window, but you can change the parameters to search for other dates, including with its “Weekend” and “2 weeks” search options.

You can also click the “Specific dates” tab to enter exact travel dates and choose the number of passengers, cabin class (economy, premium economy, business or first class) and round-trip or one-way flights.

In this example, I’ve selected business class for a trip during Christmas (when I know I’ll be off work). Prices are pretty high right now, but the search could help me set up some flight alerts to monitor prices.

What if you’re looking to depart from a different overseas airport? Just put in the airport you want to travel from. In this case, I set my “home” city as Tokyo for a weekend trip in May; here’s what Google Flights Explore pulled up for me.

This could be helpful if, say, you’ve found a one-way award ticket to Asia but haven’t been able to find a good deal using miles to return.

You can also select specific airline alliances in Explore, but not individual airlines.

But there’s a way around it if you’re intent on a specific airline: Just start your search in traditional Google Flights, where you can select airlines. In this case, I chose only American Airlines. When I change the search destination to “Anywhere,” it only shows American flights on the Explore page. That’s a fun little hack.

As one more aside, you can conduct broader regional searches — such as Mexico or the Caribbean — if you’re targeting a general area but don’t know exactly where you want to go. Here’s an array of options from New York to the Caribbean; I’ve also selected the “Oneworld” filter to focus on American Airlines or other Oneworld partner flights. Flying to Barbados for $317 round-trip sounds good to me.

Here’s a similar search for Mexico from a home base in Seattle. I’ve eliminated any specific filters in this example to widen my options.

I like the sound of nine days in Cancun for just $378 round-trip. It even includes a hotel option for as low as $59 a night.

You can also use Google Flights Explore on your mobile phone. It functions nearly the same as the webpage version. In a search on my phone, I found flights from New York City to San Francisco for as low as $150 round-trip.

Google Flights Explore can also prompt you to select “When to visit” to triangulate the best weather and thinnest crowds.

We’ve touched on many of the most useful filters on Google Flights Explore, but aside from home airport, destination, number of passengers, one-way or round-trip tickets, class of service and dates, you can also click on “All filters” to view a litany of other options. These include the number of stops, “travel mode” (places available by car and plane or just by plane), price, destinations and the number of bags you can take. You can also search in various currencies and languages.

Once you go to Google Flights itself, there are even more options. In my searches, I tend to keep the filters unchanged to get the biggest picture possible — but, of course, your plans may vary.

Google Flights Explore can also help you plan your vacation. It’ll start by helping you pinpoint a destination with bargain flights; once you’ve decided on flights, you can use Google Flights Explore to find hotels as well. When you click on the hotel bed icon in your initial search, it takes you to a whole “Hotels” tab in Google (which deserves its own guide at some point).

I’ve stayed at the Hilton Cancun Mar Caribe All-Inclusive Resort — but at $504 a night, it seems pretty pricey. A better option might be the JW Marriott Cancun Resort & Spa, bookable via Priceline for just $319 a night.

Google Flights Explore is one of the handiest trip-planning tools out there. I’ve found it’s an invaluable research tool when I’m trying to figure out when and where to go; you can find cheap trips anywhere in the world by simply plugging in your home airport. By using it as a jumping-off point and triangulating with Google Flights itself, you’re sure to find the perfect vacation.

It’s a powerful tool and well worth investing some time exploring. Just be careful — it will no doubt also inspire wanderlust.
