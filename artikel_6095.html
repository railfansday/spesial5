Cruz turns tables on Democrats crying foul over GOP's 11th-hour judicial confirmation blitz

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Sen. Ted Cruz, R-Texas, responds to Sen. Cory Booker, D-N.J., during a Senate Judiciary Committee nominations hearing on Capitol Hill on Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026.

Sen. Ted Cruz, R-Texas, criticized the "hypocrisy" of his Democratic counterparts over their selective outrage stemming from Republican senators' push to confirm President Donald Trump's judicial appointees before Democrats take control of the Senate in January.

"And what did the same Senate Democrats who just gave sanctimonious speeches do? They rammed through 22 federal judges in November and December before leaving," Cruz said of Democrats, referring to their actions following the 2024 elections.

Cruz's remarks came during a Senate Judiciary Committee nomination hearing on Wednesday, where seven judicial nominees were introduced to committee. The Texas senator was responding to accusations from Sen. Cory Booker, D-N. J., who said Republicans were "rushing" the judicial hearings and in his 13 years in office, he has never seen the committee hold "back-to-back" hearings without the minority's consent.

"You know, you don't need a particularly long memory to recognize the hypocrisy in what Senator Booker said," Cruz said, comparing Booker's "lecture" of Republicans to the theatrics of Captain Renault in the movie "Casablanca."

"He is shocked, shocked to find gambling in this institution," Cruz said.

Cruz pointed out that after President Joe Biden lost the White House and Democrats lost control of the Senate in the 2024 election, Senate Democrats "rammed through" 22 federal judges before Republicans took control in January 2025.

"You know, there's an old adage that hypocrisy is the tribute that vice plays to virtue," Cruz said.

Booker argued that Republicans were disregarding the election results and suggested that the committee wait until the next term to continue with the nomination process.

"This is not a left or right thing because I've watched both parties do this in the 13 years that I've been here," Booker said. "I'm just simply saying that this is yet another step at a time where we could have paused and done this a right way."

But Cruz noted that Booker and every other Democrat on the committee voted in favor of approving all 22 judges before the new congressional term started in 2025.

In November 2024, then-Senate Judiciary Chair Dick Durbin, D-Ill., issued a statement after the election pushing for Biden's judicial appointees to be confirmed before the end of the congressional term.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) speaks during a news conference at the U.S. Capitol on September 30, 2025 in Washington, D.C. (Kent Nishimura/Getty Images)

"The Senate must confirm every one of these highly qualified, diverse nominees before the sun sets on the 118th Congress. The American people deserve nothing less," Durbin said in a press release at the time.

There are currently 27 federal judicial vacancies with 13 nominees still waiting for approval, as Republicans work to fill the seats. Sen. Chuck Schumer, D-N. Y., who is poised to become Senate majority leader when the new Congress convenes in January, vowed to block any of Trump's "crappy nominations" from being approved once Democrats take the gavel.
