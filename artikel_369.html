How Hinges deep-learning Most Compatible feature actually works, and how to train it

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Ever since its introduction in 2018, Hinge's "Most Compatible" feature has earned equal parts praise and scorn from online daters, who can't seem to decide if it's the best thing ever or a bad joke.

Even today, eight years after it was first introduced, Most Compatible is a marvel of mathematical engineering, beginning with its foundation: the Gale-Shapley algorithm (also known as the propose-and-reject algorithm), developed all the way back in 1962 to solve a (hypothetical) mating problem.

Hinge's variation of the algorithm works dynamically, constantly taking into account user preferences and choices to predict what kind of person you would most like to date. At the start of each new day, you will receive a Most Compatible profile suggestion that expires after 24 hours unless you interact with it.

But there's a massive catch here that too many Hinge users are unaware of: your own actions and inactions are factored into the equation, so if you feed the algorithm bad data, it won't work in your favor, and you'll rarely see a compelling profile suggestion. We reached out to Hinge to gain a better understanding of their algorithm from the inside, and they described it as a "recommendation system" that combines multiple algorithms, "using your stated preferences and your activity on the app," to adjust and refine its suggestions.

Here are three key tips from Hinge to help its recommendation algorithm work for you:

Too many Hinge users (and men in particular!) cast a wide net, interacting with as many profiles as possible, sending out Likes all over the place. We get it: the more-is-more approach has its appeal, but it actually hurts the algorithm's effectiveness, since it becomes harder to narrow down your preferences when you're too indiscriminate about who you like or interact with.

Or, as Hinge told us: "If you send too many likes, we can’t tell what you truly want. At the same time, if you rarely send likes, we don’t have enough information to learn from about who excites you. By engaging with others with curiosity and purpose, your recommendations meaningfully evolve alongside you."

Instead of being overly enthusiastic in your search, be intentional: take your time to vet profiles and interact only with people you can genuinely see yourself dating. You may not even necessarily know what these profiles might have in common, but rest assured the algorithm will be at work in the background finding the common thread and refining its suggestions for you in the future.

The default Hinge filters are very relaxed, but there's actually a Dealbreakers toggle hidden in the Dating Preferences section of your profile. The normal preferences (location and age range, for example) are taken into account but treated more like guidelines than hard limits. However, if you start enabling your Dealbreakers, Hinge will treat them as hard limits and never even show you profiles that don't match your preferences. As Hinge put it to us, "Hinge will only show you each other if you both meet the dealbreakers that either of you set, such as wanting kids. This helps you focus on people who feel like a real long-term match."

Most dating app users, out of sheer eagerness to start browsing local singles, make a half-hearted attempt to fill out their profiles, usually doing the bare minimum to meet the app's requirements. But Hinge offers an extremely generous array of profile fields, and the more you fill out, the more data the algorithm can use to do its matchmaking work.

And as Hinge pointed out to us, not answering a question is treated like a negative, actively shrinking your dating pool: "When you select 'prefer not to say,' it limits who can see you. From family plans and dating intentions to drinking habits, you're excluded from being shown to people who've set Dealbreakers around those compatibility factors."

We recommend you put particular focus on filling out Hinge's prompts in a thoughtful way, taking particular care to choose ones that reflect directly on your personality or interests

In Hinge's own profile guide, they offer important advice that is too often overlooked: ditch the clichés and one-word or low-effort responses, and take the time to give a personal answer to every question. And take the time to answer a voice prompt, as you'll be rewarded by the algorithm for doing so.

Finally, there's good evidence that Hinge's algorithm rewards profiles with refreshed photos, so if you haven't updated your main photos in a minute, follow their photo refresh guide to optimize your new pics.

If you follow the steps above, you should start to see the algorithm working more in your favor, and you'll look forward to each day's new Most Compatible suggestion.
