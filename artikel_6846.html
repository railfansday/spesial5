Go to any high-end hotel or luxury chain gym these days and you’ll see a NordicTrack treadmill with a massive screen, a fancy rowing machine, and maybe a shallow pool for a cold plunge. You also may see a glowing red box in place of the traditional steam room. That’s an infrared sauna, and it’s at the cutting edge of wellness tech. At the end of the day, nothing compares to sweating it out.

Before you tell me it’s not feasible or you don’t have the space or don’t want to tear up a wall, hear me out. Plenty of people have saunas in their homes. And with an infrared sauna, the process doesn’t require complicated plumbing and ventilation. Instead, it’s all electric and easier than ever to set up in your home. Instead of traditional water-based saunas, infrared saunas use infrared light to heat you. The ambient temperature won’t get as hot as steam-based saunas, but the far-infrared light will penetrate your body directly. In short, it won’t feel as hot, and you’ll still sweat.

For all these reasons, infrared saunas started to catch on as a home alternative to spending an extra half hour at your gym or going to the bathhouse. Many of them can fit more than one person, but you can opt for something a bit more snug as well. Some of them even have Bluetooth speakers. We’ve even found an option for those who truly don’t have the space. Call it an apartment-friendly solution. No matter your needs or restrictions, these are the best infrared saunas for your home.

First and foremost, the LIT Method BeautyBox is just a great sauna. No buts or caveats, it was good enough that we called it the best home sauna of 2026 in this year’s gadget awards. It uses the full spectrum of infrared (near, mid, and far), which adds a broader range of health benefits. Far-infrared is what causes the sweating, but the often left out mid and near wavelengths penetrate your skin and blood vessels for more comprehensive relaxation and recovery.

The BeautyBox comfortably fits two people. It’s crafted with care from Canadian hemlock and nontoxic adhesives, so you don’t worry about heating up microplastics and absorbing that into your skin. Esquire executive editor, Madison Vain, had the BeautyBox installed in her basement and it’s since become a major part of her routine.

“I cannot prove that my LIT BeautyBox kept me any healthier than, say, 30-minute meditation sessions or a regular wine date with friends,” she wrote. “But meditation doesn’t make you sweat, and as good as they say socializing is for the body, I think we can all assume heat therapy does a bit more.
