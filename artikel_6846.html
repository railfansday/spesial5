10 Sophisticated Gourmand Colognes Perfect for Winter

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Sugar and spice and everything nice—but not too sweet.

If there’s one thing you can trust us on, it’s that we’re always keeping our noses on top of the latest fragrance trends. Right now, one is undeniably taking over, just in time for the cold weather: sophisticated gourmands. If “gourmand” brings to mind the sugary cupcake body sprays your sister bought at the mall as a teenager, you’re not alone in that. But these grown-up “neo-gourmands” take the comforting, familiar sweetness of edible notes and balance them with dry woods, spice, or smoke. The result? A little less syrup, a lot more intrigue and sophistication.

Instead of buttercream and chocolate sauce, think tonka bean and smoke, bitter cacao and rum, toasted sesame and musk, or vanilla and cardamom. Even coffee-scented colognes are taking off right now. There are so many delicious new gourmand combinations out there that narrowing them all down to just ten was no easy feat. But we did it, and these are our favorites right now. Consider it your invitation to smell very good (and maybe a little mouthwatering) all winter long.

When it comes to gourmands, easily one of the most common base notes is vanilla. And in fragrance, vanilla definitely does not mean boring—quite the opposite, in fact. Henry Rose’s Dave fragrance combines vanilla and tonka bean at the base for a creamy foundation that’s sweet, but not overwhelmingly sweet.

At the top, we get some nice earthy, slightly spicy notes of tangerine, neroli, and pink pepper, and its heart is softer with florals like orange flower and almond blossom. Base notes of vanilla bean, cocoa, and creamy tonka bean leave you with a smooth, velvety finish.

The great thing about Solférino’s Lumière d’Automne is that it’s both sweet and smoky, but not too much of either. It’s the perfect blend of light gourmand notes (namely vanilla and benzoin) with the hint of smoke that comes from notes of roasted chestnut. The name literally translates to “Autumn Light” and evokes the cozy feeling of wearing a sweater on a chilly day while colorful leaves fall to the ground.

For the past two years, Chai Fleur has been my go-to scent the moment the temperature drops below 50 degrees. I’m not a coffee person, but I do drink a chai latte every day of my life (honestly, I don’t know how I would get through my day without it), and somehow Belnu has captured the scent of chai and turned it into the perfect winter fragrance. It’s sweet and creamy thanks to the base of vanilla and woods, and it has the perfect amount of spice from nutmeg, cinnamon, and green cardamom.

While not technically a gourmand fragrance, Fulton & Roark’s Calle Ocho has notes of bitter chocolate and tonka bean, which give it that velvety-smooth feel that many gourmands have, even if it doesn’t smell quite as sweet as a true gourmand. When blended with cinnamon, rum, florals, smoky tobacco leaf, and musk, the chocolate and tonka bean are transformed into something dark, boozy, sexy, and very sophisticated.

Sometimes, you don’t have to spend more than $100 to smell like you have your life together. In fact, the whole goal of House of Twist is to make affordable fragrances (under $50!) that smell just like your favorite luxury colognes. Liquid Amber No.173 is a spicy gourmand akin to something like Parfums de Marly’s Althaïr (which we also absolutely love), with spicy cinnamon and cardamom, creamy bourbon vanilla, woods, and musks.

Almond is another one of those creamy, nutty scents like pistachio or sesame that thrives in gourmand fragrances. My favorite example of this is Nicolaï Paris’s Macaron Bourbon, an ambery gourmand centered around bitter almond, sweet rum, davana, and patchouli. It’s smooth and creamy without being too rich, so it doesn’t overwhelm you with sweetness. The bitter almond and spices also add an interesting nuttiness to the fragrance, which we love.

My favorite dessert has got to be a warm sticky toffee pudding with a scoop of vanilla ice cream on top. I never thought there would be a fragrance that captured the warm deliciousness of those exact flavors, but D.S. & Durga, one of our favorite niche fragrance houses, has done just that. Ain’t That Sweet is inspired by a slice of malty date cake and combines notes of cinnamon, date, praline, condensed milk, bourbon vanilla, and nutmeg for an addictive gourmand scent that we’re frankly obsessed with.

You may not expect floral and gourmand notes to go together, but they actually balance each other out quite nicely. In Van Cleef & Arpels’ Orchidée Vanille, the light floral notes of rose and violet lend a nice airiness to the rich gourmand notes of bitter almond, vanilla, and tonka bean. The effect is something sweet but delicate—it’s not the “manliest” of scents in the traditional sense, but we’re very pro-florals when it comes to men’s cologne.

Another one of my favorite new gourmands that launched this year is Abel’s Double Scoop, which truly evokes a sweet, nutty ice cream (my favorite kind). But it’s more nuanced than that—it starts off with herbaceous, slightly woody rosemary before revealing the rich notes of toasted black sesame, butterscotch, and vanilla. The dry down is nice and musky, which gives it a sweet but muted lingering aroma.

Finally, if you do want something rich that still feels grown-up and not too sugary, Free Yourself’s Savor is the way to go. It’s a true gourmand with notes of bittersweet chocolate, pistachio, toasted almond, tonka bean, vanilla, and even marshmallow—but it never feels like an overly sweet, juvenile scent in the way you might think marshmallow would lend itself to. That’s because all these rich, creamy, sweet notes are balanced out by plum, rose, and tobacco, which add a more sophisticated twist to the rich gourmand.
