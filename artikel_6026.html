Wife of American wrongfully detained in China calls for his release after Trump-Xi meeting

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Wives of Americans wrongfully detained in China appeal for their husbands' release ahead of the high-stakes meeting between President Donald Trump and Xi Jinping. Gillian Turner reports on the families of Youlin Chen and Min Zin as they share their emotional struggles and express hope for diplomatic intervention during bilateral discussions.

The wife of U.S. citizen Min Zin, whom the State Department has declared "wrongfully detained" in China, issued a fresh call for his release Thursday after President Donald Trump met with Chinese President Xi Jinping.

"It has now been nearly six months since my husband, Min Zin, a United States citizen and independent academic, was lured to China and wrongfully detained," Sylvia Zin said in a statement.

"My husband is being held by the Chinese government on false charges at an undisclosed location in solitary confinement without any access to legal counsel," she added. "We have no idea why they detained him and why they continue to hold him."

"This is the second in-person meeting that President Trump has held with Chinese President Xi since Min Zin was arrested. I am grateful for the President's commitment in continuing to raise my husband's wrongful detention and his work to secure Min Zin's immediate and unconditional release," Sylvia Zin also said. "My daughter and I remain hopeful in the President's ability to bring him home to us in time for the holidays."

When asked Thursday to respond to Sylvia Zin's statement, a White House official told Fox News Digital that Trump cares deeply about the return of Americans being held abroad and has brought home more than 100 from around the globe.

The State Department said in August that Secretary of State Marco Rubio determined Zin was "wrongfully detained" following an extensive review "of the circumstances surrounding his June 3 detention in Yunnan, China, where Mr. Zin had traveled to attend an academic conference at the invitation of the Chinese government."

"There are now two wrongfully detained U.S. citizens in China: Mr. Zin and Dr. Youlin Chen," Assistant Secretary Dylan Johnson said at the time. "The safety and security of U.S. citizens is the State Department’s top priority. We will continue to advocate for Mr. Zin and call for the release of all U.S. citizens who are arbitrarily detained or exit-banned in China."

China's Foreign Ministry claimed Zin was arrested in June on suspicion of spying and endangering national security, according to Reuters.

The Institute for Strategy and Policy-Myanmar think tank said Zin is its director, and The Associated Press reported in June that Zin was a Ph.D. candidate at the University of California, Berkeley.

Sylvia Zin also said in her statement, "We recently learned that Min Zin was hospitalized last month for severe dehydration due to stomach illness and that there is no heat in the location he is being held.

"I try to remain strong for our daughter," she added, "but my worry for my husband's health and well-being grows unbearably with each passing day."

Fox News' Nicholas Kalman and Stephen Sorace contributed to this report.

Greg Norman is a reporter at Fox News Digital.
