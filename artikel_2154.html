Leeds United, Nottingham Forest set sights on potential deal for Watford prospect

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Watford are on the lookout for new players to bring into their ranks, but they’ve found some fresh prospects in the academy setup too. Amin Nabizada has made a name for himself this term, and among those hoping to follow in his footsteps is Jamael John-Baptiste.

However, the Hornets could have a fight on their hands in the bid to keep the teenage striker. Football Insider reports that Premier League pair Leeds United and Nottingham Forest are both interested in poaching John-Baptiste from the setup at Vicarage Road.

Watford hope to retain John-Baptiste, but his Premier League admirers are watching developments closely ahead of a potential swoop. He signed scholarship terms ahead of the 2025/26 season, and with no professional deal in place, it is believed that his contract runs out at the end of the current campaign.

Watford have given John-Baptiste a chance to prove himself after spells in the Spurs and Brentford academies, and he has delivered for the U18s. That could help them in their bid to keep him, as he may well be eager to pay back those who gave him a chance – much like Sydie Peck at Sheffield United.

However, Leeds United and Nottingham Forest are attractive clubs with strong academy setups. Not only have plenty of prospects come through their ranks and into the first-team squad over the years, but they will be able to offer elite facilities and a higher level of coaching than the Hornets.

If their interest does firm up, it could make keeping John-Baptiste a tough task. For now though, he will remain focused on delivering in the Watford academy to strengthen his reputation and continue his progression.

John-Baptiste is clearly a bright prospect, and one who Watford may well hope breaks into the first-team.

However, it has been a rarity for academy-reared prospects to break through at Vicarage Road. Nabizada has done so this term, but otherwise, the likes of Albert Eames and James Clarridge have only really been called upon to offer depth.

If the Hornets want to have a better chance of retaining talents like John-Baptiste for the foreseeable, they have to forge a clearer route to the first team. If not, then clubs of Leeds United and Nottingham Forest’s ilk will be able to poach youngsters.

Time will tell what comes of John-Baptiste’s situation though, as interest starts to emerge.
