Scaramucci Says JPMorgan Could Spend Up to $1 Billion on Its Bitcoin Business

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

JPMorgan may have to spend between $500 million and $1 billion to build out its Bitcoin business, according to SkyBridge Capital's Anthony Scaramucci.

It's JPMorgan's willingness to spend money serving Bitcoin clients that tells us more about its plans than CEO Jamie Dimon calling the cryptocurrency a "pet rock."

Scaramucci also claimed that a research note circulating within JPMorgan's private bank recommended allocating 10% of a portfolio to Bitcoin, describing it as potentially the most undervalued asset of the next decade.

Scaramucci did not provide a timeline for the spending estimate. Neither the estimate nor the research note could be independently verified through publicly available JPMorgan documents.

"Know the difference between what people say and what they do with their money," Scaramucci wrote on X.

Scaramucci was referring to what it might cost JPMorgan to develop a business serving Bitcoin clients.

The 10% allocation recommendation, meanwhile, concerns how much of a client's portfolio should be invested in Bitcoin.

If you have a $1 million portfolio, that would mean allocating $100,000 to Bitcoin.

It doesn't mean JPMorgan is buying Bitcoin and adding it to its own balance sheet.

This distinction matters because a bank can profit from clients who want Bitcoin exposure even if its CEO believes they're making a bad investment.

If JPMorgan were willing to spend hundreds of millions of dollars serving Bitcoin clients, assuming Scaramucci's numbers are correct, that would suggest the bank expects substantial demand for these services.

We have a specific example of what JPMorgan already offers in a securities filing dated Nov. 24.

The filing covers $300,000 worth of notes linked to BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF, priced by JPMorgan's financing subsidiary.

Someone who invests $1,000 in these notes could receive $1,300 if the ETF closes at or above its initial price on the observation date in November 2027.

However, investors also risk losing some or all of their principal.

It's a product JPMorgan created around a financial position that clients might want to take: where will this Bitcoin ETF be trading in a year's time?

Scaramucci suggested that Dimon has regulators in mind when he publicly criticizes Bitcoin.

"He's a smart guy. He knows he has to go before regulators and say the right things," Scaramucci said.

According to Scaramucci's interpretation, Dimon can publicly dismiss Bitcoin to maintain a conservative stance while JPMorgan develops products for clients who want exposure to the cryptocurrency.

However, this is Scaramucci's interpretation of Dimon's motives.

Dimon's own public explanation has been more straightforward.

In a CNBC interview at Davos in January 2024, Dimon described Bitcoin as a "pet rock."

At JPMorgan's investor day in May 2025, Dimon raised concerns about leverage and illicit activity involving Bitcoin.

But he also made it clear that customers could buy Bitcoin and see their holdings reflected on JPMorgan statements. At the time, he said the bank would leave custody to third parties.

"I defend your right to buy Bitcoin," Dimon said.

Against this backdrop, the research note Scaramucci described would be particularly significant, assuming his claims are accurate.

Dimon has already explained why JPMorgan would allow customers to buy Bitcoin.

But recommending that clients allocate one dollar out of every ten in their portfolios to Bitcoin would be a much bigger step.

The November securities filing shows that JPMorgan is willing to offer products providing Bitcoin exposure.

However, it has not been independently confirmed that JPMorgan's private bankers are recommending such a substantial allocation to their clients.
