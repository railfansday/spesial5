Bitcoin power-law models predict a rebound without real proof

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Bitcoin's power-law model projects a $383,000 median price by 2030 using a recovery assumption that historical tests have yet to prove.

The power-law model links Bitcoin's price to its age, but the recovery forecasts in a Nov. 10 preprint also assume that departures from the long-run curve reverse, a tendency its historical analysis leaves unresolved.

Authors Carlos Baquero and Raquel Menezes argued that Bitcoin's long-run growth rate has slowed under the conditions their test allows.

The study's price record ends on Sept. 19, so its results do not explain November's selling.

Model advocate Stephen Perrenod made the attractor argument explicit in a June 5 analysis, arguing that tests of price deviations supported an attractor around which bubbles and bear markets occur.

He also cautioned that those tests did not predict next month's price or prove the relationship must continue indefinitely.

Reports on power-law likewise described an attractor framework while leaving the force sustaining it unsettled.

Baquero and Menezes used 820 weekly observations from January 2011 through September 2026. Within a declared family of possible price deviations, their test found evidence of a slowing long-run growth rate.

It rejected constant drift in expected log price at a nominal 5% significance level, compatible with a power-law trend, but does not identify it uniquely.

Their main family allowed shocks to persist as much as a random walk, where a shock need never fade. When a prespecified sensitivity check allowed even more persistent changes, the rejection disappeared, showing how the conclusion depended on the persistence assumption.

The paper also found that a Gompertz curve, which approaches a ceiling, described the historical record almost as well as the power law.

The authors said the fitted models for price deviations revert toward their trends, so most forecasts anticipate a rise from the approximately $81,000 September endpoint.

The restoring tendency entered through the chosen models even though the historical analysis cannot establish it within the evaluated approach.

For Dec. 28, 2030, the power-law model produced a $383,000 median and a 90% interval of $150,000 to $1,015,000. Across all five trend models, the medians ranged from $85,000 to $1,011,000.

The authors warned that their predictive intervals are probably too narrow and are not calibrated. Each forecast combines the fitted distribution with simulated and refitted alternatives, but the refits explore only neighboring choices for how deviations behave.

That leaves broader uncertainty about persistence only partly represented.

The result is a model that assumes a pull toward trend and cannot use its projected rebound as evidence that the pull exists.

The paper describes itself as a preregistered reanalysis of a record the authors had already studied. They reported freezing the protocol and posting its hash on Oct. 6, applying the historical test once on Oct. 7, then registering the results and forecasts later that morning.

Those forecasts target the last Saturday of each year from 2027 through 2030 and will be assessed jointly using a log-density score, which measures how well the predicted distributions match the eventual prices.

Better performance would demonstrate better prediction of those four observations. The distinction remains consequential for a Bitcoin recovery case: a historical growth curve can frame a forecast, but an assumed return toward it is still a proposition for future prices to test.
