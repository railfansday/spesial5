kato taverna and ROOVICE strip 1960s tokyo apartment to concrete shell for 50-sqm renovation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

the collaborative team organizes apartment’s living, sleeping, and wet zones with lauan plywood, a salvaged stainless-steel kitchen, and mortex finishes.

giulia taverna edited by christina vergopoulou November 17, 2026 4 min

Located in Tokyo’s Setagaya-ku, Gakudai Apartment is the 50-sqm residential renovation of a 1960s top-floor apartment by Tokyo-based designer couple kato taverna, completed in collaboration with Japanese construction firm Roovice. Stripping away a dated remodel from two decades prior that had sealed off windows and created dead zones around existing plumbing shafts and columns, the design team pares the unit back to its raw concrete shell. Organized on a continuous floor level with minimal partitions, the layout separates day and night zones around a central living-dining-kitchen (LDK) core using a restrained material palette of lauan plywood, stainless steel, and mortar.

By installing an insulated entry door, the design studio eliminates the need for a vestibule partition, allowing the enlarged mortar-finished doma, fitted with a custom bench, coat rack, and elastic-rope shoe storage mounted to an exposed concrete column, to open directly into the LDK. In the kitchen, a salvaged Maruzen stainless-steel commercial unit is adapted with adjustable legs, custom lauan plywood internal storage, and a simplified wall exhaust fan to avoid running long ductwork across the ceiling. To visually level the irregular ceiling heights caused by the roof’s drainage slope, a perimeter stainless-steel bar wraps the living area, serving simultaneously as a curtain track and hanging rail.

On the quieter side of the plan, the bedroom is enclosed like a timber box with ceiling and party-wall insulation, an asymmetrical mirrored door fitted directly against the uneven structural column and beam, and a bed frame with concealed storage and custom stainless-steel reading lights. Behind it, a compact study features a fold-away wall desk for dual use as a guest room. In the tight wet zone, replacing a prefabricated unit bath with a Mortex-finished enclosure and a TOTO poly bathtub freed up space to reopen sealed windows, filtering light through glass blocks in the toilet and a sliding rail mirror in the washroom, while wall-mounting the air conditioning units cleared the narrow veranda to function like an engawa.
