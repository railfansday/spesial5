Steelers need more from DK Metcalf, other highly paid players

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Pittsburgh Steelers are 6-3 and still well positioned to make the NFL playoffs because of a favorable schedule.

For that reason, the 38-18 loss to the Cincinnati Bengals on Nov. 15 was not a total disaster and could plausibly be written off as just a bad day against a red-hot offense led by leading MVP candidate Joe Burrow.

At least that might be true if the team's most highly paid players were producing at the levels they're paid to. They decidedly are not, and until they are, then the Steelers still profile as an upper middle class AFC team capable of reaching the postseason, but probably not winning once they get there.

Receiver DK Metcalf, who's in Year 2 of a five-year, $150 million deal, is the chief offender. Through nine games, he's on pace for the least productive season of his eight-year NFL career. It would break the lower threshold that he established last just year, his first in Pittsburgh, when he had 850 yards.

Pro Football Focus data faults him for six drops, a figure that feels generous given how frequently he fails to come down with catchable balls from quarterback Aaron Rodgers.

The contested catch numbers encapsulate the profound struggles better. PFF credits him with receptions on just 10 of 25 opportunities in 2026. That's a 40% contested catch rate, which is the worst in the NFL among receivers with at least that many opportunities. Only eight out of 38 receivers with at least 50 targets are worse overall.

Within that cohort, Metcalf ranks 33rd in yards per route run at 1.24; 32nd in yards after the catch with 125; and dead last in raw catch percentage when targeted at 50.7%.

By no metric has he been the reliable No. 1 option that he's being paid to be. Granted, it's certainly not helped that key offseason acquisition Michael Pittman Jr. hasn't been around much to draw coverage to the opposite side of the field. Still, for $150 million, you expect a player to be able to transcend his circumstances more often than Metcalf has.

He finished the game against the Bengals with 54 yards and a touchdown but was blanked before the game got out of hand. He simply hasn't been good enough.

Linebacker/pass rusher T.J. Watt is also worthy of scrutiny. In two games against Cincinnati this season, the Steelers' $42 million man this season generated zero pressures on Burrow. Not just zero sacks. No hurries, and no quarterback hits. Needless to say, that's not good when Burrow is the man he's primarily meant to disrupt.

Four of his eight sacks this season came in the first two games. And he's on pace for the fewest tackles of his career with nine total so far, meaning he's contributing little unless he's getting to the quarterback.

There are certainly games that he's taken over. His interception return for a touchdown sealed the Week 1 win against Atlanta. His two sacks against Cleveland in Week 8 helped give his team the outright division lead momentarily.

PFF, however, has graded him at 63.8/100 or worse in five of his last seven games. The consistency is nowhere close to where it was when he was winning Defensive Player of the Year honors and setting the NFL sack record.

Beyond those two, you can find fault in a lot of the more highly paid players' respective games.

Tight end Pat Freiermuth has often been invisible as a receiving target with Pittman out because of injury. He's caught just two passes in each of the past four games, and his production against Cincinnati came mostly at garbage time.

Pittman, obviously, hasn't been available much at all because of his foot injuries. He's the offense's third-highest paid player behind Metcalf and Rodgers and has just seven catches for 89 yards in three games.

Linebacker Alex Highsmith, has just one half sack in the past seven games. His cap hit is $17 million in 2026. That problem compounds itself, too, because that lack of production will likely hurt his trade value in the offseason, when the Steelers might be looking to unload him because of big commitments to Watt and fellow outside backer Nick Herbig.

And Rodgers himself was not sharp at all against Cincinnati. He didn't complete a pass for most of the first half. He'd thrown just two touchdowns in regulation in the previous three games. And his two scoring tosses against the Bengals came once the game was well out of reach. He's in a definitive slump himself.

Add it all up, and the Steelers are relying more heavily on their depth than they probably should be in Year 1 of the transition to Mike McCarthy as coach.

If this franchise is going to win its first playoff game since 2016 come January, the guys making the most need to begin playing like it. Otherwise, another disappointment like Sunday night's could be the start of a long winter once again.
