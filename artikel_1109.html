Uefa’s surprise selection of Philippe Diallo to contest the Fifa presidency against Infantino has raised questions over the process

One of Uefa’s biggest decisions in a generation was decided in a mere 15-minute Executive Committee meeting, raising questions over the process that led to backing for Fifa presidential candidate Philippe Diallo actually is.

The European body belatedly announced on Monday they were putting forward the French federation president to challenge Gianni Infantino, having spent weeks trying to convince Arsene Wenger.

That statement was greeted with some surprise, amid a feeling that Diallo has little chance, and some stakeholders even broaching whether Uefa should have put forward no one so as to delegitimise Infantino’s expected landslide victory.

The process has instead provoked recurring questions over whether some of the governance criticisms of Fifa just as easily apply to Uefa.

While the Executive Committee is empowered as Uefa’s highest body, there had been a feeling that a decision of such magnitude might go to a vote of the 55 national associations.

Uefa president Aleksander Ceferin instead put forward Diallo’s name at an Executive Committee on Monday, stating that it was initially a suggestion by the Football Association. Sources say that Ceferin acknowledged that they had been trying to convince Wenger, and that Diallo fell short of the requirements set out in a Uefa “Top Executive Programme” meeting in early October, but the feeling was they had to put up someone.

One criticism from those with knowledge of the discussions is that, since Uefa had made public noise about putting up a candidate, Ceferin felt they had to follow through despite this not necessarily being the best strategic move.

Another name that had been broached was Hungarian representative Sandor Csanyi, but this is not a popular choice, and some felt it was best to go with Diallo to ward that possibility off.

The Uefa president did ask if there were any objections or feedback but, with no resistance from the Executive Committee, they were free to carefully say Diallo was “unanimously” being backed by national associations represented on the body.

The very approach has aggravated some national association chiefs, however, especially since the general atmosphere in Uefa – as in Fifa – has been for the president to not be challenged. They also would have preferred an open vote.

Irritation at this goes back some time, including the start of this Fifa crisis. When Uefa had an emergency meeting back in July to decide on boycotting Fifa events after the World Cup privatisation plan was reported, there was no formal vote, and many smaller federations felt they didn’t get a chance to speak.

That comes amid an expectation that anywhere between five and 20 European federations will actually vote for Infantino, despite Monday’s show of unity.
