The One Piece of Cold-Weather Gear I Never Leave at Home

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

It’s cheap, packable, and useful enough that I bring it even when the forecast looks mild.

Gear-obsessed editors choose every product we review. We may earn commission if you buy from a link. How we test gear.

From 55 degrees down to 25, I bring one piece of cold-weather gear on every ride: a neck buff. It isn’t the most exciting item in my kit, and it won’t make a dramatic difference in a gear photo. But it’s useful across a wider range of temperatures than almost anything else I own, and it costs far less than most winter gear cyclists spend serious money on.

At 55 degrees, I can wear it loosely around my neck and cut the wind without feeling bundled up. As the temperature drops, that little bit of insulation becomes more valuable. Covering your neck makes you feel warmer, and the buff also helps close the gap at the top of your jacket. Cold air sneaking in around the collar can make an otherwise reasonable layering setup feel inadequate. A buff helps keep that draft out.

When it gets really cold, I pull it up over my ears and face. It’s a simple way to add coverage without committing to a heavier balaclava for the whole ride. And if the temperature changes, or I start working harder on a climb, I can pull it back down in a few seconds. It takes up almost no space in a jersey pocket, which means there’s little reason to leave it at home.

Neck buffs grew out of a practical problem familiar to motorcycle riders: keeping cold, wind, and road dust off your neck and face. Riders already used bandanas and other neck cloths; the stretchy tube made that protection easier to wear under a helmet. Skiers adopted it too, which may be why people associate it with snow sports.

There is no shortage of expensive cycling accessories that promise to keep you warm while riding. Many are quite good. But few come close to the humble neck buff in terms of how practical, simple, and cheap it is. It’s an adaptable piece of kit that’s just as useful on a cool ride that doesn’t quite call for full winter kit as it is on a truly cold ride, when every gap and exposed bit of skin becomes a problem. It’s a small thing, which is exactly why I never leave home without it.

Test Editor Dan Chabanov got his start in cycling as a New York City bike messenger but quickly found his way into road and cyclocross racing, competing in professional cyclocross races from 2009 to 2019 and winning a Master’s National Championship title in 2018.
