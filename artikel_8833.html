A WorldTour Rider Shared His Tire Pressure. It’s Lower Than You Think.

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

His answer was striking even in an era when road tire pressures keep dropping.

An Instagram Reel featuring world tour pro Luke Plapp (Team Jayco-AlUla) talking about tire pressure appeared in my Instagram feed and caught my attention, mostly because many pro riders either won't tell you or don’t know their tire pressure, and most team mechanics won’t openly share that information with the press. Now, I’ve been on the lower-is-generally-better tire-pressure train for many years. I routinely run my road bike tires between 50 and 70 psi, depending on the bike, tire, and wheel setup. I was still struck by how low Plapp’s number was, even by modern standards.

The clip comes from the September 23, 2026, episode of Stanley St. Social podcast. Plapp says he gets the same reaction whenever he tells people what pressure he runs. “This is actually one where every single time I tell people what tire pressure I run, every punter all over the world just can't believe it, so we're running 32mm tires at 3.7 to 3.8 bar.” That works out to 53.7 to 55.1 psi.

Plapp calls them 32mm tires. But the team is using CADEX Aero 30mm, so his quote likely refers to their mounted width. I haven’t measured the exact setup of the CADEX Ultra 50 wheels, which use a hookless rim with a 22.4mm internal width and 30mm external width with the CADEX Aero 30mm tire. But I’ve ridden these particular tires and reviewed the CADEX 50 Ultra for Bicycling. In my test, a nominal 28mm CADEX Race tire measured close to 30mm on the 22.4mm internal rim. So it’s very reasonable to assume that a labeled 30mm tire would inflate closer to 32mm. You can read my CADEX 50 Ultra wheelset review here.

The biggest piece of information that we don’t have from this clip is Plapp’s actual weight. His Pro Cycling Stats page lists him as 158.75 lbs, which may or may not be accurate. But using that as an estimate puts Plapp firmly on the lower end of the tire-pressure spectrum, as I would expect someone that weight to run closer to the low-sixties psi based on various tire pressure calculators. That means Plapp is either a bit lighter than PCS thinks he is, or he simply prefers the added comfort and grip of running lower pressure.

But why is any of this interesting? For one, I generally agree with Plapp’s inferred assessment that many riders still run too much tire pressure. That’s generally been true when I talk to folks in my riding circles. Because of this, I think many riders simply don’t understand just how low performance-oriented riders have gone with their tire pressure. So if you don’t want to hear it from me, I hope hearing it from Plapp, who has raced the Tour de France for the last two years, will be more convincing.

Finally, the brief quote also highlights that if you care about your performance and optimize your tire pressure, you shouldn't take the labeled tire size at face value. Instead, you need to actually measure your tires inflated on the wheelset you are using. Just because something is labeled as 30mm doesn't mean it will measure 30mm on your wheels, and that can change the tire-pressure math in a meaningful way.
