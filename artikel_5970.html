Elected Somali daycare owner with fraud ties now revealed to also be married to illegal immigrant

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Federal agents are currently raiding 22 businesses in Minneapolis, Minnesota, as part of a major federal fraud investigation. Brooke Taylor reports live that the raids are connected to a viral YouTube video from Nick Shirley, which exposed alleged childcare funding fraud in December. The Trump administration previously froze childcare funding and called for a full audit of certain daycare centers.

A Somali politician whose daycare business has been tied to fraud is married to an illegal alien, Fox News Digital has confirmed.

Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party (DFL) candidate Abdi Daisane defeated a Republican incumbent to win Minnesota’s House District 14A seat, which encompasses some of the city of St. Cloud.

Daisane's wife, Ayaan Aydid, 30, is present in the country illegally, a Trump administration official confirmed on Wednesday afternoon after an Alpha News report on the subject. Aydid crossed the U.S. southern border with Mexico in 2023, during former President Joe Biden's administration.

Abdi Daisane was elected to represent the St. Cloud area in the Minnesota House. (Abdi Daisane for State Representative)

After Daisane's victory, a video surfaced of Aydid detailing to the BBC how she crossed into the United States at the southern border, sparking questions about her immigration status. An accompanying story describes Aydid as "one of hundreds of Somalis who have jumped the US-Mexico border wall."

"It wasn't a hard journey," she said in the interview.

Daisane, who delivered part of his victory speech in Somali, is the CEO of Blooming Kids Child Care Center in St. Cloud, which has received $7.29 million from the state-run Child Care Assistance Program since 2018.

His childcare business also received $598,251.62 from the federally-funded Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP) since 2020. A nonprofit called Partners in Nutrition, which recently had to pay an $18.5 million civil settlement for fraud, was the center's sponsor for CACFP funds.

A press release from the settlement says Partners in Nutrition "knowingly defrauded the State of Minnesota out of millions of dollars in federal child nutrition funds during and after the COVID-19 pandemic, in violation of the Minnesota False Claims Act."

Fox News Digital visited Daisane's business in St. Cloud just after his election and found a quiet scene.
