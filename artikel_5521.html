Elizabeth Warren is pressing GM over its $20 billion stock buyback plan

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Massachusetts Democrat, set to chair the Senate Banking Committee in January, cited price increases and layoffs at GM

Sen. Elizabeth Warren, a Massachusetts Democrat, criticized General Motors $GM in a letter to CEO Mary Barra over the automaker’s $20 billion stock buyback plan, according to Bloomberg. The letter was seen by Bloomberg.

Warren wrote that the buyback comes while car prices are at record highs, according to Bloomberg. “This proposal will enrich company executives and shareholders, but it comes after GM hiked its prices and laid off or furloughed thousands of workers,” she wrote.

She also faulted the company for its tax and tariff treatment under the Trump administration. "GM's decision to prioritize investor profits over workers and American families, despite benefitting from President Trump's tax giveaways and tariff exemptions and refunds, is deeply concerning," she wrote, according to Bloomberg.

Warren will take over as chair of the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing and Urban Affairs once the incoming Congress takes office in January, according to Bloomberg. Democrats took both chambers of Congress in the midterm races, Bloomberg said.

GM said on Oct. 20 that its board approved a new $20 billion multi-year buyback alongside its third-quarter results. The authorization has no expiration date and may be suspended or discontinued at any time, the company said. GM also said it completed the $6.0 billion program it announced in January.

The company had 849 million shares outstanding as of Sept. 30, down from 904 million at the end of 2025, GM said. GM has spent $29.5 billion on share repurchases since 2023, according to Bloomberg. GM stock fell almost 7% on the day of the announcement.

Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed, a Michigan Democrat, also criticized the plan, according to Bloomberg. Michigan is GM’s home state. “GM just announced the biggest stock buyback in automotive industry history,” El-Sayed wrote in an X $TWTR post last month. “Instead of investing in their workers or new product lines, GM executives are robbing from the future to pay themselves and their shareholders today.”

Republican Mike Rogers, El-Sayed’s opponent in the Senate race, also denounced the plan, according to Bloomberg.
