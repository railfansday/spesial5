General Mills is weighing a sale of its Häagen-Dazs business outside North America

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Planning is at an early stage and the Cheerios maker could keep the ice cream unit, according to The Wall Street Journal

General Mills $GIS is weighing a split from its Häagen-Dazs ice cream operations beyond North America, a step that might end in a sale, according to The Wall Street Journal, which cited unnamed sources.

Planning remains preliminary, and the Minneapolis-based company may opt to hold onto the unit, according to The Wall Street Journal. General Mills declined to comment on The Wall Street Journal's story, according to Reuters.

General Mills owns and runs Häagen-Dazs operations beyond North America, among them Europe, Thailand, Mexico and the United Arab Emirates. Froneri, which operates Häagen-Dazs in the U.S., could be a logical buyer. A potential sale price was not disclosed.

The review comes after General Mills agreed in June to offload its Häagen-Dazs shops in mainland China to a group of investors, according to The Wall Street Journal. That group is headed by Ningji, which runs a chain of tea shops, according to Reuters.

General Mills' super-premium ice cream category had sales of $782.7 million in fiscal 2026.

Other food companies have been exiting the ice cream business to trim expenses, according to The Wall Street Journal. Unilever separated its ice cream arm as Magnum Ice Cream, and Nestle said in February that it plans to divest what remains of its frozen dessert operations.

The review comes during a leadership change at General Mills. The company named Chief Operating Officer Dana McNabb its next chief executive in late September. She will succeed CEO Jeff Harmening effective Jan. 1, 2027, and Harmening will become executive chair of the board.

McNabb has said she will focus on reviving sales and cutting costs across the company, according to The Wall Street Journal. General Mills reported a 3% drop in first-quarter fiscal 2027 net sales to $4.39 billion, a decline partly driven by the divestiture of its U.S. yogurt business.
