Fed Boss: Superintelligence Will Rule, Good or Bad, Going Forward

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

In remarks on Nov. 16 that will likely impact the housing market going forward, Federal Reserve Open Market Committee Chairman Kevin Warsh emphasized that new ideas borne from “superintelligence” can move things forward idea-wise, which may impact economic policy and interest rates.

“The implications for economic policy can hardly be overstated,” he said. “While an idea can be costly to discover, it’s free to use. And because an idea is non-rival, one person’s use does nothing to diminish that of anyone else; its value grows with the number of people who use it. In the U.S., over the last several decades, breakthrough ideas have been harder to find.”

Warsh did not specifically address housing or future rate hikes, choosing to target how theoretically “superintelligence”—an alternative term for AI preferred by President Donald Trump—could affect every aspect of the economy. Following his remarks, he convened panels to solicit opinions from various business leaders.

“As a result, productivity growth has fluctuated, and while in recent quarters it has been reasonably strong, it hasn’t yet broken out definitively, structurally, reliably to the upside, at least not yet. Long-run growth is also a function of the rate of increase in the number of people producing these ideas, setting them in motion. The conventional view holds that as population growth slows, there may be fewer new ideas to drive prosperity.”

Earlier on the same day Warsh spoke, Fed Governor Michael Barr, in a speech at the Seventh Annual Association for Computing Machinery International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Finance, addressed some of the same issues, offering a more critical view of AI (and using the more common terminology).

Barr also addressed specific usages of AI around lending and housing, saying that while “institutions may increasingly use AI to expand household credit access through alternative data analysis,” care must be taken to avoid errors.

“AI poses significant compliance challenges,” he said. “The technology can function as a black box, making it difficult to explain adverse decisions or ensure compliance with fair lending laws. Alternative data sources such as rental payments, utility bills or employment patterns may expand access to credit if they are used appropriately, but they may also inadvertently serve as proxies for protected characteristics like race or national origin. Thus, care must be taken. Unlike traditional credit reports, consumers often cannot review or dispute this information.”

With one more Fed meeting scheduled this year, economists are predicting that there will be a rate hike which, in turn, would likely raise mortgage rates.

The Fed, in a late October meeting, left interest rates unchanged, despite inflationary pressures continuing to mount. The move was also not unanimous, as Federal Reserve officials voted 10-2 to keep interest rates unchanged, which varied greatly from the previous meeting in mid-September, which came with a unanimous vote to hike interest rates, after many economists predicted that outcome.
