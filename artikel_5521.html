What Next-Gen Real Estate Leaders Are Leaving Behind and Embracing Instead

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Above, from left, Eric Fite, Jack Markham, Marisela Harper, Lori Muller and Adrian Provost

“All the technology in the world doesn’t matter unless it makes a fundamental impact on the relationship with the client, the way we serve them. That’s our true north.”

That was the sentiment summed up by Eric Fite, COO of CENTURY 21 Judge Fite Company in Dallas/Fort Worth, when moderating a recent panel of up-and-coming brokerage leaders about what old ways they are leaving behind and what innovative new approaches they are bringing to the fore.

AI and technology and how they can best be used dominated the discussion, but panelists also shared some of the ways they are reimagining traditional business practices to support their agents in the session titled, “Next-Gen Leaders and Their Powerful New Approach to Business,” held this past October at RISMedia’s CEO Exchange in Washington, D.C.

In addition to Fite, the panel included Marisela Harper, broker/owner of ClearView Realty; Adrian Provost, CEO of ONE Terminus & LEVEL; Lori Muller, president of Fathom Realty; and Jack Markham, chief marketing officer at Inside Real Estate.

The group also shared insights into how they are positioning their tech stacks and CRMs, how they are viewing their data and data ownership and control,as well as the future of AI and how it can assist agents through automation.

“I think everybody’s realizing that the CRM is not necessarily your tech stack,” said Markham when asked what old beliefs most forward-thinking brokerages have already abandoned. “Traditionally, you would pick a CRM, and that’s where your data is; that’s who owns the workflows. But quite honestly, the smartest brokers are realizing that the tech stack is way more than your CRM.

“Most teams are running two to three CRMs. And that’s not because everybody loves using multiple CRMs. They do it because it’s a fear of loss. They do it because if I drop a tech stack, if I drop a tool, I don’t want to lose my data. And so they’re doing it as insurance. So I think most of the smart brokers today are realizing they don’t need more tools in the tech stack. They don’t need another tool, another app. What they need is something to absorb the tech stack, something that’s going to empower their data so their agents think less about where they log in and more about how to drive conversations.”

Provost said he views the “tech stack,” or the set of software tools, apps and technologies a company uses, as more of a folder, and a platform as more of an operating system.

“Ultimately, what I look for is how (tech) helps close certain loops that lead to that appointment-to-close loop, and then to that close-to-repeat-and-referral-business. I think that if it’s something that isn’t touching one of those or enhancing the agent’s ability to perform, then it probably doesn’t really belong in the tech or in the platform.”

Fite then turned the conversation to AI, asking panelists how they are using it and consumer behavior data to identify when and how an agent should engage a consumer.

“With consumer behavior data, that data is your opportunity,” Muller said. “It’s your opportunity to be strategic with what you’re going to do with it, how you’re going to utilize it. And then AI is the actual action step. It’s how you take action with that data to create a result.”

Markham said it’s all about control over data.

“The uncomfortable question for most brokers is, if you decided to leave your tools or your tech stack, could you take your data with you? And the answer most likely for most brokers in the industry is no,” he said. “They find out far too late that they can’t take it with them. Real ownership of data means you control that data no matter what CRM or tech tool you’re using.”

On a question about what work AI should be doing for a human, Provost said:

“I look at it from an operational standpoint. How can AI assist me and my agents in enhancing our service to our clients? And in my case, my clients being the agent. And so I think that humans need to always be in the loop. There should never be one point where they’re disconnected, but it should be streamlined, and we should be able to have AI connect components that don’t normally talk to one another so we can perform better and elevate that entire experience for whoever’s on the opposite end of it.”

Muller added that AI can help with automating transaction management, marketing solutions, social media, communication or “B- and C-level” operational tasks, while leaving more time for the most important aspect of the business—client relationships.

“No matter who tries to destroy the real estate industry, you can’t take the relational side away from the Realtor® and the consumer. AI is not going to do it. Technology’s not going to do it. That’s the most important thing we have to look at,” she said.

How does the next generation of real estate business leaders view traditional ways of supporting agents, or “loving on them,” as the panel discussed?

“We really do everything so strategically to love on the agent, down to when we built our office four years ago,” said Harper. “We built this big hub and we put our offices right where people that come in can see us. And that’s done on purpose. We have our staff completely trained to love on the agent. We also train our mentors to love on the agents. And when I say love, that’s really being intentionally present with them, spending the time to go to their weddings, go to the baby showers, attend the funerals because that’s a time when they really need just someone to be there for them.”

She added, “And one of the most important things on that contact card is the agent’s birthday. And that might seem so silly, but when I open my calendar for the day, I see whose birthday it is and that’s the first phone call I make just to call them and wish them a happy birthday. Of course, we do the birthday cards and the gift card and all of that, but the call just to wish them happy birthday, see how they’re doing, it goes over so well. They absolutely love it.”

Here are some more notable quotes from the session:

What’s most important when an agent leaves the brokerage?

Lori Muller: When an agent leaves, sometimes they leave because they think the grass is greener on the other side, and they get over there and it’s not. So I think it’s super important to have some type of exit strategy when an agent leaves and have a conversation to say, “Hey, what could we have done better? What was the situation that caused you to leave?” And it’s a teaching moment for both people. We also just recently did a comeback campaign where we called agents that have left over the last two years, and we have had almost a 25% return rate.

Are we heading toward a world where technology becomes table stakes and culture, service and execution become the real differentiators?

Jack Markham: I think you could take two people and give them the same tech stack, and what’s really going to separate them is not necessarily the tools that you’re using. It’s once you plug in your data, right? Once you plug in those relationships, once you plug in the trust, once you plug in the consumer behaviors that you’ve been tracking and the history behind that, it becomes your thumbprint. What’s unique and can’t be copied is all the history there. It’s all the relationships, it’s all the trust that becomes your thumbprint. And so that can never really be duplicated.

What’s the one move you would make over the next 12 months to better position your brokerage and your agents for where the industry is going?

Jack Markham: Figure out if you own your data. Meaning if you left today, some of your tools, could you take them with you? And if the answer is no, then I would fix that.

Marisela Harper: I would say just be super, super present because (agents) need that from you, and just show the love. And when I say show the love, I really mean truly say, “I love you.” Sometimes people might know that you love them, but they need to hear it too.

Lori Muller: Embrace your relationship with not only your technology, but your people. Longevity is in the relationship with the people; tech stack and your technology is constantly changing.

Adrian Provost: Move away from tech stacks completely and create platforms. The old days are completely over. Whatever you need, you can literally build it. And so I would move away from being so reliant on a tech stack and subscriptions and actually figure out what it is that your agents need and provide that to them.
