Wisconsin Couple Seek To Enforce $1,350 Judgment Against Each of Their Neighbors in HOA Dispute

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A neighborhood outside Green Bay, WI, is locked in a yearslong legal battle, as one couple is now taking legal action against their neighbors to collect a $200,000 settlement.

What began as a dispute over a backyard fence has turned into an ongoing series of lawsuits, with neighbors being forced to pay $1,350 each in a settlement—and those who haven’t are now being taken to court.

The case is centered around Michael and Lisa Newman and their five children, who reside in the Nazcr Trac neighborhood in De Pere, WI. After some of their children were diagnosed with autism, the Newmans wanted to build a fence around their backyard, as they wanted the children to be able to play outside safely.

Citing the children’s diagnoses and that two of their children “demonstrated significant risk of elopement,” according to a press release from the Metropolitan Milwaukee Fair Housing Council, the Newmans wrote to the Nazcr Trac Property Owners Association (NTPOA) to request an accommodation to the subdivision’s “no fence” rule.

Neither the initial request, sent in July 2019, nor a subsequent request in December 2020, which included information about the association’s legal obligation to consider an accommodation due to the children’s disabilities, were answered by the association.

In August 2021, the Newmans filed a lawsuit against the association in federal court, alleging that it had discriminated based on disability, a violation of the Fair Housing Amendments Act. A judge granted a preliminary injunction in May 2022 that prohibits the NTPOA from enforcing the rule, and the family built their fence in time for summer, three years after the initial request.

While the family got their wish, the battle was just beginning for other residents of Nazcr Trac.

In August 2024, a settlement was reached, requiring the NTPOA to pay the Newmans $200,000—a payment that would be passed on directly to each household in the association.

Following the settlement, each property in the neighborhood was looking at a $1,350 bill. Residents told WGBA that they were frustrated by the situation and many had been unaware of the dispute until they received the charge.

One anonymous resident told the local station that most residents would have had no issue with the fence, and a poll of members could have saved a lot of time and money.

“If the board had polled us as members, a large amount of the members would have said, 'Absolutely, let the family do what they need to do to take care of their family,’” the resident said during WGBA’s initial coverage in 2025.

At the time, Lisa Newman told the station that she felt “not great” about her neighbors being on the hook for the settlement payment.

“It's just a really unfortunate situation, I think, for everybody involved, and the board kind of made that decision for everybody,” she told WGBA.

However, in September, the Newmans filed a new lawsuit against the NTPOA and a number of homeowners who still owe on the settlement.

Sixty-nine homeowners settled following the initial lawsuit, the Newmans’ attorney, Victoria Davis Dávila, told WGBA. The rest are now facing legal troubles of their own.

“There’s an outstanding judgment," Davis Dávila told WGBA. "You had the opportunity to pay. You chose not to pay it."

In addition to the association, 33 residents are named in the new filing.

“The Newmans want just as bad as anybody else to put this behind them,” Davis Dávila said, adding that the NTPOA membership as a whole could potentially be held liable if the outstanding amount isn’t paid.

The new lawsuit is frustrating and stressful for many homeowners who thought the issue would be behind them by now.

“Why is this coming back to us? It’s so unfair,” an anonymous homeowners told WGBA.

An answer and affirmative defense was filed by the Property Owner’s Association on Nov. 16, but the contents of the document were not publicly available at the time of publication.

Realtor.com® reached out to the legal counsel of both the association and the Newmans, but received no response.
