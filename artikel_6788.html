Your First Car Comes with Questions

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

You’ve manifested several milestones—job, apartment, a squad of ride-or-dies. Now, you need a way to get from one to the other. This is where State Farm comes in as your trusted partner to help you make informed decisions. Here’s what you’ll need to know before you enter your car owner era.

As images of weekend getaways and road trips with your besties fill your mind, so, too, should these pre-purchase considerations.

At the dealership, put on your negotiation face. Buying a car is a little like dating—you do need to be observant, but you shouldn’t put all your cards (in this case: your budget) on the table. Finally, don’t be afraid to walk away. Follow these tips to make sure you're setting yourself up for success.

Before you turn up your favorite song and hit the open road, make sure your new baby is protected. It can be a lot to take in, so let’s demystify insurance. Here’s what you need to know.

Rate: This is the baseline estimated price that would cover the potential cost of future claims. It’s determined using shared group factors such as age group; if you’re in your twenties, it would take into account the statistical driving information for other 25-year-olds in a region.

Premium: This is the adjusted individual insurance price that you’ll pay over a six- or 12-month period. It’s created by starting with the rate, then adding more specific factors that affect your claim risk level. These include your driving record, claims history, mileage usage, and vehicle type.

Overwhelmed? Don’t sweat it—State Farm can get you an insurance quote before you buy.

Ah, the eternal question. Are you commitment-minded or more of a free agent?

To answer that when it comes to car ownership, consider how much you drive annually (to avoid leasing mileage overages if you exceed the stated mileage), how long you plan to keep your vehicle, and your financial situation.

Then, know that there are insurance differences between leasing and buying, too. Leasing companies adhere to stricter requirements than those that most states set, which could translate into higher monthly premiums.

If you buy to avoid the mileage caps and wear-and-tear of leasing, but still want the benefits of a fresh ride every few years, there is a moment during your payment plan when you can get a new car. Your initial loan payments largely go to interest (what lenders charge for giving you money), but there’s a break-even point when your payments begin going to principal (the actual amount you’ve borrowed). When that happens, and the outstanding balance is equal to the value of the car, you can trade it in for that amount, which is a budget-friendly way to upgrade.

Whatever you choose, a little strategy will keep you in the driver’s seat from dreaming about your new car to signing on the dotted line.
