detour 2026 proves ‘better, not perfect’ is all it takes across 14 installations and a pop-up shop

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

exploring design through testing and revision, detour 2026 transforms PMQ with 14 experimental installations, a new pop-up shop, and matty benedetto’s asia debut.

mariana rodrigues November 22, 2026 6 min

From November 20 to 29, 2026, Hong Kong’s signature design festival deTour returns to PMQ under the guiding curatorial theme ‘BETTER, NOT PERFECT: Prototypes for Tomorrow’. The ten-day festival repositions design as a continuous cycle of rethinking, testing, and revising, proving that even the smallest adjustments can trigger profound impacts.

Across PMQ’s Courtyard and Marketplace, 14 commissioned installations and experiments from Hong Kong, Chinese Mainland, and international studios explore speculative urban concepts, material transformations, and the Asia debut of US inventor Matty Benedetto of Unnecessary Inventions. For the first time, the festival also opens an on-site pop-up shop, putting speculative prototypes and limited-edition objects directly into visitors’ hands to test how experimental ideas perform in the real world.

Organized by PMQ and sponsored by the Cultural and Creative Industries Development Agency (CCIDA) of the HKSAR Government, deTour 2026 is led by curator-at-large Shin Wong alongside a four-person team spanning architecture, spatial design, product development, and mixed media, featuring Aron Tsang, Wesley Ho, Dennis Cheung, and Chilai Howard. Taking its cues from Hong Kong’s urban fabric, the program reflects a city where design rarely starts from scratch, but instead responds to existing conditions to find better possibilities within real-world constraints.

As curator-at-large Shin Wong observes, ‘Good design doesn’t have to be groundbreaking or a grand innovation; sometimes it’s just about rethinking things you’ve taken for granted and finding better solutions.’ Set within PMQ, a revitalized creative landmark in Hong Kong’s SoHo district that now houses over 100 design studios, the festival builds on the hub’s commitment to supporting emerging talent and connecting experimental design with everyday public dialogue.

Structured around the design process, the festival unfolds across three zones, THE CONCEPT LAB, THE IRL FIELD, and THE FUTURE TRACK. Curated by Aron Tsang and Wesley Ho, THE CONCEPT LAB gathers six exhibits that reveal the testing process as everyday objects are exposed to innovative ideas. HONMI’s SECOND RAIN turns a familiar Hong Kong street sight, dripping air-conditioner runoff locally nicknamed ‘city juice’, into an ambient cooling mist by collecting, filtering, and atomizing the water.

Nearby, Jive Lau @Kowloneon presents Plasma Organism, combining traditional glass lampworking with micro-electronics to seal noble gases inside organic glass forms that glow like plasma. Exploring the blurred boundary between coral and stone, the work transforms heritage neon craftsmanship into intimate, wearable jewelry. In Seed Terrain, Kwan Wai Chan and Carolyn Tam combine hand-sculpted clay with 3D printing, translating the scholar’s rocks of classical Suzhou gardens into an undulating ceramic landscape that sprouts live plants.

Moving from early testing to physical materials, THE IRL FIELD, curated by Dennis Cheung, gathers six research-driven projects shaped through close material study. IAMMI’s Tofu turns discarded industrial polyurethane foam blocks into seats and tables hand-coated in a textured, stone-like skin that contrasts heavy visual weight with unexpected softness.

In ‘From waste to glaze, an urban alchemy’, the Hong Kong Glaze Research Lab converts reclaimed temple incense ash into colorful ceramic glazes, dispensing sample tiles through capsule vending machines. The section also marks the global debut of SYN:Ligna in Ultramar Studio by Ewan Lamm’s Rite of Passage, a composite material that recreates wood growth rings and grain patterns inside translucent, softly lit objects. Rounding out the highlights, studio dou6 geoi6 so2 challenges traditional woodturning symmetry with irregular timber off-cuts in Beyond the Circle, while Didi NG Wing Yin celebrates natural grain cracks and ink dyeing in Wood As It Is.

Speculative thinking and automation lead THE FUTURE TRACK, curated by Chilai Howard. Internet creator Matty Benedetto turns a familiar retail environment into a space for joyful discovery with the Department of Everyday Problems / Unnecessary Inventions, inviting visitors to leave their everyday hiccups at the counter or browse one of his imaginative solutions to otherwise non-existent problems. Following, Prof. Pok Yin Victor Leung, PhD, presents ‘Imagining Construction Otherwise’, built around a 3.5-meter scaffold and a robotic arm equipped with a new joint system developed at City University of Hong Kong. Visitors steer a mobile camera cart through the frame to experience automated construction from a machine’s point of view.

Beyond the main installations, the new deTour pop-up shop brings designers into a live retail setting where they can connect with the public and gather firsthand market feedback. Visitors can browse a wide selection spanning everyday essentials, thoughtful gifts, travel souvenirs, and design collectibles, alongside preorders for exhibiting prototypes, Matty Benedetto’s satirical inventions, and scale architectural furniture pieces from Miniature Pack. Elsewhere on the schedule, 20 hands-on workshops, curator-led and nine Creative Voice panel talks round out the program, exploring craft heritage, circular design, and urban futures.
