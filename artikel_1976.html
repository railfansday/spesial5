The Mag Podcast Episode 16: Newcastle United 2008-09 Chaos, Kevin Keegan and Relegation

Published: 13/11/2026 | Duration: 51 mins | Hosts: Jamie Smith, Simon Cahill & David Hutchinson

In Episode 16 of The Mag Pod, we find Jamie, Simon, and Hutch discussing relegation! Special focus is on the 2008-09 season when the combined efforts of Mike Ashley and Dennis Wise forced Kevin Keegan out and a drop to the Championship was the eventual result. So how does the current 2026-27 NUFC situation compare…?

Listen to the latest episode of The Mag Pod! Join Jamie, Simon, and Hutch for unfiltered football debate.

Newcastle United’s disastrous 2008-09 relegation season is back under the microscope as Jamie Smith, Simon Cahill and David Hutchinson revisit the chaos of Kevin Keegan’s departure, Mike Ashley’s ownership and Alan Shearer’s desperate rescue mission.

With Newcastle struggling for consistency in the 2026/27 Premier League season, the lads ask whether history offers any lessons for the current squad – and whether today’s concerns really compare with one of the club’s most turbulent campaigns.

The international break provides a welcome breather from Newcastle’s league struggles, and there’s time for a typically entertaining round up of international action, including Dan Burn and Lewis Hall representing England and Yoane Wissa finding the net for DR Congo. Managerial upheaval elsewhere in the Premier League also provides plenty of ammunition, particularly Manchester United’s latest troubles.

Then it’s back to 2008-09, when optimism surrounding Keegan’s return quickly unravelled into protests, questionable transfers and a succession of managerial changes.

Jamie recalls the toxic atmosphere following the exit of Kevin Keegan, the controversial roles of Dennis Wise and Tony Jimenez, and the extraordinary collection of players who somehow ended up relegated.

From Shay Given’s departure and Joe Kinnear’s eventful spell to Alan Shearer’s arrival with just eight matches remaining, the discussion explores where Newcastle’s season went wrong. There’s also time to remember some miserable away trips, controversial refereeing decisions and the players who eventually helped restore pride in the Championship.

Crucially, the lads compare that relegated side with Newcastle’s current squad, examining leadership, creativity, commitment and the importance of stability. Could the same mistakes happen again, or are today’s foundations considerably stronger?

With more historical comparisons planned throughout the international break, this opening instalment combines painful memories, plenty of gallows humour and a surprisingly reassuring perspective on Newcastle United’s present predicament.
