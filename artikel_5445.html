Mortgage demand pulled back as 30-year rates rose to a 3-week high

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Purchase applications fell 3% and refinance requests fell 2% as the average 30-year rate rose to 7.35% from 7.21%

The Mortgage Bankers Association said total mortgage application volume fell 2.3% last week from the prior week on a seasonally adjusted basis as the average 30-year fixed rate rose to its highest level in three weeks, according to CNBC.

The average contract rate climbed to 7.35% from 7.21% for 30-year fixed mortgages up to the $832,750 conforming limit. Points, which include the origination fee, edged up to 0.79 from 0.75 on loans requiring a 20% down payment.

"Treasury yields rose last week amid data showing inflation accelerated in October and Fed officials acknowledging that additional rate hikes may be needed in the coming months," said Joel Kan, vice president and deputy chief economist at the association, according to CNBC.

Refinance applications declined 2% over the week, leaving them 54% under their year-ago level. Federal Housing Administration refinance applications rose close to 7%. "The FHA rate was roughly 30 basis points below the conforming rate, which supported refinance opportunities for some homeowners," Kan said, according to CNBC.

Applications to buy a home slipped 3% over the week and sat 18% under their year-ago pace. Buyers are struggling with affordability and feel less confident about the economy because inflation persists, according to CNBC.

A separate rate tracker showed a larger average. HousingWire's Mortgage Rates Center showed conforming 30-year loans averaging 7.58% on Tuesday, an increase of 0.23 percentage point over the prior week, according to HousingWire. The same outlet said more than 93% of traders expect a 25-basis-point Fed rate increase at next month's meeting, up from 76% a month ago.

Rates have been flat so far this week, according to a Mortgage News Daily survey cited by CNBC. The Census Bureau is set to release housing starts and building permits this week, and the National Association of Realtors is set to publish its pending home sales index.

The latest reading follows a run of declines. Mortgage applications had fallen for a third straight week through Oct. 30, even as the 30-year contract rate eased to 7.30% from 7.37%. The purchase index was then near its weakest reading since January 2025.

The rate had reached 7.50% in the week ended Oct. 9, the highest since November 2023.
