Russian man accused of spying on US tech defence company that assists Ukraine

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The alleged foreign agent was discovered with two Russian passports

US investigators have arrested a Russian man who they say was recruited by Moscow to spy on employees of a technology and defence firm in California's Silicon Valley that has links to Ukraine.

Igor D'iu is accused of acting as an agent of a foreign government to conduct a spying operation in Palo Alto, near San Francisco.

He is also accused of stalking employees of the company and possessing falsified identity documents.

The company is not named in charging documents, but is described as a publicly traded technology and defence company that produces software used by Ukraine in its war with Russia. US prosecutors say the company was targeted by Russia due to its assistance to Kyiv.

"The allegations in this case describe a state-sponsored operation to target and harass employees of an American technology and defense company because of that company's assistance to Ukraine in its war with Russia," said US Attorney for the Northern District of California Craig Missakian.

"We will not tolerate any efforts - by foreign governments or otherwise - to turn Silicon Valley into a base for foreign intelligence operations."

Prosecutors say that D'iu entered the US unlawfully via the Mexico border, after travelling there from Istanbul in June.

He was allegedly recruited through the Telegram encrypted messaging app. His handlers referred to Russia as "'the Motherland' and directed D'iu to gather information about the company because it was supplying weapons used against Russia in Ukraine".

On their instruction, he purchased computers, phones, wireless networking equipment and remote access software - tools he needed for his spy mission.

He then went to a coffee shop located near the company's offices in Palo Alto, where he photographed laptop screens of employees who were working there.

His handlers also allegedly "instructed him to position his laptop at designated locations near the company so they could remotely access it and attempt to collect information from company employees in the vicinity".

The suspect allegedly took photos of employees screens at a cafe near the office

According to CBS News, the BBC's US partner, the company in question is Palantir Technologies. Palantir did not reply to a BBC request for comment.

D'iu also allegedly conducted surveillance of employees and their residences.

On 3 September, he followed a vehicle driven by one senior executive for nearly 30 miles. He fled after she drove to a police station. Police later obtained evidence that he had followed other employees' cars on previous days.

During the investigation, officials discovered identity cards that had been falsified - including a US passport, California ID card, and a Social Security card with the fake name "Hugo Landry Johns".

A raid on his home in October in East Palo Alto turned up two Russian passports, and a handwritten notebook that contained numerous home addresses of employees "family members' names, educational and employment histories, and assessments of their strengths and weaknesses", prosecutors say.

He faces two charges related his alleged work as a foreign agent, one charge of interstate stalking, and one count of fraud related to his identity documents. He faces over 15 years in prison if found guilty.

He has been held in jail since 2 November. It is unclear whether he has hired a lawyer who can speak on his behalf.

The arrest comes as espionage allegations roil the US tech industry, amid the news that Nvidia ex-CEO Jensen Huang had a romantic affair with a woman suspected of spying for China.

Last month, a former OpenAI employee was charged with planting a listening device in a company office. The man was later accused of having "considerable support" from the Saudi government.
