Nigerians among 12 suspects arrested in $4.5m Canada luxury car theft syndicate

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Canadian authorities say organised vehicle theft is a serious crime threatening the security of communities

Canadian police have arrested 12 suspects, including eight Nigerians, in an investigation into an alleged car theft syndicate.

They are accused of stealing luxury vehicles and shipping them to West Africa.

Police in the Peel region of Ontario said they had recovered 96 high-value vehicles concealed in shipping containers. The vehicles, valued at $4.5m (Â£3.4m) were destined for export via Canadian ports.

The trafficking of vehicles from North America and Europe to African countries has been a long-standing concern for law enforcement agencies.

The suspects face 231 charges, with arrest warrants issued for others. Police say some of the accused may face deportation after their criminal proceedings are concluded.

The arrests followed months of what police described as an "extensive auto theft investigation".

The investigations began in March in partnership with Canada's border agency after police identified a warehouse allegedly being used to store stolen vehicles.

Investigators believe the network was led by five people who coordinated the theft, transportation and export of the stolen vehicles, with the others playing supporting roles.

"Together, investigators linked the network to loading sites, container movements and the transportation of stolen re-VINed vehicles," the police said in a statement.

Re-VINing is the illegal practice of altering the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) to conceal its identity and make it harder to trace.

Canadian authorities have described organised vehicle theft as a growing threat, saying it was linked to wider criminal networks.

"Organised auto theft is a predatory business model run by transnational syndicates that terrorise families and fund broader criminal enterprises," Peel Regional Police Deputy Chief Nick Milinovich said.

Last year, Interpol said more than 100 luxury vehicles stolen from Canada and European countries had been recovered, exposing a network involved in trafficking stolen cars to West Africa.

In a separate operation the same year, Ghanaian security authorities recovered 43 luxury vehicles stolen from Europe and North America.

They attributed the success of the operation to improved cooperation between national and international law enforcement agencies.
