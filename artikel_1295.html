John McGinn warns Tartan Army it might take 18 MONTHS for Scotland to click into gear under new boss Pocognoli

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:42 EST, 14 November 2026 | Updated: 18:03 EST, 14 November 2026

Amid the clamour for a change at the helm following another poor showing at a major tournament, it was easy to lose sight of the reality of the situation.

Steve Clarke didn’t crash and burn in the US because a raft of world-class talents failed to turn up on the biggest stage. It happened primarily because a limited squad was unable to outperform two top-ten nations in Brazil and Morocco who could call upon infinitely superior footballers.

Sebastian Pocognoli has since arrived on the scene having earned a stellar reputation from his title success with Union Saint-Gilloise and his less feted spell at Monaco. But he has not come armed with a magic wand.

In opening the door to a string of fresh faces and advocating the more attacking approach which so many craved, the turbulence he’s experienced to date should only have been expected.

Limited by both the standard of players who are eligible for Scotland and the time he has to work with them, only the blissfully naïve would have seen his appointment as a quick fix.

While there were some positives to be taken from the game with North Macedonia – primarily the result – it’s likely the nation will have to endure more short-term pain for long-term gain.

Scotland boss Sebastien Pocognoli greets his players after a satisfactory evening at Hampden

This was always going to take time, perhaps, in the view of John McGinn, more than many may have imagined.

‘The manager’s got a great reputation, a good record at both places he’s been,’ said the Aston Villa captain. ‘The most important thing now in football is individuals don’t win you games. It’s unity, teamwork and everyone being on the same page.

‘You see how successful clubs and successful countries do it. And we want to be one of them.

‘It’s not going to happen overnight. There’s a lot to learn but we did create big chances against North Macedonia where we could have scored three, four or five and it could be a different story.

‘But this isn’t something that gets fixed in March or even next September. It will start to build over the next 18 months.’

If that predicted time frame might raise an eyebrow or two, McGinn makes no apology for it. Call it managing expectations from the midfielder if you please. But without a degree of patience from the Tartan Army, he feels Pocognoli’s ideas won’t take root.

‘I understand it’s frustrating,’ he added. ‘It’s freezing and you want to see four, five, six goals, but the balance has got to be there.

McGinn sends his penalty home to give Scotland a deserved lead against North Macedonia

‘They’re entitled to their opinion, but there’s got to be a mixture of patience.

‘The supporters are entitled to say whatever they like. But just as long as they hurl anything they want to shout at the experienced boys and just let the young ones flourish and try and settle into the team.’

Give credit where it’s due. With Clarke accused of causing stagnation by selecting the same old faces, Pocognoli has capped five players for the first time while recalling the likes of Oli McBurnie.

The sages in the stands can’t have it both ways. If the squad was going to evolve, it was never going to be a smooth ride.

‘I think the young players are starting to show more now,’ McGinn stressed. ‘Luke Graham on Friday night, I thought he was brilliant. It’s not easy playing in the middle of a back three. He’s been waiting for his chance and the manager’s shown that if you deserve your chance you’re going to get put in there.

‘We’ve got so many players that can step up and they will. We just need to show that belief and take it to the pitch.’

Even in victory, McGinn doesn’t dispute that there is significant scope for improvement. He’s convinced, however, that this will all come good in the end.

McGinn insists it's vital the entire Scotland squad buy into the methods of manager Pocognoli

‘We need to play slightly better,’ he said. ‘We know that, but we’re going through a period which I’ve been used to at club level, a lot of changes, a lot of detail that we need to try and remember and get used to.

‘And it’s about sticking at it. We’re keen to play a style of football that’s not easy but is going to get bums off seats. It’s important that everyone believes in it because if one person drops off or doesn’t believe in it, that’s when it all starts to fall apart.’

Friday was another memorable night on a personal level for McGinn. Having broken the tension with the opener only to see North Macedonia level, he held his nerve to put Scotland back from the penalty spot.

The brace put him on 24 international goals, now within six of Denis Law and Kenny Dalglish. But all of that is for another day.

‘It feels a bit strange, but at the minute that’s gone to the back burner,’ he stressed. ‘I had a wee chat with Scott (McTominay) about the penalties. I was designated penalty taker but, to be fair, he’s not missed one I don’t think in his career.

‘So, we had a wee chat and I think we both said the most important thing was just no matter who hit it, hit the back of the net.’

McGinn is eyeing three points against Switzerland in St Gallen to close the campaign on a high

Tomorrow, the equation is clear. To finish second in the Nations League group and secure a place in pot two for the Euro draw, Scotland must beat the Swiss and hope that Slovenia don’t beat North Macedonia.

Even if everything clicks for Pocognoli’s side, it will be the tallest of orders.

‘Difficult,’ said McGinn. ‘I know they drew on Friday night (with Slovenia) but they’ve got a couple of players back that they were missing last time.

‘It is a tough one but we want to be competing against these teams away from home. It’s not going to be perfect for a while, but we’ll stick at it.’
