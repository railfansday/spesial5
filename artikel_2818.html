San Antonio vs. New Orleans, Final Score: Wembanyama’s 50 points leads Spurs to 114-91 win

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Victor Wembanyama blew up on the Pelicans with a 32 point first half showing and 50 overall, and while San Antonio (14-3) allowed New Orleans (5-13) make two runs in the first half, they were not troubled after the first half in winning 114-91. The Spurs outscored the visitors in the first three quarters, and Wembanyama and Stephon Castle slammed the door shut to get the starters some early rest before their brief West Coast road trip. Wembanyama (50 points, 19 rebounds, 5 blocks, and 4 steals) put up the types of numbers with this transcendent performance that might be used to make the case for him in the MVP battle with Luka Doncic.

Wembanyama (31 points on 12-for-15 FG, 13 rebounds, and 3 blocks) had the type of first half that so many bigs would kill for in a whole game. Stephon Castle (19 points and 6 rebounds) found his outside shooting touch with five threes. De'Aaron Fox dished out 10 assists.

Jeremiah Fears (29 points, 5 rebounds, 4 assists, and 2 steals) fearlessly kept the Pelicans in striking distance for 2+ periods. Jordan Poole (25 points) shot the ball well to support his backcourt mate as the pair made more than half of their team's field goals and points for the evening. Second year big Derik Queen chipped in 15 points and 15 rebounds.

With Zion Williamson (eye) being a late scratch minutes before tipoff, New Orleans looked out of sorts from the get-go. When the Spurs coasted their way to a 15-4 lead, Fears decided to take things into his own hands to nearly single-handedly erase that deficit in minutes. Poole came into the game and put up nine points frighteningly fast, and in a couple of blinks, the Pelicans were up three. Jordan McLaughlin helped preserve a disastrous quarter with hustle and grit, and San Antonio appeared lucky to be up 32-29.

Wembanyama asserted himself forcefully well at the start of the second period, as the Spurs went on a 13-2 run. His scores seemingly came from all over the court throuhgout the first half. The middle of the period run happened again as New Orleans threatened San Antonio with a 9-1 sequence. Fox, in particular, while kept out of the scorebook, was a noticeable menace on defense in guarding Fears. Wembanyama was the Spurs' fulcrum on offense (32 of the Spurs' 61) to help his team get out of the half up 16.

After Wembanyama's inevitable slowdown in the third period, Castle made the most of his diet of outside shots – with his four three-pointers putting New Orleans back on their heels and supplied San Antonio with the necessary cushion to secure its 14th win of the season.

I do like this version of Fox that's more pesky on both ends – he ran down Vassell's first miss, which ultimately led to a corner three and then he harangued Fears to cause a turnover on the ensuing inbounds. Fox, later in the half, bothered Fears into a backcourt violation and was whistled for a technical as the two had to be separated.

Though I'm not a native Spanish speaker, I thought it very humourous that a local mariachi band performed a song with "llorar" in its refrain after San Antonio ran its lead to 20.

Has there been a bigger dropoff from someone who was critical to his team's chances in an NBA Finals (2022) to wasting away in obscurity (2026) as Poole? There's also probably a similar, if not more statement to be made for Dejounte Murray… and well, Williamson whenever he changes teams.

The team that introduced the corner three as a useful offensive concept in the 2000s missed quite a bit of them this evening with an alarming amount of them airballs.

San Antonio and Memphis are the only undefeated teams left on the road(?!)

Fox-Slamma-Jamma-Wembanyama #1: While New Orleans pelted the rim and backboard with some unsightly attempts, Fox found his center with a lob partway through the opening stanza to push the Spurs lead to 11.

Carter Bryant really did seem confined to the Bruce Bowen corners for his offense in the first three quarters. He did connect on two threes later in the second half.

Sequence of the Game #1: Late in a wildly swinging first quarter, McLaughlin – giving 12 inches away to Karlo Matkovic, swiped the ball away from the big man, kicked it upcourt to Fox, who found Wembanyama for a non-shunk lob.

Sequence of the Game #2: Late in the third quarter, Wembanyama swatted away a Missi dunk attempt, and then drew an offensive foul call on Pierre, Jr. to cause the turnover.

Sequence of the Game #3: After Bryant missed a three in the fourth quarter, the long carom fell into Harrison Barnes' hands, and the second year guard cut baseline, received Barnes' pass, and threw down a thunderous two-hand slam.

Tarris Reed, Jr. (lithe AND bruising big man) playing time: In contesting a Poole floater, the rookie accidentally kneed the Pelican in the torso. He did get to four rebounds (two offensive) in his six minutes of playing tie.

Vassell bricked his first three attempts, but Wembanyama tip-slammed home the third try. Wembanyama, while passing it to Vassell, ended up smacking Queen right in the face, which took the Pelican momentarily out of the action. Two Champagnie triples put the Spurs up six. The Pelicans halfcourt offense during the initial minutes was really difficult to describe – Yves Missi managed to overshoot the rim by a good 5+ feet on a baseline jumper and many other possessions ended in long jumpers. Vassell willed in his fourth three attempt, and the San Antonio lead swelled to 11. Fears, like a robot that suddenly came to life, went on a 9-point burst, and with Poole's trio of threes, the Pelicans erased the deficit because… killer-instinct-still-in-process. Wembanyama then went on his own 9-point eruption to end the quarter with San Antonio up three.

San Antonio methodically started building out its lead to start the second. Then, on a foul committed on Wembanyama, someone named Jaron Pierre, Jr. shoved him after the play, and the rookie and Keldon Johnson were assessed double technicals. (It was cool to hear the referee refer to the superstar as 'Wemby' after the decision came back from Secaucus). The free throws pushed Wembanyama's point total past 20 a mere 14+ minutes into game action. The center's corner three then put the Pelicans down 12. However, right around the same time as it occurred in the previous period, New Orleans burned hot while the Spurs went cold, and a 14-point game became seven – with a Vassell free throw was the only Spurs damage during the Pelicans' 9-1 push. Then, just like in the first quarter, Wembanyama ran off a series of buckets to get himself over the 30-point mark, and San Antonio coasted into halftime up 61-45.

Normally one of New Orleans' biggest contributors, Bey's first and only points came two minutes into the third quarter. Castle, on a pair of catch-and-shoot threes, helped get the Spurs back up past 20. After San Antonio made it 69-48, Fears tried to kickstart yet another comeback attempt, but Castle countered with his third and fourth triples of the period preventing the Pelicans from trimming the deficit. Bryant snagged a pair of rebounds in traffic despite no offensive success to that point. Johnson and Fox took turns finding their superstar center deep in the paint for lay-up on successive possessions, and then teamed with each other for a quarter-ending Johnson three. The Spurs went to the fourth up 89-66.
