Knicks’ lack of wing depth is rearing ugly head with recent injuries

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Since the mountain of injuries single-handedly derailed the Knicks' 2024 playoff run, they've been the pinnacle of health around the NBA.

Aside from dealing with the lack of durability from Mitchell Robinson, almost no long-term injuries have hampered the Knicks' playoff aspirations. Even the more bothersome injuries, Landry Shamet's shoulder issues, Deuce McBride's core surgery, and Jalen Brunson's nagging ankles, all resolve themselves before the postseason.

Coming off a grueling championship run and attempting an exceptionally difficult repeat in an age of parity, injuries are the looming threat that could derail things before they get started.

It's no wonder, then, that the team has been playing it safe with minor ailments to Jose Alvarado and Josh Hart over the last few weeks, but two more issues have sprung up over the last few days. Jordan Clarkson is dealing with a bout of plantar fasciitis, and OG Anunoby has a calf strain.

Both of these could easily be resolved in under a week, but could both also derail both of their seasons.

Plantar fasciitis can be managed, which is likely what the team is trying to do, but more severe bouts that build up over time could leave players out for months. Calf injuries in recent years have been a dangerous precursor for Achilles tears, as seen with Kevin Durant and Tyrese Haliburton in recent years. Even if it's a mild strain, it is imperative to take it slow.

That might mean no Anunoby for a while, even if no timetable has been presented. For a coach like Mike Brown who loves to experiment in the regular season, that might not seem like too bad.

For us fans? It means seeing some… interesting lineup combinations in close games. It's one thing when they played around with Dalton Knecht and Mo Diawara against the Bucks and Bulls, but Pacôme Dadiet? Now that's something.

Landry Shamet seems to have replaced Anunoby in the starting lineup, and although his incredible shooting and great defense are providing a spark to a starting five that usually starts sluggish (Starters w/Shamet replacing Anunoby have a +29.4 net rating in 15 minutes, compared to -12.9 with the regulars), he lacks size.

This presents a problem that the Knicks knew they had, but it has further exacerbated the team's quietest shortcoming: wing depth.

In a perfect world, Diawara would've taken the next step in his development and firmly entrenched himself in the rotation, but he's been passed over for Dadiet in the last week for those bench wing minutes vacated by the injuries. Part of me thinks it's an attempt to raise the expiring French wing's trade value, but it could be something in practice prompting this opportunity.

Since Diawara signed his four-year extension in July, there's been no sign of improvement. He struggled in Summer League and has been fairly regular in any action he's had since. That's opened the door for Dadiet, who, although seems more confident from three-point range, has also been unspectacular.

It's hard to hide them in a lineup offensively given the roster construction. Sure, ideally, you have Karl-Anthony Towns or Jalen Brunson on the court at all times, but that's just one player. When one is off the court, either Tyler Kolek or Andre Drummond is on, and neither are any threat to score.

Surround that with Hart's offensive struggles, Mikal Bridges' inconsistency, and the limited games of McBride and Shamet, and you have some painful offensive stretches. When Brown gets more involved, it gets better, but teams don't want to be constantly running stuff:

The options are extremely limited, and if you try to look deeper than Diawara or Dadiet, you get two guys who shoot and don't do much else (Tyler Nickel, Knecht). That's it.

This isn't that big of a deal if Anunoby's strain is mild and he's back soon. Injuries happen, and everyone has to eventually play the end of the bench in unenviable situations over a grueling 82-game season, so Brown will just hold his nose and rely on his army of small-to-medium-sized guards in these spots and hope they won't get burned when the better teams eventually come.

But long term, could the Knicks use another warm body?

The Knicks are unable to sign a 15th player until February 13, squarely in buyout market territory, and it's far too soon to tell who could be available. There's also the trade deadline with some interesting contract situations on the roster, but is getting an insurance policy the top priority over, say, an upgrade at backup center?

We're well past the Vince Goodwill fake panic of the first few weeks, and the Knicks are sitting pretty in a three-way tie at second in the East through 17 games, but maybe the front office will need to get to work on more than they originally thought they would have to.
