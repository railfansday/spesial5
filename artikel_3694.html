Megaspeed is a company with a lot of eyes on it at the moment, and many eyes makes many, uh, news stories. The US Department of Commerce and Department of Justice have thought it to be a good time to drop some details on its investigations into the Singaporean datacentre company, once led by one Alice Huang.

If you've already forgotten who Alice Huang is, she was the person first suspected of having an affair with Jensen Huang. The ex-Nvidia CEO later admitted to an affair with someone that has remained unnamed.

It wasn't just the affair that sunk Huang, it was the suspicion that Alice Huang may be a Chinese Intelligence asset, according to a New York Times report. That was later rebuffed by Attorney General Todd Blanche and the CCP. It was also that the company Alice Huang ran, Megaspeed, was suspected of chip smuggling; and the other two alleged affairs thereafter with Nvidia employees.

"After identifying suspicious activity through proactive monitoring of potential sensitive semiconductor diversion, the Department of Commerce initiated an investigation into Megaspeed in 2024 for potential export control violations.In May 2025, Department of Commerce investigators requested records from Nvidia concerning direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed.In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its 'no sell' list. Based on the Department of Commerce’s investigation, agents from the Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security, in conjunction with Singaporean law enforcement officials, conducted an on-site, in-person, end-use check at Megaspeed in June 2025 and interviewed CEO Alice Huang."

There's a lot to chew on there. Megaspeed holds it was cleared of any suspicions of diverting chips to China in contradiction of US export rules. It claimed in a statement, dated December 23, 2025 (once the above Commerce investigation had taken place):

"We want to be absolutely clear: over the past six months, our company has undergone multiple on-site inspections and reviews by key institutions, including the Bureau of Industry and Security of the US Commerce Department, the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry of Malaysia, and Nvidia. These thorough investigations have confirmed that there has been no evidence of any violations concerning the illicit transfer of chips or any illegal activities, and this demonstrates that we are a compliant and legitimate company."

Nvidia also long held that Megaspeed was in the clear, with a company spokesperson saying the accusations against Megaspeed were "thoroughly investigated and debunked last year".

That's not quite the end of US investigations into the company, however, as the agencies suggest in their joint statement:

"Following this enforcement action [by the Department of Commerce], the Department of Justice took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed."

That doesn't specify that it's ongoing, but the statement begins with the two agencies noting they wouldn't usually talk about an ongoing investigation, but felt it necessary to "provide facts and address inaccurate information circulating in public reporting."

"The Department of Justice and the Department of Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security working together will bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred. This investigation is the result of the excellent work of the Department of Commerce under Secretary Howard Lutnick and the law enforcement efforts of the Department of Justice under Attorney General Todd Blanche."

On its website, Megaspeed posted the following statement on November 12, the day Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia CEO. It is addressed from Tan Yong Pong, James, Chairman & CEO of Megaspeed:

"Megaspeed takes its compliance obligations under U.S. export control laws extremely seriously. Compliance is a fundamental priority for the company, and Megaspeed is committed to working constructively and transparently with the U.S. government.Megaspeed has cooperated with inspections by the U.S. Department of Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) and stands ready to cooperate fully with any other relevant U.S. government authority.To further reinforce our commitment to compliance, Megaspeed has retained a leading U.S. law firm to lead a comprehensive census of its graphics processing units (GPUs) and independently verify its export control compliance practices.Megaspeed welcomes appropriate government scrutiny and is committed to demonstrating through concrete actions that it operates responsibly, transparently, and in accordance with applicable U.S. laws."

Megaspeed also said that Alice Huang officially left Megaspeed on August 30, 2025 and has no role with the company.

Singapore has agreed to continue discussions with the US about Megaspeed, which just goes to show how intently the United States is looking into this whole chip affair. The country has also tightened export controls to curb China's access to Nvidia GPUs remotely, as this has been suggested as one way many Chinese models may have been trained on cutting-edge Blackwell GPUs they should not be able to purchase otherwise.
