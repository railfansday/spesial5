Tiger backs PGA Tour changes as update given on future of LIV players

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The PGA Tour board engaged in "thoughtful discussions" about the future of players at LIV Golf without confirming a pathway back; Brian Rolapp discussed LIV Golf players' future during an announcement on the PGA Tour structure from 2028

PGA Tour chief executive Brian Rolapp insists there are currently no plans to reintroduce a “Returning Member Program” to welcome back former LIV Golf players.

LIV Golf confirmed earlier this month they were winding down operations instead of launching a new version, because of a lack of financial support, leaving players looking for pathways back to traditional tours.

Brooks Koepka took financial penalties earlier this year to return to the PGA Tour via the Returning Member Program, a pathway created for a limited group of 'elite performers', with Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm and Cameron Smith elected not to follow suit.

Rahm has said he 'probably would do it' if offered a similar route back again, while DeChambeau said he would go about 'whatever is next the right way' and has since accepted DP World Tour membership, although Rolapp has played down that - as things stand - being a possibility next season.

"There are no plans for that currently, no," Rolapp told reporters on a media call, following the PGA Tour approving details for its new ​competitive structure from 2028.

Players are currently suspended for 12 months from their last LIV Golf appearance before being eligible to feature on the PGA Tour, with Patrick Reed being a full-time member on the DP World Tour this season before heading back to the US circuit in 2027.

Sergio Garcia, Dustin Johnson and Joaquin Niemann are among the players following a similar schedule on the DP World Tour next season, with the PGA Tour having "thoughtful discussion" about how to incorporate LIV Golf players going forward.

"We have heard from ​our fans and we know that they want the best players playing ‌together and we share that view," Rolapp added. "I have said that from day one when I took the job, I am interested in doing whatever makes the PGA Tour better.

"I have said that from day one when I took the job, I am interested in doing whatever makes the PGA Tour better. As we have a membership organization, we have rules and, as I shared before, they exist to ensure fairness and competitive balance.

"Rules matter and they will be enforced. We engaged in thoughtful discussion on the topic of LIV players with both boards [PGA Tour and the PGA Tour Enterprises]. As far as we have read, bankruptcy proceedings are ongoing and the process is complex.

"I hope to have more to say in the beginning of the New Year but, until that time, I will not speculate on the outcome or individual decisions players make."

The Future Competition Committee - chaired by Tiger Woods - announced Monday further details of the PGA Tour's new competitive structure, set to debut in 2028.

"I can confidently say the PGA Tour is in the best position it has ever been," Woods said. "We created a competitive model grounded in meritocracy, built to last and that offers the best opportunity for the next generation of players."

The PGA Tour is doing away with the PGA Tour Americas, instead creating a vast network of feeder tournaments throughout the year that will have one ranking system.

The Korn Ferry Tour will have at least 20 tournaments with $1m purses (£756,000) and the leading 20 players will be promoted to the Challenger Series.

The top 20 players in the Challenger Series advance to the Championship Series, with the board approving elevating the final event that would be for the top 90 players.

The winner of the season finale would be promoted to the Championship Series, even if he isn't among the top 20. The number of places on the Championship Series via the DP World Tour's "Race to Dubai" rankings has yet to be finalised.
