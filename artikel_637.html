Bela Bajaria Reflects on Her Netflix Experience and Says ‘Stay Tuned’ at International Emmy Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

France takes drama series win for period piece 'Carême'; Argentina nabs comedy for 'División Palermo'; Turkey's 'Leyla' recognized for telenovela

Former Netflix executive Bela Bajaria put the emphasis on her belief in the transformative power of storytelling while accepting the Founders Award Monday night at the International Emmy Awards in New York.

Bajaria was ousted from her role as chief content officer of Netflix in a very public fashion last month amid a larger shake-up of content leadership at the streaming giant. In her speech — her first major public appearance since her departure — Bajaria reflected on her career journey to the top echelon of Hollywood as a senior executive at CBS and Universal Television before joining Netflix in 2016. In a nod to this week’s Thanksgiving holiday, Bajaria opened by expressing her gratitude for the opportunities she’s been afforded since her family relocated to Southern California in the late 1970s, when she was in grade school.

“Gratitude isn’t passive or soft. It’s earned. Gratitude is strength in knowing nothing is guaranteed, and nothing can be taken for granted. Gratitude is the appreciation for both struggle and success, and sharing the journey with others,” Bajaria told the crowd.

During most of the past decade, Bajaria was focused on building out Netflix’s local-language TV content operation at a scale never before seen in the industry. The experience was profound as the regional teams set off on a search for new creative voices.

“Whether I was in Mumbai or Madrid, Bangkok or Berlin or anywhere in between — there’s nothing like being on the ground finding that original voice — in their homes, or sharing a meal, and looking eye to eye with openness, curiosity and respect,” Bajaria said. “I’ve seen firsthand how the saying is true: to paraphrase, you can ‘travel the world and never be a stranger.’ ”

Bajaria indicated pride in the legacy of her role having greatly expanded the global village of television during her tenure at Netflix.

“I’m full of gratitude for how far that idea — that belief — has forever transformed storytelling allover the world and not just in entertainment but in sports, brands and fandoms of all kinds,” she said. “Gratitude to all of you — you tell your stories from your own country and in your own language.Someone far away, who you will never meet, will watch and remember your work in the mostunexpected ways. You will stay with them forever.”

Bajaria’s kudos were presented by Keri Russell, star of Netflix’s “The Diplomat,” and were preceded by a video with compliments on her leadership and creative instincts from such notable stars and creatives as Kate Hudson, Hasan Minhaj, Peter Morgan, Lee Sung Jin, Shonda Rhimes, Tan France, Ricardo Darín, Alex Pina, David Beckham and Hwang Dong-hyuk.

In closing, Bajaria more than hinted to the audience that another chapter of her career is soon to come.

“I’ve never been more excited for the next season. You know I love to surprise an audience,” she said. “So stay tuned.”

Also honored during the event was Gerhard Zeiler, the former head of Germany’s RTL and former head of international for Time Warner and its successor companies. He exited Warner Bros. Discovery as part of the sale to Skydance and is preparing to mount a political run to lead Austria’s ruling Social Democratic Party. Zeiler’s Directorate Award was presented by former WBD chief executive David Zaslav.

The BBC had a big night in the competitive awards.

The award for TV movie/miniseries was claimed by the BBC for “Prisoner 951,” from Dancing Ledge Productions, which tells the true story of a British-Iranian woman imprisoned in Iran for six years. The award for actor went to Bobby Schofield for his work in the searing BBC 2 family drama “Unforgivable,” from LA Productions.

The Beeb also got the nod for documentary for “Louis Theroux: The Settlers,” from Mindhouse Productions. And it swept the children’s programming cateogies. Kids animation honor went to the “The Scarecrows Wedding,” produced by Magic Light Pictures. Live-action kid series went to “Dexter Procter: The 10-Year-Old Doctor,” produced by Terrible Productions and Various Artists Ltd. And the kids factual and entertainment kudo went to “Horrible Science,” produced by Lion Television.

France’s historical drama “Carême” (VVZ / Shine Fiction) for Apple TV prevailed in the drama category. Turkey’s “Leyla” (Ay Yapim) won for telenovela.

Comedy series honors went to Season 2 of Argentina’s “División Palermo [Community Squad]” (K&S Films / Netflix). The trophy for best actress went to Belgium’s Mona Mina Leon for the series “Holy Sh!t” (Fobic Films / Streamz).

Nonscripted entertainment went to Season 2 of Channel 4’s “Educating Yorkshire,” from TwoFour.

In the news heat, Sky News prevailed for its Ukraine documentary “The World: 24 Hours in the Kill Zone.”

South Africa claimed sports documentary honors for “The Philipstown Wirecare Grand Prix,” produced by Giant Films.

The current affairs category was taken by ITV’s “Exposure: Breaking Ranks: Inside Israel’s War” (Zandland / ITV1).

Belgium’s “De Klas van 2000 [The Class of 2000]” (De Mensen / DPG Media) won for short-form series.

The arts programming award went to Germany’s “Der talentierte Mr. F” (Neue Flimmer GmbH on behalf of MDR / BR / ARD Kultur / RBB / HR)
