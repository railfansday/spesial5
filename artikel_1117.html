Fleetwood knock out Arsenal in one of biggest shocks in League Cup history

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Leaving Highbury was a wrench for Arsenal in 2006, but it was not the case 20 years later. They could not wait to get out of the stadium with the same name after a night that left Mikel Arteta looking haunted.

Fleetwood Town did not have any marble halls; they did not even have enough space in their car park for the three coaches carrying Arsenal’s entourage, but they have a proud tradition, spirit and, as a result, they will dine out on a result that will be celebrated for months and talked about for decades.

Eighty-four rungs separated these teams at kick-off, but, now and again, you get a situation when the floodlights are on, belief is high and the sense of history is prevalent. It is an intoxicating combination and, gloriously, the stars aligned. Mission impossible was completed.

Having chiselled out a 1-1 draw in 90 minutes in which Max Dowman was sent off, Fleetwood dispatched five nerveless spot-kicks, the last one thundered in by Conor Haughey after Bruno Guimarães’s penalty was saved by Jay Lynch.

“You have got to have confidence, haven’t you?” Haughey asked. “I just thought, I’m going to run up and smack it. It’s incredible. I come from Belfast and if you don’t have confidence, you will get eaten up.”

This was ignominious for Arsenal, an elimination from this tournament to rival the 1969 loss in the final to Swindon Town or the 2012 exit to Bradford, then also of League Two. What made this so astonishing was the fact that Mikel Arteta went so strong.

You should never be surprised by anything these days, but, even still, an eight-minute delay because of a drone flying over this wonderful little venue would not have been on anyone’s bingo card. Only 57 seconds had been played when Michael Salisbury, the referee, saw a light in the sky and said “stop”.

The precaution was understandable. Only 10 days ago, one idiot escaped from a village to kick a rugby ball off the roof of an apartment block into the Gtech Community Stadium while Brentford and Liverpool were playing and could have caused serious injury.

So while the locals harrumphed as the players tried to keep themselves warm, no risk could be taken. Eventually, police apprehended the operator in a nearby park. It was something unfathomable, which was also true of what happened when the contest resumed.

The anticipation was that Arsenal would saunter clear against opponents who had lost at Exeter City on Saturday, leaving them 18th in England’s fourth tier. But it only took a few skirmishes to appreciate this was not going to be the cakewalk anticipated in what was the biggest occasion in Fleetwood’s history.

Arteta absolutely knew his team were not operating at the levels they should have been. He was wrapped up against the elements in a thick black jacket but, every so often, he would express his exasperation at Jurriën Timber, captain for the night, to “get forward!”

They were in a battle, that much was obvious, and one challenge from Shaun Rooney – so forceful you thought the ball might burst – sent Myles Lewis-Skelly spinning like a top. How Fleetwood’s fans loved it, roaring their approval as the young England international hit the ground with a thump.

Arsenal had one good chance when Dowman skipped down the right and dragged a shot across the box that Viktor Gyökeres could not divert but then, wonderfully, things took a totally unexpected turn when Fleetwood poked their noses in front.

It was a League Two goal in its simplicity, but that is no criticism. Harrison Neal helped a long ball forward, putting it behind Marli Salmon and Aaron Loupalo-Bi relished the chance to get into a foot race, grappling with the England Under-17 international.

Salmon looked like he had him at one point, but Loupalo-Bi was strong – not to mention composed – and he held off the challenge to slide a left-footed shot through the legs of Illan Meslier, who was making his Arsenal debut. What followed was unique to the magic of an English cup tie.

There is no evidence or anecdote suggesting Arsenal were the inspiration, so it appears the kit clash is a coincidence. Arsenal will be wearing their navy away strip.

The club’s most famous son is surely Jamie Vardy, who scored 31 goals in 36 appearances for Fleetwood in the 2011-12 season before Leicester City made their move.

An unfamiliar opponent at a very familiar sounding venue, Arsenal play Fleetwood Town for the first time this evening at the Lancashire club’s Highbury Stadium.

Fleetwood, 18th in League Two, are the lowest-ranked team remaining in this season’s League Cup, having booked their place in the fourth round thanks to a victory at Sheffield United.

They were playing in English football’s sixth tier in 2009, and so while their stadium may share its name with Arsenal’s former home, the two clubs are worlds apart.

The League Cup is fourth of four competitions on Arsenal’s list of priorities, but Mikel Arteta should be able to rotate extensively while progressing to the quarter-finals. With a trip to Anfield on Sunday, that is a welcome luxury.
