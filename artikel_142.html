Mexican special forces arrest drug kingpin 'Chapo Isidro'

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The alleged leader of the Beltran Leyva gang and one of the FBI's most wanted fugitives, known as "Chapo Isidro," was captured by the Mexican military following a raid and a shootout in the coastal state of Sinaloa.

A gun battle between Mexican soldiers and alleged gang members in the city of Guasave, in the state of Sinaloa, ended with special forces detaining senior members of the Beltran Leyva drug trafficking network, Mexican officials said on Saturday.

The gang is aligned with the Mayos, one of the factions of the powerful Sinaloa cartel.

Authorities said the overnight raid resulted in the capture of the suspected drug kingpin Fausto Isidro Meza F., better known as "Chapo Isidro," who is believed to be the leader of Beltran Leyva.

The operation left at least 11 people dead. Seven alleged cartel gunmen and four Mexican soldiers were killed, Mexico’s Defense Ministry said on Saturday afternoon.

The 44-year-old Mexican national is one of the FBI's ten most wanted fugitives. The US law enforcement agency claims his group is responsible for smuggling "large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl, and marijuana into the United States," and has offered a $5 million (€4.56 million) reward for information leading to his arrest.

He is also wanted in Mexico on charges including organized crime. "Chapo Isidro" was once a rival of imprisoned drug kingpin Joaquin "El Chapo" Guzman.

The three detainees were transported to Mexico City "to be placed at the disposal of the competent prosecutorial authorities," the military said.

It was not immediately clear whether authorities would extradite "Chapo Isidro" to the US.

On Saturday, Mexican authorities said the raid was conducted due to "intelligence coordination" between Mexico's military and US agencies. However, they offered few details about how authorities in both countries coordinated.

In a post on X, US Ambassador to Mexico Ronald Johnson wrote: "Results like these are not achieved overnight. They are the product of sustained cooperation and trust. This is what we can accomplish when we work together. ​We will continue pursuing criminals."

The troops used "stealth and surprise" to reach the area, establishing a perimeter around the drug dealer's location and then moving in from multiple directions, according to Mexico's US embassy.

Unverified videos from Guasave, however, showed military helicopters firing at ground targets and sports fans scrambling for safety during a baseball game. Some of the violence in the western Mexican city seems to have taken place after "Chapo Isidro" was arrested, with drug cartels attempting to retaliate against the authorities and civilians for the move.

Mexico's Defense Ministry confirmed cartel members set up roadblocks in the area, but added that security forces were assisting local authorities in restoring order.

The Guasave raid is only the latest in a string of high-profile moves against senior leaders of Mexico's illegal drug trade​​​​​​. Less than two weeks ago, the Mexican Navy announced the arrest of Rafael A., suspected of leading a cartel cell active in the states of Jalisco and Michoacan.

Mexican President ​Claudia Sheinbaum is facing pressure from Donald Trump, who has threatened to take military action against cartels on Mexican territory.
