Diving deeper into Cavs 126-115 win over Bulls: Cavaliers are discovering hidden depth

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

CLEVELAND — It wasn't the prettiest game, but the Cleveland Cavaliers did enough to get past a struggling Chicago Bulls team 126-115 thanks to a 39-point performance from Donovan Mitchell.

39 points on 16-of-25 shooting, 4 rebounds, 3 assists

Mitchell showed once again why he's one of the best scorers in the league. He repeatedly got into the teeth of the defense to create high-quality looks, going 11-for-14 on shots in the paint.

That was all the Cavs needed to get past the Bulls.

Powell has a difficult task. His teammates are all slotted one or two roles higher than they would be on a more functional team. This often forces Powell to create in a cramped half-court and playmake for teammates that the defense is mostly fine with leaving open.

Despite those limitations, Powell has been a useful player this season, and was again on Monday. He went 7-for-9 on shots in the paint. A 0-for-6 shooting performance from three kept this from being an even better night.

It's incredibly difficult to shift between roles as seamlessly as Proctor has done in the last week. He's gone from being the primary on-ball creator in the win over the Dallas Mavericks to being a mostly off-ball shooter and second-side facilitator alongside the veteran backcourt. Vastly different things are being asked of him depending on the role.

On Monday, Proctor was a good off-ball player. He initiated actions when the ball swung to him and wasn't afraid to shoot when he had openings. The Cavs need tertiary players who can attack a defense that's overloaded to stop dribble penetration from Mitchell and James Harden. Proctor has shown over the past few weeks that he can do that. He'll be a mainstay in the rotation as long as that continues.

Afterward, head coach Kenny Atkinson said that he's still figuring out what Proctor's role is when the team is fully healthy.

"It's starting to reveal itself," Atkinson said. "We all know when the playoffs come and all that they're going to take Don and James away, and you need that other ball handler to get in the paint and make plays."

Mitchell echoed that point when asked about Proctor's game. Someone who can ball-handle like that can help in the postseason

"If he's going to continue to score, then we'll take the buckets," Mitchell said. "And then if he makes the right plays, which he has been, it's just a plus for us."

This was the fourth straight game Proctor has scored 15 or more points.

The Bulls desperately needed someone who could create off the dribble. Giddey is one of the few players who has the talent to actually do that. However, he wasn't able to do that here, resulting in the Bulls benching him down the stretch.

4 points, 6 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 1 block

Omier plays basketball like the linebacker at your college that you run into at open gym. Nobody wants to try to stay in front of a guy who's that physical and single-mindedly focused on running through whoever is standing in their way. And they're stronger than everyone else they're playing with, which makes it even more unpleasant.

Unlike this annoying guy you run into at open gym, Omier can actually play basketball.

It's incredibly difficult to make it in the league as a 6'5" forward — especially if you don't have superb on-ball skills. Omier can shoot some, but nobody would say he's a stretch big. He is, however, a tank that is willing to bulldoze his opponent to get to his spots.

Omier did that here. The Cavs were sleepwalking through the first quarter with some pretty uninspiring basketball, then Omier entered the game and changed that by simply moving people out of the way.

"He's got a magnet for hands, strength, quickness to the ball," Atkinson said. "I know it's just two games, but it's pretty impressive."

Guys like this are incredibly important over an 82-game season. Sometimes you just need someone to come in and light a fire under your team.

"I'm just glad we don't have to box him out in camp anymore," Mitchell joked.

9 points, 10 assists, 4 rebounds, 1 steal

This was a good outing from an offensive process standpoint. Harden wasn't an efficient scorer (3-for-10), but he wasn't forcing the issue. More importantly, he kept the offense moving. He wasn't dominating the ball, was looking to hit outlet passes when they were available, and limited his turnovers (two). This led to him leading the team in plus/minus at a +15.

"This is starting to be similar to last year where we're playing a lot of different guys. … Last year there's a real silver lining to that. We've learned a lot about our guys. … You want to win games while you're discovering these guys, and that's a good thing about these past two games that we found a way to win the game, but now we're discovering different players and different lineups."

– Kenny Atkinson on what he's learned about the team's depth

It's been an uneven start to the year. However, learning more about guys like Meleek Thomas, Proctor, and others could be a blessing. The Cavs have gotten a good look at how they look in actually meaningful minutes. These reps can be invaluable for guys trying to find their place in the league.

Mitchell went 8-for-10 on shots in the paint.

"I love when he gets downhill like that and in the paint," Atkinson said. "I know he's a great pull-up three-point shooter. Just when he gets in the paint for us, it just causes so much havoc."

This wasn't a remarkable game for Cleveland. The Bulls are simply a bad team. They don't have the talent to compete against skilled teams on a night-to-night basis — especially when they aren't hitting their threes. Putting them away with a relatively stress-free victory is exactly what you want.

There wasn't a whole lot from this game that you'd feel comfortable extrapolating further, but that's okay. Sometimes you just need to pick up wins when you play games like this, and that's what the Cavs did.
