Rockets sputter late in Denver 133-124

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Tonight featured the Rockets third NBA Cup game, which was essentially a must-win after their opening NBA Cup round loss to Dallas. The Rockets would be playing in Denver, against a Nuggets team that seems to be finding its way after initially scuffling this season.

Many have mocked and derided the NBA Cup, and sure, it's a gimmick, and it's artificial, and so forth. But the sad truth is, professional sports are a gimmick, and are artificial, too. This is just a new wrinkle in the existing structure, not some sort of attack on the traditional and pure nature of NBA basketball. But even if you think it is some sort of assault, don't you want to watch players trying their hardest, during the regular season? Because they do try hard. These games actually are extra, because hyper competitive NBA players like being hyper competitive. It took a few years, but the players are there, they mean it (at least in the early games). By the time a 14 year old NBA fan today is in their mid 20s, the NBA Cup will always have been part of the NBA for them.

Anyhow, the Rockets won't be moving on in the NBA Cup this year. It's possible to move on with 3 wins, but not with maybe 2. Those who don't like this added competition are free to celebrate this.

As for tonight, there are two ways of looking at it.

The Rockets were on their fifth consecutive road game, which happened to be an NBA Cup game, at altitude in Denver. Denver is where good road trips go to die, and if they don't die there, it'll happen in Salt Lake City (you can look it up).

On top of the schedule, and the location, a bunch of player who had previously been kind of bad for Denver so far this season had excellent night. Aaron Gordon, Cam Johnson, and Christian Braun went 20-29 and threw in another 11 points from free throws, which they shot at 10-11.

The Rockets on the other hand, missed 12 free throws out of 35 attempts, shooting 66% to Denver's 93%.

The Rockets bench played 75 minutes and managed all of 12 points.

The Denver bench played 75 minutes as well, and contributed 37 points.

The game featured over 40 lead changes, and was anyone's game until the mid 4th quarter.

Maybe the altitude told on the Rockets starters, and Denver's stars, Jokic and Murray, whom the Rockets had held down most of the first half, came alive.

The duel between Sengun and Jokic was pretty much a dead heat individually.

Jokic went 10-15/2-3 3pt/7 rbs/11 ast/4 To for 26 points.

Sengun went 9-16/1-2 3pt/11 rbs/10 ast/2 TO/1 stl/1 blk for 28 points.

This one was painful, but not unexpected.

The second way is a little more alarming:

The Rockets held a narrow lead towards the end of a high scoring game. They couldn't keep scoring, and lost it.

As the game got tighter late, Denver clamped down on defense, and the Rockets simply couldn't execute offense.

The Rocket with the most shots was Fred VanVleet. This wasn't due to setting up Fred for shots, it was due to Fred often finding no passes, no one really doing much of anything, and being forced to take a late clock attempt.

FVV did this pretty well, but he also shot the most shots on the team, at 21, and was part of the Rockets two guards (including Amen) going 4-11 from the free throw line.

Kevin Durant took 9 shots, wasn't assertive and the Rockets couldn't get him the ball late, anyway.

Jabari Smith was on fire, but only got 8 attempts, of which he made 7.

The late game collapse, and it was a collapse in the final minutes, felt very familiar. Unable to get stops. Unable to execute on offense. There was no plan, no special action to get the Rockets best offensive players tonight, Sengun, and Jabari Smith, looks late. It was, again, a lot of Fred VanVleet dribbling under pressure, and often making a difficult midrange shot with no time remaining.

The level of competition was high, the level of intensity of that competition was high, so it's not unreasonable to have concerns about this outcome. Denver might be a first round playoff opponent, for example.

One game doesn't make a trend, and isn't proof of anything, but I saw in this game a painful reminder of painful games last season. That could just be bad memories, and sensitivity to certain sorts of issues. The season, and especially the playoffs, will tell us.
