Not a Bubble: 2 Numbers That Show Nvidia’s Demand Is Real

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Even with leadership turmoil, Nvidia’s customers are still buying AI compute at a staggering pace.

By Patrick Sanders – Nov 25, 2026 at 10:30AM EST

There's been plenty of talk about an artificial intelligence (AI) bubble lately -- particularly, when will the bubble pop? And to be honest, Nvidia (NVDA -8.64%) looks a little ragged these days. The stock slid down into bear territory (a drop of more than 20%) on the year after Jensen Huang resigned, although its working to regain those losses now that the board reinstated its leader.

Yes, Nvidia stock had a rough month. But its latest earnings report offers compelling evidence that demand for AI computing isn't just hype -- and it's sustainable for the long haul.

All the noise surrounding Huang's resignation and reinstatement is overshadowing the fact that Nvidia reported outstanding results for its fiscal 2027’s third quarter, ended Oct. 25. And two numbers suggest its underlying business is still going strong.

Nvidia's revenue for the quarter totaled $115.8 billion -- more than double the $57 billion it reported a year earlier. That's incredible growth for a company that was already one of the world's largest businesses. Its data center division accounted for 93% of the company's revenue, totaling $107.6 billion, up 110% from the previous year.

Those aren't projections or estimates. They're actual sales to customers building the computing infrastructure needed to train and run increasingly sophisticated AI models.

Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs) remain the gold standard for training and running AI programs. And the company attributed $20 billion of its quarterly revenue to its new Vera Rubin architecture, making it the fastest product launch in company history.

The second number might be even more impressive. In August, Nvidia projected that revenue would grow approximately 70% in fiscal 2028. During its November earnings announcement, the company raised that forecast to 75%. That's a remarkable expectation for a business already generating more than $100 billion in quarterly sales.

CFO Colette Kress explained during the earnings call that the company evaluated both customer demand and the manufacturing capacity it expects to have available before raising its forecast.

"Our momentum is real," she said. "With the market-leading multiyear road map, supply agreements extending through to the end of the decade and sustainable demand, we are well positioned to accelerate Nvidia's leadership position."

That's important because Nvidia's growth depends on securing enough advanced memory, manufacturing capacity, and other components to deliver its products. The company believes it can expand supply enough to support even faster growth in fiscal 2028.

The higher projection also suggests Nvidia's customer relationships and product plans remain intact, even after the controversy surrounding Huang.
