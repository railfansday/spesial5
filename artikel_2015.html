The last 7 permanent Rochdale AFC managers ranked from worst to best

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

It is fair to say it has been a difficult start to life back in League Two for Rochdale.

Despite missing out on the National League title last season after a heartbreaking final day draw with York City, Rochdale secured promotion back to the EFL following a three-year absence after a dramatic play-off final victory over Boreham Wood.

However, just a few weeks after Dale's Wembley success, head coach Jimmy McNulty departed the club to take over at League One side Stockport County, with Ian Watson making the move from National League North side South Shields to replace him.

Unfortunately for Watson, his tenure at the Crown Oil Arena proved to be short-lived, with Rochdale making the decision to part company with the 40-year-old on Sunday after a poor run of form left them just above the League Two relegation zone.

As Dale begin their search for Watson's replacement, we looked back and ranked the club's last seven permanent managers from worst to best.

After his reign came to an end, it is unlikely that many Rochdale fans will hold positive memories of Watson's time at the club.

Watson's South Shields side narrowly missed out on the National League North title to AFC Fylde last season before losing to Kidderminster Harriers in the play-off final, so he looked to be an exciting appointment for the Dale this summer after his impressive work in non-league.

However, the jump up two divisions proved to be too much for Watson, who won just six of his 22 games in charge, drawing three and losing 13, and, as well as the poor results, the lack of a clear style of play and his decision to sideline some of last season's promotion-winning team in favour of his own signings attracted criticism from the fanbase.

It was always going to be tough for anyone to fill McNulty's shoes, particularly for a manager with no previous EFL experience, but Watson's spell at the Crown Oil Arena is one that all parties will be keen to forget.

When Rochdale poached John Coleman from Accrington Stanley in January 2012, it was viewed as something of a coup for the club.

Coleman had established a reputation as one of the best lower league managers during his 13-year stint at Accrington, taking the reformed club into the EFL for the first time in 2006 before building a consistently competitive League Two side.

Having earned his stripes in the fourth tier, the move to Rochdale offered Coleman the chance to show what he could do at League One level, but after taking over with the club sitting towards the bottom of the table, he was unable to prevent their relegation.

Given his strong League Two record, Dale stuck with Coleman following relegation, but the expected promotion push failed to materialise, and the 63-year-old was sacked midway through the campaign in January 2013 after winning just 15 of his 52 games in charge, drawing 12 and losing 25.

After a nightmare start to the 2022-23 season, Rochdale turned to League Two survival expert Jim Bentley.

Dale had picked up just one point from their first six league games and were bottom of the table when Bentley arrived, but the 50-year-old had navigated plenty of fourth tier relegation battles during his eight-and-a-half year spell at Morecambe, so he looked to have the credentials to keep the club in the division.

Bentley inspired an initial upturn in form after taking over at the Crown Oil Arena to lift Rochdale out of the bottom two, but it was not long until their fortunes declined again, resulting in his dismissal in March 2023 with the club sitting 10 points from safety with eight games remaining.

While Bentley won just seven of his 36 games in charge, drawing seven and losing 22, it would be unfair to put too much blame on him for Dale's eventual relegation to the National League given the on and off-field issues he experienced during his tenure.

After their relegation from League One, Rochdale handed Robbie Stockdale his first senior managerial role in the summer of 2021.

Stockdale had spells as a coach with the likes of Grimsby Town, Sunderland, Hibernian and West Bromwich Albion prior to taking over at the Crown Oil Arena, and Dale would have hoped he could translate his coaching potential into success in the dugout.

Rochdale supporters may have been hoping Stockdale could lead them to promotion from League Two at the first attempt, but the club's limited budget meant that was always going to be unlikely, and the 46-year-old oversaw an underwhelming 18th-placed finish in his debut season.

After Dale lost their opening four games of the 2022-23 season, Stockdale was sacked after just over a year in charge, departing having won 16, drawn 18 and lost 25 of his 59 games at the helm.

Many notable teams have been emotionally exhausting to support in League Two, but which club tops our list at this moment in time?

Brian Barry-Murphy scored one goal and provided 11 assists in 77 appearances for Rochdale after joining from near neighbours Bury in July 2010, but his on-field exploits would only be the start of a lengthy association with the club.

In addition to his playing duties, Barry-Murphy took up a coaching role at the Crown Oil Arena in 2013, and he stepped in as caretaker manager in March 2019 following the departure of Keith Hill before being handed the permanent job the folllowing month.

Barry-Murphy successfully kept Dale in League One in the 2018-19 and 2019-20 seasons, but he was unable to repeat the trick for a third time in the 2020-21 campaign as his side were relegated, but they certainly did not go down without a fight.

In their relegation season, Rochdale were known as one of the great entertainers in the third tier, and they scored an impressive total of 61 goals throughout the campaign, but defensive frailities ultimately cost Barry-Murphy's men as they also conceded 78 goals.

Barry-Murphy won 35 of his 109 games in charge of Dale prior to his exit in the summer of 2021, drawing 25 and losing 49, but while relegation remains a black mark on the Irishman's CV, supporters of his former club may not be surprised by the subsequent success he has achieved with Cardiff City.

After making 237 appearances during an eight-year spell at the Crown Oil Arena, Jimmy McNulty is another former Rochdale player who went on to manage the club.

McNulty had two caretaker stints in charge of Dale in the 2022-23 season following the dismissals of Stockdale and Bentley, and while he was unable to prevent the club from dropping out of the EFL for the first time in their history, the improved end-of-season form under his guidance was enough to earn him the permanent job.

Rochdale finished 11th and fourth in the National League respectively during McNulty's first two seasons in charge before accumulating a remarkable 106-point haul last season, but although that was not enough to beat York to the title, they sealed a deserved promotion back to League Two with a penalty shootout victory over Boreham Wood at Wembley.

McNulty departed in June to take over at another of his former clubs in Stockport, but the 41-year-old delivered some unforgettable moments during his tenure, which saw him win 80, draw 35 and lose 43 of his 158 games in charge.

While McNulty produced one of the most memorable campaigns in Rochdale history last season, many supporters will agree that Keith Hill will go down as the club's best manager in recent times.

Hill first took caretaker charge of Dale in December 2006 before being given the permanent job the following month, and he led the club to promotion to League One in the 2009-10 season before making the move to Championship side Barnsley in June 2011.

The 57-year-old returned to the Crown Oil Arena for a second spell in January 2013, and he guided Rochdale to promotion from League Two once again in the 2013-14 campaign before overseeing their record finish of eighth in League One the following season.

There were plenty of other notable highlights during Hill's second stint, including a 2-2 draw at home to Premier League giants Tottenham Hotspur in the FA Cup fifth round in the 2017-18 season which earned a replay at Wembley, as well as a miraculous escape from relegation in the same campaign.

Across his two spells as Rochdale manager, Hill won 242, drew 131 and lost 197 of his 570 games in charge, and his legacy at the club will be secure for a long time to come.
