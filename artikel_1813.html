Man United's first woman on their backroom team, the fitness enforcer, a scorpion-kicking goalkeeper coach... and his son! Meet Mauricio Pochettino's team of experts flying in to whip United's flops into shape

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Published: 21:00 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 07:43 EST, 17 November 2026

Mauricio Pochettino will have little time to waste when he takes to Manchester United's training ground for the first time on Wednesday, just days ahead of their trip to bitter rivals Liverpool.

The Argentine, however, will not be doing it alone. With him, Pochettino brings to Old Trafford an experienced and familiar cast of trusted colleagues, who will be tasked with supporting him in his mission to turn things around in Manchester.

From a first-team coach who has known him since 1992 to the boss' own son, Daily Mail Sport takes a look at the backroom staff accompanying Pochettino at United...

When the Manchester United reporters turn up for press conferences at Carrington, they will notice one seat, usually in the front row and to the far left, has a reserved sign on it. It will be for Perez, who is Pochettino’s eyes and ears. Those same correspondents may well be followed by Perez on social media, so he can stay in the loop of what is being written.

Wherever Pochettino goes, so too does his shadow Perez. You have probably already heard about the time Pochettino met his idol Sir Alex Ferguson for lunch in London back in 2016. What you may not know is that Perez was there, too. He and Pochettino have been joined at the hip ever since they met in Barcelona when one was an analyst and the other was managing Espanyol.

Mauricio Pochettino (right) arrives at Manchester United with a trusted team of backroom staff

That was in 2010. Perez had studied sports science at university and completed his coaching qualifications but was considering quitting football. Pochettino convinced him otherwise, and they soon learned they shared the same principles, including the importance of creating the right culture of togetherness within a club to succeed.

In 2013, it was Perez’s turn to convince Pochettino. The Argentinian was unsure about moving to England to manage Southampton, but the Spaniard had spent a month on these shores studying how Premier League sides train and play.

He told Pochettino that the intensity of his sessions and style would fit perfectly and that he could not turn down the chance to take on the Premier League.

They now consider each other family, and Perez’s story itself is a nice one. By his own admission, he will tell you he failed at becoming a professional player, but he believes the way in which he worked his way in as a coach can inspire others who dream of finding their own place in football.

He loves the game, so much so that he would use days off to travel from London to Manchester to visit the National Football Museum. Now he will be living much closer to his favourite hotspot.

As well as being the glue which connects various club departments together, Perez is a fitness and conditioning specialist, and a normal working day might involve him arriving at the training ground at 7.30am.

After meeting with Pochettino over breakfast, he will then hold a 9am medical meeting where plans for each player are discussed and decided. Programmes tend to be tailored to individuals rather than set for the whole squad. Perez was credited with helping to establish Southampton and Tottenham Hotspur as among the fittest teams in the Premier League.

Where Pochettino is larger than life, Perez is unassuming, and has never sought to step into management as he prefers to work in the shadows.

Jesus Perez, pictured with Pochettino at Spurs in 2019, has long been his right-hand man

We talk about Pochettino’s man-management prowess, but the same is true of Perez.

For example, when Chelsea beat Leicester City 4-2 in an FA Cup quarter-final in 2023-24, Raheem Sterling had a stinker. He missed a penalty, spurned a one-on-one and skied a free-kick. Chelsea’s fans booed him, and chanted ‘you don’t know what you’re doing’ at Pochettino when he substituted Mykhailo Mudryk rather than Sterling after 78 minutes at 2-2. Perez was the first to go straight to Sterling to put an arm around him that day.

And when Cole Palmer was becoming a household name in Pochettino’s one and only season at Chelsea, Perez was among those who privately tried to help the Englishman understand the perils of the distractions of his new-found fame.

When they departed Chelsea, Pochettino and Perez were men of their word and honoured their commitment to manage the World XI in the Soccer Aid charity match a few weeks later. That was despite the awkwardness of the game being held at Stamford Bridge. They lost 6-3 to Frank Lampard’s England XI.

To give a sense of how long Pochettino has known ‘Miki’, around 1992, Pochettino purchased his first car while a player for Newell’s Old Boys. He gave his team-mate a lift home. When D’Agostino went to get out of the car, the wind caught it hard, and broke Pochettino’s door. D'Agostino never paid the bill to have it fixed – something Pochettino occasionally likes to remind him of.

Miguel D'Agostino, known as 'Miki', has heaps of experience as a former analyst and scout

The first-team coach has known Pochettino since 1992 (both pictured at Tottenham in 2019)

Pochettino clearly forgave his fellow defender, who was also plying his trade under Marcelo Bielsa.

D’Agostino was an analyst at Espanyol, initially attached to the scouting department before assuming responsibility for filming training sessions and overseeing player analysis. From there, he followed Pochettino wherever he went, from Southampton to Tottenham to Paris Saint-Germain to Chelsea to the United States national team.

While with the USA, D’Agostino would watch games in the MLS, going to Real Salt Lake or wherever there were players for scouting. In training, he is hands-on, sometimes with a stopwatch in hand or notepad containing that day’s drills.

When Pochettino tested positive for Covid-19 in January 2021, D’Agostino and Perez took temporary charge of his PSG side. They beat Angers 1-0 away from home in Ligue 1.

All of Pochettino’s confidants have a good grasp of English.

SILVIA TUYA, STRENGTH AND CONDITIONING COACH

The newest member of Pochettino’s inner circle, having joined his USA national set-up in October 2024.

With a PhD in sports science, Tuya had worked with FC Levante Badalona in Liga F, Spain’s top tier for women’s football, while she also served as a lecturer at the National Institute of Physical Education of Catalonia at the University of Barcelona.

Since teaming up with Pochettino, she was seen on the training pitch and in the gym with the players.

Tuya will become Manchester United's first-ever female coach connected with the men's team.

After winning gold for Spain at the Barcelona Olympics in 1992, Jimenez met Pochettino in 1994 when they became team-mates for Espanyol, but it is worth recalling the 2000 Copa del Rey final when they found themselves on opposing sides.

The goalkeeper represented Atletico Madrid, and Pochettino was in defence for Espanyol, who won 2-1. After full-time, Pochettino delayed his celebrations to go and console his friend, who had already endured a challenging year with his father having passed away.

Jimenez has never forgotten that kindness from Pochettino, and like the others, the two have worked together wherever the latter has managed, from Espanyol to Southampton to Tottenham to PSG to Chelsea to the USA. They have shared a fair few of their favourite mate teas in their time, and now for United, where Jimenez will work with Senne Lammens and Co.

They say training is rarely boring when Jimenez is involved. He was happy to prove to Tottenham’s players during one tour of Malaysia that he could copy Rene Higuita’s scorpion kick save, for example, and is pretty good when testing his goalkeepers in free-kick practice. Lammens should beware he is liable to do a little dance if he scores past you, however.

Hugo Lloris, the former Spurs goalkeeper who won the 2018 World Cup with France, frequently credited the charismatic Jimenez for adding new areas to his game. Pochettino has also hailed Jimenez for how he keeps second and third-choice goalkeepers motivated when they are not playing.

SEBASTIANO POCHETTINO, SPORTS SCIENTIST AND FITNESS COACH

The son of the manager. A graduate of Southampton Solent University, Sebastiano first worked with his father at Tottenham. It was said that he had become the Premier League’s youngest sports scientist when he was hired at the age of 22.

He soon became a regular sight in training sessions and on match days, seen putting the players through their paces and with the substitutes as they warmed up.

Sebastiano became a fitness coach and joined his old man at PSG, Chelsea and the USA. One of his specialties is in injury prevention, and that includes monitoring the players’ sleep and mood.

Trust is important to Pochettino, and he knows he can rely on family.

Hired by the USA as their men's national team lead analyst in August 2023 - just over a year before Pochettino took charge - Scott previously spent eight years at the FA before moving to Inter Miami.

He graduated from Liverpool John Moores University, achieving a first class BsC in Science and Football, with his major project titled 'A Comparison of the Technical Demands in Academy and Reserve Team Soccer'.

He went on to complete a PhD in Exercise Psychology & Performance Analysis there, before putting his skills to use during internships at Everton and Liverpool before joining the FA.

Scott has clearly impressed Pochettino, who confirmed before Tuesday's clash with Haiti that he was among those staying with him for that game before joining United - with Perez, Jimenez and Pochettino Jnr having travelled to England already.

'I am with Miguel and Silvia and Alec [for this game],' he said. 'After, they are going to join me in Manchester.'
