Serious Events Linked to GLP-1s; Implicit Bias Training in Medicine; Ebola in Kids

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

— Health news and commentary gathered by MedPage Today staff

Note that some links may require registration or subscription.

Ischemic optic neuropathy, gastroparesis, thyroid C-cell tumors, gall stones, pancreatitis, and more -- the Washington Post looks at some of the rare but serious side effects linked to GLP-1 receptor agonists.

HHS announced finalized changes to the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) that include a requirement for medical providers to respond to health data requests within 15 days.

NIH named John Powers III, MD, as the new permanent director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, after serving as acting director since June.

The White House is weighing whether to abandon President Trump's FDA nominee, Heidi Overton, MD, PhD, according to people familiar with the discussions. (Washington Post)

A federal judge threw out a Trump administration rule that would have set 4-year limits for student visas and would have impacted foreign researchers. (Politico)

In other court news, the U.S. Supreme Court declined to hear a challenge to California's requirement that doctors renewing their medical licenses are taught about implicit bias. (The Hill)

The rate of women dying from pregnancy or childbirth in Texas has dropped to pre-pandemic levels, as mental health issues become the leading cause of deaths and Black mothers continue to die at higher rates than others, according to a new report. (Texas Tribune via AP)

The U.S. reported 334 new measles cases in the past week, according to the Yale School of Public Health. Here's where they were.

Experts said a sustained and beefed-up measles vaccination effort in the U.S. is needed to help restore the measles elimination status. (Reuters via AOL)

What does losing the elimination status mean for future public health efforts? Tune in to MedPage Today's live webinar today, Tuesday, Nov. 17 at 4 p.m., to have all your questions answered.

More than half of nonprofit hospitals did not spend as much giving back to their communities as the value of their tax exemption, according to a Lown Institute report.

The FDA approved dexmedetomidine sublingual film (Igalmi) for at-home use to treat symptoms of agitation associated with schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, and bipolar I or II disorder, maker Teva Pharmaceuticals announced.

The agency also cleared MiniMed's tubeless insulin pump (MiniMed Fit) for people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes 7 years and up, the company announced.

After years of being excluded, the FDA proposed a rule that would again allow N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC) to be marketed as a dietary supplement; the agency as recently as 2000 sent warning letters to companies marketing NAC for hangover cures. (Medical Daily)

Nearly 1 in 5 adults surveyed in 2025 said they were a caregiver for a loved one with a health issue over the last month, with women making up a higher share than men (20.8% vs 15.7%), according to a National Center for Health Statistics report.

Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. wants to make it so faith-based addiction recovery programs can more easily receive federal funding. (STAT)

Irish Boxer Lee Reeves died at age 31, just 3 months after a brain injury during a fight left him in a medically induced coma. (People)

As Ebola rages in the Congo, kids have been hit hard, making up about 28% of the cases but 35% of the deaths, according to a UNICEF report.
