I Tried Keurig’s First-Ever Espresso Machine—Is It Worth Adding To Your Coffee Routine?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

I’ve owned a Keurig, I’ve owned a Nespresso, and I’ve spent way too much money on coffee shop runs in between. Needless to say, I’m an expert on the subject. So when I heard that Keurig was launching its first-ever espresso machine, my interest was piqued. Could the brand that turned single-serve coffee into the household standard successfully switch up its game?

Keurig’s latest and possibly greatest innovation—a two-in-one single-serve coffee maker and espresso machine called the Alta System—will turn your home kitchen into a coffee shop thanks to a multifunctional design that brews everything from regular coffee to espresso, cappuccinos, and lattes. It comes with a built-in milk frother and functions exclusively on the new plastic- and aluminum-free pods (sustainability, hallelujah!).

Here’s whether the Keurig Alta System lives up to the hype, how it compares to other machines, and whether it deserves a spot on your own kitchen coffee bar.

Let’s go a little more in-depth, shall we? The Keurig Alta System is the brand’s new single-serve coffee and espresso machine that does a lot more than your standard K-Cup brewer. Unlike the brand’s traditional lineup, which uses K-Cup pods, the Alta introduces an entirely new innovation: AltaRounds. Your machine can read a code on the AltaRounds and adjust its brew settings accordingly, whether that’s pressure, temperature, or the water-to-coffee ratio. (K-Cups are still compatible with the Alta).

The integrated milk frother is another major upgrade for Keurig, letting you make hot and cold foam.

The Keurig Alta System is $499.99, which, yes, is pricier than other Keurig models. However, classic espresso machines can run you upwards of $1,000.

As for the AltaRounds, they’re sold in boxes of eight, ranging from $9.99 to $10.99 depending on the variety. If you wanna break that up by coffee, it’s about $1.25 to $1.37 a cup. Not too shabby.

As someone who is impatient and terrible at reading directions (hello, your type B friend reporting for duty), I wasn’t overly optimistic I’d figure it out that quickly. But it’s actually an easy device to navigate, thanks to the convenient LED screen that walks you through the whole process.

I was immediately transfixed by the machine. I texted my boyfriend, “I’m in love with it,” before it even finished brewing my first coffee. The Alta feels so much chicer on my countertops, and the pods are smaller and store more easily. Once I finally took that first sip, I was ready to toss my old Keurig in the trash (respectfully).

The drip coffee and espresso shots taste like something straight from an award-winning barista. I love the flexibility to make a black coffee one day and an iced caramel latte the next. And the milk frother just takes the entire thing to the next level.

Listen, $500 for a coffee machine is a lot. And maybe because of that, this isn’t an upgrade you can make immediately. Still, the Keurig Alta System is worth the hype; if you can swap your classic single-serve maker for it, you should. It checks literally every box—it’s more aesthetic, more versatile, and more sustainable.

The only caveat is if you’re the type of person who exclusively drinks drip coffee. In that case, it might not be worth the splurge. But if you want to cosplay as a barista and whip up new creations, from frothy cappuccinos to iced lattes and espresso martinis, the Alta provides all the room to experiment (and it will ultimately save you money on those $10 coffee runs).
