NAR President Kevin Brown warns of outside forces threatening real estate marketplace

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Outgoing NAR President Kevin Brown defended the MLS model as central to transparency and competition while warning that outside interests could seek more control of the marketplace. He also addressed NAR reforms, legislative priorities and member accountability ahead of a leadership transition.  AI Summary

As his term as president of the National Association of Realtors (NAR) comes to a close, Kevin Brown is warning real estate professionals that outside forces seeking greater control of the industry could threaten the transparency and competition that are the backbone of the current real estate marketplace.

In remarks prepared for NAR NXT in New Orleans this week, Brown drew parallels between the forces that contributed to the 2008 housing crisis and those he believes are now attempting to reshape how real estate is bought and sold.

“Today, outside forces have once again entered our industry, and this time around it is our marketplace instead of our financial system, motivated by profits, control and market share,” Brown said.

He did not identify the companies or organizations he was referring to, but his comments come as the industry debates the future of listing distribution, broker control of listing data and the role of multiple listing services (MLSs).

Brown said he believes the MLS system remains essential to maintaining competition and giving buyers and sellers access to comprehensive property information.

“America is home to the fairest, most accessible and transparent real estate market in the world,” Brown said. “That is because of a pro-competitive foundation that provides the broadest, most reliable view of the market, anchored in the MLS system.”

Perhaps in a nod to the increase in private listings, he emphasized that consumer choice depends not only on the ability to make decisions but also on access to the information needed to make them.

“A fair and transparent marketplace gives homebuyers and sellers both choice and clarity — the freedom to choose and the information to understand the consequences of that choice,” he said.

According to Brown, NAR is working with MLSs, associations and industry leaders to address marketplace concerns while advocating for the MLS system in Washington.

He also urged members to take a more active role in defending the existing marketplace. “Be prepared to support and defend a marketplace that doesn’t just benefit a select few, but a marketplace that serves us all — the consumer, the industry and Realtors,” Brown said.

When it comes to the association, Brown acknowledged shortcomings. “If NAR wants to lead change, we must be willing to change ourselves,” Brown said. “We cannot wait for outside forces to set our agenda or determine our direction.”

Brown pointed to organizational changes this year, including staff reductions, streamlined governance and a renewed focus on member services. These changes are part of NAR’s three-year strategic plan, which focuses on rebuilding the member experience and modernizing the association.

“That is why we have rightsized our staff, streamlined our governance structure and sharpened our focus,” he said. “But the real test is not whether we created a plan. It is whether that plan helps you get to your next transaction, better serve your clients and move our industry forward.”

Brown also strongly addressed criticism of NAR, acknowledging the association’s shortcomings while challenging critics to provide accurate information. “Accountability starts with being honest about where NAR has fallen short, what must change and whether we are delivering value for you,” Brown said.

But Brown also took aim at what he characterized as sensationalized coverage of the association. “For some, reporting on NAR’s progress is not a profitable story. Sensationalism sells,” Brown said. “We are not interested in fueling miscommunication and half-truths to boost views, profits or egos.”

Brown maintained that criticism is necessary but said it must be grounded in facts. “We respect our critics,” he said. “Honest criticism makes us better and helps hold us accountable.”

Brown also highlighted NAR’s legislative efforts, including passage of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act.

“It took Realtors to get it across the finish line,” Brown said. He also pointed to efforts to modernize credit scoring and help delinquent veteran homeowners avoid foreclosure.

As Brown prepares to hand over the presidency to Florida’s Christine Hansen, he acknowledged that the association still has work to do. “NAR is not perfect and our work is not finished,” he said.

And while he warns that criticism of the association should be based on facts, he insists that members continue to hold NAR accountable. “Keep asking us the hard questions,” Brown said. “Keep holding us responsible for clear communication and meaningful results.”
