Sellers Vastly Outnumber Buyers as Top Metros Shift Further: Report

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Buying house, real estate deal and investment. Tiny woman carrying money in shopping cart to buy family home, agent or broker giving building model and apartment key cartoon vector illustration

Sellers have largely had the upper-hand in the housing market since the pandemic as home prices reached new peaks, giving sellers max equity to see large returns on their investments. Now, as the market is observing some shifts, a new report from Redfin found that seller’s markets are losing ground.

Homebuyers have gained more negotiating power across the country, as Redfin data estimated there were 70.5% more home sellers than buyers in the U.S. housing market in October. This was the biggest recorded gap seen since 2013, as well as the sixth month in a row this measure has reached a new record.

Redfin Senior Economist Asad Khan said that sellers are “increasingly competing for buyers’ attention,” which has led to more “price reductions, concessions and other savings.”

“First-time buyers don’t have equity from a previous home to put toward a purchase, which makes saving for a down payment a big hurdle—but they also don’t have to sell a home before they can buy or give up a low mortgage rate,” he continued. “With a historic buyer’s market and home prices that are finally ticking down after three years of increases, first-time buyers who have been waiting—and can afford today’s costs—may have an opportunity to make a move.”

Looking at the 50 most populous metros in the U.S., only three were seller’s markets in October. Redfin used “proprietary” data on buyer behavior and MLS listings to estimate the balance of buyers and sellers.

Notably, all three of the seller’s markets were located in the Northeast, where home prices remain higher than other regions. These three markets saw home prices rise 3.3% year-over-year in October, compared to prices increasing only 0.3% in buyer’s markets.

The top of the three seller’s markets was Nassau County, New York, with 24.7% fewer sellers than buyers, a gap that actually shrank from 28.3% the month prior. The other two markets were Newark, New Jersey (14.3% fewer sellers, down from 17.6% the month prior) and Montgomery County, Pennsylvania (12.1% fewer sellers, down from 20.1% the month prior).

Another difference from September’s data was the loss of a fourth seller’s market—Milwaukee, Wisconsin, which is now balanced between buyers and sellers.

“Buyers looking in these pockets of the Northeast should be prepared for a very different experience than buyers elsewhere in the country,” said Khan. “They may have less room to negotiate, especially on desirable, move-in-ready homes, and could face multiple-offer situations. If you’re shopping in one of these markets, it’s especially important to know your budget, be ready to act when the right home comes along and avoid getting swept up in a bidding war.”
