Vanderbilt University asked campus to shut down devices amid claimed ransomware attack

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

BYOD ransomware group claimed it stole student and employee data; Vanderbilt has not confirmed the group's involvement

Vanderbilt University asked students, faculty and staff to shut down university devices and disconnect from campus Wi-Fi on Monday as it responds to what it called an active cybersecurity incident.

The university said its IT team is investigating and trying to rein in the situation, which could reach university-issued hardware, internal systems and services hosted in the cloud. Vanderbilt said it is working with government agencies and other partners on its response.

In a Monday evening update, Vanderbilt said campus Wi-Fi was available, but access to most systems and applications remained restricted. The university said it will operate on a normal schedule Tuesday, with classes, student services and staff work proceeding as planned. Faculty with grant submissions due this week were told to contact Vice Provost for Research and Innovation Susan Marguiles, whose team can provide support.

Vanderbilt said it would provide its next update by 9 a.m. CT Tuesday, including the status of systems and applications.

The ransomware group BYOD claimed responsibility in a post on the leak portal it runs, saying it locked the university's databases and took data that includes more than 1 million Social Security numbers, names, email addresses and phone numbers, according to BleepingComputer.

A ransom note visible in an image of what is said to be a computer display points to a website run by BYOD, according to BleepingComputer.

Whether the group was involved, and whether any data was compromised, remains unconfirmed by the university, according to Axios.

Students on university-issued devices described screens that quickly became cluttered with duplicates of the hackers' ransom readme and pop-up windows, according to Gizmodo.

Vanderbilt Health, which operates separately from the university, instructed its employees to stay off university IT systems until they hear otherwise, according to Axios. Its own systems were unaffected, and it is still treating patients.

BYOD launched in 2026, according to BleepingComputer. The group claimed responsibility for an earlier attack on Michigan State University, according to Axios.
