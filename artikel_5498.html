U.K. drugmaker is in talks with the FDA to supply estrogen patches to the U.S.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company has expanded manufacturing as U.S. prescriptions containing estradiol, a form of estrogen, doubled in two years

Theramex, a London-based drugmaker, is in talks with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration about selling its estrogen patches in the U.S., CEO Robert Stewart told Bloomberg.

Theramex has scaled up production because it anticipates the FDA could let the sales proceed, Stewart said.

"We've been in dialogue with the FDA," Stewart said, according to Bloomberg. "They have the information that they would need to be able to issue a special import, and if they do, we'd be able to immediately respond."

Theramex has no approved products in the U.S., according to Bloomberg. In exceptional cases, the FDA has leeway to let drug companies market medications it hasn't approved for a limited time. An FDA spokesperson would not discuss details or the timing of any step the agency might take regarding Theramex. The spokesperson added that when patients struggle to get their medications, the agency can turn to a number of options, temporary importation among them.

Theramex markets Evorel, an estrogen patch, in the U.K. for relief of menopause and perimenopause complaints like night sweats and hot flashes. If the FDA permits U.S. sales, the number of patches sent each month would be "substantial," Stewart said. He said customers elsewhere would not be impacted by the move.

U.S. women have had trouble finding estrogen patches while demand climbs, according to Bloomberg. Prescriptions that include estradiol, an estrogen type, have doubled over a two-year span to 4 million monthly, according to Bloomberg, citing Symphony Health data. The increase gathered pace once the FDA, a year ago, began softening its warnings on risks of heart problems, breast cancer and dementia linked to the drugs.

The FDA does not classify estrogen patches as being in short supply nationwide, the agency spokesperson said, citing company data on supply, demand and expected future demand. The American Society of Health-System Pharmacists has listed the patches as being in shortage since January, according to Bloomberg. Unless a shortage has been declared, it is uncommon for the FDA to let a firm market an unapproved drug in the U.S.

"We know for some patients these past few months have been frustrating," Sarah Ibrahim, deputy director for regulatory programs at the FDA's drug center, said Oct. 30 on a call with reporters, according to Bloomberg. She said the agency is working to address the problem.

Stewart said Theramex has contacted the FDA since the summer. He said conversations have become more specific and higher level in the past month as it became clearer that demand would outstrip manufacturers' capacity.

Theramex intends to seek FDA approval for its estrogen patches during the first six months of 2027, Stewart said. The company is in early discussions with a business partner about U.S. distribution, in case it is cleared to sell the patches sooner. Stewart declined to name the partner.

Stewart was previously CEO of Amneal Pharmaceuticals, a top U.S. seller of estrogen patches along with Viatris and Sandoz Group, according to Bloomberg.
