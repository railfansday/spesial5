I am nothing if not entirely predictable. I've waited 13 years to play a new Grand Theft Auto game and the first thing I do when I finally get around to booting GTA 6—and definitely not spoofing my home address to access it early—is jam a mouse and keyboard into my Xbox Series X just to see.

GTA 5, once it made its way to PC, was always a game I played both with a keyboard and mouse and with a controller plugged in. Keeb and rodent for shooting, especially in the first-person mode, and the controller for just getting about in the game world. It was a real best of both worlds kinda thing that the PC does so well.

It was the same with Red Dead Redemption 2, a game I completely avoided until it finally came to the PC. Keyboard and mouse for runnin' and gunnin' and a controller for horsin' around. Yeehaw.

So, I was hoping—hoping against hope, really—that I could just do the same with GTA 6. I was hoping that I could just plug in my little Mountain keyboard and my DeathAdder mouse, and it would just work.

Sadly no, there is no mouse and keyboard support for GTA 6. There is for Xbox, and my keyboard works fine just in the home screens, but as soon as you hit the game itself… nada. Keyboard and mouse support is still very much an occasional thing for Microsoft's console. It is supported, but it's on a per-game basis where devs have to actively code support into their Xbox versions of a title in order for a keyboard and mouse to work.

It's not actually that rare—some 450 games, and big games such as Cyberpunk 2077 and Alan Wake 2, have some level of support for one or both peripherals. But it is certainly not something Rockstar has spent any time putting in place when it was spending an extra year optimising the game after the delayed release. Which is a shame, given that the company has gone to great lengths to add a huge level of detail to its world to the point it almost feels like it's thought of everything.

More frustrating given that right now I bet there are Rockstar developers playing the game on keyboard and mouse in internal PC-only builds of the game. Would I have sacrificed sitting on my comfy sofa playing it writ large on my living room wall through my 4K projector just to sit at my desk with a keyboard and mouse? Maybe. It would have certainly felt like I was playing it on PC.

Something else Rockstar hasn't put into the game is any steering wheel control. Jacob, as a racing fan, tried to get his PlayStation wheelbase running on his PS5 copy of the game… but alas that is unsupported, too. Which was a source of frustration for him as he co-opted his partner in carrying his sim rig into his living room to try it out and now she is refusing to help put it back after his failure.

But this is a situation that might have to change moving forward. The next-gen Xbox machine, codenamed Helix has already been confirmed as being able to play PC games. In a recent interview with Jason Ronald, Xbox VP of Next Gen, this was again reiterated.

"We're very clear on the design goal: Helix is designed to be a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC," says Ronald. "It is not a PC that can also play console games."

This means it's going to have to support PC games, and that potentially means baked in support for keyboard and mouse in the hardware itself, rather than enabled on a per-title basis. After all, PC games kinda take those inputs for granted, no? So, maybe Red Dead Redemption 3 on Project Helix might come with keyboard and mouse support from the get-go, even if it's not necessarily released on PC straight away.

Still, for GTA 6 right now, basically it's controller or nothing, so make sure to get yourself a good pad—that's one of the biggest upgrades I've made to my setup for GTA 6.
