Intel are being sued for "misleading" investors about "a massive glut of unwanted PC inventory" thanks to AI-fuelled price hikes

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Tech giant Intel are being taken to court for allegedly "misleading" their investors about the sheer scale of the impact of generative AI-fuelled component price hikes on PC sales. The class action lawsuit in question claims that Intel's distribution and manufacturing partners have had to deal with "a massive glut of unwanted PC inventory", and that the gains from Intel's investments in AI datacenters aren't enough to make up for slowing sales of PCs to regular Josephines who just want fancy boxes they can play Deadlock on.

All this follows Intel's last round of quarterly results, in which they announced a significant revenue fall; this triggered an Intel stock price drop from $92.07 per share on October 29th to $65.76 per share the next day, which this week's lawsuit characterises as "the largest single-day price decline for Intel common stock in over 50 years".

The lawyers in question are from Robbins LLP, who specialise in "shareholder, whistleblower, antitrust, & consumer litigation". In a press release, they say they've filed suit on behalf of anybody who bought Intel stock from July 23rd to October 29th this year.

The suit claims that Intel neglected to tell investors that falling PC and component sales were having a "materially greater negative impact" on their third financial quarter 2026 Client Computing and Physical AI Groupresults than Intel had previously stated – to the tune of "hundreds of millions of dollars" in revenue shrinkage, compared to the previous quarter, with the situation still expected to "worsen".

They also accuse the company of keeping schtum about the aforesaid "massive glut of unwanted PC inventory", together with the fact that "any positive impacts to Intel’s business and financial results resulting from data center construction and other aspects of the AI boom were more than offset by the negative effects on the Company’s business and financial results resulting from the PC market slowdown and the excess inventory buildup described above."

Finally, they say Intel bosses have failed to disclose that they're struggling to procure an adequate supply of substrates – that is, materials used to create the base layer of chips, circuit boards and similar components. As such, the lawyers claim, Intel couldn't "ramp up production to meet data center CPU demand even if the Company chose to divert production resources because of a slowdown in PC-related sales."

Once the Godzilla of personal computing, Intel haven't been having a brilliant few years. Back in 2024, they recorded a multi-billion dollar quarterly loss, pushing then-CEO Pat Gelsinger to resign. As regards sales of gaming PCs, they've fallen behind AMD, though they've reportedly taken some ground back recently as regards desktop CPUs. As regards graphics cards, they have long been dwarfed by Nvidia.

Any Intel investor who wants a piece of that lawsuit has until January 12th 2027 to sign up as a lead plaintiff – that is, a court appointee who represents the interests of other class members. As regards us little people who don't buy stock in huge technology corporations, all this merely continues the weird existential crisis wrought upon 'traditional' gaming and computing hardware by the feckless quest to sell a better chatbot.
