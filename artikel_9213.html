6 Things I Learned While Hosting ‘The Design Drop’ Podcast

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

When we launched The Design Drop, the goal was simple: have the career conversations young designers actually want to hear. Not the polished success stories or the advice you’ve heard a hundred times, but the mistakes, detours, and unspoken rules that don’t come with a job description.

As futureGen Engagement Lead at SANDOW Design Group, I spend a lot of time thinking about how to support the next generation of creative professionals. So getting to sit down with designers, industry leaders, and a few unexpected voices to talk about what they’ve learned along the way? Pretty much my dream job.

What I didn’t expect was how much I’d learn myself. About careers, confidence, taking risks, and, yes, microphone stands.

After six episodes, here are the biggest lessons I’m taking away from my first season hosting The Design Drop podcast.

When I talked to furniture designer Nicolai Czumaj-Bront, he shared how his career began at his kitchen table, after long days working as a consultant. No fancy studio or a perfectly mapped-out plan. Just an idea, enough confidence to try it, and a willingness to invest in himself.

Twenty years later, he’s a senior design studio manager at Haworth.

We spend so much time waiting until we feel qualified, prepared, or experienced enough to do something. But how are we supposed to get that experience if we never start? I’m trying to remind myself of this more often, especially when something feels outside my comfort zone.

Listen to Nicolai Czumaj-Bront’s full episode of The Design Drop, “In It, Not On It.”

One of the most comforting discoveries of this experience? Even the people you look up to are still figuring things out.

In my conversation with designer and entrepreneur Dan Mazzarini, we talked about the pressure to please everyone, especially when you’re building your career. But trying to be everything to everyone can mean losing sight of what makes your perspective valuable.

Confidence doesn’t necessarily mean having all the answers. Sometimes it means trusting yourself enough to stop looking for everyone else’s approval. And if the people you admire are still working through that, maybe you can give yourself a little grace, too.

Listen to Dan Mazzarini’s full episode of The Design Drop, “Professional People Pleasers.”

I’m a big believer that everything happens for a reason, although it took me a while to get there. After talking to interior designer and content creator Clare Sullivan, I’m even more convinced that unexpected detours deserve a little more credit.

Caroline Murphy, host of The Design Drop podcast, with interior designer and content creator Clare Sullivan, who joined the show to discuss her unexpected career path and the opportunities that come from embracing change.

Clare didn’t set out to become a design influencer. Her career evolved from residential design into building her own content platform, back when becoming a design influencer wasn’t exactly an established career path.

It got me thinking: How many of the best things in life have gone exactly according to plan? I’m going to guess… not many. Sometimes the side quest becomes the main quest. And sometimes the opportunity you almost passed up changes everything.

Listen to Clare Sullivan’s full episode of The Design Drop, “The Accidental Influencer.”

This sounds contradictory, but stay with me. As we get older, life gets more complicated. Jobs, relationships, kids, pets, houses, bills—the list goes on. But we’re also gaining experience, perspective, and a better understanding of what matters.

Kay Sargent, senior principal and the director of thought leadership in HOK’s Interiors group, helped me see this differently. There are different seasons of life and work. You don’t have to be everything, everywhere, all at once. What deserves your energy today might look completely different five years from now.

I’m taking that as permission to stop expecting every part of life to be perfectly balanced.

Listen to Kay Sargent’s full episode of The Design Drop, “Everything Is a Season.”

My mom has a saying I’ve always loved: “Think of your 20s as data collection.” Every job, relationship, mistake, success, and questionable decision gives you information you didn’t have before. The point isn’t to get everything right. It’s to learn enough to make better decisions next time.

My conversation with Jessica O. Matthews reinforced that idea. Jessica invented an energy-generating soccer ball at 19 and went on to build companies at the intersection of design, technology, and sustainability.

We talked about how much we can learn from experiences and perspectives outside our own industries. Not everything you learn has to fit neatly into your career path to be useful later. So, take the weird opportunity. Explore the random interest. Collect the data.

Listen to Jessica O. Matthews’s full episode of The Design Drop, “No One Likes a Nepo Baby.”

Every guest on The Design Drop brought something different to the conversation: different backgrounds, perspectives, and definitions of success.

In my conversation with Kelly Cooper, a merchandising expert whose career has taken her through brands like Nike, Levi’s, and Universal Music Group, we talked about personal branding, advocating for yourself, and finding a version of success that actually works for you. That idea of defining success on your own terms really stuck with me.

We’re so busy comparing ourselves to other people that it’s easy to overlook what we’re uniquely good at. Sometimes it takes a mentor, colleague, or someone with a different perspective to help us recognize our strengths.

If you haven’t figured out your superpower yet, find people who will help you see it. And while you’re at it, be that person for someone else.

Listen to Kelly Cooper’s full episode of The Design Drop, “Do the Quiet Thing.”

After six episodes of The Design Drop, I definitely don’t have all the answers—but I have more questions, new perspectives, and some pretty great advice to carry with me.

What surprised me most was how different everyone’s path looked, yet how much of the same challenges kept coming up. That’s what I hope this podcast can be: a place to talk about the stuff we’re all trying to figure out, with people who’ve been there (and often still are).

Thanks to our first six guests for being so open, and to everyone who’s listened along. Here’s to more conversations, unexpected lessons, and figuring things out as we go.

All six episodes of The Design Drop are available on SURROUND and wherever you listen to podcasts. This season of The Design Drop is presented by Haworth.

Caroline Murphy believes the future of design depends on how well the industry supports its next generation. A trained interior designer and graduate of the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, Murphy founded the Young Designers Collective in Nashville to help emerging designers learn, connect, and grow. Today, she's a speaker, connector, and self-described "Design Big Sister" dedicated to helping the emerging creatives find their place in the industry.
