Forget the temporary dip tied to yesterday’s exploit. By New Year’s Day 2027, Anthropic’s Claude AI predicts Lighter crypto will deliver one of the sharpest post-incident rebounds the perp DEX sector has seen this cycle, the kind of violent recovery that turns a scary headline into pure rocket fuel.

The LIT price took a hit on yesterday’s exploit news, dropping more than 20% to $2.50. However, the team’s response and USDC fund recovery have seen the price recover to just under $3, surging +10% over the past 24 hours.

On November 16, 2026, Lighter, the high-performance decentralized perpetuals exchange that settles on Ethereum with zero-knowledge proofs, activated Guardian mode and paused all withdrawals and new pool creation.

On-chain data showed roughly $10.39M in USDC leaving the platform’s Ethereum settlement contract in a single withdrawal. Some researchers put the total impact closer to $15.5M across two large market-maker accounts. The attacker used a leaked API key tied to a trader’s account. The root cause was insecure custom signing code that reused cryptographic nonces, allowing the attacker to reconstruct the private key from public signatures.

With the key, the attacker created a public pool from the compromised account, acquired most of its shares, moved the victim’s USDC into the pool, and redeemed it. Importantly, Lighter’s core protocol and smart contracts were never compromised; this was an account-level key compromise, not a smart-contract exploit. Funds were rapidly swapped into ETH, with portions routed to Hyperliquid. LIT initially dropped by 20% on the news before buyers stepped in.

Lighter moved fast. Within hours, the team publicly confirmed the cause, named the attacker wallet, and made clear that the protocol itself remained intact. The Security Council recovered approximately $5M in USDC and returned it to the affected trader. Guardian mode was deactivated the same day and withdrawals resumed. New safeguards followed immediately: wallet signatures are now required for transfers into public pools, API key signatures are non-public, IP allowlists are planned, per-key permission limits are in place, and automated monitoring for nonce reuse is active.

Users running custom or third-party signing software were strongly advised to rotate keys and switch to Lighter’s official SDKs. The message was clear: the platform treated the incident as a user-key hygiene issue and hardened the surrounding controls without ever having a core vulnerability.

That combination of transparent communication, partial recovery, rapid resumption of operations, and concrete security upgrades is exactly the kind of response that historically flips sentiment from fear to “they handled it cleanly.”

LIT is currently trading in the $2.65–$3.00 zone after the sharp post-incident flush. The sell-off created a classic higher-volume capitulation low, with RSI on the daily timeframe dipping into oversold territory before printing a higher low.

Price is now reclaiming short-term moving averages while volume has expanded on the green candles, classic signs of aggressive dip-buying rather than distribution. On the higher timeframe, the broader structure remains constructive: a multi-month base that previously supported a run toward the $5+ region is still intact.

A sustained hold above the $2.80–$3.00 recovery zone would open measured-move targets that align with a full post-incident squeeze, especially if volume continues to confirm and the security narrative solidifies. The setup favors a sharp mean-reversion rally once the “protocol was never broken” story fully digests.

Lighter already competes in the high-performance perp space. Turning a high-profile but non-protocol incident into a demonstration of operational resilience and rapid hardening only strengthens its positioning. In a market that rewards clear recovery narratives, that edge can compound quickly.

The stage is set for exactly the kind of over-the-top, narrative-driven repricing that catches people off-guard when confidence snaps back.
