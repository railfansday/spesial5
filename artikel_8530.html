How to Have a Successful Garage Sale – Plan Ahead and Price to Sell Out

How much a garage sale brings in depends mostly on the work you do before sale day. Pick a date, advertise it online and with signs, and price every item ahead. Then run the sale to sell everything, not to get top dollar for each item, since whatever’s left is still yours to haul away.

The money you take in won’t bring a tax bill, even when buyers pay by Venmo or PayPal, since selling your own used things for less than you paid for them doesn’t raise your taxable income, the IRS says. Payment apps have to report your sales to the IRS on Form 1099-K only when they pay you more than $20,000 in more than 200 transactions a year, the threshold the 2025 tax law restored, the Taxpayer Advocate Service notes.

Buyers choose which sales to visit from the listings and signs they see, so your advertising decides how many show up. Once they arrive, a price on every item lets them buy without waiting to ask you.

Anything that doesn’t sell still has to be stored, donated, or hauled away, so a low price that sells an item at the sale is worth more than a high one that doesn’t. A lamp you won’t sell for less than $20 and end up donating earns you nothing, while the same lamp sold for $8 puts $8 in your pocket.

When you pick the date, book a local charity’s donation pickup for the day after the sale, so whatever’s left leaves instead of going back into the garage.
