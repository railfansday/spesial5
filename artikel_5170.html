💵 What Anthropic’s $1.4 Trillion IPO Means for Crypto

Several key new details for Anthropic’s pending IPO were released on Tuesday. The IPO price range was set at $70 to $78 a share, selling 825 million Class A shares to raise about $64.4 billion (at the top of the range). Pricing is expected November 18, with trading starting November 19 on Nasdaq under ANT. Roughly 20% of the deal is set aside for retail, which is about double what a book this size usually gives individual investors.

Notably on Hyperliquid, ANT is trading at $1.8T with $100M in daily trading volume and $200M in open interest (a big enough market to provide some signal). So perhaps traders are a bit more bullish on ANT price action than Wall Street.

Alongside the IPO sits a $26 billion private placement of non-voting Class N stock, bought at the IPO price by seven of Anthropic’s own compute suppliers. Google and Nvidia take about $10 billion each, AMD $2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft about $1 billion apiece, TSMC $500 million. Those shares carry no lock-up. Combined with the IPO, Anthropic walks away with roughly $84.6 billion for general corporate purposes, mostly compute.

Regarding financials, Anthropic just reported revenue of $33.2 billion through nine months against $2.5 billion a year earlier and a $1.26 billion operating loss. It reported a $104 billion net loss, almost all of it a non-cash charge on convertible notes. And $900.9 billion in committed spending, with $70.7 billion due inside twelve months.

The liquidity drain thesis has been circulating since April, when CoinDesk laid out the case that mega-IPOs pull speculative money from the same pool that funds crypto. Retail allocations are the mechanism. A dollar earmarked for an IPO ticket is a dollar not in Bitcoin or memecoins.

We already ran this experiment. SpaceX listed June 12 at more than $2 trillion. Bitcoin fell 29% going into it, bottomed at $58,500 on June 30, and then rose 37% off that low. But notably, the Bitcoin bottom was effectively in on June 5 at $59k, a full week ahead of the SPCX IPO.

So the question is not whether an IPO pulls money out. It’s whether that already happened. Bitcoin fell roughly 15% from its early-October high through a $6.3 billion exchange hack, an AI capex selloff and a Democratic sweep.

Two things make this deal smaller than it looks for crypto. The retail slice is roughly $12.9 billion against SpaceX’s $22 billion, about 59% as much. And the $26 billion private placement never touches public market liquidity at all, because it comes from Anthropic’s vendors, who then get paid back in compute contracts.

Thus, if the SpaceX pattern repeats, and with the ~60% factor, we could expect BTC to drop another 5% or so into the Anthropic IPO (notably, that’s right where the 200-week moving average is). And we would expect the effective bottom to be in sometime in the next 1-2 days.

The good news is, the pain is mostly behind us, in that scenario. We will find out soon enough if IPO history repeats itself for crypto…
