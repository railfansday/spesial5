2027 Women's World Cup: CBF receives trophy at Brazil celebration

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The headquarters of the Brazilian Football Confederation (CBF) in Rio de Janeiro welcomed the official FIFA Women's World Cup 2027™ trophy this Thursday (12). The ceremony brought together representatives from the CBF, FIFA, the Federal Government and Brazilian women's football to celebrate the trophy's presence in the country and highlight expectations for the next edition of the World Cup, which will be held in Brazil in 2027.

The trophy's unveiling marks another symbolic moment in preparations for the competition. For the first time in history, the Women's World Cup will be held in South America. The tenth edition of the tournament will take place from June 24 to July 25, 2027, with 32 participating teams and matches in eight Brazilian cities.

CBF President Samir Xaud highlighted the significance of welcoming the trophy to the organization's headquarters and reiterated Brazilians' desire to win the title.

"I welcome the Women's World Cup trophy and extend my sincere thanks to FIFA President Gianni Infantino and Sarai Bareman, FIFA's Chief Women's Football Officer, for their continued partnership. We are very happy and honored to welcome the trophy to our headquarters, but let me make one thing clear: we will do everything possible—and impossible—to ensure this trophy returns to the Home of Brazilian Football in July next year and stays here," he said.

For FIFA's Chief Women's Football Officer, Sarai Bareman, the involvement of the Brazilian public will be crucial to the competition and to filling the stadiums. She also celebrated the trophy's arrival in Brazil and the approaching tournament.

"I hope you enjoy this event with the beautiful FIFA Women's World Cup trophy. It is a symbol that this event is getting closer and closer, and I hope you are as excited about it as I am. This is a special moment for developing women's football in Brazil and across South America. It will be an incredible celebration," Sarai said.

Sarai Bareman, FIFA's Chief Women's Football Officer, sent a video message and highlighted her excitement for the start of the World CupCredits: Luciana Vermell/CBF

Hosting the World Cup also presents an opportunity to stimulate different sectors of the economy and expand the reach of women's football in the country. Gal Barradas, Executive Director of Revenue, Marketing and Business Intelligence for the FIFA Women's World Cup 2027, spoke about the commercial opportunities and audience connections that a competition of this scale can offer.

"The Women's World Cup is not just about entertainment and sport; it is a major movement for representation and employment opportunities. I have been in this industry for many years, and for the first time I am seeing brands talk about legacy in meetings. When the economy does well, it benefits all Brazilian citizens. So, the legacy we intend to leave is the development of women's football, but economic progress is also part of the legacy we want to create," she said.

Representing the Federal Government, Juliana Agatte, Special Secretary for the 2027 World Cup, highlighted the importance of mobilizing institutions to host the event and said its legacy would extend beyond women's football.

"Across municipalities and ministries, more than 200 people are working every day to discuss all the issues that need to be addressed to make the event happen. It is an enormous pleasure for the Brazilian government to welcome the world at this moment, when women and female participation through football are being placed at the heart of the agenda," Juliana said.

"Above all, we are talking about a World Cup with a social and sporting legacy and lasting impacts, as well as an opportunity for visibility, both for our women's football and for Brazil: the diversity of our regions, cuisine, culture, tourism—everything we have to showcase," she added.

Cris Gambaré, who oversees the CBF's women's national teams, emphasized the significance of playing a World Cup at home and discussed the work of head coach Arthur Elias's team as they prepare to pursue a first-ever title for the Amarelinha.

"Having this trophy here in our home is a dream come true. Thank you to FIFA, the government and all our CBF colleagues who are committed to putting on the best possible show and providing the greatest support for our national teams. We are already taking our next step: hard work, commitment and dedication to our women in football, to our past, our present and our future."

Cris Gambaré, the CBF's Women's National Teams Coordinator, highlighted the team's desire to win this first-ever titleCredits: Luciana Vermell/CBF

Aline Pellegrino, a former Brazilian national team player, CBF Competitions Manager and Executive Director of Legacy and Institutional Relations for the FIFA World Cup 2027, highlighted the importance of connecting the history built by previous generations with the opportunities the competition can offer for the future of women's football.

"It is a pleasure to be here speaking to you, taking part in this event and welcoming the FIFA Women's World Cup 2027 trophy—the first Women's World Cup in Brazil and South America. The Home of Football is always open to women's football. We are immensely proud to welcome this World Cup, and we will work tirelessly to deliver it," Aline said.

"One of the legacies I talk about for this World Cup is bringing visibility to the many unseen women who have always played football in this country. This World Cup is also here to reclaim all that history and those memories," she concluded.

The history of the Brazilian national team was also represented by Pretinha, one of the pioneers of women's football in the country. The former player expressed her pride in having been part of the first generations to compete at the World Cup and in witnessing the sport's development through to the tournament's arrival in Brazil.

"I feel proud to have been part of the generation that played in the first World Cup and to have contributed to the sport in some way. When I look back and see where we are now, and the visibility women's football has today, I know it was all worthwhile."

Pretinha, one of the pioneers of the Brazilian national team, attended the eventCredits: Luciana Vermell/CBF

The future of the sport was also present at the ceremony. Nicolly Manuel and Letícia Pinho, who were part of Brazil's under-17 team that finished fourth at the 2026 FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup, spoke about the campaign and highlighted the importance of having strong support structures from youth level onward for the development of Brazilian women's football.

"I believe this team has a major strength: its mentality. A World Cup demands a lot from us, both mentally and physically. So, alongside talent, which is one of Brazil's strengths, we have a mentality that really helps us face the situations we encounter at a World Cup," Letícia Pinho said.

"Having this infrastructure and this match schedule from an early age really helps us along our journey and opens doors for girls who dream of playing for big clubs and even wearing the Brazilian national team shirt," Nicolly said.

The FIFA Women's World Cup 2027™ will be played in eight Brazilian cities: Belo Horizonte, Brasília, Fortaleza, Porto Alegre, Recife, Rio de Janeiro, Salvador and São Paulo. The Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro will host the opening match and the final.

Beyond sport, the World Cup represents an opportunity to strengthen women's football throughout the country, broaden access to the game and leave a legacy for future generations. The competition is expected to bring the public even closer to women's football.

FIFA announced the eight host cities for the Women's World Cup 2027 Credits: FIFA

The trophy presented at CBF headquarters is one of the leading symbols of women's football worldwide. Created in 1998 and first unveiled at the 1999 World Cup in the United States, it was designed to represent two attributes of the sport: elegance and dynamism.

Originally made by hand, the trophy takes the form of a spiraling ribbon wrapped around a football at the top. In 2012, it received a new cone-shaped base, reinforcing the sense of upward movement. The body combines gold-plated bronze and polished aluminum, while the base is made of Verde Candeias granite, a Brazilian stone.

The trophy is normally kept in Zurich, Switzerland, where FIFA is headquartered, and is making a special visit to Brazil this week for commercial engagements. In 2027, it will return to the country to be presented to the winning team of the tenth edition of the Women's World Cup.
