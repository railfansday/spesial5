Hauser & Wirth Now Represents Ser Serpas, Fast-Rising Artist Who Combs the Detritus of Everyday Life

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Hauser & Wirth now represents Ser Serpas, a fast-rising artist known for her poetically minded installations and haunting paintings. Hauser & Wirth will represent the artist in collaboration with her longtime gallery LC Queisser, which has spaces in Tbilisi, Georgia, and Cologne, Germany.

She will have her first solo show with Hauser & Wirth at its Paris space in January. For that exhibition, Serpas will show a new series of paintings, as well as debut a multimedia installation. Born in 1995, she is one of the youngest artists to be represented by Hauser & Wirth, and one of a handful Latinx artists to show with the gallery, which added to its roster rafa esparza earlier this month. Born and raised in Los Angeles’s Boyle Heights neighborhood, Serpas is an itinerant artist who is primarily based in Paris but also “regularly moves between such cities as New York, Los Angeles, Geneva, and Tbilisi,” according to a release. Her practice is equally disparate, spanning various mediums from sculpture and painting to video and photography to poetry and performance.

Serpas is best known for her sculptures and installations in which she brings together the detritus of everyday life, giving the found objects that comprise them new life and meaning. She gathers the items from the streets where she is living or in residency. “I’m building a palette every time I go into a new city,” she has said of her process. In a 2023 New York Times profile, she described “Parisian trash [as] sturdy.”

To make her works, Serpas usually works alone, constructing the pieces in situ. She often tells the venues she will show at, “OK, you have to give me the key and there’s going to be a lot of things in the recycling and I might have some friends over, but you just have to trust me and you’ll get a show. But on my terms.”

Often, these installations make poignant, if oblique, references to today’s most pressing issues. An installation for the 2024 Whitney Biennial, for example, featured a tattered US flag laying on “a disused piece of a sectional relegated to the corner—which is perhaps where the Stars and Stripes belongs during these depressing times,” as ARTnews’s Alex Greenberger wrote in his review. The 2025 work tube of brief cadavers made sadder still, which featured on ARTnews’s list of “The Defining Artworks of 2025,” combined elements that recalled the ongoing migrant crisis.

Serpas also has an active painting practice, which often features ambiguous figures, initially depicting those of her lovers and people before and after surgery. Perhaps in a nod to her interest in horror and the uncanny, she presses two wet canvases together to create a ghost image. Since 2023, Serpas has used AI to generate the bodies that animate and distort her paintings.

“For the first time, I’m showing paintings in a pretty traditional way, and I know that’s going to ask a lot from people who are familiar with my sculptural practice,” Serpas told the Art Newspaper in 2025, of her show at Kunsthalle Basel that summer. “The sculptures are difficult to contend with for many collectors and institutions, and I do wish more people had them in their homes. Some people do, but not as many as the paintings, drawings or even some of the sound-based multimedia pieces I’ve made.”

Over the past decade, Serpas has become one of her generation’s most closely watched and in-demand artists. She has had institutional solo exhibitions at Kölnischer Kunstverein in Cologne (through December 13), Contemporary Art Gallery in Vancouver (2026), Kunsthalle Basel (2025), the Swiss Institute in New York (2023), and the Museum Ludwig in Cologne (2023). In addition to the 2024 Whitney Biennial, Serpas has also been included in “Flow States: La Trienal 2024” at El Museo del Barrio, “The Gatherers” (2025) at MoMA PS1, and the 2020 Made in L.A. biennial at the Hammer Museum.

Her work is also collected by some of the world’s top collectors, who show her pieces in their private museums. They include Maja Hoffmann’s LUMA Westbau, where she had a show in 2018; François Pinault’s Bourse de Commerce, where she featured in the 2021 inaugural exhibition and had a solo in 2023; Tony Salamé’s Aishti Foundation in Beirut, which included her in a group show titled “Fresh Flowers”; and a room of work at the Rubell Museum in Miami in 2025.

“We are delighted to begin working with Ser Serpas at this exciting juncture in her growing career,” Hauser & Wirth president Marc Payot said in a statement. “Like a number of our gallery’s foundational artists—Mike Kelley and Jason Rhoades come to mind—she is fearless in her commitment to a process-based, intermedia practice that mingles the deceptively mundane with something powerfully existential. We are excited to embrace her vision and look forward to sharing it with wider audiences.”
