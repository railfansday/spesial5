Why cheap NBA tickets can leave you with a bigger bill at the concession stand

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Why cheap NBA tickets can leave you with a bigger bill at the concession stand appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

In New Orleans, $26 covered two seats at a Pelicans game in Time2Play's NBA date night study. The same couple would spend $42.20 at the concession stand if each person ordered a beer and a hot dog. That puts nearly 62% of the $68.20 total on what they eat and drink. Once they've picked their seats, what they order inside makes the biggest difference to the bill.

Time2Play found the same pattern at games hosted by the Charlotte Hornets, Denver Nuggets, Memphis Grizzlies, and Phoenix Suns. All five teams appeared among the six cheapest nights out in the study. That's what makes the split worth a look. Concessions can account for most of your spending even when the whole night still qualifies as one of the league's better deals.

Consider what a couple can do with $50 in New Orleans. The two tickets leave $24 to spend inside, which is $18.20 short of the full concession order. If both fans already know they want a beer and a hot dog, then $68.20 is the figure they need to work with. If they mainly want to see live basketball, $26 covers the seats in the study. The same game can make sense for two very different budgets.

Phoenix shows why it helps to look at the menu one item at a time. The Suns were one of those five teams where the combined food and drink bill exceeded admission, yet two hot dogs cost only $4. At a San Antonio Spurs game, a pair cost $25.06. The $21.06 difference between those two orders is almost as much as the study's entire admission bill for two in New Orleans. And the low price of a hot dog in Phoenix tells fans very little about what they'll spend if they add drinks.

Beer can shift the total just as quickly. Two 16-ounce beers cost $36.55 at a Boston Celtics game and $14.43 at an Oklahoma City Thunder game. If each person wants one, the Boston pair spends $22.12 more on that decision alone. At a Golden State Warriors game, the full concession order came to $57.81, the highest in the study. With the total outing at $175.81, about one dollar in three went to the concession stand.

The New Orleans and Golden State figures answer that question in two ways. Skipping the order saves more dollars at a Warriors game, where the two seats still come to $118. In New Orleans, it takes a much larger share off the full bill. The distinction matters if you have a spending limit. A $100 budget would still come up $18 short of the Warriors' seats alone in the study. That same budget covers both seats and the full order at a Pelicans game, with $31.80 left before any other expenses.

Madison Square Garden shows how little control the concession stand can give you when the seats dominate the total. The Knicks' $458 night out included $40 in concessions, slightly less than the $42.20 order in New Orleans. Yet a couple could walk past every stand at the Garden and still spend $418 on the two tickets. Cutting the same items from both evenings makes the New Orleans outing about 62% cheaper and the Knicks outing about 9% cheaper.

Time2Play checked SeatGeek in October 2026 for pairs of adjacent seats at November regular-season home games, excluding the NBA Cup. It used the median ticket price for each team and combined it with concession prices from the previous season. The figures offer a basis for comparing arenas. For a specific game, couples will want to check the seats available for that date and leave room in the budget for costs outside the study, including booking fees and parking.

Start with what you want from the evening. If you're going for the game and a hot dog, price that outing before assuming you'll spend what the full order costs. If having a beer and something to eat is part of the plan for both people, count that money before you buy the seats. It's much easier to decide whether the whole night fits your budget while you still have a choice of games.

The Pelicans example captures how much that choice is worth. With the food and beer, New Orleans still offered one of the cheapest outings Time2Play found. The $26 pair of seats is what gives fans so much say in how low the final bill can go. At an arena where the tickets eat most of the budget upfront, that decision starts with finding a game you can afford. In New Orleans, you can get through the door first and still have the bigger spending decision ahead of you.
