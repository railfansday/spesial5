BON Roundtable: How will No. 3 Texas show up against rival Texas A&M?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Daniel Seahorn (@DanielSeahorn) — Arkansas has been a get-right game for Arch each year, so I feel like it's more about the opponent than the game plan, truthfully. Plenty of spread concepts, quick passing and screens to counter pressures, and mixing in Arch running it has been the path to success for the offense.

Jacob Neidg (@jneidig_2) — He stood and delivered without rushing things. Sarkisian was in his bag with the play design and calling, but the biggest difference was the tempo Manning seemed to play with. The offensive line kept it clean and he was able to let things develop and deliver with anticipation, something that doesn't happen when the internal clock feels rushed.

Wescott Eberts (@SBN_Wescott) — Mostly, the Arkansas defense isn't particularly good, but I thought Steve Sarkisian did a good job of using a variety of offense in the passing game, including an emphasis on screens to Raleek Brown and the wide receivers. With the running game still operating at a questionable level, Sarkisian was operating at a high capacity with the passing game. In terms of execution, the touchdown drive before halftime was a thing of beauty.

Gerald Goodridge (@ghgoodridge) — I think it's more the game plan than the player, but then again the player has to execute. More than half of Arch's attempts were five yards or shorter, a plan designed to get the ball out of his hands quickly and mitigate the issues Texas has in the trenches. While the Arkansas defense is far from world beaters, we've seen Arch's ability to struggle despite the competition, so it was good to see him thrive in a game plan that fits his skillset.

Daniel — Not really. If you can secure a bye without having to go to Atlanta and deal with Georgia I call that a win-win to be honest. That way you can just worry about dealing with A&M this week then focus on getting guys healthy and ready to go for the playoff run.

Jacob — I've been all over the map on the pros and cons of playing in the SECCG. Even just a week ago I wanted no part of it. I'm changing my tune especially if the bye will hold true. I want to see if Texas has what it takes to give Georgia a run. Keep the competitive juices flowing. Rest anyone needed, but keep the coals hot.

Wescott —I think it does to some extent, although the prospect of playing a ninth SEC game in nine weeks is utterly insane. Like, what are we doing here? It's just so unfair to the players and their ability to stay healthy.

Gerald — We've seen how physical and tough these things can get, with Texas knocking out Georgia's starting quarterback in the game two years ago. Especially with the Longhorns' injury issues, it's hard to say there's a benefit to playing an extra game, but I'm also a firm believer that there's nothing in practice that can truly replicate the game experience, so having more than a month off does scare me.

Daniel — Muschamp is going to force-feed Hart head first through a wood chipper. Good luck, kiddo.

Jacob — I think Muschamp plays to take away quick throws from Hart. If he completes a deep ball to Horton, or Craver breaks lose that's one thing, but you want to prevent Hart from ever feeling like he is in a rhythm. Forcing him to stand and deliver on intermediate or longer developing routes does just that. I also expect to see a combination of both quarterbacks, and think disrupting Reed requires almost a unique gameplan to Hart, so curious how Muschamp attacks each of them.

Wescott — Well, I'm not sure it's going to Brady Hart instead of Marcel Reed. Maybe both play? Regardless, Hart and Reed have shown a propensity to make bad decisions. No matter which quarterback starts on Friday, some key contributors at wide receiver will be out and that will only make things more difficult for the Aggies trying to move the ball on offense.

Gerald — Texas wasn't as aggressive with KJ Jackson as I expected them to be and I wonder if he does the same, sitting back and letting whichever quarterback it is have to read the defense. There's a world in which Texas can build a lead, force A&M to pass, and then pick on the quarterbacks.

Daniel — I always expect some rivalry game weirdness. The path to weirdness in this one will be similar to how we felt prior to the Oklahoma game. The only way A&M wins this game is if Texas keeps them in the game by doing stupid stuff in all three phases.

Jacob — Special teams could play a big role in how this game unfolds. They always seem to show up in big games and can disrupt any game, no matter how the contest looks on paper. I don't feel great about how Texas stacks up in that area. The Aggies have one of the best punters in the nation, which essentially negates Ryan Niblett's impact, and a strong field goal kicker. I don't know what weirdness to expect, but Texas needing to rely on special teams makes me nervous.

Wescott — No, I really don't. As easy as it is to fall into rivalry-game mindset and cliches, A&M is actually bad this year — they're banged up and they are receiving poor leadership on offense, including how the head coach manages the offense and a bad quarterback room. The running back room is healthy and talented, but it doesn't even really matter at this point because things are not just dysfunctional on offense, but in the words of head coach Mike Elko, it's nonfunctional. Just an absolute mess that reflects horribly on Elko, who should be better than this. Right now? He's not.

Gerald — When I lean on the rivalry cliche, it's usually because the other team is exceptional in one way or another. Oklahoma and Brent Venables can call a defense and cause problems, but I don't know what I can point to with this A&M team that would be an advantage that they press. If Sark and Arch lean into what works, Kyle Field is going to be a sad place.

Daniel (8-3/4-7 ATS) — A&M is totally lost on offense and doesn't have a competent offensive staff on top of that. If they score more than 14 points then they should put it up on the stadium in College Station. Texas 28, A&M 10

Jacob (9-2/5-6 ATS) — The Aggies will play better than they have the last four weeks, especially if a few big plays go their way early. I think the Texas offensive line puts together a good performance and Texas wins by 10. Texas 24, A&M 14

Wescott (6-5/5-6 ATS) — In Aggieland, even fake toughness is in short supply these days, which really says something. I don't think there is enough juice anywhere at A&M for this game to be close, especially since Texas has mostly steadied things in terms of catastrophic mistakes, notwithstanding Arch Manning's three interceptions against LSU. In that regard, I don't see much threat from the Aggies on defense to disrupt him. This one is going to be a beatdown. Texas 31, Texas A&M 6

Gerald (7-4/4-7 ATS) — This is a straight-up bad A&M team. It feels like Mike Elko doesn't know what levers to pull with this team on either side of the ball to get them up for any games, and I don't think Week 12 is going to be any different. I think Texas rolls. Texas 35, A&M 10
