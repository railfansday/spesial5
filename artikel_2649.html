Tim Henman calls out what some people have been saying about Novak Djokovic this year, ‘I totally disagree’

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Novak Djokovic continues to split opinion about what he should do next after what has been a mixed 2026 season.

Djokovic made an impressive start to the year at the Australian Open, as he beat Jannik Sinner to reach his first Grand Slam final in two years. Djokovic missed out on the title after losing to Carlos Alcaraz, but there were high hopes for the Serbian after this.

The 39-year-old did not reach another Grand Slam final this year, with Djokovic losing in the first round of the US Open in a shock defeat to Argentinian Mariano Navone. Djokovic did win one title at the China Open, but ongoing physical issues have been a problem for him this year, which were a significant hindrance in his failed goal to qualify for the ATP Finals.

This has led to a lot of debate over Djokovic's future, and former world number four Tim Henman has revealed something he has heard about the 24-time Grand Slam champion which he completely disagrees with.

Henman discussed Djokovic with former British WTA player Naomi Cavaday on Sky Sports' 2026 tennis season review. Cavaday admitted the concern she had about the lack of tournaments Djokovic is currently playing, having only competed in 11 events this year, as she revealed her hope for him to be more active in 2027.

"It's getting tougher and tougher isn't it," Cavaday said about Djokovic. "I mean age of course is catching up with him and he's just not playing regularly enough, okay he made a final at the start of the year and it was amazing to see what he was able to achieve but there's just all of these breaks that he's having.

"Okay, it's partly because it's good for the body so he can be refreshed, but if it's extended you just feel like it's going to impact his level of play. I just really hope in 2027 to see him playing a little bit more regularly."

Henman followed up from Cavaday's comments, as he was straight to dismiss those claiming that Djokovic was damaging his legacy by continuing to compete. Despite this, Henman also admitted that he was doubtful over whether Djokovic would ever win a 25th Grand Slam title.

"Yeah, I think there's also been a little bit of question mark about some people saying that he's damaging his legacy and he should be retiring, and I totally disagree with that," stated Henman. "He's earned the right to play as long as he wants and if he's enjoying it then best job in the world.

"Do I think he can win a Slam? No I don't. I think that that is the physicality. If tennis was about standing in one corner and just not moving and pure ball striking, then absolutely he's as good if not better than everyone. But that physicality, best-of-five sets over seven matches, I just feel it's a step too far at his age…Enjoy him while he's still there, he's in my opinion the greatest male player of all time. He's not going to play forever so let's enjoy him while he's still competing."

Henman, alongside 2025 Australian Open champion Madison Keys, both later confirmed their belief that Djokovic will not retire in 2027, as he targets the LA Olympics in 2028.

Djokovic's 2026 season is now over, and it has been a clear regression in terms of both results and ranking from 2025. Djokovic ended the 2025 season as the world number four, but he is now outside the top 10 at the end of a season for the first time in nine years.

In 2025, Djokovic played 13 tournaments in total, which is two more than he did this year. Despite only playing two tournaments more last year, Djokovic won 18 more matches in 2025 than he has in 2026, but has only lost one less match this year.

Djokovic won two titles in 2025, compared to just one in 2026, but the China Open is an ATP 500 level tournament, which is bigger than the two ATP 250 events he won in Geneva and Athens last year.

The Grand Slams have been different this year, as Djokovic has reached a higher peak in 2026 by making it to the Australian Open final, something he was not able to do in 2025. However, Djokovic was more consistent at the Grand Slams in 2025, having made it to the semi-finals at all four of them.

Despite all of the doubts surrounding him right now, Djokovic has proven that he can still produce the level to beat the very best in the world, having obtained wins over the likes of Alexander Zverev, Sinner and Alcaraz in the past two seasons.

Djokovic is set to return to competition again in 2027, and he has stated his ambition to compete at least all four of the Grand Slam tournaments next year. During the 2027 season, Djokovic will turn 40 years old, as he continues his bid to become the oldest Grand Slam singles champion in history.

The Australian Open is currently set to be his first tournament of the year, as Djokovic has not yet been confirmed for any other events down under, with the entry lists still to be released.
