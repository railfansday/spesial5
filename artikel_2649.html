Ben Johnson sees Bears' 4.2 yards per carry average as "a deceiving statistic"

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

League wide, NFL teams average 4.2 yards per rushing attempt. The Bears also average 4.2 yards per rushing attempt. Bears coach Ben Johnson doesn’t much care about that.

What Johnson does care about is that the running game is efficient, and Johnson would like to see better efficiency than he has seen so far from his offense. Johnson noted that an occasional long run can skew the yards per carry average, and if there are too many runs getting stuffed for no gain, that’s a problem. On the flip side, a one-yard run will bring down any team’s average, but it’s a great play if it happens on fourth-and-goal at the 1-yard line.

“I don’t see it as bigger chunk runs, necessarily. The yards per carry is a deceiving statistic in a lot of ways,” Johnson said. “We run the ball a lot in the red zone, and when you’re down there a four-yard carry is really an explosive.”

Johnson said what he cares more about is efficiency. Many teams look at running success rate, which means the percentage of runs on which a team gains at least 40 percent of yardage needed on first down, at least 60 percent on second down, or at least 100 percent on third or fourth down. The Bears’ success rate this season is 49.2 percent, and Johnson says that’s not good enough.

“We go off efficiency, and we’re under 50 percent efficient running the ball. We set our bar higher than that. We want to be closer to 55 percent, so we’ve got some work to do and all our guys know it,” Johnson said.

Ultimately, Johnson wants to see rushing first downs and rushing touchdowns. Yards per carry is secondary to that.
