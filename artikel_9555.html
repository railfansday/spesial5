New York City, United States, November 18th, 2026/CyberNewswire/--OffSec, the world's leading provider of hands-on cybersecurity training and certifications, released more than 80 hands-on labs in the last two weeks of October as Black October crippled government and university websites across Europe.

The labs give security teams a safe place to attack and defend the same kinds of flaws making headlines. Fourteen are built on real CVEs disclosed this year. They include a free lab on PHP2Shell, the PHP flaw at the center of the month's disclosure wave.

Black October is the name the security community gave to a cluster of critical disclosures in late October. At its center was PHP2Shell (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619). The flaws let attackers run code on web servers without logging in. Researchers at Calif and Wiz found the flaws with help from AI models.

Amazon spotted an exploitation attempt on Oct. 17, four days before public disclosure. CERT-EU told organizations that could not patch right away to isolate internet-facing apps from public networks. CISA urged immediate patching. The same day GitLab patched a maximum-severity flaw that let unauthenticated users run code on its servers. CrowdStrike reported four threat actors running campaigns in which AI agents make decisions and execute commands.

The pace reflects a larger shift. AI now helps find flaws and turn them into working exploits. An AI agent can compare a vulnerable release against its patched version and see exactly what changed. That shrinks the window defenders have to understand a flaw and act on it.

"AI has lowered the skill ceiling for attackers," said Alex "Sickness" Uifalvi, Security Researcher and architect at OffSec. "Going from a patch to a working exploit used to take a skilled team up to a week. An agent can cut that by a big chunk. Defenders need to get their hands on these flaws while they are still in the news. That is why our team builds labs on real CVEs as fast as we can. You learn what an attack looks like by running it and stopping it yourself."

OffSec CEO Ning Wang said fast-moving flaws like PHP2Shell are now the norm.

"These vulnerabilities are being found by AI agents and that will happen more and more," said CEO Wang. "You need to very quickly understand how a flaw works and how to defend against it. When PHP2Shell hit, we acted instantly to develop a new lab and a blog to begin teaching people how the attack works and how to stop it. Every organization that cares about its business risk needs to be on top of these things. Your red team and your blue team both need to know how to use AI to do their jobs. It's no longer a nice-to-do. It's a must-do."
