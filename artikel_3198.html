3 Warren Buffett Rules That Make Nvidia Stock a Buy at Today's Price

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Nvidia has lost nearly 30% of its value amid ongoing scandals with its former CEO, Jensen Huang.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:15AM EST

After being a darling of the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution for nearly four years, Nvidia (NVDA -3.81%) has become one of Wall Street's most controversial stocks following the unexpected resignation of founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang. The company is facing mounting uncertainty around its leadership transition as investigations involving potential ties to Chinese intelligence and questions about its extensive AI infrastructure partnerships unfold.

These developments have shaken investor confidence, sending Nvidia shares plunging despite the company's strong financial performance. On Nov. 17, Nvidia reported earnings for its fiscal third quarter. Total revenue grew 103% year over year to $115.8 billion, while adjusted earnings per share (EPS) surged 111% to $2.68. Management also forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion, implying 91% annual growth.

Legendary investor Warren Buffett's investment philosophy offers a compelling framework for evaluating Nvidia amid all of this uncertainty. Throughout his career, Buffett consistently emphasized durable competitive advantages, strong business economics, and the purchase of high-quality companies at reasonable valuations. These principles suggest Nvidia could remain an attractive long-term investment despite its current challenges.

Warren Buffett. Image source: Getty Images.

One of Buffett's most important investment principles is identifying companies with economic moats. These competitive advantages protect a business from rivals, allowing it to maintain market share and generate attractive returns over a long-term time horizon. Nvidia has several characteristics that make its competitive position difficult to replicate at scale.

Perhaps its greatest advantage is the CUDA software ecosystem, which allows developers to optimize applications for Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs). CUDA has become deeply embedded in AI research and hyperscale computing infrastructure. This creates switching costs because customers considering a move to competing platforms need to modify applications, retrain developers, and validate the costs associated with new computing environments.

Nvidia benefits from its extensive relationships with cloud providers such as Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet, as well as hardware manufacturers like Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing, Micron Technology, and Marvell Technology.

This integrated approach stitches together Nvidia’s GPUs, networking equipment, software, and accelerated computing systems. This end-to-end stack makes it difficult for competitors to replicate the platform as a whole. Although Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom's custom AI accelerators pose competitive headwinds, Nvidia's full-spectrum ecosystem provides protection beyond the performance of any individual chip.

Importantly, these advantages are not solely dependent on Huang's personal relationships. They reflect decades of engineering investments, software development, and customer adoption that will remain in place following his departure.

Buffett frequently emphasizes pricing power as a defining characteristic of outstanding businesses. Companies capable of raising prices without sacrificing customer demand usually maintain competitive advantages. Nvidia's financial performance suggests its products command extraordinary economic value even as investment in data center build-outs accelerate.

During the third quarter, Nvidia generated a 74.8% gross margin. This means roughly three-quarters of its revenue remained after accounting for the direct costs of producing its products and services. That is an exceptional margin profile for a company rapidly expanding production of next-generation chip architectures.

Nvidia's pricing power stems from the economic benefits its products provide to customers. Compute efficiency, energy consumption, and deployment speed have meaningful financial consequences for hyperscalers developing AI models and applications. Therefore, customers are willing to pay a premium for Nvidia's technology, as its performance and accompanying software can reduce overall operating costs.

Perhaps Buffett's most relevant investment principle involves the relationship between business quality and purchase price. In his 1989 Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, Buffett famously wrote, "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."

That philosophy is particularly relevant to Nvidia today. The company's record financial performance contrasts with its recent stock price decline. The question for investors is whether Nvidia's lower valuation now offers an opportunity to buy an exceptional business at a reasonable price.

At roughly $149 per share, Nvidia trades at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 16 and a forward P/E ratio of 10. Those valuations are unusually modest for a business generating triple-digit revenue and profit growth. If Nvidia sustains even a portion of its current growth trajectory, today's valuation will eventually look attractive.

Ultimately, Nvidia appears to satisfy several important criteria of Buffett's investment philosophy. While the company's future without Huang remains uncertain, its technological advantages and financial performance provide reasons for optimism.

For investors willing to tolerate short-term volatility and maintain a long-term investment horizon, the recent sell-off represents an opportunity to buy the dip in Nvidia stock.
