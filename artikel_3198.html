How These 5 Bond ETFs Differ and When Each One Fits

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

A guide to matching the bond ETF to the job it will perform in your portfolio.

Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF (BND +0.21%), iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond ETF (NYSEMKET: AGG), Vanguard Short-Term Treasury ETF (VGSH +0.23%), iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF (TLT +0.21%), and Vanguard Total International Bond ETF (BNDX +0.15%) rank among the largest bond ETFs. While they might all look similar at first glance, these funds can be quite different.

Here's a look at what you need to know about these top bond ETFs and when each one would fit in a portfolio.

The Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF and iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond ETF rank as the two largest bond ETFs by assets under management (AUM) at $167 billion and $139 billion, respectively. They both aim to provide broad exposure to the total U.S. bond market for investors seeking income and diversification. Here's how these two top bond ETFs compare:

As the table shows, these are very similar funds (they also share the same 0.03% expense ratio). BND provides investors with a slightly higher yield, while AGG holds more bonds. There are a few other subtle differences in their holdings (AGG has higher exposure to MBS, while BND has higher exposure to AAA-rated bonds).

Overall, the differences are so subtle that either fund would be an ideal core bond holding. You'd probably only hold one of these ETFs, with the choice likely coming down to sponsor preference (Vanguard or BlackRock).

Whereas BND and AGG are close cousins, the Vanguard Short-Term Treasury ETF and iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF are opposites. VGSH primarily invests in short-term U.S. Treasury bonds (one- to three-year maturities), while TLT invests in long-duration U.S. government debt (20+ years). That makes a huge difference in returns and volatility.

VGSH offers current income with modest price fluctuations, while TLT offers higher income potential but greater volatility. The big difference is duration, which measures the sensitivity of bond ETF prices to interest rate movements. For example, if a bond ETF has a duration of two years, its price would fall about 2% when interest rates rise 1 percentage point, while rising 2% if rates fall by 1 percentage point. The higher the duration, the greater the price movement when interest rates change. VGSH's average duration is 1.9 years compared to 14.9 years for TLT. So, you'd want to buy VGSH for stability, and TLT if you expect interest rates to fall.

Vanguard Total International Bond ETF provides broad exposure to major bond markets outside the U.S. It employs hedging strategies to mitigate foreign-currency risk. The ETF currently holds 6,837 bonds from across the world. Most of its bonds have investment-grade ratings, with 79% issued by foreign governments. Its portfolio has a 5.5% yield to maturity and a 6.4-year average duration. In many ways, it would be similar to owning BND or AGG, though with a bit more rate sensitivity. This ETF would be ideal for investors seeking to further diversify their bond portfolio with some international exposure. It would be a good long-term holding to complement either BND or AGG.

Bond ETFs are lower-risk investments compared to stocks. However, they're not risk-free investments. The biggest risk is duration. If interest rates rise, bond ETFs with high average durations would see price declines that could more than offset the income they provide. We can see that in the price changes of TLT versus the other bond ETFs over the past year:

Investors need to consider this risk before adding a longer-duration bond ETF to their portfolios.

Before choosing a bond ETF, ask what job you want the investment to accomplish for your portfolio. BND and AGG are best for core bond holdings. Meanwhile, VGSH is best for money you may need soon. TLT and BNDX have more specialized jobs. BNDX provides international diversification, while TLT is a bet on falling interest rates. The key is to make sure the bond ETF aligns with the role you expect it to play in your portfolio.
