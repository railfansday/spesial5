The $4 million Internet Invitational might become golf’s next big thing — or another bogey

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

You could make the case that the most exciting golf event of 2026 involved a majority of amateurs and average joes — and the PGA wasn’t involved in the planning at all. I’m talking about the Internet Invitational, which recently wrapped up its second season by splitting a $4 million prize among the 16 members of the winning team.

The driving force behind the Internet Invitational is Dave Portnoy, the provocateur and pizza lover who is best known as the owner of Barstool Sports. To create an event that could demonstrate the influence and appeal of the “YouTube golf” scene, Portnoy teamed up with Robby Berger and his Bob Does Sports brand.

The first Internet Invitational, organized in 2025, featured Dunkin as a sponsor and a $1 million prize. The event was put on as a response to the PGA’s uneven investment in golf personalities. The Creator Classic that had signaled the PGA’s interest in digital partnerships was not held at TPC Sawgrass in 2026.

Portnoy and Berger bet that they could outdo the PGA by fully embracing golf creators, and they doubled down in 2026, when they quadrupled the size of the Internet Invitational prize. “It’s all because of Dave, man,” Barstool Sports video editor Brendan Jones told Golf magazine. “Dave puts up all this money, and that changes people. That’s not good or bad — it just changes the way that people act, right? Dave’s big thing with anything that we do at Barstool, it’s like, what are the stakes? Why do people care? How are we going to get anything out of them?”

In this case, people care because the proceedings were sillier, wackier, and more relatable than anything a PGA-sanctioned event could deliver. There were breakout stars like Paige Spiranac, wildly varied handicaps, and plenty of substance use during the tournament. And the fans loved it. Viewership hit 3.5 million during the first 24 hours and ballooned past 11 million views within a week.

“When you put the average Joe, who’s not a great golfer— that, to I think a lot of people, was riveting,” Berger told The Hollywood Reporter. “I mean, it’s kind of what Bob Does Sports was built off of.”

As exciting as the action may have been, the Internet Invitational wasn’t free from drama. Nelk member Salim Sirur‘s excessive drinking and Sam Heung Min‘s criticisms related to rules enforcement added controversy to the event’s conclusion. Given Portnoy’s controversial past and the indiscretions of some big names in the YouTube golf community (looking at you, Good Good), one wonders how long the Internet Invitational can sustain its positive momentum.

But the success of the tournament’s second iteration can’t be ignored. With the decline of LIV Golf and the presence of elite golfers on YouTube, there’s an opportunity for creators to build a viable alternative to the PGA Tour. To do that will take continued dedication from the people who made the Internet Invitational happen — and the golfers might want to cut down on the drinking, too.
