Any official GTA 6 PC port’s ability to deliver a proper first person mode would be its biggest ace in the hole, so reckon I, a traitor

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

GTA 5 and Red Dead 2 both got ‘em, but Rockstar’s latest offering feels their most first person-coded yet

To be fair, I’ve made no secret of my plans to do just that if you’ve read any of our recent roundups of what everyone’s playing over the weekend. I can understand why it might leave a sour taste in the mouth, but it is what it is. No corporate gun has been pointed at my head as a mandate to temporarily forsake my PC and play the big game of the moment is forced into my hands.

Anyway, I’m writing about playing GTA 6 on console because it’s led me to conclude that a particular aspect of GTA 5’s PC port - one I’ve always rather taken for granted - could be a bigger deal for the inevitable Windows version of 6 than even the usual go-to PC plus points. You know, better graphics, superior performance, and modding. Historically, Rockstar’s crime sandboxes are games played from the third person perspective. That shot lingering behind Niko Bellic as he jogs around Brooklyn with a rocket launcher or following the damn bike of CJ as he in turn follows that damn train. This is the default way GTA’s breakneck action is presented to the player and was with GTA 5 at launch.

As Rockstar’s offerings have shuffled away from arcadey rampage facilitators and ever deeper into the mould of uber-detailed life of crime sims, however, they've also began letting players see things directly through the eyes of the crim whose blood-splattered shoes they’ve stepped into. In order to do that, while simultaneously pushing the detail and graphical boundaries of their rendered worlds, Rockstar have needed to squeeze every bit of juice they can from the available hardware. Red Dead Redemption 2 managed to include a full first person mode at launch. GTA 6 hasn’t been so lucky, even if there are moments when bringing up your phone or reading a note temporarily lodges the camera into Jason and Lucia’s noggins.

There is a way to glitch yourself into a more permanent first person perspective, but it’s naturally not got the polish that a proper first person mode would. What it does do is make clear that, with vehicle interiors lavishly detailed and some gun animations looking natural rather than janky, any port using the PC's extra horses to deliver a proper first person mode seems a no brainer, if not something already set in stone.

Assuming that is the case, I frankly can’t see why first person won’t become the go-to perspective on PC. Even more so than RDR2, GTA 6 feels like a game built to belong in first person. It dedicates so much time and energy to engaging you in every aspect of Jason and Lucia’s routines, rendering everything from eating to working out to doing up a house (and the acts involved in doing a robbery, I guess) in such exhaustive detail and depth that it’s almost beyond parody.

Notifications ping about you needing to finish a bench pressing session where every rep involves holding and releasing R2 and L2 at just the right time. An app tells you you’re hungry, so you wander over to the fridge, pick out a protein shake to down, then press R2 to lob the bottle in the bin, assuming you’re keen to keep your flat tidy. Shopping trips see you pick up groceries off shelves, stow them in a bag, then run home with it tucked under your arm because you’ve brought a motorbike and forgotten it lacks a boot. Every stickup begins with the ritual of picking your longarm gun out of your car’s boot, where it might have sat next to a bag of drugs you’ve stowed to deliver to a fence later. Stopping off to switch cars and clothes to throw the cops off your scent is no longer just for overenthusiastic roleplayers.

The fact that the game goes to such lengths only to maintain the detachment inherent to the third person view feels bizarrely antithetical at times, like an exhibition of a tiny Fabergé egg that you’re forced to view through a telescope from the surface of Mars. The massive efforts of Rockstar’s developers - many of whom are currently trying to gain formal union recognition or have been fighting the company at an employment tribunal over firings they argue were unjust - went into putting together an artwork that regularly ends up at an arm’s length out of necessity, despite packing intricacies likely best seen through a magnifying glass.

Enter the PC release. And possibly nothing else offering the same bang for your buck around the same time. With GTA 5, the PS4 and Xbox One got an enhanced version of the game around the time of the PC port, offering a number of extra features - including a first person mode. Rockstar were quick to rule out GTA 6 getting 60 or 40fps performance modes on current console hardware due to a lack of horses, and if that also applies to a first person mode, then such a thing might only come with the next generation of consoles. That’d be the PS6 and Xbox’s Project Helix, neither of which seem hugely imminent and could well end up boasting ludicrous prices thanks to the ongoing memory shortages.

So, those who already own a PC capable of running any GTA 6 port could be in the best position to enjoy the game from a Jason and Lucia-eye view the moment such a thing becomes possible. Sure, it would also look and run better, but to be brutally honest I’ve found it looks damn gorgeous and runs acceptably on PS5. Sure, modding will no doubt spring up must-have additions, but the base game already works in plenty of the roleplaying mechanics and in-depth side-jobbing that a lot of popular GTA 5 mods delivered.

Thus, that window as the place to play GTA 6 as it feels intended, in first person, is what I’d currently peg as the best chance for the crimsim to be not just a great game you can play on PC - as I’d argue Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA 5 are - but a great PC game that’s inarguably best experienced on our platform of choice. I don’t think writing that is falling into the trap it’s easy to end up in with big games that hit console first: declaring in salesman-like fashion that they’ll of course be better on PC by default, in a manner that sometimes feels a bit like you’re trying to convince yourself that you’ve not paid more than a pre-RAM crisis console buyer for nothing.

I’d never consider playing Cyberpunk 2077 in anything but first person on PC. GTA 6, from what I’ve played thus far, could be the next uber-detailed open world I spend hundreds of hours taking in the sights of, ideally through the eyes of a highly dangerous and highly stylish operator on the prowl for lucrative underworld opportunities.
