LACMA Art+Film Gala: Nicole Kidman, Timothée Chalamet Honor Denis Villeneuve

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

“It’s a pleasure to be here. You guys are just crazy party people,” said artist Vija Celmins as she summed up the jovial atmosphere at the 15th annual LACMA Art+Film Gala during her casual and off-the-cuff acceptance speech as one of the two honorees that night.

The annual fundraiser is a party, and unique among the many galas that hit the town this time of year. Along with raising money for the museum’s initiative to make film more central to its programming (this year it raised a record-breaking almost $7 million), it brings out an eclectic mix of A-list talent, up-and-coming filmmakers, and giants of the art world.

Honoree, Denis Villeneuve, Honoree Vija Celmins, co-chair Eva Chow and LACMA CEO Michael Govan

Just a quick scan of the packed room: Al Pacino sitting with artist Ed Ruscha and art dealer Larry Gagosian; artist Alex Israel wrapping his arm around Orlando Bloom for a photo; model Alex Consani crouching down to chat with influencer and comedian Quenlin Blackwell; and artists Jonas Wood and Mickalene Thomas chatting in one corner while Sarah Paulson and Parker Posey catch up in another.

Anya Taylor-Joy set the mood for the evening as one of the first arrivals on the red carpet, where talent posed in front of Chris Burden’s famed public art installation Urban Light. There in support of Dune director Denis Villeneuve, who was honored along with Celmins, the Dune: Part Two star wore a vintage 1997 Pierre Cardin haute couture bridal gown with a sheer silver overlay that looked both of the past and of the future.

Villeneuve’s leading man, Timothée Chalamet, skipped the carpet altogether, instead slipping into the crowded tent for dinner in a seat next to Villeneuve and his wife, producer Tanya Lapointe. Chalamet arrived separately from girlfriend Kylie Jenner, who did walk the carpet in a low-back Ashi Studio gown. When Jenner arrived at her table, she was seated opposite Chalamet, reaching across to grab his hand.

Seated next to Villeneuve was Nicole Kidman (in a voluminous, ballerina-inspired Chanel dress), who flew 15 hours from Australia to give Villeneuve his award. “The truth is I’ve never really met Denis. I’ve met him on some red carpets and sort of waved at him,” she said during her remarks. “Yet somehow I feel like I know the deepest parts of him because that is what extraordinary filmmakers do. They let you in. They reveal themselves to us through their work. They invite you into their imaginations, into their dreams, into their questions, and into their fears.”

Monica Barbaro, Salma Hayek and Kim Kardashian

Before Villeneuve took the stage, a video capturing his film legacy of big, bold, and personal epics like Sicario, Blade Runner 2049, Arrival, and the Dune trilogy was played, featuring a slew of A-list directors singing his praises, including Steven Spielberg, Alfonso Cuarón, Alejandro González Iñárritu, James Cameron, and Chloé Zhao. “I’ve always felt that cinema is seen as a Pop art—the film carries the burden of being popular in the present,” said Villeneuve in his acceptance speech. “But at the end of the day, the real challenge is to make something that can enter the test of time.”

Speaking of tests (screen tests, that is) Villeneuve got a bit playful with the end of his speech, announcing to the room that he would—right here, right now—announce which actor would play James Bond. The French Canadian director is helming the next Bond film, and over the past week reports have been flying about who might be cast. Would Villeneuve put us all out of our misery and spill the beans? “The actor who will play the next James Bond is—and I will stop right now because [producer] Amy Pascal is about to have a heart attack,” he said with a smile.

The gala, once again presented by Gucci, hosted a slew of this Oscar season’s most exciting young filmmakers, including Obsession filmmaker Curry Barker and Club Kid’s Jordan Firstman, along with La Bola Negra directors Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi (known as Los Javis). The latter told me they didn’t plan to party as hard as they did at the Academy Museum Gala a few weeks ago, where they went viral for singing along to Taylor Swift’s performance of “Love Story.” Said Ambrossi, “I’m drinking only water tonight.”

Among the other guests, Joel Edgerton chatted with Abbott Elementary’s Quinta Brunson and Daniel Scheinert, one half of the Oscar-winning directing team Daniels (Everything Everywhere All at Once). Kim Kardashian wove her way through the crowd, pausing perfectly to look over her shoulder for a trailing photographer. And several stars made a date night out of the event: Sienna Miller brought fiancé Oli Green; Julia Garner sat with musician Charlie Walker; Viola Davis enjoyed the night with husband and producing partner Julius Tennon. Paris Hilton, who introduced Rosé onstage with her classic catchphrase “that’s hot,” wore a glittering Jenny Packham gown as she cascaded through the room with her husband, Carter Reum.

Eva Chow, who had co-chaired the gala for the past 15 years with Leonardo DiCaprio, announced that she’d be stepping down from her post after this year. Dubbed the “Culture Queen of Los Angeles,” she is credited with bringing together Hollywood and the art world for the event, helping grow it into the must-attend party of the season. DiCaprio couldn’t make it this year because he has a cold, but his girlfriend, model Vittoria Ceretti, was there to represent him.

But with all these big Hollywood types in the room, it was Celmins who stole the show—though she didn’t mean to. In a video that ran before her acceptance speech, the artist welcomed the video crew into her home, revealing her quick-witted humor, her ornery cat (she claims to be a dog person), and her love of Bob Dylan. Celmins is best known for her boundless work inspired by nature, including ocean waves, desert floors, and starry night skies. She charmed the crowd with her boundless personality as well.

LACMA had plenty to celebrate this year too. After a 20-year-long planning and development process, the David Geffen Galleries were completed this past year. As partygoers entered the tent for dinner, the silhouette of the formidable new building stood out in the background. “Same tent. Different buildings around it,” joked LACMA CEO Michael Govan during his opening remarks.

After his speech, Villeneuve and all the guests made their way out to LACMA’s main courtyard, where Rosé would perform under the stars. I joked with him that I thought he would make some news with that tease of a Bond casting, and then wondered if he likes being the center of attention at a gala like this. He said he values the connections he makes with other filmmakers and artists at these events. “I like talking one-on-one,” he said.

Noor Alfallah, Al Pacino, Erica Pelosini, Larry Gagosian and Ed Ruscha

While Rosé performed four songs for the black-tie-clad audience, Emily in Paris star Ashley Park and In the Heights actor Anthony Ramos swayed to her ballad “Number One Girl.” Popcorn and cotton candy were passed out to the audience, which, naturally, sang along when Rosé ended her set with her biggest hit, “APT.” Andrew Garfield led his Artificial costar Monica Barbaro by the hand through the crowd. Though the pair didn’t pose for photos on the carpet together, the couple (who have been romantically linked since 2025) spent the evening in close contact as they chatted with friends like Salma Hayek Pinault.

Villeneuve’s speech best captured the evening’s one-of-a-kind mash-up of art and film when he spoke passionately about the importance of human effort in the era of AI. “I can create something with a prompt, or I can go into the desert with actors, with a crew, and go on a real adventure where I learn about life and become better at my craft,” he said. “More important than the result is the process. The process is how the human experience acquires its meaning. In this moment in time, it is more important than ever that human beings keep making art together.”
