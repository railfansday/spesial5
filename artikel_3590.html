5 Most Ambitious LitRPG Books of All Time, Ranked by Goodreads

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Thanks to Matt Dinniman's Dungeon Crawler Carl becoming a legitimate phenomenon around the globe, a lot of casual and genre readers are starting to latch onto the massive worlds of LitRPG. Essentially taking a role-playing, progression type of game and bringing it into a literary form, LitRPG has become one of the hottest genres around. He Who Fights With Monsters has started to pick up a ton of steam in the wake of Dungeon Crawler Carl's success, and may very well be the next to get a screen adaptation. But there are so many LitRPG books and series out there to explore, and a lot of them are nothing like the ones you've already heard about or read.

There are some wildly ambitious LitRPG novels out there, each bringing something bold and new to a space that is doing nothing but expanding and evolving by the minute. Some show off what a story like Dungeon Crawler Carl would look like if the protagonist had their children with them for the dangerous event. Others take readers to remote farms on faraway planets as protagonists try and live peaceful lives. There's a genuinely great LitRPG series where the main character is literally a tree.

LitRPG writers have ambition in spades, delivering some awesome stories to check out that you may not be too familiar with. Below, we've picked out five of the genre's most ambitious, and ranked them according to the rating on Goodreads.

This eight-book Isekai adventure from author SpaizZzer just concluded earlier this fall, and it has stood for nearly a decade as one of the most unique stories in the LitRPG space. If you're not familiar, the main character of the series is a tree, and that premise was turned into eight very popular, highly loved books, which feels like a miracle in and of itself.

The first book of the series begins with a man named Matt being reborn into a fantasy world, which is the usual start to many LitRPG and/or Isekai stories. However, when Matt is reborn, he comes back as a sentient tree, unable to move from his spot. He quickly realizes there is more to his situation meets the eye, as the tree becomes the central battleground in a demon incursion, and Matt (aka TreeTree) needs to use his evolving powers to aid the heroes that find themselves beneath his branches.

So many LitRPG stories focus on adults in situations where they become players in some sort of dangerous adventure or death game, forcing them to level up and adapt to save themselves, and potentially their entire worlds. Erin Ampersand's Apocalypse Parenting series makes that entire concept devastatingly personal as it puts a regular mom in the midst of battle — right alongside her three young children.

The name really says it all. Apocalypse Parenting flips the script on the genre by exploring what it would be like to lead your own kids through the kind of crazy, other-worldly scenarios that are common in Dungeon Crawler Carl and countless other LitRPG novels. It makes for not only an emotional journey, but also one that makes you think a lot about the logistics that might plague most characters you encounter in stories like this.

The Wandering Inn is one of the biggest names in the world of LitRPG, and it has a little bit of everything. The story of Erin Solstice has some slice-of-life vibes to it, as she creates a safe harbor for creatures of all kinds, but it's also filled with a ton of great fantasy elements along the way. It checks so many boxes, which is ambitious in its own right, but it's the sheer size of The Wandering Inn that has it on this list.

The Wandering Inn is one of the longest written works in history, with 20 individual installments released over the last eight years. And those individual novels are often so long that the print versions, which finally started seeing the light of day this year, are actually splitting the books into multiple parts. For example, the original Wandering Inn book was made into two print novels consisting of 640 pages and 784 pages, respectively. It takes a lot to make a story interesting for this long, but Pirateaba has done just that.

There are a lot of slice-of-life LitRPG books out there, focusing less on world-ending battles or death games, and more on level systems can bring to the "slower" aspects of living. Seth Ring's Battle Mage Farmer series is the best of both worlds, delivering on the slice-of-life story without abandoning the bigger tales and stakes of the genre.

John Sutton's tale of trying to build a quiet life as a farmer on the outskirts of an empire comes as he tries his best to avoid the war and devastation all around him. He's at conflict from the jump, wanting nothing more than a simple existence but knowing that he's destined for a lot more. It's a brutally relatable conundrum he finds himself in, and it makes Battle Mage Farmer one of the more intriguing LitRPG series out there.

Speaking of warriors living out their days on hopefully peaceful farmland, Beware of Chicken is a layered story that takes a different approach to nearly every kind of tale you'll find in LitRPGs. It's also not at all what many are expecting from progression fantasy, but that's a big reason why it stands out.

The book begins with the protagonist waking up not only in another world, but in the body of a celestially powerful martial arts master. Instead of taking up the conflict that his former body host was a part of, the new Jin decides he'd rather take his powers home and start a farm. It's relaxing, hilarious, and constantly bubbling over with the threat of life outside the homestead.

So far, there are five books in the Beware of Chicken series (the sixth arriving early next year), and the 4.45 Goodreads rating on the first installment is the lowest of the entire series.
