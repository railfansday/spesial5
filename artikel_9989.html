How are you dealing with all the negative side effects of AI on the web?

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

It seems to me that the AI hype killed the web for good, but not for the reasons we immediately associate with it.

Search engines now rank links full of slop at the top, which ironically make LLM summaries acceptable, not because they are better than what we had before (they are not), but because they at least mention the good links which are now buried at the bottom of the page. Also, they've been rate limiting ordinary users like crazy: more often than I'd like to, Google and DuckDuckGo now either show me either an error message or tell me to wait a bit. Sometimes I waste at least a minute refreshing the page and waiting for a result.

I also can't browse GitHub freely without being logged in. Anubis is now everywhere, occasionally blocking me. I feel like my productivity has actually gone down thanks to all of that.

Edit: I see that most of the replies here are suggesting relying less on the web and going after more fundamental resources, which are great suggestions, don’t get me wrong! But a book from 5 years ago will not help you understand why your custom firmware doesn’t boot in your 1-year old mobile chipset. A existing bug report on the web probably will. Not quite my predicament at the moment, but something very similar for which the web seemed to work just fine before the AI craze.

It's the occasion of relying more on communities like Lobsters or on RSS feeds to discover new things.

I agree, but sometimes I don't need to discover new things, I need to check boring stuff that no one else is discussing to get things done.

I don't want to go on a rant but basically it seems AI killed programming as a hobby to me for reasons more than one. The experience of trying to build something myself has become both very expensive if I'm dealing with buying hardware or very frustrating when it comes to do the research I need to write the software. All of that makes vibecoding way more tempting and, to be honest, at that point I'd rather pick a new hobby as I already spend my workweek doing basically the same just to pay my bills. Bird watching is not rate limited.

I feel like I was prepared for this AIrmageddon of the Web by going more and more offline.

I’ve lot of physical books. I’ve relearned the trade (and the joy) of going through books to find information I’m looking for. If I realize a field is important enough for me, I buy second-hand books about the subject from before 2020.

I rely more and more on official documentation and manpages.

When using a library, I have more and more the reflex of checking under the hood and read the source code. It might seems daunting but, once you pass that fear, it’s extremely efficient. You don’t rely on outdated documentation: you see what you library is doing. In some case, this even allowed me to ditch the library for my own implementation when I realized that what I trully needed was a couple hundred lines of Python.

For everything for which I’ve no book, no documentation, no man page, I rely mostly on Wikipedia.

All those 4 points require an investment. Instead of having the right git command from stackoverflow, I skim through the official git book. It takes me 5 minutes instead of 1. It’s harder. But then, I realized something interesting: I had to go less and less for the book. I’m really building my skill, I’m learning stuff for which I was blindly relying on SO, thinking I understood stuff.

Of course, not everything is offline, sadly. So:

I follow a lot of interesting blogs through RSS in Offpunk (to make them offline).

My only social network is Mastodon where the vast majority is against slop. I may also hang out of some good old forums/communities.

I pay for Kagi as my main search engine. In the setting I’ve ask to remove IA slop. In Kagi, you can also give high/low priority to domains. I block completely domain with slops or deprioritize them. I use multiple "block slop" list in my ublock filters. If a website appears in my Kagi search but is blocked as AI slop, I ask Kagi to never show me result from this website anymore.

This is far from perfect, it requires effort but this makes me a lot happier in my own personal life. This requires some sacrifices.

The main problem is that it makes me live a life so different from the others that, at some point, you feel like you are not living on the same planet.

Astroturfing pre clawd-era was a bit more limited as you had to be a state actor or employ rooms full of people in low income countries to do it, but let's not pretend it wasn't already a big problem.
