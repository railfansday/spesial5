Laia Abril Photographs the Instruments of Rape Culture

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In her latest show, garments and other objects serve as stand-ins for the systems that allow sexual assault to persist.

Editor’s Note: The following story contains mentions of sexual assault and harassment. To reach the National Sexual Assault Hotline, call 1-800-656-HOPE (4673) or visit online.rainn.org.

Some institutional rape cases create a glut of content. Recent allegations against Cornell students led to the publication of the accused’s portraits, images of their text messages, and videos of their fraternity house. Tweets and articles flooded the internet, while a journalist’s attempt to identify Jane Doe launched an entire Instagram campaign: Users posted their own pictures with the hashtag #iamjanedoe to help shield the victim. On the other side of the spectrum, reported rapes in the military, the Catholic Church, and less wealthy communities around the world may be met with silence, handled privately, or otherwise fail to galvanize the digital realm.

Spanish photographer Laia Abril — whose larger project “A History of Misogyny” has also tackled feminicide, abortion, and endometriosis — faces a tricky mandate as she created On Rape and Institutional Failure, first shown in 2020 and now on view at the International Center for Photography. Her images have to be compelling without embracing spectacle, specific while addressing an enormous, global theme.

The artist makes two key choices, one conceptual and one formal: She shoots objects instead of people, all in black-and-white. Honing in on her palette, Abril gets at the seemingly eternal nature of misogyny; her distilled images stoke a similarly condensed rage.
