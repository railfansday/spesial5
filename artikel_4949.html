Judge Acquits MIT Brothers in $25M Ethereum MEV Case, Ending Prosecution

A federal judge in Manhattan has acquitted Anton and James Peraire-Bueno, the MIT-educated brothers accused of exploiting Ethereum's transaction ordering system to take $25 million from automated trading bots.

U.S. District Judge Jessica Clarke granted the brothers' motion for a judgment of acquittal on Monday, clearing them of wire fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, according to her ruling. The decision closes the criminal case without a second trial.

"The question before the Court is not whether the Peraire-Buenos' conduct was right or moral, or even whether it violated uncharged crimes," Clarke wrote. To convict on wire fraud, prosecutors had to show the brothers made a material misrepresentation, and none of the government's theories did so, she found. Ethereum, she noted, is a "trustless, highly adversarial environment with no terms of use."

While the brothers' scheme "may have frustrated the spirit" of MEV-Boost, software that lets Ethereum validators outsource the ordering of transactions to specialized block builders, that was not enough to make out fraud, Clarke said. In a footnote, she added that the prosecution "raises serious due process concerns," though she declined to rule on that question.

Because the money laundering charge relied on the proceeds of the alleged wire fraud, it fell with the fraud counts.

The case stemmed from an April 2023 exploit targeting so-called sandwich bots, automated traders that profit from maximal extractable value (MEV) by placing their own trades on either side of other users' transactions.

According to the ruling, the brothers placed bait transactions designed to lure specific bots, then exploited a bug in a relay's code to see the bots' bundled trades before committing to a block. They then swapped the bait transactions for trades that sold the tokens instead, leaving the bots with about $25 million in losses in roughly 12 seconds. Authorities arrested the pair in May 2024, saying they spent months planning the attack and took steps to hide the proceeds.

At trial in Manhattan last year, an insider testified that the brothers had allegedly planned the attack, and the defense had earlier sought to block evidence of their Google searches. Clarke declared a mistrial in November 2025, after the jury deadlocked.

The case was closely watched in the crypto industry. Clarke's ruling described it as a "first of its kind" prosecution, a label the government itself used in its indictment.
