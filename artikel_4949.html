I’ll be honest—working out in shorts was never really my thing. I’ve always been more of a leggings girl. I used to think flowy shorts felt too exposed and compression shorts weren’t flattering. But since I'm a style writer who lives a pretty active lifestyle, I figured it was time to give athletic shorts a real shot. Now, after testing over 20 pairs of workout shorts over the past almost two-and-a-half years, I can officially say I’m a convert.

Alongside stylists and other WH editors, I tested the internet's most popular workout shorts. Over the past two years, we evaluated them based on coverage, moisture-wicking abilities, comfort, size range, and fit. The result? A list of six fantastic pairs that we recommend for all types of workouts, including running, HIIT training, yoga, Pilates, and more.

I’ve tested a wide range of athletic shorts, but the Gym People High Waist Short topped my list for a few reasons: it’s super soft, has nice compression, comes in a ton of colors, and has many different pockets to store your belongings. Plus, said pockets don’t add any bulk to your legs—a rare find in a short.

My biggest pet peeve with athletic shorts is when they feel way too tight and restrictive. But this pair, made from a polyester-spandex blend, has four-way stretch that makes it feel breathable, light, and easy to move in. “The fit is a bit more compressive around the stomach, which gives a nice smoothing and lifting effect—something I actually appreciate since super stretchy biker shorts can sometimes feel a little shapeless around the hips,” says style contributor Lily Wohlner.While this short does offer some compression, it leans more on the stretchy side than super form-fitting, so it might not be the best pick for high-impact workouts. It feels supportive, but I wouldn’t wear it for running or burpees since it doesn’t hold me in quite enough. That said, the fabric is buttery soft—like, ridiculously comfortable—so it’s perfect for walking, yoga, and Pilates. I also love that it’s not see-through, so I felt totally fine stretching and bending without worrying. The only downside? The moisture-wicking could be better. The 5-inch inseam is another win: not too long, not too short—just the right amount of leg without worrying about coverage. Prefer something shorter? The Baggie also comes in a 2.5-inch version, which is great if you want a more cropped fit.

Fabric is everything when it comes to workout shorts—look for lightweight, sweat-wicking materials that move with you, not against you. According to Saladino, it's best to steer clear of cotton when looking for athletic shorts, as it doesn't wick away moisture as well as materials like polyester and nylon. After all, you want to make sure you're staying totally dry in your workout shorts (because who wants sweat stains down there?).

Most shorts on this list are high-rise, which we found to have the most coverage and look the most flattering during our workouts, as they offer flattering tummy control. Many high-rise shorts also have a drawstring, which helps you customize the fit so it doesn't slip down or ride up while you're getting a sweat on. Some of the options we tried were low-rise, but we ended up ditching most of them because we didn't love the fit. However, the Arc'teryx pair on this list is low-rise, and we actually love that it sits nicely on our hips and looks flattering.

When it comes to the best length for workout shorts, you want to think about the inseam length, AKA the distance between the crotch area and the bottom of the short. There are some longer options for those who prefer more leg coverage (ideal for burpees and squats!), and super short options on this list if you like a lightweight mini short for running or walking. After testing, we prefer something right in the middle that makes us feel nimble but also provides coverage, like our favorite of the bunch, The Gym People High Waist Shorts.

After testing, we found that some shorts are better for specific activities. For example, the Beyond Yoga pair is great for yoga because it’s super stretchy and buttery soft, so it’s easy to move in, whether you’re in downward dog or trying to master crow pose. On the other hand, the Arc'teryx pair is low-rise, super light, and has a snug fit, making it ideal for running since it feels weightless and doesn't slow us down.
