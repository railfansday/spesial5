Well, now I'm going to update on the phase III INVINCIBLE trial, which is our open-label, randomized controlled clinical study that is testing our drug INT230-6 as monotherapy, meaning just our drug alone, compared to the best standard of care drugs in second and third line treatment for three types of soft tissue sarcomas. On last quarter's call, we reported that we had initiated new patient enrollment in terms of starting the process for that in the INVINCIBLE-3 study in a limited number of U.S. sites.

To make this restart happen, this quarter, we added new CROs to help manage this clinical study, including vendors for statistical analysis and a large organization that specializes in receiving computed tomography, known as CT, images from sites around the world electronically during clinical trials. The purpose of this change is to establish consistency of the reads by radiologists across all sites, whether a patient's cancer is progressing or not, or regressing, compared to a patient's prior scan. Our key sarcoma thought leaders made a few important observations and recommendations. One was a central read, and the other was a recommendation to use a different set of criteria to determine whether a patient's disease was progressing.

In the prior version of the amended protocol, we based disease progression on tumor size, which is standard practice for systemic treatments. However, we have learned for our drug, the tumor becomes larger from the injection, from immune cell infiltration, and from the buildup of fluid as the cancer cells die. While changes in tumor size using criteria such as RECIST or WHO may be standard practice for determining progression for systemic treatments because it's based on the size of the tumor, we believe those criteria are unsuitable for our unique intratumoral drug.

Working with the expert radiology group, we finalized a set of criteria to assess whether a patient's cancer is progressing by measuring the tumor's density compared to its baseline. These criteria are referred to as Choi, and they are often used for certain types of sarcoma. While size goes up, density declines using our drug, and using Choi, we think we will have a better assessment of progressive disease. The endpoint remains overall survival. Sites will send their scans for assessment to the central review group, who will return a report on the Choi scores and provide detailed information on density changes for treated and untreated tumors.

Recruitment began in the U.S. in Q3 enrollment, and several sites are activated and seeking patients. We've also filed the needed documentation to restart the phase III in Europe and Canada in Q3. Now provide an update on our phase II INVINCIBLE-4 presurgical breast cancer study, which is our randomized, controlled, open-label, multicenter study of INT given before the administration of standard of care neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy regimen in patients with early-stage operable triple-negative breast cancer. The efficacy endpoint is pathological complete response, referred to as pCR, which is the absence of any live cancer at the time of surgery in the tumor, breast, or lymph nodes. Tumor sizes in our trial are more challenging to treat, as our inclusion criteria uses a larger minimal tumor size than what was used for approval of the current regimen. Larger tumors are more challenging to treat.

On October 23rd, we announced new data at ESMO on patients who received our drug, showing an early indication of the potential to improve the percentage of women achieving a pathological complete response, or pCR. Clinical studies have shown that if triple-negative breast cancer patients have no live cancer at the time of surgery, the pCR, the risk of the disease returning in five years after surgery is significantly reduced, from about 50% without having a pCR to about 16% with a pCR. This difference is clinically meaningful, and many regulatory agencies, including the U.S. FDA and the EMA, accept pCR as an endpoint for certain types of accelerated or conditional approvals. There are two cohorts in the study.

The first is one dose of our drug administered prior to the standard of care. This is cohort A. The second cohort B, is the standard of care alone. Patients are randomized one-to-one to each cohort. As reported previously, 71% of patients who received INT230-6 achieved a pCR compared to 42% the standard of care. Very small numbers, it's early data. But these were patients with a median tumor size of 2.6, which is quite large. We also reported that no invasive cancer cells were detected in the axillary lymph nodes in six of the seven patients in cohort A after INT230-6 treatment, despite five of these seven patients, 71%, being initially biopsy-proven node positive. We don't know the nodes.

Results also showed tumor necrosis in all seven cohort A patients. In the pre-surgery MRI, at the completion of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy, four patients in our group had radiological complete response, and three patients, 43%, had partial response. All seven patients met criteria for breast-conserving surgery. These surgical results are consistent with the results from the study in Ottawa, our completed INVINCIBLE-2 study, that the timing of the surgery is unaffected by the use of our drug, which is a very important factor for patients.

We resumed to dose new patients in the third quarter in INVINCIBLE-4 after the study enrollment was paused late last year to revise the dosing regimen for patients receiving our drug because some patients experienced localized skin issues. Our collaborators in the INVINCIBLE study, the Swiss Cancer Institute, have established an IDMB of qualified experts to review safety data and images from these newly treated patients and assess whether the skin issues have been resolved.

The institute team felt, and we agreed, that an independent group of experts reporting that the current dosing regimen had resolved the skin issue would be the best way to assure all the investigators of the safety of this drug with regards to the skin issue. As a side note, we used a higher dose for tumor size in our completed INVINCIBLE-2 study in Ottawa compared to the revised dosing regimen in the ongoing INVINCIBLE-4 study.
