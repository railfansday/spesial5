Canal+ Sends Signal To African Creative Community With Academy Launch Backed By President Of South Africa

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The President of South Africa joined with Canal+ boss Maxime Saada today to launch a skills academy that Canal+ hopes will benefit 800 to 1,000 people in its first year.

A year after Canal+ bought African pay-TV giant MultiChoice for $2B, Cyril Ramaphosa gave his full-throated backing to Canal+’s actions in South Africa and beyond, including a 20-minute speech at today’s event.

He was guest of honor at the do in Johannesburg where the Canal+ Academy was unveiled.

The academy combines Canal+’s pre-existing University model with the MultiChoice Talent Factory (MTF), which together have trained around 10,500 people across the past eight years. The new Canal+ Academy programme is aiming to train between 800 and 1,000 in its first year and will retain the MTF as a development programme within.

Next year’s progrmme will feature six intensive training courses and six open masterclasses across disciplines including acting, African series production, AI, digital filmmaking and sports video journalism.

“Africa has no shortage of talent or stories,” said Canal+ Africa CSR director Grace Loubassou. “What we need to keep building are the skills, opportunities and capabilities that allow that talent to flourish. Through Canal+ Academy, we are investing not only in people, but in the future of Africa’s creative industries. Strong creative industries create jobs, attract investment, strengthen local production ecosystems and help African stories travel beyond borders.”

Canal+ will be hoping today’s annoucement paired with Ramaphosa’s appearance will send a strong signal to the African creative community.

When Canal+ bought MultiChoice last year, Saada said the new powerhouse would “double down” on local content made with big budgets like hit period drama Shaka iLembe, but the signals since have been mixed. Soon after the deal closed, Canal+ shuttered loss-making African streamer Showmax and it has frequently talked up synergies in the region, which total €250M and are well on the way to being achieved, according to Saada several months ago.
