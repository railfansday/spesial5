Pritzker accused of turning education tax credit into partisan fight by opting out of program: 'Sad to see'

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Teach Coalition CEO Sydney Altfield argued that Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker’s decision to opt his state out of the Federal Scholarship Tax Credit program — after several other Democratic governors opted into the program — will "put kids in the middle of partisan politics."

Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker, a Democrat, is being accused of putting partisan politics over kids' educational needs after he decided to opt out of the Federal Scholarship Tax Credit program, prompting criticism from parents and education advocacy groups.

Pritzker announced on Tuesday that his state will not opt into the program, which was signed into law by President Donald Trump last year as part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The governor described the program as a school voucher, while advocacy groups argued that he mischaracterized the plan.

"At a time when public school education needs investments more than ever, we should not siphon money from public schools for private education – especially as the Trump Administration's policies decimate education," Pritzker said in a statement. "We have a responsibility to do right by all of our students. Taxpayer funding should not subsidize donations to private schools that could discriminate based on religion, sexual orientation, or other characteristics."

The program allows people to receive federal tax credits for donations up to $1,700 to authorized scholarship-granting organizations (SGOs), which distribute the donated scholarship funds to eligible families to be used on private or public educational expenses, including private school tuition, tutoring services, textbooks and more.

In an interview with Fox News Digital, Teach Coalition CEO Sydney Altfield said her advocacy group is "extremely disappointed" in the governor for opting out of the program.

"Not only did he opt kids out of his state and families out of his state from receiving scholarships, but the program allows for Illinois residents to still claim the tax credit. And what that means is that money that should have and could have gone to students and families in Illinois will now just move over the border and flow out of state — over about $2 billion in estimated scholarship money that could have stayed in the state," she explained.

Pritzker said in his announcement that his opt-out reaffirms his commitment to strengthening the state’s public schools by ensuring federal taxpayer resources remain available to the students and communities they are intended to serve. ​

But Altfield took issue with Pritzker describing the program as a voucher, contending that it does not take money from public schools.

"In the same vein of him opting out, he completely misrepresented this program, saying that it is a voucher scheme. It is by definition not a voucher. It does not take a dollar from [the] public school systems. All it does is incentivize taxpayers to use their tax dollars, their federal tax dollars, not their state tax dollars, but federal tax dollars for scholarship money. And instead of it helping families in Illinois, it will now help families outside," Altfield said.

"Public school students are able to take this money," she added. "And we know that the vast majority of Illinois students go to public schools. That means the vast majority of taxpayers in the state send their kids to public schools. So we will see the vast majority of taxpayers designating their tax dollars to help support SGOs that will, in turn, support public school kids and students."

Altfield also accused Pritzker of putting children in the crossfire of partisan politics over an issue that she believes has an opportunity for a rare showing of bipartisanship.

Three other Democrats — Colorado Gov. Jared Polis, New York Gov. Kathy Hochul and New Mexico Gov. Michelle Lujan Grisham — joined dozens of Republicans in opting their states into the program. However, some states that have already opted in will have a new Democratic governor in January, such as Colorado, where Gov.-elect Phil Weiser has already signaled plans to withdraw.

Kansas, Kentucky and North Carolina all have Democratic governors who did not want to join the program, but GOP legislatures in those states overrode the governors' vetoes.

"I think it's really sad to see — to put kids in the middle of partisan politics," Altfield said of Pritzker's opt-out. "Many blue state governors — Gov. Hochul, Gov. Polis in Colorado and the New Mexico governor — have put politics aside and have put kids first. And I think that if governors can peel back whatever misconceptions they are reading, and they can read the program for what it is, it is bipartisan. It will have bipartisan support because education, kids, families are the ones that are going to be successful in this."

"I think we're going to see, and I hope we're going to see, more Democratic governors opting into this program before Jan. 1," she added.

Altfield also noted that the program could benefit families who may be struggling financially amid concerns in Illinois and across the nation about affordability.

"We all know that affordability is the number one issue that Democrats and everyone is running on and everyone is feeling a pinch of the penny across the country. And this is a real opportunity that has money flowing into the state of Illinois that could have helped families that are pinching every dollar. For the working mom of two or three or four, she may have one or two jobs, three jobs. Where is she sending her kid after school? Is she paying for after-school care? The tax credit program could have offered her a scholarship to help cover those costs," Altfield said.

"We talk about teachers paying out of pocket. It's been estimated that teachers pay well over $800 out of their pocket for school supplies. Imagine if every low-income kid came to school with all of the school supplies needed. That teacher would not have to pay out of pocket. It's small things like this that really add up in a pocketbook that are now not available to the families and the students in Illinois," she continued.
