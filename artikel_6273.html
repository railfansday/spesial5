Client dinners, catered lunches and team celebrations can add up quickly. The right business credit card can turn those restaurant bills into a meaningful haul of points, miles or cash back.

From high category bonuses to uncapped flat-rate rewards, the best credit cards for dining can make every business meal work harder.

Here's how our top picks compare based on their dining returns, annual fees and reward flexibility.

The information for the U.S. Bank Business Essentials Plus Visa Signature Card and the U.S. Bank Triple Cash Rewards Visa Business Card has been collected independently by The Points Guy. The card details on this page have not been reviewed or provided by the card issuer.

To determine the best business cards for dining, I looked for cards with an estimated return of at least 3% on restaurant purchases, based on TPG's valuations. I focused on ongoing earnings rather than welcome offers or dining credits, then selected cards serving distinct use cases, including high category bonuses, uncapped transferable rewards and no-annual-fee options.

Other cards that don't earn at least 3% back in value based on our valuations may still be good fits for your business within a broader rewards strategy, but are less competitive when restaurant earnings are the primary goal.

The American Express Business Gold earns valuable Membership Rewards points. Cardmembers earn 4 points per dollar spent in their top two spending categories each billing cycle (on up to $150,000 in bonus category spending per year, then 1 point per dollar), with dining and restaurants falling under an available category.

That means businesses that max out their cap could earn 600,000 points each year. Even if you don't come close to that cap, 4 points per dollar is still a significant return on spend, especially when they come with all the potential of Membership Rewards.

Just be careful: Since Amex rewards extra bonus points on your top spending categories each billing cycle, if other eligible expenses overtake dining, restaurant purchases may earn only 1 point per dollar. You can use that to your advantage when your restaurant spending is low one month, but it could also catch you off guard.

The U.S. Bank Business Essentials Plus earns 5% cash back in the cardholder's top eligible spending category each month on up to $200,000 in annual eligible purchases, automatically. Dining is one of the qualifying categories, and the cap is high enough that most businesses won't have to worry about it. All purchases outside the top category earn at least 2% cash back, which is also a generous return.

The $295 annual fee makes this card best for businesses with substantial, consistent restaurant spending. Otherwise, a no-annual-fee option may provide better value overall.

If you're looking for a card with no annual fee, The Blue Business Plus earns 2 Membership Rewards points per dollar spent on all purchases (for the first $50,000 in spending each year, then 1 point per dollar spent).

The simplicity of this card means you don't need to keep track of spending categories; it's just as great for your dining purchases as it is for shipping, advertising and other business expenses. For businesses that can stay below the cap and earn 2 points per dollar spent on all their spending, the combination of transferable points and no annual fee is difficult to beat.

Like the Blue Business Plus, the Capital One Venture Business earns 2 miles per dollar spent on dining and other purchases. Unlike the Amex card, however, that earning rate is unlimited. This makes Venture Business a stronger fit for businesses that expect to spend more than $50,000 each year and can take advantage of double miles well above that spending threshold, despite the $95 annual fee.

Capital One miles are highly versatile, with options to transfer to 15-plus airline or hotel partners, book travel without blackout dates through Capital One Travel and more. In fact, a different set of transfer partners could make the Venture Business a more compelling solution than an Amex, depending on your business's redemption preferences.

The Triple Cash Rewards Visa Business Card earns 3% cash back on eligible restaurant purchases with no annual fee, and there's no cap on total rewards.

It can't match the Business Essentials Plus card's 5% return, but there's no annual fee to recoup. A business would need to spend about $14,750 annually on dining for the 5% rate to make up for the $295 fee versus the Triple Cash card's no-annual-fee 3% return. Therefore, if you're looking for simple cash back but have more moderate restaurant spending, the Triple Cash is a stronger fit.

The Marriott Bonvoy Business Amex is a good fit for businesses that frequently entertain clients, host events at Marriott properties or incur dining expenses while traveling. It earns 4 points per dollar spent on eligible restaurant purchases worldwide, which represents a 3% return at TPG's November 2026 valuations. Since it doesn't impose a spending cap on its restaurant rewards, businesses can earn consistently throughout the year.

The card becomes especially valuable for businesses that regularly stay with Marriott and can put both its hotel benefits and Bonvoy rewards to use.

Of course, loyalty to one hotel brand won't work for everyone. Businesses that prioritize more flexible redemptions may prefer a card that includes Marriott as a transfer partner rather than choosing a cobranded card specifically.

The right business card option depends on more than earning rates. You'll also need to account for how much your business spends, whether you prefer cash back or travel rewards and whether you're willing to pay an annual fee.

Start with the net value, not the largest number in the table. The Amex Business Gold and Business Essentials Plus only deliver their highest dining returns when restaurant purchases rank among the business's top eligible categories. But cards that earn lower rates of return on dining might be a better value once you factor in annual fees and spending caps.

In addition to considering the type of rewards best for your business, think about what else these cards offer. Secondary benefits like travel protections, cellphone insurance, lounge access or elite status might make up for a lower earning rate on meals when you consider the whole package.

A large welcome offer can improve a card's first-year value, but it shouldn't outweigh its long-term fit. Compare ongoing rewards after the annual fee, along with the protections, credits and other benefits your business will realistically use. That said, when two or more cards are equally strong long-term fits, a welcome bonus can be a useful tiebreaker. Especially since some credit cards currently offer welcome bonuses of 100,000 points or more.

Lastly, confirm your spending will count toward elevated earning rates if the bonus rate affects which card you pay with. Dining bonuses depend on how the merchant categorizes the purchase, and not all food vendors are considered a restaurant. For example, a hotel cafe may code as travel, while a bar might code as nightlife or entertainment.

The Amex Business Gold offers the highest potential dining return based on TPG's November 2026 valuations, but its earning rate alone likely doesn't settle the decision. Spending caps, annual fees, redemption flexibility and other card benefits will also factor into which card delivers the most value for your business.

Businesses with large, predictable restaurant expenses will come out ahead by choosing premium cards, even after budgeting for an annual fee. When your dining spending is more moderate or inconsistent, though, a no-annual-fee card may produce the same net value with less monitoring. Don't pay for an earnings rate your business won't regularly trigger.
