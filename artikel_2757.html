Warriors vs. Mavericks player grades: Will Richard stars, Yaxel Lendeborg struggles

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Golden State Warriors continued inability to play basketball without Steph Curry took another step forward on Wednesday, when they lost 116-94 to the Dallas Mavericks. It was not quite as ugly as their previous two blowouts, but it was still a one-sided affair, and it gave them four consecutive losses, as well as a strong splash of cold water that followed their eight-game winning streak.

So let's dive into some more disappointing grades. I'll try to be concise this time, because … well, you know. You're not exactly dying to read more about this game are you? This is a judgement free zone, but…

As always, grades are based on my expectations for each individual player, with a "B" grade representing the average performance for that person.

Note: True-shooting percentage (TS) is a scoring efficiency metric that accounts for threes and free throws. Entering Wednesday's games, league-average TS was 57.4%.

30 minutes, 13 points, 5 rebounds, 4 assists, 2 steals, 1 foul, 6-for-18 shooting, 0-for-3 threes, 1-for-2 free throws, 34.4% TS, -25

Lendeborg has spent much of his rookie year looking like he can be a co-star next to Curry, and perhaps even the face of the franchise when Curry decides to hang up his sneakers. But these three games since Curry has been sidelined have been a rude awakening for him that there's still a lot of work to be done before he gets there … which is as it should be for a player who has been in the NBA for less than a month.

Being the focal point of the offense has been difficult for Yaxel, who has shot 16-for-54 over those three games. His jump shot has looked really rough lately — perhaps a little rushed? — and after opening the season 27-for-61 from deep, he's gone just 4-for-21 over his last four contests, with some ugly misses. He did a good job attacking the rim in this game when his shot wasn't falling, but he only shot 6-for-15 inside the arc, and only earned one trip to the free throw line.

It wasn't all bad, though. Despite being the first option, he played more than 30 minutes without a turnover, which is extremely impressive. He had a great stretch at the end of the first quarter and beginning of the second where he was the best player on the court; it was far and away his best run since Curry got injured.

But ultimately, it was a game that highlighted what Lendeborg needs to improve on more than what he currently excels at, and while he worked hard on defense, the results weren't very good there, either.

23 minutes, 3 points, 1 rebound, 2 assists, 1 steal, 2 blocks, 1 turnover, 1 foul, 1-for-1 shooting, 1-for-1 threes, 150.0% TS, -28

I forgot that Green was on the Warriors for much of this game. That isn't entirely a bad thing, but it's certainly not a good thing, either. At this stage in his career, he struggles to make an impact when Curry isn't on the court, though he had some really nice defensive plays.

22 minutes, 10 points, 8 rebounds, 1 steal, 1 block, 4 turnovers, 3 fouls, 5-for-8 shooting, 0-for-2 free throws, 56.3% TS, -27

This was a game where Bassey really showed why the Warriors love him, and why he can be such a big asset for them, especially in Kristaps Porziņģis' absence. It was also a game that served as a sharp reminder as to Bassey's rawness. He did some great things in the paint, gobbling up rebounds, impacting Dallas shots, and scoring with both finesse and force. But he also was out of position on a handful of occasions on both ends of the court, and committed four turnovers … that's a number the Warriors just can't have from a low-usage center.

Grade: CPost-game bonus: Tied for the team lead in rebounds.

29 minutes, 10 points, 3 rebounds, 4 assists, 3 fouls, 5-for-13 shooting, 0-for-5 threes, 0-for-2 free throws, 36.0% TS, -32

Like Lendeborg, Podziemski has really struggled to step into a bigger role since Curry went down with an oblique injury. He just looked off on offense all game long. He couldn't find a rhythm, couldn't get easy shots, and couldn't get anything going to the rim unless it was heavily congested and contested. Just as with Lendeborg, it was impressive that Podziemski didn't turn the ball over, despite being more of a focal point. But beyond that, it's hard to find any bright spots in this game.

Grade: DPost-game bonus: Worst plus/minus on the team.

19 minutes, 9 points, 2 assists, 2 fouls, 3-for-8 shooting, 3-for-7 threes, 56.3% TS, -21

I learned in this game, from the ever-enthusiastic Bob Fitzgerald, that Cryer's brother Justin is a linebacker at the University of Texas. As a Longhorns fan, learning that tidbit was one of the only bright spots in this game for me. Cryer didn't play particularly poorly, but he just didn't do much in this one. He was very reliant on his three, which he made at a respectable clip, but it kind of came at the expense of him getting anything else going on offense.

Still, given that he's a two-way contract stepping into a starting role, you can't be upset with his performance.

15 minutes, 2 points, 2 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 3 blocks, 3 turnovers, 4 fouls, 0-for-4 shooting, 0-for-2 threes, 2-for-2 free throws, 20.5% TS, +3

Ike got a healthy dose of action in this game, including some run pretty early in the first quarter. The undrafted rookie is starting to earn a spot in the rotation when Al Horford is out of the lineup, and he deserves it.

As with Bassey, Ike showed why Steve Kerr and the coaching staff are so high on him … and also reminded you that he entered this game with only 104 NBA minutes to his name. The athleticism, instincts, and defense (three blocks in 15 minutes!) were incredibly impressive. The shot selection, turnovers, and fouls, on the other hand, were fairly humbling. All things considered, Ike doesn't yet look like a player who can help the Warriors win games, but he certainly looks like someone who should be able to in the not-too-distant future.

19 minutes, 0 points, 2 rebounds, 2 assists, 3 steals, 4 turnovers, 2 fouls, 0-for-3 shooting, 0-for-3 threes, 0.0% TS, -8

No need to spend much time here. Melton had a really, really, really poor game, and I expect him to shake it off and play better on Thursday against the Memphis Grizzlies. The Warriors need him to.

14 minutes, 11 points, 1 rebound, 1 assist, 1 turnover, 2 fouls, 4-for-11 shooting, 2-for-8 threes, 1-for-1 free throws, 48.1% TS, -2

A very pedestrian game from Niang. He tried to help out the offense by shooting more than usual, and the results were quite mixed. His defense was fine, not particularly good or bad. He didn't really create much, but he didn't hurt the team either.

17 minutes, 6 points, 6 rebounds, 6 assists, 1 steal, 1 turnover, 2-for-6 shooting, 0-for-2 threes, 2-for-4 free throws, 38.7% TS, +6

Santos played this game like he thought he could single-handedly bring life to the Warriors through sheer effort and will power. And damn if he didn't almost do exactly that.

If everyone were playing as hard — and yet still in control — as Gui did, the Warriors would be in much better shape. He was positively everywhere on the court, and even had one of the highlights of the season, when he tossed a backwards, no-look pass from the floor at halfcourt for an assist. Just a great effort level that made things happen on both ends of the court.

Grade: A-Post-game bonus: Led the team in assists.

16 minutes, 8 points, 8 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 1 turnover, 1 foul, 3-for-4 shooting, 2-for-2 threes, 100.0% TS, +12

Just an exceptional game from Payton. He hasn't been getting as much run with Curry and Brandon Williams sidelined, since the Dubs need offense, but he was excellent in this game. Like Santos, he had an endless amount of energy, and it almost pulled the Dubs out of their lull. His defense was as we've come to expect, but he supplemented it with some huge offensive possessions, which included plays that the box score didn't capture. He was simply making things happen … and how can you argue with a one-to-two rebounds-to-minutes ratio for one of the smallest players on the court?

Grade: APost-game bonus: Tied for the team lead in rebounds; best plus/minus on the team.

27 minutes, 20 points, 3 assists, 4 steals, 2 turnovers, 9-for-13 shooting, 1-for-5 threes, 1-for-2 free throws, 72.1% TS, +8

The Warriors are struggling, but Richard didn't get the memo. He was far and away the best part of the game for Golden State, and had not only his best game of the season (by a long shot), but one of his best games ever. He was, quite simply, a defensive menace. Richard ended the game with four steals, but it felt like a lot more, partially because of the amount of plays he disrupted and passes he deflected, and partly because of how many times one of his steals led directly to an uncontested bucket. He's on the court for defense, and he did it better than anyone on either team tonight … that he dropped in a team-leading 20 points, on great efficiency, was just an added bonus. But one hell of an added bonus, I should specify.

That Richard is out there at all given the torn UCL in his thumb is noteworthy. That he's playing this well? It's absurd.

Grade: A+Post-game bonus: Led the team in points.

7 minutes, 2 points, 1 rebound, 2-for-2 free throws, 113.6% TS, +4

Some extended garbage time for Leons, who has been a beneficiary of all these blowout losses. He didn't really make an imprint in this game, for better and for worse.
