Week 11 Observations: Don’t Despair

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

I waited all day for Sunday night. I should have finished raking the leaves.

The Minnesota Vikings played poorly in every aspect of the game and took a well-deserved, convincing 35-10 thrashing at the hands of the San Francisco 49ers in Mexico City. With that, dreams of the coveted #1 seed and the bye are gone. The NFC North remains within reach. It's now about taking our medicine, learning from the lessons, and improving over the final seven games of the season.

With apologies to Ol' Blue Eyes, I will mention them.

IT WASN'T PURPLE BIAS, BUT I DROPPED THE BALL

In hindsight, my prediction of a close 27-24 victory was well-informed enough to avoid dismissing it as letting my wishes father my thoughts.

Kevin O'Connell was 2-0 vs. Kyle Shanahan as a head coach, and Shanahan was 0-5 when Brian Flores was on the opposing sideline. Our defense handled Brock Purdy well enough in previous meetings that I didn't expect the near-perfect performance we saw Sunday night.

Looking back to the second half of last season, the defense held its own against high-powered NFL offenses, including several dominant performances. This year, we're coming off games in which we held a Lions team averaging over 30 points to just 19 at home. We did about as well as we could have against the Bills, given that we didn't have a functional offense and that it felt like the defense was on the field for 50 minutes.

The 49ers just came out of a game in which they allowed 40 points to the Cowboys. In previous outings, they gave up 29 to the Seahawks, 29 to the Falcons, and 30 to the…Cardinals (?!). They haven't been able to get the quarterback all season. It was not unreasonable to expect 27 points while holding the 49ers to 21-24.

I SHOULD HAVE PUT MORE STOCK IN KOC'S PRIMETIME ISSUES

Yeah, I should have taken a more skeptical "I'll believe it when I see it" approach here. Doing so would have made me more open to this kind of disastrous outcome. My bad.

While I've already seen the predictable, tired "Kirk Cousins in primetime" social media posts after this one, the record actually shows he's 10-6 in those games since 2020. This includes a 5-1 record as a member of the Atlanta Falcons when the lights were brightest.

Yes, I get it. A team plays in primetime because the competition is stronger. The NFL doesn't plan to showcase many 49ers vs. Titans games to a nationwide audience when it builds out the schedule in the spring. But it's no longer debatable: Kevin O'Connell's approach to these high-profile games must change. Enough is enough.

I should have been on guard for a letdown, given the emotional high from last week's win over the Packers in Cousins' return. It felt like the week after we won that exhilarating OT game in Buffalo in 2022. We were the talk of the shows on ESPN/FS1/national sports talk radio: "What if Vikings are for real?! Look out, NFC!" Then what happened the following week? A 40-3 drubbing at home by the Dallas Cowboys.

A similar dynamic unfolded this week after Cousins returned from a four-day break and led the Vikings to a win in Green Bay: "The Vikings are a real threat in the NFC now! They have an offense that can complement the defense!" A 25-point loss later, all the previous excitement was wasted in record time and quickly forgotten, replaced by the familiar feeling of: "Here we go again."

The worst part? We didn't even have to win! If we had lost in the final seconds on a FG or even a late touchdown, this week's feelings would have been completely different. Not being competitive is the only thing that could have thrown a wet blanket over it all. KOC has way too many wet blankets at his disposal, and it's now become an issue that's impossible to hide or explain away.

I DIDN'T TAKE MY OWN ADVICE ABOUT COUSINS

Last week, I cautioned against thinking Cousins would come in here and pick up where he left off in 2023. It would take a few weeks, and the idea was that what mattered was that the offense was rounding into shape as we got into December and really took off in early January heading into the playoffs. I should have listened to that incredibly insightful, learned, and handsome gentleman.

There's a reason you have OTAs, minicamps, and training camps. Familiarity with the scheme or not, reps matter. Chemistry with your receivers matters. The timing you develop and perfect all offseason matters. Not every receiver breaks into routes at the same speed, depth, or angle. A few inches off in tight coverage can turn a clean reception into an interception. Cousins didn't have any of that to work with, and it showed against the 49ers. His chemistry with Tai Felton looked off all night. And the split second longer it took him in the pocket probably cost us a touchdown when T.J. Hockenson broke free wide open in the end zone. He hesitated, and the ball was tipped, or he was hit as he threw, causing the pass to sail short.

If you had swapped this game's timing with the Jets on the Week 17 schedule, could it have been different? Maybe. That's six more games and weeks of practice. The Cousins from last week in Green Bay would likely have been good enough against the Jets on Sunday. Against the 49ers? Nope. But we played the 49ers when we played them, and I should have listened to my own advice.

Listen, I'm as jaded as the next diehard Vikings fan. I'm old enough to wear the scars from nearly every heartbreak and disappointment of the post-Bud Grant era. That said, some of the dread and doom I read in the game and postgame DN threads were even too much for me. And it wasn't confined to those threads. People are ready to proclaim the Kevin O'Connell era over, at 7-3, with seven games to go.

I've always said it. This is an enormous season for KOC. He needs postseason success, or his seat will get hot at ludicrous speed. History wouldn't be on his side if he reached a Super Bowl for the first time after his fifth season with the same team. Since 1990, there have been just two: Andy Reid with the Eagles and Jeff Fisher with the Oilers/Titans. That's it.

The 2026 quarterback room disaster and the fact that we needed to parachute Kirk Cousins in midseason to save us are totally on KOC. Who else shoulders the blame? Oh, I see J.J. McCarthy beat the 7-2 Jaguars. Wonderful. Fantastic news. Sarcasm is my own.

But it's not even December, and we're 7-3. Look at how the narrative shifted for this team…in one week. There's still a lot of football left. Let's hold off on the dire pronouncements for now.

Don't despair. This isn't 2022 or even 2024. The defense stunk in 2022. We're two years further in, with a level of familiarity with Flores' scheme we didn't have in 2024. We haven't yet seen Jordan Addison's impact with Cousins back.

I truly believe that (for now). Let's have hope and optimism. Oh, and don't forget perspective. They're all our friends.

Unless you think the most talented offensive minds in the world just figured out that the quick passing game is an effective counter to the blitz, that a light just went on in Kyle Shanahan's head, or that Brian Flores forgot how to coach defense, well, that's fine. But count me out.

ESPN's Ben Solak had a great video explaining how condensed formations and the 49ers' unique ability to use them exploited vulnerabilities in the Vikings' scheme, especially in outside leverage in coverage.

It was the perfect storm of Shanahan scheming, brilliant game-calling, and Brock Purdy playing the game of his life. Altitude issues and the absence of Isaiah Rodgers didn't help. But make no mistake: the 49ers, generally, and Purdy, specifically, ate our lunch. All the credit to them.

But let's not proclaim Flores' defense "solved" yet, shall we? Do you really think Flores won't make the necessary adjustments?

The 1985 Bears gave up 38 points in a game. The 2000 Ravens, 36. The 2013 Seahawks, 34. The 2015 Broncos, 34. If it can happen to them, it can happen to us. Better now than in the playoffs.

While the #1 seed is likely out of reach, we control our destiny for the NFC North crown and the #2 seed.

"It ain't about how hard you hit. It's about how hard you can get hit and keep moving forward. How much you can take and keep moving forward." – Rocky Balboa

That quote is perfect in the context of both Sunday's humiliation by the 49ers and the experience of being a long-suffering fan of this team. As a fan, I choose to keep moving forward. Let's hope the Vikings choose to come along for the ride.

Wishing everyone a happy and safe Thanksgiving.
