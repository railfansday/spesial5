Golf Pride Manager Reveals Tour Pros Swap Grips Every Two Weeks

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

PGA Tour players swap their grips as often as every two weeks or as rarely as every couple of months, according to MyGolfSpy. This timeline depends entirely on the specific player and the type of grip they choose to use on their clubs.

Brett Zollman, the Tour Manager for Golf Pride, shared these details while working the RSM Classic tournament grounds. His job involves moving from one equipment manufacturer's truck to another to ensure every player has the exact equipment they need for the week.

According to reports, players using traditional round grips tend to change them more frequently than others. These golfers often feel a need to swap out their grips to find a fresh feel or address wear that happens over just a few rounds of play. The frequent change is due to the way the hands interact with the club during a swing. Without a raised ridge to lock the hands into place, players might shift their grip slightly between shots or rounds. A new grip offers a consistent surface that helps them reset their feel quickly.

Golfers who choose grips with an extra rib down the back, like the Align models, change them much less often. Zollman noted that these players focus on getting the club into a perfect spot in their hands and staying there. Once a player finds that sweet spot with a ribbed grip, the raised ridge helps them locate the same hand position every single time they grab the club. This consistency removes the need for frequent swaps even as the grip wears down slightly.

This year, Golf Pride broadened its Align Max range to offer golfers increased options regarding size and feel. The new lineup includes a ZGRIP version that stands as the firmest option available to pros on the tour. Players can now select from midsize options and a jumbo version for the MCC Plus4 to match their specific hand size and strength.

The demand for specific grips varies wildly depending on the time of year and the players in the field. Zollman often hunts through stock on the trucks to find quirky styles or specific colors that players refuse to play without.

While some teams use vintage styles that are extremely difficult to locate, others insist on specific colors for their equipment. Standard grips are easy to find, but the unique ones require a dedicated search by the tour manager. The Tour Velvet remains the most popular grip choice among the professionals Zollman works with every week.

According to reports, when athletes are performing successfully, they occasionally prefer to leave their grips untouched. A hot streak can freeze equipment changes because the player trusts the current feel completely.

Players with traditional round grips swap more often than those with ribbed models.
