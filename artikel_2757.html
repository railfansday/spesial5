Simsbury girls soccer returns to Class L championship game behind Addison Girard's two goals

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

NEW BRITAIN - Addison Girard remembers what it was like to start in a CIAC girls soccer championship game as a freshman. She remembers what it was like to win the final game of a long, grueling season.

"I remember the atmosphere. There were so many people there," Girard recalled. "We all feel like we wanted it. I feel like this team has that same drive that we did in 2023."

Girard and several of her classmates will get the chance to win the final game of their careers this weekend. She scored twice to help top seed Simsbury defeat No. 4 North Haven 3-0 Wednesday night in the CIAC Class L semifinals at Veterans Stadium.

In three Class L games, Simsbury has yet to allow a goal.

"Ultimately, defense is going to win you the championship," Simsbury coach Steve Jarvis said. "Players are going to score the goals to win you the games, but if you're solid defensively and you get that habit, yeah, you want zeroes."

Jarvis said not only did Girard start three seasons ago, but so did midfielder Charlotte Diviney and defender Kylie Richie. All told, seven players on the current roster were on the varsity team in 2023. Simsbury defeated SWC power Brookfield in overtime.

The Trojans will have to get through another SWC team - No. 2 seed Pomperaug - in this weekend's final at Trinity Health Stadium. Pomperaug, which beat Wethersfield 1-0 in the other semifinal, has allowed just one goal in the tournament.

Simsbury (20-1-0) has been one of the top teams all season and is sixth in the GametimeCT power rankings. It went undefeated during the regular season.

The Trojans' only loss came against Farmington 1-0 in the CCC tournament semifinals.

"I'm glad that we lost when we did, as bad as that sounds," Girard said. "We needed to be humbled because we hadn't lost yet. That loss came at the perfect time. It obviously sucked, but I think we used it to do what we've done so far in the state tournament, using it to our advantage. It's given us a lot of motivation. We don't like losing."

Girard, who will play at Fairfield University next season, suffered what she called a "dead leg" in the first half, which she said was "when you get hit really hard in the quad and then it bruises and it hurts the muscle." She played almost the entire second half and said she would be fine for the final.

"She's a generational talent, once in a lifetime," Jarvis said. "For her to be playing at the high school level for a public school is just unreal, so blessed."

North Haven's seniors, led by Olivia Ranciato, Leila Sweet, Ava Tarasuk and Addison Whitcomb, have guided the Nighthawks through consecutive successful regular seasons and deep Class L state tournament runs.

Last year, North Haven reached its first final, falling to Masuk. This year, the Nighthawks made another run to the semifinals, where they ran into one of the state's best squads.

"We had a lot of goal scorers in this group," North Haven coach Gary Collins said. "I think the five captains all put a lot of work into it. They are disappointed the way it ended for them, but have to hold their heads up."
