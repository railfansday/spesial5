Paul George is winning over Celtics fans and making Brad Stevens look goodCeltics fans and making Brad Stevens look good | Matt Vautour

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

As Brad Stevens watched the Celtics' 123-101 win over the Sixers on Tuesday, he had to be tempted to gloat.

While all the pregame hype surrounding the game centered around the Jayson Tatum-Jaylen Brown drama, Stevens had a stake in a different matchup.

Fans and media mocked him when he called the trade that sent Brown to Philadelphia for Paul George and draft picks a "basketball trade." Brown was an MVP candidate and George was an oft-injured former star.

The presumption was that the move was made for financial reasons or to alleviate chemistry issues between Brown and Tatum. And while those things might have been true to some degree, the basketball results of that trade have looked good so far.

Twelve games into the season, Brown and George have similar numbers across the stat sheet. And on Tuesday, George outplayed Brown by a wide margin. If he stays healthy, George looks like a very good short-term fit.

George played in just 41 games for Philadelphia in 2024-25 and 37 in 2025-26. Being healthy right now doesn't guarantee anything later on. But there's been a lot to like about George so far on the court and off it.

The 38-year-old veteran has carried himself with a lot of wisdom and perspective. George understood fans would be reluctant to warm up to him at first because he was the living embodiment of the Jaylen Brown trade, which was understandably unpopular with Celtics fans.

"Listen, I understand the circumstances," George said. "I understand how huge JB was not only for the organization but here in the city. I didn't take it personally. To be honest, they should have a relationship emotionally to that trade," he said and later added. "Again, no personal feelings towards how it was received here in Boston. To be honest, it's fair. But just one foot in front of the other. Hopefully with how we start this year, they understand this is a new team, something that they can be proud of."

He's proven prophetic. It is a new team and they've played in a way the fans can get behind. And they weren't the only ones George was angling to make a useful impression on.

At the same press conference, he discussed his desire to be a valuable No. 2. George made it clear Tatum is the Celtics' superstar. He's repeated a version of that many times, including after Tuesday's win.

"My job was just to come in and support JT and the best version that I could for him," George said after scoring a game-high 36 points against the Sixers.

Does Tatum's ego need that sort of deference? Maybe? Maybe not. It doesn't matter. Either way, there's no downside to that approach from George.

He can say whatever he wants, but on the court, he looks a lot like the two-way standout that made him a nine-time All-Star. George has led Boston in scoring four times already. He's both a willing and capable defender.

The challenge now is to have him healthy when the playoffs arrive. So far, it looks like the Celtics have had an effective plan.

They've kept his minutes at 30 in every game they've won comfortably. In close games, they've leaned on him more.

George played in both games of an October back-to-back when both were at home. But after beating the Sixers on Tuesday, the Celtics played at Toronto on Wednesday and George sat out.

Officially, he was listed as having an illness. But even if he felt great, the Celtics should have considered giving him the night off anyway. It's just smart asset management. Occasional load management days off could be extremely beneficial later in the season.

So far he's felt good and has been energized by his situation.

"The most fun is just the camaraderie, being around these guys, the locker room, the flights. That's the most fun that I've had in a while," he said. "It's just a good group of guys out there competing together. It transfers on the court. You see how much fun we have off the court by how we play on the court."

In addition to seeming cowardly, what Connor Hellebuyck did — sitting out his return to Winnipeg — was simply bad for hockey.

Hellebuyck, who once said he wanted to be a Jet for life, demanded a trade and got his wish. Winnipeg sent him to the Devils. Feeling jilted, Jets fans gobbled up tickets to their team's Tuesday night game, hoping to see him come back with New Jersey.

But while Hellebuyck did come back, he sat out the game.

In a sport where players put great emphasis on toughness, honor and playing the game the right way, Hellebuyck looks awful. The Jets said he wanted to play and it was their call not to let him, to protect him from being overworked this early in a season he started late.

That's hard to believe. Even if it's true, it's not like this game just popped up on their schedule. They could have sat him out the game before.

The Devils are telling their own fans, in New Jersey of all places, that their goalie needs to be shielded from people yelling mean stuff. That's awfully soft and antithetical to Jersey's proud attitude.

Most importantly, it's bad for hockey. Hellebuyck forcing his way out was a bad look and returning to Winnipeg was a big game. It's one that people outside of Manitoba and Jersey would have tuned in for to see how he handled it.

Brady Tkachuk returned to Ottawa under similar circumstances and ate up being the villain. Hellebuyck should have done the same thing.
