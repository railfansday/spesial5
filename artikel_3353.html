Enabling the macOS Firewall (which is off by default) adds a layer of protection against external devices on your wifi network, as well as unauthorized apps from initiating a connection to your device. Turning on Stealth Mode prevents your Mac from even responding to these requests, essentially making it invisible to anything that's scanning a network you're connected to. This makes it more difficult for hackers and malware to know your device exists, much less attack it. This can interfere with certain essential activity when you're on your home network, but it's a valuable security setting when on public wifi. You can leave Firewall on all the time and enable Stealth Mode only as needed. Go to Settings > Network > Firewall, flip the toggle, then hit Options to manage Stealth Mode.

Images and screenshots contain metadata, such as timestamps, device and hardware details, and, in the case of photos, physical location. Much of this information is stripped automatically when you share files to social media or via encrypted messaging apps, but if you're sending them via text or email, posting to forums, or sharing over AirDrop, metadata remains intact. You can easily remove metadata from your images right on your desktop. Select the file and hit control + click to bring up an options menu, then go to Quick Actions > Convert Image. Uncheck the box next to Preserve Metadata, then hit Convert. (This is also useful for quickly converting HEIC to JPEG without opening a separate app.)

If you're using iCloud to store photos, files, and device backups, your data is encrypted on Apple's servers and in transit. Apple still holds the decryption key, which is useful if you somehow get locked out of your account and need help recovering it, but for sensitive data—including device backups—end-to-end encryption is the most secure way to ensure no one but you can gain access. Advanced Data Protection is an opt-in setting for iCloud that encrypts your information end to end. You'll need to set up an alternative recovery method, such as your device passcode with two-factor authentication, a recovery contact, or a recovery key. Be careful, though: If you lose access to this recovery method, you will lose your data. You can enable Advanced Data Protection on your Mac under System Settings > [Your Name] > iCloud > Advanced Data Protection > Turn On. Note that some iCloud data, including passwords and payment methods, are already end-to-end encrypted by default.
