5 Greatest Thanksgiving Episodes of '80s Sitcoms, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Thanksgiving is just around the corner, and what better way to get into the holiday spirit than with a few classic Thanksgiving episodes? The 2000s had How I Met Your Mother's Thanksgiving mishaps, while the '90s gave us the iconic turkey-on-the-head disaster in Friends. But the '80s had their fair share of memorable holiday moments, too.

There's something nostalgic about sitcoms from that decade, when Thanksgiving often meant a big family dinner, plenty of laughs, and a little chaos along the way. Of course, things rarely go smoothly in sitcom households, and these episodes are no exception. From an unexpected food fight to spending the holiday in jail, here are the greatest Thanksgiving episodes from '80s sitcoms, ranked.

Thanksgiving at the Huxtable household gets off to a chaotic start when bad weather delays travel plans, prompting Denise (Lisa Bonet) to invite Paula (Victoria Rowell), her husband Martin's (Joseph C. Phillips) ex-wife, to join the family for dinner. Meanwhile, Clair (Phylicia Rashad) keeps sending Cliff (Bill Cosby) into the pouring rain to pick up forgotten ingredients, including canned pumpkin, fresh nutmeg, eggs, and tomatoes. Each trip leaves him more soaked and miserable than the last, while the family tries to navigate the awkwardness of having Paula at the dinner table.

The Cosby Show makes great use of its ensemble cast here, giving nearly every family member a moment to shine. Cliff's increasingly miserable grocery runs provide most of the laughs, but there's plenty happening back home, too. Vanessa complains about sitting at the kids' table, Sondra (Sabrina Le Beauf) and Elvin (Geoffrey Owens) get into a little spat, and Olivia (Raven-Symoné) steals the show with her adorable Thanksgiving song. The episode also gives Paula more depth than the typical sitcom ex-wife, allowing her to bond with Denise over their shared connection to Olivia. It's a nice change from the usual drama surrounding former spouses.

Thanksgiving in Family Ties doesn't go as planned when Steven (Michael Gross) and Elyse (Meredith Baxter) decide to attend an anti-nuclear weapons protest instead of celebrating with the family. Their kids aren't happy about it, especially Alex (Michael J. Fox), whose conservative views often clash with his parents' activism. Things get more complicated when Elyse's parents arrive for the holiday, only to find out that they have been arrested at the demonstration. The couple refuse to sign a waiver promising not to protest again, so they end up spending Thanksgiving in jail. Rather than leave them there, the rest of the family brings Thanksgiving dinner to the jailhouse so they can celebrate together.

The episode is at its funniest when the Keatons' very different personalities start clashing. Alex is embarrassed by his parents' activism, while Charlie isn't exactly thrilled that their arrest has thrown his holiday plans off track. Kate is more sympathetic, though, and isn't afraid to defend Steven and Elyse when Charlie gets annoyed with them. It's a fun family dynamic that keeps the episode from feeling too heavy, even when the disagreements get heated. And instead of wrapping things up with everyone suddenly agreeing, the episode lets the family remain divided on certain issues. They don't have to share the same opinions to put those differences aside and enjoy Thanksgiving together.

Thanksgiving at the Conner household gets messy when both sides of the family gather for dinner. Roseanne (Roseanne Barr) is already exhausted from preparing the feast when her parents, Beverly (Estelle Parsons) and Al (John Randolph), and Dan's (John Goodman) parents arrive. Meanwhile, Jackie (Laurie Metcalf) reveals that she's joined the police force, but Beverly is far from supportive of her decision. Things get even more uncomfortable when Dan's father, Ed (Ned Beatty), makes advances toward Crystal (Natalie West), prompting Dan to confront him. As tensions rise around the dinner table, Roseanne makes one final decision: her parents will be spending the night at a motel rather than taking over her house.

Thanksgiving is supposed to be about appreciating loved ones, but Roseanne flips the script by showing just how exhausting family can be. Beverly's reaction to Jackie's career choice exposes her lack of trust in her daughter, while Dan's confrontation with Ed over his behavior toward Crystal brings another uncomfortable family feud to the dinner table. Still, Roseanne's decision to send her parents to a motel is easily the episode's funniest running gag. After getting up at five in the morning to prepare dinner, she has absolutely no interest in putting up with Beverly's criticism for another night. Honestly, anybody could sympathize with her decision.

The Tanners are spending their first Full House Thanksgiving without Pam, and Danny (Bob Saget) wants to make sure his daughters still have a special holiday. When his mother gets stranded in Tacoma due to bad weather, Danny, Jesse (John Stamos), and Joey (Dave Coulier) are left to handle Thanksgiving dinner themselves. With D.J. (Candace Cameron Bure) and Stephanie (Jodie Sweetin) helping out, the family tries to keep Pam's holiday traditions alive—unfortunately, not to much success. Amid the kitchen chaos, Danny and Jesse also struggle with how much they miss Pam, giving the episode a bittersweet touch.

Grief doesn't take a holiday, and celebrating Thanksgiving without someone you love can make an already difficult time of year even harder. That's what makes Danny's determination to give his daughters a happy holiday so touching. Thankfully, the episode knows when to lighten the mood, balancing its emotional moments with plenty of classic Full House shenanigans. Between the frozen turkey, the ruined pumpkin pie, and Joey getting his tongue stuck in a bottle, there's never a dull moment in the kitchen. But beneath all the laughs is a sweet reminder that Pam's memory is still very much part of the family. The Tanners may never get back the Thanksgivings they shared with her, but they can still make new memories together.

Thanksgiving isn't looking particularly festive for the Cheers gang, as everyone struggles to find somewhere to spend the holiday. When Diane (Shelly Long) discovers she's been invited to her professor's Thanksgiving party as domestic help, she opts to attend Carla's (Rhea Perlman) potluck dinner instead. With the entire gang gathered around the table, things quickly go awry when Carla's turkey refuses to cook, and tempers begin to flare. The next thing they know, the gathering soon descends into an all-out food fight.

There's no better company for the holidays than your dysfunctional friends from the bar. Cheers shows that family doesn't have to be bound by blood. Sometimes, it's simply the people who choose to spend the day together. Most of the sitcom takes place inside the Boston bar, so moving the action to Carla's apartment gives the gang a more intimate setting to let their guards down. Of course, they're still an imperfect bunch, and even the pettiest disagreements can spiral into something ridiculous. The result is one of the most memorable Thanksgiving scenes of the '80s, completely throwing off the picture-perfect idea of what the holiday is supposed to look like (admittedly, the food fight did attract some criticism, given that the episode aired during the Stop World Hunger campaign).
