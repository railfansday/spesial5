Leisure carrier Allegiant is growing again, this time teaming up with merger partner Sun Country to grow its network.

The Las Vegas-based budget airline announced 11 new routes on Tuesday, with the service split between Allegiant and Sun Country.

Additionally, five routes will switch from one airline to the other — a tangible sign of the integration that’s slowly getting underway since the acquisition closed earlier this year.

The big winner in Tuesday’s announcement: Gulf Shores, Alabama.

The Gulf Coast destination — which Allegiant began flying to in 2025 — landed four new routes with Allegiant as well as one new route with Sun Country.

It’s a remarkable ramp for the Gulf Shores International Airport at Jack Edwards Field (GUF), which now has nonstop flights to 17 destinations, according to the airport’s website. The airport had no regular airline service at all prior to Allegiant’s arrival.

When Sun Country’s new service to Minneapolis-Saint Paul International Airport (MSP) begins April 16, it will mark the first time an airline other than Allegiant has scheduled service from the airport.

Elsewhere, Allegiant added two new Florida routes from the mid-Atlantic: one each from Philadelphia and Washington, D.C.

Sun Country will add nonstop service from O’Hare International Airport (ORD) to Asheville, North Carolina, and Knoxville, Tennessee — two destinations near the Great Smoky Mountains National Park that are already served by Allegiant.

Beyond the new routes, Allegiant is poised to take over service on four routes currently operated by Sun Country. Meanwhile, Sun Country will assume Allegiant’s Cincinnati-Denver route on May 27.

The changes will effectively add three new destinations to Sun Country's network: Belleville, Illinois, near St. Louis; Gulf Shores, Alabama; and Knoxville, Tennessee. Meanwhile, MSP — Sun Country's home base and main hub — will join Allegiant's route map.

"Minnesota is an essential part of the future of our combined company, and today's announcement is another step in delivering on that commitment," Allegiant CEO Greg Anderson said in a statement. "MSP is our largest base of operations by a wide margin, and we see opportunities for continued growth there."

Allegiant and Sun Country will continue to operate as separate brands at least into 2028 “or later,” Allegiant said in the press release. It will eventually move to a single brand.

For now, however, Allegiant touted Tuesday’s new service announcement as a preview of the expanded network that customers of the combined carrier will eventually be able to access.

"As we planned for summer 2027, our focus was on creating more opportunities for travelers by leveraging the complementary strengths of each airline's network while continuing to operate separately," Drew Wells, Allegiant's chief commercial officer, said in the company's statement.

“Just as importantly, these routes are a first look at how we'll create more options, access and value for our customers in the long term,” he added.
