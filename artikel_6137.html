SFO’s Airfield Will Flood Amid Thanksgiving Rush With 9-Foot Tides Predicted, Officials Say

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

San Francisco International Airport (SFO) is facing a significant coastal flooding threat during one of the busiest travel periods of the year, with officials warning that exceptionally high tides could overwhelm vulnerable sections of its airfield. The National Weather Service (NWS) is forecasting water levels approaching nearly 10 feet or potentially higher around San Francisco Bay between Tuesday and Thursday, coinciding with the Thanksgiving travel rush.

Unusually high king tides, storm surge, warmer ocean temperatures, and an approaching winter storm are raising concerns about the airport's ability to keep its airfield and surrounding infrastructure clear of seawater. Although SFO is preparing for potential flooding, the situation could create additional complications for passengers, airlines, and airport operations during the holiday period.

According to the Los Angeles Times, both SFO and Oakland San Francisco Bay Airport (OAK) are facing potential flooding as exceptionally high tides approach Northern California. NWS meteorologists warned of extreme coastal flooding along the San Francisco Bay shoreline, with water levels potentially exceeding nine feet depending on the storm surge. The forecast is concerning because SFO's seawalls are not uniformly high, leaving certain sections more vulnerable to overtopping.

According to Deep Arrival, NWS also warned that water levels along SFO's shoreline could reach approximately 9.9 feet (three meters) during the most concerning periods. The airport's seawall stretches for more than seven miles, but some vulnerable sections are reportedly only 8 feet high. The southeastern corner near Runway 28 Left is particularly exposed, while the northern ends of both runways also feature lower sections. It remains unclear whether the runways or taxiways themselves will experience flooding.

The airport is already preparing for the possibility of seawater entering its airfield. SFO spokesperson Doug Yakel said the airport was prepared to respond and maintain safe operations, according to Deep Arrival. Crews are clearing drainage areas, inspecting pump stations, and positioning sandbags and emergency flood barriers.

The flooding threat is being driven by several overlapping weather and astronomical factors. The NWS explains that unusually high king tides, thermal expansion from warmer ocean temperatures, and storm surge are combining to push bay water levels toward historic territory. According to its forecast discussion on Sunday, major to extreme coastal flooding is expected, with an impactful storm forecast to affect the region on Tuesday and Wednesday.

The NWS predicts the San Francisco tide gauge could reach 8.4 feet on Tuesday, and 8.6 feet (2.6 meters) on Wednesday, above mean lower low water. Thursday's predicted level is 8.2 feet (2.5 meters). These figures include the forecast tidal conditions, with additional storm-related water potentially worsening the situation. The previous record at San Francisco's Crissy Field gauge was 8.66 feet (2.63 meters), recorded in January 1983, according to the Los Angeles Times.

The implications extend beyond airport infrastructure. Highway 101, an important access route for SFO passengers, could also experience flooding, potentially making it difficult for travelers and airport employees to reach the terminals. San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie has urged residents to avoid unnecessary journeys, particularly between Monday afternoon and Wednesday. The city is also suspending parking meter and time-limit enforcement during that period to help residents move their vehicles away from vulnerable areas, according to Deep Arrival.

The airport was the epicenter of a tremor less than two years ago.

The timing could hardly be worse for the airport, with Thanksgiving traditionally generating substantial domestic travel demand across the US. Any flooding affecting SFO's runways, taxiways, or surrounding access roads could complicate an already busy operating environment. However, officials have not confirmed that flight cancellations or runway closures will be necessary, and the airport is continuing its preparations to maintain safe operations.

OAK nearby is also monitoring the situation, although its infrastructure appears better positioned to withstand the predicted water levels. Deep Arrival reports that the airport has raised its perimeter dike to 13 feet (four meters), reaching 17 feet (five meters) in certain locations, and does not currently expect flooding. Meanwhile, the NWS anticipates drier conditions on Thanksgiving Day itself before another storm system potentially arrives during the following weekend.

Officials are urging passengers to monitor airline notifications and airport updates. The greatest concern is around late-morning high tides on Tuesday and Wednesday, when storm surge could push water beyond vulnerable seawall sections. Although flooding remains a forecast risk, not a confirmed operational disruption, the combination of high water levels and Thanksgiving passenger volumes presents a serious challenge for the airport and could disrupt travelers.
