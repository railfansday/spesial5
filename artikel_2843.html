‘What's different? My confidence': Joe Milton used bye week to master offense

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

'What's different? My confidence': Joe Milton used bye week to master offense originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

The bye week gave Joe Milton a chance to do stuff he really had to do.

Milton made his first NFL start after just 23 days and 10 practices with the Eagles and it went about as expected in a 19-11 loss to the Giants, the Eagles' seventh straight loss.

Two weeks later, Milton feels like a different man.

Thanks to the bye week, he's had two weeks to devote to learning an offense that he just didn't have time to learn before his first start.

It's still not a lot of time, but 37 days is a lot different than 23 days.

"What's different for me now? My confidence," Milton said after the Eagles returned to practice Wednesday. "Just confidence in knowing this offense and what these coaches and my teammates around me are instilling in me and talking to me about.

"And just my reason to ask some questions. I'm great at asking questions, and I ask Parks (Frazier) and Sean (Mannion) a lot of questions. And I don't think I can ask (more) questions than I already do. I think I do a great job at that. So just yeah, just my confidence for sure.

"I feel more comfortable than I did last week for sure. Just the verbiage and everything that we needed to do … to solve the problem and fix it."

Milton wasn't awful in the Giants loss considering the circumstances. He didn't have DeVonta Smith or Dontayvion Wicks and the offensive line was unable to protect him, on top of his lack of reps in Mannion's offense.

He'll make his second career start Sunday at the Linc against the Steelers, who are 6-3 but ranked 30th in pass defense, worse even than the Eagles.

For the record, the last Eagles quarterback to lose his first two career starts was Nick Foles in 2012.

For Milton, the bye week couldn't have come at a better time.

"It was very helpful just because I was able to communicate (with) the coaches (about) what we need to do better and what we need to get better at and where I need to be better and just echoing that and fixing the protections for myself. Learning it, studying it more, being able to pick up the blitzes whenever they show up, and just really just understanding the verbiage that we're pronouncing and saying here."

Milton completed 18 for 34 passes for 169 yards against the Giants with a long gainer of 20 yards, no touchdowns or interceptions. He also ran nine times for 64 yards and ran for a two-point conversion. The Eagles are last in the NFL in total offense and 30th in passing offense and scoring.

One thing Milton did during the bye was ask tons of questions. That's big for him, a good way for him to learn. He's big on questions.

"I ask questions every day," he said. "It could be the same question. I just want to make sure I heard correctly and I took down the right note. I'm big on writing notes. And my coaches do a great job of just like overly talking, just constantly mentioning the same thing. And I mean, you kind of hear it every day and all throughout the week, so it kind of gets stuck in your brain."

"Just what do I need to do on this play?" he said. "Like, what is what is the quarterback job specifically on this play? That goes for every play. Like, what do you exactly want me to do on this play? And if there's anything different, just tell me now."

Milton will have Smith and Wicks back Sunday, so that should help.

"Those two guys coming back obviously helps out the team," he said. "For me to throw to those guys today was amazing. I mean, shoot, I couldn't stop smiling for real if you ask me."

The Eagles haven't won a game in two months so there hasn't been a lot of smiling going on around the team facility.

A more settled, more prepared Milton could go a long way toward ending this ugly seven-game losing streak.

"I know what I need to know as far as this offense and whatever goes on in this offense," he said. "So I think I'm in the right place and the right time as far as where I'm at with this offense.

"It's not rocket science. Football is football, no matter where you go. It's all about X's and O's and just knowing football. Coverage is going to be the coverage, a play is going to be a play, and if it's a run game, just hand it off to the running back."
