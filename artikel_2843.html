32 grades on the 2026 NFL trade deadline: Who won, who lost and who did nothing?

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The NFL trade deadline went down at 4 p.m. ET on Tuesday. Here's a breakdown of the trades and grades. Yahoo Sports' Eric Edholm assessed the NFC teams and Frank Schwab tackled the AFC. Trade activity is noted from late August:

The Cowboys' big move came on Oct. 1 when they traded for CB Joey Porter Jr. for multiple picks. Looking back now, the move looks good from a value standpoint, as Jessie Bates III ended up being traded for more, and Porter has played pretty well for Dallas. The problem is that Porter has played in only four of Dallas' six games, and the Cowboys are still treading water. Team owner Jerry Jones hinted that the Cowboys were still big-game hunting in the trade market, but nothing else emerged.

The Giants have had a fascinating pre-deadline period, acquiring J.J. McCarthy for nickels on the dollar and potentially changing the team's landscape at the position. Jaxson Dart presumably remains the 2027 starter, but a strong finish from McCarthy could set up some interesting optics next offseason. They were sellers before the draft, trading Dexter Lawrence to Cincinnati, but the Giants were shopping right up until the deadline as NFC East leaders. They reportedly went in hard on Jeffery Simmons and made the Colts a strong offer on Jonathan Taylor, but nothing materialized.

It has been a surprising turn of events with the Eagles — often among the more aggressive teams around deadline time — turning into sellers. Philadelphia dealt A.J. Brown this offseason but also traded for multiple players, so that felt like a separate situation. The in-season loss of Jalen Hurts has precipitated some of this movement, but the Eagles were already in sell mode with the Cam Jurgens trade the week prior to Hurts being diagnosed with blood clots. Trading Davis brought back some potentially great returns. It's not how we imagined it going, but the Eagles were smart to pivot, especially Hurts' murky future.

The Commanders were reportedly open to the idea of trading DT Daron Payne, whose contract expires after this season, but he suffered a broken foot, thus killing off any deal. The same might have been true for Javon Kinlaw, who is also on IR. The team's salary

cap situation is fine, but the Commanders are underwater at 2-5-1 and could use the draft-pick help. They've made only 11 selections combined the past two drafts, with a roster that needs an influx of young talent.

DT Gervon Dexter Sr. (to Falcons, Aug. 30)

It's possible we look back at the Simmons trade as the most impactful of the 2026 cycle. In Simmons' first game as a Bear, he took over against the Seahawks, giving the team and its fans hope for another Super Bowl run. Two first-round picks is potentially a significant cost for a defensive tackle, but if the Bears finish closer to the top of the standings, those picks might be worth about the same as the No.10 overall selection the Bengals paid for Dexter Lawrence. The Simmons trade was aggressive and could help put the Bears over the top. What's not to like?

2028 sixth-round draft pick (to Jets, part of Andre Cisco deal on Nov. 9)

The Lions were reportedly one of the first teams to inquire about Joey Porter Jr., and when he went to Dallas, Lions fans were not too happy with general manager Brad Holmes. The Lions have added safety depth, acquiring Cisco from the Jets. Their defensive concerns likely remain, even with stronger play of late, but Holmes resisted making any big moves.

The Packers started the season 1-2 but have ripped off five wins in their past six games and are emerging as a sneaky title contender, especially with Micah Parsons on his way back. That likely will have to count Green Bay's big "acquisition" this season, although they did reportedly kick the tires on Jonathan Taylor amid Josh Jacobs' once-uncertain status. Do the Packers have enough to make a big run? We'll find out.

The Vikings shipped out McCarthy to the Giants, and it seemed to backfire initially with McCarthy showing early promise in New York and Carson Wentz struggling against the Bills in place of an injured Kyler Murray. That prompted the Vikings to bring Kirk Cousins back to Minnesota, where he played for head coach Kevin O'Connell previously, in a desperate attempt to provide offensive stability. The Vikings have a world-class defense, and it would be criminal not to give that unit a fighting chance to compete for a Super Bowl. We'll see how quickly Cousins can re-integrate into the system, but a third-round pick likely means they're serious about him starting quickly.

You can say the Falcons were technically in a divisional playoff race when they dumped Jessie Bates III for draft picks. On the other hand, you can also reasonably understand the thinking of the trade when you consider the terrific value Atlanta received and also that the Falcons likely were more fool's-gold contenders. The Panthers spent big on two defensive pieces this offseason, signing Jaelan Phillips and Devin Lloyd for big money, and they've mostly rested on their laurels since. The results have been underwhelming, and Carolina sits at 3-5. That's miraculously tied for first place. The Panthers are contenders, but they're not overextending themselves for a big run at the NFC South crown.

The Saints' first pre-deadline move was to ship out Godchaux to the Jaguars for a future Day 3 pick. It was not the kind of inspiring move some fans hoped for after a dramatic win over the Browns, keeping the Saints in the divisional hunt. Realistically, the Saints were always more likely to be sellers than buyers, with limited upside this season.

Like most NFC South teams, the Buccaneers had been more connected to selling than buying. Vita Vea's name popped up in some trade rumors in the past week, with the Giants reportedly among the teams interested, but nothing materialized after Vea signed a one-year, $30 million extension in August. And despite the Bucs needing some skill-position help on offense, they apparently were willing to wait until the offseason for any major additions.

The Cardinals sent Harrison to the Texans back on Oct. 12 in a move that all but admitted defeat on the pick. Since the trade, Harrison has caught six passes for 60 yards and a score, so perhaps Arizona did well getting what it could for a depreciating asset. The Jacoby Brissett trade was a no-brainer; there was no real reason to keep him around, getting two picks in return. This is a multi-year rebuild for a team still seeking several franchise pillars, so it's smart to continue stacking resources for the offseason without dealing off players such as Trey McBride.

The Rams stood pat at the deadline despite losing starting receivers Davante Adams and Puka Nacua to injuries. One reason is that the Rams made two major trades this offseason, landing Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie. Their pick allotment has been seriously dented over the next three draft cycles. Another reason to be quiet at the deadline: money. The Rams are cap-strapped, sitting right at the mark, with little or no realistic flexibility. They may have wanted to do something dramatic to help turn around their Super Bowl-hopeful season, but the Rams will have to roll mostly with what they have.

The Ravens traded for Jacoby Brissett just to have some hope on offense. There was none with Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley on IR. It was a little surprising to see a fourth-round pick involved, though Baltimore got a fifth-round pick back. But that move without any follow-up is a bit odd: Brissett isn't going to save the season, there was nothing else added to help out but yet the Ravens traded picks to be … what exactly? The Ravens got center Cam Jurgens weeks ago, but that came at the hefty price of a second-round pick to Philadelphia.

The Bengals made their big trade in the offseason when they sent a first-round pick to the Giants for defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence II. That trade had a profound impact on the defense, but doesn't count for this grade. It would have been nice to see the Bengals make more additions before the deadline, especially with Joe Burrow playing at an MVP level.

The one move that could have and probably should have happened was trading receiver Jerry Jeudy. Maybe there wasn't much of a market for him, but he's also not making a big impact for a Browns team that is turning to its young rookie receivers. Not making any moves is fine though a Jeudy trade would have been wise.
