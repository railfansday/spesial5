It was only a matter of time, but AI has come for astronomy accessories. I’ve yet to see a fictional elderly craftsman begging you to buy his hand-carved telescopes; however, it has absolutely muddied the waters around this viral gooseneck Galaxy Projector Night Light.

The good news is that there is an impressive little star projector to be had here, and I’m here to cut through the AI-assisted hype. To make sure you’re not hoodwinked, I’ve done the online legwork to bring you the best version of this device, and it won’t cost you a fortune to put a planetarium in your pocket.

Before we dive into what makes this Galaxy Projector Night Light so great, there are a couple of pressing issues to address. No variant of this USB-powered star projector will break up heated dinner arguments by casting a beautifully animated space whale on the ceiling, as one ridiculous AI-generated advert asserts. Its projections are eye-catching enough to entertain space fans young and old, but they’re strictly static.

Also, while you might be tempted by the lure of 24 interchangeable slides, compared to this Galaxy Projector Night Light’s 13, this lesser model’s slide selection is a letdown. Not only are the slides smaller and blurrier than this model, a large number are dedicated to seasonal holidays. As much as I enjoy Christmas, if I had Santa’s colossal face gazing down at me from the ceiling I’d never sleep again.

I tempered my expectations when I first got my hands on the Galaxy Projector Night Light. Packed in a small, relatively unassuming box, it looks remarkably similar to the standard model that’s spread across TikTok, YouTube, Facebook and so on. Just like that one, it’s powered via USB, with a flexible stalk, a slot for slides and a nose-mounted focus wheel.

However, once I hooked it up to a handy USB socket, all my reservations vanished, and I was seriously impressed by the power of this little projector. You’ll need a darkened room but the Galaxy Projector Night Light’s larger lens is a serious step-up from the run-of-the-mill version. Its images are richer and clearer than you’d expect from a projector this size, and easily capable of transforming a mid-sized ceiling or wall into a cosmic display.

This stunning James Webb Space Telescope image of the Pillars of Creation, with its vast columns of gas and dust, is a perfect example of the projections on offer. The fact the display was static didn’t diminish its wow factor, and I spent a good 15 minutes gazing at it in wonder.

The Galaxy Projector Night Light does have a four-hour shut-off in case you drift off or want to use it to lull a child to sleep (and 12 hours for its night light function). It’s easy to adjust thanks to its flexible neck, and while it never once came loose, you’ll find some USB ports have a tighter grip than others.

Once I set aside the images of Santa Claus (you’re staying in the box, Saint Nick), jellyfish and a T. rex, I was left with 10 astronomy-related slides. Several, such as the black hole, are artistic illustrations, while others, like the aforementioned Pillars, are actual astronomical images.

They all have their own distinct appeal. I wouldn’t, for example, describe the black hole slide as relaxing, but it’s absolutely captivating in a cosmic horror way. I’d have appreciated an auto-rotate option, but that would have added some bulk to the projector.

Switching slides is a breeze, each image mounted in its own slide holder. Oddly, a couple of mine had hair-like marks, but those disappeared when I brought the image into focus via the nose wheel.

• Angel and stars (B&W)• Black hole• Earth and moon• Jellyfish• Magellanic Cloud• Milky Way• Moon and stars (B&W)• Pillars of Creation• Santa• Solar system• Starfield (B&W)• T. rex• Whale and stars (B&W)

It’s mildly disappointing that there aren’t extra slide packs out there, but the selection is ample for entertainment, relaxation and even education. What better way to spark an interest in STEM than with the wonders of space?

The Galaxy Projector Night Light will run off any USB port: a laptop, desktop, charger and so on. I even got it working with my TV’s USB socket, but the real bonus was realizing I could run it off the best power banks. If you’re on the move, slip the projector and slides into one pocket, a powerbank into another, and you’re good to go for hours.

Some of the best star projectors are styled so they blend in with a room, but this one is so small you can just pack it away until you need it. The projector’s marketing even advertises it for use in the car, which seems like an accident waiting to happen. Only the Blisslights Starport is smaller, but then you’re limited to laser-dot stars.

There’s no remote and no sound options, but the Galaxy Projector Night Light is so easy to use that children should be able to get to grips with it. Literally, in fact, since while the light portion warms up, it never really gets hot. Factor in its small size and pocket-friendly price, and it’s an ideal space gift or stocking stuffer for a young stargazer.

As long as you don’t buy the hype and think you’re getting something that’ll bathe a room in beautifully animated starscapes, as several Amazon purchasers did, you’ll find this Galaxy Projector Night Light is a surprisingly capable little star projector.

Are there bigger and better models out there? Yes, and we’ve rounded up the best star projectors if this one isn’t for you. But for convenience, value for money and portability, this Galaxy Projector Night Light is a great way to bring the cosmos into your home and it’s fast cemented itself as one of my favorites.
