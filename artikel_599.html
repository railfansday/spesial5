Postcards from your graves

Wendy Nikel is a speculative-fiction author with a degree in elementary education, a fondness for road trips and a terrible habit of forgetting where she’s left her cup of tea. For more info, visit wendynikel.com.

You send me postcards from your graves, but by the time they reach me, you’re gone.

1) Quoted in Peculiar Letters from the Western Front, which I’d borrowed from the library the day before you left:

I’ve figured it out, B. The failsafe implanted in my wrist must have been misprogrammed. I never should have let AI help me write the code. Instead of pulling me back to the present whenever I’m in danger, for some reason, it keeps sending me into danger. (AI always has trouble with prepositions, doesn’t it?) Gotta go; they’re bombing again. Love, T.

The machine hums in the corner of the lab where you left it. “Just a quick test trip!” I keep hearing you say, your final words caught in a loop in my mind.

2) Delivered via Western Union, water-stained and faded, with instructions to hold for delivery until the present day:

Aloha, B. You’ll never guess where I ended up! Hawaii! A bit of a detour from “two weeks ago” (I swear, I entered it correctly! The machine must have some bugs to work out!) I’ll write again with the exact date as soon as I can find a newspaper, but it must be the early ’40s. Lots of war talk among the men stationed here. Love, T.

I plot them all out like a madman, connecting dates and places and all the catastrophes that conspire to keep us apart. I haunt disaster sites, archives and museums, searching for clues for my collection, as if completing the puzzle might bring you back.

3) Scratched onto a lifeboat oar at the Titanic museum:

Happened again, B. Not much time before the you-know-what. Wanted you to know I was here. T.

4) Sharpied discreetly on The Sphere by Fritz Koenig, displayed at Liberty Park, New York:

I hate knowing what’s coming, B. I want to warn these people. Would they even believe me if I did? T.

5) Printed in the editorial section of a 1900 newspaper:

This cryptic telegram, addressed to our editor on Sept. 8th, was the last to arrive from Galveston before the recent storm.

Please print the following in your newspaper, word for word.

“B. Trip to Hawaii was a disaster. Infamously. Zeroes got me. Somehow ended up here. Trying to figure out how to get back to you before the storm hits. Love, T.”

I am the one who’s filled our home with history, while you’ve lived your life in dogged pursuit of the future. Is it ironic, then, that you’re there, in those places I know so well, while I’m stuck here, trying to sort out what happens next?

6) From a note scribbled in the communion ledger of St Michael’s Church, Old Town, Chicago:

B — I think it’s the large language model. Like fire, it follows the path of least resistance, the most well-worn ruts in the road. What rut is more worn in the road of time-travel stories than experiencing, preventing, observing disasters? And what disasters are more well-worn than these? T.
