Netflix Proved They Are the Masters of Heartland Stories at the Texas Book Festival

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Hunting Wives author May Cobb, bestselling author Katherine Center, and Virgin River showrunner Patrick Sean Smith got real about bringing your favorite cozy stories to life.

A packed theater in Austin, Texas, got together to see three of Netflix’s biggest stars right now—Virgin River showrunner Patrick Sean Smith, bestselling author Katherine Center, and The Hunting Wives author May Cobb—talk about their latest projects at the Texas Book Festival alongside the company’s Vice President of Drama Series, Cole Galvin. Not only is the streamer the home for each and every one of them, but their small-town stories filled with comfort and escapism have become major favorites for fans who simply cannot get enough of it all.

It’s no surprise, especially when you consider how much of an impact that the stories have had on viewers. According to the streamer, “since 2025, book adaptations have driven over 13 billion global views and represented nearly 20% of total view hours.”

May Cobb admits that The Hunting Wives’s success on Netflix—season 2 will be released on November 26—brought her novel to new levels of fandom she’s never seen before.

“Not to where it's in the zeitgeist. Not to where it's a cultural phenomenon, and now it has this incredible life of its own is absolutely staggering to me and I do give all credit to our creator, Rebecca Cutter, and to Netflix,” she admitted.

According to Christine Ball, EVP & Publisher at Berkley, “Originally published in 2021, May Cobb’s The Hunting Wives surged back into the spotlight following its Netflix premiere. In just one week, they have gone back to press twice on the trade paperback and saw a +5,000% increase across all formats in the same one-week period year over year.”

An external consultant noted that in U.S. book sales across physical, audio, and e-books, “lift in the 30 days after Netflix release against the same 30-day period the year prior, and found that, on average, Netflix adaptations increase book sales 10x.”

And it’s not only the authors who are feeling the impact. Even Spotify is getting a nice boost from it with My Life With the Walter Boys bringing a “1,000 percent increase in global listens and over 1,300 percent in global searches on Spotify,” to Ali Novak’s series following the season 1 premiere.

“It's so phenomenal and great because it brings people back to the books,” Katherine Center revealed to Cosmopolitan. Her book, The Bodyguard, will soon be a Netflix film titled Guarding Stars starring Jared Padelecki and Leighton Meester. The film had a sold-out screening at the Texas Book Festival where Katherine and Jared took the stage alongside producer Genevieve Padalecki. It also stars My Life With the Walter Boys star Noah LaLonde, bringing him to another book universe on the streamer.

“There are a lot of people who find out it's going to be a movie and want to read it before they see it,” Katherine continued. “Other people who see the movie, love the movie, and then go back and find the book. It's incredibly helpful.”

And while most streamers are also feeling major successes when it comes to adaptations, it’s clear that Netflix’s focus on comfort/heartland stories is paying off with, “over 20 comfort/heartland titles appearing in the Global top 10 with 6 of them reaching the #1 spot.”

Part of that is how the shows and movies that fill these slots get to make some folks who hardly see themselves on TV finally feel seen.

“I like being able to tell stories for the older generation, just because they're becoming a bit invisible on television,” Patrick Sean Smith said. “And having Tim Matheson and Annette O’Toole to write to is just insane, so that's never an opportunity that I want to squander. We fill out their lives with their peers and their friends and it feels fresh because you don't really see that much on television.”

May Cobb credits Taylor Sheridan’s impact on TV as the reason why we get to see so many of these stories get made away from major metropolitan cities like Los Angeles, New York, and Chicago. It also allowed them to push against the grain with what people might expect from a story based in the Lone Star State.

“What they did that's so interesting with this particular heartland story is they showed this like twistier, more subversive side of Texas that I don't know had been represented yet. I feel like it's not just Texas; it could be small town anywhere,” she noted.

As fans got to check out the Netflix Reading Lounge and pick up copies of their favorite books, it was clear that they all were reacting the same to each title. Options ranged from People We Meet on Vacation to the first volume of One Piece and The Magician’s Nephew, the basis of Greta Gerwig’s highly anticipated Narnia film that will be released in 2027. All books that can offer a bit of comfort in their new read. It helps that the lounge also included bookmark making and free coffee that was perfect to combat the sudden rain and cold that hit Austin during the festival weekend.

“People find me generally when they're going through something hard because they've heard that my books are funny, and they are looking to be lifted up a little bit,” Katherine said. “I hear, I found you after I had surgery. I found you when my mom was sick. I found you after I just lost somebody I loved.”

“There’s this concept in psychology called moral elevation that I think about a lot. It's this idea that when you witness somebody doing something kind for someone else, it gives you this kind of rising feeling in your body and it makes you want to be a better person. I think I'm sort of obsessed with how to create that feeling in a story.”

Patrick notes that he’s witnessed firsthand how these stories can affect even his own cast and crew members during the filming of Virgin River, showing the impact that they have even before fans get to see what happens.

“I've gone through a lot of things in my personal life that the show has helped me move through. I went through a divorce during the wedding season, which was really hard, but was incredibly cathartic,” he revealed. “When we did season five, we did an endometriosis storyline and there were a lot of women that came up and just said, ‘Thank you. I haven't seen this before.’ There's a symbiosis to it.”

And it’s that kind of universality that allows these stories to break down global barriers. According to Netflix, “Comfort/heartland titles have appeared in the Top 10 in 93 different countries. The book adaptations that made our Global Top 10 in 2026 came from 25 different countries, from Nordic crime fiction to an Indian romance novel to Mexican historical fiction.”

May also feels that impact in her home state, especially after the recent elections, noting that there were many memes that linked The Hunting Wives with Blexas’s success.

“No, it was the grassroots, and it was all the hard work,” she said. “I think it's cool how it's ahead of the curve and ahead of its time a little bit. Last week proved that Texas is more diverse in every way.”

It’s the universality that also allows Virgin River, which is set to become Netflix’s longest-running original scripted series thanks to its season 9 renewal, to continue on with such beloved fanfare with endless possibilities.

“I think it can go for as long as the fans continue to show up,” Patrick noted. “It's a place. It's a feel. It's a vibe that people like. We just want to make sure that we keep the storytelling fresh and let these characters grow up and move through life together.”
