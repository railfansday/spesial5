The saga of MindsEye has been somewhat concerning to watch from a distance. Originally pretty interesting, given it was headed up by former Rockstar North president Leslie Benzies, it instead became a poorly-received videogame (this is me being polite). One which would be a shame under any other circumstances, but has since spun out into a trainwreck saga involving multiple layoffs and, most notably, a lot of finger-pointing at alleged "saboteurs".

Many of its actual developers happen to disagree, a whopping 93 of them alleging "systemic mistreatment, mismanagement, and mishandling of the redundancy process" back in October of last year. The studio itself has continued to try and update it, including with a mission that is about those alleged saboteurs, which is a normal thing you do when you are trying to be taken seriously.

Anyway, the layoffs are continuing, and it looks like it's sink or swim for Build a Rocket Boy. Things have never gotten sinkier or swimmier than right now, however, as both a statement of affairs and a notice of administrator's proposal filed November 16 (and unlocked yesterday) have painted a picture, as spotted by RedbullCola on ResetEra.

Build a Rocket Boy has an unsecured creditor claim—that is to say, debt—of around £61 million: "Unsecured creditor claims are presently estimated at approximately £61.752 million, principally comprising amounts due to group undertakings, Diameter, NetEase and various trade creditors."

Unsurprisingly, Build a Rocket Boy is still hooked on the saboteurs thing: "The directors formed the view that the game's commercial prospects may have been adversely affected by a coordinated campaign of negative online reviews and commentary. The directors subsequently received information which they considered suggested the involvement of a number of third parties in organising and funding such activity and commenced litigation against some of those parties."

The plan? Sue the pants off everybody. "The Company had undertaken detailed investigations and had commenced litigation against several third parties, with the assistance of the Company's former legal advisers, Brandsmiths, in connection with matters described earlier in the Proposals. The Joint Administrators consider the litigation potentially to be the largest asset of the Administration."

It's that last bit: "Largest asset". When your largest asset is the hypothetical money that'll come from the pendulum of justice finally swinging your way, you're in deep trouble. And given how little concrete evidence has emerged so far of a wider conspiracy against a game we gave an honest shot and just found to be plain bad? I do not think this is going to work.
