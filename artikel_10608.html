South Korea’s viral “Scarf Man” begs shoppers to stop buying as police manage crowds

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A South Korean scarf seller known online as “Scarf Man” has asked shoppers to stop spending money at his Namdaemun Market stall after his anti-sales routine drew crowds large enough for police to be called.

The vendor sells scarves and handkerchiefs in Seoul’s Namdaemun Market. He went viral in late October after videos showed him telling customers that scarves did not suit them, warning they were overspending, and asking why they would buy something they would not use.

His blunt jokes turned the small market stall into a social media attraction. Clips of his interactions drew millions of views, while fans began visiting the market to buy scarves, film him, or take photos. The sudden popularity caused congestion in the market’s narrow walkways and disrupted nearby businesses.

A post shared by 남대문 다다구리(남대문스카프아저씨) (@nam_dadaguri)

According to Korea JoongAng Daily, police were called to the market on November 4 after crowds gathered around the stall. The vendor closed the shop the following day for a safety inspection before later hiring private security to help manage visitors.

Police have since dispersed crowds outside the stall during additional temporary closures. The seller also stopped publicly posting his opening hours and apologized to neighboring merchants for the disruption.

The vendor previously explained that he was grateful for the attention but concerned about the size of the crowds. “With so many people coming all at once, it has been difficult to properly greet and communicate with those who came,” he said in a notice reported by ChosunBiz.

The attention has continued online. After customers repeatedly asked to buy his scarves remotely, he opened a Naver Smart Store under the name Namdaemun Dadaguri.

A post shared by 남대문 다다구리(남대문스카프아저씨) (@nam_dadaguri)

The store sold bundles of six scarves for 10,000 won, or roughly $7.50, with the vendor choosing the designs at random. Its product listing included a warning that matched his viral persona: “Absolutely no overspending.”

By the morning of November 12, the store had recorded 157,497 visits, including more than 85,000 that day, according to The Korea Herald. Every listed product had sold out.

The vendor has also temporarily suspended YouTube’s Super Thanks feature, saying, “I still feel a little uncomfortable and cautious about receiving support,” according to Channel NewsAsia.

Despite repeatedly telling shoppers not to spend their money, the viral “Scarf Man” continues to attract customers both online and at his Seoul stall.
