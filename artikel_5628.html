Vitamin D and Sleep; Metformin-Cognition Link; FDA Rejects Duchenne Drug Again

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

— News and commentary from the world of neurology and neuroscience

Higher blood levels of vitamin D were associated with longer and less interrupted sleep in cognitively unimpaired middle-aged and older adults. (The Journals of Gerontology: Series A)

A phase II trial showed that a single dose of imlifidase followed by standard-dose intravenous immune globulin was associated with rapid functional improvements in people with severe Guillain–Barré syndrome. (NEJM Evidence)

A novel once-daily oral formulation of edaravone, known as FNP122, found no benefit over placebo in the phase III ADORE trial of people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. (Lancet Neurology)

People with dementia with Lewy bodies who also had positive Alzheimer's biomarkers had a faster decline in cognitive function, retrospective data in Sweden showed. (Alzheimer's & Dementia)

Alzheimer's disease biomarkers appeared to accompany the association between metformin use and cognition in people with diabetes and other metabolic conditions, data from two cohorts suggested. (Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry)

The probability of detecting MRI activity in people with multiple sclerosis declined substantially with increasing age, high-efficacy disease-modifying therapies, and repeated prior inactive scans, according to data from 3,800 patients in Italy. (JAMA Neurology)

The FDA again rejected the investigational cell therapy deramiocel for Duchenne muscular dystrophy, saying the clinical trial data did not establish substantial evidence of effectiveness, Capricor Therapeutics said.

Investigations into former NIH neuroscientist Eliezer Masliah, MD, have led to 32 retractions to date, Retraction Watch reported.

A 74-year-old man with Alzheimer's disease is recovering in a Brazilian hospital after surviving 11 days alone in the Amazon rainforest. (AP)
