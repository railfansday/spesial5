Khawaja and Warner question Cricket Australia’s new Sandpapergate forum

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Cricket Australia: "After careful consideration, the CA Board has decided to establish a forum that will allow players and staff involved in the events of South Africa in 2018 to share their experiences and perspectives"; forum set up following explosive claims in Usman Khawaja's book

Usman Khawaja and David Warner have questioned Cricket Australia's decision to set up an independent forum to explore the 'Sandpapergate' scandal.

Debate about the ball tampering drama of 2018 has been raging Down Under since the publication of explosive claims in Khawaja's autobiography.

Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft suffered lengthy bans eight years ago after a short investigation found them to be solely guilty, but Khawaja has alleged the entire squad and staff were aware of the plot.

Warner and reserve seamer Chadd Sayers have backed his account, while current Australia captain Pat Cummins has issued a comprehensive denial.

But Cricket Australia (CA) has already ruled out the prospect of any new punishments and has instead invited all parties to share their recollections - without fear of reprisals.

The reaction of two of ​the main protagonists to the announcement was not entirely positive, with both Warner and Khawaja expressing reservations about whose version of ​events would emerge.

"I appreciate where their heart is, and I'll support the board in this. I just wanted to take accountability, and ⁠I've done it for the three guys who I think were unfairly treated," Khawaja told Fox Sports.

"I could go in there and say something, but then the board can decide that they're not going to release that, and I just feel like then we just go in a circle again."

Warner, sat with Khawaja on ⁠a punditry panel for Wednesday's ODI between Australia and England, echoed his former opening partner's comments.

"I would ​have liked an open, independent inquiry, and the people that were there at the time actually can come forward and speak, because ​we have no idea about what's happened behind closed doors," he said.

"Look, we put our hands up. We accepted what Cricket ‌Australia handed down to us. It's not about penalties. ​It's just about coming up with transparency and accountability."

Darren Lehmann, Australia's head coach in 2018, was more positive.

"I think we've done the best of a terrible situation. For me, it's the light at the end of the tunnel."

CA chief executive Todd Greenberg and interim chair David Boon appeared at a hastily convened press conference in Hobart to announce a move they hope will take the heat out of the controversy.

"After careful consideration, the CA Board has decided to establish a forum that will allow players and staff involved in the events of South Africa in 2018 to share their experiences and perspectives," said Boon.

"We believe this is in the best interests of the game. For almost a decade, the fallout from those events has remained part of the conversation around Australian cricket.

"It has been a difficult chapter for many people and, increasingly, the ongoing debate has become a distraction from the game. We believe it is time for a circuit breaker.

"This forum is not about relitigating the past. It is about creating an opportunity for those who wish to be heard to tell their stories in a respectful environment, and for our sport to listen.

"Australian cricket is at its strongest when it is united. We owe it to everyone who loves the game to make every reasonable effort to move forward together, with understanding, respect and perspective."

Also on Wednesday, Australian Test great Ian Healy questioned why Khawaja had reignited the debate with the release of his book eight years after the fact.

Healy claimed Australian cricketers on the sandpaper tour will now be remembered as "liars forever" following the claims.

"Hopefully we got close to the truth in the investigation; those who didn't get an interview and didn't go forward, they're guilty whistleblowers now," Healy said on SEN radio.

"They didn't come forward when it was saveable, now there's a massive stain on cricket and they'll be known as liars forever.

"There is no reason this should have been rehashed. This was not Uzzy's story to tell, he wasn't involved and didn't do anything. Why write about that? He should have done it the day after the occurrence. It's Davey's book to write."
