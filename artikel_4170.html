Review: Titanium Court (Switch 2) - The Freakiest Dream You've Ever Had

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

It sounds like the stuff of dreams. Fever dreams. The theatre audience is waiting, and you’re in the wings. Without warning, an MC announces that the lead actress is unavailable, so you’re thrust onstage as understudy – but without a clue what you’re supposed to do. A solicitor’s billboard.

Titanium Court, the creation of New York-based developer AP Thomson, is driven by a singular vision. It mashes up almost anything it can get its hands on – whether it’s different game genres, different musical styles, or just different images of sports or snappy tailoring or the Highway Code. Since the only common thread seems to be the developer’s mind, it feels strangely personal despite the miscellaneous compilation of ideas jumping out of nowhere. A man in a suit playing a trumpet.

Looking at screenshots, you’ll think this is a match-three game – and it sort of is: it’s a strategic, roguelike, narrative-driven match-three game. In fact, the match-three game seems as surprised to be there as you are. Having been dropped into the theatrical setting, you soon establish that your role in the drama is that of the faerie queen, ruling (cluelessly at first) over your royal court. Said court sits in a terrain of matchable tiles that can each be swapped with adjacent ones to make three in a row. Matched tiles disappear and anything sitting above them falls down the screen, with new tiles appearing from the top to fill any gaps. Depending on what you match, you will receive resources of different kinds – food, stone, wood or water. As you make moves, a bar depletes, freezing the terrain once it runs out. A pair of sunglasses.

That whole match-three-ing phase is called 'high tide'. Once 'low tide' arrives, the court goes to war. The terrain you’ve done your best to design becomes the battlefield. Usually, there are enemy fortifications on it, and you must spend your resources to be ready to deal with them by training troops or workers or setting up defences. However, this is all part of a larger campaign, so resources should be strategically preserved for subsequent rounds. A basketball swishing the net.

Between runs, you are returned to your faerie court, where mystery unfolds to an eerily wistful tune. These sections are presented in a top-down view of the royal residence, with icons to select your actions: visit the dining hall, talk to your aide, take a shower, etc. The story is told here by the characters in the play — or perhaps the cast — always keeping logic just about at arm’s length. The repetition of the high-and-low-tide gather-and-hunt routine is consistently fun, but it’s the in-betweens that provide pacing, a narrative arc, and an opportunity to put the game down until your next chapter, breaking up the addictive loops common to match-threes. A rock star smashing their guitar.

Through all of this, it’s not clear whether your wide-eyed confusion is part of your role. Are the other characters helping you according to the script, or are the actors out of character, helping you to understand the script? But the script is unfinished anyway, the MC announces up front: the writers are racing you to the ending. Even your tooltip is in on the whole charade, personified in dialogue with you. The overall effect is something bizarrely coherent. It’s a jumble of a concept but, wrapped in the framing device of the theatre, nothing can technically be out of place. A 'No Parking' sign.

If we try and strip it all down to the gameplay essentials, Titanium Court has a moreish tension going on. The strategising grows in sophistication the more you play, and the goals of the game get clearer. However, those goals always lie on the other side of the randomness of the tile-matching mechanics. The balance of skill and luck is just about right, and complemented by the interspersed court segments. Success feels like your own achievement, while failure — although sometimes due to cruel tile-generation — affords an opportunity to relax in the court and explore its eccentricities – or who knows what might happen, frankly? A sticking-plaster.

So there’s a solid game under all the theatrics, but the theatrics are what will grab and hold your attention. The writing is thoughtful without pontificating, funny without leaning on jokes, and adventurous without losing sight of its absurdity. Perhaps the most absurd thing is how naturally this gels with a genre so far removed from Titanium Court’s philosophical ambitions. You could argue that the repetitive parts of the game wear out their welcome – but that’s not before the meta game gives you an out. You can play again and again if you like, but this is a controlled story that has an opinion about how and when it should end. It wouldn’t be fair to judge it on what comes after the curtain call. A glass of wine.

The graphical style also works wonderfully. Despite the tight colour palette on a dark screen, things jump to life. The eye-catching, whimsical images that pop up will bounce or fly or slide across the window in ways that fit the action perfectly. The thrill of an out-of-nowhere, pixel-art touchdown as the battle music rocks out is just perfect. A dog catching a frisbee.

Theatrical performance, narrative adventure, strategy game, match-three… Shuffling its deck of faerie queens and love potions and slam dunks and Ray-Bans, Titanium Court is the freakiest dream you’ve ever had. It might baffle your psychoanalyst, but that’s no reason to wake up.
