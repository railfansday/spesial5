Cavs announce injury update for Evan Mobley

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Cleveland Cavaliers have announced that NBA All-Star Evan Mobley will be out for the next few games following a left great toe sprain suffered on November 11. Mobley sustained his injury against the New York Knicks.

Mobley will be re-evaluated after 7-10 days.

The Cavs' big man is averaging 16.9 points, 7.3 rebounds, 3.6 assists, 1.5 blocks, and 1.1 steals per game. He's also shooting a career-high 47.9% from the three-point line. Mobley's increased floor spacing has been his biggest positive development at the start of the season.

Mobley isn't the only Cavalier who has been banged up. Sam Merrill is out with an elbow injury, Jaylon Tyson has been working through an ankle injury, and Nae'Qwan Tomlin has yet to make his season debut with a back injury.

These injuries have opened the door for younger players like Meleek Thomas and Tyrese Proctor to rack up early minutes. This has paid off, with both Proctor and Thomas making the most of their increased opportunities. Norchad Omier is another player who deserves credit in this regard.

Still, the Cavs haven't been able to play with much consistency given the changes in their lineup on a nightly basis. This has led to a slower start than fans would like, with Cleveland posting a 7-7 record in their first 14 games.

Wins over the Dallas Mavericks with a depleted roster are encouraging. Losses to the Charlotte Hornets are not as much fun. Cleveland has much to figure out this regular season, and injuries won't make that any easier for them.

For now, the Cavs will be without Mobley for at least the next week, which includes games against the Memphis Grizzlies, Indiana Pacers, and another game against the Chicago Bulls next Monday.

Mobley's presence as an all-world defender and elite play finisher will be missed. But the Cavs will lean heavily on Jarrett Allen to hold down the fort in his absence, and an emphasis will be placed on Mario Hezonja and Thomas Bryant to step up and add further support.
