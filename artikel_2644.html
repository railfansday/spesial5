2026-27 NBA Power Rankings Week 6: Spurs stay on top while Heat, Knicks move into top 10

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Welcome to the latest edition of the weekly NBC Sports NBA Power Rankings, where the Spurs still look like the best team in the land. With Thanksgiving coming up, we focused on something each team should be thankful for.

Record: 14-3Ranking last week: No. 1Games this week: at Sacramento, at Portland, Lakers, Celtics

Something to be thankful for: Victor Wembanyama's loyalty. As much as San Antonio should be thankful for Wembanyama's MVP-level start to the season — 29.9 points, 12.3 rebounds and 3.9 blocks per game — what matters even more is his Duncanesque loyalty to the Spurs. "I think this is really the ideal environment because even in other cities, where we're not the home team, people aren't as respectful. People can be very persistent or intrusive sometimes, but never here..." Wemby said postgame recently. "I'd say it's the ideal environment to spend 15 or 20 years." One other Wembanyama note: He has 72 dunks this season, which not only leads the league but is more dunks than 20 NBA teams have. He made so many it was hurting his hand, so he decided to jump-shoot it from directly above the rim (which is officially registered as a dunk).

Record: 14-3Ranking last week: No. 3Games this week: Utah, at San Antonio, at Memphis

Something to be thankful for: Lakers role players stepping up. Of course the Lakers needed Luka Doncic to be special (and he's been playing at an MVP level), and they needed Austin Reaves and Walker Kessler to play up to their new contracts. But what really has the Lakers this sharp to start the season is the strong play from the role players. Matisse Thybulle and Ziaire Williams are forcing turnovers and defending well on one end and hitting their 3s on the other. Sandro Mamukelashvili has been fantastic and Quentin Grimes showed how well he was playing, dropping 47 on the Hornets. Lakers coach JJ Redick put it this way after the win over Charlotte: "We're 15 games in. I don't know what this is going to look like after 82 games. I don't know what it's going to look like after 50. But I do know that there's something with this group, and every special group doesn't mean you win a championship, but it's a special group of guys."

Record: 12-5Ranking last week: No. 4Games this week: Brooklyn, at Oklahoma City, Memphis, at Sacramemto

Something to be thankful for: Tim Connelly and the aggressive Minnesota front office. The Timberwolves had a good team, and most GMs would have looked at what they had around Anthony Edwards and tinkered around the edges. Connelly was aggressive and took a big swing on LaMelo Ball being ready for the bigger stage, and so far that has worked out. There were other smart moves, particularly adding Ayo Dosunmu, but also Jonathan Kuminga, Trey Lules and Bones Hyland have had their moments. Connelly also trusted Rudy Gobert to stay the defensive force he has been. When Anthony Edwards returns from getting his knee scoped, this might be the second-best team in the West.

Record: 14-3Ranking last week: No. 6Games this week: Portland, Miami, at Indiana, at Houston

Something to be thankful for: The 76ers' clutch play. Philadelphia is 12-0 in clutch games this season (within five points in the final five minutes). It's a good thing and a testament to the talent on this roster. LeBron James, Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid and Jaylen Brown are all making plays, with LeBron seemingly the glue that just finds a way to get things done. It's concerning that the 76ers have only had five non-clutch games and are 2-3 in those, but they'll take the wins and bank them early in the season as things are still coming together in Philadelphia.

Record: 10-5Ranking last week: No. 9Games this week: Dallas, at Utah, at Toronto

Something to be thankful for: Jamal Murray. He is an All-NBA, All-Star and NBA Champion, but is Jamal Murray having his best season? Could be. He's averaging 24.9 points a game, shooting 46.3% from beyond the arc. On a team where Nikola Jokic is Nikola Jokic but many of the role players have been uninspiring and inconsistent, Murray is getting the Nuggets wins with the most efficient shooting season of his career (65 true shooting percentage). Those numbers don't even include his big 45-point night Sunday against the Bulls.

Record: 12-5Ranking last week: No. 5Games this week: LA Clippers, Phoenix, Philadelphia

Something to be thankful for: Fred VanVleet taking care of the ball. Last season, the Rockets were 27th in the league in turnover percentage. This season, they are up to 14th, not elite but a huge step in the right direction. Credit that to having a true point guard in Fred VanVleet in the house. The other stat of note is that the Rockets' elite defense has been pedestrian of late, ranking 21st in the league over their last seven games, and the result is that they are 5-4 in their last nine games.

Record: 12-5Ranking last week: No. 10Games this week: at Charlotte, LA Clippers, at Timberwolves, Lakers

Something to be thankful for: The young front core of Cameron Boozer and Zach Edey, with Cedric Coward and Jaylen Wells on the wing. In 130 minutes together this season, that foursome is rock-solid defensively, giving up less than a point per possession and posting a +25.4 net rating. The soft schedule the Grizzlies have played may have inflated that number somewhat, but watch the group and it's a young core that just clicks. The goal this season was for Memphis' young core to grow and develop, but it looks like it's already become a playoff-caliber team.

Record: 11-6Ranking last week: No. 11Games this week: Cleveland, Miami, Atlanta

Something to be thankful for: Karl-Anthony Towns. While some of the Knicks' role players appear to be nursing a championship hangover at the start of the season, their two stars have picked up where they left off — and Towns is playing better than a season ago. KAT is averaging 21.3 points and 13.9 rebounds a game, and he is playing arguably the best defense in years. Those role players might be starting to come around — which is why the Knicks have won five in a row — but it's Towns and Jalen Brunson who have shown up from Day 1 this season.

Record: 10-6Ranking last week: No. 14Games this week: at Philadelphia, at New York, at Cleveland

Something to be thankful for: Davion Mitchell. Of course the Heat should be thankful Giannis Antetokounmpo chose to come to Miami and instantly made the franchise a threat in the East — and he is putting up MVP-ballot numbers (28.2 points, 12.6 rebounds, and 5.5 assists a game). However, it is the play of point guard Mitchell that has helped bring it all together and spark the Heat's current run. Mitchell, amazingly, leads the NBA in assists and is averaging 17.2 points and nine assists a game, and he dropped six 3-pointers on Milwaukee last week (video below, although that's mostly just an excuse to show off the amazing Vice City court and uniforms.

Record: 11-6Ranking last week: No. 2Games this week: at Washington, Atlanta, at San Antonio

Something to be thankful for: The way the Celtics start games. Boston has a league-best +23.5 net rating in the first quarter. Or, look at it this way: The Celtics outscore opponents by an average of 5.9 points in the first quarter (also a league-best). That falls to outscoring opponents by 4.3 in the second quarter, then they play teams basically even in the second half. Joe Mazzulla should have concerns about those second halves in the big picture, especially in the midst of a four-game losing streak that included ugly road losses to Sacramento and Curry-less Golden State.

Record: 11-6Ranking last week: No. 15Games this week: at Boston, at New York

Something to be thankful for: Kingston Flemings. It took eight games for Hawks coach Quin Snyder to replace the 35-year-old CJ McCollum in the starting lineup with 19-year-old Kingston Flemings, and once that happened, the starting five's pace picked up the and offense has looked better on the eye test (even if the numbers don't back that up in a very small sample size). As a starter, Flemings is averaging 8.6 points and 3.2 assists a game, and his potential to be the point guard of the future is evident. (He's missed the last three games with a back issue, while McCollum is out about a month with a calf strain.)

Record: 10-6Ranking last week: No. 7Games this week: at Orlando, Milwaukee, Orlando

Something to be thankful for: Ausar Thompson's offensive development, especially as a playmaker. The Pistons have one of the game's elite playmakers in Cade Cunningham, but last season's playoffs showed the need for a secondary one to take pressure off their MVP candidate, and the Pistons didn't make an offseason move to add one. Thompson has started to fill that role, and it showed in the win against the Spurs last week when J.B. Bickerstaff put the ball in his hands for stretches and he set up Cunningham and Daniss Jenkins. Thompson also is setting more ball screens than he has in his career, and he's become a clever cutter to the rim. He's not a shooter and likely never will be a great one, but he's figured out how to be a real contributor on offense by playing to his strengths.

Record: 10-7Ranking last week: No. 12Games this week: at Indiana, Minnesota, at Milwaukee

Something to be thankful for: Ajay Mitchell and Jared McCain. In a season where the Thunder have their second, third and fifth-best players out injured, these guys who were supposed to be the punch off the bench have stepped up. Mitchell is averaging 17 points and 3.6 assists a game, while McCain is averaging 13.9 points and is spacing the floor, shooting 34.2% from beyond the arc. Right now, the Thunder are just trying to keep their head above water until they get key players back, and those two — along with Shai Gilgeous-Alexander — are at the heart of that push.

Record: 10-6Ranking last week: No. 8Games this week: at Philadelphia, San Antonio, Golden State, New Orleans

Something to be thankful for: Having Damian Lillard home. This just feels right. Of course Lillard has been great on the court, averaging 20.6 points a game and shooting 34.2% from 3-point range, but having him back in Portland is more than that. It's about setting the organization on the right path. We also could have easily gone with Toumani Camara as the reason for Trail Blazers fans to be thankful, the elite defender has become a much bigger offensive threat, hitting 38% from beyond the arc this season.

Record: 9-8Ranking last week: No. 16Games this week: at Chicago, at New York, Miami

Something to be thankful for: Meleek Thomas. The second-round pick out of Arkansas has been one of the biggest surprises of this impressive rookie class, averaging 12.7 points a game (fourth-most on the team) while shooting 42% from 3-point range. He's also shown flashes of being a defensive playmaker. The Cavaliers are +1.9 per 100 possessions when he is on the court, which is promising for a rookie. The fact that Kenny Atkinson is giving him 20+ minutes a night on a veteran team should tell you all you need to know.

Record: 8-7Ranking last week: No. 18Games this week: at New Orleans, at Houston, Dallas, at Utah

Something to be thankful for: Collin Gillespie. All the rumors around the Suns' trade interest in Dejounte Murray — and all that buzz is coming out of New Orleans and Murray's camp, not Phoenix or the Suns — are based on the idea that the Suns need a point guard. Except the Suns have a quality point guard on the roster already in Gillespie. After a slow start to the season, Gillespie has found a groove of late, and it might be time to move him into the starting lineup ahead of the slumping Jalen Green.

Record: 10-7Ranking last week: No. 17Games this week: Boston, Orlando

Something to be thankful for: Cooper Flagg. Don't overthink this, Cooper Flagg is the foundation of this franchise going forward, and in his second season, he looks like an All-NBA-level player, if not top-10 in the league. And he doesn't even turn 20 until next month. Flagg has taken a big step forward this season, averaging 21.4 points, 6.5 rebounds, and 6.4 assists a game on an efficient 59.3 true shooting percentage, and he's already a high-level defender. The basketball gods smiled on Mavericks fans in their moment of pain after the Luka Doncic trade, and this could not have worked out better.

Record: 7-11Ranking last week: No. 24Games this week: Boston, Orlando

Something to be thankful for: AJ Dybantsa. Let's not overcomplicate this. Anthony Davis has been fantastic to start the season and Trae Young is… playing. However, Dybantsa is averaging 16.5 points and 6.9 rebounds per game, and is already showing he is the future and the guy the Wizards need to build around. They also need to find a coach who can maximize him, and the rumors around the league have Brian Keefe as the coach on the hottest seat so far.

Record: 9-8Ranking last week: No. 13Games this week: Sacramento, at Portland, LA Clippers

Something to be thankful for: Yaxel Lendeborg. There hasn't been a lot to be thankful for the past five games in Golden State, as they have been an offensive mess since Stephen Curry went out (and he's going to be out a few more weeks). Still, the rookie Lendeborg has been a revelation, averaging 19.7 points and 7.3 assists a game, and with Curry out, he's found the ball in his hands as a primary shot creator. Lendeborg is finding life as the top guy on the scouting report a challenge, but the rookie has been everything the Warriors needed this season (and what they hoped Jonathan Kuminga would be). Whatever and whenever the era after Curry steps away becomes, Lendeborg should be part of it.

Record: 7-9Ranking last week: No. 19Games this week: Detroit, at Washington, at Detroit

Something to be thankful for: Franz Wagner, who may be playing the best basketball of his career right now. He's thriving as Orlando's No. 1 option, averaging 25.1 points per game, plus 8.4 assists and 6.8 rebounds a night, while taking on the primary scoring and shot-creation role with Paolo Banchero still out injured. Franz even hit the game-winner to beat his brother Moe and the Brooklyn Nets.

Record: 6-10Ranking last week: No. 20Games this week: Milwaukee, Brooklyn, Denver

Something to be thankful for: Immanuel Quickley. In the Raptors' win over the Pacers this week (snapping a six-game losing streak), Quickley stepped up with his best playmaking game of the season, dishing out 10 assists to go with his 18 points. He had the ball in his hands more and ran the pick-and-roll masterfully, finding open shooters and rolling bigs. Until Kawhi Leonard returns, Toronto needs more of that.

Record: 5-12Ranking last week: No. 21Games this week: Oklahoma City, Philadelphia, at Chicago

Something to be thankful for: Pascal Siakam. This isn't the first time Pascal Siakam has been on a team that didn't have enough support around him, he experienced it before in Toronto. Siakam is averaging 23 points and 6.1 assists a night, with an impressive 60.2 true shooting percentage — he is every bit an All-Star this season. He's had to carry a lot of the offensive load with Tyrese Haliburton out (and Haliburton will remain out for a few more weeks), and that hasn't gone smoothly, with Indiana 3-10 without their star point guard. Siakam has done his best to keep this team afloat, but he needs more help.

Record: 4-12Ranking last week: No. 26Games this week: Cleveland, at Charlotte, Indiana

Something to be thankful for: Jalen Smith. While Caleb Wilson looks like a future star, he's an inconsistent rookie and, for now, he's out (fractured hand). Smith has been the Bulls' best player this season, averaging 13.4 points and a team-high 7.9 rebounds a game, but more importantly, he's just been very efficient, shooting 70.5% on two-pointers and 36.4% from beyond the arc. New Bulls coach Tiago Splitter trusts him, which is more than we can say for Matas Buzelis, who was benched in the fourth quarter of the loss to the Wizards (and that isn't the first time this season, it's concerning because Buzelis is a key part of this team's future).

Record: 4-11Ranking last week: No. 23Games this week: at Houston, at Memphis, New Orleans, at Golden State

Something to be thankful for: The Clippers 3-point shooting. The Clippers aren't taking an extraordinary amount of 3s, their 37.7 attempts per game is 14th in the league, but as a team they are shooting 38.7% from deep. Some of this may not be sustainable: Derrick Jones Jr. is shooting 48.1% (on 3.7 attempts per game), Brook Lopez is at 42.2%, Rui Hachimura is at 40.8%, and Grady Dick is at 48% (but just 1.7 attempts per game). The best news for the Clippers is that Brandon Ingram is set to return to the court Monday night, they need his offense.

Record: 3-12Ranking last week: No. 22Games this week: at Minnesota, at Milwaukee, at Toronto, at Charlotte

Something to be thankful for: Day'Ron Sharpe. There's not a lot to be thankful for in Brooklyn to start the season, but Sharpe — the longest-tenured Net — has stepped up as the starting center this season, and while he's not putting up massive counting stats (9 points and 7.4 rebounds a game (3.5 of those rebounds offensive), the struggling Nets are a net positive when he is on the court this season. He's long been a favorite of the analytics crowd, but given a larger role this year, he's showing he's ready for it.

Record: 5-13Ranking last week: No. 27Games this week: Phoenix, at LA Clippers, at Portland

Something to be thankful for: Jeremiah Fears. I have no idea what the future of the Pelicans will look like, but whatever it is, Fears has to be a big part of it. The second-year point guard has taken on a larger role this season and is averaging 26.7 points, 5.3 assists and 4.9 rebounds a game. Even when his shot is not falling, he keeps attacking, and Pelicans fans have to love that mindset.

Record: 4-11Ranking last week: No. 25Games this week: Memphis, Chicago, Brooklyn

Something to be thankful for: Christian Anderson Jr. As ugly as things have been for the Hornets lately, there are positives. Watching them against the Lakers reminded me that Naz Reid is just a reliable, solid basketball player. However, the Hornets are about the long term and finding young players who can be part of the future, and Christian Anderson looks like someone who fits that mold. In his last five games he's averaging 9.4 points and shooting 44% from beyond the arc. "Just getting more comfortable in what positions I need to be in, getting more confident, and being ready to attack and be aggressive, whether it's scoring the ball or facilitating," Anderson said last week.

Record: 3-15Ranking last week: No. 29Games this week: at Los Angeles Lakers, Denver, Phoenix

Something to be thankful for: Darryn Peterson. While it seems a bit on the nose to be thankful for the No. 2 pick, he has started to figure it out lately. He scored 20 points with 10 rebounds and eight assists against the Suns on Friday, and before the game Devin Booker had nothing but praise for the rookie: "He's definitely [someone] I see a lot of potential in. He already plays like he's been in the league a couple of years. Just the maturity in his game. The pace to his game. He's got a real bright future ahead of him." Peterson is a huge Booker fan and has said he modeled his game after him, so postgame Booker gave Peterson a signed jersey.

Record: 2-14Ranking last week: No. 28Games this week: San Antonio, at Golden State, Minnesota

Something to be thankful for: Darius Acuff Jr. Of course there are the stats: 25.4 points per game, and now, after dropping 36 on the Suns last week, he has five 30+ point games in 15 career games. There is legitimate concern about his efficiency numbers, which need to improve, but one thing that stands out is that when he struggles early, he pushes through, he doesn't defer or go into a shell — shooters shoot, and Acuff is that. It's a good long-term sign. Also, the Kings needed the home win and to light the beam, like they did Saturday night thanks to Keegan Murray's tip-in.

Record: 1-16Ranking last week: No. 30Games this week: at Toronto, Brooklyn, at Detroit, Oklahoma City

Something to be thankful for: Tyler Herro breaking out, and Jaime Jaquez Jr. It's difficult to find the positives in Milwaukee, but there are some. Jaquez has been a good player for most of his young career, but this is the first season he's knocking down 3-pointers at this rate (42.5%) while averaging 14.6 points a night. Herro has been up and down since returning to Wisconsin, but he may be finding his stride, and he dropped a career-best 50 on the 76ers this week.
