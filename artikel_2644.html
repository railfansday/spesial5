Rishabh Pant record in away Tests: Is he India's best bet in SENA and overseas conditions?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Rishabh Pant has built a strong case as India's most dependable overseas batter.

His away numbers hold up well and include a rare milestone from Wellington.

The figures also show how he compares with India's other leading batters since 2018.

Rishabh Pant's record in away Tests is the first thing that comes up whenever India's fans and selectors discuss who can hold the batting together on a tough tour, and the numbers from the last eight years back up much of that talk.

The wicketkeeper-batter is the highest-scoring designated keeper in away Test history, and his century in Wellington this month has made the debate over his place in India's overseas plans even louder.

Across 36 away Tests and 63 innings, Pant has piled up 2,617 runs at an average of 43.61, with seven hundreds and 11 half-centuries to go with them.

More than two-thirds of his 53 Tests have been played outside India, which makes it all the more notable that his away average sits only 1.19 below his overall career average of 44.80. In other words, the pace, bounce and swing of foreign pitches have not dragged him down in any meaningful way.

England has been the busiest overseas stop for Pant, who has played 13 Tests there and scored 1,035 runs at 43.12 with four hundreds. Australia comes next with 879 runs in 12 matches at an average of 46.26, and his unbeaten 159 at Sydney remains his highest Test score.

Outside the big two, Sri Lanka has been his most productive destination on average at 64.50, although he has played only two Tests there. He also averages 49.33 in Bangladesh, while his returns in the West Indies (19.33) are the weakest on the list.

Note: Pant's neutral-venue appearance in the 2021 World Test Championship final in Southampton is counted under matches played in England.

The SENA nations are regarded as the hardest places for Asian batters, and Pant has handled them better than most. In 31 Tests across South Africa, England, New Zealand and Australia, he has made 2,288 runs at an average of 43.16 and a strike rate of 69.14, including seven hundreds and eight fifties.

His 128 off 153 balls in Wellington in November 2026 added a special entry to that record. The knock made him the first Indian and Asian wicketkeeper-batter to score a Test hundred in all four SENA countries, and only the sixth Indian overall, following Mohammad Azharuddin, Sachin Tendulkar, Rahul Dravid, Virat Kohli and KL Rahul. Seven of his nine Test centuries have now come in these four countries.

It is worth noting that two of the four English hundreds came in the same Test at Headingley, where Pant scored 134 and 118 in a single match in June 2025.

On most measures, the answer is yes, and the case rests first on how consistently he has scored away from home. Most batters from the subcontinent see their numbers fall sharply outside Asia, whereas 77.8 per cent of Pant's Test hundreds, seven out of nine, have come in SENA conditions.

He averages 46.26 in Australia and 45.00 in England, the two toughest and most regular overseas assignments for Indian teams.

The comparison with his peers makes the point even clearer. Since his debut in August 2018, he has scored 3,853 Test runs at 44.80, ahead of Virat Kohli (3,436 at 38.60) and Shubman Gill (3,141 at 44.23).

In away World Test Championship matches, his 2,077 runs put him at the top of India's list, well clear of KL Rahul (1,891) and Gill (1,642).

Tempo is the other strength. A career strike rate of 74.44 allows him to change the mood of a session when more cautious batters are being tied down by the moving ball.

His fourth-innings efforts, such as the unbeaten 89 at the Gabba in 2021 that sealed a historic series win and the 97 in Sydney in the same series, show that he can rescue a collapse while also carrying the extra workload of keeping wicket.

However, the picture isn't flawless, as his returns in the West Indies (19.33) and New Zealand (37.60) are modest and leave room for improvement. Even so, no other Indian batter combines this record with such an ability to turn a game quickly, and that makes him the first name on the team sheet for any overseas tour.
