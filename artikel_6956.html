Patrick Stewart Was a Serious Watch Guy. Here Are 5 of the Late Hollywood Legend's Best.

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

From a treasured Rolex to his beloved IWC, the late actor had a lifelong appreciation for the art of watchmaking.

“Someone once told me that time was a predator that stalked us all our lives," begins Captain Jean-Luc Picard, played by Sir Patrick Stewart in Star Trek Generations, in one of his most memorable lines. "I rather believe that time is a companion who goes with us on a journey, reminds us to cherish every moment, because it will never come again.”

Those musings on time feel particularly poignant now, following the British actor's death. There’s something rather comforting about the idea of time as a companion, particularly when you consider Stewart’s lifelong fascination with the very objects we use to measure it.

Because, as it turns out, Patrick Stewart was quite the watch guy. Not simply someone who happened to wear an expensive timepiece on the red carpet, but a genuine enthusiast with an appreciation for the craftsmanship, history, and ingenuity behind them. He bought his first watch at 18 and, by his own admission, never threw one away.

“The watch industry never ceases to amaze me,” he told the Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie in 2019. “Because a watch is more than just an object that gives the time. Behind every watch there is an idea, a design, a concept, an innovation, and the watchmaker's desire to create something that is greater than anything that has ever existed before.”

It's an enthusiasm that stayed with him throughout his life, from the Rolex he reportedly spent almost all his savings on after arriving in Hollywood to his enduring affection for IWC Schaffhausen. And, naturally, there were some rather lovely watches along the way.

So, in celebration of an extraordinary career and a life well lived, we've taken a look back at some of Patrick Stewart's finest watch moments.

Perhaps the most meaningful watch in Stewart's story is one for which we don't have an exact reference. Long before he became one of Hollywood's most recognizable actors, Stewart was given a rather unexpected piece of career advice by the actress Eva Marie Saint. She advised him that appearances mattered, and that an expensive watch might help convince producers he was already a successful actor. So, she took him to a Beverly Hills jeweller, where he picked out a Rolex. The only problem? It cost him his entire savings. “I was eating spaghetti and boiled potatoes for months after that!” he recalled in a 2019 interview with the Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie.

It's touching anecdote given Stewart's modest upbringing. Having grown up in poverty, he admitted that it took him years to feel comfortable spending money on beautiful things. But eventually, he came to realize there was nothing wrong with enjoying the rewards of his success.

Tissot Le Locle Powermatic 80 to the 2017 Canadian Grand Prix

One of Stewart's most entertaining watch moments came at the 2017 Canadian Grand Prix, when he joined Daniel Ricciardo on the podium and celebrated the Australian driver's third-place finish by drinking champagne from his racing boot. Yes, really.

And while most people were understandably distracted by the sight of Captain Picard downing champagne from a sweaty shoe, there was a rather lovely watch on his wrist, a Tissot Le Locle Powermatic 80. The reasonably priced Swiss automatic features a silver guilloché dial, Roman numerals, a black leather strap, and an impressive 80-hour power reserve. Proof that even a relatively affordable dress watch can look the part.

Long before his appearances at watchmaking galas, Stewart was already making a convincing case for the understated dress watch. In this archival portrait, the actor wears a small, gold-tone, rectangular timepiece, seemingly a Cartier Tank, on a dark leather strap. Paired with a pale linen-style suit and a knitted white top, it's a particularly handsome combination.

First introduced in 1917, the Cartier Tank has become one of watchmaking's most enduring designs, worn by everyone from Andy Warhol to Muhammad Ali. Its appeal lies in its simplicity: a rectangular case, elegant proportions, and a design that has barely needed changing in more than a century. And judging by this photograph, Stewart understood the appeal of a good dress watch long before his later affection for IWC became public knowledge.

IWC Portofino Automatic to the 2018 Academy Awards

When it came to dressing for the red carpet, Stewart understood the appeal of keeping things classic. At the 90th Academy Awards in 2018, he opted for an IWC Portofino Automatic, reference IW356504, a rather beautiful dress watch that proved you don't need an enormous, diamond-covered timepiece to make an impression. Housed in a 40mm case of 18-carat rose gold, the watch features a silver-plated dial, slim gold hour markers, and a discreet date window at three o'clock. It's finished with a dark brown alligator leather strap and powered by an automatic movement. There's nothing particularly flashy about it, which is precisely what makes it so appealing.

IWC Portugieser Chronograph Classic at the 2017 SIHH Gala

By 2017, Stewart's appreciation for fine watchmaking had brought him into the company of some of the industry's most celebrated names. That January, he attended IWC Schaffhausen's Decoding the Beauty of Time gala in Geneva alongside his wife, Sunny Ozell, sporting a rather handsome IWC Portugieser Chronograph Classic.

First introduced in 2013, the reference 3904 is a particularly elegant piece of watchmaking. Featuring a 42mm case, distinctive Arabic numerals, and a railway-track minute scale, it's a slightly sportier proposition than the Portofino. Inside sits IWC's automatic calibre 89361, complete with a flyback chronograph function and an impressive 68-hour power reserve. The evening celebrated the Swiss manufacturer's renewed Da Vinci collection, but Stewart's choice of wristwear showcased another of IWC's most recognizable designs. And given that he once named IWC as his favorite watchmaker, it's hardly surprising that he had such good taste.
