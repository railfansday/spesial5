As coal burn declines, rising power demand will be primarily met by renewables: EIA

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Let Utility Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The electric power sector could install more than 26 GW of battery storage next year, helping support a renewable resource base expected to supply almost a quarter of 2027 consumption, EIA said.

Solar and wind generation are expected to add new capacity of about 72 GW and 20 GW, respectively, this year and next, EIA said.

"Generation from other energy sources is likely to remain relatively flat or decrease,” it added.

While commercial and industrial sector demand will continue to see robust growth next year, that could be “offset somewhat by lower residential electricity demand resulting from forecast milder summer weather next year,” EIA said.

The federal government’s energy data arm is forecasting U.S. gas generation’s share of the electric power generation mix to hold relatively steady at an average of about 40% in 2026 and 2027, and nuclear’s share to average 18%.

Coal’s share of generation will likely decline from 17% in 2025 to 15% by 2027 as 13 GW of capacity is scheduled to retire by the end of next year, EIA said.

“Although the share of generation from those fuels is falling, total generation from those fuels is relatively flat in the forecast, remaining in line with the combined generation from 2025,” according to the short-term outlook.

The electric power sector’s coal consumption has declined, relative to 2025, in eight of 10 months so far this year, EIA noted.

“The decreases have been driven by significant increases in wind and solar capacity as well as a 9% decline in the average natural gas price in the electric power sector during the second and third quarters of this year,” EIA said. “We expect coal consumption to decline an additional 5% in 2027, driven by a 19% decrease in natural gas prices for electric generating plants and further increases in wind and solar capacity.”

EIA’s report also forecasts significant battery storage growth. Capacity will rise from 28.9 GW in the first quarter of 2025 to 66 GW by the end of this year and 91.7 GW at the end of 2027, counting systems with a capacity of 1 MW or greater.
