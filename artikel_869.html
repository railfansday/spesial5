Chelsea Q&A: Are Blues in crisis? What next for Fofana after viral interview?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Your questions answered by Sky Sports News' James Savundra on Chelsea's season, with Blues having lost three of last four in Premier League; Wesley Fofana’s unauthorised interview, the absences of Danny Welbeck and Estevao, and January transfer plans all addressed

After Chelsea fell to back-to-back losses with a 4-1 humiliation by Leeds at Stamford Bridge last Saturday, Sky Sports News reporter James Savundra examines the latest developments at Stamford Bridge.

As well as suffering a third defeat in four Premier League games, Chelsea and manager Xabi Alonso also faced the added frustration of defender Wesley Fofana giving an unauthorised interview this week, in which he laughed off his penalty miss in last month's Carabao Cup defeat to Liverpool and revealed he had contemplated retiring during his time at Stamford Bridge.

Here, Savundra addresses Fofana's future, Joao Pedro's injury, transfer plans and much more...

Jake: Is Fofana expected to face any disciplinary action for his impromptu interview? Some of the things said in that were unacceptable from a footballer, especially one involved in as much off-the-pitch controversy as him.

James says: Xabi Alonso addressed this at Thursday's press conference. Fofana has apologised following the interview. The matter has been dealt with internally and there are no suggestions that the defender is set to miss any matches.

Whether the club look to punish Fofana with a fine is not known at this stage. Alonso made clear that this will not be repeated. It's completely understandable to emphasise with Chelsea supporters. Fofana simply hasn't justified the £75m that Chelsea paid Leicester City.

Benny: Why is Fofana still being chosen when every single fan watching football can see how poor his form is?

James says: At the moment there is a lack of real alternative options. Xabi Alonso stood behind the Fofana-Colwill axis during Thursday's press conference despite their clear struggles dealing with Dominic Calvert-Lewin and Brian Brobbey.

Liam Delap will prove a similar physical threat tomorrow [Friday] with an added point to prove. Ultimately, Chelsea trimmed down their squad in the summer in light of no European football. Maxence Lacroix would be starting, possibly ahead of Fofana right now if he was fit.

Colwill has found things difficult this season having missed the majority of the last campaign. In hindsight, should Chelsea have sold Trevoh Chalobah? Perhaps it was time for the academy graduate to end his Chelsea chapter but he rarely let the side down.

You could feasibly play Josh Acheampong at centre-back. The youngster has been a shining light this season. Yet he looks comfortable on the right. Do you want to upset something which actually seems to be working. And if Acheampong moves to centre-back, then does Marco Palestra play as a right-back?

The Italian impressed in Serie A as a wing-back but with Chelsea opting largely for a flat back four, is Palestra defensively good enough to fulfil that role. Aaron Anselmino remains an option but hasn't played a minute for the club this season.

Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso of Wesley Fofana: "We have addressed that with him and for sure it was unfortunate. He has apologised. He knows it wasn't the right moment. The content wasn't right. "We are here to take things seriously and he said it wouldn't be repeated. We know it's something we don't want and they are not going to be repeated."

Mikey: Will Chelsea sell and buy any defenders and strikers in January? I feel we need stronger defenders with a better aerial ability and maybe a more out and out No 9 to challenge Joao Pedro and give us something different.

James says: Chelsea will spend money in January if they believe it will help them achieve their target this season - qualifying for the Champions League. They were pleased with what they did in the summer but accept that the squad still requires investment.

It seems unlikely any defenders will be sold given the lack of current options. A loan move would seem to suit Aaron Anselmino given his lack of options. The winter window is a difficult one. There's a sense that Chelsea could benefit from signing a more experienced centre-back, considering the moves for Emi Martinez, Jordan Henderson and Danny Welbeck in every other area of the pitch in the summer.

The club have shown a willingness to tweak their transfer model and sign players for the here and now, in addition to targeting players for the future. A striker would appear unlikely given the presence of Emmanuel Emegha and Danny Welbeck. It will be interesting to watch Emegha's development if given a run of games in the absence of Joao Pedro.

The fact that Xabi Alonso opted for a false nine in three matches prior to Leeds would suggest he believes there are shortcomings in both Emegha and Welbeck's games. It feels more likely that Chelsea will revisit the forward line next summer.

JD: How has Joao Pedro's injury affected how Chelsea has been playing as an overall team, and how has Xabi Alonso had to adapt to it?

James says: The stats are undeniable. Since the start of last season, Chelsea average 1.8 goals per game when Joao Pedro plays, compared to 0.6 when he doesn't. It's quite staggering the difference that the Brazilian seems to make.

Cole Palmer and Morgan Rogers look far more potent when they are playing off him. Danny Welbeck initially started in his absence at Brentford. However, he had virtually no impact and appears to have fallen down the pecking order.

Playing Rogers or Pedro Neto as a false-nine has been experimented with but Chelsea have looked blunt without a focal point - though Emmanuel Emegha scored in his first Premier League start which was really encouraging.

Xabi Alonso doesn't tend to give much away but he has described Emegha as being a player with "special" qualities. There's clearly an adaptation period required and Emegha missed a lot of football last season due to injury.

He was also signed prior to Alonso's arrival so it was very much a club signing. Chelsea's season has been derailed by the absence of key players such as Reece James, Joao Pedro and Moises Caicedo.

Alonso needs his big players to step up to ensure they can cope with the casualties - albeit to really key players.

Adam: Why does Alonso not start Welbeck whilst Joao Pedro is injured? Given his experience?

James says: The treatment of Welbeck has been puzzling. He performed really well in the opening two Carabao Cup matches of the season but has featured sparingly since the Brentford defeat.

It was interesting to see how late he was brought on against Tottenham despite Chelsea's need to turn the tide. The 36-year-old (happy birthday today, Danny!) clearly cannot get around the pitch with the same intensity and speed he did when he broke through at Manchester United but that was obvious when he was signed.

Alonso has deflected when asked about Welbeck's lack of minutes but perhaps his inability to press with intensity is one of the main reasons behind him spending more time on the bench than on the pitch.

He's still got a brilliant goalscoring touch - as seen last season at Brighton. He may benefit from more opportunities during a busy December period.

Tim: Should Chelsea be starting Estevao more often? If not, then what should we do with him? Where could we send him on loan if we can't guarantee minutes?

James says: Estevao has only started twice this season - both times at home. He made a huge impact on the pitch and on the supporters when he started in the win against Manchester United. His lack of physical robustness and defensive acumen would appear to be why Xabi Alonso has used him so sparingly.

However, I don't think his lack of minutes is going to be sustainable long-term. There's an argument given Cole Palmer's drop off that Estevao should get an opportunity at No 10. Chelsea see Estevao as one of their "untouchable" players and they would be extremely reluctant to let him leave.

I asked Alonso last month about whether a loan could be an option in January. The Spaniard said that it had not been discussed. Something is going to have to give at some point.

Estevao is still a teenager so has lots of learning and development left. However, he would be a key player for many of the top clubs in Europe.

He needs to find a way to force his way into Alonso's plans. A January departure is very unlikely but conversations will need to take place in the summer if his lack of football continues.

Saying that, if Chelsea do qualify for Europe next season then there should be more opportunities.

Sam: Which manager is in more need of a win on Friday, Oliver Glasner or Alonso?

James says: Both clubs feel like they are in very similar situations right now. Both signing managers to deliver a cultural reboot and sustained success. Yet both have had challenging starts.

I was at Glasner's press conference on Thursday and he spoke about lengthy conversations he had with the club owner earlier this week about January transfers and standards. Forest gave him an extremely lucrative contract and will want to give him as much time as they can.

However, given they have had five managers in the space of a year, it's difficult to predict anything at Forest.

Chelsea have full confidence in Xabi Alonso but home defeats against Spurs and Leeds have really harmed his approval ratings amongst supporters.

It would feel like a crisis if Chelsea lost three league matches on the bounce, so I would say Alonso needs a win just a little bit more than Glasner.

If Forest can create an atmosphere at the City Ground similar to their win against Manchester City then Chelsea will really be tested. Liam Delap will be desperate to prove a point.
