Summer signings join Lionel Messi among MLS’s top earners: Takeaways from the league’s salary release

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

How much in player salaries did it take to earn a point in the 2026 MLS regular season? The latest batch of salaries lends some insight

Lionel Messi has closer company at the top of MLS’s list of highest-paid players, thanks to another pair of former Barcelona attackers. The Inter Miami captain is still comfortably the league’s top earner, on a guaranteed compensation of $28.3m. Robert Lewandowski (Chicago Fire) and Antoine Griezmann (Orlando City) have entered the list with the second and third highest wages, while Atlanta United’s Breel Embolo, another summer signing, ranks fifth on a guaranteed compensation of $10.8m. Second-year LAFC forward Son Heung-min sits fourth on $11.2m.

Casimiro, who joined Messi in Miami at the end of his contract with Manchester United, is his team’s fourth top-earner with a guaranteed compensation of $3.54m. This comes after Miami announced his signing as a non-designated player to the surprise of some as the Brazilian arrived on a Bosman transfer rather than a loan-with-purchase, as Rodrigo De Paul had last summer, to keep Miami rules compliant. The MLS Players’ Association lists Casimiro on a base salary of $896,004.

All of these earnings figures were revealed as part of the MLS Players’ Association’s twice-a-year data dump, with the latter installment dropping hours before the league’s postseason commenced. While these figures don’t include transfer fees and therefore don’t tell the whole picture of a team’s investment in its roster, it’s a useful bit of insight in regards to how each team is spending on player wages.

Figures are players’ guaranteed compensation inclusive of base salary and all guaranteed bonuses; does not include marketing and other bonuses.

Thirteen of MLS’s 30 clubs have a new highest earner after a busy post-World Cup transfer window. Two – Anders Dreyer (San Diego, $3.6m) and Mateusz Bogusz (Houston, $2.5m) – rose to the top after the departure of a higher-paid teammate. Lozano overtook the injured Riqui Puig after arriving with the LA Galaxy on loan from San Diego. Lewandowski, Griezmann, and Embolo hold similar status, while Charlotte replaced Wilfried Zaha with Allan Saint-Maximin ($7.2m) earning nearly three times as much as the Crystal Palace great.

Other top-earners signed from beyond the league are Brais Méndez (Columbus, $5.2m), André Luiz (Kansas City, $3.3m), and Kai Wagner (Philadelphia, $1.6m). Two strikers who were traded within the league mid-season signed fresh terms that made them their new teams’ highest-paid players: Rafael Navarro (St. Louis, $4.95m) and Dejan Joveljic (Seattle, $3.7m).

The standout failure of the new big earners is Alexis Sánchez. The 37-year-old Chile legend came to North America this summer, joining CF Montréal and making 15 league appearances for the Quebecois club but failing to register a single goal or assist. His $2.97m guaranteed compensation makes up 24.6% of the team’s wage bill.

Griezmann’s arrival means that Messi no longer carries the largest share of any team’s wage bill, as Orlando has dedicated slightly more to the Frenchman (49.5%) than Miami has to the Argentinian (47.7%). Lewandowski narrowly trails by accounting for 47.3% of Chicago’s wage spending. Importantly, Messi’s contract includes add-ons; a portion of Apple TV subscriptions, merchandise sales through Adidas and Fanatics, and an optional ownership stake in Miami when he retires.

Other notable new wages include former Middlesbrough attacking midfielder Morgan Whittaker (Colorado, $2.0m), former Watford and Everton midfielder Abdoulaye Doucouré (Charlotte, $1.0m), former Manchester United left-back Tyrell Malacia (Cincinnati, $1.04m), former NYCFC playmaker Santi Rodríguez (Columbus, $3.9m), South Africa striker Lyle Foster (Houston, $1.8m), former Celtic striker Kyōgo Furuhashi (LA Galaxy, $1.1m), Tunisia winger Elias Saad (Nashville, $1.7m), former Leeds winger Jack Harrison (New England, $3.65m), Côte d’Ivoire winger Bénie Traoré (NYCFC, $2.5m), former Netherlands striker Vincent Janssen (Portland, $1.75m), Tunisia winger Elias Achouri (San Diego, $2.6m), Scotland goalkeeper Angus Gunn (San Jose, $1.6m), and Southampton loanee Damion Downs (St Louis, $1.6m).

While this exercise cannot confidently include every transfer fee paid, the timing of this year’s drop after the regular season’s end allows us to size up how teams spent on wages against the league table.
