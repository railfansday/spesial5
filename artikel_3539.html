Pregnant Savannah Chrisley Slams 'Gold Digger' Claims Amid Age-Gap Marriage

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Savannah Chrisley has something to say about the speculation she’s a “gold digger” for marrying husband Jason Galanis despite their 27-year age difference.

“The way that I live my life, I am in a very healthy, happy relationship. I don’t have chaos, I don’t have turmoil [and] I’m not wondering how I’m going to pay my bills,” Savannah, 29, told her dad, Todd Chrisley, on the Tuesday, November 17, episode of her “Unlocked” podcast. “My life is very peaceful, very calming, very just zen, OK? I love it, it’s great. So, what better situation than to bring a child into a home that is stable and loving and all the great things?”

Savannah and Galanis, 56, started dating in 2025 before announcing last month that they are expecting a baby. The couple quietly got married in the Cayman Islands in July amid the reality TV alum’s pregnancy.

While Savannah has frequently gushed about her newlywed bliss, social media critics have branded the podcaster a gold digger over the pair’s age gap.

“Me, being a gold digger? I don’t,” she started, clapping back at the assumptions. “Sure, I have expectations, like, I am the mother of your child, you’re putting a roof over my head. You’re providing for me, but I work. I’m sitting here … and [I] had my own money.”

She continued, “I have worked. I am sitting here seven months pregnant still doing a podcast. Yes, I do it because I love it, but also because I’m not doing it for free. I make money off of it; I have contractual obligations.”

Todd, 57, noted that he hopes to see his daughter continue to work once her baby boy arrives. (In addition to Savannah, Todd shares sons Chase and Grayson with wife Julie Chrisley. They also adopted daughter Chloe, the biological daughter of Todd’s son Kyle from his first marriage.)

“I have to say to you, because you literally are like, ‘I don’t know if I want to do podcasting anymore. I want to spend time with my baby [or] I want to do this.’ And I’m like, ‘Savannah … your identity is based on what you accomplish on your own,’” Todd stated. “Your podcast does very well, and there’s a lot of people that want to listen and hear what you have to say.”

He added, “Your identity will be tied up in what you believe feeds you the most, and if that’s podcasting, if that’s going back on television, if that’s creating another brand or if it’s creating another child, which I’m hoping is going to be the case ‘cause I’ve already named them.”
