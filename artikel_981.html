England cruise to ODI series win over Australia

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

England beat Australia by 97 runs in Hobart for 2-1 ODI series win; teams meet in five T20 internationals, starting in Melbourne on Saturday (8.15am UK); 'Sandpapergate' storm rumbles on with Cameron Bancroft backing up Usman Khawaja's claims that every member of team aware of 2018 plot

England secured a come-from-behind ODI series win over Australia as Ben Duckett's century and Sonny Baker's new-ball wickets teed up a thumping 97-run victory in the Hobart decider.

Harry Brook's side had been skittled for 131 in Perth on Friday en route to slipping 1-0 down in the three-match contest but rallied in Adelaide on Sunday with a 135-run triumph before this convincing result at Ninja Stadium.

Australia were shot out for 231 chasing 329 in the third ODI, with Brook taking the final wicket of tailender Spencer Johnson after seamer Baker claimed 4-45 and spinner Liam Dawson 3-42.

Wednesday's win, England's first over Australia in Hobart, was similar to their success at the weekend: a total above 300 underpinned by a century from an opener - Ben Duckett here after Tom Banton in Adelaide - proving too much for the hosts, whose top order struggled once more when looking to drive.

England 328 all out in 48.3 overs (elected to bat): Ben Duckett (133 off 115 balls), Will Jacks (59 off 48), Tom Banton (44 off 35); Jack Edwards (4-31), Matt Renshaw (3-51)

Australia 231 all out in 46.5 overs (target 329): Matt Renshaw (55 off 70 balls), Alex Carey (37 off 53), Jack Edwards (31 off 36); Sonny Baker (4-45), Liam Dawson (3-42)

England win three-match ODI series 2-1; five-match T20I series starts in Melbourne on Saturday (8.15am UK)

After Duckett's 133, his seventh ODI ton and first outside England and Pakistan, helped propel his side from 195-5 to 328 all out in 48.3 overs, Australia plummeted to 5-2 and 34-3 in reply - Baker nicking off Mitch Marsh (1) and Josh Inglis (14) with out-swingers.

Australia were eventually dismissed in 46.5 overs with Alex Carey (37) the fifth man to fall, run out after a dismal mix-up with Cameron Green, who later left the field bloodied after being cracked on the helmet by a searing bouncer from Gus Atkinson (1-44) and was replaced by concussion sub Ollie Peake (5).

Just like in Adelaide, the margin of England's victory could have been even greater had they proved sharper in the field, with Baker shelling Matt Renshaw (55) on six running in from deep third and Brook grassing the same player on 54 at wide long-off during the batter's 74 run-partnership with Carey for the fourth wicket.

Banton also spilt Johnson (15) off part-time seamer Brook, two balls before Duckett claimed the winning catch at deep midwicket.

There was one distinct difference from Sunday's game, though, in that England made the maverick call to send Jofra Archer (7 off 7) out to bat at No 6, ahead of Will Jacks (59 off 48), who went on to crack a second successive ODI fifty, this one from 40 deliveries.

The teams will now meet in a five-match T20I series, starting in Melbourne on Saturday (8.15am UK), with England out to do a white-ball double and Australia keen for a victory to bring attention back to on-field matters following the latest 'Sandpapergate' claims.

While the final ODI in Tasmania was ongoing, Cameron Bancroft - the player caught applying sandpaper to the ball in Cape Town eight years ago - wrote an article in the West Australian newspaper backing up Usman Khawaja's allegations that every member of the side back then was aware of the scheme.

This was not a great day for Australia. It was not a perfect one for England, either.

The tourists stuttered with the bat once a 74-run opening stand between Banton (44 off 35) and Duckett was snapped in the ninth over as Marsh brilliantly caught the former at mid-off.

Part-time off-spinner Renshaw (3-51) starred with the ball in the absence of injured frontliner Adam Zampa (back spasms), firstly by bowling a charging Joe Root for 12 and having Jordan Cox stumped for the same score and then by ending Duckett's fine knock.

England captain Brook (17) fell cheaply for the third time in the series, after efforts of one and five in Perth and Adelaide respectively, as he skied Cooper Connolly to deep midwicket, while Archer holed out leg-side off Travis Head.

The visitors were tottering at five down for under 200 at the point of Archer's dismissal but Duckett, who was dropped by Carey on 29 and reached three figures from 94 balls, and Jacks combined for a sixth-wicket stand of 56.

Jacks also added 41 with Sam Curran (14) once Duckett fell from a top-edged sweep, before stitching a further 36 runs with the tail.

Atkinson, whose six took England past 300, then continued his dominance of Head (4) by removing the Australia opener for the third time on the trot in this series - Head slashing to Root at slip from the fourth ball of the chase.

When Marsh and Inglis swiftly followed, the home team looked toast and although Renshaw, Carey, Connolly (29) and Jack Edwards (31) - the latter backing up his 4-31 with the ball - gave Australia a flicker of hope, England ran out handsome winners.

Dawson justified his selection ahead of leg-spinner Adil Rashid once again with the wickets of Renshaw caught and bowled, Connolly castled and Edwards pouched on the sweep, while Baker added to his new-ball scalps by removing Peake and Xavier Bartlett (16).

England captain Harry Brook, speaking at the presentation:

"It wasn't an ideal start to the series, notoriously we've been a bit slow getting into series.

"But the way we bounced back in Adelaide and the performance we put in today was outstanding. The boys should be really proud.

"The openers [Banton and Duckett] are playing beautifully well together. The height, the left-hand and right-hand combination makes a difference.

"If they keep going nicely, it helps us kick on at the back end of the innings."

Australia skipper captain Mitchell Marsh, speaking at the presentation:

"England outplayed us in the last couple of games and there is lots of room for improvement for our team. We have a bit of work to do.

"I am still excited about this group - a good group of young lads on a journey. The World Cup is 12 months away so bring it on."

ODIs 3.30am UK time, with T20s 8.15am UK time
