‘The Traitors’ Greatest Key to Success: Drama Without Romance

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

And just like that, another season of The Traitors is about to come to a close. The two-part finale of New Blood airs Thursday and right now it could be anybody’s game. Victor has been the only Faithful making smart decisions this season, but now in the final stretch he’s put his trust in the worst possible person. Shane is now a Traitor and completely off Victor’s radar. Mr. Sweaty Boy could end up getting taken out by his biggest ally in the game. The stakes could not be higher, but let’s be honest—chaos and drama are where this series thrives.

Yet despite the vast array of emotions and personalities that have graced these hallowed halls, The Traitors’ most impressive accomplishment, by far, has always been its refusal to incorporate romance into any part of its narrative. You’d think confining a group of individuals inside a beautiful, remote Scottish castle with an open bar would be a hormonal powder keg waiting to happen. (Think Love Island, but with murder and Alan Cumming in fierce looks.) But The Traitors has made a point to never cross that line.

In four seasons of the celebrity version and one season of the civilian version, there have been no secret hookups, no lover alliances, no steamy makeout sessions in the wine cellar—just platonic game play. That’s a unique approach considering how many reality shows like The Bachelor and Too Hot to Handle fully lean into the whole love and dating genre. Even more strategy-centric “gamer” shows like Big Brother are known for their iconic showmances. Yet The Traitors partakes in none of it, because it doesn't need romance to create must-see drama.

That’s not to say that flirtations and crushes never take place to some degree. (We were all thankful for Rob Rausch’s overalls last season.) Yet the show does not rely on hookups or romantic entanglements. Instead, Traitors has always preferred to highlight the close-knit friendships that develop among the contestants each season—making any potential fallout all the more satisfying to watch unfold.

Just look at Season 1’s winner, Cirie Fields. No one ever suspected her or said her name at the round table because she had formed such solid alliances with so many of her fellow players. The three other remaining finalists were convinced they were splitting that money. They weren’t worried at all that a Traitor could still be among them. That’s how fully they trusted Cirie, which made her treachery such an epic and truly heartbreaking revelation.

Meanwhile, Season 4’s finale saw Rob Rausch completely blindside Maura Higgins when he revealed himself as a Traitor and won the game. These two did flirt a bit on camera, sure, but they also seemed to develop a real friendship throughout the course of the season. Maura put all of her trust in Rob and he proved to be a snake in the grass. (You know he’d appreciate the snake comparison.) Romance and heartache may go hand-in-hand, but to be hurt by a good friend? That’s a different kind of pain, and arguably a lot more fun to watch implode.

Maura was a good sport about it in the end (the show is literally called The Traitors, after all), but the genuine shock that she felt made for some great television. Her quip about how he’s never going to get a girlfriend after this because he’s so good at lying was legendary and deserves a place in the Sick Burns Hall of Fame.

Ultimately, this show knows how to play to its strengths. It’s never been about finding a love match. It’s about murderrrrrrr. Why deal with matters of the heart when it’s much more entertaining to watch these contestants stab each other in the back? Metaphorically speaking, of course. (Or maybe not?) Either way, we love you just the way you are, Traitors. Never change.
