We have a new #1 channel in the Global Top 50. Fitu K Kitu picked up 1.19 billion weekly views, a sum that accounts for nearly two-thirds of the channel’s lifetime traffic. Talk about a hot start! Not even chart regulars like T-Series and MrBeast could keep up with Fitu K Kitu’s face.

Elsewhere in the ranking, a one-time collaborator of MrBeast’s is making waves on his own. One of Latin America’s biggest creators has taken advantage of spooky season while tying in a cultural phenomenon that has had a consistent presence within our charts.

MrBeast isn’t involved in Vigevani’s biggest video of 2026, but a creepy clown is. After claiming to purchase the clown on the dark web, Vigevani used it as the primary prop in a multi-part series that drew inspiration from the smash-hit, YouTube-born franchise Backrooms.

Vigevani dropped his Backrooms-inspired series in the middle of spooky season, and that approach paid off. The ninth installment of the series, which arrived on November 11, was particular popular. It already has nearly 40 million views, and Vigevani has called it his biggest video of the year.

The culmination of Vigevani’s clown serial pushed him up to 27th place in the Global Top 50. He collected 413.1 million weekly views, increasing his YouTube traffic by 28% week-over-week in the process.

English-speaking consumers might think that clown horror is so ten years ago. Back in 2016, a viral clown craze turned into a veritable panic in the days surrounding Halloween. That experience convinced many that clowns are too creepy to belong at children’s parties.

In Latin America, however, clown content still has legs. One of the biggest channels in the region is El Payaso Plim Plim, a clown who isn’t afraid to remain kid-friendly. We’ve had regular Plim Plim sightings in our charts for years, and that trend has continued into 2026. There hasn’t been a week over the past six months in which Plim Plim has collected fewer than 250 million views.

Vigevani has found a clever way to apply that interest in clowns to a more adult audience. By leaning into the inherent horror of pale faces and colorful makeup, Vigevani has revived a decade-old craze and picked up tens of millions of views in the process.

I doubt Plim Plim, who appeals to young children, would approve of Vigevani’s recharacterization of clowns. But that’s what happens when you get your clown on the dark web — you don’t always get what you pay for.
