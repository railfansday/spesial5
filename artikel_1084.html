Man United chiefs set for Monaco summit TOMORROW to decide Michael Carrick's fate... with manager taking training today despite facing the sack

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER and NATHAN SALT - FOOTBALL REPORTER

Published: 02:40 EST, 10 November 2026 | Updated: 09:07 EST, 10 November 2026

Michael Carrick’s fate as Manchester United head coach is set to be decided on Wednesday when chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox meet with minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco.

Berrada and Wilcox will make their recommendation to Ratcliffe, with all the indications pointing to Carrick being sacked after United dropped into the relegation zone.

It’s understood that the Old Trafford hierarchy would want to inform Carrick of their decision face to face, so there may not be any confirmation from the club until Thursday.

The 45-year-old turned up for work at Carrington at 7.15am on Tuesday to oversee training with the players who are not away on international duty amid increasing uncertainty over his future.

United sources maintain that a final decision has yet to be made, with the club reluctant to engage in a running commentary over Carrick's position.

The manager's position increasingly precarious after they dropped into the relegation zone despite salvaging a 4-4 draw against Aston Villa at Old Trafford on Sunday.

It left him with just one win from ten Premier League games this season – United’s worst start since Sir Alex Ferguson replaced Ron Atkinson 40 years ago.

Carrick is taking training as usual this morning - with a cut-down group of players as a number of United's stars have departed on international duty.

Michael Carrick is pictured arriving at Manchester United's training ground at 7.15am

Mauricio Pochettino has emerged as a new frontrunner to replace Carrick. The Argentine is currently in charge of the US national team, but his contract includes a £7million release clause.

Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann, who were both under consideration when United gave Carrick more time to turn the season around following a 3-0 home defeat by Bournemouth last month, could also be in contention.

In the three games since then, Carrick’s side lost at Chelsea, staged a remarkable comeback to beat Roma in the Champions League in midweek and then fought back again to draw with Villa.

Although it’s understood that no final decision has been reached over Carrick’s future and insiders say that speculation over his sacking is premature, the expectation within Old Trafford is that a new man will be in charge for the visit to Anfield a week on Sunday.

Carrick replaced Ruben Amorim in mid-January and the former United midfielder was handed a two-year contract in May after guiding United to a third-place finish and qualification for the Champions League, losing just two of his 17 games as head coach.

But this season has unravelled alarmingly for United with his only two wins in 15 games in all competitions coming at home to newly promoted Ipswich and Sabah of Azerbaijan.

Asked after Sunday’s chaotic draw with Villa if he would consider quitting, a defiant Carrick said: ‘That's not going to happen.

‘We’re in November. I don’t like this situation and where we are. But we can do something about it. I'm quite clear with what direction the club needs to go in, and it's got to win games.’

Avram Glazer and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, co-owners, are pictured at Old Trafford in September

Sacking Carrick would leave United searching for their eighth permanent manager or head coach since Ferguson retired 13-and-a-half years ago.

Pochettino was strongly linked with the job when Erik ten Hag was appointed in 2022 and again when the Dutchman was sacked two years later.

The 54-year-old has extensive Premier League experience at Tottenham, Chelsea and Southampton and was also in charge at Paris Saint-Germain.

Howe was previously seen as the favourite but it’s understood there has been no contact between United and the former Newcastle boss.

It would cost United around £3million in compensation to appoint the 48-year-old under the terms of his departure from St James’s Park in July.

Nagelsmann is available after leaving his role as head coach of the German national team in the summer when they were eliminated from the World Cup following a penalty shootout defeat by Paraguay in the round of 32.
