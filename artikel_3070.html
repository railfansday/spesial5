SDY Just Hit A New Record High -- Up 8.2% Since Oct. 12. Should You Buy This Dividend ETF?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

If you're worried about a stock market crash, high-yield dividend stocks are worth considering. This ETF lets you own some of the best.

When growth stocks start to feel too risky, some investors find a relative sense of safety (and steady income) from high-yield dividend stocks. The SPDR S&P Dividend ETF (SDY +0.26%) reached a new record high on Nov. 4. As of this writing, the SDY dividend index fund has gained about 8.8% in the past month.

Let's look at what's driving the growth of this dividend ETF and why investors like you might want to consider it.

For some investors, the artificial intelligence (AI) boom has started to feel like a bursting bubble. The tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index has entered a correction, with a decline of about 11.2% since reaching a record high on Oct. 6. If you're worried about the risk of AI stocks going through a prolonged downturn, high-yield dividend stocks could be a good way to diversify against the risk of a tech stock crash.

The stocks held by the SPDR S&P Dividend ETF are big, well-established, financially strong companies that have a long-term track record of paying high dividends. In case of an AI stock crash, this fund might perform better than the broader S&P 500 index (^GSPC +1.31%).

Here's what happened in 2022, the last time there was a year-long bear market. SDY strongly outperformed the Nasdaq-100 and the S&P 500 that year.

There's no guarantee that any stock, ETF, or sector of the economy will be "safe" from the next bear market or recession. But during tough times, dividend stocks tend to do better than tech stocks. That's because investors often make a "flight to safety" during a stock market crash or recession. Growth stocks and less profitable companies tend to fall out of favor when investors have less appetite for risk. Instead, investors seek bonds and relatively safe stocks in defensive sectors like consumer staples and utilities.

What stocks are in SDY? This fund holds 154 stocks and tracks the S&P High Yield Dividend Aristocrats® Index. (The term "Dividend Aristocrats™" is a registered trademark of Standard & Poor's Financial Services LLC.) That's a select group of companies that have raised their dividend for at least 20 consecutive years. There's no such thing as a totally "safe" stock. But the stocks held by SDY tend to be high-quality companies with financially strong balance sheets and time-tested business models, not volatile stocks that might take a big hit in the short to medium term.

Some of the top holdings in SDY include household-name brands like Verizon (VZ +0.63%) (2.8% of the fund), PepsiCo (PEP +0.23%) (2.1%), and McDonald's (MCD -2.61%) (1.65%). Unlike many other exchange-traded funds (ETFs) that are weighted by market cap, SDY uses a unique approach: This fund weights its holdings by yield. And this fund delivers excellent dividends. Its 30-day SEC Yield is 2.35% as of Nov. 6. That's competitive with some of the best dividend index funds.

The SPDR S&P Dividend ETF has a long track record. Since the fund's inception in November 2005, it's delivered annualized returns of about 8.7%. That's lower than the long-run average annual return of 9.98% for the S&P 500. For example, in the past five years, SDY has delivered annualized returns of about 7.3%, underperforming the S&P 500.

But buying stocks isn't about the past; it's about what you think will happen in the future. If you're worried about tech stock volatility and want to put some of your money into an ETF that earns steady dividend income -- and that might avoid the worst effects of a possible AI-related stock market crash -- SDY could be a good choice.
