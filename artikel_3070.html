Upstart: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Upstart's move to fund loans with customer deposits could cut capital costs, but rising macro credit risk remains an open question.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 3, 2026, Upstart (UPST +2.14%) announced that full-year fee revenue will reach $1.32 billion, topping initial expectations despite rising macroeconomic headwinds. For long-term investors, the company's ability to drive loan volume through machine-learning underwriting models matters far more than short-term balance sheet revaluations. Three things management said on the call stand out.

Upstart expanded margins in its collateral-backed loans by automating underwriting and reducing manual review expenses. Higher automation accelerates loan processing and lowers variable operating costs. This pushed the secured business to positive contribution profit -- fee revenue minus variable acquisition and servicing costs -- a full quarter ahead of schedule.

The company is preparing to launch Upstart Bank, its planned banking subsidiary, in early 2027 to replace high-cost warehouse borrowing with low-cost customer deposits. Moving existing balance sheet loans into the bank will release trapped equity capital as cash while improving overall take rates, which measure net fees earned as a percentage of total originations.

Upstart is reducing its balance sheet risk by shifting loan originations toward multiyear committed funding partnerships. Lowering equity capital requirements insulates the platform from macroeconomic volatility while maintaining credit expansion across core lending categories.

Upstart faces credit headwinds from the Upstart Macro Index (UMI) -- its proprietary metric tracking macro-driven borrower default risk -- which reached 1.53, above the average in October. However, core technology enhancements continue to widen model accuracy over traditional credit scoring benchmarks, sustaining origination expansion across market cycles.

Management is tracking pricing discipline, cost of capital, and loan automation rates to secure multiyear operating leverage.

The long-term thesis rests on converting technical differentiation into operating profits as deposit funding opens up lower capital costs and broader market reach.
