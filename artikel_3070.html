Stock Market Sell-Off: History Says This Is the Smartest Move Investors Can Make Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Keep calm and carry on, the famous World War II slogan from Great Britain, is perfect advice for a bear market.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 21, 2026 at 8:15AM EST

The S&P 500 index (^GSPC -3.70%) is near all-time highs despite multiple geopolitical conflicts, inflation, rising interest rates, and the risk of the AI bubble bursting. Wall Street is justifiably worried that a bear market could soon arrive. The smartest move you can make right now is to prepare yourself emotionally for a market downturn. My preferred approach is to distract myself with dividends. Here's why.

History is clear: a bear market is on the way, though nobody knows exactly when it will arrive, and your emotions will be taxed. It could be a sizable downturn, too, given the similarities between the artificial intelligence (AI) bubble today and the dot-com bubble at the turn of the century. I remember the dot-com crash very well. It was brutal.

Then, just as the market was finally putting that deep and prolonged bear market in the rearview mirror, the Great Recession hit. That economic downturn and the associated bear market were just as brutal. There were legitimate concerns that the global financial system might collapse. And yet, history is very clear: the S&P 500 index, generally considered "the market", has recovered after each bear market and gone on to hit new highs. So the best course of action during a big downturn is to stay invested.

Staying invested, unfortunately, is easier said than done. It is hard to remain calm as you watch the value of your portfolio decline day after day. Bear markets like the dot-com bust and the Great Recession are relentless, and you will almost certainly feel panic building as the days drag on to weeks, months, and even years.

Having invested through some really bad bear markets, I know exactly how hard they can be on one's emotional well-being. The fear you feel can take physical form in sleepless nights, an unsettled stomach, and mental stress. My cure for the emotional hit of market volatility has long been to focus on the dividends my income-focused portfolio throws off each month.

That may sound a bit like King Midas counting his gold, but I know from experience that watching my dividends helps me ignore stock price volatility. And I focus on owning a diverse collection of well-run dividend stocks, including Dividend Kings like Procter & Gamble (PG +0.91%), Federal Realty (FRT -0.14%), and Black Hills (BKH +0.55%), all of which have increased their dividend annually for at least 50 consecutive years. For reference, that's a consumer staples company, a REIT, and a utility, with diversification being another of my investment focuses.

However, the key piece of this story isn't that I track my dividends during bear markets. It is that I track them all of the time. I have a spreadsheet that I update every month, in bull markets and bear markets. I'm not waiting for the next bear-market stress test to deal with the emotional toll of a downturn. I'm proactively preparing right now and have been for years. Simply put, I've developed a habit of focusing on dividends rather than stock prices.

I'm not suggesting that stock prices aren't relevant. They most certainly are important. But I also know there will be another bear market, and that sticking to my long-term investment approach through it to benefit from the next bull market is likely to lead to the best outcome. So I invest in a way that prepares me to buy and hold, no matter what the market is doing. For me, that means buying reliable dividend stocks and focusing on dividends more than stock prices. It's how I keep calm and carry on, to quote the famous British World War II slogan.

Only you can decide what will help you stay calm during a bear market. For me, it's focusing on the dividends I collect from reliable dividend stocks. But the smartest move right now is to figure out what you need to do before the next bear market comes along. Once it is here, your emotions will be elevated, and that means you will be more likely to make the kind of short-term decisions that could be bad for your long-term performance. Don't wait; prepare today for the bear market that history says is on the way.
