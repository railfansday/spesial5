Prediction: These Will Be the Best-Performing Industrial Stocks of 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Infrastructure spending could fuel another year of strong growth.

By Jeff Siegel – Nov 20, 2026 at 9:45AM EST

The industrial sector could produce some of the market's biggest winners in 2027, particularly among companies supplying the equipment and infrastructure needed to meet America's growing electricity demand. Three companies I'd put near the top of that list are Quanta Services (PWR -3.49%), Eaton (ETN -2.69%), and Vertiv Holdings (VRT -5.72%).

Quanta Services is positioned to benefit from the construction of new transmission networks, while Eaton supplies the electrical equipment needed for those projects. Meanwhile, Vertiv stands to benefit as new AI data centers require increasingly sophisticated power management and cooling systems.

All three have substantial opportunities extending into the coming year, supported by existing orders and multiyear infrastructure investments. And while their valuations could limit returns, continued spending on these projects gives all three a credible chance of outperforming the broader market in 2027.

Quanta Services builds and maintains the infrastructure that delivers electricity and natural gas. Its work includes transmission lines, substations, pipelines, and other infrastructure needed to support the expansion of power demand.

In Q3 2026, Quanta reported revenue of $12.01 billion, up approximately 57% from $7.63 billion a year earlier. Adjusted earnings per share jumped 82%, from $3.33 to $6.06.

Meanwhile, its total backlog reached a record $63.7 billion, providing considerable visibility into future revenue. Management subsequently raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to between $41.3 billion and $41.6 billion, with adjusted earnings per share expected to reach $17.90 to $18.30.

Quanta's key advantage is its ability to handle large, complicated infrastructure projects. Utilities need additional transmission capacity, data centers require reliable electricity connections, and aging electrical systems need upgrades. These projects can take years to complete, potentially supporting revenue well beyond 2027.

The risks, however, include labor shortages, project delays, and a valuation that may already reflect substantial future growth. But Quanta's backlog and earnings momentum make it a very strong industrial stock play.

Eaton manufactures electrical equipment used to manage, distribute, and protect power. Its products are essential components in data centers, factories, commercial buildings, and utility infrastructure.

In Q3, revenue reached a record $8.9 billion, increasing 28% year over year. Organic revenue growth, which excludes acquisitions and currency fluctuations, reached 19%, and adjusted earnings per share climbed to $3.68, while segment operating margins reached a record 25.3%. Demand is also accelerating.

Eaton reported that its electrical businesses experienced a 46% year-over-year increase in total backlog. Meanwhile, rolling 12-month organic orders increased by 39% in Electrical Americas and 51% in Electrical Global. Those numbers suggest customers are continuing to invest heavily in electrical infrastructure.

Management expects full-year 2026 adjusted earnings per share of $13.70 to $13.80. The company is also expanding manufacturing capacity to meet growing demand.

Overall, Eaton's biggest advantage is its exposure to multiple industries. It benefits from data center construction, but also serves utilities, manufacturers, aerospace customers, and commercial facilities. That diversification could help sustain growth even if spending in one market slows.

As artificial intelligence drives demand for more powerful computing systems, data centers require increasingly sophisticated cooling and electrical equipment. Vertiv is benefiting directly from that spending.

Third-quarter revenue reached $4.31 billion, increasing 61% year over year. Organic revenue growth, which excludes acquisitions, reached 55%. Meanwhile, adjusted earnings per share jumped 78% to $2.21, while adjusted operating profit increased 83% to approximately $1.09 billion.

Organic orders more than doubled compared with the same quarter a year earlier, and the company also generated $652 million in adjusted free cash flow during the quarter, an increase of 50%.

Management subsequently raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to between $14.45 billion and $14.85 billion. Adjusted earnings per share are expected to reach $7.10 to $7.20, representing approximately 69% to 71% growth. Those numbers suggest Vertiv could enter 2027 with substantial momentum.

However, the stock carries considerable risk. Its rapid growth has attracted significant investor enthusiasm, and any slowdown in data center spending could pressure both earnings expectations and its valuation.

Of these three companies, Vertiv could deliver the strongest earnings growth in 2027. But I'd be careful about assuming that translates into the biggest stock gains.

Quanta's record backlog offers visibility into future infrastructure spending, while Eaton's diversified electrical business provides exposure to several growing markets. Vertiv has the fastest recent earnings growth but potentially the greatest sensitivity to shifting expectations around AI infrastructure spending.

My prediction is that all three will benefit from continued investment in electrical infrastructure during 2027. The biggest winner, however, may be the company that delivers earnings growth exceeding what Wall Street has already priced into its shares.
