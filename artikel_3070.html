CoStar: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Margin expansion and a $730M acquisition in aerial imagery position CoStar to accelerate subscription growth, but sales productivity remains the wildcard.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 27, 2026, CoStar (CSGP -0.21%) raised its full-year profit outlook after cost reductions doubled its quarterly operational earnings. The company is driving growth in adjusted EBITDA, or earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, even as sales force turnover slowed new subscription bookings.

Three statements from management stand out.

Management identified $200 million in cost efficiencies across procurement and organizational design, building on $150 million in reductions already completed this year. These operational savings strengthen profitability and support the company's long-term target of $1.25 billion in adjusted EBITDA by 2028.

Annualized net new bookings fell to $37 million after sales headcount dropped 10% year over year, primarily among inside sales staff in Richmond. Management is decentralizing sales recruitment directly to city hubs to place experienced field professionals in front of local real estate agents.

The $730 million acquisition of Vexcel, an aerial imagery company, allows the business to use artificial intelligence, or automated computer vision software, to extract detailed property features globally. Capturing data directly from aerial imagery eliminates manual building inspections and speeds coverage expansion across international markets.

Operating margins are expanding across core commercial and residential platforms. The company relies on proprietary databases, broad audience reach, and high customer switching costs, which make changing software providers difficult. Management is watching sales force productivity and cross-selling discipline to compound earnings.

Scaling local field sales and integrating aerial imagery will settle whether subscription growth accelerates in 2027.
