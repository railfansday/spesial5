The Best 4K TVs Bring Everything You Watch and Play to Life

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

4K is the standard, and there are so many great options right now.

Now more than ever, you should hold your TV to a higher standard. More and more streaming content is available in 4K UHD resolution, which has four times as many pixels as standard Full HD (1080p). Video games, including PlayStation 5 and Nintendo Switch 2, support higher refresh rates that only TVs from the past few years support.

4K TVs are the standard, and have been for some time. Technically, 8K TVs are available, but there’s no indication that anyone is making content to take advantage of them anytime soon.

While tech prices are abnormally high in 2026, the tech is still improving rapidly. And since most of us buy a TV every five to 10 years, even last year’s models will offer a huge upgrade and are generally much more reasonably priced. New TVs are out there for everyone, at every price point.

I handpicked 4K TVs based on my testing and included options at various price points from current leading manufacturers: LG, Samsung, Hisense, and TCL. The selection process involved parsing through hardware specs, attending live demo sessions, and consulting with other industry experts.

Unless indicated otherwise, I usually test the 65-inch version of each TV, since it usually offers the best balance of price and capabilities as the primary home entertainment centerpiece in your space. Most sets are also available in smaller, less expensive versions. And many are available in even XXL formats, 75 inches and higher, for households with larger spaces and bigger budgets.

A note on these picks: Even though we’re well into 2026, we still primarily recommend TVs that launched in 2025, even when a newer version is available. From what my colleagues and I have seen so far, I believe that last year’s models represent the best possible value right now. In time, as last year’s models sell out, the value proposition will change. But trust me when I say that these TVs provide the best value and will make your shows, movies, and games look great for years to come.

Now that the price has come down a bit, we’re prepared to recommend this year’s LG C-series TV, the C6 evo, as the best option for most people. Though it isn't dramatically different from its predecessor, this mid-range OLED maintains the line’s legacy as impressively slim, elegant, and capable of delivering a stunning picture. It also runs a feature-packed platform with deep smart home integration.

With millions of individually backlit pixels, the C6’s OLED display is a rock-solid contender for the best in its price range. The panel’s flawless black reproduction delivers perfect contrast and vivid colors, making any content—from a newscast to a blockbuster movie to an AAA video game—look its best.

Speaking of gaming, the C6 is also one of the best TVs on the market for the task. In addition to being a great companion for the latest gaming consoles, the TV can double as a top-tier gaming monitor, thanks to a 165 Hz refresh rate and support for AMD FreeSync Premium and Nvidia G-Sync. It can also deliver high-powered cloud-based gameplay via an Nvidia GeForce Now subscription.

As with previous years, the C6 also gets extra credit for its wide size range. Depending on your needs and budget, you can choose a compact 42-inch, a private movie theater-worthy 83-inch model, or one of several additional sizes in between. Keep in mind that larger screen models have brighter Tandem OLED screens.

With a price cut shortly after launch, the TCL QM8L is ready to replace its predecessor, the QM8K, as the top Mini-LED TV. It looks incredibly sleek sans top and side bezels, and the picture looks just as impressive. While OLED technology still broadly produces the richest and sharpest image quality, mini-LED TVs are no slouch, delivering great detail and a brighter screen that improves performance in less-than-stellar lighting conditions.

With thousands of local dimming zones and an upgraded AI-powered processor to control them, the QM8L’s super quantum-dot mini-LED screen delivers a captivating picture with rich colors and deep contrast. The TV’s picture quality comes close to a top-tier OLED TV. Its higher peak brightness and lower cost sufficiently make up for its lack of individually backlit pixels.

If you are looking for a mini-LED 4K TV with SQD color proficiency on a tighter budget, I think this model’s more affordable cousin, the TCL QM7L, is also a very good choice. You lose some peak brightness and local dimming zones, but it still provides amazing performance for its much lower price.

RGB Mini-LED TVs deliver a unique combination of high peak brightness and stellar color accuracy, with the latter made possible by separate red, blue, and green light diodes instead of a single one in their display panels. Of all the TV newcomers to offer this screen technology in 2026, the Hisense UR9 is the one I’d recommend to most buyers. Beyond the exceptional picture quality from its new display panel, the TV also delivers surprisingly powerful sound and a rich set of futureproof connectivity features.

In addition to producing a bright picture with vibrant-yet-accurate colors and deep contrast, the Hisense UR9 surprised me pleasantly by handling direct light reflections surprisingly well. Dubbed “Obsidian” by its maker, the standout glare-reduction feature results in a top-tier viewing experience in bright spaces.

The TV’s screen supports a high 180Hz refresh rate, cutting latency in video games and smoothing blurred movement in live sports. I’m also a big fan of its 4.1.2-channel speaker system tuned by Devialet — a French luxury audio brand — and its two-year warranty.

Consider the Hisense UR8 if you are looking for an RGB Mini-LED TV on a tighter budget. It is a step down in peak brightness and audio performance from the UR9, as its model number suggests, but the lower price makes up for the shortcomings. Unlike its flagship sibling, the UR8 is also available with a 55-inch screen.

TCL’s QM6K is my favorite 4K TV on a budget. Spending less than $600 before additional deals and discounts, gets you a TV with a sleek design, superb picture quality, a feature-packed platform, and an abundant set of connectivity options. With a high-performance game mode and support for variable refresh rate, the TV is also a top-notch gaming console display.

The TCL QM6K delivers superb picture quality and color rendering for a TV at such an agreeable price point. Its quantum-dot mini-LED display panel with hundreds of local dimming zones produces punchy colors and dramatic contrast that defy its price category. I can’t think of a better 4K TV for the money.

On the audio front, the TV is as impressive as it is in terms of picture quality. Its speaker system, tuned by Onkyo, has a built-in subwoofer, allowing it to deliver immersive sound with surprisingly powerful bass.

TCL offers the QM6K in several screen sizes, with the 98-inch model as the largest. The biggest screen size costs a little over $2,000, which is quite a bargain for the full-blown private movie theater experience it provides. The 85-inch version at a tad above $1,000 is also a great value for money.

The Samsung S95H is the 4K TV to get if you want stunning design and picture quality to match. The TV’s metal frame gives it a unique presence, especially when wall-mounted, and makes any rival look generic in comparison. Its super-bright QD-OLED display predictably produces vibrant colors and deep contrast. Plus, a sophisticated onboard processor with access to AI neural networks makes lower-resolution content look sharper than ever.

Most importantly, the Samsung S95H can effortlessly deliver this viewing experience from any angle, even in bright spaces. The TV’s screen has a glare-free finish, which makes it practically impervious to light reflections. This means its picture quality stays superb even when it’s facing a lamp, a window, or another light source. This quality alone makes the TV worth every penny.

In keeping with its price category, the Samsung S95H is also a bona fide gaming powerhouse, capable of delivering exceptional visuals when connected to a gaming console or a high-powered computer. Samsung’s Tizen platform also supports cloud gaming, so all you need is a wireless controller and a monthly subscription to enjoy AAA titles.

We like that the TV’s intuitive remote has a rechargeable battery that you can charge via a light source or a USB-C port, so you never have to swap batteries. Its multi-speaker Dolby Atmos audio system, meanwhile, defies its form factor.

The TCL X11L is the best big-screen TV on the market today, with an elegant design, outstanding picture quality, and immersive sound tuned by Bang & Olufsen. We often think of TCL as the bang for your buck, but this set shows the brand can also deliver something out of this world for folks willing to spend big for the best possible picture.

The secret is the X11L's first-of-its-kind SQD Mini-LED display—that's "Super Quantum Dot"—which blends brightness-enhancing quantum dot technology with Mini-LED dimming zones to boost high-dynamic-range performance.

At a $7,000 launch price, it was hard to imagine not insisting on an OLED TV over the TCL X11L, but things have changed dramatically since then. Now that it starts at $3,000 for a 75-inch version, the high-end TCL offering is a great buy. Its color accuracy and contrast are both top-notch, and the overall picture quality is tremendous. Frankly, I’d take the TV over any OLED on the market today.

TVs that double as home decor pieces by displaying artwork or photos became a thing when Samsung launched The Frame almost a decade ago. Since then, frame-like TVs have grown from a niche category into something with broader appeal and a surprisingly rich selection across the price spectrum. The Hisense S7 CanvasTV is the one I’d recommend to most buyers in 2026, because it strikes a fine balance between elegant design, stellar picture quality, and a rich selection of onboard features, all at a reasonable price and in multiple screen sizes.

Thanks to a quantum-dot display with a matte finish to reduce pesky reflections and an ambient light sensor, the S7 CanvasTV can display beautiful visuals in any scenario, whether it’s live sports or a vacation photo. Still, you should know the device’s picture quality can’t match that of similarly priced conventional Hisense TVs or rival brands with quantum-dot or RGB mini-LED screens. The matte screen, customizable frame, and slim wall mount make the S7 CanvasTV worth considering, since it will look great when not in use.

When it comes to alternatives, the first that comes to mind is Samsung's The Frame Pro. It offers a more polished design and a fancier quantum-dot mini-LED panel with superior glare resistance and artwork display capabilities. The Frame Pro will look much nicer as an art piece, but I’d still take the Hisense S7 CanvasTV for offering Dolby Vision HDR support and a full set of built-in connectivity features. Samsung has these essentials in a separate box for The Frame Pro.

Though they all look like black slabs on the wall, the quality of the images produced by 4K TVs can vary dramatically from model to model. Many also have distinctive features that make them better for specific applications—like a high refresh rate for gaming. And, of course, all that impacts how much they cost. There is all manner of exotic formulae for determining the exact distance you should sit from a large screen TV—X-times the screen height, X-times the screen’s diagonal size—but there isn’t a right or wrong answer. The best rule of thumb is if the screen fills your frame of vision from where you’re sitting, and you don’t have to turn your head to see one side of the screen or the other, you’re fine. (Note: Sitting too close to a 4K TV won’t ruin your eyes despite what your mother may have told you.)

What size TV you choose, however, may be predetermined by your room geography and/or furnishings, such as the interior of an existing TV cabinet, or dictated by a spouse’s objections to a larger-size set. If you plan to buy a new TV larger than your current set, measure the width, height, and depth of your home’s TV space, and then check the actual width and height of the TV with stand, not the diagonal screen size. A flat panel 65-inch screen 4K TV usually measures less than 60 inches wide, less than 36 inches tall, and about 15 inches deep with the stand.

Some TVs come with center stands, while many larger models have feet at either end of the set, which may be too widely spaced apart for your TV stand. Check the width of your TV tabletop and the space between the feet of your prospective TV purchase to make sure its feet will fit.

Planning to hang your TV on a wall? All TVs follow a version of the VESA (Video Electronics Standards Association) wall-mounting standard, including variations such as VESA MIS-D, MIS-E, and MIS-F, which mainly address a TV’s rear mounting-hole spacing, supportable weight, and size.

A VESA wall mounting kit can work with virtually any wall-mountable TV, but double-check your TV’s specifications to confirm any VESA variation.

A TV screen's image speed is measured by its refresh rate, expressed in hertz (Hz). This equals frames flashed per second; for comparison, movie film is projected at 24 frames per second, while Normal TV broadcasts refresh at 60 Hz, or 60 frames per second.

If you’re a heavy gamer, you want a display that more accurately reflects the eye-blinking speed of the animated action, which means you’ll want a TV with a refresh rate of at least 120 Hz or higher, and a set equipped with VRR (variable refresh rate), which automatically adjusts the refresh rate depending on the content being displayed.

Another screen technology gamers want is low latency, or input lag, which is how quickly the display responds when you push a button on your controller or joystick. Many higher-end TVs include Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM), which automatically switches your TV to a faster, more responsive mode measured in milliseconds (ms).

Different HDR versions include the standard and most common HDR, as well as the more specialized HDR-10, HDR-10+, Dolby Vision, and HLG (Hybrid Log Gamma), designed to deliver HDR benefits to lower non-4K broadcast, cable, and satellite TV fare.

While there are subtle visual benefits associated with each type of HDR, and while some TV makers, 4K Blu-ray producers, and streaming channels opt to deploy some HDR versions but not others, compatibility really isn’t an issue since all include a plain and widely compatible HDR version.

Bottom line: Any set featuring any type of HDR will produce a visibly better 4K picture than a set without HDR.

8K TV makers may not want to hear this, but probably never. 8K TVs offer a display with a resolution of 7,680 x 4,320 pixels, or 33 million pixels, more than four times the 8 million pixels that comprise a 4K TV display.

Prices are coming down to near 4K TV price levels for 8K models. But, for a variety of reasons, there is no existing 8K content—no 8K broadcast or cable, no 8K streaming, no 8K Blu-ray—and there isn’t likely to be for the foreseeable future.

The only benefits of an 8K TV are via upscaling or up-conversion of lower-resolution programming to 8K. But realistically you’ll need at least a 75-inch TV to appreciate the difference between 4K and 8K.

Imagine watching the movie "Jaws" without feeling that "bum-bum, bum-bum" bass in your gut, or not being able to understand character dialogue over a swelling orchestral passage, or subtle sounds that lend a dramatic scene its key ambience.

A 4K TV may produce excellent picture quality, but most do not produce equally excellent sound, which can dull or ruin your viewing experience. There is a fix for this. Instead of relying on a TV’s poor audio, you may want to pair your 4K TV with a soundbar via an ARC (Audio Return Channel) or eARC (enhanced Audio Return Channel) HDMI connection.

An ARC-compatible soundbar connected to a TV via an ARC HDMI jack lets you control your TV’s picture and sound with a single remote, as if the soundbar were your TV’s speaker.

Just make sure to measure the space between your TV tabletop and the bottom of the TV display area so your prospective soundbar won’t block either the screen or the TV’s IR remote control sensor.
