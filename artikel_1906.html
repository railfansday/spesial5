Robertson: Scotland ‘don’t have time’ to turn things around

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Andy Robertson conceded Scotland do not have time to turn things around after they were beaten 4-0 by Switzerland in the Nations League on Monday.

Robertson, making his 103rd appearance for Scotland and becoming the nation's most-capped men's player, watched on as his team slumped to another disappointing defeat.

Lewis Ferguson's own goal had given Switzerland a 22nd-minute lead before quickfire strikes from Winsley Boteli and Alvyn Sanches put the game out of sight.

The win was sealed in the 89th minute by Zeki Amdouni, with Scotland head coach Sebastien Pocognoli suffering another damaging loss so early into his tenure.

Indeed, Scotland suffered their outright biggest defeat in the Nations League. It was their heaviest loss in all competitions since losing 5-1 against Germany at EURO 2024.

Pocognoli has won just two of his first six matches since replacing Steve Clarke, both against North Macedonia, and conceded 10 goals in that time.

"First half, I thought we were in the game, we lost a stupid goal, but I thought we had chances and could have been 2-1 at half-time," a frustrated Robertson said.

"By the time you get to 50 minutes, and you're 3-0 down, that can't happen in international football. It is really tough for us.

"I don't think you've got time in international football, you have to do it. These supporters who travel everywhere, are they going to give us time? No.

"Are the people inside Hampden going to give us time? No. Are the people outside Hampden who are going to be critical and rightly so? No.

"We don't have time, the players do not have time, we have got to step up.

"We have a long break now until March, which is not a good thing, and it is going to be a tough one to swallow."

Scotland's defeat saw them finish Group B1 in third, meaning they will be placed in pot three for Euro 2028 qualifying.

The loss came after Switzerland boss Murat Yakin make eight changes to his team, with the average age of his staring line-up being 26 years and 250 days, the youngest he had named in his time in charge (71 matches).

Boteli (20y 134d) became the youngest player to both score and assist in a game for Switzerland since Johan Manzambi against the United States in June 2025 (19y 240d).

Switzerland have now won consecutive games against Scotland in all competitions for the first time ever, while they are now unbeaten against them in five home games (W4 D1).

When asked if he was confident the team is on the right path, John McGinn said: "Well, obviously, the trajectory of the results means it's impossible for me to say yes.

"But we have been here before. Unfortunately, we've been in moments where you need to stick together, show your qualities, players need to come into the group and add more and you need to turn results around, which we have shown before.

"We've qualified for three out of four major tournaments and if we are going to qualify for a fourth, we need to be much better.

"We know that, we know what it takes, but you don't have time in football like a bunch of players to do that, so come March we need to be a lot better and improve.

"The manager can't kick the ball for us. It's up to us to make the right decisions in those moments, execute them, show a bit of quality and be a lot better on the ball, be a lot better off the ball, and that doesn't matter what tactics you have or what formation you play."
