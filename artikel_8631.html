When food money is tight, build your family’s meals around a dozen cheap staples, such as rice, dried beans, eggs, and potatoes, and cook them from scratch instead of buying boxed or frozen dinners. Make enough at dinner to pack the next day’s lunches.

Penn State Extension priced a chicken, rice, and vegetable dinner two ways, premade and homemade, and the homemade version cost about half as much.

At the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ October 2026 average prices, a pound of white rice costs $1.10 and cooks into about 7½ cups, roughly 7 cents per half cup. A pound of pasta costs $1.38, about 7 to 13 cents per half cup cooked, depending on the shape. Dried beans average $1.68 a pound, and a whole chicken $2.03 a pound. A dozen eggs costs $2.32, about 19 cents an egg.

The USDA’s most recent fruit and vegetable prices, from 2023, put dried beans at 23 to 31 cents a cooked cup. Potatoes, cabbage, carrots, and bananas run about 30 to 40 cents a cup, and frozen mixed vegetables 66 to 76 cents. Oats and peanut butter round out the dozen.
