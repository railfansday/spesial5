Shop from a written list, carry a basket or the smallest cart, and buy nothing that isn’t on the list. Supermarkets and big-box stores stack displays at the end of each aisle and line the checkout with candy to sell you more than you came for.

A quick stop for milk shows how the layout works. The dairy case often sits along the back wall, so the walk there can take you past the bakery and the end caps, the displays at the end of each aisle.

End caps sit right in your path, and a product on one sells far more than it does on its usual shelf, marked down or not. A display alone doesn’t mean a lower price. Pricier brands often sit at eye level, the shelf you see first, with cheaper ones above and below. Look up and down, and compare the unit price, the cost per ounce or count, where the shelf tag shows one. Checkout candy sits within reach while you wait in line.

Stores use pricing tricks too. A $4.99 price can feel closer to $4 than to $5, because your eye lands on the first digit. A “10 for $10” sign suggests you need to buy 10. Unless its small print says you must buy them all, one usually rings up at $1, so buy the number you need.
