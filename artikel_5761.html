NextEra-Dominion merger hearings kick off with focus on scope

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The case is “simply not the one to litigate the cost of service [or] who’s paying their fair share ... including the data centers,” said Joe Reid, an attorney representing NextEra and Dominion.

The proposed merger between Dominion and NextEra, announced in May, would create the largest regulated electric utility in the world, with a 130-GW large load pipeline. Clean energy groups, local Virginia officials and leaders of the state’s executive branch have raised concerns about the potential impact of the deal on rates and data center development.

McGuire Woods partner Joe Reid, representing the joint petitioners NextEra and Dominion, said the case “is simply not the one to litigate the cost of service, and nor is it the case to litigate who's paying their fair share and ensuring everybody pays their fair share, including the data centers.”

Reid noted that the proposed acquisition is a “consequential combination, but its size does not change the requirements of the Transfers Act or the analysis required to determine whether the statute's requirements have been met.”

SCC Chair Kelsey Bagot asked Reid if the commission “should not consider longer-term benefits in evaluating this proposed transaction,” and Reid replied that the commission “should consider all the evidence that is in front of it.”

Nate Benforado, a senior attorney with the Southern Environmental Law Center, argued that the Transfers Act is less narrow than the joint petitioners claimed.

“The analysis that the Utility Transfers Act requires is broad,” Benforado said. “Petitioners must prove that this transaction will not impair or jeopardize adequate service at just and reasonable rates … This is not simply a ‘do no harm standard’. This looks at virtually everything a utility does.”

Will Reisinger, representing the Piedmont Environmental Council, pointed out that the statute provides that “before approving this merger, the commission ‘shall be satisfied’ that adequate service to the public at just and reasonable rates will not be impaired or jeopardized.”

“That standard, satisfied, is subjective,” he said. “This is not a matter of calling a ball or a strike. This decision is 100% within your judgment, within your discretion.”

Will Cleveland, the attorney representing Google, said that the company plans to present testimony from energy economist Carolyn Berry, “who will testify that the minimum demand charge approved as part of Dominion's 2025 biennial rate case discourages large loads like data centers from taking advantage of their statutory right to retail choice.” Dominion has said “they don't think this testimony is relevant to the case,” Cleveland said, but Google disagrees.

Amazon’s attorney, Brian Greene, said that that company would want to see Dominion address two issues if the commission were to approve the transaction: “One is reforming the large load interconnection queue, and second is addressing the need for new capacity through bring-your-own options,” he said.

Greene said that Amazon proposes that “if the transaction is approved, Dominion be required to reform its study process through a cluster study approach that evaluates projects together and looks for coordinated system-wide solutions … Approving the merger without reforming the interconnection queue would be like putting more power behind the same undersized wire.”
