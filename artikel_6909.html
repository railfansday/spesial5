One Martini to Rule Them All

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The case for ordering a vodka martini, always.

At a dinner a few years ago, my fiancé’s mother, an elegant former Wall Street banker, ordered a vodka martini, dry, with three olives. I grew up in New Orleans, where I was weaned on sickeningly sweet, sticky cocktails, but I was always fascinated by the martini’s sophistication. I’d seen the drink in the hands of characters like Roger Sterling from Mad Men, who would expertly balance his V-shaped glass as he dropped a cynical witticism. So that night, I told the waiter to make it two. Ever since then, I have favored vodka martinis over gin, the base spirit for the by-the-books recipe.

I’m not the only one. Bartenders in New York have started to notice a trend away from gin. Chris Amirault of Bar Chimera in Manhattan says that he makes four vodka martinis for every one gin martini. “Vodka pays the bills, and it’s what a lot of people prefer,” says Brian Evans of the Lobby Bar at the Hotel Chelsea, also in Manhattan.

For those that insist that a true martini must only be made with gin, Tristan Stephenson, host of The Curious Bartender podcast, says, “History isn’t on the side of purists. The martini has been a moving target since the 1880s. It started sweeter, with maraschino, orange curaçao, and sugar syrup making appearances. Then it dried out over decades, then had the vermouth all but exorcised from it. Then came olive brine and everything since.”

Why do many martini drinkers prefer vodka? Ti Adelaide Martin, coproprietor of New Orleans staple Commander’s Palace, succinctly explains one reason: “Vodka is the more approachable spirit.” Unlike gin, whose botanicals impart noticeable flavors to a martini, vodka doesn’t overpower other ingredients. It’s more of a blank canvas, which is why it’s used for variations like the espresso martini, which is now the fifth-most-ordered drink at bars, according to Drinks International. Instead of strong flavor, vodka provides “texture, weight, and a kind of icy austerity that can be genuinely beautiful when the vodka is good and the drink is properly cold,” says Stephenson.

And, let’s be honest, we also drink vodka martinis for the booze. “Vodka tastes bracing,” says Esquire editor-in-chief Michael Sebastian. “I want to take a sip of my martini and get the feeling that it’s strong and it's cold, and it goes through your whole body and you feel better and loose immediately.”

Now I order my vodka martini with a splash of vermouth, just dirty enough to make me thirsty, and shaken so that thin ice shards float on top. You might prefer yours vermouth forward, citrus focused, or dirty as swamp water. It’s all fair game. And that’s the beauty of a vodka martini: It’s a personal drink. My future mother-in-law has her order, and I have mine. What’s yours going to be?
