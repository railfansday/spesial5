live-build documentary captures 1:1 timber pavilions at studio in the woods workshop

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

marking the 20th anniversary of studio in the woods, the documentary crossing, lookout, horizon chronicles the design and construction of three full-scale timber installations built from site-felled timber at the AA’s hooke park campus.

Rob Fiehn edited by christina vergopoulou November 4, 2026 4 min

Marking the 20th anniversary of the architectural live-build summer school Studio in the Woods (SiTW), a new documentary short titled Crossing, Lookout, Horizon records the initiative’s 2026 session hosted at the Architectural Association’s (AA) Hooke Park campus in Dorset, UK. Founded two decades ago by Piers Taylor of Invisible Studio alongside architects Meredith Bowles, Kate Darby, Gianni Botsford, and Peter Clegg, with early mentorship from Ted Cullinan, the program functions as a hands-on pedagogical alternative to traditional studio-based design education.

Operating without predetermined briefs, the annual workshop convenes roughly 50 students, practitioners, and timber specialists over an intensive four-day build cycle. Participants design and assemble 1:1 structural interventions using timber harvested and milled directly from the surrounding woodland. Sited within Hooke Park’s working forest, the 2026 iteration produced three site-specific timber installations, Crossing, Lookout, and Horizon, testing material optimization, reciprocal framing, and direct fabrication methods.

Across its twenty-year history, Studio in the Woods has convened a broad network of practitioners, structural engineers, and timber fabricators to lead its on-site workshops. The teaching roster includes Stirling Prize winner Niall McLaughlin, Lebanese-French architect Lina Ghotmeh, and collaborative design practice Architecture 00. Instruction pairs architectural theory with structural pragmatism through contributions from master timber framer Charley Brentnall, former Hooke Park director Martin Self, Lee Ivett of Baxendale, and structural engineers Tim Lucas (Price & Myers) and Toby McClean (Tall Engineers). Additional past workshop leaders include Akos Juhasz, Fergus Feilden of Feilden Fowles, Smith Mordak, Shin Egashira of the Architectural Association, Hannah Durham, Zoe Berman, and Je Ahn of Studio Weave. This multi-disciplinary teaching body grounds the program in a synthesis of structural calculation, joinery craft, and material testing directly in the field.

enjoying the ‘lookout’ structure firsthand

Parallel to the physical construction, the 2026 iteration integrated a specialized film mentorship program directed by architectural documentarians Nyima Murry and Jim Stephenson. Designed to lower barriers of entry into architectural cinematography for students from historically underrepresented backgrounds, an open call selected three architecture students with limited film experience: Tong Hao, Jaime Nelson, and Hamani Altraiji. Following technical modules on visual storytelling, equipment rigging, and sound recording, the fellows embedded within the Hooke Park working forest for four days. Working from a documentary brief devised by SiTW co-founder Piers Taylor of Invisible Studio, the film team tracked the live assembly of the three installations, Crossing, Lookout, and Horizon, recording structural trials, ambient woodland acoustics, and real-time timber manipulation to produce a permanent document of the collective live-build methodology.
