Four-Legged Surfers Make a Splash at International Surf Tournament

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Sayulita, on the coast of Nayarit, Mexico, is a dog-friendly surf town. Many pups spend their mornings with their paws in the sand while their owners head into the water to catch some waves. They dig the sand, run and play along the shore, and many sit patiently waiting for their parents to return. But, dogs being dogs, it's not hard to imagine how some of them have naturally followed their human right into the water to discover what the fun is all about.

For a surprising number of beach-going canines, surfing has become part of their world, and this year, the first dog-surfing contest in Sayulita took place as part of the International DÃ­a de Muertos Surf Tournament, held October 29 through November 1, 2026, at Playa Principal. Twelve dog-and-handler teams participated, making this a pawticularly special addition to the town's surf calendar. The main tournament, now in its third edition, brought together more than 120 athletes across several surfing disciplines.

The official program listed the dog category as an exhibition, with a prize pool of 10,000 Mexican pesos distributed among the top three. But for the pups, there was no question about the attraction: a surfboard, some waves, the chance to have fun with their favorite person, and a beach full of admirers? Paw me in!

Sayulita has been part of my life for a long time. As a teenager, I spent many weekends camping there, long before the main entrance road was paved or I imagined I would return as a veterinarian or spend time pet-sitting here.

In July and August this year, I looked after a wonderful rescue pup in Sayulita, and our daily walks took us along the main beach, past the local cemetery, for our daily swim in the beautiful Playa de los Muertos. It was a lovely way to experience the town beyond its busy surf beaches.

Sayulita is genuinely pet-friendly. Dogs are welcome on its beaches, and most restaurants accommodate four-legged visitors. They are part of the scenery, whether accompanying their pet parents through town or waiting patiently on the sand while someone catches a wave.

That makes the new dog-surfing event feel like a natural progression. Dogs have long been part of the local beach scene. This time, they got their own chance to take to the spotlight in the water.

First place went to Suleiman Navarro and Toto, a blind Pug from Punta de Mita. Toto's visual impairment didn't keep him from joining the fun and riding the waves with Sule. Second place went to Fernando Stalla and Miky, who already enjoy surfing in Sayulita from time to time.

Risa Mara Machuca, known through @surfitoutmexico, helped bring the dog-surfing category to the event. Her own surfing partner, Chia, is already familiar with the sport beyond Sayulita. In August 2025, Chia and Risa became the first Mexican team to compete in the World Dog Surfing Competition in Pacifica, California.

Their surfing story began when Chia, who loved following Risa into the ocean, climbed onto a soft-top surfboard one day and headed straight for the front. Risa looked up at the sky and shouted, "Thank you, universe!"

It's hard not to appreciate a pup who discovers and genuinely enjoys a hobby shared with her favorite person. Chia's enthusiasm has helped give Sayulita a local connection to a sport attracting canine-human teams internationally.

My visit to Sayulita during DÃ­a de Muertos offered another glimpse into how much the community values their pups. The plaza and surrounding streets were specially decorated for the occasion. On the evening of November 1, we watched a procession leave the plaza as people gathered to honor loved ones who had passed away. Families continued the tradition of remembering the departed through decorations, flowers, and time spent together.

Many local establishments created traditional altars dedicated to their departed pets, with photographs and little tributes to beloved dogs included among the traditional decorations. It was lovely to see these four-legged family members remembered and celebrated as part of the town's DÃ­a de Muertos traditions.

October 27 has become a popular date in Mexico for remembering pets during the DÃ­a de Muertos season. Families may prepare small altars with photographs, favorite treats, toys, and water. This particular observance is a relatively recent popular custom rather than an ancient tradition followed everywhere, but it has found a meaningful place in many households.

Anyone who has loved and lost a dog can understand why. Pets become part of our daily routines, our families, and our memories. Seeing their photographs included in these celebrations gave me a lovely glimpse into the affection people in Sayulita have for their canine companions.

It also brought together two sides of the human-animal bond: remembering the dogs who are no longer with us while celebrating the ones still sharing our adventures.

Of course, the fun of watching dogs surf is only part of what made this event special. It also brought together two things that are so much a part of Sayulita: its surf culture and its affection for dogs.

One of the nicest parts of returning to town was seeing how naturally dogs fit into both everyday life and local traditions. From the pups waiting patiently on the beach as their owners enjoy the waves, to the little altars honoring those who had passed away, there were reminders everywhere of the special place dogs hold in this community. Watching these dogs enjoy the waves alongside their pet parents was a lovely addition to a weekend already filled with celebration, music, and remembrance.
