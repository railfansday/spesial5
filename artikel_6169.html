China Eastern’s Ex-Chairman Gets 14 Years For $10 Million In Bribes—He Didn’t Follow Xi Jinping’s Instructions

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

China Eastern’s former chairman has been sentenced to 14 years in prison in a bribery case involving more than $10 million. The court says Liu Shaoyong sold favors over a period stretching from 2002 through 2023, using positions at both China Eastern and China Southern. He’d left China Eastern in 2022 with a glowing sendoff from the board. Three years later, the Communist Party announced it was investigating him. Corruption can be real, many state bureaucrats are at least a little corrupt Prosecuting often serves a political purpose. A conviction tells us what (who) the government decided to punish. So what’s always interesting in these cases is why this one, and why now. The Shenyang Intermediate People’s Court handed down its verdict on November 3. It found that Liu took more than 67.5 million yuan in bribes (US$10.1 million) in exchange for help with business operations, project contracts, and airline route management. He was also fined 4 million yuan (US$600,000) and disgorgement of his criminal proceeds. Liu admitted guilt at his July 24 trial. The court says it gave him a lighter sentence because he confessed and disclosed bribery facts investigators hadn’t yet uncovered. The party’s earlier disciplinary findings accused him of taking money for hiring favors, improperly holding shares in unlisted companies, helping relatives’ businesses including private equity funds, and accepting payments connected with aircraft leasing and flight management. There were also allegations about gifts, banquets, golf outings, resisting the investigation, and superstitious activities. A former pilot and deputy head of the country’s aviation regulator, Liu went on to lead China Southern before returning to China Eastern in 2008. He was credited with helping turn around both airlines. At China Eastern, his tenure included the Shanghai Airlines merger, entry into SkyTeam, and bringing private investors into parts of the business. The Party Was Already Looking At China Eastern I’ve written about this before with the detention of HNA’s leaders and the government’s takeover of Anbang Insurance. With Liu, there’s a more concrete place to start than guessing which faction he offended. A central party inspection team examined China Eastern from October through December 2023. A February 2025 report covered corruption leads and tightening controls over procurement. Liu was expelled from the party in January 2026. The report puts following Xi Jinping’s instructions, supporting Chinese aircraft, operating at Beijing Daxing, and strengthening party supervision alongside its discussion of corruption. So this was as much about carrying out the party’s priorities as about how he conducted business. The inspection records don’t tell us what finally triggered Liu’s prosecution. They do show specific problems during his tenure, and where running the airline could run into the party’s priorities: Party supervision and insider dealings. In 2015, inspectors reported executives setting up related companies in the same business, extensive use of cargo intermediaries, duplicative rewards for ticket agents, and large numbers of free or discounted tickets. Liu jointly headed the cleanup yet a 2019 inspection again flagged corruption and incomplete fixes. Bringing in private investors while maintaining party control. Liu’s 2017 restructuring of Eastern Air Logistics left China Eastern’s group with 45% ownership, outside investors with 45%, and employees with 10%. A 2020 report flagged shortcomings in integrating party leadership into companies with outside shareholders. It seems like Liu’s efforts at privatization may have provided insufficient Party control. Developing Beijing Daxing even when the airline had commercial reasons to prefer Capital airport. Reporting in 2019 described China Eastern resisting a wholesale move, with concerns about losing business travelers on routes like Beijing–Shanghai. Liu nevertheless publicly outlined plans for around 200 aircraft at Daxing. The government called for more capacity, a better route network, and faster construction of facilities supporting maintenance. By late 2024, party discipline officials were monitoring aircraft deployment and passenger numbers there. Liu resisted building up the Party’s showcase airport. Making Chinese aircraft succeed commercially. Liu was there when China Eastern signed its first firm order for five C919s in March 2021. The airline had also established a subsidiary to operate Chinese regional jets. The C919 only received its type certificate in September 2022, after he left. So Liu didn’t refuse Chinese planes. But the government called for broader route coverage, stronger marketing, and better operating support. They bought the planes but didn’t make the aircraft program work (and they can’t really blame the aircraft, so blame the airlines). Following Xi’s safety instructions. The report published in March 2020 described units giving insufficient attention to important safety and operating issues, failing to escalate major safety matters, and failing to act effectively on identified problems. It called for stronger safety oversight, clearer accountability, and changes involving flight operations, maintenance, and operational control. China Eastern had a crash after the report where the party said Liu’s airline needed to improve. China Eastern flight 5735 crashed in March 2022 and killed 132 people. Liu left the group’s top job four months later. In June 2025, a scholar suggested the crash helped trigger scrutiny of him. Prosecuting Liu enforces discipline over top leadership running Chinese state airlines. I don’t know which patron, rival, or specific transaction made Liu vulnerable. China Eastern’s August 2022 resignation filing said Liu had “faithfully and diligently performed his duties” and thanked him for his “outstanding contributions.” The court now says his bribery stretched through 2023—beyond that farewell. I’ve written about this before with the detention of HNA’s leaders and the government’s takeover of Anbang Insurance. With Liu, there’s a more concrete place to start than guessing which faction he offended.

A central party inspection team examined China Eastern from October through December 2023. A February 2025 report covered corruption leads and tightening controls over procurement. Liu was expelled from the party in January 2026.

The report puts following Xi Jinping’s instructions, supporting Chinese aircraft, operating at Beijing Daxing, and strengthening party supervision alongside its discussion of corruption. So this was as much about carrying out the party’s priorities as about how he conducted business.

The inspection records don’t tell us what finally triggered Liu’s prosecution. They do show specific problems during his tenure, and where running the airline could run into the party’s priorities:

Bringing in private investors while maintaining party control. Liu’s 2017 restructuring of Eastern Air Logistics left China Eastern’s group with 45% ownership, outside investors with 45%, and employees with 10%. A 2020 report flagged shortcomings in integrating party leadership into companies with outside shareholders. It seems like Liu’s efforts at privatization may have provided insufficient Party control.

Developing Beijing Daxing even when the airline had commercial reasons to prefer Capital airport. Reporting in 2019 described China Eastern resisting a wholesale move, with concerns about losing business travelers on routes like Beijing–Shanghai. Liu nevertheless publicly outlined plans for around 200 aircraft at Daxing. The government called for more capacity, a better route network, and faster construction of facilities supporting maintenance. By late 2024, party discipline officials were monitoring aircraft deployment and passenger numbers there. Liu resisted building up the Party’s showcase airport.

Making Chinese aircraft succeed commercially. Liu was there when China Eastern signed its first firm order for five C919s in March 2021. The airline had also established a subsidiary to operate Chinese regional jets. The C919 only received its type certificate in September 2022, after he left. So Liu didn’t refuse Chinese planes. But the government called for broader route coverage, stronger marketing, and better operating support. They bought the planes but didn’t make the aircraft program work (and they can’t really blame the aircraft, so blame the airlines).

Following Xi’s safety instructions. The report published in March 2020 described units giving insufficient attention to important safety and operating issues, failing to escalate major safety matters, and failing to act effectively on identified problems. It called for stronger safety oversight, clearer accountability, and changes involving flight operations, maintenance, and operational control.

China Eastern had a crash after the report where the party said Liu’s airline needed to improve. China Eastern flight 5735 crashed in March 2022 and killed 132 people. Liu left the group’s top job four months later. In June 2025, a scholar suggested the crash helped trigger scrutiny of him.

Prosecuting Liu enforces discipline over top leadership running Chinese state airlines. I don’t know which patron, rival, or specific transaction made Liu vulnerable.

China Eastern’s August 2022 resignation filing said Liu had “faithfully and diligently performed his duties” and thanked him for his “outstanding contributions.” The court now says his bribery stretched through 2023—beyond that farewell.
