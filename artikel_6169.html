6-Hour Delay: easyJet Airbus A321neo Diverts After Engine Stall 2 Minutes Into Flight

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Edinburgh Airport (EDI) to London Gatwick Airport (LGW) is one of easyJet's top UK domestic routes, with the orange-clad budget carrier typically operating five flights a day on this Anglo-Scottish corridor. On Thursday evening, the last of these had to return to Edinburgh shortly after takeoff in dramatic fashion following an engine shutdown, with witnesses reporting a loud bang and streaks of flame.

The aircraft involved in the incident, an Airbus A321neo with the registration G-UZMC, had taken off punctually, but the need for it to return to Edinburgh ended up delaying its passengers considerably. With the plane subsequently having to be taken out of service, easyJet had to dispatch a replacement up from London Gatwick. As such, the flight's passengers only reached England early on Friday morning.

easyJet flight U2-816 is usually scheduled to leave Edinburgh at 8:45 PM, and flight tracking data shows that it did so punctually on Thursday night, taking to the skies at 8:46 PM. However, having lifted off from runway 24, things took a dramatic turn when, as reported by the Aviation Safety Network citing ACARS data, the number one engine suffered a stall just two minutes after the aircraft's departure.

This, the Aviation Herald notes, prompted the crew to level off the climb at an altitude of 5,000 feet and shut down the affected engine. They then entered a holding pattern, as seen above, and circled for a short while before touching back down on runway 06 at 9:29 PM. As detailed above, the flight to London normally takes 1.5 hours. easyJet spokesperson reported a "technical issue" to Simple Flying, adding:

"The flight landed normally and was met by emergency services in line with procedures and as a precautionary measure only. A replacement aircraft was arranged to operate the flight to London Gatwick later that evening."

Along with the stall, excessively high exhaust gas temperatures are also said to have been a contributing factor in the decision to shut down the engine and return to Edinburgh. Local residents in and around the Scottish capital city reported a loud bang at around the time of the incident, although, according to The Independent, Edinburgh Airport later confirmed that "there was no impact on airport operations."

With the aircraft safely back on the ground, easyJet was able to take it out of service and assess its next steps. At the time of writing on Friday afternoon, it has yet to leave Edinburgh. However, the airline also had a plane-load of passengers requiring onward travel to London, and no spare aircraft to hand in the Scottish capital city. As such, it had to fly one up from London Gatwick later that night.

This, the Aviation Herald notes, ended up being a 17-year-old Airbus A320-200 that bears the registration G-EZTH. It departed LGW at 12:51 AM on Friday under the ferry flight number U2-9006, reaching EDI 61 minutes later at 1:52 AM. Its turnaround took just over an hour, and U2-816 left at 2:56 AM. It touched down an hour later, representing a five-hour and 41-minute delay vs the planned 10:15 PM arrival.
