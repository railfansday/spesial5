$3.4 Billion: Boeing Finalizes Order For Up To 20 787-10s From Philippine Airlines

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Philippine Airlines has finalized contracts with Boeing for 15 Boeing 787-10 Dreamliners, with purchase rights for five additional aircraft, completing a deal that could cover up to 20 widebodies. The agreement, announced on November 15, is the largest widebody purchase in the Philippine flag carrier’s history. The aircraft will be powered by GE Aerospace GEnx-1B engines and are intended to support fleet modernization and growth across medium- and long-haul routes.

Earlier this year, Reuters reported an estimated headline value of $3.4 billion if all 20 aircraft are acquired, based on post-discount pricing estimates reported by Reuters in July. Boeing and Philippine Airlines have not disclosed the finalized contract value. The firm commitment is for 15 aircraft, while the remaining five are not firm orders. The agreements were signed on November 10, converting a commitment first announced at the Farnborough International Airshow in July into definitive contracts.

The November contracts formalize Philippine Airlines' July commitment for 15 Boeing 787-10s and the opportunity to acquire five more. The five additional aircraft remain purchase rights, not guaranteed deliveries. If exercised, they would bring the total to 20. GE Aerospace's GEnx-1B engines are included in the agreements, giving the aircraft a confirmed propulsion supplier. The selection extends a relationship between GE and Philippine Airlines that spans more than five decades. The GEnx family is also used on Boeing 787 variants operated by other international carriers, providing an established engine platform rather than introducing a new type into the airline's maintenance planning.

Deliveries are scheduled for the next decade, although the latest announcement did not provide a detailed timetable. Reuters reported in July that deliveries were expected to begin in 2031, while Philippine Airlines' November announcement used the broader timeframe. The airline has not identified individual delivery years or specified when the five additional purchase rights might be exercised. The order is therefore a long-term commitment rather than an immediate capacity increase. Philippine Airlines will need to plan aircraft financing, crew training, maintenance arrangements, and fleet transitions well ahead of the first arrival.

As the largest member of the 787 family, the 787-10 is designed to carry more passengers than the smaller 787-8 and 787-9. Boeing lists a typical passenger capacity of 300 to 375, depending on cabin configuration. Its composite airframe and modern systems are designed to improve fuel efficiency compared with older-generation widebodies.

Boeing says the 787 family uses approximately 25% less fuel than the aircraft it typically replaces. Actual savings depend on route length, passenger load, cabin layout, and operating conditions, but improved efficiency can help airlines reduce costs on fuel-intensive international services. The aircraft also offers passenger features associated with the Dreamliner family, including larger dimmable windows, higher cabin humidity, and systems designed to reduce the effects of turbulence.

The 787-10's larger capacity makes it particularly relevant to routes with substantial demand. However, it does not have the same range capability as the smaller 787-9, meaning Philippine Airlines will need to match the aircraft to suitable markets rather than assume it can serve every long-haul destination in its network. The airline has not announced which routes will receive the new type or disclosed its planned seating configuration.

The three 787 variants are tailored to different market segments to fill niches and offer a placement for different previous-generation aircraft.

Dreamliners will form part of a broader modernization strategy as Philippine Airlines prepares to replace older widebody aircraft and support international growth. The carrier currently operates Boeing 777s alongside Airbus A330s and A350s. Adding the 787-10 will introduce another widebody type, but its capacity and efficiency could provide a replacement option for older aircraft as they approach the end of their service lives or lease agreements.

The Boeing purchase also follows Philippine Airlines' July announcement of a separate agreement with Airbus. The airline signed a memorandum of understanding for nine A350-1000s, with purchase rights for five additional aircraft. That proposal is separate from the finalized Boeing contract and is intended to strengthen the carrier's ability to serve its longest international routes. The two programs suggest a differentiated fleet strategy. The 787-10 can provide substantial capacity on medium- and long-haul services, while the larger-range A350-1000 is suited to longer intercontinental operations. This approach gives Philippine Airlines options for matching aircraft size and performance to different markets rather than relying on one widebody model.

Beyond the aircraft deliveries, the Boeing agreement also reinforces an 80-year relationship between the manufacturer and the Philippine flag carrier. For Boeing, the finalized purchase adds a significant widebody customer commitment in Southeast Asia. For Philippine Airlines, the aircraft will represent a major investment in its next-generation fleet, although the full benefits will not materialize until deliveries begin in the 2030s.
