US Aviation Back To 'Largely Normal' 3 Days After Nationwide Ground Stop

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

It's the Monday after the weekend before, and America's airlines are finally showing promising signs of recovery as they bounce back from what was a very eventful weekend. After a rare nationwide FAA-issued ground stop on Friday, airlines and airports all across the United States of America spent Saturday and Sunday coming to terms with a huge backlog of displaced aircraft, crews, and passengers.

3 days later, Monday is looking like a better day at the office, with delays and cancelations at a much lower level compared to Friday, Saturday, and Sunday as airlines return to what they are calling 'largely normal' schedules. While a degree of disruption will continue to persist as the last of the crew and aircraft displacement is sorted out, Secretary Sean Duffy says that operations are nearing 'full capacity.'

The 'big three' US legacy airlines (American, Delta, and United) and the US Transportation Secretary all gave updates on Sunday night as they looked towards a less manic Monday. According to the BBC, Duffy explained yesterday that "as we look to the end of the day and into tomorrow, we should be running at full capacity." This followed Delta's earlier statement that "most flights are operating normally."

Elsewhere, Scott Kirby, the Chief Executive Officer of United Airlines, took a moment on Sunday night to emphasize his pride in United's response to the crisis, but said that "this moment must be a call to action" in terms of staffing and modernizing US ATC facilities. Looking to Monday, American said that it was "prepared to operate a largely normal schedule" on November 16. The oneworld member added that:

"With crews and aircraft in position and favorable weather expected across our system, we are set up for a good start to the week and do not anticipate additional broad cancellations."

Friday's nationwide ground stop was the longest in the US since the 9/11 terror attacks brought the country's airspace to a standstill in 2001. While plenty of flights were delayed and canceled in the four-hour window itself, the knock-on disruption has also been heavy, with the BBC noting that more than 17,000 US flights had been canceled by Sunday. Data from FlightAware underlines the widespread chaos.

According to the site, Friday saw American, Delta, Southwest, and United Airlines all cancel more than 1,000 flights, accounting for 26 to 34% of their schedules that day. The 'big three' also delayed more than 800 flights apiece, while Southwest's Friday delays topped 1,300 (30%). Saturday was American Airlines' worst day, with 1,428 cancelations (42%) and 1,062 delays (31%) registering on the system.

While no other US airline had a four-figure cancelation total on Saturday, Delta, Southwest, and United all had over 1,000 delays. By Sunday, the situation was beginning to clear, with no four-figure cancelation totals, although delays remained widespread. At the time of writing on Monday morning, things were much better, with Delta and United registering 10 cancelations apiece vs 159 at American.

FAA ties delayed pay to efficiency metrics that demand more on position time amid a 3,000 controller shortfall.

Of course, while Monday is looking like a much better day for US aviation, it isn't as simple as going from the chaos of the weekend to a disruption-free day, as some aircraft and crews remain displaced. This is why, as detailed above, delays and cancelations are still being seen. On this front, Delta said that "some customers may still experience delays or cancellations," and thanked them for their patience.
