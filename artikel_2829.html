Arizona football overcomes elements, Kansas State to win in overtime

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It began raining before the opening kick and was still coming down after the final whistle. And for much of the time in between it looked like Arizona's chances to escape The Little Apple with a win were getting washed away.

But even a rain-soaked Noah Fifita is better than most quarterbacks in the country.

Fifita threw touchdown passes in the final two minutes of regulation and in overtime, then the UA defense made three stops inside the 3-yard line to seal a 35-28 OT win at Kansas State on Saturday night.

No. 19 Arizona (8-3, 5-3 Big 12) ended a 6-game losing streak in road games immediately ahead of the Territorial Cup by rallying from down 11 points in the final six minutes. Kansas State (4-7, 1-7) led 28-17 after a bad snap in the end zone was recovered for a TD, but then Fifita led the UA on three consecutive scoring drives.

Fifita finished with 323 yards and two TDs on 29-of-42 passing and added 58 rushing yards (87 without sacks) and a TD as the traditional run game again was mostly non-existent. Gio Richardson caught 12 passes for 88 yards, including the game-winner in OT, while Isaiah Mizell had five catches for 122 yards and a TD.

Kansas State ran for 239 yards, 144 by Rodney Fields Jr., while K-State QB Dillon Duff had a long rushing TD but only threw for 110 yards.

Arizona had the chance to break open the game early in the 2nd half but managed only three points out of a blocked punt and turnover on downs, then gave up the lead and fell behind 11 in the 4th quarter before coming alive to force overtime.

The UA needed two plays to go in front for good in OT, both throws to Richardson with the game-winner another sweet pass by Fifita to where only Richardson could catch it.

K-State, which had been able to run mostly at will all night, quickly got inside the 5 on its OT possession before the UA defense made its stand. Fields twice gained nothing on his runs, then Duff kept it on 4th and 1 and was first met by Quinn Olson before Dominic Lolesio and Zac Siulepa finished him off.

Tied at 14 at the half, Arizona started with the ball for only the third time this season. It had scored TDs the previous games but went 3-and-out to open the 3rd quarter.

A strip sack by Tyrese Boss moved K-State behind the chains on its first drive of the 2nd half, then Dash Fifita blocked a punt to give the UA the ball deep in enemy territory. Arizona could only get three out of the block, though, with Michael Salgado-Medina hitting a 33-yard field goal for a 17-14 lead.

K-State returned the ensuing kickoff to midfield but after three run plays went for it on 4th and 1 and the UA defense stuffed Rodney Fields Jr. short of the line. But just like the previous drive, Arizona did nothing with the sudden change and punted.

Given a third chance, K-State took advantage for its first lead when Duff took off and ran almost untouched for a 58-yard TD run with 5:34 left in the 3rd.

The conditions got to Noah Fifita after that, with a wobbly through the was almost intercepted then a fumble while scrambling on 3rd and long. K-State took over at the UA 28 but gave it right back, with Cam Chapa intercepting a pass in the end zone.

Arizona did nothing with that takeaway, either, or early in the 4th quarter after getting into K-State territory via a 28-yard Cole Rusk catch on a flea flicker. It went for it on 4th and 3, with Fifita findingRichardson on a swing pass out of the backfield, but K-State's Ben Osueke tripped Richardson up just before the line to gain.

Another defense for the 3-and-out was followed by K-State's punt getting downed at the 2 after Caleb Smith let it bounce. That was followed by a rolling snap from center Zach Henning, which Fifita couldn't contain in the end zone and was recovered by K-State's Wendell Gregory for a TD and a 28-17 lead with 10:55 left.

Down two scores, the UA finally put together a sustainable 2nd-half drive and got inside the K-State on a Fifita keeper. But another sack and a misthrow led to taking the three points, with Salgado-Medina drilling a 26-yarder to cut it to 28-20 with 6:03 to go.

Arizona forced K-State to punt from plus territory, and the UA took over from its 20 with 2:09 to go. Two plays later—with a 15-yard sideline penalty against K-State—the game was tied.

Fifita took off for a 23-yard run, laterally to Richardson for seven more, then during the 2-minute warning K-State was penalized for 15 yards. The first play after the TV timeout, Fifita connected with Mizell for a 35-yard TD pass.

Fifita then threw a 2-point pass to Richardson and it was 28-all with 1:52 to go.

K-State got into Arizona territory but then penalized themselves out of a chance to kick a go-ahead field goal, giving it back to the UA with 29 seconds left. One completion was followed by the fourth sack of Fifita and off to overtime we went.

Three straight 3-and-outs started the game before Arizona mounted a lengthy drive, getting a couple of 3rd down catches and a long Mizell reception into the red zone. But the Wildcats couldn't punch it in, going for it on 4th and goal from just outside the 1 with Fifita's throw toRusk incomplete.

The turnover on downs worked out for the UA, though, as K-State punted from its end zone and CalebSmith returned it into plus-territory. With a short field, Arizona got on the board early in the 2nd quarter on Fifita's 8-yard run.

K-State didn't get its first 1st down until its fourth possession and then kept getting conversions, including a 35-yard catch down to the UA 16 that should have been tackled after five yards (and brought back to at least the 30 but a replay ignored the runner going out of bounds). Another play sawFields hurdle a Wildcat defender along the sideline and keep going then score on a 3-yard run to tie it with 9:01 left in the second.

Arizona put together another lengthy drive, this time coming through close to the goal line with Kedrick Reescano scoring on a 1-yard run for a 14-7 lead with 3:59 to go before halftime. But multiple defensive miscues let K-State tie it just before the break.

A pass interference call on Lee Mollette, a whiffed interception by Jay'Vion Cole and missed tackles on some runs set the stage forDuff to throw a 34-yard TD pass to Derrick Salley Jr. with 33 seconds left.

Arizona finishes up the regular season next Saturday evening at home against ASU. The Wildcats are seeking their fourth Territorial Cup win in the last five meetings with the Sun Devils.
