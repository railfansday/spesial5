Luiten Backs McIlroy’s Plan To Cut PGA Tour Cards

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Rory McIlroy wants to slash the number of European players who earn PGA Tour cards, and Joost Luiten agrees with the move. The Dutch veteran told GolfMagic that limiting the spots is actually good for the DP World Tour. Limiting the spots could stop the drain of top talent moving across the Atlantic before their prime.

Luiten believes the current system of ten cards is too generous and hurts the European circuit. He told GolfMagic, "It means there will be less cards next year, and I think three or four cards will be a good number so that we don't lose all of our top players." McIlroy made similar comments last week at the Abu Dhabi Championship. "I thought five was a nice number," McIlroy said. "I think it would probably be a little less than that. I'd say it'll probably be four. If I know what I'm talking about, which I usually do!"

The strategic alliance between the two tours runs until the end of 2027, forcing a new deal before 2028 changes take hold. Luiten noted the urgency of this timeline in his interview with GolfMagic. "All I know about the alliance is that 2027 is the last year and we need to come to a new agreement for 2028," Luiten added. "It will be very interesting to see how that deal is going to look." The current setup lets the top ten in the Race to Dubai earn cards if they do not already hold membership. That path might disappear or shrink significantly under the new structure.

McIlroy thinks the PGA Tour was leaning toward three spots while the European Tour wanted five. He predicts they will meet in the middle at four. This reduction means fewer Europeans get a direct shot at the biggest purses in the world. It forces players to stay and fight on home soil rather than taking a lateral move to the Challenger Series. Luiten sees this as a necessary step to keep the tour competitive.

The fear is that top players will leave if the pathway becomes too hard or too easy. Too many cards mean Europe loses its stars. Too few might kill the dream for rising prospects. Luiten argues that three or four is the sweet spot. It keeps the pressure high but ensures the best remain visible on the DP World Tour. McIlroy's influence as a six-time major winner gives his weight to this argument.

The upcoming DP World Tour Championship in Dubai will test the current standings before the new rules arrive. Eugenio Chacarra leads the Race to Dubai, but McIlroy is close behind. The winner on Sunday earns a crucial 2,000-point bump according to Yahoo Sports. With 1,335 points awarded for second and 752 for third, the payouts are set. How Chacarra performs will determine if McIlroy secures his eighth Race to Dubai title, catching Montgomerie. Until 2027 concludes, the strategic alliance stands, necessitating a fresh deal before 2028 changes occur.
