Ranking all the scary comments Steph Curry makes in the ESPN article

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Three days ago, ESPN's Anthony Slater published a story that served to remind us all that time slows for no one and that Steph Curry's playing days are numbered. It was startling. The specifics of it were so definitive, so obviously instructive of how the music would suddenly stop, that many fans were (rightfully) shook. Every quote feels like a night night dagger; but, since this is a sports blog, let's rank them by order of "BANG!"-itude.

First: if you haven't read the piece, go and read it, because I'll be jumping around.

Is this actually a scary quote? Not totally. We must always remember that a professional athlete who has played at the highest level possible for a human being during this moment in time lives in the moment most of the time. But you know the end is on the horizon once the athlete begins to discuss the end. Most of our stars have been stuck in some version of that kid who loved the game they played and wound up making millions for doing and mere recognition of mortality is revolutionary in terms of self-reflection. But the scare factor is that Steph's aware the party's winding down. His body has been telling him, and now his conscious thought has joined the fray.

This is a heightening of that recognition. A recognition of context, but also "the ability to convince myself" is a heckuva self-reflection. His mind is asking his body to prove it, and — for now — it's satisfying his standards. Will he lower his standards to keep convincing himself? Or does he have a "If I can't do 'x' anymore, then that's it" in mind?

Slater gets quotes from Steve Kerr in this piece, too, and for this segment he relates:

"He's staying off his feet [more often]," Kerr said. "Relying on the fact that he's built up this incredible skill level that's not going to leave him. Then, he's found a way to stay really fit without pounding the knees, the ankles. He's on the bike, underwater treadmill. He's creative."

But I'm putting this 8th because I'm a little scared by how this aging curve seems to have surprised Curry. Slater begins the article by talking about earlier this year, and that being the first time he really seemed to have grappled with his aging body.

Every athlete — every human being! — is different, of course, so this isn't a criticism, it's just a note that Steph Curry is not one of those athletes who plans too far ahead. His greatness has been defined by putting the pedal to the metal at all times; but, everyone in their mid-thirties would be wise to reassess their body maintenance. You can see how the idea of leaving something on the table doesn't appeal to him. It's not a lack of wisdom or intelligence, it's just the way that he is wired. But as a fan reading this, the fear factor is in how he's just going to keep dancing until the music stops.

Well, okay, and then there's an acknowledgement that he needs to slow some aspect of it down. This line definitely amps up the bummer vibes of the piece. It's like he's counting how many sunrises he's got left.

Or counting how many more sandwiches he'll have left to eat in his life. But you can see why Dylan Thomas urged rage because contemplation or otherwise quiet reflection is just a bummer. It sucks to be reminded that this is all going to end — better to burn out than fade away, right? But that's the tension of being a professional athlete. That's certainly the trajectory Steph is on; will this slowdown prolong his career?

This is the first quote of the aptly titled article, "The clock is ticking on Steph Curry's final act with the Warriors." Slater starts with an anecdote about an off-day workout going awry and quickly pulls in the oblique abdominal strain, but the constant throughout the article is Steph's knee.

"a weird soreness and pain that didn't go away"

"It was basically holding. It wasn't going either direction."

There's even discussion of how much it held him back, where he was just going through the motions while on the court at times. This is as close to a medical assessment as we get in the piece, and it's devastating. Cartilage doesn't come back as we age thanks to how blood flow works in the area. That's where things like stem cell therapy come into play. But, at this point, it's pretty clear that after all the miles Steph Curry has logged over the years, we're talking about patching an engine that's getting near the end of its life. Consider this part one of a two-part idea. Part two:

As terrible a setback as the oblique abdominal strain/tear is, this quote plus the one before it really does point to his knee being the ticking time bomb, and that he's well aware that's the case.

The story is that Stephen Curry had the biggest setback of his professional career this calendar year and because his body has started to break down. It doesn't get any scarier than that. This year is almost over. What horror(s) will next year bring?

But I'll end this post … the least scary thing Curry says in the whole piece.

If circumstances demanded it, Curry said he would've been fine sliding into more of a complementary role late in his career, similar to most fading stars. Perhaps he eventually will. "It would not kill my ego," Curry said. "I want to win. Whatever that means, you make decisions based on what's in front of you."

You're not going to find any Stephen Curry followers who don't think the guy has a degree of humility about him. That's part of what makes him special. He might be the greatest. At times, even he might even acknowledge that he's "up there," in terms of the all-time greats. But he's also aware of how fleeting that feeling can be. It won't be long before he's an afterthought. He's certainly been in the league long enough to see opinions, rankings, or simply stature diminish or outright disappear overnight. And there's also the thing where, sometimes, we need to say something out loud just to convince ourselves it's true.

We don't know when Curry will return from his current injury, but there's still just a bit more time left for us to enjoy a bit more history created by his play. If this isn't the last ride, then it's the penultimate one.
