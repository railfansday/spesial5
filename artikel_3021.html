Monday.com vs. Okta: Which Tech Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Monday.com trades at a far lower forward P/E than Okta, but its growth and cash flow quality raise a question about which software leader deserves a spot in your portfolio.

By Erin Kennedy – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 7:48AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Monday.com ›

As digital transformation evolves, many investors weigh high-growth software platforms against established security leaders. Deciding between Monday.com (MNDY +3.23%) and Okta (OKTA -7.44%) requires balancing rapid expansion against critical infrastructure stability.

Monday.com offers a highly flexible work operating system that allows businesses to build custom applications without writing code. Okta specializes in identity management, ensuring that only the right people have access to specific corporate systems. Both companies are vital to modern workforces but serve different niches within the software market.

Monday.com provides an AI-powered work platform designed to help teams manage projects, sales cycles, and software development tasks in one place. As businesses prioritize efficiency, many are turning to tech companies that offer versatile software-as-a-service solutions. Monday.com serves roughly 250,000 customers globally, focusing on a multi-product strategy that encourages users to adopt its CRM and service tools alongside core management features.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $1.4 billion, reflecting a growth rate of approximately 22.4%. This expansion helped the company achieve net income of close to $143.4 million during the same period. The transition from a single-product tool to a comprehensive enterprise platform has been a primary driver of this financial performance.

As of Monday.com's December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio stands at roughly 2.5. This ratio measures a company's ability to cover its short-term obligations with assets that can be converted to cash within one year. The debt-to-equity ratio, which compares total debt to shareholder equity, is approximately 0.3. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $309.9 million. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 53.1% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Okta serves as a foundational layer for enterprise security by providing identity and access management solutions. The platform helps more than 19,300 organizations secure their employees and customers across various cloud and on-premises environments. To strengthen its ecosystem, Okta maintains deep integrations with major providers like Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), ServiceNow (NOW -1.42%), and Salesforce (CRM -1.47%).

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached close to $3.1 billion, an increase of approximately 11.2%. The company reported a net income of nearly $296 million during this time frame. While its growth has moderated compared to previous years, the company continues to benefit from the essential nature of cybersecurity spending.

As of Okta's January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 0.1. This indicates a relatively low level of debt used to finance the company's assets compared to its equity. The current ratio stands at roughly 1.4, suggesting a sufficient cushion for short-term liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was approximately $863 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 61.5% of operating cash flow.

Monday.com faces significant competition from well-capitalized rivals in the productivity software space. While the platform is versatile, it must compete against established tools from Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) and Atlassian (TEAM +1.23%). If enterprise spending on new software licenses slows due to macroeconomic pressures, the company could see a deceleration in user acquisition. Additionally, maintaining high growth requires constant investment in research and development to stay ahead of rapidly evolving artificial intelligence trends.

Okta operates in a crowded security market, competing with large infrastructure providers such as Microsoft. Any cybersecurity vulnerability or breach of Okta's own systems could severely damage its brand reputation and trust among enterprise clients. The company also depends on the retention and expansion of its existing customer base to sustain revenue growth. Operational risks include the complex task of integrating identity solutions across a vast array of third-party platforms and mobile hardware.

Based on its significantly lower earnings multiples and sales-based valuation, Monday.com appears to be the more attractively priced option compared to Okta.

Okta and Monday.com are reporting roughly similar net margins, but the latter is seeing stronger revenue growth. That said, I think Okta is more attractive here. Monday.com has to compete with the likes of Microsoft and Atlassian, which is a tough proposition. Microsoft's software is ubiquitous and entrenched. Furthermore, I think Okta offers a stickier product. The company provides critical software to maintain organizational security and identity management. It's more important than ever for companies to lock down data for reputational, regulatory, and legal reasons. Cybersecurity isn't a "nice to have"; it's a pivotal need for any organization. I'm not convinced Monday.com's offerings are equally necessary.

Finally, while past performance is no indication of future results, Okta stock has put in a stronger showing so far this year. Okta gets my vote for 2027 and beyond.
