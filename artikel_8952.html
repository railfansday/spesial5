living heather roof and poplar bark cladding anchor timber office in dutch heathland

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

on the tafelberg heath in blaricum, the netherlands, studio massimo models a timber office on the regional plaggenhut, a traditional sod-roofed dwelling integrated into the terrain.

Max edited by christina vergopoulou November 25, 2026 4 min

Situated on the Tafelberg heath in Blaricum, east of Amsterdam, Office by Nature, Tafelberg is a 1,120-sqm timber office designed by Studio Massimo. Built within a protected glacial landscape and drinking-water catchment area, the project required low-impact construction to protect the surrounding soil. Taking structural and formal cues from the regional plaggenhut, a traditional earthen-walled dwelling topped with a sod roof, the building merges directly with the terrain. The ground slopes upward without an edge or material break to form a walkable living roof planted with more than one hundred native, organically grown species.

Each elevation responds to its orientation. On the north side facing the road, the planted slope terminates at a mortarless retaining wall of stacked reclaimed paving tiles, punctuated only by a timber entrance door. The east and west gables are clad in untreated poplar bark shingles, a timber-industry byproduct that weathers from umber to silver-gray, while six-meter-wide sliding glass doors on the south facade open the workspaces directly onto the heath.

Constructed from a laminated timber and cross-laminated timber (CLT) frame insulated with wood fiber, the interior is finished in a continuous palette of Red Grandis timber across walls, ceilings, doors, and joinery, with ventilation slots milled directly into the wood panels to eliminate visible grilles. A seven-meter-high central hall illuminated by an overhead skylight anchors the plan, floored in terrazzo aggregate containing split Kunrader stone from South Limburg, the same stone used for the exterior plinth. The design studio integrates a rainwater harvesting system that treats and reuses runoff for roof irrigation and toilets, guiding water down the south facade via reused crane chains, where each bay incorporates a secondary, higher overflow chain that discharges only when gutters require clearing. Outside, a sunken seating area built from reclaimed mooring poles and glacial boulders sits within untamed native planting.
