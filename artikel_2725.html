Florida football WR Eric Singleton Jr mum about future after breakout game

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Florida football redshirt junior Eric Singleton Jr has played most of the 2026 season through a high ankle sprain he suffered on the seventh play of the season in Week 1 against Florida Atlantic.

Against Vanderbilt, the 5-foot-10, 184-pond Singleton finally displayed the tantalizing promise he showed during spring and fall drills with the Florida Gators.

Singleton finished with six catches for 117 yards during UF's 34-27 win over the Commodores, including a key fourth catch in the fourth quarter that required some luck and concentration.

With the score tied at 27, Florida quarterback Aaron Philo fired a pass over the middle that deflected off the hands of receiver Micah Mays and then helicoptered into the hands of Singleton, who ran another 10 yards to complete a 37-yard pass play.

"Just in the right place at the right time," Singleton said. "Seen the ball and air and decided had to get it, wanted to make a play for the team."

A Vanderbilt roughing the passer penalty against Philo moved the ball down to the Vandy 10-yard line, and on the next play, Jadan Baugh scored a 10-yard TD run to give UF a 34-27 lead it wouldn't relinquish.

"I'm never going to be above taking some luck," Florida coach Jon Sumrall said. "We got some today on that play. I'm happy for E. The year in a lot of ways on his individual performance hasn't gone the way any of us probably predicted or would have thought. He's worked his tail off."

Earlier in the game, Singleton also caught a 50-yard pass from Philo in the second quarter. which set up UF's first TD of the game, a Philo 1-yard TD run.

"That's something I've been emphasizing with Philo," Singleton said. "I don't care about the post safety being there, he's still got me because I can still run by him. He gave me a shot today and I made a play."

An Auburn transfer, Singleton could have walked on Senior Day before UF's win over Vanderbilt but didn't. As a fourth-year player who began his career at Georgia Tech, Singleton still has one year of eligibility left under the NCAA's new five-for-five rule.

Singleton said at this point he's unsure whether he will declare for the NFL draft or return to school.

"I haven't really made any kind of decision," Singleton said. "I'm just trying to finish this season."

Singleton entered the 2026 season as the fastest player on UF's roster, running a peak speed of 23 miles per hour. The ankle injury robbed him of some of that breakaway playmaking ability, but he said he's feeling closer to 100 percent each week.

"Obviously it hasn't gone the way I wanted it to, dealt a big injury early on," Singleton said. "But I think God has a plan for me and it's coming to light now, and I think everything is going to fall in line for me."
