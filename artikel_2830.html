Weekly Recap 2: Hawks take a step back

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

We are just shy of the season being a month old, the Atlanta Hawks find themselves as the number four team in the Eastern Conference at 8-6. Despite being one of the better teams in the conference, this past week may have been a step back for a team that found themselves as winners of four straight.

The week's first matchup was against the Los Angeles Lakers at home. The Lakers were winners of five straight heading into Monday's game in Atlanta and were led by Luka Doncic, who was averaging more than 40 points per game at the time. The Hawks' roster is a near ideal group to slow down Doncic's historic pace with multiple versatile defenders on the perimeter. Dyson Daniels drew the assignment of Doncic, which was not a surprise given his reputation as one of the best on-ball defenders in the league.

While Daniels began the night containing Doncic from one of his signature 20-point first quarters, the perennial All-NBA guard displayed his other-worldly passing ability, as he racked up five assists during the opening period, which opened up the Lakers' offense and led them to an early nine-point lead which they did not relinquish for the rest of the game.

Unfortunately for the Hawks, they were plagued by turnovers and poor interior play. Lakers' center Walker Kessler was a matchup nightmare for the Lakers, as he shot 8-for-12 from the field on his way to 21 points and ten rebounds. Kessler's contributions were a significant factor in the Hawks surrendering 52 points in the paint. The poor performance down low has become not just a trend, but a full-blown habit for the Hawks, as they currently allow the third most points in the paint in the entire league.

The Hawks also turned the ball over 20 times, which the Lakers turned into 29 points, as opposed to the Hawks turning their forced turnovers into 14 points. Despite these discrepancies, the Hawks were still in the game late, trailing by only two with just under seven minutes to go. Dyson Daniels was making life tough for Luka Doncic and anyone else he guarded, as he picked up four steals along the way, which has become a signature for the league's leading pickpocket thief.

Even with his strong performance defensively, Doncic proved to be too much for the Hawks down the stretch, as he waved off multiple screens while being guarded by Daniels, which then turned into back-breaking buckets.

The Hawks' two leading scorers, Jalen Johnson and 'NAW' struggled mightily from the field, combining for 12-for-33 shooting, which just simply put is not good enough to beat most teams, let alone a squad that is clicking on all cylinders like the Lakers. Even with CJ McCollum and Aaron Wiggins having big nights off the benches, the poor outing from Johnson and 'NAW' was too much to overcome.

Luckily, the Hawks got their toughest test of the week out of the way with the Lakers. Their remaining two opponents, the Chicago Bulls and Milwaukee Bucks each only had a single win apiece. Smooth sailing, right?

The first matchup of the two was against the Bulls, who would be without their phenom rookie, Caleb Wilson. What should have been a sure-fire win against an undermanned Bulls team turned into a frigid display of three-point shooting from the Hawks that made winning quite difficult.

Frigid may be an understatement for a night that featured them shooting 8-for-40 from behind the arc, which equaled an abysmal 20%. The poor shooting especially plagued them in the overtime period, shooting 0-for-6 and totally stifling their offense.

The biggest star for the Bulls was the newly-acquired Norman Powell, who tallied a season-high 33 points on an impressive 12-for-19 shooting. Josh Giddey also had a triple-double, which included a staggering 14 assists.

On the Hawks side, Jalen Johnson also had a triple-double, which was his first of the season. Despite notching another triple-double under his belt, the feat was outweighed by his poor 11-for-26 shooting night along with five turnovers. CJ McCollum had another productive night off the bench with 20 points and Onyeka Okongwu also chipped in 17 points.

However, these contributions couldn't overcome an unprecedentedly bad shooting night for the Hawks, as they outperformed the Bulls in major categories such as forced turnovers, points in the paint, and fastbreak points.

The week concluded against the Bucks whose poor season has made them perhaps the runaway choice as the worst team in the entire league. Despite their woes of the season so far, they seemed to be the Hawks' second consecutive upset, as the Bucks raced out to 37-18 at the end of the first quarter. The deficit was attributed to red-hot deep shooting from the Bucks and a continued drought from behind the arc from the Hawks.

Luckily for the Hawks, the Bucks did what they do best- get in their own way. The teams essentially traded the shooting trends in the first quarter in the following period, as the Hawks claimed the lead headed into halftime. While the Hawks quickly raced out to a double-digit lead in the third, their shooting struggles returned, as they shot 28% from three in the second half.

Both teams had a sluggish second half, as the periods were plagued by bad shots, sloppy passes, and defensive lapses, which looked more like a bad day of pick-up ball at your local rec center instead of an NBA game. Luckily for the Hawks, the Bucks' worst day is probably the worst day for any team in the league.

Despite late surges from the likes of Gary Trent Jr., whose most recent contract was so inconceivable that it summoned an investigation from the league, the Hawks held on for the win with final score at 113-106.

Luckily for the Hawks, 'NAW' cleaned up his shooting struggles, leading the way with 20 points on 7-for-15 shooting. CJ McCollum also put up 20 points, which included shooting a perfect 9-for-9 from the free throw line and some clutch buckets down the stretch as the game neared its end.

Despite the win, the continued lackadaisical passing, poor shooting, and poor rebounding from the game are trends that look to make the rest of the season a struggle for the Hawks. As I mentioned last week, this long season will not be won or lost in November.

However, poor habits can compound quickly and send teams on a downhill slope that could be tough to recover from. With both the Oklahoma City Thunder and Detroit Pistons both on the docket this week, the margin for error will be much smaller than the competition they faced last week at the hands of the Bulls and Bucks.
