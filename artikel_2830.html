Puka Nacua's suspension arises from diversion program

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The NFL’s statement announcing the three-game suspension for Rams receiver Puka Nacua didn’t specify the reason for it. Further information provided by the league makes the basis for the ban clear.

It comes from Nacua’s entry into a “diversion program,” related to the incident that triggered a civil lawsuit alleging (among other things) that he bit a woman on New Year’s Eve.

In September, we explained that Nacua had begun the steps required to complete a diversion program, which happened without Nacua entering into a formal agreement to do so. It’s one of the unique facets of California law; namely, California gives some defendants the ability to embark on the steps to comply with a diversion program without the kind of express deal that would have automatically triggered review under the Personal Conduct Policy.

The approach ultimately didn’t avoid the NFL scrutiny.

The suspension of Nacua for three games, with the acknowledgment from the league that failure “to satisfy the conditions of his diversion program” will result in “additional and more substantial discipline,” means that the mere commencement of the process of compliance, even without an express agreement with prosecutors, was enough to get the process rolling.

It also led to a negotiated conclusion that entails Nacua being suspended for games he otherwise would have missed, due to his status on injured reserve. While it all looks convenient at best and fishy at worst, it represents the outcome of a bargain aimed at resolving the situation without full-blown activation of a process that would have entailed a formal hearing before Judge Sue L. Robinson, the person jointly appointed by the NFL and the NFL Players Association to hear the evidence and to issue a preliminary decision.
