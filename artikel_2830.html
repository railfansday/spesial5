3 quick takeaways from Wisconsin’s loss to Murray State: Houston, we have a problem

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Following their 74-69 loss to the Seton Hall Pirates on Thursday night, the Wisconsin Badgers followed that one up with an even rougher performance, falling 70-53 to the Murray State Racers on Friday in the Baha Mar Championship third-place game.

Heading into the night, I thought the matchup would be tough for the Badgers, as Murray State played with a very fast pace, which could speed Wisconsin up and be tough to play catch-up. Additionally, the Racers have looked quite good to start the year, including against NC State in a loss on Thursday.

Well, Wisconsin was just not competitive enough on Friday, as they were blown out in the first half and didn't have a second-half comeback in them unlike the Seton Hall game.

Here are three quick takeaways from the Badgers loss to Murray State on Friday night.

For the second game in a row, Wisconsin just wasn't competitive at all in the first half. The Badgers shot an ugly 9 of 32 from the field in the opening 20 minutes, including a putrid 1 of 14 from three. And there were issues everywhere.

Victory Onuetu, who continues to start at center, struggled again. He was 1 of 3 from the field and had three turnovers in six minutes of action, posting a -9 in the box score.

Owen Foxwell was a non-factor again, scoring just two points in the period. After repeated calls for aggression from head coach Greg Gard, Nolan Winter didn't have a shot attempt until 3:14 remaining in the first half, which came off an offensive rebound.

Eian Elmer was also a non-factor for the second straight night, scoring just two points on 1 of 5 from the field in the first half, while also picking up a pair of fouls. Braeden Carrington also missed all four of his three-point attempts and ended the first 20 minutes scoreless.

The bench was even worse. Zach Kinziger, Jack Janicki, Jackson Ball, and Austin Rapp all posted plus-minuses of more than -10. Ball at least looked to be aggressive, scoring five points on 2 of 5 from the field. He was one of the lone bright spots on Friday in terms of aggression and trying to make something happen.

It was just an ugly half of basketball all-around for the Badgers, who scored just 22 points and averaged only .647 points per possession.

It's not like Murray State was special offensively. They shot 35.1 percent from the field and 33.3 percent from three in the first half, but they attacked the offensive glass (10 offensive rebounds), and were by far the more aggressive team.

After the game, Greg Gard called on his veterans to step things up and lead the way for the new games. That goes mainly to Nolan Winter, who had an efficient eight points and six rebounds, but still felt like a non-factor for much of Friday's game.

Owen Foxwell had another rough shooting night, scoring six points while hitting only 2 of his 7 field goals. Victory Onuetu looked unplayable, posting five turnovers in just 12 minutes. Braeden Carrington was 1 for 6 from three. And Eian Elmer was extremely disappointing once again, finishing with only two points in 16 minutes.

Jackson Ball, as I mentioned, was one of the lone bright spots from Friday night, looking to attack and be aggressive with his minutes.

But it was another extremely disappointing day for the Badgers, who shot poorly, turned it over 17 times, didn't finish at the rim, and remain without an identity on either side of the ball.

It's extremely early, and we saw last year's team turn things around going into conference play.

But this Wisconsin team looks far different style-wise than the last few, and the offense has sputtered as a result. First off, as I noted a few nights ago, the Badgers are not only not taking as many threes as they have the last few years, but they're also not hitting them at all.

The Badgers shot 25 threes on Friday, hitting only five of them. They also were awful at the free throw line, hitting 6 of 12 of their free throws.

Right now, this Badgers team isn't good at anything offensively. They don't finish well. They don't shoot well. They turn the ball over too much. They don't hit their free throws. And it looks like a lot of selfish basketball on the court without enough aggression.

It could very well be integrating a number of new starters into the system. But this team is in real trouble six games into the season, and they need to turn things around fast.
