Latin Grammy Winner Urias Stars in CARRANCA, a Film by ODE

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

CARRANCA draws on the eponymous sculptures, anthropomorphic and zoomorphic figures traditionally believed to ward off evil spirits, also associated with Afro-Brazilian religious traditions such as Candomblé. The project references Exu, the orisha who mediates between the material and spiritual worlds and is associated with movement and the creation of possibilities. Inspired by ancient Egyptian and Yoruba cultures, the carrancas’ physiognomy blends human and animal traits, evoking pre-dynastic Egyptian deities.

The palette of black, red, indigo, and white references these traditions and Brazilian popular representations of Exu. Using Afro-surrealism as a conceptual framework, the series visually narrates Urias’ eponymous album, with each photograph corresponding to a track. The project explores transcorporeality and how reclaiming divine and ancestral figures can become a means of liberation from Eurocentric identity norms.

Ode (b. 1998) is a Brazilian multidisciplinary artist working across filmmaking, creative direction, styling, writing, and curatorial practice. Her work explores self-representation as a means of challenging Eurocentric notions of identity through transcorporeality, Afro-Brazilian spirituality, memory, and transformation. Her photobooks and films, including A Rose and A Prayer (2022), Divina (2022), and CARRANCA (2025), bring human figures, deities, and Afro-surrealist narratives into dialogue. Her work has been exhibited at MEP, Instituto Moreira Salles, Lafayette Anticipations, ICA London, Photo London, and SP-Arte. She founded the platform Dúdús (2016–2019) and has curated exhibitions dedicated to Afro-diasporic art. Her work has been featured in Vogue, Dazed, ArtReview, and Harper’s Bazaar, and she has collaborated with Comme des Garçons, Jean Paul Gaultier, and COLORSxSTUDIOS.
