What Exactly Was Barry Keoghan’s Skin-Lifting Treatment?

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Even during his busy filming schedule for the upcoming quartet of Beatles biopics, Barry Keoghan manages to find time for self-care. Just last week, an Instagram video by skincare practitioner Nilam Holmes showed him undergoing his second XERF treatment at her UK-based clinic Dermaspa. The non-invasive treatment uses radio frequency to support collagen production and improve skin firmness without risking any fat loss on the face. According to its website, results last around 12 to 18 months, with pricing around $6,000 for two sessions (or about $3,300 for one).

“Barry is filming a big project at the moment with a very intense schedule, so he hasn’t been able to get in much and he can’t have any downtime,” Holmes tells GQ, referring to the recovery period more invasive procedures require. “That’s why I chose XERF for him. It gives a firmer rested look to his face without interrupting his work.”

According to Holmes, XERF is popular because people say it offers a “non-surgical facelift,” but she says the results are not that dramatic. The best candidates for XERF are people who want “subtle, natural-looking results” and have “mild laxity,” which is skin that’s only slightly loose or saggy.

She tells us that Keoghan has been a client of hers for “a while now” and that she’ll tailor his treatments based on what his skin needs. For example, if he’s feeling tired from filming, she might do some RF skin tightening—another form of radio frequency for addressing sagging skin—or lymphatic drainage. If his face is “being troubled from makeup” she’ll do a mild laser with a deep-cleaning HydraFacial. She says Keoghan manages to come in every four to six weeks for appointments during which she’ll offer Skinpen Microneedling—a treatment that reduces the appearance of fine lines, acne scars, and uneven skin texture.

“Once a year we’ll do a more intensive skin-tightening treatment such as Ultraformer III HIFU"— a non-invasive ultrasound device for skin tightening—“or Morpheus8”—a tool that combines microneedling with radio frequency—“which do involve some downtime,” she adds.

Keoghan has been pretty tight-lipped about those Beatles biopics he’s starring in. But in recent years, the 34-year-old Oscar-nominated actor has been open about other aspects of his life like growing up in foster care—which resonated with him while he was making the movie Lemonade—and being bullied online for his appearance. In an interview with SiriusXM in March this year, Keoghan talked about the “abuse” he receives online about his looks and how it makes him “not want to go outside.”

In Holmes’ Instagram post, Keoghan voices over: “I feel better about myself; I look better.” From Holmes’ perspective, the actor’s trips to her clinic are simply about taking caring of his skin.

“Looking after your skin isn’t about changing who you are—it’s part of looking after yourself, in the same way people look after their health, fitness and well-being,” Holmes says. “When it comes from that place, it’s a really positive thing.”

She says a lot of her clients want to enhance their features. In the past, she says it used to be mostly younger women. Nowadays, she’s seeing “more inquiries from young men with the rise of ‘looksmaxxing.’”
