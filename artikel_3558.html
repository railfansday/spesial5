Tidal Is the First Streaming Platform to Ban AI Music

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

You have a few options when it comes to choosing a music streaming service. If you own an iPhone and a Mac, you might be tempted by Apple Music; if you have more friends on Spotify, you may feel that's the best platform for you. But if your goal is avoiding AI music as much as humanly possible, there appears to be a new uncontested winner: Tidal.

On Tuesday, Tidal announced plans to ban AI music from its platform entirely. Starting Dec. 9, the company will begin taking steps to remove music it believes is entirely made with AI. Tidal says it thinks the move “will resonate with both artists and listeners alike.”

This isn't the first stand Tidal has taken against AI-generated music. Back in July, the company announced that it would start labeling songs completely made with AI, as well as remove tracks it found to be "fraudulent and deceptive." Other platforms have similar policies, or are rolling out similar features. Apple Music, for example, is working on AI labels for its app, too, and Spotify has pushed back on AI to some degree. However, this most recent move makes Tidal the first music streaming service to outright ban AI-generated music from its catalog.

Robert Anderson, Tidal's Music Lead, posted on X about the change. According to Anderson, Tidal identified 43% of the tracks it received in the last 90 days were AI-generated. Per the company's plan, Tidal will remove 29 million songs by the beginning of 2027, roughly 80,000 to 85,000 removals per day. Some 96% of Tidal's AI-generated songs have zero plays whatsoever.

I wouldn't be surprised to see other music streaming services follow suit, particularly Apple Music. Despite rolling out AI-powered features over the past couple of years, including Apple Music's AI-generated playlists, Apple has taken some stands against AI-generated material in general. The latest Apple TV intro was shot in-camera with practical effects, in an era where the company could have easily generated something with AI. If other companies plan to follow Tidal's lead here, my money's on Apple being first in line.

However, as it stands, Tidal is the only streaming service that supports this initiative. Soon enough, it'll be the sole music app you can use with near-zero ambiguity that the music you're listening to was at least mostly created by humans. (What a sentence.) Avoiding AI-generated music can be tricky, frustrating, and, frankly, exhausting. If that's important to you, Tidal might just be your new pick.
