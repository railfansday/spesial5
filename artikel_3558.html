Penélope Cruz Says She and Husband Javier Bardem are “Very Careful” About Choosing When to Work Together

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Penélope Cruz and Javier Bardem are very selective about when they work on a project together, but the opportunity to costar in director Florian Zeller's timely psychological thriller Bunker was too enticing to pass up.

"When [Zeller] came to us with this powerful, very necessary story to tell, we looked at each other and there was no way to say no to this," Cruz said during a post-screening discussion at Vanity Fair’s Screening Room on Sunday. "I'm very, very happy that we did it."

Bunker revolves around a married couple, Miguel (Bardem) and Sofia (Cruz), whose 17-year marriage is tested when Miguel, a famous architect, is presented with a life changing work opportunity.

“I really wanted to work with them because I truly believe they are [two] of the greatest actors working today,” Zeller said during the panel discussion moderated by VF's West Coast Editor John Ross. “I was also very excited and interested to have them together onscreen because they are also a real couple. I thought it would be an interesting and unique opportunity to approach something that has to do with authenticity, with the truth. Not to say that this is their story, because of course this is not their story.”

Florian Zeller at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 15, 2026

Cruz said she felt a kinship with her character's concerns. “We have so many things in common in terms of this sadness and frustration and anger that you feel in terms of where is the world going if we don't wake up soon,” she said. “I hope it's not too late, but especially the experiment that is being done on younger people, children, teenagers, it just makes me very furious.

“It's something that affects me so much as an actress, as a mother, as a citizen of the world, that I felt completely a need to play Sophia,” she continued. “And of course it was not the only reason, but it was a big privilege to be able to say those lines. And [Zeller] is such a master of writing and I am a fan of all his work. And same for Javier, who is not here with us today, he's doing theater, but we are very careful in terms of choosing when to work together.”

Pénelope Cruz, Florian Zeller and John Ross at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 15, 2026

Cruz praised Zeller’s ability to provide a supportive, safe working environment and his strong point of view. “We felt really, really safe with him, he's so talented. He doesn't need any kind of manipulation and he never treats the audience as not intelligent because the audience can feel everything,” she explained. “And that has become really the most important thing for me. Don't over-explain, don't underestimate the audience ever because the thermometer is really working. And also with young people more and more and more. That is my hope to continue to go to see films on a big screen. They're coming back thank God. So let's create more possibilities so they can keep doing that.”

Cruz also said that she and Bardem have similar approaches to their work and the preparation it requires. “It's true that we share the same passion and we have done this since we were teenagers,” she said. “We love exploring and studying and feeling that healthy fear of the unknown because every time you begin a project, in a way, it's really starting from zero. So it's a really good thing to share.”

Zeller called his collaboration with the couple “a fascinating journey. It takes a lot of courage or fearlessness to do that, I think, for actors.” He also revealed that he initially referenced Stanley Kubrick's seminal thriller Eyes Wide Shut as a point of reference for Bunker, but realized it might not be setting the proper tone, given the circumstances.

“I remember that the first time I spoke with [Cruz] and Javier about this project, I mentioned a film that was somewhere in my mind, Eyes Wide Shut. Not to say that there is any kind of connection between the two films, but as you remember, Eyes Wide Shut at the time, Tom Cruise and Nicole Kidman, they were together," he said. "And I remember as a viewer that it was so interesting to be in a position where you do not know exactly what you're invited to watch… but Javier [later] told me, ‘You should not mention this film too often because remember at the end of the shooting they broke up.’ And then I realized that the director, Kubrick, died soon after. So I was like, ‘Okay, I'm going to stop mentioning this film.’ But the real difference I would say is that we knew that the destination of that film was the last image of the painting that you saw, which means that it was about how to reconnect with the other.”
