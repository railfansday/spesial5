Photographer Encounters Legendary Grizzly Bear Known as ‘The Boss’ Ahead of Hibernation

A photographer had an incredible encounter with a famous grizzly bear known as “The Boss” in Banff, Canada.

Jason Leo Bantle was in Banff National Park when he came across fresh bear tracks in the snow. Knowing the big bruin must be in the area, Bantle came back on November 6 and spotted The Boss foraging along the railway tracks that run through the park.

“Known for grains being dropped from railcars or other animals being hit, The Boss has learned that when food is not plentiful in the backcountry, the tracks may offer some subsistence,” Bantle tells PetaPixel.

Bantle is aware of the risks of approaching a grizzly in the wild, so he waited at a great distance, observing the bear through an 840mm telephoto lens and binoculars.

The New York Times ran an article over the weekend about The Boss. It revealed that he is categorized as “habituated” by the park’s management, which means he is not bothered by people so long as he is left alone.

“The biggest thing is his gentleness and his respect towards the people,” Paul Klra, a gas station owner and photographer, told The Times. “He has never done any harm to anyone.”

While The Boss has never done any harm to humans, he certainly has done harm to other bears. His biggest rival in the area is a bear called Split Lip; The Boss gave him that lip.

He’s also survived being struck by a train and has been known to kill and eat black bears. However, a park ecologist tells The Times that 90% of The Boss’s diet is made up of berries, roots, and other plants.

“As a professional photographer, you are focused on your craft and capturing an image that is the essence of the animal and their environment, so you do lose yourself in the moment,” Bantle says of photographing The Boss.

“I always reflect back on moments like this with The Boss; he is a living legend! The one image of him as he lumbered toward me with light snow falling was just magnificent: the grandeur of this bear, an apex predator, yet the gentleness of his quiet presence — no wind and a light snowfall, it was humbling to be in his presence.”

The Boss is thought to be 28 years old, advanced for a bear. He has become internationally famous, and it makes the news when he comes out of hibernation.
