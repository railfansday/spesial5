Kobbie Mainoo warns Manchester United ‘no one’s coming to save us’ as crisis deepens

Kobbie Mainoo has urged Manchester United to demonstrate resilience and unity, warning that "no one’s coming to save us" after declaring the squad has hit the "lowest we’ve ever been".

The team suffered a further setback in a difficult campaign during Mauricio Pochettino’s opening fixture in charge, as rivals Liverpool took advantage of a controversial early red card shown to Leny Yoro at Anfield.

Dominik Szoboszlai and Florian Wirtz put ten-man United behind before Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz completed a 4-0 defeat, a scoreline that reflected the home side’s control of the match.

It marked the joint-worst managerial debut for United since player-manager Lal Hilditch lost by the same score against Bolton a century ago, leaving the club 19th in the standings.

"It’s all about character now," homegrown midfielder Mainoo told MUTV.

"We’re at the lowest we’ve ever been and no one’s coming to save us.

"There’s no helping hands coming so we’ve got to work together and lean on each other and keep working and hopefully it’ll come good."

Mainoo expressed clear frustration following the defeat, a sentiment mirrored by several United players who walked straight through the post-match mixed zone without answering questions.

"It’s tough," the England international said. "I mean, I feel like the game was killed early with a decision by the referee that I don’t think can be a red card.

"The ball is already with the keeper when he’s gone down, two players running at full speed. I mean, it’s impossible to be a red, but it is what it is.

"It is a tough place to come, but I can’t fault the boys on their fight. We have to take the pain from the game and move on with it.

"The games are still coming thick and fast, so we don’t have time to dwell on it."

The current team stands in sharp contrast to the side Michael Carrick managed to a third-place finish last season, with Sunday’s loss extending United's run without a Premier League victory to nine consecutive matches.

Sitting just one point clear of bottom side Crystal Palace, United face a challenging period as Pochettino insisted the squad "need to accept the reality" and "need to be united and all together" in order to rebuild.

"We’re all committed," Mainoo said. "No one is going anywhere and we’re all leaning on each other.

"We all know what we have to do and we all know with the new manager we need to get behind him, keep working in training. That’s all we can do."

United will target only their second Premier League win of the season when they host Brentford on Saturday, following the second fixture of Pochettino’s tenure in Europe.

Sporting provide the opposition in Wednesday’s Champions League match, with United seeking to build on the dramatic 3-2 comeback victory over Roma achieved in their previous European tie.

"Of course we’re looking forward to it," Mainoo added. "It’s coming quick, it’s only in a couple days, so we have to pick ourselves back up and go again."
