Djokovic maintains ATP Finals chances with opening win at Paris Masters

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Novak Djokovic maintained his Nitto ATP Finals qualification hopes at the Rolex Paris Masters, where he rallied from a set and a break down to defeat Valentin Vacherot.

The seven-time Paris champion was pushed hard by the Monegasque and overcame a mid-match shoulder issue to defeat the Basel champion 6-7 (5-7) 7-5 6-1.

Djokovic requires at least a semi-final run in Paris to give himself a chance of qualifying for the season-ending ATP Finals in Turin.

Djokovic is 11th in the Race To Turin on 2,940 points but must finish inside the top nine to qualify. Rafael Jodar, still alive in Paris, is currently ninth on 3,234 points, 294 points clear of Djokovic.

Asked on Sky Sports how he was feeling physically, Djokovic, who next plays Australian Rinky Hijikata, said: "Not the freshest but obviously glad to turn this match around.

"A lot of players tell me it's completely different conditions to what it was last year.

"It's very slow and bouncy this year, just super, super quick, and Vacherot won his tournament title in Basel a few days ago, super confident and you could feel that in his game, particularly off that first serve. Not too many double faults, he's going for his second serve as well.

"His third serve is quicker than my first so it's very difficult to face someone like that. I should have taken that first set, just an awful volley at 5-4 and next thing you know you're down a set and down a break."

"I'm glad that I kind of found a way to turn this match around."

British No 1 Arthur Fery may have hit a career-high world No 32 and produced the shot of the match with a between-the-legs drop-shot winner, but he was ultimately taken out by home favourite Quentin Halys 7-6 (8-6) 7-5.

Fery was unable to take two set points in the first-set tie-break with big-serving Frenchman Halys claiming the only break of serve in the second for victory.

British No 2 Cameron Norrie also made an early exit, as he was swept aside 6-3 6-4 by US Open finalist Ben Shelton.

Shelton, who has captured four tour-level titles, including his second ATP Masters 1000 crown in Canada and reached a maiden Grand Slam final at the US Open this year, announced that he will add Alcaraz's former mentor Juan Carlos Ferrero to his team in 2027.

"Juan Carlos is one of the great minds in tennis," Shelton said.

"His experience of winning at the highest level and developing champions will be a huge asset to our team."

Ferrero said: "Ben has extraordinary talent, power and character. I'm looking forward to contributing my experience and working together with everyone to help Ben achieve his ambitions."

Taylor Fritz rallied from a set down to defeat French qualifier Benjamin Bonzi 3-6 6-3 6-4 to reach the second round where he faces another qualifier, Alex Molcan.

Tommy Paul, currently 13th in the Race To Turin on 2,775 points, beat Holger Rune 7-5 6-4.

Flavio Cobolli has announced his withdrawal from the Masters 1000 event on social media.

"I was really hoping I could make it," Cobolli wrote in his Instagram Stories. "Today, after media day, I tried training in the gym, but I'm still feeling some discomfort and I don't want to take any risks. So I've decided to withdraw from Paris.

"This one really hurts. This city gave me the two biggest weeks of my career this year, and playing here again would have meant a lot. Now it's all about recovering. Thank you for always being there."

Despite his withdrawal from the ATP Masters 1000 event in Paris, Cobolli remains in contention to make his Nitto ATP Finals debut.

The Italian is currently sixth in the Race To Turin, 466 points ahead of ninth-placed Rafael Jodar, who is effectively in the final qualification spot.

Britain's Jodie Burrage suffered a first-round loss in a gruelling encounter at the Chennai Open on Tuesday.

Taking on local wild card Vaishnavi Adkar, Britain's Burrage looked poised for victory when she led 5-3 in the deciding set but she lost four games in a row to fall to a 3-6 6-3 7-5 defeat after two hours and 47 minutes.

Burrage, who has not won a tour-level match since Eastbourne last summer, appeared to struggle with the humid conditions and called the doctor with her opponent about to serve for the match.
