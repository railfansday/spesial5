Hyatt bought Mr & Mrs Smith to bring boutique hotels into its loyalty program, and that’s working: World of Hyatt members now make most of its bookings. But those bookings bring in less money each, the UK business posted a loss in 2025, and Hyatt has taken a goodwill charge in the segment that includes it. Whether the deal pays off depends on gains elsewhere in Hyatt, which the company doesn’t break out.

Mr & Mrs Smith handled far more bookings last year than before Hyatt bought it, but earned less on each one, and its UK business posted a loss, according to newly filed accounts.

Smith Global, the UK company behind the boutique hotel booking site, made 209,565 bookings in 2025, up 41% from the year before, according to accounts filed with the UK’s Companies House business register. More than half of those bookings came from members of World of Hyatt, Hyatt’s free loyalty program.

Mr & Mrs Smith, the boutique hotel booking site Hyatt bought for £58 million ($72 million) in June 2023, grew volume sharply in 2025 but made less money on each booking, and its UK company posted a loss. Smith Global handled 209,565 bookings, up 41%, with more than half coming from World of Hyatt members, according to accounts filed with Companies House. Total booking value rose 19%, meaning the average booking fell about 16% to roughly £633 ($842), and the share Smith kept per booking dropped to about £112 from £139. Revenue grew 14% to £23.4 million while cost of sales rose 57%, pushing gross margin down to 67% from 76% and producing an operating loss of £1.5 million and a £2.7 million loss after tax. The directors said the mix of booking channels continued to place pressure on profitability. Hyatt's chief commercial officer Mark Vondrasek called the business "a strong success," citing access to European destinations where Hyatt has had limited brand presence, more than three-quarters of Smith bookings through Hyatt channels coming from elite members, and a doubling of World of Hyatt's share of luxury room nights in Europe, the Middle East and Africa. Hyatt recorded a $38 million goodwill impairment in its distribution segment in 2024 — the same amount as the goodwill booked on the deal — and has not said which business the charge related to. Hilton took a different route, licensing rather than buying with its Small Luxury Hotels of the World loyalty deal. Co-founder Tamara Lohan now oversees luxury across all of Hyatt.
