FIFA presidential election 2027: Infantino facing Diallo and Tashima — could an alliance change everything?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Two challengers to Gianni Infantino have registered for next year's FIFA presidential election. What do Philippe Diallo and Kozo Tashima stand for – and do they stand a chance against the incumbent?

Three men are vying for one of the most powerful positions in sports when delegates from more than 200 member nations of FIFA, soccer's global governing body, cast their ballots at the FIFA presidential election, to be held in Rabat on March 18, 2027. DW looks at the field of candidates.

Gianni Infantino was first elected FIFA president in February 2016, promising greater transparency and a fresh start in the wake of the corruption scandals that marked the Sepp Blatter era. Under Infantino's leadership, FIFA's revenues have continued to grow, the Club World Cup has been revamped, and the men's World Cup has been expanded to include 48 teams.

During his decade in office, the 56-year-old Swiss has built a global network of support. Infantino enjoys significant backing in Africa, South America, and large parts of Asia — a result of multi-billion-dollar development programs and financial grants provided to national associations. Many smaller member associations view him as a president who takes their interests more seriously than his predecessors did.

At the same time, critics accuse Infantino of consolidating power within the organization around himself. During his tenure, oversight and control bodies have been restructured multiple times, and key positions have been filled with his own loyalists.

Infantino's ostentatious closeness to political leaders has also sparked debate — whether exemplified by his conduct during the World Cup in Qatar, his tight ties to Saudi Arabia or his public appearances alongside US President Donald Trump (though that relationship is said to have cooled significantly after the 2026 World Cup). Detractors accuse him of seeking proximity to authoritarian rulers and intermingling political interests too closely with football.

A failed initiative to grant private investors a larger stake in lucrative FIFA competitions (FIFA Forward Enterprise) has also placed Infantino under considerable pressure. As the FIFA election approaches, he faces the prospect of legal challenges; European football's governing body UEFA is aiming to take action over the investment plan, while other critics are calling for an investigation by the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS).

However, his time in office has brought many of FIFA's 211 member associations additional development funding and other revenue. This makes him the man to beat in the March vote.

Philippe Diallo is Europe's candidate. The 63-year-old, who was elected president of the French Football Federation in 2023, is seen as an experienced sports administrator with a pragmatic and measured leadership style.

He has the backing of UEFA, as well as many of its 55 member associations, including the German Football Association (DEB).

"Philippe Diallo stands for a version of football that embraces responsibility and tackles future challenges with determination," DFB President Bernd Neuendorf said in a statement announcing the German FA's position.

"We want a FIFA that serves the sport in the interest of all its member associations. That is why we support Philippe Diallo with great conviction."

Diallo is positioning himself as a clear alternative to Infantino. His supporters see him as a candidate who stands for sound leadership, transparency, and a stronger dialogue with member associations. Unlike the incumbent, however, he lacks a global network of allies.

The third man in the race is Kozo Tashima. The 68-year-old was president of the Japan Football Association (JFA) from 2016 to 2024 and has been one of the most influential officials in Asia for decades.

During a press conference in the Japanese capital Tokyo on Friday, Tashima mentioned the words "integrity, fairness, trust and transparency" several times in explaining his decision to run.

"I just don't only want to be the FIFA president, my intention is to make FIFA better," he said.

"Being the president is the shortcut to making that happen. I just want to clean up the federation and make it a fair organization. I want to help rebuild the whole organization."

Tashima also acknowledged that failing to have won the endorsement of any of the regional confederations — the Asian Football Confederation is supporting Infantino — makes him a longshot to win the election.

But he could still play a key role in the outcome. At a press conference on Tuesday, Diallo said he intended to reach out to Tashima to "unite forces" to defeat Infantino. Tashima hasn't ruled out the notion.

"We do share some moral or ethical positions, so he's a great person to team up with," he said.

"Diallo is also very strict in governance and he does have a lot of experience in management," he added.

However, Tashima also noted that he hadn't had any thoughts of withdrawing from the race.

If Infantino's challengers do manage to join forces in some way, they might just stand a chance of upsetting the highly favored incumbent.
