Senior US official undercuts CNN’s alarming Iran ‘dirty bomb’ report: ‘It’s not new’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

President Donald Trump warned Iran on Monday that it would face unprecedented retaliation if it were to carry out an attack on Washington, D.C.

A senior U.S. official is challenging a new report on Iran developing plans for a "dirty bomb" attack on Washington, D.C., which drew a warning of overwhelming retaliation from President Donald Trump this week.

The prospect of Iranian-directed violence on American soil has shadowed U.S. security officials for decades. From a plot to bomb a Washington restaurant to assassination schemes targeting former officials and dissidents, Tehran has repeatedly sought ways to reach its enemies inside U.S. borders.

"I’m not sure why this was leaked because it’s not new," a senior U.S. official told Fox News' Jennifer Griffin in response to the report.

"Iran is very aspirational, but not suicidal. If they were to attack D.C. or any other U.S. city it would cause us to have a full out war with them. Their friends would not come to their aid."

CNN reported this week that U.S. intelligence indicated Iran was developing potential plans to target senior Trump administration officials in Washington with a radiological device or chemical weapon.

Sources described the planning as an effort to develop options rather than evidence of an imminent attack, with officials divided over whether Tehran could or would execute it.

"We’re looking into it," Trump said in Manila on Tuesday when asked about the new report.

Pressed on whether Washington faced an imminent threat, he warned: "Well, if there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before."

"I hope it’s not, but we take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy," the president added.

White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said, "We remain vigilant monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland."

Iran could make a dirty bomb, but would need someone inside the United States with the expertise to handle the materials and carry out an attack, said Andrea Stricker, deputy director of the Foundation for Defense of Democracies’ nonproliferation program.

A dirty bomb combines conventional explosives with radioactive material, spreading contamination without producing a nuclear explosion.

The White House is seen from the Washington Monument on Nov. 4, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Kevin Carter/Getty Images)

U.S. authorities have spent nearly two decades building defenses against that possibility.

DHS launched its Securing the Cities program in 2007 to help local agencies detect and deter nuclear and radiological threats, including dirty bombs, beginning in the New York City region.

"Making a radiological dispersal device would be within Iran’s know-how, as well as a chemical weapon," Stricker told Fox News Digital.

Iran’s chemical weapons program dates back to the 1980s. "More recently, they've been looking at how to weaponize chemical weapons like fentanyl and disperse them through the air," Stricker said.

Fentanyl can suppress breathing to the point of respiratory arrest.

"I think for an attack using chemical weapons like this, they would probably want to go with something rudimentary. Industrial scale chemicals like chlorine would be easy to obtain," she added.

Rather than risk transporting hazardous materials into the country, Tehran could look to obtain them here, she said.

"They may want to simply try to steal them or procure them from inside the United States. That would probably ease their task," Stricker said.

But the technical demands would complicate Iran’s reliance on hired criminals to execute its operations.

"In the past, they've used sort of everyday criminals for hire to execute some of their other plots, and luckily we foiled a lot of these," she said.

Fox News’ Jennifer Griffin contributed to this report.
