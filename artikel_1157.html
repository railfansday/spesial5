Man United launch new matchday mental health and suicide prevention hub at OId Trafford for Brentford clash

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 06:19 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 08:02 EST, 27 November 2026

Manchester United will launch a new matchday mental health and suicide prevention initiative at Saturday’s game against Brentford at Old Trafford.

Working with the Premier League and Talk About It Mate, a support group based in Salford, United will host 10 sessions of The Check-In this season aimed at providing a welcoming space where supporters can drop in to talk with trained counsellors and volunteers.

Rhys Bennett, who was the captain of United's last FA Youth Cup-winning team in 2022, has spoken about the benefits of a recent Talk About It Mate session at Salford Lads Club. Bennett’s father David tragically took his own life last year.

‘It’s probably the most powerful thing I’ve ever done,’ said Bennett. ‘I’ve never done anything like this before. It’s made me feel even better than I did when I came in the door two hours ago. I’ve enjoyed every minute of it.

‘My dad passed at 51 and I think he grew up in the, “you’ll be alright, just crack on” (generation). He was an honest working man, and he went about his daily business until it became too much for him.

‘For him it would have been a really important thing to get things off his chest. A judgement free zone. It would have helped him massively. People are sharing the same experiences, people sat in a circle all going through different things.

‘To be honest, I was a little bit nervous. I'm not a nervous kind of person, but I think within five minutes, it's just no judgment and having that full confidence in being able to talk about how I'm really feeling.

‘I'm coming up for a year of my dad's passing. My dad committed suicide, and I played football for a living. I think it's hard navigating through being in a high-pressure environment.

‘I think it is the judgment thing, and not wanting to burden other people. But I think it's so important that you realise that most of the time it's not a burden.’

Bennett, who made a permanent move to Fleetwood in the summer and played in the League Two club’s shock Carabao Cup win over Arsenal last month, added: ‘You put the brave face on, and I feel weaker. But the more I talk about it, and the more I let my emotions out, that definitely does make me stronger in day-to-day life.

‘I used to just shut it out because I'm rocking up on a Saturday and a Tuesday in front of fans, and I've got to perform, and I feel everything. You get the adrenaline and everything of a football match, and then I'm back down to square one.

‘Just how open everyone was and how open I could be myself, really did surprise me. There wasn’t at any point that I couldn’t share something I was feeling. Everything came from the heart, it was really authentic and really powerful.’

Running five weekly men’s groups across Salford and with more than 1,000 group sessions delivered, Talk About It Mate creates spaces where men can connect, share experiences and support one another.

With a community-led approach to improving mental health and well-being, the organisation helps men build connections and reduce isolation.

Mike Richard, founder of Talk About It Mate, said: ‘We've spent years creating spaces where men feel safe enough to open up, and we're proud to be working with Manchester United to bring that approach to supporters.

‘Football has an incredible ability to bring people together, but being surrounded by thousands of people doesn't necessarily mean you feel connected. We know that some of the most important conversations can start in ordinary, familiar places – that's the kind of environment we want to create at Old Trafford.

‘This isn't about waiting until someone reaches crisis point. It's about making conversations around mental health a normal part of everyday life.’

According to The Samaritans, 5,738 suicides were registered in England in 2025, which equates to 11.1 deaths per 100,000 people.

The male suicide rate was 17.2 per 100,000 – compared to 5.4 per cent among females – with men aged 50-54 having the highest rate of any population group.

Rachel Fraser, United’s internal communications and inclusion director, said: ‘Our fans give so much to this club, and ensuring they feel supported in return is incredibly important to us.

‘Through our partnership with Talk About It Mate, we are proud to provide a welcoming space where supporters can access help on matchdays.

‘Football creates friendships and communities for fans that span generations. The Check-In builds on that sense of connection and seamlessly fits into the matchday routine, providing a safe and accessible space for those needing support.’

If you are affected by any of the issues raised in this article, please call the Samaritans for free on 116123.
