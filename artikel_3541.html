Two Budget Smartwatches That Make Perfect Gifts

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

If you or someone on your shopping list wants a new smartwatch, you have plenty of options to choose from. The problem is, a lot of them can be quite pricey. Apple's cheapest smartwatch, the SE 3, starts at $249, and its other selections only skyrocket from there. On the flip side, the risk in picking a smartwatch that's too inexpensive is that the manufacturer likely made a series of sacrifices to get the price that low. At a certain point, the tradeoff just isn't worth it.

Luckily, you do have options. There are two smartwatches on the market that aren't just cheaper than the competition, they're not even in the same ballpark. And while you shouldn't expect the same capabilities as you would on flagship wearables, these devices are perfectly usable, both as fitness trackers and smartwatches—all while spending well below $100 a pop. CNET put both smartwatches through their paces to make these determinations, so you know whichever model you get, you won't be picking up a wearable paperweight.

Contrary to what you might expect, Amazfit has no ties to Amazon—other than that you can buy these watches from the latter. Amazfit is its own company, and produces a wide selection of smartwatch options. The Bip 6 happens to be the company's latest budget smartwatch, with an MSRP of just $79.99. In most cases, a price that low would be a red flag for a smartwatch, but not in the Bip 6's case.

The Amazfit Bip 6 holds its own against other flagship smartwatches. That's true on a surface level: This watch looks your average smartwatch, rather than a cheap imitation. In fact, at first glance, I'd bet most people would assume the Bip 6 is actually an Apple Watch. It has a rounded square design with a large 1.97-inch display with a resolution of 390 by 450 pixels (though there's no Always-On display). This isn't a cheap display, either: Amazfit put an AMOLED screen on the watch, which looks great indoors—though CNET Labs struggled to see the screen in direct sunlight.

Where the Bip 6 really shines, however, is with fitness tracking. This watch supports GPS, which makes tracking outdoor runs possible without a smartphone. CNET Labs found that it takes about 10 seconds to find a GPS signal, but once it connects, you're set. While running, the Bip 6 reliably tracks your heart rate. CNET Labs compared its sensor to the Polar chest strap HR monitor and found it on par, though, like other smartwatches, the Bip 6 is slower at detecting heart rate spikes. You'll get data like cadence and stride while tracking runs, and strength training and muscle groups while working out indoors. For example, CNET Labs found that it correctly identified arm muscle exercises while testing the watch during a pilates session.

You also get some health and wellness features, too. The Bip 6 can measure heart rate variations, body temperature changes, sleep stages, and even your breathing quality. Its One Tap Measuring mode supports a single reading for heart rate, SpO2 (blood oxygen saturation), stress, and breathing rate, and it supports cycle tracking—though CNET specifies it can't track skin temperature like other smartwatches and health trackers. There's an optional subscription that provides AI coaching tools and looks out for health conditions like sleep apnea and insomnia. While health and wellness tracking is solid, note that CNET Labs found Amazfit's app, Zepp, to be confusing, since it simply displays your data without much context or explanation.

For an $80 smartwatch, the Bip 6 has an excellent battery life. CNET Labs found that the watch could be pushed to its limits—multiple workouts using GPS, heart rate tracking, keeping the display on during workouts—and still run for eight days before needing a charge. In fact, reviewer Vanessa Hand Orellana speculates you could push the Bip 6 to Amazfit's two-week battery life claim by limiting some of the most intensive settings and features. The only downside here is that there is no fast charging, so it will take about two hours to get back to 100%. But if you're only charging the watch once every week or two, that isn't much of a burden. There's also no charger in the box, so you'll need to use a separate USB-C cable.

As a smartwatch, the Bip 6 can be a mixed bag. CNET found that the UI was clunky and sluggish, and the OS requires some user intervention before it's as usable as a standard Apple Watch or Galaxy Watch. While the watch has a voice assistant, Flow, CNET found it to be borderline unusable. However, CNET says Android users can reply to texts with a keyboard or dictation without needing the assistant. iPhone users can't do the same by default, but by downloading a third-party app, they can jerry-rig the watch to send and receive texts.

Based on CNET Labs' testing, the Amazfit Bip 6 is one of the best budget smartwatches you can buy. For $80, you get a reliable workout tracker that looks like most standard watches on the market. And while you're making some sacrifices at this price point, they're largely worth it for $80.

While the Amazfit is a good choice for both iPhone and Android users, the Galaxy Fit 3 might be the best choice for Samsung users. This isn't an option for iPhone users, and while it works with all Android devices, you get some Samsung-specific perks if you have a Galaxy phone.

The Galaxy Fit 3 is even cheaper than the Amazfit Bip 6: This watch is only $60, which makes it among the cheapest smartwatches you can buy. Now, this doesn't look like your average smartwatch. Samsung opted for a fitness tracker-like design, with a long 1.6-inch (40mm) AMOLED display. But for a fitness tracker/smartwatch hybrid, it's a solid look.

In CNET's testing, one of the Galaxy Fit 3's biggest strengths is its battery life—though it pales in comparison to the Bip 6. When pushed to its limits, the Fit 3 can run three days in between charges, though Samsung claims you can get it up to 13 days with the right settings. While the Amazfit has it beat, the Fit 3 might sound like a dream compared to other smartwatches, even from Samsung. Unlike the Bip 6, the Fit 3 supports fast charging. You can fill the watch up completely in just over an hour.

CNET did find that the "smart" elements of this wearable were a bit lacking, however. The Fit 3 doesn't support voice dictation or mobile payments like other watches do, and unless you have a Galaxy phone, you won't be able to access the camera remote or sync your alarms. And while the Amazfit Bip 6's "Flow" is hardly reliable, the Fit 3 doesn't have an assistant at all. You can see incoming calls and decline (but not answer) them, and you can send prewritten responses to text messages, but not initiate them. Still, CNET found that Samsung added the essentials: You have an alarm, calculator, weather app, camera remote, and, of course, the Samsung Health app. (What kind of health tracker would this be without it?)

CNET found that the Fit 3 is a reliable fitness tracker, particularly for $60. You can track over 100 different workouts, and the watch automatically logs exercises like runs, walks, rowing, and elliptical sessions—if you enable auto-tracking, that is. It also supports swimming with its IP68 water resistance rating. CNET Labs found that the heart rate tracking was "generally accurate" when compared to the Polar chest strap, though its slower with registering heart rate spikes. Interestingly, the heart rate tracker was a bit delayed in testing, but once CNET reviewed the data in the health app, the data had corrected to be largely accurate. You also get high and low heart rate alerts, though no ECG app. (The $249 Apple Watch SE 3 also omits this feature.)

In CNET's testing, the sleep tracker on the Fit 3 was useful, if not entirely accurate. The watch can detect your sleep stages and look out for snoring using your Galaxy phone's mic. Like other health trackers, you get a sleep score, but CNET found it a bit inaccurate, since it rated a six and a half hour sleep session "Excellent" (91/100).

Still, the Fit 3 is really the only other smartwatch option at this price point that people should seriously consider. Until another company drops a product around this MSRP, you have two solid choices in the Amazfit Bip 6 and Galaxy Fit 3.
