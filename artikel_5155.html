Cardano Eyes 2,000% Replay From Current Base

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Home » Crypto Market News » Cardano Eyes 2,000% Replay From Current Base

As 131 million ADA tokens sit idle in retired pools, the weekly chart structure points to $1.37 as next-cycle destination.

Cardano is trading near $0.19 on a weekly chart that one analyst compares directly to the base that preceded a roughly 2,000% rally in the last cycle. At the same time, on-chain data shows 131.63 million ADA remains tied to retired stake pools, leaving tens of thousands of holders without rewards until they redelegate.

360Trader highlighted the weekly structure and noted that ADA remains well below the level where a true bull-market stage would begin. That stage, in the analyst’s view, only starts once price clears $1.37.

The more immediate requirement is a reclaim of $0.25. Until then the chart is still forming the same kind of low base that previously launched a 2,000% advance once the broader altcoin market turned. The comparison is structural rather than a near-term price target.

From current levels a move to the prior cycle high would still require a multi-hundred-percent gain, and the larger $1.37 threshold sits even further away.

ADA has not yet reclaimed $0.25, the level 360Trader said would be needed before the setup becomes interesting again. The token has been consolidating near the lower end of its multi-year range while the wider crypto market digests recent pressure.

The analyst’s point is that the base itself is not the signal. Confirmation only arrives when price begins to push out of the accumulation zone and the broader altcoin cycle starts to expand.

Until those conditions appear, the historical 2,000% precedent remains a reminder of what has happened from comparable structures rather than a forecast for the next few weeks.

In related Cardano news, Cexplorer data flagged a practical on-chain issue. Roughly 59,025 delegators still have funds assigned to 2,720 retired stake pools, totaling 131.63 million ADA.

Those pools no longer produce blocks or distribute rewards. Holders retain full control of their tokens and can move the delegation to an active pool at any time. The funds are not locked or lost, but every epoch spent on a retired pool is simply a missed reward.

Community responses noted that checking a pool’s status on an explorer and redelegating takes only a minute and restores eligibility for the next epoch’s rewards.

The technical observation and the staking data sit side by side. One points to a historical base that once produced a 2,000% move once conditions aligned.

The other shows that a measurable portion of the staked supply is currently idle because pool operators retired and holders have not yet updated their delegations.

ADA remains near $0.19, below the $0.25 reclaim level and far from the $1.37 threshold that would mark a shift into a broader bull-market phase.

Whether the base develops the same way as last cycle depends on price reclaiming those levels and on the wider altcoin market providing the liquidity. In the meantime, any Cardano holder still delegated to a retired pool can restore rewards by simply choosing an active one.
