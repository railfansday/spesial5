XRP proposed lending vaults lock deposits and expose holders to risk

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Proposed XRPL lending rules would lock XRP vault withdrawals during investment periods, exposing depositors to borrower repayment risk.

Proposed V1.1 rules for the XRP Ledger (XRPL) would require new loan brokers to use closed-ended native-lending vaults, forgoing withdrawals during the investment period and leaving depositors with shares exposed to borrower repayments.

In a Nov. 12 snapshot, XRPLWin showed LendingProtocol and LendingProtocolV1_1 each at 22 of 35 trusted-validator votes, with SingleAssetVault at 23 of 35. The explorer displayed a 29-vote threshold, and XRPL’s amendment rules require more than 80% support maintained for two weeks.

Under Ripple’s proposed V1.1 transaction rules, newly created loan brokers would have to attach to closed-ended vaults. Brokers created before that amendment activates could continue originating loans against open-ended vaults.

During subscription, depositors can add assets and withdraw, while both actions are blocked and capital can fund loans during investment. Redemption reopens withdrawals, while new deposits and new loans are barred.

The lending mechanism transfers requested principal to the borrower, excluding fees. Once XRP is lent, the depositor holds a claim through vault shares while the borrower controls the disbursed tokens.

A deposit made with already-owned XRP does not establish a market purchase, and an investment-period withdrawal lock also does not permanently immobilize the XRP passed to borrowers.

The amount held idle in vaults, the amount outstanding on loan, and what borrowers do with it would have to be measured separately before drawing conclusions about tradable supply.

XRPL’s design uses fixed-term, uncollateralized loans and off-chain underwriting, so holders take on exposure to borrowers’ ability to repay and the broker’s credit decisions.

In an XRP lending vault, borrower-paid XRP interest could increase the XRP claim represented by those shares, while losses could reduce it. That keeps the investment denominated in XRP without guaranteeing the number of tokens ultimately recovered.

A broker may deposit the same asset in a separate account to buffer defaults, but that protection is optional and configurable. Its presence does not guarantee repayment or eliminate depositor losses.

V1.1 would also put newly created vaults on cash-basis accounting, with interest becoming income when paid. Vaults created under the earlier whole-life model, which books scheduled interest at loan origination, would retain that accounting permanently.

The vault must permit transfers, and a recipient must be eligible to hold both the shares and the underlying asset. Private-vault credentials and applicable asset restrictions can prevent a transfer.

Even where ownership can move, a sale requires a willing buyer and an executable price.

Arf’s Oct. 22 announcement with Ripple noted that its first phase would provide direct RLUSD working capital on XRPL, with a planned move to native lending infrastructure after amendment activation. Underwriting would remain off-chain, and RLUSD would be the asset disbursed and repaid.

For holders, the next tests are amendment activation, funded XRP vaults, borrower drawdowns, paid interest, defaults and repayments.

Successful share sales would separately show whether an early exit is usable. Until those outcomes are observable, the proposed bargain for a closed-ended XRP vault is a withdrawal commitment in exchange for credit exposure, with borrower performance determining the income.
