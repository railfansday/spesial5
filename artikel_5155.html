Ethereum’s quantum defense plans could delay a major upgrade to stop transaction censorship

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Ethereum developers weigh how to deliver transaction censorship protections alongside work on quantum-resistant consensus.

Ethereum’s core developers kept a proposal to curb transaction censorship on the schedule for an upgrade called Hegotá at their Nov. 26 meeting.

They deferred a final decision while client teams considered whether the work could run alongside efforts to make the network’s consensus system resistant to quantum attacks.

The proposal, known as FOCIL, remains Hegotá’s lead consensus change. The All Core Devs call record puts the next review of the fork’s scope around Dec. 10.

Under draft EIP-7805, a committee of validators would broadcast lists of transactions waiting in the public queue. Validators that vote on the next block would check the lists they received before a cutoff.

If a builder leaves out a listed transaction that is still valid at the end of the block and fits in the remaining gas, those validators would withhold their votes. They would ignore lists from a committee member they saw send conflicting versions.

For someone whose eligible transaction makes a timely list, a builder’s decision to leave it out could cost that block its votes.

EthPandaOps’ October Heze devnet plan carried forward tests of list propagation, timing, and how inclusion lists interact with the separation of block builders and proposers.

The team kept Frame Transactions, another Hegotá proposal, on a separate test track. The proposed Bogotá devnet would focus on execution-layer testing while teams wait for a decision before running a combined test.

FOCIL co-author Jihoon Song argues that developers and testers can move inclusion lists toward release while researchers work on post-quantum decoupled consensus, a redesign of how Ethereum agrees on blocks.

He would start with preregistration so validators can register new keys ahead of a switch in how they sign. Song expects most FOCIL work to be done by the time the consensus design is ready for client teams to implement for mainnet.

At the Nov. 24 post-quantum breakout, developers discussed how to restore a test network that stalled after some clients took too long to generate new validator keys. The test is part of the early consensus work the Ethereum Foundation described in its Nov. 23 update.

Ethlabs researcher Caspar Schwarz-Schilling argues that a small group of researchers and client developers with a broad view of Ethereum would be needed to deliver both changes safely.

He favors deferring FOCIL so that group can focus on consensus. The extent of the overlap depends in part on what developers expect the first version of FOCIL to handle.

The FOCIL draft relies on BLS signatures to authenticate the lists themselves, and those signatures would eventually need a quantum-resistant replacement.

In a separate technical analysis, Song also identifies work on how lists would carry larger post-quantum transactions or the proofs that could accompany them without overloading the network.

The decoupled consensus design also leaves open which group of validators would enforce the lists.

Barnabas Busa proposed a narrower version that would enforce inclusion for certain existing transaction types and excuse builders from including Frame Transactions.

Inclusion lists could then protect users of those existing transaction types in Hegotá, while users of Frame Transactions would have to wait for enforcement to cover them.

The next post-quantum consensus session is scheduled for Nov. 27 at 14:00 UTC. When developers return to Hegotá’s scope, the practical test is how much work the first version needs from the people designing the new consensus system.
