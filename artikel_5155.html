CoinShares has sold all of the Ether it held in its corporate treasury, worth $16.6 million, along with half of its Solana holding, worth $2.7 million. The sales raised $19.3 million to help pay for a share buyback of up to $40 million. The company disclosed the move in its third-quarter 2026 earnings report on November 18.

The reason is a change in how the company wants to use its capital. Rather than hold digital assets on its balance sheet, CoinShares is moving that money into buying back its own stock.

Companies usually buy back shares when they believe their stock is undervalued, since fewer shares outstanding increases each remaining shareholder’s stake. By choosing buybacks over holding Ether, CoinShares is signaling that it sees better value in its own equity than in its crypto holdings.

The ETH sale also comes as Ether has fallen sharply, losing about 14% recently.

The buyback came alongside mixed quarterly results:

The higher profit on much lower revenue suggests that earnings were helped by items outside core fee income. The company has not said which ones in the figures released so far.

Ether is still testing support between about $1,730 and $1,800 on the daily chart. The level has been tested several times over the past few days, but no daily candle has closed below it.

The near-term outlook is for Ether to hold above its recent lows for at least one to two weeks, in a pause from the crash rather than a full reversal. The daily RSI is starting to recover but remains in oversold territory, which leaves room for more sideways or relief trading. Bullish momentum is still missing.

A break below current support is not expected in the next few days, but it remains a risk over the coming weeks. If it happens, the $1,650 level would come into play first, followed by the $1,510 to $1,570 zone.
