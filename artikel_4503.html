The Scion Brand Returns to America With a 3-Cylinder Kei Truck for the U.S.

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The formerly youth-focused brand is becoming Toyota’s new “freedom lab” for pushing the boundaries of mobility.

Scion lived a short life, the victim of bad financial timing and millennial misunderstandings. But its parent company, Toyota, is about to give the brand another go—and its market research appears to be spot-on this time, as the Japanese automaker announces a new three-cylinder Kei truck for the U.S. market.

Officially known as the Scion Kei-01, the new model takes traditional Japanese compactness and adds North American levels of capability. The heart of the Kei-01 is a 660cc three-cylinder engine paired with a continuously variable transmission and proper four-wheel drive with push-button lock-up. At 11 feet long and five feet wide, the Kei truck will be even smaller than a modern Mini Cooper.

The Kei-01 will be right-hand-drive, have a maximum speed of 25 miles per hour, and is intended to comply with federal safety requirements for low-speed vehicles. An easily accessible three-way fold-down cargo bed, a payload capacity of 600 pounds, and an extended cabin design with a raised roofline were included specifically for the North American market. Scion has not revealed power figures for the Kei-01, but Toyota did confirm the Kei truck will be available in the U.S. market.

Initially, the Kei-01 will arrive in the U.S. as part of a fleet vehicle pilot program. Scion said this new Kei truck will be an intentionally low-volume, niche vehicle for specific kinds of fleets; it’s targeting small business owners looking for an affordable urban commercial vehicle, farmers requiring a dependable daily workhorse, and municipalities, universities, and corporate campuses seeking efficient fleet vehicles. Last-mile delivery services and outdoor enthusiasts are also being targeted by Toyota. Fleet sales will route through select Toyota dealerships, and the truck will be produced at Toyota Motor Manufacturing Indiana in the second half of 2027.

However, Toyota says it is open to expanding availability of the bite-sized pickup. “Based on market conditions, customer demand, and operational considerations, Toyota may consider expanding availability to limited retail sales at a later date. Additional information and the broader sales strategy will be shared as plans are finalized,” the carmaker stated.

Two additional Scion concept vehicles were showcased alongside the Kei-01 at Toyota Motor North America events in Ypsilanti Township, Michigan, and Frisco, Texas, on Tuesday, November 10. The Scion SxS Concept takes on off-roading with a side-by-side frame and a high-output turbocharged four-cylinder hybrid powertrain. The off-road-focused model will feature over 300 horsepower, significant suspension articulation, and a nimble footprint; there will even be a Silent Mode that shifts motive power to the electric drivetrain.

Additionally, Scion is showing off the BKE Concept, a new kind of mobility tool for the revived automotive brand. Packing only two wheels, the BKE Concept is a personal mobility scooter made for the great outdoors. Scion didn’t provide any other details about the BKE Concept, but it appears to be a comfortable urban commuter.

Regardless of the ultimate plans of Scion’s new Kei truck and concept vehicles, the return of the fun-loving brand is worth celebrating. Toyota has been razor-focused on car enthusiasts with its Gazoo Racing and off-road-focused models, and Scion offers another opportunity for enthusiast appeal. But instead of focusing on young buyers exclusively, Scion will be a “freedom lab” for Toyota, with a focus on experimentation.

“The future of mobility will not be defined by one vehicle, one technology or one way of thinking,” said Simon Humphries, chief branding officer, head of design, Toyota Motor Corporation. “Scion by Toyota gives us a place to challenge conventional thinking, explore new possibilities and create mobility in new ways. The KEI-01, SxS, and BKE concepts each represent a different possibility—and together, they show what can happen when curiosity leads the way.”
