Are the Sixers destined to deal with irrational haters this entire season?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

People hating on the Philadelphia 76ers is nothing new. The Process rubbed some people the wrong way and Joel Embiid became a totem for that era of Sixers basketball. Embiid is a lightning rod for criticism, whether due to the perceived poor work ethic narrative around his early conditioning habits, or the social media trolling that has now almost entirely subsided, or his flopping tendencies that still persist to this day. So those haters are still going to be hanging around, waiting for Joel, and by extension, the Sixers, to fail. That's sadly a part of modern sports discourse.

Now, LeBron James is a Philadelphia 76er and he has his own legions of haters. The perceived arrogance of The Decision turned some people off. Folks of a certain political identity took a side during the Shut Up and Dribble saga. People who loved Michael Jordan take offense at LeBron being in the GOAT conversation. Kobe stans feel the need to interject themselves into the dialogue. We knew signing LeBron came along with all the associated hoopla and nonsense, but it's still equal parts fascinating and frustrating watching it play out in real time.

Once or twice per week, somebody writes something or talks about the Sixers in a way that seems detached from reality, or simply wishcasting failure on the team. I'm sorry to report that the latest comes from right here at SB Nation, 'The Philadelphia 76ers are not as good as they think they are.'

First, anyone who has followed the team closely could tell you how the guys on the Sixers are constantly discussing how much the team has to work on and improve. There has been no victory lapping here whatsoever. Then, the author centers the majority of the article around the loss to the Cavaliers two weeks ago, Philadelphia's worst performance of the season in which Embiid and James played by far their worst games in hugely uncharacteristic fashion. If we focused on any team's worst game, we could come up with a scathing rebuke of that team's chances. But maybe the Eastern Conference-best 12-3 record against a fairly difficult schedule is more indicative of how things are going than one game a couple weeks ago?

Yes, the fact that the team's success in the clutch is likely to regress to the mean to some degree is a topic worth exploring. I obviously wish the Sixers would do a better job putting opponents away earlier. But framing it as some fatal flaw makes no sense to me, when usually success in the clutch is perceived as an admirable advantage. Perhaps injecting this roster with a couple guys who have succeeded at every possible level of basketball is helpful in staying calm and executing in the pressure-packed final minutes of a game. Almost sounds like…playoff basketball?

Even within what I felt was a thoughtful article from ESPN's Dave McMenamin a couple days ago, we're still hearing things out in NBA circles like the following quote:

Added a scout from an Eastern Conference team: "My initial thoughts on them is they have three guys who need to have the ball on the floor to score, a center who needs the ball but is never healthy and a stud in VJ, who could be the odd man out and his development/growth is going to take a hit."

How is the 'one ball' narrative still a thing? LeBron is taking by far the fewest field goal attempts of his career. Tyrese Maxey is running off the ball more than ever. Jaylen Brown largely assumes a cutter/spot-up role with the starters before shifting into the offensive hub alongside bench units. People still trotting that line out aren't aware of what's actually happening. And VJ Edgecombe's development? He's been crushing it on both ends of the court, a 'doing the little things' superstar. Plus, he's learning alongside one of the greatest basketball minds ever. Taking two fewer shots per game compared to his rookie season isn't going to derail an incredibly promising career.

I'm not saying the Sixers are perfect. Far from it. The iso-heavy offense likely needs tweaking. They probably could use some more outside shooting. The backup center situation is a mess as always. If people want to explore legitimate question marks around the team, fair game. But it seems ridiculous to ignore everything that has been going right for the team, while sticking to some debunked narratives, just because you seemingly don't want the Sixers to succeed.
