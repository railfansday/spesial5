Tanner Jeannot’s Viral Fight Sparks Debate On NHL Culture

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A brawl on Wednesday night has everyone talking about the state of the NHL.

Bruins enforcer Tanner Jeannot dropped the gloves with Winnipeg Jets' Tyrel Bauer in a clash that fans are already calling the "fight of the year." The two players skated off to the penalty box after a back-and-forth scrap that lasted well over a minute. Jeannot's fight with Tyrel Bauer raises old questions about violence in the game while revealing exactly who Jeannot is today.

Jeannot is no stranger to this kind of action. His entry-level contract was signed in April 2018, marking the start of his career with the Predators. According to a report, he spent two seasons with the team's AHL affiliate in Milwaukee, where he racked up 126 penalty minutes in 94 games. Having accumulated 217 penalty minutes across 152 games, he departed Nashville midway through the 2022-23 season. His path took him through the Tampa Bay Lightning and Los Angeles Kings before he landed in Boston for his second season there.

While Jeannot draws heat for fighting, other players face strict financial penalties for bad hits. The NHL Department of Player Safety recently suspended New Jersey Devils defenseman Brenden Dillon for four games. He delivered an illegal check to the head of Columbus Blue Jackets defenseman Denton Mateychuk. The league noted that the head contact was avoidable and caused an injury. Dillon has a history with the league, having been fined once and suspended twice before. The NHL press release stated, "Under the terms of the Collective Bargaining Agreement and, based on his average annual salary, Dillon will forfeit $82,474.24. The money goes to the Players' Emergency Assistance Fund."

Fans love the raw emotion of a fight, but the game is changing fast. Jeannot's viral moment proves that physical play still grabs attention in a league focused on speed and skill. His history with the Predators shows he was built for this role early on. He even played a short stint in the ECHL with the Florida Everblades, where he scored seven goals in just eight games. That grit defined him during his time in Nashville. Now, he brings that same energy to the Boston Bruins. The fight on Wednesday night was a reminder that some fans still want to see the gloves come off.

The contrast between a celebrated fight and a costly suspension shows the fine line players walk. Jeannot gets social media love for his scrap, while Dillon loses money and playing time for a dangerous hit. This split view defines the modern NHL. The league tries to protect players from injury while allowing space for traditional toughness. Jeannot's career path from the AHL to the NHL shows how players can survive by adapting to these rules. He has moved through multiple teams to find his spot. The fight on Wednesday night was a clear signal that his style of play still has a place in the league.

Jeannot played 152 games with the Predators and picked up 217 penalty minutes during his time there.
