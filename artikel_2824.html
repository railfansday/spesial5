Giants’ defense has helped create NFC East lead, but matchup with Jaguars is a different beast

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Heading into Week 11 of the NFL season, one of the biggest surprises in the league has been the New York Giants. The Giants were widely seen as a bottom-feeder in the NFC East division, behind the Dallas Cowboys and the Philadelphia Eagles.

Well, more than half of the season is behind us, and the Giants have a healthy lead atop the division. Injuries have forced the Eagles into a rebuild, and the Cowboys have failed miserably on all of the bets they made to revamp their defense. As a result, the Giants appear headed toward a playoff spot despite sitting at just 5-3-1 on the season.

A big part of that has been the identity brought in by new head coach John Harbaugh. This Giants team plays in the same mold that a lot of Harbaugh's old Ravens teams played during his successful tenure in Baltimore. They run the ball effectively, control the clock, take care of the football, and have a stingy defense that is led by a fearsome front seven.

Now, the Giants will face their biggest test of this breakout 2026 season against the 7-2 Jacksonville Jaguars on Sunday in Week 11. The Jags are arguably the best team in the NFL and have been dominant in most of their victories this year, so they will provide a stiff challenge for the Giants on both sides of the ball.

Slowing down the Jacksonville offense is going to be the most difficult task that the Giants have to face on Sunday. What advantages can they create, and how will the Jags try to attack?

Kevin R. WexlerNorthJersey.com / USA TODAY NETWORK via Imagn Images

The passing game gets a lot of the shine for the Jaguars, and rightfully so. All of the cool and shiny parts of this Jags offense are on display in the passing game. Trevor Lawrence is having his best season, Parker Washington has blossomed into a superstar, and Liam Coen is able to use all of his different weapons effectively in a harmonious symphony that is the Jacksonville aerial attack.

But the running game builds the foundation that the Jaguars operate off of. They don't have an elite running game, but they have a top 10 run rate on first down and use that to set up the passing game. It's an above-average attack on the ground from an efficiency standpoint, ranking 12th in EPA per rush. But the success rate and yards per play are both below average, which makes for an interesting matchup against the Giants.

The Giants have a below-average run defense themselves. New York ranks in the bottom 10 in the NFL in yards per rush allowed and EPA per rush allowed, so teams have been able to get after them on the ground at times this season. The loss of Dexter Lawrence up front is a big part of that, and the young guys on the front seven in a new defense have struggled to bow up in the trenches at times.

But there is one area where this matchup is a true weakness for both teams, and it comes in the first few seconds of the play. Jacksonville is one of the worst teams in the NFL when it comes to yards before contact, ranking 30th at just 0.69 per Next Gen Stats. Only the Packers and Raiders, two of the worst running games in recent NFL history, are below them. Bhayshul Tuten and the rest of the Jaguars backs do a good job breaking tackles and getting yards after contact, but Jacksonville has really struggled to block plays up on the ground this year.

On the other side, the Giants are giving up the sixth-most yards before contact (1.65) in the NFL, so it has been relatively easy for opponents to find success with the design of the running game this season.

This is a pretty evenly-matched battle coming in, and the winner is so important because…

Trevor Lawrence has absolutely diced teams up this season, and he has done a lot of his damage on play-action dropbacks. Even when the Jags have struggled to run the ball at times, Jacksonville has used play-action effectively as defenses have to respect the threat of the run.

On play-action passes this season, Lawrence is completing more than 72% of his passes with 740 yards and five touchdowns without an interception, per Next Gen Stats. His +0.47 EPA per dropback. He is putting up a crazy 10.9 yards per attempt on those plays, also the best in the NFL. Lawrence is one of the best quarterbacks in the NFL both targeting the middle of the field and the intermediate area of the field, both of which re unlocked by play-action dropbacks.

This is all bad news for a Giants team that has gotten carved up by play-action plays so far this season. New York is allowing +0.14 EPA per pass on those plays this year, the sixth-highest mark in the NFL. Their pressure rate on those snaps is less than 30%, the seventh-lowest mark in the league. When opposing offenses are able to set up play-action against the Giants, the quarterback is usually comfortable and the Giants don't have the answers for it.

Arvell Reese, New York's star rookie middle linebacker, is one of the best young defenders in the sport. He may very well win Defensive Rookie of the Year for his stellar play. But he is still a young player who is very inexperienced. Look for Liam Coen to take advantage of that with a lot of play action and misdirection on Sunday to open up those throwing windows over the middle of the field.

Lawrence is really finding his groove lately thanks to the emergence of Parker Washington as a true star wide receiver. Washington is a ball-winner on the outside who is also effective over the middle of the field and in the slot. He complements the likes of Jakobi Meyers and Brian Thomas Jr. extremely well, and he will be a handful for the New York secondary to handle. If the Jags start to get into their play-action bag again on Sunday, Washington could be in for another big day.
