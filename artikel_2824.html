Recap: Wizards lose 144-140 in double-OT thriller vs. Pistons

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Washington Wizards' struggles continued Thursday night, as they fell to the Detroit Pistons 144-140 in double overtime (8-3) at Capital One Arena to extend their losing streak to six games.

Playing the second night of a back-to-back after a 114-101 loss to the Indiana Pacers on Wednesday, Washington (4-9) once again could not close out a close game in the fourth quarter, first or second overtime periods, which led to its second loss to the Pistons in the past four days after Monday night's 122-111 loss in Detroit.

Washington got out to an early 17-10 lead thanks to triples from Alex Sarr, Kyshawn George and Trae Young – which looked to be a positive sign from a team that entered the contest shooting 29.8% from distance.

Sarr started the game a perfect 4-for-4 from the field, quickly notching 10 points in the first quarter. His counterpart in the front court, Anthony Davis recorded 10 points on 5-of-7 shooting in first-quarter, to join Sarr in double-figure scoring.

However, in a lineup starring Trae Young, Tre Johnson, Bub Carrington, Bilal Coulibaly and Deandre Ayton, Washington's 10-point lead dissipated as the Pistons completed a 11-point swing to take a 33-32 lead over the Wizards to end the first quarter. After protecting the ball for the first nine minutes of the game, Washington coughed up four turnovers in the final 3:15 of the quarter to contribute to the Pistons run.

Washington steadied the ship before halftime with the help of Young, who scored eight second quarter points to help the Wizards stay within three and trail 64-61 entering the half. Davis paced Washington with 16 points and eight rebounds, while Sarr tacked on 14 points and Young scored 12. The trio combined for 69% of the Wizards first half points, and Sarr shot a perfect 6-for-6 from the field.

Rookie AJ Dybantsa appeared to truly struggle for the first time this season when matched up with Ausar Thompson. Dybantsa shot 0-of-2 from the field and looked uncomfortable with the ball in his hands when guarded by Thompson. Yet, the BYU product managed to record two assists and not turn the ball over in the first half, to finish with a team-high +11 when he was on the court.

After keeping the game close in the second quarter, Detroit erupted for an 11-point lead of its own with the help of Duncan Robinson who shot a perfect 3-for-3 from three point range during the run. Cade Cunningham also tacked on seven third quarter points to keep Washington at an arms length throughout the period.

The Wizards cut the lead back to four thanks to Alex Sarr, who remained perfect from the field, tacking on seven third quarter points to enter the fourth with 21 points and four rebounds. Tre Johnson was also in rhythm in the third, scoring six of his 13 points at the time while shooting 3-of-4 from the field.

Washington entered the fourth quarter trailing 94-90, with a chance to snap their five game skid in front of an energetic home crowd in D.C. — and Young matched the energy in Capital One Arena. He scored a quick nine points in the first four minutes of the fourth quarter to give Washington a 103-102, and found Coulibaly streaking down the court for an assist on a fast break.

But head coach Brian Keefe subbed Young out for a two-minute rest, and Detroit capitalized immediately with a 6-0 run to take a 108-103 lead. Washington kept it close, including taking a 118-117 lead with 31.5 seconds remaining thanks to a three from George. But Cunningham drew a foul from Dybantsa on the following possession, and knocked down two free throws to essentially seal the game. But 1.8 seconds remaining, Young buried a three at the buzzer to send the game to overtime, knotted at 121.

George knocked down a triple to give Washington a 124-121 lead in OT before picking up his sixth foul and being forced to sit the rest of the game. But in his absence, Young found Davis in the paint for a floater, and then drew a charge on the ensuing possession, which lead to Davis finding Sarr for the alley-oop to start overtime on a 7-0 run.

Detroit responded with a 6-0 run of its own led by Cunningham — who finished with a game-high 52 points and a team-high 11 assists. He sealed the game on back to back possessions, both in transition. First he nailed a triple to tie the game at 130, and then assisted Robinson on the break for a jumper to take a 132-130 lead with nine seconds remaining in the extra period.

Dybantsa, the rookie, took matters into his own hands and drove to the paint and pulled up for a jumper that tied the game at 132 at the buzzer to send the game to double-overtime.

Davis fouled out early in the second overtime, which led to Detroit taking a 137-132 lead without Washington's primary big — one they would hold for the remaining of the game.

Young and Davis both finished in double-double territory, but it was not enough to halt Washington's losing streak. Young scored 37 and dished out 14 assists — both season-highs — while Davis scored 30 points and grabbed 14 rebounds. Sarr also tacked on 30 points and seven rebounds, while George scored 14 points and Johnson finished with 13.

Washington is off until Sunday, Nov 15 when it hosts the Houston Rockets at Capital One Arena at 7 p.m. ET on MNMT.
