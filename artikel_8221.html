The Best Outdoor Gifts for Babies, Toddlers, and Little Kids Ages 0 to 5

The best outdoor gifts for babies and toddlers have to check a few boxes. Outdoor toys need to be simple enough for little hands to enjoy but engaging enough that you’re not frantically hunting for another activity 15 minutes later. Apparel and equipment gifts for kids ages 0 to 5 should provide a comfortable, safe way to experience nature, so they associate being outside with non-stop fun—not being cold, wet, and miserable.

To help you find the best outdoor gifts for babies, toddlers, and little kids, we polled our editors and other Outside employees. They shared their genius hacks, their cheap-but-awesome finds, and a few higher-end gifts that promise outdoor fun for the whole family. Every gift on this list has been personally vetted and recommended by someone at Outside—and, more importantly, by their kiddos.

A cozy fleece bunting never goes out of style, especially when it’s from a legacy brand and comes in classic colors.

What Outsiders Say: “Buying a Patagonia bunting for your baby feels like a splurge, but it’s an excellent gift to receive. It’s incredibly warm and soft, yet it still lets little ones move freely once they start walking. I’d size up so there’s room for layers underneath—and so it lasts multiple winters.”

The biggest obstacle to getting outdoors is usually adverse weather, but cold and wet won’t keep you inside if you have this cute, colorful rain suit. Zip your toddler in and get out there—those puddles aren’t going to stomp themselves.

What Outsiders Say: “Both of my girls have these for year-round outdoor play. We call them their ‘yard suits,’ and they make the answer to ‘Can we go outside?’ always yes.”

If this looks like a relic of your own childhood, it probably is (and it might be time to start using retinol). The Radio Flyer wheelbarrow has been a classic toddler toy for generations, and for good reason.

What Outsiders Say: “Parents sometimes overthink outside time, assuming they need elaborate games or planned activities. But sometimes kids just like working in the dirt. Put a wheelbarrow out, and you’ll be amazed at the random things they decide to do.”

Every parent knows the struggle of wrestling a toddler into an overstuffed puffy jacket or a pullover. Outside parents love this one because it’s lightweight and easy to clean.

What Outsiders Say: “If you have a texture-sensitive kid who refuses pullovers and sweaters, this lightweight full-zip is a perfect way to keep them warm all winter. It’s super washable and a good fit for any preschooler who needs warmth without the bulk of a heavy fleece.”

This mini beach chair lets little kids feel like one of the grown-ups, whether they’re lounging on the beach or gathered around the campfire.

What Outsiders Say: “This chair is awesome for what my family likes to call ‘easy beach deployment.’ My kids love to wear it as a backpack, and they feel a sense of pride and ownership over their own beach gear. It’s easy to hose down and has room for snacks.”

Bedtime story? Check. Now all you need is the campfire.

What Outsiders Say: “This sweet picture book makes bedtime fun and sparks curiosity about the wild places we call home.”

If the little kid you’re shopping for lives somewhere known for frigid temperatures, this under-$30 gift is a guaranteed winner.

What Outsiders Say: “My mom got each of my kids two of these last Christmas, and they were incredible at extending our time outside as temperatures dropped on the East Coast. I wouldn’t have thought to buy them myself, but they were a huge lifesaver!”

Safari? Dinosaur hunt? Spying on the dog next door? The possibilities are endless for little kids with these binocs.

What Outsiders Say: “These binoculars are durable and lightweight, and they’re an easy way to get kids curious about exploring outside—whether they’re spotting backyard birds or heading out on a big adventure.”

It’s a classic for a reason. Sometimes a set of toy trucks is just what the doctor (or the exhausted parent) ordered.

What Outsiders Say: “Want to know how to keep a toddler happy in the snow? Trucks! They’re not just for sand. And if you’re stuck at home on a snow day trying to work, these can buy you a few hours of focus time.”

Got a preschooler who loves exploring in the snow? These snowshoes are awesome, especially when they get to tag along on an adventure with you.

What Outsiders Say: “If you have a kid who likes to hunt for Bigfoot on a snowy trail, it’s worth the splurge to get them big-kid snowshoes, especially if they’re on sale (like these are). Paired with insulated knee-high Bogs, these let my four-year-old keep up.”

Fair warning: These shoes may inspire a lifelong love of climbing, whether your little kid is scaling climbing walls, rock faces, or the side of the garden shed. Proceed with caution.

What Outsiders Say: “When you’re buying shoes for the youngest climbers, budget matters more than performance: 99 percent of kids will grow out of their shoes before they wear through them. Mad Rock’s Mad Monkeys are durable, sticky enough for kids to practice their footwork, and come in sizes for the littlest crushers.”

What could be better than sharing the joy and freedom of a Saturday ride with your toddler?

Several Outsiders recommended Kids Ride Shotgun’s seats, with both the Shotgun 2.0 Seat Combo and the Shotgun Pro Combo getting votes in our employee poll. Not sure which to choose? Associate Editorial Gear Director Jenny Wiegand recommends the 2.0 for most families: “It’s more affordable and works for tots as young as 18 months, while you’d want to wait until at least age 2 for the Shotgun Pro Combo.” If you’re riding a carbon bike, the Pro is worth a look: It was redesigned so it doesn’t touch the frame, which means it’s less likely to scratch your other baby (your bike, of course).

What Outsiders Say: Of the Shotgun 2.0 Seat Combo, one Outsider says, “These seats are surprisingly stable, and kids love going along for the ride!” Another Outsider, who uses the Shotgun Pro Combo, writes, “With this seat, you can ride real singletrack with your little one and give them an early taste of how fun mountain biking is.”

Having young kids doesn’t mean your camping days are over—it just means you get to ask for upgraded gear. The Alpha Breeze got rave reviews from our crew thanks to its family-friendly features and multiple entry points.

What Outsiders Say: “Perfect for parents of new kids! My husband and I are obsessed with the newly rereleased Snow Peak Alpha Breeze. It’s a three- to four-person tent, which is perfect for two parents and a little or two. It’s super easy to pitch and is just, well, pretty. The best part, and why it’s so kid-friendly, is the extended covered front porch, which provides ample shade for babies and toddlers—crucial for those of us who like to camp in the desert and at high elevations. There are also four entry points, making it easy to take littles out for emergency bathroom breaks in the middle of the night without waking the rest of the fam.”

Know a parent of multiple young kiddos who’s been on the very, very nice list this year? This is the perfect outdoor gift. The Thule Chariot Cross is a splurge, to be sure, but it’s also something outdoorsy parents can use nearly daily for years.

What Outsiders Say: “Get this gift for any new outdoorsy parent looking to bring little ones along for the ride—literally. The Thule Chariot Cross does it all: It attaches to your bike, converts into a regular stroller, and—with some additional kits—can also become a toboggan to pull behind your cross-country skis or a proper jogging stroller. Think of it as a Transformer for adults. It comes in both a single and a double: We started with the single for my now four-year-old when he was a baby and loved it so much that we traded it in for the double when I got pregnant with my now 19-month-old. The storage is clutch: It’s got a massive (waterproof!) trunk in the back and lots of organizing pockets for both parent and kid. It also comes with a sun shade and rain fly, so your toddler stays comfy no matter the weather. It’s a big investment, yes, but it’s the one we’ve gotten the most use out of, hands down.”
