Camino Frances 500 Mile Hike Ends

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Today was the day. After walking hundreds of miles across Spain, we had just 12 miles standing between us and Santiago de Compostela. We started our day at 8:00 AM over a quick cup of coffee under cool, overcast skies. The early morning crowds had already left town, leaving us to enjoy a brief moment of quiet before setting out.

Leaving O Pedrouzo, the town faded quickly as the trail ducked into a beautiful forest framed by mossy stone walls. Coming around a sharp bend, we were suddenly inundated with large groups of hikers. It felt as though tour buses had just unloaded scores of weekend walkers onto the trail. While everyone was buzzing with energy, the crowded paths frequently forced us to slow our pace.

Despite the heavy foot traffic, the forest remained stunning. We walked along an ancient medieval path so deeply worn by centuries of footsteps that the earthen banks rose 15 to 20 feet above our heads, creating a dark, atmospheric tunnel through the woods.

As we climbed up toward a eucalyptus forest, we were saddened to see that the area had been recently clear-cut. Massive three-foot stumps covered the hillsides where giant trees once stood. Although losing the tree canopy took away some of the magic, the open landscape revealed broad views of the surrounding valleys.

After descending the hill, the trail skirted past the Santiago-Lavacolla Airport—a major hub for pilgrims wrapping up their journey or arriving for the final 100-kilometer leg.

In San Paio, we stepped inside a small chapel to get a passport stamp before dropping down into the village of Lavacolla. Historically, medieval pilgrims stopped at the local creek here to wash themselves (Lavacolla translates roughly to “wash private parts”) before entering the holy city.

From Lavacolla, we tackled a longer climb up to a ridge. We eventually reached Monte de Gozo (Mount of Joy), the hilltop spot where pilgrims for centuries have caught their very first glimpse of the Santiago Cathedral towers three miles away. Our tramily was waiting for us there alongside two of their friends from Australia. We all shared a quick drink together before beginning our final descent into the city.

After walking nearly 500 miles from France—crossing mountain ranges, enduring the Meseta, and pushing through cold, rain, heat, and fatigue—we were finally walking the streets of Santiago de Compostela.

We navigated the bustling city streets until we passed through an arched tunnel and emerged into Praza do Obradoiro, the massive granite square facing the cathedral.

The energy in the plaza was electric. Over 2,000 pilgrims arrived today from various routes across Europe. People were crying, laughing, dancing, and embracing, celebrating the end of their journeys.

While it felt amazing to stand in the square, I didn’t experience an overwhelming wave of emotion right away. To me, it felt like another major milestone on our journey—especially since we plan to take a rest day here before continuing on toward Finisterre to reach the Atlantic Ocean.

We navigated the narrow, winding alleys of the old town to locate our albergue. To our surprise, it was located right next to the cathedral! Our room was a stunning corner apartment in an ancient stone building, complete with two-foot-thick walls and floor-to-ceiling balcony openings overlooking the cobblestone street below. From my seat in the window, I could look straight up at one of the cathedral’s soaring bell tower spires.

Later that evening, we joined our tramily for the 7:30 PM Pilgrim Mass. We arrived an hour early to secure seats near the transept, hoping to see the famous Botafumeiro—the massive 116-pound silver incense burner that swings from the cathedral ceiling during special liturgies.

Although the Botafumeiro didn’t swing during our service, the homily hit home in a profound way. Using a translation app, Knockerz and I read along as the priest spoke about how short life is, how scary it can feel, and the peace that comes with letting go and trusting God. Hearing that message brought tears to my eyes; it was the exact lesson I had been seeking throughout this long journey.

After mass, we paid our respects at the crypt containing the relics of Saint James beneath the main altar. We then returned to our apartment, opened a bottle of wine, and sat by the open window watching the city below. Sitting there looking out at the cathedral felt reminiscent of our very first nights in Pamplona—a perfect moment of reflection on an incredible journey.
