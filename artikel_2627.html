‘I don’t understand how that’s relevant’: Stephen A. Smith sparks outrage with comments on white NBA players

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Los Angeles Lakers are officially Luka Dončić's team, following LeBron James' departure for the Philadelphia 76ers in free agency. The next two top stars on the roster? None other than Austin Reaves and Walker Kessler.

Last week, ESPN's Stephen A. Smith, who has never been shy to give a hot take, expressed his doubt that a team led by three white stars could find success at the highest level of NBA basketball.

"I do not think that they're getting past the second round," he said of Los Angeles on his Mad Dog Sports Radio show. "Not a team with its top three players being white dudes. I'm sorry. I think that's controversial in some people's eyes. It ain't controversial in mine. I'm just telling you how it is. I'm calling it like I see it: I have never in my lifetime seen an NBA team with its top three players being white win a championship. It has never happened, and it ain't gonna happen."

Provocative as his comments were, this week, with the Lakers surging and in first place in the Western Conference, Smith challenged "the brothers" in the league to step up and stop the Lakers.

"It's hard to believe that Smith was being 100% serious with his comments, but they rubbed plenty of people across the sports world the wrong way all the same.

"Imagine when Tiger Woods was coming up if some pundit on ESPN looked at the television and told white golfers you all gonna let this happen? Same thing," Fred J. Robledo, a sports editor for Southern California News Group, posted on X.

"What happens if a white guy on TV said white quarterbacks have to keep a black quarterback from winning a Super Bowl? He would never work in TV again," conservative sports pundit Clay Travis added.

"This sounds like something you'd hear in the 1930s or 1940s, just with the racial dynamics reversed. Think about how disturbing that is. We spent generations fighting to ensure athletes were judged by their talent, not the color of their skin. Now we're openly questioning whether Black players should allow white players to win? At what point does fighting against discrimination turn into perpetuating the same mentality we fought so hard to overcome?" Jay Williams, Smith's colleague at ESPN, wrote.

"Just let this slide I guess?" former NBA player Chandler Parsons wondered.

"He keeps harping on the Lakers' racial makeup and it's just really weird. No one cares that the team has a lot of white players," Algemeiner correspondent Corey Walker posted.

"Why does it gotta be black versus white?… I don't understand how that's relevant," Former NFL player turned NBC analyst Doc Holiday said in a video shared to X.

Smith, who recently came under fire for his sit-down interview with United States President Donald Trump in the White House, may have another fire to put out. However, knowing Smith, it wouldn't be the biggest surprise if he leaned further into his take and fanned the flames.
