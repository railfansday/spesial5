Why Another ‘Hunger Games’ Movie Isn’t Coming Anytime Soon

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

“The Hunger Games” franchise has caught fire once again.

“Sunrise on the Reaping,” the sixth installment in the billion-dollar dystopian franchise, has defied expectations and opened to $120 million at the box office, nearly tripling the $44 million opening of the 2023 prequel “The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes.”

Surely part of the success here is the Jennifer Lawrence factor: The star’s return (alongside Josh Hutcherson) was first reported by Variety nearly a year ago — and the duo’s reunion with fellow franchise veterans Woody Harrelson, Elizabeth Banks and Jena Malone at the film’s New York premiere ignited seismic levels of buzz online. According to social media analytics firm RelishMix, ‘Sunrise on the Reaping’ amassed a social media reach of 652 million. That doesn’t trail far behind the 680 million pre-opening reach of Christopher Nolan’s “The Odyssey” — and represents a staggering 66% jump from “The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes’” 393 million (more on par with the pre-opening reach of Disney’s “Hexed” at 391.9 million).

So, in today’s IP-fueled Hollywood landscape, the blazing success of “Sunrise on the Reaping” would light a fire under the asses of most studio execs to get a seventh “Hunger Games” movie on the calendar as swiftly as possible. While it’d be a fool’s bet to call this the definitive end of the franchise on the big screen, the odds of Lionsgate gifting fans another movie announcement this holiday season aren’t exactly in our favor.

The key here is Suzanne Collins, who has maintained an active creative role in the franchise since the film rights to her planned trilogy were acquired for $200,000 in 2009. Collins co-wrote the screenplay for the first film and has remained deeply involved in each film, working closely with franchise director Francis Lawrence on everything from story development to reviewing final cuts.

After the Jennifer Lawrence-led saga concluded in 2015 with “Mockingjay — Part 2,” the franchise sat dormant until 2020 when Collins published “The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes.” The prequel, set 64 years before Katniss Everdeen entered the arena, explores how an 18-year-old Coriolanus Snow became the tyrannical president of Panem. Lionsgate adapted the novel into a 2023 film and remained in lockstep with Collins, announcing its adaptation of “Sunrise on the Reaping” on June 6, 2024: the same day Scholastic unveiled her latest novel.

At the “Sunrise on the Reaping” premiere, when the topic of a seventh “Hunger Games” movie came up, everyone from the franchise’s creative forces to Lionsgate’s top brass delivered the same message: We’re waiting on Suzanne.

Lionsgate chairman Adam Fogelson says there won’t be another #TheHungerGames movie until Suzanne Collins writes another book, promising to “honor and respect” her as the franchise’s “creative architect.” pic.twitter.com/zgPh2Ddebu

“She does not do anything in a frivolous fashion,” Lionsgate chairman Adam Fogelson told Variety at the “Sunrise on the Reaping” premiere. “When she believes she has an important story to tell, I believe she will write it and we will be the beneficiaries of it. But nothing I can say or do — nothing anyone could say or do — is going to shake her core fundamental desire to be inspired to write a great story.”Collins attended the “Sunrise on the Reaping” premiere, joining a behemoth group photo that brought together Lawrence and the vets who worked on the original four films, Rachel Zegler and the “Songbirds and Snakes” crew and the fresh crop of tributes from the latest installment.

The press-shy author didn’t give any interviews, but while signing autographs for fans, she offered an ambiguous two words when asked whether another novel was on the way: “We’ll see.” (Fans on X were considerably less restrained, with one parody account declaring: “SUZANNE COLLINS CONFIRMED A NEW HUNGER GAMES BOOK”).

Perhaps the fans are right, and this is all one big game of Werewulf, but Lawrence insists that he hasn’t caught so much as a whiff of another book.

“I’m telling you — the studio doesn’t believe us, I don’t think anybody believes us — but she does not tell us a word,” Lawrence explained. “She gives us no hints if she’s writing or not.” The director added that he doesn’t think the studio is necessarily “comfortable” with the prolonged waits between Collins’ novels: “I think they wish that she’d just be pumping them out. But, you know, she does her thing. And that’s part of what makes them so great.”

Whichever direction the franchise heads next, the numbers suggest there are limits to its appeal — and that not every trip to Panem is guaranteed to draw the same crowd.

Haymitch Abernathy’s origin story has struck a significantly stronger chord with the fanbase than President Snow’s, both on the page and on screen. “Sunrise on the Reaping” sold more than 1.2 million U.S. copies in its first week, twice the first-week sales of “Songbirds and Snakes” and three times those of “Mockingjay.” By January 2026, worldwide English-language sales had surpassed 4.4 million copies.

That success translated to the big screen: “Sunrise on the Reaping” currently holds the highest audience score on Rotten Tomatoes (94%) and Letterboxd rating (4.1/5) in the franchise, even surpassing the beloved “Catching Fire” (89% audience RT score; 4.0/5 Letterboxd).

Hollywood has also seen the pitfalls of stretching a franchise beyond its source material to capitalize on its commercial success. Most infamously, HBO forged ahead on a final season of “Game of Thrones” as George R.R. Martin struggled to complete his sixth “A Song of Ice and Fire” novel, culminating in a wildly divisive final season that prompted more than a million fans to petition for a remake. Martin later revealed in 2022 that he had been largely “out of the loop” during the show’s final seasons.

“If you look at our relationship with Stephenie [Meyer] on ‘Twilight,’ Chad [Stahelski] and Keanu [Reeves] on ‘John Wick,’ or our longstanding relationship with the ‘Saw’ creators, this is a studio that has prided itself on making great content, but never underestimated how important it is that the architects of that creation continue to have a voice in the process,” Fogelson said, reaffirming Collins as “the creative architect” of the “Hunger Games” franchise. “We have made sure that she is a true partner with our studio across the board. And I think the movies, the campaigns, and everything we do are better for it.

“The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” is playing in theaters now.
