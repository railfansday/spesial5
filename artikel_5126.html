Citigroup has cut its 12-month projection for Bitcoin to $99,532 from $113,000, and that of Ethereum from $2,838 to $3,028.

Its reasons for the downgrade include a recent price slump, negative ETF flows and momentum indicators, lowered open interest, and mounting liquidations.

BTC has to maintain prices above the $60,000 support for it to reclaim 65,000, while Ethereum's floor lies at $1,705 with $1,800 as the next upward resistance.

Citigroup today trimmed its 12-month forecasts for Bitcoin (BTC) and Ethereum (ETH), citing weaker institutional demand amid the recent market selloff.

For Bitcoin, the bank has lowered its target from $113,000, given on October 1, to $99,532. Meanwhile, Ethereum’s projection was shifted from an initial $3,028 to $2,838.

At press time, Bitcoin was trading at $62,769, while Ethereum traded at $1,759, with both having shed roughly 1.5% each in the day. The new targets now mean Bitcoin and Ethereum would have to surge 55% and 58% respectively from their current prices.

Led by Citigroup analyst Alex Saunders, the bank’s statement highlighted several reasons for its latest revision.

The first is that the two assets have declined since their last update, dipping more than 20% in the past month alone.

At the current price, Bitcoin is also well below its 200-day simple moving average, which sits between $72,283 and $72,795. Saunders cites this as the reason for downgrading Bitcoin’s short-term flow outlook to neutral.

ETF demand has also worsened, with November 17 flows for Bitcoin and Ethereum at -$242 million and -$26 million, respectively.

In the derivatives market, Ethereum futures have experienced the sharpest unwind. Total open interest is now at $9.12 billion, down $3 billion in under two weeks and now at its lowest level since October 2024.

Bitcoin futures market experienced a similar, albeit milder, contraction, falling more than 20% in the past week ($52.66 billion to $41.95 billion between November 9 and 18).

Even more, liquidations mounted to about $3.6 billion between November 13 and 15, while funding rates plunged to 0.0020% for Bitcoin and 0.0001% for Ethereum, both below the 0.005% baseline.

“BTC flows have turned into net outflows year-to-date, and ETH has recorded outflows in each of the last seven weeks, while futures positioning indicates a reduction in leverage across tokens.”

Citigroup’s latest update implies possible long-term recovery on a bumpy short-term path. For now, Bitcoin has to maintain above the $60,000 floor, and reclaim 65,000 to pace way for a $70,000 target.

Ethereum, on the other hand, has a lower support of $1,705 while facing an upward hurdle of $1,800. $1,900 is the initial recovery market, and $2,000 is the psychological support level. All these projections depend on ETF flows stabilizing and indicators turning positive.
