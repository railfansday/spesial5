XRP Briefly Drops to $0.89 as Crypto Liquidity Retreats

XRP has joined the wider cryptocurrency sell-off with a particularly sharp move, briefly touching about $0.89 as investors cut exposure to riskier assets. The level is notable because it takes the token back toward prices last seen in late 2024, but the more important story is what lies behind the drop: falling liquidity, negative momentum and a market in which even established cryptocurrencies are struggling to attract fresh buyers.

This is not a claim that XRP's network has stopped working or that a specific legal ruling triggered the move. The latest price reference comes from market reporting published on November 15, and prices can change significantly after that snapshot.

CoinDesk reported that XRP traded as low as roughly $0.89 amid the downturn. The token's decline for the quarter was around 36% at the reporting snapshot. Bitcoin and Ether were also under pressure, which points to a market-wide reduction in risk rather than a wholly isolated loss of confidence in the XRP Ledger.

Altcoins frequently move more aggressively than Bitcoin in both directions. Their order books can be thinner, the pool of marginal buyers smaller and speculative positioning more sensitive to sudden changes in sentiment. That combination can turn a difficult trading week into a dramatic percentage move, particularly when traders close leveraged positions into weak liquidity.

The same report cited approximately $19.13 million in net weekly withdrawals from US XRP exchange-traded funds, while noting that cumulative inflows since the products launched in November 2025 were still around $1.5 billion. Both figures can be true. A negative week signals near-term caution, but it does not erase a longer period in which investors built exposure through regulated market products.

What matters next is whether withdrawals persist and whether they are accompanied by genuinely weaker spot demand. ETF flows are only one distribution channel for XRP, so they should not be treated as a perfect measure of every holder's intentions. The wider Bitcoin ETF outflow trend covered by NewsBTC suggests the retreat is not confined to XRP products.

It is easy during a sell-off to treat a token's market price as a verdict on every development effort associated with it. In practice, those are separate issues. Developers can continue building features while market participants reduce their exposure, just as a strong price rally can occur without any corresponding improvement in network use.

For example, NewsBTC recently examined the XRP Ledger's work on quantum-resistant accounts, a longer-term technical initiative that should be assessed on its own timetable. Investors following XRP need to distinguish changes in network capabilities, adoption and liquidity from movements driven by broader risk appetite.

The obvious question is whether XRP can reclaim and maintain higher prices once trading conditions normalise. But watching a single support line will not settle the outlook. Rising spot volume on recoveries, steadier derivatives funding and improving ETF flows would each offer different evidence that demand is returning. Continued stablecoin contraction across crypto markets could work in the opposite direction.

The central takeaway is that XRP's fall is severe without necessarily being mysterious. A market that is reducing leverage and cash-like liquidity can punish liquid altcoins quickly. Until there is convincing evidence that that process has run its course, calling an exact bottom would be a guess rather than analysis.
