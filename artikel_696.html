‘Everyone Comes Here to Be Wowed’: Las Vegas Grand Prix Hits Live Music Jackpot With Backstreet Boys, Kacey Musgraves & More

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Sean Paul, Natasha Bedingfield and Ja Rule also performed on the T-Mobile Zone stage by the Sphere.

The Las Vegas Grand Prix might be the only Formula 1 race where the intensity of the sound of cars thundering down the track is rivaled by the city’s lights — and that’s saying something.

But this weekend’s LVGP was easily as bright as it was loud, from the 1.2 million LEDs shooting out of the ginormous Sphere looming over the circuit to the flashing casino signs that dotted the horizon as 22 drivers sped past Sin City landmarks and thousands of fans for 50 laps on Saturday (Nov. 21). Max Verstappen secured a first-place finish for Red Bull Racing after spending much of the drive fighting off 2026 World Champion Kimi Antonelli and George Russell of Mercedes, who dropped down to Nos. 4 and 9, respectively, in the final few laps.

In the end, Verstappen was joined instead by 2025 champion Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri, both from McLaren, on the podium Saturday — but the night was far from over as the trio sprayed each other down with the ceremonial champagne.

Revved up by the excitement on the track, the thousands of attendees who left the grandstands and flooded into the Sphere for the official T-Mobile F1 Afterparty were already ready to party when the Backstreet Boys took the stage for a special one-off show between blocks of the group’s Into the Millennium residency in Vegas. The jaw-dropping light displays and innovative, immersive special effects — which at times made fans feel as though they were hurtling through space in an enormous rocket ship or traversing snowy mountain ranges with rock formations shaped like Nick Carter, Howie Dorough, AJ McLean, Brian Littrell and Kevin Richardson’s faces — proved that the Sphere was the only concert venue that could possibly match the grandeur of the race itself.

The Backstreet Boys finale capped off a weekend of not just racing, but numerous other live music offerings. T-Mobile — one of the Vegas Grand Prix’s main sponsors — also presented pop, R&B and dance acts such as Sean Paul, Disclosure, Two Friends, Natasha Bedingfield and Ja Rule on its T-Mobile Zone stage, located right next to the Sphere, across three days leading up to the main race.

And then there was Kacey Musgraves’ disco rodeo at MGM Grand Arena, hosted on Friday (Nov. 20) with Uber x Zoox, which found the country superstar riding out on horseback to sing a collection of Middle of Nowhere tracks, as well as older favorites like “Justified” and “Butterflies.” Before her entrance, attendees were treated to a full rodeo experience; after walking into the main room to find that a large pen and dirt floor had been set up in the center, 12 professional riders clung for dear life onto angry, bucking bulls, in what might be one of the only sports that’s as risky and dangerous as F1.

The various pieces of live entertainment in Vegas built on the evolution of the sport — which only exploded in popularity in the United States in the past few years — into a multi-day festival-like experience, something F1 has specifically been pushing for in partnership with local race venues. Zedd, Nelly, Guns N’ Roses and more performed at the Miami Grand Prix in the spring, while Maroon 5, Post Malone and Alesso took the stage at the U.S. Grand Prix in Austin, Texas, over the summer.

But pulling it off on the scale of a city with such a rich history of live entertainment is an especially tall order, according to Brendan Dolby, vp of partnerships for the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

“We wanted to host one of the biggest cultural events in the world,” he said during a panel about the race’s programming and technology in the T-Mobile Paddock Club suite. “Doing it at a town like Las Vegas, you can’t just bring your run-of-the-mill experiences. Everyone comes here to be wowed.”
