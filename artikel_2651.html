Fantasy Football Week 11 Panic Meter: Trey McBride's ceiling will be limited if Cardinals' offense continues to struggle

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

This week, I'm starting with the team that might surprise you because I've reached the point where there's nothing left to panic about.

New England totaled 151 net yards in a 35-10 loss to Detroit. Drake Maye exited on the opening drive, and Tommy DeVito completed 12 of 24 passes for 81 yards in relief. The passing game already offered little margin for error.

The bigger concern is Maye's ankle injury. Reports have raised the possibility that he could miss the remainder of the season, and Mike Vrabel doesn't expect him to be available immediately after the Week 11 bye. A season-ending absence isn't confirmed, but the fantasy playoff implications are serious.

That brings us to A.J. Brown. He's dealing with injuries of his own, and the temptation will be to put him right back into lineups whenever he's cleared. The numbers don't support it. Brown has 11 catches for 128 yards and no touchdowns in four games with Maye. DeVito doesn't improve that outlook.

Hunter Henry has a slightly different argument. Tight end has been a difficult position all season, and an inexperienced quarterback leaning on a veteran tight end makes sense in theory. But Henry's two catches against Detroit both came on the opening drive with Maye. He didn't record a reception from DeVito. He's a desperation option.

So, are Maye and Brown droppable in seasonal redraft leagues?

Maye is approaching that point if the injury timeline rules him out for the fantasy playoffs, particularly in leagues without an available IR spot. Brown is more dependent on league size and replacement options. In a standard 10- or 12-team league, moving on is reasonable if the waiver wire offers someone with a clearer path to usable production.

There's no obvious quarterback rescue coming for this passing game.

Panic Meter: NO PANIC. Expectations have already been reset. New England isn't an offense to chase for fantasy production until the situation changes.

Four targets for Chris Olave against Carolina. That's the number that stands out.

He finished with three catches for 30 yards, just one week after catching seven passes for 98 yards on 12 targets against Cleveland. The Cleveland game broke a streak of three straight outings under 60 yards.

Meanwhile, Jordyn Tyson caught 11 of 13 targets for 80 yards. The question is how Tyler Shough is distributing the ball, not whether Tyson has taken over.

Olave had reached double-digit targets in six of his first eight games. That volume helped offset the inconsistency of the Saints' passing attack and gave him a dependable path to fantasy points, even when the offense struggled. Fewer targets would widen his weekly range of outcomes.

One week doesn't establish a permanent shift, but Tyson's involvement is worth tracking. New Orleans doesn't consistently generate enough passing production to make an unexpected four-target afternoon easy to absorb.

Olave should remain in starting lineups. He still has the ability to produce strong fantasy weeks, and a single low-volume performance doesn't outweigh his season-long role.

Panic Meter: SOME PANIC. The concern isn't Olave's talent. It's whether the target advantage that has supported his production is becoming less reliable.

If you've watched the Rams' backfield recently, Blake Corum's explosiveness has been difficult to miss. Sean McVay has noticed too, praising Corum's twitch and explosiveness after his 100-yard performance against Arizona.

From Weeks 8 through 10, Corum rushed for 231 yards on 39 carries, averaging 5.9 yards per attempt. Kyren Williams totaled 124 yards on 43 carries, just 2.9 per attempt. Corum generated nearly twice the yardage on fewer opportunities.

Against Arizona, Corum finished with 100 yards and a touchdown on 17 carries. Williams managed 23 rushing yards on 12 attempts. The Rams won comfortably, but the backs had very different afternoons.

Williams still matters to this offense. His work in pass protection and the passing game gives him a role that doesn't disappear because Corum has been more efficient on the ground. The question is how much of the rushing workload he can retain while Corum keeps producing.

There's no confirmed depth-chart change, but the old workload assumptions deserve another look. Corum has given the coaching staff a reason to keep him involved.

The Week 11 bye gives Los Angeles time to evaluate its rotation. Watch the touch distribution coming out of it rather than assuming the old split automatically returns.

Panic Meter: SOME PANIC+. Williams is still a viable fantasy starter, but his weekly ceiling is getting harder to project with Corum earning more work.

Detroit scored 35 points against New England, and Sam LaPorta caught one pass for 16 yards.

That sums up the problem. The Lions' offense can function at a high level without LaPorta making much of an impact in the box score.

Over his last four games, LaPorta has nine receptions for 99 yards and no touchdowns on 16 targets. The opportunities haven't been there. Jahmyr Gibbs and Amon-Ra St. Brown remain central to the offense, and Detroit has enough ways to score without featuring its tight end.

For fantasy managers, that leaves LaPorta heavily dependent on touchdowns. It's a difficult way to operate at an already volatile position, especially when he hasn't found the end zone in four straight games.

There's reason for patience. Detroit routinely creates scoring opportunities, so a usage increase could turn into a productive week quickly. The problem is identifying when that usage is actually coming.

LaPorta shouldn't be viewed as an unquestioned starter based only on the quality of his offense. Matchups and available alternatives matter more now than they did earlier in the season.

Panic Meter: SOME PANIC. Detroit's scoring potential keeps the upside alive, but it hasn't protected LaPorta's weekly floor.

Trey McBride caught every pass thrown his way Sunday. There were only five.

He finished with five receptions for 37 yards against the Rams, but Arizona's overall performance tells the bigger story. The Cardinals ran 34 offensive plays, earned six first downs and failed on all six third-down attempts.

Carson Beck threw for 108 yards with two interceptions in his first NFL start. Arizona scored six points and has managed just nine over its last two games. That's not enough sustained offense to support reliable fantasy production, regardless of how good McBride is at earning targets.

When drives end after a few snaps, even a strong target share means limited opportunities. McBride didn't lose his role. Arizona simply couldn't keep its offense on the field.

Total Panic would be premature. Beck drew a difficult Rams matchup in his debut, and one start isn't enough to determine what the passing game will look like over the final stretch. Kansas City is next, so the evaluation won't get easier, but the rookie deserves another opportunity before his impact on McBride is treated as settled.

The comparison with LaPorta is useful. Detroit can score without feeding LaPorta. Arizona needs McBride involved, but its offense hasn't been able to sustain drives. Same position, very different paths to disappointing fantasy results.

McBride should remain in starting lineups. Very few tight ends combine his talent with his share of their team's passing opportunities. The production may be inconsistent until Arizona improves, but benching him would create a different kind of risk.

Panic Meter: SOME PANIC. Give Beck another start. If Arizona continues to struggle to move the chains, McBride's ceiling will be limited by the offense around him.
