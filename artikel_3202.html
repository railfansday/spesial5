Switzerland confirms first AI agent cyberattacks on critical infrastructure

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Two of three AI agent cyberattacks on infrastructure operators succeeded, the agency’s director told SRF. The number of attack agents reachable online has almost doubled since October.

Attackers are now using AI agents against companies and critical infrastructure operators in Switzerland. The country’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) confirmed its first such cyberattacks in an advisory on Tuesday. The federal agency said the attacks hit at least two sectors.

“In all cyberattacks known to the NCSC, the use of AI agents led to the successful compromise of the victim’s IT systems,” the agency wrote.

Florian Schütz, the NCSC’s director, gave the first figures on Thursday. He spoke in an interview with SRF, the Swiss public broadcaster. The agency has five confirmed cases, all reported in recent days, he said. Three targeted critical infrastructure operators, and two of those succeeded. Across all five, attackers got into systems four times.

That is one fewer than Tuesday’s advisory implied, when the agency said every known attack had worked. SRF’s report does not say why the count changed.

In Switzerland, critical infrastructure includes electricity, drinking water and telecoms suppliers, SRF reported. Attackers also hacked small and medium-sized companies, which came forward voluntarily. Schütz said there were no major outages. The NCSC will not name the victims and is leaving it to them to speak for themselves. Who is behind the attacks is still unclear.

The attackers used what the NCSC calls agentic offensive frameworks. These AI agents search for weaknesses on their own, pick attack tools, check the results and plan the next step. Security teams use the same tools for penetration testing.

The agents exploit default or easily guessed passwords and known flaws in systems connected to the internet, the agency said. They need a fraction of the time a human attacker would. When they hit a defence, they switch to another known method. That leaves companies far less time to spot an attack and respond.

The number of such agents reachable online has almost doubled since October, according to the NCSC. It named ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI as examples. It did not say which tools the attackers used in the Swiss cases. The FBI has said ARTEX can break into networks in under three hours. This week, Microsoft said state hackers from China and North Korea now use it.

The NCSC expects more fast and successful attacks. It wants companies to install critical security updates on internet-facing systems within 24 hours of release. Webmail, VPNs and other services reachable from the internet should all sit behind multi-factor authentication or passkeys. Companies should also expose as few systems as possible. The agency also shares technical indicators of these attacks with critical infrastructure operators.

“If there is no vulnerability, an AI agent cannot get into a system either,” Schütz told SRF.

Alex Stamos, the former Facebook security chief, said this month that AI has pushed cybersecurity into a dark age. Raphael Reischuk co-founded the country’s National Test Institute for Cybersecurity and works at consulting firm Zühlke. He told SRF he expects a major wave of attacks. Many smaller companies lack the resources to keep their systems up to date, he said. He wants the federal government to consider jointly funded support for them, as it does for drinking water and electricity.

“Companies are not taking the problem seriously enough,” Reischuk said.
