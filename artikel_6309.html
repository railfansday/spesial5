American Airlines Pilots Told Not To Eat The Tiramisu, Now Union Says Never Mind

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

UPDATE: The Allied Pilots Association has withdrawn its warning about tiramisu served in American Airlines crew meals in Phoenix. Following an investigation involving American’s safety team, Phoenix inflight staff, and the caterer, the union now says it was a false alarm.

Yesterday, APA ​Aeromedical was advised by the ​PHX ​Flight ​Office that ​a dessert ​included ​with ​some crew meals may ​have contained ​alcohol.

Out of ​an abundance of ​caution, APA issued an immediate ​advisory recommending that crewmembers ​not ​consume the dessert, ​specifically ​the ​tiramisu, ​until ​additional information ​could be obtained. ​Following coordination ​with ​AA ​Corporate ​Safety, PHX Inflight, ​the catering ​provider, and ​other ​involved ​parties, APA has confirmed ​that ​the desserts ​in question ​are safe ​to ​consume ​and ​do not pose a ​risk to ​aviation safety.

We appreciate the ​PHX ​Flight ​Office ​for promptly ​raising the concern and ​working with ​both American Airlines ​and APA to investigate the potential ​issue. The ​initial ​advisory ​was ​issued ​based on the information available ​at ​the ​time ​and ​with crew ​and ​flight ​safety ​as ​the priority. ​We appreciate your patience ​while additional fact finding ​was ​conducted.

For those pilots ​involved ​in a HIMS ​program ​we ​would like to ​use ​this ​as an opportunity to ​remind you that ​consuming ​certain ​food additives ​and extracts in large quantities may pose a ​risk to ​abstinence ​monitoring. Please consult ​the APA ​HIMS Subcommittee ​for additional ​information.

As ​always, ​please reach out to APA ​Aeromedical ​with any ​questions or ​concerns.

APA explained that its initial warning was precautionary, based on information available at the time…though I’m wondering what information

American Airlines pilots have been warned to skip the tiramisu in some crew meals out of Phoenix because it contains alcohol. That is an awkward ingredient to find in a meal provided to people who are supposed to be flying airplanes…

JonNYC shared a warning attributed to the Allied Pilots Association’s Aeromedical–HIMS Committee concerning meals supplied by American’s contracted caterer in Phoenix:

“The contracted catering provider has included tiramisu containing alcohol on some crew meal trays. Because the dessert is not cooked after the alcohol is added, alcohol may remain in the finished product.

“We recommend that no crew member consume this dessert.

“APA cannot determine the amount of alcohol contained in an individual serving or what effect consumption could have if a crewmember were subsequently required to undergo DOT alcohol testing.”

"PHX Crew Meal Alert – Tiramisu Containing Alcohol

The APA Aeromedical–HIMS Committee has been made aware of an issue affecting crews based in or transiting PHX.

The contracted catering provider has included tiramisu containing alcohol on some crew meal trays.…

The notice says the committee is coordinating with the Phoenix Flight Office to have the dessert removed and replaced. In the meantime, crews are advised to accept an incomplete meal rather than eat it.

Pilots who have already consumed the tiramisu and have concerns about their fitness to fly are instructed to contact their union domicile representatives immediately. The warning expressly tells them not to let schedule pressure influence that decision.

The notice does not establish that a serving would cause impairment or a failed alcohol test. The committee says it does not know the alcohol content or the potential testing consequences. But crews should not have to evaluate that uncertainty while eating an airline-provided meal!

The FAA explains that a confirmed alcohol concentration of 0.02 to 0.039 requires temporary removal from safety-sensitive duties, even though that result is not itself a federal testing violation. A confirmed result of 0.04 or greater carries more serious consequences.

American’s Phoenix caterer apparently supplied an alcohol-containing dessert in crew meals, prompting a union warning to leave it untouched while a replacement is arranged.
