6 ways to use AI to shop smarter—without giving it access to your wallet

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Thanksgiving is fast approaching, which means Black Friday is too. In the era of AI-assisted shopping, tech companies offer tools that allow you to shop directly on their platforms, or through integrations with online retailers and payment providers.

Personally, I’m not a Black Friday shopper. I also don’t feel comfortable giving an AI model my credit card information (or letting it invest for me or make other financial transactions on my behalf). But I do feel comfortable sharing low-stakes information about my needs and interests and stretching a model’s search capability to its limit. Treating my chatbot like a research assistant has saved me time and money, and helped me avoid the annoyance of navigating online stores. 

Below are a few tips for using artificial intelligence to shop more efficiently, without handing over your credit card information to a bot.

Use AI to find exactly what you need, faster

Shopping online can be a nightmare, especially if you need something really specific.

Say you need to find a bookshelf that fits in a space with very specific dimensions. Why waste time sifting through myriad websites—and endless advertisements—when AI can search for a product that meets your exact needs? The same goes for everything from household repair items to bridesmaid dresses of a certain hue: AI saves you time by searching for exactly what you need, whether that’s a certain size, color, or operating system.

And it’s less mentally taxing than going through an endless number of websites.

Tell AI where you have a subscription—and where you don’t

Many of us have subscriptions to services we prefer, and platforms we prefer to avoid. Telling AI where you already have memberships, and where you might even be collecting points, can help you shop more efficiently. If you’re part of a loyalty program and have a credit card from Lowe’s, there’s little reason for AI to recommend products from Kmart or Walmart.

Tell your AI where you like to shop and have it tailor its results accordingly. This might involve telling AI that you want links only from Costco, not Amazon, or that you prefer to read suggestions from Fast Company, where you have a login (ahem). This advice also applies to frequent flyer miles and credit card rewards.

Have AI search for coupons and other cost-saving measures

Use AI to find coupon codes and other money-saving opportunities. This isn’t any special application of AI, but simply involves exploiting the powerful search capabilities of large language models to benefit your wallet.

About to buy an expensive product? Ask a model where else you can buy it, and whether it’s available for less. You might even ask it to investigate whether a purchase qualifies for reimbursement through a Flexible Spending Account, and what documentation you’d need.

Develop a shopping plan with AI

Sometimes you really want to see something in person before you buy it.

AI can help you come up with an itinerary of stores to check out in person, and direct you to the best neighborhoods for window shopping. It can also help you prioritize your time and travel, drawing up efficient routes and suggesting the best time of day to head out.

Ask AI for suggestions

Perhaps there’s a product that will make your life better and you just don’t know it yet. AI might have some good ideas, though.

It can also help you find reviews that speak to specific concerns you might have about a product, searching the web more thoroughly. This might mean investigating whether anyone has complained about safety issues with, say, a robotic cat litter box, or searching for resort reviews written by parents who can speak to how well a place works for families.

Help understand warranties and refunds

Sometimes a purchase that seemed perfect turns out to be anything but.

AI can help you navigate complicated warranty and refund policies—and potentially get your money back. Have it review the terms of your purchase, identify the strongest grounds for a refund, and even draft a persuasive request on your behalf.
