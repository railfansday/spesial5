Less than half the price for a single stick...

The memory market is so sketchy just now that pricing is a little all over the place; hence, we sometimes see pricing quirks like this. While you might be looking for a deal on a 32GB RAM kit, often found in 2 x 16GB configurations, buying a single stick twice can actually save you money. This Corsair Vengeance RGB RS DDR5 16GB (1x16GB) kit is $229, so you can pick up two for $458. Buying 2 x 16GB in that manner is cheaper than the same regular 2 x 16GB kit, which costs $475.

Of course, these aren't deals compared to prices a year ago, but it seems we're a long way from prices returning to that point. If you're also looking for a motherboard and/or CPU, you can generally save more on memory by picking up a RAM bundle. You can look at our roundup of the best Black Friday RAM bundle deals if you're interested in that.

Get a single stick of Corsair Vengeance RGB RS DDR5 RAM with CL38 CAS latency and addressable RGB lighting, available in both black and white. Buying two of these is cheaper than buying a 2 x 16GB kit of the same model.

Corsair's RS line is a slightly budget version of its famed Vengeance line, but this is still very solid RAM, with 6000 speeds for the AMD gaming sweet spot. This memory supports both AMD EXPO and Intel XMP 3.0 for one-click optimization, so it will work seamlessly on either platform. As noted, the key selling point here is that this RAM is cheaper to buy as individual sticks of 16GB, rather than as a set of two.

On my travels, I've seen this phenomenon isn't confined to Amazon, either. The G.Skill Ripjaws M5 Neo RGB Series ranks among our top picks for the best gaming RAM. 2 x 16 GB will set you back $559 on Newegg. However, if you buy a single stick with Newegg's promo code, it's just $249, meaning you can pick up 32GB for less than $500, a $60 saving. The timings on the product listings are slightly different, but the part numbers are identical, so that appears to be a typo. Ostensibly, these are the same sticks, just different capacities.

Get 16GB of DDR5 6000 RAM with support for both AMD EXPO and Intel XMP 3.0, with timings of 36-36-36-76. Buying two is cheaper than picking up a single 2 x 16GB kit from Newegg.

These two deals are a great starting point, but it pays to shop around when RAM costs around $500 for 32GB of DDR5-6000 in normal conditions right now. In our experience, retailers will suss out these pricing enigmas pretty quickly, so move fast if you want to secure RAM at a cheaper price than expected.
