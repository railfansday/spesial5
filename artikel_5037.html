Peter Schiff argues Bitcoin may fall below $16,500 by year-end if $50,000 breaks next

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

His Nov. 14 call extends earlier $20,000 warnings while Bitcoin and Ethereum traded above the conditions he identified.

Bitcoin critic and gold advocate Peter Schiff said on Nov. 14 that a Bitcoin break below $50,000 could send it toward a level below $16,500 before the end of 2026. The call extends his earlier downside warnings, while Bitcoin traded near $65,850 on Saturday afternoon.

In his 05:53 GMT post, @PeterSchiff said a break of $50,000 could lead to “a quick trip to the 2022 low below $16.5K.” He said a retest before year-end would not surprise him.

According to CryptoSlate data at 15:02 GMT on Nov. 14, Bitcoin traded at $65,850, down 4.36% over 24 hours. It was 47.82% below its Oct. 6, 2025 all-time high of $126,198.

Schiff’s latest post gives a more specific downside marker than his earlier warnings. On Feb. 19, @PeterSchiff said a break below $50,000 would make a test of $20,000 “highly likely.” On June 2, he said the same break should be followed by a quick fall below $20,000.

The November call retains that $50,000 condition while explicitly naming a level below $16,500 and a timeframe before year-end. His June wording already allowed for prices below $20,000, so it should not be read as having established a precise floor there.

Bitcoin and Ethereum remained above his first conditions in the Nov. 14 afternoon observations. The downside markers are his forecasts.

In a later Saturday post, @PeterSchiff argued that prospective buyers were already invested and that artificial intelligence had diverted attention from Bitcoin. He also alleged rising hacking risk and said the Trump-driven rally was over. Those were his explanations for expecting further weakness.

Peter Schiff attached a warning about Bitcoin-holding company Strategy to the forecast, saying a year-end retest would mean “curtains for $MSTR.” MSTR is Strategy’s listed common stock.

Strategy’s Nov. 9 filing reported holdings of 850,364 BTC as of Nov. 8, acquired for about $64 billion at an average cost of $75,456 per bitcoin, including fees and expenses. A lower Bitcoin price would reduce the market value of those holdings.

The filing also reported about $5 billion in its USD Reserve for preferred-stock dividends and debt interest, plus $146 million in USD Cash for broader treasury purposes. Those balances do not establish that a fall to Schiff’s target would force Strategy to sell Bitcoin or close.

Schiff’s criticism of Strategy predates Saturday’s post. CryptoSlate reported in March on his objections to its preferred-stock funding model.

In his 13:30 GMT post, @PeterSchiff also warned about Ethereum. He said a break of an uptrend around $1,600 could bring the next support level at what he described as the 2022 low just below $1,000.

Ethereum traded at $1,851 at 15:03 GMT, according to CryptoSlate data, down 4.95% over 24 hours. It was 62.64% below its Aug. 24, 2025 all-time high of $4,954, but remained above the $1,600 level Schiff identified. The year-end timeframe appeared in his Bitcoin post; his Ethereum post described chart levels without assigning that same timeframe.

The immediate price test in Schiff’s argument is still $50,000 for Bitcoin. In the afternoon CryptoSlate observations, both Bitcoin and Ethereum remained above the respective trigger levels he cited.
