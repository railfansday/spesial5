Strategic Education (STRA) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Visa curbs in Australia triggered a $134.1 million impairment charge.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Strategic Education, Inc. (STRA -21.33%) reported third quarter results characterized by growth in its Education Technology Services and U.S. healthcare portfolios, offset by regulatory challenges in its Australian segment. Management announced a plan to increase growth investments by $50 million in 2027 to accelerate product development in high-demand areas. The company recorded a $134.1 million non-cash impairment in the Australia/New Zealand segment as international student visa restrictions proved more severe than expected. Despite these headwinds, the company maintained a debt-free balance sheet and reported that productivity savings would limit operating expense growth to 2% in the coming year.

Third quarter revenue was $320 million, essentially flat with the prior year. We did hold operating expense growth to less than 1%, resulting in operating income of $50 million, a 2% decrease, and an operating margin of 15.7%, 30 basis points below the prior year. Earnings per share increased 4% to $1.69, reflecting a lower share count from share repurchases.

Given the strong demand across ETS, and given our desire to accelerate our product roadmap to serve that demand, our intent is to increase our growth investments in ETS in 2027. These investments will slightly reduce the ETS segment-level operating margin next year, but will also result in higher revenue growth in our current five-year plan, defend Sophia's market leadership position, and strengthen Workforce Edge's ability to sign new corporate clients. Turning now to U.S. Higher Education. Total U.S. Higher Education enrollment declined 1% to approximately 84,700 students, as growth in employer-affiliated enrollment was offset by lower unaffiliated enrollment. Revenue was essentially flat at $214 million, as higher revenue per student offset the enrollment decline.

Our productivity initiatives continued to hold operating expense growth to less than 1%, including continued investments in growth. Operating income was $22 million, a 4% decline, and an operating margin of approximately 10%. Student retention reached 89.2%, a new all-time high for U.S. Higher Education. Our healthcare portfolio continues to be the strongest source of growth in U.S. Higher Education. Healthcare enrollment grew 9% in the quarter and now represents 54% of total U.S. Higher Education enrollment, up from 49% a year ago, an increase of 500 basis points over the last year. Healthcare is also central to our broad employer strategy. Roughly 38% of our healthcare enrollment comes from employer partners, and employer-affiliated enrollment overall reached 35% of U.S.

Higher Education total enrollment, up 200 basis points from the prior year. We are now extending that portfolio into licensure track programs. Earlier this year, Capella enrolled the first cohort in its pre-licensure Bachelor of Science in Nursing program, and Strayer enrolled its first students in the new Associate of Applied Science in Radiologic Technology. Early demand for both programs has exceeded our initial assumptions. These two programs join Strayer's Licensed Practical Nurse diploma and Capella's expanded nurse practitioner portfolio, all aimed at the healthcare talent our employer partners increasingly need. To support that demand, we intend to also increase our growth investments in U.S. healthcare in 2027 with funding for new programs, clinical faculty, expanded clinical and instructional capacity.

This will allow programs like pre-licensure nursing and radiologic technology to grow at the pace their demand supports. Turning to Australia. Total enrollment declined 9% to approximately 17,100 students, and revenue declined 7% to AUD 64 million. The Australian team continued to manage costs well, with operating expenses down 4%, and operating income was AUD 10 million compared with AUD 12.5 million in the prior year, and an operating margin of approximately 16%. The decline is entirely a function of international enrollment. Over the course of this year, the Australian government restrictions on international students have become materially more severe than we anticipated.

New visa approvals for prospective students have slowed sharply, and in some months declining as much as 80% versus the prior year. In addition, the government has effectively closed the onshore transfer market by requiring international students already in Australia to obtain a new visa in order to change institutions. Given this environment, we have lowered our near-term growth expectations for the ANZ segment, which resulted in this quarter's $134 million non-cash impairment. Notwithstanding the headwinds on international enrollment, our domestic enrollment continues to fare well. Domestic new enrollment has grown every quarter this year, including 9% in each of the second and third quarters, and domestic students now represent close to 70% of new enrollment in Australia.

The quality of Torrens' academic experience remains strong and has shown significant improvement over the past year. We were pleased to see Torrens University's performance in the 2025 Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching Student Experience Survey, the most comprehensive national survey of current higher education students in Australia. Torrens achieved widespread momentum across all undergraduate indicators, including a rise to sixth nationally for undergraduate teaching quality, up significantly from 19th the year before. At the postgraduate level, Torrens continues to lead at the top tier of Australian higher education, ranking in the top four nationwide for both skills development and peer engagement, alongside a seventh place ranking in postgraduate teaching quality.

This performance against national sector benchmarks reinforces Torrens' value proposition and supports student retention and long-term brand equity in industry-aligned education. The quality of Torrens' academic programs, combined with our own success in growing domestic enrollment, gives us confidence to increase our growth investments in Australia's domestic market in 2027. We are expanding our existing Adelaide campus and will launch several new health science programs, and we also plan to open a new campus in Perth in late 2027. Western Australia is the fastest-growing state in the country, with population growth 50% higher than the national average.

Greater Perth is projected to add more than a half a million residents over the next decade, and its economy is concentrated in the sectors where our new programs are focused, namely healthcare, engineering, construction, and defense. Perth will also position Torrens well for international recruitment from Asia when those constraints ease. Turning now to an update on our outlook versus our notional model. Given our current forecast for the balance of this year and the fact that the international enrollment environment in Australia deteriorated since the first half of the year, it appears unlikely our full-year results will fall inside of our notional outlook.

In terms of 2027, given that our forward revenue growth is mostly a product of current-year enrollment, we think the most likely outcome for consolidated revenue next year is to be flat with this year. We expect the cost of the growth investments that I have described this morning to be approximately $50 million in 2027. However, the productivity savings achieved this year will offset nearly half of that, such that year-over-year increase in operating expenses will be limited to just 2%.

Given that these incremental investments are targeted to support areas of proven strength at SEI, that is, our two high-growth, high-margin platform businesses, our U.S. healthcare portfolio, and our domestic market growth in Australia, we have a high degree of confidence in their ability to return the company to its notional growth rates in 2028 and beyond. Regarding capital allocation, in addition to our regular quarterly dividend, we repurchased approximately 347,000 shares during the quarter for a total of $28.5 million. We ended the quarter with $139 million of cash and marketable securities and no debt.

Finally, as always, I'd like to take this opportunity to thank all of my colleagues here at SEI for their ongoing commitment and support to our students and our corporate partners. With that, Victor, we'd be happy to take questions.
