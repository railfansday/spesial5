How Disney helps brands interact and transact with holiday audiences

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The entertainment giant’s holiday ad sales are pacing 20% ahead of the same point last year, with demand across the retail, financial services, CPG and technology sectors.

The 60th annual Country Music Association Awards, which aired last night on ABC and Disney+ and will stream tomorrow on Hulu, showcased Disney’s evolving advertising strategy in action. Chevrolet debuted new custom creative, partnered with musician and Chevy truck owner Zach Top for several in-show moments and put out social-first, behind-the-scenes content.

In addition, Bath & Body Works, a new partner to the awards show, was the first brand to be a presenting sponsor of a live integration on TikTok that captured behind-the-scenes content and was hosted by musician Lauren Alaina and creator Haley Baylee. Financial services company Ally made its CMA Awards debut, activating on in-show, social, streaming and linear channels, including content with personality Kristin Cavallari.

“We're looking at new ways beyond your traditional 30-second ad in order to be able to engage fans as well as drive new revenue opportunities,” said John Campbell, senior vice president for streaming, entertainment and multicultural solutions at Disney.

The CMA Awards arrived with just a week until Thanksgiving. The holiday period is in full swing, with the National Retail Federation forecasting holiday retail sales of more than $1 trillion. With a portfolio that spans film, TV, streaming, theme parks and consumer products, Disney reaches 54% of holiday gift-givers monthly, per the company’s research, providing advertisers with a large audience across channels.

For Disney, advertising bookings in streaming and linear across Halloween, Thanksgiving and the holidays overall are pacing 20% ahead of the same point last year, with demand across categories including retail, financial services, CPG and technology, the company said.

The holidays are now a yearlong concern, with Disney selling its first holiday deal in January and more than 10% of its holiday revenue coming in since Nov. 1. The company also saw 10% year-over-year advertising revenue growth in Q4 of its fiscal year 2026 for a total of $1.69 billion. Across the advertising landscape, holiday ads are starting as early as September, per Nielsen.

“The market is still very active,” said Campbell. “Not only are [brands] starting way earlier to build out these larger programs, but they're also coming in as we get closer to the holiday season, and we're seeing total investments grow, as well.”

Brands flock to Disney for three reasons, Campbell said. The company is a trusted source for storytelling with titles that span generations, from “Home Alone” to “The Nightmare Before Christmas;” it delivers live cultural moments, including holiday TV specials, that serve as shared experiences; and it gives advertisers multiple ways to reach consumers across platforms.

Disney’s multichannel approach relies on a StorySync strategy that works with brands to develop seven key elements: sponsorships, in-show integrations, custom content, social and influencer, commerce, experiential and halo enhancements. The company is working to add new shoppable activations to the StorySync framework that can help drive brand revenue during the holiday period.

“We not only encourage people to participate and interact with these brands, but also give them the levers in order to be able to transact with these brands through our shoppable moments,” Campbell said. “How do we add value for our consumers and our viewers, and how do we bring brands to be able to help us add that value?”

“Dancing with the Stars,” now in its 35th season, continues to be a durable property for Disney, with the competition show seeing an 11% episode-to-episode viewership increase en route to the season finale on Nov. 24, Campbell said. Sephora served as the seasonlong beauty sponsor for the show, partnering on social-first custom content and becoming the first show partner to incorporate Disney’s Shop the Stream solution.

The show will keep the action going with a holiday special on Dec. 1. Ocean Spray will have a sponsored dance, custom content, in-show mentions and lower-third graphics, while Christmas retailer Balsam Hill provided decorations and will appear in lower-third graphics.

“That franchise has expanded now,” Campbell said. “No matter the opportunity, we're seeing brands really want to attach themselves.”

In addition, Kraft Mac and Cheese is partnering with Freeform’s “25 Days of Christmas” as part of a long-term strategic partnership between Kraft Heinz and Disney that kicked off this summer. Capital One will join as the first presenting broadcast sponsor for the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Induction Ceremony on Dec. 8, and Disney has secured new and returning sponsors for the “Wonderful World of Disney: Holiday Spectacular” and New Year’s Rockin’ Eve programming.

After the holiday season, Disney Advertising will continue apace, with the college football playoffs and national championship game, the Grammy and Oscar ceremonies and the Super Bowl, together making up what Disney ad chief Rita Ferro has described as the company’s “biggest year ever.” Next month, the company will begin introducing creator content to Disney+ through a partnership with TikTok that was announced earlier this year.

“We're very excited about some new content formats that streaming is going to be able to provide,” Campbell said, citing microdramas as a possibility.
