Police groups condemn Mamdani over axing elite NYPD unit

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Multiple police groups condemned NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani's order disbanding an elite unit on Monday.

Multiple police groups condemned NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani's order disbanding an elite unit on Monday.

Mamdani's order eliminated the Strategic Response Group and established new "citizen-led" teams that will handle protests in the city. NYC Police Benevolent Association President Patrick Hendry said the move is proof that Mamdani is siding against police and with disruptive protesters.

"Today is not a good day for New Yorkers who just want a safe and orderly city," Hendry said in a statement. "Mayor Mamdani is showing his true colors: He's willing to put our safety and police officers' safety at risk to appease his radical base. So the next time your life is disrupted by demonstrators who are blocking bridges and roadways or causing chaos on our streets, remember: Mayor Mamdani is on their side, not yours."

Scott Munro, president of NYC's Detective's Endowment Association, also condemned the move, saying Mamdani's administration is completely disconnected from "the realities of policing in New York City."

"Mayor Mamdani campaigned on the baseless notion that the NYPD's Strategic Response Group was the problem. In reality, these brave men and women in blue have held the line against dangerous radicals who threaten public safety. Disbanding this vital unit proves just how disconnected City Hall is from the realities of policing in New York City," Munro said.

"With diminished crowd control capabilities, NYPD Detectives will now be pulled away from investigating crimes and seeking justice for victims to help police the hundreds of protests across our city. That's not making New Yorkers safe," he continued.

"The Mayor and elected officials applauding this decision need to remember: They're no longer standing on a protest line. They're responsible for leading a city. It's time to start acting like it," he added.

Munro pointed to the 2024 student protests at Columbia University when demonstrators took over a university building. He said the police operations that were required to remove the students and make over 100 arrests will no longer be available to officers under the new policies.

He also criticized the reliance on civilians to address violent protests, saying Mamdani's past efforts to replace police with civilian teams have failed.

"The big question is, how did it work out with his, the unit that he made for the EDP–Emotionally Disturbed People–How'd he work out with that? I don't think that's working out too well," Munro said.

NYPD's Strategic Response Group monitors a crowd outside 26 Federal Plaza on Oct. 21, 2025, in New York City. (Adam Gray/Getty Images)

Mamdani and Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch announced the NYPD reforms Monday morning. The mayor's office stated that the SRG's counterterrorism functions will be folded into an existing command, while a brand-new organization will now lead protest response, the Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division (CDDD).

The CDDD, which will lie within the Mayor's Office of Community Safety, will work to "improve the NYPD's protest response" via a specialized "dialogue team," known as the First Amendment Support Team (FAST), they said.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani speaks during a news conference at Gracie Mansion alongside NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch on March 9, 2026. (Angelina Katsanis/AP)

"The CDDD will not have weapons or arrest powers. FAST will consist of 30 uniformed officers trained in communication and de-escalation. The NYPD will also establish a new Patrol Support Division (PSD), a unit within the NYPD’s Patrol Services Bureau, with vetted officers who will spend most of their time on patrol-related duties and receive specialized crowd management training to support responses to demonstrations," the mayor's office said in a statement.
