Iran takes pointed jab at Trump Cabinet secretary for moving onto military base

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Denying reports of tension over a statement about Republican midterm losses, President Donald Trump confirmed support for Vice President JD Vance's comments regarding the war. While highlighting Navy operations and oil prices, Trump labeled Iran's leadership as evil and detailed historical conflict. Ultimately, he emphasized that U.S. cannot allow Iran to obtain a nuclear weapon under any circumstances.

Iran mocked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, calling the official "sweetie" on social media after reports emerged that he had moved from his Washington, D.C., home onto a military installation amid security threats attributed to Tehran.

Bessent made a public appearance Friday, sitting nearby as President Donald Trump addressed reporters in the White House briefing room.

"Are you interviewing from a military base sewer right now, sweetie?" Iran's South African embassy posted on X alongside a screenshot of Bessent from an earlier television interview.

An Iranian official confirmed to Fox News Digital that the X account belongs to the embassy.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent listens as President Donald Trump speaks during a news briefing in the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room of the White House Nov. 20, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images)

Bessent is the latest senior Trump administration official to relocate to a military base, joining a growing number of top officials living behind military gates as federal authorities confront security threats extending beyond the White House.

"Obviously, it's a high threat level for us to move," Bessent said while addressing the move during a Nov. 15 appearance on Fox Business' "Sunday Futures with Larry Kudlow."

A source familiar with the situation previously told Fox News Digital, "After The Wall Street Journal published an article featuring an online photo that allowed the location of the secretary’s Georgetown home to be geo-located, security concerns began to increase, which have now forced the secretary to relocate to a military base for his safety.

"The news media has an obligation to act responsibly amid growing threats against public officials."

BESSENT BLASTS NEWSPAPER EDITOR OVER 'RECKLESS JOURNALISM' AFTER IRAN THREATS FORCE MOVE TO MILITARY BASE

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth and White House chief of staff Stephen Miller during a news briefing in the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room of the White House Nov. 20, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images)

Threats against Bessent had reached "more than an advanced stage" amid reported Iranian assassination threats, a senior administration official told Fox News Digital when news broke of the decision to move the Treasury secretary on base. The official did not elaborate on the nature of the threats.

Attorney General Todd Blanche, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, White House chief of staff Stephen Miller and other administration officials have moved to military installations or protected government housing.

Under Bessent, Treasury launched Operation Economic Outcast in August to disrupt Iran’s oil-smuggling, financial and cryptocurrency networks.

BESSENT WARNS IRAN'S OUTSIDE BACKERS TO 'STAY AWAY' AS TRUMP SEEKS TO 'ASPHYXIATE' TEHRAN'S ECONOMY

President Donald Trump takes questions from reporters during a news briefing in the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room of the White House Nov. 20, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images)

He said Treasury would "sever every economic lifeline" sustaining the Iranian regime and warned that entities facilitating money laundering for Tehran could be cut off from the U.S. dollar system.

Since then, Treasury has targeted Iran’s oil-smuggling shadow fleet, sanctioned eight Canada-based money services businesses it says helped Iranian entities move funds and moved to cut crypto exchange MEXC Global off from the U.S. financial system over alleged illicit transactions.

On Nov. 18, Treasury announced action against a gold-and-cash smuggling network it said was run by Hezbollah and Iran’s IRGC-Quds Force. Bessent said the U.S. would continue to "disrupt Iran’s attempts to exert influence in the region and fund its proxies."
