Netflix’s 6-Part Miniseries Brings One of the Greatest Novels of All Time to Life

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Taylor Vollman is a contributor at Collider. She has long quipped, “T.V. is my name, TV is my game.” Fortuitous initials aside, at the heart of a tired joke is a simple truth — she has a lifelong passion for television. And along with it, an affinity for movies, music and pop culture.

In a childhood time capsule, she had listed Friends, PB&J Otter, and Days of Our Lives among her favorite shows. But her tastes have not narrowed with age. Her viewing habits span TV Land classics, network hits, buzzy reality shows and streamer critical darlings.

Hoping to merge this passion with her love of writing, Taylor earned a bachelor’s in media arts and design with a minor in film studies from James Madison University. After working in marketing for years, she wanted to sharpen her skills and went on to graduate with a master’s in journalism from Georgetown University. Taylor has since pursued entertainment writing and reporting.

Jane Austen is one of the preeminent romance authors of all time, giving her heroines a sense of agency when their fates largely depended on the men in their lives. Her stories have been finding new life in recent years, with romance readers driving an uptick in the genre across mediums. Netflix is no stranger to historical romance, behind the hit Bridgerton franchise and previously adapting Austen’s Persuasion in 2022. Now, the streaming platform is returning to the author’s most successful work, Pride and Prejudice. It was Austen’s second novel to be published, released in 1813.

Dolly Alderton was tapped to adapt the classic novel into a six-episode miniseries, following Elizabeth “Lizzy” Bennet (Emma Corrin) and Mr. Fitzwilliam Darcy (Jack Lowden) as they clash against their social station, ego, and misconceptions on their journey to love. Meddling elders, long-standing friendships, and secretive rivalries only threaten their happiness. Olivia Colman, Rufus Sewell, Freya Mavor, Rhea Norwood, Hopey Parish, and Hollie Avery round out the Bennet family. Hot off another Austen adaptation, Sense and Sensibility, Fiona Shaw stars as Lady Catherine de Bourgh. The cast also includes Jamie Demetriou, Daryl McCormack, Louis Partridge and Siena Kelly.

Pride and Prejudice continues to be relevant, even centuries after its publication. And while Alderton promises a faithful adaptation, she admits they reanalyzed some moments for the modern audience. The Good Material author admitted that age gave her a different perspective on the characters. “The thing that I was most drawn to as a single 35-year-old woman was how much Lizzy feels like a woman who is thinking out loud, who is trying to work out what she feels about marriage and love — what it is that she wants, what she’s willing to negotiate on,” Alderton explained to Netflix. This exploration of pragmatism in conversations with marriage is a relatable topic, even amid the modern dating landscape.

Lizzy is, in particular, a character open to a life without marriage, admitting in the trailer her willingness to be "a governess and a spinster." Corrin discussed the miniseries’ ability to interject a modern sensibility into its historical setting. “I don’t think we’ve had an adaptation that does the modernity and the authenticity that we love,” they told Netflix. “Some of the actual social commentary that comes out is really brilliant.”

Mrs. Bennet (Colman) is a complicated figure for many fans, who see her preoccupation with marriage as an inevitability of the era but a hindrance to her relationships. But she will be the first to speak the novel’s iconic opening line in an adaptation, “It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.” Originally just narration, Alderton explained that Mrs. Bennet speaks the famous line as it’s “the central drama of her story.” “It’s a preoccupation that any time a single man comes into her orbit, she’s thinking this might be safety for one of my daughters,” she explains while breaking down the trailer. “This might be salvation, not just for one but for all of us. So, the fact that this is so in her mind all the time, it felt that it had to be her line.”

Netflix is not just bringing Pride and Prejudice to a new generation, but is also reaffirming its literary status. It “is the ultimate romantic comedy,” as Netflix executive Mona Qureshi said in an announcement. Austen’s best-selling novel continues to appear on lists of the medium’s best, with The Guardian ranking it third among its “Readers’ top 100 novels of all time” and Goodreads ranking it first among the “Best Books of the 19th Century.”

As any casual shopper in a bookstore knows, Pride and Prejudice is frequently re-released as special editions. In fact, it has continually stayed in print and sold more than 20 million copies in the 213 years since its publication, according to Jane Austen’s House. And if the idiom about imitation is correct, Austen should be very flattered thanks to the many remakes and spinoffs it inspired.

Netflix’s upcoming miniseries is entering an ongoing war over the best Pride and Prejudice adaptation, with an expansive multiverse of Elizabeth Bennet and Mr. Darcy love stories. It has been adapted for stage, television, film, and even the Emmy-winning web series The Lizzie Bennet Diaries. But fans are most ardently divided between the 1995 miniseries starring Colin Firth and Jennifer Ehle, and the 2005 film featuring Keira Knightley and Matthew Macfadyen.

Austen’s work continues to be adapted, retold, and reimagined, with even literary retellings and spinoffs finding their way to screen. In fact, the modernized Bridget Jones’s Diary starring Renée Zellweger, the horror reimagining of Pride and Prejudice and Zombies, and Death Comes To Pemberley led by Matthew Rhys were all novels first. Most recently, BBC and BritBox brought the story of Mary (Ella Bruccoleri) to life in The Other Bennet Sister, which includes an upcoming 3-part Christmas special.

With the resurgence of romance, it only makes sense Netflix would adapt such a classic novel by a beloved author. Pride and Prejudice is a story told over and over again, recontextualized for each generation. Alderton promises to breathe new life into a timeless tale, even including an iconic line for the first time. The 6-part miniseries premieres on Netflix on December 3, 2026.
