Magic: The Gathering Designer Reveals 2026’s 11 Best New Commanders

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Principal Designer Gavin Verhey picks 2026's standout commanders

With 2026 delivering a packed release schedule for Magic: The Gathering exploring classic planes and several Universes Beyond crossovers, Commander players have had plenty of exciting new legends to build around. From returning to familiar fantasy worlds in sets like Lorwyn Eclipsed and Secrets of Strixhaven to epic crossovers like Marvel Super Heroes, The Hobbit, and Star Trek, the year brought an unprecedented wave of Commanders for casual and competitive tables alike.

When Polygon spoke with Magic: The Gathering Principal Designer Gavin Verhey about the year's lineup at MagicCon: Atlanta 2026, he walked through each major release to highlight what he considers some of 2026’s most powerful Commanders. Rather than focus strictly on raw power ceilings, Verhey pointed toward the legends that spark creative brewing, surprising table politics, and memorable gameplay moments across every 2026 release. Here's a look at this year's 11 best Commanders in the order they were released.

We all love abilities that trigger when something enters the battlefield, don’t we? Well, Maralen, Fae Ascendant takes that to the tenth degree by bridging two of Lorwyn's most recognizable kindred types into a nimble value engine that’s entirely too fun to abuse. Whenever Maralen or another elf or faerie enters under your control, she exiles the top two cards of a target opponent's library, letting you cast one exiled spell each turn for free if its mana value is equal to or less than your Elf and Faerie count.

Verhey noted that "Maralen turned out awesome" and pointed out that "a lot of people have built that one." He has pitched team-up legends for years, and seeing her succeed makes him eager to explore more dual-type pairings like Rin and Seri or Olag and Miau

Rather than singling out a single hero from the Turtle Power! Commander precon, the standout attraction here is the modular roster built around the “Partner — Character select” mechanic. Inspired by classic arcade beat-'em-ups, the keyword lets you mix and match eligible legends, like Leonardo, the Balance alongside Raphael, the Muscle or Donatello, the Brains, to tailor your Command Zone synergies.

As Verhey explained, "I think the interchangeable Character Select partner mechanic in the Commander deck is pretty awesome there." He added that he loves how these legends "can be interfaced and spliced together," noting that players have gravitated toward the pairings because they combine partner flexibility with punchy abilities. Pairing Leonardo, the Balance with Raphael, the Muscle gives you a prime example, since putting a +1/+1 counter across your board on token entry and doubling damage from creatures with counters lets modest boards turn lethal in a single turn.

At six generic, one black, and one green mana, Witherbloom, the Balancer carries affinity for creatures and extends that exact same cost reduction to every instant and sorcery spell you cast. Flooding the battlefield with tokens or cheap mana producers turns hefty spells like Army of the Damned and Shamanic Revelation into effortless plays. Once you pair that discount with token doublers like Chatterfang, Squirrel General, even splashy finishers like Exsanguinate or Torment of Hailfire become trivial to fire off for lethal.

Secrets of Strixhaven is loaded with what Verhey described as "banger commanders," and the designer admitted he could easily name around ten standout legends from the set. Even in that crowded lineup, Witherbloom, the Balancer rises to the top as a blast to build around, especially alongside spell-copying payoffs like Professor Onyx and graveyard recursion staples like Eternal Witness.

This double-faced mythic rare gives Azorius builds strong flexibility at flash speed. On his front face, King T'Challa draws you a card whenever any player draws their second card each turn, and you can pay six mana total to transform him into Black Panther, Hope Enduring. The opposite side of the card yields a double-striking threat with flash that prevents damage dealt to him and draws cards on combat hits, making him especially lethal with equipment staples like Sword of Feast and Famine or unblockable auras like Steel of the Godhead.

Verhey pointed to T'Challa and Black Panther as a standout among cards that fit traditional color expectations while still "tearing up tables." Surrounding him with symmetric draw engines like Howling Mine or wheel effects ensures his front side triggers across every turn cycle before he flips to close out the game.

At a single white mana, Nick Fury, Agent of S.H.I.E.L.D. looks modest on paper until you read his Power-up ability. Since Power-up reduces its activation cost by his mana cost when he enters that turn, Fury adds two +1/+1 counters to himself while digging seven cards deep to drop a Hero, Equipment, or Vehicle straight onto the battlefield. It’s solid flavor for a 2/1 creature, and has a lot of potential if you know the right way to abuse it.

Verhey described Fury as "really unassuming" yet potent, noting that "Nick Fury, the one-mana white commander, has been really strong." He also shared that he was running all five free spells once Fury hits the board for a single mana.

The Hobbit brought Middle-earth's more whimsical side to Commander with standout legends like Bilbo Baggins and Thorin Oakenshield, yet Smaug the Magnificent is an absolute beast among them. Built to snowball fast, the 4/3 legendary dragon with flying and haste, and even nets you a treasure token at the start of your upkeep. Worst of all, however, is Smaug’s main ability, which deals damage to any creature equal to the amount of treasures you have whenever he attacks.

Verhey noted that "Smaug Treasure decks look pretty cool" and called him a "really powerful Commander," which is saying something. For just four mana, the creature not only stacks treasures for you every single turn, but also deals a little extra damage where needed, best used to chip away at a player’s life total or creature-filled battlefield. Even worse is that Smaug can swing right when he gets onto the battlefield thanks to haste, so if you already have a slew of treasures by the late game, he’s dealing some serious damage that can be made even more devastating with red staples like Furnace of Rath and Fiery Emancipation.

Just like Smaug, Radagast of Rhosgobel also hails from The Hobbit set, bringing instant-speed tempo to mono-green decks. His static ability discounts the first creature spell you cast each turn by two generic mana and lets you cast it as though it had flash, essentially saving you mana for interaction before deploying threats on an opponent's turn.

The Hobbit offers strong options across the board, according to Verhey, who said, "I like the Radagast decks I've been seeing that discount your spells when you play one a turn." That opens the door for surprise plays like flashing in Kogla, the Titan Ape for instant removal or dropping discounted threats like Ghalta, Stampede Tyrant right before your untap step. While I’m not running him as a commander, I have a Radagast in my Thalia and the Gitrog Monster deck, which is all around sacking creatures and land.

As a color-shifted twist from the Reality Fracture Commander decks, Dack Fayden, Helping Hand, puts a fun spin on a well-known Magic character. Upon entering, Dack reveals cards from the top of your library until you hit creature cards equal to the number of your opponents. He then puts those creatures onto the battlefield permanently goaded and hands one to each opponent, stirring up chaotic table politics with a hint of group hug thrown in.

Calling the white Dack Fayden "kind of a funky one,” Verhey suggested he might be the answer for raw power in the set. Players who pair his trigger with blink staples like Ephemerate or symmetrical payoffs like Homeward Path and Trove of Temptation can quickly turn messy handouts into a one-sided advantage.

Liliana the Faultless flips familiar Magic lore in the Echoverse-themed Reality Fracture, becoming a white-aligned version of the character that gains you a life whenever another creature or planeswalker you control enters. You can also pay one mana and discard a card to grant another creature or planeswalker you control hexproof until the end of turn, giving mono-white decks a steady form of protection.

While Verhey also shouted out the set's white Leonin legends and his own black Abzan precon designs, Liliana fits neatly alongside those experiments. Her discard-to-protect clause pairs cleanly with planeswalker or token commanders like Elspeth, Sun's Champion and Mondrak, Glory Dominus that want extra shield equity.

True to his trickster reputation, Q, Stacking the Deck shuffles your library on entry and exiles cards from the top until he hits a nonland card, letting you repeatedly copy that exiled spell by discarding cards from your hand on your turn. Various brewers have already pushed this to remarkable extremes with 98-land decks built to guarantee hitting a specific combo piece.

Verhey admitted he still loves Q and wants "to believe that Q has something pretty wacky," framing him not necessarily as the strongest card in the set, but as one "on my radar" that plenty of players are already building around. Slotting Glaring Fleshraker into a 98-land Q deck, for example, shows just how wild things can get, since exiling lands from your hand to chain copies of Fleshraker floods the board with Eldrazi Spawn tokens that pay for the next copy and ping the entire table out of the game in a single turn.
