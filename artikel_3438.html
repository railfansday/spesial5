The Best Early Black Friday Smart Home Deals

Amazon's early Black Friday 2026 sale is already bringing some substantial discounts on smart home devices, with savings ranging from $23 to $700 across security cameras, video doorbells, smart locks, home security systems, thermostats, and robot vacuums. Several products are more than 50% off, including indoor security cameras from Blink, Ring, and Arlo, while higher-end devices from Eufy and Roborock are seeing price cuts of hundreds of dollars.

You can also check out our other Black Friday coverage for more tech deals as the sale continues. For now, here are some of the best early Black Friday 2026 smart home deals worth checking out.

Named the best security camera of 2024 by PCMag, TP-Link’s Tapo MagCam C425 is now down to $54.99 from $89.99, making it an affordable option for keeping an eye on your driveway, backyard, or front entrance without having to run power cables. It records in 2K resolution and runs on a rechargeable battery, while support for microSD storage lets you save footage locally without paying for a cloud subscription.

Reolink’s 4K Solar Security Camera is down to $144.99 from $229.99, saving you $85. It connects over 4G LTE, so it can keep watch over a barn, vacation home, construction sites, or other areas where a regular wifi security camera won't work because there’s no home network to join. Its microSD support lets you save footage locally without a cloud subscription—you will, however, need an active cellular data plan to get the camera online.

Eufy’s SoloCam S340 is $119.99, down from $219.99, and it pairs a 3K wide-angle lens with a 2K telephoto lens, giving you a view of the whole yard and a closer look at faces or packages at the same time. The camera pans a full 360 degrees and tilts to follow people as they move, and the included solar panel keeps it charged in a sunny spot. Footage is stored on 8GB of built-in storage with no monthly fee—one of the reasons why the S340 earned an Editors’ Choice award in PCMag’s review.

Eufy’s Video Doorbell E340 is $99.99, down from $169.99, and it uses two cameras to cover your front door, with one recording visitors in 2K and the other pointing down at the doorstep to keep an eye on deliveries. It also has color night vision, a removable rechargeable battery, and 8GB of built-in storage for recording footage without a cloud subscription. You can read more about its performance in PCMag's review.

At $28, down from $79.99, the second-generation Arlo Wireless Video Doorbell 2K is one of the more affordable ways to keep an eye on your front door. Its 2K camera provides a detailed view of visitors, while the wide field of view helps capture packages left near your doorstep—making it easy to see why PCMag named it the best video doorbell of 2024.

The Blink Battery Doorbell 2K+ is down to $34.99 from $79.99. It records in 2K resolution and offers a head-to-toe view, so you can see who's at the door as well as any packages left on your doorstep. And since it runs on batteries, installation is relatively simple, especially for renters or anyone who doesn't want to deal with doorbell wiring. For more on it, check out PCMag’s review.

The Eufy Security FamiLock E34 is down to $179.99 from $279.99, saving you $100 on a smart lock that uses palm vein recognition to unlock your door, which Eufy says takes just 0.6 seconds. It can store palm data for up to 50 people locally, making it convenient for households with multiple users, while PIN codes and app-based controls give you other ways to get inside. You also get built-in wifi and compatibility with Apple Home, Alexa, Google Home, and Matter.

The Ultraloq Bolt Fingerprint smart lock is $139.99, down from $199.99, and it replaces your deadbolt with a lock you can open using a fingerprint, a keypad code, or the U Home app over built-in wifi. It can also unlock automatically as you walk up to the door, using your phone’s location. PCMag named it the best smart lock of 2024.

SimpliSafe’s DIY 8-piece Wireless Home Security System is down to $119.99 from $209.99, making it a relatively affordable starting point for securing multiple entry points around your home. The kit includes a base station, keypad, entry sensors, a motion sensor, and a panic button, so you can monitor doors, windows, and movement inside the house. For more details, check out PCMag’s in-depth review.

The Arlo Home Security System is $142.49, down from $199.99, and this starter kit pairs a Keypad Sensor Hub with two All-in-One Sensors, each of which can be set to detect motion, open doors and windows, water leaks, and other events across eight functions. It runs in the same Arlo Secure app as Arlo’s cameras and doorbells, which makes it a natural addition for homes that already use them. For more on its features and performance, check out PCMag’s review.

At just $15.99, down from $39.99, the Blink Mini 2K+ is one of the cheapest ways to add indoor monitoring to your smart home. This compact plug-in camera records in 2K resolution, and also supports motion alerts, two-way audio, and Alexa integration. PCMag has a review of the Blink Mini 2K+.

Arlo’s Essential Pan Tilt Indoor Cam 2K is $18.99, down from $49.99, and it’s part of the lineup Arlo released in 2025, with a motorized head that rotates a full 360 degrees and tilts 180 degrees so one camera can cover an entire room. This PCMag review goes into more detail on how it performs.

The Sensi Smart Thermostat ST55 is currently $79, down from $129.99, and is a good option for anyone looking to control their home's heating and cooling from a phone—it lets you create schedules, adjust temperatures remotely, and use geofencing to make changes based on whether you're home or away. It also works with several popular smart home platforms, including Alexa, Google Assistant, and Samsung SmartThings, plus Apple HomeKit when a C-wire is connected, notes this PCMag review.

The Amazon Smart Thermostat is a more affordable alternative at $56.99, down from $79.99. It works with Alexa to let you adjust temperatures through voice commands or the Alexa app, where you can also set schedules and automate temperature changes based on your routines. For more on its setup and features, check out PCMag's review.

Ecovacs’ Deebot X8 Pro Omni is down to $699.99 from $1,099.99, saving you $400 on a robot vacuum that takes care of both vacuuming and mopping. It uses a roller mop that continuously rinses itself with clean water as it works and extends outward to clean along walls and furniture. You also get 18,000Pa of suction and a self-maintaining dock that empties the dustbin, washes the mop with hot water, and dries it afterward. For more on its cleaning performance, check out PCMag’s review.

The Roborock Qrevo CurvX has dropped to $799.99 from $1,499.99, saving you $700. It’s a 2025 update to the Qrevo Curv that raises suction from 18,500Pa to 22,000Pa and slims the robot to 3.14 inches tall so it fits under more couches and beds, while keeping the AdaptiLift chassis for climbing thresholds and a dock that washes the mop pads with hot water. This PCMag review covers the original Qrevo Curv, which should be a useful reference for the design and dock the two models share.
