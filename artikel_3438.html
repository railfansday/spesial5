Skydance Sets Film Leadership Team: Don Granger and Kira Goldberg to Head Paramount Motion Picture Group, Jesse Ehrman Promoted to WB Pictures Chief

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Skydance has set its film leadership team.

Don Granger, a veteran Skydance executive, and Kira Goldberg, a leader of Netflix’s film team, have been tapped as presidents of Paramount‘s Motion Picture Group.

As part of the moves, Jesse Ehrman has been promoted to president of Warner Bros. Pictures, while Mikey Ireland will remain as president of production. Their task will be to stabilize Warner Bros.‘ film output while rejuvenating or extending franchises like “The Lord of the Rings” and “The Conjuring.” Skydance executives have been horrified by the extent of the losses racked up by Pam Abdy and Michael De Luca, with sources estimating them in the hundreds of millions of dollars. As part of this restructuring, Jeff Goldstein, who oversaw Warner Bros. distribution for many years, will retire, and Bill Damaschke will exit Warner Bros. Pictures Animation, which he led. Skydance said that in the interim, Jennifer Dodge and Traci Balthazor will oversee all animation across both Paramount and Warner Bros. Pictures. Richard Brener, a survivor of several mergers, will remain as president and chief content officer of New Line, where he guided hits like “Weapons.”

Others remaining include: Lia Buman, EVP, head of global acquisitions and Republic Pictures; J.D. Lifshitz and Raphael Margules, heads of the genre division, Paramount Primal; and Christian Parkes, head of the indie label Clockwork; and Charlie Tabesh, SVP and general manager of Turner Classic Movies.

Last week, Skydance announced that James Gunn and Peter Safran, who had led DC Studios, were leaving the company. Jeremy Latcham, a former Marvel executive, replaced them. It wasn’t the only overhaul at the top. Last month, as Skydance took full control of Warner Bros., Michael De Luca and Pamela Abdy were ousted. De Luca and Abdy were beloved by the creative community, but they spent liberally and accumulated too many flops, including “Digger” and “The Bride!”

Both Granger and Goldberg will report to Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, the co-chairs of Skydance’s Motion Picture Group. Across Paramount and Warner Bros., the mandate will be to get more films into production. To get the merger over the finish line, Skydance founder David Ellison agreed to make more than 30 films annually, far exceeding the output of any traditional studio.

In a memo to staff, Goldberg and Greenstein praised Granger as “a great partner as we’ve repositioned the studio, bringing in key talent relationships and nearly doubling output.” They noted that at Netflix, Goldberg “oversaw a slate that included ‘Don’t Look Up,’ starring Leonardo DiCaprio and Jennifer Lawrence, and the upcoming ‘Bad Day,’ with Cameron Diaz, and ‘The Take’ starring Denzel Washington and Robert Pattinson. Goldberg was also an executive at Fox, working on films such as “The Greatest Showman” and “Bohemian Rhapsody.”

“Together, Don and Kira will work hand in hand going forward,” Goldberg and Greenstein wrote, adding, “We have both an incredible legacy to build upon and an enormous opportunity ahead of us: to champion the world’s best filmmakers, invest in ambitious storytelling and deliver an unparalleled slate of films that captivate audiences everywhere.”

Ehrman is a WB veteran who has worked on hits such as “A Minecraft Movie” and “Sinners.” Ireland joined Warner Bros. after years at Paramount. He left the studio after it was sold to Skydance in 2025.

“This team brings deep knowledge of our businesses, strong creative instincts and longstanding relationships across the industry,” Goldberg and Greenstein wrote. “We have tremendous confidence in these leaders and are grateful for their partnership.”
