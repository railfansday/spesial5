Bitcoin shorts collapsed 79% in five weeks, then the market turned lower

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Hedge funds sharply reduced net bearish exposure before Friday's selloff, leaving their next positioning shift in focus.

Leveraged funds reduced their net short positions in CME Bitcoin futures by 79.3% over five weeks before Bitcoin plunged below $60,000 on Friday.

The funds held 1,211 more short contracts than long contracts on Nov. 17, down from 5,844 on Oct. 13, according to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission's Nov. 20 report. The shift was driven primarily by declining short exposure. Over the period, short contracts fell from 3,889 to 7,475, while long contracts increased from 744 to 6,264.

Together, those changes narrowed the gap between the two sides by 4,633 contracts, equivalent to 23,165 BTC.

The reduction occurred at every weekly snapshot. Net shorts fell to 4,942 contracts on Oct. 20, 3,979 on Oct. 27, and 2,591 on Nov. 3 before reaching 1,243 on Nov. 10.

However, the retreat nearly stalled in the final week, with net shorts declining by just 32 contracts as both long and short positions contracted.

That was the smallest weekly reduction in the five-week period, compared with declines of more than 900 contracts in each of the preceding four weeks.

The timing leaves a gap in the CFTC data. Friday's selloff occurred three days after the latest positioning snapshot, so any resulting changes in futures exposure were not recorded.

A fresh wave of short selling could reverse the trend, although funds could also become more net short by closing existing long positions.

The two outcomes would carry different implications for how traders responded to Bitcoin's decline.

However, the CFTC's leveraged-funds category includes hedge funds using futures to offset spot holdings or pursue arbitrage strategies. Some of the earlier reduction in short contracts may therefore reflect the unwinding of those trades rather than changing expectations for Bitcoin's price.

The CFTC's next report, scheduled for Nov. 30, will reflect positions held on Nov. 24.

The breakdown between long and short contracts will show whether funds rebuilt short exposure following the selloff or continued reducing positions despite Bitcoin's losses.
