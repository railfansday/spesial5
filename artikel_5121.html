Japan weighs safeguards for Bitcoin holders who keep their own keys

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

One option in a regulator’s paper would make customers with new exchange accounts wait before sending crypto to their own wallets.

Japan’s Financial Services Agency returned to the issue of Bitcoin self-custody in a Nov. 26 paper for its study group. Japanese exchanges already have to record details of covered withdrawals. The paper asks what can protect customers once they hold the signing keys themselves.

An exchange manages the signing keys while it holds Bitcoin for a customer. In a self-custody wallet, the customer controls the keys needed to authorize a transfer. The FSA’s November discussion paper points out what happens if customers lose access to those keys. They may be unable to move their Bitcoin.

The Travel Rule requires providers to share information on certain transfers between them. An exchange sending Bitcoin to a customer’s own wallet has no receiving provider to notify. On a covered withdrawal, Japan’s recordkeeping rules require the exchange to collect and record details such as the destination wallet owner’s name and wallet address.

On a covered transfer from a self-custody wallet into the exchange without that notice, the exchange records the information it can obtain. The FSA’s supervisory guidelines cite the rules implementing Japan’s Act on Prevention of Transfer of Criminal Proceeds and call for exchanges to examine the wallet’s characteristics and assess its money-laundering risk.

The duties apply to exchanges that handle transfers. When two people transfer Bitcoin directly between wallets they control, neither has an exchange carrying out those checks. Under Switzerland’s planned crypto tax reporting, providers would report transactions they handle, while later transfers between personal wallets would fall outside that reporting.

The November staff paper cites a withdrawal waiting period for new exchange accounts as an example of an anti-fraud measure for a Cabinet Office ordinance. It gives no duration. Japan’s Diet passed amendments to the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act and the Payment Services Act on July 15. As of Nov. 27, the FSA’s public rulemaking register listed no implementing ordinance or effective date for the proposed delay.

A February summary of the working group’s recommendations gave an example of how an exchange could intervene before a withdrawal. Immediately after opening an account, a customer could face a waiting period before sending crypto to a wallet they control.

In August, the FSA and Japan’s National Police Agency separately asked the industry’s self-regulatory association to strengthen withdrawal restrictions after cash deposits, crypto purchases or registration of a withdrawal destination. The request also called for scam warnings and transaction monitoring. The agencies also asked exchanges to check customers who make large or frequent withdrawals soon after restrictions lift.

For people who already hold their own keys, the November paper asks how to warn them about wallet risks and where regulation might help if problems arise. It also asks whether further measures are needed to curb money laundering and terrorist financing.
