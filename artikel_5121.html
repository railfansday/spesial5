Binance Says It Has Fully Covered the $6.34 Billion Hack With Company Funds

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Binance said Thursday it has completed coverage of the $6.34 billion Oct. 29 hack from company funds, two weeks after the largest crypto theft on record, even as withdrawals continue to reopen in phases.

In a Thursday announcement, the exchange wrote that it “has completed coverage of assets impacted in the security incident” and published a new proof-of-reserves snapshot. It repeated the same message on X.

That wording matters. Coverage means Binance has replaced the affected tokens from its own books, not that it has recovered the stolen coins from the attackers. The exchange said it committed more than $6.34 billion in company funds and bought the affected tokens through market and over-the-counter channels, drawing on a partial deployment of its Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU), a portion of its futures insurance fund above the level required under its risk-management framework, and its corporate treasury.

Binance has also committed to replenishing SAFU to about $1 billion after that partial draw, a step it said would keep the fund fully resourced as an emergency user-protection reserve.

A very small number of tokens the exchange no longer lists were also affected. Binance said those holdings will be handled under its delisting process and that users who hold them will be covered.

Binance took a new proof-of-reserves snapshot at 00:00 UTC on Nov. 12, or 7 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday, covering 198 tokens. That is a sharp jump from the 10 tokens included in the Oct. 31 snapshot published in the days after the breach. The new figures are now posted on the exchange’s proof-of-reserves website.

The snapshot, Binance said, “provides an updated view of assets that are fully accounted for.” Users cannot yet check that claim themselves. The exchange said the full zero-knowledge proof will take about 40 to 50 hours to generate because of the computational workload. After that, users holding the affected tokens “will be able to independently verify that their assets are backed at a minimum of 1:1.”

Until the proof is ready, the 1:1 backing is Binance’s statement, not something customers can confirm on their own.

Thursday’s update does not reopen the platform all at once. Binance said it continues to reopen withdrawals in phases and will share further details once they are confirmed. The exchange first reopened withdrawals on Nov. 1, starting with bitcoin and then expanding to other networks, and has kept the restart on a phased timetable since then.

The announcement also does not say how much of the stolen assets have been retrieved, and it does not name an attacker. Covering customer balances leaves that recovery question unanswered. The theft, which Binance first detailed in a Nov. 3 incident report, began with a fake job interview and a poisoned developer tool and drained hot wallets on Oct. 29, in what remains the largest crypto hack on record.

The exchange had already pledged more than $6.34 billion to cover customer losses in the hours after the breach, reported coverage of 90% and then 95% as it reopened withdrawals, and later said it had replenished 98% of affected tokens, with the remainder in less liquid assets that took longer to buy. Thursday’s statement is the first time Binance has said that process is complete.

Binance also said security remains a priority and that work on its infrastructure is still underway. For customers, the immediate tests are simpler: whether the zero-knowledge proof, due in about two days, backs the new snapshot, and how quickly the rest of the withdrawal queues come back.
