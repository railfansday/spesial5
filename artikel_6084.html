Southwest Priority Card Review: Strong Airline Benefits For Frequent Flyers

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Southwest Airlines has several co-branded credit cards with Chase. Of the three personal cards, my favorite one is the Southwest Rapid Rewards® Priority Credit Card.

This is my in-depth Southwest Priority Card review, covering the card’s benefits. I’ll also explain how despite this being the highest annual fee personal card in the portfolio, I still think it represents the best deal. Now is also a good time to consider applying for the card if you’re a fan of Southwest’s popular Companion Pass perk.

If you’re thinking about applying for the Southwest Priority Card, you’re probably most interested in the welcome bonus it’s currently offering, along with its annual fee and general eligibility rules for getting the card. So let’s dive into those. I’ll also explain a recent change to how Southwest cards factor into qualifying for the Companion Pass, which pertains to all cards, not just the Priority.

The Southwest Priority Card is offering a limited time welcome bonus: Earn 90,000 bonus points after spending $3,000 on purchases in the first 3 months from account opening. This is higher than the standard welcome offer for the card, and its minimum spending requirement should be easy for most spenders to meet.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, and based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Rapid Rewards points to be worth 1.2 cents each. So, to me, the points from this welcome offer are worth $1,080.

Chase and Southwest have some pretty strict rules when it comes to who is eligible for the card. The Southwest Priority Pass is not available to anyone who currently has a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card, or anyone who has received a welcome bonus on a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card in the past 24 months. However, you are eligible if you have a Southwest business credit card.

This is in addition to Chase’s general application rules, including the 5/24 rule, whereby you typically won’t be approved for a Chase card if you’ve opened five or more new card accounts in the past 24 months. However, that’s not enforced consistently.

When it comes to credit scores, there’s not a consistent score that you need to get approved for the card, though I’d hope to have a score in the “excellent” range. That being said, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been rejected, as it’s one of only many factors that determines card eligibility.

The Southwest Priority Card has a $229 annual fee. As a point of comparison, the other personal cards have annual fees of $99 and $149, so the annual fee on this card is a bit higher. However, as I’ll explain below, the benefits could justify the premium annual fee.

The card also has no extra cost to add authorized users, so you can add those for $0 each, so that authorized user spending can help you rack up rewards.

The Southwest Priority Card has no foreign transaction fees, so this could be a useful card for your spending abroad. This is a Visa as well, and Visas are accepted pretty widely around the globe.

Another rule worth mentioning here involves how Southwest credit card welcome bonuses help you earn the Companion Pass, which lets you bring a companion with you on flights (both cash and award tickets) and pay just taxes and fees.

For a long time now, the points earned as part of Southwest credit card welcome bonuses have counted towards the 135,000 qualifying points needed to earn the Companion Pass. But starting soon, credit card welcome bonuses will no longer count toward qualification.

You’ll need to apply for a card by December 2, 2026, for your welcome bonus to count toward the Companion Pass for a final time. So apply for the Priority Card sooner rather than later if you’re hoping to earn that perk with the help of credit card spending.

Now is also your last chance to get an annual boost of 10,000 Companion Pass qualifying points as a Southwest credit card holder. Starting next year, instead of a boost of 10,000 TQPs for having a Southwest card, you’ll get 10,000 TQPs if your TQP balance is 130,000 by December 31, 2027. That would be enough to get you to Platinum status, which will include unlimited Companion Pass trips as a benefit.

This change eliminates the easiest way to earn this valuable benefit, and it’s not the only new rule: referral bonuses, anniversary points, bonus points from spending, and partner spending won’t count toward the Companion Pass either. In fact, Southwest’s rolling the Companion Pass into its highest elite tier, Platinum, and requiring either 140,000 TQPs or 100 qualifying flights to earn it.

Beyond the Southwest Priority Card airline benefits, a major draw is the card’s points earning on every purchase you make — plus how it can help you work toward Southwest elite status through earning Tier Qualifying Points.

Personally, I’d generally only put spending on the card if you’re going for A-List status and/or Companion Pass, because there are better cards for everyday spending otherwise. But still, those are some fairly solid bonus categories.

As part of an overhaul of the Rapid Rewards program, Southwest is simplifying TQP earning on credit cards. Starting in 2027, every dollar spent on a co-branded Southwest credit card will earn you one TQP. In other words, $35K in spending would earn you Silver, $65K in spending would earn you Gold, and $140K in spending would earn you Platinum.

This earning rate is the same across cards, so this isn’t specific to the Southwest Priority Card.

I have a dedicated post that covers how to redeem Southwest Rapid Rewards points for maximum value, but let’s briefly look at how you can use the points you earn from the Southwest Priority Card.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, meaning that the value you get from your points is tied to the cash cost of Southwest tickets. There are a few different levels, ranging from Basic tickets to Choice Extra fares, so the exact value can vary a bit.

Basic award fares tend to offer the best redemption value. You’ll also usually get the best value on international Southwest routes to destinations like Costa Rica and Aruba. I’ve been able to get about 2.0 cents per point on these redemptions.

Starting in spring 2027, Southwest will add points earning and redemption opportunities with select airline partners, which should make your Rapid Rewards points more valuable for international travel. The planned partners include Icelandair, China Airlines, and Etihad Airways — I look forward to seeing how these add value to the program.

One great thing about the Southwest Rapid Rewards program is that there are no change or cancellation fees, and rebooking a fare when the price drops gets you a refund equal to any difference in points. Just note that Basic fares can only be changed by upgrading to a higher fare bundle, so for a cheaper Basic award you’d cancel and rebook rather than change.

As Southwest’s highest annual fee personal credit card, the Southwest Priority Card has the most benefits, including several that can improve your travel experience with the airline. Here are the card’s most notable ongoing perks.

Southwest Priority Card holders can select a Preferred seat on Southwest at the time of booking for no extra charge. If Preferred seats aren’t available on your flight, you should be able to select a standard seat for free. Preferred seats are located near the front of the cabin, so having one usually means you’ll board and deplane faster.

Preferred seats generally cost about $30 to $60 extra, and selecting free Preferred seats at booking is a perk of Southwest Silver elite status. If you don’t have status and you’re able to use this benefit, you could get real value even after just a few Southwest flights.

The Southwest Priority Card also offers unlimited upgrades to extra legroom seats within 48 hours prior to departure. This perk applies to the main cardholder as well as up to eight additional passengers on the same reservation.

Extra legroom seat prices vary by route, but they generally cost roughly $40 to $100. On a route where they’re at the higher end of this range and you have multiple companions on your reservation, you could even recoup the Priority Card’s annual fee through one trip alone.

Keep in mind that complimentary extra legroom seats within 48 hours of departure is another benefit of Southwest Silver status. So if you have that level of status or higher already, you don’t need the Priority Card to enjoy this perk.

The Southwest Priority Card gets the primary cardholder and up to eight additional passengers on the same reservation a first checked bag at no additional cost. Checked bags on Southwest cost $45 each way, so this can save you up to $90 roundtrip on checked bag fees per person.

With even a couple of roundtrip flights per year, this benefit can offset the annual fee — and about five one-way checked bags per year could cover it as well.

With the Southwest Priority Card, you’ll be able to board with Group 5, or earlier if you have elite status or a fare or seat type that qualifies you for an earlier boarding group.

Southwest has eight total boarding groups, so while there are several ahead of Group 5, it’s better than boarding last. This benefit applies to the main cardholder and up to eight passengers on the same reservation.

On your Southwest Priority Card anniversary every year, you’ll receive 7,500 bonus Rapid Rewards points. Southwest Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, and you can generally expect that you’ll need around 83 Rapid Rewards points for every dollar of airfare you book.

In other words, 7,500 bonus points get you around $90 in Southwest airfare annually. That covers more than a third of the annual fee right there.

If you pay with your Southwest Priority Card you can save 25% on inflight Wi-Fi and drink purchases. You’ll receive statement credits for the 25% within two billing cycles of your purchase (though usually much faster than that).

Even though the Southwest Priority Card has the highest annual fee of any personal Chase Southwest card, it might be worthy of consideration.

Many people love flying Southwest for the ability to earn the Southwest Companion Pass, the lack of change fees and vouchers that don’t expire. For others, Southwest is just a practical airline to fly on occasion, given its massive route network. Regardless, there’s lots of value in having points and benefits on Southwest.

That said, not everyone needs a Southwest credit card — and even among Southwest credit cards, there’s a cheaper option that could make more sense if you only fly the airline a few times a year. Let’s look at some alternatives and how they stack up.

When providing credit card advice, my answer to whether or not it makes sense to get a certain card is usually “it depends.” and this is the case as well with the Southwest Priority Card, especially given the latest changes to the Southwest Rapid Rewards program.

If you fly Southwest several times a year and want a free checked bag and complimentary Preferred seat selection, not to mention complimentary extra legroom upgrades, this card can be well worth it. That’s especially true if you don’t have elite status with Southwest already, since it offers some of these same benefits.

If you’re interested in the Priority Card and also want the chance to earn the Companion Pass, consider applying sooner than later. Southwest credit card welcome bonuses will no longer count toward Companion Pass qualification in 2027, and this card’s 90,000-point welcome offer can get you a good chunk of the way there.
