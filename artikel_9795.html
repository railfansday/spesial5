ULI Explained: APIs, Data Connectivity, and the Future of Digital Lending

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

How APIs, connected data, and configurable lending workflows could reshape India's digital lending ecosystem.

India's UPI revolution demonstrated what shared digital infrastructure can achieve. Now, the Unified Lending Interface (ULI) aims to bring similar connectivity to lending.

Developed by the Reserve Bank Innovation Hub (RBIH), ULI connects lenders with data service providers through standardised digital interfaces.

For fintech professionals and developers, the interesting question is: how can connected data improve lending workflows and support better credit decisions?

Digitising a loan application doesn't necessarily digitise the entire lending journey.

Lenders still need to verify identities, retrieve financial information, assess income, validate assets, and evaluate repayment capacity.

These activities can involve multiple systems and independent integrations.

For MSMEs and agricultural borrowers, the challenge can be greater. Relevant information may exist across bank records, land registries, credit bureaus, and other institutional databases.

The problem isn't always a lack of data. It's accessing the right information efficiently.

ULI addresses this challenge through a common API framework.

Instead of independently building every connection, lenders can access participating data services through standardised interfaces.

ULI is an important development in India's digital public infrastructure, extending interoperability principles into lending.

Connected information could help lenders understand borrowers whose financial circumstances aren't fully reflected in conventional documents.

Actual outcomes depend on how effectively lenders integrate these services into operational workflows.

Data connectivity alone cannot guarantee better lending.

Lenders still need robust credit policies, affordability assessments, fraud controls, and governance processes.

This requires coordination between data services, loan origination systems, and decision engines.

As lending requirements evolve, banks and NBFCs need adaptable technology.

No code credit decisioning platforms help lenders configure and modify lending processes as business requirements change.

This flexibility can help connect evolving verification requirements with operational lending workflows without treating every process modification as a separate development exercise.

UPI transformed how India pays. ULI could influence how India borrows.

The opportunity lies not simply in connecting lenders with more data, but in turning that information into responsible, informed credit decisions.

For fintech developers and lending technology teams, the next challenge is making data connectivity, operational workflows, and decisioning systems work together.
