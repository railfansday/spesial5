Arsenal 1-1 Borussia Dortmund: Gunners held in thrilling Champions League clash

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Your weekly briefing on Arsenal, featuring team news and expert analysis from Matt Verri

Arsenal dropped points from a winning position for the second time in as many games following a 1-1 Champions League draw with Borussia Dortmund at the Emirates Stadium.

Mikel Arteta’s men led at the interval after Viktor Gyokeres scored his first goal on home soil in 206 days with a wonderful 25-yard opener inside 10 minutes.

But just as they did in the Premier League at Newcastle on Saturday, Arsenal were unable to see out the victory, with Maximilian Beier netting for the visitors after half-time.

Jobe Bellingham had a goal ruled out for offside, while Gyokeres could perhaps count himself fortunate not to give away a penalty when a Dortmund corner struck him on the arm.

Gyokeres then wasted a golden opportunity to restore Arsenal’s advantage when he headed wide unmarked from six yards, while the ball cannoned off Serhou Guirassy to twice strike the Dortmund post in the closing stages. Declan Rice hit the woodwork again for Arsenal with five minutes left.

The draw leaves Arsenal fifth in the table, but they could be on the bubble of the top-eight places – which are assured of automatic qualification for the next phase – if results work against them on Wednesday.

Gyokeres was introduced only as a stoppage-time substitute in Arsenal’s 2-2 draw at St James’ Park despite the Gunners chasing the game for most of the second half.

Here, he began from the start and opened the scoring with his first meaningful contribution of the game.

Noni Madueke had time and space to bring down a crossfield ball before finding Gyokeres, who immediately tried his luck from range, and perhaps catching Gregor Kobel in the visiting goal by surprise.

But that was to take away nothing from the velocity and dip behind Gyokeres’ effort – the sort of strike which encouraged Arsenal to spend £68million last summer but has not always been repaid. It was Gyokeres’ first at the Emirates since May.

Rice fired over from range and then only a last-ditch tackle from Dortmund captain Nico Schlotterbeck denied the England midfielder moments later. Arsenal led at the break, but they did in the North East before a second-half collapse saw them emerge with just a point, rather than three.

Beier afforded Arsenal a warning almost immediately after the interval when he ghosted to the front post only to fail to get any sort of connection on Daniel Svensson’s low cross.

But Beier made no mistake in the 55th minute. Joane Gadou was the architect following a fine run from inside his own half. He looked up to find Beier, who took one touch past Gabriel before unleashing a low drive. Kepa Arrizabalaga, deputising for the injured David Raya, never got close to saving it.

Piero Hincapie’s header was saved by Kobel as Arsenal mustered a response. Bellingham thought he had put Dortmund ahead when he turned away Kepa’s bungled save but the assistant referee’s offside flag came to Arsenal’s rescue.

Then came the Arsenal onslaught. Madueke’s curling shot grazed the side-netting before Gyokeres sent a diving header wide from six yards to leave Arteta on his knees. Arsenal also struck a post on three occasions.

In all, the Gunners had 29 shots but only four of those were on target, and the need for a world-class finisher remains a top priority should Arsenal want to win the Champions League for the first time in their history. They will face Jose Mourinho’s Real Madrid next.
