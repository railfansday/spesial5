Banjo-Kazooie Mentioned Alongside Active IP By Activision Job Listing

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

There hasn't been a new mainline Banjo-Kazooie game for almost 20 years, which is just wild when you really stop and think about it. Despite glimmers of hope here and there, there has been no revival, but now fans of the series might have been thrown their most hopeful rope yet, as Activision Blizzard seemingly views the dormant IP as one of the most important under its newly expanded gaze.

In a new job listing for an Activision product manager that would handle and help promote emerging franchises, Banjo-Kazooie is one of a handful of major franchises that now reside under the Xbox-owned studio. "Activision is home to iconic gaming franchises including Call of Duty, Crash Bandicoot, Spyro the Dragon, and now... Halo, Age of Empires, Sea of Thieves, and Banjo-Kazooie," the job description reads.

Just like the rest of us, Toys for Bob wants to see Banjo return.

Certain studios now answering to Activision were among many big changes rolled out during Xbox's most recent round of layoffs. The next Halo will be an Activision game, and World's Edge and Rare are both under Activision now too, which is why Age of Empires and Sea of Thieves are mentioned in the job listing.

Banjo-Kazooie may well be Rare's best-loved and most famous export, so it makes sense that Activision would want to brag about now having it under its umbrella. Mentioning it in a job listing in this way, though, has renewed hope that we're finally getting a new Banjo game. Not just because every other series mentioned is active in one way or another, but because it's mentioned in a job listing for someone who will be responsible for emerging franchises.

When Xbox revealed that Activision will be in charge of Halo's future, fans of the series were left feeling a little uncertain. Banjo fans might now be feeling the same way, although they probably shouldn't be. As Activision highlights, it also owns Crash and Spyro, two colorful platformers who are often lumped together with Banjo when people list the best and most famous games and characters from that genre, and the little purple dragon is getting his first new game since 2004 next year - Spyro: A Realm Beyond.

Both Spyro and Crash have enjoyed huge success via their remastered trilogies too, both selling in the millions. Perhaps that will be Activision's first order of business with Banjo now that it is directly working with Rare. A remastered trilogy that introduces the original game, Banjo-Tooie, and Nuts & Bolts to a whole new generation of potential fans.

It is just the name of the series listed alongside some other games for now, and we have kind of been here before. There have been rumblings that Banjo-Kazooie might be getting revived for years, only for those rumblings to repeatedly come to nothing. However, this is the biggest change the series has experienced in terms of who exactly is calling the shots when it comes to making a new Banjo game, and it's a studio that has more experience with platformers cut from the same cloth as Banjo than just about any other studio on the planet.

A new Banjo has been rumoured for some time, and Xbox fans clearly want it to be true.
