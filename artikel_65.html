Mid-year budget update will ‘cut enough, but not too much’, Jim Chalmers says

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Under growing pressure to sell government’s strategy, treasurer says Myefo will ‘help ease immediate capacity pressures without putting growth at risk’

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

Jim Chalmers has promised next month’s mid-year budget update “will cut enough, but not too much”, as he flagged “substantial savings” to help in the fight against inflation.

The treasurer has come under growing pressure to sell the government’s economic strategy after five interest rate hikes, and amid rising unemployment and cost-of-living pressures.

Chalmers, speaking at the Australian Securities and Investment Commission conference in Sydney, said the mid-year economic and fiscal outlook (Myefo) “will strike some fine balances”.

“It will cut enough, but not too much,” he said.

“It will help ease immediate capacity pressures without putting growth at risk, and make room for the responsible cost-of-living relief we’ve been rolling out without adding to price pressures.”

Amid plunging confidence among mortgaged households, the treasurer pointed to financial markets, which “seem to think rates have now peaked”.

“In contrast, rates are expected to continue climbing in every major advanced economy.”

As the treasurer spruiked Australia’s relatively low debt and small budget deficits versus other advanced nations, he explained why savings would be the government’s “main focus” for the budget update.

“First, because of the intensifying spending pressures coming at us, including higher debt interest, rising costs of essential services, and other unavoidable spends and variations,” he said.

“Second, because we take seriously and embrace our responsibilities in the fight against inflation.”

Economists say federal and state governments are contributing to the inflation challenge, although soaring fuel prices and the pressures from massive datacentre investment are also playing a major role in keeping price pressures high.

Despite the treasurer’s claim of $180bn in total savings since Labor came to office in 2022, the budget papers project a deficit just shy of $10bn in 2024-25, $22.3bn in 2025-26, and an expected shortfall of $31.5bn this financial year.

Chalmers’ address comes as the Albanese government attempts to pass major tax measures announced in the May budget, including a 30% minimum tax on income from discretionary trusts.

The government has also come under pressure to redesign its changes to capital gains tax, including an alternative, business-backed model suggested by independent MP Allegra Spender.

That proposal would cut the CGT discount from 50% to 40% for “active” investments, such as shares and equity in small businesses, and leave the inflation-linked CGT model for property.

In his speech Chalmers also announced that the Australian Taxation Office would begin consulting with the business sector on “modernising Australia’s business tax system”, which he said could save companies and sole traders tens of millions of dollars a year.

“We’ll immediately begin consulting on two specific options,” he said.

“First, reducing the burden on sole traders by pre-filling contractor income – saving an estimated 400,000 hours and $37m in costs each year. Second, simplifying the minor benefits rules in the FBT system, by scrapping complex and subjective assessments of small benefits below $300.”
