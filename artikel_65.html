Cigarettes on silver platters at Bondi ‘clean’ beauty business lip gloss launch referred for investigation

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Copini Beauty referred to New South Wales health regulators, while federal government says it is ‘deeply concerned’

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

An Australian beauty company that promotes “clean” ingredients “free from skin irritants” has been referred to state health regulators after appearing to offer cigarettes to guests at a beachside lip gloss launch.

Photos from an event held earlier in November at a venue in Bondi, Sydney show what appears to be loose cigarettes without health warnings on a tiered silver platter alongside a bowl of lip glosses.

“Celebrating the launch of our soft gloss with our community,” one post from the event shared by the Copini Beauty account on Instagram read, with an accompanying carousel of images showing what appeared to be cigarettes on display with Copini-branded match boxes, lipglosses and glasses of wine.

The images are also featured in a highlight reel from the event on the official Instagram page for the brand.

Copini is co-founded by influencer Casey James and product developer Carla McGraw.

Under the Public Health (Tobacco and Other Products) Act 2023, advertising or promoting smoking or the use of tobacco products is prohibited. Both state and federal laws prohibit businesses offering, giving or distributing a free tobacco product.

Any tobacco supplied must also meet strict product standards, such as displaying mandatory graphic health warnings. The cigarettes at the Copini launch do not appear to carry any health warnings on the filter as required, indicating they are illicit products. Buying or supplying illicit tobacco is illegal in Australia, regardless of the quantity.

Cigarettes in Australia cannot legally carry brand logos. The cigarettes appear to carry the Manchester “sapphire blue” branding, one of the most common illicit tobacco products in Australia. They are considered unlabelled under Australian law because they do not carry official government health warnings and compliant packaging.

A spokesperson for the NSW health minister, Ryan Park, said of the event and images: “This has been referred to NSW health’s centre for regulation and enforcement.”

The centre works with state and federal authorities to regulate smoking products and has powers to inspect premises, seize illicit stock, issue fines and prosecute offenders.

A spokesperson for the federal health department said the government is “deeply concerned about advertising or promotion that glamorises smoking or the use of tobacco products, or helps normalise tobacco use, particularly where it might reach young people”.

“There is no appropriate place for tobacco products to be used as a promotional prop or to make smoking appear fashionable or appealing,” the spokesperson said.

“The government strengthened Australia’s tobacco laws to reduce the visibility and normalisation of tobacco products and protect Australians from the harms of smoking.”

The spokesperson said allegations of non-compliance with laws are taken seriously, saying the maximum penalty for criminal advertising or sponsorship of tobacco under federal laws is $728,000 for an individual, or $7.2m for a company.

Smoking accelerates ageing and causes more than 16,000 cases of cancer in Australia each year. The display of cigarettes at the launch appears to contradict Copini’s philosophy: “We’re 100% vegan, cruelty-free, and free from skin irritants and endocrine disruptors,” the website says, stating that “clean ingredients” are used in all products.

The company did not respond to requests for comment.

It comes amid growing concerns about the use of cigarettes by brands, and in images of celebrities, especially online, with health experts warning it risks normalising and glamourising smoking.

Becky Freeman, a professor of tobacco control with the University of Sydney, said it was “shocking to see the resurgence” of smoking in pop culture and on red carpets.

“But to see this really local example, in Bondi, shows this is not an international phenomena – it’s happening in a country like Australia where we have low smoking rates through strong regulations and health policies,” she said.
