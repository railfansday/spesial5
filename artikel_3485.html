Warning: This article includes mild SPOILERS for Percy Jackson & The Olympians season 3, episodes 1 & 2 and The Titan's Curse by Rick Riordan!

Camp Half-Blood might be the best-known group in the Riordanverse, but the Percy Jackson TV show has just introduced the Hunters of Artemis, a group that will play a significant role this season and return down the line. Percy Jackson & The Olympians has returned for a new season on Disney+, and the first two episodes of season 3 have already dropped. Each week, we’ll be treated to two episodes, which is a change from the past two seasons.

For the first time ever, the Percy Jackson book The Titan’s Curse has come to the screen. We’ve already met characters who are central to the story going forward. Bianca and Nico di Angelo were saved from the military school, where Annabeth was kidnapped, and they have made it to Camp Half-Blood. Atlas, Kronos’ right-hand man, is out from under the Titan’s curse, and he’s ready to help raise his fellow Titan. The most epic introduction so far, though, came from the Hunters of Artemis.

From their musical motif to the Hunters’ costumes, everything about their introduction was perfect, and they feel just as regal and dangerous as their book counterparts. The group will play a major role this season and again in Percy Jackson season 5, if Disney+ continues the show that far. Unfortunately, they didn’t give viewers much information about the Hunters of Artemis, and not everyone has read the books. If you’re wondering about the mysterious elite group, here’s everything you need to know about the group’s rules and structure and their motivations in the TV show.

The Hunters of Artemis are girls who pledge themselves to the goddess Artemis and join her on the Hunt. They serve as her companions, handmaids, and fellow hunters. Once they take the oath, they can’t live outside the Hunt. In the book, their oath is, “I pledge myself to the Goddess Artemis. I turn my back on the company of men, accept eternal maidenhood, and join the hunt.” We haven’t heard the vow in the TV show yet, as nobody has joined.

In the source material, the average age of the Hunters when they take the vow is around 12, and almost all are kids. However, they don’t have to be children, and they don’t have to be demigods. Instead, Artemis accepts any unmarried girl or woman, whether Half-Blood, human, nymph, or something else. The TV show ages the group up, making them look more like older teenagers.

Upon joining, the Hunters become immortal with the caveat that they can die in battle. They stay frozen at the age they were when they joined unless they break their vow. On top of that benefit, when they take the vow, they get heightened strength, speed, agility, and archery precision. Each of them uses a specialized bow and arrow as a weapon. Technically, they are allowed to leave at any time, at which point they revert to the state they were in before they joined.

One of the changes Percy Jackson season 3 makes to The Titan’s Curse is the reason why the Hunters were at the military school when the Manticore attacks and their goal. In the book, they catch the Manticore's scent while hunting an ancient beast and follow it, hoping it’s the one they’ve been searching for. They may also have been in the area to recruit Annabeth, though this happens before the book starts.

However, Disney+’s Percy Jackson has them show up to protect Thalia and Percy, the Forbidden Children. They explicitly tell Annabeth that they aren’t there to get her answer about joining the Hunters. The reason she cares about Percy and Thalia is simple. Artemis knows the Great Prophecy, and she obviously doesn’t want Olympus to fall because that would be the end of her. Also, she knows the Bane of Olympus is back, which they’ve been hunting. Zoe tells Mr. D and the people at the camp that this creature can destroy Olympus if it devours a Forbidden Child.

As of Percy Jackson & The Olympians season 3 episodes 1 & 2, there’s no answer about who or what it looks like. I’m keeping spoilers mild in this article to preserve some of the big moments that could come up in the TV show. If you want to know what the Bane of Olympus is, it’s revealed in The Titan’s Curse chapter 15, “I Wrestle Santa’s Evil Twin,” on page 228.

Rather than just protect Thalia and Percy, Artemis orders her Hunters, led by Zoe, to confine the Forbidden Children inside Camp Half-Blood, even if the Oracle gives them a quest prophecy. Zoe takes this to the extreme, trying to tie them up in mental netting toward the end of Percy Jackson & The Olympians season 3, episode 2. The only reason they stop this plan is that the Oracle appears outside and reveals that Artemis has been kidnapped, and they can only save her with the help of Campers.

One of the best changes the Percy Jackson TV show makes is having Annabeth reveal to Percy that the Hunters of Artemis are recruiting her. This is a great way to lay the foundation for the group before they show up, and it also answers one of the book's biggest unanswered questions: why Annabeth considers joining. Fans have speculated about her logic for years, but the book never explains it. The only answer provided related to Annabeth and the Hunters is why she doesn’t join them in the end, which comes in The Demigod Files.

Annabeth explains to Percy, while dancing, that she can be used as a weapon against Percy and Thalia. She knows they would do anything to save her, bringing up Percy’s line, “If I had to choose between saving you or saving Olympus, Annabeth, I’d burn it all down.” If Annabeth joined the Hunters, she wouldn’t be around. Kronos and his goons could try to come get her from the Hunt, but Artemis and the other Hunters would protect her. Plus, she would have enhanced abilities.

Ultimately, her suspicion that others will use her to manipulate Percy and Thalia is correct. Atlas and Dr. Thorn kidnap Annabeth because they know that Percy and Thalia will come to rescue her. Kronos’ goons need the Forbidden Children and the Bane of Olympus to bring down Olympus, and they know that the two Campers won’t give up on Annabeth.
