Why ECB collateral eligibility could boost demand for tokenized bonds

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Past ECB decisions offer clues to the value of using bonds as collateral, while US derivatives exchanges face a different calculation over their Bitcoin holdings.

The European Central Bank (ECB) plans to expand the range of tokenized bonds banks can use as collateral to borrow cash, a move that could lower borrowing costs for issuers and give bond funds additional ways to earn securities-lending fees.

For a borrower preparing a bond sale, the choice of where to issue it could affect the interest bill. From June 2027, the ECB plans to accept qualifying bonds issued on platforms licensed under the EU’s distributed ledger technology pilot regime.

A bank that buys one could raise cash from the Eurosystem using the bond as collateral without selling it. That flexibility may be worth a higher bid at the sale.

Historical bond prices offer a clue to the potential impact. A study of 160 changes in Eurosystem collateral eligibility found that yields on newly eligible corporate bonds fell by 4.6 to 20 basis points relative to bonds still waiting to qualify.

Demand to borrow eligible securities also rose, according to the study. A bond fund could earn a fee by lending the security to a bank that wants to pledge it as collateral. Luxembourg’s 1.7 billion euro ($1.86 billion) tokenized bond already qualified through an earlier route.

Banks bought 47% of the October issue, close to their 51% share of Luxembourg’s conventional 10-year sale in March. Those figures show banks were willing buyers in both markets. As eligibility expands, the more revealing figures will be the yields banks accept on newly eligible debt and how much of it they actually pledge.
