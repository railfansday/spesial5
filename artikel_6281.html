Frontier Airlines Starts Selling First Class From $129. I Am Seriously Considering Booking It.

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Frontier Airlines is now selling its new first class seats, with wider seating and no middle seats in the first two rows. Extra-legroom seating, USB-C power, and Starlink internet are also part of the cabin transformation. I welcome the investment, though a better seat will only get Frontier so far if the rest of the experience does not improve alongside it.

Installation is underway, with completion expected March 5, 2027. Reservations are open for travel beginning that day.

Promotional pricing requires purchasing the flight and bundle together by November 16, 2026, at 11:59 pm ET, for select nonstop domestic departures March 5–April 5, 2027. Availability is limited.

USB-C power is planned for the first eight rows. Both power and Starlink installations begin in 2027 and progress aircraft by aircraft throughout the year. Booking the new seat does not mean every other advertised improvement will be available on your flight.

For me, reliable internet and the ability to keep a laptop charged substantially determine how useful a flight is. A comfortable seat is welcome, but on a daytime trip I also must get work done…it is an essential part of the package.

I have to say, I’m quite excited about these developments.

As much as I loved warmed mixed nuts, a hot meal, and sparkling water saved with lime and ice in a glass…the Frontier network is respectable and a comfortable seat with Wi-Fi and power are more important. If Frontier can charge half or what American or United are asking on the same route, I’m totally up for flying Frontier more.

The bigger question is whether Frontier can persuade passengers to pay more and then give them a reason to come back.

A first class seat loses much of its appeal if a cancellation leaves you with poor alternatives or resolving an ordinary problem becomes an ordeal…two areas where traditional network carriers still have a huge advantage. Selling a premium product raises expectations even when the price is relatively low.

I do not expect Frontier to become a full-service airline simply because it installs eight larger seats. But I would expect the airline to understand that passengers purchasing those seats are buying some relief from the compromises associated with budget travel, again myself included.

That makes this an opportunity. If Frontier can offer a comfortable seat at a sensible total price and deliver dependable transportation and maybe a little bit more service than the “Big Front Seat” on Spirit Airlines, I think there is a market for it.

Frontier Airlines is moving forward with first class, and I welcome more choice at the front of the aircraft. The new seats could make the airline more attractive to passengers who value comfort but remain sensitive to price, like me. Even the extra legroom seats are something I would consider, with Starlink being an even bigger game-changer.

While I would happily try it, the real test will be whether Frontier delivers an experience that makes me want to book it again. A larger seat with Starlink can get someone through the door…but Frontier must deliver that.
