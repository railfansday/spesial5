Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO amid ongoing intelligence probe

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Nvidia shares surged nearly 29% after the company’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO following consultations with the US government.

Nvidia’s board unanimously voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and CEO after consultations with senior US government officials, concluding that his return posed no national security risk despite an ongoing investigation into an alleged foreign intelligence operation targeting him.

In a Monday announcement, Nvidia said its directors were satisfied with Huang’s candor and ability to lead following a series of discussions. The independent investigation will continue despite his reinstatement.

Nvidia shares surged 28.91% to $161.82 shortly after US markets opened on Monday, according to Yahoo Finance data.

Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, will return to her previous role, while Harvey Jones will remain chair of the board.

The chipmaker also said it had recently formed a National Security and Export Control Committee comprising independent directors to oversee compliance and security risks. Nvidia plans to appoint a chief compliance officer and said it would continue cooperating with government investigations.

Huang resigned on Nov. 12 following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a former executive whom US intelligence agencies reportedly believed was passing information to Beijing. Nvidia shares plunged as much as 14% intraday following his departure before recovering some losses.

Days later, Silicon Valley executives reportedly organized a campaign to bring Huang back, including efforts to secure White House support. A public petition calling for his reinstatement had gathered more than 187,000 signatures by Nov. 16.

Despite the campaign, Nvidia’s board was reportedly not considering reinstating Huang, instead focusing on its investigation into his conduct. President Donald Trump said he would not mind seeing Huang return, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged the company to appoint an outside leader.

The uncertainty surrounding Nvidia came amid a broader market selloff on Friday, when the Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.28% and the S&P 500 fell 3.69%. Bitcoin (BTC) also dropped below $60,000 for the first time since July, with CoinShares head of research James Butterfill attributing the cryptocurrency’s weakness partly to deteriorating sentiment toward AI stocks.

Huang’s reinstatement comes as he faces ongoing congressional scrutiny over Nvidia’s dealings with China. He has agreed to appear before three House committees on Dec. 1 and separately agreed to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee, although a date for that hearing has not been announced.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren called Huang’s reinstatement “reckless” on Monday, saying Nvidia still owes Congress answers about its compliance with US export controls. She also called on the Trump administration disclose which senior officials provided input in support of his return.
