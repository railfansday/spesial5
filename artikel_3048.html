Prediction: Some Anthropic Investors Are About to Learn an Expensive Lesson

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Anthropic broke records this week, but some investors may be setting themselves up for disappointment.

By Katie Brockman – Nov 21, 2026 at 7:41PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Anthropic (ANT -8.50%) made history this week with the largest stock market debut on record, securing $81 billion in funding and surpassing SpaceX's initial public offering (IPO) earlier this year.

There was no shortage of investor enthusiasm either, as the stock popped by more than 17% during its first day of trading. While nobody can say how the stock will fare in the coming days or weeks, I predict that some Anthropic investors are about to learn a hard, expensive lesson.

Dario Amodei. Image source: Getty Images.

Blockbuster IPOs are often turbulent straight out of the gate, experiencing extreme price swings in their initial weeks and months.

SpaceX, for example, has fallen by more than 20% from its initial price, as of this writing. Semiconductor breakout star SK Hynix has plunged by nearly 30% since it debuted in July, and Meta Platforms – then known as Facebook – sank by more than 50% in its first three months of trading in 2012.

In Meta's case, the stock eventually bounced back, delivering total returns of around 1,400% since its IPO. But the investors who reaped those rewards were those who stayed invested through all the ups and downs rather than expecting immediate riches.

The same thing could be said for Anthropic. Over time, it could be a lucrative investment. The company recently generated $17 billion in third-quarter revenue, significantly outpacing its $1.4 billion figure from 2025.

But Anthropic also has some major bills to keep up with. Its IPO prospectus disclosed over $900 billion in expense commitments and a $104 billion net operating loss in 2026, and time will tell whether the company's revenue is sufficient to fund the enormous computing power its technology demands.

This isn't to say that Anthropic is a bad investment. But as with any IPO, there are a lot of unknowns right now, and anything could happen in the coming weeks and months.

If you believe in the business model and are willing to hold your investment for at least a few years despite any potential short-term volatility, it could be a smart buy – assuming it's part of a well-diversified portfolio.

However, investors who are buying primarily because of the hype around the stock, hoping to make a quick buck, have the most to lose. The short term is likely to be a bumpy ride, and those who are only buying with the hope of earning a fortune overnight could end up sorely disappointed.

Fundamentals beat hype every time, and the strongest investments are those with solid underlying business fundamentals and long-term growth potential.
