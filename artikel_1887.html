GdS: ‘Ahead of the curve’ – Amorim’s positive start compared to predecessors

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Ruben Amorim might not have unanimously won everyone over yet, but AC Milan are off to their best Serie A start in four years.

La Gazzetta dello Sport (seen below) write this morning that Milan under Amorim have started better than under Massimiliano Allegri in 2025-26, Paulo Fonseca in 2024-25 and even Stefano Pioli’s final season in 2023-24.

The best start belongs to the coach who delivered the Scudetto though, with 31 points in the first eleven rounds of the 2021-22 season, which ended with the most recent Italian league title added to the trophy cabinet.

Amorim has also hit the ground running, with an average that, in previous seasons, would have placed him even higher. A year ago, with 24 points at this same stage of the tournament, Milan would have been the league leaders.

The numbers don’t lie: 24 out of a possible 33 points – garnered through seven wins, three draws, and only one Serie A defeat – amount to 2.18 points per match. By comparison, Allegri had reached 22 points, Fonseca 17 and Pioli also had 22 in his final year.

The coach behind the 2021-22 Scudetto also started with great momentum in the previous season (26 points in 2022-23) and in the 2020-21 campaign (27 points).

These figures were double the 13 points from the 2019–20 season, a campaign that began with Marco Giampaolo at the helm, only for him to be replaced mid-season by Pioli. While still seeking to find that same rhythm in Europe, Milan have been surging in the league.

They got off to a blistering start with wins away at Torino and in their San Siro debut against Venezia, followed by a draw at Juventus – a match in which they were pegged back just minutes from the end -and another stalemate away to Lazio.

After falling two goals behind, Milan fought back against the team currently leading the league. Given Lazio’s subsequent form, that result takes on added significance. Next came a victory over Lecce, followed by a lacklustre draw in Reggio Emilia against Sassuolo.

From that point on, the turning point arrived: four wins in the next five matches, including away victories against Udinese and Genoa away from home, as well as the derby. The only stumble during this stretch was against Bologna.

Sensing that the squad was under significant stress, Amorim has granted a week off, with training set to resume next Monday. For those without other commitments such as international duty, this provides a chance to truly switch off.

Many players have headed away, while those who remained are either following strict injury recovery programs or simply taking a break. This pause is longer than usual: the typical break in professional leagues is generally shorter.

This isn’t the only unusual aspect: the return to training ahead of the match against Frosinone the following week will also take place only after the players returning from international duty have been given ample time to rest.

There are precedents for this, though. For instance, the team was given a Tuesday off just two days before their European away match against Bournemouth. They returned to the pitch on Wednesday, the same day they departed for England. The result was a 3-0 loss and a dark night.

There is certainly enough evidence now to speak of new methods introduced by Amorim and his staff. On the pitch, Rúben has brought in new ideas: training sessions are never overly gruelling, and the data from GPS trackers and distance-monitoring tools is never pushed to ‘excessive’ levels.

“We train by playing,” the Portuguese coach has often explained, implying that matches themselves offer the best opportunities to test tactical schemes and movements. This is especially the case when playing every three days, with such little time between games.

While this approach may have initially raised some eyebrows among the players and was even speculated to be a cause for the cluster of injuries the Rossoneri experienced, such concerns were dispelled once the coach provided further clarification.

In the meantime, no one is taking a break fully. Even while away, every player has been assigned specific training programs to follow, ensuring they return in peak condition to keep the good league form going.
