Phillips Evening Sale Tops $104 M., Led By $44.6 M. Modigliani Sculpture

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Phillips’s modern and contemporary evening sale, held at its Park Avenue headquarters in New York on Tuesday night, garnered $104.4 million with fees on 22 lots, led by a $44.6 million total for the 1911–12 Amedeo Modigliani sculpture Tête.

With one lot withdrawn prior to sale and only one lot going unsold, the sale posted a healthy 95 percent sell-through rate by lot and an even more impressive 99 percent sell-through rate by value.

In a post-sale email, the auction house, which has recently faced scrutiny following cost-cutting and a major leadership shake-up in August, touted the total as a 55 percent increase over the equivalent sale last year. That figure is correct, but it does not tell the whole story. The 2025 edition of the modern and contemporary evening sale generated $67.3 million on 33 lots with two unsold. This year’s edition was pulled up considerably by the Modigliani result. Remove that from the total and the take drops to $59.8 million, though that is spread across 20 lots rather than 31. Still impressive, all things considered, but not quite the mega-result the house is touting. But credit where credit is due: Phillips specialists did secure that Modigliani consignment, and it significantly outperformed its $28–$35 million estimate. Other strong results for the night were the eight-panel David Hockney group portrait David, Celia, Stephen and Ian, London 1982, which landed within its $10–$15 million presale estimate for a total of $12.2 million with fees; the Alexander Calder sculpture Snow Flurry, May 14 (1959), which reached $7.5 million with fees on a $4–$6 million estimate; and the Helen Frankenthaler landscape Seabreeze (1980), which sold for $4.5 million with fees, well above its $2.5 million high estimate.

Phillips noted that the Hockney is the artist’s highest result at auction this year. Three other Hockney paintings appeared at Christie’s and Sotheby’s this week, the highest selling for $8.05 million at Sotheby’s contemporary evening sale on Monday, per ARTDAI.

In the same email, Robert Manley, chairman and global head of modern and contemporary art, said the sale’s strongest results were due to offering “fresh-to-market, museum-quality material.” He added, “Tonight’s results are a powerful reminder that truly exceptional works continue to inspire extraordinary competition in the market.”

That takeaway aligns with the results of the week’s other major sales so far: Sotheby’s The Now and contemporary evening sales on Monday and Christie’s 20th-century evening sale on Tuesday. At both houses, there was strong, deep bidding for truly exceptional works, but also more than a few unsold lots and spotty interest in those that didn’t reach that A+ level.

As art advisor Ralph De Luca put it succinctly to ARTnews during Tuesday’s sale at Christie’s, “The flight to quality is astounding.” Indeed.
