Treasury, IRS finalize new Opportunity Zone designations

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Treasury and the IRS designated 6,576 census tracts as new Qualified Opportunity Zones under the Working Families Tax Cuts, effective Jan. 1, 2027, with a 10-year term and enhanced incentives for rural areas.  AI Summary

The U.S. Department of the Treasury and the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) designated 6,576 census tracts across all 50 states, territories and the District of Columbia as new Qualified Opportunity Zones, according to a Thursday announcement.

The action, which will take effect Jan. 1, 2027, will permanently renew the federal Opportunity Zone tax incentive and add enhanced benefits for investment in eligible rural communities. It also establishes a process to redesignate opportunity zones every 10 years, meaning that the newly announced map will remain in place through the end of 2036.

IRS Chief Executive Officer Frank J. Bisignano, in a press release, said that the permanent extension and expansion of the program will “give states an opportunity to attract long-term investment into underserved, rural and economically distressed areas”, and that “the IRS and Treasury are enabling targeted investment and growth in communities where it is needed the most.”

Opportunity Zones, initially created as part of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, are designed to incentivize investment in economically disadvantaged communities by allowing investors, including builders and developers, to defer or reduce capital gains taxes when they invest in qualified projects.

For housing developers and homebuilders, the updated map and permanent extension of the incentive should provide more clarity on eligible locations and long-term planning for the next decade.

That certainty stems from changes made by the Working Families Tax Cuts, which President Trump signed into law in 2025.

The legislation made the Opportunity Zone program permanent, established enhanced tax incentives for rural communities and created a process to redesignate eligible census tracts every 10 years.

In an announcement, Treasury said that the Working Families Tax Cuts build on more than $110 billion in private-sector investment made in Opportunity Zones since the program was launched in 2017. The bill narrowed the pool of eligible zones from 8,764 and strengthened incentives for rural communities. Although urban areas will continue to represent a significant share of designated zones, the changes could encourage more investment in rural markets with limited access to housing capital.The changes complement a provision in the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act that allows the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) to favor grant applications if their housing development is located in or primarily serves a designated Opportunity Zone. The aim of that provision is to spur more housing supply in economically distressed communities.

The program, which applies to both market-rate and below-market housing, is popular among residential developers. A 2025 Urban Institute analysis found that 64% of Opportunity Zone capital in Ohio went toward residential development, for example. And a separate 2025 analysis by the Economic Innovation Group concluded that the program roughly doubled housing supply in eligible zones.
