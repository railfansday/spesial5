Hedge funds sold tech stocks at a near-record pace after midterm elections

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Gross leverage at U.S. fundamental long-short funds fell 8.7 percentage points to 207.5%, the biggest weekly drop since March 2020

Hedge funds unloaded global tech shares last week at a rate that nearly matched the record in Goldman Sachs $GS figures dating to 2016, according to Bloomberg.

Tech saw more net selling than any other sector, both worldwide and in the U.S., according to Bloomberg. The selling produced the largest surge in net sales of individual equities in four months, based on Goldman's prime brokerage figures through Nov. 6. Hedge funds also offloaded American industrial shares faster than at any point since April 2020.

Investors cut risk after the U.S. midterm elections prompted doubts over how fast data centers would be built and how much chip and other infrastructure demand would follow, according to Bloomberg.

Gross leverage at U.S. fundamental long-short funds shrank by 8.7 percentage points to 207.5%, a weekly drop not exceeded since March 2020, Bloomberg said. Net leverage fell by 3.7 percentage points to reach 43.3%, its steepest one-week drop since the week of “Liberation Day” in April 2025.

Selling eased by the end of the week. Hedge funds ended the week as net purchasers in figures from Goldman's equity trading desk, lifted by Friday buying, according to Bloomberg. A large part of that buying reflected short covering in macro trades involving indexes and ETFs.

Tech stocks dropped sharply on Nov. 5. Nvidia $NVDA stock fell 9.3%, AMD $AMD stock fell 17.3%, Micron $MU stock fell 17.5%, Broadcom $AVGO stock fell 10.4% and Microsoft $MSFT stock fell 3.6%. CoreWeave stock fell 10%.

Dozens of municipal-level ballot proposals across 10 states, each aimed at limiting data-center construction, went to voters on Nov. 3, according to CNBC. Kyle Rodda, a senior financial market analyst at Capital.com, wrote in a note that investors were assessing what a Democrat-controlled Congress could mean for technology oversight and the AI boom, according to CNBC.

Hedge funds had already been reducing AI exposure. They posted their worst monthly performance relative to the S&P 500 in more than two decades in July as popular AI positions unwound. Goldman strategists led by Ben Snider said July marked one of the sharpest hedge fund de-grossing episodes of the past decade.
