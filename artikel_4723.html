Netflix’s <i><em>The Altruists </em></i>Tells a Compelling Story of Love, Money, and Fraud. Here’s What Really Happened.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The true story behind Sam Bankman-Fried, Caroline Ellison, and the $8 billion crypto collapse is even messier than the show lets on.

NETFLIX’S LATEST HEADLINE-MAKING limited series The Altruists has a lot of big names attached to it. There are Irish and American actors Anthony Boyle and Julia Garner, respectively, playing up their dorkiest selves to portray Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison. (Ostensibly while Garner desperately awaits casting in a possible Madonna biopic that has been in development hell for what feels like forever.)

Who, you ask? Well, if you’ve avoided the cryptocurrency market and carbon-positive NFT gorillas in the past several years, we can’t really blame you. But what you need to know is that Bankman-Fried and Ellison were the heads of the cryptocurrency exchange FTX as well as lovers. FTX, and the love between them, notoriously collapsed when Bankman-Fried was arrested and accused of stealing over $8 billion in customer funds.

Why is this story about fraud (of which Bankman-Fried was eventually convicted) called The Altruists? Ironically, FTX was purportedly created as a company that gave away as much as it earned, based on the principle of effective altruism—words that got everyone in the finance world hot and heavy with excitement. Until news came out that Bankman-Fried and other FTX executives had spent billion-dollar-sized wads of cash “on luxury hotel stays, private planes, DoorDash orders, trips to Jimmy Buffett's Margaritaville, and, in the case of one top executive, a $2.5 million yacht,” Business Insider reports. FTX was also known for hosting an ultra-luxe conference in the Bahamas that featured famous folks including Bill Clinton, Tom Brady, and the musicians Steve Aoki and the late Liam Payne of One Direction, who in 2024 fell to his death from a hotel balcony in Argentina at the age of 31.

So, yeah, we’re dealing with a twisted and extremely millennial story full of curveballs and screwballs. But whether you’re watching The Altruists or simply hearing about it, what do you need to know about the true FTX story at the heart of it? We’ve got you covered below.

The Altruists is as much a love story as it is a story of financial doom: The two were intertwined with Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison, who met at an investment firm and fell in love. Bankman-Fried would found FTX as well as its sister cryptocurrency trading firm Alameda Research, while Ellison was CEO of Alameda Research, which funneled money from FTX.

FTX was on an upward trajectory in the early 2020s, garnering Bankman-Fried the nickname of “Crypto King.” The company, once worth $32 billion, nosedived into bankruptcy in 2022 while also being investigated by the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Justice Department. Bankman-Fried “sounded surprisingly calm” in an interview with The New York Times amid FTX’s freefall. “You would’ve thought that I’d be getting no sleep right now, and instead I’m getting some,” he told the publication. “It could be worse.”

Yes. In fact, things could and did get a lot worse for this once seemingly altruistic finance dude. He and Ellison broke off their romantic relationship amid FTX’s collapse in 2022. (“She wanted more from it than I was able to give,” Bankman-Fried testified, according to the Wall Street Journal. To which one can only wonder: More than $8 billion?)

On March 28, 2024, Bankman-Fried was sentenced to 25 years in prison, three years of supervised release, and was additionally ordered to “pay $11 billion in forfeiture for his orchestration of multiple fraudulent schemes,” the Justice Department announced.

In total, he was convicted of two counts of wire fraud, two counts of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, one count of conspiracy to commit securities fraud, and one count of conspiracy to commit money laundering.

What’s curious is that his right-hand woman, Ellison, only got less than two years in prison for her part in the cryptocurrency fraud, though she did plead guilty to two counts of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, two counts of wire fraud, one count of conspiracy to commit commodities fraud, one count of conspiracy to commit securities fraud, and one count of conspiracy to commit money laundering. She was also ordered to forfeit $11 billion.

Her relatively light sentence can be explained by the fact that, though the “very math-y” Stanford graduate helped orchestrate the multibillion-dollar scheme alongside Bankman-Fried, she became a star witness for the prosecution against her former boyfriend. The prosecutors offered her a sweetened deal to shorten her prison time in exchange for cooperation, information, and testimony.

She testified, NPR reports, that as Alameda found itself in a financial hole, Bankman-Fried directed her to take more money from FTX customers to bail the company out. “We were in a bad situation,” she said.

U.S. Attorney Damian Williams said of Bankman-Fried’s scheme that it was “one of the largest financial frauds in history, stealing over $8 billion of his customers’ money.”

Their love may be over, and they are both convicted felons, all of which gets packed into the final episode of the eight-part The Altruists. But at least Ellison is moving on. Sort of. After being imprisoned for less than two years, she is now a free woman.

In September, Ellison formally joined Manifund “to develop our funding platform and research how to effectively direct philanthropic dollars,” the company’s CEO Austin Chen announced, per Oxygen. She had reportedly joined the nonprofit funding platform and philanthropic organization on a trial basis at first and went by the innocent-sounding pseudonym “Carol.” This work is in line with her stated belief in effective altruism, which in turn became a multibillion-dollar con, which doesn’t sound all that altruistic or effective.

“On FTX itself, I remain conflicted. It was their money and spirit that enabled the Future Fund, and I still admire many aspects of FTX and the individuals who worked there,” Chen wrote. “But also: FTX did wrong. Its collapse harmed users (myself included), and damaged the movement which I deeply care about.”

Chen is a big believer in redemption. “I’d like to give her the opportunity to contribute back to the world,” he wrote. “I hope that the world will concur.” Which is very generous of him. Maybe she’ll even get a Nathan Fielder-hosted documentary one day.

Bankman-Fried, meanwhile? He’s still behind bars on that 25-year sentence. Thankfully for him, however, he is incarcerated at the low-security California federal prison Federal Correctional Institution Lompoc, which is known for its open dormitory and room-type housing.

Maybe he will find a new way to be altruistic. Or, at least, stop committing federal crimes.
