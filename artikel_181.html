Member of anti-migrant group found guilty of Staffordshire murder

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Raise the Colours activist Billy Allison, 36, killed man in ‘completely unprovoked and unjustified’ attack, say police

A member of anti-migrant group Raise the Colours has been found guilty of murdering a man in a “completely unprovoked and unjustified” attack.

Billy Allison, 36, delivered a “very, very hard” punch with the “stance and skill of an experienced fighter” which killed 44-year-old Matthew O’Gara while he was on a night out in Lichfield on 24 May.

A jury of seven men and five women were not told of Allison’s link to Raise the Colours, which has been responsible for hanging hundreds of union jack flags on lamp-posts and roundabouts across the country, during the trial at Stafford crown court.

Allison was also found guilty of grievous bodily harm with intent on Wednesday for twice punching O’Gara’s friend, 62-year-old Adam Cooper, on the same night as the murder, leaving him with a fractured eye socket and nose.

O’Gara’s family thanked the emergency services who cared for him and said he remained “deeply loved” and “will always hold a special place in our hearts”.

“While the conclusion of the court proceedings has brought a measure of justice, it can never replace the loss we have suffered or bring Matthew back,” they said in a statement.

The trial heard how Allison had been drinking and had taken cocaine before he attacked the two men – neither of whom he knew.

He had spent the day with his partner, Charlotte Dean, and fellow Raise the Colours members Ryan Bridge and Ben Cullen and their partners.

CCTV images capturing the lead-up to the assault showed Allison in the corner of the dancefloor alongside Bridge at about 11.30pm.

Prosecution counsel Timothy Hannam described how Allison, without warning, then took three “purposeful” steps toward an “oblivious” O’Gara and delivered a “very, very hard punch” causing him to fall to the floor and become “motionless”.

The punch was “hard enough to break several bones” in O’Gara’s neck, Hannam said, and caused a massive brain haemorrhage which resulted in the 44-year-old going into cardiac arrest. He died in hospital two days later.

Allison, who had participated in and won boxing competitions, then delivered two “powerful boxer punches” to Cooper’s face. “In total, three devastating punches had been delivered in about six seconds,” Hannam said, adding that the two men had been “demolished by them”.

O’Gara’s partner, Rebecca Mullally, gave evidence during the trial and said O’Gara and their friends were in a “good mood” before the assault.

She said she heard Allison’s punch because it was “really loud” and she did not see “anybody provoke anyone” before the attack.

“I‘ve never seen a punch like that before,” she said. “I did think: ‘Has that killed him?’ It was hard.”

Cooper also gave evidence during the trial and said he felt an “almighty blow to my face” when he was punched by Allison, likening it to a “sledgehammer”.

“I didn’t know it was coming [and] I made no attempt to defend myself,” he said, before describing how blood then poured out of his face.

After the attack, Allison fled the bar via a back entrance with Cullen and the pair swapped T-shirts. Allison was later found by police at the Cathedral Hotel in Beacon Street at 5am, where Cullen had booked to stay the night. He later answered “no comment” during police questioning.

Police body-worn camera footage showed Allison being told his victim had died and he was facing a murder charge. Allison could be heard muttering “fuck” and “fuck’s sake”.

In his evidence to the court, Allison said he was “really sorry” for what he had done and “really sorry my actions caused the end of someone’s life”.

He said he had never intended to cause anyone really serious injury or death, but accepted O’Gara and Cooper had not done anything to provoke the attack.

Allison added: “I just don’t have an explanation for why … I had a bit of a sense I was being looked at, in my drunken state, maybe I was a touch paranoid.

“I didn’t intentionally punch him that hard.”

DI Lisa Holland, who led the investigation, said: “Matthew O’Gara’s family have been left devastated by his death and our thoughts remain firmly with them.

“This was a wholly unprovoked attack carried out by Allison on a man he did not know and who posed no threat to him. In a matter of seconds, Allison’s actions changed the lives of Matthew’s loved ones for ever.

“The evidence in this case showed that Allison approached Matthew without warning and struck him, causing catastrophic injuries from which he never recovered. He then went on to seriously assault another man.”

Allison, who is facing a mandatory life sentence, has been remanded ahead of his sentencing at Stafford crown court on 5 January.
