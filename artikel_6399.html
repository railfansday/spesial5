Asteroid Bennu's Sample Delivers Another Surprise

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

One of the most interesting discoveries in recent years is that some of the molecules necessary for life are found on asteroids and comets. Sample return missions from asteroids have shown that these rocky bodies are far more than just rock. These discoveries are a collective a-ha! moment. It's becoming clear that some of the precursors for life could be widespread in the Solar System. And if they're widespread in ours, they could be widespread in others. The idea that life could appear elsewhere in the galaxy got a boost.

NASA's OSIRIS-REx was a big part of this when it returned a sample from asteroid Bennu in 2023. Examination of those samples revealed the presence of 14 of the 20 amino acids that make up proteins in Earth life, along with four of the essential building blocks for DNA and RNA.

Now, researchers have compared one of those molecules with the same molecule from another source, the Murchison meteorite, to see how they differ. A mission to collect an asteroid sample and return it to Earth is an extremely complicated endeavour. Scientists were hopeful that in the time that passed between planning and launching the mission and getting their hands on the sample, new laboratory techniques would be developed to investigate the sample as deeply and thoroughly as possible. That's what has happened in this case. The researchers were able to measure and compare the different isotopic compositions of Î²-alanine in the Bennu sample and in the Murchison meteorite sample.

They not only compared bulk compositions. In this work, they were actually able to measure and compare isotopic carbon differences in carbon atoms in the same molecule. This level of detail is new.

A device unique to Penn State allowed the measurement of isotopic averages in extremely small samples. It's called pico-CSIA, for Picomolar-scale compound-specific isotope analyses. That means the source materials were distinct isotopically. So they must have came from different locations and environments in the young Solar System. The authors say that the differences are hard to reconcile with any known processes taking place inside parent bodies. Instead, they reflect different chemical pathways in different parts of the protoplanetary disk.
