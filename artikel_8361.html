Hikers Stuck in a Stampede & Catch a Miracle Hitch After Dark

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

This morning we packed up early at our tent site, knowing that within in a couple of hours we would be meeting our friend, Gorf, at a nearby trailhead. We were only about 6 miles out from the trailhead from the spot that we camped at last night. Those 6 miles were actually some of the easiest that we’ve had out here on the Arizona Trail. There was hardly any elevation gain, and the trail wasn’t covered in any loose rocks. A lot of the miles of trail along the Arizona Trail have loose, rocky sections which can definitely slow me down. Being able to start our day on such nice terrain was delightful. Plus, we were both excited to see Gorf and get some food from her.

The sun coming up as we made our way to the trailhead to meet up with Gorf.

Both of us planned to leave about 2-3 days of food with Gorf so that we wouldn’t have to carry a huge resupply for this section. She offered to drive out and meet us somewhere and this wound up being the perfect opportunity. Of course, we weren’t expecting another big storm to roll in. But it all worked out, nevertheless. By around 8:15AM, Slide and I got to the trailhead and sat down to wait for Gorf. We couldn’t have been there for more than a minute or two when she drove up and parked in the pull off. It was really nice to see her again after having spent so much time with her and Slash on this trail so far. The day we spent hiking down into the Grand Canyon with Slash and Gorf was the only day so far that we actually hiked with anyone else out here.

I really liked the way these tall plants looked in the rising sun.

We hung out at the trailhead with Gorf for a little while. She had brought us both the resupplies that we left at her house but also brought us each a chocolate milk and a breakfast sandwich from McDonald’s. That was an incredible way to start off the day. I actually somehow packed the perfect amount of food for this section of trail. If this storm wasn’t coming and we were going to hike all day today and tomorrow, I would’ve nailed it! Even still, I only had a little bit of food left this morning before meeting Gorf. And the amount of food that I had left at her house wasn’t too crazy. Slide on the other hand had left an obscene amount of food at Gorf and Slash’s place! He definitely eats more than me, as he should, but also still had a good amount of food leftover last night and this morning. Watching him stuff his food bag into his backpack was hilarious, as it basically took up the space of his entire pack. I could not have carried that backpack around all day.

Unfortunately, we still had 28 miles to hike for the day, so we couldn’t stick around at the trailhead with Gorf for too long. We gave her a rundown on our plan to get to the upcoming highway in 28 miles and hitch into Strawberry, AZ this evening. I did the math and it looked like we would be getting to the trailhead sometime after dark. So, this morning I was texting numbers off of the Arizona Trail Angels list to see about scheduling a ride into town this evening. But we were out of range for a lot of people, and a few people were out of town or otherwise unavailable. I had gotten one response from a couple who said they could bring us into town first thing tomorrow morning, which was an excellent backup plan if all else failed. However, I wasn’t able to secure us a ride just yet. It looks like we’re just going to have to wing it this evening! Of course, Gorf is the absolutely sweetest human ever and offered to drive all the way back out here this evening to drive us into town. But we couldn’t possibly let her drive that far so late at night for that! She would get home so late after dropping us off. We told her that we would figure it out one way or another. The trail hardly ever fails you when you’re out thru hiking, so we’ll just have to hope for the best.

When we left that trailhead, we briefly continued through a wooded area before crossing over another dirt road and second trailhead. There was a trail register at this spot, so we stopped to sign it and shed some of our morning layers. The sun was rising and already starting to warm us up nicely. I’ve been loving the trail registers on this hike because there are actually a bunch of people ahead of us. It’s fun to see where everyone is and be able to get excited about catching up to some hikers. Our friends Woods, Strix, and Leaf signed this trail register this morning. They had said that they were trying to camp by the trailhead last night and were probably a couple of hours ahead of us. Everyone is just trying to get to that highway up ahead and go into town from there if possible. I texted Wood’s to see about joining forces and coordinating on a trail angel ride. But lucky for them, they actually might reach the road before it gets dark. I hope that they do and we can just figure out our own ride into town somehow.

Those next couple of hours after leaving the trailhead were nice and easy. The trail continued through pine forest which was occasionally adorned by small wildflowers. The time was passing by quickly and the trail was following along a wide-open prairie filled with cows. We could hear the moo-ing grow closer and closer as we continued on. Eventually the hoard of cows was all around us and I had the feeling that things were about to get interesting. I could see masses of cows all across the trail in front of me. It was like we were in the center of all of them! Then I heard the undeniable sound of a cowboy’s whistle, and I knew what was happening. They were herding all of the cows through this area, and we were smack dab in the center. We were able to continue on a bit further before a cowboy asked us to stop where we were so they could get the rest of the cows past us. Slide and I stood by a group of trees and watched as a few different men and women on horseback, and one dog, herded all of those cows right on by. That was honestly one of the coolest things that I’d ever been in the middle of! It felt like we were back in Montana, certainly not here in Arizona.

The calm before the storm. There were hundreds of cows alongside the trail, but I certainly didn’t expect to encounter and massive group of them all around us.

With the enormous herd of cattle now behind us, we continued on through prairies and scarce pine forests. Those miles flew by and terrain was taking it easy on our feet for once. A few miles after our encounter with all of the cows we stumbled upon a massive group of antelope off in the distance. At first, it only appeared to be about 10-20 of them, but once we startled the group and they began to run off, we noticed just how many there were. It had to have been nearly 50 antelope all running by in the distance. I hadn’t seen anything like that since the CDT and really wasn’t expecting to see antelope out here. I guess that is one of the nice things about attempting a thru hike with minimal research prior to setting out. Everything out here winds up being a delightful surprise!

Tons of cows lining the forest up ahead of us.

Over the next handful of miles, the trail did get a bit rockier which wasn’t ideal. My body and especially my feet were tired from the big mile days back-to-back. I was definitely hoping that the miles would be as easy as they possibly could be. Regardless, we continued on just the same. And sometime around 3PM we passed by what would likely be our last water source for the day. There was a water cache right by the highway, but I was hoping that we wouldn’t be needing that! If we got a ride into town then we wouldn’t have to be cooking dinner tonight. This seemed like as good a place as any to stop off for a quick break and reset. We had been keeping our heads down as much as we could today and were making really great time. But it still wasn’t looking humanly possible for us to make it to the highway before the sun went down. Especially if the terrain was going to be even remotely muddy or rocky, which would just slow us down. There were still about 12 miles standing between us and the highway.

This is what most of our day looked like. I’ve been loving the way the wheatgrass moved in the breeze alongside the trail.

When we set out from there saw that the trail was going to be crossing over a dirt road in about 5-6 miles. It looked like we could hop onto the road and walk it for a few miles toward the end of the day. Maybe that would help us buy some time and be able to make it to the road before dark? That was at least what I was hoping. For those next couple of hours, the two of us were hustling and trying to keep a good pace going. By the time we reached that dirt road crossing it was after 5PM and we still had a way to go. The sun was already starting to go down so we stopped off to put some layers on. I checked my phone and had a message from Wood’s saying that she had made it to the road crossing and managed to get a quick hitch into Strawberry. Strix and Leaf were still behind her somewhere and were also hoping to get a hitch before dark. I figured we might catch them along the way today but hadn’t caught up to them yet. Knowing that Wood’s had gotten a quick hitch definitely gave me hope. But hitch hiking in the dark is its own beast entirely.

It was a relief to hop onto the dirt road and know that those miles would be quick and easy. We walked along it for nearly an hour and did have one ATV and one truck go by us, though they weren’t going in the direction that we were headed. Then the sun finally set and we put our headlamps on and continued on in the dark. At this point hope was dwindling, but I wasn’t ready to give up. I told Slide that we were going to get a magical hitch one way or another. Pretty soon we would be approaching the point where the trail crossed back over the road, and we would continue on the trail for a little less than 2 miles before reaching the highway. And just then, that same truck that had driven past us earlier was now driving back by us the other direction. The man inside rolled down the window and asked us the million-dollar question, “Do you guys need a ride?”. We were 2 miles shy of the highway crossing where we were planning to hitch into town, but those extra miles didn’t have to get hiked tonight! Yes, we did need a ride to town!

The guys were out scouting the area for hunting and were heading all the way back to Payson. In order to get to Payson, you have to drive right through Strawberry, and then through Pine. When we told them where we were headed, they offered to drive us all the way there! Slide and I were stoked. I was trying to remain hopeful, but in my heart I knew how hard it was going to be for us to get a hitch after dark. And this one landed right in our laps. We got into the car and the guys continued down the dirt road until we were out at the highway. I mentioned that we would actually be driving right past the Arizona Trail trailhead and was pointing it out to the guys. They asked if there might be any other hikers there that we knew, and I responded that there definitely might be! If Strix and Leaf hadn’t gotten a ride yet, then they might be standing right there at the trail trying to hitch. When we drove by the guy who was driving couldn’t see anyone standing there but Slide and I saw the gleam of their red lights alongside the road. We told the driver that we saw our friends and he pulled over right away.

Slide and I in the back of the truck bed, riding to town in the dark.

I think the best part about picking up the girls was seeing how surprised they were when the car pulled over and Slide and I got out of the truck. Strix screamed in excitement and we were just laughing, standing there along the road. There was only space for two people inside so we offered for the girls to warm up in the truck, and we could sit in bed. I’ve never ridden in the back of a pickup truck at night before! Once we got in the truck bed, I put my Sambob alpha layers on and my puffy jacket. It was going to be cold back there once the car was moving. But it was a beautiful night, and I didn’t mind it at all. The ride to Strawberry was about 25-30 minutes and we got to watch the stars and the view all from the back of the truck bed. That was pretty awesome.

Those guys drove us all the way to Strawberry and even went down the numerous side roads to get us to the Airbnb where all of the other hikers are staying. Then they dropped the four of us off and we headed inside to see our friends. When I opened the door, everyone gave a little cheer as us remaining stragglers arrived to beat the incoming storm. There on the couch was 9 Lives, Kitty Hawk, Lazarus, PI, Lizard, and Wood’s. We haven’t seen most of this group since the other hiker Airbnb back in Flagstaff. It was so awesome to get there and know that we didn’t have to deal with packing up in the rain tomorrow and trying to get a ride into town. The first thing 9 Lives did was come over and give me a big hug, then showed me to the room that we would be able to stay in for the night. There were two sets of bunk beds each with full sized beds at the bottom. There were going to be 6 of us in that room tonight! But I was just so glad to be sleeping anywhere indoors tonight.

Once we dropped our things we hung out with everyone and joined them in watching a movie. They had made burgers for dinner and offered us some food which was wonderful. Then I took a long, hot shower and we even got things together to do laundry. Their Airbnb was this adorable cabin and apparently there have been a lot of elk in the yard the last couple of days. This group got into town 4 days ago and have been hanging out waiting out the storm. A couple people are dealing with some foot and ankle issues, so it just made more sense for them to all relax and wait out the weather, rather than pushing big miles to get up ahead before the storm. I’m sure glad that they did, because now we are actually almost caught up to this group! When we all get back on trail in a couple of days, Slide and I will still be able 38 miles behind the big group. But we were 150+ miles behind before. That means we definitely stand a chance of catching up to everyone soon. And Strix, Woods, and Leaf all got off at the same road crossing as us. So, we’ll all get back on trail together in a couple days too.

A bunch of us wound up staying up and watching another movie after that first one ended. It was really nice to just sit there on the couch, surrounded by other hikers, and just relax. We’ve been pushing big miles for the past couple of days to get here. I’m looking forward to the rain coming and us having no other choice but to slow down and rest.
