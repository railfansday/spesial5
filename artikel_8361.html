LaCrosse Alphaburly Aero 2000 Boot Review: The Warmest Boot LaCrosse Has Ever Made

Insulated rubber boots are warm and waterproof. But they’re also heavy, hard to walk in, and uncomfortable to wear for a long period of time — or at least they used to be.

Last week I got the chance to field test LaCrosse’s new Alphaburly Aero 2000 insulated rubber boot on a whitetail hunt in Saskatchewan. The program for this kind of hunt is as follows: A cheery Canadian guide drops you off at a ground blind somewhere way back in the bush; you sit there overlooking a small shooting window from dark to dark; you never leave the blind because the biggest buck of your life could show up at any moment.

Temperatures on this hunt ranged from the mid 20s to low 40s, which is chilly but not as cold as we were hoping for (temps in the teens with a good foot of snow really get the bucks moving during daylight up there, or at least so I’ve heard).

This was the perfect testing ground for a rubber boot that’s designed for cold-weather deer hunting, among other duties. Here’s how the new LaCrosse boots held up.

The heart of the Aero line is the ETPU midsole, which borrows its design from the high-performance running world. The midsole returns energy as your foot strikes the ground (or more simply put, the boots are springy). The effect is that the boots are incredibly comfortable to walk in. If you’ve ever walked, say a mile or so in heavy boots, you know that they can wear out your legs and feet quickly. But with the new LaCrosse design, the weight of the boots is offset by the springiness of the midsole.

But your feet won’t stay comfortable long if they’re cold. It’s common for rubber boot wearers to get sweaty feet early in the day, and then freeze later on. To combat this, LaCrosse is using a completely new type of insulation called Solarcore. This is a kind of aerogel, which is woven into the interior of the boot. It’s promoted as a very light, very warm kind of insulation. In fact these boots are up to a pound lighter than other insulated rubber boots in their class. That lighter weight makes them easier to walk in as well. Importantly, Solarcore also repels water, unlike Thinsulate. So if your feet do get sweaty or wet inside the boot, that moisture doesn’t soak into the insulation.

There is a Pro version of the boot, which is all rubber, but I prefer the neoprene. I think it’s more comfortable on those long, all day sits.

There’s also an 800g insulation version of the boot if you don’t anticipate hunting in very cold weather. If you’re going to mostly be hunting in the mid-20s and 30s, I’d say you’d be fine with the 800g boot. If you’re hunting in temps below that, or your feet get cold easily, go with the max insulation.

During any long sit in the cold, your feet and hands are most likely to get cold first. But over the four days that I hunted, essentially sitting still for 10 hours at a time, my feet never got cold. I wore a light wool sock with a heavy wool sock over it. No foot warmers. No heater. I was impressed with how well the Solarcore insulation worked.

After the Saskatchewan hunt I took the boots to my family deer camp in Wisconsin and did a morning of still hunting with them. As advertised, they were extremely comfortable to walk in. I stepped in a stream that was shin deep and stood in it for about 2 minutes (just as a test) and experienced no leaks.

Only time will tell how these boots will hold up over the long term. I think one benefit of the full rubber Pro version is durability. Any sort of neoprene can wear out with hard use. So if you’re planning on hiking in these boots in any sort of rugged terrain, you’d want to go with the Pro version.

These are expensive boots, but they are jam packed with new technology. They are a true upgrade from basic insulated rubber boots that we’ve all worn in the past. If you hunt in cold weather, if you want to walk comfortably in rubber boots, or if your feet get cold easily, the Alphaburly Aero 2000 is a good option to consider.

I’m a Midwesterner who likes winter and I think I could wear these boots in zero degree temps without fear of getting cold. If you really wanted a bulletproof setup, pair these boots with a pair of heated socks and never get cold feet again.
