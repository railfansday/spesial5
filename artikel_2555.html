Most common players on eliminated teams from Week 11 in Yahoo Fantasy Death leagues — plus FAB waiver wire advice

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Every week I'll examine the most common NFL players on the teams eliminated in Yahoo Death Leagues, trying to figure out where the puck is headed. I will also offer some FAB advice ($1,000 cap is the Yahoo default), but please remember this area is highly context-sensitive and manager-specific.

The strategy becomes more and more context-dependent as we meander through the late stages of the season. And it's critical that you audit the FAB budgets remaining for your competitors. It's not unlike poker, where a good player is constantly reevaluating the chip dynamics of the table, who's deep and who's thin. Leverage is everything.

In most leagues the waiver wire will be especially rich every week onward, as the cut teams become stronger and stronger. It's also more plausible to find useful players on the wire for nothing, players who would never be free agents in a casual league. Remember, your manager pool keeps shrinking in size.

You always audit every injury closely, but also pay keen attention to bye weeks. While we don't have any teams on bye in Week 12, we also have the Thanksgiving slate. There's one game on Wednesday, three on Thursday, one on Friday, and then the usual Sunday slate, plus Monday Night Football. Plan accordingly.

Death Leagues are back and better than ever. (Photo by Grant Thomas/Yahoo Sports)

My weekly FAB recommendations will come in three buckets:

Proactive: This means you highly covet this player, perhaps might even need this player. I realize these offers might not win if someone in your league is lighting money on fire, but as described above, that's often a short-sighted mistake in this format.

Reactive: This means you desire the player but the offer has to make sense for your short- or long-term strategy.

Keep Them Honest: This is an offer that is not designed to win, but might surprisingly land a player if your opponents are distracted by other talent available or unexpectedly passive that week. I often compare notes with other experienced Death League players and we're surprised at how often the Keep Them Honest offers actually win (this is also a portable strategy for regular season-long leagues; you never know when strange market behavior will come about).

One last note: Understand that the FAB recommendations are with the assumption that you won't be making offers for every available player.

This late in the season, the FAB recommendations need a major grain of salt. Be sure to extensively audit the budgets your opponents hold, as that info will significantly alter your own offering plans.
