Asha Sharma Issues Statement On Xbox's 25th Anniversary And The Future Of Gaming

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

"Our largest studios will be able to pursue ambitions as far beyond today’s games"

We've been waiting all week for Xbox to acknowledge its 25th anniversary in a formal fashion, and Xbox CEO Asha Sharma has now issued a statement discussing the past, present and future of the brand to celebrate its birthday.

It's a pretty lengthy statement, and a lot of it is just about recapping the history of Xbox and Microsoft Gaming as a whole. Sharma talks about how things have come a long way since "Microsoft Adventure" in 1979, and then how Xbox was "impossible to miss" in 2001 while also revolutionising online play and being there for the launch of Halo.

There's also a cool high-definition ad for the original Xbox console that you can see down below.

"When the original XBOX launched in 2001, it was impossible to miss. It was 2-3x larger than rival consoles, with a giant X molded into a black enclosure and a bright alien green jewel at the center..."

"A year later, Xbox Live revolutionized online play with persistent friend lists and matchmaking, as well as a common service for developers to build connected experiences. And bringing all of this together was Halo: Combat Evolved, a launch title that not only defined XBOX but proved its first advertising campaign: “Games Will Never Be The Same.”

After going through the history, Sharma mentions that each of the 111 people who have worked at Xbox since the beginning will be getting "a special engraved console to commemorate what you’ve helped build".

She then references the fans, Xbox FanFest and how today's FanFest event in Seattle will "bring the celebration home".

"The story of XBOX has never been ours alone. Our fans have challenged us, cheered us on, held us accountable, and stuck with us. We’ve been celebrating this anniversary with fans around the world and special launches. Today, we are bringing the celebration home to Seattle."

And finally, the most notable bit in this statement is probably what she says about the "next 25" years of Xbox, highlighting that the "cost, resources, and risk required" to create modern games has grown dramatically, and that it's had an impact on the ability to turn ideas into games, or at least allow those ideas to reach their potential.

As a result, she says it's something that "must" be changed, and that people and small teams will "soon" be able to achieve things that currently require much larger teams and financial resources in today's world.

"While modern games demonstrate the heights of human craft in ways unimaginable just decades ago, the cost, resources, and risk required to create them have grown just as dramatically. As a result, too many ideas never become games, and too many games fall short of what their creators imagined. For decades, this has been the unavoidable tradeoff of ambition. We must change this."

"Throughout human history, the scale of what we could create was determined by the people, capital, and resources we could command. That relationship is beginning to break. A single person will soon be able to wield capabilities that once required entire nations. A small team will be able to build worlds that today demand hundreds of developers and hundreds of millions of dollars. And our largest studios will be able to pursue ambitions as far beyond today’s games as Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 is from its first entry in 1982."

"I believe the future of gaming will look more like its beginning, when a small team with a big idea could change how the world plays. Our responsibility is to deliver great games, unmistakable hardware, and give more people the chance to make something the world has never seen."

There's some interesting stuff in here for sure, and we reckon it might be worth keeping an eye on tonight's Seattle Xbox FanFest event for any potential announcements - after all, it's the final stop and "home" destination for the FanFest tour.
