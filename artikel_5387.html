Delaware insurance regulator said it was intentionally misled in Mark Walter probe

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The state regulator is investigating alongside federal authorities as prosecutors and the SEC examine loans by Walter's insurers

The Delaware Department of Insurance said it is looking into Mark Walter's network of holdings, and it believes it was "intentionally misled" about lending by his insurers to affiliates under his control, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The regulator's statement said its probe runs in parallel with federal authorities, including the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Loans from Walter's Delaware Life and Clear Spring ended up financing other businesses he controls, and U.S. prosecutors and the SEC are examining them, according to The Wall Street Journal. They are looking at whether anyone defrauded others by hiding the ties between the companies. Delaware regulates both insurers.

The regulator wrote that "the insurance companies themselves, their auditors, and their regulators were intentionally misled," according to The Wall Street Journal. It said the continuing inquiries had prevented it from addressing a "drumbeat of inaccuracies" about claims that Delaware and other states ought to have spotted the affiliated loans.

Insurance analysts, researchers and Apollo CEO Marc Rowan have faulted the department, saying it overlooked or disregarded indications that billions of dollars in loans at the two insurers were directed to affiliated entities, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Federal prosecutors are looking into why Delaware Life and Clear Spring never revealed that over $20 billion of their holdings was channeled to entities funding Walter's other ventures, according to Bloomberg. Loans to affiliates are permitted but must be reported, and the insurers did not report theirs until Justice Department subpoenas arrived.

Investigators have not alleged any misconduct by Walter or his businesses, according to Bloomberg. His holding company, TWG Global, has said "there is no victim here. No one has been harmed, and no one has claimed they were harmed."

A spokesman for Group 1001, which owns Delaware Life and Clear Spring, declined to comment, according to The Wall Street Journal. A spokesman for TWG Global did not respond right away to a phone request for comment.

Walter is chief executive of Guggenheim Partners and the controlling owner of the Los Angeles Dodgers, according to The Wall Street Journal.

TWG Global agreed in August to buy up to $6.5 billion in affiliated assets from Delaware Life in exchange for an equal amount of unaffiliated assets. Clear Spring separately cut related transactions by $90 million. Both insurers had previously conceded that more than $20 billion in loans carried undisclosed affiliated status after federal subpoenas tied to the Justice Department probe.
