Every guy needs the best beard trimmer for his situation. Whether he grows it patchy, keeps it perma-stubbled, or is as lustrous as the A-List beards, a trimmer is mandatory in every beard kit. And not all beard trimmers are alike, which is why the best one for your facial hair style may be different from the next guy’s.

I tested a bunch of beard trimmers to deliver you a real who’s who of men’s grooming devices. Can you use a beard trimmer to cut head hair?

Beard trimmers are not meant to be used as hair clippers, since clippers require much more power to cut through thicker patches of hair. However, some beard trimmers have the muscle to multitask, and they’ll proudly hype that skill—usually by declaring the work for “beard and head” or similar language. We usually verify those claims against product reviews, too. And frankly, we’d probably just opt to buy a dedicated hair trimmer (you can find our roundup of the best hair trimmers here), especially if you’ll be using it often.

Barbers choose tools to suit the job, from adjustable trimmers for controlling length, to detail trimmers for sharper edges. The Wahl Pro Series High Visibility is our professional pick for precision work, while the Philips Norelco Multigroom range offers broader versatility. For home use, start with your beard length and the kind of maintenance you do most often – a tool built for crisp outlines won’t necessarily include the guards you need to trim a longer beard evenly.

How do I maintain and clean a beard trimmer?

Switch it off and disconnect the charger before cleaning, then remove trapped hair from the blades and combs after each use. Follow the instructions for your particular model – a washable cutting head doesn’t mean the whole handle can go under the tap. The Remington Vacuum trimmer, for example, has removable washable parts, but its body should stay out of water.

Let washed components dry before putting them away. Oil the blades if the manufacturer tells you to, but don’t assume every trimmer needs it. Philips’ current Multigroom blades are designed to work without oiling. Replace a damaged or persistently blunt cutting unit with the correct part for your model.

How do I choose the right trimmer if I have sensitive skin or ingrown hairs?

Avoid “zero-gap” trimmers, which is a trimmer that cuts as close to the skin as possible, effectively a 0mm cutting length—those can mess your skin if you're prone to irritation or ingrowns. Otherwise, since you're trimming, you usually won't have to worry about irritating your skin because the blades shouldn't ever actually make contact with your face.

What’s the best way to travel with a beard trimmer—any TSA or voltage concerns?

One of the big concerns for frequent travelers—at least in the USA—is whether they're going to be able to get their beard trimmer through security and not having it buzz during transport. If this is a priority for you, look for a model with a travel lock like the Braun All-in-One Trimmer Series 9 9440, which prevents the trimmer from turning on in transit.

How do I clean a waterproof vs. non-waterproof trimmer?

Waterproof trimmers are a breeze: Just hit ‘em with the (usually included) cleaning brush, then wash them off in the sink before drying and applying some oil. Non-waterproof trimmers just skip the rinse, so you’ll have to take extra care with the brush. You can also use spray cleaners specifically designed for grooming electricals depending on your trimmer's care instructions.

What should I do if my trimmer starts pulling hair or losing power?

First, make sure you've thoroughly cleaned the trimmer. Next, make sure it's fully charged and/or has new batteries. If it's still pulling hair, it's probably time to swap the blades (plenty of companies sell refills on their site, and make sure to check the warranty to see if you qualify for a fresh set). If it's losing power, it might be time to swap the internal battery (check your warranty!) or get a new trimmer altogether. Luckily, we haven't had that issue with any of the trimmers listed above.
