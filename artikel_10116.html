When I was working for a startup in the early 2000s, we had our own servers. We were growing, so were adding servers at a pretty good clip. After one particular expansion, all of a sudden, Saturday night at 3 am, we blew breaker. A week later, the same thing happened. I suggested that it was the RAID check kickoff that might be doing it. And, indeed, it was. A couple of dozen servers all seeking their drives at once was too much for the circuits they were on. Staggering the startup time fixed it.

Breakers are analog devices. They have a trip curve which specifies the amount of current it will take to trip them. This is to handle inrush current as well as perform their duty to protect the wiring behind the walls.

The trip curve is time to trip versus current. And it can be surprisingly long -

Motor starting inrush current is massive compared to the running current, but it happens so briefly that it *usually* doesn't matter.
