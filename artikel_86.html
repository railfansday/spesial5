Tears, celebrations and selfies: US midterms in pictures

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Americans are electing members of Congress, which is made up of the House of Representatives and the Senate. All 435 House seats are up for election. In the Senate, only 35 of 100 seats are being contested because senators serve six-year terms. This results tracker shows both the races being decided now and the 65 seats that are not up for election this year - 34 held by Democrats and 31 by Republicans.

Hugs, cheers and smiles broke out as the Democrats picked up a string of victories in Republican strongholds, delivering them the Senate and the House.

The win gives them control of Congress for the rest of Donald Trump's presidency.

Meanwhile, it was a challenging night for Republican supporters, after a succession of losses in reliably Republican states.

Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms defeated Republican Rick Jackson, a billionaire businessman endorsed by President Trump.

Bottoms becomes the first black woman elected to be governor of Georgia.

The state has not had a Democratic governor in more than 20 years.

Republican Senator Jon Husted shed a tear whilst surrounded by his family after losing his Ohio seat to Democrat Sherrod Brown.

Another Democrat victory was seen in Georgia where Senator Jon Ossoff was re-elected, giving him a second term.

Speculation surrounds Ossoff, considered a potential leading contender to run for the Democrats in the next presidential election in 2028.

James Talarico, the new US senator in Texas, and Gina Hinojosa, the state's new governor, toasted their successes.

In Portland, Maine, supporters reacted to the news that Democratic Senate candidate Troy Jackson won his seat. He beat Republican veteran Susan Collins who has been in the Senate for nearly 30 years.

Susan Collins was pictured looking at election results coming in on a laptop.

The mood was different for Republican supporters at a midterm election night watch party in Des Moines, Iowa. Republican Ashley Hinson lost her Senate seat to Democrat Josh Turek.

Democrat Chris Pappas made history as the first openly gay man elected to the Senate, beating his rival Republican Senator John Sununu.

Pappas celebrated his New Hampshire win on stage with his husband.

Attendees paused for thought after a watch party for Maine Republican Senate candidate Susan Collins, following her defeat by Democrat Troy Jackson.

In North Carolina, the Democrats secured the night's first flip of a Senate seat.

The state's former Democratic governor, Roy Cooper, won against Republican candidate Michael Whatley.

A supporter raised a jubilant fist to celebrate the North Carolina win.

In Michigan, Democrat Abdul El-Sayed will become the first Muslim US senator. He told voters: "You took a chance on the unlikeliest of candidates."

The mood was subdued amongst Republican supporters, after a string of defeats in reliably Republican strongholds.
