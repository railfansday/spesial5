Will a Democratic Blue Wave Cause the Trump Bull Market to Crash? History Weighs in With a Potentially Unpleasant Answer.

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Democrats’ gains may contribute to Wall Street’s pain if the artificial intelligence (AI) narrative shifts.

The voters have spoken. Democrats flipped several seats in the House of Representatives and the Senate, retaking both houses of Congress. By early January, President Donald Trump will be working with a Democrat-led Congress.

Although the Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI -0.38%), S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.36%), and Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +0.83%) have delivered outsize average annual returns under President Trump, when compared to most other presidents since the late 1890s, this "blue wave" may threaten the Trump bull market and heighten the odds of a crash.

President Trump's bull market has a new headwind. Image source: Official White House Photo by Daniel Torok.

While record S&P 500 share buybacks and initial public offering euphoria have helped power the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq higher, arguably nothing has been a bigger catalyst for the Trump bull market than the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure build-out. Empowering software and systems with the tools to make autonomous, split-second decisions is a multitrillion-dollar global opportunity that investors don't want to miss.

Democrats taking control of both chambers of Congress presents a unique challenge for AI stocks.

On the one hand, President Trump believes that AI regulation should be minimal. Less oversight can, in theory, expedite innovation and provide a serious boost to Wall Street and U.S. gross domestic product.

Most Democratic lawmakers don't share President Trump's vision on AI. They'd prefer to limit the pace of data center expansion and ramp up the testing and vetting of large language models/agentic AI solutions.

The Democratic blue wave by itself is unlikely to be enough to push the Trump bull market over the edge. History shows that the S&P 500 has been higher at the six- and 12-month marks following midterm elections since 1950!

Nevertheless, a Democrat-controlled Congress could put the spotlight on AI regulation. Even though President Trump maintains the power to veto AI legislation from Congress (short of a two-thirds vote from both congressional chambers), highlighting deficiencies for a historically pricey stock market's No. 1 catalyst probably wouldn't go over well with investors.

But it's the historical priciness of the Trump bull market that truly complicates things.

The S&P 500's Shiller Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio, also known as the Cyclically Adjusted P/E Ratio (CAPE Ratio), has surpassed 30 for only the sixth time in 156 years. The previous five instances in which it exceeded 30 were eventually followed by downturns in the Dow, S&P 500, and/or Nasdaq Composite ranging from 20% to 89%.

In other words, the stock market has repeatedly shown that premium valuations aren't tolerated over extended time frames. The AI-driven Trump bull market is the second-priciest on record.

Connecting the dots suggests that Democrats putting AI regulation under the microscope may increase the odds of Wall Street's historically pricey stock market tumbling down the proverbial cliff. Mind you, this in no way guarantees a stock market crash will occur.

But based on what history tells us, a less-friendly environment for AI innovation could spell disaster for an expensive stock market.
