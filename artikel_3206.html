As Apple works on AI agent features, banks are already blocking them

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

One of the most dramatic shifts in the AI landscape is the emergence of intelligent agents that can use iPhone and Mac apps in order to carry out tasks on our behalf.

We can already use third-party chatbots in this way, and we know Apple is working hard to turn Siri AI into an agent. However, before the company even gets the chance, banks are already blocking them, and this may be just the start …

The science fiction promise of AI has always been to be able to tell a computer what we want done and have the task carried out for us automatically. That’s starting to turn into a reality with the emergence of AI agents.

We’ve already seen Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, and Muse gain agent capabilities, and Apple is expected to bring these to Siri AI at some point next year.

Earlier this month, Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman said that the company ultimately wants to go in this direction and may add these abilities next year. CEO John Ternus has reportedly been pushing Craig Federighi for faster software development, with specific mention of “moving beyond necessary improvements to Siri,” which seems likely to mean agentic capabilities.

Last week also saw a since-deleted Apple job listing detailing plans for autonomous AI agents.

We’ve already seen some of the ways in which this can go wrong, and we recently outlined three significant risks. Wired reports that banks are now responding to these by either blocking AI agents or warning customers that using them can be dangerous.

JPMorgan Chase changed its terms to ban third-party agents that log in with your credentials. That same day, Bank of America warned its customers not to share login info with agents. Along with the announced change coming from Wells Fargo, Citibank and PNC have also changed their terms to include warnings about agents.

Wells Fargo even goes as far as using capitals in its latest terms, stating that it will not be responsible for the consequences.

Your use of any AI Agent is AT YOUR OWN RISK

We can likely expect similar pushback in sectors like online shopping as companies fear high returns costs and other consequences from mistakes by AI agents.

Personally, I don’t give any AI agents access to any of my banking or other financial services, considering the risks to be too high. I don’t see my position changing any time soon.
