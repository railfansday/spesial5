Everton And EDSA Highlights Accessibility Progress At Hill Dickinson Stadium

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Everton Football Club and Everton Disabled Supporters’ Association (EDSA) have reaffirmed their commitment to working together to improve the matchday experience for disabled supporters, following their latest quarterly meeting at Hill Dickinson Stadium.

The meeting, held on Wednesday 18 November at Royal Liver Building, provided an opportunity to review the progress made since the Club’s move to its new waterfront home, recognise improvements delivered through supporter feedback and identify further opportunities to enhance accessibility.

EDSA representatives welcomed the Club’s continued engagement and the positive changes introduced across accessible parking, ticketing and matchday services, recognising the value of regular dialogue in helping shape practical improvements.

SIGNIFICANT PROGRESS ON ACCESSIBLE PARKING

Among the most notable improvements has been the significant expansion of accessible parking provision at Hill Dickinson Stadium.

Since the beginning of the 2025/26 season, the number of parking spaces available to disabled supporters has increased, placing Everton among the Premier League clubs offering the highest number of accessible parking spaces.

Working closely with EDSA, the Club has also introduced revised traffic management arrangements in the Ten Streets area, helping more Blue Badge holders park closer to the stadium.

The introduction of dedicated parking permits, improved stewarding arrangements and managed access to Nelson Dock has further contributed to a smoother and more organised matchday experience.

EDSA members acknowledged the progress made, with the Club and supporter representatives continuing to work together to address specific traffic management challenges and further improve access.

SUPPORTER FEEDBACK DRIVING TICKETING IMPROVEMENTS

The meeting also highlighted the positive impact of changes to accessibility ticketing, particularly the introduction of an improved resale process for disabled Season Ticket Members.

Originally proposed by EDSA through its discussions with the Club, the process allows accessible seating and wheelchair spaces to be made available to other eligible disabled supporters when Season Ticket Members are unable to attend.

The initiative has provided greater flexibility for supporters, while helping ensure that accessible spaces are utilised wherever possible.

EDSA welcomed the Club’s willingness to involve disabled supporters in developing these processes, recognising the ticket resale initiative as a positive example of how meaningful engagement can translate into practical benefits.

The Club also confirmed that EDSA will continue to be involved in discussions around Season Ticket arrangements for the 2027/28 campaign, with a dedicated meeting planned for January.

CONTINUED INVESTMENT IN MATCHDAY ACCESSIBILITY

Beyond parking and ticketing, a range of additional improvements have been introduced across Hill Dickinson Stadium to enhance comfort, accessibility and independence.

These include the installation of low-level shelving on concourses and cup holders for Personal Assistant seats, alongside the planned introduction of Accessibility Bag Exemption Tags for supporters who need to bring essential medical or accessibility items into the stadium.

A new Access Passport has also been introduced, providing useful stadium information and enabling supporters to share individual accessibility requirements with the Club to help inform the support available to them.

Further measures are being progressed, including the provision of waterproof ponchos for supporters in exposed accessible seating areas during adverse weather.

The quarterly meeting also provided an opportunity for EDSA to raise feedback on the delivery of at-seat food and beverage services, catering options and access arrangements in certain areas of the stadium, including accessibility lift logistics for Club View Members.

The Club has committed to reviewing these matters with the relevant operational teams and catering partner Aramark, ensuring supporter feedback continues to inform improvements.

The continued focus on accessibility comes as Everton prepares to host the launch of the second edition of Level Playing Field’s Accessible Stadia Guide at Hill Dickinson Stadium later this week.

The event will provide another opportunity to highlight the importance of accessibility across football and the role clubs and disabled supporters can play in driving positive change.

Everton and EDSA will continue their regular programme of engagement, with the next quarterly meeting scheduled for February 2027.

Through this ongoing relationship, the Club remains committed to ensuring that feedback from disabled supporters plays a central role in shaping improvements and making Hill Dickinson Stadium a welcoming, accessible and inclusive home for all Evertonians.
