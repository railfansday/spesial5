iOS 27 includes a really clever new feature that aims to solve the awkward middle ground between cellular and Wi-Fi connectivity. Here’s how it works and how you can turn it on.

In a support document, Apple explains that Connectivity Assist uses cellular data to improve your connection when Wi-Fi is slow or unusable. Apple uses examples like your backyard or driveway, as well as “coffee shops, airports, sports venues, and everywhere in between.”

Essentially, the feature detects when your Wi-Fi connection is weak or unusable and intelligently uses cellular data to keep you connected.

One practical example I’ve noticed is when I walk out of my house and get in my car. Oftentimes, my iPhone will cling to my house’s weak Wi-Fi connection, making it hard to start playing Apple Music and queue up directions in Apple Maps.

Connectivity Assist solves that problem. My phone can now intelligently use cellular data to help that transition away from Wi-Fi. It’s one of many really nice quality-of-life improvements in iOS 27.
