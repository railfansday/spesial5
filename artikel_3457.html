Forget James Bond — we've never had to wait this long for a new Michael Bay movie. The filmmaker's most recent film was released in 2022, and while he's circling a return to the Transformers franchise under the new Paramount leadership, there has been no confirmation yet. In his own way, Bay has operated on a one-for-me, one-for-them strategy. For a decade, he balanced one new Transformers movie with what was, for him, a small-scale genre experiment. Interestingly, none of his smaller-scale movies broke out at the box office, although each of them received praise from a section of the audience. Now, his most recent movie is streaming on Netflix in the United States, waiting to be discovered by admirers of the action genre.

The movie featured Eiza González, Jake Gyllenhaal, and Yahya Abdul-Mateen II in the leading roles. González and Gyllenhaal reunited earlier this year on Guy Ritchie's latest action-thriller, In the Grey, which underperformed at the box office with less than $30 million worldwide against a reported budget of $70 million.

Bay's 2022 movie wasn't as expensive to produce and was more successful​​​​​​​ at the box office. It grossed $52 million worldwide against a reported budget of $40 million, emerging as the lowest-grossing movie of Bay's blockbuster-fueled career. That said, it remains his best-reviewed movie on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes since The Rock, which was released in 1996.

We're talking, of course, about Ambulance. Like Mad Max: Fury Road, the movie essentially unfolds like an extended chase scene, with two bank robbers going on the run after taking a paramedic and her patient hostage. The movie holds a 68% score on Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "At top speed and with sirens wailing, Ambulance comes riding to the rescue for audiences facing an emergency shortage of Michael Bay action thrills." The film has certainly developed a cult following at home, given its 87% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. You can check it out on Netflix before December 1.

The Transformers franchise is currently in limbo, with Paramount's latest attempt at a reboot — Transformers: Rise of the Beasts — underperforming​​​​​​​ at the box office. It was previously reported that the studio wants Steven Spielberg to have more creative control over the series going forward, after he served as an executive producer on Bay's movies. However, Bay ignored Spielberg's advice to step away from the franchise after the third installment, so it's unclear if he'll be willing to return. Stay tuned to Collider for more updates.
