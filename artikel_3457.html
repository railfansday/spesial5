10 Classic Rock Songs That Will Make Your Thanksgiving Playlist Unforgettable

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Thanksgiving means a lot of different things to different people, but there are a few universal themes that can't be ignored. There's family, there's gratitude, and there's good food. Some people will have their favorite artists to accompany them during special, but for those who are still looking for inspiration, we got you covered.

It's normal to go through many moods during a long family dinner. If you haven't seen them for a long time, it's bound to be emotional. If you're used to seeing them, chances are there will be a lot of laughter and joking around. And of course, it wouldn't be Thanksgiving without a huge dinner that leaves you panting. The Beatles, John Denver, Led Zeppelin, and many more artists and bands have created the perfect songs for your Thanksgiving playlist, and we have put them together.

We're opening this list with the most famous rock-country anthem, "Take Me Home, Country Roads," also known simply as "Country Roads." Who hasn't belted this beautiful track at the top of their lungs with a huge grin? Written by John Denver, Bill Danoff, and Taffy Nivert, and released on Denver's 1971 album, Poems, Prayers and Promises, it perfectly embodies the spirit of togetherness, gratitude, and belonging.

While it was John Denver who made it popular, the first draft of the song was originally written by Bill Danoff and Taffy Nivert. The songwriters were a couple, and were looking for a home for this song. At first, Danoff explained, they were hesitant to give it to John Denver, since they felt it might not be his style, but Denver not only loved it but helped make it into the great track it is. When asked if they regretted "giving away" their greatest hit, Danoff waved it off.

"Left to our own devices, Taffy and I may have never gotten that record cut," Danoff admitted. "It wasn't a country record. We could've beat up Nashville and nobody would've recorded it. One thing I learned in this business is that things turn out other than you planned them to, no matter what it is. And you can't predict what's going to happen." Besides, they're still credited as songwriters, so they took a big paycheck home anyway.

The title alone would earn this Led Zeppelin song a spot on the Thanksgiving playlist, but the content, despite not being strictly about the holiday, makes "Thank You" sound like it was made for Thanksgiving. It was written by Robert Plant for his first wife, Maureen Wilson. "Kind woman, I give you my all / Kind woman, nothing more," he promises in the song. "And so today, my world it smiles / Your hand in mine, we walk the miles / Thanks to you it will be done / For you to me are the only one."

Plant's relationship with Wilson is deeply romantic yet tragic. The couple were married for 14 years before splitting up, and they went through everything together. They had three children, and their middle child tragically passed away at five years old. Despite splitting up, they remained friends, and while the song might have changed meaning through the years, it never stopped being true.

The ultimate song about loving your homeland, "Sweet Home Alabama" was Lynyrd Skynyrd's response to Neil Young's songs about slavery, like "Alabama" and "Southern Man." The band felt that those songs painted an unfair picture of the modern Southern culture, so they decided to write a rebuttal about their love for their state.

"Big wheels keep on turning / Carry me home to see my kin / Singing songs about the Southland / I miss Alabamy once again and I think it's a sin, yes," the song opens. Young is even name-checked later on in the track. "Well, I heard Mister Young sing about her (southern man) / Well, I heard ol' Neil put her down / Well, I hope Neil Young will remember / A southern man don't need him around, anyhow."

But despite the diss part of the song, the underlying message is perfect for Thanksgiving. Returning to where you belong to spend time with the people you love, and being grateful for the chance to go back. "Sweet home Alabama / Where the skies are so blue / Sweet home Alabama / Lord, I'm coming home to you."

"Harvest Moon," from Neil Young's album by the same name, is one of his most famous and beloved tracks. A song about celebrating life and treasuring the time with your loved ones, "Harvest Moon" is an essential part of any Thanksgiving playlist. "Come a little bit closer / Hear what I have to say / Just like children sleepin' / We could dream this night away," he sings. It's a song about the passage of time and how love prevails, and the full moon is used as a metaphor for tradition and endurance. "Because I'm still in love with you / I want to see you dance again / Because I'm still in love with you / On this harvest moon." Perfect for the most moving moments of a family holiday, as it's guaranteed to make you reflect and appreciate your time with your loved ones more.

Another song about coming home, which is a recurrent topic for the holidays. After all, holidays are, at their core, an excuse to come home. "Homeward Bound" is one of Simon & Garfunkel's most famous songs, and partly that's because of the universal feeling of the lyrics. "I'm sittin' in the railway station / Got a ticket for my destination / On a tour of one-night stands / My suitcase and guitar in hand / And every stop is neatly planned / For a poet and a one-man band," they sing wistfully. In the song, however, the narrator is not taking the train to go home. He just wishes he was. "Homeward bound / I wish I was / Homeward bound / Home where my thought's escapin' / Home where my music's playin' / Home where my love lies waitin' / Silently for me," they sing in the chorus.

The lyrics are about a very real memory. Paul Simon remembers sitting down at a train station in Liverpool when he was just 22. A young artist trying to make his way into the world, away from home, feeling homesick. What's more relatable than that?

A classic folk rock song that is perfect for the whole family to sing along. It's pretty long, has plenty of verses, and a fun, easy chorus that makes it great to sing along to, especially later on in the evening. The most surprising thing about "The Weight" is that it was created almost accidentally. Written by guitarist Robbie Robertson, it was considered a "back-up" song, since Robertson wrote it while trying to overcome writer's block.

“I played it for the other guys, to get this thing started,” Robertson explained. “And everybody was like, ‘Hell, well that sounds pretty good!’ And I said,‘Well, it’ll just be a back-up song in case some other things don’t work out.’” To this day, it's The Band's greatest song.

"Savoy Truffle," from The Beatles' legendary White Album, is an often overlooked George Harrison composition, and it's perfect for the Thanksgiving feast. In fact, Harrison wrote it about the risks of gorging on sweets. He allegedly wrote it for his best friend, Eric Clapton, to warn him about his "chocolate addiction," and it matches the feeling of overeating during the holidays.

"Creme tangerine and Montelimar / A ginger sling with a pineapple heart / Coffee dessert, yes, you know it's good news / But you'll have to have them all pulled out / After the Savoy truffle," Harrison sings, before warning about the side effects in the bridge: "You might not feel it now / But when the pain cuts through / You're going to know and how / The sweat is going to fill your head / When it becomes too much / You'll shout aloud."

A beautiful love song, and often considered the best song by the Beach Boys, "God Only Knows" should start playing towards the moment when the family is going around sharing what they are thankful for. It's a song about appreciation and unconditional love, and it never fails to bring tears to people's eyes. "I may not always love you / But as long as there are stars above you / You never need to doubt it / I'll make you so sure about it / God only knows what I'd be without you," Brian Wilson promises on this timeless anthem.

"Two of Us" is the opening track of the controversial final album by The Beatles, Let It Be. It's a soft, upbeat folksy ballad that Paul McCartney wrote about his adventures with his first wife, Linda McCartney, and it's about exploring and having fun with your partner, and the beauty of always going home with them at the end of the day.

"You and I have memories / Longer than the road that stretches out ahead," McCartney promises in the song. "Two of us wearing raincoats / Standing solo in the sun / You and me chasing paper / Getting nowhere on our way back home."

"Our House" by Crosby, Stills, Nash, & Young is a song about domestic bliss that every young family should absolutely add to their Thanksgiving playlist, especially if it's their first time hosting. This track, written by Graham Nash, is about his love story with another rockstar, Joni Mitchell, and an epiphany he had during an ordinary day watching her pick up flowers in their garden.

"Our house is a very, very, very fine house / With two cats in the yard / Life used to be so hard / Now everything is easy 'cause of you," Nash sings happily. It's a song about the beauty of everyday things, and it's perfect for a day of giving thanks.
