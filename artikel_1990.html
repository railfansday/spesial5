Arsenal hunger is ominous for rivals as they dismantle Man City

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Was it the early goal that should have put Arsenal’s rivals on red alert? No. While Ricardo Calafiori’s intervention was dramatic, it wasn’t that. Was it the seamless introduction of the new signings, then? No, it wasn’t that either, despite the excellence of Bruno Guimarães and Christos Tzolis.

How about the emphatic scoreline – surely that was it? Again, the answer is no. Three unanswered goals against Manchester City will always grab a headline, but scorelines do not always define the nature of a performance or a team’s capabilities.

So what was it that should have those with pretensions of ripping Arsenal’s crown from their grasp shuffling uncomfortably? One word: hunger. The reason this was such an ominous afternoon for the chasing pack is the fact Mikel Arteta’s team played with intensity of a team with everything to prove.

Title wins can sometimes sate ambition. Players don’t always want to climb the same mountain again, not least if reaching the peak was initially so draining, but everywhere you looked on a day when Cardiff was painted red and white was evidence that Arsenal remain ravenous for success.

First Guimarães pounced out of the shadows to mug Jérémy Doku, leaving him in a heap. Then there was Gabriel Magalhães thundering into a befuddled Doku, celebrating in front of City’s fans as if he had scored, before Ben White flung himself in front of the Belgian, denying a clear-cut scoring opportunity.

Ben White to the rescue with an incredible block 🦸📺 Watch live on TNT Sports & HBO Max pic.twitter.com/ZK0cNnFz5N

Another notable incident was Martin Odegaard, not long after he had glossed a fabulous display with a third goal, sprinting 30 yards to spook Gianluigi Donnarumma and make the Italian hurry his clearance. The victory was good, the trophy was nice, but the energy heartened Arteta more than anything.

“They are really good examples,” Arteta said. “The way the team tracks back is unbelievable, especially when, fitness-wise, we aren’t there yet. We had desire and composure on the ball. When you put your soul and your heart into every action, this is what happens. We want to do it again.

“The desire comes from them thinking: ‘I want to live that moment again. I want to have all the moments again and I want them to be even bigger.’ We know what it is going to take. We have never done it [retain the Premier League] at this football club. We know it will demand something special.”

You could flip that and say it will take something special from the chasers. George Graham and Arsène Wenger could never hold on to the biggest prize during their reigns and you must return to Herbert Chapman, in Arsenal’s thriving 1930s, to find the only manager who went back-to-back.

It can feel premature (and can be foolish) to go out on a limb before the first weekend, but there was just something about them that felt different. Often in the past they have been overtly tentative when playing City, but they came out of their corner this time swinging – and they landed.

Enzo Maresca revealed afterwards that he “really didn’t like” how Arsenal had scored their first goal and that was putting it mildly, as there were 10 passes – broken only by the ball bouncing off Elliott Anderson’s shins – before Myles Lewis-Skelly ushered Calafiori into wreak havoc.

You had to go back to 1968, when City played West Brom, to find the last first-minute goal – City’s Bobby Owen was the scorer that day – and how Calafiori enjoyed it, shaking his fist in delight as Arsenal’s noisy fans reminded everyone of their status.

Here was a rude awakening for Anderson, City’s new £116m man. He was ever so slightly off the pace and that allowed Lewis-Skelly to slip a beautiful ball in behind the FA Cup winners’ defence and invite Calafiori to do the rest, which he did with assurance.

When Maresca watches back the video, he will see Abdukodir Khusanov sleeping and Phil Foden failing to press, but that should not detract from the quality of Arsenal’s interchanging – and it should also make whoever is hawking Lewis-Skelly around to clubs to think again. Why would he leave this?

It should not have been the case that a goal so soon should have governed how the game progressed, but that is exactly what happened as Arsenal completely dominated. Whenever they lost possession, they recovered it as a matter of urgency. Without Pep Guardiola on the touchline, City were like Samson after Delilah shaved his hair, devoid of all power.

City, in contrast to their opponents, showed a complete lack of desire and the second goal arrived when Odegaard was allowed time to flip a ball to the back post, where new boy Tzolis was arriving. The Greek headed back into the six-yard area and Kai Havertz did the rest.

Arsenal were criticised – fairly – during their title-winning campaign for being too physical and relying on gamesmanship, but if Havertz and Odegaard can stay fit for a prolonged period, the difference in style will be like night and day.

Throw in Tzolis, who was tricky and dynamic in Cardiff, to the mix and there will be another enhancement. He looks considerably better than Gabriel Martinelli, with more end product, and Bukayo Saka will do more down the right than Noni Madueke, who started here.

Maresca knew, with his team lacking fitness, that it would be difficult to overhaul the deficit once they had gone two goals behind but, really, he was lucky that Arsenal did not administer a genuine hiding which, at one stage, seemed as though they might.

Goal number three arrived just after the interval, again Lewis-Skelly and Tzolis were involved, combining to enable Odegaard to waltz into the area, hoodwinking Donnarumma so he sat down before sliding a contemptuous finish beyond him.

The Community Shield returns to Cardiff’s Principality Stadium for the first time since 2006, and a different dynamic is at play between Arsenal and Manchester City.

When the two clubs played at Wembley in this fixture three years ago, it felt like a more significant occasion for Arsenal as they sought to prove that the gap was closing to Pep Guardiola’s champions.

Now it is Arsenal and Mikel Arteta who have a league title behind them, while Enzo Maresca is the manager hoping to build credibility as City turn the page post-Pep Guardiola.

In truth, these two teams will look very different by the time the season gathers pace in autumn. World Cup exertions mean Elliot Anderson, Erling Haaland, Declan Rice and Bukayo Saka will be minimally involved in this game, while transfer plans remain up in the air for both camps.

City appear bound to lose Rodri, in addition to Bernardo Silva and Tijjani Reijnders in central midfield. Rodri’s sale would expedite a move for Lille’s Ayyoub Bouaddi, and possibly Enzo Fernandez despite Chelsea’s insistence that the deadline to sign him for £120m has passed.

Arsenal are also open for business, hoping to add a right-sided defender to help them cope with William Saliba’s absence. The £75m purchase of Bruno Guimarães means the champions’ midfield options look formidable, but so far it has been another summer without a level-raising forward signing. Christos Tzolis in and Leandro Trossard out looks canny business, but falls into the category of like for like.

Although Arsenal held their nerve to see off City in the title race, they failed to win in three attempts against their rivals, including a limp defeat in the Carabao Cup final at Wembley. Arsenal also failed to win, or score, against Liverpool in the Premier League last season so their record in the big games is an area for improvement.

The victors this afternoon will claim to have laid a marker down, the losers will write the game off as a warm up exercise.
