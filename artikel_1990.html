Sebastien Pocognoli optimistic about Scotland future after heavy defeat as he identifies ‘next step’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Sebastien Pocognoli has insisted Scotland’s bruising 4-0 defeat to Switzerland was not a fair reflection of the contest as their Nations League campaign ended on a sour note in St Gallen.

A second-half collapse against a second-string Swiss side who had already secured promotion to League A condemned the Scots to their heaviest loss since their 5-1 thrashing by Germany at Euro 2024.

The result leaves Pocognoli's men third in Group B1 with seven points from six matches. It also means Scotland will enter the Euro 2028 qualifying draw in Belfast in a couple of weeks in pot three, potentially presenting a more challenging route towards the tournament.

However, the head coach was keen to highlight the positives from his side's performance, particularly their first-half display, arguing that the final scoreline was harsh on his players.

“The score is heavy with the 4-0, but I don't think it's a reflection of the game, if I'm really honest and objective," Pocognoli said. “We had some very good moments in the game, we started the game like we wanted, aggressive away from home. We could decide to be low on the pitch, but we did what we wanted to plan.”

Lewis Ferguson's unfortunate own goal handed Switzerland the advantage, but the visitors responded well and created opportunities through Andy Robertson, Aaron Hickey and John McGinn.

Pocognoli felt his side had done enough to trouble the hosts before the interval, despite failing to convert their promising openings.

"We played a good first half, we created chances, we were dominant in some moments in the game, and we faced Switzerland with different shapes and adapted well from the first minute,” he continued. “We conceded the goal from maybe one of the first chances they had. We also created chances, but we missed it. It means we were not clinical in that moment in the box.”

The second half, however, exposed Scotland's defensive vulnerabilities in alarming fashion. The Swiss scored twice within six minutes of the restart through Winsley Boteli and Alvyn Sanches, effectively ending the contest.

Pocognoli admitted his team’s start to the second period was unacceptable, adding: “We started poorly from the first minute, a poor corner we gave away. Then you feel that, OK, we will get punished. But I think when we made the substitutions, they made an impact, and we should have scored two goals in that period.

“We got punished for the same reason like in the first half. At the end, it's a bad feeling, the score, but I think that the players try to do their best and create moments that we want to see.”

Despite the emphatic nature of the defeat, the Belgian was adamant there were enough encouraging signs that his players were beginning to understand his more progressive style, even if the execution remains inconsistent.

“If we were in confidence, we would not produce the moments we produce in the game," he summarised. “We create good moments on the ball, on pure bravery decisions to play forward through the lines or the pressure. We create chances also for pressing, sometimes we should have to manage better the transition from pressing, but it is all the things we want to see.

“So the next step is to have the quality to just finish this action, and the more we have games together, the more we will try to fix these things. We have a lot of good response from this camp and it's good for the future as well.”
