Tottenham beat Arsenal to exciting transfer coup as new signing confirmed

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Your weekly briefing on Tottenham, featuring team news and expert analysis from Sam Tabuteau

Teenage defender Isaac Allen has confirmed that he has joined Tottenham after leaving rivals West Ham in the summer.

In August, reports claimed that Spurs had beaten Arsenal to Allen’s signature, and the 15-year-old has now announced the move on social media.

The England U15s captain wrote in a post on Instagram: “Happy to be signed with Tottenham.”

Allen, who can operate either at centre-back or in defensive midfield, is regarded as one of the most promising young talents in England and his arrival continues Spurs’ busy youth recruitment strategy.

During last month’s Fan Advisory Board (FAB) meeting, the club confirmed that as part of their commitment to the academy, they had actively recruited players aged 16 and under and invested in the U10 to U17 age groups.

Allen is the latest highly-rated teenage talent to arrive at Hotspur Way, with Cole Ramsey (19), Kwasi Senyah (16), Gabriel Rigg (15) and Jake Maloney (14) all signing in recent months.

Tottenham are understood to be pairing football development with private schooling and strong professional terms once players turn 17, to beat competition from rival clubs.

The change in approach has been brought on by an acknowledgement internally that Spurs have struggled not only to offer academy players a pathway into the first team, but also to maximise their value by selling them on if they don’t believe they have a future at the club.

Tottenham hope to highlight the progression of 18-year-old winger Luca Williams-Barnett, who scored and assisted on his full senior debut in the Premier League against Ipswich last weekend before being handed a new long-term contract, as an example of the pathway into the first team.

Meanwhile, the success of the current Championship leading goalscorer Will Lankshear, who joined Middlesbrough in a deal worth up to £20million in the summer, showcases Spurs’ willingness to find productive permanent solutions for players who can’t be guaranteed regular first-team minutes after coming through the academy.
