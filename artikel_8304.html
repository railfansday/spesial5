A Close Encounter with a Mama Bear & Cub on the Arizona Trail

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

This morning I woke up a little before 6AM and boiled water for coffee. Then I got packed up and we were walking by 6:30AM after the sun had come up. The section of trail that we were passing through this morning was a vast field filled with small white wildflowers. Off in the distance past the tall wheat grass and wildflowers was the massive snow-covered Mount Humphreys. We continued through the valley for some time, eventually making our way toward a small forest of pine trees.

There in the forest we passed by a bear box water cache stocked with gallons of water for hikers and bikers. Then we crossed over a dirt road by a trailhead before getting back into the forest. The trail continued through the thin pine forest for the next couple of hours. The trail itself was nice and soft beneath our feet thanks to the fallen pine needles. And our course was relatively flat and it made for some “easy” hiking.

The views this morning were truly breathtaking. I loved the color of the wheatgrass as the morning light began to shine down on it. Plus, the little white wildflowers that covered the trail matched perfectly with the snow covered mountain in the distance.

About an hour or so after leaving the water cache, we were hiking along a wide dirt road briefly. All of a sudden a group of about 4-5 friendly dogs ran up to greet us. Their owners were doing some hunting, or at least some scouting. The dogs were all adorable and really social. We got to give them all a good pet before continuing on. Then right as we were leaving one of the men mentioned that there was another dog not far behind them. This other dog was a golden retriever and was doing its own thing apparently.

Stopping off at our first water source of the day, a nice stocked up water cache.

Originally, when we were planning this section of trail from the South Rim of the Grand Canyon to Flagstaff, I packed a whole bunch of food and even was able to leave extra food in Gorf’s car. But when we did actually grab our food from her car I thought I had packed way too much and decided to leave a day or two of food behind. Now, on day 5 out of town, I was definitely running incredibly low on food. Even yesterday I really had to ration my snacks and Slide wound up giving me a couple of snacks. Today my rations were even lower, and every time Slide ate something he would give me a little bit. I think I started the morning with around 5 small bars or snacks. And today we have to hike around 35 miles if we want to make it to the trailhead to go into Flagstaff.

Slide walking on the trail ahead of me. The small white flowers adorning the trail combined with snow capped Mount Humphreys off in the distance made for an incredible morning.

It probably would have been much more relaxing to slow down and go into Flagstaff tomorrow morning or afternoon instead. But by this evening I’m going to be ravenous and it will be nice to get into town and be able to load up on food. Plus if we’re going to be charging up cameras and other electronics, it just makes more sense to stay in town overnight. Some of my devices charge fairly quickly, like my headlamp and GoPro. But my battery banks take hours to charge if they are completely out of juice.

In the late morning the trail brought us past a rusty metal sign stating that we had 611 miles remaining along the Arizona Trail. Apparently we’ve traveled 189 miles so far. At least as of this morning. The trail continued to weave through fields of tall wheatgrass and scarce clusters of pine trees. Today is a Saturday and typically the days of the week are meaningless out on the trail. But not a Saturday afternoon near Flagstaff! In my opinion, Flagstaff is to Arizona what Denver is to Colorado. Sometime in the late morning a couple of mountain bikers began going past us. In the end about 20 bikers wound up riding past in a few minutes’ time! I certainly wasn’t expecting that. We would wind up seeing many more over the course of the day as well.

Only 611 miles to go! At least as of this morning.

Well before the afternoon I was absolutely ravenous and starting to get hangry. I’m really not the type of person to typically be majorly bothered by hunger. These last couple of days though I’ve been pretty low on food. Now today I am starting to notice it making me a little bit more agitated. At least that’s how I was feeling by the time we were about 12 miles into the day. So Slide suggested that we sit down for a few minutes to each make something hot to eat. I might not have many snacks but I do have oatmeal and some ramen noodles.

Around 10:30AM Slide and I stopped off to eat hot brunch. I enjoyed some filling oatmeal and a hot chocolate. I was starving and this was just what I needed.

There was a nice spot just off the trail with a large log and a couple rocks. We sat there and boiled water for our late breakfast/early lunch. I had 2 packets of oatmeal and a hot chocolate. By the time we were packing up I felt way better.

Right as we got moving again another mountain hiker went by us. I was so glad that he didn’t catch us while we were cooking and more sprawled out. From there, the trail fluctuated between some pretty wide open views of grasslands with soft blue mountains in the distance, and denser pine forest with fairly frequent downed trees. The mountain bikers were not loving all of the down trees, but we didn’t mind. They were all easy enough to climb up and over. It would certainly be more of a hassle to be lifting a bike up and over a downed tree versus just climbing up and over it.

I’m not sure whether to call this area a prairie, valley, or just grasslands. But it sure is beautiful. A lot of the day was spent hiking through terrain such as this.

After our late breakfast break I had a whole lot more energy for the rest of the morning and afternoon. But by around 3PM my ravenous hunger had returned, and I really didn’t have anything to eat that sounded even remotely appetizing to me. All of my quality snacks were long gone, and more than anything I was just craving something filling. That is what is so amazing about town food. Afterwards you actually feel full! The food that I eat on the trail typically only makes me feel full for a little while. And the amount of snacks that it takes to get full is immense. As we continued along I was getting very excited to make it back to Flagstaff and have something delicious for dinner.

We didn’t investigate the “secret” trail but it was tempting.

The latter part of the day consisted of more hiking through patchy forests of pine trees, rather than wide-open grasslands. It made for very easy hiking and the time passed by quickly. We saw a few more bikers and people out on the trail, but not too many. Then around 4:30PM we came up to a trailhead parking lot with a privy and some cars. There were some nice rocks lining the gravel lot and we enjoyed a quick break there. At this point we had another 2.5 hours of hiking to go, which didn’t seem too bad. And we were just shy of 30 miles into the day already.

Sitting on a nice big rock at a trailhead a couple of hours before dark.

The next section of trail that we passed through was also quite forested, but traded in its pine trees for brightly colored aspens. One particular stretch of trail was bright yellow all throughout. Even the forest floor was covered in nature’s own yellow confetti. The temperature was starting to cool down around this time as the sun began to set. The early evening is typically my favorite time of the day. I love a good sunrise, but more than anything I love watching the sun set on another successful day of hiking. One of the best rewards for a long day on the trail is a perfect, cool evening.

Our previous break had been pretty short and sweet, so we wound up stopping off one last time just before 6PM. The trail passed by a couple of massive boulders just off the trail. So we decided to sit on top of one and watch the sun begin to set. We had to hike for another or so to make it to the upcoming trailhead. From there we could walk right down the road to a gas station. Then get picked up by Gorf to stay at their place for the night.

When we left our last break spot we just put our heads down and kept it moving. The surrounding area was really beautiful and traded in the pine covered trail for some deep red clay trail. Before it got dark we could see a large rock formation looming over us. Then the sun went down and we continued along to the trailhead. But before we could make it there I caught up to Slide who was stopped on the trail looking at something.

Toward the end of the day the trail changed from pine covered dirt to a red clay.

When I caught up to him he pointed at a set of eyes up in a tree which he thought were mountain lions. That was pretty exciting as I’m always eager to see some wildlife. But upon further inspection we noticed two sets of eyes, and one of them was moving down around the base of a tree. Then eventually we could see that the animal at the base of the tree was actually a small bear cub! When it had its hands up on the tree trunk it was pretty undeniable. The momma bear was actually way up in the tree above, squished onto a narrow branch.

Instead of continuing down the trail we got off and walked a little ways away to put distance between us and the bears. At this angle we could see the mom very clearly and she looked so massive up on that tiny tree branch. Then we got out of there and within a handful of minutes we were out at the trailhead. The two of us walked down the road to the gas station and Gorf pulled in just as we got there. Slide and I grabbed some goodies from the gas station and then got in the car to head back to Gorf and Slash’s place. And on our way we picked up pizza for everybody too.

Passing through a tunnel right before the trailhead into Flagstaff.

It was such a nice feeling to be back at the house again. On a thru hike it is so uncommon that you go to the same place more than once, so this is a lovely treat. It was so nice to see Gorf again and get settled into their cozy and adorable home. Slide and I showered, started laundry, and devoured a ton of Hawaiin pizza and pepperoni pizza. Then I got some other chores done such as plugging in my electronics, uploading photos and videos, and cleaning my TOAKS pot and spoon.

By now it was quite late but the night wasn’t over yet. Two other hikers, Woods and Strix, that we know are also staying here at Gorf and Slash’s place tonight! They actually got here first and had everything planned since we originally thought we might arrive tomorrow. So tonight they’re in the guest room and we made a cozy nest on the floor of the spare “yoga room” across the hall. It was perfect. At this point I was exhausted but definitely wanted to get a little bit of editing work done. I made myself stay up for another hour or so before eventually doing my stretching routine and calling it a night. Tomorrow we will have to do our resupply in the morning and then hopefully get back to trail at a fairly reasonable time.
