Vegetable Wallpaper and Animal-Clad Handmade Tiles Add Playful Charm to This Maryland Farmhouse

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

A gut renovation gave a family-owned regenerative farm a modern path forward.

By the time the nine-foot-wide headboard arrived, Angeline Guido Hall and her team at Angeline & Co. had already navigated the unique brand of logistical (and architectural) gymnastics that come with gutting a 1979 home and rebuilding it around a growing family. For five nerve-wracking minutes, the team contemplated whether they could maneuver the enormous custom headboard through a tight vestibule into the primary bedroom, or whether they’d have to remove the windows to get it inside. Fortunately, they puzzled it together, and the bed—large enough to accommodate two parents and their three children—found its rightful place.

It’s an amusing anecdote, but also a telling one: At this Hampstead, Maryland, property, nearly every design decision could be traced back to the wonderfully particular rhythms of the homeowners’ lives. Owners of the regenerative farming business Crescent Moon Farms, they purchased the house overlooking their farm with the intention of putting down roots. The existing residence, however, wasn’t exactly equipped to handle the life they envisioned.

Hall and her team at Angeline & Co., including principal designer Reno Grossi and then-project lead Sarabeth Maher, embarked on an extensive renovation, taking the 6,092-square-foot house down to the studs, expanding its footprint, and reconsidering the floor plan from nearly every angle. “It is technically a remodel, but so much changed that it almost felt like a new build with a lot more constraints,” Hall recalls.

Throughout the renovation, the house’s original character remained part of the conversation. The staircase and two fireplaces survived the gut, while decorative stair brackets spotted during the team’s first walkthrough offered a detail worth preserving. Outside, new stonework, extended porches, and custom shutters punctuated with crescent-moon cutouts nod to the family’s namesake farm.

Inside, a vegetable-patterned wallpaper animates the olive-green butler’s pantry, alongside hand-painted tiles that depict backyard wildlife often seen around the farm. In the reading room, embroidered stars shimmer against grasscloth, accompanied by celestial-shaped ottomans. Antique furniture, unexpected color combinations, and an enviable assortment of collected curiosities give the interiors a personality that couldn't be specified on a floor plan.

Much of that character comes from homeowner Megan, an enthusiastic collector whose art, pottery, and furniture assortment helped shape the interiors. “It isn’t often that we have the opportunity to work with a client who is such a design enthusiast,” Hall says. “She has the most incredible collections of dishes, art, pottery, and antique curiosities she has been curating over the years.”

The finished house is a study in making room for real life, from muddy farm boots and energetic children to an ever-growing collection of beautiful things. Here, even the most extravagant gestures have a practical purpose—including that bed.

A hardworking kitchen doesn’t have to read as utilitarian. Hall and her team expanded the home’s footprint to create a generous central gathering space that functions as a second living room, complete with a fireside seating area, breakfast nook, and adjoining butler’s pantry.

A newly raised 10-foot ceiling accommodates a dramatic brass hood and hand-hewn beams, while corner windows bring in views of the backyard. The original fireplace, now outfitted with a wood-burning stove, ensures there’s always somewhere to settle in while dinner cooks. “This family loves to cook, so they wanted the kitchen to feel inviting and spacious enough for people to want to linger and visit,” Hall adds.

Centered around a rather extraordinary antique—a table sourced at Round Top that once belonged to the Versace mansion—this cozy nook is the ideal spot for morning coffee or a quick lunch. Antique Dutch chairs and a custom banquette upholstered in a cheerful Jasper stripe offer a comfortable spot to perch, while interior windows were added to bring additional light into the kitchen.

The vegetable-patterned Schumacher wallpaper was a fitting choice for the home of regenerative farmers, but the references to the family’s livelihood don’t stop there. Hand-painted tiles feature wildflowers and rabbits inspired by the surrounding property, while olive-green cabinetry and skirted storage lend the room a charming, old-fashioned sensibility.

Two entry points—one into the kitchen and one into the serving area—allow this space to act as a practical throughline. “This room almost acts as a second kitchen, so we wanted it to have as much attention and access to natural light as the kitchen itself,” adds Hall.

Stuffy formality was never on the menu in this dining room; instead, Hall wanted the space to feel “relaxed and informal,” in line with how they typically entertain. The homeowner’s existing table anchors the space, joined by vintage Henning Kjærnulf chairs, which Hall had reupholstered in a practical indoor-outdoor fabric.

An antique hutch sourced from Parson’s Nose Antiques displays her collection of handmade plates, while a vintage rug adds another layer of history. Among the room’s more amusing details? An oil portrait depicting the family’s pet as a naval admiral.

Given permission to design without parameters, Hall and her team made the most of this petite powder room. A constellation-like installation of sculptural mirrors provides an unexpected counterpoint to the room’s traditional skirted marble vanity, while botanical wallpaper and deep green trim add another layer of drama.

Even the toilet became a design talking point: the decorative model from Kohler’s Heritage collection, realized in a soft sage green, was enthusiastically embraced by the homeowner. “It’s not often we get excited about a toilet, but this one got us excited!” says Maher.

“One of the first materials selected for this room was the Cowtan & Tout Galaxy wallpaper that has gold and silver sequined stars embroidered on grasscloth,” says Grossi. “We all fell in love with it, and this space was the perfect place to use it.” Custom moon- and star-shaped ottomans continue the theme, offering a subtle nod to Crescent Moon Farms.

The original fireplace was a keeper, but the rest of the living room was reconsidered with family life in mind. A new bay window makes room for the Christmas tree come December, while plush seating—including a sage mohair sofa and antique spindle chairs—encourages cozy family gatherings.

The defining feature of the primary bedroom isn’t immediately obvious in photographs: The custom bed measures a whopping nine feet wide. Designed to accommodate the entire family for movie nights and morning cuddles, it required careful consideration of the room's eight-foot ceilings.

Elsewhere, antique-inspired details soften the scale, including a custom-painted cabinet. “We went to Round Top to source for this project and were really inspired by the painted bridal cabinets we saw there from the 1800s,” says Maher. “We couldn’t find one that was the right size or color palette, so we worked with a painter to recreate one, and it turned out beautifully!”

For a family juggling three children, several dogs, and a working farm, the mudroom needed to earn every inch of its footprint. Custom cabinetry conceals a dog crate and food storage, while dedicated lockers corral backpacks, jackets, and more. A utility sink doubles as a potting station for the homeowner’s gardening hobby, while playful wallpaper, checkered marble floors, and pretty details ensure that practicality still comes with a healthy dose of personality.

An antique American flag from the homeowner’s collection provided the jumping-off point for this spirited bedroom. The team paired it with Cole & Son’s imaginative Macchine Volanti wallpaper, whose red and blue motifs established the room’s palette. Tailor details, like the deep blue trim, plaid fabric, and leather bed, reinforce the Americana references without making the room feel overly themed.

“We knew we wanted this space to be whimsical and thematic, so when we stumbled upon this grasscloth wallpaper with butterflies on it, we knew it would be the perfect fit,” says Grossi. “We used the wallpaper as an anchor point and then added pops of color throughout, such as the seafoam green backplates on the sconces and pink nightstands, to tie it all together.”

If Hall could choose one calling card for the whole home, it would be this basement bathroom. “Of all the rooms in the house, I think this space really demonstrates how much this client embraces color and pattern,” she adds. “What a dream for a design team! The thing I love most about this space is that there are some design elements in here you would think aren’t supposed to work together, but they just do. That is the magic; the client wanted a whimsical wonderland of a bathroom down here and I think we gave that to her.

“We have designed a lot of playrooms, but this was our first children’s room to design that really answered to another level of play,” says Mahar. “Our client wanted a space that was not only fun and vibrant, but also where the kids could flip, run, and burn off some energy.” The team worked with the contractor to ensure the ceilings were properly blocked to accommodate the monkeybars, and incorporated concealed storage to tuck toys out of sight.
