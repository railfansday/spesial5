SpaceX makes history by catching a Starship rocket returning from orbit

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Starship deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites in orbit before returning to its Texas launch site 22.5 hours after liftoff

SpaceX caught the upper stage of its Starship rocket on Saturday as it returned from orbit to Starbase, Texas, using the arms of its launch tower. It was the first time the company has caught Starship.

Starship lifted off from Starbase on Friday on its 15th flight and returned to the same launch site 22.5 hours later, SpaceX said. Three Raptor engines ignited for a landing flip and slow descent into the tower arms, which closed around the vehicle.

After reaching orbit, Starship deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, the company said. SpaceX made contact with every satellite, and they are beginning to raise their orbits to join the Starlink constellation.

Flight controllers spent more than 20 hours monitoring the vehicle in space before preparing its return, according to SpaceX. Starship relit a single Raptor sea-level engine for a deorbit burn, navigated to the Texas coast and flew a final swoosh maneuver over the Gulf before approaching the tower. Before the burn, the spacecraft was moving faster than 17,000 mph 170 miles above Earth, according to The New York Times.

The Super Heavy booster relit 32 of the planned 33 Raptor engines for its boostback burn, which ended early because of low fuel mass, SpaceX said. The company is investigating the root cause. The booster's guidance system triggered an automated abort and diverted it to an offshore landing location, where it completed a landing burn over the Gulf. Its flight termination system triggered when the burn ended.

SpaceX did not manage to catch the booster, which blew up in the Gulf of Mexico just short of the water's surface, according to CNBC.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman congratulated SpaceX on X $TWTR, according to The New York Times. SpaceX Chief Executive Elon Musk shared footage on X of Starship seen from underneath, taken while the vehicle was examined after landing. Under NASA's Artemis program, Starship is one of two lander designs chosen to carry astronauts down to the moon's surface.

SpaceX has spent the last 10 years flying Falcon 9 boosters back to the ground and launching them again, while the second stages of those rockets are expended on every mission, according to The New York Times.

SpaceX had planned Flight 15 as its first attempt to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage. Musk said on the company's third-quarter earnings call last month, "I'd say it's more likely than not that this succeeds next flight." SpaceX is aiming for Starship to reach full capability around the end of 2027.
