Meta is pausing issuance of non-voting stock while shareholders challenge the plan in court

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The charter creating the new class takes effect Jan. 19 under the filing, and a proposed schedule sets a trial for April

Photo by Laurent Hou/Hans Lucas /AFP via Getty Images

Meta $META Platforms said it will not issue any Class C stock, through a dividend or otherwise, while shareholder lawsuits over its reclassification plan are pending in Delaware. The company made the commitment in a stipulation filed Wednesday in the Delaware Court of Chancery.

The stipulation needs approval from Chancellor Kathaleen St. Jude McCormick. It sets trial for April 19-23, 2027, in Wilmington, and Meta's response to the consolidated complaint is due Nov. 24. Defendants say they do not expect the trial to need all five days.

The court consolidated the shareholder cases on Nov. 6 and named California State Teachers' Retirement System and PFA Pension forsikringsaktieselskab as lead plaintiffs, according to the filing. The 10 lawsuits allege that CEO Mark Zuckerberg and other board members breached their fiduciary duties, according to Bloomberg.

The filing says nothing in the order prevents the amended charter that creates the new class from taking effect Jan. 19, 2027. If a final, non-appealable order declares the charter invalid, Meta must file certificates of correction with the Delaware Secretary of State stating that it has been rescinded. The same step would apply to the certificate of designations for Meta's 6.50% Series A Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock, the filing says.

"In the best interest of our shareholders, we are seeking a final determination as quickly as the court will allow, and we will vigorously defend against these claims," Meta said in a statement, according to Bloomberg.

Meta had sought a fast-tracked trial, while the plaintiffs wanted McCormick to allow more time before the case is heard, according to Bloomberg. Meta has said it needs the new class to fund its AI infrastructure buildout and must act quickly, citing companies' loss of access to the bond market.

Meta's board approved the new non-voting class in October after a special committee made up of independent directors with no stake in the deal unanimously recommended it. The board declared a one-time dividend of one Class C share for each Class A and Class B share, contingent on the charter taking effect. The board will set the record and payment dates by later action, the company said.

Under an agreement with Meta, 20% of the Class C stock Zuckerberg receives would be freely saleable, and the other 80% would carry sale restrictions, according to the company's filing.
