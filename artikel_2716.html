5 takeaways from Bills win over Jets

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Buffalo Bills beat the Jets 31-12 in Week 10 on inside MetLife Stadium, in a game that went from defensive slugfest in the first two quarters to blowout in the second half.

At 8-1, the Bills are out to their best start since 1991.

These are my takeaways from Buffalo's victory.

During the Bills' four-game win streak entering this game, which included dominance after the bye week, it felt natural to narrow focus on Buffalo's entire operation, checking for any component of the team that could improve.

And in wins over the Rams, Raiders, Ravens, and Vikings, Buffalo seemingly lacked efficiency from the pass game in a classic sense. You know, Josh Allen standing in the pocket and completing the football to pass catchers within the intended design of the play. It hadn't happened at the frequency one would expect in games in which the Bills scored 37, 33, 36, and 23 points (against the No. 1 defense in football on the road).

But in the second half against the Jets, the Bills demonstrated their ability to move the ball through the air in a normal, rhythmic way… at all levels of the field. And there was an explosive element that's been mostly missing over the past four outings.

For as good as Allen has been creating this season, and for as reliable as Buffalo has been on the ground, this team needs to be able to win with classic dropback passing too, and in the third and fourth quarters against the Jets, the Bills did just that.

The turf at MetLife Stadium has one of the most notorious reputations in the NFL. And a handful of Bills were dinged with injuries in this game, unsurprisingly.

Here's to hoping the likes of Christian Benford, Dorian Williams, and Dee Alford are OK. In their absence, Buffalo's defensive depth was immediately tested. And it shined.

The Bills defense didn't skip a beat with Kaleb Elarms-Orr, Jordan Hancock, and Maxwell Hairston on the field. But it wasn't just that trio making relief appearances for those sidelined in-game with injury.

Rookie T.J. Parker played the second-most snaps of his NFL career to date (38) and had five pressures with two sacks on 26 pass-rush snaps. Javon Solomon chipped in 12 snaps and registered a pair of pressures in his two pass-rush opportunities.

As we've come to know during the Allen era, depth can be vital in the playoffs, and Buffalo's backups are not only getting a decent amount of run this regular season but they're playing respectable football.

Brown was listed as doubtful going into this game. Ankle injury. He didn't practice Thursday or Friday. Then he told reporters while warming up pre-game he was going to play.

And I don't think anyone would've blamed him if he struggled against surging rookie, No. 2 overall pick David Bailey, on the edge. But that didn't happen.

Per Next Gen Stats, Brown squared off with Bailey 13 times and didn't allow a single pressure of Allen.

He was also the blocker attached to star veteran linebacker DeMario Davis 10 yards downfield on James Cook's 74-yard touchdown run that put the game away in the fourth quarter.

It was such a gutsy performance from Brown, who is still getting better in Year 7 at right tackle. With his height, agility, explosiveness, balance, and power, he's one of the most unique players at his position in football.

And this game was the latest piece of evidence that Brown is incredibly tough too.

So maybe not a takeaway just from this game, but I was reminded of just how tremendous of a football player Cook has become in his 157-yard performance in Week 10 that was capped with the long touchdown sprint.

Cook isn't Bijan Robinson or Derrick Henry. He's not regularly jump-cutting the opponent's entire linebacker group or running it over.

He's incredibly flexible in his lower half with outstanding vision as he works through blocks from his offensive line and one of the most dynamic open-field running backs in the game.

Only Jahmyr Gibbs has more 10-plus yard runs (34) than Cook (27). And Gibbs is the only back in football with more 15-plus yard runs (16) than the Bills feature back (14). Cook is up to 971 yards on the ground this season at 5.2 yards per. He can wear down a defense three or four yards at at time — like vs. Vikings — on 31 carries, or hit explosive plays on the ground like he did against the Jets in this one.

Through the first month of the 2026 campaign, this specific subheading would've been insane. But it's a testament to how things can change for a team — and a position group — throughout a long season in the NFL.

Terrel Bernard is 27 years old. Prime of his career. Dorian Williams is playing for a second contract. Elarms-Orr looks as ready to go in Year 1 as a team can expect from a fourth-round pick at linebacker.

In this contest, that trio had 19 tackles, two tackles for loss, three total pressures, and a sack without any missed tackles. And it aligned with how the those three have played over the past month or so and certainly since the bye week.

And Bernard, Williams, and KEO aren't simply thriving downhill. In this game, they allowed five catches for 30 yards in their coverage area. And that aligns with how well the group has collectively covered this season.
