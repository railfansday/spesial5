How Does Owen Wilson Find His Zen?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Owen Wilson is a spiritual guy—and long has been. That’s why it fits so naturally that he would star in a golf series for Apple TV, Stick, in which his character, Pryce Cahill, is often searching to find his perfect zen. And as Wilson jokes, he’s started calling himself “Johnny Flowstate” on the green as he talks to himself through shots.

“I just believe there’s something more going on that you can tap into,” he tells Esquire editor in chief Michael Sebastian on the latest episode of “What I’ve Learned.” The fifty-five-year-old actor, best known for his roles in The Royal Tenenbaums, Wedding Crashers, and Midnight in Paris, even admits that he found himself reaching into the beyond for advice from his late father, TV executive Robert Wilson, in a moment of pure panic before hosting Saturday Night Live in 2021.

“He was already gone at that point, but the night before I was freaking out like, I don’t know if I can do this. I was so nervous, and I was thinking a lot about my dad,” Wilson says. The two were incredibly close, and he reveals that his father actually worked with him and his brothers, actors Luke Wilson and Andrew Wilson, on several of their films. One of which was Wes Anderson’s Rushmore, in which their father wrote Bill Murray’s now-iconic church address to the students: “Take dead aim on the rich boys."

So, as Wilson found himself full of doubt and fear the night before hosting SNL, he recalls looking up and “literally asking [his father] for help” before falling asleep. “I woke up, just incredibly calm, and I had this conviction of this joke my dad would say that needed to go in my monologue.”

What was the joke? “That he was the charter member of the 3970 club,” Wilson says, “which stood for ‘three sons, nine high schools, seven colleges, and zero degrees.’”

“That wasn’t something he was proud of,” Wilson continues. “That was his way to cope with disappointment, but you could almost say that that joke was worth us having all those struggles in school because that joke has lived longer than another diploma would have.” You can watch the episode above as well as listen on Spotify and Apple Podcasts.

Luckily, it all worked out for the Wilson brothers. Next up, Owen is set to appear in season 2 of Stick on Apple TV and the Meet the Parents legacy sequel, Focker-in-Law with Ben Stiller. He also takes a liking to Esquire’s suggestion of a Christmas movie with another one of his longtime collaborators, Wes Anderson, in the episode.

“It’s one of the biggest relationships of my life,” Wilson says of his friendship with the director. “Had I gotten into one of the colleges that I applied to, I would never have met Wes. And there’s something I always say to my boys—the idea that you can’t do it alone. If there’s something going wrong, I know I can always call and he’ll always be there for me.”
