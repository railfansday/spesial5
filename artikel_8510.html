Share a Nanny With Another Family – Each Pays Less, the Nanny Earns More

Hire one nanny together with another family, and each family usually pays about two-thirds of a nanny’s going rate while the nanny earns a third more. Put the nanny share’s terms in writing first, including how much notice a family gives before leaving, and pay the payroll taxes on your own share.

Care.com’s 2026 Cost of Care Report puts the average cost of a nanny for one child at $870 for a 40-hour week, or $21.75 an hour. At two-thirds of that, each family in a share pays about $580 a week instead of $870, roughly $15,000 less over a year.

The nanny comes out ahead too. Two families paying $14.50 an hour each give the nanny $29 an hour, a third more than the $21.75 one family pays, for watching both families’ children at once.

At about $580 a week, a share still costs more than a day care center, which averages $332 a week in the same report. The extra $248 a week buys care in a home with one other family’s children instead of a center’s classroom.

Look for a family nearby that needs care during the same hours and has a child close in age to yours. Then ask what nannies near you charge one family, and set each family’s share at about two-thirds of that rate.

Each family pays the nanny its share separately and counts as a household employer, so each owes Social Security and Medicare taxes once its pay for the year reaches the IRS threshold.

Put the terms in writing before the first day, starting from the nanny share work agreement that Hand in Hand, a nonprofit network of household employers, publishes. Settle at least these:

A family that quits the share leaves the other paying for a nanny of its own, $21.75 an hour at the national average instead of $14.50, until a new family joins. Agree up front that a family leaving gives at least four weeks’ notice or keeps paying its share for four weeks.
