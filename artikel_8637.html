Your Bill Due Dates Aren’t Fixed – Move Them to Follow Your Paychecks

When your bills come due before the paycheck that covers them, ask to move the due dates. Most card issuers let you pick a new date online, and many lenders and utilities will do the same. Set each bill to land after the payday that covers it, and the late fees and overdrafts stop.

Say you’re paid every other Friday, and rent, the car payment, and a card bill all come due between the 1st and the 5th. One paycheck often has to carry all three, so a pricey week before the 1st can end in an overdraft or a late payment.

Pay that comes every two weeks follows a pattern you can plan around. Any 14 days in a row hold exactly one payday, so one check always lands between the 1st and the 14th and another between the 15th and the 28th.

List every bill with its due date and amount, then split them between two dates. Bills due early in the month draw on the check that landed between the 15th and the 28th of the month before. Bills due around the 17th draw on the one that lands by the 14th, at least three days ahead. Leave rent where your lease puts it and build the early group around it. In the example, the car payment and card bill move to the 17th, and each paycheck now carries its own share. If you’re paid on the 15th and the last day of the month, put the mid-month bills on the 18th, three days after that paycheck.

Start with your cards. They’re the easiest bills to move, and a card payment that reaches 30 days late generally goes on your credit report. Expect a billing cycle or two before the new date kicks in, and keep paying by the old one until a statement shows the change.

Car loans allow fewer moves than cards. Chase lets you change an auto loan’s due date twice over the life of the loan, and Wells Fargo just once. Many utilities run a preferred due date program, and some cap changes at one a year. Pick dates you can keep, then set each bill’s autopay for its new due date.

It’s an afternoon of clicks and calls, so do it this week. Then build a month of cushion in checking. Once it’s there, due dates stop mattering at all.
