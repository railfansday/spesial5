Family Meal Planning on a Budget – Let the Week’s Sales Set the Menu

Before you shop, spend 20 minutes planning the week’s dinners from what’s on sale and what’s already in your kitchen. Pick five dinners that share ingredients, save a night for leftovers, keep a backup meal on hand, and write your grocery list from that plan. You’ll make fewer trips and order less takeout.

Without a plan, you pick dinner in the store, where whatever looks good goes in the cart. Then you pick it again at 5:30 on a busy night, when takeout is easier than turning those groceries into a meal.

A plan saves money because it settles what you’ll buy before you walk in. Start it from the week’s sales, and the cheapest meat and produce set the menu. Start it from the fridge and pantry, and you eat what you already paid for before it spoils.

Say your family orders takeout twice a week at about $40 an order. This week chicken thighs and ground beef are on sale, so the plan includes tacos, sheet-pan chicken, and a double pot of chili, with Thursday for the leftovers. If the leftovers night and the backup meal replace both orders, and the extra groceries for them cost about $20, you save about $60 a week.

To make it stick, keep a two-week rotation of 10 dinners your family already likes, and let the kids pick one night’s dinner from it. A meal planning app can save that rotation and turn each week’s dinners into a grocery list.

Put the 20 minutes on the calendar for the evening before your shopping day, and write nothing on the list until the five dinners are set.
