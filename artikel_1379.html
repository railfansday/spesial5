Sergio Aguero Named 3 Toughest Opponents He Faced in His Career

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Former Manchester City and Barcelona striker Sergio Aguero included an English icon when naming the three toughest opponents he faced during a stellar 18-year career.

In his playing days, the 38-year-old regularly topped the goalscoring charts in the Premier League, La Liga, and Champions League, making it look like natural-born instinct to always get the better of his rivals.

His track record includes having managed 184 goals in just 275 English league appearances. But even the best strikers meet their match from time to time, and he has revealed the three players who got in his way the most.

When asked by Nissan to name the toughest players he has faced, Aguero made no mistake about the fact it wasn't an easy question to answer.

Among the best players he snubbed include Liverpool captain Virgil van Dijk, as well as Manchester United legends Rio Ferdinand and Nemanja Vidic. He said, as per Football365: “Nowadays it’s difficult to choose.”

“The most difficult I’ve faced would be [Sergio] Ramos, another one would be [John] Terry, and I can also name a goalkeeper, [Iker] Casillas.”

Aguero certainly found it easier to name the most exciting talent he has played with – but there may be a few raised eyebrows over the last name on his list of three. “Well, the three players… obviously [Lionel] Messi. And the other, because I play with him, and we get along well and understand each other very well, [David] Silva,” Aguero added.

“The third one who excites me the most, in terms of the effort he puts in from the very moment he gets his feet on the pitch until the end, would be (Javier) Mascherano.”

"Captain, leader, legend" - John Terry proved himself as one of Chelsea's most dedicated servants - amassing 717 total competition appearances for the Blues while also chipping in with 67 goals and 27 assists. In the Premier League, his 41 goals are the record for a defender and nicely contrast his all-or-nothing approach to defending.

He contributed to 214 clean sheets in his Premier League career, more than any other defender, while he was the backbone in a record-breaking Jose Mourinho defence that shipped just 15 goals in the 2003/04 season.

Terry played every minute of his penultimate title-winning campaign in 2014/15, before winning the Trophy in his final season at Stamford Bridge. In that final season at Chelsea in 2017, he became the first-ever player to captain a team to the Premier League title on five occasions. He ended his career with five FA Cups, as well as three League Cups, one Europa League and one Champions League title.

The first player to score an international goal at the new Wembley Stadium, this was one of many England highlights as the stopper made 78 appearances, scoring six goals. He edges it as the best centre-back England have had in the modern era, though many would argue World Cup winner Bobby Moore still sits top all time.
