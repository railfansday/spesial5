Chelsea's Malo Gusto named 'biggest disappointment of the Zidane era'... and Liverpool's £123m man Bradley Barcola 'lacked accuracy and composure' in damning verdict on France's Premier League stars

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Published: 06:01 EST, 17 November 2026 | Updated: 11:31 EST, 17 November 2026

The French media have come down hard on a number of Premier League players in the national team after less than impressive displays under new coach Zinedine Zidane.

Over the break France scraped a 2-1 win against Turkey in the Nations League but only after an unexpected 3-1 loss to Italy at the San Siro.

And among the team's most glaring underperformers – according to some observers in France – were Chelsea's Malo Gusto, Bournemouth's Adrien Truffert and Liverpool's £123million summer signing Bradley Barcola.

French outlet RMC Sport was particularly critical of the pair, with journalist Alexandre Jaquin describing Gusto as 'probably the one who disappointed the most during this second international window of the Zidane era'.

Jaquin added: 'Fielded in Italy despite little playing time with Chelsea, the right-back completely fell into the San Siro furnace.

'Overwhelmed by his opponents' energy and little helped by Michael Olise's lack of defensive defense, the former Lyon player lived a nightmare in Lombardy, notably conceding a penalty. A match quickly erased from his memory.'

RMC was not much kinder to Barcola as well, concluding: 'The Liverpool winger was one of the few to start both matches in November. But in the end, it didn't really take off, neither in Italy nor against Turkey.

'Sometimes on his left flank, sometimes in a false nine, the former PSG supersub was often imprecise in his final moves. Like his failed one-on-one against the Turks, the Lyon native lacked accuracy and composure to make the opposing goal tremble.'

Even newcomer Adrien Truffert was not afforded some sympathy as Jaquin said: 'After a rather satisfying debut in October, the Bournemouth left-back benefited from the trust of Zinedine Zidane, who played him in Italy. But despite his efforts, the Cherries player delivered a very mixed performance.'

This recent criticism adds to growing dissatisfaction in France with the overall state of the national team under Zidane.

Liverpool's £123million man is yet to make a major impact for club or country so far this season

Journalist Walid Acherchour made his feelings known on the After Foot programme, saying: 'Zidane hit a wall of reality with this second international break... I found the attitude of some individuals against Turkey to be very poor.

'In the second half against Italy and in the match against Turkey, how do you manage without the ball when you're being torn apart in midfield? Zidane will have to work on all these questions.'

Acherchour closed by saying bluntly: 'The honeymoon is over.'

After an ultimately disappointing World Cup in which the French were again by Spain in a major semi-final – following their Euro 2024 exit – long-term manager Didier Deschamps left after 14 years in charge.

Legendary midfielder Zidane succeeded him in the summer and has already led France to the top of their Nations League group – but some of his players may have to go much further to win over their critics.
