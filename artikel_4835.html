I’ve tested more bras than I can count as a style writer. And over the years, I’ve learned that comfort is a nonnegotiable—if it digs, pinches, or rides up, it doesn’t deserve a space in my drawer. The thing is, no two boobs are exactly alike (not even your own). One might sit higher, be fuller, or have a different shape than the other. So finding the right bra isn’t about hitting a standard size—it’s about understanding your body. According to Navya Mysore, MD, a primary care physician and Women’s Health advisor, the right bra boosts comfort, improves posture, and eases back pain by evenly distributing breast tissue. If your band rides up, your straps slide, or your underwire doesn’t lie flat, it’s a clear sign something’s off.

And if your usual size suddenly stops working? Totally normal. Bra fitter and undergarment educator Kimmay Caldwell says weight changes, hormone shifts, or your monthly cycle can impact your size. To make it trickier, some brands still use outdated sizing methods (like the “plus four” rule), while others go by your exact underbust. Bottom line: your size can change and vary between clothing brands.

All of that can make bra shopping feel like a bit of a minefield, which is exactly why I tested 20 different styles across fits, fabrics, and support levels to find the ones that are actually worth your money. Comfort is about more than just size—it’s also about ensuring the wire doesn’t create pressure points or cause discomfort throughout the day. Look for bras with flexible, padded, or cushioned underwires, which offer support without digging into your skin. The underwire should sit flat against your ribcage and follow the natural curve of your breasts. If it feels like it’s poking or shifting, it’s not the right fit. If you prefer a wire-free bra, opt for styles with flexible boning, molded cups, and wide, flexible bands to provide support.

The most accurate way to find your size is to get fitted in person. Once you have your size, your new bra should feel snug and secure. The band should be comfortably tight, but not dig into the skin. A good rule of thumb is that you should be able to fit two fingers between the band and your skin, but no more. The cups should also lie flat against your chest.
