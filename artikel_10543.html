Harry Potter YouTuber loses entire channel after Warner Bros copyright strikes Malfoid videos

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A YouTuber known for creating viral Harry Potter fan videos has lost his entire channel after Warner Bros. issued a series of copyright strikes against his Malfoid content.

The Harry Potter franchise has experienced a resurgence in fan-created content thanks to the popularity of Malfoid, an internet trend that reimagines Draco Malfoy as a female character. The trend has spawned fan art, AI-generated animations, and alternate-universe stories featuring characters from the franchise.

However, several creators have reported having their videos removed from YouTube amid preparations for HBO’s upcoming Harry Potter television adaptation. Warner Bros. has now confirmed its involvement in at least one channel’s removal.

In correspondence shared with That Park Place, Warner Bros. Vice President of Anti-Piracy & Brand Protection Ahassim Rashid confirmed in a November 9 email that the copyright notices came directly from the company.

“We confirm that the notices are genuine and sent from WB,” Rashid wrote.

The confirmation follows speculation among fans that individuals impersonating Warner Bros. could have been responsible for the copyright claims.

Holland has since called on the company to establish clear guidelines surrounding noncommercial Harry Potter fan content.

“I’m not fighting Warner Bros. They own this world, and I respect that. What fans and creators are asking for is simple: tell us where the line is,” he said.

“Most of us are spending our own money to make these videos because we love these stories, and right now nobody knows what’s allowed and what isn’t. Clear guidelines would be good for the fans and good for Warner Bros.”

The YouTuber has also launched a Change.org petition asking Warner Bros. to clarify its Wizarding World fan content guidelines, which has already attracted more than 1,000 signatures.

Other creators involved in the Malfoid trend, including Katechon Studio, JA Vazquez, and Galakushi, have reportedly experienced video removals or disruptions to their channels.

While Warner Bros. has confirmed its involvement in the copyright strikes against Next Realm Studios, it remains unclear whether the company was responsible for the other reported takedowns or which specific elements of Holland’s videos prompted the notices.
