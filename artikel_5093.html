JPMorgan, HSBC and StoneX Trade Digital Gold in London as Tokenized Gold Holdings Jump 48%

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

London is testing tokenized gold as Tether Gold and PAX Gold holdings climb 48% to $4.6 billion despite falling gold prices.

JPMorgan, HSBC and StoneX completed the first live wholesale trades of fractional tokenized gold in London last week. The test is meant to keep the world’s largest gold market ahead of Asian rivals. The three firms also used the digital gold as collateral, without moving a single bar out of the vaults.

Bloomberg reported the trades on Tuesday. They ran on a system built by the World Gold Council (WGC), the industry body that led the project. David Tait, the WGC’s chief executive, called it “a historic step forward for the gold market.”

The banks did not say how much gold changed hands. The system is still in testing, and there is no date yet for a wider launch. The timing is awkward for gold itself. The metal trades near $3,626 an ounce, 32% below its January record and 11% lower than a year ago.

What changes for London’s bullion market, and does it matter for crypto’s own gold tokens?

The WGC calls the new format “pooled gold interests.” Each one is a fractional, electronically transferable share in a pool of physical bars held in approved London vaults.

That fills a gap. Most London trades today are in unallocated gold, which is cheap to hold but leaves buyers as creditors if their bank fails. Allocated gold means owning specific bars, which is safer but costly and hard to split. The WGC proposed the pooled model with law firm Linklaters in September 2025 as a third route between the two.

The collateral piece matters most. Banks could pledge gold for loans and margin as easily as they post bonds, without trucks or vault transfers.

London has a lot riding on it. Its over-the-counter market handles more than $180 billion of gold trades a day, and its vaults held $1.4 trillion of gold in August. Hong Kong, working with the Shanghai Gold Exchange, began testing its own gold clearing system in July.

The London pilot is a closed, bank-only system. Crypto has run its own version for years with Tether Gold (XAUT) and PAX Gold (PAXG), and those tokens have been growing fast.

We read the supply of both tokens straight from their Ethereum contracts, where each token equals one troy ounce. We compared that with CoinGecko’s figures from a year ago.

So crypto’s digital gold kept adding ounces even as the gold price fell. Still, the two tokens hold about 0.33% of the value of gold sitting in London’s vaults. The whole tokenized gold category traded about $329 million in the past 24 hours on CoinGecko, against London’s $180 billion a day.

PAX Gold tracks the metal closely. On Binance, PAXG trades at $3,637, below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $3,945, and its 200-day EMA at $4,195.

Last week’s sell-off pushed it to $3,491 on Saturday, its lowest level in 300 days. Sellers led on Binance’s spot market, where the token itself changes hands. Orders that sold at the market price right away outweighed buyers by a net $13.4 million over the past seven days.

The WGC still has to settle governance rules and a launch date. If the big banks adopt digital gold for collateral, the gap between London’s vaults and crypto’s gold tokens could start to close.
