Bitcoin drops to $55K after $670M liquidations – What changes now?

Last week, Bitcoin [BTC] fell to a low of $55,130 on Saturday, the 20th of November. This was lower than $57,800, which the leading crypto registered on the 1st of July.

It led to debate around whether the bear market was truly over, or whether what we saw in August-September was only a pause within the longer-term bearish trend.

Over the past two weeks, Bitcoin liquidations exceeded $600 million on four separate days.

The 11th and the 20th of November, when BTC breached key support levels at $73k and $61.4k, saw the heaviest long liquidations. Also, contextually speaking, these two days witnessed $690 million and $670 million in long liquidations, respectively.

The persistent downward price action was accompanied by relentless selling. Since the 5th of November, every trading day bar one has seen negative spot ETF flows.

Last week’s net outflows alone reached $1.245 billion. This incessant selling would need to ease before Bitcoin could find room to recover.

As things stand, a weekly trading session close below $57,800 is needed to flip the structure bearishly. The previous week’s correction, though deep, might be just a liquidity sweep before a recovery.

Bitcoin holders are under severe strain as prices approach or drop below their cost basis, and Open Interest has plunged dramatically. It was a result of tightening monetary policy and the global equity market downturn.

Over the past year, the BTC Open Interest was down by $4.5 billion.

In July, this figure fell as low as $6.5 billion. It was the largest OI downturn in Bitcoin’s history in dollar terms, observed crypto analyst Darkfost.

There have been eight trading days in 2026 with over $1.5 billion in liquidations across the crypto market. A decline in risk appetite among investors, several liquidation cascades, and the overarching bearish price action for most of the year helped explain the OI drop.

A leverage reset can be healthy for the market.

Alongside the weekly BTC chart, there was hope for a bear market bottom. Even so, it remains risky to buy, as a bear market continuation cannot yet be fully ruled out.
