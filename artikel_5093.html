Wagyu’s $13.5 million token crash exposes another coin with 99.9% held by one wallet

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The account that supplied the seller held 99.97% of Wagyu’s separate Zcash-linked token in Bitquery’s Nov. 20 snapshot.

Wagyu’s Monero-linked token plunged on Hyperliquid on Nov. 20 in a sell-off that generated about $14 million in gross proceeds for the seller, according to blockchain data firm Bitquery. The account that sent XMR1 to that wallet also created Wagyu’s Zcash-linked token, ZEC1, and held 99.97% of its supply at 13:16 UTC that day.

ZEC1 trades separately from XMR1. A transfer from the shared deployer could put a large block of ZEC1 in the hands of a seller, just as the XMR1 transfers did in the Monero-linked market. The Industry Spread reported that a Nov. 21 balance check put the same account’s holdings at about 20.99 million ZEC1. The figure counts wrapped tokens on Hyperliquid. How much native Zcash the bridge could pay out is a separate question.

The same Hyperliquid account sent XMR1 to the seller and held almost all of the separate ZEC1 supply.

The XMR1 bar compares the amount sold with the amount transferred. The ZEC1 bar shows the deployer’s share of total ZEC1 supply.

Bitquery’s November 20 Hyperliquid analysis counted XMR1 sales through 13:00 UTC and measured ZEC1 supply at 13:16 UTC. The XMR1 track represents 5.2 million transferred tokens, filled in proportion to 369,623 sold. The ZEC1 track represents total ZEC1 supply.

The branches show account relationships. The ZEC1 branch measures the wrapped-token balance and does not establish a transfer, native-chain reserves or redemption availability.

Bitquery traced three transfers totaling about 5 million XMR1 from the deployer to the selling wallet between 04:46 and 05:16 UTC. The seller had moved only a fraction of that amount through the order book by Bitquery’s 13:00 UTC cutoff. The tally covered 369,623 XMR1 sold to 420 buying accounts. The token’s spot price fell from about $500 to about $1 in roughly half an hour. The first large sales began at 04:49 UTC, and Bitquery tracked smaller sales as late as 10:28 UTC.

Three-quarters of the proceeds came from buy orders already on Hyperliquid’s order book. The largest buying account paid about $4 million. The buyers’ final losses depend on what they can recover.

CryptoSlate previously reported on a separate trading-key risk on Hyperliquid following the Lighter incident.

Wagyu’s documentation describes how users deposit native Monero or Zcash to receive XMR1 or ZEC1 on Hyperliquid. To redeem, a holder sends the wrapped token through the bridge, which pays out the corresponding native coin.

Wagyu’s homepage and its bridge page displayed a temporary outage notice on Nov. 21. The bridge widget was still checking availability, leaving it unclear whether holders could complete redemptions.

Wagyu operator @PerpetualCow said on Nov. 20 that the XMR1 deployer key had been stored on an old server at a hosting provider that was breached. He said he would personally reimburse people who lost money and relaunch Wagyu, without giving a timetable.

Bitquery found no incoming transfers from other Hyperliquid accounts to the ten largest buying accounts between 06:00 and 13:16 UTC on Nov. 20. That check ended less than half an hour after the operator’s 12:48 UTC post. Completed reimbursements remained unverified.
