UK can’t keep its Apple encryption order secret, tribunal rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Judges found that officials had briefed newspapers about the order while the Home Office refused to confirm it. Much of Apple’s challenge can now be heard in public next year.

The UK government can no longer keep its Apple encryption order secret, a tribunal has ruled. The Home Office had argued that confirming the order would damage national security. The Investigatory Powers Tribunal rejected that claim in a judgment on Thursday.

The order is a technical capability notice under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016. Such notices can require companies to change how their services work so the authorities can get access to data. Until now the Home Office had refused to confirm or deny it had served one on Apple.

“Anyone with even a passing interest in this topic already knows that the respondent has served a technical capability notice on Apple,” the three judges wrote.

The judges said the government had not kept to its own secrecy policy. The Times and the Financial Times had reported that government sources confirmed the order. The tribunal found that those briefings took place, and that ministers, senior officials or special advisers authorised them.

The Home Office said leak investigations had found no source, but it gave the tribunal no details of them. The government had also let Apple brief senior US officials. The judges called that a further clear departure from its policy. They noted that the answer could be found by typing the question into Google’s chatbot.

The ruling does not make the order’s content public. The judges agreed that publishing all the material would damage national security. But that does not mean every detail must stay secret, they said. The tribunal will decide that question later.

The parties expect a full hearing in the first half of 2027. Much of it can now take place in public, the judges said. Apple has five grounds of challenge. The tribunal can decide the first two entirely in public. One asks whether the power can reach interception outside the UK. The other asks whether it can be used to regulate the security a company offers its customers.

The Home Office said the ruling was procedural. Apple has helped police and intelligence agencies for years, and the notice simply requires that it keeps the ability to do so, it said in a statement published by The Register.

“Decisions affecting the safety of the British public must be made by democratically elected governments and independent judges, not in the boardrooms of technology companies,” a government spokesperson said.

Apple filed its second claim in July, after the tribunal dismissed its first. In February 2025 it withdrew Advanced Data Protection for UK users. The feature extends end-to-end encryption to iCloud backups and more. Meta, TikTok and X also challenged UK data demands this autumn, from Ofcom, and reportedly lost.
