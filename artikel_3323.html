‘Much Ado About Nothing’ With Tom Hiddleston & Hayley Atwell Takes Hefty $2.4M; Billy Crystal’s ‘860’ Breaks House Record – Broadway Box Office

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Broadway got an early jump on the usual holiday business boost, with star-driven productions like Much Ado About Nothing, Billy Crystal’s 860 and Other Desert Cities with Julia Louis-Dreyfuss doing excellent box office last week.

In fact, Much Ado, the Jamie Lloyd staging starring Tom Hiddleston and Hayley Atwell, was standing room only at the Winter Garden, grossing a gleeful $2,352,287, while Crystal’s solo show 860 broke the house record for a play at the Imperial with a big $1,720,253 for eight performances (up from the production’s usual seven).

The Imperial’s previous house record holder? Crystal’s 700 Sundays, which grossed $1,403,415 for the holiday week ending December 29, 2013. Watch on Deadline

As for Other Desert Cities, which also stars Ed Harris, Allison Janney, Joe Keery and Lily Rabe, did its usual stellar business, grossing $2,570,589, second only to Hamilton‘s $2,794,516 (Much Ado and 860 were in the #3 and #4 spots, with Harry Potter and the Cursed Child at #5 with $1,670,888).

Ironically, the Broadway newcomers geared specifically to holiday crowds – Blue Man Group: A New Holiday Surprise, It’s A Wonderful Life: A Live Radio Play and the Now You See Me Live illusionist show – were still finding their places in the line-up. In its first five previews, Blue Man, making its Broadway debut after decades as an Off Broadway and touring show, filled only 56% of seats at the Lunt-Fontanne, grossing $189,274 with an average ticket price of $45.82. (Opening night is November 17.)

The Wonderful Life production starring Andrew Rannells, Megan Hilty, Tituss Burgess, Harry Hamlin and Lisa Rinna, filled 85% of seats at the Broadhurst, grossing $448,945 with an average ticket of $56.25.

Now You See Me Live, the magic and illusion show inspired by the film franchise, began previews November 11 at the Hirschfeld, opened November 13 and filled 85% of seats, grossing $639,332 for two previews and six performances. Average ticket was $67.52.

Look for the holiday shows to pick up steam as Thanksgiving and the December holidays draw near.

Other recent arrivals did excellent business:

Still in previews were Inter Allia, filling up the Music Box for its first five shows last week, grossing a hefty $745,573 (opening night is December 1) and Galileo, taking an impressive $745,573 for its first five previews, at 99% of capacity with a $104.51 average ticket (opening night is December 6).

In addition to Hamilton, Inter Alia, Much Ado and Other Desert Cities, the sell-outs last week included A Few Good Men, Oh, Mary! and, in its final week of performances, School Girls; Or, the African Mean Girls Play, which took in $520,733 (revenue from the livestream performances during the final week are not included in the box office tally).

In all, the 39 Broadway productions grossed a total $40,302,815 for the week ending November 15, up about 10% from the previous week and about even with last year at this time. Attendance was 321,358, up 11% over the previous week and 8% year-to-year. Average ticket price for all shows was $125.41.

In the 25th week of the 2026-27 season, Broadway has grossed $803,067,274 season-to-date, down about 8% from last year at this time, with total attendance of 6,539,781 down 3% year-to-year.

All figures courtesy of The Broadway League. For more box office information visit the League’s web.
