8 Apple TV Mystery Shows Where Every Episode Is a Masterpiece

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

There's a whole lot of mystery waiting to be uncovered on Apple TV. The award-winning streaming platform knows how to set the stage, placing mysteries in the unlikeliest situations while experimenting with the genre along the way. From an anthropological comedy to a slick noir thriller, the format can be just as intriguing as the mystery itself.

Starring some of Hollywood's biggest names, these shows know how to keep audiences glued to their screens, whether through a slow burn or by constantly keeping the truth just out of reach. Their performances make the wait worthwhile, too. From an arson case gone wrong to life on a near-isolated island, here are Apple TV mystery shows where every episode is a masterpiece.

A mind-blowing sci-fi mystery and a scathing commentary on late-stage capitalism, Severance is every salaryman's nightmare come true. At Lumon Industries, employees undergo a lobotomy-like procedure that separates their work memories from their personal lives. When grieving employee Mark Scout (Adam Scott) starts noticing the abnormalities beneath the company's corporate facade, he realizes that nobody actually knows what they're working on or why.

Severance takes the idea that employees aren't supposed to question their loyalty to the company and pushes it to extremes. Through its eerie worldbuilding, even the office feels suffocating. Employees are discouraged from interacting with other departments, while corporate pleasantries hide an unsettling level of passive aggression that makes your skin crawl. Even the smallest workplace interactions feel strangely unsettling, turning an otherwise drab and dull environment into a claustrophobic prison of cubicles and computers.

A sleeker, more stylish version of Bosch, the neo-noir series Sugar follows John Sugar (Colin Farrell), a private detective hired by wealthy film producer Jonathan Siegel (James Cromwell) to track down his missing granddaughter, Olivia (Sydney Chandler). Teaming up with her former stepmother, Sugar investigates her disappearance and uncovers secrets surrounding the family. As far as investigative skills go, John has a sharp eye for detail, but just like the Siegels, he has a few secrets of his own.

The mystery itself is compelling, but just like the sweet stuff, Sugar is addictive largely because of Farrell's performance. Private investigators have a reputation for being detached from their cases, yet John is deeply empathetic toward others and treats people with genuine kindness. Add multiple film references, including The Maltese Falcon and Chinatown, and watching Sugar feels like a cinematic experience in itself.

The Afterparty is a Rashomon-style series that changes the way murder mysteries are told. With Tiffany Haddish playing the determined but questionable homicide detective Danner, there's no such thing as a serious bone in this show. Across two seasons, each follows a different murder case. Season 1 centers on the death of a pop star at his own high school reunion after-party, while Season 2 follows a groom who turns up dead on his wedding day.

Each episode essentially retells the day of the murder from the POV of a different suspect, but here's the catch: every retelling experiments with a different genre. It's like whiplash. One minute, you're watching a full-blown musical thanks to Yasper (Ben Schwartz), and the next, you're thrown into an action movie through Brett's (Ike Barinholtz) perspective. It's a clever way of telling the same story throughout an entire season while keeping things fresh and giving every cast member ample time to shine.

Exploring one of America's most infamous assassinations, Manhunt follows Secretary of War Edwin Stanton (Tobias Menzies) as he leads the search for John Wilkes Booth (Anthony Boyle), the man responsible for assassinating President Abraham Lincoln (Hamish Linklater). With the chaos of the Civil War still lingering, the hunt becomes a wild goose chase during the nation's fragile Reconstruction era.

Many know about Lincoln's death, but far less attention is given to what happens afterward. Without the modern technology we have today, tracking down a killer is considerably harder. News travels slowly, and every piece of information requires extensive verification, making the investigation feel like old-school espionage at its most demanding. With a $100,000 reward on his head, Booth does everything he can to stay under the radar, from relying on Confederate sympathizers to evade capture to constantly changing his whereabouts.

A great detective is really just a personality with a method attached, which is why we keep remaking the same four of them. Here is yours, what it says about you, and the case for them being the best in the business.

You let people think you are not a threat and then you simply never go away. Columbo is the only major detective show that tells you who did it in the first ten minutes, because the pleasure was never the puzzle, it was watching a rich man slowly realise he is in trouble.

You are the most interesting person in the room and you know exactly how to use it. Rian Johnson built Blanc as a gentleman detective who is also an outsider everywhere he goes, which lets him be charming at people who would never otherwise talk to him.

Everybody tells you everything, and you have never once asked them to. Jessica Fletcher is a crime novelist from a small town in Maine who is so warm and so obviously trustworthy that suspects confess to her essentially as a social courtesy.

You expect to be lied to and you are almost never disappointed. Chandler wrote Marlowe as a man with a rigid private code in a city that has none, which is why he takes the case, solves it, gets nothing out of it and takes the next one.

Dark Matter follows Jason Dessen (Joel Edgerton), a Chicago physics professor who is perfectly content with his quiet life alongside his wife and teenage son. However, everything takes a bizarre turn when Jason is abducted and wakes up in a version of his life that looks familiar but is clearly not the one he knows. Realizing that there's an alternate version of him lurking somewhere, he sets out to find his way back home.

As Jason encounters increasingly strange versions of reality, Dark Matter evolves into a mystery about his kidnapping and the events that brought him there. Each alternate universe reveals a different path his life could have taken, but these possibilities come with their own consequences. All the scientific theories aside, the series is about the lengths they might go to change how their lives turned out.

MI5 isn't exactly known for giving second chances, but its biggest screw-ups have a place of their own at Slough House. Slow Horses follows this uncanny bunch of disgraced intelligence agents, led by the foul-mouthed and unapologetically brilliant Jackson Lamb (Gary Oldman). Stuck in a rundown office with little respect from their colleagues, the agents are treated more like professional embarrassments than actual spies. But being in a workplace full of rejects doesn't mean they can't get the job done.

In between the sarcasm, bad decisions, and office bickering, these agents have a knack for getting tangled up in Britain's biggest security threats. A seemingly straightforward operation can quickly escalate into international espionage, political conspiracies, and dangerous old secrets. Each season only ups the stakes further, giving this dysfunctional bunch plenty of chances to show MI5 what they're missing.

Set on a picturesque New England island, Widow's Bay follows Mayor Tom Loftis (Matthew Rhys), who is determined to turn the drab island into a chic tourist destination. However, his plans take a bizarre turn when Widow's Bay is plagued by everything from deadly fog to murderous clowns. Tom soon realizes that the island has a much darker history than he ever imagined.

Sleepy towns make the best backdrops for supernatural mysteries. For all its scares, Widow's Bay is surprisingly comedic, largely thanks to the eccentric residents who call the island home. Horror fans will also spot multiple nods to classics like The Witch and The Others. Rather than taking itself too seriously, the series lets its oddball residents bring some much-needed humor to an island where even the mayor has no idea what he's gotten himself into.

A father's worst nightmare becomes a reality in Defending Jacob. Public defender Andrew Barber (Chris Evans) is called to court when his teenage son Jacob (Jaeden Martell) is accused of murdering a classmate. As the investigation uncovers more and more evidence that threatens to tear the family apart, Andrew finds himself questioning just how well he knows the boy he's raised and whether he's a capable father at all.

Evans steps away from his usual action-hero image for a much more emotionally demanding role. Andrew is caught between his instincts as a lawyer and his unconditional love as a father, making it increasingly difficult to separate suspicion from denial. Much like Adolescence, the series presents a hauntingly realistic possibility that parents might not know everything about their children.
