Rosalind Eleazar Got the Role of a Lifetime

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Every item on this page was chosen by an ELLE editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

In the new film Misty Green, the Slow Horses actress shows just what she’s capable of.

As soon as the credits rolled on the Toronto International Film Festival premiere of Misty Green, the word was out: The film’s star, Rosalind Eleazar, had delivered a career-defining performance. Her portrait of a struggling Hollywood actress in the heartbreaking drama, written and directed by Chris Rock, was a showstopper, critics wrote. Overnight, it seemed like a foregone conclusion that she would be in contention for an Oscar. Misty fights, does drugs, and lies, but also sings beautifully, looks out for her mentally ill brother, and loves her best friend unreservedly. She’s damaged by her upbringing and the cruelty and racism of her industry, which has made her strong and funny, but also vulnerable and fragile, which she masks with a tough exterior. The performer playing her needed to be able to embody all the sides of a very complicated character; it would be an incredible challenge for even the most seasoned actress. For Eleazar, it was her first leading role in a feature film.

Prior to starring in Misty Green, Eleazar, who has had an accomplished career in theater in her native U.K., was little known internationally outside of her role on Slow Horses. She gives a beautiful performance on the acclaimed Apple TV series, but it’s the kind of ensemble part unlikely to springboard a British actress into a starring role in a Hollywood film. When her agent sent her the script for Misty Green, Eleazar recognized immediately that it was unlike anything she’d read before. “Chris really wrote her fearlessly, and he wasn’t frightened to show the uglier side,” she says. “He and I were keen not to sanitize her and make her soft and palatable. I was pretty bowled over with what he wrote and how unapologetically he wrote it.” Misty speaks her mind to a pathological degree and has a hard time turning down cocaine. The film follows a crazed seven days in which the actress, facing eviction, gets a chance at her dream role. On the other side is an offer to make the money she needs in an unthinkable way, which hangs over her head unsettlingly. “The character just came off the page to me,” Eleazar adds.

She auditioned for the part via a string of Zoom auditions from London. After one was plagued by an iffy Wi-Fi connection and booming heavy metal music nearby, she was certain she’d lost her chance. Rock disagreed. When she was later asked to do a chemistry read, she bought her own ticket to Los Angeles, sure that reading for the part in person was her best bet. She waited weeks before getting the call from Rock that the part was hers, and she was on a plane back to California within a week.

Rock has said he was dazzled by Eleazar’s understanding of the role, casting her over bigger-name stars. She says he trusted her and gave her leeway to shape the part. “He has this expression, ‘My movie, your character,’” Eleazar says. “He allowed me to really explore Misty, because she’s unique and wild and untethered. In order for me to do that, I needed to also feel untethered and without boundaries.”

Misty’s wildness is in contrast to the more restrained character she plays on Slow Horses. As Louisa, an intelligence officer deeply impacted by the loss of her colleague and love interest, Eleazar paints a vivid picture of prolonged grief. Slow Horses, which premiered in 2022, has steadily gained popularity over its six seasons, offering Eleazar consistency at a pivotal time in her career. “Going off doing other jobs and coming back is interesting, because you see your development of a character over six years,” she says. “It feels like family, like your home.”

The rapturous response to Eleazar’s performance in Misty Green will undoubtedly transform her career, but she doesn’t yet know what impact it will have on the roles she is offered. “I do hope that it will give me more choice,” she says. “But our job is not like a lawyer, where you start as a junior associate, and you can see your trajectory to partner. It’s just not like that. It depends on what’s around. It depends on where you are in your career. It depends on your age.”

Whatever comes next, this year has brought incredible joy to Eleazar’s life. The day after Misty Green premiered, she was given a TIFF Tribute Performer Award alongside A-list stars. “I can’t even put into words how special a moment it was,” she says of the premiere. “Then, the next day, to be recognized for my work in Misty, with Helen Mirren and Penélope Cruz, was the icing on the cake. Seeing my mom’s face when I was making the speech was a reminder of the things that are important to you.”

Early on, your CV is mainly the plays you’ve done in drama school. At one of my first TV auditions, they said something like, “You seem to have done a lot of theater, and you haven’t done loads of TV.” I was like, “Well, if you give me this part, it’ll be a start.” And I actually got it.

Tilda Swinton has been really sweet to me. I was really nervous doing The Personal History of David Copperfield, and she just sensed it. She subtly squeezed my hand. She knew that’s all she needed to do, rather than making a big deal. That was enough to be like, “It’s okay.”

I would really like to hang out with Misty and analyze her. But she doesn’t seem to eat much.

If I gave myself advice when I was 15, I wouldn’t have listened to it. I needed to mess up on this, this, this, and this in order for me to understand stuff. Just be a sponge and let it unfold.

I would love to see more female lead roles. You often look on IMDb and see the synopsis of potential projects coming up, and 80 percent of them will go, “A man who is…” It’s still baffling to me how many male-led films are being made in comparison to those with women.
