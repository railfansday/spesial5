Kevin Phillips names the 3 EFL clubs he's most worried about right now

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Although the EFL Championship season is only 15 games old, former striker Kevin Phillips has outlined his current worries for three sides in the division at this moment in time.

The second tier of English football is renowned for pulling up plenty of surprises throughout almost every 46-game season, and no doubt many more are lying in wait in the coming months.

At present, the top end of the Championship is extremely congested, with only eight points separating the 12 sides occupying places in the divison's top-half, with one of Phillips' many former sides, Birmingham City, currently leading the way on goal difference.

However, some are currently at risk of cutting themselves adrift when it comes to an unwanted relegation to League One, and it is fair to say that the one-time experienced forward, who is now a regular fixture on our television screens, has his concerns over multiple clubs in said situation.

Phillips, 53, enjoyed an esteemed playing career in both the Premier League and Championship, which included promotions from the division with all of Birmingham, Crystal Palace, Leicester City, Sunderland and West Bromwich Albion.

Netting 134 of his career goals at second tier level, the former striker knows exactly what it takes to be successful in the division both collectively and individually, as well as seeing the other side of the game at first-hand through an array of coaching and managerial roles.

Speaking to Football League World via Best Betting Sites, Phillips explained his worries over three clubs, two of which he has affiliations with in the form of Watford and Derby County.

"Well, there are a few, to be fair. A couple of my old teams I used to play for, and one I coached at," he said. "The team I'm most worried about, and the team where they shouldn't be, they should be a lot higher and doing better things, is Watford. I speak about them quite a lot.

"I never know what to expect from them on and off the pitch, but history should suggest that they should be doing a lot better than what they're doing. So I fear for my old club. They've been in and around the relegation zone, avoided relegation, and I really fear for them this season," Phillips added.

"They might slip through. I really hope they don't, and I hope they find a bit of form, but I fear for them.

Ranking the Championship clubs based on how successful they've been since the start of 2001/02

Phillips then discussed the situation at Burnley, with the Clarets in danger of becoming the third club in as many seasons to suffer successive relegations from the Premier League to League One.

"And of course Burnley as well, from where they've been to where they are now. The season they're having for such a big club, in the Premier League not so long ago, to be where they are now, having only won one game in the Championship," the former striker continued.

"It's been really disappointing, so I'm worried for them. And I think Derby as well. I hope they pick up form. So two of my old clubs are in there, but Watford and Burnley for sure."

Phillips broke through the Watford ranks, meaning it is no real surprise he has concerns over the Hornets at present given his attachment to the club.

Alessio Dionisi was drafted in as the 24th head coach of Gino Pozzo's ownership in June on a two-year contract, but after making an encouraging start 2-1 victory against high-flying Southampton on the opening day, it was downhill from there for the Italian.

Winning three games since and given the ownership consortium's reputation for firing-and-hiring saw intense speculation over another sacking come to the fore last month, and after Saturday's 4-0 loss to Sheffield United at Bramall Lane, Dionisi was sacked after just 18 matches in charge.

Many would also agree with Phillips' concerns over Burnley and Derby right now, with the two sides making up the bottom three alongside Preston North End after 16 games.

The Clarets have only defeated Watford so far this term, and in recent weeks, the vulnerability of the squad at Turf Moor has been laid bare for all to see.
