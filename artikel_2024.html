Dave Challinor drops Sheffield Wednesday transfer hint - Owls are missing something

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Dave Challinor has issued a clear transfer message after just three games as Sheffield Wednesday's new head coach, with the January window now just over one month away from opening.

The Owls' winning start to League One life under the former Stockport County boss was halted by a goalless draw against last season's play-off semi-finalists, Stevenage, at the SPORTA Arena on Tuesday.

Wednesday are currently fourth in the table, having taken 27 points from their first 16 encounters, just five less than Challinor's former employers who top the standings.

The upcoming window is just the second of the Arise Capital era at Hillsborough, and the first in which the 51-year-old will be reunited with former Stockport chief, Simon Wilson, who is now the Steel City side's sporting director.

However, it feels rather apparent that Challinor has already dropped a transfer hint to the hierarchy.

Wednesday's squad have certainly shared their 27 goals so far this season around, with Jamal Lowe currently topping the individual charts with seven.

When all fit and available, Challinor has plenty of attacking options at his disposal, including Ali Al-Hamadi, Harry Gray, Mason Burstow, Davis Keillor-Dunn and Louie Barry.

Yet, whilst Keillor-Dunn has impressed in recent weeks with his own four-goal haul, the Owls boss has admitted that the club lacks a natural centre-forward ahead of the January window.

“We definitely are,” he stated. “With Ali being out, he's (Keillor-Dunn) probably the closest. Harry, I think, will develop into a nine. But as an 18-year-old playing in League One, the physical part of that tour is going to be tough for him.

“They are hard to come by because a number nine, you're looking for the complete centre-forward, aren't you? Somebody that can hold the ball up when you go up to them, is an aerial threat, still wants to be able to run behind, gets themselves in the box when a cross is coming in," he added.

"Difficult to find if I was looking for them. I hope that we get the answers from what we've already got in the building, but we've got time to time to judge that.”

Despite the attraction Wednesday have in League One in terms of stature, realising such transfer wishes could be difficult for the South Yorkshire side to realise.

Keillor-Dunn's short-term loan from Wrexham is a prime example, as, irrespective of the 28-year-old's quality in the division, only an agreement until January could be struck due to budgeting restraints, with the club still working under a strict business plan from the EFL having exited administration in the summer.

An unwanted return to the Racecourse Ground from the Owls' perspective could free up much-needed funds, but with the January window notoriously hard for doing business in, there are no guarantees that Challinor can solve such a conundrum with players from elsewhere.

The potential sale of Huddersfield’s Joe Taylor could really shake up the League One promotion race.

There is a consensus amongst Wednesdayites that more quality is needed in attack, despite the blend of experience and energy which is at Challinor's disposal.

Five goals within his first two league encounters against Plymouth Argyle and Barnsley demonstrates exactly what this squad is capable of, but despite having six attempts on target against Stevenage, none of Keillor-Dunn, Barry, the returning Al-Hamadi and Jamal Lowe could find target.
