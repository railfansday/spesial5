The Biggest Snubs & Surprises at the 2026 CMA Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The pundits didn't guess every category correctly.

Everyone expected Ella Langley to sweep the 2026 CMA Awards, and sure enough, she did, setting a new, all-time record of six awards in one night. That broke the old record of five awards in one night first set by Johnny Cash in 1969 and then tied by Vince Gill in 1993, Alan Jackson in 2002 and Lainey Wilson in 2023.

But that doesn’t mean there were no snubs or surprises at the awards show, which was held at Bridgestone Arena in Nashville on Wednesday (Nov. 18) and hosted by Wilson for the third year in a row (second year in a row as solo host).

The show opened with a tribute to Dolly Parton, who died in August, with Vince Gill and Wilson singing the country icon’s 1974 classic “I Will Always Love You.” There was also a tribute to John Anderson, who died in October, with Dierks Bentley and Zach Top singing his 1983 hit “Swingin.’”

Perhaps because it was the show’s 60th anniversary, there were several backward-looking performances. Taylor Swift included a bit of her 2007 hit “Our Song” (her first No. 1 hit on Billboard’s Hot Country Songs chart) in her performance of her current hit “I Knew It, I Knew You.” Tim McGraw performed his 1994 hit “Don’t Take the Girl” (his first No. 1 on Hot Country Songs). Tucker Wetmore performed Hank Williams Jr.’s 1984 hit “All My Rowdy Friends Are Coming Over Tonight.” Brooks & Dunn performed a hit medley.

But let’s get back to what you came for: Here are snubs and surprises from the 2026 CMA Awards.

Musgraves went 0-5 on the night. She had more nominations without a win than anyone else this year. Musgraves hasn’t won a CMA Award since 2019. But she and Lambert teamed for a winning performance on their song “Horses and Divorces,” about two people who managed to mend a fractured friendship.

Most pundits figured Tucker Wetmore would win best new artist of the year. Wetmore had won new male artist of the year at the ACM Awards six months ago. But in a major surprise the award went to Stephen Wilson Jr. (who, at 47, set a new record as the oldest winner in the history of the category).

Both Wilson and Wetmore were nominated for new artist of the year last year as well, losing to Zach Top. (The CMA gives artists two shots at the award in this category.)

We all knew voters would be “Choosin’ Texas” every chance they could, but Ella Langley’s album of the year win for Dandelion was less assured. She beat two past winners in that category – Kacey Musgraves, who was nominated for Middle of Nowhere, and Luke Combs, nominated for The Way I Am. Musgraves won in 2018 for Golden Hour; Combs won twice, in 2020 for What You See Is What You Get and in 2022 for Growin’ Up.

The other nominees in the stacked category were Zach Top’s Ain’t in It for My Health and an eponymous album by Parker McCollum.

You couldn’t call it a big surprise when Chris Stapleton won male vocalist of the year. After all, he had won in the category eight times previously. But Stapleton didn’t win the award last year, and Billboard’s country experts Melinda Newman and Jessica Nicholson called it for Zach Top and Cody Johnson, respectively.

But Stapleton took the award, becoming the first winner in the history of the category to have three separate stints as a winner. He won every year from 2015-18, then lost to Luke Combs in 2019 and 2020, before winning again from 2021-24. He lost to Johnson last year but came back to win again this year.
