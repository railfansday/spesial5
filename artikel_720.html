Delta Sets New Business Class Offerings With Collectible Missoni Kits, Wagyu Burgers and Blanton’s Bourbon on Board

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The airline is heralding its new dining and hospitality offerings as the "next era of Delta One."

Missoni pillows, a dark chocolate square and a pour of Blanton’s single-barrel bourbon isn’t just reserved for hotel turn-down service anymore — it’s also the new standard for Delta travelers flying in the airline’s Delta One cabin.

The U.S. airline unveiled its so-called “next era” of Delta One last month, with food and hospitality offerings that aim to position Delta as the “industry’s most premium end-to-end travel experience,” according to a release.

The new offerings include a reimagined Delta One menu, with a wagyu brioche burger among the highlights, along with a Hong Kong-style sea bass, Japanese grilled salmon and a “seasonally rotating” risotto dish, all served on new Delta-branded tableware. The airline is also touting its “fresh cracked pepper service,” with flight attendants coming by with a pepper grinder similar to top restaurants and steakhouses. Dessert now includes the passenger-favorite ice cream cart, with the added option of a fruit sorbet paired with a splash of Taittinger Champagne. And the wines and spirits list has been fully updated with sommelier-approved bottles, including a Silver Oak Cabernet Sauvignon from Napa, which often retails for upwards of $200 in stores.

Travelers can now also request Blanton’s Single Barrel Bourbon on board, with the beloved Kentucky straight bourbon whiskey served in mini versions of its iconic decanter-style glass bottles with horse and jockey stopper. New Delta One flights to and from Tokyo-Haneda airport, meantime, will have a sake program designed by renowned sake brewer Dassai 45 from Japan’s Yamaguchi Prefecture. The menu on the U.S. to Japan flights will also feature regional cuisine designed in collaboration with Michelin-starred chef, Norio Ueno.

The most visible upgrades begin as Delta One customers arrive to their seats. While Delta’s next-generation Delta One suites will start rolling out next year, with longer lie-flat seats, larger TV screens and even a glasses hook, the current suites feature new amenity kits from Missoni along with a mini Missoni pillow and compression socks from Bombas. That’s in addition to the existing Missoni bedding and an over-the-seat mattress pad which adds about a quarter-inch of extra cushioning.

The amenity kits have already drawn comparisons to KLM’s keepsake Delft Blue ceramic houses as “in the know” collectibles for frequent fliers. Delta says it’s working with Missoni to release different prints each season, following the vein of the Italian fashion house’s runway collections each year (the Missoni-print pillow is not a take-home accessory unfortunately).

Delta says the new enhancements were “informed by two decades of customer insights” and part of the airline’s larger $1 billion investment into upgrading its fleet.

Stephanie Laster, Delta’s Managing Director of Onboard Experience, likens it to a “hotel and restaurant [experience] in the sky,” noting that a number of hours were spent perfecting even the tiniest details, like “getting the hot towel right with the temperature and moisture and consistency,” to selecting the “right whipped cream at altitude for our ice cream sundaes.”

The Delta One Wagyu burger, meantime, came from the success of the Delta One Burger in Delta One Lounges across the U.S., and from customers craving some “comfort food” in the air. And the Champagne Sorbet offers a lighter, dairy-free addition to the dessert cart. As for the Blanton’s offering: a Delta rep says whiskey is Delta’s top-consumed spirit category, and after doing customer research on various brands, Blanton’s was rated “most-premium” of all the bourbons surveyed.

Laster says non-drinkers will also have increased options, including new mocktail creations. And with flyers valuing flexibility more and more, Delta has rolled out a “Lighter Later” option, with the ability to pre-select meals that fit your diet (I.e. gluten-free dishes or protein-added salads) and even skip a full breakfast in favor of a to-go coffee and croissant, just before landing (all the better to get some extra shut eye).

The new overhaul has launched on transoceanic Delta One routes departing from Los Angeles International Airport, and will expand across the full global network in 2027. As for what passengers in premium economy and economy can expect? Laster says the goal is to provide a “premium for all” experience when flying Delta, with the airline hoping to eventually unveil upgrades to the rear cabins as well.

“Luxury is about having the experience designed around you — today’s premium traveler values choice as much as quality,” says Kristen Manion Taylor, SVP – Brand Experience, in a release. “These enhancements offer greater flexibility, ensuring every Delta One customer can enjoy the journey in the way that suits them best.”
