Skift's capital tracker for early November 2026 shows travel's largest companies putting new money into two very different places: consolidation and shareholder returns. The headline transaction is Lufthansa Group's signed agreement with the Portuguese government to buy an initial 44.9% stake in TAP Air Portugal for €1.025 billion (about $1.19 billion), with the holding able to rise to 49.9% and the total to roughly €1.2 billion if employee-reserved shares are not taken up. Portugal keeps majority ownership, and closing requires regulatory approval. Everything else logged in the window is balance-sheet work rather than expansion: Marriott disclosed roughly $4 billion returned to shareholders through October 28 while expanding its revolving credit facility from $4.5 billion to $5 billion and issuing $1.25 billion in senior notes; Southwest Airlines closed a $1.25 billion two-tranche bond offering ahead of a November maturity; Ryanair authorized a new €800 million buyback to begin in February 2027 after completing a €750 million program in September; and aftermarket parts distributor AvAir closed $170 million in revolving credit facilities with a six-bank group to fund inventory and acquisitions. The pattern across the tracker is capital flowing to M&A, refinancing, and returns rather than to demand generation or distribution.
