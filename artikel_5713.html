Market reforms bolster grid, may lower costs this winter: FERC

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Improved coordination in the West will help offset lower hydropower output due to historically low water levels in the Colorado River basin, while New England risks overreliance on oil, FERC said.

Electric market reforms in the West and Northeast may bolster grid reliability and, in some cases, lower costs this winter, according to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s annual winter market and reliability report, released Thursday.

In the West, the California Independent System Operator launched the Extended Day Ahead Market and the Southwest Power Pool expanded into the Western Interconnection earlier this year.

“EDAM and SPP’s expansion should provide ratepayers in much of the West with production cost savings and improved reliability in winter 2026-2027, particularly during cold weather events,” FERC staff said in the report.

This winter, the markets’ improved coordination of generation and reduced need for reserve power supplies will help offset lower power output from the Glen Canyon and Hoover dams due to historically low water levels in the Colorado River basin, staff said.

“On a day-to-day basis, these conditions can create congestion and affect system operators’ ability to respond to voltage support challenges, event response mitigation needs, or any additional grid stressors,” staff said in the report.

About 45% of the continental United States and 95% of the Intermountain West region is in a drought, according to the report.

In the Northeast, ISO New England lowered its capacity performance pay rate, which should lower electricity prices during emergency conditions or capacity performance events this winter, staff said.

Also, the New York Independent System Operator changed its installed capacity market rules to reflect uncertainty about how much power Hydro-Québec will deliver over the 1,250-MW Champlain Hudson Power Express transmission line in the winter, they noted.

The U.S. subsidiary of the Canadian utility Hydro-Québec — the primary reservation holder — has an obligation to serve native load in its Canadian territory, which is a winterpeaking system.

This obligation, along with ongoing reduced runoff to its hydroelectric resources, “has left little or no surplus capacity to export to NYISO in recent years’ peak winter months,” staff said.

The Northeast — especially New England — also faces risks around its reliance on oil-fired power in the winter, according to the report.

On Jan. 25, for example, oil-fired generation reached 7,864 MW in a single hour, supplying about 40% of ISO-NE’s total net generation in that period, staff noted.

However, U.S. and regional distillate fuel supplies are low, according to the report. New England’s supplies were 46% below the prior five-year average as of early October, staff said.

FERC expects natural gas prices will be lower this winter compared to last winter across most of the U.S., in part because of higher storage inventories in some areas and forecasts for warmer weather caused by El Niño, according to the report.

As of Nov. 10, Henry Hub gas prices — a key U.S. benchmark — were trading at $3.19 per million British thermal units for the upcoming winter, 42% below last winter’s settled price of $5.52/MMBtu, staff said.

FERC staff expects power plant gas demand to increase by 0.8 billion cubic feet per day, or about 2%, to 35.7 Bcf/d on average this winter compared to last winter. Power burn is expected to account for 25% of total gas demand this winter, according to the report.
