Beaks & Geeks: Cardinals, Rams Winners & Losers: Arizona taken to the woodshed

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Arizona Cardinals needed a win. They already defeated the Los Angeles Rams earlier this year with six Chad Ryland field goals. Can they defeat this divisional rival again?

This one is completely different from the first contest. For one, instead of 10-year veteran Jacoby Brissett under center, rookie Carson Beck will be starting his very NFL game. Is he ready? Prior to Week 1, the answer from Beck was a resounding yes. His coaching staff agrees.

For another thing. All-World EDGE rusher Myles Garrett made his Rams debut. Yikes.

The Rams came into this game 5-4-0 while Arizona is 3-6-0. Both clubs need a win badly. When the contest finished, Arizona was taken to the woodshed by Los Angeles to the tune of 34-6.

What is odd is that in two games against the Rams this year, kicker Ryland has scored every point.

So, who played well for the Cardinals? Who didn't?

First quarter offense – Things sure did begin well. Two drives: two scores. But like what mostly this offense has done all year, the points ended up as two field goals instead of touchdowns. The first drive was eight plays highlighted by a 10-yard completion to TE Trey McBride followed by a 21-yard gain by WR Simi Fehoko. But a third-and-two at LA's 24 became a sack instead and a three-point lead. On the Cardinals next possession, it was also unicorns and lollipops that began on their own 35 with a huge 31-yard gain by WR Devin Duvernay's and short runs by RB Tyler Allgeier to the Rams' 26. Then a false start, coupled with an incomplete pass made it fourth-and-10, and out trotted Chad Ryland as Arizona led 6-3 going into the second quarter. Unfortunately for the Cardinals, there were three more quarters to play.

LB Corey Simon – Holding a three-point lead, the Rams gave it up on their next possession. What a great play by Simon on the pick as Los Angeles had driven to the 24-yard line. The play was intended for WR Konata Mumpfield, but Simon made an acrobatic catch and the defense averted a costly score. He nailed TE Colby Parkinson after just a one-yard gain in the second quarter. Six total tackles.

Kickers – When your kicker and punter are in the running for offensive MVP, you know the offense is a complete disaster. K Ryland nailed both field goals, each from 49 yards. P Blake Gillikin had a great game, booting three kicks for a 50.0 average per punt, with one downed inside the 20 with a long of 56 yards. One of the league's best punters. And with that, we must give a big shout out to long snapper Casey Kreiter who consistently passes the ball exactly where the punter likes it, and a swift spiral right to the holder.

S Andrew Wingard – The talented safety came off an injury and was everywhere. On a third-and-10 in the first quarter, Wingard stopped RB Blake Corum for a nine-yard gain as he broke free to the third level. On the 22-yard completion to WR C.J. Daniels late in the first quarter, Wingard left his man and raced all the way over to the opposite side to assist in the tackle. Led all defenders with 11 total tackles.

LB Mack Wilson – Wilson was also an active tackler with so many runners coming to the second level. Had 10 tackles with one tackle for loss and one pass defense.

Offensive line – Well, did anyone in the Cardinals fanbase expect anything different than what happened along the offensive line? Pretty much jailbreak all game. Poor QB Carson Beck. The rookie never had a chance all game.

The Rams had five sacks, while the rushing attack had a grand total of 34 yards. Geez. Can't run and no time to throw. Back in the origins of American Football, if the offense didn't get anywhere, oftentimes they would punt on second or third down and try to work on field position. Our next poll: Should the Cardinals punt on third downs?

QB Carson Beck – The best thing to happen to the rookie in his maiden NFL start, was that at no time was the cart summoned. Beck was flushed from the pocket for most of the contest, and he had no run game to depend on. Which meant after a while, the defense no longer thought about runners and was all out for the pass. Miserable day, and a miserable game. The second pick wasn't his fault. Went 11 for 18 for 108 yards, a 6.0 average per pass completion, zero TDs, two picks, sacked five times (which could have been double digits), and a QB rating of 38.4. Welcome to the NFL.

Defense three quarters – The Rams were stymied in the first quarter being held to a field goal. Then the first drive in the second quarter was the Simon pick. But the wheels fell off from there. The Cardinals could not stop the run, and were burned repeatedly with accurate passing from QB Matthew Stafford who completed passes for 31 yards, 22, 18, 27, 10, 22 (TD), 44, 10, and 19 (TD).

Offense final three quarters – When it was apparent that the Cardinals could not run the ball, and the offensive line wasn't going to keep their pass rushers out for long, the Rams began stacking the box with seven and eight defenders. Final numbers for this offense: 142 total yards, 6 first downs, 0 for 6 third down efficiency, 0 for 1 on fourth down, 3.6 yards per play average, 34 rushing yards, allowed five sacks, zero Red Zone appearances, two turnovers, 3.9 yards per pass completion average, and time of possession: 17:37. Yes, it was that bad. Can you imagine how tired the Arizona defense was today? Cardinals' drives beginning with Quarter 2: punt, punt, INT, half, INT, downs, punt.

CB Kalen King – On the Rams first possession, King allowed WR T Atwell to gain 31-yards to the Cardinals' nine which eventually set up a field goal. King was way off Atwell and then missed the tackle before scoring the TD. In the second quarter on a second-and-10, King lost WR Xavier smith who rambled 22-yards to Arizona's 46.

With 9:37 left before halftime, King played off Smith who gained 18-yards. After the Cardinals were intercepted, the Rams were back in business at Arizona's 32. On their third play, King was way off Smith and then missed the tackle before scoring the TD with just 13 seconds left in the half.

DT L.J. Collier – Had the sack in the second quarter, but the Rams ran up the gut too many times to count. Three tackles with one sack, one tackle for loss, and one QB hit.

WR Devin Duvernay – He had the 31-yard gain in the first quarter, but with 37 seconds to play in the first half, Beck rolled out and found an open Duvernay who tipped the ball up instead of securing the rock, and Rams' S Kamren Kinchens not only got the interception, but Los Angeles scored another touchdown with just ticks left in the first half. But yet Beck got the negative stat.
