Notre Dame Football Stats: Week 10 Review of Win over Miami (FL)

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Notre Dame football did the thing. Week 10 has been marked on all of our calendars since the 2026-27 preseason and the Irish showed up in the way we hoped with a 31-24 win over the Miami (FL) Hurricanes.

The game's first quarter went 3-0, in favor of Notre Dame and the team extended their lead by going 14-3 in the second quarter. I watched the halftime coverage riding on a pretty good high but was brought back down to Earth for some of the most white knuckle college football I've had the fortune/misfortune to watch in a long time. Miami (FL) came out of the locker room and responded with a 14-7 third quarter in their favor. Notre Dame maintained a seven-point lead heading into the final stretch and the teams tied 7-7 in the fourth quarter.

The Irish are averaging 9.8 points in first quarters, 13.0 points in second quarters, 7.4 points in third quarters and 9.0 points in fourth quarters.

The +7 overall point differential represented the slimmest victory margin for Notre Dame through Week 10. The runner-up was the +11, 37-26 over North Carolina in Week 5. The team is averaging 39 points scored so far. Their 31 points against Miami (FL) is the third lowest total, following the 27 points scored against Michigan State and 28 points scored in the BYU loss.

The Irish offense was +35 in the overall yards category, recording 412 yards of total offense compared with the Hurricane's 377 yards.

ND's offense is averaging 439 yards of total offense through Week 10. This game was just on the lower end of the spectrum, with the 426-yard game last week against Navy representing the median.

In a game defined by quarterback play, Notre Dame had a +43 differential in the passing yards category. They put up 319 passing yards and are averaging 263 passing yards per game at this point in the season. It was the second most productive passing yard game for the Irish, only following the 360-yard performance against Rice in Week 2.

Rushing production was the only major yardage category where Notre Dame had a deficit. They were -8, recording 93 rushing yards compared with Miami (FL)'s 101 rushing yards. The team is averaging 175 rushing yards per game and this was their lowest total produced through Week 10. The next weakest outing, in terms of production, was the 111-yard outing against Wisconsin in Week 1.

CJ Carr had one of the most impressive statistical performances of his career in the win over #1 Miami (FL). It was his highest passing yard production game of the 2026-27 season. His 319-yard outing is ranked #3 in his career log up to this point, only following the 342-yard performance against NC State and the 354-yard performance against Arkansas in 2025-26. Prior to this season, Carr hadn't made more than 40 pass attempts in a single game. He crossed that line for the first time against BYU, when he completed 27 of his 49 attempts for a 55.1% completion rate. This week he completed 29 of his 40 attempts for a significantly improved 72.5% completion rate. He threw three touchdown passes and zero interceptions.

It was a very modest night for Irish rushers, with the bulk of the utilization centered around Nolan James Jr. and Jonaz Walton.

James finished the game with 16 carries for 52 yards. He averaged 3.3 yards per carry and was responsible for the team's sole touchdown earned on the ground. James is averaging 63.4 rushing yards per game through Week 10.

Walton walked away from the win with ten rush attempts for 37 yards and 3.7 yards earned per attempt. He is averaging 64.2 rushing yards per game through Week 10.

A lot of ball catchers contributed to the Irish's big win over the Hurricanes.

Wide receiver Micah Gilbert led the group, recording 98 yards on nine catches. Gilbert was also responsible for two of the team's three receiving touchdowns. He now has 33 receptions for 528 yards and five touchdowns through Week 10.

Wide Receiver Jordan Faison had six receptions for 77 yards and claimed one touchdown catch. He now has 44 catches for 574 yards and five touchdowns through Week 10.

Notre Dame running backs had a collective 76 receiving yards in Week 10.

Nolan James Jr. led in production, with one catch for 36 yards. He now has 14 catches for 141 yards and three touchdowns through Week 10.

Jonaz Walton had two catches for 26 yards. He has eight receptions for 96 yards through Week 10.

Aneyas Williams closed things out with two receptions for 14 yards. He now has 11 catches for 123 yards and one touchdown through Week 10.

The Hurricanes were the nation's #1 offense heading into the game and even without star Malachi Toney, the Irish defense faced their most formidable test against Miami (FL).

The defense held them to 377 total yards, the highest total by an opponent so far. The runner-up was the 360 yards of offense produced by BYU in Week 7. Opposing teams are averaging 251 yards of total offense against Notre Dame through Week 10. Miami (FL) is #1 in passing production amongst the opponents the Irish have faced up to this point. They recorded 276 yards through the air and opponents are averaging 164 passing yards per game through Week 10. The Hurricanes are #3 in rushing production amongst the opponents Notre Dame have faced. They tallied up 101 yards on the ground and opponents are averaging 87 rushing yards per game through Week 10.

We saw the defense return back to form in terms of impact plays, closing out the win with two sacks, five tackles for loss and seven pass deflections. And depth is the key takeaway, as HC Marcus Freeman noted during his halftime comments.

Junior linebacker Kyngstonn Viliamu-Asa had one sack and two tackles for loss.

Senior defensive lineman Boubacar Traore recorded 0.5 sacks and 1.5 tackles for loss.

Junior defensive lineman Bryce Young finished the game with 0.5 sacks and 0.5 tackles for loss.

Junior cornerback Leaonard Moore had one tackle for loss.

Senior cornerback Christian Gay recorded two pass deflections.

Senior safety Luke Talich had one pass deflection and also came up with the pivotal third quarter interception.

Junior defensive lineman Tionne Gray had one pass deflection.

Sophomore cornerback Jayden Sanders tallied up two pass deflections.

Senior linebacker Jaylen Sneed finished the game with one pass deflection.

Notre Dame's offense had eleven full drives and converted five of them into points.

The stats don't really do this game justice.

They are accurate in showing the areas where the team excelled against Miami (FL) and highlighting some very promising trends about how the team and program are both developing.

It's clear that CJ Carr is coming into his own as the leader of the offense and Marcus Freemen era defenses continue to deliver as units in incredible ways. But the way in which the players stuck it out from the first to the last whistle is what the real story is behind this week's win.

I spent most of the game having a group therapy session with two of my close friends from the undergrad days roaming around campus and South Bend. We all graduated in 2013 and have experienced more than our fair share of Irish-football induced trauma as well as some super highs. And the way Freeman and Company showed up last night definitely puts this one at the top of the euphoria list for us.
