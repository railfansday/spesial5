32 things we learned in NFL Week 11: 49ers leave no doubt as league's elite teams emerge

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The 32 things we learned from Week 11 of the 2026 NFL season:

1. While Sunday night's matchup between the San Francisco 49ers and Minnesota Vikings pitted the league's No. 1 offense against its stingiest (in terms of points allowed) and, arguably, most ferocious defense, the Niners left no doubt who's currently atop the NFL mountain with their resounding, blitz-beating, 35-10 victory in Mexico City. A lot can happen between now and Valentine's Day, but the side effects of the Santa Clara substation might be the only thing standing in San Fran's path to a third Super Bowl appearance under brilliant HC Kyle Shanahan.

1a. Minnesota hadn't allowed more than 23 points in a game this season ... domestically anyway. But this is the third time the Vikings haven't scored more than 10.

1b. Fernando Mendoza, the No. 1 pick of the 2026 NFL Draft, is going to be experiencing a lot more firsts in the coming weeks now that he's elevated to QB1 of the Las Vegas Raiders. Sunday brought his first start against an AFC West rival, first start on the road, first regular-season TD pass – first three actually – and first INT, a fatal pick-six against the Denver Broncos. But make no mistake, Mendoza continues to show flashes, kept his team in a game against an opponent likely to be in the AFC's Super Bowl mix and – most importantly – survived an early baptism by fire against the Seattle Seahawks and Broncos, who feature two of the league's most formidable defenses.

2. The number of TD passes reeled in Sunday by Kansas City Chiefs TE Travis Kelce, nearly four years to the day since he last scored multiple times in a regular-season game. K.C. boat raced the Arizona Cardinals 34-7 on Mr. Swift's big day.

3. Number of sacks registered by DT Jeffery Simmons in the Chicago Bears' 21-13 defeat of the New Orleans Saints on Sunday. That's five sacks in three games since Simmons came to Chicago in what could still prove to be the biggest deal of the season, Kirk Cousins included.

4. The number of TD catches San Francisco All-Pro TE George Kittle has in two Mexico City appearances in his career, including two in Sunday night's bashing of the Vikings.

4a. Also the number of starting quarterbacks the Cardinals and Baltimore Ravens have used this season.

5. Jacoby Brissett is the common denominator between those teams, dealt from Arizona to Baltimore before this year's trade deadline. However, he lost his second start in six days with the Ravens on Sunday … despite giving them every opportunity to win during a wild game in Charlotte, which the Carolina Panthers won 35-33.

6. The number of NFL teams that were off this week, aka "bye-mageddon" in fantasy circles – the Atlanta Falcons, Cleveland Browns, Green Bay Packers, Los Angeles Rams, New England Patriots and Seahawks all idle in the league's lightest scheduling week of the season.

7. The number of touchdowns the Dallas Cowboys scored in their 51-30 rout of the Tennessee Titans.

8. The number of possessions Dallas had Sunday, one kneel-down by QB Dak Prescott notwithstanding, Brandon Aubrey booting a field goal on the one legit drive when the Cowboys didn't find the end zone.

9. The number of international games the NFL staged this season, an all-time record, the final game abroad in 2026 played Sunday night in Mexico City's Estadio Banorte between the Vikings and 49ers.

9a. The Niners improved to 5-1 in international games − 2-0 this season − and their .833 winning percentage tops among teams with at least five appearances abroad.

10. The number of international games the NFL could play annually going forward, at least under the terms of the current collective bargaining agreement, which runs through the 2030 season. Said another way, expect 10 international games in 2027.

11. Back to the Cowboys, who won by three TDs … but still surrendered 30 points and 346 yards with DC Christian Parker not calling the plays. Both figures represent the second-best outputs of the season for the Titans, who entered Sunday with the league's second-worst offense. Also, add two-hand touch issues to Dallas' endemic tackling issues.

12. The number of consecutive regular-season games the Pittsburgh Steelers have lost in Philadelphia. They last beat their intra-state rival Eagles on the road in 1965, roughly 15 months prior to the first Super Bowl.

13. But you're bound to lose any time 42-year-old QB Aaron Rodgers is your leading rusher … with 29 yards.

14. The number of times Cardinals TE Trey McBride was targeted in Sunday's defeat at Kansas City. (He caught nine of them but only for 54 yards.)

15. The number of NFL teams new Arizona QB1 Josh Johnson has been a member of – he didn't play for all of them – but any veteran as wise as this 40-year-old knows feeding McBride is the best course of action when you join a new team with an All-Pro pass catcher.

15a. The number of TD passes Johnson has thrown in his 14-year career (though it's worth noting he was actually drafted in 2008, fifth round by the Tampa Bay Buccaneers).

16. Who says 347-pound Bucs NT Vita Vea doesn't have abs?

17. The jersey number, at least most recently, for QB Daniel Jones, fired by the Indianapolis Colts after they were smoked 26-3 by the Houston Texans on Thursday night. And while Jones' next NFL employer is already a subject of debate, is a guy only a year removed from an Achilles tear and fresh off football shellshock in Indy really the answer for anyone in 2026?

18. The number of starts Cousins could potentially make this season given his trade from Vegas to Minnesota may confer him an extra game rather than a bye.

19. The number of points Philadelphia scored in their 19-16 defeat of the Steelers, the second most the Eagles have posted since QB Jalen Hurts was lost for the season after Week 5.

20. Congrats to Philly for breaking a seven-game slide and also to third-year QB Joe Milton, who notched his first regular-season victory.

21. As for the Steelers? At least their loss to the Eagles wasn't a direct result of WR Makai Lemon (1 catch for 3 yards), whom Philadelphia EVP/GM Howie Roseman scooped from Pittsburgh with the 20th overall selection of this year's draft – one spot before Steelers GM Omar Khan planned to take him.

22. However, Pittsburgh might need another wideout for its Black Friday game against the Broncos if coach Mike McCarthy opts to discipline DK Metcalf, who was ejected in the third quarter of the Steelers' defeat at Lincoln Financial Field. Didn't matter much from a performance standpoint given Metcalf had one reception for 2 yards on Sunday.

23. The New York Jets (3-8) suffered a second straight devastating win at SoFi Stadium … in terms of their pending draft position. They beat the Rams there in 2020, despite entering the game with an 0-13 record – the victory effectively knocking them out of the Trevor Lawrence sweepstakes atop the 2021 draft. Sunday, they edged the Chargers 27-24 – meaning the Bolts (1-9) stand a much better shot at obtaining Ohio State WR Jeremiah Smith at or near the top of the board … and/or holding the Jets hostage for draft capital given Gang Green's obvious need for a quarterback next spring.

24. The age of Detroit Lions RB Jahmyr Gibbs, who scored two TDs on the ground in Sunday's 31-21 defeat of Tampa Bay. Gibbs now has 52 career rushing TDs, tying him with Lions legend and Hall of Famer Barry Sanders for the most in league history by a player yet to reach his 25th birthday.

25. The Panthers are the only current division leader that has been outscored on the season.

26. Yet along with the Niners, Carolina is also the only team in the NFC with a two-game lead in the divisional win column.

27. The Buffalo Bills, Broncos and Lions have all win six in a row, and the Cincinnati Bengals' heater could match that if they knock off the Washington Commanders on Monday night.

28. The Bears have won five in a row and have not trailed throughout that hot streak, which coincides with QB Caleb Williams' Week 6 return from his hamstring injury.

29. But as the playoff picture in both conferences begins to crystallize, much of the intrigue seems to be in the middle of the pack. In the AFC, for instance – and all due respect to the Ravens and Patriots, both without their QB1s – the Steelers, Browns and Texans will quite likely wind up fighting for the final two wild-card spots.

30. The NFC should provide more high-quality drama. While the NFC East and South will almost surely be one-bid divisions, the NFC North and West ought to create plenty of tension as their teams vie for the final five playoff spots – some quality clubs sure to be left behind. Wednesday night's matchup, the first the NFL has ever played on Thanksgiving Eve, will pit the Packers and Rams in a contest that could have serious ramifications come January.

31. As we approach Thanksgiving, many – some? – will also flash back to that Tryptophan-induced nightmare that was the Butt Fumble … which celebrated its 14th anniversary Sunday. Maybe the Jets beating the Chargers isn't so bad after all.

32. RIP, Tom Jackson – a great player, analyst and person, the outpouring of love, respect and grief from ESPN telling you everything you need to know.
