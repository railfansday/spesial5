The next step for the Eagles at DT after trading Jordan Davis

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The next step for the Eagles at DT after trading Jordan Davis originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

The Eagles made a massive trade on Monday, shipping Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick.

After this Davis trade, it suddenly makes sense why Eagles general manager Howie Roseman reportedly kept telling teams that he wasn't looking to move Ojomo before Tuesday's trade deadline. In fact, he was telling them he wanted to re-sign him. That should now be the next step.

While the Eagles clearly made this Davis trade because they got excellent return, it does open the door to pairing Ojomo with Jalen Carter long-term in the middle of this defense.

The Eagles used a seventh-round pick to take Ojomo out of Texas back in 2023 and he has become a very good NFL player. But when the Eagles extended both Carter (4 years, $152 million) and Davis (3 years, $78 million) this offseason, it looked likely that Ojomo would play out his rookie contract and leave in free agency.

Ojomo seemed destined to follow the path of former Eagles DT Milton Williams, who played out his rookie deal before signing with the Patriots before the 2025 season.

But with Davis gone, Ojomo has a real chance to stay and the Eagles should want to keep him. He's a 25-year-old homegrown talent with pass rush ability at a premium position.

In recent seasons, Roseman hasn't handed out in-season extensions because he hasn't wanted to create locker room divides that could hurt a Super Bowl push. Roseman won't have to worry about that in 2026, so he shouldn't hesitate to extend Ojomo if a deal is possible.

Back in the summer, Ojomo said there had been discussions between the team and his agent.

And as recently as a couple weeks ago, Ojomo still thought it was possible he could stay in Philly. That was obviously before the Davis trade.

"I don't think any door is shut," Ojomo said before the Washington game. "I wouldn't say that. But you don't know what the future holds."

Last year, Ojomo had a breakout season. He played in all 17 games, made 9 starts and had a six-sack season. Even though he doesn't have a sack yet in 2026, he has still been disruptive. In fact, he has the highest pressure rate of any Eagles' interior defensive lineman and the highest of his career, per NextGen Stats.

And while pressure rate isn't the only thing that matters and there are other variables like attention from opposing offensive lines, how's this for comparison: All-Pro Jeffery Simmons has a pressure rate of 10.2% this year, just ahead of Ojomo at 10.1%.

Here's a look at Ojomo's pressure numbers by season:

2023: 0 sacks, 2 pressures, 5.4% pressure rate2024: 0 sacks, 5 QB hits, 23 pressures, 9.1% pressure rate2025: 6 sacks, 12 QB hits, 6 TFLs, 29 pressures, 6.9% pressure rate2026: 0 sacks, 5 QB hits, 4 TFLs, 22 pressures, 10.1% pressure rate

So while Ojomo hasn't been able to finish at the quarterback, he has 22 pressures in 9 games after having 29 in 17 games in his breakout season. Those numbers at least tell you that Ojomo has been disruptive. And he has to trust that the sacks will follow.

It's not easy to figure out what Ojomo's next contract will look like but there are 20 defensive tackles in the NFL who are making $20+ million annually. Carter tops that list at $38 million, but Davis and Williams are in that list at $26 million. Ojomo is probably going to join that group or at least get close.

The only difference is that now it's more likely to happen in Philly.

"Howie (Roseman) and (Jeffrey) Lurie mean so much to me for giving me this opportunity to play for this team," Ojomo said a couple weeks ago. "I think I was the 23rd DT picked up. If we can make it work, I love playing for this city, I love working hard for this city. I want to produce results for this city."
