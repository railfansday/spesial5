Knicks missing three key pieces to start their homestand

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Knicks began their homestand shorthanded.

OG Anunoby (left calf tightness), Karl-Anthony Towns (illness) and Jordan Clarkson (right plantar fasciitis) were all out for the Knicks' 97-96 buzzer-beating win over the Trail Blazers on Saturday night at Madison Square Garden.

It was the first game Anunoby and Towns missed this season, as the Knicks have largely benefited from good health. They were at full strength throughout their four-game road trip.

Dalton Knecht, who was not with the team during their road trip, was called up from the G-League and was active Saturday as a result.

Saturday was the first leg of a back-to-back, as the Knicks host the Pacers on Sunday.

Blazers star Deni Avdija, who was questionable ahead of Saturday's game, was available for Portland and scored 20 points to go along with nine rebounds and five assists in Portland's loss.

"For a young guy, he's one of the best in the NBA when it comes to gamesmanship with the officials," Brown said. "He knows how to drive, and as soon as he feels the slightest hit, he can react good enough, like, I'd call a foul for him. He can react good enough to get the officials' attention and get him to the free-throw line."

Avdija entered Saturday averaging 8.5 free throw attempts per game, third highest in the league.

"He's figured it out at a really young age," Brown said. "He's also worked extremely hard on his 3-point game, so you can't give him space. You gotta play up on him, and now he's strong enough and quick enough to go through you and get by you and finish and/or get fouled. He's a handful."

Saturday was Alumni Night at Madison Square Garden.
