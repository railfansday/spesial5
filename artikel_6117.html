Jordan’s tourism income is growing only because Arab and Asian visitors are replacing Western ones, and they are heading for Amman, not Petra. Until Western governments lift their travel warnings, the sites Jordan built its tourism on will keep struggling.

Jordan is earning more from tourism this year even as Western visitors spend far less.

Tourism income rose 4.9% to $6.35 billion in the first nine months of 2026, according to the Central Bank of Jordan, as spending by Arab visitors jumped 18.8% and income from Asian visitors rose 12.3%. Income from European visitors fell 23.7%, and from U.S. visitors 16.3%.

Jordan has been caught in the fallout from the U.S.-Iran war, both directly and indirectly. At times, Iran has targeted bases hosting U.S. forces inside Jordan in retaliation for American strikes. The Jordan army said in April that 281 missiles and drones from Iran had targeted the country since the war began in late February.

Jordan's tourism income rose 4.9% to $6.35 billion in the first nine months of 2026, but the growth came entirely from new sources. Spending by Arab visitors jumped 18.8% and Asian visitor income rose 12.3%, while income from European visitors fell 23.7% and from U.S. visitors 16.3%. The fallout from the U.S.-Iran war is the backdrop: the Jordan army said in April that 281 missiles and drones from Iran had targeted the country since the war began in late February. The recovery inside the country is uneven. Average hotel occupancy was 38% in September, with Amman at 59% and Petra at just 12%, where some hotels have closed, against the 70% to 80% the Jordan Hotels Association's Hussein Hilalat said would be typical for the season. Domestic visits to Petra are up 26.6% while foreign visits are down 17.8%, and most Jordanians travel on day trips, which leaves hotels short. Inbound operator Experience Jordan Adventures said bookings remain more than 50% below last year, and CEO Ayman Abd Alkareem attributed the biggest drop to markets where government travel warnings remain in place, citing their effect on travel insurance rather than traveler perception of safety. Air links are returning slowly, with British Airways back from Nov. 4 and Air Arabia Dammam launching Amman as its first international route, while the EU aviation safety agency's airspace warning runs until Jan. 31, 2027. Policy is following demand: the Jordan Tourism Board signed a deal with VFS Global in October to promote Jordan in Gulf markets, and the government is promoting Jordan in China and Japan. Morocco, farther from the conflict, grew arrivals 5% to 17.4 million with France up 7%.
