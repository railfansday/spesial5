Room & Board Ella bed review: A stylish upgrade or an expensive frame?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Here's a closer look at the Room & Board Ella bed's style, comfort, construction, and $2,000 price tag

A bed frame has to do more than look good in a bedroom. It needs to feel solid, support your mattress, and hold up to years of getting in and out of bed. And if you're spending close to $2,000, you probably want it to make a design statement, too. The Room & Board Ella bed aims to cover all those bases with a padded upholstered frame, an angled headboard, and slim metal legs.

The Ella starts at $1,999 in stocked fabrics, with made-for-you upholstery options starting at the same price. It's available in five sizes, from twin to California king, and several hundred fabric choices. The design is relatively simple, but the combination of upholstery and contrasting steel legs gives it a more finished look than a basic platform bed.

A hands-on showroom evaluation found plenty to like about the Ella's appearance and proportions, particularly its supportive angled headboard. But it also raised a question worth considering before you buy: The frame didn't feel quite as sturdy as some of the brand's other beds.

Here's what to know about the Ella's construction, customization options, price, and everyday practicality.

The Ella is an upholstered platform bed with a fully padded hardwood frame and reinforced joinery. Its angled headboard gives it a softer silhouette than a conventional rectangular headboard, while the exposed steel legs add contrast and keep the design from looking too bulky. You can choose from several leg finishes, including graphite, brushed gold, and stainless steel, depending on the fabric combination.

The bed starts at $1,999 in stocked fabrics, including Brava camel, Sumner linen, and Tepic white. Made-for-you fabrics also start at $1,999, with some upholstery choices priced at $2,399. There are 245 made-for-you fabric options, ranging from neutral shades to more colorful choices, so it's relatively easy to coordinate the bed with an existing bedroom.

The Ella comes in twin, full, queen, king, and California king sizes. A queen measures 65.5 inches wide by 89 inches deep, with a 46-inch headboard. The frame includes a mattress support system with 12 steel slats, so you don't need to purchase a separate box spring or foundation. The mattress isn't included.

The bed is made in North Carolina using U.S. and imported materials. Delivery includes assembly, which is a welcome detail for a piece of furniture this large.

The Ella's strongest selling point is its design. The upholstered frame softens the look of the bedroom, while the angled headboard gives you something comfortable to lean against when reading or watching television. In a showroom evaluation, the headboard stood out as supportive, and its two available heights gave the design a little more flexibility.

The proportions are another plus. The bed has a substantial presence without looking oversized, and its clean lines make it easy to work into different decorating styles. Upholstery choices do much of the customization work here: A light fabric can keep the room feeling airy, while a darker or more textured option gives the bed a stronger visual presence.

The frame also offers useful flexibility beneath the mattress. There's 7 inches of underbed clearance, enough for low storage bins, even though the Ella doesn't have built-in drawers. The legs are removable, and the bed is compatible with Room & Board adjustable mattress bases.

The construction is more substantial than what you'd typically find in a basic upholstered bed. A padded hardwood frame, reinforced joinery, and steel mattress-support slats give it a solid foundation on paper. Owner feedback is generally positive, with buyers frequently praising the appearance, fabric quality, and delivery experience.

The biggest concern is sturdiness. During a hands-on showroom evaluation, the Ella showed some wobble when compared with other Room & Board beds. Its design uses slimmer metal legs rather than the large, solid wood bases found on some competing models in the collection. That doesn't mean the bed is poorly made, but it's worth considering if a particularly rigid, heavy-feeling frame is high on your priority list.

Storage is another limitation. The Ella is the only bed in the showroom comparison that didn't offer an optional storage drawer. Its 7-inch clearance does leave room for low bins, but you'll need to pull them out manually rather than using built-in drawers. If keeping extra bedding or clothing tucked away is important, a storage bed may be a better fit.

The price also deserves a close look. At $1,999 to start, the Ella is a considerable investment, especially since the mattress is sold separately. Some fabric choices cost more, and the final price can climb depending on the size and upholstery you select. The bed's design and customization options help explain the cost, but shoppers focused mainly on a sturdy place to support a mattress can find less expensive alternatives.

Finally, pay attention to mattress height. The bed is designed for a mattress alone, and a mattress 13 inches tall or shorter creates the intended proportions. Adding a box spring isn't recommended and could make the overall setup look too tall.

The Ella makes the most sense for someone who wants an upholstered bed with a distinctive but versatile look and plenty of fabric choices. Its angled headboard, padded frame, and contrasting metal legs give it more personality than a basic platform bed, while the range of upholstery options makes it easier to tailor the design to a room.

It's also a practical choice if you don't need built-in storage and are happy using low bins underneath. The included slat system means there's no need to budget for a separate foundation, and delivery includes assembly.

But if your top priority is a particularly heavy, rigid frame or convenient drawer storage, the Ella may not be the strongest choice. At nearly $2,000, you should feel confident about both its appearance and its construction before committing.

For shoppers who value upholstery, customization, and a clean-lined design, the Ella is an attractive option. Just make sure its lighter feel and lack of built-in storage aren't deal-breakers.
