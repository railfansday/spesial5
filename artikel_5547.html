Over the past few months, lower-cost generic versions of the blockbuster GLP-1 drug semaglutide, which is sold as Wegovy by Novo Nordisk for combating obesity, started to become available in some countries and will soon arrive in still others. Meanwhile, the pharmaceutical industry is racing to develop newer GLP-1 medicines that, to varying degrees, are forecast to be more effective, cause fewer side effects, and are easier to administer.

On one hand, these developments suggest more people than ever before may find needed help for losing weight. But there is another possibility: Obesity medications may become the next big battleground over access to medicines in lower-income countries, according to Ken Shadlen, a London School of Economics professor who studies the politics of pharmaceutical and health regulation. We spoke with him about the looming challenges that may soon confront drugmakers, governments, and patients. This is an edited version of our conversation.
