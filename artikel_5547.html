Over the past six months, science advocates have been consumed by the fight over a White House proposal to subject all federal research contracts to more political oversight. Thousands of scientists and organizations have decried the potential changes as fundamentally rewriting the rules of American science.

So they were disappointed when Senate appropriations bills released Monday for the Department of Health and Human Services did not include language blocking the plan’s implementation. As she has so often in the past, the chair of the Appropriations Committee, Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine), came to their aid, telling STAT on Tuesday that she is intent on blocking the rule before the funding measures are finalized.

From her perch atop the Senate Appropriations Committee, Collins has frequently pushed back on the White House’s attempts to slow down science funding, or use it as a political tool. But, her time making headlines as a member of President Trump’s party forcefully pushing back on his agenda will soon come to an end, with her midterm loss to Democrat Troy Jackson. While many science advocates celebrated the election of scientists and physicians to a record number of seats in Congress, some worry that Collins’ defeat could hamper their ability to push back on the administration.
