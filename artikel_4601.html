Toyota sells 3 electric SUVs, but which one is best for you?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Toyota now offers three fully electric SUVs: the bZ, C-HR, and bZ Woodland. Here’s how they stack up.

After introducing the new and improved bZ (formerly the bZ4X) in late 2025, the electric SUV quickly became one of the most popular EVs in the US.

In the first nine months of 2026, it was the fourth-best-selling electric vehicle, behind the Hyundai IONIQ 5, Tesla Model 3, and Tesla Model Y.

With two additional electric SUVs now on sale, the CH-R and bZ Woodland, Toyota has suddenly become one of America’s top-selling EV brands.

Its EV sales have nearly tripled this year. According to Cox Automotive, Toyota ranked as the third most popular EV brand behind Tesla and Rivian in the third quarter.

Toyota offers three distinct electric vehicles: the 2027 bZ, C-HR, and bZ Woodland, each designed for different lifestyles, so which one is the best fit for you?

After launching the updated model last year, the 2027 bZ starts at $34,980. With the $1,595 delivery fee, the starting price is $36,576.

That’s for the standard 2027 Toyota bZ XLE FWD with an EPA-estimated driving range of up to 236 miles. For longer range, you can upgrade to the FWD Plus model, which offers up to 314 miles and starts at $37,980 (excluding the delivery fee).

The Toyota bZ is available in XLE and Limited grades, with front- or all-wheel-drive (AWD) powertrains.

Two battery options are offered: The 57.7 kWh battery on the standard XLE FWD provides an EPA-estimated range of up to 236 miles, while the larger 74.7 kWh battery provides up to 288 miles of range on the XLE AWD and 314 miles on the XLE FWD Plus model.

FWD models produce either 165 hp (XLE FWD) or 221 hp (XLE FWD Plus and Limited FWD), while AWD models deliver a combined 338 hp.

The 2027 bZ also has a built-in NACS port, so you can use Tesla Superchargers without needing a separate adapter. Toyota said it takes around 30 minutes to charge the battery from 10% to 80%.

Inside, the 2027 bZ features Toyota’s updated cabin design. The setup features a 7″ LCD customizable Multi Information Display (MID) and a new 14″ Toyota Audio Multimedia display screen with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.

The bZ is essentially designed as an everyday driver with a long driving range, modern tech, and available AWD.

For those looking for a bigger, more rugged option, the 2027 bZ Woodland may be a better fit. The Woodland is 6″ longer than the standard bZ and comes with standard AWD.

The longer body opens up more interior space, with up to 74.3 cubic feet of storage compared to the bZ with up to 67.1 cubic feet.

Unlike the bZ, the Woodland model has a standard AWD powertrain with Toyota’s X-Mode control system.

The setup delivers a combined 375 horsepower, 37 hp more than the standard bZ, making it Toyota’s most powerful EV currently.

It also adopts a more commanding, upright stance with 8.3″ of ground clearance and 3,500 lbs of towing capacity.

Although it uses the same 74.7 kWh battery as the standard bZ, the Woodland’s EPA-estimated range drops to 281 miles because of the added weight and power. Upgrading to the optional all-terrain tires sacrifices even more range, with up to 260 miles.

Like the bZ, the Woodland also has a built- in NACS port. Charging the battery from 10% to 80% takes around 30 minutes.

Inside, the bZ Woodland features the same 7″ LCD customizable Multi Information Display (MID) and 14″ Toyota Audio Multimedia display screen with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.

The 2027 Toyota bZ Woodland is available in Base and Premium trims, starting at $45,380. All variants include standard roof rails, Dual Qi-capable wireless device chargers, heated front seats and steering wheel, and Digital Key capabilities.

Last, but not least, the C-HR is a smaller, sporty, coupe-like electric SUV. The 2027 Toyota C-HR starts at $37,080, or $2,100 more than the standard bZ.

It’s about 6.7″ shorter than the bZ, with a smaller wheelbase, but also comes with standard AWD. The dual-motor AWD setup delivers a combined 338 hp, not quite as much as the bigger, more powerful Woodland.

The C-HR is available exclusively with the 74.7 kWh battery, which provides an EPA-estimated range of up to 287 miles for the SE grade with 18″ wheels.

Upgrading to 20″ wheels adds a street-ready look but offers slightly less EPA range, with up to 273 miles.

The coupe-like design adds a sleek look and opens up interior space, with up to 59.5 cubic feet of rear cargo space when the rear seats are folded. That’s slightly more than the bZ, which offers up to 56.9 cubic feet.

Inside, the C-HR features the same setup as its other EVs, including a 7″ slim driver’s display and 14″ central infotainment screen.

Like Toyota’s other electric SUVs, the C-HR has a native NACS port. Charging the battery from 10% to 80% takes about 30 minutes.
