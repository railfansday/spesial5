As Netflix and Disney sniff around, YouTube hypes its creators’ “world-class, studio-quality” programming at L.A. Premieres event

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Tonight, YouTube brought back its film-festival-style event, Creator Premieres, to showcase a new batch of “world-class, studio-quality creator programming” coming exclusively to its platform.

Last year’s Creator Premieres–the first YouTube held–took place at New York City’s historic screening space Metrograph and showed off projects from creators like Ms. Rachel, Brave Wilderness, Dhar Mann, Deestroying, and Cleo Abram.

This year’s edition was hosted by House Guest‘s Scott Evans at The Hollywood Roosevelt in L.A., and highlighted upcoming projects from YouTube OGs and greats like Emma Chamberlain, Nick DiGiovanni, Lilly Singh, Wesley Wang, Grace Reiter, and more.

Google President Sean Downey took the stage to highlight YouTube’s continued dominance on TV screens, and gave a nod to participating creators for fueling the UGC machine that keeps viewers coming back to YouTube–on TVs and across other devices.

Of course, he also appealed to advertisers in the same breath, reminding them that this “world-class” content is what they could be marketing alongside, if they cough up the cash to Google.

“YouTube creators are not just shaping modern culture, they are redefining the entire entertainment landscape. This slate of shows demonstrates how creators are commanding primetime attention on the big screen and building unmatched fan loyalty,” Downey said. “For brands, this isn’t about buying thirty seconds of ad space; it’s about moving past standard commercial breaks and partnering directly with the storytellers who are defining modern culture.”

YouTube has spent years at the top of TV watch time charts–a fact that really gets under the skin of competitor Netflix. Up till recently, though, Netflix’s main response to this irritant was talking a lot of sh*t about YouTube and occasionally signing back catalog deals with view-drivers like Ms. Rachel and CoComelon.

But in the last 12 months, something’s shifted. Netflix has begun tapping creators like Salish Matter, Alan Chikin Chow, and the Stokes Twins not for back catalog agreements, but for development deals–deals which result in exclusive original programming that won’t appear on YouTube.

And YouTube’s not happy about it. The platform has been offering creators money, including production financing and sweet ad deals, in exchange for turning down offers from Netflix. Conversely, creators who decide to sign with Netflix are put on YouTube’s naughty list, and denied perks like revenue shares from primo ad deals.

(That being said, Netflix-friendly creators aren’t entirely blacklisted. DiGiovanni signed a catalog crossposting deal with Netflix in July, and was still one of the headline creators at tonight’s showcase.)

The point we’re making here is this: With Netflix–and Disney–sniffing around, YouTube has more incentive than ever to hype up its creators and their projects on a big stage.
