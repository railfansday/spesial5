Down and synthetic insulation are two of the most common options.

Synthetic insulation is made from polyester fibers, more affordable than down, and handles wet weather better. Popular options include PrimaLoft and Thinsulate, which use a blend of synthetic fibers designed to trap heat while remaining lightweight and breathable.

Down is made from clusters of goose or duck plumage, and is prized for its lightweight warmth. It compresses easily for packing and offers superior heat retention in dry conditions. Some coats even blend down and synthetic fill to combine the benefits of both.

Look for a waterproof shell, sealed seams, and a protective hood to help keep rain and snow from soaking through. Features like adjustable cuffs and storm flaps can also help block out wind and moisture. If you’re dealing with wet, snowy winters, prioritize a fully waterproof design over a water-resistant finish.

A winter coat should leave enough room to layer a sweater or hoodie without feeling bulky or restricting movement. The shoulders should sit comfortably, the sleeves should cover the wrists, and the hem should provide enough coverage for your needs. If you plan to wear it over heavier layers, consider sizing up, but always check the brand’s fit recommendations.

Look for fleece-lined hand pockets to keep your fingers warm and interior pockets to protect your phone, wallet, or AirPods from the cold. Extra details like zippered or hidden pockets add practicality, especially for commuting, travel, or outdoor adventures.

For most of us, the terms "jacket" and "coat" are probably interchangeable. However, the main difference comes down to length and coverage. A winter coat's main objective is to keep you warm, which is why it's bigger and thicker, extends past the hips or toward the knees, and is filled with some type of insulation. A jacket, on the other hand, is considered a combination of style and warmth. Jackets don't typically go past the waist and they're designed to be worn indoors at times. Winter jackets can be layered more with outfits too—sometimes even under a coat.

Winter jackets can work as your main outer layer in cold weather, especially when they’re insulated and long enough to provide adequate coverage. For colder temperatures or extended time outdoors, a longer, more heavily insulated coat or parka can provide additional warmth and protection. The right choice ultimately depends on the weather, your activity level, and how much you plan to layer underneath.
