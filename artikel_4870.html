This 322-Square-Foot Apartment Gives Classic Style a Contemporary Update

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Some gifts are best kept a surprise until the moment they’re handed over to their recipient. That was the case with this small 322-square-foot apartment located in the Vidny Gorod residential complex, about four miles from Moscow, which the owner, Lara, received as a gift from her daughter, Marta, and her son-in-law. “The couple personally oversaw the renovation, keeping the project a secret until the very end,” says interior designer Polina Chumakova, founder of Lagolga Buro, who oversaw the design. “Lara knew nothing about it and didn’t see the space that they were going to give her until it was completed and she first set foot in her new home.”

In the living room, the custom cabinet by Avantiz complements a vintage table and chairs by Tok. On the tabletop, an arrangement of vintage vases by Mak.buro adds a decorative touch.

Lara’s passion for cooking and baking guided some of the design choices, as her daughter envisioned turning the 322-square-foot apartment into a home capable of welcoming the entire family. “It was important to create a comfortable space not only for cooking but also for spending time together, so that on weekends the apartment could be filled with the aroma of homemade desserts,” explains Chumakova. “Lara has two children—Marta and her brother—and two grandchildren, ages 13 and 9. The family often gets together on weekends and at the holidays.”

The apartment’s contemporary-meets-classic design reflects Vidny Gorod’s architecture and its urban context. “The style was carefully defined, choosing solutions suited to both the home’s size and the budget for this project,” says Chumakova. “We invested in high-quality finishes and carefully selected furniture, lighting, and accessories. The idea was to combine a contemporary, understated design with natural walnut accents and a few vintage details. The result is an interior with a classic character, yet without references to any specific era.”

The wall of the living space is painted an olive green by Little Greene that adds depth and warmth. The Synergy Grace sofa bed by Ormatec is paired with the Firmo bedside cabinet by AM.PM; a vintage wall sconce on the left and an Umbrella floor lamp by Artefact on the right complete the scene. On the wall hangs a work by Ksusha Dmitrieva.

There were certainly some challenges. The original floor plan had issues that required a careful reorganization of the space. “The builder had left a rather bulky load-bearing pillar, as well as two separate small bathrooms. It wasn’t possible to move the column, so we decided to integrate it into the layout, concealing it inside a large closet in the entryway,” explains Chumakova. “This allowed us to create a more functional space while making the structure less noticeable. We also combined the two bathrooms. This created a more comfortable home and also provided additional storage.”

The kitchen was custom-designed by Avantiz and includes elegant vintage pendants.

Another challenge was the lack of natural light. “The apartment receives little direct sunlight, so we paid close attention to the choice of colors and finishes,” says Chumakova. “For example, the light, milk-colored porcelain tile, initially selected for the floor, appeared much more beige in the space than we had expected. We replaced it and also adjusted the other colors to account for this effect, striving to keep the interior as bright and warm as possible.” The lighting design also took into consideration the various needs of daily life, without relying on single light sources. “We planned several different lighting scenarios: integrated ceiling lighting, pendant lamps, accent lights for display cases, and other spotlights,” explains Chumakova. “Throughout the apartment, there are about five different types of lighting.”

To make the best use of space, the design focuses on versatile furniture and solutions that serve multiple purposes, helping to avoid clutter in the rooms. “Instead of the classic light fixture above the dining table, we chose a solution that would maintain the open feeling of the space and not limit the table to a specific position,” says Chumakova. “We also opted for a sofa bed instead of a traditional bed. This allowed us to keep more open space and make the room more versatile: During the day, it can be used as a place to relax and spend time with the family.”

In the kitchen, as in the entryway, the floor is covered with white tiles with black square accents, a graphic detail that picks up the colors of the vintage lighting fixtures.

The layout, however, prioritizes the kitchen, designed to be the heart of this home. “We deliberately wanted to dedicate a significant portion of the apartment to it, because it’s one of the most important areas of the home for its owner,” says Chumakova. “The work area is located near the window overlooking a courtyard: It’s a pleasant spot to spend time cooking or simply looking at the scene outside.”

All the storage systems were custom-designed to blend seamlessly with the interiors. “We wanted the cabinets to be almost invisible,” says Chumakova. “There are two large storage units in the apartment: one in the entryway and one in the living room.”

In the living room, custom cabinets by Avantiz are embellished with retro details. The Dovlet House rug, the vintage table, and the chairs combine contemporary pieces with retro charm.

The color choices help define the different areas without visually fragmenting the space. “We used color to distinguish the entryway from the living room, while the ceiling and the doors of the built-in closet in the entryway were painted the same shade,” Chumakova explains. A natural color palette and materials with distinct textures were chosen to create a warm and inviting atmosphere. “The main colors are olive green in the entryway and a milky white in the sofa bed area,” says Chumakova. “More intense touches of orange appear only in certain details, such as the pouf’s upholstery and the floor lamp. Everything complements the natural walnut, one of the key materials in the interior.”

The entryway features walls painted in an olive green from Little Greene paint and a white tile floor dotted with small black accents. A vintage pendant, and the orange Justyna pouf from La Redoute that adds a vibrant pop of color, complete the look.

In the entryway, the floor is covered with white tiles dotted with small black accents, in keeping with the contemporary classic style. The moldings, found in the entryway and on the wall behind the sofa, add rhythm and depth to the surfaces, giving a modern twist to a traditional detail.

The small size of the apartment required some very careful choices when it came to furnishings. “It was important to find furniture on the right scale: pieces that wouldn’t overwhelm the small apartment and that, at the same time, would be consistent with the contemporary classic aesthetic,” says the interior designer. “So we reinterpreted some traditional elements, from walnut moldings to vintage lamps with distinctive shapes, adapting them to a modern residential complex and the compact dimensions of the apartment.”

In the bath, the wooden cabinet is custom-made by Avantiz; the Evana fixtures by Villeroy & Boch and the Oxford faucet by Migliore complement the vintage sconces.

The finished apartment won over Marta, who oversaw every aspect of the renovation along with her husband. “It was a perfectly successful experiment,” the designer says. “Perhaps, if it had been a more conventional project with the future owner, it would have involved them in every phase and it would have been more challenging. It’s possible that some of the bolder solutions might not have been approved.” In this case, the fact that Marta wanted to surprise her mother also provided greater design freedom, resulting in a space that would be perfectly suited to its future resident.
