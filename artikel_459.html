The best Kindles of 2026: We tested Amazon’s new e-readers to find the winners

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In October, Amazon launched a brand-new round of Kindle e-readers, making it even harder to pick one. It doesn't help that the new 2026 Kindles are more expensive, too. I've spent years testing Kindles for Mashable, and I can tell you that the latest batch is impressive, and it shows exactly why Kindles are still the most popular e-readers.

After testing the newest Kindles, we have a new favorite. The Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition has edged out the standard Paperwhite as the best Kindle to buy in 2026. However, that doesn't mean it's the best Kindle for everyone, and so we've tested the whole Kindle lineup to make expert recommendations for every type of reader.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

Once you have a Kindle, there's still so much you can do to make the most of it. For avid readers, Stuff Your Kindle Day and Libby let you cross every last book off your annual reading list. You can even buy Kindle accessories like remote page-turners and gooseneck stands so you don't have to hold your Kindle while reading. (By the way, we love Amazon's new Kindle Click and Page Turn Cover, and the latter even reminds us of the cult-favorite Kindle Oasis, RIP.)

So, after extensive testing, reading so many books, checking in with BookTok authors, and attending BookCon, I created this guide to the best Kindles of 2026. Plus, with Black Friday coming up, I've found the best deals on Kindles.

The 2024 e-reader saw a $50 price increase recently, but now it's down to $129.99, which is even cheaper than before. Most readers: Amazon's latest release, the Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition, is the best Kindle. While we previously saw it as an upgrade pick, the new aluminum casing makes it essential with a sleeker, sturdier case. Inside, it's still the same speedy Paperwhite we love. It turns pages without stuttering, never slowing down when you’re in the thick of the action.

People who read on the go: The 7-inch screen has adjustable brightness and warmth but it also auto-adjusts. That means if you're commuting or the sun changes overhead, the Paperwhite Signature Edition will adjust the screen based on your environment. The 2026 model is lighter and thinner than ever and fits comfortably in small crossbody bags. Plus, it’s waterproof, keeping it safe from poolside vacations and spilled water bottles in bags.

When picking the best Kindle, it always comes down to this question — Paperwhite or Paperwhite Signature Edition? Historically, I've preferred the Paperwhite, finding that the Signature Edition doesn't have enough features to justify its price. The 2026 Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition finally fixes that. With a new aluminum case, the Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition feels like a high-end piece of tech. With a thinner body and square edges, this new Kindle feels more like a smartphone or tablet than it does an e-reader.

But inside the more advanced chassis is still the same e-reader you love. It's fast with a high contrast 300ppi resolution. The matte display is glare-free and easy on the eyes. With adjustable warmth and brightness you can read with it in bright summer sun at the beach and then turn it down and warm it up when you're in a dim bedroom. And speaking of the beach, its IPX8 rating makes it safe to use in the bath or at the pool.

It is a more expensive e-reader at $249.99, especially compared to the Paperwhite's $199.99, but here's what you get for an extra $50. The upgraded aluminum case makes that $50 nearly worth it compared to the plastic exterior of the Paperwhite, but the Signature Edition also has an ad-free lockscreen, 32GB of storage compared to the Paperwhite's 16GB, wireless charging, auto-adjusting brightness, and best of all, compatibility with the new Kindle Page Turn Cover.

Plus, the Kindle Paperwhite Signature comes in more exciting color options. While the Paperwhite just comes in graphite, the Signature Edition also comes in a stunning seaglass green.

Budget shoppers: Especially in light of the Kindle price increases, if you want to read books on an e-reader but need a budget-friendly model, the regular Kindle is the best option. It recently got an update, too, and the 2026 Kindle is better than ever. It’s the smallest Kindle, with a 6-inch screen that makes it pocket-sized (if you have big enough pockets). But with a budget device, keep in mind you have to make some sacrifices. The basic Kindle isn't waterproof nor does it have adjustable warm light.

Amazon made some updates to the 2026 Kindle that make it worthy of its higher price tag. It has a new, flush front design for a sleeker look. Plus, the design team made a change we've been dying for — they moved the power button to the side of the device. Plenty of specs have stayed the same between generations. It still has a 6-inch screen, 300 ppi resolution, and six weeks of battery life.

What truly makes it stand out is just how lightweight it is. The 2026 Kindle weighs 18 grams (.66 ounces) less than the previous generation and it's a noticeable difference. It feels like you're barely holding anything; plus, it's even better for travel as it takes up very little space and weighs just half a pound.

It's a speedy little e-reader, too. Its screen is responsive and loads books within seconds. The only thing that could make the e-reader better is if Amazon added an adjustable warm light and made it waterproof. However, according to Amazon, the lack of those features keeps it affordable.

The device is now available in its basic format in either ube or graphite. However, it's also available with an aluminum backing, making it more like a tablet. The aluminum Kindle is more expensive at $189.99, but it does come with 32GB of storage.

If you don't like the new prices for Amazon's new 2026 Kindles, then you can still buy the 2024 Kindle Paperwhite. Up until October, this was our pick as the best Kindle, and it's just as good now. Best of all, during big sales like Black Friday, you can pick up this popular e-reader for $129.99. The new model costs $199.99, which is a big ask for most readers.

The screen's front light is vibrant and has adjustable brightness, meaning it can adjust when transitioning from reading in the sun or shade. It also comes with adjustable screen warmth, perfect for evening readers who want to give their eyes a chance to relax. It’s lightweight and fits comfortably in small crossbody bags. Plus, it’s waterproof, keeping it safe against poolside vacations and spilled water bottles in bags.

I used this Kindle for two years, and it's as good as the day I took it out of the box. Plus, it doesn't suffer from the back cover design flaw readers have said they've encountered with the 2026 Kindle e-readers.

There's a recurring question we see about Kindles: What’s the difference between the Kindle Paperwhite and Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition? The Signature Edition has twice the storage of the Paperwhite (32GB), auto-adjusting screen brightness, an ad-free lockscreen, and wireless charging, and it costs $40 more. Sure, those things are helpful, but I don’t think those extra features are necessary.

Not a ton changed between the 2022 and 2024 models of the Kindle Paperwhite, but we can be certain that the 2024 Kindle Paperwhite is the sleeker, faster e-reader. The 2024 Paperwhite got a slight upgrade in screen quality compared to the 2022 Paperwhite. While the resolution is still 300 ppi, it appears to have higher contrast, straying away from the like-paper look and edging closer to the look of a tablet. Don’t worry, though — unlike tablets, the Kindle Paperwhite still has a gloriously glare-free screen. It still has an IPX68 waterproof rating, so you can get some leisurely reading in the bath.

Young readers: If you're raising a voracious reader, the Kindle Paperwhite Kids is the best Kindle for kids. It's waterproof, which means it stands up to juice spills and dips in the pool. Plus, it comes with a Kindle case included. It's designed to be kid-proof, not just physically, but also with built-in parental controls. If you're concerned about monitoring your kid's bookshelf, this Kindle allows you to approve downloads by kids and displays age-friendly titles that kids can easily access.

More than anything else, a Kindle for kids has to be waterproof, which is why the Kindle Paperwhite Kids is the best choice. It comes with everything we like about the standard Paperwhite: a beautiful, high-resolution screen that works faster than any previous generation.

But what makes it great for kids is that it's a distraction-free device. It doesn't come with ads, notifications, or the temptation of games or video. I also like that it's a bit heavier than the standard Paperwhite, giving it a sturdier design. Plus, the built-in case adds an extra level of protection.

The parental controls are easy to use and set up, and the device also comes with a six-month free subscription to Amazon Kids+, which provides unlimited access to thousands of age-appropriate books. The library of books is easy for kids to navigate and offers classics like Lois Lowry's The Giver and modern classics like Diary of a Wimpy Kid.

What I don't love is the price, especially now that it just rose by $50. At $229.99, it's more expensive than the standard Paperwhite. If you want to go for a budget kids' pick, I'd go for a standard Kindle, which you can set up as a kids' device during setup to ensure parental controls and add an Amazon Kids+ subscription for $5.99 per month. Plus, as your kid ages, you won't have to upgrade to a more teen-friendly e-reader.

For color enthusiasts: The Kindle Colorsoft would probably be a better option for kids than for adults, serving as a bridge between colorful storybooks and more challenging chapter books. But it works for adults too, especially those who want color or to use the Page Turn Cover.

The Kindle Colorsoft makes some modest improvements over its predecessor, introducing the same design changes as the rest of Amazon's 2026 Kindle lineup. That means the new Kindle Colorsoft has a slimmer build, magnetic Pogo pins, and edge-to-edge color, so the bezels now match the device's color. Overall, it's an improvement, but it doesn't solve the problems of the original device, nor fully justify the $289.99 price (or $319.99 for the Signature Edition).

Unfortunately, E Ink technology has extremely dim and muted colors. Even if you crank the vibrancy settings all the way to the max, the colors are disappointing. When you compare the Kindle Colorsoft side-by-side with graphic novels or books with illustrations, the differences are extremely obvious. If you want to read comics or similar color artwork digitally, you're better off with a tablet.

With those major caveats, if you want a Kindle with color, this is the best option. On the plus side, we love the fig colorway (it matches the burgundy iPhone), and the color lockscreen artwork does look great.

We have yet to thoroughly vet the new Kindle Paperwhite, but before the device's release, we got a sneak peek of the new models. We were impressed by the new aluminum models of the Kindle Paperwhite. Plus, I'm pleased to see that the power button has been moved to the side of the device. Based on how much we've loved previous generations of the Kindle Paperwhite, we feel comfortable saying the new gen will be a good purchase.

Note takers and annotators: If, looking through your bookshelf, your books have dog-eared pages, highlighted lines, and scribbles in the margins, your instinct might be that an e-reader isn't for you, but I'd like to introduce you to the Kindle Scribe. It's the only Kindle with writing capabilities and comes with an included stylus pen that magnetically attaches to the side of the e-reader; it's ready for all your handwritten thoughts.

The pen moves nicely across the screen, mimicking the texture of writing on paper. Its larger screen also makes it better for writing and reading PDFs.

In addition to a library of books, the Kindle Scribe also comes with a journal section where you can take notes. The notes feature is especially great for students who want to handwrite notes, convert their handwriting into text, and manage documents through Google Drive.

The 2025 Kindle Scribe got a much-needed upgrade. All the good stuff has held over with the addition of some major improvements.

It's still big, with an 11-inch display, which does make it a little more awkward to hold while reading, but for writing or annotating, it's optimal. Unlike other tablets (I'm looking at you, iPads) that require you to buy a pen separately, the Kindle Scribe comes with a pen that conveniently attaches to the side so it doesn't get lost. However, it's not the most secure magnetic hold, so be careful not to bump the pen off accidentally.

Writing on the Kindle Scribe is a joy. Before testing this e-reader, I used my iPad and Apple Pencil to annotate readings, but I never loved it because the screen felt too slick for the Apple Pencil. The Kindle Scribe does not suffer this problem, as the matte screen lends some friction to the pen, making it feel like you're writing on paper. I enjoyed writing with the pen, and I swear my handwriting looked better on the Kindle Scribe than on my iPad.

It finally got a processing update that makes it as speedy as every other Kindle. Gone is the lag when switching between journals and books. Plus, the displays have greatly improved, too, as the 2025 Kindle Scribe has virtually no ghosting. Like the Kindle Paperwhite, the Scribe's battery life lasts 12 weeks. It also has multiple storage options, as you can choose between 32GB or 64GB.

It's not your only option if you want a Kindle that can write. The Scribe Colorsoft is also available, and while we appreciate that model, it's quite a bit more expensive. The 2025 Scribe is a better value, it just doesn't have the wow factor of color.

For years, Kindle prices have stayed stable, but in August 2026, Amazon finally increased prices. Every model except the Kindle Scribe received a price hike. We can thank RAMageddon, as an Amazon spokesperson told Mashable that the increases were necessary due to rising memory and component costs.

Before you choose a Kindle, you need to ask one question first: Do you want it to be waterproof? If you prefer reading near a body of water — whether in the bath, by the pool, or at the beach — that immediately narrows down your options. Since the Kindle Oasis is no longer available, your waterproof options are the Kindle Paperwhite, Paperwhite Signature Edition, Colorsoft Signature Edition, and Paperwhite Kids.

Beyond water resistance, here are a few other features to consider:

Display size: The basic Kindle has a 6-inch display, while the largest has a 10.2-inch display.

Storage capacity: Kindles range from 16GB to 32GB of storage capacity.

Battery life: The new Kindles generally have outstanding batteries. On the low end, they last up to six weeks per charge, while others last up to 12 weeks.

Writing capability: The Kindle Scribe is the only model that allows you to write with the included stylus.

The latest Kindles all boast a maximum brightness of 94 nits and a 300 ppi, high-resolution display with zero glare, so you can enjoy sharp text and images at any viewing angle. Kindles are also equipped with WiFi connectivity, allowing you to download all the reading material you could ever want. Plus, you can listen to audiobooks on Audible via the built-in Bluetooth connection.

And if you were a die-hard Kindle Oasis fan and are attached to page-turning buttons, your best option is to get the Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition with the Page Turn Cover.

Out of all the e-readers I've tested (and I've tested a lot), Kindles have the best compatibility with Libby. I love the digital platform because I can read for free and support my local library. Some other e-readers require you to download your library e-book to a computer and then upload it to an e-reader, but with Kindles, your e-books are sent straight to your device with a click.

Libby allows you to read free e-books on a Kindle. Through Libby, you can borrow e-books through libraries, and all you need is a library card. Inside the Libby app, select the "Read with Kindle" option when you borrow a book, which allows you to send these e-books to your Kindle library.

Another option for finding free e-books is through Stuff Your Kindle events. These are author-driven events that offer limited-time deals on e-books, often making them heavily discounted or totally free.

In the past, the Paperwhite has been Amazon's flagship e-reader, with the Paperwhite Signature Edition being the more luxe option. However, with the 2026 redesign, it seems like the script has been flipped, with Amazon pushing the Signature Edition while the standard Paperwhite takes a back seat.

Plenty remains the same between the devices. They have the same 7-inch screen with 300 ppi resolution and adjustable warmth and brightness. Both are waterproof with IPX8 ratings and 12-week battery life. The new devices also have easy-to-open back panels to make replacing the battery that much easier.

The Paperwhite is the cheaper option at $199.99, but it comes with only 16GB of storage (which, to be fair, is still plenty) and is available in graphite with a plastic casing. The Signature Edition is more expensive at $249.99, but comes with 32GB of storage and is available in the aluminum finish in seaglass green and graphite. Beyond its nicer finish, it also has an ad-free lockscreen, automatic front-light adjustment, a rotating screen, and wireless charging. The real perk of the Paperwhite Signature Edition is its compatibility with the Page Turn Cover, which adds page-turning buttons to your Kindle.

As of May 20, 2026, Amazon no longer provides software updates or web services to devices released in 2012 or earlier, essentially bricking them. Users will still be able to read currently downloaded books on the device, but to add new books, they will need to connect the device to a computer. This is a huge hit, especially to Libby users who need web service access to read borrowed books.

Owners of these Kindle models are obviously upset, as it means they'll need to purchase a new device to maintain access to all features. While some are considering switching to a different e-reader, like a Kobo, Amazon still makes great e-readers, and it's a testament to the brand that devices that are at least 14 years old are still going strong.

Unfortunately, while a lot of Amazon devices get regular discounts throughout the year, we only see Kindle discounts during big shopping events like Prime Day and Black Friday. However, even at full price, these devices earn a strong recommendation from Mashable.

Kindle Kids editions come with built-in parental controls. It displays a library geared toward kid-friendly and age-appropriate content. Plus, it allows your kid to request books to read that you have to approve in the Kindle app — that way, you know exactly what they're reading.

Plus, it comes with a worry-free guarantee: If the Kindle Kids gets damaged within two years of purchase, they'll replace it totally free.

However, any Kindle can technically be a suitable device for kids. When setting up a Kindle, you'll receive a prompt asking if the device is for children, which enables parental controls on it.
