Pure dating app: 5 reasons to subscribe

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

It's no secret that people are souring on dating apps (Gen Z is opting out of the swipe), but while more mainstream apps like Hinge, Bumble, and Tinder struggle to attract new users or retain their stock valuations, one hyper-niche app is experiencing explosive growth.

Pure has been around for a while, but it largely flew under the radar, existing as a kind of curiosity reserved for the kink scene. But 2025 was a breakout year for the anonymous dating app: Pure reported nearly 100% year-on-year growth in its user base while being named one of our 12 best dating apps of 2026, all while generating over $100 million in annual revenue.

What secret sauce does Pure have that other apps don't? If you're curious about this anonymous hookup app but on the fence about whether or not to pay the subscription price, read on to learn the five best reasons to become a member.

And while it's true that many dating apps offer more substantial features for free users, we would argue that the Pure experience is better because people pay for it. Sick of being messaged by bots or matching with fake profiles, catfishes, and Instagram ads masquerading as dating profiles? Pure dispenses with traditional dating app headaches by only allowing paying customers behind the velvet ropes.

They also did something especially clever, reminiscent of club culture: they made it free for women. Not only does that entice more women to sign up — it also ensures that the gender ratio never gets too lopsided.

As a free user, your search parameters are extremely limited. Essentially, Pure will show you all profiles within a given radius that match your stated gender preferences. And while that's totally sufficient for the trial period, paying users can unlock a much more granular way to filter through ads, including Age, Height, Couples, Languages, and Turn-Ons, which includes everything from "kisses" and "spanking" to Shibari (Japanese rope bondage — if you know you know) and wax play.

Even better: as a paying user, you can opt to sort through Pure ads by "new," so you always get the first chance to shoot your shot. That's a valuable perk in any dating app, obviously, but it's especially important on Pure, where users don't swipe on permanent profiles. Instead, they interact with time-limited ads that usually disappear within 24 hours of being posted.

Pure bills itself as an "anonymous dating" app, but once you start interacting with people in a one-on-one chat, they can see when you're online in the app. But if you're really after anonymity, this mechanic is self-defeating. You might be in your single era, looking to meet as many people as possible, or you may just tire of responding to one person non-stop and want to focus on another contact. Incognito mode hides your online status for up to 24 hours, meaning you can browse new ads or chat with select people while still keeping a low profile.

It also turns off read receipts, which are normally enabled by default, so you'll be able to sneakily read every message sent to you without your interlocutor knowing. Beware, though: once your 24 hours of anonymity expire, read receipts return, including for messages you read while in Incognito mode.

If your idea of a spicy dating app is Tinder, Pure will force you to evaluate your opinions real quick. The entire Pure community runs on a progressive, sex-positive mindset, where people are as comfortable listing their favorite hobbies as their favorite bedroom kinks. In fact, mutual kinks are often the entire basis of a match!

On more conventional dating apps, you interact with a person's profile, where they have curated an image of themselves carefully designed to have broad appeal. They might list their job, their educational background, or whether or not they have a pet, for example, and learning more requires a conversation. On Pure, however, user profiles are hidden by default, so instead of matching with a profile, you match with a temporary ad describing what that person wants at the moment.

An ad on Pure is as likely to be about a late-night tryst or a spicy conversation as it is to be about looking for a third person to join a couple for a threesome or a sub posting in search of a dom/domme.

And because you can even filter out profiles based on your preferred kinks, you can let your freak flag fly and still easily find people who match your energy, whatever that might be!

Online dating carries a certain amount of inherent risk, but that risk increases on hookup sites, where in-person meets often happen faster and in less public locations than at restaurants and bars. While Pure does a great job curating its online community, never hesitating to ban accounts for abusive behavior, it really goes the extra mile to protect user safety.

The first big safety measure is something they call My Media Rules, which lets you filter which kinds of photos can be shown in your account, even in private chats. The service uses AI to determine when someone has sent a risqué or nude photo. Before showing you the photo, you'll be asked to decide between three possible responses, quoted verbatim:

Allow photos and videos in this chat. Spicy pics may stay blurred, depending on your settings.

Block explicit photos across all chats, plus all photos and videos in this chat.

You'll even notice that when browsing ads, you'll have to actively choose to see certain photos, which might include lingerie or a lot of bare skin (Pure doesn't allow public-facing nudity on the app).

Late last year, Pure took things a step further by introducing Safety Signal, which helps make offline meetups a lot less stressful. If you activate Safety Signal, your account generates a one-time, password-protected link that you can send to anyone. Once clicked, the link gives insight into where you are in real-time, as well as who you're meeting up with (including photos, if they were posted in the person's ad) and how long it has lasted.

Safety Signal is a fantastic step in the right direction for giving daters greater peace of mind, and a great example of how Pure takes care of its users.
