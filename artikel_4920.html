MoonPay Applies for National Trust Bank Charter to Grow Custody and Stablecoin Business

Crypto payments firm MoonPay has applied for a U.S. national bank charter, joining a growing list of crypto companies seeking a federal banking license.

The proposed MoonPay National Trust Bank would be based in Miami Beach, Florida, according to the company's November 17 application to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), Bloomberg reported on Thursday. The charter would help MoonPay grow its custody business and expand its stablecoin services, according to the application.

MoonPay confirmed the move on Thursday, tweeting that it was "proud to share" that it had applied for a U.S. national bank charter with the OCC.

Unlike a full-service bank, a national trust bank can hold and manage assets for clients, but can't take deposits or lend money. The OCC did not immediately respond to Bloomberg's request for comment.

Founded in 2019, MoonPay is best known for providing on- and off-ramps between fiat currencies and crypto. It has been expanding into new areas including AI, having launched a crypto wallet designed for AI chatbots, let users buy crypto through ChatGPT, and acquired AI trading copilot startup Dawn Labs.

It has also been courting institutions. In April, it acquired Sodot, a Tel Aviv-based crypto key management firm, to form the basis of MoonPay Institutional, a unit serving banks, asset managers and trading firms. The unit is led by former acting Commodity Futures Trading Commission chair Caroline Pham.

MoonPay is the latest crypto firm to seek a federal charter under President Donald Trump's administration. Earlier this month, Coinbase joined Circle and Ripple in receiving final OCC approval for a national trust bank, after Ripple won approval for a trust bank behind its RLUSD stablecoin. Jack Dorsey's Block has also applied for a charter to custody Bitcoin, while World Liberty Financial, the crypto venture tied to the Trump family, won conditional approval for its own trust company in August.

The approvals have drawn pushback. A banking group has sued over Block's application, accusing crypto firms of seeking "side door" access to banking, while Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) has argued that the OCC's approvals of Coinbase and Ripple were illegal, a claim the crypto industry has disputed.

Warren is expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee from January, and is expected to examine World Liberty's charter as part of a wider look at Trump-linked crypto ventures.
