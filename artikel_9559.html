Route 53 Advanced Hosted Zones: Guardrails, Change History and What They Cost

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Most Route 53 incidents I have seen written up are not exotic. Someone deletes a record that is still getting traffic, or removes an NS delegation for a subdomain another team owns. The zone does exactly what it was told. On 20 November AWS added a tier that tries to stop that: Advanced Hosted Zones.

Here is what it does, what it costs, and where I would switch it on first. Everything below comes from the launch post, the Route 53 docs and the pricing page. I have not run it yet.

The guardrails have names in the docs: ALIAS_TARGET_CHECK, ACTIVE_RECORD_CHECK and ACTIVE_DELEGATION_CHECK. Two others, NS_FORMAT_CHECK and NS_DELEGATION_AUTHORITY_CHECK, are always on in both tiers.

In CloudFormation it is HostedZoneConfig: HostedZoneTier: ADVANCED on AWS::Route53::HostedZone. These are copied from the docs, so test them in a sandbox account before you put them in a pipeline.

AWS's own example: 25 Advanced zones is $137.50 a month. That is nothing next to one bad DNS change on a production domain, so I would not agonise over it for the zones that matter. For hundreds of throwaway zones, pick carefully.

AZ-affinity routing, which launched alongside this, is billed separately at $2.40 per million queries.

Do you have a zone where you already had to bolt on your own "don't delete this" check? That one goes first.
