Not Mustard Or Mayo: Upgrade Your Ham Sandwich With One Rich Ingredient

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A ham sandwich can be a pragmatic bite, assembled fridge-side during the late hours of the night. And yet with the right attention to detail, it's a template that readily turns luxurious, too. Take the jambon-beurre, for example — a Parisian sandwich enjoyed since the 19th century. The dish relies on classic French ingredients like baguette and jambon de Paris, a gently spiced wet ham. Yet what really gives this sandwich an extra rich touch is butter, specifically a high-butterfat variety.

Also called European butter, this extra-decadent style packs in up to 90% butterfat, significantly higher than the 80% composition popular in the USA. Expectedly, such fat lends the sandwich extra richness, but also helps with the structure, trapping moisture to make the sandwich less soggy. Anything fattier than the U.S. standard butter is an enhancement, but 85% butterfat is the ideal.

The benefits of using a rich butter aren't exclusively textural. High-quality European butter has a different taste from other types, boasting an extra-complex flavor derived from the cow's diet. It's often available cultured, slightly tart by way of fermentation. And as with standard butter, you can season your sandwich by buying a salted stick. Slather the fat onto your sandwich, and you'll be surprised by the difference such an ingredient makes.

A package of European-style butter is a worthy investment, even if you keep the other sandwich elements modest. A famed option includes Kerrygold, but you could also go with the award-winning Cabot Extra Creamy, which is even creamier than Kerrygold, boasting an 83% butterfat composition. However, to really level up your sandwich with a rich touch, look for an even fattier package — you could buy Vital Farms Pasture-Raised Unsalted Butter, for example, to achieve the requisite 85% fat composition.

Especially if you use only three ingredients like in a jambon beurre, it's such small details that make the difference. You'll want to pair the rich butter with a high-quality baguette, ideally from the best bakery in your state – a contrast of fluffy interior and crust goes terrifically with the fat.

And pay special attention to the ham you're using, too. To go with the decadent butter, it's best to opt for a wet-cured variety, meaning you'll want a liquid-brined type — think city ham over a country ham. Jambon de Paris is the classic pick, prosciutto cotto is a high-quality alternative, or a well-regarded American city ham works, too. Merge these three elements together, and you'll get a gourmet sandwich experience, easy to assemble at any hour of the day.
