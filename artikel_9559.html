What Are the Best Frameworks for Developing AI Agents?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Posted on Nov 14 • Edited on Nov 14 • Originally published at agentic-affairs.com

There is no best AI agent framework. There is only the framework whose lock-in you can live with — and the honest answer most vendor roundups won't give you is that the orchestration you build today is the part you will have to throw away tomorrow.

Every framework on the market does the same five jobs: run the loop, call tools, keep memory, plan, and leave a trace you can inspect. What actually differs is how much of the loop they seize from you — and how painful the exit is when you outgrow them. Gartner says 40% of enterprise AI deployments will be multi-agent by 2027. That long tail is exactly why the choice matters more than the demo.

Prompts, plain-function tools and evaluation cases will probably carry over. The orchestration won't — a LangGraph graph, a CrewAI crew and an AutoGen group chat are built on different models, and checkpoint formats don't travel. Assess each part for reuse before you commit, not after you're locked in.

Match the framework to the job: solo assistant, go OpenAI Agents SDK or LangGraph for long jobs that must survive restarts. Team of specialists, CrewAI for roles or Microsoft Agent Framework for critique-and-revise. Enterprise workflow, Semantic Kernel for .NET, Google ADK for Google Cloud, LlamaIndex for retrieval over internal docs.
