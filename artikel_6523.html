There’s an Actual Holiday for Eating Thanksgiving Leftovers Over the Kitchen Sink

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A 1991 tuna salad snack started it all, and yes, there's a fan club.

It’s the morning after Thanksgiving. The good dishes are still drying, the fridge is a Tetris board of foil-wrapped leftovers and you’re standing over the kitchen sink eating cold stuffing with your bare hands. No plate, no judgment.

As it turns out, there’s an entire holiday dedicated to this very specific dining experience. It’s called Sinkie Day, and it’s been around since long before TikTok made girl dinner a thing.

For that, we have Norm Hankoff to thank. An advertising consultant from California, he had his lightbulb moment in 1991 “while wolfing down some tuna salad over his sink, using extra-strength potato chips as utensils,” according to a 1997 Associated Press report.

Convinced he couldn’t be the only one, Hankoff founded the International Association of People Who Dine Over the Kitchen Sink and declared the day after Thanksgiving Sinkie Day. By 1997, more than 1,000 people had joined, and each one received a membership certificate stamped with a double sink and the group’s motto: “Everybody does it. Sinkies proudly admit it.”

Naturally, there was a cookbook—or rather, a don’t-cook book. Published by a company cheekily called Digester’s Reader, The Official Sinkies Don’t Cook Book included such culinary masterpieces as “cakeless frosting” and “meat loaf in your fist.” Other recipes called for little more than a handful of mashed potatoes or a piece of chocolate cake dunked in a glass of milk.

Hankoff even set his sights on 1600 Pennsylvania Ave. When Hillary Clinton was looking for a chef in 1994, he sent her a copy of his book and suggested the first family give sink-side snacking a try. No word on whether they took his advice, but the White House did send him a thank-you letter.
