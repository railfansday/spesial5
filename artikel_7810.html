Salt spreaders don’t get the attention they deserve. Though freshly fallen snow has a beautiful way of blanketing neighborhoods in a peaceful ambiance, it’s not long before cold concrete can turn that snow into slippery ice. Enter salt spreaders, which are simple tools that make it much easier and quicker to apply deicing salt to driveways, walkways, and other outdoor areas.

Though I’m a firm believer in safety first, I also acknowledge that using too much salt to melt accumulated snow and ice can damage concrete, harm plants and animals, and contaminate water. Salt spreaders help mitigate this problem by maintaining even distribution and keeping salt confined to intended areas.

To determine which salt spreaders are worth their price, we researched dozens of popular spreaders before testing six top options on our driveway and walkways, as well as on our front steps and deck. Our favorite was the Scotts Elite Spreader for its even and easy application, convenience and control, and ability to accommodate both granular and rock salt. When choosing the right salt spreader, there are a few important factors to keep in mind. These include the size of the surfaces in need of salting, which type of spreader you prefer, and the quality of materials, among other things.

The snow salt spreaders we tested for home use are exclusively walk-behind and handheld models. However, larger commercial settings may require a truck salt spreader or a salt spreader for a tractor.

Many residential salt spreaders are designed as compact handheld units. Scoop-style models are sometimes fully open, and others have lids with various-size holes to accommodate both granular and rock salt while helping control the amount of product dispensed at once.

A slightly more complex handheld salt spreader may feature a hopper up top and a mechanism that opens to allow salt to project from the front of the spreader. Open-top hopper-style units are available in hand-crank or battery-powered versions with dials to allow various sizes of ice through. These types of handheld units generally work best with granular salt and may clog with rock-style products.

Walk-behind salt spreaders are more substantial than handheld units, incorporating larger hoppers as well as wheels, handles, and a range of additional features. They’re better suited to larger surfaces where users want to cover a decent amount of square footage quickly. On the flip side, their size makes them tricky to use in tight spots and on stairs.

Our top picks for walk-behind spreaders project salt in widths between 4 and 6 feet and feature at least one fin or deflector to help control spread width as desired. Most walk-behind spreaders have a wide range of opening settings to accommodate small materials like fertilizer, substantial pieces of rock salt, and everything in between.

While we only tested handheld and walk-behind spreaders in this guide, towable and hitch-mount salt spreaders are also available for larger properties or commercial use. Towable electric salt spreaders can help users avoid having to rely on snow removal services for the job, although they do require mounting to a truck, ATV, or lawn tractor.

Perhaps the biggest factor to consider when choosing the best salt spreader is the size of the area in need of treatment before or after a storm. Since handheld units often hold only 5 pounds of salt or less, they’re better for smaller spaces like stairs or detailed work alongside the edge of a driveway.

Walk-behind units are helpful for driveways, sidewalks, and decks since they allow quick application over larger areas and often incorporate fins or deflectors to help increase accuracy. The walk-behind units we tested have a capacity of 80 to 130 pounds, though smaller and larger models are available. In our perfect world, we would own both a walk-behind and a handheld spreader for speed and precision.

Towable salt spreaders can hold over 100 pounds of salt, making them ideal for deicing sizable surfaces quickly. Generally speaking, though, they aren’t as accurate as smaller units.

Most salt spreaders are made with plastic hoppers since the material is lightweight, relatively durable, and easy to maintain. Steel or stainless steel salt spreaders may be more durable over the years, but they generally weigh more and will require additional maintenance to prevent rust.

Salt spreader handles and broadcast plates may also be made of either metal or plastic. As with the hopper, the use of metal in these parts may make them more durable but also heavier and in need of additional maintenance.

Salt spreaders are an efficient and affordable way to reduce the time and effort it takes to keep potentially slippery surfaces safe in winter. They can also help save on snow removal costs. We think they are worth it, especially if you live anywhere snowfall is a relatively common occurrence.

Many fertilizer spreaders work with salt, provided the salt is small and granular. If the opening is big enough, your current fertilizer spreader may even accommodate rock salt. If you don’t have a salt spreader but need to melt ice, our guide on how to melt ice without salt provides a few additional methods.

Yes, the best time to spread salt is before it snows. Salting an hour or more before a snowfall helps prevent snow from sticking. If you miss your window to salt before a storm, be sure to use a snow shovel to clear as much accumulation as possible before applying salt using a spreader.

There are two reasons why it’s far from ideal to leave salt in a salt spreader. First, salt is corrosive and can damage your spreader over time. Second, if moisture from the air or wayward rain or snow gets into your spreader and combines with the salt, you may end up with a frozen block of ice inside your machine.
