Chase Adds 10,000 Vacation Homes To The Edit In 2027—Use Your Hotel Credits, But No Points Boost

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Chase Sapphire Reserve® (See rates and fees) cardmembers will be able to use their hotel credits at more than 10,000 vacation homes starting January 1, 2027. And Chase tells me consecutive bookings at the same home can each trigger a credit, potentially letting someone use the personal card’s full $500 annual allowance on one trip. There’s a significant limitation, though: Points Boost won’t apply to these homes at launch. The Edit name also won’t bring breakfast or upgrades. The home benefits require four nights, while the statement credit only requires two. Two Night Bookings Qualify For Credits, And Back-To-Back Bookings Are Allowed Chase already added Plum Guide to Chase Travel in April. The January expansion moves a selection of those homes into The Edit, alongside more than 1,500 hotels and resorts. The annual Edit statement credits available for bookings made in 2027 is: Chase Sapphire Reserve® and J.P. Morgan Reserve: up to $500, with a maximum of $250 per eligible booking. Chase Sapphire Reserve for Business: up to $1,000, with a maximum of $250 per eligible booking. The business card’s increase to $1,000 was announced in September. And Chase confirms that a prepaid two- or three-night home booking qualifies for the statement credit, even though it receives none of the vacation home benefits because those require a minimum four night stay (and individual properties can impose longer minimum stays). Bookings will be instantly confirmed, and full prepayment is required for The Edit benefits. I also asked whether you could book consecutive reservations at the same property. A Chase spokesperson tells me: Yes. Cardmembers can book back-to-back stays at the same property. Each eligible booking of two or more prepaid nights can qualify for the annual Edit statement credit, subject to the annual credit limit. For on-property benefits, however, consecutive reservations at the same property are considered a single stay, so only the first eligible stay will receive The Edit benefits. Two eligible two-night reservations could use both $250 credits from a personal Reserve card on a single four-night trip, or book a two-night and four-night reservation for a six night trip to use both credits and receive The Edit benefits on the stay. But you only get benefits on one four-night mininum stay. Back-to-back bookings aren’t entitled to benefits twice even if each reservation is for four nights. Chase commits to matching Plum Guide’s pricing. Asked about the identical home, dates and occupancy, the spokesperson replied: Yes. For the same home, dates and occupancy, the total price and cancellation policy offered through Chase Travel will match those available through Plum Guide. The credit shouldn’t get eaten up by a higher price than Plum Guide charges for the same reservation. Chase says it worked with Plum Guide to select homes based on design, guest ratings and distinctive features. Plum Guide does the vetting, not Chase. Chase isn’t sharing representative homes or pricing examples yet, so we can’t compare actual bookings. No Points Boost At Launch I’d hoped putting these homes into The Edit would create better opportunities to redeem Ultimate Rewards points. I asked and was told: No. Points Boost will not be applicable to vacation homes in The Edit at launch. Cardmembers can still redeem points, as they already can for Plum Guide through Chase Travel. But joining The Edit won’t bring better redemption value. This follows American Express adding Airbnb Luxe to its Platinum lodging credit. Eligible prepaid Luxe stays of at least two nights can use up to $300 each half-year, and a qualifying booking opens access to a separate $250 annual Airbnb Experiences credit. Amex doesn’t allow Pay with Points for those Airbnb bookings. Chase does allow points, but without Points Boost that’s not useful. It’s really just a new way to use the $250 statement credits. Four Nights Gets A $150 Credit, But No Breakfast Or Upgrades I asked Chase to confirm exactly which hotel benefits would carry over. At launch in January, the vacation-home benefits will include a $150 in-stay credit, plus early check-in and late checkout when available. Breakfast, room upgrades and welcome amenities are not part of the vacation-home benefit package. The benefits have been designed specifically around the vacation-home experience and how travelers use these properties. I’ve found The Edit credits useful on two-night hotel stays priced below $300 per night, with breakfast and a $100 property credit included. The home package adds $50 to the service credit but requires at least four nights, and drops breakfast and upgrades. Not surprising – there’s little to upgrade into when you’re booking a unit of one. And there’s nobody to make you breakfast! The $150 credit actually sounds better than just private chefs and massages highlighted in the announcement. Here’s what guests can buy and whether they’ll see prices before booking: Cardmembers will be able to see the eligible services available for a vacation home on the Chase Travel booking page before booking. Offerings vary by property and can include services such as fridge stocking, babysitting and dry cleaning. Pricing will depend on the specific service and request and will not be displayed at booking, however cardmembers can work with Reserve Travel Designers to understand available options and obtain pricing estimates for the services they are interested in. Fridge stocking and babysitting are things a family might actually buy. But the booking page won’t show how much the credit buys. After booking, guests will receive an email explaining how to use the credit. Reserve Travel Designers can help arrange services, and Chase recommends doing so at least 72 hours in advance. Early check-in and late checkout don’t actually seem like a Chase value add. A hotel has a pool of rooms. A house with guests leaving that morning needs to be cleaned before the next guests arrive. If another group is coming that afternoon, there isn’t much room for a late departure. So late checkout is either available or not, based on whether other reservations butt up against yours. Plum Guide already accommodates early check-in and late check-out requests subject to availability, albeit sometimes with fees. I asked whether Chase would waive those charges, require hosts to grant requests when possible, prioritize cleaning or offer earlier confirmation. Early check-in and late checkout are benefits offered to eligible vacation-home stays through The Edit, but both remain subject to availability and cannot be guaranteed in advance. We Don’t Know Yet If We Can Double Dip With Loyalty Credit Chase’s “The Edit” bookings earn hotel loyalty program points and elite stay credit, and receive elite benefits. What’s interesting is that Plum Guide properties earn 5% of eligible spending, excluding taxes, in GHA DISCOVERY Dollars. GHA Discovery is effectively the loyalty program for Plum GUide. (There’s no elite status earning bonus, all members earn the same, and these dollars expire after 12 months and can be spent at GHA hotels, but not on Plum Guide rentals). Direct bookings also earn qualifying nights, spend and brand credit toward status, with Plum Guide counting as one GHA brand. The partnership terms exclude non-Plum Guide booking channels. So it wasn’t clear to me whether Chase’s The Edit would count as a third party booking or not? They don’t for hotels. Chase responded that they “don’t have information to share on this yet.” It would be a great stack, though! For a Chase Sapphire Reserve® cardmember with unused Edit credits, the ability to apply several to one vacation home trip at Plum Guide’s pricing is the strongest part of this. I already use my hotel credits, so moving them to a home means giving up those hotel uses. Of course you can always identify a property, search for the owner or management company and book direct – often for less.
