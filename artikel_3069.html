"The Big Short's" Michael Burry Issued a Blunt Warning about Anthropic

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Burry has made some pretty impressive short bets over the past two decades.

By Bram Berkowitz – Nov 19, 2026 at 4:28PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Dr. Michael Burry made a name for himself during the Great Recession in 2008, when, prior to the collapse of the housing bubble, he bet against mortgage-backed securities that would eventually fail, earning hundreds of millions of dollars in profits for him and the investors in his hedge fund.

Due to Burry's track record, the market still pays close attention to his investment calls. In recent weeks, he has issued a blunt warning about Anthropic (ANT +17.07%), which just went public Thursday.

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images.

Since the emergence of artificial intelligence and large language models (LLMs), Burry has largely been bearish on the AI revolution. At this point, he believes a bubble has formed in the industry, and that the market has gotten far too exuberant about the values of the companies involved in it.

As part of its IPO process, Anthropic had to disclose many details about the company's inner workings, including its financials. In the second quarter, it generated spectacular revenue growth of 144% sequentially to nearly $11.6 billion. The company even managed to generate an operating profit.

However, in the third quarter, revenue was nearly $17 billion. That nearly 47% sequential growth disappointed some onlookers because it slowed quarter over quarter, although it was still incredibly strong. Anthropic is a frontier AI lab, and its models have found strong resonance with enterprises. But there are now more open-weight models that are reportedly much cheaper to use.

"Not only is the company priced for optimism... it is betting its entire existence on that optimism," Burry wrote in a Substack post, according to Business Insider.

But Burry's even bigger concern has to do with the magnitude of the spending Anthropic has committed to on compute to power and train its models. Vast quantities of compute will really be required in perpetuity to support its business model. Anthropic said in its registration statement that it had already committed to spend roughly $895 billion on compute, a significant portion of which is projected to be spent by the end of 2030. This also doesn't include an additional $125 billion to $150 billion of compute the company plans to purchase between 2028 and 2031.

Burry noted that these commitments are equivalent to roughly 12 times the more bullish revenue estimates for Anthropic of about $100 billion over the next year. He also noted that while the market is not blind to what's going on, he doesn't believe investors are concerned enough, and he has no plans to invest in the stock.

"Under take-or-pay, Anthropic could end up effectively paying more for compute at a time when it is not needed, which would increase its costs and reduce competitiveness with other language model providers," he added.

Burry’s warning hits the question sitting at the crux of the debate over the AI trade: Is spending hundreds of billions of dollars annually — and soon-to-be trillions — going to yield adequate returns on investment?

Certainly, no one can scoff at Anthropic's product or its revenue growth, but its third-quarter results highlighted that its growth may not be linear, and it may slow from here.

Anthropic priced its IPO at $1.5 trillion. Annualizing Anthropic's third-quarter revenue of nearly $17 billion would give it an implied annual recurring revenue (ARR) run rate of $68 billion and a price-to-ARR multiple of about 220.

Bullish investors, of course, can be tempted to assume that revenue will keep growing like a weed, but we still don't fully know how competition from open-weight models will impact its business, nor how future innovations in the rapidly-evolving AI segment could potentially erode Anthropic's moat.

Burry's warning is that the company will have to spend hundreds of billions of dollars on data center compute, even as its growth trajectory remains uncertain. Furthermore, the cost it is paying for compute now may look high in several years.

That's why I think investors should remain on the sidelines in the coming months and gather more intel on Anthropic's business before deciding whether to buy the stock.
