AMD Shares Just Got Crushed on a Short Report. Why the Drop Is a Golden Buying Opportunity.

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

By Geoffrey Seiler – Nov 27, 2026 at 3:44PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Advanced Micro Devices ›

Following the takedown of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang after an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, a short-seller has decided to go after rival Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +4.56%)based on allegations that the company's chips are also being illegally directed to China. The report sent AMD shares plunging 20% in the two days following the short attack.

In its report, Hunterbrook Media, which is short AMD stock, claimed that the company is supplying support to its graphics processing units (GPUs) to banned Chinese companies. It alleged that U.S. blacklisted Chinese server maker IEIT Systems was continuing to get firmware updates that allowed it to use its chips on its servers. It also said that it gets around 20% of its revenue from customers in China and Hong Kong and that a Chinese partner finishes these chips.

However, the short-seller admitted that its own research didn't find any actual export-law violations. It added that it does not know how IEIT got AMD's code or whether it has a license for the firmware. So basically, it's accusing AMD of something with no proof, while saying it's actually not accusing it.

Overall, the short report is pretty flimsy; however, the market has been so on edge following the Nvidia news, and with artificial intelligence (AI) stocks in general struggling, Hunterbrook took advantage of the situation to try to make a quick buck.

For its part, AMD said that it "complies with applicable U.S. export control laws and regulations and maintains rigorous processes to ensure compliance across [its] global operations." Otherwise, it did not engage with the short-seller regarding its accusations.

The chipmaker is allowed to sell its server central processing units (CPUs) in China, as well as certain older-generation GPUs to some Chinese customers, for which it has an export license.

The sell-off in AMD is a compelling entry point, in my view. The company is riding two huge waves with inference and agentic AI that should drive significant growth over the next few years.

AMD has built its new Helios system, which combines its GPUs and CPUs, around inference and agentic AI. Its chiplet GPU design, which can be packaged with more memory, is ideal for inference.

Meanwhile, the company has made some interesting acquisitions that could eventually push this further in future rack-scale solutions. This includes its purchase of predictive memory optimization company MEXT, and Taalas, which makes chips where specific AI models are embedded into them to speed up inference.

The launch of Helios comes at an opportune time, with Anthropic recently demonstrating that its Mythos Claude model could switch running models on Nvidia GPUs to AMD GPUs in just a few days. This could significantly weaken Nvidia's CUDA software moat at a time when AMD is just starting to ship Helios to large customers, including OpenAI, Meta Platforms, and Anthropic. With Helios costing nearly half of Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform per gigawatt, AMD has the potential to take significant share in the inference market over the next few years.

On top of that, the company is just starting to ride a huge wave with agentic AI for its server CPUs. AI agents require a significant amount of high-performance CPUs, which is a market AMD has been taking share in for years. The company's CPUs are helping run Meta's Muse AI agents, and it even has a foothold in Alphabet's lucrative tensor processing unit (TPU) architecture due to its strong security features, with its chips being chosen over Alphabet's own ARM-based CPUs.

Overall, AMD has a huge growth opportunity, and I'd use this pullback to buy the dip.
