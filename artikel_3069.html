Samsung Galaxy S27 Lineup Could Have a Personal AI Assistant Built-In

Galaxy Claw could make calls and send text messages for you.

Samsung is expected to unveil its Galaxy S27 lineup early next year, and a personal AI assistant might be arriving as well. The so-called Galaxy Claw could perform a variety of functions, including making phone calls, sending text messages and creating appointments, according to Android Authority.

The tech news website discovered Galaxy Claw while digging into Samsung’s Personal Agent Core app, which is a new AI system that connects AI across Samsung apps and Google services. This new system can also pull data from your calendar, Gmail, text messages, delivery info and other sources and also autonomously perform tasks upon your instructions.

Android Authority said that coding details for the Personal Agent Core app were shared by The Cipher Project, a Telegram channel that often leaks Samsung software updates, One UI features and unreleased device functions.

A representative for Samsung did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Samsung has had AI functionality on its phones and other devices since January 2024, when the company introduced Galaxy AI. The main capabilities at that launch included Circle to Search — allowing users to search for anything on the screen — as well as live language translation and photo editing.

Over the past two-plus years, Samsung has integrated more AI functionality into its devices, including Google Gemini and Gemini Live. Although Samsung devices have plenty of AI to do things for you, it primarily operates on your command rather than acting as an autonomous agent.

The new Galaxy Claw system might launch at Samsung Unpacked in January or February, when Samsung could release the Galaxy S27 series, which could include the Ultra flagship. Samsung has not officially announced what products it will launch at Unpacked.

According to Android Authority, the Galaxy Claw could connect to various apps on your phone and perform functions for you. Those apps could include Calendar, Contacts, Gallery, Gmail, Messages, Notifications, Perplexity, Phone and Voice Recorder.

So, in essence, the Claw could create appointments, write and send emails and make phone calls. For example, you could have Claw set up a meeting and send calendar notifications to the attendees with an attached agenda. Or perhaps Claw could call someone for you to tell them where you’re going.

Android Authority said other components could be included — Daily Brief, Device Cleaner, Travel Planner and a dedicated widget.

Those tasks would be a major step up from what Galaxy AI can currently do on Samsung phones — live translation, text drafting, photo editing, scam call detection and more.

Adding agentic AI won’t justify the $1,400 or even $1,500 price tag for the S27 Ultra but could help generate customer loyalty, says tech reviewer Jon Rettinger.

“People buy the Ultra for the camera, the screen and the S Pen, and that won’t change next year,” Rettinger told CNET. “Where an agent could matter is keeping people. If Galaxy Claw is reading my Gmail, my calendar and my texts and quietly handling the busywork, switching phones gets a lot harder, because I’d be walking away from an assistant that knows how my week runs.”

Rettinger said Samsung would be wise to include the Galaxy Claw with the new devices and not as a separate subscription service.

“So, now the pitch is $1,500 for the phone, then a monthly fee for the feature that’s supposed to justify the phone,” Rettinger said. “That’s a tough sell. If Samsung wants this to move hardware, it should come included on the Ultra.”

People apparently will maintain authority and control over Galaxy Claw, according to Android Authority. The new system would request access to sensitive data such as contacts, photos and calendar events, as well as permission to execute functions such as sending text messages and making calls. The Claw wouldn’t go rogue on you, in other words.

Samsung users might also be able to schedule the Claw to do tasks and functions regularly. Perhaps you need to email or text weekly reminders to someone about something. You could instruct the AI on the task and when to do it each week. It might only require a simple prompt from you, such as, “Please remind my son to take out the garbage by 8 a.m. Monday mornings.”

Android Authority said tasks could be scheduled by hour, day, weekly and custom intervals. Users would also be able to cancel or modify scheduled tasks and check task history.
