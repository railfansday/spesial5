Is the Stock Market in the Early Stages of a Crash?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Stocks have been shaky in recent weeks, and things could get worse next month.

By David Jagielski, CPA – Nov 24, 2026 at 9:46AM EST

10 stocks we like better than S&P 500 Index ›

At the beginning of November, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.25%) was at highs of around 7,700 and doing well, with 2026 looking like it might be another strong year for the market. However, after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned a few weeks ago, the market went into a tailspin. The index rallied yesterday amid his reinstatement as CEO, but it wasn't enough to get it back to anywhere near its previous highs.

What's worse is that next month, the Federal Reserve meets again and will make its latest decision on interest rates. It appears to be a near certainty that a 25-basis-point hike is coming. And that could spark more fear in what's proving to be a vulnerable market right now. Could a market crash already be in its early stages?

A big reason for the S&P 500's success in recent years has been tech, specifically growth related to artificial intelligence. However, if interest rates rise, as they're widely expected to next month, then that may hinder those growth opportunities, as it will be more costly and challenging for companies to raise money to fund their operations. That, in turn, will create a ripple effect, diminishing demand for the products and services of major tech giants such as Nvidia.

At a time when valuations are elevated and investor expectations are high, this potentially sets the stage for disappointing quarterly results ahead, which could lead to stock prices coming down significantly.

The S&P 500 recently showed how vulnerable it was to a single stock -- Nvidia -- as it fell below 7,000 last week. That may prove to be an ominous warning of just how shaky and too reliant the index is on just one company right now.

Predicting a crash is nearly impossible, as the market can move quickly in response to economic and geopolitical developments. And so even if many stocks look overpriced and a correction looks overdue, that's by no means a guarantee it'll happen.

Rather than trying to predict when a market crash might occur, a better move for investors may be to simply diversify their portfolios to ensure they are well positioned to handle whatever may happen. Holding a variety of different investments and funds may be a more practical approach these days than simply loading up on the hottest growth stocks.
