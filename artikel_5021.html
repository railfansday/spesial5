9 Decorative Plates for When You’re Sick of Boring Dishes (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The great thing about decorative plates is that they don’t have to disappear into storage once dinner is over. Hang a few on a display wall, prop one up alongside your favorite cookbooks, or work it into a shelf vignette. So what makes one worth showing off? It’s the details that spark curiosity: A scalloped edge, an unexpected color combination, or a hand-painted pattern can be enough to make you look twice. “Any plate with a fun decorative motif, bright colors, or an interesting shape makes it more than just simply functional,” says Olympia Irving of Carolina Irving and Daughters.

Of course, good looks are only part of the appeal. The most memorable pieces often have a story to tell, whether it's a vintage find or a handmade design with charming, artisanal characteristics. Even the finish can make a statement. “I often find myself drawn to surfaces that look like they’re in flux—glazes that pool unpredictably or finishes that suggest movement or fragility, even in solid materials," says Anna Jewsbury, founder and artistic director of Completedworks.

There's no need to play by the rules of a perfectly coordinated table setting. A little contrast, a playful border, or a beautifully imperfect finish makes for a more interesting table—one that looks like it can only belong to you. Ahead, nine decorative plates that deserve a spot in your regular rotation and perhaps a prime position in your china cabinet.

For a plate that has lived a completely different life before landing on your table—try this Completedworks dinner plate made from recycled glass. Sculptural and subversive, it’s made from broken windows, melted glass bottles, and is repurposed using old engine oil as fuel. “We often play with the illusion of softness in materials that are anything but. Those moments where form and material are at odds are the ones I find most compelling,” Jewsbury shares.

Transport yourself to the French countryside with this Villeroy & Boch dinner plate. Part of the brand's beloved French Garden collection, its fruit-and-floral motif brings to mind sun-drenched afternoons and leisurely lunches in the garden. Even fruit and pastries straight from the market become a proper breakfast spread.

Does anything scream summer more than seashell-shaped splatterware? These Concha plates by editor-favorite brand Carolina Irving & Daughters bring together a seaside-inspired shape along with a creative, painterly glaze. “It is easy to make a table setting pop by only adding a few decorative touches, whether that is a dessert plate or a salt dish,” Irving says. “Truthfully, we love to use decorative plates as our everyday dishware!”

Designed by Cabana and made by heritage Italian porcelain house Ginori 1735, the painterly motif on this plate adds an element of rustic Italian charm to the table. Whether you stick to a cohesive set or play around with different colors and patterns for a more collected vibe, these plates can make even the simplest meals feel storied.

Score a vintage-inspired find without the hours spent scouring antique shops. Olive Ateliers did the legwork with its Vincent Plate, featuring a hand-painted floral motif, an aged blue rim, and an ivory ceramic base. “A plate becomes more when it holds history,” shares Laura Sotelo, cofounder of Olive Ateliers. “The wow isn’t about perfection—it’s about the story it carries. It should feel like it belonged somewhere long before it ended up in your hands.”

Each plate is finished by hand, giving it the character of a one-of-a-kind discovery. One important distinction: This plate isn't food-safe, so save it for a spot on your wall or shelf instead.

The soft pale yellow tones and the scalloped edge are some of our favorite details about these affordable, vintage-inspired dinner plates by West Elm. They are perfect for those who want to dip their toes into a decorative tabletop but aren’t quite ready to splurge. Sold in sets of four or eight, they strike the right balance between charm and accessibility and are ideal for daily use.

Your next dinner party can channel a little Italian holiday spirit with these hand-painted plates, crafted in a family-run workshop on the Amalfi Coast. They're available in two sizes, so they're equally charming piled high with antipasti or passed around the table with the main course.

A collaboration between L’Objet and South African artist Ruan Hoffmann, this plate brings a bold edge to the table with its painterly design and hand-applied 24k gold accents. While it’s smaller in dimension (6.5 inches), it makes a subtle statement and can complement your existing dinnerware.

If you're a fan of British artist Tess Newall's painterly work, you'll be happy to know her designs have made their way to the dinner table, courtesy of Porta. The “Ottoline” plates take inspiration from English country houses, Bloomsbury interiors, and traditional European ceramics. These hand-painted pieces are adorned with scrolling motifs, dotted edges, and wave detailing in contrasting shades of slate blue and rust. Sold in sets of four, they're a lovely way to bring a little art history to the table.
