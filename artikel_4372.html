Our Long-Term Hyundai Palisade Hybrid Is a Bona Fide Road Tripper

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Our Palisade Hybrid has embarked on many long adventures in its first 20,000 miles, and it’s easy to see why.

Everyone at Car and Driver loves a road trip, and it’s clear that our editors play favorites when choosing a long-term car for said road trips. Often when traveling, an editor is rolling deep with family, friends, pets, and gear, so one of our more spacious vehicles usually ends up going along for the ride. With a few large cars at our disposal, the team has options, but after 20,000 miles, it’s clear that our long-term Hyundai Palisade Hybrid tends to be the first choice.

Since our last update around the 10,000-mile mark, the Palisade has seen shorter trips to Elk Rapids and Traverse City in Michigan, as well as to Cedar Point in Ohio, but it’s also made it as far as Colorado and West Virginia. Deputy video editor Carlos Lago loaded the Hyundai with his family, their dog, a travel crib, a dog bed, and luggage, and the Palisade Hybrid handled it all without issue. In the logbook, Lago noted that the Palisade was “smooth, decently powerful, and adept at maintaining the higher freeway speeds up north, though the road and tire noise was louder than I’d like at those speeds.”

Entry and exit haven’t proved as smooth as the Palisade’s ride, though. Lago—and other editors, including yours truly—bemoaned the hands-free door access, which will occasionally lock the doors instead of unlocking them. At that point, we just pull out the key fob, eliminating the point of that tech entirely.

The Palisade Hybrid’s largely white interior is another point of contention for staffers with kids, friends, and pets that ride in the back seat. Those of us with young children in car seats, particularly rear-facing ones, cringe at the footprints and mess we know is coming. Senior features editor Greg Fink hopped in at around 15,000 miles and wondered, “Is it just me, or is this interior looking a little worse for the wear?” With 20,000 miles still to go, we’re hoping that wear stays within reason.

Testing director Dave VanderWerp thought the Palisade Hybrid was a fantastic family SUV with a lot of features not available in other competitive SUVs, but he was still frustrated by parts of the road-trip experience. He found the cargo hold lacking capacity with the third row in use, though his teenagers did manage to occupy that row comfortably. Folding and unfolding the second row proved tricky for VanderWerp’s passengers. A recall on those seats may have added some difficulty there.

Technical editor Austin Irwin had high praise for the Palisade’s interior: “You can just about close your eyes and imagine you’re driving a Genesis.” That being said, he wasn’t blown away by the hybrid powertrain on the highway, where it was flipping between EV and internal-combustion propulsion too often.

Technical assistant Christi VanSyckle drove the Palisade Hybrid out to Colorado with her husband, daughter, and dogs in tow. “Even after a 15-hour driving day,” she wrote, “the comfort and ride were impressive the entire time.” However, her passengers also had issues stowing the second-row captain’s chairs, choosing instead to just squeeze past them to access the third row. VanSyckle called out the Palisade’s steering during her road trip, appreciating its heft when the going got mountainous.

Since the last update, the Palisade took one trip to the dealer for its routine 16,000-mile service, which included an oil and filter change, a tire rotation, and the usual inspections. The cabin filter also should have been replaced, per the vehicle’s maintenance schedule, but for some reason, the dealer decided not to change that out. As is, the total for that visit came out to $164.

While we can certainly find tidbits that annoy us (we’re very good at that), our long-term Hyundai Palisade Hybrid doesn’t seem like it will be dethroned as the regent of road trips anytime soon. With 20,000 miles left, perhaps we’ll even be able to figure out how the second-row seats work.

Going up north in Michigan often involves running the gamut of wild game crossing your path. With summer finally here, our trips up north have increased, as has our usage of the Hyundai Palisade Hybrid. Most of the fauna has been completely avoidable—with one exception.

Our 2026 Palisade Hybrid is equipped with factory built-in dash cams in the front and rear of the vehicle. They feature continuous-loop recording and parked motion/impact detection. The footage is stored to a MicroSD card. You can access the footage from the infotainment display and are able to look back at about two weeks' worth of clips (important to remember when you have this SUV in your possession).

Our most interesting recent clip came from managing testing editor David Beard, on his way up to Bear Lake, Michigan. A small bear crossed the Palisade Hybrid's path and didn't quite make it across before the right front corner of the SUV tagged it. Thanks to Beard's speedy reaction time, he slowed the SUV enough that it came away without any damage. As for the bear? At the time, we weren't quite sure. Would you check on a potentially very angry bear in the dead of night? A return trip the following day revealed that everyone appeared to make it out the unfortunate situation unscathed.

Beyond its ability to dodge live animals, the Palisade Hybrid has proven to be a great road-trip SUV. Many of our staffers cannot stop raving about the interior of this new Palisade. Vehicle testing director Dave VanderWerp said it's "comfortable, quiet, and seriously upscale." Buyer's Guide director (and current Genesis owner) Rich Ceppos thinks that "this is a full-fledged luxury car now, good enough to wear a Genesis badge. The ride is creamy, the powertrain smooth and quiet, the interior plush, and infotainment straightforward. It's a properly coddling driving experience." Sarah Taylor, online production assistant, agreed that the interior is comfortable and luxurious and further pointed to the Palisade's smooth ride. She also noted: "Slim A-pillars are much appreciated: no huge blind spots." Buyer's Guide managing editor Drew Dorian, who hauled some friends down to Cedar Point in Ohio, praised the seats. He said, "The adjustable side bolsters are a nice touch, and I even found our long-termer's kinetic seat feature useful after four days of riding rollercoasters."

However, there have been a couple small gripes along the way. VanderWerp thought that the Palisade Hybrid "feels substantially larger and less wieldy than the previous model behind the wheel." And despite all the features it has, the Hyundai is missing one key capability: You cannot lock/unlock the rear doors with a touch of the door handle. That's "something every parent of small children would use daily," he said. As a mom of two handling no less than a purse, backpack, water bottle, and certainly a coffee mug at any given time, I fully agree.

At around 8400 miles, it was time for the Palisade Hybrid to head to the dealer for routine service. This visit included an oil change, tire rotation, and multipoint inspection for a total of $146. In addition, there was an open recall that needed to be addressed. Recall 297 pertains to the third-row seatbelt and wiring harness. If the third-row seatbelt is quickly pulled and creates sudden tension, the internal wiring harness could break or become unseated. Luckily, we had not experienced this problem. The dealer inspected the belt buckle and then installed a wiring harness extension to relieve the tension, which was all covered under warranty. Speaking of recalls and seats, our Palisade also got an over-the-air-update fix for the unfortunate issue with the power second- and third-row seats that initially had inadequate pinch detection and caused a brief stop-sale for the new Palisade.

With another 30,000 miles to go, there's no telling where the Palisade Hybrid will take us next. Wherever it goes, we hope the on-board cameras don't pick up any more encounters with bears—or other wildlife.

Hybrids continue popping up in greater numbers across the three-row SUV segment, and one such flower has grown tall enough to catch our eye rather quickly. After Hyundai introduced the redesigned Palisade for 2026, we snagged the new hybrid variant for our annual 10Best competition, where it became an instant favorite. We decided to bring in the new award winner for an in-depth 40,000-mile evaluation.

We ended up with the top-trim Palisade Hybrid Calligraphy model, which starts at a modest $60,380, including the $2000 upcharge for all-wheel drive. Under the hood lies a turbocharged 2.5-liter inline-four and two electric motors nestled within the standard six-speed automatic transmission. Since the Calligraphy is the Palisade's poshest trim, its list of standard equipment is pretty long, including all manner of active safety equipment, rain-sensing wipers, nappa-leather seats with massage for the driver, a tow hitch, a digital rearview mirror, power-adjustable second-row seats, and a head-up display. The only added option on the car was a $245 set of carpeted floor mats, bringing our Palisade Hybrid's total to $60,625.

Our initial testing brought in some respectable numbers for this large hybrid, which produces 329 horsepower and 339 pound-feet of torque. The SUV hustled to 60 mph in 6.6 seconds. Top-gear passing from 50 to 70 mph took place in a tidy 4.4 seconds, and the Palisade sprinted through the quarter-mile in 15.0 seconds at 94 mph. It managed 0.84 g of lateral grip around the skidpad (more than most families will need) and required just 170 feet to stop from 70 mph.

Unfortunately, it didn't take long for our latest long-termer to hit a snag. Upon receiving our new Palisade Hybrid, we began our normal long-term onboarding process, which consists of removing the wheels and measuring the thickness of the brake pads. When we went to remove the pads, we found a wheel stud that was already stripped and spinning in place. The Palisade was promptly whisked away to the dealer and then disappeared for two months. Upon its return, we learned the dealer had to drill out the wheel stud and replace the damaged wheel hub. Ouch.

From there, it took only two weeks before another issue popped up: The engine started overheating. As soon as the car reached higher speeds—especially on the highway—the dashboard would launch into a series of chimes and warnings alerting us that the engine temperature was nearing the danger zone. The Hyundai went back to the dealer, who informed us that the water-pump belt had shredded itself alive. A new belt was installed, attachment points were tightened down, and we were assured it shouldn't happen again.

The dealer also took care of a few recalls and service bulletins that had cropped up. The service department replaced front-bumper wiring related to the active front air flaps and installed two software updates, one of which was required to prevent potential instrument-cluster failure. All repairs and recalls were done under warranty at no cost.

Since the Palisade Hybrid returned for a second time, we've had no further issues. It went on a spring-break road trip to South Carolina and got back with no problems. We are hoping for more of the same as we rack up the miles on this well-dressed family hauler.
