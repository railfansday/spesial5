A dozen cities in Los Angeles and Orange counties raised or updated lodging taxes in recent elections as cities prepare for a surge in visitors during the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics.

Five months after Los Angeles voters rejected an increase to the city’s lodging tax, the surrounding suburbs hiked their own.

Preliminary results show 12 cities in Los Angeles and Orange counties approving changes to lodging taxes on November 3, according to the California Local Government Finance Almanac. Ten created or raised rates, with two cities pushing their hotel rates above Los Angeles’ 14% tax. Two expanded their current tax to cover more types of stays.

The changes come as Los Angeles and the surrounding area prepare for a surge in visitation for the 2028 Olympics, which are expected to draw in roughly 2 million visitors to the greater Los Angeles area, and as local lawmakers work to address widening budget gaps. Five months after Los Angeles voters rejected Measure TT, which would have raised the city's 14% lodging tax to 16%, 12 cities in Los Angeles and Orange counties approved lodging tax changes on November 3, according to preliminary results from the California Local Government Finance Almanac. Ten cities created or raised rates, and two expanded coverage to more types of stays. Hawthorne, which borders Inglewood and its Olympic venues, jumped from 12% to 17% and expects as much as $3.1 million in annual revenue. Lynwood approved its first hotel tax at 12%, and Orange and Placentia each went from 10% to 14%. The revenue goes to general funds rather than tourism-specific uses, and the measures come as the region prepares for roughly 2 million visitors for the 2028 Olympics and as local lawmakers work to address widening budget gaps. Short-term rentals lost some of their tax edge: Alhambra extended its 12% occupancy tax to vacation rentals, Monterey Park will tax short-term rental guests at 16% against 14% for hotels, and Santa Monica's council can now apply the lodging tax to corporate stays over 30 days during "major event periods." Statewide, 29 of 34 lodging tax measures were passing, the second largest wave in a decade. Outside California, results were mixed — Tulsa nearly doubled its rate to 9.9% with hotel association backing and tourism earmarks, while voters in Oklahoma, Oregon, Florida, and Mississippi rejected measures.
