Delta Platinum Amex Card Review: Big Benefits & Strong Welcome Offer

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Delta SkyMiles® Platinum American Express Card is the second most premium personal credit card in the Delta SkyMiles and Amex co-brand card portfolio.

If you fly Delta several times a year and want perks like a companion certificate and the ability to spend toward elite status qualification, the Delta Platinum Amex is worth a look, and can easily be worth more than the fee. This post is my in-depth Delta Platinum Card review, so let’s get into all the details.

Let’s cover the welcome bonus, eligibility details, and fees, of the Delta Platinum Card. After all, these are major factors that people consider when applying for cards.

The Delta Platinum Card currently has the following welcome offer: You may be eligible for as high as 90,000 Bonus Miles after you spend $3,000 in eligible purchases on your new Card in your first 6 months of Card Membership. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, SkyMiles are worth 1.1 cents each. That means to me, the welcome bonus is worth up to $990. While Delta SkyMiles is far from my favorite mileage currency, it does have quite a high floor value, so there are good ways to redeem Delta SkyMiles.

The welcome offer on the Delta Platinum Card is “once in a lifetime,” so it’s not available if you currently have the card, or if you’ve had the card in the past. You’re also not eligible for the welcome offer on this card if you have or have had the personal version of the Delta SkyMiles® Reserve American Express Card (learn more). However, you are eligible for this card if you’ve had any version of the business Delta Amex cards before.

One thing worth emphasizing is that if you’re going to apply for multiple personal Delta Amex cards, you’ll want to pick up the less premium cards first, and then work your way up. That way you’re potentially eligible for the welcome offers on each of the cards. Meanwhile if you start with the most premium card, you wouldn’t be eligible for the offers on the less premium cards.

You can read more about Amex’s general application restrictions here.

The Platinum Delta Amex has a $350 annual fee (Rates & Fees). You can add additional users to the card at no extra cost (Rates & Fees). As mentioned above, this is the second highest annual fee personal card in the Delta and Amex portfolio.

If you are going to put spending on the Delta Platinum Amex, the good news is that there are no foreign transaction fees (Rates & Fees), so you can use the card abroad without paying high fees.

When applying for a credit card, the rewards structure is obviously a major consideration for people. With airline credit cards, the question typically comes down to both the miles offered on spending, as well as the ability to qualify for elite status with spending. How does the Delta Platinum Card do on that front? Well, among airline cards, I’d say it’s pretty competitive, so let’s cover those details.

Since I value Delta miles at 1.1 cents, I think this card offers a 3.3% return on its 3x miles categories (purchases made directly with Delta and directly with hotels), a 2.2% return on its 2x miles categories (restaurants worldwide and U.S. supermarkets), and a 1.1% return on non-bonus spending.

The card has solid bonus categories for a co-branded airline card, but purely in terms of rewards for spending, I think there are still better cards out there for airfare purchases, hotel purchases, restaurant purchases, and grocery store purchases.

If you’re serious about earning elite status with credit card spending, you’ll want to consider the more premium Delta SkyMiles® Reserve American Express Card (learn more) instead, since it earns MQDs twice as fast — you earn $1 MQD per $10 of eligible purchases, rather than $1 MQD per $20 of eligible purchases.

While Delta SkyMiles is far from my favorite mileage currency, it does have a high floor value, so there are good ways to redeem Delta SkyMiles. You can redeem SkyMiles for flights on Delta or SkyTeam partners, like Air France, China Airlines, and Korean Air. You can also redeem SkyMiles on airlines like LATAM and WestJet, which aren’t part of SkyTeam.

Partner award flights can be an especially good option if you focus on flights that don’t touch the United States, since flights that do are more expensive and get you less value for your miles. I’ve also seen some good award prices for Delta One (business class) awards, specifically for booking roundtrip rather than one way.

The Platinum Delta Credit Card has a nice welcome offer. In general, I’d say it could be worth putting spending on the card if you’re trying to earn Medallion elite status with Delta SkyMiles. For most people, the reason you’ll want to keep this card long term is because of the perks and benefits that it offers, which for many people will more than offset the annual fee. Let’s go over some of those benefits.

Every year upon renewal of your Delta Platinum Card, you can receive a Delta Main roundtrip companion certificate. You just have to pay the government-imposed taxes and fees for the companion. This is a great deal that can be worth significantly more than the annual fee in and of itself.

If redeeming the certificate there are some things to be aware of:

Just for having the Delta Platinum Card, you can receive up to $150 in Delta Stays credit annually. The way this works, you can receive $150 in statement credits per calendar year when you use your card to pay for a new Delta Stays prepaid hotel or vacation rental booking on the Delta Stays platform.

While I’m not usually a fan of using airline portals to book hotel stays and vacation rentals, this could prove worthwhile, as you can use it for a hotel stay costing right around $150, if you wanted to. If used right, there’s real value here.

Just for having the Delta Platinum Card, you receive up to $120 in rideshare credits annually (Enrollment required). The way this works, you can receive up to $10 in statement credits each month after using your card on U.S. rideshare purchases with select providers. This is super easy to unlock, though admittedly the increments as such are quite small.

Just for having the Delta Platinum Card, you receive up to $120 in Resy credits annually (Enrollment required). The way this works, you can receive up to $10 in statement credits each month after using your card on eligible purchases with U.S. Resy restaurants.

Just for having the Platinum Delta Card you get a first checked bag free. With the ever-increasing cost of a checked bag with Delta, this can really add up.

Depending on how many bags you’re checking, a single trip could pay for the card’s annual fee.

You receive priority boarding just for having the Delta Platinum Amex. This helps maximize your odds of finding space for your carry-on and getting to your seat sooner. To receive this benefit, you’ll need to ensure the primary cardmember’s SkyMiles number is linked to the reservation (though there’s no need to actually pay for the flight with the card).

This benefit is valid for a total of up to nine people traveling on the same reservation with the primary cardmember.

If you have the Delta Platinum Card, then you can save 20% in the form of a statement credit for eligible pre-purchased meals, and in-flight purchases of food, alcoholic beverages, and audio headsets, on Delta flights. Allow 8-12 weeks for the statement credit to post.

The Platinum Delta Amex offers a Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credit. This benefit applies once every four years for the application fee for TSA PreCheck or Global Entry. This can save you time both at security and immigration, and is something I highly recommend signing up for if eligible. (Enrollment required)

Delta has a Pay With Miles feature, where if you have a Delta co-branded credit card, you can redeem miles for one cent each toward a Delta flight purchase (in increments of 5,000 miles per $50). That’s not the best deal in the world, though this is a feature that’s exclusively available to those with an eligible Delta Amex card.

Some people find this to be useful since they can redeem their miles toward flights, and then they still earn miles and MQDs on the portion of the fare they pay in cash.

With the TakeOff 15 perk (exclusively for those with select Delta Amex cards), you can save 15% on the cost of a SkyMiles award ticket when traveling exclusively on Delta (the discount doesn’t apply to taxes and fees). Delta SkyMiles award pricing is dynamic, and generally you’ll find that each mile gets you a bit over a cent toward the cost of a ticket.

Saving 15% on these awards is valuable, and means that you’ll generally get somewhere around 1.2-1.3 cents of value per mile (or sometimes even more). In many cases this will end up being a better value than using the Pay With Miles option, so it’s a real perk of the card.

One of the great features of Amex cards is access to Amex Offers, which offers savings or bonus points on purchases with all kinds of retailers. There’s huge value to be had in getting as many Amex cards as possible, so that you can get these offers on multiple cards.

Amex Offers could save you hundreds of dollars per year. You can access these offers by logging into your account and scrolling down to the bottom of your account summary page. (Enrollment required)

The Delta Platinum Credit Card isn’t necessarily the best card for the casual Delta flyer, and it’s also not a card I’d recommend putting much spending on, unless you value the MQDs that you can earn with the card. Even if you value MQDs, you’re better off with the more premium card, since you can earn them twice as fast.

However, this card has solid perks, ranging from a companion certificate, to free checked bags, to priority boarding, to savings on award flights, to access to exclusive award redemption opportunities, to valuable statement credits, and more.

Bigger picture, is the Delta Platinum Card worth it, though? There are a few things to consider.

If the Delta Platinum Amex sounds interesting to you but you are instead looking for a card for your small business, consider the Delta SkyMiles® Platinum Business American Express Card.

Most of the benefits of the card are the same. The main earning difference is that the business card earns 1.5x miles on transit, at U.S. shipping providers, and on single eligible purchases of $5,000 or more (on up to $100,000 of combined eligible purchases per year), while the personal card offers 2x miles at restaurants worldwide and U.S. supermarkets (both cards offer 3x miles on Delta purchases and hotel purchases).

The business version of the card also offers an annual Delta Stays credit of up to $200, rather than one of up to $150, so it’s arguably more lucrative.

The Delta Gold Card offers the same discounts you get with the Delta Platinum Card, as well as the rideshare credit, but in this case it only kicks in after your first renewal. The Delta Gold Card doesn’t earn MQDs toward status, so that’s another potential downside to keep in mind.

For the very frequent Delta flyer, there’s the Delta SkyMiles® Reserve American Express Card (learn more), which has a $650 annual fee (Rates & Fees). What differentiates this card?

If you are looking to earn Delta SkyMiles through credit card spending then consider using a card earning Amex Membership Rewards points. These points can be transferred to Delta SkyMiles at a 1:1 ratio, and you can in many cases earn points at a much faster rate.

For example, the American Express® Gold Card (learn more) offers 4x points at restaurants worldwide (on up to $50K per calendar year, and then 1x) and at U.S. supermarkets (on up to $25K per calendar year, and then 1x), and 3x points on flights booked directly with airlines or at amextravel.com

Furthermore, earning Amex points gives you more flexibility to redeem in other ways, given all the great uses of Membership Rewards points.
