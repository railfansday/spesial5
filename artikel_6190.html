The best cruise lines for families allow parents and kids to have fun together and apart while taking the stress out of group travel.

Our favorite family ships mix wow-factor activities with attractive kids clubs, plentiful and convenient dining and thoughtful touches like bathtubs and babysitting.

Your family might be happy anywhere there’s a beach or pool, but if you want an at-sea family vacation you’ll always remember, book a sailing with one of our top five cruise lines for families.

No one will be surprised that entertainment juggernaut Disney offers an amazing family cruise experience.

Disney Cruise Line ships are known for meet-and-greets with Mickey Mouse and Disney princesses, interactive movie-themed dining experiences and enormous kids clubs and play areas.

What might surprise the uninitiated is how good the line is at taking care of the whole family, not just the Mouse-obsessed little ones.

Disney thinks of all the little things that can help parents have a stress-free trip with kids in tow. From split bathrooms (allowing one family member to take a bath while another uses the toilet) to waiters who will cut up your child’s food, Disney Cruise Line takes the pressure off parents.

While other cruise lines limit water play to kids who are toilet trained and drop-off child care to ages 3 and up, Disney caters to all kids with onboard splash areas and nurseries for kids ages 6 months to 3 years.

Kids ages 3 to 10 live it up in the expansive Oceaneer Club, with attractions like an interactive replica of Star Wars’ Millennium Falcon and a play area designed to look like Andy’s Room from “Toy Story.”

Teens might roll their eyes at the ever-present Mickeys, but Disney Cruise Line does not ignore its older kid guests. Tweens and teens get their own cool hangouts, Edge (ages 11 to 14) and Vibe (ages 14 to 17), with video games, foosball and seating areas.

Additionally, first-run movies and Broadway-caliber musicals, waterslides, teen spa treatments and grab-and-go food options (and free room service) keep older kids busy and happy.

Sister ships Disney Wish, Disney Treasure and Disney Destiny feature Hero Zone sports areas for free play and competitions attractive to this age group. (Keep an eye out for the Disney Believe, too, debuting in late 2027.)

Grownups on Disney get to play as hard as they parent. Ships offer adults-only pool decks and restaurants, a nightlife scene and couples spa treatments to revive exhausted parents who haven't had a date in months.

The only caveat is that Disney Cruise Line is the most expensive cruise line on this list. At press time, seven-night Caribbean cruises start at $3,180 per person.

With some of the biggest ships at sea and an amusement park-like private island in the Bahamas, Royal Caribbean is one of the best cruise lines for active families.

Want to try surfing, sky diving, rock climbing, laser tag, bumper cars or zip lining? Families don’t have to leave the ship to find their adrenaline highs.

Even the sedentary pursuits are high-octane on Royal Caribbean. Broadway musicals, festive parades, water acrobatic performances and ice skating shows wow the crowds in the evenings and on sea days.

Expansive play spaces for kids includedrop-off nurseries for babies (ages 6 to 36 months) and Adventure Ocean for kids ages 3 to 12 (often broken into separate activities for kids ages 3 to 5, 6 to 8, and 9 to 12) with science labs and art studios on the biggest ships. Teens and tweens ages 13 to 17 get their own cool hangouts, often with activities scheduled around the ship.

While the kids live it up at cruise camp, parents can sneak off to the adults-only solarium or thermal suite.

After a hard day of play, worn-out families can refuel at a variety of restaurants, including burger and milkshake mecca Johnny Rockets, Italian and Mexican eateries and an all-day buffet.

Family cabins and connecting staterooms give larger families their needed space. The Ultimate Family Suite (and similar Ultimate Family Townhouses on some of the line’s biggest ships) can sleep eight to 10 people, and parents and kids alike might be tempted to stay up late playing air hockey, scrambling up the climbing wall, soaking in the private outdoor hot tub or watching the giant TV.

Royal Caribbean’s biggest ships – Icon, Star, and Legend of the Seas – also offers an entire section of the ship devoted to family-friendly accommodations and an outdoor dining-and-play space targeted at parents with young children.

Families should keep an eye out for kids-sail-free deals on select departures, which allow kids ages 12 and younger to pay no cruise fare when they sail as the third or fourth guest in a cabin with two full-fare-paying adults. (Note that families will still have to pay taxes, fees and port charges for kids sailing free.)

Fares for Royal Caribbean cruises can vary based on ship, and its newest, biggest ships often command premium pricing. Seven-night Caribbean cruises start from $444 per person on an older ship, but cost more than $1,000 per person on its biggest Icon Class ships.

Practicalities first: Carnival Cruise Line is an affordable and convenient cruise line for families who don’t live the jet-set lifestyle. With home ports all around the U.S., Carnival puts its ships within driving distance of many Americans.

A variety of short-cruise destinations, affordable-yet-spacious inside cabins (some that sleep up to four people) and lots of onboard attractions and restaurants included in the fares combine to make it one of the best value cruise lines afloat.

Carnival doesn’t dub its fleet the Fun Ships for nothing. The whole family will have a blast on board, whether you like getting down to live music, busting a gut at family-friendly comedy shows, being soaked at an onboard water park or eating green eggs and ham with Dr. Seuss characters.

The cruise line also embraces simple pleasures like free-flowing soft-serve ice cream and pizza and a day spent lounging by the pool.

Families don’t need to pull out their wallets to feed ravenous teens or sugar-fueled children. Guy Fieri burgers and barbecue, burritos for breakfast and lunch and Shaquille O’Neal’s fried chicken are all included in your cruise fares. No one’s going to make you dress up for dinner, either.

Carnival ships have plenty of cabins that can sleep four or five passengers, but six of its newerst ships truly treat families right. Book a Family Harbor cabin or suite on Vista or Excel Class ships and get access to a family lounge stocked with all the essentials — snacks and coffee, games, Wi-Fi, movies and a concierge to make bookings so mom and dad can spend more time playing with the kids.

Seven-night Caribbean cruises start at $443 per person.

It might not be a household name yet, but any cruise line that has a partnership with Lego should be on every family’s radar,  so MSC Cruises is a line families should know.

As the Mediterranean line grows its U.S.-based fleet, it's adding newer and bigger family-friendly ships to more home ports, including Miami, Port Canaveral, Galveston and Seattle.

MSC World America, the line’s newest and biggest ship sailing the Caribbean, features the Family Aventura district, a hub for kids and their grownups. It features The Harbour, an outdoor space with a ropes course, waterpark, playground and 11-deck-long dry slide; indoor sports area for family games and bumper cars; and an enormous Doremiland kids club.

Speaking of kids clubs, MSC’s ships offer play spaces for babies, kids, tweens and teens. Its ships not only offer a drop-off Baby Club (ages 6 months to 3 years) with educational toys by Chicco but also a special baby laundry service to take care of that pile of dirty onesies.

MSC divides young cruisers into Mini Club (ages 3 to 6), Junior Club (ages 7 to 11) and Young Club (12 to 14). Kids club activities go beyond arts and crafts and video games to include cooking events, sports competitions and high-tech interactive games. Teens (ages 15 to 17) can sing karaoke, dance at the onboard disco and attend themed parties with their new friends.

Outside of the kids club, families can race in a Formula One simulator, experience virtual reality, be soaked in a water park or brave their fear of heights on a suspension bridge.

Several MSC Cruises ships offer thrill rides that will appeal to all ages of adventurous cruisers. MSC Seascape features Robotron, a three-seat robotic amusement ride and MSC World America and MSC World Atlantic (coming late 2027) have the Cliffhanger, an overwater swing ride – both are hits with thrill-seeking families.

Multigenerational groups can choose connecting cabins for more space, but families keen on rooming together can take advantage of frequent "kids sail free" fares and pay only the taxes and fees for children under 18 who are the third and fourth passengers in a cabin. Seven-night Caribbean cruises start from $299 per person.

Norwegian Cruise Line is all about giving cruisers options to create different versions of the perfect cruise vacation, all within the same ship.

The flexibility and choice make it one of the best cruise lines for families, especially those who need to satisfy diverse interests or who are easily bored with the same activities day after day.

Kids clubs and adults-only sundecks allow for age-appropriate fun time apart, but Norwegian wants families to have fun together. Its Splash Academy engages kids ages 3 to 12 with group activities like team challenges and crafts, while Entourage is the place for teens ages 13 to 17 to make friends, hang out playing foosball and video games or show off their moves during evening dance parties. Adults can hide out in the 18+ Vibe Beach Club and Spice H2O for some sunbathing and hot tub time while the kids are engaged in youth activities.

Norwegian also wants families to have fun together. Teaming up to solve a murder mystery, mastering a ropes course, singing along to a high-energy musical show or live concert or screaming down a waterslide are guaranteed to create those unbreakable family bonds.

Norwegian’s flexible dining system and a plethora of onboard eateries let families plan meals to suit their schedules and tastes.

Also, free kids meals at extra-charge restaurants mean parents can sample Brazilian, Japanese, French or seafood dishes while the littles gobble up pasta and chicken fingers. Casual all-day options suit fussy eaters who need unfussy dining areas.

The cruise line also gets a shoutout for its family-friendly Hawaii itineraries. As the only big-ship cruise line that sails weeklong itineraries round trip from Honolulu, Norwegian provides an easy way for families to visit multiple islands.

Beach time, boat rides, water sports, luaus and volcano tours combine for an epic family trip, with the ship taking the stress out of meals and transportation.

In the Bahamas, Norwegian’s enhanced Great Stirrup Cay private island now features a waterpark with a variety of slides, a cliff jump and a not-that-lazy river (entrance fee required), as well as free use of a giant pool and a splash playground for little kids, making beach days even more fun for vacationing families.

To make it all more affordable, families should keep an eye on Norwegian’s Free at Sea promotion, which sometimes includes free third and fourth guest fares. That means that kids sharing a room with two paying adults sail free. Fares for seven-night Caribbean cruises start at $399 per person.

Families can have a great time on almost any cruise line, hanging out by the pool, visiting exciting new destinations and simply spending time together. But if you’re looking for a ship that really caters to kids and their adult companions, you’ll want to book a vacation with one of these five best cruise lines for families.
