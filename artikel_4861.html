Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Vitalik Buterin called for verifiable chips to counter evolving hardware wallet threats, computer scientist Scott Aaronson warned that leading AI labs are likely sitting on undisclosed cryptography breakthroughs and US regulators authorized SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million satellites for orbital AI data centers.

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin on Thursday called for enhanced verifiability in computer chips, citing evolving security threats in the hardware wallet sector.

Buterin said in a blog post that most commercial open-source chips do not disclose enough detail to verify their physical construction. Publishing what a chip is programmed to do is different from providing a blueprint against which inspectors can check the hardware itself.

“This concern is not just theoretical,” Buterin said, adding that the crypto industry has seen attacks that involve inserting spy implants into hardware wallets before they get sold to consumers.

Buterin warned that future attacks could be harder to detect, with malicious functions built into replacement chips that resemble legitimate ones.

“This, you cannot detect with the naked eye,” he wrote.

Theoretical computer scientist Scott Aaronson says leading AI laboratories are likely sitting on undisclosed breakthroughs in cryptography.

In a blog post on Wednesday, Aaronson, a former OpenAI researcher and professor at The University of Texas at Austin, warned that algorithms emerging from AI-driven mathematical advances will eventually break currently deployed cryptosystems.

“I’m virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources, that the AI labs are now sitting on one or more bombshells in that area,” Aaronson said. “However, until I either see details or hear directly from someone at a lab, I feel much, much less confident about the specific nature of those bombshells.”

The remarks add to concerns that AI’s accelerating mathematical capabilities could eventually undermine the cryptography used to secure crypto wallets and blockchain transactions. Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin and researcher Justin Drake raised those concerns in October.

The US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has authorized Elon Musk’s SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million satellites as part of a planned network of orbital data centers, in what the agency described as a historic first.

According to a Wednesday FCC announcement, the satellite constellation, dubbed “Starmind,” is designed to provide orbital computing infrastructure to meet growing demand for artificial intelligence (AI), processing large amounts of data for use on Earth.

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said the authorization opens a new frontier for computing infrastructure, as SpaceX and other companies seek to move data processing capacity beyond Earth. The agency said it is continuing to review additional orbital data center proposals.

The Starmind satellites will communicate with one another using optical links, with radio frequencies serving as a backup for communications and emergency operations. The FCC said the system complies with its orbital debris rules.

The FCC did not specify a deployment timeline or estimated cost for the Starmind constellation.
