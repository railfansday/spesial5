The 9 Best Hiking Towns to Spend Your Holidays In

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

(Photo: Photos from Terry's photostream via Flickr Creative Commons)

But not everyone can access hiking right out their front door. So why not turn your holiday break into a hiking vacation? You’ll not only get to make special festive memories outside, but you can choose the geography and even the climate.

With that in mind, we’ve put together a list of the best places for hikers to holiday. Want to spend your time traipsing through deserts, among cacti and palm trees? We’ve got you covered. Looking for a more traditional experience, with snowy trails and evergreen forests? No problem. And, of course, if you like long walks on the beach and among tropical rainforests, we’ve got just the places for you.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Mild weather, southwestern culture, diverse landscapes, and amazing food December temps: 40-65℉ What to expect: Both desert and mountain landscapes with rocky, sandy terrain, and no snow

For those hikers who’d rather decorate a saguaro cactus than a lodgepole pine, Tucson, Arizona is calling your name. The wildernesses around town are packed with desert flora and fauna, and crisscrossed with tons of hiking trails. This can be a tough place to explore during late spring, summer, and even into fall because it gets so scorching hot. However, in the winter, the weather is perfect for hiking, making this a prime place to spend the holidays. Beyond the trails, the food is world-renowned—Tucson has been a UNESCO City of Gastronomy for over a decade.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Fair weather, proximity to Gila National Forest and Cliff Dwellings Monument, colorful western history, and a great art scene December temps: 21-55℉ What to expect: Colder, high desert climate, with very clear weather, and tons of trails exploring the Pinos Altos and Mogollon Mountains

Situated on the outskirts of the Gila National Forest, Silver City is a gateway to desert hiking adventures. The Continental Divide Trail passes nearby; City of Rocks State Park is just 30 miles east of town, and the famous Gila Cliff Dwellings National Monument is just 44 miles north. The monument area gets over 200 cloudless nights a year, making it a prime destination for winter stargazers. If you want a warm soak after your hikes, Faywood Hot Springs and Gila Hot Springs are both close to town.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Mild temperatures, direct access to Zion National Park, regional access to Bryce Canyon or Grand Canyon, and a lively restaurant and art scene December temps: 30-55℉ What to expect:  A striking red sandstone desert landscape and trails both in the park and outside of it

Winter is Zion National Park’s slow season, and yet it’s just as beautiful as it is any other time of year. Springdale sits just outside the park’s eastern entrance and is a prime location for a hiking holiday basecamp. Not only will you have direct access to the national park and all of its trails, but there are also worthy routes around town that don’t require an entrance fee. And if you aren’t afraid of a day trip, Bryce Canyon is 86 miles away.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Mild temperatures, access to Lake Mead, fun holiday atmosphere, and a historic downtown December temps: 35-55℉ What to expect: Wide Mojave desert terrain with rolling hills and rocky mountains, canyons, panoramic lake views, and old mining and railroad infrastructure

The deserts of southern Nevada have a lot to offer beyond the flashing lights, casinos, and nightlife of Las Vegas. About an hour southeast of Sin City, Boulder City is a much quieter historic town that offers access to some amazing trails and outdoor recreation areas. It’s just a short drive from Boulder City to Lake Mead Recreation Area, where there are a lot of trails, walkways, and observation points. Some hikes close to town are short and approachable, while others challenge hikers with scrambles and steep climbs. The city itself is full of history; it was once an old mining town and played an important role in the Hoover Dam’s construction.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Mountain scenery and trails, access to Blue Ridge Parkway, and relatively mild temperatures and weather December temps: 35-55℉ What to expect: Forested mountains, waterfalls, and (potentially) snowy trails

Within minutes of Asheville, you’ll find waterfall hikes, mountain overlooks, and plenty of access to public lands. The Blue Ridge Mountains make a beautiful backdrop for town, and an even more spectacular place to explore by trail. Asheville’s thickly wooded forests are magical to hike, with trails following icy creeks and ascending ridges to spectacular views. After your hike, the town is renowned for its craft beer scene and festive holiday light displays. If a cozy Appalachian holiday is your vibe, look no further.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Situated high in the Rocky Mountains, lively restaurant and bar scene, trails in every direction, and close to two ski resorts December temps: 12-42℉ What to expect: Cold-weather mountain hikes, snowy trails, old-growth forests, and high desert terrain

Durango, Colorado, is a pure western mountain ski town and a genuinely cozy place to spend the holidays. The San Juan Mountains offer incredible hiking for all levels, whether you just want to stroll along a creek or climb for panoramic views of the Rockies. This region gets a lot of snow in the wintertime, so be prepared for cold weather with the right gear, knowledge, and expectations. For holiday adventurers, Durango is packed with things to do in the winter. Beyond the hiking trails, ride the historic Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, or go skiing at Wolf Creek or Purgatory Resorts.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Warm, subtropical winter weather, surrounded by ample inland and beach hiking, and deeply historic roots December temps: 50-70℉ What to expect: Sand dune beaches, flatland ecosystems, wetlands, and marshes

St. Augustine offers a unique take on the traditional winter holiday. It’s coastal, subtropical, and offers landscapes and ecosystems you’ll only find in Florida—from swamp conservation areas to beach parks and historic forts. There are over 30 trails and walkways around St. Augustine, and it’s within an hour’s drive of six state parks. Most of the hiking is very approachable, following beaches, boardwalks, sand dunes, and mostly flat terrain. The town was settled over 450 years ago, so it’s also a great place for history buffs, and it annually hosts a Christmas parade and “Night of Lights” throughout December.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Warm, tropical weather, dramatic natural scenery, laid-back island culture, and great hiking access December temps: 65-80℉ What to expect: Lush rainforests, beautiful beaches, mountains spouting waterfalls, and the opportunity to surf big swells

Hawaii is a famous holiday destination for obvious reasons. In the winter, the climate is pleasant; the landscape is dramatic, there are beaches a’plenty, and the hiking access is hard to beat. Waterfalls spout from the mountains, and trails wind through thick rainforest, popping out on beaches that you might have all to yourself. Hanalei, on Kauai’s North Shore, offers some of Hawaii’s best (and most diverse) hiking. Nearby are several forest reserves, Kōke’e State Park, and the Hanalei National Wildlife Refuge.

Why it’s a great holiday town: Bavarian influence, Christmas spirit, and ample winter trails to explore December temps: 15-35℉ What to expect: Snowy trails through old-growth forests and mountains, and a town that really loves to celebrate Christmas

Tucked away in the Cascade Mountains of Washington, along the Wenatchee River, this Bavarian-influenced town is one of the best places to spend the holidays on this list. The hiking in the surrounding mountains offers access to postcard-worthy alpine lakes, waterfalls, and ridges. It ranges from strenuous and challenging to easy and mellow. You’ll want snowshoes and/or microspikes if you plan on holidaying in Leavenworth, as the trails will likely be snowy and/or icy in places. The town is well-known for its Christmas zeal: It has a “Christmastown,” a Bavarian village that comes to life with live music, carols, ice carving, fireworks, and, of course, appearances by Santa Claus.
