Science Finds that Dairy May Not Lead to Excess Fat as First Thought

For a long time, the public were cautioned against regular dairy food consumption, but a recent study from the University of Toronto, published by the Journal of Nutrition, appears to show that frequently delving into eggs, milk, and cheeses, could actually benefit your health and won’t make you fat. Here’s why.

In the ‘70s, a war on saturated fat consumption encouraged us to avoid products like whole milk, butter, and cheese because of the high levels that they contain. Then wellness culture in the new millennium further derailed the case for diary, citing inflammation, acne, and disrupted hormones on this food stuff. Of course, some people are lactose intolerant, and have problems processing dairy food, but the Toronto study has challenged the campaign against dairy, with some surprisingly edible results.

74 overweight adults undertook a 12-week study and were randomly assigned to one of three groups:

All the groups were counseled on healthy eating, so that they could manage their food intake. At the completion of the trial, the team took measurements including body weight, composition, cholesterol, blood markers, and metabolic health.

In both of the regular high fat dairy eating groups, the participants experienced improvements in their blood pressure while consuming a greater amount of protein, calcium, and vitamin D than the first group. Importantly, neither their waistlines nor BMI suffered. “Those that had three servings of dairy didn’t have adverse levels of blood cholesterol or lipids or evidence of insulin resistance,” explained Gerald Harvey Anderson, who was the lead author and works in the Faculty of Medicine at the University of Toronto.”

With full-fat dairy containing more saturated fat than reduced fat products, how could this be? The experts believe that dairy’s combination of fats and protein is what makes it a positive food. “With dairy products, it’s got two proteins, casein and whey, that are bound together with fat and with nutrients mixed in,” said Anderson. The “Dairy Matrix Hypothesis” suggests that the physical structure of dairy determines how the body processes its nutrient profile.

Of course, ice cream, milkshakes, and other treats that contain a ton saturated fat as well as massive amounts of sugar is a different story, but the Toronto study said its results with natural dairy products showed that  “Neither treatment nor time affected waist circumference, fat and fat-free mass, resting metabolic rate, fasting blood cholesterol, and urine creatinine.” The team concluded that “Frequent and daily consumption of full-fat dairy as part of a healthy diet is consistent with CFG (Canada’s Food Guide).”

This news also adds to another promising benefit of dairy, that was reported earlier this year. The American Academy of Neurology showed that those who regularly ate high fat cheese presented a 13% lower risk of dementia overall, and a 29% risk of vascular dementia. Cream was also associated with a 16% lower risk of all-type dementias.

The Toronto study found dairy to be a significant source of magnesium and potassium to boot. “The results align with the conclusion of an expert group that there is no evidence to support the avoidance of high-fat dairy in diets,” it said.
