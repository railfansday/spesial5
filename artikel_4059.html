Peter Moore says this is "more than the release of a hugely anticipated game"

As GTA 6 brings in rave reviews and – predictably – takes over the internet as millions of players dive into Rockstar's long-awaited blockbuster, industry veteran Peter Moore says "the video game industry could really use a moment like this."

Moore, who previously served as president of Sega of America, corporate vice president of Microsoft's home and entertainment division responsible for Xbox marketing and operations, and CEO of EA, has taken to Twitter in the wake of Rockstar's latest launch. First, he offers an AI-modified image of himself with a GTA 6 tattoo in place of his infamous GTA 4 tattoo, which was revealed at E3 2006 to confirm the game's simultaneous launch on Xbox 360 and PS3.

But "in all seriousness," he adds, GTA 6's launch is an important one, and not just for the fans who've been waiting 13 years to play a new Grand Theft Auto game.

"We've been through a challenging period," he acknowledges. "Studio closures, redundancies, canceled projects and enormous uncertainty have taken their toll on an industry that has given so much to so many, myself included.

"That's why the launch of GTA VI feels so significant," he continues. "It's more than the release of a hugely anticipated game. It's an opportunity to remind the world of the extraordinary power of this industry to create, innovate, entertain and bring people together."

As someone who's spent so much time in the games industry, Moore says, "I've seen this industry reinvent itself time and time again." With that in mind, he hopes GTA 6 is able to deliver "the commercial success we all expect, but perhaps more importantly, I hope it provides a much-needed shot in the arm (no pun intended…) for the entire ecosystem: developers, publishers, studios, retailers, technology partners and the next generation of creative talent." After all, he says, "The games industry has always been at its best when it dares to dream big."

Well, as far as things go on the commercial front, it looks like the game has had an extremely strong start. Rockstar hasn't revealed any official figures, but with analysts' old launch week predictions going as high as $5.2 billion, and more recent estimates placing the revenue as of yesterday at $1.65 billion, it's safe that plenty of folks are buying it.
