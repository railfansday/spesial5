The rumored OLED iMac may be canceled or delayed

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Apple just launched a new iMac in October, but rumors are already swirling about the next one, and they're not entirely positive.

The Elec reported on Thursday that Apple is considering delaying or even canceling a hypothetical OLED-equipped iMac model, which was theoretically supposed to launch in 2029 or 2030. According to the report, which cited an unnamed industry source, Apple has been working with Samsung Display (a prolific supplier of device displays) on the project, and has been discussing a potential delay or cancellation with the Korean firm.

"For now, discussions are focused on delaying the schedule and monitoring market conditions," an anonymous industry official said, per The Elec.

Unsurprisingly, money seems like the biggest factor here. Apple is reportedly concerned about the cost of equipping its all-in-one desktop computers with fancy (but expensive) OLED displays, given that iMacs don't sell at nearly the same volume as some of Apple's other flagship products, like the iPhone. The Elec also cited weak sales of the iPad Pro, which has a ProMotion OLED display, as a factor here. Given that Apple also has a new CEO as of September, it makes sense that some plans made under Tim Cook's direction might be reconsidered now that John Ternus is in charge.

While an OLED iMac would certainly turn some heads, it also makes sense that these plans are for three or four years from now. Apple launched an M6 model just last month with a $1,499 starting price, but besides the new chip, the all-in-one is very similar to previous iMacs, dating all the way back to 2021.

However, the company did recently release an OLED-equipped MacBook Pro for the first time, so it's clearly interested in giving its Mac devices superior displays. The MacBook Pro OLED is also $900 more expensive than regular MacBook Pros, a steep increase.

We'll just have to wait and see if the iMac ever gets that treatment, or if Apple gives up on it because of the high cost of production.
