£329m spent and still no goals for sorry Tottenham

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Tottenham Hotspur have never before in the club’s history begun a league season without scoring a single goal in the first four games, although having done so after spending £329m on new players really does take some doing.

“Please score a goal!” was the homemade sign from two Spurs fans upon which the television cameras lingered at the end of the game, although the jaunty felt-tip design and glued-on team crest were rather upbeat compared to the gloom of the stadium. Spurs have tried to outrun their past but it just keeps catching up with them.

A team reimagined, a manager freshly launched and yet they are back in the same grip of anxiety that accompanied them on two consecutive 17th-place finishes in the last two seasons. By the end of this they might have been glad to escape with a single point against Everton, the one team in the Premier League who closed the window with a net transfer profit.

For Roberto De Zerbi, there was another passionate defence of his team afterwards. “The people, the fans already understand if I make excuses or not,” he said. “I came last season when the s--- was like this”, he gestured to a level somewhere around the midpoint of his forehead. “I have always been… not positive but realistic. I am sorry for the result that it’s not immediately the improvement. We played as a team. We didn’t score. We didn’t win [but] we played as a team.”

Indeed he co-opted the Italian term “sbloccare” to describe his team’s problem. They simply need to unlock – or unblock – the team and the goals would flow. He joked that in Italy he would be lauded for two consecutive clean sheets in this game and the previous one against Nottingham Forest. “What did you write?” he demanded of one of the Italian newspaper correspondents. “You have the passport.”

Spurs have one last game at home this month, on Saturday against Aston Villa, to end the streak in September. They were into the second half before the first attempt on target that obliged Jordan Pickford to make a save – Mateus Fernandes’s shot slapping limply against the Everton goalkeeper’s gloves to ironic cheers from the home fans.

“There is a term in Italian, ‘sbloccare’ that literally translates as ‘unblock’… I know we can score goals and win games. We have to respect the history of the players – if you forget [Dominic] Solanke and [Omar] Marmoush the others haven’t scored many in their careers. I think we can find the goals with the quality of the players.”

Spurs play Liverpool at Anfield in the Carabao Cup on Tuesday before Villa a week today. The Spurs record at home is dismal. In the last 35 games they have won just five, drawn 10 and lost a remarkable 20. This new stadium has become a problem for the team in it – or maybe it is the other way around.

A good result for Everton, who were under pressure for most of the first half but had much the better of it after the break. David Moyes’s team finished the game with more shots on target than the home team. Moyes managed the game well and trusted his team to withstand the Spurs possession in the first half until they reached a point where Everton could start to push back.

Hard to pick out any major chances – it was that kind of game – but Brennan Johnson should have won it for the away team in the second half. The ball was cut back to him by Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall and the former Spurs man skied his shot.

Some surprised faces then when Moyes said later that he had expected a lot more from his team. He acknowledged the resilience in defence and the importance of the clean sheet. “We played well enough,” he said, “but when we had the ball we turned it over more often than we should have done.”

The game had started with Vitaliy Mykolenko being tortured by the pace of Sávio and ended with the Ukrainian left-back being given the man of the match award. Like many of his team-mates, he had hung in there. There was another fine performance from Jarrad Branthwaite at centre-half and Moyes said that he hoped it might earn him a place in the England squad. “He is still finding his way back from injury,” Moyes said, “it would be great if he could get a call-up.”

In the directors’ box were the usual pained expressions of Vinai Venkatesham, the Spurs chief executive, and chairman Peter Charrington. They sat alongside Vivienne Lewis, daughter of the patriarch Joe. There is a certain fatigue about the home crowd when faced with afternoons like this which suggests they have seen so much that the bar is quite high – or low – to provoke a really splenetic response. Perhaps it is just too early in the season although the final whistle was met with boos.

The first shot on target of the entire game for either side was a free-kick struck by James Garner in added time at the end of the first half. Spurs had started at a ferocious pace although they could not sustain it.

De Zerbi complained about the pre-season injuries on tour in Australia and also the lack of time he had to integrate Sávio and Marmoush. He said that his team were not in the right “physical condition”.

“We played very well in the first 30 minutes and maybe the energy and the fact we didn’t score – we lost confidence.” By the end, he had sent on James Maddison, Tel, Lucas Bergvall and Mohammed Kudus to little avail. Everton looked more likely to win it.

Speaking to Sky Sports, the Everton defender said: “You look at the game from the start, they put us under pressure and we had to dig deep to stay in the game. Then we grew into the game towards the end of the first half and showed that mentality again in the second half.

“We had chances to win the game as well. The backs to the wall mentality will help us go a long way. If we can keep clean sheets, you can go a long way in the league. But it’s not just us the defenders. It’s the whole team working hard. It’s a team effort.”

Midfielder took exception to negativity surrounding club after moves for players on deadline day failed to materialise.

“We signed Jack [Grealish], which was nice, and I’d been speaking to him,” says Dewsbury-Hall, sitting in a café at the club’s Finch Farm training base. “But I had seen all the stuff [online], all the meltdowns about this and that and, in all honestly, it annoyed me a bit.

“I get where people are coming from. Everyone wants to sign everyone and have the perfect squad, but there are still 19 lads in this building who are going to hopefully take this club to good things this year. That was getting disregarded. I was seeing people saying: ‘We’ll be lucky to stay up this year.’

“What message is that sending to the lads who are already here? I’m not blaming anyone. We get it. It didn’t work. But let’s all be positive – right, it’s done, bang, let’s go. That’s what we’ve been trying to say in the dressing room.”

Earlier this week, Mykhailo Mudryk picked up an ankle injury in training which has ruled the Chelsea loanee out of action for as long as two months.

The winger only made his debut for Tottenham as a late substitute against Nottingham Forest last week, but his attempt to rebuild his career following his anti-doping ban has been dealt a major blow.

Folarin Balogun’s proposed £39m move from Monaco collapsed on transfer deadline day, Richarlison’s return to the club didn’t materialise and Beto was sold to Fiorentina, leaving Thierno Barry as Everton’s only recognised striker.

Hello and welcome to our live coverage of this Premier League meeting between Tottenham and Everton in the capital.

Are Tottenham in must-win territory already? It may seem reactionary and premature, but after spending £380m in the summer to revamp an underperforming squad, Tottenham sit inside the relegation zone, with just one point and zero goals scored in three games.

Roberto De Zerbi’s men opened the 2026-27 top-flight season with worrying back-to-back defeats to Brentford and Newcastle United, before ending their losing streak last weekend thanks to a goalless draw against Nottingham Forest.

This game gives Tottenham another opportunity to reverse their dreadful home form. Last season, Spurs managed just three league wins at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium and lost 10. The recent 2-0 loss against Newcastle was a painful reminder of Spurs’ shortcomings at home, but they’ll be expected to be authoritative and seize the initiative here today. A much-needed three points would be the perfect remedy to ease the pressure and unwanted attention on De Zerbi and his squad.

Meanwhile, Everton have made an unbeaten start to the season, accumulating five points from their first three league matches. David Moyes’ side looked set for defeat last time out, when they were trailing Manchester United 2-1 at the Hill Dickinson Stadium heading into stoppage time, until Ainsley Maitland-Niles’ stunning 96th-minute equaliser salvaged a draw.

Alongside drama on the pitch, there has also been noise off it. A bizarre transfer window has left Everton with a serious lack of depth up top and in both full-back positions. Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall responded to the fans’ negativity over this situation, saying their meltdowns “annoyed” him.

Nevertheless, they arrive in North London with all eyes on Tottenham and if the hosts can find a first league victory this season.
