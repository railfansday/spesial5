13 Winners From the The Royal Photographic Society Awards 2026

The Royal Photographic Society (RPS) has announced the recipients of its 2026 Awards, with cinematographer Sir Roger Deakins among this year’s honorees.

Deakins has received the RPS Centenary Medal, the Society’s most prestigious honor. Widely regarded as one of the greatest cinematographers in film history, Deakins has won two Academy Awards for Best Cinematography and five BAFTA Awards in the same category.

British photographer Timon Benson has received the RPS Award for Achievement in the Art of Photography (under 30). At the beginning of his career, Benson was already attracting significant national and international recognition. His work spans documentary photography, choreography, and abstraction, exploring the nature of photography and its relationship with contemporary life.

The RPS Progress Medal in the Science of Photography has been awarded to Professor Marc Levoy, an American computer graphics researcher and Professor Emeritus of Computer Science and Electrical Engineering at Stanford University. Levoy is also a vice president and fellow at Adobe and a Distinguished Engineer at Google.

British photographic artist Mandy Barker is the recipient of The RPS Award for Environmental Responsibility. Barker is internationally recognized for her work documenting marine plastic debris. Working alongside scientists, she uses photography to highlight the scale of plastic pollution in the world’s oceans and its impact on marine life and the wider environment.

Documentary photographer and filmmaker Polly Braden has received the RPS Award for Social Impact. Her long-term collaborative projects explore how people live and work, and their place in society. Her latest project, Against the Tide, produced with The Guardian, followed young people in 17 coastal towns. The project was exhibited at Arnolfini in Bristol and Firstsite in Colchester, and also brought 16 young people to Parliament to give evidence to MPs.

Now in its 148th year, the Royal Photographic Society Awards date back to 1878. The Society describes them as the world’s longest-running photography awards. They recognize achievements across still and moving images, celebrating individuals rather than individual photographs. The awards cover a broad range of disciplines, including moving image, new media, science and imaging, education, publishing, and curation.

Victoria Humphries, CEO of the Royal Photographic Society, says the awards reflect the breadth of the photographic community and the many ways people contribute to the medium.

“The RPS Awards provide an opportunity to recognize and celebrate those who are making a difference, whether through their creativity, innovation, research, education or their contribution to the wider photographic community,” Humphries says.

“As an organization, we believe that photography is for everyone, and these RPS Awards demonstrate that there are so many different ways to engage with, contribute to and shape its future.”

Deakins says he is particularly pleased to be recognized for his still photography, given his career in cinematography.

“I’m very honored to receive this award from the Royal Photographic Society. Although I have been more known for my work in cinematography, still photography has always been a passion and more personal to me. To be recognized by the Royal Photographic Society for my work in stills is amazing and means a great deal to me.”
