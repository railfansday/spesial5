EU Countries Renew Call To Exclude Russia from Venice Ahead of 2027 Architecture Biennale

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Ten European Union countries are demanding “further and sustained efforts” from the European Commission to keep Russia out of the 2027 Venice Architecture Biennial, according to Rapporteur.

The report cites a diplomatic note signed by Poland, Austria, Croatia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Latvia, Lithuania, and Sweden, which says Moscow’s presence on the prestigious cultural stage risks normalizing its war on Ukraine. “In light of the current geopolitical realities… there should be no room for ‘normalisation’ of Russia’s participation in international sporting and cultural events, as long as there is no just and lasting peace in Ukraine,” the note reportedly reads. The group urged the European Union to take measures against Russia’s participation ahead of a meeting of EU culture ministers on Thursday and Friday. Russia returned to the Venice Biennale in May for the first time since invading Ukraine in 2022, to the condemnation of politicians, artists, and cultural workers worldwide. (In 2024, Russia did not participate, choosing instead to lend its pavilion to Bolivia, which does not have a dedicated exhibition space on the Biennale grounds.)

An open letter circulating this year addressed Biennale president Pietrangelo Buttafuoco directly. “The claim that ‘culture is above politics’ is never neutral. In the case of contemporary Russia, this formula has become a political instrument used to promote aggression and advance state agendas while disguising them behind the language of cultural exchange and dialogue,” the letter read. It was signed by Pina Picierno, an Italian politician who serves as vice president of the European Parliament; Francesca Thyssen-Bornemisza, a collector who runs an art space in Venice; and artists Ragnar Kjartansson and Tomás Saraceno, among others.

The European Commission was among the most high-profile voices urging the Biennale’s organizers to exclude Moscow from the exhibition, even threatening to cancel a €2 million grant to the Biennale. (In July, it did suspend the grant.) In a March statement, the Biennale said it “rejects any form of exclusion or censorship of culture and art.” The European Commission consequently withdrew its funding.

Critics were quick to point out that the Biennale’s stance in 2026 appeared to contradict statements it issued in support of Ukraine in 2022, after Russia’s artistic representatives shuttered its Pavilion in protest of the Kremlin-led invasion.

The 2026 Russian Pavilion was the target of protests during the vernissage, including a joint demonstration from the Russian dissident band Pussy Riot and the feminist activist group FEMEN. The protest spilled onto the broader Biennale stage, as pavilions including Japan, Finland, the UK, France, Belgium, Austria, Italy, and the Netherlands saw temporary closures, shortened hours, or staff walkouts during the opening weekend. The pavilion was open during the preview period (May 5–8) for live performances tied to its exhibition, “The Tree Is Rooted in the Sky,” but ultimately closed from May 9 through the remainder of the Biennale, with digital documentation of the performances presented on screens installed in the building’s windows.

In an interview months later with the Italian publication Corriere della Sera, Buttafuoco doubled down on his decision to include Russia. “The Biennale is independent; I’d do it all over again for the Russian pavilion,” he said, adding that, “With us, art wins.”

The upcoming Venice Architecture Biennale is set to open to the public on May 8, 2027, following preview days on May 6 and 7, and runs through November 21, 2027. The theme is “Do Architecture — For the Possibility of Coexistence Facing a Real Reality,” curated by Wang Shu and Lu Wenyu.

ARTnews has contacted the Venice Architecture Biennale organizers for comment.
