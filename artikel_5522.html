Continental is considering closing its South African tire factory, risking about 950 jobs

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company cited underused capacity and low-cost imports; operations in Gqeberha could stop by early 2027 if no alternative emerges

Continental is considering ending tire production at its plant in Gqeberha, South Africa, a move that could affect approximately 950 employees, the company said on Friday.

Continental has started a consultation process under Section 189A of South Africa's Labour Relations Act to explore alternatives and discuss ways to reduce the impact on workers. If the process identifies no viable alternative, operations at the site could be discontinued by early 2027, the company said.

Continental said the plant's production capacity has been underused for several years despite turnaround efforts. The company stated that the intensifying price competition from low-cost imports has placed significant pressure on the African tire market. It added that costlier energy, disrupted supply chains and shaky infrastructure have driven up manufacturing costs and complicated forward planning.

The company said South Africa and the wider African continent remain important markets. Its local sales organization will keep serving customers in South Africa and across the region out of Gqeberha, and its BestDrive retail network will not be affected by any changes to production.

The plant has made tires in Gqeberha since 1947. Production of Continental-branded tires began there in 1985, supplying automakers operating in South Africa and the African replacement market, the company said.

A Continental closure would follow other plant shutdowns in South Africa's auto industry, according to Bloomberg. Manufacturers there have had difficulty holding their own against a growing wave of shipments from China and India. Goodyear Tire & Rubber shut down its tire manufacturing site in South Africa in 2025, ending a run of 78 years.
