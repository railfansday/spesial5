Newrez partners with Upgrade to add personal loans for servicing customers

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Newrez is adding unsecured personal loans for eligible servicing customers through a partnership with fintech firm Upgrade, which will originate and service the loans via issuer Cross River Bank. The product gives existing Newrez borrowers a way to access cash without using their home as collateral or changing their mortgage, with no prepayment fees and soft-credit prequalification.  AI Summary

Multichannel lender Newrez partnered with financial technology firm Upgrade to offer unsecured personal loans to eligible mortgage servicing customers, giving homeowners another option to access cash without tapping home equity or refinancing.

The loans, originated and serviced by Upgrade and issued by its partner Cross River Bank, do not change the existing Newrez mortgage. Newrez funded $12.9 billion in mortgages in the third quarter and reached $878.9 billion in unpaid principal balance across its mortgage servicing rights (MSR) portfolio at the end of September.

According to the company, the loans can be funded in as little as one business day for qualified borrowers, have no prepayment fees and allow customers to see prequalified offers through a soft credit inquiry.

Newrez framed the launch as an extension of its relationships with homeowners beyond the mortgage, with loans designed for uses such as debt consolidation, home improvements and unexpected expenses.

“Our relationship with homeowners doesn’t begin and end with a mortgage,” Leslie Gillin, Newrez’s chief commercial officer, said in a statement. “Homeowners’ financial needs often extend beyond their monthly mortgage payment, and we’re proud to broaden the ways we can support them, including helping identify opportunities for savings as they navigate important financial decisions.”

The launch comes as many homeowners are struggling with revolving debt. In a June 2026 survey conducted by Morning Consult on behalf of Newrez, 51% of homeowners reported carrying credit card balances, with 59% saying the debt is negatively affecting their finances. The online survey of 2,203 U.S. adults was conducted in June 2026.
