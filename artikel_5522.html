Moonshot AI rented restricted Nvidia AI chips inside China, documents show

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Filings valued the 32 Super Micro servers at 432 million yuan ($64.8 million); Moonshot disputed the documents' accuracy

Moonshot AI agreed in August to lease 32 Super Micro Computer servers fitted with Nvidia $NVDA's B300 chips, and filings locate the machines in China, according to Bloomberg. The documents were filed with Beijing regulators.

U.S. rules bar the chips from being shipped to China unless Washington has expressly approved the sale, and Bloomberg said it could not establish whether such approval existed. The filings assigned the servers a combined worth of 432 million yuan ($64.8 million). Moonshot challenged the reliability of the records.

"We neither participated in nor authorized the related financing or guarantee arrangements, and had no knowledge of them," a Moonshot spokesperson said in an emailed statement, according to Bloomberg. The spokesperson said Moonshot has no business relationship with Lingmeixin (Beijing) Technology Co., the server owner.

Bloomberg reviewed two financing contracts signed Aug. 4 by Lingmeixin and Beijing Zhongguancun Bank, the financier. Lingmeixin pledged the servers and 345.6 million yuan in earnings from a service contract with Beijing Dark Side of the Moon Technology Co., Moonshot's main entity, as collateral for a 200 million yuan ($30 million) loan, according to Bloomberg.

A Lingmeixin statement attached to the contracts said the hardware was designated for the Moonshot service contract. It said Moonshot "only has right to use the compute service, with no ownership of the equipment," according to Bloomberg. The contracts list the servers as stored in Qingyang, in western China.

The bank submitted the documents to a database run by the credit agency of the People's Bank of China on Aug. 6, according to Bloomberg. After Bloomberg sent requests for comment on Nov. 3, the database documents carried no mention of Moonshot. Lingmeixin and the bank did not respond to requests for comment.

An Nvidia spokesperson said the companies behind the purchase "are not Nvidia customers," according to Bloomberg. A Super Micro spokesperson said the company "does not conduct business with these entities," according to Bloomberg.

A White House official asserted, once Moonshot's Kimi K3 model came out, that the startup had obtained use of controlled Nvidia hardware, according to Bloomberg. The White House said in July that Moonshot had accessed Nvidia's Blackwell chips by way of a site in Thailand, according to CNBC.

Moonshot has also discussed renting capacity from neoclouds hosting Nvidia chips in Europe, according to CNBC, which cited four unnamed people familiar with the matter. Moonshot did not respond to CNBC's request for comment.

Chinese filings have exposed similar purchases before. Semi-Tech Leasing Group Co., a Chinese financing company controlled by local government entities, financed purchases of servers carrying restricted Nvidia chips. One contract covered 32 Asustek Computer servers with B300 chips, and most of the servers were listed as deployed in a China Mobile data center in Ningxia. Semi-Tech Leasing replaced its filings with redacted copies by the end of June, removing server model designations, supplier names and installation locations.
