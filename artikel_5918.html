Rep. Jimmy Gomez to resign after sexual misconduct probe

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Rep. Jimmy Gomez, D-Calif., pictured leaving the Capitol on Nov. 9, will resign Dec. 1. (Tom Williams/CQ Roll Call)

Rep. Jimmy Gomez, D-Calif., will resign effective Dec. 1 after the House Ethics Committee said it found “substantial evidence” that Gomez engaged in sexual misconduct with a congressional staffer.

Gomez, who was defeated in his bid for reelection this month, announced his resignation Monday, hours after the Ethics panel released its final report which also said Gomez’s conduct “could constitute criminal misdemeanor sexual abuse.”

The report was one of four released by the Ethics panel Monday. Three of them are about members who are set to depart the House after the 119th Congress, and the fourth is about the conduct of a former chief of staff.

In a statement, Gomez said he took “full responsibility for the personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage,” but that he also had “strong disagreements with the Committee’s report and its conclusions.” Despite his concerns with the process, though, he said he saw no alternative other than to resign because “he was not given an opportunity to respond” to the final report.

A statement issued by lawyers representing the female staffer with whom Gomez was accused of engaging in misconduct applauded the “thorough and sensitive” handling of the investigation.

“However, Representative Gomez’s conduct did not occur in a vacuum, and immediate steps must be taken by Congress to rein in what the report describes as a ‘toxic and predatory culture,’” the attorneys, Lisa J. Banks and Sarah E. Nesbitt, added. “Democracy cannot function properly when women must endure harassment and abuse as a condition of public service.”

The committee began investigating Gomez after a New York Post story alleged the congressman was seen kissing a congressional staffer at a party hosted by then-Rep. Eric Swalwell, D-Calif. At the time, Gomez said he did not violate any House ethics rules. Swalwell resigned earlier this year amid a slew of sexual assault allegations from four separate women, one of whom was a former staffer.

But the more serious allegations addressed by the Ethics probe involve another female staffer, identified in the report as Witness 1. She testified in a deposition with the committee that on one occasion, Gomez touched her genitals without consent at a Washington bar. Gomez said in his own interview with the committee that while he did engage in sexual conduct with the staffer, “she did not say no, she did not push me away.”

The same staffer, who did not work for Gomez, said he made inappropriate advances in a separate incident in Gomez’s congressional office, which Gomez downplayed as just a “beso,” or kiss, and hug.

In its report, the committee determined that the congressional staffer’s testimony “was clear and was supported by evidence that she contemporaneously complained of his conduct” to another witness, and was part of an intolerable “toxic and predatory culture” on Capitol Hill.

A statement issued by House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., and other House Democratic leaders called the report “serious and deeply troubling,” and that Gomez’s resignation was “appropriate.”

“Decisive action to set a new standard for reform is an urgent priority for House Democrats,” the statement said.

Gomez was one of two House members from the Golden State to lose to a fellow Democrat earlier this month. Northern California Rep. Mike Thompson lost his bid for a 15th term to former venture capitalist Eric Jones.

Gomez faced off against Angela Gonzales-Torres in the general election after both advanced from the state’s all-party primary in June. Gonzales-Torres, a Democratic Socialist and housing advocate, had the backing of several progressive groups, including Justice Democrats and Leaders We Deserve. Her supporters criticized Gomez after the House Ethics panel opened its investigation in August. Her campaign also sought to publicize the inquiry in the weeks leading up to Election Day, The Washington Post reported.

The committee also released findings of its probes into Reps. Mike Collins, R-Ga., and Troy Nehls, R-Texas, as well as Brandon Phillips, Collins’ former chief of staff

Collins, who lost his Senate bid earlier this month, was under review following an Office of Congressional Conduct referral alleging that Phillips hired his girlfriend as an intern. Collins’ lawyers denied the allegations, and Phillips was fired earlier this year.

Collins and the committee reached a mutual agreement under which he would return $2,880 to the U.S. Treasury after the committee found he had overpaid the “intern,” allowing it to close the case.

Phillips, who did not comply with his congressional subpoenas, has been recommended by the committee to be held in contempt of Congress.

“At no point did Mr. Phillips offer a satisfactory legal justification for his failure to comply with the subpoena,” the committee wrote.

The committee probed Nehls for allegedly using campaign money for his personal benefit. Nehls did not run for reelection, but his twin brother, Rep.-elect Trever Nehls, will replace him in January.

The committee found that the Texas Republican “improperly used a company that he owned as a political committee; the company, Liberty 1776, received and disbursed funds for the benefit of Representative Nehls’ campaign and other unverifiable purposes.”
