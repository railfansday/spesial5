Jazz beat Lakers 109-97 after a 15-0 run ended their 9-game win streak

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Utah beat the 13-3 Lakers 109-97 after a 15-0 fourth-quarter run. Three storylines weigh fatigue against the Jazz's own play.

The standings said this game would be a mismatch, and it was, with the weaker team on top. The Jazz, who have won just three games all season, according to AFP, beat the Lakers, one of the strongest teams in the NBA. On paper, Utah should not have been competitive with Los Angeles. That means the story is about what changed on the floor that night, not about which team is better over a full season.

Utah did not win this game on a last shot. The Jazz pulled away and won 109-97, according to AFP, and a double-digit margin leaves little room to call the result a fluke. Why it happened is less clear, and two explanations compete. One holds that the Lakers were too tired to keep up and had nothing left by the end. The other holds that Utah's young players went out and took the game from them. The difference matters to anyone trying to judge either team. If fatigue explains the loss, it is a blip for Los Angeles that a night of rest will fix. If Utah earned the win, the team at the bottom of the league showed something real against one of the teams at the top.

CBS Sports looked at the main threads of the night Utah knocked off a Lakers team that had come in at 13–3, including how badly the Lakers shot and how the Jazz beat both Los Angeles clubs in two days. Those threads hold evidence for the fatigue explanation and for Utah's own play. Neither one settles the game alone, and each deserves a close look, starting with the stretch in which the game turned. Together they lead to a simple question: Was this one strange night for both teams, or the start of something for Utah? The answer will say as much about the Lakers as it does about the Jazz.

The Jazz stayed even with the Lakers late into the game, and the two teams were tied at 83 before it turned, according to CBS Sports. Utah had kept pace with one of the league's strongest teams into the closing stretch. From that tie, Utah scored 15 points in a row. A level game became a Utah game in a single stretch. The upset came down to that run, not to an advantage Utah built slowly across four quarters. Any explanation for the result has to account for those minutes.

If fatigue were the whole story, the points could have come from any Utah player against a slowing Lakers defense. Instead, two Utah players each scored heavily in the closing quarter. Ace Bailey scored 10 fourth-quarter points for the Jazz, and Keyonte George added 9 more. Two players attacking as the game turned looks more like a team taking control of a close game than one player's lucky streak, and it points toward Utah winning with its own play. Still, a long run of unanswered Utah points could also mean the Lakers had run out of energy, and the scoring alone does not settle the question.

The Lakers shot worse against Utah than in any other game this season. They made 38.2% of their shots, their lowest mark of the year, according to CBS Sports, and hit just 27.3% of their three-pointers. The schedule offers the easiest explanation. The Lakers were playing for the second night in a row, and the first of those games went to overtime. Tired players commonly miss jump shots, and the Lakers missed a great many of them. If fatigue is the whole story, the loss says little about either team, and the Lakers can move on from it.

Fatigue should affect a whole roster, but much of this slump came from one player. Lakers star Luka Doncic went 2-of-12 from three-point range, while the team as a whole went 9-of-33 from deep, according to Bleacher Report. His misses made up a large share of the Lakers' failed three-point attempts that night. That pattern fits a Utah defense focused on stopping one player at least as well as it fits a tired team. How much credit Utah deserves remains an open question.

The fourth quarter shows Utah was ready to take the game. Whether the Lakers gave it away is a question about their shooting.

Doncic also lost the ball far more often than usual. Only one game this season saw him turn it over more than the nine times he did against Utah, according to Bleacher Report. Fatigue commonly explains missed jump shots, but a high turnover count points at least partly to the defenders guarding him. Doncic himself did not mention the schedule. "Honestly, I just got to do way better than this," Doncic said. The player with the most reason to cite a second game in two nights blamed his own play instead.

That weakens the fatigue explanation without ruling it out, and whether Utah did more than catch a tired team is a question for the rest of the week.

Utah's win over the Lakers came a day after a win over the city's other NBA team, which gave the Jazz victories against both Los Angeles opponents in two days, according to CBS Sports. Two Utah wins over two Los Angeles opponents in two days turn one odd result into a pattern that needs an explanation. The sweep also matched the worst form in this game against the best. The Jazz had won once in their first 14 games. The loss ended a nine-game Lakers winning streak, according to AFP. A rout of a slumping rival could be shrugged off, but ending a streak that long is harder to dismiss as luck.

The Lakers were not the only team playing for a second straight night. Utah's win over the other Los Angeles team came the evening before, so the Jazz also had a game behind them when they pulled away late. Rest makes a weaker explanation when the winning team was also playing its second game in two nights, even if the Lakers carried the heavier load. That doesn't erase the fatigue explanation, but it shrinks it, and it leaves room for the other reading: Utah's young players took the game rather than waiting for the Lakers to give it away.

Two wins still don't make a turnaround. Even after the sweep, Utah sits at 3–13, and two wins can't undo what the first weeks of the season did to that record, so this still reads as an upset. The next test comes on November 23, when the Jazz and Lakers meet again before this result has time to fade.

If Utah wins again in a game where the Lakers can't blame the schedule, the case that its young players are taking games gains ground; if the Lakers win comfortably, the sweep will look like two lucky nights.
