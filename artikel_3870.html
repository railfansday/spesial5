I'm aware the Fractal Scape might be a bit of an unexpected headset recommendation, so allow me to justify it to you a little.

A lot of gaming headsets are very overt - you know from looking at them that they're for gaming, and they appeal to that with their designs. The Fractal Scape takes more of an Apple-esque approach? It's got a very clean-cut look and feel, with ample cushioning, and even though it actually has RGB lighting, it's used to enhance that classy feel instead of appealing to the mass market. It's built with premium materials and sits really comfortably on your head.

More importantly, it has really excellent audio quality for a mid-range headset, which I'd argue outclasses the SteelSeries Arctis Nova 7 I also have in my setup. It's not got the same simultaneous Bluetooth and 2.4GHz connectivity, which is a shame, but you can hot-swap back and forth between both modes. It comes with a fancy charging stand which I really like, and if I had to say it was missing one thing, it'd be a 3.5mm connection for wired use.

Being able to add NVMe M.2 SSDs to the PS5 is honestly one of the console's best features.

Unfortunately, thanks to AI companies swallowing up the earth's supply of RAM and NAND tech, SSDs have become so, so, so much more expensive this year.

When I was reviewing SSDs regularly, 1TB drives were costing around $70, which was incredible. Now, you'd be lucky to find one for under $150, and that's without a heatsink on top.

SSD prices are actually ludicrous at the moment, to the point where honestly, I wouldn't recommend upgrading at all. This technology just shouldn't cost as much as it does, so I'd argue waiting to see if prices decrease is the better option, unless you really, really need more storage space and it's an emergency.

I'll be keeping an eye out for PS5 SSD deals and updating you if I find any I'd actually think are worth buying, but if you're wondering why there isn't a lot of SSD content on this live report, that'll be why.
