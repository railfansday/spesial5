Robotics component maker coprod raises $6M to take on robotics ‘integration hell’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Robotics component maker coprod Inc. announced today that it has raised $6 million in new funding to reduce the time and costs associated with building new robotic platforms.

The funding is intended to enable the company to bring its first line of components to market. Founded in 2024, coprod offers an ecosystem of compatible hardware components for robotics and physical artificial intelligence applications.

It works alongside a software application programming interface and a front-end configuration tool to interconnect the components and standardize the communication between them, while preserving the real-time performance required for safe and reliable hardware.

At the core of coprod’s offering is the co:system, which combines the company’s modules on a single real-time EtherCAT control plane with one API in C++ and Python. The product family includes co:power, co:battery, co:sensor and co:motion, with each module able to be used as part of a complete system or integrated on its own.

Coprod is tackling the issue wherein most component suppliers support many protocols in the hope of catering to any integrator, which means their parts rarely work together out of the box. The result, according to coprod, is that building a system from mix-and-match components puts a heavy burden on the integrator. It requires two to three years of custom electronics and software work common before a system is reliable, in a phase known in the industry as “integration hell.”

The company claims that with its stack, the same work requires only a small team of roboticists, $5,000 to $25,000 per design iteration and a few months of time.

“We believe that a robot with purpose-built hardware designed for a specific task will do that task faster, cheaper and more reliably than any general purpose robot ever could, regardless of which model runs on it,” said co-founder and Chief Executive Loïc Delineau.

Coming into its new funding round, coprod’s components are already powering multiple robots in Switzerland, including the ETH Robotics Club’s XALUN, the first student-built Swiss humanoid robot, which took its first steps in Zurich in recent weeks. The company is also establishing partnerships with robotics integrators and well-known labs to validate its first hardware prototypes before launching them on the market early next year.

The funding is also intended to support a new hardware prototyping facility in Lausanne. Equipped with CNC lathes, mills, lasers and electronic assembly and testing equipment, the workshop is expected to cut the company’s development cycles from two weeks to 24 hours. Testing at the site will be used to produce datasheets and digital twin models for each component, an effort aimed at narrowing the gap between simulated and real-world performance that arises in robotics development.

By the end of 2027, coprod plans to have its entire component line on the market, qualified and certified. Large-scale production is planned for both Shenzhen and Texas, with the company’s headquarters and research and development remaining in Switzerland.

Component supply has also emerged as a constraint for robot makers, particularly in humanoids. A McKinsey & Co. analysis published in April found that supply chain bottlenecks for humanoids are most likely to emerge around actuators and sensing and perception systems, the type of hardware coprod is developing.

Delineau formed coprod with Chief Technology Officer Vincent Nguyen and Chief Scientist Matthias Schuller (pictured), all graduates of the École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne. The three started out by building a bipedal robot demonstrator in a parent’s garage in the Swiss Alps to show what compatible components could do.

Founderful AG and Prototype Capital led the pre-seed round, with General Catalyst Group Management, Pace Ventures, Tiny Supercomputer Investment Company, Kima Ventures, SDAC, Unruly Capital and Venture Kick also participating. Angel investors in the round include Hugging Face Inc. co-founder Thomas Wolf, the Dassault family and Florent Perrichon. Venture Kick awarded coprod CHF 40,000 ($47,600) earlier this year.

Andreas Klinger, founder of Prototype, said coprod is fixing “the biggest problem in hardware.” It argues that robotics founders need a single standard for basic components and middleware so they can focus on the parts of their products that create value for customers.
