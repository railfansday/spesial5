EXCLUSIVE: Inside the $2.5 Million Laurel Canyon Home Once Rented by the Hitmaker Behind Harry Styles’ ‘Harry’s House’ and Miley Cyrus’ ‘Flowers’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Kid Harpoon’s former Los Angeles rental is ready for its next act.

The Grammy-winning producer, whose real name is Tom Hull, previously rented the 1957 Laurel Canyon home that’s now on the market for $2.5 million, according to listing agent Trey Alligood of Douglas Elliman.

The three-bedroom property has a view of the Hollywood sign and a backyard deck that spans more than half the size of the house itself.

Harpoon rented the property as a workspace, transferring several parts of the residence into producing spaces, where he helped bring some of the biggest songs to life.

Music fans might also recognize the street name. The house sits at 2143 Ridgemont Drive, and the credits for Miley Cyrus’ “Endless Summer Vacation” name a Los Angeles recording location called Ridgemont High.

Ridgemont Studio also appears in the credits for Harry Styles’ “Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally.” Neither set of credits gives a street address, but Hull’s role in both artists’ careers is well documented.

His work on Styles’ “Harry’s House” earned him a Grammy for Album of the Year. A year later, Hull and fellow producer Tyler Johnson shared in another win when Cyrus’ “Flowers” took home Record of the Year. Hull’s contributions extended well beyond the production booth. On “Flowers,” he also played bass and drums.

By Laurel Canyon standards, the producer was part of a relatively recent wave of musical talent. The house was already standing when artists including Joni Mitchell and Cass Elliot helped turn the neighborhood into a gathering place for musicians in the 1960s.

Decades later, the Grammy Museum devoted an exhibition to the people who lived and worked there, with artifacts as personal as Elliot’s hand-painted chair.

“Laurel Canyon has always pulled in creative people, and buyers feel that,” Alligood tells Realtor.com®. “When a house has real history behind it, you’re not just buying square footage, you’re buying into a story, and that’s something new construction can’t replicate.

“Homes like that stay in the conversation long after other listings have come and gone.”

At the Ridgemont Drive property, the most generous entertaining space is out back. A 1,250-square-foot deck has its own kitchen, complete with a barbecue and refrigerator, so whoever is cooking can stay outside with guests.

A pergola shelters the fire pit, while a separate terrace gives people room to peel away from the main gathering. The 9,270-square-foot lot also accommodates a stretch of lawn and a storage shed.

Inside, the home’s midcentury design is easiest to appreciate from the living room. A sculptural fireplace anchors the space, and a bank of floor-to-ceiling Fleetwood sliders opens to the deck.

With the doors drawn back, anyone sitting by the fireplace has a clear line to the backyard.

The dining area and kitchen share that same open room.

The kitchen island gives guests a place to pull up while dinner is underway. A Thermador range handles the cooking, and there’s a wine refrigerator indoors as well as the fridge out by the grill.

Elsewhere in the 2,245-square-foot house, a dedicated office leaves all three bedrooms available for sleeping, a practical perk in a home where the main living spaces flow together.

The primary suite has a backyard entrance of its own. Its sliding doors offer a direct route outside in the morning, while the adjoining bathroom gives owners a choice between a soak in the tub and a steam shower.

All of the rooms occupy a single level, and the attached two-car garage opens directly into the house.

Both bathrooms have been renovated, along with updates that range from new hardwood flooring and lighting to fresh paint inside and out. Even the exterior wood got some attention. It was sanded and stained again. A new drip irrigation system serves the drought-tolerant landscaping.

Alligood, who holds the listing through the Alligood Group at Douglas Elliman, says buyers were already paying attention during the property’s opening week.

"As for the market, L.A. buyers are selective right now, [but] when a home is priced right and presented well, it moves fast,” he says. “We’ve received extensive traction on this home having only been on the market a week.”
