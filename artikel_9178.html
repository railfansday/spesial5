National Geographic Reveals its Pictures of the Year for 2026

National Geographic has unveiled its Pictures of the Year 2026, a collection of 25 extraordinary photographs revealing the moments, discoveries, and untold stories that defined the past year.

Selected from more than one million images captured by Nat Geo photographers around the globe, this year’s collection offers a striking look at the natural world, human ingenuity, and the remarkable ways people and wildlife navigate a changing planet.

The images transport audiences from the depths of Greenland’s icy waters to the mountains of Peru, capturing moments of wonder, resilience and discovery that might otherwise go unseen.

Made possible by the unmatched access and expertise of National Geographic photographers, the collection embodies the curiosity, discovery, and spirit of exploration that are distinctly National Geographic.

Among the standout photographs are the first-ever images of moray eels spawning in the wild, captured by photographer Tony Wu after nine months in the field in Japan; a climber suspended from a sea cliff in Spain just after a total solar eclipse; and a spine-tingling encounter with a Greenland shark, one of the planet’s longest-lived vertebrates, photographed by Kiliii Yüyan in the 28.4°F waters of Ammassalik Fjord, among the coldest conditions endured by a Nat Geo photographer this year.
