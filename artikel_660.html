Steven Hack, Original ‘Cats’ Castmember and Busy Onscreen Character Actor, Dies at 68

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

His credits ranged from ‘Jake and the Fatman’ and ‘Seinfeld’ to ‘Pam & Tommy’ and ‘Amsterdam.’

Steven Hack, the theater veteran who spent five years on Broadway as an original castmember in Cats, then appeared as a character actor in dozens of films and TV shows, has died. He was 68.

Hack died Thursday at home following what was described as “a sudden and unexpected cardiac event,” the Interact Theatre Company announced. He was a founding member of the North Hollywood-based group that launched in 1989.

“Steven was a gifted and versatile performer, a superb graphic artist and a cherished member of our artistic family,” ITC directors Barry Heins and Walter Ware said in a statement. “His creativity and talent helped shape Interact in its earliest years, and his artistry, humor, warmth and generous spirit enriched everything he touched.” Related Stories Lifestyle The Pulitzer-Winning Play 'Purpose' Finally Came to Los Angeles. Born in St. Louis on April 20, 1958, Hack studied at CalArts and The American Academy of Dramatic Arts in Los Angeles. In 1978, he was a background dancer in the film version of Grease and appeared in the off-Broadway musical The Coolest Cat in Town.

Hack was there when Andrew Lloyd Webber’s Cats opened at the Winter Garden Theatre on Oct. 7, 1982, and he portrayed several characters, including Coricopat, Mungojerrie, Carbucketty, Tumblebrutus and Pouncival, over the next five years.

He toured with Michael Bennett’s A Chorus Line for nearly two years and then joined the Broadway production at the Shubert Theatre for one show only — its record-breaking 3,389th Great White Way performance on Sept. 29, 1983. (He did Cats that night, too.)

Hack started showing up on television in the 1990s on shows including The Trials of Rosie O’Neill, Jake and the Fatman, Matlock, Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman, Married … With Children, Seinfeld, Mad About You and Caroline in the City.

More recently, he found work in the series American Horror Story, Ratched, Perry Mason and Not Dead Yet, in the miniseries Pam & Tommy and Angelyne and in the films Thor: The Dark World (2013), Fantastic Four (2015), The Happytime Murders (2018) and Amsterdam (2022).

In 1995, he received an Ovation Award for his performance in an L.A. production of Elmer Rice’s venerable drama Counsellor-at-Law, then acted in the radio version for L.A. Theatre Works. And in 2003, he was nominated for another Ovation prize, this one for his turn in the Interact one-man drama Willie’s Son.
