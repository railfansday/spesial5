Brad Stevens talks Mitchell Robinson, Celtics’ start, and more

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

SACRAMENTO — Brad Stevens took a lot of heat this offseason for pulling the trigger on the trade that sent Jaylen Brown to the Philadelphia 76ers and ended a near-decade-long partnership with Jayson Tatum.

And, though it's too soon to tell, the Celtics are 11-4, have the second-best net rating in the NBA, the second-best defense, and the sixth-best offense. Paul George, the team's biggest offseason acqusition, has been generally awesome. The team's significant depth has been on full display regularly.

Still, Stevens said it's too soon to judge who this team really is, and that he normally waits until mid-December to make those kinds of evaluations.

"I've been encouraged by a lot of things," Stevens said. "I think we've still got a lot to figure out to be the best version of ourselves. But usually, I'm probably another couple of weeks or a month away from getting too deep into, okay, this is who we really are. Yet, I think we have played some good basketball. We've shown ourselves capable of it. We've gotten good play from a lot of different guys, and the team has really tried to buy in, I think, well, to what the coaches are asking them to do on both sides of the ball."

Paul George has been one of the brightest spots of the Celtics' early-season success. George has been tremendous defensively, and he's also averaging 19.9 points, 5.5 rebounds, and 3.7 assists per game, while shooting 46.5% from the field and 42.3% from three. He's had two games with at least 35 points and has been one of the team's most reliable defenders.

"Paul's been a good fit," Stevens said. "Paul's a really good player…. You don't come with those accolades if you're not able to impact the game. Everybody talks about what he can do on offense, and I think the minute he stepped on the court with our guys defensively, our guys, a lot of them were kind of taken aback, even though playing against him all these years, with how good he is, how active he is, how smart he is, how aware he is, and he's just really had a good impact on the group. He's just been a joy to be around. He's been great."

The Celtics' other marquee offseason acquisition, Mitchell Robinson, hasn't transitioned as seamlessly as George. Robinson has the worst on-off rating on the Celtics at -30.1 (that number measures the difference in net rating when a player is on the floor versus off of it). The historically prolific rebounder is also averaging the fewest rebounds per game (5.4) of his career.

"You can tell it's obviously a transition for him, and that's not all that atypical for guys, and so again, it's so early that we'll see how this all plays out," Stevens said. "But I trust that what he does when he's at his best will translate because it always has, right? And I think that getting him to the rim on both sides of the floor, getting him to be in that area where he can be most impactful, [is] something that we'll continue to work on — continue to put him in position where, hopefully, he can soar with his strengths."

Then, there's rookie Chris Cenac Jr., who began the year with the Maine Celtics but has found himself in the Celtics' rotation over the past two games. Cenac, who was drafted 27th overall, tallied 6 points and 6 rebounds in 17 minutes in the Celtics' loss to the Nuggets on Wednesday night.

"He's been solid," Stevens said. "I'm really encouraged. I really like him. I think he's got a great work ethic. He's obviously got physical tools. He's got a good mind for the game. He's gonna be good, right? I do think that with each step, there are different expectations. So he's in that honeymoon phase of, well, he did one good thing and it looks awesome, and wow. And then your expectations grow and grow, and it's just like we've seen with Jordan and Hugo and Baylor. Once you taste it, once you get a little feeling, then the expectation is to be even better, and we see that all the way up and down our roster. All of our guys, for the most part, have been through that. But I'm encouraged by the start. It's hard not to be, and I think the other part of it is he does have the physical ability to continue to raise his level in big moments."

One of the other interesting aspects of the roster is the logjam at the wing position; Sam Hauser (20.3 minutes), Baylor Scheierman (19.4 minutes), Jordan Walsh (15.7 minutes), and Hugo Gonzalez (12.9 minutes) have all seen significant minutes, but no one player in that group has pulled away to play the majority of the minutes.

Each brings something different to the table; Hauser (46.8% from three) and Scheierman (42.2% from three) have been the biggest threats from beyond the arc, while Gonzalez (7.1 rebounds/36 minutes) has been the best rebounder of the bunch. From the wings, Walsh has probably been the team's best on-ball defender and its most consistent starter, with 10 starts in 15 games.

But the players have seemed interchangeable at times, and no one has clearly emerged as the best player in the pack.

"I said at the start of the year, the biggest challenge for the coaching staff is the wing differentiation," Stevens said. "That's been the hardest part, and I think they've managed it beautifully, because nobody has really differentiated themselves, and they're all good players. And so that's just kind of the hard part about having depth, which in this league you need now. It's really important, but it also comes with the challenges of — not everybody's going to get exactly what they want on every given night. Is it easier to have seven or eight guys that you know are going to play every night? Yeah, until two of them get hurt, then you're in trouble, right? And so that's part of the challenge in this day and age with how the league is going and how hard it is to play the full 82, and certainly game to game with what's required on both ends."

As the team continues to improve on both ends, Stevens is generally encouraged by what he's seen.

"One of the things that this group really has is a lot of feel for how to play, and you see that when we're at our very best on both ends: the reaction, the reads, the ability to play off one another, the ability to cover for one another on the defensive end," Stevens said. "I think that those are things that, as the season goes on, we should continue to benefit from, to be able to grow, kind of however Joe and the staff want us to play throughout that given stretch, and ultimately to be the best that we can be."
