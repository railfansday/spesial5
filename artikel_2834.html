What we learned as Brandin Podziemski, Warriors outlast Celtics to end skid

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

What we learned as Brandin Podziemski, Warriors outlast Celtics to end skid originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO – Home is where the heart is, and it's miraculously where the Warriors snapped their five-game losing streak.

Was it pretty? No. Was it gritty? Yes. The Warriors failed to score 100 points for the fifth straight game, but survived the Boston Celtics for an 88-87 win Sunday night at Chase Center.

The most important Warriors stat of the night was offensive rebounding. Golden State secured 19 offensive rebounds for 22 second-chance points. That helped mitigate the Warriors' 20 turnovers for 24 Celtics points.

Brandin Podziemski scored five clutch points in the fourth quarter to score a team-high 18 points as the Warriors had four players reach double figures.

Celtics star Jayson Tatum scored 23 points in the first half, but the Warriors held him to only four in the second half.

Here are three takeaways from the Warriors improving to 9-8 on the season.

Threads is Green's preferred social media outlet, but on Sunday, the 36-year-old sent a plethora of posts on X (formerly known as Twitter) leading up to the Warriors' game. Most were in response to Eddie Johnson calling him out while replying to a Steve Kerr interview on 95.7 The Game where the Warriors coach said Steph Curry's absence "really limits Draymond's impact offensively."

If you thought Green would come to Chase Center fired up and determined to quiet the noise, you thought right. The Warriors began the game setting up Green for a wide-open three from the right corner. Their first six points were two Green threes, both of which he took from the right corner.

Green entered Sunday having made four threes all season. He left the first quarter with three, and a season-high nine points for an entire game.

Although Green didn't score another point the rest of the game, his defense on Tatum in the final seconds secured the Warriors' win.

Gui Santos might have been the best story in the NBA last season, earning a three-year, $15 million contract. But his encore hasn't been the same.

Struggling to find his rhythm while playing through injuries, Santos coming into Sunday night was averaging just 6.3 points per game while shooting 38.2 percent from the field and 24 percent from three. A different Santos showed up against the Celtics. Santos scored 13 points off the bench to tie his season high.

The last time he reached 13 points was nearly one month ago, on Oct. 26.

Santos also was a game-high plus-12 as an energizer who had four offensive rebounds. His signature footwork around the rim was on point, and Santos went 3 of 5 from deep for his first game of the season with multiple threes.

Although Kerr wouldn't commit to anything long term, he made it clear in his pregame press conference that he wanted to give Charles Bassey an extended look. And if all goes well, Bassey will remain the Warriors' starting center the rest of the regular season, barring roster changes.

The journeyman proved why he's plenty capable against the Celtics. By halftime, Bassey's impact was felt all over the floor, as he piled up seven points on a perfect 3-of-3 shooting, along with five rebounds, three assists and three steals.

Bassey, at 6-foot-10, allows Green to slide down to play power forward. His athleticism and scoring touch also gives the Warriors another element they're missing. Bassey has good hands around the rim and can get up for lobs, and his floater makes him a real scoring threat. It helps that he's willing to do the dirty work, too.

The five offensive rebounds Bassey grabbed says everything about the energy he brings to the Warriors. Defensively, he blew up sets and tied a career high in steals. Offensively, Bassey kept possessions alive and had his seventh game in double figures this season, ending the night with 13 points on 6-of-7 shooting.
