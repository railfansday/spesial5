Taking on Starlink: Europe’s largest satellite factory opens in push to rival Musk

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The largest satellite factory in Europe was inaugurated in Belgium this week, boosting the continent's efforts to challenge Elon Musk's Starlink and reduce reliance on U.S. space infrastructure.

Privately held Belgian satellite manufacturer Aerospacelab secured a 2.4-billion-euro ($2.6 billion) contract in September to manufacture 264 of the 348 satellites planned for IRIS², the European Union's answer to SpaceX's Starlink business.

Aerospacelab said this will "form the backbone of Europe's sovereign, secure, and resilient connectivity infrastructure by 2030." It will be named Ectro from Nov. 30.

Its new oval-shaped, 20,000-square-meter facility in southern Belgium, inaugurated by Belgian King Philippe, will be able to produce up to 500 satellites annually, which would make it the world's third-largest satellite production site, according to the Belgian royal palace.

Employees are expected to move into the megafactory in January, and production is slated to begin by the end of 2028, according to AFP.

Satellite connectivity has become increasingly important to everyday life as well as national security.

It provides internet access to remote locations and defense equipment like warships and fighter jets, and lets governments and militaries communicate securely where Earth-based networks are unavailable or disrupted.

The importance of such capabilities has become particularly apparent in Ukraine, as the country's military relies heavily on Starlink connectivity to fight Russian forces. Recent restrictions to the service over Russian-occupied territory reported by Ukrainian officials further underscored the way geopolitical conflict may depend on commercial businesses.

But Europe still has a long way to go in challenging Starlink's dominance.

SpaceX says it has more than 11,000 Starlink satellites in low Earth orbit, making it by far the largest satellite constellation in the world, and plans up to 100,000 satellites.

Among the challengers is Amazon, whose satellite business, Amazon Leo, has deployed over 400 satellites and plans an initial in-orbit constellation of more than 3,200.

Meanwhile, French Eutelsat operates more than 600 satellites in low Earth orbit through its OneWeb constellation. The company is also part of the SpaceRISE consortium alongside SES and Hispasat, tasked with developing and operating IRIS².

The European Commission has called IRIS² a "strategic asset to achieve space sovereignty in the face of commercial satellite constellations, such as Starlink." The aim is for first launches to take place by the end of 2029, with initial services expected soon after.

In August, the Commission and SpaceRISE consortium announced they were expanding the main constellation from 282 to 348 satellites, increasing its secure communications capacity for EU governments by 60%.

Defense and security applications were the main drivers of the expanded scope, the Commission said.
