Grandview controls 2nd half to end Fort Collins' run in 5A quarters

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The balance of power in Colorado Class 5A high school football is still down in the Denver area.

No. 5 Grandview reaffirmed that notion, romping past No. 4 Fort Collins with a big second half for a 44-23 win in the 5A quarterfinals Nov. 20 at French Field.

A tight 22-16 ballgame at halftime turned into a cruise-control win in the fourth quarter for the Wolves.

That shift happened right out of the locker room, too.

Grandview's Tim Arnold III took the opening kickoff of the third quarter back 96 yards for a touchdown. That was already a momentum shift after Fort Collins ended the first half strong.

The backbreaker came moments later, as Kolt Jackson picked off Jace Garcia on the Lambkins' second play of the half and returned it for a touchdown.

All of a sudden, a 6-point deficit was 21 points what had been Colorado's last remaining unbeaten team in the 11-0 Lambkins.

Make that 11-1 now, as Grandview ended one of the great seasons in modern Fort Collins football history.

Neelein Shead Jr. set an early tone, scoring a pair of rushing touchdowns and joining with Allen III with some ankle-breaking juke moves.

Meanwhile, Garcia found the Wolves defense to be plenty ferocious. He was hit early and often and sacked five times, a season-high.

But Fort Collins was still in the game at halftime, as Garcia hit Donovan Dumas for a touchdown to make it 22-16 late in the second quarter.

French Field was rocking and there was major buzz in the home crowd that a memorable second half could be coming.

Instead, Grandview doused that enthusiasm in a quick blitz to start the final 24 minutes.

It was a reminder why the Wolves are a perennial power in Colorado. The visitors controlled the line of scrimmage, had athletes all over the field and more depth across the board.

This wasn't a comprehensive blowout, but it was clear the Lambkins hadn't been challenged by a team on Grandview's level during an unbeaten regular season.

The fifth seed moves on to face No. 1 Cherry Creek in the 5A semifinals on Nov. 28. It's actually the Wolves' first Final Four appearance since 2021.

For the Lambkins, a season that won't soon be forgotten up north is officially over.

But not before an 11-0 start, a conference championship, new program passing records, a city title and more from a 2026 team that set a new standard in city football.
