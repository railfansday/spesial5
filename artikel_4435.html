Petit Planet’s Gacha System Will Only be For Cosmetics

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

HoYoVerse’s bread and butter is, of course, gacha games. From its first big hit in Honkai Impact 3rd to the breakout success of Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail, and Zenless Zone Zero, gacha is what brings home the bacon. The items distributed through these games’ gacha banners usually include characters, weapons, equipment, and other assorted elements that directly impact your experience with the game. If you want to play, you gotta be willing to roll the dice.

With the looming release of Petit Planet, HoYo’s new Animal Crossing-style life sim game, players have been curious as to how, exactly, the gacha element will factor into this particular style of game. It’d be pretty lousy, for example, if you had to keep rolling on banners just to see all of the game’s villagers and experience their stories.

The new life sim gacha game is launching ahead of several other titles this winter.

However, in a departure from its established formula, HoYo’s actually taking a slightly different approach to monetization in Petit Planet. There will still be a gacha system, of course, but the only items this gacha will dispense will be strictly cosmetic, with all of the actual gameplay stuff being readily accessible to everyone whether they’re willing to roll or not.

At the HoYoverse Media Tour 2026 in Seoul, South Korea, two representatives from the Petit Planet dev team, lead game designer Murphy and lead artist Yinli Huang, explained their philosophy to the surrounding press.

In an interview with gaming outlet Geek Culture, Murphy explained that, rather than paywalling access to the game’s content through gacha banners or direct purchases, the goal is to make all of the actual gameplay elements available to all players, with whatever paid elements there being strictly limited to extra cosmetic stuff like character outfits and furniture.

“All of Petit Planet’s core gameplay, including its life simulation and all content related to forming friendships and building stories with the Neighbours, is included for all free players,” Murphy said. “In terms of our monetisation model, we can confirm that it will only be associated with cosmetics, meaning things like furniture and clothes.”

The goal is to make all of the actual gameplay elements available to all players.

This is quite a departure from HoYo’s other major titles. I play Honkai: Star Rail, for example, and because I don’t have every single character and all of their optimal gacha-earned equipment, I’m basically locked out of the game’s hardest endgame game modes.

Wuthering Waves is one of the biggest Gachas on the planet, and they do far better than most at answering player complaints. Let's go over exactly how!

Petit Planet is slated to specifically be a cozy life sim game rather than a management life sim. Many games of this genre purport to be cozy, but because they demand such constant, consistent engagement from you, they kind of stop being cute little daily life simulators and start being second jobs. The dev team is hoping to use the game’s live-service model to offer consistent, interesting content to the players without directly stoking FOMO.

“As a live-service title, we will constantly add new content to the game, like festival events, which are inspired by real-world festivals, alongside the concept of seasons,” Murphy told Geek Culture. “Each season, players will be able to meet new Neighbours, alongside new clothes and furniture to unlock.”

The crux of this pursuit is an as-of-yet undetermined system that would allow players to properly experience and reap the benefits of seasons’ past even if they weren’t playing the game at the time. Again, Star Rail does regular time-limited events, and while they all offer limited-time currency rewards, the actual gameplay elements remain accessible long after their endpoints. Even so, Murphy wants these types of events in Petit Planet to be as low-pressure as possible so players are interested without feeling obligated.

“We always come back to the randomness in daily life, so there’s no growth curve in our game, we just want to provide more of those little surprises and small pleasures in life for players.”

We were able to get some extensive time with HoYoverse's upcoming life simulator, Petit Planet, and were left grinning from ear-to-ear.
