Why 16 MLSs want a united front against Compass in the battle over private listings

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

MLSs sued by Compass asked the JPML to consolidate or coordinate pretrial proceedings across 16 antitrust cases and 2 SDNY declaratory judgment actions. Compass plans to challenge the SDNY cases and says each MLS rule is local and distinct.  AI Summary

Compass International Holdings may have filed 16 separate antitrust lawsuits against 16 separate MLS, but according to court documents filed earlier this week, those MLSs are seeking to combine these lawsuits into one.

In a motion filed on Monday to stay the lawsuit Compass filed against it, California Regional MLS (CRMLS) told the court that it and the other MLSs Compass has filed lawsuits against have asked to consolidate or coordinate the pretrial proceedings for the various Compass v. MLS lawsuits currently pending across the country in federal court in the Southern District of New York (SDNY). If the panel declines to select New York, the motion identifies the Western District of North Carolina and the Middle District of Florida as alternative venues for the consolidated litigation.

According to the motion, the MLSs have also asked to include the two declaratory judgement lawsuits nine MLSs have already filed against Compass in the SDNY.

“Compass has chosen to wage the same legal fight in courts across the country, creating unnecessary costs and the risk of conflicting rulings,” Richard Haggerty, the CEO of OneKey MLS, said in a statement. “Each of the 16MLS organizations are challenging that approach. We believe these cases belong before one judge, and we will continue defending the cooperation rules that ensure no brokerage gains an unfair advantage by withholding publicly marketed homes from the shared marketplace.”

This request comes after Compass informed the SDNY court that it plans to file a motion to dismiss the declaratory judgement lawsuits or to sever and transfer each MLS’s claims to the district where Compass has brought its own antitrust case against that MLS.

After months of posturing and clashes over policies, the legal dispute between Compass and the MLSs kicked off in earnest this September when Compass sent letters to MLSs across the country demanding that they stop fining agents for publicly marketing office exclusive listings. Later that month, Compass CEO Robert Reffkin told a room full of MLS executives gathered at the Council of MLSs (CMLS) that he would sue any MLS that refused to give in to Compass’s demands.

Despite Reffkin’s threats, it was actually CRMLS that made the first move, filing a lawsuit in early October seeking a declaratory judgment that its policies on listing cooperation are “fair, procompetitive, and legally sound,” according to a company announcement. CRMLS has since been joined by New York’s OneKey MLS in this lawsuit, while MLSListings, Arizona Regional MLS, bridgeMLS, Canopy MLS, REcolorado, Hive MLS, and Doorify MLS have filed their own lawsuit, also in the SDNY, seeking the same judgements as CRMLS and OneKey.

In the midst of these lawsuits hitting the court docket, Compass filed its own lawsuit against CRMLS, alleging that its rule unlawfully restrict how homeowners and their agents can publicly market properties outside the MLS.

Roughly a month later, Compass reiterated those same claims in 15 separate lawsuits filed against 15 MLSs. MLS defendants in those lawsuits include bridgeMLS, Bay East MLS, Contra Costa MLS, MLSListings, The MLS-CLAW, San Diego MLS, Canopy MLS, Hive MLS, Doorify MLS, Arizona Regional MLS, Recolorado, NorthstarMLS, Houston Association of Realtors, Stellar MLS and OneKey MLS.

In an emailed statement a Compass spokesperson told HousingWire that the filing is “a procedural request  that does not change the substance of [Compass’s] claims.”

“Each MLS applies its own rules, and the impact of those rules on agents, home sellers, and buyers is specific to its local jurisdiction,” the spokesperson added.

It remains to be seen if court will allow the MLSs to consolidate their lawsuits. This is not the first time real estate industry defendants have sought to consolidate similar lawsuits. Several real estate brokerage defendants in the copycat commission lawsuits attempted to consolidate their cases, but their request was rejected.
