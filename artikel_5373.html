Post-quantum cryptography startup Q.security launches with $11.5M in funding

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Post-quantum cryptography startup Q Security Inc. formally launched today with $11.5 million in new funding for a platform designed to take enterprises from discovery through to completion of their quantum-safe migrations.

Founded this year, Q.security offers a remediation platform that runs the move to post-quantum cryptography as one coordinated program. The company argues that security teams need business context to decide which cryptographic findings to address first, since the time and resources available for the work are limited.

At the core of the Q.security offering is a map of an organization’s business structure and applications. Findings from the cryptographic inventory are tied back to that map, with the highest-risk assets put at the front of the queue.

Teams are said to be able to investigate exposure and work through remediation using a conversational artificial intelligence interface. Through integrations with existing cybersecurity and infrastructure tools, the platform drives fixes to completion using AI-guided runbooks, ticketing and automation.

Once a fix is in place, Q.security verifies that it works. Monitoring of the organization’s cryptographic posture then continues as standards and threats change, an approach the company claims lets security teams reduce risk faster and at lower cost.

“Quantum computing is making cryptographic risk an urgent and ongoing business priority,” said Guy Peer, co-founder and chief executive of Q.security, who previously co-founded Unbound Security and led its research and development through its acquisition by Coinbase Global Inc., announced in 2021.

Peer’s co-founder, Nir Steinherz, was director of product at Coinbase and vice president of strategy and product at Unbound Security. He was also a senior vice president at Citigroup Inc.’s Security Innovation Center, where he led secure digital banking initiatives for more than 100 million customers. The pair are said to have led a cryptographic migration protecting hundreds of billions of dollars in assets.

The funding is intended to support Q.security as it brings its platform to enterprise customers through direct sales and channel partners.

Castle Island Ventures led the seed round, with New York Life Ventures, Coinbase Ventures, Fin Capital, SBI US Gateway Fund and Ground Up Ventures also participating.

Matt Walsh, general partner at Castle Island Ventures, said enterprises know they need to prepare for the day quantum computers can break current encryption. With harvest-now, decrypt-later data collection already under way, “the challenge is determining how to prioritize the migration across business and technology environments,” he added.
