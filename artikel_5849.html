BYD E6Y Odyssey

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A couple of friends embark on a road trip to pick their EV purchase, and it turns into an Odyssey.

The BYD E6 is a BEV classic. Globe-trotting friends have ridden in them as taxis in Singapore. Majella and I even took one for a test drive (it reminded me of a ’70s Chrysler Valiant). It was the best selling pure electric car in China in 2016, with about 600,000 sold globally, and it is viewed by many as ahead of its time. So, when one showed up at our coffee and cake morning tea, I had to interview the owner, Joanne, and her road trip friend, Meredith. What they shared with me was truly an odyssey of determination to get Jo’s car home. Please note: this story took place 6 years ago. Charging availability has improved and so has Joanne’s knowledge of living with an electric vehicle.

Joanne told me that she had been looking for an EV for two years when a search on carsales.com found an ad for an E6Y (the taxi version of the BYD E6). It was a pre-COVID import and had been mothballed at Sydney airport after the company importing the vehicles had decided not to go ahead with taxis. Photos of all the BYDs at the airport were widely circulated as evidence that no one wanted to buy an EV. The cars were being “sold by a mate out of the back of the factory.” At the low cost of AU$32,000, claiming a 400 km range from its 83-kWh battery and only 100 km on the odometer, Joanne saw it as a bargain.

There was some consideration of putting the car on a flatbed and having it shipped, but in the end, the two friends decided on a road trip.

Jo informs me that: “They only brought 120 of the BYD E6Y taxi versions into the country. BYD sold some GLX versions, which had more bells and whistles.”

She was soon to find out that all is not as it seems. Her lack of knowledge of EVs meant that her trip in the car from Sydney back to Brisbane would be an odyssey of Greek tragedy proportions. Jo reminisces: “Meredith reminded me our original plan had been to spend just two days driving back to Brisbane with the one overnight stay. I thought I could ‘fast charge’ the car and make it back to Brisvegas on the second day. Hahahaha. Lesson learned the hard way.” They expected it to be just a twelve-hour trip. Meredith adds, “I told her we would laugh about it one day.”

Joanne contacted the seller and planned her flight to Sydney. Meredith got excited and joined the “road trip” in Newcastle. Jo paid the money and took ownership of the car. “It took two hours to get out of Sydney.” To be fair, that wasn’t the car’s fault — just Sydney’s congested traffic. However, the fact that the previous owner had only charged the car to 50% didn’t help. She was able to charge the car to 80% on the outskirts of Sydney. “But I had no idea that I wouldn’t get the expected range at highway speeds.”

Another learning curve was the state of charging points — sure, there were plenty listed on PlugShare, but many were unavailable or broken. Again, dear reader, I must emphasize this was 6 years ago.

Her next planned stop was accommodation she had booked at Newcastle. She got to within 2 km when the battery readout said “zero.” She was unable to find a place to charge — the car had not come with a granny charger. Jo clarified her Newcastle experience by email: “the Uni in Newcastle was the first I tried to charge. My friend Meredith and I tried three other places to charge all with no luck. I found out later that the Newcastle chargers were notorious for not working.” She confided to me that she now knows that the car can go a little further even when it says zero battery. She also had to learn the difference between AC and DC charging. The BYD she had bought could only handle AC charging.

With the battery reading zero, she felt she had no choice but to leave the car at a nearby garage. The owner of the accommodation she had booked kindly came and picked her and her friend up. The next day, the BNB owner dropped them back to the garage and a tow truck was ordered. The tow truck took them and their car to a local winery where they trickle charged next to the golf carts. The winery also had a Tesla level 2 charger for customer use. It was charging when they arrived. “We left a note on the windscreen of the Tesla that was using the level 2. During breakfast the Tesla owner rang and said the charger was available. We left the winery fully charged and off up the highway.” Not a bad place to get stuck, methinks.

280 km up the road to Kempsie, there was access to free charging in the council carpark, but no available accommodation! That’s what happens when you travel on the Queen’s Birthday long weekend. “We were checking out the back seat of the car as a place to sleep when a friend of a friend told them he had a farm in area. ‘Have I got a place for you!’ he told them. After we finished charging, we had to drive down this dark farm track. We couldn’t plug the car in because we still didn’t have a granny charger!” They made themselves comfortable in the available beds in the barn. The next morning in the light of day they found they had driven alongside of a lagoon! “We could have easily driven into that!”

Meredith adds: “I didn’t think I’d be sleeping in a shed with a tractor and then I couldn’t sleep because of the smell of diesel.” The trip was not just a challenge, but torture.

Their next adventure was just a few kilometers up the road at Gladstone NSW. Low on juice, they contacted people through PlugShare and got a message from a local café owner. You can check out the café here. He already owned a Hyundai Ioniq, and so had a granny charger to lend. The café owner knew a fellow who was visiting his mum in town. This friend had ordered an Atto 3 from BYD and was waiting on this first shipment of BYDs to arrive. Jo took him for a ride around town in her car. “This was his first ride in a BYD.”

He arranged for them to leave their car at his mother’s home in the next town (Smithtown). Jo and her friend were now in a hurry, as both had to get back to Brisbane to go to work the next day. Leaving the car charging, “We belted back to Brisbane in this horrible little roller skate hire car.” Home in Brisbane, Jo had to plan how to retrieve her BYD purchase. Meredith did not want another “road trip.”

This time, Jo travelled south by train. Even this trip was not without drama. She missed her stop. Thankfully, she was able to get off at the next stop and change to the northbound train to make it back to where her car waited faithfully for her. She took a taxi to her car, returned the borrowed granny charger, and began her journey home. She was so keen that she ended up with a speeding ticket.

Joanne arrived at her motel in Balina with 10 km of range showing on the dashboard. But the available ocular charger soon filled up the car. By 9:00 pm, with a full battery, she couldn’t wait to go home. The fireworks display in town was a fitting sendoff for the final leg of her journey.

Jo would like to acknowledge all the good advice and offers of help she received from fellow users of PlugShare, as well as all those referred to above. She is now a confident and competent driver of her BYD E6Y and has made many trips back and forth to Sydney from Brisbane with less drama. The BYD E6Y Facebook group has been a great help. She says she would not part with her green E6Y Singapore taxi — “a comfortable and solid basic car.” Rumour has it that there are about 250 on Australian roads, with the record distance travelled over 1 million km.

Another owner commented: “This unstoppable tank will outlive me I’m sure, best of all its super basic … no internet, no lane assist, no self-driving and plenty of new and used cheap parts available. This is why it seems like there are so few … they are so good NO ONE who has one is selling them.”

Jo has even taken her car camping. “The camping trip was to Yandina up the coast. I did sleep in the back of the car on that trip very comfortably. But that time I had a mattress and bedding. I did take out the bottom half of the back seat from the car so I could get a completely flat rear area for the mattress. When I returned from the camping trip, I popped the rear seat bottom back in the car. It’s just two bolts and a couple of plastic clips that hold it in.”

And in the future, I asked? “It will be hard to part with my E6. I am waiting on the first solid state 1000km BEVs before I would consider parting with it. I just love the two-tone paint job.”

Jo is another happy battery electric vehicle owner, sharing her learning experience “odyssey” and having fun. “Thanks for the opportunity to immortalise the odyssey,” she told me in her latest email. The future is bright and electric as we learn to live with our new vehicles.

David Waterworth is a retired teacher who divides his time between looking after his grandchildren and trying to make sure they have a planet to live on. He is long on Tesla [NASDAQ:TSLA].
