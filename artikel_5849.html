Beginning in the new year, minors will be required to pass a five-step seat belt fit test before they can ride without a booster seat or sit in the front seat of a vehicle. The bill's author, Assemblymember Lori Wilson, cited figures showing that child injuries and fatalities in crashes have risen since the pandemic, with children ages 8 to 12 disproportionately affected.

"Extending the required age for booster use and implementing straightforward testing can significantly enhance child safety," CalMatters reported.

"So the best thing to do to keep your child safe while riding in a vehicle matches what our law is," Wilson said of the bill in April 2025. "It really is to take into consideration all the varying types of seats that we sit on in the car. This is about safety.

"This is about making sure that if something happens, you are safe, and I think that’s the key thing that we’re trying to drive home is that it is not about treating people like babies."

Improper car seat and booster use remains widespread, according to federal and industry studies.

A 2011 National Child Restraint Use Special Study found that 46% of car seats and booster seats had at least one major installation or use error that could reduce their effectiveness in a crash.

Nearly all seats had some form of misuse, according to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.

A 2021 study by AAA and the National Safety Council found that more than half of all car seats brought to child passenger safety technicians for inspection were improperly installed or used, and that only one in five parents and caregivers sought expert help securing their child's seat.
