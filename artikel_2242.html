Free transfer signing is rediscovering his best form at Watford: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Watford have started this season poorly under Alessio Dionisi. Despite promising signs to start the year, Dionisi’s side have been stuck in a rut with led to talk of yet another managerial sacking from the Hornets.

Watford, who host Middlesbrough this weekend, need inspiration from somewhere as they look to climb away from the wrong end of the Championship table, and Myron Boadu could be providing exactly that.

The Dutch striker arrived at Vicarage Road on a free transfer in September after a difficult spell at PSV, but recent performances suggest he is beginning to rediscover the qualities that once made him such an exciting prospect.

His brace against Norwich City on October 31 was followed by an assist in the 1-0 victory over Lincoln City three days later. More importantly, these contributions have come alongside encouraging performances in other areas of his game.

Boadu is looking sharper, making intelligent runs and showing the work rate that made him so effective earlier in his career. There is a reason Boadu was once valued at more than €40m and attracted a €17m move to Monaco.

He scored 32 league goals in 64 appearances for AZ Alkmaar before enjoying another productive spell on loan at Bochum, where he registered nine goals in 19 Bundesliga appearances. Injuries and inconsistent opportunities subsequently disrupted his progress, but the underlying talent has never disappeared.

His early Watford numbers are encouraging. Across seven Championship appearances and 379 minutes, Boadu has scored twice and provided one assist, averaging approximately 0.7 goal involvements per 90 minutes, according to FotMob.

His 1.40 expected goals compared with two actual goals also suggests he is taking his opportunities well rather than relying on an unsustainable volume of chances.

There are encouraging signs beyond his attacking output, too. Boadu has registered five tackles, nine recoveries and 18 duels won, while recording 20 touches inside the opposition penalty area. These figures reflect a forward becoming increasingly involved in both Watford’s pressing game and their attacking transitions.

His developing partnership with Iker Bravo is another significant positive. Boadu’s understanding with the fellow forward appears to be growing, with their movement complementing one another without requiring extensive preparation.

The Norwich performance demonstrated their potential, while his contribution against Lincoln showed he can also influence games through his work without necessarily finding the net himself. Of course, seven appearances do not represent a complete revival, and Boadu still needs to deliver consistently over a longer period.

However, Watford cannot afford to overlook the encouraging signs. If he maintains this intensity and continues building his understanding with Bravo, the Hornets could have a valuable attacking weapon for the remainder of the campaign.
