Desai Chia designs Bethesda Residence to balance "openness and discretion"

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

A large light well brings daylight deep into this private home that was designed by Desai Chia Architecture for a tight site in a dense Maryland neighbourhood.

The project takes its name from its location in Bethesda, a city located northwest of Washington DC.

“Its compact lot and the close proximity of neighbouring houses required a careful response balancing both openness and discretion.”

The architects intended the house to “rethink suburban-urban hybrid living through a modern lens”.

Roughly rectangular in plan, it has three levels and a basement. Setbacks help break up the mass, particularly on the street-facing elevation.

The building appears “as a two-storey building from several vantage points,” the team said.

“The home is carefully scaled to blend with neighbouring houses, balancing a strong architectural identity with sensitivity to its suburban context,” the team said.

Facades are clad in grey fibre-cement panels made to last for a century. The material is known for protecting against fire, pests and extreme weather, according to the team.

“A durable, low-maintenance exterior with a strong architectural presence was a key client priority from the outset,” the architects said.

“Glass-fibre-reinforced concrete (GRFC) panels were selected to create a crisp, sculptural facade that captures texture and shifting light throughout the day.”

A modern-style bay window adds dimension and visual interest to the front and back facades.

Within the home, public spaces are located on the ground level. The rear portion opens into a yard with a lap pool.

The upper levels contain bedrooms and two offices, and the top floor has a rooftop terrace. The basement holds a gym, ping-pong area and media room.

“This vertical, faceted volume allows sunlight to cascade downward, distributing diffuse light from a small skylight across circulation spaces on multiple levels while minimising solar heat gain,” Desai Chia Architecture said.

The all-electric house has a geothermal heating and cooling system. To enable the homeowners to age in place, the house has a shaft where an ADA-compliant elevator could be added in the future.

The homeowners are physicians, one of whom can walk to his practice in downtown Bethesda.

“When needed, the dedicated home offices allow both physicians to work from home in spaces with calming natural light,” the studio said.

“Its location – easily walkable to downtown amenities and robust public transit – supports a connected pattern of work and family life.”

Other homes in Maryland include a restored carriage house by Colleen Healey Architecture that features colourful facade panels and a family home by Ziger Snead clad in stone shingles, charred wood and weathering steel.
