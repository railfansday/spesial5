Citi and Circle join World Bank in tests with USDC, unlocking a massive aid shift

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The World Bank is exploring USDC payments with Citi and Circle to speed up development funding across eligible countries.

The World Bank Group said Nov. 17 that it would work with Citi and Circle to explore using USDC to settle development payments.

The group wants to see whether a dollar stablecoin can make those payments faster, cheaper, and more transparent.

Under the proposal, Citi would provide links to established banking networks, while Circle’s infrastructure would help issue USDC and settle payments in it.

The World Bank would examine where that route could work alongside its existing arrangements, which rely on correspondent banks to handle payments across banking networks.

The initial exploration will engage borrower countries where digital assets are supported and permitted and focus on an intermediary-based model. The World Bank gave no launch date or list of participating borrowers, and said it would explore working with other banks over time.

The World Bank uses its original FundsChain platform to give project teams a record of where development money goes.

Authorized users can trace payments from the initial disbursement to the final recipient and inspect supporting documents. The group says more than 350 projects across 96 countries are on the tracking platform.

Borrowers use the same process to apply for withdrawals, and projects continue paying suppliers and beneficiaries as before. FundsChain lets project teams document transactions through designated project accounts and local accounts farther down the payment chain.

The records can also feed into expenditure statements that borrowers submit through the bank’s client portal.

Linking an invoice to a transaction lets authorized reviewers check what a payment covered. That oversight can extend beyond the initial transfer to a borrower and into the spending that happens within a project.

The bank has separately explored whether it can build spending rules into payments themselves. In a July update, it said a FundsChain Evolution pilot had gone live in Ethiopia using programmable money to place conditions on how the funds can be spent.

The proposal leaves open where USDC would enter the payment chain, whether recipients would get tokens, and how any tokens would be converted to local currency.

Those details would determine what the change in settlement would mean for the people and organizations that ultimately receive the money.
