Hearts' European hopes hang by a thread but Vrancken proud

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Hearts' hopes of a memorable draw with Monaco were dashed in the 94th minute

There was a bittersweet feeling at Tynecastle after Folarin Balogun's 94th-minute winner preserved Monaco's perfect Conference League record and condemned Hearts to a third defeat from four.

Wouter Vrancken's side had gone toe-to-toe with one of the best teams in the tournament, scored two glorious goals, but ultimately came away with nothing for their efforts.

Now they have little to no margin for error in their two remaining league phase matches.

They will likely have to beat both Borac Banja Luka of Bosnia and Cyprus' Pafos to reach the knockout stages, but if they perform as they did against Monaco, they will give themselves a fighting chance.

Hearts have garnered a reputation as entertainers under Vrancken - scoring a raft of goals while being porous at the back.

This game was their season in microcosm. The goals - scored by Jordi Altena and Sabah Kerjota - were things of beauty, picture-perfect strikes worthy of any stage.

But as thrilling as Hearts were in attack, they were as baffling at the back.

Jamie McCart unnecessarily tripped Matthis Abline, allowing Balogun to score from the penalty spot and Monaco's second came from Altena giving the ball away.

"It's Gladiators - are we not entertained? It's sensational," said Hearts legend John Robertson at half-time, with the game finely poised at 2-2.

Both teams had chances to win it in the second half, but it was Monaco who took theirs in the final stages. Mamadou Coulibaly was afforded too much space and he slipped Balogun through to beat Beau Reus.

"It's a lack of nous and a little bit of naivety," former Hearts midfielder Michael Stewart said on TNT Sports. "McCart backs off and allows the ball to be played into Balogun's feet."

Despite the frustrating nature of the late concession, Vrancken's overwhelming emotion was of pride.

"I'm very disappointed with how we give the goal away at the end," he said.

"The biggest thing is the pride for the performance today. Against a side of Monaco's [quality] - we're still missing nine players through injury - this performance was really pleasing."

Before the recent international break, Hearts were beaten 3-1 by Swiss outfit Thun, before a shock 5-2 home defeat by high-flying Falkirk in the Scottish Premiership.

However, they bounced back with a ruthless 8-0 win at Dundee on Saturday and carried that confidence into this game against a team with a far bigger budget.

"You saw that the confidence was there from the weekend and there was conviction around their box," Vrancken added. "It will not work every time, but the conviction and energy was there again.

"Our team kept their heads in the game and kept going for it, not being pleased with 2-2. Really pleased with what I saw."

Aside from his dismay at Balogun's winner and how it came about, Vrancken's main gripe was with the officials.

He felt there was a foul on Kerjota in the build-up to Monaco's second goal and was bemused at the fact neither Takumi Minamino and Denis Zakaria were sent off.

Minamino - already on a yellow card - wiped out Sabri Guendouz on the edge of the Monaco box, but got away with a warning. And VAR opted not to intervene after Zakaria went over the top of the ball and caught Pierre Landry Kabore on the shin.

"The second goal, they pulled Sabah [Kerjota] and he can't do his defensive work," Vrancken said.

"It was not the only refereeing decision that was interesting tonight. I don't know how it's possible not to give a red card for the tackle on [Pierre Landry Kabore]. It's clearly above the ankle, so it's always a red card.

"And why didn't Minamino get a second yellow before half-time? He prevents a shot from Sabri at the edge of the box.

"The explanation of the fourth official was even more crazy - he explained it could be for his first yellow card, but not for his second yellow. It was clearly a yellow card and then we were against 10 in the second half."
