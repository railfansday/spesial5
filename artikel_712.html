Glam Slam: Hollywood’s Top 26 Beauty Moments of 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

From Inde Navarrette’s all-natural vibe to Florence Pugh’s anti-blush flush, THR tallies the most notable makeup and hairstyle red-carpet trends of the year.

A certain vibe defined Hollywood style this year: Looking real instead of bot-perfect, and sprezzatura and effortless instead of try-hard dominated red carpet looks across awards and gala season. Whether subtle (Florence Pugh’s anti-blush), maximalist (Doechii’s animal-print nails) or retro (the slew of young actors in Brad Pitt’s Fight Club warrior cut), the trends that popped also elevated careers, drove product and ticket sales, and consumed the internet.

AI may be infiltrating, but zoomers aren’t here for it. For Obsession star Inde Navarrette, authenticity is crucial. “We never try to create something so perfect that it looks like it could be AI-generated,” says her hairstylist, Marc Mena. “Little imperfections make her feel most like herself.” At the Toronto Film Festival, midcentury Mexican actress Maria Félix provided inspiration as Navarrette has been “embracing her Latin culture.” All-natural Navarrette still resonates strongly: Sombr’s “My Body Isn’t Ready” music video, co-starring the actress and garnering 20 million-plus YouTube views, showcases her “beautiful, clean skin and an emphasis on her eyes,” says makeup artist Kayli Rachelle. “It’s her, just amplified.”

Amid the forces of blush flush (think Sabrina Carpenter), red carpet rebel Florence Pugh went muted at the Emmys, a choice by makeup artist Alex Babsky. Between Pugh and Gen Z queen Navarrette, who donned a Dior Toffee blush at the Academy Museum Gala, cooler beiges took off in the fourth quarter, driving a 290 percent surge in search for the hue, per Spate, in the wake of the hot pinks that dominated earlier in the year. Explains Rachelle, “A lot of people don’t want to feel like they are wearing a ton of makeup.”

If anyone can get Angelenos of all ages to leave behind their decades-long love affair with lengthy tresses, it’s Zendaya. In September, Coree Moreno created a confident pixie on the Dune star inspired by late-’90s-era Halle Berry. “It’s a statement-making moment,” he says of his client, who “wanted a romanticized pixie cut that feels feminine but also powerful.” The gamine cut took over Paris Fashion Week (PFW) the following month. Makeup artist Ernesto Casillas leaned into “frosts, satin, a pretty lash and pretty skin that let the hair do the talking.”

For Hudson Williams, dedication to his multistep skin care routine charmed his Heated Rivalry fans and landed him a brand ambassadorship with Korean beauty brand Skin1004. The Yaga star considers his relationship with groomer-turned-friend Aika Flores, who crafted his swooping heartthrob hair, one of his most important today. Online speculation had Flores unilaterally deciding that Williams keep his now-signature look, but the actor clarifies that he made the call, saying, “I want my hair to be same.” At the same time, Williams’ sense of style adventure has been well documented. “I think men should get on the barrettes [trend] because they’re so cute,” he opines. His exaggerated cat-eye liner and tousled wet-look hair made his Met Gala look top many 2026 lists, but for Williams, there’s another that stands out. “I liked the hair you did when I was going to the Armani show,” he tells Flores. “Oh, the cigarette that he got in trouble for,” she responds, referring to the photo that flooded the internet of the 25-year-old lighting up on the streets of Milan, conjuring a bit of old Hollywood energy. A security guard scolded him to put it out. “I wasn’t even trying to smoke that,” he swears.

July saw an obsession sparked by Sadie Sink’s born-with-it copper waves, created by hairstylist Ryan Richman and framed by her backless Prada gown at the Spider-Man: Brand New Day L.A. premiere. Searches for “Sadie Sink hair color” rose 5,000 percent year-over-year, per Google, as outlets proclaimed red to be the lit shade of 2026. To complement her hair, makeup artist Nina Park applied “sheer layers of coverage so her freckles still peeked through, like a pair of sheer pantyhose over the skin, where everything looks a little more even but you can still see what’s underneath.” Between the freckles and undyed hair hue, the authenticity drove a fervor among Sink fans.

Chappell Roan, Doechii, Khloé Kardashian and Hailey Bieber flicked clean nails aside for animal prints: Roan with a snakeskin pattern at PFW; Doechii with a tortoise-shell pedi for the Grammys and peacock accents on her digits for the L.A. Fashion Awards; Bieber in leopard tips for an appearance in South Korea; and Kardashian setting socials afire with a fawn-print mani. “It starts with fashion and then transfers to nails,” says Zola Ganzorigt, Kardashian and Bieber’s manicurist. Rachel Sun did the talons of fellow trendsetters Doechii and Roan.

Originally inspired by dancers’ street attire, balletcore, which has been fluttering as a trending topic for a few years, soared into all areas of style, from sneakerina shoes to ballet-inspired makeup, in 2026. According to Google Trends, the search term leaped 550 percent year-over-year as of August. At center stage was Rosalía, whose 57-show arena Lux Tour featured prima-chic costumes from Dior and makeup by Sergio Antón De Las Nieves. He managed to deftly combine the soft and ethereal aesthetics of a ballerina with the practical goal of making the onstage looks readable to the audience with defined brows and lips. But the showstopper was the skin, which had a radiant dewy finish.

Parallel trending with subtler cheeks are vibrant makeup looks. U.K.-based Esther Edeme led a “painted-by-Esther blush” movement on Instagram, with bright washes across the cheekbones of Tyla, Love Island‘s Olandria Carthen and Manon from Katseye going viral. “I think it has something to do with me being Black and as fearlessly loud with it as I am,” she says. “I don’t think we’ve seen that since Pat McGrath.” Edeme says “consuming a lot of Marvel and Drag Race growing up” influenced her love of bold, colorful makeup, as did images of Grace Jones. Makeup artist Sophia Sinot also pushed the envelope with a Hello Kitty look for pop star Zara Larsson on her Midnight Sun Tour. Says Edeme of her and her colleague’s daring work, “Before [Sinot], before me, it was very bland. It was very, ‘You can’t take a risk because you’ll look like a clown,’ and we were like, ‘No.’ ” Adds Sinot, “My main goal is to end the clean-girl era.” All of her looks for Larsson hit with her 1.4 million followers across socials.

Style mavens may be forgiven if they start nodding off during stretches of awards season’s typically safe red carpet looks. Renate Reinsve jolted us awake by arriving at the European Film Awards wearing a Calvin Klein minimalist frock with a novel headscarf that was somehow both chic and picnic-appropriate. Kaia Gerber followed suit in an iteration of the Klein look for an L.A. party celebrating sustainable brand We Are Uni, and The Buccaneers actress Alisha Boe and hairstylist Mashal Afzalzada put their own spin on the trend in scarf-to-toe Miu Miu during the Venice Film Festival.

Overly filled, overlined lips officially became passé this year. Stars began requesting of their makeup artists the K-beauty-inspired trend of cloudy, popsicle and blurred lips. “It’s so wearable and enhances with subtlety but is also polished,” says Margot Robbie’s makeup artist Pati Dubroff of the softer, universally flattering lip. For Bridgerton promos, makeup artist Naoko Scintu gave season four star Yerin Ha lips that, fittingly, looked like they’d just been kissed. “They’re more lived in with feathered edges,” says Scintu, who prefers the brown undertones she used on Ha to make it feel more modern, though any lip shade works. TikTokers made blurred lips the viral lip trend of 2026. Pinterest saw the uptick as well, with an 86 percent increase in searches for blurred lips.

Confident, extra-curly styles, in the past an occasional look, became a Hollywood staple. Chase Infiniti brought disco curls by Coree Moreno to the Met Gala, Emmy Rossum repeatedly sported the do courtesy of hairstylist Marcia Hamilton, and Kerry Washington wore the ’70s style to the Gotham Awards. “What’s happening in the beauty world is everyone is embracing their freedom. It’s fun,” says Washington’s curl queen Nai’vasha. The natural hair texture trend extended well beyond the red carpet. Explains Trendalytics’ Abigail Hoagberg, “We’re seeing strong consumer interest in products that enhance curls rather than change them.” Nai’vasha says the key to creating cloud-like curls right now is a light touch when it comes to styling: “Two products, three max, because I want the hair to be bouncy and free. Make sure it’s healthy and moisturized, then do a cartwheel in it.”

Basketballer Angel Reese’s on-court hairstyles, which included pink hair by hairstylist Jamal Bryce for All-Star Weekend, helped the Bayou Barbie score multiple beauty and fashion contracts, such as her Victoria’s Secret campaign, a first for any WNBA player. Olympic freestyle skier Eileen Gu, whose shimmering Met Gala makeup by Alicia Martinez mimicked the iridescence of her bubble-covered dress, just landed a contract with Japanese skin care brand SK-II. Figure skater Alysa Liu’s halo hair, with its brown and blond horizontal stripes styled by Kelsey Miller, made her as instantly recognizable on the ice as the Vanity Fair Oscar party carpet; the Louis Vuitton ambassador inspired the models’ hair at the brand’s NYC Cruise show, with parfait hair layers ruling that runway. The boys brought their glam off the bench as well. Men’s hairstyles drove the style conversation, with FIFA World Cup star Erling Haaland’s blond “Viking flow” long hair getting buzz-cut in a YouTube video that has been viewed more than 10 million times.

Sarah Paulson calls her hairstyle at the Actor Awards “a tweener” — an in-between length “that’s neither here nor there.” The American Horror Story star was trying to grow it long but didn’t feel like herself “with so much hair.” Bridget Brager created a relaxed crop, later dubbed a boyfriend bob thanks to its equivalence to the boyfriend jean: lived-in, unfussy and cool-girl confident. Paulson says the transitional cut echoed a challenging transition in her life when her good friend Diane Keaton died just before the ceremony. Says Brager, “That was a difficult time for her; she knew she had to do a difficult thing [presenting the ‘In Memoriam’ segment], and she wanted to be comfortable.” Brager not only delivers style with sensitivity, she also acts as Paulson’s voice of reason. “I’m a very impulsive person, and I’m like, ‘Cut it, chop it, bleach it, make it red!’ And Bridget’s like, ‘Hold on, hold on.’ ” Adds Brager: “I’m thinking about what she has to do next more than she does.” At the moment, Paulson’s hoping there’s a role with pink hair in her future.

Producer/star Margot Robbie’s method glam on the red carpet worked so well for Barbie, she brought it back for Wuthering Heights. It’s not about “taking the character out of the movie and putting her on the carpet but leaning into the essence so that people are even more excited to know the character after seeing the actress give you a glimpse,” says Robbie’s makeup artist Pati Dubroff, who embraced reds that were prominent throughout the film, including a red wash on the eyelids for the London premiere and using blush to create a just-ran-across-a-moor flush. Hairstylist Bryce Scarlett brought romantic hairstyles that alliterative influencers called Brontë buns and Wuthering waves, while colorist Jacob Schwartz deepened the base to add moodiness. While trend reports credited the look for increased interest in Regency (actually, Georgian) romantic beauty, the real metric is the $241 million in the movie’s global box office.

“It” Brits like Louis Partridge, Kit Connor and Christopher Briney (all styled by Brady Lea) joined Hollywood’s youngest leading men Timothée Chalamet and Austin Butler (both work with hairstylist Jamie Taylor) in sporting the male hairstyle of the year: a textured, disheveled style dubbed the warrior cut, à la Brad Pitt in Fight Club. Hair pro Lea is updating the Tyler Durden-inspired ’90s version by “tapering in at the ears so we get a nice angle that accentuates the jawline and cheekbone.” Why this cut now? Explains Lea: “It’s a rebellion against the romantic, glossy approach to men’s hairstyling.” Ratifying the trend: Millions of hashtags for #warriorhaircut, #fightclubhair and #tylerdurdenhaircut are populating tutorials and posts across socials.

Hockey romance series were inescapable this year, and when Prime Video’s successful stab at the genre, Off Campus, broke containment, the internet couldn’t get enough of star Mika Abdalla’s signature curls. “The Allie,” much like “The Rachel,” inspired by Jennifer Aniston’s iconic Friends look, had women young and old running to their hairdressers for a shaggy-meets-chic cut to match the both on- and offscreen look that Abdalla’s go-to artists Marisa Mitchell and Kristin “Kiki” Heitkotter created. With the 26-year-old actress set to star in the show’s upcoming second season, it’s likely “The Allie” will continue into 2027.

After the furor over early images from the set of Love Story showing Sarah Pidgeon wearing a questionable wig as Carolyn Bessette, Ryan Murphy called in colorist Kari Hill to accurately re-create the style icon’s hair color. Hill took Pidgeon’s hair from dark brunette to 1992 Bessette blond over two days at Mèche Salon, before flying to the New York set to update the hue during filming to mirror Bessette’s increasing blondification throughout the decade. “Her hair was a character,” says Hill. “In the early ’90s, it was just like her — more carefree. When she became a Kennedy, her hair reflected who she was, more pulled together, more conservatively styled. Once we got it right, there were no more questions asked.”

Makeup maven Sandy Ganzer did a blue-gray wash on Hunter Schafer’s eyelids for the Met Gala, while Rokael Lizama, David Velasquez and Ernesto Casillas had their clients singing the blues, too, respectively: Jennifer Lopez at Paris Haute Couture Week, Teyana Taylor at the Academy Museum Gala, and Zendaya for press tours for both The Drama and The Odyssey. “That went viral. That had a lot of people online re-creating it,” Casillas says of the Drama turn. Says Ganzer, “As long as you get the right tone, the sky’s the limit.”

Thuso Mbedu, star of the Gina Prince-Bythewood’s much-anticipated adaptation of the best-selling novel Children of Blood and Bone, says she’s done playing it safe on the red carpet. Launching into Hollywood with her starring role in 2021’s The Underground Railroad, she says: “I didn’t know how the industry worked. Now I’m in a place where I can have a voice, I can have an opinion.” She brought this confidence to CinemaCon as she promoted Blood and Bone, set to bow in January. Collaborating with hairstylist Sharif Poston, she created a strong fishtail braid to channel her character, Zelie, complete with simple, strong makeup by Rebekah Aladdin. “She is very much a warrior, so how do we give warrior? How do we start planting the seeds of this fierce character that people are going to be introduced to?” says Mbedu, who plans to push the envelope further with her upcoming press tour. “Get ready, because we mean business.”

Weeks after hairstylist Jenny Cho fashioned a low ponytail with a knot on Grace Gummer for the Emmys, Owen Gould created a low do with a knot detail on Ana de Armas for the Louis Vuitton runway show. “For a while, we’ve seen so much structure with Kendall Jenner and Hailey Bieber hair — a center part that’s slicked back,” says Gould. “People are ready to see something a little different. This feels relaxed, less try-hard.” Cho agrees: “That element of, ‘Let’s just go, let’s accentuate what we have,’ is really attractive at the moment.” Hoagberg of Trendalytics says what’s making socials pop these days is “beauty [that] is moving toward a more natural, relaxed aesthetic.”

After the client has been safely stowed inside the Escalade, the kit has been packed and the looks posted, that’s when the onslaught begins, with fans clamoring for tutorials and the list of products used. Every one of Whitney Peak’s Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping looks prompted an avid response, particularly on Reddit boards. Hairstylist Marcia Hamilton created “cool girl” cornrows as a nod to the braids that appear throughout the Games franchise. Makeup artist Tasha Reiko Brown worked with deep-rose neutrals, including the much-inquired-about Chanel Rouge Coco Hydra Gloss in Icing lips. “She’s got a once-in-a-lifetime face,” says Brown, with Hamilton adding, “Because you’re starting out with such a gorgeous canvas, you can take her in any direction.”

Celebrities went to the chopping block in ’26: Ariana Grande had Brandon Pietsch snip the last of her long Glinda locks away, Billie Eilish sampled a copper-blond layered crop by Benjamin Mohapi, hairstylist Dimitris Giannetos helped Penélope Cruz flaunt a breezy highlighted weekend bob, and Shrinking‘s Lukita Maxwell went further with an ear-grazing length engineered by hairstylist Derek Yuen. Charlize Theron’s myriad hairstyles dotted the carpets for The Odyssey, Apex and the Academy Museum Gala, where she was honored. “I asked myself how many different ways can I interpret this strong bob,” explains Theron’s longtime hairstylist, Adir Abergel. “It was about showing people that one haircut can give you a multitude of looks when you want to play with it.”

Coincidence or clever marketing? In 2026, Jennifer Lawrence became her own billboard when she embraced devil-may-care loose waves and bangs by hairstylist Gregory Russell. In January, Mark Townsend styled her tresses for the Dior Haute Couture show in Paris, where J.Law arrived wearing jeans, a white tank and an elegant men’s coat with fur cuffs. “We couldn’t give her a massive hairdo for that,” Townsend says of the low-key, near-natural look. “Seeing her natural texture opens the door for so many other people to accept their texture.” Not to mention to use the products that she does, including from her hair care line, Mes Beauty, which was launched in October.

Greta Lee and her K-squad “are always trying to push the look without committing to it,” says hairstylist Jenny Cho, “so I used a little fake bang. The ends were wispy, not straight across like Bettie Page, but more modern.” The base of the look, at the Toy Story 5 L.A. premiere, was a sculptural Dior dress. To “balance everything out,” makeup pro Nina Park says she brought emphasis to the lips “using richer, pigmented colors to anchor the face. I didn’t feel the need to add another statement.” Her light touch is well regarded, with more than 32 million posts on TikTok that mention “Nina Park.”

Rose Byrne’s awards season glam oeuvre was stunning overall, but the look that got the most attention, says hairstylist Harry Josh, “was not from the carpet, it was for a Chanel party the night before the Oscars, the look with the bow. That one went nuts, people lived for that.” Why? “Because the audience wants drama.” With awards season red carpets, potential nominees tend to choose between playing it safe to keep the focus on the actor and their work — and going for broke. “That’s what a lot of hairdressers and makeup artists grapple with,” says Josh. “You have to say, ‘This isn’t about me, this is about her.’ “
