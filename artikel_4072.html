Feature: GTA 6 Made Me Want to Be a Good Guy (or Girl)

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

I’m playing a new GTA game but I’m actually abiding by the traffic rules.

I’m playing a new GTA game but I’m clicking L3 and R3 to focus and targeting non-vitals.

I’m playing a new GTA game but I’m actually opting to defuse the situation when an NPC gets mad at me.

The more I reflect on my time with GTA 6, the more I realise this is not the GTA I necessarily grew up with, even though the carnage is always there bubbling beneath the surface.

That’s because just like GTA Vice City and GTA San Andreas, I do have the option to go on a reckless murdering spree.

I could stock up on grenades and sticky bombs and create chaos on a highway.

I could go to a beach and mow down innocent bystanders in a scene so gruesome it’d make Jack Thompson pass out.

I think this is the most remarkable thing about GTA 6 – it’s made me play differently to past titles, even though nothing has actually changed.

So, why is this? Why am I choosing non-lethal as much as humanly possible, when the game allows me to go mental if I want to?

Firstly, Jason and Lucia certainly aren’t saints by any stretch, but they just don’t seem like the type to go on a killing spree.

This isn’t the first GTA game to have protagonists with a slightly softer personality – GTA 4’s Niko immediately springs to mind, as does GTA 5’s Franklin.

But I think with romance being one of this game’s central themes, you really get to know these characters intimately.

It’s about the quiet moments, rubbing your partner’s feet in front of the TV.

It’s relatable – and if it’s relatable, then I think the vast, vast, vast majority of us are kind and caring to others.

Of course, this is still a video game and I racked up kills in the hundreds by the end of the campaign, but I never felt like I was doing it on purpose.

I made mistakes – I sometimes knocked cyclists clean into the air. I wouldn’t do that in real-life. But I did try to avoid these moments as much as possible here.

One other wrinkle is that the realistic graphics mean the violence is pretty visceral. It’s fascinating to think how much criticism the PS2 trilogy got at the time, when it looks like a cartoon compared to this game.

I wouldn’t say the violence necessarily deterred me from being a cold-blooded killer, but I can see how it would affect others.

And also it’s a gameplay mechanic, right?

Behaving recklessly and showing no real care for other virtual life will alter your criminal profile, and change how the world reacts to you.

I ended up with a personality trait sitting somewhere between saint and sinner, but I think that’s probably normal as you’re still going to have to punch an NPC in the mouth from time-to-time.

I’m sure there’ll be people who see the GTA series as more of an arcade game than the life sim it’s become in GTA 6, and there’ll be many who don’t like this change.

For me, I’m fascinated by how Rockstar subtly convinced me to play a GTA game like it’s not a GTA game. And I didn’t even really notice it until recently.

That’s just one of the title’s many, many achievements – and one of the reasons why it’s now the highest rated game of all time.
