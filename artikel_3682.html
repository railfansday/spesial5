Benito Skinner’s ‘Overcompensating’ Returns for Season 2: More Laughs, More Sex, More Gay

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

It’s been a year and a half since the first season of Benito Skinner’s Prime Video comedy series, Overcompensating, debuted, and a lot has changed for him in that time. For one, Skinner—also known as Benny Drama, his online alter ego—is blond now. It’s a change he says may be permanent or perhaps semipermanent. “It’s how I see myself—as a blond,” he tells me over Zoom. “So we’re going to keep going with it for a sec.”

But Skinner has transformed in much less superficial ways since the launch of his hit Amazon series, which is based on his experiences coming out as gay as a Georgetown undergrad. Equal parts raunchy and heartwarming, the first season follows Benny (Skinner), a closeted former high school football player who attempts to navigate the chaos of college, where he forms an unlikely bond with a fellow student named Carmen (Wally Baram).

Skinner shares that making the second installment of the series was “way more fluid, way less stressed.” As it should have been. The first season was a popular hit for the streamer (it has a 93% fresh rating on Rotten Tomatoes), hard-launching Skinner as an exciting new voice plucked from the internet and ready for prime time.

Benny and Carmen begin the season as enemies.

The second season, which Vanity Fair can exclusively reveal will debut on February 17, 2027, expands the world he created with new storylines for the supporting characters—and bigger stakes for Benny as he comes into his own with his sexuality. Benny navigates not only coming out to his sister, Grace (Mary Beth Barone), and his parents (Connie Britton and Kyle MacLachlan), but also his own sexual exploration—and all the awkwardness that comes with it.

So much of what made the first season a hit—the awkward comedy, the silly themed parties, the talking Megan Fox poster—returns, but this time many of the characters grapple with deeper issues. Yet Skinner felt lighter making the show this time around. “I think in season one, Benny is that stressed, so that kind of worked,” he says. “But in this one, we’re having more fun. He has some joy, so we can loosen up a little bit.”

Connie Britton, center, reprises her role as Benny's mom.

After Overcompensating premiered, Skinner was approached, he says, by “a few gays” who told him, “I thought it’d be gayer, the show.” He promises that the second season delivers just that: “We’re creeping out of the closet,” says Skinner.

In the first season, Benny is trying to find his place on campus while constantly living in fear that the people around him will discover he’s gay. He falls for a dashing British classmate named Miles (Rish Shah), only to find out he is straight and has kissed Carmen. The season ends with Carmen revealing that she has also slept with Grace’s boyfriend, Peter (Adam DiMarco), and in the final moments, Carmen accidentally lets slip in front of Grace that Benny is gay.

Skinner and Lukas Gage reunite in season two.

In the upcoming season, Benny heads back home with his sister for the start of Christmas break, struggling because he hasn’t yet come out to his family. “If season one was about friends, I think this season is about family, and trying to reintroduce yourself to your parents after starting to become yourself,” says Skinner. The way his father finds out about it makes for an especially tense moment, and Benny goes to great lengths (like joining the crew team) to mend that bond with his dad. “That relationship between gay men and father, I think, is always fraught and confusing,” he says. “I just wanted to make it as honest as possible.”

At the start of the new season, Benny and Carmen are at odds; Grace and her boyfriend, Peter, have broken up; and many of the characters are exploring those feelings of loneliness that can crop up in college. A handful of exciting new faces also join this season. Australian actor Aisha Dee (The Bold Type) plays a new student who becomes an instant It girl on campus. AnnaSophia Robb (The Carrie Diaries) joins as an intense coxswain (the one who does the yelling) on the crew team. And Tony-nominated stage actor Tom Francis (Sunset Boulevard) plays the captain of the team, serving as Benny’s newest love interest.

Peter (Adam DiMarco) is looking for his path forward.

For Skinner, the second season is about capturing the ups and downs of coming out. It’s not just one reveal and then you’re out of the closet and everything is better. “I get to be even more specific about coming out and also tell a story that I think I haven’t seen yet done in this way, which is the complication of coming out—how you can take 10 steps forward, a hundred steps back,” he says.

Benny joins the crew team, where he meets a new love interest (Tom Francis).

There’s also a lot more sex. “There are a couple scenes that are definitely going to be off-limits for the family,” Skinner says. But of course, in Overcompensating, the sex scenes aren’t always smooth sailing. “I think our show is about trying to be sexy and then it going wrong, or feeling like you start to come into yourself but you get made a fool,” he says.

Skinner adds the title of executive music producer to his résumé this season. “It feels like a return to my radio-DJ roots, and that feels right,” he says. There’s more Britney Spears and Charli xcx (who appeared in a cameo last season), and he wanted the show’s music to remain an important part of the world. He even wrote a letter to Coldplay when he wanted to use the band’s song “Fix You” in this new season. “I love doing the letters to these guys because I’m like, So is Chris Martin reading this? Is this sent by owl? What’s the vibe?” he says with a smile.

Overcompensating’s return will come almost two years after its debut, and Skinner, who says he always plans to keep this story set in the characters’ college years, wants to make the wait a little shorter next time. “I apologize to everyone that it took a while,” he says. “TV takes a long time, so I’m trying to go faster, but daddy has to write it.”

“I don’t want any of these characters to feel like we’re pandering to maybe what we think the internet will want,” says Skinner of crafting the second installment after a hit first season.

“What I wanted to do is surprise people with where we took it,” says Skinner of the new season, “and have them be like, ‘Oh, this isn’t what I asked for, but it is something kind of delicious and new.’”
