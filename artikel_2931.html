Even With Jensen Huang Back as CEO, Nvidia Is Down 31% From Its High, While Broadcom Is Down 35%. Here’s the Better Buy Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Artificial intelligence (AI) stocks are down big, even after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO.

By Daniel Foelber – Nov 24, 2026 at 11:02AM EST

The stock market just had its worst week of 2026 that left many artificial intelligence (AI) stocks in a full-blown crash, rapidly dropping 10% to 20% or more.

As of market close on Nov. 20, Nvidia (NVDA -8.73%) was down 42.1% in just one month and 32.7% since CEO Jensen Huang admitted to marital infidelity. Reports indicate that the Securities and Exchange Commission is conducting a civil investigation into Nvidia for investor disclosures and accounting issues.

Nvidia is also under scrutiny for potentially violating U.S. export controls by allowing restricted AI chips to make their way to China. On Nov. 19, California Attorney General Rob Bonta hit Nvidia with a subpoena based on Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy. On Nvidia's Nov. 17 earnings call, management made it clear they did not intend to bring back Huang.

But before the market opened on Nov. 23, Nvidia published a press release that its Board of Directors had unanimously voted to reinstate Huang as CEO after “a series of discussions and meetings with Mr. Huang, after which the Board was satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead NVIDIA. In the process of reaching its decision, the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”

But Huang and the company still face ongoing probes for past actions.

In a stunning reversal, Nvidia gained 31.5% in a single session to close Nov. 23 at $165.05 per share. Even when factoring in that gain, Nvidia is down 31% from its all-time high, while Broadcom (AVGO -4.21%) is 34.9% off its highs.

Here's what investors need to see from Nvidia, and whether Broadcom is a better semiconductor stock to buy now.

Nvidia makes the world's most advanced AI chips and has grown its data center market share by delivering rack-scale supercomputers that integrate graphics processing units (GPUs), central processing units, and networking infrastructure. It is attracting spending from hyperscalers, AI labs, start-ups, and enterprises. Nvidia has only been shipping chips under its most advanced platform (Vera Rubin) since August — giving it a clear runway for near-term growth, with management already forecasting 75% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, even though Nvidia is still in the middle of its fourth quarter of fiscal 2027.

So based on financial results alone, Nvidia looks too cheap to ignore and arguably the best stock to buy in the entire market. There's just one problem.

Nvidia was in free fall because the investment thesis seemed to fall apart in a dramatic, sudden way. Nvidia seemed unbreakable, like accidentally knocking a basketball off a shelf; investors were confident it would bounce back eventually. Only that basketball ended up being a glass trophy rather than court-approved. And investors are left picking up the pieces, wondering what went wrong.

Huang's return as CEO isn't a magic wand that automatically mends the investment thesis. There are still cracks, such as potential repercussions of external and internal investigations. And given how quickly Huang resigned and the board and management moved on, only to end up bringing back Huang anyway, it's clear that Huang's second stint as CEO is much more fragile than his first. There's still the risk that investigations could provide grounds for removing him down the road. Or that Huang gobbles up even more power against a senior leadership team that seemed so willing to move on without him.

Still, Nvidia is certainly better off with Huang at the helm than without. Without Huang, Nvidia risked deteriorating some of its relationships with customers and suppliers and, even worse, losing some of its top talent.

If Nvidia maintains its innovative edge, improves its governance and organizational structure, and positions the company to function without Huang, the stock will look like a generational buying opportunity in hindsight.

As tempting as buying the dip on Nvidia may seem, I think a better approach is to scoop up shares of other top growth stocks that have suffered collateral damage from the broader industry sell-off.

Broadcom fell a staggering 12.2% on Friday and only recovered 3% on Monday. It is in an even bigger drawdown than Nvidia.

Broadcom is arguably the most well-rounded semiconductor stock on the market. It designs custom AI accelerators for some of the largest hyperscalers. You may have heard that Alphabet (GOOG -1.70%) (GOOGL -1.74%) is making more of its own custom AI chips for AI training and inference and has begun selling some of these chips to third-party customers. These chips, called Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), are designed in partnership with Broadcom.

Alphabet uses TPUs for its Gemini large language model, to power AI features across its platforms, and rents out TPU compute on Google Cloud. TPUs reduce reliance on Nvidia GPUs and give Alphabet greater control over its AI stack. And because the latest TPU 8t and 8i chips are custom-built for AI, they have proven to be highly efficient. Which is why Alphabet and Broadcom are now selling compute to hyperscalers, including a deal with Anthropic announced in April for multiple gigawatts of next-generation compute to train and run Claude on TPUs.

As large as Broadcom's business is with Alphabet, Anthropic (ANT -13.42%) and OpenAI could soon surpass Alphabet and Meta Platforms (META -1.81%) as Broadcom's largest customers.

Aside from AI chips, Broadcom is a leader in AI networking, general-purpose semiconductor solutions, and infrastructure software. In its latest quarter, which was third quarter fiscal 2026, Broadcom reported $29.6 billion in total revenue, 56.4% of which was AI semiconductor revenue. So AI is by far Broadcom's fastest-growing segment, but there's a lot more to the business outside of AI.

That diversification supports high free cash flows, a stable and growing dividend, and stock buybacks. In the first three quarters of fiscal 2026, Broadcom paid $9.28 billion in dividends and repurchased $8.45 billion of stock, compared with $2.45 billion in the first three quarters of fiscal 2025.

It has also paid a growing dividend for 15 consecutive years. Hock Tan is an exceptional CEO and has led the company for 20 years. Investors looking for a balanced semiconductor company with strong leadership may want to take a closer look at Broadcom.

While Broadcom hasn't sold off as much as Nvidia, its valuation has also gotten far more attractive. Broadcom now trades at just 16.2 times forward earnings compared to 17.1 for Nvidia.

Even with Huang back as CEO, Broadcom's investment thesis is far more intact than Nvidia's. And while Broadcom would see an earnings hit from an AI spending slowdown, it has the fundamentals to endure.

Add it all up, and Broadcom stands out as one of the best AI stocks to buy now.
