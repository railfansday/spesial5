Looking back at Steelers' historic losing streak vs Eagles in Philadelphia

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It's one of those sports streaks that seems hard to believe.

The Pittsburgh Steelers have not won against the in-state rival Eagles in Philadelphia since 1965. Really.

Terry Bradshaw, "Mean Joe" Greene and the "Steel Curtain" dynasty of the 1970s didn't do it. Neither did Greg Lloyd, Kevin Greene and the dangerous teams of the 1990s. Nor did Ben Roethlisberger and the mini Super Bowl-winning dynasty of the aughts.

That's mostly the result of NFL scheduling. Because the two teams are in different conferences, they've only met 20 times total in those 61 years. That's translated to just 10 matchups in the City of Brotherly Love.

The '70s Steelers only made the trip twice. The first was in 1970, before most of the Hall of Famers from that era had arrived. The only visit by a Super Bowl-winning side came in 1979.

Roethlisberger, likewise, somehow appeared at Lincoln Financial Field just twice in his 18-season career that's likely to get him into the Hall of Fame.

It's nevertheless been a difficult place for Pittsburgh to win, regardless of who's on the roster. Here's USA TODAY's look back at some of the standout games in which the Steelers have come up short in their journeys to Eastern Pa.

John "Frenchy" Fuqua – who'd become famous for his role in the "Immaculate Reception" two years later – had a huge day, piling up 218 rushing yards and two touchdowns on 20 carries. Eagles quarterback Norm Snead was efficient through the air, however. He completed 21 of 29 passes for 276 yards and two scores to hold off a Steelers team that struggled to get a passing game going in support of Fuqua.

Turnovers were the undoing of coach Chuck Noll's team that went on to win the franchise's fourth Super Bowl. Bradshaw tossed a pair of interceptions. The first, deep in his own territory, set up one of the Eagles' touchdowns. The other killed a drive into the opposing red zone. Fellow Hall of Famer Franco Harris later fumbled at the Philadelphia 2-yard line to take more possible points off the scoreboard. It was too much for even those legendary figures to overcome.

It was a bad day for "Slash." Dynamic quarterback Kordell Stewart threw three picks and completed less than half of his passes in a game that the Steelers never led despite making it look close in the final score. Future Hall of Fame running back Jerome Bettis also lost a fumble to contribute to a total of five turnovers for Pittsburgh that day. The Eagles committed none to earn a solid win against a Pittsburgh team that advanced to the AFC championship.

It was an ugly day for both teams offensively. They committed three turnovers each and combined to produce just 440 total yards. Roethlisberger got the worst of it, though. He was sacked eight times and had to give way to backup Byron Leftwich, who took a ninth sack of the day from the Eagles' vaunted defense. Eagles quarterback Donovan McNabb had better protection and was able to score enough to clear a low bar for his offense that day.

The Steelers' Killer Bs were at the height of their powers, but Le'Veon Bell was suspended for the first three games of the season for missing drug tests. Carson Wentz, of all people, put on a clinic with 301 yards and two touchdowns on 23 of 31 passing. Roethlisberger was sacked four times and threw an interception. Antonio Brown was the only Steeler to have a good day with 140 yards on 12 receptions.

This one is remembered for the dominant 21-play drive that the Eagles used to kill the last 10:29 of the fourth-quarter clock to remove any hope of a Pittsburgh comeback. They'd taken control of the game well before that. Pittsburgh quarterback Russell Wilson could not get much going offensively. He finished the day with just 14 completions for 128 yards. No Steelers back rushed for more than 14 yards. It would have been hard to imagine that changing even if they'd gotten the ball back. Still, that doesn't make the clinic Eagles QB Jalen Hurts and running back Saquon Barkley any less impressive on their way to their franchise's second Super Bowl win.
