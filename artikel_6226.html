United Airlines Pilots Defy ATC Orders To Fly Through “The Worst Part” Of A Severe Thunderstorm

United Airlines pilots scheduled to operate a nonstop flight from San Antonio International Airport (SAT) to Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD) refused to accept the air traffic control's proposed routing to the US capital, after they identified that the route would take them directly through a severe thunderstorm.

A recording shared by Aviation MP4 shows the moment the pilot questioned the clearance from ATC, which, instead of taking the service around the worst part of the weather, put the jet on a trajectory to travel through the worst part of heavy rain and thunderstorms. The pilot in command ultimately has the final say as to whether the flight can operate, as they hold the operational responsibility and safety for the flight. For this event, the pilots' concern was also backed up by United dispatch.

United was scheduled to operate flight 471 from San Antonio to Washington Dulles on Friday, November 6. As the plane had pushed back from the gate and was taxiing to the runway, the recording caught the moment that the crew queried the proposed route. Initially, air traffic control had rerouted the Boeing 737-700 to depart south and travel around the severe weather.

During the flight's taxi, the moments shared had the flight crew challenging the decision, with the pilot noting that he was going to speak with United dispatch, and that the crew was refusing to fly through what would have been up to four hours of challenging weather conditions for the crew, and unfavorable comfort for the passengers on board.

Simple Flying reached out to United for comment, and the airline confirmed the flight landed safely. It is believed that this incident occurred on Friday, November 6, but has not been confirmed by Simple Flying

Pilots onboard the flight quickly noted that they were unable to fly the proposed route, instead requesting that the initial routing, which would have taken the Washington-bound flight south of the severe weather. The air traffic control reevaluated the plan, while the United pilots spoke to airline dispatch, which backed them up, noting that they should not be flying through the adverse weather.

In the end, the crew was permitted to fly an alternative route, highlighting the authority that pilots have to reject an unsafe clearance request and opt for an alternative. While air traffic control provides the instructions and proposed flight paths, pilots in command have the final responsibility for the aircraft's safety, and all of those onboard, including passengers and crew.

United flight 471 is a daily service from San Antonio to Washington Dulles that has a scheduled departure time of 7:00 AM, with an expected arrival time into Washington of 11:13 AM. The usual flight duration is two hours and 45 minutes. For the proposed route through the thunderstorm would have added almost an hour of extra flying time according to the pilot's commentary in the video shared above.

The lasting impacts of the unthinkable on ATC.

The Pilot-In-Command (PIC) holds the final authority over the operation and safety of an aircraft. They are able to challenge or disagree with air traffic control or airline dispatch if they believe that the situation could be unsafe. Pilots have specific rights and protocols that they are allowed to use.

This can include rejecting clearance and requesting an alternative. They can also declare an emergency and deviate from any air traffic control clearance without prior permission if safety is compromised.

From San Antonio, United and United Express maintain regular scheduled flights to Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD), Denver (DEN), Houston George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH), Newark (EWR), San Francisco International Airport (SFO), and Washington Dulles.
