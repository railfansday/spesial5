Norse Atlantic Cancels All Flights As Norwegian Operating Company Files For Bankruptcy

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Norse Atlantic has canceled all flights and stopped selling tickets after its Norwegian operating company decided to file for bankruptcy. Efforts to save the remaining business continue, but for passengers with upcoming bookings, the immediate reality is grim. Another airline built around affordable longhaul travel has run out of money, further calling into question the sustainability of the low-cost business model.

Norse Atlantic announced today, November 27, 2026, that the board of its Norwegian operating subsidiary, Norse Atlantic Airways AS, will file bankruptcy petition with the Agder District Court in Norway.

The company could not secure sufficient funding to meet its immediate cash needs. Its board concluded that continued operations at the Norwegian subsidiary were no longer viable. The publicly listed parent, Norse Atlantic ASA, and other group companies are outside this filing. Management is still seeking a solution for the remaining business, including the UK and USA subsidiaries.

But all flights are canceled and ticket sales have ended. CEO Eivind Roald said:

“We are deeply sorry for the impact this has on our passengers, employees and partners.”

The bankruptcy announcement followed another serious development earlier today.

According to Reuters, the lessors of all 12 aircraft in the Norse fleet sent notices terminating their lease agreements. Norse disputes the validity of those notices.

That leaves the remaining business facing an enormous challenge. Finding new financing is difficult enough. Resolving access to the aircraft needed to operate the airline adds another obstacle.

The deterioration has been rapid. On November 25, Norse warned that its financial situation was critical. It suspended flights the following day. Today came the lease termination disclosures and the decision to put the Norwegian operating company into bankruptcy.

I’m not optimistic about this carrier ever flying again.

When I first wrote about Norse in 2021, I welcomed the prospect of another airline offering inexpensive transatlantic flights after Norwegian abandoned longhaul service.

My enthusiasm was primarily about competition. Even travelers who never booked Norwegian benefited when its fares forced larger airlines to respond. I hoped Norse could provide similar pressure while avoiding the problems that had undermined Norwegian.

Earlier this year, I covered Norse withdrawing from Los Angeles as fuel costs surged. Cutting routes that could no longer support themselves seemed sensible, even as it raised concerns about how much room the airline had left to maneuver.

Reuters identifies soaring fuel costs associated with the Iran war as a major pressure on the business. That is a brutal challenge for an airline trying to sell inexpensive tickets on flights that spend many hours in the air. But the broader difficulty with this model remains. A low advertised fare can fill seats without producing enough revenue to sustain the operation. Charging separately for meals and bags helps, but the airline simply could not sustain fares high enough to break even.

Established network carriers have advantages that are difficult to replicate, including connecting traffic and customers willing to pay substantial premiums for flexible schedules or premium cabins.

If you have an upcoming Norse booking, the airline has confirmed that all flights are canceled. If you’re left with a cancelling itinerary, contact your card issuer about the options available for a canceled service (if you paid extra for travel insurance, you can start there). If you booked through a travel agency or as part of a package, contact that provider too.

There will be plenty of people saying this outcome was predictable. Indeed it was, but that offers little comfort to employees facing uncertainty or passengers whose plans have fallen apart.

Norse gave travelers another option. Losing that option is disappointing even for those of us who generally preferred to fly on other carriers. I hope something viable can emerge from the remaining business. But another temporary infusion of cash into an operation that will soon need the same help again would be foolishness.

Norse Atlantic has canceled all flights and ended ticket sales as its Norwegian operating subsidiary moves to file for bankruptcy. The parent company and UK business remain outside that filing, with efforts to secure a future continuing.

For now, passengers need alternative travel arrangements, and employees face an uncertain future. I welcomed Norse because more competition benefits travelers. I am sorry to see it reach this point, even if the warning signs have been visible for some time.
