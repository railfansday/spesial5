Robertson eclipses Dalglish to become Scotland's most capped man

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

You've done Liverpool and Scotland proud - Kenny Dalglish on Andy Robertson's career

Andy Robertson has become Scotland's most capped men's international after starting in Monday's Nations League defeat in Switzerland to reach 103 appearances for his country.

Twelve years on from his debut - off the bench in a Poland friendly - he surpassed his friend and fellow Liverpool legend, Kenny Dalglish, whose record haul had stood for 40 years.

After moving level with the man they - and he - will always call King, the Tottenham Hotspur left-back shifted chat towards the former forward, his commendable humility laid bare.

But, in St Gallen, the spotlight shone on one of Scotland's favourite sons as he moves solo at the summit, even if the scoreline was a disappointment.

Inside the fairytale journey of Scotland captain Robertson

Andy Robertson on his hero, Kenny Dalglish

For much of this year, Dalglish was the only man to have pulled on the navy blue of Scotland more times than Robertson.

'King Kenny' topped the list on 102 caps - earning his last in November 1986 against Luxembourg when in a player-manager role at Liverpool.

Few since have even come close to toppling him - and Robertson is keen to stress the throne is still reserved for Dalglish - but it is perhaps fitting it is the current captain, given their great friendship.

Which allows Dalglish to say he's not having any of it.

"He always says: 'You've got millions more games. Nations League, what's that? They didn't have that in my day!'" Robertson joked on the Stick to Football podcast recently.

"I think he might bring up a ratio for years-to-games-played so he's still on top.

"It's obviously something that's been on my radar for a while now. When you get to 80-odd caps, you start to look up."

Dalglish can console himself that his record of 30 goals - held jointly with Denis Law - is safely out of Robertson's reach.

But not out of John McGinn's - the midfielder's double against North Macedonia on Friday took him to within six of the legendary duo.

The Aston Villa captain is lurking only eight caps behind Robertson on the all-time caps list, too, so the Scotland men's centurion club could have three members soon.

Since being given the armband by Alex McLeish after Scott Brown retired in 2018, Robertson has captained Scotland 33 times more than any other player.

At the time, he had earned just 22 caps and was just 24 but had established himself as Liverpool's left-back as they reached the Champions League final.

Now he is the only man to have led Scotland out at three major finals, reaching the milestone at the World Cup this summer.

Scotland and Napoli midfielder Scott McTominay enthused: "He's such a great captain and he's got that good balance between being one of the senior players and saying you need to wake up if you're not playing well.

"Then he's also got the personable side, organising things with the manager, helping young players and stuff. He's brilliant with that, brilliant."

One of those young players is 20-year-old Findlay Curtis, who told the media the night before Robertson's historic night that his captain has "probably been the biggest role model" he's looked up to since joining the international set-up earlier this year.

Perhaps that was because Robertson did him a turn back in the day. Unbeknownst to the skipper.

Rangers winger Curtis was only 14 during the Covid pandemic and was partial to playing a bit of Fifa, and Robertson was a star in his team.

"I was just thinking, I had him in my Fifa team," he explained. "[For] defending and he was just fast as well.

"What a servant he has been for the country."

'I had him in my Fifa team!' - Findlay Curtis on going from fan to team-mate of Robertson

Robertson has played under four permanent Scotland team bosses in his 12-year international career.

It was Strachan who plucked him from the under-21s to hand him his debut as a 19-year-old in a win over Poland in March 2014.

McLeish made him captain in 2018, calling Robertson the "outstanding candidate" and an "inspirational figure" - and now he has played five times for new boss Sebastien Pocognoli.

He even played one game under interim manager Malky Mackay in a friendly against the Netherlands at Pittodrie in 2017 as the Scottish FA searched for Strachan's replacement.

However, it is Steve Clarke's seven-year stint that Robertson will be mainly associated with, as the pair led Scotland to three major tournaments.

Robertson played 68 times for Clarke, all of them as captain, and the recently departed head coach consistently praised his figurehead.

"He's been a fantastic captain for Scotland," Clarke said at the World Cup.

"He's worked ever so hard for the country and he leads the group like the leader he is - and he's very, very important to me and the squad."

Over the piece, Robertson's caps are spread fairly evenly among friendlies and qualification games.

Crucially, though, he has also played nine times in major tournaments. What will rankle with him is there is only one win among those nine - June's victory over Haiti in Boston at the World Cup.

Overall, though, Robertson's Scotland career can be characterised by consistency.

He has started 101 of his 103 caps, rarely ever being substituted. He has not missed a camp in three years.

The only question seems to be how many Scotland caps will Robertson end up with?

At 32 and still playing in the Premier League, it appears there will be plenty more to come.
