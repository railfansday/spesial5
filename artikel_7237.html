Why Great Leaders ‘Clear the Runway’ ft. Alaska Airlines&#8217; Eric Edge

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Transformation gets talked about like it’s a reinvention. But Eric Edge, VP of brand and marketing at Alaska Airlines, sees it differently.

In this episode of Marketing Vanguard, Jenny Rooney welcomes Eric for an unpacking of what it takes to scale two heritage airline brands without flattening what made either one valuable.

He explains why Alaska and Hawaiian are operating under one airline group while remaining distinct consumer brands.

They also discuss what gave Alaska the right to expand globally, and why the fundamentals matter even more when the stakes get bigger.

Eric Edge is VP of brand and marketing at Alaska Airlines, where he helps lead the brand strategy behind Alaska and Hawaiian Airlines as the two heritage brands expand their global footprint under one airline group.

His career began in the airline industry before moving into advertising and later technology, where he worked across Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest during major periods of digital transformation. Across those chapters, one theme has stayed consistent: strong fundamentals matter, but so does the curiosity to adapt when the rules change.

[04:13] The Leadership Lesson Eric Learned in the Chaos of Sept. 11 — Early in his career at United Airlines, Eric was part of the Flight 93 emergency response effort. In the middle of uncertainty, sorrow and incomplete information, he made a deliberate decision to focus on the mission in front of him and on helping those more deeply affected. That experience shaped a leadership mindset he still carries today and one that’s useful for marketers everywhere: control what you can control and do not let chaos immobilize you.

[11:27] You Don’t Earn Global Ambition by Abandoning What Made You Good — Alaska’s move into routes like Rome, London, Seoul, Tokyo and Iceland was not built on reinvention for reinvention’s sake. Eric argues the company earned the right to expand by first proving its operational performance, financial performance, culture of care and brand promise. The scale changed. The foundation did not.

[14:15] The “My Job Is to Clear the Runway” Leadership Principle — Eric sees leadership less as directing every move and more as removing whatever prevents people from doing their best work. He sets the vision, gives people ownership over outcomes and focuses on clearing blockers. The goal is not to become the center of every decision but rather make execution easier for the team.

[16:23] AI Isn’t the First Marketing Earthquake (And It Won’t Be the Last) — Eric wants marketers grounded in fundamentals but curious enough to accept that the tried and true may be disrupted. His own career included Facebook’s dramatic desktop-to-mobile pivot, a shift that felt existential at the time. AI is different, but his response is familiar: learn the new thing, experiment and use it to create more room for higher-stakes work.
