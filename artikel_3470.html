Sorry, Vice City, but You’re the Most Boring Part of GTA 6

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

When Grand Theft Auto 6 was first announced, fans were thrilled at the prospect of returning to Vice City. The Miami-inspired location has always been one of the series’ most memorable locations, its neon-lit streets standing in contrast to the grittiness of Liberty City. While I was as excited as anyone to go clubbing and spend some time on the beach, after 70 hours, GTA 6 has struck me with one surprise thought: Vice City is the least interesting part of Leonida.

I don’t mean to say that Vice City sucks — far from it! It’s an immaculately designed video game city with more environmental diversity in any given corner than some open-world games fit into an entire map. The fact that it’s one of my least favorite places to spend time is more a testament to every other location in GTA 6’s Florida-inspired world. From small towns to the deep wilderness, every corner of Leonida is a delight to discover because every area feels so different.

I blame GTA 6’s opening chapter for spoiling me rotten. Jason and Lucia’s story begins in the Leonida Keys, a brilliantly constructed introductory area that I fell in love with instantly. As someone who is fascinated by the Florida Keys, the real islands that the location is based on, I was thrilled to find a digital space that so accurately captured a genuinely surreal part of America. The real Keys feel like an isolated nation, densely packing stores and suburbs onto narrow islands. The Leonida Keys feel both like an idyllic vacation hideaway and a mundane suburb, which gives GTA 6’s opening chapter such a distinct feel from the rest of the game. The small town nature of it makes for one of my favorite chunks of any GTA game.

Even after getting out of the Leonida Keys, it’s still a while before you get to Vice City. First, Jason and Lucia spend a stint upstate hopping around some small towns separated by vast farmland. Port Gellhorn has the feel of an industrial town, the kind of place that’s lined with used car lots. Some great action set pieces come out of that setup; one time I busted up a well-guarded abandoned bowling alley and wound up in a gritty gang shootout.

Way south of there, you’ll find Capri, another highlight. The squeaky-clean upper-class town is full of boutique clothing stores and high-end cafes. When Jason and Lucia arrive there, they are immediately profiled by street cops who clock them as poor outsiders who don’t belong in town. It stings considering how hard up for cash the two are when they get there, which turns the town itself into one of GTA 6’s greatest antagonists. Screw this place!

Even the stretches of map that seem empty at a glance are fascinating. Some of my favorite moments so far have been spent in Lummox County, which is largely made up of woods and mountains (despite IRL Florida not technically having those). Some of the best date spots and activities are up there, hidden alongside hiking trails. It’s also home to some true weirdos, like a group of “conservationist” gun nuts who say they are controlling the ecosystem by shooting at invasive lizards. Those weirdo discoveries make what could have been a dull, empty area into a place that I keep feeling compelled to bike around.

Between all of those areas, there are so many little pockets of the map to love. Hullet Marsh hides one of the game’s weirdest secrets amid the alligators. Tiny islands like Belpoint are quaint little escapes worth seeking out. Even unassuming stretches of road, like the area under Buckskin Swamp, will lead you to decaying mini-golf courses that make you feel like you’re in a state with a lot of history to it. I’ve seen every corner of the map, and very little of it has felt like a waste of exploration.

Vice City is excellent too, of course. It’s a perfect representation of Miami, right down to the hotel pools that I’ve sat by while visiting my brother, who lives there. Vice Beach is a highlight, and the various Keys around the city give the area a lot of diversity. It’s more just that I expected all of that. I know that Rockstar can get the hustle and bustle of cities right, because I’ve been playing GTA games since I was a kid. It’s more exciting to see GTA 6 branch out into more intimate towns and figure out how to make those just as exciting without the sports cars and nightclubs. GTA games have explored these spaces before, but the sheer mass of them here helps Leonida feel like a fully realized state with its own economic and political nuances from region to region.

My relationship to Vice City as it stands is similar to the relationship I have to Manhattan as a Brooklynite. It’s work island. Clock in, make some money, and leave the city limits at sunset to actually live a little. I’ll remember my mini-golf games more than the hours I spent sitting in traffic on Vice City’s freeways.
