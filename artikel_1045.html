Verstappen trusts F1 chiefs over plan to proceed with Middle East races

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Max Verstappen has backed F1's plans to proceed with the final two races of the season in Qatar and Abu Dhabi; the Red Bull driver had previously expressed doubts; watch the Las Vegas GP on Sky Sports F1, with qualifying at 4am on Saturday and the race at 4am on Sunday

Max Verstappen says he trusts Formula 1 to "make the right call" as the sport pushes on with plans to hold the final two races of the season in Qatar and Abu Dhabi despite continued conflict in the Middle East.

F1 had to cancel races scheduled in April in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia, but the Qatar Grand Prix from November 27-29 and Abu Dhabi Grand Prix from December 4-6 are currently set to go ahead.

While F1 bosses had maintained throughout the year that their intention was to proceed with the events as planned, it was only after the Sao Paulo Grand Prix on November 8 that a contingency plan to hold a season-ending race in Italy was aborted.

Therefore, Wednesday evening's media sessions ahead of this weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix, which forms a season-ending triple header with the races in Qatar and Abu Dhabi, was the first opportunity for the drivers to publicly react to the latest developments.

Red Bull's Verstappen, who had in September voiced his opposition to ending the season in the Middle East, appears to have changed his position.

The four-time world champion said on Wednesday: "I trust F1 and FIA to make the right call on that, right?

"I don't have all the intel. I'm not an officer. I just hope that they make the right call on that. And that's all that you can rely on, really.

"They also send over their findings and understanding. So I trust them."

While there haven't been notable recent incidents in Qatar or Abu Dhabi, a US military base in Bahrain was targeted over the weekend, and Houthi rebels in Yemen are advancing in their war against the country's Saudi Arabian backed government.

Ferrari driver Charles Leclerc acknowledged that the situation remains "tricky" but also expressed faith that F1 and the sport's governing body, the FIA, will act responsibly.

"It's a situation that is kind of tricky," Leclerc said. "At the same, I feel like we are up to date with whatever is organised, and I guess that on our side we have to trust the process, and that's what I'll do.

"I'm sure the FIA is taking everything seriously to make sure that everybody is safe. It's in the best interest of absolutely everyone.

"So, yeah, me as a driver, I'll just trust that process, and be in Qatar if it's safe to be there."

British drivers Lando Norris and George Russell both said they have been reassured by friends of theirs who live in the Middle East.

Russell often represents his rivals in his role as a director of the Grand Prix Drivers' Association (GPDA), but he explained that the seriousness of the situation has resulted in that organisation having little involvement.

"We're [the GPDA] not involved because it's way above our heads, and the truth is, we will be going if nothing is escalated," the Mercedes driver said.

"F1 is not making those decisions, it's the people in the high powers of politics.

"I trust in Formula 1. I'm not concerned. I know a lot of people who live in the region, and they say pretty bluntly, 'we're living there with our families, and if we didn't feel safe, we wouldn't be.'

"They feel safe as it is, and I'm sure and I hope everything will be ok."

Norris revealed that he will be attending a friend's wedding in Bahrain ahead of the races in Qatar and Abu Dhabi, but said he was more concerned for members of his McLaren team than himself.

"I have my friend's wedding in Bahrain next week, so I was planning on going there and then going on to Qatar and then Abu Dhabi," the 2025 world champion said.

"But it's not about me. It's a lot more people. It's the fans, it's the team personnel, so the question is for them, more than I would say it is for myself."
