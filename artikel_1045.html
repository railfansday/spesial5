How Xabi Alonso's brittle Blues have wilted this season: KIERAN GILL runs the rule over all TWENTY Premier League goals conceded as new manager faces urgent demand to patch up leaky defence

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 17:33 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 17:33 EST, 26 November 2026

Bullied by Sunderland’s Brian Brobbey, then by Leeds United’s Dominic Calvert-Lewin. Liam Delap, of all people, is probably the last striker that Chelsea’s defenders want to be facing next.

A striker who is seen as something of a brutish throwback. A striker with a point to prove versus his old employers who were happy to sell him after a single season. A striker who will know, having trained with Levi Colwill and Wesley Fofana, how to make this an uncomfortable match.

Delap will lead the line for Nottingham Forest, and as if Colwill and Co needed any additional spotlight on them, this Premier League encounter will take place on Friday under the lights of the City Ground – about as intimidating a setting as they could have asked for.

Chelsea have conceded 20 Premier League goals this season. It is as many as they have scored, and so as much as we applauded their attacking earlier this term, we must likewise scrutinise the defending to the same degree.

Daily Mail Sport watched back all 20 to analyse, see who was at fault and issue each a Keystone Cops rating. Yes, there have been some 10 out of 10s.

Chelsea have conceded 20 goals already in the Premier League after losing 4-1 at home to Leeds. Pictured: Noah Okafor scores Leeds first goal in their win at Stamford Bridge

Xabi Alonso's defence must be scrutinised over the nature of the goals they are conceding

1 – Fulham, Josh King, 23rd minute, for 1-1

Xabi Alonso’s first taste of a setback on day one. All King had to do was make a run behind Morgan Rogers, whose defensive awareness was on show with England in the last international break but less so here for Chelsea. King blasted on an angle and from 16 yards, and Robert Sanchez was beaten at his near post as Alonso clocked why this goalkeeper is not for him.

2 – Fulham, Gonzalo Garcia, 54th minute, for 3-2 to Chelsea

While Chelsea binned Sanchez and replaced him with Emiliano Martinez after this game, this goal involved an error from a centre-back, first and foremost. Colwill failed to deal with a long ball, heading fresh air rather than clearing, then Sanchez spilled his save for Garcia’s rebound.

3 – Brighton, Malick Yalcouye, 35th minute, for 3-1 to Chelsea

This originated from a long throw taken quickly in Brighton’s own half and sent down the line. Pedro Neto flew across to try to tackle on the wing, but forgot to take the actual ball. Yalcouye charged forward and played in Charalampos Kostoulas, who ran behind Maxence Lacroix. His shot was saved, but Yalcouye finished with oomph as the ball pinged in off the post.

4 – Brighton, Joao Pedro own goal, 63rd minute, for 3-2 to Chelsea

An unfortunate deflection from Joao Pedro as Pascal Gross took aim. In the build-up, Cole Palmer neglected his defensive duties by allowing Gross to run behind him, much like Rogers v Fulham the week before. The nick from Joao Pedro wrongfooted Martinez in goal. It happens.

Chelsea's vulnerabilities were on show as they overcame Brighton 4-3 back in August

5 – Brighton, Pascal Gross, 96th minute, for 4-3 to Chelsea

A long throw. A spot of head tennis with Valentin Barco initially beaten in the air by Diego Gomez. Then Gross nods beyond Martinez as Joao Pedro asks Rogers why he didn’t have him. A soft consolation to concede.

6 – Arsenal, Kai Havertz, 25th minute, for 1-1

This one was largely on the goalkeeping as a few wondered whether Martinez was Sanchez in disguise. Havertz dashed inside from the right wing and away from Fofana, then scored from 18 yards into the small sliver of light available at Martinez’s near post. With that, Chelsea's lead was lost.

7 – Arsenal, Martin Odegaard, 50th minute, for 2-1 to Arsenal

Sometimes, you just have to say the opposition's move worked well, and this was a fine one from Arsenal rather than a defensive disasterclass per se, even if Fofana was fooled by Havertz’s dummy. That allowed the pass from Christos Tzolis to roll through to Odegaard to score the winner after Reece James had not spied how his fellow captain was unmarked in front of him.

8 – Hull, Mohamed Belloumi, 28th minute, for 1-1

Do not let the ball bounce. That is defending 101. The no-no we are all taught as kids. Jorrel Hato broke that basic rule here. Hato let the ball bounce in front of him and loop over him after it was launched into the box from the left wing by Ryan Giles, with Chelsea’s other full back Malo Gusto having failed to stop that cross from coming. Belloumi was waiting behind Hato to score. Hato was hooked at half-time for good reason.

Mohamed Belloumi scored the first of two goals for Hull against the Blues after Jorrel Hato allowed the ball to bounce in the box

9 – Hull, Mohamed Belloumi, 34th minute, for 2-1 to Hull

Martinez came to clear Hull's corner. He didn’t. He flapped as Semi Ajayi, who did not even have a marker on him as Colwill and Fofana were marking zonally rather than taking care of the 6ft 4in centre-back, headed into the crossbar. The rebound fell to Belloumi, who, unmarked at the edge of the box, scored via two deflections which made Chelsea’s six-yard box resemble a pinball machine. Not a good look for Austin MacPhee, the new set-piece specialist.

10 – Brentford, Jaidon Anthony, 61st minute, for 1-0 to Brentford

Another one which MacPhee will want to leave off the showreel. The corner came in. It went to the back of the pack. It was headed back across by Jannik Schuster, then in by Anthony. Fofana, Lacroix and Danny Welbeck formed a nice little triangle around the attacker without anyone ever actually marking him.

11 – Brentford, Igor Thiago, 83rd minute, for 2-0 to Brentford

Jordan Henderson tried and failed to punch a pass forward. It turned over possession, and Brentford countered. Chelsea had a high line as they were pushing for the equaliser, which was understandable. One lovely through-ball from Mikkel Damsgaard later, Kevin Schade was through. Martinez denied him one-v-one, but Thiago finished the rebound.

12 – Brentford, Fabio Carvalho, 94th minute, for 3-0 to Brentford

It originated from a long throw, cleared partially by Chelsea but recycled to the wing by Brentford. A low cross came in with a deflection from Josh Acheampong helping it find Carvalho, who had sneaked in behind Pep Chavarria.

Fabio Carvalho scored Brentford's final goal in their 3-0 win in September after sneaking in at the back post, after Chelsea only partially cleared from a long throw

13 – Bournemouth, Evanilson, 51st minute, for 3-1 to Chelsea

Never let it be said that Chelsea do not give confidence to strikers. Evanilson had not scored in the Premier League since February 2026. Then he visited Stamford Bridge in this fixture in October. Since then, he has scored in every league game played, now on six in a row. This goal came from a Bournemouth corner. It was flicked on at the front of the pack. Moises Caicedo tried to clear the ball amid a goalmouth melee, but only blasted his clearance into Evanilson for a gimme of a goal.

14 – Tottenham, Mike Penders own goal, 77th minute, for 1-0 to Tottenham

Whatever you do, when starting in the Premier League for the first time and against your side’s fiercest rivals no less, do not punch the ball into your own goal. Anyway, Penders came for a corner, and you know what happened next. It did not help that the 21-year-old goalkeeper was challenged by Jan Paul van Hecke, who was allowed a free run at him after being left by Lacroix, but even so. This was a horrific moment for Penders, and happened despite Chelsea trying that trick where they send players out of the box to ease the congestion.

15 – Tottenham, Mathys Tel, 87th minute, for 2-0 to Tottenham

Acheampong lost the ball over on the right wing and couldn’t catch up with Tel as he flew forward. There was a weak attempt at a tackle by Lacroix, who almost sheepishly stuck out a lazy leg. Then Tel scored from 25 yards as Alonso violated a non-negotiable: you do not lose at home to Tottenham.

Mike Penders punched a corner into his own net as Chelsea suffered a rare home loss to Tottenham

16 – Sunderland, Enzo Le Fee, 36th minute, for 1-0 to Sunderland

A penalty, but we will analyse the build-up. Colwill in particular had been getting bullied by Brobbey in this game. So much so that he went on to be hooked at half-time while on a yellow. On this occasion, Sunderland launched a long ball towards Brobbey, who used his strength to turn Colwill and charge into the box. He then dropped down – controversially so as Chelsea did not believe it warranted a spot-kick. Le Fee stepped up, and scored the game’s only goal.

17 – Leeds, Noah Okafor, 28th minute, for 1-1

Chelsea fans could take their pick of players to blame here. First, there was Fofana not heading back James Trafford’s long ball from whence it came. One of Willie Isa’s jobs as Chelsea’s ‘cultural architect’ is to act as a human shield while the defenders practise winning headers in their warm-up. They still seem to mess up a fair few mid-match, as Fofana did here. Then there was Acheampong letting the ball bounce over him à la Hato, and Colwill backtracking as if stuck in reverse. All three were surrounding Okafor as he then made it 1-1. Martinez did not even attempt to dive as the ball rolled by him. Colwill appeared to ask him why as Leeds celebrated. Excruciating by everyone involved.

18 – Leeds, Dominic Calvert-Lewin, 53rd minute, for 2-1 to Leeds

Another penalty, but one which we believe should not have been awarded for a handball by Rogers. It was given by Michael Oliver in real time, and backed up by Michael Salisbury on VAR. Any reasonable fan would hate it if this was given against their club. Anyway, it all originated from a long throw. Three Chelsea players tried to head it clear at the same time – good communication, lads – and they failed as Fofana only flicked it on behind him. What followed was this gift of a spot-kick, which Calvert-Lewin scored for 2-1.

19 – Leeds, Noah Okafor, 58th minute, for 3-1 to Leeds

Yet more ball-watching from Chelsea’s defenders as opposed to taking note of the chap that had already scored one goal lining up for his second. Okafor was always unmarked as he moved into the box from around the penalty spot to meet Ethan Ampadu’s deflected cross.

20 – Leeds, Brenden Aaronson, 91st minute, for 4-1 to Leeds

A ball forward from Leeds left Colwill in a bind. Because he was supposed to have both Calvert-Lewin and Harry Wilson, he was stuck in between, and actually had neither. Calvert-Lewin headed to Wilson, who had time to pick his pass. Gusto, on as a substitute, noticed Aaronson making a burst in behind Chelsea’s back line. He tried to play him offside by suddenly stopping running with him but timed it wrong. Aaronson was onside. Gusto raced to try to stop him, but the Leeds attacker simply checked back inside, and scored through the legs of Martinez, who gave the woodwork a kicking in frustration.
