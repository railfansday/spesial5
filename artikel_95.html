‘Shame old Aussies’ to ‘fresh chaos’: what the UK papers say about Bancroft’s explosive ball tampering claims

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Sports sections of British newspapers deliver their take on the unfolding Cricket Australia sandpaper saga

The sports sections of the UK’s major mastheads are relishing the never-ending crisis engulfing Australian cricketing, after a key player in the ball tampering controversy, Cameron Bancroft, alleged everyone on the team knew about the 2018 plot.

Bancroft’s allegation came five days after a lengthy media conference by the current Australia Test captain, Pat Cummins, during which he denied that everyone on the team was in on the sandpaper plan. Cummins was responding to the allegation first aired in teammate Usman Khawaja’s biography, that actually, everyone did know about the sandpaper Bancroft was later caught holding.

The Guardian wrote “Australia turmoil” before giving readers a full rundown of “who said what” and an op-ed from commentator Geoff Lemon, who wrote Bancroft “delivered a devastating repudiation of Cummins”.

The Daily Star’s sport section promised readers “an idiot’s guide to sandpapering a cricket ball”. Its opening paragraphs ran through a quick account of the controversy, which the Star labelled a “CRISIS”.

The tabloid’s final words on the matter? “SHAME OLD AUSSIES: Australian Captain Pat Cummins is deep in the didgeridoo-doo.”

The Mirror ran with the headline: “Bancroft: Whole team knew we were CHEATING”. The tabloid said the allegation had sent Cricket Australia “into an even bigger crisis”.

The unfolding scandal also made it to the front page of The Times’ sports section, where the masthead led with the headline: “Cummins did know of plot, says Bancroft”. Playing a straight bat, the paper ran readers through the unfolding controversy, running down to the denial in 2021 from the four senior bowlers – Cummins, Mitchell Starc, Josh Hazlewood and Nathan Lyon – that they did not know there was a “foreign substance” taken on to the field to alter the ball.

The Daily Telegraph also ran the story on the front of its sports pages, with the headline “Explosive Bancroft claim leaves Cummins in peril” accompanied by a headline for an opinion piece calling for the truth to finally come out.

The paper pointed out that the “dramatic intervention in the sandpaper furore came on the same day that Australia were well beaten by England in Hobart, as the tourists clinched the one-day international series”.

The Daily Express went with alliteration – “Bancroft Bombshell” – also painting the allegations as sending “Australian cricket into an even bigger crisis”.

Finally, The Daily Mail headlined with “Aussies plunged into fresh chaos” before giving its rundown.

Meanwhile, Cricket Australia is rushing to contain the “crisis”, announcing the creation of an independent “forum” for players and staff to share their accounts of the infamous South Africa tour.
