Joshua Kimmich critcizes Bayern bosses over Musiala health comments

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Joshua Kimmich has slammed Bayern Munich's leadership over comments about Jamal Musiala's health issues. The bosses have questioned whether the player can return to previous form – and is still worth his high salary.

Bayern Munich's disappointing 2-1 Champions League defeat at Lille on Wednesday night – in which Jamal Musiala came on for the last quarter of an hour – was overshadowed by comments the club's captain made afterwards.

"It must never happen that something like this is played out at a player's expense. Of course, the club always comes first and is bigger than the player, but it's not bigger than the person," Kimmich told reporters in the mixed zone.

"That's an absolute no-go and not Bayern-like," he added.

Kimmich was responding to recent comments made by Bayern supervisory board members Uli Hoeness and Karl-Heinz Rummenigge.

"If he (Musiala) doesn't return to being the Jamal of old, then we need to have a conversation with his family, with the player, and, above all, with his agency – because, frankly, the current financial figures would no longer be justifiable," Karl-Heinz Rummenigge told Sky on Friday.

Honorary President Uli Hoeness has described the situation as a "dilemma" for the club, while CEO Jan-Christian Dreesen raised the prospect of talks with Musiala's management and family.

While theGermany player has struggled with a number of injuries over the past couple of seasons, his health came into sharper focus in August, after he collapsed on the pitch in a couple of preseason matches. He subsequently revealed that he had been diagnosed with a neurological condition which causes him to suffer from occasional "absence seizures."

Kimmich added that criticism of a players' performances was legitimate, but that concerns about Musiala's situation should have been dealt with behind closed doors.

"That's something you should sort out internally, around a table, rather than trying to resolve it publicly," he said.

"It's not that he has problems because he's unprofessional. He's there every day trying to get the maximum out of himself."

Meanwhile, Bayern bosses met with Musiala's agents on Tuesday, and clarified the timeline of events, dismissing speculation that the player could have concealed the condition when he signed his last contract extension.

Bayern said the two sides agreed that he was first examined for seizure symptoms in April 2025, two months after he had signed his contract extension, which makes him one of the club's top earners and is to keep him in Munich until 2030. Discussions between Bayern management and Musiala's representatives are reportedly ongoing.
