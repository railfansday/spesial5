Unemployment to surpass 5% and ‘things will get a bit harder’, treasurer Jim Chalmers warns

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Economists flag rising recession risk as government forecasts economy will grow by less than 2% in 2027 and 2028

Jim Chalmers has warned Australians that “things will get a bit harder before they get easier”, revealing next month’s budget update will show unemployment climbing above 5% alongside slower economic growth for the next two years.

As economists flagged rising recessionary risks, the treasurer used a ministerial statement during question time to reveal what he described as “concerning” economic numbers in the upcoming mid-year economic and fiscal outlook.

Alongside higher unemployment, high fuel costs meant inflation would slow from 4.7% now to only “the mid-3s” by the middle of next year – or about 1 percentage point higher than anticipated in the May budget.

The economy was now expected to grow by less than 2% in both 2027 and 2028.

“We say with humility and with honesty that things might get a bit harder before they get easier,” Chalmers told parliament.

“If Treasury was updating the mid-year forecasts today, they would reflect this difficult environment we’re in. In the near term, they’d forecast softer growth, higher inflation and higher unemployment.”

With a majority of Australians blaming public spending for driving this year’s inflation outbreak, Chalmers promised that the mid-year economic and fiscal outlook will include “net savings” to assist in the ongoing battle against persistently high price pressures.

“It’s not a mini-budget full of new initiatives but it will have some budget-sized savings. At this stage, we expect to more than offset our new spending decisions, without slashing and burning the services people rely on,” he said.

News last week that the unemployment rate had climbed to 4.9% in October has turbocharged fears that a series of Reserve Bank interest rate hikes are weighing more heavily than anticipated – and that the economy could even tip into a deeper downturn next year.

Chalmers said the government’s short-term goal was “cutting spending enough, without threatening growth” – an approach he described as “a delicate balance”.

“We know that no realistic amount of savings makes inflation go away, but too much new spending can make it worse,” he said.

With the full effect of the five rate hikes only likely to become apparent through 2027, Shane Oliver, AMP’s chief economist, predicted economic growth would slump from 2.3%, to below 1%.

“We could flirt with recession next year,” Oliver said, putting the chances of a more severe downturn at a “high and rising” 40%.

Official figures on Wednesday are likely to show inflation climbing to nearly 5%, and Oliver agreed with the treasurer that getting the settings right in the mid-year budget would be “a difficult balancing act”.

“The government has been copping political blame for inflation, and Chalmers is under a lot of pressure to cut [spending]; but if he cuts too much it could accentuate the recession risks.”

The treasurer foreshadowed that any new spending decisions – such as Monday’s announcement that the $25 price cap on prescription medicine would be extended until the end of the decade at a cost of $242m – would be more than offset by savings measures.

But he also flagged that the mix of a weaker economy and “big unavoidable spending commitments” meant bigger deficits in the later years of the forward estimates period.

“It would not be responsible to fully offset these big changes to revenue and payments that flow through automatically from weaker economic parameters,” he argued.
