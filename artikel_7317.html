The New Competitive Edge in AI-Powered Advertising

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

This post was created in partnership with Atlas Cloud

An ad that once required weeks of production and a six-figure budget can now take only a couple of days and a few thousand dollars to create. As AI lowers the barriers to producing creative, brands now have to decide how to use the newfound capacity to make advertising that consumers actually want to see.

At the ADWEEK House Commerce Summit, a panel co-hosted by Atlas Cloud examined the implications for creative strategy, agency relationships, and the future of personalized advertising.

Kicking off the discussion, Matthew Spradlin, VP of new business development at Atlas Cloud, illustrated the cost difference with an ad he produced over a couple of days using AI tools. Including post-production, he estimated the expense at roughly $3,000. A traditional production of similar scope could run into hundreds of thousands of dollars.

The savings are striking. The possibilities they create are considerably more interesting.

With production costs falling, brands can experiment with variations that once would have been impractical. Yet more volume brings its own challenge: understanding which messages will resonate with whom.

Colin Mo, head of product marketing at Atlas Cloud, captured the ambition as creating “tailored, bespoke experiences for each individual.”

For Kai Tier, executive director of creative technology and AI at Barbarian, a creative and innovation agency, the priority is making that level of personalization practical across the consumer journey.

“You’re producing maybe 100,000 versions of the ad, but the user should only see the four that make sense for them along the purchase funnel,” Tier explained.

AI-powered production tools are making it easier to adapt existing creative for different audiences and markets. Spradlin described customizing an animated Adidas advertisement for international markets, with versions in seven languages. “We did that in a day,” he shared.

Another opportunity lies in how brands allocate their budgets. Money once consumed by the basics of creative production can now support more ambitious work, including intelligent systems that tailor brand interactions to individual consumers.

Integrating AI into established business operations draws on a wider range of agency expertise.

Agencies are now helping clients manage the risks of adopting new technologies, particularly when existing governance processes and brand protections need to carry over into automated workflows.

These responsibilities have pulled creative teams into closer collaboration with legal counsel. Tier described working with clients to evaluate AI models, assess generated content, and maintain the internal safeguards that protect their companies. Establishing those protections, he suggested, gives brands greater confidence to pursue ambitious creative ideas.

Consumer expectations will also shape how far brands can take personalization. Tier expects AI agents to give people greater control over their purchasing journeys, potentially challenging brands’ access to customer data.

Maintaining consumer trust requires “being transparent with the user about what the value exchange is and making sure that they’re bought in,” he added.
