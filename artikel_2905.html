Jensen Huang has agreed to appear before Senate banking committee, Chairman Scott says

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has agreed to appear before the Senate Banking Committee, panel Chair Tim Scott, R-S.C., said on CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Wednesday.

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has also agreed to testify, Scott said. The agreements to appear voluntarily come as pressure builds on Capitol Hill to investigate Huang's affair with a suspected Chinese spy, which led to his ouster at the chipmaker he founded.

"They both have agreed," Scott said. "We will hopefully have this done before Christmas."

Huang reached an agreement on Tuesday with the chairmen of three House committees for a closed-door transcribed interview on Dec. 1 jointly before the three panels. Scott had asked Huang to appear publicly in the Senate.

A spokesperson for the banking committee did not immediately respond to questions about when Huang and Lutnick would testify. In initial letters to the pair, Scott requested Huang appear on Dec. 3 and Lutnick appear on Dec. 10.

"Nvidia seemed to have fairly lax controls, and I want to know how much of the relationship with Alice [Huang] impacted Nvidia's unwillingness or inability, or frankly, just blindness to Megaspeed's providing access to China of the most advanced chipset," Scott said of Jensen Huang's testimony.

The details of Huang's appearance before the three House panels on Dec. 1 are still being determined, House Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman Rep. Brian Mast, R-Fla., told reporters at the Capitol on Wednesday. He said the people who will likely conduct the closed-door interview will be the top Republican and top Democrat on his panel and the Select Committee on China and Select Committee on Intelligence along with their attorneys. The location is still being worked out, Mast said.

A date for an appearance before the committees by Jeffrey Kessler, the Commerce official who oversees export controls, is not yet set, Mast said. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is scheduled to appear before the panels Dec. 2.

Scott, in his CNBC appearance, also homed in on Lutnick, calling for answers as to why the commerce secretary did not act after being warned about Megaspeed — the Singaporean company where the alleged Chinese spy, Alice Huang, was an executive. Nvidia sold its chips to Megaspeed, which U.S. investigators suspect of diverting them to China.

"They were warned as early as March of this year, several months ago, that Megaspeed should be on the 'entity list,'" Scott said, referring to a Commerce Department list of companies the U.S. views as national security risks. The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security "didn't do it, Howard didn't do it, we want to know why."

Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent have been embroiled in an intra-administration battle as the fallout from the Huang affair grows. Bessent on CNBC on Friday appeared to blame Lutnick for a lack of action on Megaspeed.

"You will have to ask them about their internal processes," Bessent said, referring to Commerce and noting his department has long supported imposing restrictions on Megaspeed.

A spokesperson for Huang declined to comment on Scott's announcement that he has agreed to appear.

Huang's upcoming appearances are likely just the start of the embattled former CEO's problems on Capitol Hill. Democrats will take control of Congress in January and have demanded sweeping investigations into the affair and Huang's cozy relationship with President Donald Trump.

The House Energy and Commerce Committee asked interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress for a briefing no later than Dec. 2 on the company's chip exports and information about the relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang. The panel also asked Lutnick and a top deputy to appear to discuss chip export controls. The Wall Street Journal first reported the letters to Kress and Lutnick, and a committee spokesperson confirmed the report to CNBC.

— Megan Cassella and Ashlee Trujillo contributed to this report.
