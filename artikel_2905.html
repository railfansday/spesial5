Selected Shots Monday: The Longhorn Chronicles

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Another Mizzou Athletics sports season is upon us! While the last few years I've assigned this written piece to one of our talented photographers, I'm taking it over this year, and will be selecting my favorites of the week. Since I am not a photographer at heart, my analysis will be more surface level, but I'll try my best!

This week, Tiger Style returned, and we also were treated to women's hoops, men's hoops x2, volleyball x2 and football.

While I was working the polls— GO DEMOCRACY!! — Mizzou Men's Hoops was tipping off its season with a black & gold tidal wave of their own with a 111-50 win over Cleveland State and former Mizzou staffer Rob Summers. Toni Bryant (15pts, 6 reb, 3 blocks), Mark Mitchell (11pts, 5 reb, 10 assists) and Kennard Davis, Jr. (19pts, 4 reb) led the first trifecta of the season. Fellow freshman Jason Crowe, Jr. had the highest +/- of the night, with a +51 with 23pts, 3 reb, and 2 assists.

In what will be Brian Smith's final season at the helm for Mizzou Wrestling, the Tigers dominated the Missouri Duals, losing just three duals across three matches against Central Methodist, Maryville and Central Missouri.

Per Colin Simmons' recap, despite the big day across the board for Mizzou's 20 wrestlers that competed, head coach Brian Smith was dwelling on what needs to be fixed going forward.

"We weren't very good in a lot of situations, just guys not trusting their shape," Smith said. "They figured it out at some point in the match, but they've got to have that trust from the beginning. But those are things we can work on. We had a leg a million times and didn't get turns.  We had turks and didn't get turns, so those are the things my wheels are turning about, that we've got to be better in those positions."

While many (most?) of Mizzou Nation was tuning in to the Mizzou-SLU game, Dawn Sullivan & Co. welcomed in a nice-sized crowd of 3,600-plus at Hearnes and fought sooo hard against no. 13 Texas, though ultimately losing in three straight sets, 25-27, 13-25, and 23-25.

For the first time in 25 years, my two favorite teams — I grew up going to Billikens games — faced off against one another… and it was glorious, even if the black & gold lost this one. It was the difference between one connected team, SLU, who brings back 4/5 starters, vs. a team still trying to figure things out with two 5-star freshmen, six incoming transfers, and a late addition in Mark Mitchell.

Both Margo and Dan were on hand for the Tigers v. Longhorns game on Saturday, and each came away with very different shots throughout the course of the game. Unfortunately, their photos could not change the outcome of the game (or make the officials do their jobs correctly), and after a 34-20 loss, Missouri's CFB hopes effectively are over.

If you aren't ready to put this game behind you just yet, here's some stuff to read:

Facing their second top 25 team in the span of three days, the Tigers swept the defending national champs, no. 17 Texas A&M Aggies, in three straight sets, 25-20, 25-21, 25-23 on a dominant defensive performance by Kimani Johnson, who had 11 kills and a .526 hitting percentage.

Per Aaron's recap, this was the first sweep of a ranked opponent since October 2023, and the Tigers' second victory over a defending national champion in program history.

When asked about bouncing back after the Texas sweep, Eva Smith said, "After that loss, everybody was pretty upset. It was a bad loss. So we used that and it pushed us to want to be more hungry to play this game and take care of business."

I'll be back next week with photos from men's and women's hoops, wrestling and postseason cross country.
