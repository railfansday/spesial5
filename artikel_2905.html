What we learned from the Spurs’ loss to the Trail Blazers

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

One of the few things I hope for as a spectator is that teams playing the late-night game bring it. It's too hard to stay awake as it is after a long day, so fans need excitement to keep them from falling asleep. Fortunately, it was not a problem for these teams, yet the only issue the San Antonio Spurs had late was their basketball IQ.

I had this game circled on the calendar because the physicality was off the charts the last time they played on Nov. 7. It appears it was not a distant memory for the Portland Trail Blazers, as the ESPN broadcast highlighted how they felt embarrassed by that game and wanted their lick back. Nonetheless, it was grown-up hoops and it wouldn't be a surprise if these squads didn't like each other.

Wednesday night being an Emirates Cup game added to the fun because the winner would keep their hopes alive of getting to the quarterfinal, still needing to get by Friday's game. Keep in mind that getting to the next round guarantees the players $60,499 and half of that for the two-way guys.

I wish that coach Mitch Johnson hadn't stuck to his usual substitution patterns in the third quarter, because when Wembanyama sat the Spurs were instantly outscored by eight. They got back on track when Victor checked in to clean up the mess, and the team likely could have separated itself had he had a longer shift. Games like this with absences to key guys magnify every decision. While the most part Mitch was good, I shook my head as he tried to buy time for the starters with Carter Bryant and Ja'Kobi Gillespie at the start of the fourth.

Johnson was probably thinking about preserving his main guys for later games, but this wasn't this a big one? Every now and then, 40 minutes against good teams is not so bad, and he probably would have broken 50 points for the third time this year.

Aside from their cup chances, the three late turnovers and failure to stop Portland's two 3-pointers ruined Wemby's 44-point masterpiece. Of course, the Trail Blazers deserve credit, but the Spurs also beat themselves down the stretch. Stephon Castle's eight-second violation was a killer.

Props to coach Micah Nori. He has a serious team, while he's basically on a prove-it deal, and they've been without Ja Morant. A lot of it has to do with them having one of the most balanced teams in the league, and it helps that Damian Lillard is still one sport'a clutch shooters. He has come back from his Achilles tear with his first step intact, and his outburst in the second half was vintage.

Without De'Aaron Fox and Dylan Harper, the Spurs were without two of the league's best penetrators (sorely needed against a high-level defense) which is a big reason why Wemby was the only one to score more than six points in the lane. The Spurs only had two quarters in which the offense was running smoothly (posting a 112.1 offensive rating) and that is one of their four-worst showings of the season. Portland was able to slow them down by making them pass less often.

Wembanyama stepped up. He poured in jumpers as Portland got physical with him. He kept the pressure on Donovan Clingan and fouled him out with four minutes left. In the past, Wembanyama wouldn't always assert himself in those situations and now it's like radar is beeping when his man gets five personals.

The Spurs mostly get the shot they are looking for when running the horns set, which has a screen to the left and right for the ball handler. The team has the best results on these when the screener is the scorer and the ball handler is the decoy.

Bryant should model his game after Keldon Johnson's. The latter barely scored but has mastered the art of hustle and had 12 rebounds, including five offensive, on Wednesday.

Portland is arguably the best paint-attacking team in the league, averaging 61.7 drives per game, and Deni Avdija has a lot to do with that.

Wembanyama had 12 points in the third quarter when he was subbed out and had only logged 21 total minutes to that point. As his rampage was paused, I was reminded of the old joke about how the only person who could stop Michael Jordan was Dean Smith.
