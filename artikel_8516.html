Of the precious metals, only gold is bought mostly by investors, central banks, and jewelers, and it has tended to hold up in market crises. Platinum, palladium, and the rest depend on carmakers and factories, so they swing with the economy. Keep your metals slice small and mostly gold, and treat any other metal as a bet.

Precious metals are often pitched as one safe place for your savings, but each metal’s price depends on who buys it. That decides whether owning it steadies your portfolio or adds to its risk.

In 2025, investors and central banks bought about 61% of the gold sold worldwide and jewelry buyers about 31%, while electronics and other uses took about 6%, the World Gold Council reports. With so little going into products, gold has tended to act as a hedge, an asset that holds its value when others fall. From December 2007 to February 2009, as stocks and most commodities tumbled, gold rose 21%, the council’s Gold as a strategic asset report shows.

The other metals go mostly to industry, from electronics and solar panel makers to carmakers. Catalytic converters, the devices that clean car exhaust, use most of the palladium and rhodium sold each year. Factory and car demand rises when economies grow and falls in recessions, so these metals are bets on the economy, not hedges. Palladium shows the gap. Its yearly average price fell 59% from 2021 to 2024, from $2,419 an ounce to $995, while gold’s rose 33%, from $1,801 to $2,388, U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) summaries for platinum-group metals and gold show.

Copper and lithium aren’t precious metals, but they’re often pitched alongside them.

The metals themselves pay no interest or dividends, so cap all of them together at 5% to 10% of your portfolio, with most of that in gold. Any other metal, platinum or palladium included, goes inside that slice as a bet, sized so losing half its value wouldn’t hurt your plans.

Gains on bullion held more than a year are taxed as collectibles, at up to 28% rather than the 20% top rate on most stock gains, IRS Publication 550 says. The same goes for shares of an ETF set up as a trust that holds the metal.

Once a year, rebalance your portfolio so a rally doesn’t leave your metals above your cap.
