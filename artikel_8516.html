Deliver for DoorDash if you want short shifts on your own schedule in a busy delivery market, and turn down any offer whose pay barely covers what its miles cost you. DoorDash requires no minimum share of accepted offers, so declining one won’t get you deactivated.

Dashers deliver as independent contractors, so each offer’s pay has to cover car costs of about 76 cents a mile, the IRS’s business rate, plus self-employment tax of about 14% of the profit. Then divide what’s left by every hour you’re logged in, waiting included, for your real hourly pay.

Dashing is worth it for drivers in busy zones who can fill short shifts with offers that pay well beyond what their miles cost. Package delivery blocks fit drivers who want a shift’s pay known in advance, and grocery shopping gigs fit drivers who know their local stores.
