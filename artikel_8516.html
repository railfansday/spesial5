This quarter, the Fundâs relative outperformance came from the categories that held back results in the second quarter. Financials returned 4.32% and Core Growth returned 2.23%, both well ahead of the Indexâs 5.84% decline. Disruptive Growth declined 6.16%, roughly in line with the Index, as exceptional gains from Shopify Inc. (up 29.96%) and Moderna, Inc. (up 173.94%) were offset by declines in Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX) and Tesla, Inc. Real/Irreplaceable Assets declined 11.48%. This category is heavily dominated by consumer-oriented hotel and lodging businesses. Investors are concerned that higher oil prices, interest rates, and inflation could pressure consumer and travel spending. Hyatt Hotels Corporation, Red Rock Resorts, Inc., Gaming and Leisure Properties, Inc., and Choice Hotels International, Inc. were all impacted. This same narrative hampered returns in many of our Core Growth consumer companies like On Holding AG and Birkenstock Holding plc.

Last quarter, we wrote that several of our Core Growth and Financials holdings were being penalized by investor fears that they would be disrupted by AI or that heavy reinvestment would pressure near-term earnings. We said we believed this perception was divorced from the fundamental reality, and that when such issues are resolved or sentiment shifts, these stocks should perform well. That shift appears to be beginning. In the quarter, stocks that had been perceived as AI âlosersâ rebounded sharply. Gartner, Inc. rose 43.76%, Morningstar, Inc. rose 22.16%, FactSet Research Systems Inc. rose 17.79%, and Guidewire Software, Inc. rose 15.86%. Together, these four holdings contributed 275 basis points to the Fundâs relative return, more than the Fundâs entire margin of outperformance versus the Index for the quarter.

FactSet shares rose following fiscal third-quarter results, with organic revenue and annual subscription value (ASV) growth both reaccelerating and FactSet completing its largest-ever quarterly share repurchase. Shares advanced again following fiscal fourth-quarter results, with record organic ASV, guidance for a return to margin expansion in fiscal 2027, and AI solutions bookings that more than doubled during the year. We retain long-term conviction in FactSet given its large addressable market, consistent execution across both new product development and financial results, and robust free cash flow generation.

Shares of property and casualty (P&C) insurance software vendor Guidewire rallied as software multiples expanded. Guidewireâs InsuranceSuite platform serves as the core system of record for insurance carriers, functioning as the single source of truth for policies, claims, premiums, and payments. Given the complexity, highly regulated nature, and digital format of insurance policies, we believe this system is particularly critical and valuable. We think the core-system opportunity alone represents nearly $20 billion of annual recurring revenue potential. In our view, AI will meaningfully expand this opportunity by enabling automation and intelligence on top of the platform. Guidewire is already bringing new AI-enabled capabilities to market and signing customers, and we expect adoption to accelerate over the coming year. Finally, we expect Guidewire to benefit from AI-driven productivity gains, which should help the company grow faster while reducing costs.

The third quarter had substantial and sudden market shifts. In July, investors rotated sharply away from high-momentum and speculative AI and technology stocks. The Fundâs key two largest holdings, SpaceX and Tesla, pulled back 36.57% and 26.01%, respectively. These same stocks then rebounded 32.59% and 18.23% in August as AI-linked stocks recovered.

This experience illustrates why we believe it is important for the Fund to be constructed in this manner. The concentrated but categorically diversified structure of the portfolio allowed the Fund to weather a difficult quarter for our largest Disruptive Growth holdings, as our Core Growth and Financials holdings recovered.

Top contributors to performance for the quarter

Shopify Inc., a leading commerce platform for merchants of all sizes, contributed to performance. Second quarter results marked another quarter of exceptional execution, with gross merchandise volume (GMV) rising 32% year-over-year to $116 billion and revenue increasing 34%. This was the fifth consecutive quarter of 30%-plus growth across GMV, revenue, gross profit, operating income, and free cash flow. Merchant solutions revenue grew 37% on higher payments penetration, operating income increased 68%, and free cash flow margin expanded to 18% from 16%. Growth was broad-based across merchant sizes, geographies, and channels, with business-to-business GMV up 76% and offline GMV up 32%. Third-quarter guidance for revenue growth in the low 30% range and further operating expense leverage exceeded expectations. We retain conviction in Shopify as a durable compounder and believe its two decades of proprietary merchant and buyer data position the company as a key beneficiary as commerce shifts toward AI-driven discovery and agentic checkout.

Shares of syndicated research provider Gartner, Inc. rallied amid multiple expansion across information services stocks. We believe Gartnerâs contract value growth is poised to reaccelerate as the company enhances its product offering, retrains its sales force, and benefits from easier comparisons in its public sector end market. We also view Gartner as an underappreciated beneficiary of AI, as organizations increasingly seek guidance on the risks and opportunities presented by the technology. In addition, we expect earnings and free cash flow to grow meaningfully faster than revenue as Gartner expands margins and returns excess capital through share repurchases. Management is on pace to deploy nearly $2 billion of free cash flow toward share repurchases this year, which we estimate could reduce the companyâs share count by roughly 15% to 20%.

Moderna, Inc. is a biotechnology company developing mRNA-based vaccines and therapeutics across infectious disease and oncology. Shares rose sharply during the quarter. Modernaâs personalized cancer vaccine, developed with Merck, met key endpoints in a Phase 3 melanoma trial, a milestone the market viewed as validation of the broader neoantigen oncology platform. We retain long-term conviction in Moderna given its established mRNA manufacturing base, disciplined cost structure following recent workforce reductions, and expanding late-stage oncology pipeline. Together, we believe these strengths support a path to durable, diversified growth beyond the companyâs legacy COVID franchise.

Top detractors from performance for the quarter

Space Exploration Technologies Corp. develops and launches advanced rockets, satellites, and spacecraft, with the long-term goal of making humanity multi-planetary. The stock declined 11.71% in the quarter as shares pulled back from highs reached after the initial public offering, despite strong underlying fundamentals. SpaceXâs first results as a public company reinforced our conviction, with revenue nearly doubling year-over-year and losses narrowing sharply on high incremental margins. Growth was driven by rapidly scaling, multibillion-dollar compute-hosting deals with AI leaders, including Anthropic and Google, alongside Starlink momentum. Management guided to a $100 billion annualized revenue run rate by year-end 2026 and up to five times more compute exiting 2027, well above Street expectations. SpaceX also closed its acquisition of Cursor, a premier enterprise AI-powered coding platform, accelerated product development with Grok Bot and more advanced Grok models, and progressed Starship toward full reusability. We started accumulating SpaceX shares in 2017 when the company was valued at approximately $21.6 billion. The Fundâs position is currently worth approximately $5.6 billion. We remain long-term shareholders and believe the company is in the early stages of a multi-decade growth opportunity.

Tesla, Inc. manufactures electric vehicles, solar products, and energy storage solutions and develops real-world AI technologies. Shares fell 15.64% as record second quarter revenue and deliveries were overshadowed by near-term profit pressure from increased investment in autonomy, robotics, and AI chips. As Tesla funds this ambitious multi-year investment cycle, investors are also seeking clearer evidence of robotaxi scaling. We see momentum across the business as automotive demand improves, and Full Self-Driving (FSD) adoption accelerates. The active FSD base reached a record 1.48 million, up 56% year-over-year, with 55% of North American deliveries now including FSD. Unsupervised robotaxi miles are compounding rapidly, with an impeccable safety record across more than one million miles. Cybercab production is ramping, and given the importance of scaling robotaxis safely, we believe Tesla is right to take a deliberate approach to execution. The next-generation AI5 inference chip will also play a central role in the Optimus program. Together, these developments reinforce the robust long-term physical AI opportunity, despite a lengthy path to realizing it.

Global hotelier Hyatt Hotels Corporation detracted from performance as investors grew concerned that higher oil prices, interest rates, and inflation could pressure consumers and travel budgets. However, consumer spending remains resilient, particularly on travel and experiences. Hyatt continues to grow units at a mid-single-digit rate, while revenue per available room is accelerating from low to mid-single-digit growth. We expect this to support margin and cash flow expansion, generating capital to reinvest in the business, pursue accretive tuck-in acquisitions, and increase share repurchases. Hyattâs strong balance sheet provides additional flexibility to fund growth or allocate more capital to buybacks if management believes the stock price does not reflect its underlying value. The stock continues to trade at a significant discount to peers despite a similar mix of fee-for-service business. We believe this discount should narrow as investors see continued growth in the business.

We believe what especially sets the Fund apart from other growth funds is its historic ability to sustain value over more challenging economic periods. The nine-year period from the Internet Bubble collapse through the Great Recession (12/31/1999 to 12/31/2008) saw the Fund produce annualized returns of 1.54%. However, the Index declined substantially. A $10,000 hypothetical investment in the Fund at the start of this period would have been worth $11,479 after those nine years. A $10,000 hypothetical investment in a fund designed to track the Index would be worth only $6,488, more than a 35% cumulative decline. The Fund preserved (and slightly grew) capital during this difficult economic time because its investments in a diverse set of growth businesses could weather the environment and enhance their competitive positioning.

The COVID-19 pandemic and its lingering macroeconomic issues caused excessive market volatility. Over the course of three years, there were two sizable market corrections during which most major indexes fell more than 25%. But the Fund performed admirably in both protecting and growing clientsâ capital. During the COVID pandemic and its aftermath (12/31/2019 to 12/31/2022), the Fund had an annualized return of 23.65%. The Indexâs annualized return was significantly lower at only 3.85%.

Since the COVID Pandemic and subsequent market downturn ended, the Fund has performed even better on an absolute and relative basis.
