Four Types of Bank Accounts – Most People Need Only Checking & High-Yield Savings

Start with a free checking account for the money you spend and a high-yield savings account for the money you keep. Those two cover most people’s needs, so add a money market account or a certificate of deposit (CD), the other two types of bank accounts, only when a specific goal calls for one.

Savings left in checking earns next to nothing. Money for next month’s bills, locked in a CD, costs a penalty to pull out early.

All four are covered by FDIC insurance at an insured bank, or by similar federal insurance from the NCUA at a federally insured credit union, for at least $250,000 per depositor at each institution. They differ in how you reach the money, what it earns, and what it can cost.

Open a free checking account where your paycheck lands and a high-yield savings account for the money you’re keeping, at the same bank or two different ones.

Add a money market account only if you want to write checks against a large cash balance, since high-yield savings often pays as much. A CD fits money you’ll need on a set date.

A new graduate, for instance, might open free checking at a bank with ATMs near home and high-yield savings at an online bank, then add a 12-month CD for a down payment she plans to make in a year.

Look up a bank on the FDIC’s BankFind, or a credit union on the NCUA’s Credit Union Locator, to confirm it’s insured.
