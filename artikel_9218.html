International photography museum Fotografiska opens in Shenzhen

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Fotografiska Shenzhen is located in Shenzhen Bay MixC, an upscale retail and culture complex © SFAP Architectural Photography

The international photography museum Fotografiska has recently launched in Shenzhen, its second space in mainland China, after Fotografiska Shanghai opened in 2023. “The strategy was always to self-fund the first space in Shanghai, and then expand through strategic partnerships for subsequent locations,” says Christian Devillers, the managing director for Fotografiska China.

The organisation, founded in Stockholm in 2010, also currently has locations in the Swedish capital, New York, Berlin and Tallinn; and a new venue, in Oslo, is scheduled to open in 2028.

“Museum-going culture [in Shenzhen] isn’t as deeply entrenched as it is in Shanghai or Hong Kong,” Devillers says, and its art-going demographic skews heavily toward young families and professionals. Fotografiska Shenzhen is replicating Shanghai’s late hours and lifestyle offerings, which draw office workers commuting to the city centres, and its first public day on the 1 October national holiday drew more than 1,000 paying visitors.

The project opened with exhibitions across the 5,300 sq. m facility, including a Shi Guowei solo show and Toiletpaper, showcasing Maurizio Cattelan and Pierpaolo Ferrari’s titular magazine (both until 14 March 2027). They have joined a show of photography award Prix Pictet (until 29 November), the six-artist group show Transcendent Realities (until 1 October 2027) and a Michael Najjar solo exhibition (until 17 January 2027). Subsequent planned events include a show of Hong Kong photographers featuring, among others, Ho Fan (1931-2016).

In Shanghai, even with Fotografiska’s busy traffic, admissions and memberships account for only around 20% of total revenue, Devillers says. The museum instead depends on commercial partnerships, and retail and food and drink sales. Fotografiska Shenzhen’s six-year partnership agreement with landlord China Resources Land aims to draw a profit by year three. The institution has set an initial target of 70,000 visitors for 2027.

Fotografiska Shenzhen is located in the upscale retail complex Shenzhen Bay MixC, which is also home to a new immersive art concept called Base. They join several new and forthcoming cultural projects in the city, which is making a concerted push to raise its artistic profile, such as the recently-opened Shenzhen International Art Museum and Shenzhen Bay Cultural Plaza. This month will also see the long-running Hong Kong fair Fine Art Asia relocate to the mainland city (26-29 November).
