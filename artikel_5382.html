Lower mortgage rates could bring buyers back. What market will they return to?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Mortgage rates could fall below 7%, but what does that mean for local housing markets? HousingWire Data examines housing inventory, home asking prices, price reductions and pending home sales to reveal how housing affordability and buyer demand could shape opportunities heading into 2027.  AI Summary

The possibility of mortgage rates falling below 7% before the holidays is back on the table. In Tuesday morning’s Analyst Insights update in HousingWire Intelligence, HousingWire Lead Analyst Logan Mohtashami noted that the 10-year Treasury yield had reached 4.89%, approaching the 4.85% threshold he is watching, though mortgage spreads still need to improve.

But for housing professionals hoping to reengage buyers who stepped away from the market, mortgage rates are only part of the equation.

The homes available, the prices sellers are asking and the competition buyers face may look considerably different from when those buyers last searched.

Nationally, active single-family inventory stands at 894,692 homes, approximately 8% higher than a year ago. New pending listings increased 5.7% in the week ending Nov. 20, while new listings increased 1.2%.

That increase in contract activity is encouraging, but the broader picture remains more complicated.

HousingWire Data shows that 44 of 57 large metro areas (77.2%) recorded an improving ratio of new pending listings to new listings over consecutive four-week periods, even as pending activity remained below year-ago levels.

During the comparable period in 2025, just three of 54 qualifying metros (5.6%) showed that same combination.

An improving relationship between incoming listings and new contracts does not necessarily indicate strengthening buyer demand. And if lower mortgage rates do bring buyers back, the opportunities they encounter will vary considerably by market.

Consider Denver and Kansas City, two metro areas averaging nearly identical numbers of new pending listings per week but offering buyers different inventory and pricing environments.

Over the four weeks ending Nov. 20, Denver averaged 497 new pending listings per week, while Kansas City averaged 491. Both markets are experiencing substantially weaker contract activity than a year ago.

That’s where the similarities begin to fade.

Denver’s housing market offers buyers a different landscape heading into the holidays, with more available inventory, lower asking prices than during the spring and widespread price reductions.

For buyers who paused their home searches earlier this year because of affordability, Denver’s changing inventory and pricing conditions provide a reason to revisit their options, even before mortgage rates move meaningfully lower.

Kansas City’s median asking price has declined from its summer peak as price reductions have become more prevalent, although median asking prices remain above year-ago levels.

Kansas City offers buyers considerably more selection than a year ago. And while asking prices have moderated since the summer, higher median asking prices compared with last year could offset some of the affordability gains from lower borrowing costs.

Both markets offer more available homes than a year ago, but the pricing environments are different.

In Denver, a decline in mortgage rates could coincide with lower asking prices and more available homes. In Kansas City, greater selection and recent pricing adjustments may create opportunities, even though the median asking price remains higher than it was a year ago.

Changes in median asking prices can reflect shifts in the composition of available inventory, not just changes in individual home prices. That’s why examining inventory, asking prices and price reductions together provides a more useful assessment of local buying conditions than any one metric alone.

Another consideration is whether contract activity is already beginning to strengthen, even with mortgage rates above 7%.

Cincinnati, Ohio, recorded a similar pattern, with weekly new pending listings increasing from 397 to 459 over the same period.

But Cincinnati presents a different inventory and pricing environment. Its median active list price is up 1.9% year over year, and the market has 2.44 months of inventory, compared with 3.01 in Denver.

While both markets have recorded three consecutive weekly increases in contract activity, buyers returning to Cincinnati could encounter a tighter inventory environment than those returning to Denver.

Neither market has recovered to last year’s level of contract activity, and several weeks of increases are not enough to establish a sustained recovery, particularly heading into the holidays.

Still, these are developments worth monitoring. If contract activity continues to strengthen while new listings decline seasonally, competition for available homes could increase.

Housing professionals don’t need to wait for mortgage rates to fall below 7% to start preparing for that possibility.

The more immediate opportunity is to understand how local market conditions have changed since buyers last evaluated their options.

For buyer’s agents: Revisit the searches of clients who paused their homebuying plans earlier this year. Has available inventory expanded in their price range? Are more homes experiencing price reductions? Changing market conditions may create opportunities worth exploring before rates move lower.

For listing agents: More available inventory means more competition for sellers. Monitor new pending listings alongside active inventory and price reductions to help sellers evaluate whether their pricing strategy remains competitive as buyer activity changes.

For mortgage lenders: Reengaging prospective purchase borrowers doesn’t have to begin with a conversation about mortgage rates. Understanding how available homes and asking prices have changed in a borrower’s target market can help make that conversation more relevant. Revisit purchasing power using current local asking prices and different mortgage rate scenarios.

For brokerage leaders: National mortgage rate movements can provide a reason to launch buyer reengagement campaigns, but local housing conditions should inform the messaging. A market with expanding inventory and declining asking prices presents a different opportunity than one with tighter inventory and strengthening contract activity.
