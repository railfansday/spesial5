Star Catcher Partners With Common Space To Power Humanitarian Satellite Constellation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The first power grid in space will contribute in-kind on-orbit power so that energy is never the limiting factor for Earth observation

ST. LOUIS, Nov. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Common Space, the nonprofit building the world's first independent, community-governed high-resolution satellite constellation for the public good, today announced Star Catcher Industries, Inc. ("Star Catcher") as its first technology partner. Star Catcher, which is building the first power grid in space, will contribute in-kind on-demand orbital power to Common Space's humanitarian missions. The partnership gives the Common Space constellation flexibility to respond to the impacts of climate and conflict, and to benefit local communities when they need it.

The world does not lack satellite imagery. More than 1,000 Earth observation satellites image the planet every day. Yet, the people who respond to disasters, document human rights abuses, and protect ecosystems still cannot reliably get high-resolution data or task a satellite when and where the need is greatest. This is because commercial satellite companies are optimized for government and commercial customers, which naturally shapes pricing, access models, and tasking priorities.

Common Space aims to establish a new model for Earth observation

Common Space is working to fill this gap. Co-founded by Bill Greer (Esri, Albedo, Maxar) and Rhiannan Price (NASA Lifelines, Maxar), Common Space is moving satellite imagery away from commercial and military incentives to reliable public infrastructure for humanitarians, climate advocates, researchers, journalists and civil society. Common Space's community designed how imagery is tasked, prioritized, shared and protected from misuse through rigorous governance that puts humanitarian needs first

"Earth observation satellites have been used for everything from tracking climate change to disaster preparedness and response, stopping illegal fishing and documenting genocide," says Bill Greer, co-founder of Common Space. "We have proven this technology is super important to protect our planet, but the access has been limited, expensive and restricted by governments. We're building a transparent and independent system to serve the community of people that are trying to make the world better."

Common Space aims to establish a new model for Earth observation in which critical satellite data functions as shared public infrastructure rather than a narrowly controlled source of visual evidence. Star Catcher is building shared infrastructure for powering space: the Star Catcher Network uses optical power beaming to deliver concentrated solar energy to a satellite's existing solar arrays, which can increase available power by up to 10x with no retrofit required.

"I am a firm believer that a company can simultaneously be commercially viable and make the world a better place," says Andrew Rush, co-founder and CEO of Star Catcher. "With Common Space, we have an opportunity to scale our technology with an incredible humanitarian use case built in; we hope other leaders in our industry will choose to do the same."

Common Space launches with broad backing: more than 50 letters of support from academia and from global for-profit and nonprofit impact organizations. Humanitarian groups including MapAction, the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team and Justice Rapid Response have voiced support for the mission.

The Star Catcher partnership is the first of what Common Space hopes to be a model for others in the space industry. Both organizations are inviting satellite operators and data providers, across SAR, InSAR, EO and other modalities, to join them in showing what's possible when space companies share their services for the public good. "Together, we're showing what's possible when space infrastructure is built for the public good," says Rhiannan Price, co-founder of Common Space. "When space companies share what they've built, everyone working to make the world better gets to see it more clearly."

Common Space is a nonprofit building an independent, community-governed humanitarian satellite constellation for open, public-benefit Earth observation. Its high resolution imagery is openly licensed, tasked by humanitarian need, with governance designed by and for the people and organizations it serves. Organizations interested in membership, partnership, or funding can learn more at commonspace.world.

Star Catcher is an energy company building a power grid in space, providing the infrastructure to enable the next generation of spacecraft and advanced missions. Star Catcher removes power as a limiting factor for spacecraft, allowing continuous mission execution, increased operational endurance, and the ability to support higher power payloads without redesigning existing platforms. Founded in 2024 by Andrew Rush and Michael Snyder (Made In Space, Redwire), alongside Bryan Lyandvert (T-Bird Capital, Amazon), the company has raised $88M to date from B Capital, Shield Capital, Cerberus Ventures, Initialized Capital, among others. Based in Jacksonville, FL, the company currently holds the world record for optical power beaming.
