South Korea Announces Delay in Crypto Taxation Policy

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

South Korea announced a delay in the introduction of its cryptocurrency capital gains tax until 2029, extending the tax-free period by two years as authorities plan on strengthening regulatory oversight and connecting data from exchanges with the United States, yna.co.kr reports.

Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il announced the proposal Thursday, acknowledging that the government would be postponing the controversial tax for a fourth time already.

Originally, the policy was scheduled to become active in January 2022: it would impose a 22% tax, local income tax on all cryptocurrency-related gains that exceed 2.5 million won.

While the proposed delay could provide a relief for the South Korean cryptocurrency market and even support the domestic trading activity, it remains a substantial risk factor for both local and overseas investors.

Officials argue that implementing the tax before international information-sharing systems become operational could disadvantage local exchanges and complicate enforcement against overseas trading activity.

Future tax liabilities aren't going to be eliminated, additional time to develop a comprehensive taxation network is more likely to enforce even more limitations on investors in the future, rather than a premature implementation with various legal loopholes.

The government also intends to introduce provisions allowing investors to carry forward cryptocurrency investment losses for up to three years, potentially reducing taxable gains in subsequent periods.

South Korea is also going to establish a separate working group that is going to further refine taxation rules and hopefully simplify reporting requirements for investors, considering the complexity of reporting digital assets transactions and traders on exchanges.

Legislative amendments are expected to reach the National Assembly in 2027.

Authorities reported the continuous coordinating with the Financial Services Commission to accelerate digital assets legislation, providing more transparency and investors protection.

For the industry in general, the proposed extension is more of a transition stage that should improve the competitiveness of the digital assets market, despite introducing stricter tax enforcement. Unfortunately for retail investors, it creates another barrier that might push fresh funds further away from the industry.
