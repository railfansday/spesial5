Poland signs $300M Palantir deal for Maven AI defence software

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Polish defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz during talks at the Pentagon in Washington, D.C., in May 2025

Poland signed the contract on Friday. The five-year deal for Palantir’s Maven Smart System is worth an estimated $300M. The defence minister gave the figure, Bloomberg reported. This article has been updated with the signing, the value and the minister’s comments.

Poland’s defence ministry signed a contract with Palantir Technologies on Friday to buy Maven Smart System. The AI software helps armed forces analyse battlefield data and make decisions. Defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz put the five-year deal’s estimated value at about $300M, Bloomberg reported.

The ministry announced the signing on Thursday, Reuters reported, citing a ministry statement. The contract is with Palantir’s Polish subsidiary. NATO and the US military already use the same system. Palantir has said it cannot switch off NATO’s Maven system.

Kosiniak-Kamysz called the deal historic when he spoke to reporters in Warsaw. He said Poland would be the sole owner of the system’s data and infrastructure.

Poland will own the “keys, servers and information” under the agreement, Kosiniak-Kamysz said, according to Bloomberg.

“Whenever possible, we choose a Polish product,” he said. Where no Polish system has comparable capabilities, the ministry picks the best one in the world, he added.

Palantir has not commented publicly on the deal or its terms.

The contract is the first binding step in a partnership that began with a cybersecurity agreement in December 2024. In October 2025, the minister and Palantir chief executive Alex Karp signed a letter of intent covering AI, IT and cybersecurity. At that signing, the minister said testing had begun in December 2023. It included dozens of meetings, exercises and demonstrations across all branches of the armed forces.

Poland’s Cyberspace Defence Forces Component Command led the negotiations. The minister has defended the choice by pointing out that NATO and Ukraine’s armed forces already use Palantir’s software.

Poland is moving in the opposite direction to some of its allies. France and Germany have pledged to build a European Palantir rival, and Germany is testing 30 AI tools for its military. The Netherlands wants a full replacement for the company’s software within two years.

Warsaw has also been deepening defence technology ties with Washington. Earlier this week, Poland and the US signed an agreement at the Pentagon on joint defence research and development, ending 17 years of negotiations.
