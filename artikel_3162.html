Churchill Downs: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

A new racing series and flexible gaming licenses position the company to monetize beyond its iconic annual event.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 27, 2026 at 2:28PM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, Churchill Downs (CHDN +1.57%) announced record net revenue of $696 million and adjusted EBITDA, a metric tracking cash operating profit, of $264 million. The company is divesting lower-margin regional casinos to refocus capital on its core horse racing and gaming venues. Three key takeaways from the call stand out for long-term investors.

Management expects to execute sales transactions for nine regional casino properties by the end of 2026. Selling these non-core gaming properties narrows the portfolio around higher-return assets. The transaction proceeds will allow the business to cut debt, repurchase shares under a newly authorized $750 million program, and fund expansion at Churchill Downs Racetrack.

Starting in 2027, the company is launching a five-month racing series across three premier tracks to extend fan engagement past Derby Week. By connecting marquee events into a single season-long narrative, management creates new long-term revenue channels.

A new casino in Petersburg increased regional competition for central Virginia gaming venues. However, management retains statutory authority to shift historical racing machines (HRMs), which are video gaming terminals driven by past race results, and operational licenses across the state to maximize returns in underserved locations.

Ticket sales for the 153rd Kentucky Derby are pacing 25% ahead of last year's rate as customer demand expands. Long-term value relies on pricing power at Churchill Downs Racetrack, expanding historical racing machine floors, and growing the TwinSpires wagering platform.

Management is working to lower bank leverage below 3.0x following casino sales while compounding cash flow across core platforms.
