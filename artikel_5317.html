JPMorgan Buys More Strategy and Bitcoin ETF Despite MSCI Saylor Blow

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

JPMorgan is quietly increasing its Bitcoin exposure while the crypto market is being beaten up. The banking giant raised its Strategy holdings by 36% and added another 1 million shares of BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT).

JPMorgan Bitcoin positioning makes an interesting contrast with the current market panic. Bitcoin has plunged below $70,000, wiping out leveraged bullish bets and dragging major altcoins lower. Meanwhile, Michael Saylor’s Strategy faces another institutional headache after MSCI confirmed its removal from major equity indexes.

Wall Street might be seeing something that the market is missing. JPMorgan’s latest holdings show institutional exposure was growing before the latest sell-off, even as Strategy faces mounting pressure.

Could this be smart money positioning for Bitcoin’s next recovery?

JPMorgan held 848,932 Strategy shares worth $131.3 million at the end of September. That’s a 36% increase from its 624,387 shares reported three months earlier.

Its exposure to BlackRock’s Bitcoin ETF grew even further in dollar terms. The bank reported 11.4 million IBIT shares worth $540 million, compared with 10.4 million shares valued at $355.7 million in June.

The IBIT position increased by 9.6% in share count, while its reported market value jumped 52%. JPMorgan also reported smaller positions in other Bitcoin funds, including Grayscale’s Bitcoin Mini Trust and Bitwise’s Bitcoin ETF.

Together, the Strategy and IBIT positions were worth $671 million at September 30. That’s a sizeable institutional footprint across two different ways of gaining Bitcoin exposure.

However, the timing makes this filing particularly interesting. These holdings paint JPMorgan’s third quarter, ending September 30. JPMorgan was building exposure before Bitcoin’s latest breakdown, leaving investors wondering if the bank still sees value in the correction.

There is another detail worth watching. JPMorgan also disclosed substantial put-option exposure against Strategy and IBIT, alongside call positions. Its derivatives book shows a more complicated positioning strategy than a straightforward bullish bet. Still, the increased share counts show that the bank expanded its underlying holdings.

JPMorgan’s growing Strategy position comes at an awkward moment for Saylor. MSCI confirmed that Strategy will leave its MSCI USA Index following the November 30 market close. The company is also losing its place in the MSCI World and ACWI benchmarks as the index provider implements new eligibility rules for non-operating companies.

This decision could create another source of selling pressure. Passive funds tracking affected benchmarks generally need to adjust their holdings when constituents disappear. Strategy previously warned that potential index exclusions could trigger billions of dollars in outflows.

JPMorgan previously estimated that MSCI exclusion alone could result in $2.8 billion in selling pressure. The figure could climb substantially if other major index providers follow the same approach.

Yet JPMorgan increased its Strategy holdings during the quarter leading into this decision. That creates an interesting contradiction: one of Wall Street’s largest banks expanded its exposure while the company was heading toward a major index setback.

Bitcoin slipped below $69,000 this morning, its lowest level since August, while crypto liquidations reached $1.6 billion over 24 hours. More than $1.3 billion came from liquidated long positions, highlighting just how aggressively leveraged traders were caught on the wrong side of the move.

This selling can create opportunities for investors with longer time horizons. When leveraged positions disappear, weaker hands are forced out, leaving more room for institutional accumulation once volatility settles.

The MSCI setback, no doubt, creates a potential disconnect between index-driven selling and longer-term investment demand.
