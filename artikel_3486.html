Court to Hear Christa Pike's Challenge to her 'Indefinite Isolation' in Prison

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

After Christa Pike’s attorneys challenged her current prison conditions following her failed execution, a federal court is set to hear arguments in the case.

In an emergency motion filed October 30, Pike’s attorneys called on the court to enforce the terms of a 2024 settlement agreement that ended her 28 years in solitary confinement as the only woman on Tennessee’s death row, allowing for her to have group meals, engage in work and other social and recreational activities.

Pike’s attorneys argued in the filing that the Tennessee Department of Correction is wrongly “holding Ms. Pike in indefinite isolation in the prison infirmary,” away from other women in custody, in violation of that agreement.

In a response filed November 4 viewed by Us, Tennessee Attorney General Jonathan Skrmetti argued the court should deny Pike’s emergency motion, writing prison officials have a “constitutional duty to offer medical care to Pike” and “she can be discharged from the infirmary when medically appropriate.”

A judge will hear the opposing arguments from Pike’s legal team and the state on December 1, according to a Friday, November 13, court order.

Pike, 50, who was sentenced to death at the age of 20 after she was convicted of killing her classmate Colleen Slemmer in January 1995, survived two injections of pentobarbital during a botched execution and was taken to a hospital on September 30.

Following her hospital discharge on October 9, her attorneys contend she’s remained wrongly separated from other women receiving medical care at the Debra K. Johnson Rehabilitation Center as she continues to recover.

In a November 6 reply to the state, Pike’s attorneys wrote, “[The Tennessee Department of Correction] cannot botch the execution of Christa Pike and then point to the injuries it caused as the reason Defendants no longer must honor their Settlement Agreement with her.”

“The Agreement guarantees Ms. Pike group meals, work, lobby time, religious services, and recreation. Defendants do not and cannot deny that they have failed to provide these since at least October 9th. The breach is clear, uncontested, and causing daily harm,” the filing continues.

The state contends in its response to Pike’s emergency motion that it’d be premature to transfer Pike to a separate housing area with other inmates in the prison’s infirmary due to her medical needs and designation as a maximum security incident.

In the state’s response, Skrmetti noted Pike once allegedly tried to kill another inmate and faced discipline for an alleged prison escape attempt. He argued that if she were to rejoin others in custody, it could jeopardize other inmates’ safety.

The attorney general wrote that prison officials “have not breached” the 2024 settlement agreement, “and Pike is not entitled to dictate the terms of her medical treatment and housing.”

The order scheduling the December 1 hearing on Pike’s emergency motion doesn’t indicate whether Pike will appear in court.

Pike’s attorneys didn’t immediately respond to an inquiry from Us on Tuesday, November 17.
