Leaseholders in England and Wales to have ground rent capped in 2028

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Housing minister hails ‘historic moment’ as he publishes bill to eventually ban almost all new leasehold properties

Leaseholders in England and Wales will have their ground rent capped in 2028, the housing minister has said as he publishes a bill that will eventually ban virtually all new leasehold properties.

Matthew Pennycook will introduce the government’s leasehold reform bill on Thursday, including measures to cap charges, end the sale of new leasehold flats and make it easier to extend and buy out leases.

He said one of the first elements of the bill to come into force would be the long-promised £250 cap on annual ground rent – though it will not be enacted as quickly as some MPs have pushed for.

“The ground rent can be expected fairly early,” he said. “I think it will be a really historic moment … leasehold denies individuals control over the homes they live in and have paid for.

“It then allows freeholders and managing agents acting on their behalf to extract excessive amounts of money from them, whether that’s unreasonable or extortionate charges, punitive and escalating ground rents, unjustified administration and permission fees – you name it.

“Leasehold is an anachronism in the 21st century. I really do think that the bill that we’re introducing tomorrow [Thursday] marks the beginning of the end for the feudal leasehold system.”

Pennycook and his Conservative predecessors promised to end the leasehold system, which exists in only a few countries worldwide and dates back to feudal property laws.

Thursday’s bill will mark the biggest step towards making those promises a reality, extending the ban on leasehold for new houses to flats, which make up the bulk of leasehold properties. Pennycook has previously said he expects the ban to come into force after the next election.

It will also make it easier for those in leasehold properties to switch to a collective system known as commonhold, ensuring that such a move can be made with the agreement of half the residents in a block rather than all of them.

And it will end freeholders’ right to evict leaseholders for relatively small unpaid bills.

One of the first changes to become law will be the £250 cap on ground rent.

MPs on the housing select committee had urged Pennycook to introduce it as soon as next year, but he insisted it was not possible because of the “technical process” of beginning its enforcement. The 2028 date would not be explicitly included in the bill but ministers would enact it through secondary legislation, he said.

He also said many other leaseholders’ demands would have to come in a second law to be introduced later in the parliament.

They include establishing an independent regulator for property management agents and changing the rates that determine how much it costs to extend or buy out a lease.

Ministers are still considering proposals, meanwhile, which would strip out the costs of prospective future development from the cost of a lease.

Pennycook said that wrestling with the legal technicalities of property law had slowed the process of drafting the bill, arguing that doing so had minimised the risk of the government having to go back and rewrite parts of it.

“This is by far and away the most complex and technical piece of legislation I have ever dealt with,” he said.

“We are undertaking the most ambitious overhaul of land and property law in a century, if not longer … it is just ferociously complex.

“We’re taking on some big vested interests, and those vested interests have very deep pockets.

“Leasehold law is extremely litigious – we’ve had challenges brought to the 2024 Act … [and] we will get challenges to this piece of legislation as well, almost certainly.”
