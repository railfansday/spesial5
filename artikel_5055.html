Strive (ASST) Buys 251 Bitcoin for $17.5 Million as Shares Drop 10% Premarket

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Strive bought another 251 bitcoin last week for $17.5 million at an average of $69,853 per coin, Chairman and CEO Matthew Cole posted Monday, lifting total holdings to 43,404 BTC.

“Strive acquired 251 $BTC for $17.5M at an average cost of $69,853 per bitcoin, bringing total holdings to ₿43,404,” Cole wrote. A Monday 8-K filing shows the purchases ran from Nov. 7 through Nov. 13, inclusive of fees and expenses, and took the Dallas company’s stack up from 43,153 bitcoin a week earlier.

Cole said Strive raised $10 million through its SATS at-the-market program. The filing puts that total at $10.02 million in gross proceeds from the sale of 104,478 shares of the company’s 15% Series A perpetual preferred stock.

Strive sold no Class A common shares and no shares of its variable-rate SATA preferred during the week. Cash and cash equivalents fell $22.3 million to $465.3 million as of Nov. 13. Cole also posted a preferred dividend coverage figure of 133 months and an amplification ratio of 76%, two metrics the 8-K does not break out.

The 251-coin add is larger than the 29 bitcoin Strive bought the prior week at an average of $75,721 and the 124 bitcoin it bought in late October at $80,526, but it remains a fraction of the October sprint that built the stack. Holdings stood at 28,000 bitcoin on Sept. 30 before Strive acquired 15,124 coins between Oct. 1 and Oct. 30, including a 6,182-coin buy.

The company closed a registered offering of 7 million SATS shares on Oct. 20 for about $609 million in net proceeds and later lined up a $1 billion SATS at-the-market program, the facilities that have funded the larger purchases. Effective common shares outstanding were unchanged at 127,331,109. SATS shares outstanding rose to 7,141,037, while SATA remained at 14,030,176.

Strive still holds 505,000 shares of Strategy’s variable-rate STRC preferred, marked at a fair value of $50.5 million as of Nov. 13. On Nov. 12, the board raised the regular dividend rate on SATA to 13.25% for periods beginning Dec. 1 and declared daily December cash dividends of $0.0502 a share on SATA and $0.0569 a share on SATS, which continues to carry a 15% rate. Those daily payments total $1.1044 a share on SATA and $1.2518 a share on SATS for the full month.

Strive’s Class A shares fell around 10% in Monday premarket trading to $19.34 as of 8:45 a.m. ET, from Friday’s close of $21.36. The move tracks a broader selloff in bitcoin-linked stocks. Strategy, the largest public corporate holder, was down 8.1% in premarket trading to $102.60 as of 8:45 a.m. ET, from Friday’s close of $111.62.

ASST has already given back a large share of its October run. The stock closed Oct. 21 at $30.75 and Oct. 30 at $22.19. Monday’s premarket price is down about 13% from that October close and about 37% from the Oct. 21 close. The 52-week range runs from $7.02 to $32.08. Year to date, the shares are still higher: they finished 2025 at $14.76, leaving the premarket price up about 31% from year-end.

Bitcoin traded near $64,300 as of 8:45 a.m. ET, after dropping below $62,000 over the weekend, its lowest level since July 14. A graphic Cole attached to his post valued the 43,404-coin treasury at about $2.99 billion, using a bitcoin price of $68,811 as of 4 p.m. ET on Nov. 13. At Monday morning’s spot, the same stack is worth roughly $2.79 billion.

Last week’s coins were purchased at $69,853, about 8% above the current price. Strive has not disclosed a companywide cost basis in Monday’s filing.

The open will test whether the weekly add can steady the stock after the weekend break in the bitcoin price, or whether treasury shares keep sliding as the cash session begins.
