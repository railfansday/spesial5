CryptoUK Urges FCA Not to Treat DeFi Devs as Regulated by Default

The UK's main crypto trade association has called on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) to make sure that developers and governance participants in decentralized finance protocols are not treated as regulated firms by default.

CryptoUK set out its position in a response to the FCA's consultation paper CP26/36, which proposes guidance on the "perimeter" of the UK's new crypto regime: which activities fall inside it and need FCA authorization, and which don't. The group, which says it represents more than 150 crypto companies, submitted its response on Wednesday, November 18, the day the consultation closed, and published it a day later.

Yesterday, CryptoUK submitted its response to the Financial Conduct Authority’s Consultation Paper CP26/36, which proposes amendments to the Perimeter Guidance Manual (PERG) to reflect the FSMA 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026.

"Software development, maintenance, security work and participation in decentralised governance should not, by themselves, establish that a person carries on a regulated activity," CryptoUK wrote. That would be consistent with the FCA's "clear controlling person" approach to DeFi, under which regulation should hinge on who effectively controls a financial service, it argued.

CryptoUK said it "broadly" supports the draft guidance, which reflects further amendments to the UK's crypto legislation that the government laid in September. The FCA aims to publish its final guidance in January 2027. It welcomed new exclusions from the regime for temporary settlement and payment arrangements, assets backing UK-recognized stablecoins, proprietary trading and market making, and technical service providers.

But in several places, the draft "goes further than the legislation," the group said. It pointed to a proposed link to the UK based on where a protocol is developed or governed, requirements in the exclusions for payments and proprietary trading, and indicators that would narrow the exclusion for technical services. The FCA has acknowledged that the timing of the latest amendments created uncertainty for some firms, and said it will give applicants extra time where questions over the technical services exclusion held up their applications for authorization.

The group asked the FCA to publish a public record of stablecoins that qualify under the UK regime, to clarify how stablecoins differ from electronic money, and to include more examples of activities that typically fall outside the perimeter.

CryptoUK also urged the FCA to raise with the Treasury outcomes that put UK firms at a disadvantage, citing the treatment of UK technical providers that offer access to regulated overseas trading venues, and of payments using stablecoins issued abroad. The UK should align with the EU's MiCA framework "where appropriate," it said. The response was prepared with law firm CMS and signed by CryptoUK policy director Nick Brooks.

The consultation is part of the FCA's work to build out the UK's crypto rulebook. The regulator finalized its core rules for crypto firms in June, ahead of a regime due to take effect on October 25, 2027, and later issued guidance two weeks before its authorization window for crypto firms opened. It has also set out a shared vision for tokenization with the Bank of England.
