BlackRock single-handedly ends Bitcoin ETFs’ $1.9 billion exodus as seven rivals keep bleeding

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

IBIT's $76.5 million inflow pushed the broader ETF market into positive territory despite continued withdrawals from seven competing funds.

BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF ended an 11-session outflow streak totaling $2.35 billion on Monday, even as the broader market remained underwater for 2026.

The iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) attracted $76.5 million on Nov. 16, recording its first positive session since Oct. 29, according to Farside Investors.

The inflow helped US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds post a combined $7.9 million gain, ending seven consecutive trading sessions of withdrawals totaling approximately $1.91 billion.

However, BlackRock accounted for the entire recovery. Seven competing funds collectively lost $68.6 million, while four recorded no net flows.

Grayscale's GBTC led Monday's withdrawals with $34.6 million, followed by Fidelity's FBTC at $11.3 million. ARKB, BTCO, EZBC, MSBT and Grayscale's smaller BTC trust also registered outflows.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs reported combined net inflows of $7.9 million. Positive values indicate net inflows and negative values indicate net outflows. Four funds reported $0.0 million.

Source: Farside Investors, Bitcoin ETF flow table, Nov. 16, 2026

The contrasting performance comes as Bitcoin struggles to recover from its latest selloff, trading around $64,871 as of press time after briefly approaching $61,000 over the weekend.

Despite its recent withdrawal streak, BlackRock remains one of the few major Bitcoin ETF issuers attracting more capital than it has lost this year.

ETF market data showed that IBIT had accumulated approximately $2.10 billion in net inflows during 2026 as of Nov. 13, while the broader market recorded $1.82 billion in net withdrawals.

The divergence is also evident in cumulative flows since launch.

BlackRock's cumulative net inflows reached approximately $64.27 billion following Monday's positive session, exceeding the $54.71 billion attracted by the entire US spot Bitcoin ETF market.

Meanwhile, IBIT held approximately $53.99 billion in assets as of Nov. 13, reflecting the impact of Bitcoin's decline on the fund's market value.

Bloomberg ETF analyst Eric Balchunas highlighted BlackRock's resilience, describing its ability to retain positive inflows despite deteriorating sentiment as remarkable. He said:

“I mean seriously $IBIT is still +$2b net inflows YTD which is unreal given the sentiment and price action.”

However, he acknowledged that Bitcoin remained in a particularly difficult market environment.

Indeed, trading firm Wintermute has identified weakening demand across several sources of capital. For context, the market is currently grappling with a $14 billion to $16 billion contraction in stablecoin supply over 30 days and also weakening demand from corporate Bitcoin treasury buyers.

Wintermute also warned that Bitcoin's recent decline below its 200-week moving average near $68,000 had weakened the market's technical structure. According to the firm:

“It is now clear we haven’t cleared the bear market yet, and the rally off the July low was a fake-out.”

In view of this, the market maker said it remained defensive after approximately $1.7 billion in liquidations over the weekend, arguing that a short-covering-driven rebound would not necessarily signal renewed capital inflows.

Wintermute identified a daily Bitcoin close above $68,000 accompanied by positive ETF flows as one condition that could change its short-term bearish stance.
