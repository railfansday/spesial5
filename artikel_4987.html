Clarity Act Defeat ‘Failure of American Leadership’: Trump’s Former Crypto Chief

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Clarity Act Defeat ‘Failure of American Leadership’: Trump’s Former Crypto Chief

The Senate's failure to pass the Clarity Act was "a failure of American leadership," according to Patrick Witt, President Donald Trump's former top crypto adviser.

"It was extremely disappointing," Witt said in an on-stage interview at Solana's Breakpoint conference in London. "I was knee deep in Clarity. I lived it for a year plus negotiating that bill," he noted, adding that the bill, "would have been incredibly impactful."

Witt served as executive director of the President's Council of Advisers for Digital Assets until last month and led the White House push for the bill, which would have set out a framework for regulating crypto markets. It failed a key procedural vote in the Senate in September.

"We ultimately lost against the clock," he said, adding that, "The policy was good. We resolved pretty much all of the policy differences." But with the midterm elections approaching, "the political issue of ethics really consumed everything else," he said.

Democrats had raised concerns over the Trump family's crypto ventures. Witt acknowledged that Trump's involvement in crypto was "arguably the issue," but said the president had agreed to two ethics provisions. The second would have barred federal officials, including the president, from issuing or sponsoring digital assets, forced the president to divest certain digital assets or put them in a blind trust, and let state attorneys general enforce violations. "There's no precedent for those kinds of things in American law," he said.

The GENIUS stablecoin bill also lost an initial procedural vote in 2025 but recovered because it came early in Trump's term, Witt noted. With Clarity, "we knew that we were going to get one shot at it, and unfortunately we just came up short," he said.

Looking ahead, Witt said the odds of passing a comprehensive replacement are low. "I would expect that market structure in the United States is going to be less written on Capitol Hill and more written in the Federal Register, which is where the agencies do their rulemakings and issue their guidance," Witt said.

Parts of the Clarity Act could still pass in larger legislative packages, and lawmakers could grant targeted powers, such as giving the Commodity Futures Trading Commission authority over spot crypto markets, he said.

Witt said regulators already have "ample authority," and that the administration had to "hold them back a little bit" while the bill played out. The SEC and CFTC have since moved ahead with their own crypto rules, though House Financial Services Committee Chairman French Hill (R-AR) has said those efforts fall short of what the legislation would have achieved.

SEC Chair Paul Atkins and CFTC Chair Michael Selig "are committed to moving markets on chain," Witt said, framing the issue as one of American competitiveness. "Capital is going to go where it's treated best," he said.

Despite Democrats winning control of both chambers of Congress in this month's midterms, Witt said he is "not bearish on the industry overall," since the regulators and the president remain in place. Democrats are reportedly planning to push their own, broader market structure bill.

He said opposition to crypto is increasingly generational rather than purely partisan. While some Democrats, such as Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), are "going to be 100% opposed to anything that seemingly is favorable to crypto," younger lawmakers on both sides see it as "part of the fabric of financial infrastructure," he said, pointing to Sens. Angela Alsobrooks (D-MD) and Ruben Gallego (D-AZ).

Witt left the administration in October, The Hill reported. He said he has held talks with crypto firms, venture capital firms and defense companies, and hopes to announce his next move in the coming weeks.
