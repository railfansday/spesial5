Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Standard Life Chair Wants Tokenized Gilts to Open UK Government Bonds to Retail Savers

Tokenization could help ordinary British savers buy government bonds more easily, and should not become a tool only for big financial institutions, according to Sir Nicholas Lyons, chairman of Standard Life.

"Why should it be so unnecessarily complicated for retail investors to buy gilts that fund the nation's needs?" Lyons said at Solana's Breakpoint conference in London.

Lyons, a former Lord Mayor of the City of London whose company has about £480 billion ($633 billion) in assets under administration, said Britain should use tokenization to bring more households into investing. About £850 billion ($1.1 trillion) of household wealth remains in cash, often losing value to inflation, he said.

Tokenization could cut administrative costs, make transfers simpler and let savers invest in small amounts in products that would otherwise be uneconomic, Lyons said. More retail ownership of gilts would make the UK less reliant on foreign buyers and could help erode the risk premium the country has paid on its debt since the September 2022 mini-budget, he added.

The remarks come as the UK prepares to issue its first digitally native government bond, known as DIGIT. Earlier this month, the Treasury confirmed roles for six banks supporting the pilot, with issuance expected in the first quarter of 2027, Lyons said.

"Government bonds sit at the heart of our financial system," he said, noting that they underpin liquidity, provide collateral and set reference rates for other assets. Showing that tokenized gilts can be issued, settled and used as collateral efficiently would lay "an important foundation for much broader digital capital markets," he said.

Lyons credited the Financial Conduct Authority, the Bank of England and the Treasury for progress so far, including the Digital Securities Sandbox and work to let tokenized securities be used as collateral.

The Bank of England has also turned its attention to stablecoins: an executive has said it is treating them as a new form of money, it eased its planned stablecoin rules in June, and it has since been handed a legal duty to foster stablecoin innovation. Lyons cited a July report by Chris Woolard, the government's wholesale digital markets champion, which estimated that faster adoption of digital markets could add as much as £33 billion ($43.5 billion) a year to UK economic output by 2035.

But the competition is moving fast, he warned, pointing to the SEC's innovation exemption for tokenized stock trading in the U.S., and initiatives in Europe, Singapore, Hong Kong, Switzerland and the Middle East.

Lyons set out four priorities: making the digital gilt pilot a success, accelerating tokenized collateral and repo markets, promoting interoperability to avoid "a collection of competing digital platforms that recreate the fragmentation of the existing system," and ensuring retail investors share in the benefits.

He also acknowledged the risks, citing concerns raised by the Financial Times' Gillian Tett that AI-accelerated cyber threats and faster-moving money could fuel a run on deposits. "Tokenization does not automatically make an investment safer, more liquid, or more stable, or suitable for retail investors," he said. "Fractional ownership of a risky asset remains ownership of a risky asset."

"We're not pursuing tokenization for the sake of technological leadership," Lyons said. "We're pursuing better capital markets, more productive investment, greater economic prosperity, and wider participation in the creation of wealth and jobs."
