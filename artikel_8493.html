You can get hired with little or no experience in dozens of jobs, so choose by how much training time you can give now. Restaurants, warehouses, and delivery companies train you within weeks, while a paid apprenticeship or a short certificate program takes longer and usually leads to higher pay.

Most U.S. jobs ask for no more than a high school diploma. The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) puts 59% of U.S. jobs in 2025 in occupations that typically need a diploma or less to get in, including 23.6% that need no formal credential at all.

Jobs that need no credential paid a median $36,400 that year, and jobs that need a diploma, another 35.4% of the total, paid $48,050, against $50,980 for all jobs. Jobs that typically call for a certificate from a program after high school paid $49,910, or $13,510 a year more than jobs that need no credential.

Each job below shows its median pay in May 2025 and BLS’s projected change in jobs from 2025 to 2035, against 3.5% growth for all jobs. The pay figures count only workers on a payroll, so they leave out the self-employed, including most rideshare drivers.

This list is aimed at adults starting out or changing careers. If you’re in high school, summer jobs for high school students fit your schedule better, and if you need to work from home, look for remote jobs you can do from anywhere.

These typically ask for a high school diploma at most, and you learn on the job, in anything from a few days to a paid apprenticeship of several years. Longer training usually pays more. Servers learn in a month or less and earn a median $35,230 a year, while plumbers apprentice for four or five years and earn $63,800. The longest apprenticeships lead to high-paying jobs that don’t require a bachelor’s degree.

These also typically ask for a high school diploma at most, but before you can work you need a license, a certificate, or a registration, which usually takes a short course, an exam, or both.

BLS lists a certificate from a program after high school as the usual way into these jobs, and programs run from three to six months of truck-driving school to one or two years for medical assistants. If you’re weighing one against a four-year degree, compare trade school and college on total cost against the pay each path brings.

Before you accept any offer, ask how long training lasts and whether you’re paid during it.
