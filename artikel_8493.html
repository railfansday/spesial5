Choose a TV show by the money skill you want. A business show teaches how companies make money and what they’re worth, a personal finance series covers budgets and debt, and a fraud documentary shows the warning signs victims missed. Watch market shows for the news, and treat any stock tip on air as entertainment.

A good money show puts real numbers on screen and lets you watch someone decide what to do about them, which no finance book can do.

The lesson is in the minute the money comes up, when a founder names a price for the company or a couple adds up their card balances. Pause there and ask what you would decide in their place.

Stock picks on air are the exception, because the guest is giving a national audience an opinion, with no idea of your goals or when you’ll need the money.

A couple who watch How to Get Rich together can follow it with a monthly money date, one evening each month to go over the budget and one goal they share.

A fraud documentary is a good prompt to check whoever handles your investments. Look them up on BrokerCheck, a tool from FINRA, the group that polices brokerage firms, to see their work history, licenses, and any violations.

Keep a notes app open while you watch, and write down one move to make with your own money before the next episode starts, such as canceling a subscription you forgot about.
