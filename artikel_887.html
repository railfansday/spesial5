Van Gerwen: I need my A-game to beat Wade at Grand Slam

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Michael van Gerwen on his second-round opponent: "James Wade is playing some fantastic darts; he's probably in some of the best form of his life"; pair's meeting a rematch of 2016 final which MVG won; watch the Grand Slam of Darts on Sky Sports Main Event at 7pm, Thursday

Michael van Gerwen believes James Wade is "in some of the best form of his life" and says he'll need to bring his "A-game" in order to win their second-round meeting at the Grand Slam of Darts.

Their clash on Thursday night - live on Sky Sports+ and Main Event from 7pm - is a rematch of the 2016 final, in which Van Gerwen claimed the second of his three consecutive titles.

But it's Wade, a three-time runner-up at the event, who is the form player of the pair leading into the last 16 matchup, having dropped only one leg across his two group-stage wins.

Van Gerwen, contrastingly, saw both his wins over Henry Coates and Jeffrey de Graaf come down to last-leg deciders. He averaged 100 against Coates, but that dropped to 90 against De Graaf, a match in which he missed seven match darts.

"It wasn't a good game at all," Van Gerwen, seeded 10th at the tournament, told Sky Sports after his victory over De Graaf.

"Sometimes you're not doing what you want to do. That's frustrating, because I know I'm capable of doing a lot more.

"What can I say? I am through to the next round - that's all history now - and it's onto the next one."

The former world No 1 added: "James Wade is playing some fantastic darts, you can't deny that. He's probably in some of the best form of his life.

"I need to make sure I play my A-game if I'm going to beat him - simple as that."

Wade has been in sensational form across 2026, making it to the final of the UK Open, winning the Australian Masters, Flanders Darts Trophy, and the opening Players Championship event back in February.

He knows that, with his form at the minute, he has the game to beat anyone, especially if the contest is a bit of a grudge match.

"I think I am way better than the top 10 but I have had to bumble along," said the seventh seed, who has averaged over 100 in both of his Grand Slam group stage wins against Cam Crabtree (5-1) and Ricky Evans (5-0).

"I think if I was probably a little bit more consistent, I would be better than I am.

"But talk is cheap and all that rubbish. I think I am good enough to beat anyone at the moment, because I like a scrap and no darts player wants to have a scrap.

"Most of them just want to have a little gallivant along the meadows and stuff like that, but I am quite happy to take them to a place where most of the younger players don't want to go and that is when I win games."

Luke Humphries, the 2023 Grand Slam champion, is also in action on Thursday night, against Tom Sykes, while Gian van Veen faces Martin Schindler and Nathan Aspinall takes on Gabriel Clemens.

"There has been so much made of James Wade's form. He is playing great.

"I don't really get Michael van Gerwen at the moment. A couple of weeks ago he was playing in a Pro Tour event and was averaging 114, 115. He also looked great in his opening group game here, but then looked really edgy in his second.

"Having said that, normally with Michael, the longer the format the less edgy he looks. I'm not saying he'll beat James, but I don't buy into this narrative that Michael is off. He's just not as consistent as he used to be.

"He portrays a lack of confidence at times with what he says, which I don't like.

"I believe he is capable of growing into a game. His confidence grows after a couple of decent legs.

"But if James Wade gets on top of him, I don't like Van Gerwen's chances from there."
