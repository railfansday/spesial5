Gears Of War: E-Day Is Bringing Back Legacy Maps, Modes And Skins For Season 2

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

And tomorrow they’re giving away a free General RAAM skin!

The Coalition kicked off Gears' 20th anniversary celebrations by reviving a classic Versus mode, introducing a new Horde mission, and adding a few extra bits and bobs to Gears of War: E-Day.

It might not have seemed like much, but reintroducing Guardian to Versus was an excellent callback to the mode's debut in Gears of War 2. The game's first season is coming to a close at the end of November, making way for E-Day's Season 2 content: Legacy.

Tomorrow, E-Day players can claim a free General RAAM skin for multiplayer. All you have to do is log in - that's it. Season 2 begins on December 1st, promising to bring back fan-favourite maps, modes and skins to "honour the greatest hits of Gears over 20 years".

"And beginning December 1, we launch our next Season – titled Legacy – which continues to honor the greatest hits of Gears over 20 years in our maps, modes and multiplayer skins. Season 2 not only brings back some fan favorites from the history of the franchise but lets us reimagine characters in ways we’ve always wanted to see them – like Solemn Memories Dom, whose character skin depicts him in the suit he’s wearing in the famous photo of him and Maria from Gears of War 2."

Does this mean we're going to see Kalona's version of Gridlock, Blood Drive and Canals? I sure hope so! I'd also like to reunite the Carmine family in E-Day. Can you imagine a full squad of Carmines voiced by the one and only Michael Gough? I can, and I want it!

@Fiendish-Beaver Whilst I agree with you about the next Gen, I don't think Consoles will last and they aren't really fulfilling the role they used to do.

Consoles were always the cheap mass market gaming device because Computers were too expensive. The Console was a 'kids' toy where teens/adults had Computers like Commodore 64 or Sinclair Spectrum but thanks to Sony pushing their Playstation to the young Adults with their marketing, that helped make consoles a more acceptable and affordable way for adults to game.

PC's at that time too were very complicated with multiple Graphics Cards too complicating matters further. Microsoft entered the Console space to bring PC games to a more mainstream audience and establish their 'DirectX' API as a PC standard too - if its required for their Box, it will be used for their Windows PC too - so neglected their PC platform to establish their 'Xbox' Console.

Now, you can buy a handheld PC that plays games at 'Console' like settings as well as desktop PCs that also play games at Console settings and with DLSS too, you can make games look like they are running at much higher resolutions and even delivering more frames too all for around Current Console pricing. These also don't require a Sub just to play online, to unlock the full range of content and features and have more store fronts too.

Young gamers may start with a Steamdeck for example and be Computer literate so won't buy a Console, prefer the more 'open' nature of a PC, don't want a 'locked' box that can control what games you are allowed to play, even censoring some before they'll allow them on their platform. Consoles too are pricing themselves out of that Mass Market too and with Streaming and Steamdeck, as well as affordable PC's and handhelds, the Consoles may be too expensive to survive beyond the next gen - at least as the mainstream option. They could of course become the expensive option for the Hardcore affluent gamer but everyone else is playing on more affordable hardware and maybe even streaming more. Some games could still run locally but streaming in assets and Data.

I don't see Consoles as Sustainable - if each has 1TB of Storage and 24GB of RAM, 20m consoles is a LOT of Storage and RAM they need to source and distribute globally with rising costs inc the fuel to distribute. Increase the RAM by just 8GB, that's 160m GB of extra RAM for 20m consoles - let alone each individual chip, set of outputs, plastic boxes etc etc. I can see Consoles either being 'cheap' for streaming or retro gaming or extremely expensive just for the affluent dedicated gamer - much like available Record players today - cheap or extremely expensive for the Audiophile vinyl collector...
