I stepped back into the ballroom, pulse racing. The band was still playing, but my world had tilted. Nothing felt the same.

I was threading through the crowd toward the bar when I heard my name.

I turned to find Helen Gurley Brown, radiant in a sequined mask and velvet gown, a champagne coupe in hand. Of course she’d be here. If there was a room with power to be had and people worth dazzling, Helen would be in it.

“You look marvelous,” she said, studying me with a kind of knowing that felt almost maternal. “But something’s off. You’ve got that storm cloud look.”

I let out a laugh that came out thinner than I intended. “Is it that obvious?”

“Only to a woman who’s been exactly where you are,” she said, steering me toward a quieter alcove away from the crush of bodies. “So. Tell me. What’s on your mind?”

There was something in her tone, the mixture of curiosity and command, that cracked me open. Before I could stop my­self, the words tumbled out: Burt had refused me partnership. Years of loyalty, wins, and sacrifice, and still I was expected to be grateful for scraps.

Helen tilted her head, listening without interrupting. “Men hate sharing power. It’s practically coded into their DNA. I can’t say I’m surprised.” She touched my arm. “My offer still stands, you know. Come run features at Cosmopolitan. You’d thrive.”

The thought was more tempting than ever, but something about it still didn’t sit quite right, either. I didn’t want to inherit someone else’s table; I wanted to build my own.

“I appreciate it,” I said softly. “Truly. But I don’t think I want to be an editor.”

“Then don’t,” Helen said easily. “But don’t waste another de­cade making someone else rich while you do the work. If Burt won’t make room, build your own room. PR is your kingdom; you could start your own agency tomorrow if you wanted.”

The idea struck a nerve deep within me. Start my own. It was an audacious, reckless thought. Suddenly, though, even the champagne in my coupe seemed to fizz with anticipation.

Helen raised her glass to me. “To women who write their own stories.”

We clinked, and for the first time that evening, I felt a jolt of something close to joy.
