Allison Ellsworth Sold Poppi for $2 Billion. Now She’s Doing It Again.

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The prebiotic soda co-founder is back with a new hydration drink called Jetty.

In ELLE’s Office Hours series, we ask people in powerful positions to take us through their first jobs, worst jobs, and everything in between. This month, we talk to Allison Ellsworth, the co-founder and former chief brand officer at Poppi, the prebiotic soda company she built from the ground up with her husband, Stephen, and then sold to PepsiCo in 2025 for an incredible $2 billion.

The Ellsworths could have sailed off into the sunset to enjoy their success, but instead, the entrepreneurial-minded couple is doing it again. Today, they are launching Jetty, a new hydration beverage for real people, not athletes. They say it’s better for you, too, with no artificial colors, dyes, or sweeteners.

Below, Allison shares the personal inspiration behind both of her companies, the riskiest move she made, and why she was eager to start Jetty.

My first job was working at a dance studio, where I did everything from cleaning the toilets in the locker room and sweeping the floors to teaching classes and selling shoes. I started working from a really young age; I think I was 14 when I started at the studio. I was blessed to learn to do something that I absolutely loved, and there was a great community there as well. But also, it is very humbling at that age to have to be cleaning toilets and taking out the trash. So, I learned the basics in life early on, which I think every kid needs.

One of the worst jobs I had, even though I was awesome at it, was at a fitness company. I was No. 5 in the nation in sales—I absolutely killed it. But I remember why it was a bad experience, and that was because I didn’t have a good boss. Even though I was No. 5 in the nation and No. 1 in Texas, he tried to tell me I shouldn’t dress the way I dress and shouldn’t speak the way I spoke. And I was like, “But it’s working.” It was just his ego; he had to be right. And he’s the one that drove me away and made me quit, because if you don’t have a good leader, it’s really hard to be in a job even if you’re great at it and making good money.

Before starting Poppi, I worked in oil and gas research. Unfortunately, it’s not like the show The Landman with Billy Bob Thornton, which seems much more exciting than what I did. I used to live in teeny little towns and work on multimillion-dollar projects for oil companies, figuring out who owns mineral and surface rights and permits and things like that. I would live in these small towns for years, and I just love that that was my foundation because it taught me how to be really independent. I was out there by myself. You don’t have a boss out there telling you what to do. You are making a real impact and getting to know people and learning how they work.

I was a self-starter; I was doing that job when I was like 20. And it’s a very male-dominated industry. But it prepared me for the beverage industry, which is also very male-dominated. I mean, I had the largest exit of any female founder ever in beverage history. I’ve always just taken it as a challenge to play the game better.

I was on the road all the time for my oil and gas job, and when you’re traveling constantly, you don’t feel good. I had horrible tummy problems, so I started drinking apple cider vinegar, but I hated the way it tasted. So I went to my kitchen because I love to cook and I was like, “I got to make this taste good.” That first version was awful—let me just tell you—it was like rocket fuel. But I learned through trial and error. I would give it to my husband and neighbors until it got to where everyone loved it. There was a lot of googling and asking to hop on calls with people who had done it before. And then I took it on Shark Tank and got a deal.

When I went on Shark Tank, it was with the first product I created, which was called Mother Beverage. We had to figure out how to get our ingredients, how we would manufacture it, but also, like with any startup, how we were going to make money to put food on the table. We sold our cars—we sold everything; we maxed out our credit cards to buy bottles and start manufacturing. After Shark Tank, we were like, “Let’s take a beat.” We took nine months off and did a total rebrand into Poppi, and then launched it in March 2020, literally the first week of COVID. At the time, it felt very devastating, but it was the first time everybody in the world was thinking about their health and wellness—some people for the first time. They were reading the ingredients and thinking about boosting their immunity, like, “Maybe I shouldn’t be drinking soda with 40 grams of sugar.”

So we had triple-digit growth year over year. We were one of the fastest-growing beverages in the history of beverages. We went from zero to $500 million in 4.5 years, and then sold to Pepsi for $2 billion in March 2025. So it’s not been normal, and I’m very blessed, but I also think sometimes hard work looks like luck, but it was years of sacrifice to get it to that point. Everyone’s like, “Oh my gosh, that seemed so quick and easy.” And I’m like, “It wasn’t.”

There were so many moments, but I think a really big one that changed the whole trajectory of Poppi was that we were one of the first brands to get on TikTok. It sounds crazy now because everyone’s on there now, but it was unusual back in 2020. I remember everyone told me I was stupid to do it, and that no brands could be on there because all it was was dancing kids, and I said, “Yeah, but I think there’s something here.” So I said, “I will take my weekends and my nights, and I will do this by myself.” I did that for like three months, and no one cared because it wasn’t working. But then I sat down one night, and I told my story of how I started Poppi in my kitchen, and how I was nine months pregnant on Shark Tank, and it blew up and we did $100,000 on Amazon overnight. Grocery store shelves were cleared. We were trending on Google. So then everyone was like, “Oh, Alison, I guess your TikTok idea wasn’t the worst.” We ended up moving 80 percent of our digital advertising budget over to TikTok; it changed the whole trajectory of how we marketed. Now, being digital-first is a no-brainer, but it was pretty new six years ago; no brands were really doing it.

A really big career moment was when I realized that we were growing really fast at Poppi and that I might not be the right person to run our marketing anymore. As an entrepreneur, you don’t start a company to have people tell you what to do. But we were growing so fast and we were looking at buying a Super Bowl ad, and I was like, “I’ve never done any of this before.” So I went to the board and our CEO at the time and I said, “I think we should bring someone in to run marketing.” It’s a really vulnerable place to be, to ask for help. And not a lot of people will do that; they let their ego get in the way of growth. But they heard me and we brought someone in who ended up doing all the stuff that I wasn’t good at. It really was one of the best decisions, but it was really hard. I cried. It felt like a loss of control at the time. But it was a bold decision to ask for help, and I think a lot of people downplay how powerful that can be.

I really think it was really when we sold to Pepsi. When we were starting out, we always knew we wanted to sell the company. I know it can sound really harsh to be like, “We started a company to sell it,” but that’s not what I’m saying. In the beverage industry, we wanted to grow, as our mission was to revolutionize soda for the next generation. If you want to get your product everywhere, you have to sell to one of three companies: Keurig Dr. Pepper, Coca-Cola, or PepsiCo, because they have huge distribution. We would never be able to go everywhere on our own. We couldn’t be on college campuses; we couldn’t be in stadiums. We could never be at Subway, which we’re in now. We’re in Starbucks. All of these things would never have been options if we hadn’t sold. And we knew that day one, so it was always the goal. So before we sold, any time something big would happen, like we’d work with a celebrity or something, I’d say, “We’re not done yet.” I’m one of those people who never stops to smell the roses. I’m a Capricorn. I work 80 hours a week. I’m so hardcore, but finally when we sold, I was like, “Oh wow, we really built something incredible.”

It was an incredible moment of “I’ve made it,” but it was also a time of immense loss. Which everyone says to founders who exit, like, “Oh, poor baby,” but we worked for years to build something that involved so much sacrifice, blood, sweat, and tears that it’s hard to just hand it over like, “Here you go!” So, it was the highest high, like “Oh my gosh, we’ve made it. We created a generational brand.” But also, “Now what?”

Before you get acquired, you date for years. At first, all the big beverage brands were like, “You’re cute, but you will never disrupt us.” They were not paying attention to us. But when we got into Target, Kroger, and Walmart, they noticed us. When you hit $500 million in revenue, that is a really good time to start thinking about being acquired before you are too big to be bought.

Pepsi made an initial offer the year before we sold, but it didn’t feel right, so we said no. They offered $1 billion, but we knew with our numbers and our growth, it wasn’t a good deal. We knew we had something special—we were one of the fastest-growing beverages in history. You have to know your worth, but it was still so stressful to say no to $1 billion. But they watched us continue to grow; they watched our household penetration increase; we were gaining market share. And when they came back and gave us the second offer a year later, we knew it was really good, and it was easy to say yes. We talked about it for about three days and then accepted. And we closed six weeks later. So it was very quick in the end, because we’d been talking to them for years.

What I have learned since selling Poppi is that being retired at 39 is not a thing. My husband and I absolutely adore working together. He’s everything I’m not, and I’m everything he’s not when it comes to business and in life. I missed the build. I missed the team. I missed creating something culturally relevant and building a legacy—all of the above. I think it’d be crazy not to try again.

And what’s fun about doing it this time is I can do it with all the knowledge that I’ve gained. Also, the pressure to sell is off. But I do think I would still give everybody equity in the new company, with the goal of monetizing it, because that is still the American Dream. I saw it happen at Poppi. We made more than 44 people millionaires and changed people’s lives. They bought houses, paid off their cars, bought their mom’s houses, so many things. So continuing to be an entrepreneur is absolutely in the cards.

Poppi started because of my health, and Jetty is the same. Sometimes I will get to 11 A.M. or noon and be like, “Huh, I haven’t had any water today.” Maybe I will have had coffee or matcha, but I was just really struggling to hydrate. So we have created the first-ever “anti-hydration” hydration company. Traditionally, people have created hydration drinks for athletes; they call them sports drinks. Nobody has created approachable, everyday hydration for normal people. You’re not running a marathon; you didn’t just go to the gym for two hours; you need something not crazy overloaded with sugar and electrolytes. Everything’s just so intense in that space. Other products have dyes, artificial ingredients, 1,000 milligrams of sodium—they were giving me tummy problems. My husband said he would make me a better one, because he’s like, “We know how to do that.” And so he worked with one of our previous food scientists to develop it. One day he brought samples to the house and I was like, “Oh my gosh, this is so good.” I was drinking it every day, and he was like, “Do we need to share this with people?” And everyone’s like, “Guys, hello, you need to do this again.” And then I was like, “We can build again. We know what we’re doing. We know the recipe for success.”

So we created Jetty, a better-for-you electrolyte drink and powder. We’re going to have five flavors: fruit punch, watermelon, orange mango, black raspberry, and white cherry, and we will launch nationwide in Target, Whole Foods, and Sprouts. With Poppi, we had this mission to revolutionize soda for the next generation. And if we can disrupt soda, we can disrupt hydration too.
