What to Watch After Finishing <i><em>MobLand</em></i>

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Though season 2 of the much-loved crime series has come to an end, we've got some shows that'll fill the void.

MobLand season 2 has come to an end. (For now, at least—Tom Hardy and his motley crew have already started working on the third season.) Fortunately, there's an excellent selection of shows on Netflix that'll fill that gory Guy Ritchie–crime thriller–shaped void in your weekend watchlist. Here's our pick of the five best ones that you should stream now that season 2 is over. And if you haven’t yet, make sure to read our breakdown of MobLand’s explosive season 2 finale—and that cliffhanger.

The obvious choice, if you haven't seen it already, is the other Guy Ritchie series. The Gentlemen similarly revolves around a large mob family, has a lot of gore, and has the very same wicked sense of humor.

When an aristocratic army officer returns home after unexpectedly inheriting his family's estate instead of his older brother, he quickly discovers that it's at the heart of a notorious drug empire. While he initially tries to shrug it off, he quickly becomes more involved in the criminal underworld than he imagined he would ... and it seems it comes more naturally to him than he'd have hoped.

The Gentlemen is twisty, wildly entertaining, and will be the closest you'll get to something that feels like MobLand, even though the shows are narratively quite different.

Dept. Q is another Netflix original that packs a punch – adapted from the Department Q novel series, it's a crazy crime thriller that only has one season so far, so is easy to catch up on (though a second is on the way).

After a top-rated detective returns to work from an attack that left his partner paralyzed and another policeman dead, he finds himself caught up in the investigation into a cold case that completely takes hold of him.

The plot, the sharp sarcastic writing and the way the whole show looks are enough to make Dept. Q an excellent watch, but it's the cast (led by an outstandingly complex performance from Matthew Goode) that makes this one particularly special.

The spy thriller with the sharpest sense of humor, the most enthralling fight scenes—and, somehow amidst all the explosions—a whole lot of heart. It can be none other than Black Doves, Netflix's original espionage series that has quickly become one of the streamer's best-loved shows.

Helen Webb, a spy working for an organization called the Black Doves in an undercover marriage to a politician, has her position compromised, and mostly simple”'spy wife life” upended, when her lover is assassinated. In order to protect her and the secrecy of the Black Doves, her supervisor calls in an old friend to save her and sort out the mystery. However, the web of deceit proves unexpectedly complex and the three are soon in over their heads.

Other than being excellently acted (thanks to the legendary Keira Knightley, Ben Whishaw, Sarah Lancashire, and the supporting cast) and delightfully explosive, Black Doves is also surprisingly heartfelt, and you just can't help but root for its kooky cast. Even if they are all a bit nuts.

Once you're on board the Harlan Coben train, it's hard to hop off. I Will Find You, led by Sam Worthington and Severance's Britt Lower, is certainly one of the better adaptations of a Coben novel. Ridiculous, for sure, but undeniably entertaining the second you suspend your disbelief.

Five years after a man is wrongly imprisoned for the murder of his son, he receives information from his ex-wife's sister that his son might still be alive.

If you've read the book (or seen enough Coben shows that you know what's coming), then maybe you won't be quite as surprised by the twists and turns in this one. That being said, it's breezy, easy-watching, and it's intensely bingeable if you're into that kind of thriller.

Aside from the sense of humor, the thing we all love most about Guy Ritchie's films and others in that realm is the stylised action sequences. If that's what you'll be missing when MobLand is over, then give Gangs of London a watch.

Based on the video game of the same name (but fortunately much better), the series revolves around the power struggles of rival gangs across London, all of which have different allegiances and international connections. When one young undercover police officer is tasked with infiltrating one of them to exploit a moment of weakness, things get ... very messy. Very fast.

It's perhaps not as funny (read: not at all comedic) as some of the other entries on this list, but it's classically gritty, gory British crime drama at its finest, and a must-see for fans of the genre.
