5 Ways Warmth Has Shaped Interior Design in 2026, According to Experts

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Warmth is more than a palette—it’s the secret to a home you never want to leave.

After years of cool neutrals and minimalist interiors, there’s been a shift toward warmer, more inviting spaces that feel layered, personal, and lived-in.

“Warmth has become more than just a color palette or a particular material. It's about spaces that feel authentic, comfortable, and reflective of the people who live in them,” says Laura Gerber, founder of ODE Design.

At the end of the day, a warm home should feel inviting and effortless, with materials and details that make people want to spend time there. If you’re looking to add some warmth to your home, take inspiration from these designer-approved ideas.

Wood’s organic grain brings instant warmth to both modern and traditional homes, adding depth without making a space feel overly formal.

“We’re incorporating more white oak, walnut, and rift-cut cabinetry than we have in years, along with wood ceilings and paneled walls,” explains Rachel Simpson, senior interior designer at Revive Design and Renovation.

There’s been a shift from stark white countertops toward natural quartzite, travertine, and marble with warmer veining. These materials have natural variation and movement that bring character into a space.

“Honed finishes, in particular, feel softer and more organic than high-polish surfaces. They allow the beauty of the stone to speak for itself without feeling overly formal or glossy,” explains Simpson.

These days, people really want their homes to feel like a sanctuary.

“In a recent project, the client envisioned a grown-up treehouse nestled among the evergreens. We wanted to create a warm yet contemporary mountain retreat that felt luxurious but also comfortable and welcoming,” says Charles Greenfeld, founder and principal designer at Ethan Charles Design.

Rather than relying on one or two dramatic statement pieces, the interiors were designed to be experienced as a whole, with each detail contributing to that warm aesthetic.

Creating warmth is really about creating layers of thoughtfully curated design elements. This helps to create a home you want to enter and never leave.

“Well-proportioned rugs, textures on walls, loads of art, draperies, and lighting overhead and at eye level are just some of the things we can add to our spaces to make them feel cozy and accessible,” says Ashley Powell, cofounder and interior designer of Roseberry Allen.

Designers have lately been drawn to rooms where the colors are close to one another, but the materials do different things.

“A woven rug, a textured wall, and a piece of wood with a visible grain each catch the light differently,” says Jess Klein, founder and principal of Jess Klein Studio.

That variation adds visual depth and makes the room feel warmer and more inviting, even when the color palette is understated.
