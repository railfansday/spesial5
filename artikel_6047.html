I've never flown with Hawaiian Airlines, yet the Hawaiian Airlines® World Elite Mastercard® is the latest addition to my wallet. With Hawaiian and Alaska Airlines sharing the same loyalty program, Atmos Rewards, the Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard is an attractive option for anyone who regularly flies with either airline.

This card offers common airline-associated perks, but like many cobranded airline cards, it will likely prove hard to justify if you aren't flying at least a couple of times each year with Alaska or Hawaiian. That said, if you're able to properly take advantage of its perks, it can offer a lot to boost your travels. Card rating*: ⭐⭐⭐

The information for the Hawaiian Airlines World Elite Mastercard has been collected independently by The Points Guy. The card details on this page have not been reviewed or provided by the card issuer.

The $99-per-year Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard comes with some of the benefits you'd expect out of a mid-tier airline card, including:

You won't find lounge access or statement credits on this card, but that simplicity may be welcome for those tired of needing a spreadsheet to help them justify a card's annual fee.

Living in the Pacific Northwest, I often fly Alaska Airlines domestically. As someone who unsuccessfully tried to get the earlier this year, I'm happy that my Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard offers perks on both Hawaiian and Alaska. They may not be as comprehensive as those offered by the Summit, but this card is substantially cheaper.

Receiving two free checked bags isn't too common for a mid-tier card, so I appreciate this perk. But it's important to note that free checked bags with this card are subject to a few restrictions:

If you're traveling with a companion, you could check their bag as your own bag to navigate around this card only allowing the primary cardholder free checked bags.

I face a $45 checked bag fee for my first bag and a $55 fee for my second when flying Alaska within the continental U.S. and Alaska. Even as someone who usually only checks one bag, I just need to do so on one round-trip with Alaska to almost entirely cover this card's annual fee.

Aside from that, using the full $100 annual companion discount on a trip you’d otherwise book can offset the $99 annual fee. New cardholders also receive a one-time 50%-off companion discount. Both discounts can be used on eligible round-trip Main Cabin travel between Hawaii and North America on either airline.

I wish the Hawaiian Airlines card had priority boarding, since overhead bin space can be hard to come by and I'm not a fan of gate-checking my bag. Still, it isn't a deal-breaker for me given this card's annual fee is easy to at least break even on.

This card has no foreign transaction fees and offers standard travel protections like trip cancellation/interruption and travel accident insurance.

The Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard earns Atmos Rewards points, which is the loyalty currency both Hawaiian Airlines and Alaska Airlines use. It earns:

Through Dec. 31, you'll earn 50% bonus points on every dollar spent if you're a resident of Hawaii (up to 5,000 points).

With this card, you'll also earn 1 Atmos Rewards status point per $3 spent, with no cap. You won't get an elite status boost at the start of the qualification year, but this card can help you close the gap to elite status if you're close.

The best way to use your Atmos Rewards points is for flights on Hawaiian and Alaska or with partner airlines, such as American Airlines. Alaska Airlines in particular offers a lot of value for your points, especially on short-haul domestic flights.

Recently, I redeemed 5,000 Atmos Rewards points (plus $5.60 in taxes and fees) for a flight between Portland International Airport (PDX) and Spokane International Airport (GEG). Given the cash price was $184, I received over 3.5 cents per point in value (after taking taxes and fees into consideration). That's more than double TPG's November 2026 valuation of Atmos Rewards points at 1.6 cents per point.

It's this combined loyalty program that allows me to justify holding this card despite never setting foot on a Hawaiian flight and having no current plans to do so. It's hard to earn Atmos Rewards points, since they currently only partner with Bilt as a transfer partner. Without an Alaska Airlines-associated card, the Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard is my best bet for earning points I can use with this program.

As an alternative to redeeming your points for flights, you can also use them for Alaska Vacations packages and Alaska Airlines-curated experiences.

As I mentioned previously, the Hawaiian Airlines card lacks priority boarding and a comprehensive elite status boost at the start of the year. In ways like this, it's clear this card hasn't received a refresh recently, unlike many other popular airline cards.

That means this card may fall short of expectations if you're anticipating a card with well-rounded airline perks. The inability to use this card's free checked bag perk with companions is a non-issue if you're only traveling with one person and two bags, but it becomes a larger limitation if you're traveling with many bags or a group.

If you fly at least a couple of times each year with Hawaiian or Alaska and don't have a cobranded Atmos Rewards card, I believe this card is more than likely worth it. Despite lacking several perks we've come to expect from airline cards these days, it's still quite easy to get outsize value from the $99 annual fee.

Currently, new Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard cardholders can earn up to 70,000 bonus points:

According to TPG's valuations, this welcome offer is worth up to $1,120. In an era when many welcome offers ask for thousands in spending, it's refreshing to see a valuable one that only requires $2,000 in total spending.

Even if you're on a limited budget, this should be an attainable welcome offer. I recommend applying now, given how much this bonus is worth and how easy it is to earn.

Just remember that Barclays can be inquiry-sensitive. If you've opened six or more cards in the past 24 months, you may have a hard time getting approved.

I'm happy I chose to add the Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard to my wallet. Between two free checked bags and the annual $100 companion discount, I expect I'll be able to easily get outsize value against the $99 annual fee each year.

While this card offers significant value to the right traveler, if you don't fly with either Alaska or Hawaiian at least a couple of times each year, you will likely struggle to find a lot of value in this card. If you're an Alaska Airlines loyalist and you qualify for a Bank of America card, I'd suggest the instead.

That said, if you fly at least occasionally with Hawaiian or Alaska, or you want to earn Atmos Rewards points to use with partner airlines, I recommend giving this card serious consideration.
