For kids with even the slightest interest in cars, trucks, tractors, or fire engines, an electric ride-on is a gift that can transform a childhood. Yes, they can be pricey—but at this point, you might pay gold to have some quiet time in the house. If your kid is ready to take to the not-so-open road, here’s what to look for in an electric car for kids.

While it can be tempting to get a ride-on that your child will grow into and use for years, there are safety risks involved with choosing one that’s too large or powerful for your kid to control easily.

Most electric ride-on cars feature speeds between 1.5 and 5 mph, but the more powerful you go, the higher that top speed climbs. Check the manufacturer’s recommendations for age and weight. Some even offer parental remote controls and seatbelts, while others let you remove features such as side panels as your child grows.

The biggest concern safety-wise with these toys is not so much the product itself but where the children take them. Hilly terrain can cause them to flip, and bodies of water aren’t meant to be driven through. It’s also very important to convey that these aren’t meant to be taken near a road, as their small profile can be difficult to see by drivers of normal-size vehicles.

Electric ride-ons come in multiple styles to match kids’ varying interests. Classic cars and trucks mimic real-life vehicles, offering features such as working headlights and sound effects for a realistic experience. ATVs and UTVs are built for rougher terrain with “all-terrain” tires and higher clearances. For kids who love motorcycles, bike-style ride-ons can often include training wheels for added stability.

Most electric ride-ons are powered by rechargeable batteries, typically ranging from six to 24 volts. Higher voltage generally translates to more power and a longer run time, but it’s important to match the power to the terrain and age level.

Models with 12-volt batteries provide a good balance of speed and battery life for mixed-terrain use, while 24-volt versions can handle tougher trails and inclines. Be mindful that all batteries will gradually lose capacity, so check for compatible replacement options if you plan to use the ride-on for years to come.

Interactive features can make a big difference in the overall experience. Some ride-ons include Bluetooth speakers or FM radios, letting kids listen to their favorite songs while driving. Others offer realistic details such as functional doors, working headlights and taillights, and engine sound effects.
