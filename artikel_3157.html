Social Security Is Getting Its Largest COLA Since 2023 Next Year. Why It Likely Won't Be Enough for Many Seniors.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

A benefit boost is on the way, but it might not go as far as you'd expect.

By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 21, 2026 at 5:04AM EST

There's good news and bad news for seniors on Social Security. The 2027 cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will be the largest since 2023 at 3.6%, reflecting the high inflation Americans have faced throughout the year. This should add $75 to the average Social Security check (as of October 2026).

But despite the above-average increase, many seniors will likely still have to make some tough budgeting decisions for next year. And it all has to do with the way the government calculates Social Security COLAs.

The Social Security COLA formula looks at changes in average third-quarter inflation data from one year to the next. To do this, it looks at the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) for July, August, and September of the current year and the previous one.

The problem with this is that the CPI-W focuses on urban households in which at least 50% of household income comes from wages and at least one person has worked for at least 37 of the last 52 weeks. This rules out most retiree households by default.

They're tracked through a separate index known as the Consumer Price Index for the Elderly (CPI-E). And the numbers are often quite different from the CPI-W. That's because senior spending habits typically look different from their younger counterparts'. Seniors often spend more on healthcare, for example, which can cause their average expenses to rise faster than those of younger adults.

Many have been calling for the Social Security Administration to start calculating COLAs using the CPI-E, which they claim is fairer to seniors. It would also result in larger COLAs in most years. However, that's not the case for 2027.

If the government were to calculate next year's COLA using the CPI-E, seniors would only be in for a 3.4% benefit boost. That would only increase the $2,092 average monthly retirement benefit as of October 2026 by $71 per month.

In this case, the CPI-W will give the typical senior an extra $4 per month, but this is unlikely to make much of a difference. The program's buying power has been slowly eroding for decades, and it will take more than a few extra dollars to reverse this trend.

That's why organizations like The Senior Citizens League have called on the government to issue a one-time $1,400 benefit increase to make up for the loss of buying power over the last several decades. However, there are no current plans to make this happen.

That means it's up to seniors to figure out how to bridge the gap. If you have personal savings, this is your best bet. But if that's not enough, you might have to explore alternatives, like a job or other government benefits.
