Santa appeared 70,778 times in 2026’s breached passwords, Specops finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Specops analysed more than 260 million breached passwords from 2026 and found holiday terms more than 242,000 times. Every one of those passwords had already leaked in a breach.

“Santa” turned up 70,778 times in passwords exposed this year, according to new research from Specops, the Swedish password security company owned by Outpost24. That is more than 25 times as often as “christmas”, which appeared 2,796 times.

The analysis, published today on the Specops blog, covered more than 260 million breached passwords from 2026. Specops told TNW they came from a mix of honeypots, infostealer logs and public breach dumps.

The research team searched for 26 holiday and seasonal terms. Together, they appeared more than 242,000 times. The counts are total occurrences, and they include predictable variations such as “Chr1sTmas” and “sN0w”, Specops said.

After “santa” came “snow” (45,671) and “noel” (45,026). The short form “xmas” appeared 7,481 times, more than twice as often as the full word.

Month names were common too. “November” appeared 12,838 times and “december” 10,037 times, ahead of “advent” (8,722) and “merry” (4,378). Other Christmas words were far rarer: “rudolph” appeared 999 times, “reindeer” 371 and “mistletoe” 73. Shopping terms barely registered, with 195 uses of “blackfriday” and seven of “cybermonday”.

Specops notes that not every hit is a festive choice. “Snow”, “noel” and “november” all have meanings unrelated to the holidays.

“Santa appearing more than 70,000 times might raise a smile, but it shows how often familiar words find their way into passwords. Holidays provide plenty of inspiration for people looking for something memorable, and attackers know it,” said Darren James, senior product manager at Specops Software, in a statement.

Cracking tools test dictionaries of common words along with predictable tweaks, such as capital letters, swapped characters and numbers. Adding “123” or an exclamation mark can satisfy a complexity rule without making a password much harder to crack, the company said.

Every password in the study had already leaked, and Specops says that is the bigger risk. Attackers can use those passwords directly. In credential stuffing, they try stolen logins on other accounts where people have reused them.

Old credentials keep doing damage. Attackers cracked 75,000 Fortinet firewalls this year using old passwords, not a zero-day. Earlier this month, TNW reported that infostealers had reached 72% of firms.

Earlier in 2026, Specops found that 19% of passwords compromised in a single month met typical enterprise complexity requirements.

“A password can meet every complexity requirement and still put an organization at risk if it has already been compromised,” James said.

Specops sells tools that block breached passwords in Active Directory. Alongside the research, it added more than 460 million compromised passwords to its Breached Password Protection database, which now holds more than 6.5 billion.

A separate list behind Password Auditor, the company’s free audit tool, gained more than 4.6 million records and now holds more than 1.1 billion. TNW has reported that the passwordless future is still years away for most businesses.
