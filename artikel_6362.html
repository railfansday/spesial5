Getting pet hair out of carpets and off of tile and hardwood is a constant battle many are familiar with, and trying to remove those persistent hairs that have embedded themselves into your upholstery is another beast. Choosing the right vacuum can significantly improve your cleaning routine if you’re a cat or canine parent.

The top pet hair vacuums are designed with powerful suction and superior filtration (plus, they come with special attachments) to capture fine hair, thick fur, and pet dander. Regardless of whether you have a fluffy cat, a long-haired pup, or both, investing in a vacuum specifically made for pets will help you keep messes in check for a tidier home.

There are a few factors that make a vacuum well-suited for dog and cat fur—namely, how much hair its bin holds, how it empties, how well it filters fine particles, and the types of attachments included.

The ideal bin capacity depends on the size of your home’s dimensions and the amount of pet hair you usually manage on a regular basis. For households with multiple pets, or animals that shed heavily, a vacuum with a spacious bin capacity (one liter or more) reduces the number of times you have to empty it during cleaning.

If you have a smaller home or you just have one pet, a bin capacity of 0.5 to 1 liter will most likely suffice. If you’re still unsure of what you might need, here’s a breakdown of the bin capacity each type of vacuum typically offers.

These normally have the largest capacity, ranging from two to four liters to hold more pet hair, dander, and debris before needing to be emptied. This design and capability could be ideal for heavy-duty cleaning or households with more than one pet.

Upright vacuums come in a close second, often with a capacity between one and three liters, so they’re a good option for folks with a pet that sheds mildly, or only during part of the year. These vacuums are also sufficient for standard home cleaning.

Because cordless vacuums are better used as supplementary cleaning tools, they have much smaller dustbins—usually ranging between 0.3 and 0.7 liters—which is really only enough for spot-cleaning, picking up dust bunnies between deep cleaning sessions, and use in tight spaces.

Similar to cordless vacuums, robot vacuums live on the smaller end of bin capacity. If you decide to go with this type, I recommend a model with a self-emptying bin. Most robot vacuums have small onboard dustbins, but they fill up faster. Self-emptying models have a larger dust collection area in the base, so you won’t have to manually empty as frequently.

For allergy sufferers, it’s essential to have a vacuum with an excellent filtration system, so look for models with a HEPA filter, or “high-efficiency particulate air” filter. These filters trap 99.97 percent of particles that are 0.3 microns or larger in size. Pet dander can be as small as 2.5 microns in diameter, and a HEPA filter efficiently captures these particles along with pollen, dust, and other allergens your pets may have tracked into the house.

A fully sealed filtration system, sometimes called an anti-allergen seal, is another common feature on pet vacuums. This means the components of the vacuum are tightly sealed so fine particles can’t escape back into your home once they’ve been sucked up.

Powerful suction is vital, but the included tools and attachments are often what pet owners use to successfully pull hair out of upholstery and carpeting. The following attachments are useful for tackling pet hair, and they come with many of the models I recommend above.

A standard attachment that comes with almost all vacuums, these elongated, narrow tools are great for accessing tight areas, like behind the toilet or between couch cushions. They’re useful for cleaning up clumps of hair that may have gathered in corners of your home where the vacuum’s floor head cannot reach.

Non-motorized upholstery brushes have bristles or a strip of microfiber to clean off furniture. As you pull the tool across fabric, the bristles lift pet hair and dust to be sucked up through the vacuum hose.

For pet hair that’s worked deep into upholstery, whether on your furniture or in your car, a motorized pet tool provides the most lifting power. These tools have small, spinning brush rolls, usually equipped with bristles, that agitate hair to pull it from the fabric, and they tend to work much more efficiently than non-motorized tools.

If you have pet hair or dander on your window screens or baseboards, a dusting brush has long, soft bristles that pick up dust and fine particles without scratching surfaces.
