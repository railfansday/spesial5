6 Antique Trends Designers Are Watching for 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Above: The kitchen in designer Phillip Thomas’s Bellport retreat.

Do the words “antique” and “trend” really belong in the same sentence? In many ways, a great antique shop feels like the antidote to trend fatigue: a space where the true must-haves are hidden in dusty corners, with part of the fun in the thrill of the hunt. Yet, as we round out 2026, there’s no denying the current design cycle is casting off perfection for patina. Antiques are en vogue, and top designers are keeping a close eye on the periods, pieces, and yes, trends, that are poised to find new life in 2027.

“The return of interest in antiques reflects a trend towards more personal, unique interiors, and away from strict modern rooms which can often be cool and generic,” says ELLE Decor A-List designer Jamie Drake. This shift paves the way for more playful interactions between pieces, unexpected layering, and unabashed personality.

Other experts second the motion for more character. “I’m seeing clients really drawn to antiques with more to them than meets the eye: pieces that have a bit of humor or whimsy, or pieces with great provenance,” says ELLE Decor Rising Star William Cullum. (He lists a “ridiculously giant butterfly light fixture” from New Orleans, rattan chairs from Bing Crosby’s estate, and a disco ball from Cyndi Lauper’s Bonham’s sale in his list of recent acquisitions—professional and personal.)

Luckily for the amateur antiquer, not all trending vintage styles require a substantial space commitment or glamorous provenance—although an interesting backstory helps. “Antiques are always about a story: where you found them, who owned them, and how that story will continue on after us,” Cullum says.

Ahead, we asked top designers and experts to share the most inspiring antique trends of the moment. From neoclassic mirrors to hand-painted chests paired with modern art, their takes are sure to inform your next estate sale rummage, or inspire you to restyle an old heirloom.

We called mirrored surfaces a major trend to watch for fall 2026, and antique trends have followed suit. Unexpected (or better yet, artfully manipulated) reflections can add rich dimension and movement to any space, but in this category, antiques create a window to the past that’s tough to beat.

“One of the easiest ways to add a sense of history to a room is to include an antique mirror,” says Drake. “Whether you choose a sober and strict neo-classic piece or a more flamboyant baroque one, reflections of the past add depth and sparkle.”

“One trend I’m observing is an uptick in antique dowry chests,” antiques dealer Ali Mahon says. From Pennsylvania Dutch to Swedish Folk and Spanish carved, she’s noticed clients gravitating toward more delicately adorned storage over spare. With thoughtfully mixed furniture (from style to period) becoming more desirable than matching sets, Mahon sees more hand-painted antique surfaces in our future: “With their decorative tulips, leaves, birds, and hearts, dowry chests create a colorful splash in any room.”

ELLE Decor Rising Star Reagan Gail Struble agrees—although delicate designs are not a requirement of the trend, in her book: “Something about a playful wash of color on an awesome old piece of furniture is very alluring, especially when you can see the age and texture of the wood peeking through,” she says. “Hand-painted case pieces add a little punch of charm to any room.”

Luckily, these antiques can be just as functional as they are fashionable. Mahon recommends using a painted chest as a coffee table, at the foot of the bed, or in a space that could benefit from a bit more romance (and a few extra drawers). When styling your find in a modern space, Struble says that contrast is key: try a “chippy Swedish chest of drawers” with an unexpected photograph or painting above it.

According to ELLE Decor A-List designer Nick Olsen, Art Deco pieces are setting the tone for 2027 antique trends—although, if you ask him, they were never out. “I'm particularly inspired by the [art deco influence in the] Gujarat palace in India,” he says. “I'm fascinated by how every element seems to have a wide border: marble floors, carpets, walls of veneer and mohair velvet upholstery.”

According to Olsen, using Art Deco design compositions can do as much for a room as actual pieces from the period. “I like the idea of contrasting borders: solid textures punctuated by smaller graphic prints on furniture and more delicate lighting,” he explains. And while borders are easy to create with paint, paneling, and carpet seaming, Olsen challenges clients to push the Art Deco agenda further by bordering with luxe materials.

Whether you’re new to antiquing or simply trying to add more character to a space, lighting is an excellent place to start. “Lighting is the jewelry of the home, so it’s one of my favorite places to bring in something vintage,” says A-Lister Corey Damen Jenkins. “An Art Deco sconce or an antique brass lantern has a warmth to it that’s hard to find in something brand new.”

Adding to an existing space? Jenkins advises starting small: “Swap one overhead fixture or put a pair of vintage lamps on a console, and you’ll be surprised how much the whole room shifts,” he says. “It’s an easy way to add history without starting over.”

“In light of America’s 250th birthday, Americana in general is in vogue,” says Mahon. Among her clients, the trend has manifested in quilts and textiles, portraits of sea captains, trade signs, and ironstone—culminating in an aesthetic she describes as “elevated country—not corny.”

If you’ve noticed that no particular design period seems to reign supreme among designers’ top antique trend picks, it’s not an accident. Heading into 2027, there’s a strong argument to be made that mixed-period design is more influential than any singular reference.

“I love a room that doesn’t belong to any one decade,” Jenkins says. “A nineteenth-century chest with a 1970s lamp on top, or a Lucite chair pulled up to an antique desk. Those are the pairings that make people stop and look twice.” Drake adds that seemingly out-of-place seating is an easy way to add intrigue. “I love including antique chairs as sculptural notes, contrasted against modern upholstery and tables,” he says.

But when it comes to antiquing, there’s really only so much one can plan for. Letting a great find lead the way often turns out to be a better strategy than arriving to an estate sale with a rigid checklist. The best way to keep up with top designers when it comes to antiquing this year? Find a piece that has something to say, place it where it seems to belong, and then try it where it doesn’t.
