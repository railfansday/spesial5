State law caps what you can charge as a notary for each notarization, at $10 or less in most states, so notarizing alone pays little. To earn more, charge travel fees for mobile appointments and qualify as a loan signing agent, since a loan signing typically pays $50 to $200.

A routine notarization takes 5 to 10 minutes, and its fee can’t exceed $2 per signer under New York law or $10 per act under Florida law.

No state caps a signing agent’s fee, because the job is more than a notarization. Lenders, title companies, and signing services hire you as a contractor to print the loan documents, meet the borrowers, notarize their signatures, and send the package back. The National Notary Association says the signing itself takes 45 minutes to a few hours, not counting travel.

At a signing, send every question about the loan’s terms to the loan officer, since a notary who isn’t a lawyer can’t give legal advice.

Fees for notarizing are taxable income, but the IRS’s Schedule SE instructions leave them out of self-employment tax, the Social Security and Medicare tax on work you do for yourself. That exemption covers the notarizations alone, so travel fees and the rest of a signing fee count as self-employment income. Record each signing’s notarization fees apart from the rest of its pay, and set aside part of every payment for tax.
