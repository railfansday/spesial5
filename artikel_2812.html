Ravens Player Marlon Humphrey Apologizes for Saying Baltimore Is ‘Best at Making Criminals,’ as Mayor Says It’s ‘Not Enough’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Baltimore Ravens player Marlon Humphrey made a comment saying the city is "best at making criminals" in a now-deleted episode of his podcast, The Marlon Humphrey Show

He then apologized for the comments, saying, "I do know the effect that words have"

But Baltimore Mayor Brandon Scott criticized the apology, saying, "I wonder how [Humphrey] would feel if prominent Baltimoreans used their platforms to imply that Baltimore once produced great defensive backs but we have recently lost our way"

Baltimore Mayor Brandon Scott denounced Ravens player Marlon Humphrey's recent apology after his disparaging comments about the Maryland city.

Humphrey discussed Baltimore's contributions to the U.S., including National Bohemian beer and "The Star-Spangled Banner," before remarking on the city's crime rate on the now-deleted Wednesday, Nov. 18 episode of his podcast, The Marlon Humphrey Show.

"They're best at making criminals," the 30-year-old NFL player said, according to ESPN.

Humphrey, who was drafted by the Ravens in 2017 and has played for them throughout his 10-year NFL career, apologized for his remarks to journalists after practice on Wednesday, Nov. 18, The Athletic reports. "I would like to apologize for some comments on my podcast I had to the city of Baltimore," Humphrey said. "As you guys know, I've been here my whole career."

The player, who is playing the final year of his $98.75 million five-year contract, continued: "Spent lots of offseasons here, welcomed a son here. I'm an investor in the city, an investor in different areas [and] parts of the state. Obviously, I do know the effect that words have, and just would like to apologize for those comments."

But Humphrey was met with disappointed Baltimore residents, including the city's mayor, Brandon Scott, who issued a statement on Instagram. Scott, a Baltimore native elected in 2020 and reelected in 2024, criticized the athlete's apology, saying, "A verbal apology is not enough."

"Baltimore is a city full of young Black boys who look to the Ravens as a beacon of light in their lives and to have someone that looks like them who wears a Baltimore Ravens jersey relegate them to becoming criminals cuts deeper," Scott, 42, wrote.

Mayor Brandon Scott in Baltimore, Maryland, on September 05, 2025.Credit: Marvin Joseph/The Washington Post via Getty

"I wonder how he would feel if prominent Baltimoreans used their platforms to imply that Baltimore once produced great defensive backs but we have recently lost our way," Scott added. "They wouldn't do that because our team and city are supposed to be united as one. Baltimore versus the world."

He then invited the football player to join him to speak with other Baltimore citizens "who have re-entered into our community and are now preventing others from making the mistakes they made."

In addition to the mayor's comments, Humphrey's coach, Jesse Minter, reiterated that he loves Baltimore.

"Just speaking for myself, I love this city," Minter said, ESPN reports. "I know what this city means to me and my family. I take a lot of pride in being here. It's a great city with a bunch of great people. We desperately want to represent this city the right way by how we play most importantly."
