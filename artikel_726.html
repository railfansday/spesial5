England's All Blacks Tests 'World Cup practice' | 'Ireland still not contenders'

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Final weekend of Nations Championship takes place at Allianz Stadium; Ireland vs Australia on Friday (8.10pm), England vs New Zealand on Saturday (4.40pm), France vs South Africa in final on Sunday (4.40pm); coaches Steve Borthwick, Andy Farrell and Dave Rennie speak to media

Head coaches Steve Borthwick of England, Andy Farrell of Ireland and Dave Rennie of New Zealand speak to media ahead of the final weekend of the inaugural Nations Championship.

England, having come into the tournament under a cloud of pressure following the worst Six Nations display in their history earlier this year (four losses, fifth place), have stormed to victories over Australia, Japan and New Zealand this month.

Last week, Borthwick's side secured a record 25-point victory over the All Blacks at Allianz Stadium. With England finishing second behind France in the northern hemisphere standings and New Zealand runners-up to South Africa in the southern hemisphere table, the two sides are now set for an immediate rematch.

England face the All Blacks again at Twickenham on Saturday, with Borthwick viewing the latest clash as valuable preparation for the Rugby World Cup in Australia in 10 months' time.

"The nature of this tournament, it's a new tournament, is the understanding that international games have that level of jeopardy," Borthwick told the media on Wednesday.

"The consequence upon each of these games. This is an important step.

"It's a real positive step in this competition towards the jeopardy of being involved in World Cup games.

"In the World Cup, you want to be in situations where you're having to back up big games in multiple weeks.

"I think it's going to be an excellent Test match this weekend, a really tough Test match against a super dangerous New Zealand team.

"We're expecting New Zealand to come hard at the breakdown, and we're expecting them to really challenge us there.

"All our focus this weekend is we're expecting the very best of New Zealand. They're full of so much talent and so much experience. We know this is going to be a fierce Test match.

"We've got a young squad. We've got some players that are very experienced at the end of the spectrum and a lot of players that are young and haven't played against teams like New Zealand that often. So I think it was an important step in our development.

"This Saturday is another very important step. Each and every international experience we have is vital for the long-term development of this team."

Ireland head coach Andy Farrell began this Test window by telling the media that, unlike in 2023, this current Ireland side - based on performances to date - are not Rugby World Cup contenders.

What has followed this month is dominant victories over Argentina and Fiji in Dublin, followed by a stunning 20-17 success over double world champions South Africa last week.

Ireland face Australia at Twickenham on Friday, but Farrell reiterated his point that Ireland are not in World Cup-title conversations.

"No [Ireland are not World Cup contenders]," he said. "We took a step in the right direction last week, but that's got to continue to be that, hasn't it? To make sure that we keep feeling that way.

"That's always going to be the challenge [emotional challenge a week after win over Springboks].

"Plus, we have a six-day turnaround, and we play Scotland in the World Cup with a six-day turnaround as well. Getting yourself back up, that's the way you get some consistency in your performance, so it's pretty important.

"You are testing your depth, and you're always going to get that with four quality games over such a short space of time. But the Six Nations is five games, and then the World Cup is seven games if you've got any ambition, really.

"It's not ideal [shortage at No 8], but again, these are things that happen over a tournament, and that's what we've found this year.

"Obviously, from the start having so many experienced players in Caelan Doris and Jack Conan out - played plenty of times for us - and then we've lost a few as we've gone on.

"But you've got to be able to adapt with the squad that you've got, and you've been able to do that so far.

"It is what it is [Dan Sheehan's two-Test ban]. He had a fair hearing, and we move on."

New Zealand head coach Dave Rennie has overseen a first All Blacks defeat to Wales since 1953 and a record loss to England this month.

Such results have led to huge pressure and reports he should be sacked.

"I don't listen to anything. I don't read anything, so that probably helps," Rennie said.

"Look, it's the nature of the job, and it's the expectations of this role too.

"But international rugby now is really tight. You look at the recent results, and I think four years ago heading into a World Cup, Ireland and France were ranked No 1 and No 2, so things can change really quickly.

"It wasn't that long ago South Africa and us were No 1 and No 2. We're well aware of how tough it is, and England are a really good side.

"But we believe if we get our game right, we'll be competitive at the weekend.

"We're just excited to wear the jersey again and put on a performance that we're proud of and the country's proud of. We'll talk about fatigue next week.

"You want it to hurt, because we're all hurt after the weekend.

"Sunday's still a bit of a struggle, but you can't afford to sulk because you've got to look forward and think: 'How do we fix things?'

"Our job is to try and bring energy and connection and we're a really tight group. We want to get a result on the weekend for us, but also for the country.

"We're really appreciative of all the support we've got, especially after last week's performance. We're determined to rectify that."
