There’s Joe Budden, and then there’s everyone else. Despite the proliferation of rapper-led podcasts and talk shows in the last few years (itself a subset of the ever-ballooning celebrity podcast bubble), there’s no question who leads the pack—at least that’s how Budden sees it. It’s hard to argue with him: in February, Budden’s self-titled and pioneering podcast will turn 12,. Not too long ago, he posted a screenshot that “accidentally” revealed his yearly take-home was a number in the low eight figures.

Through it all, there've been dramatic cast changes, tense moments with rappers, hilarious must-see breakdowns of big moments in culture, and several dust-ups with Drake, a frequent subject of Budden and his co-host’s. Being part-New Jerseyian, I’m one of the vocal minority that believe it’s a travesty to reduce Budden’s rap career to “Pump It Up” (this is never far from my Recently Played to this day). Our paths actually crossed during his time at Complex, when I served as a sit-in for test episodes of Everyday Struggle while he searched for the co-host that would eventually become Akademiks—and even helped them launch the show with a series of digital short skits I wrote and produced that are probably lost to the internet by now.

As an occasional pod listener, I find it fascinating that Budden has defied all odds, and everyone who said he was bound to burn bridges, and now finds himself commanding attention, revenue and even reconciliations with his past employers. Despite how things went with State of the Culture, he’s back in business with Revolt. Joe Budden Network Presents, his new weekly series, premieres on November 13th on its linear network. To mark his next chapter, we talked about the current landscape of rap media and podcasting, the dangers of getting too comfortable, and how he plans to maintain his reign.
