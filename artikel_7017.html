DOJ sues Penn State over in-state tuition for undocumented students

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Let Higher Ed Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The federal agency appears to be turning its sights on individual colleges after filing lawsuits over all such policies at the state level.

A Penn State spokesperson on Friday said the university is reviewing the DOJ’s complaint.

Undocumented students at Penn State are eligible to pay in-state tuition if they attended a Pennsylvania high school for at least four years and graduated with either a high school degree or a GED in the state, among other requirements.

In 2026-27, Penn State’s sticker price for in-state tuition at its main campus is $20,878. That’s less than half of what it charges out-of-state students, $44,574.

As in its state lawsuits, the DOJ argued in court documents that Penn State’s policy illegally provides benefits to undocumented students that are not available to all U.S. citizens.

Pennsylvania has not faced such a DOJ lawsuit as it does not offer in-state tuition to undocumented students at the state level.

Under federal immigration law, undocumented students cannot receive postsecondary education benefits based on state residency “unless a citizen or national of the United States” is also eligible regardless of residency. Some states fighting back against the DOJ in court have argued that their tuition residency policies apply equally to both citizens and U.S. residents regardless of legal status.
