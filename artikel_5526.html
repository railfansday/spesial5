Mortgage Mix: MBA Petitions Senators for ‘Take Care of America’s Veterans Act’ Changes; Newrez Widens Inventory

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Editor’s Note: The Mortgage Mix is RISMedia’s highlight reel of need-to-know mortgage-industry happenings. Watch for it every other Friday afternoon.

The numbers: Despite mortgage rates’ downward trend for the past few weeks, the latest Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey® reveals that the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage increased sharply to 7.28%, up seven basis points from the previous survey’s result of 7.11%.

The 15-year fixed-rate mortgage also increased to 6.66%, up from last week’s 6.45%. Both the 30-year and 15-year rates are more than a full percentage point higher than those from the same period last year.

What does this mean: Economists generally attributed the spike in rates to inflation-related pressures, stemming from global conflicts and the rise in overall prices caused by it.

Realtor.com® Senior Economist Anthony Smith referenced the October Consumer Price Index (CPI) report as his basis for why the market expects another rate hike in the upcoming Federal Reserve December meeting.

“The October CPI report came in above expectations on both the headline and core measures, cementing market expectations for a December rate hike and giving the 10-year little room to fall,” he said.

The CPI report saw an all-items index increase of 0.4%, which is a slight decrease from September’s increase of 0.6%, but was still described as “hotter than expected,” by Navy Federal Chief Economist Heather Long.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh kept his cards close to his chest at his Nov. 16 speech and did not reveal whether a rate hike is on the table, but Fed Governor Michelle Bowman did note her disappointment at the central bank’s progress towards lowering inflation.

She stated that inflation has “proven to be more persistent than (she) had anticipated,” and pointed to the Middle East conflict, Trump Administration-imposed tariffs and “AI-related price pressures” as the main cause, in her speech to the Council on Foreign Relations.

How is the market reacting: As a byproduct of the spike in mortgage rates, the volume of mortgage applications decreased in the latest Market Composite Index from the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA).

The MBA reported that mortgage application activity decreased 2.3% on a seasonally adjusted basis, with MBA Vice President and Deputy Chief Economist, Joel Kan emphasizing that “renewed affordability challenges from elevated mortgage rates have pushed prospective homebuyers to the sidelines.”

Overall, the third quarter of 2026 saw a significant decline in mortgage and lending activity. ATTOM reported that purchase and refinance mortgage originations were down 12% in Q3, and were down 15% compared to last year’s data.

MBA writes to Congress: On Nov. 18, the MBA sent a letter to four members of the Senate—John Thune, Jerry Moran, Charles Schumer and Richard Blumenthal—in the hopes of altering the “Take Care of America’s Veterans Act,” after its passing in the House of Representatives, and prior to the Senate’s vote on the bill.

Also known as H.R. 9237, the act is a legislative package that includes more than 60 proposals to modify compensation programs for veterans, including extending entitlements to remarried surviving spouses and establishing a supplemental monthly allowance for certain disabled veterans.

The MBA noted in its letter that they “fully support the laudable goals of this legislation” but that it cannot fully support the bill in its current form, specifically pointing out the VA Home Loan program funding fee increases.

Within the Veteran’s Act, it includes modifications to the VA Home Loan Program that would turn the Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL) from 0.50% to 1.42%, and turn loan assumption from 0.50% into 1.00%.

“The funding fee offsets in (The Take Care of America’s Veterans Act), if enacted, would have an inverse and negative impact on veteran homeowners and homebuyers (and other eligible servicemembers) who rely on their earned VA Home Loan benefit to achieve the American Dream of homeownership,” the MBA stated.

Newrez launches Personal Loans: The residential mortgage lender and servicer, Newrez, now offers Personal Loans for all eligible Newrez homeowners.

According to a release, Newrez Personal Loans are available through a partnership with Upgrade—a financial technology company that offers consumer credit products—and are issued by Upgrade’s bank partner, Cross River Bank.

“Our relationship with homeowners doesn’t begin and end with a mortgage,” Newrez CCO Leslie Gillin stated.

The release notes that the new offer expands the financial tools available to eligible Newrez homeowners, providing access to funds for a variety of financial needs. These include debt consolidation, home improvements and unexpected expenses.

Rental demand rises: Zillow’s latest Rental Market Report revealed that demand for single-family homes has risen.

Within the report, consumers noted that they would need a $14,408 pay raise to cover the monthly cost of owning a home, prior to saving for a down payment. “For a growing number of households, renting a house is a legitimate financial strategy,” said Kara Ng, senior economist at Zillow. “You get many of the lifestyle benefits, but it takes less income than owning and keeps more flexibility in the monthly budget. That’s the appeal for the deliberate renter.”
