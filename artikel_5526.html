How the Kennedys Spent Thanksgiving at Their Hyannis Port Compound—Where JFK and His Father Plotted a Presidential Run

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

When John F. Kennedy came home to Hyannis Port for his first Thanksgiving as president, his father still had the best seat in the house.

Joseph P. Kennedy presided over the family table, the children ate first, and the cranberries served with dinner came from bogs near the family’s Cape Cod retreat.

Five years earlier, it was what happened after Thanksgiving dinner that the family would remember. John, then a Massachusetts senator, slipped into the study off the living room with his father.

He had a list of reasons to think twice about running for president. Joseph had an answer for every one.

By the time they emerged, the Kennedys had a campaign to plan.

The house where that conversation took place began as a summer rental. In 1926, Joseph and his wife, Rose, brought their children to Malcolm Cottage, a waterfront home overlooking Nantucket Sound. John was just 9.

According to the John F. Kennedy Hyannis Museum, Joseph bought the property two years later for $25,000 and expanded the house to accommodate his growing family.

John eventually bought a place of his own behind his parents’ home. His brother Robert and sister-in-law Ethel came to own an adjoining property.

Together, the three residences formed the 6-acre compound, an arrangement that put several generations of Kennedys within walking distance of Thanksgiving dinner at the Big House.

In "White House by the Sea," author Kate Storey describes a family with surprisingly little use for a formal seating plan. Joseph and Rose took opposite ends of the mahogany table.

Everyone else picked a spot. Family cook Ziptha Anderson remembered far less flexibility when it came to carving the bird.

“They all like the same thing—the white meat,” she said.

The weather had a say in the menu, too. A chilly Thanksgiving called for clam broth, while a mild afternoon meant tomato juice to start.

Dinner was at 1 p.m. in Storey’s account of the 1956 gathering, which still left the family time for Mass and a sail before they made their way to the table.

By Thanksgiving 1961, the guest list included 13 Kennedy adults and 15 Kennedy children. There was room for another family, too. Paul Fay, then undersecretary of the Navy, joined the celebration with his wife and their three children.

One family outing took the president and several of his siblings to the skating rink in Hyannis named for their late brother, Joseph Jr. John watched while Ted and his sisters rented skates and played tag on the ice.

Back at the compound, Bobby and Ted also got a touch football game going. That evening, the adults sat down to Vermont turkey, candied sweet potatoes, and their choice of pies.

Ted found another way to work up an appetite in 1979. A United Press International photographer caught the senator waving from his boat as he took relatives and friends sailing off Hyannis Port before Thanksgiving dinner.

Ethel carried on hosting Thanksgiving at Hyannis Port long after her children had families of their own. In 2010, she enlisted her son Robert to bring home two live turkeys so her grandchildren could see the birds up close. One promptly escaped.

“For three days they chased it everywhere,” Ethel told the Cape Cod Times.

A couple of months later, the newspaper ran a story about a turkey causing trouble in nearby Centerville, where it had even taken on a moving postal truck. Ethel called the newsroom. She thought she knew where the bird had come from.
