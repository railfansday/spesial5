Will the Fifth Circuit keep FinCEN anti-money laundering rule off the books?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

ALTA and TLTA urged the Fifth Circuit to uphold a March ruling that vacated FinCEN’s residential real estate AML reporting rule. The groups argue FinCEN underestimated compliance costs and exceeded its authority under the Bank Secrecy Act.  AI Summary

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network’s (FinCEN) Residential Real Estate Rule is not currently in effect and the American Land Title Association (ALTA) and the Texas Land Title Association (TLTA) would like it to stay that way.

In late October, ALTA and TLTA filed amicus briefs asking the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit to uphold a ruling that vacated FinCEN’s nationwide anti-money laundering (AML) rule.

The AML rule mandated reporting for any non-financed residential real estate transfer where ownership was held by an entity or trust — with no geographic or price threshold.

The rule was overturned in late March by a federal judge in the Eastern District of Texas after the law had been in effect for less than three weeks. In early May, FinCEN appealed the ruling.

This ruling and subsequent appeal are part of a lawsuit filed by Flowers Title Companies, LLC, which argued that FinCEN lacked authority under the Bank Secrecy Act to impose such sweeping reporting requirements.

In their briefs ALTA and TLTA argued that the rule “imposes significant costs and operational burdens on title and settlement professionals without adequately demonstrating that its benefits justify those requirements.”

According to ALTA, FinCEN’s cost estimate of implementing the policy of $559.4 million was too low, as the association felt FinCEN did not fully account for the cost to title companies for employee training, technology changes, record keeping and the time it takes to get information from buyers and sellers. Additionally, ALTA argues that title professionals have no authority to force buyers or sellers to provide them with beneficial ownership information and that financial institutions already collect beneficial ownership information from their business customers when accounts are opened.

In its filing, TLTA adds that the transactions FinCEN’s rule targets are routine and legal, noting that FinCEN’s legal authority only applies to “suspicious” transactions, not an entire class of transaction.

It remains to be seen how the appeals court will rule on this issue. However, FinCEN has also said it will not require retroactive reporting for transactions that would have been reportable during these legal proceedings.

FinCEN had argued the rule was necessary to close loopholes that allowed money launderers, drug traffickers and other illicit actors to use shell companies and trusts to purchase property with anonymity.

Fidelity National Financial (FNF) has also challenged FinCEN’s AML rule in court, but this resulted in the rule being upheld. That decision has since been appealed by FNF in the Eleventh Circuit.
