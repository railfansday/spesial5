Moody: I felt ready to play after receiving Haka

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Former England captain Lewis Moody joined England to receive the Haka ahead of their 37-12 victory over the All Blacks on Saturday; Moody fronted England's arrow-head formation for the pre-match tradition

Lewis Moody says he "felt ready to play" after he joined England to receive New Zealand's Haka, ahead of Maro Itoje's side's 37-12 victory over the All Blacks on Saturday.

Emotional scenes unfolded at Allianz Stadium, as Moody, who was diagnosed with motor neurone disease (MND) in 2025, walked arm-in-arm with Itoje onto the field.

New Zealand's players clapped as the former England captain took up position at the front of England's arrow-head formation - between Itoje and Ellis Genge - to accept the challenge of the Haka.

Moody, who won 71 international caps during his career, had presented England's players with their matchday shirts at their Pennyhill Park base on Friday, and revealed Ollie Chessum had jokingly urged him to stay on the pitch and chase down the first kick.

"I honestly felt ready to play," Moody told BBC Breakfast.

"When the lads were hugging me after the Haka, I was like: 'Right, I'm ready to go'."

"The night before, Ollie Chessum told me where they were going for the first kick-off.

"He was like: 'Mate, if you're up for it, you can chase it!'

"I was almost ready to take him up on that."

In support of Moody's Front Up to Motor Neurone Disease campaign, England's players wore boots as well as special warm-up and arrival kit with the campaign's logo printed on them.

The players also signed warm-up tops and match shirts, which were donated to the My Name'5 Doddie Foundation, a charity founded by the late Scotland international Doddie Weir that provides funds for MND research.

"I was really aware that this was something that I would never get to feel again and hadn't felt for over 15 years," Moody said.

"So, I wanted to try and fully take in the moment.

"When I got out there, Maro [Itoje] brought me out there and positioned me.

"Ellis [Genge] embraced me, the bundle of physical energy that he is. That's inspiring in itself, to feel the power that's emanating through his body.

"The noise was almost overwhelming. And then [I tried] to recentre myself and focus on the Haka, through the peripheral vision of the England team that fanned out in front of me.

"It just brought a huge smile, and brought back a lot of wonderful memories of playing with all my former team-mates."

He added: "It was really unique and I feel humbled to have been a part of it."

Front Up to MND are organising a series of coastal walk fundraisers on June 12 2027 to raise money for the My Name'5 Doddie Foundation.

"To have the response that it did and to have the support that it has is only good for the cause that I was there for, which is the Front Up campaign supporting My Name'5 Doddie Foundation, to raise as much money as possible, to have a serious impact on this disease," Moody said.

"We need more drugs that have a significant impact for patients. They are the breakthroughs that we're looking for and this campaign will get us near, if not over the line with that.

"But we need thousands, if not tens of thousands, hundreds of thousands of people to support and get involved.

"And hopefully, if you're a little bit inspired or if you just loved watching the game at the weekend, and everything that represented, and you fancied taking on a challenge, come and join us on June 12, walking the coast."

Asked whether Moody's presence motivated England, Seb Atkinson told Sky Sports News:

"Definitely, he came into the hotel last night and gave us some reflections on his career and some reflections on his diagnosis.

"It was really special to be a part of that. I think the lads really took it on board.

"Then to hear the noise of him coming out and taking part in our response to the Haka was really special.

"Also to see his family and his sons and speak to himself [Moody] in the changing rooms after, it just topped it off."
