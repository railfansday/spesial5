Crypto exchange Binance has issued a "one-letter" security warning to crypto users, urging them to carefully check website addresses before logging into their accounts. According to Binance, a fake site can be just one letter off.

A post on X by the crypto exchange pointed out that a small difference in a website URL, sometimes just one letter, can lead users to visit fake websites impersonating legitimate platforms.

"Sometimes the biggest red flag is just one letter," Binance wrote.

Sometimes the biggest red flag is just one letter. Before you log in, double-check the URL. If you’re still not sure, use our official verification tool below 👇 https://t.co/HT068SANmn https://t.co/pBOQeU8GmC

The exchange advised users to double-check URLs before entering their login credentials. It also encouraged anyone uncertain about a website's authenticity to use Binance's official verification tool to confirm whether the URL belongs to the platform.

The Binance Verify platform allows crypto users to stay safe from scammers, as they can enter a URL, email address, phone number, Telegram account, or social media account to check if the source is verified and officially from Binance.

Impersonation scams are quietly growing across the crypto space, with one of the most troubling tactics seen being the deepfake: lifelike, AI-generated impersonations of crypto executives and employees soliciting money on video calls that look and sound real enough to make anyone hesitate. The personas are fabricated, but the sophistication is genuine.

Every day, people are contacted by impersonators using the names, likenesses, and even job titles of crypto personnel, including Binance staff, to solicit investments or financial transactions via WhatsApp, Telegram, and other channels.

Binance stated that scammers prey on people's growing interest in investing and digital finance and on their trust, often masquerading as its employees or executives to exploit its credibility.

With advances in AI and deepfake technology, they can create personas that look and sound real. Crypto users are therefore urged to beware of unsolicited advice or "special opportunities" and to refrain from clicking on unknown links or URLs.
