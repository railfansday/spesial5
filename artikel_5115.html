Bitcoin’s next big threat could come from two oil tankers in the Middle East

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Brent spot was more than $30 below its October average before the strikes. US rate expectations are the next test.

UK Maritime Trade Operations reported two Hormuz tanker strikes on Saturday in which projectiles hit outbound vessels, putting a recent drop in oil prices to the test. Recovering shipments had helped bring Brent down. For Bitcoin, cheaper crude could take some pressure off US inflation and interest-rate expectations if the decline holds.

UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported that a projectile struck an outbound tanker’s stern near Khasab, Oman, at 5 a.m. UTC. Its second warning described a hit to an outbound tanker’s engine room 50 minutes later. UKMTO said no casualties had been reported at the time and the environmental impact was unconfirmed. The warnings gave no estimate of cargo losses or delays.

The US Energy Information Administration said Brent spot was more than $30 a barrel below its October average of $124 as of Nov. 18. It credited rising Hormuz flows with part of the drop. The International Energy Agency had also credited recovering Gulf exports with easing the oil market in its Nov. 13 report.

In the seven days through Nov. 18, UKMTO counted 115 outbound transits, down from 139 the previous week. Its totals combine vessel tracking with US-reported facilitated crossings that UKMTO could not independently verify.

The vessel tracker hormuz.now counted five outbound commercial crossings on Saturday, Nov. 21, and three on Friday. Its Saturday logs show four outbound crossings after the second reported strike, including two tankers. The tracker counts commercial vessels of at least 80 meters with transponders switched on. The amount of oil shipped after the strikes remains unclear.

Reuters reported that front-month Brent futures settled Friday at $92.57 a barrel, up 59 cents. Treasury data show the two-year yield fell to 4.63% from 4.76% on Thursday. Both moves preceded the strikes.

A December rate hike was still widely priced in. In its Friday market recap, Kiplinger cited CME FedWatch for a 96% chance of a quarter-point increase. Traders can compare that reading with the next one and watch whether early-2027 fed funds futures point to further tightening.

Federal Reserve staff estimated that oil prices fed through to core inflation more strongly after the pandemic in a model pooling five advanced economies, including the US. A 2023 IMF working paper found that US monetary tightening weighed on broad crypto prices as investors took less risk. A lasting oil rebound could keep expected borrowing costs higher if traders also anticipate higher inflation and tighter Fed policy.

A sustained drop in loaded outbound shipments, a rebound in the same Brent futures contract and higher implied Fed rates into early 2027 would strengthen that case. If shipments hold up and rate expectations barely move, a brief jump in Brent would say little about the outlook for dollar borrowing or crypto investors’ appetite for risk.

A sustained drop in oil shipments would matter for dollar funding if it lifted Brent futures, inflation expectations and the expected path of US rates.

A brief oil spike would carry less weight if cargo flows and rate expectations hold steady.
