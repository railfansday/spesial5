Google’s lightweight Gemini matched two top models on a chemistry test

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

All three identified known compounds in about 97% of attempts when given the same chemistry tools and web access.

Google DeepMind released tools for interpreting mass spectrometry data in its Antigravity AI platform on Nov. 25. In the company’s test, the software helped its lightweight Gemini 3.8 Flash match two frontier models at identifying known compounds. With the tools and internet access, all three identified the exact molecule in about 97% of attempts on a set of 100 compounds whose fragment patterns were already recorded in public libraries.

The models were farther apart with internet access alone. In DeepMind’s benchmark, Flash missed more than three quarters of its attempts to identify a compound, while Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 4 Carbon missed roughly a quarter to a third. All three scored below 4% with neither internet access nor the specialist tools.

DeepMind drew the test spectra from the Enamine REAL collection in the public MoNA library. The compounds’ structures were also cataloged in PubChem. The team screened out recordings in which the molecule had produced too few useful fragments. The benchmark ran three attempts per spectrum. Runs that took more than an hour counted as failures, while temporary infrastructure and quota errors were excluded.

Tandem mass spectrometry measures the charged fragments a molecule breaks into. To identify the molecule, a chemist can compare that pattern with a reference spectrum recorded from a known compound.

For the internet-only runs, DeepMind said scripts written on the fly struggled with noisy peaks and with choosing which masses to search. The new Mass Spec Skills give Antigravity established software to clean up a signal, look for matching reference spectra and check whether a proposed structure fits the measured fragments.

DeepMind also reported that adding the skills reduced the number of tokens each model generated per attempt, with the largest proportional drop for Flash. Flash with the tools was more accurate than either frontier model with internet access alone.

In a test without a spectral-library search, Antigravity used fragment measurements to infer a formula and narrow down candidate structures in PubChem. The compound it selected was already cataloged.

For a compound that is genuinely new, a predicted formula and explanations for some of the peaks can give a chemist a starting point. DeepMind says some structures, including mirror-image molecules, break apart in such similar ways that telling them apart can take another kind of lab measurement.
