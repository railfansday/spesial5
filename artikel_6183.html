How I Travel: Audra McDonald Needs a Humidifier in Her Hotel Room

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Theater legend Audra McDonald possesses one of the most powerful, lauded voices of her generation, a soprano so stunning that she’s been nominated for more Tony Awards than any other performer in history. So what elixirs, potions, and special teas does she keep with her to ensure the quality of her voice as she travels the world to sing? “I mask up on planes. I’m traveling all over the country to do concerts; I can’t land and be sick,” she says. “And I try to make sure there's a humidifier in the hotel room. Those are the two main things.”

While her role in the period drama The Gilded Age doesn’t require singing—yet!—McDonald and the many Broadway legends in the cast released a holiday album this month, ‘Voices of the Gilded Age: The Christmas Album,’ and the fourth season is currently airing on HBO. She spoke with Condé Nast Traveler from Australia, where her husband Will Swenson is currently starring in the Oz production of A Beautiful Noise: The Neil Diamond Musical, about traveling with their 10-year-old daughter, the unspeakable glories of New Zealand, and why you simply can’t top New York City in the final months of the year.

I don't think you can beat New York City. I've lived there for 36 years and it's always such a beautiful time. We have dear friends with a kid around my youngest child's age, and we've been going to the holiday spectacular at Radio City. My sister makes sure that she takes my daughters to The Nutcracker every year at City Ballet. We always try to go see the tree at Rockefeller Center. The one day I don't like in New York City during the holidays is Santa Con, but other than that, I'm down.

Her favorite venue on Earth in which to perform:

There's something about Carnegie Hall [in Manhattan] that is magic. I last sang at Carnegie Hall about six, seven months ago—I'm on the board there now, as well—and the nerves I normally get [before a performance] are always tenfold when I'm getting ready there.

Then the minute I step out on that stage, the hall just gives you a hug that says, You're here to do what you're supposed to do. You're supposed to make music, you're supposed to celebrate music, you're supposed to commune with your audience, and glorious sounds are supposed to be heard. Carnegie Hall is built for all the sound to be glorious. That [venue] takes care of me.

Don't worry about how they act up on a plane. Do what you can, but it's not the flight attendants’ first time at the rodeo. You're never going to see any of these other people again. When we flew out to Australia, my daughter got some weird stomach bug that started the minute they shut the door, and she vomited for the first eight hours of that 15-hour flight. The flight attendants just kept bringing bags and stuff and were like… [mimes a blasé hand gesture]. There was nothing we could do! We were over the ocean. I was giving myself grace. I was giving my poor daughter grace. I just wanted her to feel better, and I was doing the best I could. That’s what I would say to other parents. They’ve seen it all, so don’t worry about what other people are thinking. Everybody has headphones now! We’re fine!

I am not preparing for work! That's the ADHD in me. I am also not sleeping because I cannot sleep on planes very well, unless I'm really knocked out with the help of sleeping pills or something, and I don't like to do that. I try to watch movies, but I'm not good at it, either. My dad was killed in a plane crash about 20 years ago, so normally what I'm doing on a plane—when I'm not taking care of my daughter vomiting—is watching the flight path and seeing where we are in the sky. Then, if there's Wi-Fi, I check to see the weather ahead. Like I'm helping! I don't know what I'm doing, but it calms me down to be like, “Okay, I see that there is weather up ahead, so I should expect that it's probably going to be bumpy.” I don't know if in my mind, I'm thinking [as if] I can warn the pilot. I’ve got to go to therapy, but that's what I do on a plane.

New Zealand, holy wow. My husband and I were asked to sing on a Playbill cruise in Tahiti. We flew to Tahiti with our two daughters, and then I had three and a half weeks off before I started my Australian tour, so we went to New Zealand. I'm going to get emotional talking about it. Every place we visited—the North Island, the South Island, Queenstown, Rotorua, Hahei Beach, the Southern Alps, Christchurch, Wellington—all of it was just one wow after another. The people were lovely. We went to Hobbiton. We drove everywhere, and every second of it was mind-blowing. We even did the mailboat, where you go with this guy on a boat to deliver the mail in an area you can only access by water. I want to go back!

The destination she swears she loved before everyone else:

When we first start going, I wasn’t hearing about a lot of people there, but now everybody goes to Portugal. We have friends who live there, so we started visiting a lot, and not just Lisbon or the Algarve or Porto, though we love Porto. It was also the beautiful countryside and hills and tiny little villages. The topography there is stunning. It’s getting more expensive now, but we used to get incredible bottles of wine for €3! In fact, we have a farm there now, we love it so much.

Toothbrush and toothpaste. Not every hotel provides that, and I just love it when they do. If they've got a toothbrush and toothpaste in there, they've probably gotten a lot of the other amenities right too.

The farthest flung place she’s ever been recognized:

Here in Australia. I was in the South Melbourne neighborhood we've been living in, and I went into a little bookstore near my daughter's school, and someone recognized me. They said, “Were you the one who recorded Emily Wilson's translation of The Iliad book on tape? I loved your work in that.” And I was like, “Whoa, what? Yes I did, wow, thank you.” Doing The Iliad on tape almost killed me. It was the hardest thing I've ever done, so the fact that she wanted to thank me for it? I don’t think my mom knows I did that!

I really want to take a trip to Africa. Obviously, I want to go to Ghana and see where our journey started. I want to go to Kenya and do a safari. I want to go on a trek to see the gorillas. I've been to Egypt, to Cairo, but that's the only time I've been to Africa. I want to wait a couple more years, until my daughter's about 13, and then I will take her. That's going to be our trip.
