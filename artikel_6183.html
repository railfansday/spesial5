Buy Etihad Guest Miles With 60% Bonus (2.0 Cents Each): Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Etihad Guest has just launched its latest promotion on buying miles, which is being marketed as the biggest offer of the year. With this, members can buy Etihad miles with a 60% bonus, which is an opportunity to acquire them for as little as two cents each.

Is that offer worth it? Let’s cover all the details, but I’ll say right away that I think this offer has fairly limited appeal, and you should only take advantage of this with a specific use in mind.

Link: Buy Etihad Guest miles with a bonus

Between 12:01AM GST on November 12 and 11:59PM GST on November 22, 2026, Etihad Guest is offering a bonus on purchased miles. The offer is tiered, and you get a bigger bonus the more miles you buy, as follows:

Note that purchased Etihad Guest miles post within 72 hours of completing the purchase (typically faster than that), and that purchased miles don’t count toward Etihad Guest elite status.

Etihad Guest ordinarily charges 3.2 cents per purchased mile, and there are no further taxes and fees. That’s before any bonuses or discount. With the 60% bonus, if you maxed out this promotion you’d receive a total of 320,000 Etihad Guest miles at a cost of $6,400, which is a rate of two cents per Etihad Guest mile.

Etihad Guest limits members to purchasing 200,000 miles per account per calendar year, before any bonuses. The exception is Etihad Guest Platinum members, who can purchase up to 300,000 miles per year, before any bonuses.

The current Etihad Guest buy miles promotion is described as the biggest offer of the year. To compare this to other offers we’ve seen so far this year, we’ve seen bonuses of 25-50% (2.56 cents to 2.13 cents per mile), as well as discounts of up to 35% (2.08 cents per mile).

As you can see, Etihad Guest’s current offer gives you the best cost per mile so far this year. However, Etihad isn’t exactly aggressive with selling miles, so it’s hard to get too excited about any of these amounts.

Etihad Guest mileage purchases are processed by points.com, meaning they don’t count as an airfare purchase for the purposes of credit card spending. Therefore I’d recommend using a card on which you’re trying to reach minimum spending, or otherwise a credit card that maximizes your return on everyday spending.

There are lots of ways to redeem Etihad Guest miles. While the airline doesn’t belong to one of the major alliances, there are many ways to redeem the miles on both Etihad and on partner airlines.

Below you can find the award chart for travel exclusively on Etihad. The pricing is based on the one-way distance of travel. Note that these redemption rates are specifically for situations where there’s GuestSeat availability, which is Etihad’s capacity controlled, saver award space.

If you see higher award pricing than what’s listed below, that generally means there’s not saver award space, and redemptions won’t be a very good deal. Furthermore, in some situations you’ll find higher saver level award costs, including for travel in the carrier’s A380 first class.

Etihad Guest has a separate award chart for travel on partner airlines, which is also distance based, and you can find that below. This is based on the one-way distance, and you can’t mix airline partners on a single award.

The main reason to consider collecting Etihad Guest miles is because the program has access to a lot more Etihad award space than partner programs do. Partner programs, like American AAdvantage and Air France-KLM Flying Blue, typically only have access to a subset of premium cabin Etihad availability. Furthermore, if you want to fly in Etihad’s The Residence, your ticket needs to be issued by Etihad, so an Etihad Guest award redemption would qualify.

There are two major catches when it comes to Etihad Guest, in addition to not really having slam dunk redemption values:

I’d really only recommend buying Etihad Guest miles with a very specific use in mind, if you know what you’re doing.

Everyone will value mileage currencies differently. Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Etihad Guest miles to be worth 1.1 cents each. I tend to value points pretty conservatively, and there are many ways to get more value from Etihad Guest miles than that.

Etihad Guest miles don’t expire as long as you have at least some account activity once every 18 months. Note that you can only reset the expiration of your miles by crediting a flight to the program every 18 months, so other activity wouldn’t help in that regard.

Keep in mind that there are many ways to earn Etihad Guest miles, so outright buying miles isn’t the only option. Etihad Guest also partners with select transferable points currencies. In the United States, you can move points to Etihad Guest at a 1:1 ratio from Bilt Rewards, Capital One, and Citi ThankYou, so that gives you quite a bit of flexibility.

Admittedly not everyone has access to credit cards in the United States, and some people may also prefer to just buy miles if they can acquire them at a reasonable cost.

Etihad Guest is selling miles with up to a 60% bonus, which is an opportunity to buy miles for as little as two cents each. This could be worth it if you have a specific use in mind, but Etihad Guest just seems rather unserious with its buy miles promotions, since the program isn’t that lucrative, in terms of redemption rates, and the policies for changing and canceling awards is also incredibly frustrating.

So for a vast majority of people, I’d recommend giving this offer a pass, unless you’ve done the math, and it checks out.
