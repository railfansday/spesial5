Bitcoin Opens to Russian Savers With a 300,000-Ruble Cap, Just 0.048 BTC a Year Per Broker: How Much Can Russians Really Buy?

Russians without qualified-investor status will be able to buy Bitcoin, Ether and Tether’s USDT through brokers, but only up to 300,000 rubles a year each, under Bank of Russia rules officially published on Friday, November 6.

At Friday’s prices, that is about $3,668, or 0.048 BTC.

The cap was set out in Directive No. 7441-U, signed by Governor Elvira Nabiullina on September 11 and registered with the Justice Ministry on October 30. It takes effect 10 days after publication, on November 17. Qualified investors face no limit.

The venue is almost ready. The Moscow Exchange plans to open trading in BTC, ETH and USDT on December 1, starting with pilot trades. Sberbank and VTB are among 25 companies on the central bank’s register of licensed crypto exchangers as of Friday.

The cap has lost value since it was proposed. By a Tokenist calculation, 300,000 rubles bought 0.057 BTC on August 11 and 15% less now.

So how much Bitcoin can a Russian saver actually buy, and what changes on December 1?

About 0.048 BTC a year through each broker. We converted the cap at the Bank of Russia’s official dollar rate, 81.79 rubles on Friday, and Binance’s BTCUSDT price.

Selling does not free up room. The directive counts the cap as the ruble sum of what a non-qualified investor paid for crypto through a broker during the calendar year, so a buyer who sells in March cannot use that money to buy again until January.

Every investor must also pass a test first. The Bank of Russia said in August that all buyers, qualified or not, need to pass it and read the risks before trading, and that the cap applies separately at each intermediary, whether broker, crypto exchanger or asset manager.

Why only three coins? The law limits retail buyers to assets with large market value and trading volume, and at least five years of price history on foreign venues.

One caveat: the directive itself only covers purchases through brokers, so how the limit works at exchangers will depend on their own rules.

Russia gets an organized crypto market. The Moscow Exchange said on November 2 that it plans to let market participants trade digital currencies from December 1.

The start will be slow. “In the first stage, pilot trades are expected, with participants connecting to the new market gradually as they become ready,” the exchange said.

Banks are lining up to sell crypto directly too. The central bank’s register of crypto exchangers lists 25 companies as of Friday, including Sberbank, VTB and T-Invest Lab. Sberbank has said it plans to launch its first crypto products on December 1.

The rules also close older doors. The Bank of Russia says individuals cannot send crypto to non-custodial wallets. From July 1, 2027, residents will be able to trade only through licensed intermediaries.

BTC trades at $75,772, down 0.4% on the day, after Thursday’s sell-off took it below its 200-day exponential moving average (EMA) for the first time since August.

Russian retail money will arrive in small amounts at first. For Bitcoin, the 200-day EMA matters more this month than the cap.
