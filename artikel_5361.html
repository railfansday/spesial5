Why the Best-Looking Rate May Not Be the Best Deal

Two exchange services can show similar rates but offer different conditions. One may use a floating rate that changes with the market, while another may offer a fixed rate for a specified period.

Consider two hypothetical offers. The first promises a slightly higher estimated return, but the amount can change before processing. The second displays a lower figure under clearly stated conditions. The first might still be attractive, but its initial advantage is less meaningful if the rate changes.

There is no universal winner. Compare the expected amount received, the conditions attached to the quote, and any applicable charges rather than choosing based on one number.

Find out whether the rate is fixed or floating. A fixed rate may apply only for a limited period and under specific conditions. A floating rate can change as the market moves. Knowing which applies helps you understand how reliable the displayed amount is.
