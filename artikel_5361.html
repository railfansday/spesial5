Nvidia-backed Firmus is weighing a bond of about $1.5 billion after shelving its IPO

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The bond would form part of a debt package of as much as about $4 billion, down from about $7.5 billion two months ago

Firmus Grid, an Australian AI company backed by Nvidia $NVDA, is weighing a bond sale of roughly $1.5 billion that would sit within a broader borrowing package, according to Bloomberg. The plan follows the company's decision last month to shelve its initial public offering.

Firmus spoke with banks and investors as recently as last week about a financing that combines senior and mezzanine tranches and could reach roughly $4 billion, according to Bloomberg. That is well below the roughly $7.5 billion in debt the company was seeking just two months earlier.

Firmus could attempt to sell the bond before the year closes, according to Bloomberg, though the schedule and other terms could shift depending on market conditions.

Proceeds would be used mainly to buy Nvidia's GB300 AI chips destined for a data center in Indonesia, which Firmus is developing together with DayOne Data Centers, according to Bloomberg.

Both the bond and a distinct bank loan being discussed would carry 69-month terms and make up the package's senior tranche, according to Bloomberg. The bond would yield around 8%, and the loan might carry a spread of roughly 325 basis points above the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, which serves as a benchmark for dollar borrowing.

Firmus has obtained BBB grades on the proposed bond from a minimum of two global ratings agencies, according to Bloomberg. Both the bond and the loan are slated to rank equally if creditors must recover money, which means each would stand on the same footing when seeking repayment from what the company owns. Morgan Stanley $MS is leading the financing, and Firmus is asking other banks to join the underwriting group.

"Firmus doesn't comment on market speculation. Funding arrangements and any details will be announced at the appropriate time," the company said in response to a request for comment, according to Bloomberg. Morgan Stanley declined to comment.

Firmus withdrew its planned IPO on the Australian Securities Exchange on Oct. 9, citing market volatility and prevailing conditions. The company said at the time that it would pursue private market capital and consider other public and private options.

Firmus has so far completed 46 megawatts out of a planned 912-megawatt slate of data centers, according to investor documents seen by Bloomberg. The fundraising arrives as investors become more cautious about steep AI valuations and expensive borrowing worldwide.
