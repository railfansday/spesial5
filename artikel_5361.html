The Nasdaq jumps 2% to start Anthropic's IPO day

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Futures markets point to a 25% jump for Anthropic stock at the open, while the S&P 500 gained 1% and the Dow added 0.4%

Anthropic is set to debut on the Nasdaq $NDAQ on Thursday under the ticker ANT after setting an $83 per-share price for its IPO, higher than the earlier guidance of $70 to $78, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The Nasdaq Composite rose 2% on Thursday, while the S&P 500 gained 1% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 230 points, or 0.4%.

Perpetual futures markets indicate Anthropic stock will open about 25% higher, according to CNBC. Trading is slated to begin around midday, and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei showed up at Nasdaq MarketSite in New York’s Times Square $SQ ahead of the debut, according to The Wall Street Journal. Anthropic is the first artificial-intelligence lab to list its shares on a public exchange.

The company upsized the offering, which raised $81.3 billion and topped the $75 billion base deal in SpaceX’s June IPO, according to CNBC.

The pricing buoyed the technology sector, according to CNBC. Nvidia $NVDA stock rose 1.5%, Advanced Micro Devices stock gained 2.6% and Micron $MU Technology stock climbed close to 5%. Microsoft $MSFT and Meta $META Platforms each gained more than 1%.

Chip stocks also drew support from Texas resuming certain data center permits, according to CNBC. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality is back to its "normal permit review process," according to a spokesperson, which calms worries that the AI buildout could stall once the midterm elections have passed.

Investors have grown wary of AI this month, as attention turns to whether outlays for the buildout will eventually generate earnings, according to CNBC. The iShares AI Innovation and Tech Active ETF has fallen 11% in November, and Nvidia stock has dropped 26%. CoreWeave stock is down close to 13% for the month.

"It'll be an important barometer for investors' AI appetite," Callie Cox, director of investment strategy at Plancorp, told CNBC.
