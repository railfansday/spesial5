Bitwise challenges Grayscale’s $489 million Zcash ETF as CIO predicts Bitcoin-to-ZEC rotation

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The asset manager's Nov. 17 filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposes allowing authorized participants to redeem ETF shares directly for ZEC or receive cash, unlike Grayscale's existing Zcash ETF, which currently permits only cash redemptions.

The proposed fund would trade on the Texas Stock Exchange under the ticker ZEC, expanding competition in a market where Grayscale's ZCSH has traded on NYSE Arca since August.

Grayscale's fund recently held approximately $489 million in assets, while its September quarterly report disclosed $50.5 million in gross redemptions during the preceding three months.

The difference in redemption structures could influence trading costs and how closely the competing funds track the value of their underlying ZEC holdings.

Under Bitwise's proposal, authorized participants could return blocks of 10,000 ETF shares to the fund and receive the corresponding amount of ZEC.

That would give professional trading firms greater control over how they sell or hedge the cryptocurrency, potentially allowing them to capture differences between the ETF's market price and the value of its holdings.

For instance, when ETF shares trade below their net asset value, a firm could purchase discounted shares, redeem them for ZEC, and sell the coins at a higher combined value after accounting for costs.

Such transactions can help narrow the discount between ETF shares and their underlying assets.

Bitwise’s proposal would send ZEC to a wallet designated by the authorized firm. At Grayscale, a liquidity provider receives the ZEC and the firm is paid in cash.

Bitwise’s proposed in-kind route gives the authorized firm or its designee a way to receive ZEC directly.

Its prospectus requires authorized participants to receive cash, while a liquidity provider delivers the money and receives ZEC from the fund.

Depending on the order arrangement, the liquidity provider or authorized participant bears the difference between the fund's calculated value and the actual price obtained when disposing of the coins.

Bitwise's approach would allow participating firms to manage that execution themselves, potentially reducing costs when they can access sufficient liquidity.

The proposed flexibility also extends to creating new shares, with Bitwise allowing authorized firms to deliver ZEC or cash.

However, the approach would place greater importance on the firms' ability to trade ZEC efficiently, particularly during periods of heightened volatility.

In a November interview, Hougan argued that Zcash has become increasingly differentiated from Bitcoin as institutional adoption accelerates.

That investment thesis presents an interesting distinction in Bitwise's proposed fund.

Although Zcash supports shielded transactions that conceal certain transaction details using zero-knowledge cryptography, the ETF itself would not use those features.

Several cryptocurrency exchanges have delisted or restricted ZEC over compliance concerns, while some platforms that continue supporting the asset permit transactions only through transparent addresses.

Bitwise identified restrictions affecting Zcash trading on exchanges including OKX and Binance, warning that reduced market access could affect prices and liquidity.

The filing also highlighted European Union anti-money laundering rules taking effect in July 2027 that will prohibit regulated financial providers from maintaining accounts that enable transaction anonymization.

The rules' application to cryptocurrencies supporting both transparent and shielded transactions remains uncertain, leaving Zcash's institutional ambitions exposed to further regulatory scrutiny.
