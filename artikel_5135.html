PEPE Whales Are Sitting On $21 Million In Tokens, And One Wallet Keeps Getting Bigger

PEPE whales have accumulated more than six trillion tokens worth approximately $21.16 million, according to new data from blockchain monitoring platform Lookonchain. Most of that position belongs to a single unidentified wallet that has been accumulating the memecoin since November 17.

The size of the position is unusual even by memecoin standards. One address now holds more than 1% of PEPE’s entire supply, and its owner has continued adding tokens despite weakness in the market.

The wallet, identified by its opening characters 0x5011, accumulated 5.15 trillion PEPE over eight days. Lookonchain valued the holdings at $18.28 million, representing approximately 1.22% of the cryptocurrency’s total supply.

The more interesting detail is how quickly the position has grown.

Lookonchain’s transaction monitoring history shows that by November 19, the wallet had withdrawn around 1.9 trillion PEPE from Binance. That figure climbed to 2.878 trillion by November 23 and exceeded 3.3 trillion the following day.

On Wednesday, Lookonchain reported that the address had accumulated 4.16 trillion tokens. By Thursday, its reported holdings had reached 5.15 trillion.

Another wallet, beginning with 0x6246, accumulated 868.16 billion PEPE valued at approximately $2.88 million, bringing the combined reported value to more than $21 million.

Large withdrawals are nothing new for the memecoin. NewsBTC previously covered a separate PEPE whale moving $14.7 million out of Binance. What makes the latest activity particularly interesting is the repeated accumulation within such a short period.

Before treating these transactions as a bullish signal, it’s worth looking at what other large holders have been doing.

Data from blockchain analytics platform Deep Blue Alpha showed that withdrawals accounted for approximately 36% of tracked PEPE whale transfers between wallets and exchanges over the preceding seven days, according to its November 26 update.

In other words, exchange deposits outweighed withdrawals within that particular group of wallets.

That doesn’t invalidate Lookonchain’s findings. It does, however, complicate the idea that large investors are collectively preparing for a PEPE recovery.

One group of holders may be increasing its exposure while others are looking to reduce theirs. The distinction matters, especially in a market where a handful of transactions can attract more attention than the activity of dozens of other wallets.

Exchange deposits aren’t necessarily sales, just as withdrawals don’t establish that a fresh purchase has taken place. Centralized exchange trading records remain largely invisible to outside observers.

For an investor holding 5.15 trillion PEPE, even a movement of $0.000001 in the token’s price would change the value of the position by $5.15 million.

That illustrates both the attraction and the risk of holding such a large quantity of a speculative asset.

Building an $18 million position is one thing. Exiting it at the quoted market price is another. The amount an investor can actually realize depends on available liquidity, execution and the market’s ability to absorb the tokens.

Concentrated holdings can therefore work in both directions. If tokens remain outside exchanges, they may reduce the supply immediately available for trading. If the same tokens return to exchanges, traders may suddenly find themselves watching for potential selling pressure.

The next development worth following is whether these wallets continue accumulating or begin transferring tokens back to trading platforms.

For now, two addresses hold an unusually large amount of PEPE. Whether the rest of the market shares their apparent appetite for the memecoin is a different question.
