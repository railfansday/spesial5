My Wallet Introduces 1:1 Cross-Chain Stablecoin Swaps After Topping WalletConnect’s Listings

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Disclaimer: The below article is sponsored, and the views in it do not represent those of ZyCrypto. Readers should conduct independent research before taking any actions related to the project mentioned in this piece. This article should not be regarded as investment advice.

My Wallet, a self-custodial, open-source crypto wallet serving more than 10 million users, has launched 1:1 swaps for USDT, USDC, USDe, and USDS across supported blockchains. On eligible routes, users can switch both stablecoins and networks in a single in-app flow — with the amount they’ll receive fixed before confirmation, and no spread, slippage, or swap fee charged by the wallet.

The launch follows My Wallet’s listing at the top of WalletConnect’s WalletGuide. The wallet is also WalletConnect Certified and connects users to more than 80,000 compatible apps.

Demand for stablecoin movement has never been higher. Research published in October 2026 by Coinbase and The Block Pro Research found that organic stablecoin transfer volume exceeded $400 million per hour during Q3 2026. Yet stablecoins span multiple blockchains and token issuers, creating friction whenever a user needs a specific token on a specific network. Even when funds arrive on the right chain, users may still be holding the wrong stablecoin for a payment, trade, or application.

My Wallet addresses this through its in-app swap aggregator — powered by LayerZero, NEAR Intents, and other bridges to source the best rate. On eligible stablecoin routes, the aggregator offers a fixed 1:1 token rate, with no exchange-rate spread, slippage, or My Wallet swap commission. My Wallet says it’s the first among major crypto wallets to offer 1:1 swaps between different stablecoins across blockchains in a single in-app flow.

The fixed 1:1 rate can apply whether users switch to a different stablecoin or move the same token to another chain. On a supported cross-chain USDT-to-USDC route, for example, a user sending 10,000 USDT receives exactly 10,000 USDC. The same holds when the token stays the same: a supported transfer of 10,000 USDT from Ethereum to TRON delivers exactly 10,000 USDT on TRON.

The 1:1 option is available for swaps above $30 on supported routes, with no exchange-rate spread, slippage, or My Wallet swap commission. Applicable blockchain network fees are paid separately, and the exact destination amount is shown before confirmation. Other routes may carry different rates and fees.

“Stablecoins are supposed to make moving money simpler, but users still have to think about which token they hold and which blockchain it’s on,” said Alexander Zinchuk, founder of My Wallet. “We’re removing that friction. If you hold USDT and need USDC on another network, you should be able to make that change in one step, know exactly how many tokens you’ll receive, and not pay an additional swap commission to your wallet.”

The stablecoin launch builds on the recent addition of native Bitcoin support in My Wallet. Users can hold, send, receive, and swap BTC alongside assets on Ethereum, Solana, TON, and other networks — and buy Bitcoin with a bank card or sell it for fiat through MoonPay.

My Wallet now also supports gasless transfers on Ethereum, Base, BNB Chain, Arbitrum, and Optimism, adding to existing support on Solana and TON. For these EVM transfers, the network fee is deducted directly from the tokens being sent — so users never need to acquire a separate native token for gas. Supported assets include USDC on all five EVM networks, USDT on Ethereum and BNB Chain, bridged USDT on Base and Optimism, and USDT0 on Arbitrum, with no minimum transfer amount and no daily transaction cap.

Together, these features advance My Wallet’s broader “All You Need to Enjoy Crypto” vision — combining asset management, seamless trading, and ease of use in a single self-custodial app.

The new features are available now in My Wallet. Swap availability depends on the selected assets, networks, and routes.
