Why did Steelers bench Aaron Rodgers? Insiders reveal details

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The NFL world is buzzing after the Steelers' late-night decision to bench Aaron Rodgers in favor of Mason Rudolph for the team's Black Friday matchup against the Denver Broncos on Nov. 27. Multiple sources have reported that Rodgers has not been seen at the team facility as of Wednesday morning.

As league insiders have begun to weigh in, a theme has begun to emerge about why the future Hall of Famers was benched: There were meetings involving Rodgers, coach and play caller Mike McCarthy, quarterbacks coach Tom Arth and others earlier in the week with the team on short rest, and those meetings appear not to have gone well.

NFL Network's Mike Garafolo offered some specific details about what those meetings were about on "Good Morning Football"

"There was going to be a change in the process this week," he said. "Tom Arth is the quarterbacks coach. He was going to be the one that was going to radio the plays into Rodgers.

"You remember earlier in the season, there were times when Rodgers was saying 'C'mon, c'mon, c'mon, c'mon, c'mon.' There was also Mike McCarthy, a shot of him saying something he later regretted back to Rodgers.

"Well, Arth relaying the plays in and communicating with Rodgers is part of the two-minute drill. That's how they do things. Well, now, the change was that this was going to be how things were going to go for the entire game.

Garafolo said the team tested that process during a walkthrough on Tuesday and continued to have meetings into the evening.

"Now, at some point, late last night," Garafolo said. "And I truly believe this, because based on McCarthy's answers and the conversations I was having, nobody was talking about Rodgers being benched. So I do believe that it was late last night when Mike McCarthy finally made the decision to bench Aaron Rodgers. The question is, why? Is it performative? Is it punitive? And what led to that eventually being the decision?"

NFL Network colleague Ian Rapoport and ESPN's Adam Schefter also mentioned the meetings in their reporting, with Schefter characterizing them as "some good, some bad."

Prime Video production captured some profanity-laced discord between Rodgers and McCarthy during the Week 4 loss at Cleveland.

Hot mics caught Rodgers demanding McCarthy get the call in for a play in the first quarter. Cameras then panned to McCarthy calling Rodgers a profane name.

All of this points toward communication being a significant issue between coach and player amid offensive struggles in losses against the Cincinnati Bengals and Philadelphia Eagles the past two weeks.

It's a surprising development considering the long working history between the two during their 13 seasons together in Green Bay between 2006 and 2018. They won a Super Bowl and Rodgers won two of his four league MVP awards during that prosperous period.

Expectations weren't nearly that high for their reunion in Pittsburgh, but both have referenced positive conversations during their years apart and familiarity with each other as reasons that Rodgers signed on for a 22nd season back in May.

No one seems to have a good feel for what's next. Rapoport said it "certainly seems [Rodgers] has" played his last downs with the Steelers, and the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette's Gerry Dulac termed Rodgers' future with the team "uncertain."

Rodgers had previously said that he planned to retire after the 2026 season. It seems possible this decision could move that timeline up to the week of Thanksgiving, with the team pointedly saying that Rodgers would be inactive against the Broncos, and not simply moving to the backup role behind Rudolph.

Instead, second-year signal caller Will Howard and rookie Drew Allar will be in the reserve roles should something go wrong with Rudolph.
