Nvidia sellers seek new buyers as U.S. curbs China AI chip access, sources tell CNBC

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Nvidia chip sellers are seeking new buyers for the semiconductor giant's hardware, three people told CNBC, after the Trump administration tightened rules to curb Chinese companies' remote access to the most advanced artificial-intelligence systems.

Companies have put an unusually high volume of Nvidia graphics processing units, or GPUs, on the market since new measures restricting Chinese companies renting access to advanced Nvidia chips were announced on Nov. 16, the people said.

Jack Duhamel, president of AI infrastructure marketplace GPU Mart, and two people familiar with the matter told CNBC the surge in GPUs for sale was directly attributable to the new rule. Duhamel added that one company looking to sell Nvidia chips told GPU Mart the sale was related to the new rule.

The two people said it appeared chip sellers were seeking to offload them because they were originally for use in data centers in which Chinese companies would rent access. They asked not to be named because they were discussing private information.

The Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, announced an interim final rule as part of a clampdown on Chinese companies bypassing export controls, which effectively ban the sale of the most advanced Nvidia chips to China, by accessing them remotely in overseas data centers. The rule came into effect on Nov. 19.

Deals to find buyers for thousands of B300 GPUs, one of Nvidia's most advanced offerings, have been floated since the rule was announced, said Duhamel and one of the people.

Nvidia declined to comment. BIS did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Nvidia has been facing increasing scrutiny from U.S. lawmakers over Chinese companies' access to its advanced chips.

The chip giant warned its customers that the proposed Remote Access Security Act — which has passed the House of Representatives but is yet to pass the Senate — would give the U.S. government broader authority to control access to cloud services, potentially exposing both parties to "legal action," sources previously told CNBC.

In July, the U.S. accused a Chinese AI lab, Moonshot AI, of accessing Nvidia chips via a facility in Thailand. CNBC reported on Nov. 5 that the company had sought access to the chips via data centers in Europe.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Wednesday that the new rule announced last week was intended to close "loopholes."

"For a long time, we have not allowed the sale of Blackwell chips, which are the highest AI chips or similar chips to China directly," he said.

"Some rules were issued by the Commerce Department last week to close off indirect access, either smuggling through places like Southeast Asia or remote access from data centers in third countries."

During Nvidia's third-quarter earnings call on Nov. 17, then-interim CEO Colette Kress said the company was still studying the controls, but expected performance to remain strong because demand for chips far exceeded supply.
