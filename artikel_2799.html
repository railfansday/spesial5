Nets vs. Bucks preview: Brooklyn looks to end five-game losing streak

Bad basketball teams find ways to lose games. On Monday night, the Brooklyn Nets tied the game against the Minnesota Timberwolves at five early in the first quarter. And that was the closest they got. Brooklyn never led and trailed by as much as 30 points on national TV. They lost by 26 points, 142-116, withe the losing streak sitting at five entering Wednesday's matchup against the 2-16 Milwaukee Bucks.

They've had their fair share of struggles this year.

The Bucks are at the bottom of the NBA standings, but they had a good time in Canada on Monday night. They took on the Toronto Raptors and won 116-111. The win snapped their 13-game losing streak, which was the second-longest in franchise history. You take the wins where you can get them.

Matchup: Brooklyn Nets (3-13) vs. Milwaukee Bucks (2-16)

By the numbers, these are the two worst defensive teams in the NBA. Brooklyn allows 120.3 points per 100 possessions, worst in the NBA. Milwaukee isn't that far behind at 119.8 points per 100 possessions — the second-worst in the NBA. These two teams allow easy buckets and bad defense makes games a lot harder to sit through if you're rooting for the team with the bad defense.

Look for this one to be a high scoring affair and whoever can put together enough stops on defense should walk away with the win.

Where the Nets have doomed themselves this year is in the turnover department. Brooklyn commits the second most turnovers in the league this year and when you combine that with a bad defense, it explains why this team is in the mud.

If the Nets want to start putting wins together, they're going to need real play out of Michael Porter Jr. MPJ tied a season low in field goal attempts with seven on Monday night and was virtually non-existent out there. It's not easy when the team is in a rut, but as one of the vets on the team and a former NBA champion, more is expected of MPJ. He's admittedly trying to find his rhythm, and tonight would be a good time to find the right mix heading into a weekend back-to-back.

Myles Turner is another one of those players you forget has been in the league a long time. This is his 12th season in the league and he figures to be a trade candidate this winter. In theory, Turner provides floor spacing, rim protection, and deep postseason experience for any teams out there in the market. The Raptors are certainly looking for help at the five and if they ever get fully healthy, maybe Turner is the player that pushes them over the top.

Herro was the centerpiece of the trade that sent Giannis Antetokounmpo to the Miami Heat, and Bucks management is looking to see how he fits into their future plans. From Dalton Sell at Behind the Bucks Pass:

"With each passing game, it's becoming clearer that Brayden Burries and Kasparas Jakucionis need as much playing time as possible. Just 21 and 20 years old, they are oozing the type of potential that could make them the franchise's backcourt for the next decade plus. It's hard to let them entirely off the leash when players like Herro are commanding so many minutes, though.

If the Milwaukee Bucks truly believe in these two, it would make sense to move Herro and potentially even other guards like Ryan Rollins to unleash the duo. It wouldn't hurt to collect assets as well. The Bucks should be on the hunt for future draft picks and young players, and if someone offers a solid combination of those things, it would make trading Tyler Herro that much easier to stomach."

We'll see. Herro has been on a hot streak of late as he's shot above 50% from the field in three consecutive games.

Mikel Brown Jr got his first start of the season, and he'll get all the minutes he can handle now that Nolan Traore is out for the next few months. Brown Jr. looked good in his time out there against the Wolves with 17 points, and we'll see if the Nets let him play 30 minutes amid an early-season minutes restriction. Brown Jr's development is the only thing that is preventing Nets fans online from staging a full-on revolt at this point.

Even as the losses pile up for Brooklyn, Brown Jr playing well would at least give a sense of hope that things are heading in the right direction. The next step is leading the Nets in a win they should get.
