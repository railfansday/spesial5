3 things we learned about the Cowboys against the Eagles

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Dallas Cowboys hosted the Philadelphia Eagles on Thanksgiving afternoon. Here are three things we learned from the game.

We came into 2026 expecting the offense to be great. And for the most part, they've been pretty solid. But there have been bumps in the road, including the lowest points of the year thus far against Tampa Bay and Green Bay. The last three weeks, however, Dak Prescott and the offense have been on fire.

Scoring 40 against San Francisco, 51 against Tennessee, and now 38 against Philadelphia, the Cowboys offense might be the hottest in the NFL. Javonte Williams got the train rolling with the first touchdown of the day and finished with three total on the day. In totality, Dallas would put up 38 points in a shootout against their NFC East rival. Unfortunately, it was not enough, yet again losing after scoring nearly 40 points.

The offense wasn't as perfect as it needed to be, however. Failing to convert 4th-and-1 near the 50 to seal the game handed the Eagles the ball back close to striking range for the game winning field goal. It's unfair to expect perfection, but the 4th-and-1 failure certainly hurts after an amazing day from Dak Prescott and the offense.

After beating a bad Titans team last week, the Cowboys faced another offense in a slump this week, going up against former Cowboys quarterback Joe Milton.

While the defense started hot, this unit eventually allowed far too many explosive plays to an offense without a starting quarterback and with plenty of players missing elsewhere. The back end of the Cowboys defense seemed to disappear at times, especially when the pass rush didn't get home. The edges also allowed Joe Milton to break contain throughout the day, successfully throwing the ball from outside the pocket on a consistent basis.

The defense allowed a huge fourth quarter comeback in a game that Dallas should have walked away from without an issue. Going into half, the Cowboys were up 28-10. The game would end up 39-38 Eagles. A blocked punt in the fourth quarter certainly did not help matters. This was not good enough against a bad team and certainly won't be good enough against adequate competition.

This Cowboys defense is truly lost at sea with no hope for redemption this season.

If Dallas was to stand a chance at catching the Giants for first place in the NFC East, they needed to win the games they were favored, plus steal one or two against better competition. With this loss against the Eagles, Dallas finds themselves in nearly insurmountable odds to win the East and make the playoffs. The Cowboys and Giants are headed in opposite directions with time running out for a recovery.

With their playoff hopes hanging on by a thread, the defense rolled over in the second half, allowing Joe Milton and a banged up Eagles offense to make play after play. The special teams wasn't any better. A blocked punt and a missed field goal were a ten point swing that amounted to the Eagles stealing one in Dallas.

The high flying offense continues to dominate games while the defense gives them away. I'm not sure the league has ever seen such a disparity between the two sides of the ball in it's history. It's unfathomable that Dallas continues to lose games where they're scoring 35 or more points, yet here we are again.

The summary of the Cowboys season was on display Thursday afternoon for the world to see. And it likely cost Dallas any remaining, realistic hope for the playoffs.
