Bassett: World Cup hero Briana Scurry takes on new challenge on the golf course

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Briana Scurry, 1999 World Cup Women's Soccer champion goalie, has found a new passion.

Scurry is filming a series called "Breaking 99" for the LPGA, focusing on her learning to play golf. The first episode is available to watch on the LPGA's YouTube Channel.

Her coach, Trillium Rose, director of Instruction at Woodmont Country Club in Rockville, Maryland, started with short game instruction for the 55-year-old.

"I think as an Olympic gold medalist and world champion, people may kind of brush it off and say, oh yeah, she's a good athlete, of course she can do that, and it's just not true. You don't go from one sport and then automatically be good at another sport," Rose said. "No, you go to the bottom, you go to the back of the line with everybody else, and you have to start and grind out with the fundamentals. There's no skipping steps."

I spoke to Rose after she returned from the LPGA's Annika event at Pelican Golf Club in Belleair, where she played alongside Scurry in the Nov. 9 Pro-Am. Cameras documented the round and Scurry's progress for the second episode of the series.

Rose said Scurry never owned a pair of golf clubs until this year.

"To be vulnerable and debut a new golf swing in a professional LPGA-sponsored event when you've only had five hours of golf is remarkable. It takes a lot of courage," she said.

The "Breaking 99" series was created after Scurry served as a celebrity golf caddy for WNBA player Caitlin Clark during last year's Annika tournament. Clark and Scurry are both paid ambassadors for Gainbridge, the financial company that sponsors the Annika tournament.

"I've always loved a challenge. It's what drove me throughout my soccer career, and that competitive spirit hasn't gone anywhere," said Scurry in an LPGA.com report. "I was so inspired by my experience last year … I'm excited to take on a new sport with the goal of breaking 99 in honor of the '99ers ahead of the 2027 Women's World Cup."

The soccer star/golf collaboration serves as a promotion for the game of golf as well as the upcoming World Cup. Episodes will air periodically until the FIFA Women's World Cup in Brazil starts on June 24.

Scurry, a National Soccer Hall of Famer, made a huge save when the USWNT won the 1999 World Cup at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, Calif., in a penalty shootout against China in front of 90,000 fans.

Scurry's memoir, "My Greatest Save: The Brave, Barrier-Breaking Journey of a World-Champion Goalkeeper," gives insight into why Scurry is uniquely built for this challenge.

In the book, she candidly speaks of her recovery from severe head trauma and depression following her retirement from soccer.

Scurry's brain injury from a knee to the head in a professional soccer match caused her severe physical and emotional stress. She was unable to get her health insurance to pay for her ongoing concussion treatment and fell into debt. She pawned her gold medals.

Briana Scurry looks on after the United States beat Mexico 1-0 at Red Bull Arena in Harrison, New Jersey, on July 13, 2024. (Elsa/USSF/Getty Images for USSF)

Her greatest save came with the help of a woman who would become her wife. Chryssa Zizos, working as her hired publicist, helped her retrieve the medals eight months after she sold them for $18,000. Zizos also successfully pressured Scurry's insurance company to cover surgery and therapy that aided her recovery.

In her book, Scurry noted that she overcame adversity as a Black, gay woman throughout her career. Her national soccer team jersey is in the Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture. She's also a concussion awareness advocate who has testified before Congress.

She and her golf coach are an ideal fit. Rose is recognized among the Top 50 Teachers by Golf Digest and the LPGA. She volunteers her time as an instructor for the Howard University men's and women's golf teams, helping student-athletes grow through mentorship and performance development. She also volunteers her time for veterans and individuals with disabilities through her involvement with the Wounded Warriors and adaptive golf communities. She works with youngsters in the First Tee program.

"It's so important to me. I've just gotten so much from the game and so many doors have opened for me thanks to golf," she said. "I really truly believe that women can benefit from playing golf … and if you can play well enough to get invited to a pro-am or a business outing, then you have a seat at the table, you're in the conversations."

The same is true for minorities. Her work with Howard University students, in addition to instruction, is designed to help young minorities feel welcome on the golf course.

"Golf is for everyone and it's important for me to help beginners as well as high-level players. I mostly work with people who are under a 10 handicap and I'm proud of myself that I can simplify it and explain the basics to someone who hasn't heard them before," she said.

During the broadcast, Scurry gives a shoutout to Rose's techniques as a certified PGA and LPGA teaching professional.

"Trillium is fantastic. I mean, she has great eye contact with me, which is important for me when I'm trying to learn something," Scurry said.

Scurry's show is also recognizable to older golfers because her swing has to account for the wear and tear her body sustained during her professional soccer career. She suffers from spinal issues with "crushed discs" in her back, which viewers find out during the first episode.

"That's something we can all relate to, right?" Rose said.
