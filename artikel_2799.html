Two new housing reports point to bleak winter ahead, with record low sales

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Two new reports on the housing market show activity slowing even more, after a September spike in mortgage rates sent it into a deep freeze.

Pending home sales in October dropped 4.7% month-over-month and were down 16.7% from October of last year, according to the National Association of Realtors. This is the lowest level in the index's 25-year history.

Analysts had been looking for a much smaller decline, likely because mortgage rates pulled back slightly in October, only to rise sharply again last week. The average rate on the 30-year fixed started October at 7.54% according to Mortgage News Daily and ended the month at 7.27%.

The pending count is based on signed contracts — people out shopping and signing deals during the month. It is therefore an indicator of future closed sales.

"Two consecutive months of record-low pending contracts point to very sluggish fourth-quarter home sales closings, and annual existing-home sales could be the lowest since the mid-1990s," said Lawrence Yun, NAR's chief economist in a release.

Regionally, sales were down across the nation with the sharpest drops in the South, which is usually busiest for sales and the West which is most expensive.

Another report released Wednesday showed gains in home construction in October but raised a red flag going forward. Single-family housing starts rose 4% from September, according to the U.S. Census, but were down 3.2% year over year. Building permits, an indicator of future construction, dropped 8.7% for the month and were down 12.9% from October 2025. That is the lowest level for permits since May 2020, the start of the Covid-19 pandemic.

"Builders are dealing with a litany of obstacles to selling homes, whether it's rising costs of land, labor, and materials, high financing costs, or weak buyer demand," wrote Joel Berner, senior economist at Realtor.com. "This month's data shows them slowing down activity at the top of their funnel, which will result in slower months of starts and completions later on."

The construction report follows a bleak reading on homebuilder sentiment, which fell to the lowest level in 14 years in November, on the monthly index from the National Association of Home Builders. Builders cited multiple challenges, including higher mortgage rates, higher costs for energy, materials and labor, as well as weaker consumer confidence.
