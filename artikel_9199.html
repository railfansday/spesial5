Prize dedicated to African women artists expands to Senegal and announces 2026 winners

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Centre: Fatoumata Sy and Kwama Frigaux’s uncle, Mr. Pacx, representing the artist at the award ceremony. To their left (from left): prize jury members Dominique Heyse-Moore, Touria El Glaoui and Alexa Waley-Cohen. To their right (from left): Leila Zakhem, the prize director, and Valerie Lesbros, the director of the Institut Français du Sénégal

The Ghanaian-French artist Kwama Frigaux and the Senegalese Fatoumata Sy have been announced as the joint winners of the Yaa Asantewaa Art Prize. The prize, founded by the London and Accra-based Gallery 1957 in 2021 to support women artists in Ghana and its diaspora, has this year expanded to include artists from Senegal, in partnership with Institut Français du Sénégal. Frigaux and Fatoumata have been awarded $5,000 each, as well as a three-month residency (in Senegal for the Ghanaian and in Ghana for the Senegalese), and solo exhibitions.

The prize is named after the former Ashanti queen mother, renowned for her anti-colonial resistance when Ghana was under British rule. The call for applications for this year’s edition opened in June and ran until September, and was open to Ghanaian and Senegalese women artists aged 18 or older, based either in their home countries or in the diaspora. The winners were announced during Accra Cultural Week (29 October-2 November), which is organised by Gallery 1957. On view there until 5 December is The Lore of Care: Maternal Acts of Devotion, a solo show by Theresah Ankomah, the winner of the 2025 Yaa Asantewaa Art Prize.

Installation view of The Lore of Care: Maternal Acts of Devotion, Gallery 1957's show by Theresah Ankomah, who won the 2025 Yaa Asantewaa Prize

Frigaux and Sy were selected by a jury comprising Touria El Glaoui, the founder of the 1-54 Contemporary African Art Fair; the Ghanaian artist Serge Attukwei Clottey; Marie Hélène Pereira, a senior curator at Berlin’s Haus der Kulturen der Welt; Jeanne Barral, a curator at the Fondation Cartier; the collector Alexa Waley-Cohen; and Dominique Heyse-Moore, the senior curator of contemporary British art at Tate Britain.

Runners up participate in a mentorship programme, which provides support on topics such as grant applications and entrepreneurship. Mentors this year include the Nigerian artist Modupeola Fadugba and Aisha Ayensu, the founder of the Ghanaian luxury fashion brand Christie Brown.

"I think [it's] an incredible programme that helps drive the longevity of [the prize's] impact,” Leila Zakhem, the director of the prize, tells The Art Newspaper. “And it really creates a beautiful community.”

Zakhem explains that her team considered several countries for the expansion, including Nigeria. A key part of this search was finding a "very good implementing partner". The Institut Français du Sénégal, which is a non-commercial space, has a lot of credibility, she adds, “and they were very excited to partner with us on the prize”. Zakhem says that she hopes the expansion will help develop and create a bridge between art communities in English- and French-speaking West Africa.

The planned next steps are to expand further into West Africa, and then potentially beyond, with winners selected from different regions of the continent. “The goal is eventually to be the leading Pan-African female art prize on the continent,” Zakhem says.
