Rebounding Continues to Be a Problem for the Washington Wizards

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The Washington Wizards fell to the Atlanta Hawks 120-105 on Wednesday, snapping their two-game winning streak. While there were several factors that contributed to the loss, one particular statistic continues to stand out when evaluating Washington's performance this season: rebounding.

The Wizards were outrebounded 57-41 by Atlanta, including a significant 19-9 disadvantage on the offensive glass. That allowed the Hawks to generate additional scoring opportunities while limiting Washington's chances to create second-chance points of its own.

Unfortunately, this has become a recurring issue for the Wizards.

In their second game of the season against the Toronto Raptors, Washington was out rebounded 53-40, including a 19-6 disadvantage in offensive rebounds. The result was another loss. The same problem followed them into their matchups against the Charlotte Hornets and Cleveland Cavaliers. Washington was outrebounded 54-41 (20-10 in offensive rebounds) against Charlotte and 47-27 (15-5 in offensive rebounds) against Cleveland.

There is a noticeable pattern developing. The Wizards have been outrebounded in five of their first eight games. Although rebounding isn't the only factor that determines the outcome of a basketball game, the numbers suggest it has played a significant role in Washington's success and struggles.

What makes the issue particularly frustrating is the personnel Washington has available. This is a team with considerable size, length and athleticism throughout its starting lineup. Against Atlanta, the Wizards started 6-foot-8 Kyshawn George, 6-foot-9 AJ Dybantsa, 6-foot-10 Anthony Davis and 7-foot Alex Sarr. With that kind of size on the floor, Washington should have the physical tools to compete on the glass against just about any opponent.

Of course, having size doesn't automatically translate into being a good rebounding team. Positioning, effort, communication and the ability to box out are all important aspects of securing rebounds. Simply relying on height and length isn't enough. Players have to consistently find their assignments, establish position and pursue the basketball when a shot goes up.

That is where Washington needs to improve. The Wizards cannot afford to give opponents repeated opportunities to score, particularly when they are already working to establish consistency on both ends of the floor. Giving up offensive rebounds can erase otherwise solid defensive possessions and put additional pressure on the defense to get another stop.

Following Wednesday's loss, head coach Brian Keefe was asked about the team's rebounding struggles. His response, however, focused on a more recent stretch rather than addressing the larger season-long concern.

"If you've watched us, you'd know we were the number one defensive rebounding team the last two games. Give them credit … We'll take a look at the film, and we'll get better tomorrow." Keefe said.

While Keefe pointed to the team's defensive rebounding performance over the previous two games, that doesn't necessarily address the overall trend. Washington has been outrebounded in five of its first eight contests, and the disadvantage on the offensive glass has been particularly concerning.

The Wizards need to find a way to make their rebounding efforts more consistent. Whether that means placing a greater emphasis on boxing out, getting more players involved on the glass or making adjustments to their rotations, something needs to change.

Washington has shown flashes of what it can accomplish, including putting together a two-game winning streak before Wednesday's defeat. However, if the Wizards want to build on those performances and become a more competitive team, they cannot continue allowing opponents to control the boards.

There is simply too much size and talent on this roster for rebounding to remain such a consistent weakness. The season is still young, but it is an issue that needs to be addressed sooner rather than later. Otherwise, what has been a recurring problem through the first eight games could become a much bigger obstacle as the season progresses.
