Wesley Bryan clarifies his controversial tweet after The Internet Invitational finishes

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Wesley Bryan has claimed that he agrees with everyone who insisted that the Internet Invitational would not be better had the best eight players in the world replaced the worst golfers at Big Cedar Lodge.

The finale of the second Internet Invitational was posted on Thursday. Incredibly, the prize for the winners rose from $1 million in 2025 to $4 million.

The event brought together 64 content creators, with the likes of Brad Dalke and Ryan Ruffels named as team captains.

One of the big selling points of the event is the fact that some of the weaker players will make it through to the final, and potentially win. None of the three champions of the 2026 event were selected before the 20th pick in the initial draft.

But it seems that not everyone is a fan of having the higher-handicap players involved.

Wesley Bryan asked on social media whether people would prefer to see an Internet Invitational involving the best eight players in the world – including Scottie Scheffler and Rory McIlroy – replacing the worst players.

That led to plenty of backlash, particularly with Bryan highlighting the eight golfers with the highest handicaps.

But speaking on the Fore Play Podcast, Bryan insisted that he agreed with everyone who dismissed the idea of having the PGA Tour heavyweights involved instead.

"It was just a thought-provoking question of would a golf event be better if you had better players, or the best eight players in the world, and I think the general consensus, the answer was no," he said.

"When I put that out on the internet, I knew exactly what the answer was going to be because I've toyed around with those types of scenarios before. Would you rather play golf with the Bob Does Sports guys or the last three winners on the PGA Tour? I know it also goes out to my audience and the spicier golf audience on Twitter, so I kind of knew what I was getting into.

"There was obviously no harm in the tweet. And as far as just getting people talking about an online YouTube golf event, I feel like it kind of accomplished its task. Now, did I throw myself in harm's way? Absolutely, I did, but that's nothing new."

It was definitely a poor move from Bryan. While he can argue that he was always in favour of the weaker players being involved, it is not a good look to essentially admit that the question was asked to create engagement.

Certainly, the worst part of Bryan's post involved highlighting who the eight worst players were, even if he was basing the claim off of their handicaps.

That has the potential to make things feel unnecessarily personal, particularly when many would have just seen how costly playing with The Duke was for Ruffels' chances in the semi-final.

It is hard to imagine that there was a viewer of this year's Internet Invitational that liked every single creator involved. The personalities were wildly different.

But the Internet Invitational is already one of the most popular golf events in the game, with more than three million people watching the final within its first 24 hours online.

There is little reason to make wholesale changes to something that clearly resonates with many.
