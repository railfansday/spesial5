Jake LaRavia is the glue to the Lakers’ starting lineup

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

With Walker Kessler catching his breath on the bench, Devin Booker saw his opportunity.

Saucers for eyes, Booker made a dash to the rim. He caught the pass and went up for what he thought would be his easiest shot of the game.

Trailing him like his own shadow, however, was Jake LaRavia. The forward swatted the layup attempt away and then found Luka Dončić leaking out ahead for a bucket.

On the Suns' next possession, Jalen Green decided to try his luck against the Lakers' small lineup. The result was the same.

Green's drive and shot at the rim were stonewalled by the 6'8" LaRavia who acted as the team's backline protector. On the other end, the Lakers got a dunk.

These were ultimately just two possessions in another Lakers' win. But they also encapsulate why LaRavia has been an invaluable cog to the team's impressive 12-2 start.

After being inserted into the starting lineup earlier in the season due to an injury to Ziaire Williams, LaRavia's play and fit next to the Lakers' stars since has made it difficult to take him out.

While he doesn't have the springiness or shooting diversity that Williams does, LaRavia makes his impact felt by filling in the gaps.

This is because, unlike the other four players in the Lakers' starting lineup, LaRavia isn't elite or even really good at a singular thing. Instead, he helps everything click into place by being solid in multiple areas.

First and foremost, LaRavia plays hard. It's cliché, but there's immense and often overlooked value in having a player who approaches every game with care.

Sometimes his motor can run too hot, and his aggressiveness leads to whistles. LaRavia has been called for 45 fouls this season — fifth-most among all players.

But in the aggregate, his tendency to enthusiastically do all the "little things" that lead to winning is best illustrated by LaRavia's fingerprints being found all over the Lakers' leaderboards.

He currently is second on the team in deflections, second in offensive rebounds, third in blocks and fourth in total rebounds and assists. These stats also display how LaRavia functionally greases the wheels on both ends for the Lakers.

Sturdier and more stout than Williams, LaRavia has helped the Lakers' starters defensively (86th and 90th percentile in block and steal rates) and on the glass.

The team is performing better but is still poor at preventing second chances with LaRavia on, but has compensated for its weakness on one end by making it a strength on the other end. With LaRavia on, the Lakers' starters have an absurd offensive rebound percentage of 37.9% compared to just 18.2% when replaced with Williams.

LaRavia isn't the sole reason for their recent dominance on the offensive glass, but he is a big part of it.

Through his excellent timing on his crashes and knack for playing physical, LaRavia has gobbled up 8.8% of the Lakers' misses this season. That ranks in the 95th percentile among all forwards.

Beyond his work on the glass, LaRavia has also made major strides in his 3-point shooting.

After a disappointing season where LaRavia struggled from the perimeter and admittedly hit a "mental lapse," he's bounced back in a big way thanks to new shooting mechanics and rekindled confidence.

While not the most aesthetically pleasing windup, it's hard to argue with the results. LaRavia is currently shooting a blistering 53% from the corners and 48% overall from beyond the arc this season.

Those numbers will surely come down with a larger sample, but seeing them go in early, hopefully, will encourage him to keep firing throughout the year.

It's early, but this version of LaRavia on this version of the Lakers has been humming.

The starting five with LaRavia in it is outscoring their opponents by 28.3 points per 100 possessions — the second-best lineup in the NBA — and is holding teams to a defensive rating of just 93.2.

For context, not only would that be the best defense in the league, but it would be 12 points better than the current league leader.

LaRavia's game is an acquired taste. We're not talking caviar. It's more like Brussels sprouts. Hearty, versatile and kinda weird sometimes.

He'll frustratingly still pass up a shot or make an ill-advised drive. But he'll also be the first on the floor for a loose ball and in the right spot for a rotation.

He's nowhere near a perfect player, but LaRavia may be the perfect fit on this team especially at his price point.

After the Lakers were unable to land a certified starting four to replace the gargantuan hole left by LeBron James over the offseason, they headed into the year hoping to fill the void by committee. And they have so far.

To be clear, LaRavia isn't James. Nor is he even likely the long-term answer to the Lakers' starting forward position.

However, he has provided a blueprint for the type of player who works well next to the Lakers' trio of the future. He and the Lakers should chalk that up as a win. Even if he fouls a little too much.
