Ridley Scott's 148-Minute Action Epic Officially Named Paramount+ Super-Hit

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

2026 hasn’t exactly been a record year for Ridley Scott, who finally returned to the sci-fi genre at the end of the summer for his new movie, The Dog Stars. Scott enlisted stars such as Josh Brolin and Jacob Elordi to headline his new sci-fi feature, which was also the first he’d directed in almost 10 years, but the film struggled at the box office before being added to VOD platforms like Prime Video and Apple TV. Scott was once viewed as the king of the sci-fi genre with his work on older classics like Alien and Blade Runner, two franchises he is still involved with to this day. However, most fans would agree that he’s lost some of his juice, but he’ll have a chance to prove everyone wrong again next year with Hugh Jackman’s new Treasure Island remake.

Before he returned to the sci-fi genre for his new movie earlier this year, Scott had been focused mostly on historical epics the last few years. Back in 2023, he reunited with original Gladiator star Joaquin Phoenix for Napoleon, but the film bombed at the box office under the weight of its $200 million budget. Another film that also struggled at the box office was the legacy sequel to Gladiator, Gladiator II, which cost around $250 million to make. After grossing only $460 million and falling short of its break-even point, Gladiator II was recently added to Netflix, where it surged into the top 10. However, the film has been part of Paramount+’ streaming library since the start of 2025, and it’s been on a run of 300 days in the top 10.

Russell Crowe was famously left out of Gladiator II, but it took him some time to break his silence over this. However, last year, Crowe came out and slammed Gladiator II with a damning review, saying that the film is a classic example of what happens when the people involved in making a movie didn’t understand what made the first movie so special. Most fans and critics seemed to echo this sentiment as well, as most were in agreement that Gladiator II offered little more than some intense cinematic moments set against a technically proficient backdrop.
