Genesis AI Raises $500M At $3.5B Valuation To Bring Its Robots To Factories And Warehouses

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Genesis AI, a startup teaching robots to perform physical tasks by learning from human movements, announced Tuesday it has raised $500 million in Series A funding at a $3.5 billion valuation.

Premji Invest, Khosla Ventures and Eclipse co-led the round, which brings the company’s disclosed funding to $605 million since its 2024 inception. Genesis previously raised $105 million in a round announced in July 2025.

The company is developing both robots and the AI software that controls them. Its approach combines recordings of human hand movements with training in simulated environments, where robots can practice skills before attempting them in the physical world.

Genesis is initially targeting manufacturing, logistics and laboratories with its robot, Eno.

“We just want to bring something more elegant, more timeless,” co-founder and CEO Zhou Xian told Crunchbase News in an interview. “In the end, this is a very full-stack company. … We have to build all different pieces across the entire stack to make sure we can tightly integrate our technology stack together, and hopefully build the most well-polished and reliable product.

Other participants in the new financing include T. Rowe Price Associates, Durable Capital Partners, Bpifrance, HSG, Nvidia, AMD Ventures, Zoom, Samsung Next and Ericsson. Individual backers include Eric Schmidt, Eric Yuan, Jerry Yang, Xavier Niel and Zach Kirkhorn.

The company plans to use the capital to expand its computing infrastructure, increase robot production and hire more employees, Xian told Crunchbase News. Presently, Genesis has close to 100 employees. It is headquartered in Paris, with offices in San Carlos, California.

Venture funding to robotics startups has soared in 2026, with most of the investment going to companies based in China and the U.S., Crunchbase data shows. All told, global venture funding to robotics startups totaled $9.9 billion in Q3, more than double the $4.3 billion invested in Q3 2025, per Crunchbase data.

The closely related field of physical AI has also seen a surge in venture investment this year.

Genesis’s approach grew out of a challenge Xian encountered during his robotics research: The difficulty of finding enough examples for robots to learn from.

Language models have vast amounts of text to draw on. But robots need a different kind of data to learn how to move and for example, pick up and handle objects. They also need that data to better understand what they’re touching and what happens when they act.

Genesis works by using cameras and gloves to capture people’s hand and finger movements as they work, along with touch information. The company says its robotic hand closely matches the human hand and recording glove, making it easier for robots to learn those movements. Historically, collecting this kind of training data involves hiring people to remotely control robots, Xian said. Genesis aims to collect it from people doing their regular jobs instead.

Those recordings help train the company’s AI model, GENE. Genesis also uses its simulation software, Genesis World, to train and test robot skills in virtual environments. The company claims this speeds up a process that would take longer with physical machines alone.

Genesis traces its origins to a research collaboration Xian started in 2022, also named Genesis, while he was pursuing a robotics Ph.D. at Carnegie Mellon University. The project brought together researchers from more than a dozen universities to develop robotics simulation tools.

After graduating at the end of 2024, Xian teamed up with Theophile Gervet and decided to start a company. Genesis formally launched in March 2025 and expanded beyond simulation into AI software and robot hardware.

Xian said developing those components together would help Genesis make them work reliably as a finished product.

“That requires the intelligence, hardware and everything that goes into training and testing the robots to work as one system,” he said.

Genesis is exploring a rental model in which customers would pay for the robot and its software as a combined service. It has announced a partnership with LG CNS and has other Fortune 500 companies in its prospective customer pipeline, according to Xian. He declined to disclose revenue and did not provide a paying customer count.

Premji Invest partner Vedant Agrawal said in a release that the firm was drawn to demonstrations of robots completing extended tasks independently.

“Completing full tasks in real time, not just short clips, is what turns a robot into something a business can rely on,” he said.

Genesis says it is ramping up Eno production for industrial customers. Looking ahead, it plans to expand into hospitality and healthcare, then consumer and outdoor uses.
