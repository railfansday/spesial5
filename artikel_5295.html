Global stocks move upward despite shock warning from Panmure Liberum analyst who sold all equities in his personal portfolio

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

With America offline for Thanksgiving, the rest of the world enjoyed a break from the market chaos caused by U.S. tariff policy, U.S. AI investment, and U.S. interest rate rises. In Asia, stocks broadly rose. Europe was up strongly. And although New York was closed for the holiday, S&P 500 futures were up.Nasdaq 100 futures were up 0.63%, and Nvidia was up 1.29% in after-market trading.None of this should be happening!

There is nothing but bad news on the horizon, multiple Wall Street and London City analysts said in emails to Fortune.

The most dramatic warning came from Panmure Liberum’s Joachim Klement (who writes a well-regarded Substack on investing whose subtitle is “Thoughts on financial markets by a grumpy, middle-aged German. What more do you want?”)

He gave a video talk in which he announced he was selling all the equities—all of them!—in his personal investment portfolio because of the recent selloff in the S&P 500: “I think that we are heading towards a major bear market with more than 30% drawdown risk.” His official forecast for 2027 is that the S&P 500 will sink to 5,000—a level that right now seems unthinkable. His concern is that the index is currently trading below its 200-day moving average: “As Paul Tudor Jones the famous investor once said, nothing good happens below the 200-day moving average.”

Firstly, inflation is still too high. The Fed’s preferred metric, the index for personal consumption expenditures, was up year-over-year to 3.8% in October, up from 3.7% the month before. “Undesirably strong,” as Lawrence Werther and Brendan Stuart of Daiwa Capital Markets said in a note. That will almost certainly push the Fed into raising interest rates in December in order to make money more scarce—a scenario that generally augurs poorly for stocks.

Many analysts pointed to a speech given Monday by John Williams, the President of the New York Fed. He all but telegraphed that the Fed was going to kick the rate up soon: “We must take actions over time that will ensure that we get inflation back to our 2 percent goal in a timely manner and on a sustained basis. There is still work to do to get that done.” (There is a good analysis of the speech by economist Claudia Sahm here.)

Secondly, the U.S. economy is strong, so the Fed will feel it has the breathing space to dampen inflationary demand.

New jobless claims came in near their three-year low, and the long-term trend in jobless claims continues to point down, according to Thomas Simons and Blake Birkins of Jefferies.

“The big highlight … is GDP, which at 4.3% shows that the economy is booming, but unfortunately the inflation data is still too high and is unlikely to convince the Fed to remain on hold,” Chris Zaccarelli, chief investment officer at Northlight Asset Management, told Fortune via email.

“Between the AI angst and the newly announced tariffs, it doesn’t look like the Santa Claus rally is in store for us this year,” he said.

Although U.S. stocks may be sliding, that’s mostly because the tech sector is a fiasco right now. Outside the tech sector (and the outperformance of European stocks vs U.S. stocks is a good example of this), things are going rather well. Before the turkey holiday, 372 stocks in the S&P 500 moved higher, and the equal-weight index was up 0.82% on the day, Deutsche Bank’s Jim Reid noticed.
