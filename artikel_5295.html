AMD fell 8.7%, Micron dropped 10%, and Broadcom lost 6.3%, with 10 of 19 S&P 500 semiconductor stocks down at least 4%.

Nvidia declined 6.9%, extending its losing streak, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell around 4%.

The Nasdaq and S&P 500 weakened as investors reassessed AI valuations, infrastructure spending and the returns from the industry's massive capital commitments.

The AI stock sell-off is widening beyond Nvidia, with semiconductor manufacturers, memory stocks and data-center suppliers facing another round of heavy selling. Micron Technology, Broadcom, AMD and Marvell were among the biggest losers on Friday, while Nvidia extended its decline.

The momentum of the selloff became more significant: 10 of the 19 S&P 500 semiconductor stocks were down at least 4%, highlighting a sector-wide repricing rather than isolated weakness. Anthropic’s record IPO has failed to restore confidence, leaving investors focused on the cost and profitability of the AI buildout.

Semiconductor stocks were among the market’s weakest performers, with losses extending across chipmakers, equipment manufacturers and AI infrastructure suppliers.

The breadth of the decline is notable. All 19 S&P 500 semiconductor stocks were trading lower in the latest market reading, while 10 had fallen at least 4%. The group was averaging a decline of approximately 5.7% among the biggest losers.

Nvidia stock fell approximately 7%, putting shares near their lowest levels since June 2025 and extending the company’s losing streak. The decline has become a broader concern for investors because Nvidia remains a key benchmark for AI spending and semiconductor demand.

AMD, Broadcom and Micron have also faced pressure, despite strong underlying demand for AI chips and memory. Investors are increasingly questioning whether elevated valuations adequately reflect the industry’s capital requirements, competition and potential returns.

The selling has also reached companies further down the AI supply chain. Marvell, Coherent, Bloom Energy, CoreWeave and Nebius have experienced significant declines, suggesting the market is reducing exposure across the broader AI infrastructure trade.

Anthropic’s public debut was expected to provide a fresh catalyst for technology stocks. Instead, the company’s shares fell sharply following their initial surge, while chip stocks continued to weaken.

ANT stock opened at $111.50, reached $115.10 and closed its first session at $97.17—17% above its $83 IPO price. However, the stock subsequently came under pressure as the broader AI sell-off intensified.

The technology sell-off has weighed heavily on broader indexes. The S&P 500 fell 1.4%, while the Nasdaq declined 2.5% in Friday trading. The Dow Jones remained comparatively resilient, reflecting its lower exposure to technology stocks.

The market is also approaching a potentially significant losing streak, with the S&P 500 on track for its seventh consecutive daily decline. The weakness follows a sharp previous-session sell-off in which the S&P 500 dropped 2%, the Nasdaq fell 2.3% and the Dow lost 2.2%.

If Nvidia and other major chipmakers stabilize, the sector could recover some of its losses. But continued weakness across semiconductor stocks would signal that investors are still reducing exposure to the AI trade.

For now, the sell-off is less about whether AI demand exists, and more about how much investors are willing to pay for that growth.
