Winter Essentials Worth Giving—and Keeping

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Bring warmth and classic style to city days and outdoor adventures with gear from The North Face.

IT’s AN OFT-EXPRESSED sentiment, but it’s true: The holidays turn people’s attention toward friends, family, and community. There’s a beauty in the selfless, caring gifts people give to others. And while all that giving feels great, the holidays are also a good time to treat yourself. Plus, it’s getting colder.

Whether you equate The North Face with everyday city wear or outdoor adventures, it’s a solid, time-tested spot to get a gift for a loved one (or yourself). The brand has jackets and accessories with familiar winter looks, as well as products with less expected silhouettes that still offer protection from the elements in an understated style.

Here’s a look at some of the best cold-weather gear that will have you wrapping up the gift of warmth and durability… even if that gift is for yourself.

This is an easy gifting win because the warm, hooded, insulated jacket is a recognizable The North Face staple, evoking an unpretentious spin on the brand’s iconic Nuptse Jacket.

It’s familiar, but it’s also practical—the kind of gift you feel good about because it’s going to get a ton of use. (And it can withstand the repeated wears.) The attached hood offers protection from biting winds, and the straightforward color options make it easy to pair with a variety of outfits, whether it’s something fun or part of a heavy bundling during the coldest days of winter. This is the perfect type of winter coat: warm but not too puffy.

The Zaneck Jacket is a great alternative to the Aconcagua, stepping away from the outdoorsy look the brand is best known for. It’s still waterproof and insulated, but it has a longer, more modern cut.

The Zaneck comes with a removable faux fur lining for the hood, which, in addition to being warm, means there are a couple of different looks that can be teased out of it. The straight cuffs add to its stylish feel in a way that doesn’t scream “I’m dressed for warmth and not fashion,” even though it’s doing both.

The Lone Peak Triclimate 2 has a lot in common with the Zaneck. From a distance, they cut a similar silhouette. However, the Lone Peak adds a little versatility if you’re looking for a jacket that can handle a variety of climates.

A removable, insulated inner jacket is attached to a removable, insulated top layer, which is fit for rainy conditions or as a spring or fall jacket. The insulated layer converts the whole thing into a winter-ready jacket. It also sports underarm vents capable of releasing heat in the middle of a long hike, ideal for outdoorsy types. The hood is removable and it’s got adjustable cuffs and an adjustable hem, making this a do-it-all jacket.

It might be easy to ignore the Pertex Jacket. It’s a bit unassuming. It doesn’t cut as stylish of a figure as the Zaneck, but it’s endlessly useful. The lightweight Pertex fabric will keep you dry in a downpour, but it also offers warmth without marshmallow-like bulk.

The lightweight aspect makes this the kind of coat you toss in the backseat on the way to a hike, just in case. It’s also great for layering, whether it’s tossed on over a sweater or layered under an overcoat to make a favorite jacket more winter-ready.

This edition of the classic Base Camp Duffel echoes the design details we love in The North Face’s jackets. While it’s got enough style and a heap of color options for daily use, it’s built for adventure.

Made with water- and tear-resistant fabric, it boasts 71 liters of storage space, a roomier option to the similar 31-liter version. Like all of the brand’s products, it’s built as if the designers have been through the rigors of outdoor adventures (and they probably have). It’s got a sweeping D-zip opening, backpack straps, mesh pockets, and compression straps in case you’re curious if you can pack 73 liters worth of gear into the 71-liter space. (You know you’ve tried.)

The North Face has a lot of hats, none more iconic than this. The Salty Lined Beanie has a classic feel, fitting snuggly over the head with rib-knit texture, a cuffed shape, soft fabric, and ample color options to ensure it will match any jacket or accessory. The Salty Lined Beanie encapsulates the entire The North Face vibe in a small way: It’s stylish enough for a subway ride to work and practical enough for an outdoor adventure. Best of all, it’ll keep your ears warm in either instance.

Often, the best glove is the one that disappears into the outfit. It looks seamless, keeping hands warm without drawing too much attention. The Etip Recycled Glove is about functionality first and foremost, but the simple design and understated colors makes it look good with a variety of winter outfits.

While we all like to pretend we aren’t attached to our phones, the Etip makes a great modern gift because its stretchy fleece delivers a snug fit and the palms have touchscreen compatibility. That means you won’t have to take off your gloves to respond to that important text while the snow is falling.
