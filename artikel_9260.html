Casa Mouda opens Lagos gallery to promote design in Nigeria

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Papa Omotayo, Gemma Sfeir and Nifemi Marcus-Bello have helped design two spaces in Lagos for the local creative studio Casa Mouda to host exhibitions that promote design in West Africa.

Casa Mouda’s initiative includes the launch of an exhibition to mark the opening of two spaces in Lagos – aiMe Gallery at Eko Hotel and aiMe Concept on Eko Atlantic.

AiMe Gallery was redesigned by Lagos-based architect Omotayo at the entrance to the hotel’s conference centre, while aiMe Concept was designed with interior designer Sfeir and designer Marcus-Bello in a storefront in the larger Eko Atlantic development.

Both spaces feature exposed concrete walls and columns with touches of warmth, including an earthen bar and screen in aiMe Gallery.

Casa Mouda founder Caline Chagoury Moudaber said that the spaces, together, were designed to highlight the work done by designers in Lagos.

“As we began to conceive our first chapter of Casa Mouda, we knew we needed to start by recognizing some of the people who had already built so much of its foundation,” said Chagoury Moudaber.

“There are incredible designers producing on the continent that have done extraordinary work, often taking on alone what usually takes an entire industry elsewhere.”

The inaugural exhibitions include designers from Lagos, but also those who have chosen to work in the city from abroad.

Among the latter group is American designer Germane Barnes, who showcased his wooden Corona Chair, which features inverted pyramidal legs, and his take on a peacock chair, both produced in Lagos in collaboration with Casa Mouda.

Beyond his contribution to the interior layout, which includes permanent seating for the hospitality at aiMe Concept, Marcus-Bello showcased his LM stools and M2 shelves. The works showcase the production possible in Lagos, but also a more modern, minimalist angle for Marcus-Bello, who is known for his massive metal collectible design pieces.

“Nifemi Marcus-Bello has been quietly doing the legwork to set up real pathways for production for years,” said Chagoury Moudaber.

On the more craft-oriented side, the collection showcased works in bronze and wood by craftsman Jean Servais Somian, also produced in collaboration with Casa Mouda.

Servais Somian’s work includes an armoire with gridded screen wooden doors, a console with stacked oval shelves, stools in wood with painted recesses that have a graphic appearance and textural cast bronze stools and side tables. It is an impressive array showcasing the craftsman’s range.

French studio Hall Haus showcased its metal basketball hoops and chairs, a blend of African aesthetics with Parisian urban ephemera, reflecting the cross-cultural background of the studio’s members.

Meanwhile, Moroccan designer Hajar Daide showcased a mix of delicate Nigerian woven wood techniques with lush blue Moroccan fabrics for her Friction Bench.

Atmosphere was top of mind for the designers, and Casa Mouda worked with US musician Malloy James to create custom speakers at the entryway for aiMe Gallery, while aiMe Concept features a listening station.

The spaces have also been set up to further promote craft in the region through display and retail, such as textiles, bags and other smaller items such as jewellery.

“The designers who have forged the way are sharing information on what they have learned; the ones new to this area of production are bringing new techniques and perspectives,” said Chagoury Moudaber.

“It has been an incredibly collaborative, generous, and inspiring process – and we are only just beginning.”

Other recent design showcases include the inaugural Walker Design Triennial in Minnesota and Danish design initiative The Mindcraft Project, curated by Christian + Jade.
