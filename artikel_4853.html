Nasdaq exec pushes for common EU rules on tokenized securities

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Nasdaq’s Roland Chai backed exploring common EU rules for tokenized securities and said its Swedish subsidiary had applied for a DLT license.

Roland Chai, executive vice president and head of digital liquidity networks at Nasdaq, urged European Union lawmakers to explore creating a dedicated legal framework for tokenized securities to avoid legal uncertainties arising from fragmented regulations.

“We do see merit in exploring a 28th regime for dedicated, harmonized EU-level framework for tokenized securities issuance,” Chai said, speaking at the European Commission’s tokenization roundtable in Brussels on Thursday.

The term “28th regime” refers to the concept of an optional EU-wide set of rules that would apply alongside the national systems of the bloc’s 27 member states.

Chai said that Nasdaq’s Swedish subsidiary had applied for its first distributed ledger technology (DLT) trading and settlement license. Nasdaq was also considering accepting tokenized collateral, but faced constraints under current EU rules.

Chai’s comments echo a recent warning from European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde, who said Friday that Europe risks developing a fragmented tokenized securities market unless it establishes common standards. Without them, trading platforms could be unable to settle transactions with one another.

BlackRock’s head of digital cash Hannah Winter said institutional clients seeking access to regulated money market funds were driving demand for tokenization.

“For us it’s absolutely demand-driven,” Winter said at the roundtable. “The ability to leverage a tokenized money market fund increases the return on those balances, but also frees up the ability for operations to be unlocked on a 24/7 basis.”

Winter said the primary uses of tokenized funds include treasury and liquidity management, as well as collateral for derivatives positions.

In August, BlackRock announced plans to offer tokenized versions of select European money market funds using JPMorgan’s blockchain infrastructure, including pound sterling, euro and US dollar share classes from BlackRock’s Institutional Cash Series.

BlackRock’s BUIDL fund, total assets under management and monthly returns. Source: RWA.xyz

BlackRock launched its first tokenized US dollar-denominated institutional liquidity fund, BUIDL, in March 2024. The fund had $1.55 billion in assets, down nearly 30% over the past 30 days, and a trailing one-year return of 3.59%, according to RWA.xyz.
