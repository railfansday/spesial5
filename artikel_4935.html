Alan Cumming Can’t Be Tamed

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

At 61, the Traitors host has made a career of playing by his own rules. From leading his dark new series Tip Toe to reprising his X-Men role as Nightcrawler in Avengers: Doomsday, he's always eager to take on a different form.

“I’M GEARING UP towards hacking it all off,” Alan Cumming says as he twists a finger around an errant silver tendril of hair dangling across his forehead. “I usually like to end a job and do something—get it cut off or get it colored. Something.”

The 61-year-old Tony-winning-Broadway-star-turned-Emmy-winning-reality-TV-host is the consummate chameleon. In just the past year, he’s played the “dandy Scottish laird” host of The Traitors: New Blood, reprised his role as the X-Men’s resident lizard-like teleporting mutant Nightcrawler in Avengers: Doomsday, and took on one of his most emotionally and physically challenging roles as gay bar owner Leo in Tip Toe.

The impulse to change—both on screen and off—is therapeutic. “Over the years, I've had so many different hairstyles, I think, as a reaction to my childhood trauma about my father's obsession with my hair,” he says. Cumming’s memoir, Not My Father’s Son, opens with a scene of his father taking a rusty pair of sheep shears to his temple in a fit of rage. “They were blunt and dirty and they cut my skin, but my father shaved my head with them, holding me down like an animal,” Cumming writes in the first chapter.

The trauma followed him into early adulthood, and he experienced visceral anxiety whenever he got his hair cut. Eventually, however, the act of transformation transformed Cumming. “Hair was always something I felt I had no control over as a child,” he says. “So I wanted to have as much control over it and change it as much as possible, almost in a slightly perverse way.”

Of course, the choice to change isn’t always his, and sometimes the choice is taken away from him. It was “painful,” he says, to be unable to change his hair when he was working on certain projects, like his seven seasons on The Good Wife. “Over the years, I've had so many different hairstyles, but I don't often have my own hair,” Cumming adds. “It's usually for some job.”

Today, however, his natural silver hair seems purposefully unstyled as his fingers coax through it. That is, when he isn’t using his hands to assume the statuesque pose of his Traitors persona or demonstrate the boxing stance from his Marvel superhero training. Simply talking about a performance becomes a performance, his eyes bulging behind a pair of thick-rimmed black glasses and his arms gesturing widely underneath his faded red T-shirt as he describes the immense physicality required of these recent roles.

The walls of his Scottish home are white and surprisingly sparse. You’d have no idea that the person living there is now, by his own admission, best known for “swanning around” a Scottish castle “like an insane little prince” in increasingly outrageous outfits (so outrageous, in fact, that Traitors contestants often applaud when he enters the room after a wardrobe change). Or perhaps the walls are white so he stands out all the more. Either way, the Scottish charm is working overtime on a Sunday evening.

“I think both with my hair and, more lately with what I wear, I do sort of feel like a little dolly that people dress up,” he says before adding, with a good-natured laugh, “I don't mind it.”

In a brief moment between transformations, Cumming sat down with Men’s Health to discuss his ever-changing style on Traitors, how he trained for Avengers: Doomsday, and what he considers to be the ultimate fountain of youth.

MEN’S HEALTH: You often talk about your fashion choices with each Traitors season, but what goes into choosing your hairstyle?

ALAN CUMMING: There was a feeling that because we were doing the civilian version, we could reboot and bring things down a little. So, we decided just to go a little more simple and classic with the hair. But it was hilarious, because every morning I would walk downstairs and say, ‘This is toning it down. What the fuck?’ I guess I wasn't dressed as a coffin or a bride. The plan was to go back to the dandy Scottish laird a little, but it still was pretty mental. But that's why I didn't have lots of color and things in my hair. But we're about to start another Traitors season in a couple of weeks, so all bets are off again.

MH: You're in your 60s now, and you still have wonderful hair. Did you ever have a fear of going bald when you were younger, or would that have maybe been freeing in a way?

AC: I've never feared going bald. I don't think I would care. It's easy when you’re not the bald person saying this, but I don't quite think going bald has the same power it used to. I feel like so many people shave their heads nowadays anyway. And there are loads of actors I know who are bald and wear wigs all the time. When you're an actor, it's obviously easier—and actually probably better—to be bald, because you get more hairdo options.

MH: Outside of Traitors, you're also reprising your role as Nightcrawler in Avengers: Doomsday. How was doing your own stunts in your 60s?

AC: I did some stunt work, but I didn't do all my own stunts. Please don't think that. There are lots of things I'm not allowed to tell you about. I got into trouble for giving the game away earlier on. But I mean, I fight some people. There's loads of fighting in it. If you enjoy fighting and you enjoy superheroes, this movie's for you.

I loved the training. They always train you for much more than you actually need, but I did it. I was boxing with these French boys. And I’d just made a film in France, so they were very impressed with my French and the fact that I could box a little and not have a heart attack.

It’s really funny when people, especially younger people, are so impressed by your fitness. I'm quite bendy as well, so people are always impressed by my flexibility. And I guess I am 61, but, I mean, I'm not dead. I'm not a thousand years old. I've luckily not had any sort of physical things going wrong. I don't really feel any different, to be honest, but it’s also hilarious when young people are just amazed that you've been able to stand up and not die.

MH: How long were you doing this boxing training for?

AC: I only did a week or so with those boys. But I used to take boxing lessons in New York, and I really want to get back into it again. It's such good exercise, especially before you actually get up and spar—all the core stuff and strength stuff—and it's such fun as well.

My boxing teacher was called Mo, and he'd been a big sort of world champion. But one day I went to the gym, and he was really pissed off because he’d seen a music video with some rappers and singers, and they were all playing boxers. He said, "They were just terrible."

And he went, "Drake. Drake was the worst."

And he said to me, "You could take Drake." So now, every time I see Drake, I just think, “I could take you.” Drake better watch out.

MH: Let him know it's on sight. How else did you prepare your body for Avengers?

AC: I actually stay pretty fit all the time. When I'm doing The Traitors, I go to the gym every day, or I do three days gym, two days swim or yoga. There have been times in my life when I've had to get really, really super fit or super skinny or something, so you go nuts. But I like being fit, and I like stretching. I like doing things like that, so I’m pretty constant.

Filming Tip Toe was much more physically demanding, because I knew I was going to have a lot of emotional weight to carry. It was a really disturbing show. So, I would exercise a lot, but I would also get two Thai massages on the weekend because my body was so tense.

If you do a film like X-Men and you're jumping around, yes, sometimes your body can get hurt in that way, but emotionally it wasn't really very demanding, whereas Tip Toe was so intense. Physically, I'm so stressed a lot of the time, and David Morrissey, the other actor, is shoving me around. So, my body was all screwed up. What's really important in those situations is to plan ahead and to make space for you to give your body some chance to recover.

MH: How do you think about your body differently as you take on each new role?

AC: I'm a very physical actor. I think about how the person physically behaves and how they move and stand and… [laughs]. I'm laughing because my husband is just over there, and he always watches me in things and says, "Oh, you look so different. You just disappeared into this character." And then there'll be a scene when I'm running, and he can tell it's me because I run in a weird way. My running always gives me away. But you know who's got the weirdest run ever? Woody Allen. I was once in a film with him and he had to run, and I've never seen anything like it. It just was like, "How is this all happening? How are all these limbs managing to propel someone forward?" It was the craziest thing.

But I'm always very conscious of the physicality of a character. The Traitors is a big example. He's very erect, very sort of grand, quite dancy. He moves like a dancer. And then in Tip Toe, it was really fascinating becoming more cowed as the show went on. He got less confident, so I got more crushed.

With Nightcrawler, he's really fascinating because he's like a little weird lizard or something. So it's a lot of neck stuff. In the first film, I did a lot of work with a movement person to develop the language of that. So, I just remembered all that stuff for Avengers. I suppose it’s muscle memory. After 22 years, I really did remember all that stuff. Also, they gave me a gray wig, which I thought was hilarious. That was a concession to my elder statesman-like thing. I've got gray hair as Nightcrawler now, which is just hilarious. Nothing else has changed, just his hair has grown gray.

MH: You mentioned your longtime husband, Grant. What do you think makes a relationship last?

AC: Having a laugh, being open and kind. Being kind is the biggest thing, but being honest and always trying to talk things through and having a laugh. Also, having time apart.

When I go away to make a film or whatever, we don't see each other for a few weeks. It's actually really great because you miss that person, and you look forward to seeing them and you value the time you do have. It's not just about missing them, but you remember the things that you've always liked, and you're constantly reassessing your relationship because you're looking forward to seeing that person again. Also, it means you maintain your own identity. Obviously, we're a very strong couple, but we have our own lives as well.

So it's a combination of those things, but I really do think you can't underestimate how much being able to laugh is. Also, I think it's good to still have sex. Sex and laughter. I think if either of those go or both of those go, get out, taxi for one. Because they're both the same thing, I suppose—they're sensual pleasures. Those are very important for me, anyway.

MH: Agreed. And so, you mentioned that you're 61 now—

MH: As we all should. But tell me, what's the best part about being 61?

AC: There are lots of nice things. I mean, partly that I forget I'm 61. On the show I'm doing right now, I feel like I can just chat with the kids on the crew who are in their 20s. For me, what's great is being in a position where I'm able to have such strong, long relationships, friendships. With Tip Toe, David Morrissey and I have known each other since the late '80s. Acting against each other in such a horrible way was so great because we were so close and trusted each other. And that was a really good thing about being older—you're so sure of someone and so trust someone. Also, I just love having a life where I do have friends in their 20s and 30s and 40s.

I equate oldness with lack of curiosity. You can be very old and still be very curious, but whenever anyone says, “Oh, I'm too old for this,” I think it's like a death knell somewhere. A little fairy dies when you say that.

I enjoy not giving so much of a fuck. I'm really happy in my life and things don't bother me as much as they used to. I think it'll all come out in the wash. We'll work it out. I feel I'm much more sanguine about things, especially as I've got such a crazy life with so many things going on. It's always this sort of mental schedule and trying to make it all work, but I just now think, “It'll be fine.” So that's a good thing about being older.

MH: What are you most curious about these days?

AC: Lots of things. I'm running a theater now and every day there are things I've got to deal with and talk about and find out about, and that's amazing. I joined TikTok today, so I decided to do that. I want to learn to play the bagpipes. I want to learn to type properly because, I mean, I use my computer, obviously, and I've written books and films, but I've never really properly learned to type. And I want to get better at Spanish, and I have a sort of trad wife fantasy of making gluten-free scones in my AGA, which is a kind of country stove.

AC: I don't really feel this age, but when I was 33, I moved to New York, and I feel like my life hasn't really changed that much since then. I still live in the same area. I still have a lot of the same friends. I do a lot of the same things. I have the same kind of life. I don't go out as much as I used to, but I love dancing still, and I would do that more if people didn't take photos of me all the time. Before mobile phone cameras, you didn't have to worry about being off your tits and dancing in a club with your chums. Now you do.

I'm obviously not 33. I don't look 33. But I feel like I'm as fit as I was when I was 33, kind of.
