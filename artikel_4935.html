Gratitude for Our Role Models

In these challenging times, new research shows that gratitude practice can bring greater resilience and well-being. Research participants who engaged in a gratitude practice for four weeks reported lower stress, increased positive emotions, and a greater ability to cope with challenging situations (Flores and colleagues, 2026).

In this season of Thanksgiving, we can express gratitude for our role models, people who have inspired us by their example (Sariani and Nugraha, 2026). Research has long shown the benefits of gratitude and how much we are influenced by our role models (Bandura, 1986; Bricheno and Thornton, 2007; Emmons, 2007).

Recent studies show that we can find role models in our teachers and professional athletes (Obenda, 2026; Sariani and Nugraha, 2026). We can also find them in entertainers, and, for many of us, our first role models are our parents (Assibey-Mensah, 1997; Clark, Martin, and Bush, 2001).

My first role model was my father, Colonel Frank H. Dreher, who grew up on a small Kentucky farm during the Depression.

While working on the farm one afternoon, he saw a small plane flying overhead and ran to the local airport. Pilots would give a boy a quarter to help tie their plane down, and a quarter was a lot of money during the Depression.

When Frank reached the airport, a bright yellow biplane had just touched down. Inspired by its power and freedom, he knew he had to fly. Each day, after school, he’d return to the airport, helping the pilots tie down their planes, wash and refuel them. Sometimes they took him up on short flights. He did odd jobs around the airport and persevered to follow his dream. At age 16, he got his pilot’s license and was featured in the Louisville Courier-Journal as the youngest pilot in Kentucky.

My father became a flight instructor, a pilot during World War II, and an Air Force colonel who flew jets and air rescue helicopters. I’ve always admired him for his courage to soar high above the ground to follow his dreams (Dreher, 2025).

I’ve also been inspired by Eleanor Roosevelt, who had an unhappy childhood—constant criticism from her mother and an alcoholic father. Both parents died when she was a child, leaving her to be raised by her mother’s neglectful relatives. Yet she developed resilience and a heart of compassion, offering hope to so many people during the Depression and World War II. Her example inspires me to this day.

I’m also inspired by Jane Goodall. She grew up during World War II as a sensitive and curious child who loved nature. Climbing the tall tree beside her house, she dreamed of studying animals in Africa. She followed her curiosity and persevered. Her close observations of chimpanzees led to new scientific discoveries. And her love of nature led her to travel through the world, encouraging us to care for all the life on this beautiful planet.

Was it a parent, teacher, relative, or neighbor? Was it someone in history, someone in the news, or someone else?

If you feel grateful for this person, what are you thankful for?

What qualities about this person do you admire?

And how might you want to develop more of these qualities for yourself?

Acknowledging our role models and our gratitude for them can help us cope with our own life’s challenges with greater resilience and hope.
