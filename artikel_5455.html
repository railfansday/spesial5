Grupo Supervielle Reports 3Q26 Results

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Return to loan growth, with net loans up 1.3% QoQ in real terms and accelerating to 3.7% MoM in September

NPL ratio improved to 5.4%, approximately 200 bps below industry, with net cost of risk down 100 bps to 4.6%

â Lower cost to serve, with personnel expenses excluding severance down 14% QoQ and administrative expenses down approximately 10%

BUENOS AIRES, Argentina, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Grupo Supervielle S.A. (NYSE: SUPV; BYMA: SUPV), ("Supervielle" or the "Company") a universal financial services group headquartered in Argentina with a nationwide presence, today reported results for the three- and nine-month period ended September 30, 2026.

Starting 1Q20, the Company began reporting results applying Hyperinflation Accounting, in accordance with IFRS rule IAS 29 ("IAS 29") as established by the Central Bank.

Commenting on third quarter 2026 results, Patricio Supervielle, Grupo Supervielle's Chairman & CEO, noted: "The third quarter marked a return to loan growth as we began to deliver on the priority we outlined last quarter: selectively expanding lending in strategic products and sectors with stronger credit profiles while maintaining disciplined risk management and a clear focus on profitability. Our net loan portfolio grew 1.3% sequentially in real terms, with momentum strengthening in September, when loans increased 3.7%, and 2% in October.

Asset quality continued to improve. Our NPL ratio edged down to 5.4%, remaining 200 basis points below the industry, while net cost of risk declined by 100 basis points sequentially to 4.6% and loan loss provisions fell 14.5%. Although conditions remain challenging, particularly for some SMEs, these results reinforce our confidence in our approach to managing credit risk and rebuilding lending momentum.

On the funding side, we actively managed our deposit base as interest rates increased, reducing high-cost institutional peso deposits (as interest rates increase) to better align funding with our lending needs and contain interest expense, while dollar deposits continued to grow.

Net interest margin decreased to 16.0%, while the year-to-date margin stood at 18.0%, within our full-year guidance range. As anticipated, the temporary benefits we highlighted last quarter unwound as asset yields repriced, following the prior quarter decline in funding costs. Lower inflation reduced income from inflation-linked loans, and lower investment securities gains also contributed to margin compression. Renewed loan growth is an important step toward strengthening our earnings capacity, with its contribution is expected to build as origination volumes recover. We reported net income of AR$6.8 billion, equivalent to 2.2% ROAE. Excluding extraordinary severance charges associated with the completion of our rightsizing plan, adjusted net income was AR$10.7 billion, equivalent to 3.4% adjusted ROAE.

The transformation of our operating model is translating into a lower cost to serve. Personnel expenses excluding severance charges declined 14% sequentially, while administrative expenses fell approximately 10%, reflecting savings from branch consolidation and a leaner operating structure. Although lower revenues weighed on efficiency during the quarter, the year-to-date efficiency ratio excluding extraordinary severance charges stood at 55.5%. Building revenues on this lower cost base remains central to improving operating leverage and sustainable profitability.

On November 5, we marked an important milestone in Supervielle's transformation, unveiling a new brand identity that brings to life the integrated financial ecosystem we have been building over the past two years. Our renewed SuperApp connects everyday banking, family finances and investing through "SuperFamilia", closer integration with IOL Inversiones and AI-assisted customer service needs within a simpler, more personalized experience. We also launched "Pollen X", our Banking as a Service platform, enabling companies, fintechs and digital platforms to embed our financial services into their own offerings. Together, these capabilities strengthen our role in customers' everyday financial lives and open new opportunities for growth through a broader range of channels.

At IOL, assets under custody increased 20% sequentially in U.S. dollar terms to US$3.5 billion, while total accounts reached 2.5 million. Assets under management in mutual funds managed by IOL also continued to grow, reaching US$ 492 million at quarter-end. This growth underscores the opportunity to connect our banking and investment capabilities more closely and support a broader range of our customers' financial needs.

Looking ahead, our priorities remain clear: sustain the improvement in asset quality, build on the return to lending growth and continue our permanent agenda of lowering the cost to serve into stronger, sustainable returns. Argentina's low level of financial penetration presents a significant long-term opportunity. With a solid CET1 ratio of 13.5%, we are well positioned to pursue it through disciplined risk management, close customer relationships and a more efficient operating model. As we mark ten years as a publicly listed company, we look forward to sharing our Five-Year vision and strategic priorities at our Supervielle Day on November 23," concluded Mr. Supervielle.

The Company reported an Attributable Net Income of AR$.6.8 billion in 3Q26, compared to Ps.13.6 billion in 2Q26 and an Attributable Net Loss of AR$.67.0 billion in 3Q25. In 9M26, the Company posted an Attributable Net Income of Ps.1.1 billion, compared to an Attributable Net Loss of Ps.35.9 billion in 9M25. Group headcount was 16% below year-end 2025 levels at quarter-end, following the headcount rightsizing plan implemented in 1H26 across its Banking ecosystem to align its workforce with a more efficient distribution model, as a growing share of customer activity migrates to digital and virtual hub service channels. Excluding severance charges, which in 3Q26 were limited to residual charges related to the headcount rightsizing plan implemented in 1H26, the Company posted an Adjusted Net Income of AR$.10.7 billion in 3Q26 and AR$.56.5 billion in 9M26.

During 3Q26, the structural improvements implemented in recent quarters became increasingly visible in the Company's efficiency metrics, with a lower cost base driving the cost-to-assets ratio to 7.9%, its lowest level in the last five quarters. Credit costs also continued to decline, reflecting the sustained impact of portfolio management, collection and refinancing initiatives undertaken since 2025, together with a disciplined and cautious origination strategy. These trends translated into improved asset quality indicators, with the NPL ratio improving to 5.4% and net cost of risk easing to 4.6%.

However, these improvements were more than offset by lower Net Financial Income, reflecting the anticipated unwinding of the temporary spread benefits observed in the previous quarter, together with lower returns on UVA loans and CER-adjusted securities as inflation slowed. As a result, Net Income declined sequentially. In 9M26, results continued to reflect a transitional period, with underlying cost and credit trends improving.

3Q26 ROAE was 2.2% while adjusted ROAE was 3.4%, and Structural ROAE, which reflects the full run-rate of the salary savings associated with the headcount reductions under the voluntary retirement program, as if those reductions had been fully in effect throughout the period, was 3.5%. ROAA was 0.3%. 9M26 ROAE was 0.1%, while adjusted ROAE was 6.1% and structural ROAE was 7.2%.

In 3Q26, the Company reported Profit Before Income Tax of AR$.12.2 billion, compared to a Profit Before Income Tax of AR$.20.4 billion in 2Q26 and a Loss Before Income Tax of AR$.108.3 billion in 3Q25. This result included approximately AR$.5.9 billion in residual severance charges, compared with AR$.38.1 billion in 2Q26. Excluding these charges, Adjusted Profit Before Income Tax, was AR$.18.1 billion in 3Q26.

The sequential decline primarily reflects a 22.4% QoQ decrease in Net Financial Income, to AR$.241.8 billion, as the lagged repricing of interest-earning assets to lower market rates and to lower inflation, compressed spreads following the exceptional margin expansion recorded in 2Q26. This was partially offset by a 26.5% QoQ reduction in personnel and administrative expenses, to AR$.141.5 billion (down 12.2% excluding severance charges in both quarters), and a 14.5% QoQ reduction in Loan Loss Provisions, which declined to AR$.61.5 billion, extending the downward trend from the AR$.133.9 billion peak reached in 4Q25. Additionally, the loss from the net monetary position (RECPAM) declined 1.8% QoQ as inflation moderated. Net Service Fee Income decreased 6.7% QoQ to AR$.53.2 billion, primarily reflecting lagged fee repricing, as account package repricing was only implemented in September, and lower credit card commissions amid lower  loan volumes, together with a weaker performance in asset management fees, partially offset by a 4.1% increase in net fee income from the IOL brokerage business.

On a cumulative basis, the Company posted a Profit Before Income Tax of AR$.7.4 billion in 9M26, compared to a Loss Before Income Tax of AR$.73.7 billion in 9M25. Excluding extraordinary severance charges, Adjusted Profit Before Income Tax amounted to AR$.92.6 billion in 9M26.

3Q26 Net Financial Income totaled AR$.241.8 billion in 3Q26, decreasing 22.4% QoQ and increasing 45.0% YoY. The sequential decline reflects the unwinding of the temporary spread benefits observed in the previous quarter, as anticipated. In 2Q26, the sharp decline in market rates, with average TAMAR falling to 22.8% from 32.0% in 1Q26, was passed through quickly to funding costs, while asset yields adjusted with a lag, temporarily widening spreads. In 3Q26, average TAMAR edged up slightly to 23.5%, keeping funding costs broadly stable, while interest-earning assets continued to reprice to the lower rate levels reached in the prior quarter, compressing spreads, together with lower inflation, which reduced the accrual on inflation-linked (UVA) loans and CER-adjusted securities. YoY, the increase reflects the recovery from the exceptionally compressed margins of 3Q25, when the monetary tightening ahead of the mid-term elections drove the average TAMAR to 46.4%, alongside historically high reserve requirements. Net Financial Income plus the Result from exposure to changes in the purchasing power of the currency totaled AR$. 208.5 billion, decreasing 24.9% QoQ, but increasing 63.1% YoY.

In 9M26, Net Financial Margin amounted to AR$841.1 billion, increasing 16.4% from 9M25.

Net Interest Margin (NIM) was 16.0% in 3Q26, declining 430 bps QoQ from the exceptionally high level recorded in 2Q26, but 520 bps above YoY. As anticipated in the previous quarter, the sequential compression reflects the lagged repricing of interest-earning assets to the lower rate environment, after funding costs had adjusted faster in 2Q26, as well as lower inflation, which reduced the accrual on inflation-linked (UVA) loans and CER-adjusted securities. The impact was reflected across the balance sheet: Loan Portfolio NIM was 17.2%, down 360 bps QoQ, and Investment Portfolio NIM was 13.7%, down 410 bps QoQ. AR$ NIM was 19.4% in 3Q26, 480 bps lower QoQ but 770 bps higher YoY. In pesos, loan portfolio NIM fell 450 bps to 21.8%, as loan yields caught up with lower market rates, while the AR$ cost of funds remained broadly stable. The peso investment portfolio NIM contracted 560 bps to 14.4% reflecting lower yields on AR$ government securities although 1,250 bps above 3Q25 level.

YoY, the expansion in NIM reflects the normalization of funding costs from the exceptionally elevated levels of 3Q25, when the monetary tightening ahead of the mid-term elections drove average TAMAR to 46.4%, compared to 23.5% in 3Q26.

The Total NPL Ratio was 5.4% as of September 30, 2026, down from 5.5% as of June 30, 2026 and approximately 200 bps below the industry average. While the ratio continues to reflect the carry-over effects of credit stress from prior quarters in a challenging macroeconomic environment, portfolio dynamics continued to improve, with delinquency indicators declining consistently since March. The 90-day delinquency ratio was 4.4% at the end of 3Q26, 100 bps below the reported NPL ratio. This gap reflects exposures classified as non-performing under Central Bank criteria that were performing or less than 90 days past due according to the Bank's records. The Retail NPL ratio declined to 8.6% from 9.6% in June, while the retail 90-day delinquency ratio improved to 7.1% from 7.9%.

Quarterly NPL formation declined for the third consecutive quarter and stood approximately 25% below the 4Q25 peak, with retail NPL formation down 44% from that peak.

Measured in constant pesos, the stock of retail loans more than 90 days past due declined every month since February 2026, ending the quarter 27% below its peak and 16% below June. This reflects improved collection and refinancing dynamics resulting from active portfolio management and a disciplined origination strategy in place since early 2025, with origination cohorts since 4Q25 continuing to perform meaningfully better. In contrast, the commercial portfolio showed some deterioration during the quarter, with higher delinquency and NPL formation, reflecting the lagged impact of the high interest rate environment in prior months on SME customers. While conditions remain challenging, the sustained improvement in retail and the stronger performance of recent origination cohorts reinforce our confidence in our credit risk management approach.

Loan Loss Provisions (LLPs) declined 14.5% QoQ to AR$61.5 billion in 3Q26. This reduction was consistent with easing delinquency trends throughout the quarter and reflects the early benefits of the collection and refinancing initiatives implemented since December 2025, together with disciplined risk-adjusted loan origination. The Coverage Ratio was 92.8% as of September 30, 2026, compared to 98.9% as of June 30, 2026, and 112.2% as of September 30, 2025. The decline reflects the contraction of the retail portfolio over recent quarters, where loans require higher provisioning. At the same time, NPL formation increased in the commercial portfolio, where loans require lower allowances relative to their outstanding balance under the IFRS 9 expected credit loss model. On an adjusted basis, considering loans to individuals more than 90 days past due at the Bank, the coverage ratio was 105%.

Efficiency Ratio was 61.3% in 3Q26, compared with 63.4% in 2Q26. The sequential improvement mainly reflects lower severance charges, which declined to approximately AR$5.9 billion in 3Q26 from AR$38.1 billion in 2Q26, with 3Q26 charges mainly reflecting residual costs related to the voluntary retirement program. Excluding these items, the adjusted efficiency ratio would have been 59.2% compared with 52.3% in 2Q26. The increase in the adjusted efficiency ratio reflects the 19.7% sequential decline in revenues, driven by lower net financial income as asset yields continued to reprice downward while funding costs remained broadly stable, compressing spreads. This more than offset the 9.2% decline in operating expenses excluding severance charges, reflecting continued cost discipline and the benefits of the structural efficiency initiatives implemented across the organization. In 9M26, the efficiency ratio was 64.6%. Excluding AR$ 85.2 billion of extraordinary severance costs, the 9M26 efficiency ratio would have been 55.5%.

The Loans to Deposits Ratio was 82.8% as of September 30, 2026, compared to 72.6% as of June 30, 2026, and 67.3% as of September 30, 2025. The QoQ increase mainly reflects the 11.6% decline in deposits during the quarter reflecting asset and liability management, combined with the 0.9% growth in the net loan portfolio.

Total Deposits were AR$ 5,587.6 billion at quarter-end, decreasing 11.6% QoQ and 17.2% YoY. The sequential decrease was driven by asset and liability management initiatives reducing high-cost institutional peso deposits and a seasonal decrease in savings accounts.

Total private sector deposits were AR$ 5,367.6 billion, decreasing 11.5% QoQ and 17.8% YoY. AR$ deposits totaled AR$ 3,676.0 billion, decreasing 15.9% QoQ and 17.8% YoY.

The QoQ performance of AR$ Deposits, down 15.9%, or AR$ 695.2 billion, primarily reflects lower institutional funding, together with the reversal of the seasonal increase in retail deposits recorded in the previous quarter. During the quarter, the Company continued to actively manage its funding mix, reducing higher-cost institutional time deposits as well as corporate and public sector deposits, and partially replaced them with alternative funding sources, mainly repo transactions. As a result, the blended AR$ and US$ cost of funds declined approximately 50 bps QoQ, while the AR$ cost of funds increased only 10 bps sequentially, significantly less thanthe 70 bps increase in average TAMAR, limiting the impact of higher market rates on funding costs.

Foreign currency deposits amounted to US$1.3 billion, increasing 1.5% QoQ and 0.9% YoY.

Total Assets were AR$9,114.6 billion as of September 30, 2026, decreasing 1.3% QoQ and 8.4% YoY. The sequential performance mainly reflected: i) a 9.3%, or AR$201.0 billion, decrease in the public sector investment portfolio and repo transactions reflecting asset and liability management decisions following the reduction in institutional deposits, partially offset by: ii) a 17.1%, or AR$43.1 billion, increase in the corporate notes investment portfolio. In addition, iii) the loan portfolio (net of provisions) increased 1.3%, or AR$ 58.1 billion, driven by 13.4%  increase in US dollarâdenominated loans partially offset by a 1.7%   decline in peso-denominated loans, as the Company prioritized disciplined origination and attractive risk-adjusted returns over volume, while iv) cash and due from banks remained broadly stable declining 1.0%, or AR$ 14.1 billion, reflecting lower regulatory liquidity needs at quarter-end. The leverage ratio (Assets to Shareholders' Equity) was 7.3x, decreasing 10 bps QoQ, from 7.4x as of June 30, 2026, and 60 bps YoY, from 7.9x as of September 30, 2025. The average leverage ratio in 3Q26 was 6.9x, stable compared with 2Q26 and 3Q25.

Total Loans amounted to AR$4,624.0 billion as of September 30, 2026, increasing 0.9% QoQ and 1.8% YoY and 155.5% since March 31, 2024. Loan growth since March 31, 2024, has significantly outpaced the industry's 142% increase, while remaining in line with the industry on a YoY basis. Net loans amounted to AR$ 4,383.0 billion as of September 30, 2026, increasing 1.3% QoQ and 1.1% YoY.

The sequential performance was primarily driven by a 13.4% increase in U.S. dollar loans, mainly to corporate clients in export-oriented sectors, which continued to lead economic activity, while pesoâdenominated loans decreased 1.7% reflecting soft loan demand and a disciplined origination strategy focused on attractive risk-adjusted returns.

By segment, quarterly performance was driven by commercial loans, which increased 4.2%, while retail loans declined 5.1%. Lending activity gained momentum toward quarter-end, with total loans increasing 3.7% in September compared to August. Retail loan origination also picked up, with origination volume in September 20% higher than in August. On a YoY basis, commercial loans grew 24.6%, while retail loans declined 19.4%, reflecting the disciplined approach to retail origination maintained since early 2025.

Common Equity Tier 1 Ratio (CET1) was 13.5% as of September 30, 2026, declining from 14.2% in the prior quarter but remaining 30 basis points higher than a year earlier. The sequential decline primarily reflects higher RWA density, driven by an increase in loans and an increase in market risk associated with the expansion of the corporate notes portfolio.
