Amazon acquires key assets of AI startup Decart for reported $7.5B

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Amazon.com Inc. today announced plans to hire the technical staff of startup Decart.AI Inc. and acquire its core technology.

The company didn’t disclose the terms of the deal. However, sources told Bloomberg that Amazon is paying about $7.5 billion. That’s nearly twice the valuation Decart reportedly received after its most recent funding round in May.

In August, reports emerged that the company had drawn takeover interest from Anthropic PBC. The artificial intelligence provider reportedly sought to buy Decart for $6 billion but walked away from the deal in September. Rumors that Amazon could swoop in emerged the following month.

Decart founders Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf will join the tech giant along with Decart’s research team. Additionally, Amazon is acquiring the technology behind the company’s DOS platform, which helps boost the efficiency of AI models. Decart claims that the software can reduce the cost of some neural networks by a hundredfold.

The company hasn’t shared detailed information about how DOS works. However, the blog post in which Decart announced its May funding round did reveal that the platform optimizes AI models by “tuning the kernels.”

Kernels are the basic building blocks of neural networks. They’re relatively simple code snippets designed to run across a large number of chip cores. One common approach to optimizing them, kernel fusion, combines multiple kernels into a single code snippet that uses less hardware. In July, Decart disclosed that a custom model it had developed with DOS uses kernel fusion.

The company says that the platform also includes “proprietary compilers.” AI compilers translate neural networks into the platform-specific programming language of the host chip. They also optimize the models along they way. For example, the compiler that Amazon Web Services offers for its AWS Trainium machine learning chip uses quantization to compress models into a more memory-efficient form.

It’s unclear whether Decart positions DOS as an alternative to the default compilers provided with AI accelerators. According to Amazon, Decart has “worked closely” with its engineers since 2025 to optimize the performance of workloads deployed on its Trainium chips. The newest commercially available iteration of the chip, Trainium3, provides 2.52 petaflops of performance when processing FP8 data.

Amazon stated that the acquisition will help “further optimize both its inference and custom silicon performance.” According to the company, the goal is to not only speed up customers’ models but also lower their cost.

“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, Amazon’s senior vice president of foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing. “More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost.”

The acquisition doesn’t include Decart’s portfolio of internally developed AI models. Its two newest algorithms, Lucy 2.5 and Oasis 3, are world models that generate virtual environments based on prompts. The former algorithm is optimized for low-latency content creation, while the latter is designed to generate training data for robots.
