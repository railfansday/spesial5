Back to the Future: Real Acquisition Helps Montana Broker Return to REMAX

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Back in 2014, DJ Smith launched his real estate career in Missoula, Montana, as an agent with REMAX. From there, he ventured in several different directions, including management roles at other brands and leadership positions in both local and state associations.

This past September, however, when Smith decided to open his own brokerage, he went back to where it all began and opened REMAX Confluence in Missoula. With market challenges on the upswing and brokerage consolidation steamrolling through the industry, Smith believed that aligning with REMAX was more important than ever, especially as the brand reinvents itself under the Real banner.

Here, Smith shares the thinking behind his decision, his gameplan for success, and what he believes lies ahead, both for himself and the industry at large.

Describe your journey since your early days as an agent, and what led you back to REMAX.

DJ Smith: As I evolved in my career, I had opportunities to manage at Engel & Völkers as well as Berkshire Hathaway. Then I made the decision: I want to be the owner. I don’t just want to be the face of decisions. I want to own the decisions. I had this measuring stick, looking at what these other franchises had to offer against what REMAX had to offer, and for a variety of different reasons, they just never met that same level of standard. So I started doing research, and at that particular junction, Real announced its intention to acquire REMAX. For me, that was actually even more positive.

DJ Smith: I have been past president of not only the local association, but the state of Montana, and I had the distinct privilege of being the president during the (Burnett v. NAR) settlement, and working through and navigating those changes. I think real estate as a whole is drastically changing, and to affiliate yourself with a brand that is adapting with it rather than trying to just stick to the old model was intriguing. In this landscape, where agents are finding that they don’t need a full-time office, but there’s value in (in-person) collaboration, I think the companies that are going to thrive are somewhere in that middle.

And you view Real REMAX as that type of company?

DJ Smith: I think Real’s acquisition of REMAX confirmed that. (Real has) had tremendous growth, but full cloud has its ceiling. Some people, most people, still want to interact with a team and have that collaborative nature. I think the REMAX of tomorrow is going to be that hybrid model that gives the latest and greatest tools, but still has the functionality of an office space. I think REMAX is uniquely positioned to thrive, and that’s why I associated myself with a brand that is for tomorrow.

How does being part of Real help facilitate a physical office?

DJ Smith: At my previous office, we had 60 agents and six support staff, including myself. That is a really hard ratio to be competitive in this commission landscape where margins are getting thinner for brokerages. I think the product suite that Real is going to offer has a lot of that backend so that you can be competitive and still have a physical office space. I’m going to be able to be efficient on the backend to provide what agents desire, which is competitive caps and commission plans, as well as still receiving that support that they expect.

I understand you participated in a broker call with new REMAX President Alex Vidal. What are your initial thoughts?

DJ Smith: His energy level is to a whole other degree. The coolest part was he was referencing doing exactly what I have done with recruiting, and understanding those obstacles. Having a (leader) that has been exactly where you are is such a unique thing. Alex is going to bring what’s happening on the ground to corporate. It’s not someone who’s been trained to say certain things. It’s someone who’s actually been where we have been, to be able to give perspective. That’s the power that Alex is going to bring.

How did you choose the name, REMAX Confluence?

DJ Smith: In Missoula specifically, we’re in a naturally beautiful place where we have three river confluences within a mile or two. But more importantly, the message that I want agents to hear is that when two brands collide, the agent’s brand and the brokerage’s brand, it makes a stronger river. And so as we flow in hopefully the right direction, we are making each other stronger, not overpowering; we’ve come together versus floating our separate ways.

Prior to opening the brokerage, you were heavily involved in association leadership at critical times for residential real estate…

DJ Smith: Yeah, I had the unique privilege of being the local president during COVID in 2020 and the unique privilege of being the state president in 2023.

How did you lead members during the changes brought about by the NAR settlement in 2023?

DJ Smith: I think a lot of Realtor members felt betrayed by that settlement and felt like they got the receiving end of a bad deal. My goal in that was listening to them, understanding that I’m an operator and practitioner just like they are. In this world of post-settlement, why we got sued was because we had a lack of communication as an industry to consumers, so what we can do now is go above and beyond in our communication value.

This is my first career and one that I absolutely love, and I recognize that if I’m going to be in it in the next 30 years, there’s going to be a lot of change that I have to accept, and thrive in that. That’s what’s going to define me.

Will you pursue association involvement in the future?

DJ Smith: I’m evaluating ways to affect change. Is NAR participation the way, or can I affect change here locally by leading the best office I possibly can and produce practitioners that are the best in the business? I think that’s where my energy is today. It doesn’t mean it won’t shift in the future, but right now I know if I spend three more hours with an agent rather than three hours in a committee, they’re going to get better, and consumers are going to get better because of that.

How do you feel about what’s happening in the MLS environment?

DJ Smith: If we want to thrive as an association or MLS, we have to stop getting in the way of business operators so that they can find the path, and then put the nice boundaries around it. There are some entities out there right now that are really pushing down on the model of MLSs, and hands down, one of the best evolutions, and what most countries try to strive for, is a multiple listing service that actually thrives. I don’t think people recognize the impact it has on the secondary housing market and government agencies relying on the data that’s coming from this. If we start crumbling and compromising that because we don’t want to share data anymore, it’s going to get really bad really fast for consumers.

What’s going on in your market, and how are some of the macroeconomic trends everyone’s talking about playing out for you?

DJ Smith: In Missoula specifically, we’re a regional hub. Generally, people go as far as 60 miles, maybe even 120 miles out. Our median sales price is $680K, and we have 4.8 months of supply. But what is hidden in that is we have two distinct markets. We have a market under $750K, and we have a market above $750K. And in Missoula and the region specifically, under $750K, we have months supply hovering right around 2.5, 2.4. So it’s actually a really aggressive buyer’s market with over 50% of those listings receiving offers at or above list. Now, if you turn that and we go above $750K, that’s where the story changes. It accelerates drastically. The next benchmark, $850K to $950K has over eight months of supply. And then when you get past $1 million, that jumps up to 21 months of supply. So this is a really bifurcated marketplace.

How are you helping agents navigate the market?

DJ Smith: More important than ever, I think you have to be strategic about the consumer that you’re going after, because I could get a listing tomorrow for $2 million, but I’m also going to spend tens of thousands of dollars to market that for 21 months, and not receive much commission at the end. So at this particular time, it’s really about being analytical and understanding, okay, this is the consumer that’s turning right here. Although we have the step-up buyer frozen, there are still life changes. 3.9 million homes are going to sell this year. Those are all life-change opportunities. So it’s identifying those changes. Get involved with your local hospital so that when doctors are shifting into your marketplace, you’re going to be in the position to talk to them. Talk to the human resource officers of major employers in your marketplace so that you are the person they call. Targeting those life-change opportunities, that’s where you’re going to gain traction in this market.

What’s the plan for your firm’s future? How much do you want to grow, and how are you going to get there?

DJ Smith: Right now we’re at 10 agents (at press time). My goal is by the end of this year, we’re going to be at 20. And I think that is completely manageable given the fact that this is a relationship business. Just because the affiliation attached to my current brokerage has changed, it doesn’t change who I am. And that’s why I launched my podcast (Recon: by REMAX Confluence). I recognized I was managing the second-largest brokerage in Missoula, and I was so focused on bringing content every single day that mattered to them. Now I have it on a platform that they can easily access. That’s the value proposition that’s associated with my brand—that I’m always going to be making sure they’re on the cutting edge of what’s to come.

Given all the challenges right now, it seems like agents are craving that type of value and relationship from their brokerage…

DJ Smith: It’s understanding who they are as a person. My wife and I are probably going to be slow to bring people on, but I know it’s going to be intentional. Our philosophy is, before we let them in the door at REMAX, we’re inviting them over to dinner at our house so we get to know who they are, and they get to interact with our kids, because this business can feel transactional, but it’s so much more important than that.

Last week, I got two phone calls. One from a team and one from an individual agent. I asked them why they called me and they told me, ‘I (asked) AI, what brokerages should I look at?’ AI said, ‘if you want someone that is up and coming and breaking things, call REMAX.’ I could not be more proud that in a matter of a week or a month of ownership, we’re breaking into the algorithm of AI to recognize what we’re doing. I think that says a lot about where we’re headed.
