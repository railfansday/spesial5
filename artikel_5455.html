5 Kohl’s Black Friday offers, from dawn hours to a no-purchase $10,000 sweepstakes

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Kohl’s opens at 5 a.m. on Black Friday, 2026, with free Kohl’s Cash, Sephora coupons and a $10,000 sweepstakes. See what is worth the trip.

Black Friday remains one of the biggest shopping days in the U.S., but much of that shopping now happens on phones and laptops rather than in store aisles. TheStreet reports that Neil Saunders, managing director of retail at GlobalData, considers the day enormous but no longer the event it once was for shoppers. A retailer can't simply unlock its doors at dawn and count on a line outside. Stores now have to compete for the visit itself.

"So, retailers have to do more to entice people into stores and draw crowds," Saunders told TheStreet. Kohl's $KSS faces that challenge partway through a recovery, and Michael Bender, Kohl's CEO, says the company is not yet where it wants to be. Kohl's still has something to prove in its stores this weekend. The company has built its early hours and handouts to bring people into its stores, more than to mark the holiday.

Kohl's Black Friday plans include a dawn opening, Kohl's Cash envelopes, beauty coupons tied to its Sephora shops and a $10,000 sweepstakes, according to Mashable. Most of those offers reward a shopper for being inside a Kohl's at the right hour. That suits a company that wants people in its aisles rather than on its website. Some of the offers justify setting an early alarm, and others don't.

Kohl's $KSS is keeping its stores open from before sunrise until the last minute of Black Friday instead of relying on one rush at the door. Stores open at 5 a.m. on Friday, Nov. 27, and stay open until midnight local time, according to Mashable. With those hours, Kohl's can serve whoever comes in, whenever they come in, and doesn't have to reward only the shoppers who waited outside in the dark.

A 5 a.m. opening used to mark the moment the discounts began, but retailers now cut prices well before Black Friday. Kohl's is opening its doors to compete with sales that have been running for weeks and with shoppers who buy from home. For shoppers, 5 a.m. is when Kohl's unlocks its stores, not when the bargains start.

For the reader, the generous part is the length of the day, not its early start. Someone who only wants Kohl's prices doesn't have to choose between a 5 a.m. alarm and skipping Black Friday entirely.

Kohl's $KSS keeps every one of its stores closed on Thanksgiving, Thursday, Nov. 26, and shoppers can reach its holiday deals only online that day, according to Mashable. On the holiday, Kohl's depends entirely on its website, the part of the business that has been shrinking. Online sales slipped 1% while comparable sales in its stores climbed 2%, according to TheStreet. That helps explain why its in-store offers cluster in the hours and days after Thanksgiving.

Kohl's closes on Thanksgiving as a standing rule, not as a one-off choice for this Black Friday. TheStreet reports that Kohl's also locks every store in the country on Easter Sunday and Christmas Day, under a holiday hours policy that covers three days a year. JCPenney and Macy's $M close their stores on Thanksgiving as well, so a department-store shopper who skips Kohl's that day finds neither rival open. The closure costs Kohl's no ground against those chains. Among them, the contest for in-person shoppers begins when they open their doors Friday morning.

For a shopper, Thursday is a day to browse online and plan a list. The offers that reward showing up in person begin the next morning.

Kohl's $KSS will hand a Kohl's Cash voucher valued between $5 and $100 to each of the first 200 people inside every store once it opens at 5 a.m., according to Mashable. Kohl's ties the reward to showing up rather than to buying anything, and that is the kind of pull retail analyst Bruce Winder says stores now need. "Retailers need to offer an attractive incentive for shoppers to visit their store first," Winder told TheStreet. Kohl's sets the limit store by store, so a shopper's chances depend on the crowd at the local Kohl's, not on demand across the country. With the envelope, Kohl's is trying to turn an early opening into an actual crowd.

Kohl's is making a modest promise to a lot of people, and everyone near the front of the line goes home with something. Target $TGT is chasing the same early crowd with a similar offer. The first 100 guests at each Target store get a gift card worth $25, $50 or $100, and a single shopper at each store wins a $1,000 card, the largest amount Target is offering, according to TheStreet. The $100 is the top of the Kohl's range, not a likely result, and no breakdown of how the amounts are split has been given. The prize is store credit rather than money, so whatever it is worth gets spent at Kohl's.

Getting an envelope may take more effort than the offer suggests. Winder predicts that shoppers will wait a little longer in line on Black Friday, while the supply at each Kohl's stays fixed at 200 envelopes. Anyone who wants one should arrive well before the doors open, not right at 5 a.m. Saunders says that a gimmick can succeed, though shoppers expect their time in a store to pay off. Nobody should make a predawn drive for a $5 voucher alone.

For shoppers who already had a Kohl's list, the envelope is a low-risk bonus. For everyone else, it is a lure, and Kohl's is counting on them to take it.

Kohl's $KSS is aiming its Saturday giveaway at a shopper it has not traditionally relied on. On Saturday, Nov. 28, each Kohl's store will hand a beauty coupon worth between $5 and $100 to the first 200 people through its doors, according to Mashable. Kohl's has long depended on older shoppers, according to CNBC, and the Sephora shops inside its stores are where the company looks for someone new. Blake Anderson, the analyst covering Kohl's at Jefferies, said of those shops, "What's been a really interesting development for them is a creative use of their square feet and a way to try to drive not only sales, but new and younger customers."

As with Friday's envelopes, there is no word on how the amounts are split among the 200 coupons. Shoppers receive the coupon instantly, so they can put it toward beauty products during the same visit. For someone who already restocks makeup or skin care at Sephora, an early Saturday line is a reasonable bet. For anyone else, it means giving up a morning for a prize of unknown size.

Kohl's is willing to hand out these coupons because it is planning for shoppers it hopes to keep for years, not just this weekend. CNBC reports that Bender sees the younger generation as the customers the company can expand alongside in the years ahead.

The Saturday line will offer an early answer on whether a beauty discount can turn a one-time visitor into a regular.

Kohl's $KSS asks almost nothing of the people who enter its $10,000 sweepstakes. Entry is free, limited to one per U.S. adult and open from Friday, Nov. 27, through Monday, Nov. 30, with times set in Eastern Time, according to Mashable. Unlike the Friday envelopes and the Saturday coupons, the sweepstakes doesn't require beating a crowd. A shopper on the West Coast should still watch the clock near the end, because Kohl's sets the deadline by Eastern Time.

Kohl's has not said how many people will win or how it will divide the prize, so the odds are impossible to judge. With entry free, the sensible move is to enter once and then forget about it.

By keeping the sweepstakes open through Monday, Kohl's keeps its name in front of shoppers after the Friday cash and the Saturday coupons are gone. Kohl's opens this offer to more people than any other in the campaign, even if its value to any one entrant remains a guess.
