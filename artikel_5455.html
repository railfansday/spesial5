Consumer Groups Request California Officials Investigate Zillow

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Several consumer groups have penned a letter to California state officials, requesting an investigation into Zillow’s partnership with Redfin, as well as its referral and mortgage practices.

“We write because the California housing market is broken in ways that leave families suffering dire economic harm—and one of the most aggressive actors in distorting the market, to its own profit and to the detriment of the public, has been Zillow,” the letter reads.

Signed by five consumer-focused advocacy organizations—namely, the Consumer Federation of America, Demand Progress Education Fund, Americans for Financial Reform Education Fund, Children’s Advocacy Alliance and Revolving Door Project—the the letter requested officials to investigate Zillow’s agreement with Redfin, and business practices the organization described as “steering.”

This is not the first letter of its kind made calling out the portal, as several consumer groups—also including the Consumer Federation of America—sent a letter to the National Association of Attorneys General back in September requesting investigations into Zillow for similar reasons.

In a statement on the letter, Zillow said its position is “unchanged” from previous challenges, denying any wrongdoing alleged.

The FTC had previously sued both Zillow and Redfin over the two portal’s rental-focused partnership, alleging Zillow effectively paid $100 million (and other benefits) to Redfin to end its advertising customers’ contracts, stop competing in the multifamily advertising market for up to nine years and serve as an “exclusive syndicator” of Zillow listings. That suit, however, has since been settled after the portals agreed to “unwind” some aspects of their partnership, with Redfin set to once again offer its own separate service.

The consumer groups’ Nov. 17 letter described said settlement as “far less than California consumers deserve,” as Zillow said it would maintain some level of partnership with Redfin “through at least 2030.”

“It (Zillow) retains the customer relationships the illegal arrangement produced and continues to benefit from the market consolidation it achieved during its term,” the letter reads. “And Redfin is left to rebuild its competitive position from scratch, in a market where Zillow remains the 800-pound gorilla.”

Zillow said in its statement that its partnership with Redfin “is pro-consumer and procompetitive.”

In terms of the allegations of steering, the letter referred to Zillow’s practice of having its “Contact Agent” buttons direct consumers to Zillow-affiliated agents rather than the listing agent as “deliberate consumer deception.”

“The Zillow Preferred Agent is essentially an interloper paying Zillow to execute a man-in-the-middle attack,” the letter read. “This has triggered class-action litigation that includes RICO claims, alleging that Zillow and the participating brokerages meet the definition of a criminal enterprise.”

This has also been the point of a lawsuit against Zillow, which was later combined with another suit against the portal over its alleged steering to Zillow Home loans through its preferred agents. The combined suit was dismissed back in July, with a judge saying that a “reasonable consumer” would not be deceived by Zillow’s “lead diversion” practices, but the plaintiffs have refiled with hopes to once again reopen the case.

On this suit, Zillow said in a statement that “The court dismissed all of plaintiffs’ claims in July, and the amended complaint simply repackages the same already-rejected theory related to free Zillow Home Loans pre-approval letters.”

The portal also noted that for its premarketed Zillow Preview listings, the “Contact Agent” button leads “directly to the listing agent, free,” and that “any listing agent revenue share is paid by Zillow at no additional cost to consumers or agents.”

“Zillow’s tools are free and optional, and our business relationships are clearly disclosed,” the statement continued.

Also in terms of alleged mortgage business steering, the letter referred to a previous suit from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) against Rocket Homes for similar allegations as are laid against Zillow.

However, this suit was later dropped by the CFPB in 2025 after what the letter described as the Trump administration’s “illegal effort to dismantle the CFPB in 2025.”

The letter also cited several academic studies as evidence for its allegations against Zillow, including a study from Georgetown University Law Center Professor Steven Salop that claimed to show Zillow Home Loan borrowers pay higher rates (that study, notably, was funded by CoStar, one of Zillow’s main competitors).

Zillow previously disputed these findings and alleged Salop relied on “selectively chosen data and inadequate controls to produce a distorted view of Zillow Home Loans.”

The letter requested that the California Department of Real Estate enact its “disciplinary and rulemaking authority” to investigate the alleged instances of steering.

Additionally, the letter requests the officials to bring “enforcement actions for the deceptive ‘Contact Agent’ and ‘Request a Tour’ interfaces, undisclosed Flex commissions, and the failure to disclose that agent recommendations reflect Zillow’s financial interests, not the consumer’s,” and potentially file an antitrust lawsuit.

Lastly, the groups asked that the officials “coordinate with the multistate coalition that brought the Redfin action so that Zillow cannot exploit jurisdictional gaps between state enforcers.”
