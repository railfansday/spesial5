53 Healthy Thanksgiving Recipes to Brighten Up the Table

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

What makes a healthy Thanksgiving recipe? To us, it’s less about cutting out butter or skipping pie and more about bringing balance to the table. Crisp, bitter greens make creamy mashed potatoes taste even better. Tart cranberries cut through rich turkey gravy. Beans, whole grains, and plenty of colorful vegetables bring flavor, texture, and variety to a meal that can otherwise skew decidedly beige.

These healthy Thanksgiving recipes cover the entire feast, from crunchy salads and make-ahead appetizers to satisfying plant-forward mains and festive desserts. Some offer more fiber or protein; others give seasonal produce a starring role. What they have in common is that every one is delicious. You’ll also find plenty of vegan, gluten-free, and dairy-free options too. Because there’s room for everyone—and everything—at the Thanksgiving table.

Deep purple cabbage, bitter radicchio, juicy citrus, and ruby-red pomegranate seeds make a strong case for putting salad front and center. Give the cabbage a good massage to soften it without sacrificing its crunch.

“In the midst of a rich meal, something fresh and acidic keeps your taste buds awake,” writes Test Kitchen editor Rebecca Firkser. Here, thinly sliced pears get a shower of honey-roasted seeds and shaved cheese.

Instead of tender greens that might wilt before everyone sits down, consider a fennel salad that stays crunchy for hours. Walnuts, Parmesan, croutons, and lemon give it plenty of flavor and texture.

Who says salad has to be cold? Caramelized cauliflower, warm feta, crunchy pistachios, and sticky dates meet peppery arugula and a tangy vinaigrette. Hearty enough to satisfy, with plenty of greens to keep things fresh.

Bitter radicchio meets sweet apple and mustardy croutons in this hearty autumn salad. Tossing the apple slices with cider vinegar helps keep them from browning before the feast begins.

A Thanksgiving salad should be sturdy enough to hold its own on the buffet table. This one only gets better after it’s dressed, as the kale softens and the nutty pecan vinaigrette works its way into the leaves.

Cranberry sauce isn’t just for turkey. A tart dressing made with the canned stuff brings holiday spirit to bitter greens, sweetened pecans, and Manchego cheese. Consider it a little insurance against salad fatigue.

Warm spices and sweet carrots give this hearty lentil salad plenty of character. The legumes add plant-based protein and fiber, making it a substantial addition to a vegetarian Thanksgiving spread.

Panzanella isn’t just for summer. Swap ripe tomatoes for juicy oranges, add bitter chicories and shaved fennel, and toss everything with toasted pumpernickel croutons and a balsamic-Dijon vinaigrette.

Roasted carrots, chickpeas, almonds, and smoked paprika blend into a vibrant dip with a little protein and plenty of personality. Make it up to a week ahead, then serve with seeded crackers, crudités, or good bread.

Another excellent addition to the crudité platter. Hazelnuts bring out the earthy sweetness of beets, while garlic and lemon keep everything lively. Like the carrot dip above, this one can be made a week ahead.

A jar of olives, a block of feta, and some garlicky olive oil: That’s nearly all you need for this bright, briny appetizer. Have some crusty bread handy for the flavorful oil left in the bowl.

Yuzu kosho gives these deviled eggs a jolt of citrusy heat, while pickled shallots, fresh herbs, and thinly sliced radishes bring brightness and crunch. Arrange them on shiso leaves for an especially striking holiday appetizer.

A bowl of party nuts is never a bad idea, particularly when they’re seasoned with chile powder, fried curry leaves, and crispy onions. Set these out while the turkey finishes roasting.

A tangy, Parmesan-rich dressing clings to crisp-tender green beans, with pickled shallots bringing a sharp little kick. All the satisfaction of a proper Thanksgiving side, without the casserole dish.

Crispy onions are the crown on this roasted squash side; pomegranate seeds are the jewels. Add fresh herbs and a swoop of lemony yogurt, and you’ve got a dish worthy of the center of the table.

These collards aren’t cooked into submission. Simmered until just tender, they stay pleasantly substantial beneath a sharp mustard vinaigrette. Tarragon and sweet-salty walnuts finish this fresh take on a holiday greens side.

When the oven is too crowded for green bean casserole, make this stovetop alternative. Browned mushrooms, crisp-tender green beans, butter, and frizzled shallots deliver plenty of familiar holiday flavor.

One BA reader calls this their signature Thanksgiving sprouts recipe. Separating the individual leaves takes patience, but the payoff is worth it: tender-crisp greens with lots of caramelized edges.

Toss sprouts with spicy, nutty gochujang brown butter. Or, for something simpler, go for classic roasted brussels sprouts.

Coconut milk gives this bubbling, breadcrumb-topped vegan gratin its creamy consistency without any dairy. A little lemon zest and nutmeg round things out.

White miso gives these glazed carrots a deep savoriness, while scallion gremolata adds freshness. You can cook the carrots a day ahead and loosen the glaze with a splash of water when reheating.

Maple syrup plays up carrots’ natural sweetness, while a pinch of chile flakes keeps the glaze from going full candy. This is a classic Thanksgiving side with just enough heat to keep things interesting.

Or skip cooking the carrots altogether. Shave them into thin ribbons, let salt soften them slightly, then toss with sticky dates and an orange vinaigrette. Sweet, tart, crunchy, and fresh.

Blanch, smash, and dress the beans up to 12 hours ahead so they can soak up the lemony sumac vinaigrette. Finish with Asian pear, parsley, and crunchy pumpkin seeds just before serving.

Silken tofu gives this steakhouse classic its creamy texture without dairy. Aromatics add savory depth, making it a satisfying side to serve alongside turkey, stuffing, and potatoes.

Bitter greens, tangy heat, and salty crunch are especially welcome among Thanksgiving’s softer sides. Toss tender broccoli rabe with garlicky onions and pickled cherry peppers, then shower the lot with crisp Pecorino breadcrumbs.

The French bistro classic gets a holiday-friendly update: Cut the leeks into rounds rather than long stalks, making them easier to serve to a crowd. A sharp vinaigrette, toasted walnuts, and fresh tarragon finish the dish.

Steamed broccolini gets a salty-sweet dressing of tahini, miso, soy sauce, and mirin, then a scattering of lemon and sesame seeds. Make the sauce a day ahead to take one more task off your Thanksgiving list.

Sweet roasted beets meet tart grapefruit, sharp shallots, and sizzling rosemary. The colors alone could wake up a Thanksgiving spread. Plus, you can roast and peel the beets up to two days ahead.

Inspired by Cantonese steamed fish, this squash side gets a generous pouf of frizzled scallions. Ginger and chile bring the heat, turning a humble vegetable into something special.

Bharta is a Bangladeshi preparation of seasoned meat or mashed vegetables. This roasted squash version gets its punch from mustard oil, chiles, spices, and fresh cilantro.

Raw cranberries keep their tart snap in this bright relish, while toasted walnuts, mustard seeds, Fresno chile, and a little maple syrup bring crunch, heat, and sweetness. Make it up to four days ahead.

A quick parboil and a vigorous shake rough up the surfaces of these potatoes, helping them develop exceptionally crunchy crusts in the oven. The fluffy centers are practically made for soaking up gravy.

Baking potatoes instead of boiling them concentrates their flavor, while roasted garlic adds savory depth. With just six tablespoons of butter for four pounds of potatoes, this creamy mash is a little less indulgent than many holiday versions.

Steaming sweet potatoes makes their flesh especially soft and fluffy, ready for generous smears of tahini butter. Lime juice, soy sauce, and sesame oil make the flavors especially dynamic, and a welcome savory counterpoint to classic sweet potato casserole.

Thin rounds of sweet potato are arranged upright in concentric circles, then roasted with harissa until tender and crisp-edged. A crunchy dukkah of pistachios, sesame, and fennel seeds finishes this striking holiday side.

This gluten-free stuffing skips the usual process of baking cornbread, cubing it, and drying it out. Instead, mushrooms, kale, and herbs bake right into the batter.

Chewy wild rice, tossed with sautéed leeks, toasted pecans, and fresh herbs, makes a stunningly delicious (and gluten-free) alternative to traditional Thanksgiving stuffing.

If you’re firmly Team Dark Meat, these stock-braised turkey legs are the ticket. The meat becomes tender enough to pull from the bone, making for especially good leftover sandwiches the next day.

Prefer white meat, or feeding a smaller crowd? A bone-in turkey breast delivers juicy, flavorful slices without the work of roasting a whole bird. No basting or oversized roasting pan required.

Or give a whole roasted salmon fillet the holiday treatment with a crunchy salsa of toasted nuts, garlic, chile, and vinegar. Showered with fresh mint and cilantro, it’s a striking centerpiece with plenty of protein.

A crisp, golden shell of saffron rice gives way to spiced chickpeas, caramelized onions, and tart dried cherries. Crown the whole thing with a bracingly fresh herb salad for a vegetarian centerpiece everyone will love.

For a meatless main with all the ceremony of a holiday roast, this homemade tofu loaf gets filled with savory mushroom-pecan stuffing and brushed with a smoky maple-soy glaze. Pass the mushroom gravy alongside.

Tearing roasted sweet potatoes into rough chunks creates more surface area for charring and catching a spicy maple-soy glaze. Serve them over tangy lentils with scallions and pistachios for a substantial plant-based main.

A mixture of mushrooms, pearl onions, carrots, and red wine makes a rich, deeply savory vegetarian braise. Serve it over mashed potatoes or polenta, with crispy mushrooms scattered over the top.

Transform humble cabbage into a deeply flavorful vegetarian centerpiece. Here, it caramelizes while slowly braising in a spiced tomato broth. Serve the tender wedges over grains to make the meal more substantial.

Almond flour makes these warmly spiced cake layers exceptionally tender, while shredded carrots, walnuts, golden raisins, and coconut bring texture to every bite. Tangy cream cheese–Greek yogurt frosting seals the deal. Make it up to three days ahead.

Whole wheat flour and oats bring subtle nuttiness to this apple crisp, while brown sugar and butter help the topping turn golden. Serve warm, with vanilla ice cream for anyone who wants it.

This Spanish almond cake gets a seasonal turn with chopped pears and plenty of citrus zest. Naturally gluten-free and dairy-free, it’s lovely after dinner—and just as welcome with coffee the next morning.

Rich chocolate mousse gets a crackly crown of sweet sesame brittle. Serve it in individual glasses for a holiday-party-worthy capper that’s entirely vegan—and a little different from the usual parade of Thanksgiving desserts.

Golden turmeric, fragrant spices, and coconut milk give this custard pie its silky filling. The almond, oat, and coconut crust is naturally gluten-free, making the finished dessert suitable for guests avoiding both gluten and dairy.

A platter of seasonal citrus—think grapefruit, mandarin oranges, and whatever else looks good at the market—gets a crunchy topping of pistachios, almonds, pumpkin seeds, and toasted coconut.
