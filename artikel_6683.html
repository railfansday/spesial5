40 More Million-Dollar Watches Just Sold at Auction

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

This is an edition of the newsletter Box + Papers, Cam Wolf’s weekly deep dive into the world of watches. Sign up here.

Back in June, I reported on the bonanza of watches crossing the million-dollar-plus mark at auction. What once felt like a unicorn price for watchmakers, auction houses, and the industry as a whole no longer feels so magical. “I joke with people that $1 million is like the new $250,000 of five years ago,” Paul Boutros of Phillips auction house said over the summer.

In just the first half of 2026, the number of million-dollar watches sold nearly eclipsed the total from all of 2025. Now, after a raft of high-profile auctions in Geneva a couple of weeks ago, the industry has set yet another record. In just the last month alone (including Monaco Legend Group, which kicked things off in late October), 40 million-dollar watches hammered, according to numbers provided by EveryWatch. That’s more sold than in any entire year before 2020.

If you’re keeping score at home, we’ve seen 133 watches sell at or above seven figures this year. And we’ve still got plenty of year left.

The question is: How much steam does the auction world have left? And what’s driving this radical spike in big-ticket timepieces?

A $10 million Patek Philippe Perpetual Calendar Chronograph ref. 2499

Mike Nouveau, WatchTok King and dealer, has told me that if more kids knew how much growth potential there is in the vintage market, they’d put down the creator-style tiny microphones and start studying reference numbers. The vintage market spiked in the middle of the pandemic, when prices on sport watch grails like the Patek Philippe Nautilus and Rolex Daytona went crypto-mode as a new wave of speculators flooded the high-end collecting scene.

But while the steel sport watch craze has cooled—or at least leveled off—the market has continued riding a remarkably durable wave, helped in part by the arrival of a previously quiet category: the insiders-only independent brands. “For many years at auction, we always said, ‘You have Patek Philippe and Rolex. What’s the third brand that can compete?’” Alexandre Ghotbi, a deputy chairman at Phillips auction house, told me in 2024 for a story on the beloved watchmaker Rexhep Rexhepi. “Today’s third brand is not a brand—it’s a genre: independent artisanal watchmakers.”

Think F.P. Journe, Berneron, Phillipe Dufour, Rexhepi’s Atelier Akrivia—companies helmed by watchmaking auteurs turning out hyper-limited runs, most of which sell out long before a perfectly-lit product photo ever hits the web. Oftentimes, the only way to get your hands on one of their wares—if you haven’t yet achieved friend-of-the-brand status—is by raising a paddle at auction.

When I asked dealer Eric Wind if he thought the indie insanity would remain strong, he was unequivocal: “At the top end, yes.” The dealer Zoe Abelson, of Graal Watches, actually believes we haven’t reached the peak yet. “I do think there's still room for the pieces that are actually rare to continue to grow in value with a continuance in new interest in collecting watches and the yearning to build the most impressive collection,” she said.

Two weekends ago, a new independent brand joined the list of million-dollar watchmakers. Sylvain Pinaud cut his teeth restoring antique watches and established his eponymous brand in 2018. He’s known for showing his work—the instrumental components of his watches are often highlighted on the dial. A unique version of his Origine, a model that only launched in 2022, sold for 1,011,200 Swiss francs (roughly $1,211,500).

Pinaud’s success is indicative of what happens to the elite independent brands that hit the big time. Compared to many of the heavyweight Swiss makers working at scale, independent watchmakers are seen as artisans who hand-produce their designs in extremely limited quantities. It’s why a watchmaker like Kari Voutilainen, considered a master of his craft, has sold more million-dollar pieces this year than many of the big fellas getting rap lyric shoutouts. “There's a growing appreciation for what these watchmakers are producing,” said Ben Horesh, EveryWatch’s CEO. “It’s great to see more independents challenging the old guard."

Look at that list above. Voutilainen ranks just after Rolex and Patek Philippe but ahead of Cartier for the most million-dollar watches sold at auction in 2026. The list is heavy on independents, including Rexhepi’s Akrivia, George Daniels, Philippe Dufour, and Vianney Halter. The real headline, though, is seeing F.P. Journe—the godfather of the indie scene—at the very top. If one area of the market doesn’t feel entirely sustainable, it’s the inferno around FPJ’s pieces. For deep-pocketed newcomers to the “hobby” of catching big-ticket whales, the Geneva-based maker has come to represent the grailiest of grails. Nearly every Journe piece that comes to auction sells for multiples of its estimate. And I mean everything: an empty watch box made for a set of FPJs sold for $230,000 at Christie’s! “The less rare—or in Journe's case, current production—pieces that get swept up in the overall market growth will slow down soon,” Abelson said. “I don't think it's sustainable; at a certain point the values will price out enough people that there should be a correction.”

A $13 million F.P. Journe Chronomètre à Résonance "Régence”

If six-figure watches were once rare, 10-million-plus pieces were true spectacles. Between 2020 and 2023, not a single watch crossed that threshold. In the last decade, from 2015 to 2025, there were just seven 10-million watches total. But with some time still on the clock, seven watches have already topped that threshold in 2026. A solid chunk of that is thanks to the wild one-of-one Daytonas that Rolex made for a charity auction—three out of the four sold for seven figures—but it’s still an astounding feat. These days, it’s hard to find a category of watches that isn’t doing well at auction. Cartier hasn’t fallen off—in fact, the brand keeps setting records: a very cool clock known as Portico No. 4 hammered for approximately $5,768,400 at Phillips the weekend before last, becoming the most expensive Cartier ever sold in the process. Breguet is, rightfully and finally, on the rise. And after relaunching just last summer, Urban Jürgensen’s vintage pieces are hotter than ever. A super sexy pink-gold version of the brand’s Reference 2 went for 358,400 Swiss francs (about $430,000) at Sotheby’s.

To put a fine point on all this, just look at how overall sales are soaring. Last year, Phillips set the record with $83,018,538 worth of timepieces sold in a single auction. It only took 12 months to shatter that number. This month saw Phillips reset the record with a $112.3 million auction. I would say 2026 is an inflection point for the industry…if I didn’t anticipate that we’ll be back here talking about even more record-setting sales every year for the foreseeable future.
