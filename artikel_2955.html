'There aren't any weaknesses' | How the Bills offense is preparing to face the blitz-heavy Minnesota Vikingshttps://www.buffalobills.com/news/there-aren-t-any-weaknesses-how-the-bills-offense-is-preparing-to-face-the-blitz-heavy-minnesota-vikings

For the Bills, their focus is on the Vikings' unique defensive scheme, which is blitzing and creating pressure on the quarterback at a historic rate to begin the year.

"They've got so many different looks," QB Josh Allen said. "They play a few different personnel packages where the same looks mean different things. You've got to be in tune with who's on the field and where they're at on the field. So that's up to us and the coaching staff to try to decipher that and figure that out, and just have the best plan that we can have going into it and adjust accordingly."

Through its first seven games of the season, Minnesota has blitzed on 73.6% of dropbacks, according to TruMedia. That blitz rate is far and away leading the NFL, also marking the highest rate for a team in the Next Gen Stats era (since 2016) by a wide margin.

Week 8 featured another level of blitzing for the Vikings, setting the record for the highest blitz rate in a single game (89.7%), after initially taking hold of the record just one week prior (85.2% in Week 7).

"There's a lot more going on that meets the eye from just watching the film," G O'Cyrus Torrence said. "So I feel like it's definitely going to be a lot of bullets flying. A lot of communication is going to have to be going on our part. There's going to be some mess-ups; there's going to be some plays we'll want back, but we have to be able to brush off our minds and get to the next play."

Defensive coordinator Brian Flores has his unit pressuring quarterbacks on 46.4% of dropbacks, leading the NFL.

"It's not even how much they blitz; it's how productive they are when they do blitz," Allen said. "They bring it from both sides, they'll blitz the middle, they'll drop guys that you think are coming. It's a very in-tune defense. Those guys are very smart; they're playing so well together. It's hard to game plan for a team like this because there aren't any weaknesses."

The Vikings have been great at following through on their pressures as well, currently ranked second in the league in both sacks (25) and QB hits (57) through Week 8.

"The easiest way to affect a team is to affect the quarterback in the NFL, and they do it better than anybody, I would say, in the NFL right now," T Spencer Brown said. "It's just about taking pride in what we're doing, making sure we're communicating to the extent we need to."

Flores has found success sending extra rushers in ways offenses haven't been able to figure out, keeping play-callers constantly guessing. Bringing a fleet of Vikings snap after snap has become a mainstay for the highly touted coordinator, giving Bills head coach Joe Brady a lot to gameplan for.

"He's one of the best [defensive coordinators] in the game," Brady said of Flores. "He's evolved; it's not like it's the same defense every year. Continuing to evolve the scheme, the players have a great understanding of how they're attacking protections and coverages. There's a clear identity in the way that they play. If you're a fan of football, you have to enjoy how they play football."

Note: This assumes the team sign Mac Jones and brings back Mason, Hockenson, Brandel, Van Ginkel, Cashman, and Rodgers plus releases Fries and Metellus.
