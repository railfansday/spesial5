Stock Market Today, Nov. 25: Dow Erases 1,100-Point Loss as Wall Street Stages Dramatic Late-Day Comeback

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Markets recovered from steep intraday losses as investors returned to select sectors, although persistent inflation and continued weakness in technology stocks left the broader market under pressure.

By RJ Fulton – Nov 25, 2026 at 4:57PM EST

Wall Street staged a strong comeback on Wednesday, November 25, as the Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI +0.33%) erased an intraday decline of more than 1,100 points to finish in positive territory. The reversal followed another volatile session marked by persistent inflation concerns and continued selling pressure across technology stocks, highlighting a growing divergence between sectors.

The Dow climbed 0.33% to close at 50,183, outperforming the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%), which declined 0.21% to 7,186. Meanwhile, the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.62%) fell 0.62% to 23,586 as weakness in artificial intelligence (AI) stocks continued to weigh on the technology-heavy index.

Elsewhere, gold edged 0.29% higher to $3,572.40, while the 10-year Treasury yield declined slightly to 4.91%. Energy stocks led sector gains, while consumer discretionary and technology shares remained under pressure.

HP Inc. (HPQ +11.49%) surged 11.49% following better-than-expected quarterly earnings and an encouraging outlook for fiscal 2027. Charter Communications (CHTR +8.39%) also outperformed the broader market, climbing 8.39% as investors gravitated toward more defensive sectors.

Meanwhile, technology stocks continued to face substantial selling pressure. Ambarella (AMBA -19.77%) plunged 19.77% following disappointing revenue guidance, while Nutanix (NTNX -15.19%) tumbled 15.17% amid broader weakness in high-growth technology stocks.

Wednesday's dramatic market reversal suggests investors are becoming increasingly selective rather than abandoning equities altogether. Despite the Dow's recovery, continued weakness in technology stocks indicates that concerns surrounding artificial intelligence (AI) spending, elevated valuations, and the profitability of major infrastructure investments have yet to subside.

Adding to these concerns, October's core personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index rose 0.3% for the third consecutive month. Although consumer demand remains relatively resilient, persistent inflation and renewed tariff uncertainty could complicate the Federal Reserve's ability to lower interest rates. This presents a particular challenge for high-growth companies whose valuations depend heavily on expectations of future earnings.

Nevertheless, the broader market outlook is not entirely negative. Bank of America (BAC -0.04%) strategists have projected that the S&P 500 could reach 7,800 by late 2027, reflecting expectations for continued earnings growth despite near-term volatility. However, achieving that target will likely require corporate profits to expand beyond a relatively narrow group of technology companies.

Looking ahead, investors should pay particular attention to whether the rotation into other sectors can sustain the broader market while technology stocks stabilize. A recovery supported by stronger market breadth and improving earnings expectations would be more encouraging than another short-lived rally driven primarily by AI stocks.
