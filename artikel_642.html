Live Nation Adds Venue on Australia’s Gold Coast

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Surfers Music Hall will be a 2,500-capacity live music space in Surfers Paradise, with a target to open its doors in mid-2028.

GOLD COAST, Australia — Live Nation continues to expand its venues portfolio in Australia, adding a new facility on the Gold Coast.

Announced this week, Surfers Music Hall will be a 2,500-capacity live music space in Surfers Paradise, the hometown of chart-topping pop star Amy Shark, with a target to open its doors in mid-2028.

Surfers Music Hall will be the concerts giant’s first venue on the Gold Coast, and the first dedicated live music venue of this scale in the region. When complete, the space will bring more national and international tours to the Gold Coast, the second biggest city in Queensland, “while giving local artists a new stage,” reads a statement. The timing of the new venue is critical. Queensland’s music and entertainment communities are preparing for the opportunity of a lifetime, when Brisbane hosts the 2032 Summer Olympic Games and Paralympic Games. The Gold Coast, about 50 miles south of Brisbane, won’t miss out. Hockey, triathlon, marathon swimming, and basketball games are earmarked for the city.

The buzz is building for the local music scene, too, which has hosted concerts from the likes of Pearl Jam, Foo Fighters and Queen at Carrara Stadium. Also, Live Nation and the City of Gold Coast hosted a Ones to Watch showcase in 2022, and the 2026 Queensland Music Awards (QMAs) were presented on the Gold Coast for the first time in the event’s 20-year history.

Surfers Music Hall will be owned by The City of Gold Coast and operated by Live Nation, and is located within the former Surfers Paradise Transit Centre and Bruce Bishop Carpark precinct. The development, according to LN, is expected to contribute an average of A$20 million ($13 million) each year to the local economy, support some 100 ongoing jobs and create jobs for the creative industries.

As previously reported, Live Nation, OVG and Plenary have been green-lit to build the Gather Brisbane arena ahead of the Games, a 17,000-plus capacity facility that will make the Sunshine State’s capital a two-arena town.

Currently, LN operates the Fortitude Music Hall (3,000) and The Triffid (800) in Brisbane, and boasts a national network of sites, including The Palais Theatre (2,900 capacity) and Festival Hall in Melbourne; Hindley Street Music Hall in Adelaide (1,800), Anita’s Theatre in Wollongong (1,200); and the 13,000-capacity Spark Arena in Auckland, New Zealand.

Earlier, LN announced it would deliver and operate a purpose-built, 5,000 capacity venue at Arden Central in North Melbourne, and its Northbridge Music Hall in Perth is expected to open to the public in the summer of 2026-27.

Brisbane won the rights to host the Olympics in 2021 when the IOC declared the city as the “preferred candidate,” thanks to its proven track record for organizing international events, with the Commonwealth Games — two, including the Gold Coast in 2018, an Expo and the Goodwill Games in its good books.
