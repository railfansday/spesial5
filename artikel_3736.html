Grand Theft Auto 6 has been granted an Adults Only rating in South Korea by the country's Game Content Rating Board. But in the weeks preceding that decision, the board had nearly refused to issue the game an age rating—effectively leaving it ineligible for sale in South Korea—due to ratings officials' concerns that GTA 6 might be way too comfortable hanging unprompted hog.

According to GCRB meeting minutes obtained by South Korean outlet Inven (via Kotaku), GTA 6 required a total of three ratings reviews, in which committee members repeatedly debated the presence of what they described as "out-of-context genital nudity"—a perception that seemed to have been informed by the leaked GTA 6 developer build footage circulated by cryptopeddling cornball master criminal, Cyberleek.

The GCRB's initial rating review for GTA 6 took place on September 10, during which committee members argued over whether the game's "out-of-context genital nudity" was grounds for refusal. Previously, the GCRB's rating standards automatically rejected games with exposed genitalia, but those standards were relaxed in 2025 to permit an Adults Only rating for "cases where suggestive bodily exposure is directly and specifically depicted," as long as that depiction didn't extend to graphic sexual acts.

Despite that revision, the committee members arguing to refuse an age rating for GTA 6 said that, while the GCRB had previously permitted "a certain degree of genital exposure where there is narrative justification," they "did not believe genital exposure to this extent falls within acceptable limits."

As the report notes, that first review meeting occurred before the GCRB received a playable GTA 6 build, meaning that the GCRB members' perception of the extent of visible in-game genitalia could have been informed by Cyberleek's leaked footage of an in-game nudist colony—an area that, despite the abundance of human flesh on display, didn't actually entail much sex going on. It was mostly just nude people standing around doing people things.

The GCRB committee members in favor of an Adults Only rating noted as much, saying that "in this game, scenes with heavy exposure are not particularly sexual," while "conversely, in sexual scenes, exposure is limited." The first meeting ended in a 3-3 tie vote, leading the committee to postpone its decision and instead recommend to Rockstar that the game be modified.

The GCRB did eventually receive a playable build of GTA 6 on September 28—just six days before its scheduled second review meeting on October 2. While an initial report from its gameplay researcher indicated that "genital exposure scenes mostly appeared as one-off mission backgrounds and did not lead to sexual interactions," the committee decided to once again postpone its decision simply because the game was too damn huge for it to be confident that it had seen all the relevant cases of penises and/or vulvas in the wild.

A few on the committee remained skeptical, however: "The basic principle of rating refusal applies when bodily exposure of sexual acts are depicted explicitly or excessively so as to disrupt social order," one GCRB committee member said. "By that standard, the current bodily exposure is sufficiently explicit and excessive."

In its third and final review meeting on October 16, the GCRB—informed in part by the fact that 10 other institutions like the US's ESRB and Europe's PEGI had issued their own ratings—ultimately voted 8-1 in favor of granting GTA 6 an Adults Only rating. Its researchers concluded that, after playing the game more thoroughly, scenes featuring full-frontal nudity didn't allow for any sexual interaction, and that cutscene sequences where protagonists Jason and Lucia did do sex at each other didn't expose any genitalia.

Interestingly, the GCRB researchers also confirmed that they couldn't replicate the ability to fully strip Jason and Lucia of their clothes that was shown in Cyberleek videos, inadvertently confirming that the GTA 6 build that'll be available to the public won't allow players to roam Vice City in the nude. Apologies if you were particularly invested in seeing Jason's dong.

And additional apologies to the one dissenting GCRB member who still voted for refusing an age rating for GTA 6, in part due to their fear that the rating could serve as precedent for other genres, meaning "background genital exposure will frequently appear in future RPGs as a result." I suspect they're going to have a rough time once Divinity comes out.
