Leaked Credentials Are an Enterprise Problem: GitGuardian Extends Its Good Samaritan Program

New York, USA, November 16th, 2026/CyberNewswire/--AI attribution has linked more than 3,913 high-impact leaks to 1,532 companies since spring; security experts handle critical disclosures to enterprise security teams.

GitGuardian, the secrets security platform, today announced the Enterprise Good Samaritan Program, an expansion of its longstanding responsible disclosure efforts aimed at strengthening security across the broader digital ecosystem.

Combining AI-powered attribution with expert security analysis, the program identifies high-impact credential leaks that could give attackers a path into an organization's systems or data. It traces these exposures to affected companies and delivers actionable disclosures to their security teams, showing how an attacker could exploit the credentials and what they could gain access to. For the first time, GitGuardian can extend these warnings to any company in the world, regardless of its size or whether it's a GitGuardian customer.

Since 2018, GitGuardian's original Good Samaritan Program has detected more than 83 million exposed secrets on public GitHub, sending 11.9 million alerts to 5.9 million developers. But notifying a developer doesn't necessarily lead to revocation. Approximately 90% of valid secrets remain unrevoked a week after the developer responsible for the leak has been alerted.

To date, the Enterprise Good Samaritan Program has linked more than 3,913 high-impact leaks to 1,532 companies. GitGuardian followed up with them, typically reaching at least five people at each organization.

The scale of the problem is growing. GitGuardian's 2026 State of Secrets Sprawl report found that 28.65 million new secrets were exposed on public GitHub in 2025, a 34% year-over-year increase. The Enterprise Good Samaritan Program gets exposed credentials to the security teams that can revoke them.

"For years, we've warned developers, but they're not always the ones who can revoke it. Our expertise combined with AI now helps us identify at scale the organizations behind serious exposures at scale, and our researchers work to reach the people who can respond. Attackers now have the ability, with AI offensive harnesses like ARTEX, to use these credentials to target companies at scale. Those companies deserve to know, whether they're our customers or not. And if a company has no clear way to receive a warning, the warning doesn’t help. Every company needs a door for these reports, and someone behind it.” — Eric Fourrier, CEO at GitGuardian

The Enterprise Good Samaritan Program combines continuous public monitoring, AI-assisted analysis, and human-led disclosure to identify serious credential exposures and help organizations respond.

GitGuardian monitors public sources, including GitHub and Docker Hub, for exposed credentials. Its AI agents now screen more than 1 million public incidents each month and investigate over 100,000 in depth, helping identify the affected organizations and assess the potential impact of exposure, including credential validity, permissions, and the systems or data at risk.

Human experts review high-impact findings before disclosure, providing the affected organization with the context needed to investigate and remediate the exposure. Rather than relying exclusively on notifications to individual developers, GitGuardian contacts multiple people within affected organizations, works to reach the relevant security teams, and escalates manually when automated outreach goes unanswered.

The value of this escalation was demonstrated in May 2026, when GitGuardian researchers identified a public GitHub repository containing sensitive CISA-related data and valid cloud credentials. Nine automated alerts had failed to resolve the exposure. After GitGuardian researchers escalated through CERT/CC and established contacts, the repository was taken offline within 26 hours of human escalation.

The need for timely disclosure is becoming more urgent as attackers automate credential discovery and exploitation. In November 2026, Microsoft documented attackers using agentic AI to search public GitHub repositories for exposed credentials. In one observed case, an AI agent went from searching for a leaked secret to extracting data from Azure Key Vault in five minutes.

The speed of these attacks makes unresolved credential exposures especially dangerous, and reinforces the need for organizations to have a reliable way to receive and act on external security disclosures.

With only 35% of contacted organizations responding to the program’s outreach, GitGuardian is calling on companies to establish clear, monitored channels for receiving security disclosures from external researchers, such as a security.txt file and a staffed security inbox.

The Enterprise Good Samaritan Program operates under GitGuardian's published disclosure principles, which emphasize timely notification, non-destructive verification, transparency, traceable research activity, and persistent follow-up.

Organizations receive the full details of their exposures without needing to sign up or become customers.

To learn more about the Enterprise Good Samaritan Program and GitGuardian's responsible disclosure principles, visit GitGuardian's Enterprise Good Samaritan Program Page. Organizations can also assess their exposure on public GitHub using GitGuardian's GitHub Security Audit.

GitGuardian is the secrets security platform built to protect the credentials that let code, machines, and AI agents access critical systems. The platform helps organizations detect exposed credentials across code, endpoints, and public sources; remediate incidents at scale with identity context, risk prioritization, and guided workflows; and prevent new leaks with security controls embedded in developer and AI coding workflows.

More than 600,000 developers worldwide trust GitGuardian. Enterprise customers include DigitalOcean, ING, BASF, Datadog, Euronext, and Bouygues Telecom.
