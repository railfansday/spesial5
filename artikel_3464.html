Alexander Skarsgård’s Hit Fantasy Romance Officially Removed From More Than 500 Theaters

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

One of the most surprising sleeper hits of the fall is an erotic romance fantasy that serves as a neat companion piece to Guillermo del Toro's Oscar-winning The Shape of Water. What makes the new film's box-office success even more unexpected is that it was distributed by Black Bear Pictures, an outfit that hadn't delivered a single domestic hit in the past year. Black Bear released the sports drama Christy, the Jason Statham vehicle Shelter, the Guy Ritchie action-thriller In the Grey, and most recently, the Matthew McConaughey-led neo-Western The Rivals of Amziah King.

This is a diverse slate, and the new romance fantasy continues the trend with its unique approach to familiar themes. Starring Olivia Colman and Alexander Skarsgård, the movie in question exceeded expectations in its box-office debut a few weeks ago, and subsequently displayed impressive holds. However, at the end of the day, it's still a niche title. Movies like it invariably have to make room for newer, shinier releases.

The film lost a small chunk of theaters for the first time in its run last week and another chunk this week, as it heads toward a PVOD release later this month. The good news is that the movie has already tripled its reported budget of $10 million, having grossed $20 million domestically and $30 million worldwide. It could make further gains if it garners attention during the awards season, which is entirely possible given its excellent reception.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

We're talking, of course, about Wicker. Directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson, the film holds a "Certified Fresh" 94% critics' score and a "Verified Hot" 92% audience score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes. The site's consensus reads, "Wicker is a charming fable that strikes the right balance between naughty and nice, drawing much of its appeal from deftly deployed salty humor, seamless creature effects, and Olivia Colman's eminently lovable performance."

Also starring Elizabeth Debicki, the movie opened in nearly 1,500 theaters three weeks ago. Its theatrical footprint was expanded to almost 2,400 theaters in week two, before dropping to around 2,100 theaters in week three. Wicker will enter its fourth week of release in fewer than 2,000 theaters nationwide. The movie is slated to debut on digital on November 24, along with Aaron Sorkin's high-profile box-office bomb The Social Reckoning.
