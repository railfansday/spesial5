OpenAI Slapped By California AG For “Unfair & Unsafe Practices”; Sam Altman-Run Tech Firm Accuses State Of Improperly Singling Out Company

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Executive Editor, Legal, Labor & Politics

Already facing a Hollywood supported lawsuit from the New York Times, plus more legal actions by the state of Texas, a collection of non-profits, and a wrongful death case, OpenAI now will have to go up against the state of California in court.“Government intervention is necessary to stop OpenAI’s unfair and unsafe practices and to prevent the company from causing severe harm to California, its people, and beyond,” states a complaint filed Monday in state court in the Bay Area by CA Attorney General Rob Bonta‘s office.

Casting a wide net on the foibles on the rapidly expanding AI industry, the two-claim complaint has a specific focus on the July 2026 attack by a hoard of roaming OpenAI agents on machine learning system Hugging Face. Facing millions in fine and penalties if the Golden State’s case is successful, the Sam Altman-run OpenAI, which currently has a $1.25 trillion market valuation, has undeniably often appeared to take Mark Zuckerberg’s 21st century tech mantra of “move fast and break things” to a more harried and frayed level. Simultaniously, OpenAI and sometimes rival Anthropic have been almost solely responsible for a budget balancing uptick in California’s public finances with the vast investment and potentiality the two Silicon Valley-based companies have brought to the AI industry. As the sex scandal that toppled Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has shown in its own market fluctuations the past few days and Donald Trump‘s ongoing insistence that the techlords can oversee themselves, AI has a captive hold on the American economy. Responding to today’s lawsuit, and its accusations that Altman and crew have blown off previous attempts by Bonta’s team for more info, OpenAI never uses the word “politics” or “midterms,” but they do claim they are being unfairly picked on. “Since the Hugging Face incident, we’ve undertaken an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity,” OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri told Deadline Monday.

“We’ve published a detailed technical report, commissioned and published a separate independent report, notified affected organizations, strengthened safeguards across our research systems, and created a framework for additional identification and disclosure of misalignment,” Pusateri added. “This lawsuit appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that’s been identified by every major AI lab. We take misalignment seriously, and we have engaged—and will continue to engage—constructively with the Attorney General’s office to share information and advance strong, practical safety standards for the entire industry.”

With over 1,200 AI agent in on it, the Hugging Face assault has been estimated to be the first full-on instance of AI making moves outside human control and oversight. In September, outgoing CA Governor Gavin Newsom reacted by issuing an executive order for AI companies to put in place a “‘kill switch,’ for frontier models.”

To that, OpenAI was seen in many circles as way too slow to reveal the invasive move — a point the CA AG offers as the foundation of its suit this week. “While public reporting and OpenAI’s self-serving statements about the Hugging Face attack have evoked science fiction fantasies of ‘rogue’ AI systems run amok, the simple truth is that the attack was entirely foreseeable in light of OpenAI’s decision to test models it knew to be dangerous in a flawed and exploitable testing environment without proper oversight,” the filing notes.

In the weeks since OpenAI opened up about the Hugging Face attack by its agents, a number of similar actions in scope and depth have occurred. Not that the AI giants have been that shamefaced about it, or, according to Bonta’s office, helpful.

“While touting its transparency and openness to the public, OpenAI has refused to substantially comply with subpoenas and interrogatories served by the Attorney General to obtain information about OpenAI’s security practices,” the AG attack on Altman and OpenAI emphasizes. “The company’s recent and much-publicized interest in ‘transparency’ is also at odds with its practice of discouraging employees from raising safety issues, which further risks public safety. Finally, the company’s conduct leading up to and during the Hugging Face attack demonstrates that OpenAI has repeatedly failed to follow its own safety protocols.”

Add to that in political protocols, recently re-elected CA AG Bonta has felt the sting of the creative community for his much criticized late September settlement of the antitrust suit against Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery for their now completed $111 billion merger. Today’s action is sure to win Bonta a few fans in the C-suites and guild group chats, at least in the short term.

In terms of creative protocols, CAA and WME have both filed briefs in the nearly two-year old consolidated copyright infringement battle between OpenAI and the New York Times, Ta-Nehisi Coates, a collection of Florida and Arkansas papers, The Intercept and other outlets. CAA client Robert De Niro joined fellow agency repped talent Ariana DeBose, Helen Mirren, Emma Thompson, Julia Roberts, Scarlett Johansson, Vince Gilligan, Rosario Dawson, Dan Levy, Anne Hathaway and more in putting his name on a petition telling Altman and OpenAI “that technical silence is not legal consent.”
