The Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset improves on its predecessors in nearly every way to deliver one of the best gaming headsets of 2026. Sony’s Tempest 3D Audio works in conjunction with the headset’s planar drivers, creating excellent life-like, rich and directional sound. The Pulse is mighty comfortable and looks great. It offers up to 30 hours of battery life, and its mic performance is much better than its pricier sibling.

But it pains me to say that this headset isn’t perfect. While customization is easy through your PS5’s settings, I wish there was a mobile companion app for those using the headset with their computers or phones. And while the overall sound quality is amazing, the bass sounds a little thin.

Should you still buy it? I think so. To find out why, read my full Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset review.

PlayStation 5, PlayStation Portal, PC, macOS, Android, iOS

From its suave looks and respectable battery life, to its detailed sound and affordable price point, the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset is an excellent headset for PS5 gamers.

As someone who’s been loyal to PlayStation for a decade, I’ve always loved the consoles’ sleek and premium design. While the original Sony PlayStation Pulse 3D Wireless Headset ($99) wasn’t bad-looking per se, I much prefer the minimalist approach Sony has taken towards the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset.

The new headset sports an understated design with round earcups that is still, at its core, quintessentially Sony. Available in either Midnight Black or White, I think the Pulse looks brilliant. It doesn’t draw attention to itself, and its simple design means you can use it as a regular set of cans when you’re traveling.

In addition to its good looks, the Pulse is mighty comfortable, too. The faux leather ear cushions are soft with a matte texture, and the earcups are spacious enough that they engulf my ears perfectly. Even after seven straight hours of wear, my ears didn’t feel sweaty or too hot. The pads can be twisted off, too, in case they get damaged and need replacing.

On the Pulse’s headband, you’ll find foam cushioning that helps alleviate pressure you’d otherwise feel on the top of your head. However, this cushioning feels like it’s made up of more air than anything else. I don’t necessarily think this is a massive drawback as the Pulse is a fairly lightweight headset.

The Pulse weighs just 10.58 ounces. That’s similar to the Pulse 3D (10.37 ounces) but lighter than the first-party Pulse Elite ($149, 12.2 ounces) and third-party Cherry XTRFY H3 ($99, 11.46 ounces). Sony hasn’t compromised on the Pulse’s build quality while maintaining its light frame either, as the headset feels solid.

As has become tradition for Sony at this point, it has fitted with PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset with custom planar magnetic drivers (four magnets per driver). These have been developed in collaboration with Audeze, the audio giant of Maxwell 2 ($329) fame, and a brand Sony acquired back in 2023. The drivers are programmed to deliver three-dimensional life-like sound across genres — and the Pulse excels at doing just that.

To take full advantage of the sound quality, I recommend diving into your PS5’s settings and creating your personalized 3D sound profile (it takes around five minutes). PlayStation’s Tempest 3D Audio, in conjunction with the Pulse’s planar drivers, delivers phenomenal spatial and directional audio. I tested it out by playing Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced, and Cyberpunk 2077 on my PS5 Slim.

In AC Black Flag Resynced, I appreciated how immersed the 3D Audio made me feel. At this price, the directional audio is phenomenal. I could tell exactly which direction the sounds were coming from. When surrounded by small and big ships during naval combat, I could pinpoint their locations even when I couldn’t see them. My ship’s creaking was loud and clear, adding to the immersion. When on land, I loved hearing rain falling on the palm trees right overhead, like they were actually falling above me.

Directional audio proved effective in Cyberpunk 2077, too, where I used it to my advantage to listen out for footsteps and determine my enemies’ positions. This made stealthily infiltrating enemy camps a walk in the park. The Pulse also has a wide soundstage with every aspect sounding well-balanced. Bullets sounded sharp as they whizzed past and ricocheted off the walls.

Dialogues across all three games sounded detailed and life-like, especially in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 which has some of the best voice acting known to man (argue with the wall). I could feel the emotions in the characters’ voices, and the wobble in their throats was clear during certain scenes. The Pulse made cutscenes feel dramatic and immersive. My only complaint with the Pulse is the bass… which I’ll discuss in detail shortly.

Does the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset have the longest battery life in its price range? Nope, the Cherry XTRFY H3 with its 100-hour rating takes the crown, but the Pulse doesn’t give you much to complain about. Sony says that the headset should last you up to 30 hours between charges — that’s 18 hours longer than the Pulse 3D (12-hour rating) and the same as the pricier Pulse Elite.

Given that I have a full-time job, I couldn’t play games for 30 hours to test the Pulse’s battery life (I wish I could…), so instead, I left it running overnight with Cyberpunk 2077 running at 50% volume on my PS5. I was prompted to charge the headset around the 29-hour mark. Your mileage will naturally depend on volume and other settings, but good to see that Sony’s rating is fairly accurate.

When we reviewed the OG Sony PlayStation Pulse 3D, we found its microphone to be underwhelming. Similarly, the Sony PlayStation Pulse Elite’s mic was simply okay and nothing special. That’s why I am so pleased that the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset’s mic is much, much improved.

The Pulse features a built-in integrated mic with AI-powered noise rejection, and it makes you sound loud and clear. I tested the mic by calling my friend, Harry, who was also testing the headset for TechRadar (our sister site). I was pleasantly surprised by how warm he sounded — and he said the same about my voice. There was plenty of nuance in his voice and his words didn’t bleed into one another. When I was hovering over the Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 icon, his voice still came through clearly without being overpowered by the background music.

Even though it doesn’t feature a detachable boom mic, the Pulse’s mic’s performance feels on par with that of the Cherry XTRFY H3, and it even upstages the Turtle Beach Stealth 500 ($99).

The Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset is compatible with the PlayStation 5, PlayStation Portal, Windows and macOS machines, and iOS and Android smartphones. There are two ways of connecting the Pulse to your device. Simply plug in the included PlayStation Link USB-C dongle into your console or laptop for lag-free audio, and you’re good to go. There’s also Bluetooth connectivity for connecting to the Portal (which has PlayStation Link built into it).

What’s even better is that the Pulse features audio mixing and dual-point connectivity. What this basically means is that if you’ve got the Pulse connected to your phone via Bluetooth and to your PS5 using the dongle, you’ll be able to listen to both at the same time. No need to swap sources manually or pause one to listen to the other. Want to watch/listen to a YouTube tutorial while playing GTA 6? Easily done.

Audio mixing isn’t a brand new feature as we’ve seen it on headsets like the Razer Barracuda V2 Pro ($199)... but it’s also missing from headsets that cost more than the Pulse, like the Logitech G Pro X3 Lightspeed ($249) and the Fractal Design Scape ($199). That’s a win for Sony in my book.

One of the best things about the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset is that it doesn’t break the bank. It has a retail price of $99 at Amazon U.S. / £89 at Currys U.K. and given the trend with other Pulse models, I expect the Pulse to receive major price cuts during sales events next year.

The Pulse is priced similarly to the OG Pulse 3D ($99), the Cherry XTRFY H3 ($99), and it’s cheaper than the Pulse Elite ($149). I also think it offers more bang for your buck compared to the latter. The Pulse is also $100 cheaper than the Pulse Explore earbuds ($199). If you’re willing to wait until early 2027, the Pulse Edge will be launching with even more features, but naturally, I expect it to be pricier than the Pulse.

There isn’t a lot wrong with the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset, but it’s important to note that there’s no smartphone app, and the bass sounds slightly thin.

While I love the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset’s wide soundstage and life-like sound (as I waxed lyrical about earlier), I really wish the bass was more powerful. In Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, I found that my magic attacks didn’t land with much oomph. Luna’s Elemental Trick and Elemental Genesis are meant to rumble and shake the ground, but that wasn’t the case through the Pulse.

Similarly, in Control, the Hiss’ low-end drone felt more like a buzz, which meant that the alien-like enemies didn’t feel as unnerving as they do through a high-end headset, like the Razer BlackShark V3 Pro ($249). You’ll need to spend a lot more to get full-bodied sound.

As I mentioned earlier, the Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset’s settings can be adjusted via the PS5’s settings — and there’s no other way to do it. Yes, even though the headset is compatible with computers and smartphones, you won’t be able to change its equalizer and other settings unless you have access to a PS5.

I wouldn’t say this is a dealbreaker per se as the Pulse is a PlayStation-first headset. But I would caution against getting it if you play across multiple platforms. A headset like the Logitech G325 Lightspeed ($79) or the Turtle Beach Stealth 500 might be better suited to your needs if that’s the case.

The Sony PlayStation Pulse Wireless Headset might not be perfect but it’s still a darn good set of cans. Sporting plush ear cushions that feel like hugs for your ears, the Pulse is supremely comfortable for long gaming sessions. 30 hours of battery life, dual-point connectivity, and strong mic performance are great, but it's the headset’s 3D audio that will quickly win you over.

While its soundstage is commendable, the Pulse struggles to reproduce ground-shaking bass (which is to be expected at this price). Customizing the headset is easy through your PS5’s settings but if you plan on using it with your smartphone or computer, you won’t find any companion apps on those platforms.

At the end of the day, if you’re after detailed, life-like sound that feels immersive, and a comfortable fit that doesn’t hurt your ears after a few hours, I can’t recommend the Pulse enough. It punches way above its weight.
