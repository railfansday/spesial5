Our relationships are foundational to our lives—our sense of safety and how we understand the world around us. When someone close to us suddenly, imperceptibly shifts, it can be unsettling, especially because you can’t be sure what’s changed or why. Those are the waters where Hulu’s Phony swims, delivering a perfectly pitched dramedy about a teenager (Sam Nivola) who emerges from a brief coma convinced that his mother (a pitch-perfect Connie Britton) has been replaced with a double. If you loved the series (and similar shows) and you’re looking for more intriguing stories about doubles and impostors close to home, you should definitely watch Goodnight Mommy.

Goodnight Mommy definitely leans into the psychological horror aspect of an impostor mother much, much harder than Phony, but it’s just a deeper dive into the show’s darker implications (and, fair warning, this movie is dark).

A woman identified only as Mother returns from having cosmetic surgery with her face totally enveloped in bandages, and her twin sons observe strange differences in her behavior that convince them she isn’t their real mother—she appears to ignore one of her sons completely, and disciplines them much more harshly than before. The boys also find clues that bolster their fears, including a photograph showing their mother posing with a woman dressed identically to her.

The boys’ attempts to force her to confess rapidly descend into a kind of madness, ultimately leading them to tie her to the bed in an effort to force her to admit she’s not their real mother. It all leads to a reveal as affecting as Phony’s ultimate explanation (albeit a lot more grim and violent). The film goes way harder than the series, but if you were totally into the premise, this is the more horrifying version you’ll want to see. The original film is German; there was a 2022 remake starring Naomi Watts, and while it's pretty good, the original is the right choice. Stream Goodnight Mommy on Kanopy or Plex, or rent it on Prime Video.

Looking for more impostor stories that dig into the psychological and emotional impact of suspecting someone has been replaced? Here are a few more movies that dig into the concept.

The Hole in the Ground offers an inversion: A mother who becomes convinced that her son has been replaced by an impostor. Firmly in the supernatural horror genre, the story still plays the same game with the viewer: Is Sarah (Seána Kerslake) crazy, or has her son, Chris (James Quinn Markey), been replaced by a creature living in the woods near their home? It’s got the same disturbing energy as Sarah struggles with how much her son’s changed behavior is her own psychological projection and how much is clear evidence that he’s no longer the same person—or, possibly, even the same species. Stream The Hole in the Ground on HBO Max or rent it on Prime Video.

Taking a slightly more literary approach (it’s based on a novella by Fyodor Dostoyevsky), The Double explores what happens if you find yourself replaced by a doppelgänger. Simon (Jesse Eisenberg) is a nonentity—ignored at work, lonely, and silently longing for the girl living across the street, Hannah (Mia Wasikowska). One day, a new employee named James appears at Simon’s job—and James looks exactly like Simon. But James is assertive, confident, and charming, sending Simon into a spiral. As the two versions slide into a battle for dominance, Simon finds himself turning to increasingly deranged moves to rid himself of the new and improved version of himself. If you loved the psychological implications that Phony explores, check out The Double. Stream The Double on Kanopy, Hoopla, or Tubi, or rent it on Prime Video.

On one level, Denis Villeneuve’s 2013 film is about a college professor named Adam (Jake Gyllenhaal) who discovers an actor named Anthony who looks exactly like him (also played by Gyllenhaal). When he tracks his double down, their lives merge in messy ways, ending in tragedy and one of the most striking (and confusing) final shots in cinema history. On another level, Enemy is a deft trick, exploring someone’s subconscious using dream logic and misdirection. If the best part about Phony was the disorienting sense that a secret just under the surface will explain everything, Enemy is the perfect choice. Stream Enemy on HBO Max or rent it on Prime Video.

If you’re looking for a deeper, crazier, and more terrifying story about doubles, Andrzej Żuławski’s 1981 film is a terrific choice. Inspired in part by his own feelings in the wake of a painful divorce, the story follows Mark (Sam Neil), a spy who returns to West Berlin at the height of the Cold War only to find out his wife, Anna (Isabelle Adjani), has been having an affair and wants a divorce. But Anna’s behavior becomes increasingly strange, and then Mark meets their son’s teacher, Helen, who looks just like Anna. And that’s before Anna kind of ... grows a replacement Mark from a tentacled creature kept in her bathroom (and that’s just the beginning of how weird this movie gets), resulting in two doubles. How much of the film is meant literally and how much simply represents the trauma of romantic betrayal and divorce is up to you, but Possession will definitely leave a mark. Stream Possession on Shudder, Kanopy, or Hoopla, or rent it on Prime Video.

Obsession looks at the Doppelgänger Effect from a different perspective. Cliff Robertson plays Michael Courtland, a wealthy businessman whose wife (Geneviève Bujold) and young daughter, Amy (Wanda Blackman), are kidnapped in 1959. When he follows police advice against delivering the ransom, both appear to be killed in an explosion. Courtland spends the next 16 years hating himself, until he encounters a young woman, Sandra (also Bujold), who looks almost exactly like his late wife. He works to make Sandra look as much like his wife as possible, and eventually proposes—but then Sandra is kidnapped in precisely the same way as his wife and daughter sixteen years before. This all leads to a head-spinning series of reveals and a twist that echoes the Hitchcock classic Vertigo. Rent Obsession on Prime Video.
