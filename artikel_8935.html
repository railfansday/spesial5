Here’s What to Know About the Nike Vaporfly 5

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

We earn a commission for products purchased through some links in this article. Why Trust Us?

Nike announced a whole slate of innovations it has in the works, including a new method for creating Air units, the return of the Nike Free, and a look at the future of its sportswear. But the most interesting product unveiling is the Vaporfly 5—Nike is calling it the “most aggressive Vaporfly yet.”

The big highlight is that the road racing shoe is basically going to have a “spring” in it. “At the center of Vaporfly 5 is a new, wave-shaped Flyplate,” Nike’s announcement read. “A ripple through the midfoot creates a spring-like sensation under the arch.”

I got a look at the prototype in Chicago last month, when I visited Nike’s headquarters during marathon weekend. There, I asked Bret Schoolmeester, vice president of running footwear product, if he was concerned about calling the shoe a spring. He is not, and told me that shoes basically serve as springs already given the bouncy modern foams being used.

Remember back to the first carbon-plated shoes, when much of the uproar around them was because people believed the plates would act like springs. That’s not really what came to fruition, as we found that the plates basically were used as stiffening mechanisms to create leverage.

World Athletics rules permit a plate in a single layer, and that’s what Nike is technically creating here. The difference is in the shaping. The bump in this new plate, combined with the midsole geometry, will allow the plate to flex when you land and snap back to shape to propel you ahead.

Nike also looked at ways to strip weight, which you can see through the big carve-outs in the foam, the super thin Vapor Mesh upper, and sparse use of Goatek outsole. Another weight-saving measure is adding ZoomX LT, the lightweight version of ZoomX that we saw in the Alphafly 4. A thin layer of that sits between the plate and your foot, while the standard ZoomX is used between the plate and the road for control and responsiveness.

With these changes, Nike is positioning this shoe to be a shorter-distance road racing shoe. Given the overhaul of the Alphafly 4 and the announcement of the Apex, a hyper shoe designed for elite marathoners, the Vaporfly is being pitched for races up to 13.1 miles. Sure, some runners will lace it up for a full marathon, though it’s going to be lighter and snappier to excel at faster speeds.

The shoe won’t be available until “fall 2027,” but Nike is showing it off early as it drums up excitement for upcoming footwear and apparel at its World of Athletes event.

Beyond the Vaporfly 5, there were some other cool new innovations announced, including “Tuned Air.” Through a new manufacturing process, Nike is able to create lighter Air units more quickly, which can be customized for various applications.

I spoke with Phil McCartney, executive vice president, chief innovation, design and product officer, last week about Tuned Air. He explained that through a proprietary digital print and selective lamination process, Air units can be created with different amounts of cushioning and support. Those characteristics are delivered not only through the physical geometries of the Air unit, but also by varying the pressure within each unit.

Nike has previously talked about “Tuned Air,” and we saw it in use with the Pegasus Plus 2. But that was really just offering a few different air bags. The idea is that bigger shoes are worn by bigger runners and, thus, bigger Air units with greater gas pressures are required. So, shoes any smaller than a women’s 7.5 (M6) got an Air unit that’s 9.5 mm thick with 15 psi. Moving on up to a men’s 10.5 (W12), the bag gains 2 mm thickness. And, those runners with larger feet sit atop the 11.5mm-thick unit pumped up with 20 psi.

These new units, however, are different altogether. Nike found a way to remove the need for pins and rigid zones that formed the Air units, so that each can offer better cushioning and responsiveness.

Like the Vaporfly 5, Tuned Air won’t make an appearance until later in 2027. The prototype shoe teased in the image above has one massive Air unit making up the entire midsole. No word yet on which model or franchise that shoe will reside.

While we wait, the fastest Nike available to any of us remains the Alphafly 4.
