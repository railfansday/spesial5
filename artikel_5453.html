Why some regional brokerages are avoiding ancillary services

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Large national brokerages are expanding into mortgage, title, and servicing to offset thin margins. Several regional firms are opting out, leaning on lender relationships and advertising partnerships while others call ancillaries essential for control and profitability.  AI Summary

It’s no secret that the U.S. housing market may see fewer than 4 million existing home sales in 2027. In an attempt to boost ever-tightening margins and claim a larger slice of the transaction pie, many large national brokerages have spent the past year doubling down on ancillary services.

From new mortgage joint ventures to diving into mortgage servicing, companies from eXp Realty to HomeServices of America are looking to further integrate the disparate parts of a real estate transaction into one vertical platform. While this may be the way some of the nation’s largest brokerage companies are thinking about the future, many regional brokerages with strong market share in their states or metros are taking a different tack.

“Our company has been around for 153 years and our strategy has always been to be masters of our craft and not to chase every adjacent revenue stream,” Matt Leone, the chief marketing officer at Brown Harris Stevens (BHS), a RealTrends Verified brokerage, said. “I think that we are a residential resale, new development and property management brokerage. Mortgage and title may offer additional income, but our greatest opportunity is to stay relentlessly focused on real estate and be the best at that. I think, when you venture into other arenas, your core gets saturated and your eyes come off the prize.”

Like BHS, Linda O’Koniewski’s RealTrends Verified firm, Leading Edge Real Estate, is also staying out of ancillary services.

“While margins are critically thin for brokerages, and the income would be welcome, we have given it all up,” O’Koniewski said, noting that her company had previously had partnerships with insurance and mortgage companies. “What our clients need is the best loan officer and product. They need a super-responsive insurance agent. We are dedicating ourselves to developing processes and strategies that pre-resolve preventable problems in a transaction and create the best outcomes for our clients.”

O’Koniewski added that her firm’s in-house legal counsel has also suggested homing in on real estate as industries like mortgage and title are not their primary area of expertise.

Although firms like Leading Edge and BHS may not own or have ancillary services joint ventures, this doesn’t mean they lack relationships with lenders or title companies.

At BHS, instead of offering mortgage or title services directly or through a joint venture, the company works with trusted mortgage or title professionals to contribute to the company’s blog, sponsor its podcasts and participate in educational panel discussions.

Leone said BHS works to establish relationships with the top mortgage and title professionals through a “massive in-house commitment to marketing and content creation,” bringing voices from ancillary services industries that BHS feels are the most aligned with its culture and mission.

“They’re providing their expertise to our audience that follows us, as well as providing advertising support to expand the audience that views our 18-plus podcasts and in-house created episodic series shows,” he said. “It is legitimate advertising and earned media exposure at fair market value that allows us to get their expertise to broadcast that to our client base and it also allows us to have an ancillary source of legitimate income.”

Through these posts and marketing material, Leone said BHS is looking to educate its agents and clients, which is why the brokerage likes to partner with creative and experienced ancillary services providers through the program.

“We’re bringing experienced professionals that have seen it all before, and we like to bring those individuals in to get their insights,” Leone said. “Hopefully it allows everyone to stay informed and that is our goal when we’re creating content.”

While not yet as advanced as BHS’s marketing machine, this is what Peter Voss is looking to do with the in-house video production and editing segment he and his team have launched at 3% Listing Co.

“Agents can schedule a time with the in-house video team whenever they want and work with our director on a script and then get in front of the camera and record. We take care of all the editing and produce the video,” Voss said.

Voss said, for his firm, the video production started as a way to provide services to their agents that are beneficial to their business, but also something his firm could afford without significantly increasing overhead costs.

“It is a way to help our agents get their branding out and hopefully generate more transactions, which helps them but also helps our business,” he said.

In the future, Voss said they are looking to expand this service beyond just 3% Listing Co agents, allowing the company to have an additional revenue stream similar to BHS’s operation.

While brokerages like BHS, 3% Listing Co. and Leading Edge may not feel the need to go all-in on ancillary services, Michele Harrington, at First Team Real Estate, a RealTrends Verified firm, sees them as a must-have.

“It is kind of a no-brainer. The profit margins in the real estate brokerage business are just so tiny, so any little bump in the market or a downturn in the market is really going to hurt,” Harrington, a HousingWire Woman of Influence, said.

This is why Harrington believes anyone who owns a brokerage should be exploring ancillary services. Even prior to opening First Team, Harrington, who previously led a 300-agent company based in Southern California, had her own escrow and mortgage companies.

At First Team, Harrington runs an escrow company and a mortgage brokerage operation. She previously had mortgage joint ventures, but said she preferred going the owned mortgage operation route in order to maximize margins.

“The joint venture business isn’t my favorite type of mortgage operation because it is hard to be competitive and have good rates for consumers when everyone involved is trying to make a profit,” she said. “So, your best bet is being a broker where you can go directly to the banks or Rocket or United Wholesale Mortgage and broker the loan. It makes you way more competitive.”

By owning ancillary services, not only can you boost your profit margins, Harrington also feels it enables brokers to provide even better service to consumers.

“It gives you more control. If something goes wrong with escrow or mortgage, the agent calls me, and I tell the president that they need to fix it. That level of control is a nice perk for the agents,” Harrington said.

Beyond providing her agents with more control over transactions, Harrington said having ancillary businesses within her company also helps First Team be more competitive as a brokerage.

“If I want to be more competitive in my splits or be more competitive by offering signing bonuses, I can do those things because ancillary services increase my margins,” she said.

While Voss sees the value of having ancillary services operations, especially when it comes to revenue opportunities, he still doesn’t believe the juice is necessarily worth the squeeze.

“We have talked about bringing mortgage in-house, but what we have found is that in-house operations can be a turn off to a lot of agents,” Voss said. “They feel like it is kind of forced on them to have to send their clients to their firm’s mortgage lender. We are really focused on recruitment right now, so that is why we have shied away from this.”

Leone agrees, telling HousingWire that in-house mortgage or title operations often feel like a “forced option” that agents have to suggest to their clients.

“We don’t want to get in between the relationship of the client and our agent and the agents understand who is best for their client and who is best for that transaction,” he said. “Different lenders make sense for different neighborhoods, price points and scenarios and we don’t want to get in the middle of that.”

Instead Leone and Voss feel that their companies can generate more revenue by focusing on being the best at helping buyers and sellers transact real estate.

“We really focus on our relationships with mortgage lenders and title companies and making sure that we make their jobs easier so that they may be more open to sending business to us and our agents because they know we will get the job done,” Voss said.
