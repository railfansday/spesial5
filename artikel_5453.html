Zoom’s conversations-to-completion strategy is starting to pay off

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

ANALYSIS by Zeus Kerravala

Zoom Communications Inc. has spent the better part of the past few years building products to become more than just the video company everyone used during the pandemic. Its fiscal third-quarter results, posted Monday, showed the strategy is working, though more work remains.

Total revenue grew 4.9%, but enterprise revenue rose 8.6%, its fastest pace in 13 quarters. Zoom raised its full-year outlook and announced its first quarterly dividend of 25 cents per share.

The acceleration should be viewed not as a one-quarter anomaly but as a manifestation of the strategy the company outlined at Zoomtopia about a month ago – moving from “conversations to completion.” That strategy gives customers a reason to buy more of Zoom and builds a competitive moat wider than most investors realize.

The concept of conversations to completion has enough merit that Microsoft used those exact words at its recent Ignite 2026 event. In fact, it held an entire session on the topic. Though having an 800-pound gorilla like Microsoft jump on a trend might seem threatening, Zoom has a significant lead and can use Microsoft’s marketing to add momentum to its current tailwind.

Nearly all work starts with a conversation: a meeting, a call, a chat thread or a customer interaction. Then everyone leaves, and the real work begins: updating the customer record, writing the follow-up and scheduling the next step. Collaboration vendors have always owned the first step, leaving the second to someone else.

Zoom wants both. “We’d like to leverage AI to transform our business from a conversation-centric vendor to a system of completions,” Chief Executive Eric Yuan said on the earnings call. He later put it more plainly: “Conversation is step 1. Step 2, AI will take over.”

His proof point was Zoom’s own sales organization, which uses ZoomMate, the company’s agentic framework. Sellers, Yuan said, “no longer log into the backend CRM system.” The agent takes over after the call ends. In enterprise software, value is shifting from where work is recorded to where it originates.

AI agents are quickly becoming a commodity, and so are the models behind them. What is scarce is context, the knowledge of what was decided, by whom, and why. Zoom sits on the richest source of it in most companies: what people actually said to one another and to customers.

“Any agent without context really cannot get work done,” Yuan said. That’s why the unified context layer Zoom showcased at Zoomtopia is central to the story. It links conversations to chats, documents and third-party systems, ensuring people and agents work from the same information.

My Notes, the company’s AI note-taker, extends that reach to third-party and in-person conversations. Yuan said it is nearing 2 million monthly active users, with one financial services firm rolling it out to 80,000 wealth advisers. Chief Financial Officer Michelle Chang added that online customers using My Notes have been associated with a 50% reduction in churn.

That is what will create a deeper competitive moat for Zoom. The more context a customer stores with Zoom, the more useful its agents become and the harder it is to leave. Zoom’s moat is not the meeting. Think of it as the memory of the meeting.

A context layer is only as good as the number of conversations it ingests, and this is where Zoom’s breadth of platforms matters. Meetings, phone, chat, contact center and now sales intelligence all feed into the same foundation, and each product gets smarter from the others.

Customers are consolidating. DSI Systems is replacing five separate products with Zoom. SolarWinds expanded its Zoom licenses while “recalibrating its Microsoft package,” according to Yuan. Enterprise net dollar expansion returned to 100%, its highest level in 11 quarters. “Strong RPO growth reflects our continued success in landing larger, longer-term multiproduct platform deals, demonstrating growing demand for our AI-first platform,” Chang said.

Customer experience is where completion gets paid for. Zoom CX recurring revenue grew in the high double digits for the seventh straight quarter, and all 10 of its largest CX deals included paid AI. More telling, Zoom said at Zoomtopia that 90% of its top 100 CX deals in the first half were with customers new to Zoom. Contact center is no longer a cross-sell. It is a front door.

Chang described the differentiation as “the ability to think about customer experience in a whole and fresh new way.” Specifically, she said, “to be able to seamlessly hand off from virtual agents to agents assisted with AI is a big winner for us.” AI-native startups have the virtual agent but not the human side. Legacy contact center vendors have the humans but not the pace of innovation. Zoom has both on one platform.

There is also an economic moat. Zoom runs most of its AI workload on its own small models and saves the expensive frontier models for harder tasks. “I fundamentally believe no single model can satisfy everything,” Yuan said. Gross margin rose even as AI usage grew, a feat that a point product reselling another company’s tokens cannot easily match.

The quarter was strong, but analyst questions revealed several concerns.

Some will read the dividend as a sign of maturity. With more than $7 billion in cash and free cash flow margins above 40%, I see a company that can fund innovation and return capital at the same time.

Although these issues came up during the call, most of the investment community remains positive about Zoom. In his note, Arun Bhatia of William Blair wrote, “Churn and downsell rates are becoming more favorable, AI usage and monetization are increasing, and adoption of new products such as Phone and Zoom CX is strong. We are encouraged by these trends and see potential for a growth uptick in the future.”

Zoom’s headline growth resembles that of a mature software vendor, but the company is in transition, so it’s important to look beyond the numbers. The underlying enterprise business is built to be a platform that uses context to drive customers toward more products and longer commitments. Investors should watch enterprise growth, net expansion and CX AI attach rates rather than the top line.

For information technology and business leaders, the lesson extends beyond Zoom. Conversation data is a strategic asset, so decide deliberately where it lives and which agents can act on it. Insist on openness so that context can feed the tools you choose. Start where completion is easy to measure, in the contact center and in sales.

That is where Zoom is proving that the conversation was never the product. The outcome is.

Zeus Kerravala is a principal analyst at ZK Research, a division of Kerravala Consulting. He wrote this article for SiliconANGLE.
