CleanSpark Weighs Selling More Bitcoin Than It Mines After $987M Loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

CleanSpark (Nasdaq: CLSK) may sell more bitcoin than it mines as it works to lower risk on its balance sheet, President and CFO Gary Vecchiarelli said Tuesday, after the miner posted a $987.1 million net loss for its fiscal year ended Sept. 30, as lower bitcoin prices hit the value of its holdings.

“We see the selling of a small amount of bitcoin in excess of the quarterly production to be one way of derisking,” Vecchiarelli said on the company’s earnings call. He added that CleanSpark will be “very reactionary to markets” and use that cash to avoid dilution or new debt.

Vecchiarelli said CleanSpark already aims to sell virtually all of the bitcoin it mines to run the business, and that it has sold some bitcoin since year-end to build cash for acquisitions it expects to make soon. “It is reasonable to expect that we will fund highly accretive acquisitions and development of projects by selling or borrowing against our bitcoin,” he said.

The Las Vegas-based company reported full-year revenue of $576.5 million, down 25%. The loss came to $3.74 per basic share, and adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $304.2 million. Vecchiarelli said about two-thirds of the swing from last year’s $364 million profit came from noncash mark-to-market losses on bitcoin.

CleanSpark recorded a $281.6 million loss on the fair value of its bitcoin for the year, versus a $425.6 million gain a year earlier. It also took $177.1 million in impairment charges, mostly tied to turning its Sandersville, Georgia, mining site into a data center. Bitcoin ended the fiscal year near $83,600, down from about $114,000, the CFO said.

The fourth quarter looked different. CleanSpark earned $9.8 million, or 4 cents a share, helped by a $305.6 million gain on its bitcoin, even as revenue dropped 46% from a year earlier to $120.9 million. Analysts had expected a loss of 33 cents a share and revenue of about $134.3 million, Investing.com reported.

The miner produced 7,253 BTC during the year, down 8% as network difficulty rose and it curtailed some machines, and ended September with about 200,000 machines running, down from 225,000 at the end of June. That cut lifted fleet efficiency to 14.8 joules per terahash, while average hashrate fell to 36.0 EH/s.

CleanSpark held 13,530 BTC, including pledged collateral, at the end of September, worth about $1.1 billion then. Its annual report shows it sold 1,532 bitcoin during the year and used another 6,068 to settle derivatives. At Wednesday’s price near $57,700, 13,530 BTC would be worth roughly $780 million.

CleanSpark stock fell to a new 52-week low of $4.43 shortly after Wednesday’s open, before trimming losses to about $4.85 around 10 a.m. ET, down roughly 5% from Tuesday’s $5.12 close, as the Nasdaq sank about 4% at the open. Shares had slipped about 3% in premarket trading.

At least three firms cut their price targets while keeping buy-equivalent ratings. Needham lowered its target to $15 from $23, citing weaker mining results, while H.C. Wainwright cut to $18 from $22 and Cantor Fitzgerald trimmed to $14 from $19, saying the stock near $5 prices in little beyond Sandersville.

Miners broadly are under pressure with bitcoin below $60,000 and down about 24% in November. Bitdeer sold all 281 bitcoin it mined last week, while American Bitcoin lined up a $150 million credit line backed by its BTC. JPMorgan warned in June that mining costs had worsened.

CleanSpark now calls itself an “energy native data center developer.” Its 20-year, $6.6 billion lease at Sandersville is funded by $2.276 billion of 7.875% notes due 2031, and CEO Matt Schultz said the first data hall is on track for the fourth quarter of 2027.

Texas is moving slower. Exclusivity periods at the Sealy and Brazoria sites, which offer up to 885 MW, expired without a lease. “The Texas regulatory landscape for data center development is evolving, and that has extended project time lines across the state,” Schultz said on the call.
