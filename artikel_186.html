Florida to carry out record-breaking 20th execution of the year

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

State to execute man convicted of two counts of first-degree murder while Texas also plans to put a man to death

Florida is set Thursday to carry out a record-breaking 20th execution of the year on a man convicted of killing a pregnant woman and her husband during a home invasion, while Texas plans to put a man to death for the killing of a Houston jeweler during a $3.6m diamond heist.

Thomas Mitchell Overton, 71, was scheduled to receive a three-drug injection at 6pm at Florida state prison in Raiford. Reinaldo Dennes, 70, was set to receive a lethal injection about an hour later at a prison in Texas and become the ninth person executed by that state this year. The planned executions of the two septuagenarians in one day reflect the ageing of the US death row population.

Overton was convicted of killing Michael and Susan MacIvor and their unborn child during an August 1991 break-in at their Florida Keys home. Dennes was convicted of capital murder in the death of Janos Szucs, a diamond dealer found shot multiple times in his office in January 1996.

On Thursday, Overton woke up at 5am and remained compliant, a Florida department of corrections spokesperson, Jordan Kirkland, said during a news conference. He did not meet with any visitors or a spiritual adviser.

Florida has carried out more than half the nation’s 37 executions this year under Ron DeSantis, the state’s outgoing Republican governor who has made aggressive use of the death penalty like few other governors. Thursday’s scheduled executions come amid renewed scrutiny of capital punishment in the US after Tennessee’s failed execution of Christa Pike in October.

Texas set a record for executions there when 40 people were put to death in 2000.

On Thursday, the US supreme court denied a final appeal in the Florida case while an appeal remained pending in the Texas case.

Overton was convicted in 1999 of two counts of first-degree murder and received a death sentence for each. Defense attorneys had argued that investigators had planted the DNA evidence.

The MacIvors were found dead in their Tavernier, Florida, home in August 1991, according to court records. Autopsies determined both were strangled and that Susan MacIvor, who was eight months pregnant, had been raped. The medical examiner said her unborn male child likely survived for about half an hour after her death.

Investigators collected DNA evidence from the scene but initially found no match with samples in the state’s database. But Overton, who had worked at a gas station near the MacIvors’ home, was arrested on an unrelated burglary charge in 1996. Authorities said a sample taken from him matched the sample collected at the MacIvors’ home, and he was charged with their killings.

In the Texas case, Dennes, a jewelry designer, had an office in the same building as Szucs, according to court documents. A cleaning woman testified at trial that Dennes had arranged for her to open the building’s back door after hours. According to court records, she opened the door for him and his brother the night of the shooting and also distracted the security guard at Dennes’s request.

The guard, who was shot twice but recovered, identified Dennes as his attacker.

Police found the jeweler’s body upon entering his office, according to court filings. The documents said officers found the jeweler’s safe empty with no sign of the diamonds or the $200,000 in cash he was supposed to have on hand. The diamonds have never been recovered.

Dennes’s attorneys later argued there was insufficient evidence to prove that he killed Szucs.

Prosecutors noted in a recent court filing that Dennes admitted guilt during a Spanish-language interview with Telemundo in 2022.

“I did those things, so I am guilty,” Dennes told Telemundo, adding that he regretted taking a man’s life, the prosecutor’s filing said.

A year after Dennes was convicted, his brother, Jose Albert Dennes, pleaded guilty to murder in the case and was sentenced to 45 years in prison. He was released on parole in 2019.
