GTA 6 comes out before Raptors can break losing streak vs. Wizards

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It's been nearly 13 days since the Toronto Raptors last won a game.

The slump continues after the Raptors fell to the Washington Wizards 103-99 on Wednesday.

On a night when the highly anticipated Grand Theft Auto 6 is released to the public, here are four takeaways from the game, partly to lighten the mood in Toronto, and also to make you wonder how long fans will have to wait before the Raptors get back into the win column.

One of the main attractions of GTA 6 is the ability to switch between the two new protagonists Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos. Both players move through the campaign as a unit, but they also have their own unique set of skills, story beats, and responsibilities.

The same goes for Immanuel Quickley and Jamal Shead, especially as the point guards on a night without Kawhi Leonard, Scottie Barnes and RJ Barrett.

As the lone guard on the court, both Quickley and Shead were exceptional. Quickley finished with a team-high 25 points on 42.8 per cent shooting. He also added five assists and four made threes.

"We're just playing together. We're short a lot of guys, we can't just do it with one person," said Quickley. "We did a pretty good job of moving their defence around and gave ourselves a chance to tie it in the end."

Meanwhile, Shead led both benches in scoring with 15 points.

As in prior games this season, the duo was also deployed as a backcourt tandem. After losing the opening 12 minutes 29-23, Quickley and Shead were instrumental in helping Toronto win the second and third quarters.

Outside of the main heroes, there is a litany of new side characters in GTA 6 destined to take their seats alongside some of the franchise's most iconic figures. Brian Heder (landlord and smuggler) serves a narrative clutch, allowing Jason and Lucia to live rent-free during the game.

At Scotiabank Arena, rent comes in the form of wins and success, and it's unfortunately past due.

While Allen Graves hasn't been able to properly capitalize on his 13-point performance against the Boston Celtics on Nov. 11, the rookie is slowly coming along after 15 games and is on track to be the Raptors' most important "brand-new" side character.

"They're instilling a lot of confidence in me throughout this whole process," said Graves. "I feel like as each game goes on, I'm just getting better and better and understanding it more."

Graves finished with nine points and five rebounds while playing a career-high 31 minutes. He also shot 50 per cent from the field.

"You have to study film but being in the game is the best experience you can get," said Graves. "The more I'm in the game, the more I can go out there and make mistakes and learn from them."

In the second quarter, while Toronto was in one of its signature droughts, it was Graves who provided the spark. From the left wing, Graves' dribble-drive resulted in two points. Moments later on defence, his relentless motor powered him to poke a ball loose for an offensive rebound. On the ensuing offensive possession, Graves' strong finish at the rim resulted in two more points.

His tenacity and hustle alone convinced Raptors head coach Darko Rajakovic to play him 14:18 in the first half, marking a new career high through two quarters for Graves.

"He matched up in similar situations with Trae Young and he was matched up with Anthony Davis," said Rajakovic. "For him, those minutes are super valuable."

With Collin Murray-Boyles exiting the game at halftime due to illness, Graves started the third quarter. He immediately rewarded Rajakovic's trust in him by scoring the first bucket of the half with a leaning jumper from the left block.

GTA has always been about the side missions as much as it has been about the main storyline.

Without several of the Raptors' main weapons, the team needed its auxiliary options to step up. Jamison Battle and Ja'Kobe Walter answered the call. Truthfully, they didn't have a choice after being inserted into the starting lineup.

Battle has comfortably transformed into the clear-cut best shooter on the roster, but he's now also showing a flair for the dramatics. His three-pointer at the buzzer just before the half was theatrical. But when he used a Trayce Jackson-Davis screen on the left wing before knocking down an off-the-dribble three-pointer, it definitely opened some eyes in the building. Battle finished with 19 points.

"It's controlling what we can control," explained Rajakovic. "Our preparation, our work, our intentionality, how hard we play and compete."

Walter also added 18 points of his own, including three triples. His perimeter game is a known commodity, but it was his contributions across the board that were impressive. Walter tied his career-high in assists (6) and also added seven rebounds, trailing only Jakob Poeltl (9) on the team.

Unfortunately, Walter's glaring miscue occurred in the closing minutes, when on the fast break, he dribbled the ball off his foot and out of bounds.

Battle had a chance to tie in the end but was unable to sink the three from the top of the arc.

There's a plethora of new game mechanics that fans of the GTA franchise have spent the last year gushing over. It's fitting that several Raptors went deeper into their bag of tricks on a night that GTA 6 finally releases.

It started with Quickley, who did less probing and more attacking on the offensive side of the ball. He frequently resorted to step-back jump shots, pull-up dribble jumpers, and turnaround fadeaways.

On defence, Quickley even drew a charge to stop a Wizards 2-on-1 fast break late in the third quarter.

Jackson-Davis, who doesn't often get a chance to flash his athleticism, took the stage twice to showcase his sneaky pop. On his first attempt, he nearly put a Wizards defender through the rim. Later in the quarter, Jackson-Davis almost posterized Anthony Davis.

Finally, in the fourth quarter, it was Malachi Smith, who effortlessly created dangerous space with his dribble before finding Quickley in the left corner for a wide-open three.
