Dylan Harper plays the hero as Spurs beat Jazz, extend win streak to 12

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

It was an ugly one and too close for comfort, but the Spurs got their 12th win in a row in their visit to Salt Lake City. Despite not playing particularly well, San Antonio escaped with a 110-102 victory to hand the Jazz their 12th straight loss, in large part thanks to Dylan Harper fourth-quarter heroics.

All the ingredients required for a strange game that could get more complicated than expected for the Spurs were there. It was a Sunday matinee; De'Aaron Fox was out; the Jazz had already played the Silver and Black twice in the young season; it was a one-game road trip at altitude. Yet it was hard to imagine just how weird things would be, especially in the first half. Mo Bamba turning into Stephen Curry was hard to predict, and so was Jordan McLaughlin being the first guy to reach double digits for the visitors. San Antonio's defense being lethargic early was no surprise but the way Utah was making them pay by hitting jumpers was. On the other end, meanwhile, Fox was missed in the halfcourt, so transition offense had to pick up the slack. It wasn't the most well-played bout of the season, but it was entertaining and close, as the score was tied after one.

The Spurs have been relying on their talent to overwhelm opponents even when the execution and occasionally the energy have been far from ideal, and to some degree, they achieved that against the Jazz in the first half. This time, however, getting a superstar performance from Wembanyama and a couple of other contributors to step up simply wasn't enough to create a lead, like it has in past games. Utah was confident after the strong start and didn't let a few missed jumpers discourage them. Defensively, they had no answers for The Alien, who had his three-point shot going, but were happy to sit back and watch others fall into bad habits and take the bait of driving or shooting when the other option would have been preferable. It was clear that San Antonio was going to have to take the win, because the Jazz were not going to give it away without a fight.

During two different stretches of the third, it seemed like the Spurs were finally going to impose their will. A strong start allowed them to carve a double-digit lead, but errant shooting and lackadaisical defense followed, and the home team quickly caught up. Then when the bench checked in, they managed to put together a rare run for a group that has struggled often, putting the Silver and Black up nine after an alley-oop dunk from the oft-maligned Luke Kornet. Alas, Utah once again rallied after a timeout, shooting their way back while San Antonio's jumpers refused to fall. Holding serve while the second unit was in would have normally been enough if the starters had come in rested to close the frame strong, but it just didn't happen. The game was tied going into a final period, and it didn't feel impossible to see two streaks being snapped on a shocker of an ending.

Fortunately, an overall strange game finished with the most logical result. The Jazz, who have struggled with maintaining leads, especially at the end of games, looked like the young, shorthanded team that they are in the clutch. Keldon Johnson, last year's Sixth Man of the Year, was the sparkplug San Antonio needed all game long, but elevated his impact with some crucial plays late. And Dylan Harper, who showed in the playoffs that he's not scared of the big moments, took over as the first option that Wembanyama couldn't be because of how much attention Utah was paying to his every move on offense. The young guard dropped 18 of his career-high 31 in the final period to keep the Spurs close enough to take advantage of the mistakes the home team would make down the stretch. It wasn't the prettiest of wins, but it was enough to keep the streak going.

What makes Dylan Harper such a scary young player is that a lot of what he does at this point in his career, on both ends, seems mostly done on instinct and sheer talent. He's a smart, polished player in some ways, but in others, he just… does things that happen to get good results more often than not. For most of the game he wasn't particularly efficient and at times forced things, but when the team actually needed someone to take over, that same hyper-aggressiveness and confidence that sometimes leads to poor decisions was the reason why he was ready to explode offensively. Harper is already a fantastic player who will only get better as a decision-maker, which should terrify every other team in the league.

While Harper's heroics will rightfully get most of the attention, Wembanyama's first half was also needed for the Spurs to escape with a win. Moreover, the Jazz' devoting so much attention to him on dives to the basket opened things up for others, including Harper on his consecutive fourth-quarter threes. The big guy did his job.

The third spot on the podium belongs to Keldon Johnson. In a game in which Devin Vassell and Julian Champagnie combined for 10 points on 17 shots and Harrison Barnes and Carter Bryant didn't move the needle off the bench, San Antonio needed someone who wasn't a guard or a center to step up. Johnson's made contributions in different areas, as accurately reflected by his stat line. A great mix of aggressiveness on offense and energy plays for a veteran who continues to reinvent himself.

The Spurs missed Fox, which isn't surprising, since he's their best half-court orchestrator, but Stephon Castle slotted in nicely in his old role as primary playmaker and had 20 points and nine assists. Jordan McLaughlin was decent off the bench as well, hitting some shots in the first half. Against better opponents, Fox's absence could be a bigger problem, but San Antonio survived without him.

The Spurs will kick off a three-game homestand by taking on one of the best teams in the East. It should be a good test for a San Antonio team that has not faced a lot of good opponents lately.
