The Suns beat the Kings 128-107 to get to .500 for the first time this season

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Phoenix Suns are now .500 for the first time this season as they won convincingly against the Sacramento Kings, 128-107. The Suns were firing on all cylinders offensively with both Devin Booker and Dillon Brooks scoring 29 points a piece to lead the Suns. The Kings were led by rookie sensation Darius Acuff Jr., who scored 36 points on 11-of-22 shooting, including 6-for-11 from 3, while Zach LaVine scored 18 as well on 8-of-12 shooting.

The Suns were not sharp defensively in the first half but had long stretches in the third and fourth quarters where they were active in creating Kings turnovers and deflections. Even when they were sharper on defense, the shot-making from LaVine and Acuff Jr. kept this game closer than what it should have been through three quarters.

The Suns' game plan against the Kings was to make Acuff Jr. defend, and he simply did not hold up. Many of Brooks 29 points came against Acuff Jr. with his back to the basket in the mid-post, and Miles Bridges scored 13 points on 6-of-8 shooting, taking advantage of smaller mismatches in the post as well. The Suns' ball movement was exquisite all night long because of the Kings' inability to stop the Suns' scorers, and the Suns rarely ended possessions without generating a quality look. The Suns shot 57% from the field and 44% from 3 on 15-of-34 shooting.

It was the same story again for Jalen Green as it was against the Nuggets. He struggled to get going in the first half, with missed shots and turnovers from his loose handle that gave the Kings momentum and stalled out Suns runs. But he stayed in the fight, and in the second half, he looked more effective. He finished with a team-high seven rebounds and a team-high seven assists. He was doing the little things well and also scored 18 points on 4-of-7 shooting from long distance.

The Suns bench scored an adequate 33 points tonight, and their scoring may continue to trend down slightly as Jordan Ott once again had Brooks in with the second unit to start the second and fourth quarters. By far the biggest takeaway from this game was how much better the starters are playing together on both ends of the court. Albeit against a now 1-13 Kings team, the main four Suns players are starting to look more confident and more connected as a group that has shaken off its slow 1-5 start to the season.

This game started off with a lot of tough shot-making. Dillon Brooks got the Suns going with a backdown layup and a 3-point jumper. NBA rookie leading scorer Darius Acuff Jr. came out firing, scoring 10 early points for the Kings. The Suns have a professional bucket getter of their own with Booker, and he scored 12 points in the first period. Six of his 12 points came on pull-up 3-point shots; his ability to knock those shots down has opened his game in a big way, and that continued to be the case against the Kings.

The Suns again subbed out Brooks first to give him minutes to start the second quarter with the bench, and Jalen Green showed some aggressiveness and energy but only went 1-of-5 from the field, and his loose handle continues to get in his way of being a solid creator for this offense. Outside of Green, both teams showed up offensively in this game and shot over 50% from the floor as the Suns led 36-31 after one, setting up the bench to take the game over.

The Suns started the second quarter strong as Gillespie knocked down a 3-point jumper and Brooks scored in the midrange. Brooks got the ball again isolated in the mid-post and missed, but Goodwin skied in for the offensive rebound tip-in to give the Suns a 43-35 lead. The Suns still could not stop the Kings as LaVine knocked in a long-range triple that Bridges matched on the other end. After a Leaky Black layup at the basket, Green found himself all alone in the corner for a wide-open 3-point shot that missed, and Monk hit a wide-open shot from the corner to get the Kings back within three.

After the timeout, the Suns got a thunderous dunk from Bridges on a nice feed from Green driving baseline. The starters continue to look more comfortable creating offense for themselves and each other the past few games. Then it was the Dillon Brooks show; he made a turnaround over LaVine, hit a 3, then scored at the rim in transition in almost consecutive possessions. He continues to look more and more like the Brooks we saw last season. The Suns were up 55-48 and had the chance to go up even more, but Luke Kennard missed back-to-back wide 3s. The Kings capitalized with Acuff Jr. and Sabonis's baskets to make it 55-52 with 4:30 left to play in the half.

They came out of the timeout with the starters plus Maluach on the floor and started what looked to be a commanding 10-0 run to finish the half. Green got to the free-throw line out of the timeout, and the Suns forced a Kings miss. What happened next was the best possession of the night: Brooks drove to the rim in transition, kicked it out to the corner, and the ball swung all the way back from one corner to the other for a wide-open Brooks 3 that he, of course, knocked down. The Suns then capped off the run with a wide-open Green 3.

But then the Suns got in their own way and allowed Acuff Jr. to hit back-to-back 3s, and a Green turnover led to a wide-open Hunter layup on the other end to close the half on an 8-0 run. Suns led 65-60 at halftime. Both teams shot over 50% in the first half in a fun-to-watch, up-and-down game. Acuff Jr. scored 21 points in the first half to lead the Kings, and Brooks led the Suns with 17, scoring 12 points in the second quarter.

The Suns did not let off the gas in the third quarter as Booker found Ighodaro for the alley-oop on their first possession. Then Green got right to the rim for an open layup, and a Booker made midrange floater went in as he was attempting to foul bait, but no call came. Booker picked up a technical foul after he was stripped by Acuff Jr. at the rim after not getting the calls he is typically accustomed to getting. After a missed pull-up jump shot by Hunter, the Suns starters executed another flawless possession as Bridges was doubled in the post, and the ball swung around the perimeter, and Brooks knocked down another 3 to add to his total. Timeout Kings as the Suns led 78-69 with eight minutes left to play.

The Suns continued the onslaught on both ends, forcing back-to-back deflections that went out of bounds. However, LaVine and Acuff Jr. each made extremely difficult 3-point shots on each of those possessions. Undeterred, the Suns continued to play excellent defense that resulted in a Booker-to-Maluach alley-oop, a wide-open Booker layup in transition, and after forcing another stop, Brooks nailed another 3 to give the Suns an 87-74 lead.

The Suns starters did exactly what they needed to start the third quarter, and the bench unit plus Booker finished it off. The Suns found Maluach for a layup right out of the timeout as he continues to show his soft touch around the rim. The Suns continued to attack the rim and draw fouls as Gillespie and Booker each cashed in on free throws. The straw that finally broke the camel's back was a Ryan Dunn steal and aggressive dunk in transition to give the Suns a 20-point lead, 96-76, Kings timeout.

The Kings closed the quarter strong as Acuff Jr. continued to show his scoring prowess, getting to the line and knocking down a 3. The Suns led 99-84 after three quarters of play.

The Suns bench did not keep it going to start the fourth quarter as Kennard missed another wide-open 3. The Kings scored 6 quick points attacking the rim in transition while the Suns offense went cold for the first time all night. With the Kings down nine, Jordan Ott quickly called a timeout two minutes into the quarter to try and stop the Kings' run.

Out of the timeout, Green slipped a screen for Bridges and cut to the rim for an easy dunk for the Suns' first points of the quarter. The Kings responded quickly off of a poor pass from Brooks as LaVine scored easily in transition. The Suns, as they did all night long, continued to attack Acuff Jr. defensively, and it was Bridges' turn to isolate and get downhill and score. After an offensive foul on Monk, Green drove hard to the rim and missed the layup but got the offensive rebound; he quickly found Kennard in the corner, who swung it to Booker, then back to a cutting Kennard, who swung it to Brooks, and then to Green in the corner for a wide-open 3 that went in to give the Suns a 14-point lead.

LaVine and Acuff Jr. continued to keep the Kings in it as they each made difficult midrange jumpshots to cut the deficit back to 10. Finally, Kennard connected on a wide-open 3, and then got a stop on the other end. The Suns went right back to what had been working all night with Brooks in the post, and he shimmied his way free for a midrange fadeaway that was all net. Acuff Jr. finally started missing, and Booker took advantage, drawing a foul in transition and hitting both free throws; then on the next possession he cut backdoor for a wide-open layup with Bridges getting the assist from the mid-post. The Suns led 115-96 with 5:22 to play.

The Suns were not finished either, as they went right back to Brooks in the post against Acuff Jr., forcing a Kings double that resulted in another swing-swing-swing where Green knocked down another corner 3. The very next possession Brooks posted up Acuff Jr. again, and the Kings doubled again. This time Brooks spun baseline away from the double for the layup to score his 29th point of the night. The Kings could not stop the Suns' mid-post offense tonight, and it led to big games from Brooks and Green.

The Suns won 128-107, and for the first time this season are at .500 with a 7-7 record.

The Suns travel to Salt Lake City to face the Utah Jazz on Friday at 7:30 Arizona time.
