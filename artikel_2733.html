Michigan basketball blasted by Creighton in Players Era 16 opener

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Another blowout for Michigan basketball in Las Vegas. Only this time it was the Wolverines getting run out of T-Mobile Arena.

A year after sweeping its three games in Vegas by a combined 100 points, Michigan lost, 99-73, to Creighton on Tuesday, Nov. 24, in its first of three games in the Players Era 16 tournament.

It didn't start out as a blowout, however; after some back-and-forth action, Michigan took a six-point lead roughly eight minutes in. And then the Blue Jays went on a 43-16 burst in the final 12 minutes of the first half, grabbing a 21-point lead at the break.

Mike Boynton Jr.'s team got as close as 15, when J.P. Estrella scored five straight with 13:50 left, but the Jays ballooned their lead to 28 less than seven minutes later and cruised the rest of the way.

In two seasons under coach Dusty May, with Boynton as the defensive guru, U-M never gave up more than 89 points in regulation. But Tuesday, Creighton had its way both inside and out; the Blue Jays went 16-for-31 on 3s and 11-for-21 on dunks and layups.

The 99 points allowed was the most surrendered by Michigan in regulation since a 99-67 loss to Purdue on Jan. 23, 2024; the program was one point away from giving up 100 for the first time since Dec. 10, 2016 (in a 102-84 loss to UCLA).

Most notably, U-M failed to take care of the ball, while Creighton was almost perfect in that regard. The Wolverines lost the turnover battle, 17-8, but half of Creighton's turnovers came in the final three minutes with its deep reserves in the game. That led to a 27-7 advantage for the Big East squad in points off giveaways.

Michigan had no response for Wes Enis, who scored a season-high 30 points while BJ Davis added 21, Austin Swartz had 13 and Hudson Greer scored 12.

Estrella, the Tennessee transfer fantastic in U-M's first five games, was again the team's best player, with a team-high 21 points to go with eight rebounds. Moustapha Thiam added 12 points and eight boards while Michigan's backcourt struggled, with Trey McKenney and Elliot Cadeau scoring 10 points each on a combined 6-for-19 shooting.

Once again, U-M's lack of depth was also exposed. Michigan got just 11 points from its reserves (some of which came in garbage time) as freshman Quinn Costello missed all seven of his 3-point tries. Jalen Reed led the reserves with just four points.

Michigan slides over to the consolation bracket in the Players Era 16 and will face Miami (Florida) on Thursday (3:30 p.m., ESPN2), with its third game against either Alabama, Baylor, Gonzaga or Kansas State on Friday. Eliminated from the title game, the Wolverines will keep their game against Mississippi Valley State on Tuesday, Dec. 1. But before then, they'll face the No. 17 Hurricanes, who fell, 84-71, to TCU on Tuesday.
