Iran says three tankers hit mines in Hormuz despite U.S. escort, as Treasury widens sanctions

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Iranian forces said three oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, the latest in a string of incidents in the waterway, as the U.S. escalates sanctions aimed at cutting off Tehran's financial lifelines.

In a statement carried by state news agency IRNA, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said three "offending" oil tankers "suffered a severe explosion and fire after hitting naval mines" in the Strait of Hormuz.

The Guards said one tanker was near the southern coast and two were in the eastern part of the strait. All three were under extensive U.S. military air cover and escort that failed to prevent the explosions.

"U.S. military cover and escort will not replace compliance with maritime regulations and instructions from the IRGC Navy Traffic Control Center," the statement said.

Iranian forces have repeatedly warned commercial vessels that only the northern shipping lane has been approved by Tehran for safe passage, while attacking or threatening ships that use alternative routes near Oman.

The U.S. added a third aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, to the Gulf last week, joining two other carriers in the fleet enforcing a naval blockade of Iranian ports.

The tanker incidents came after the U.S. Treasury on Monday announced new sanctions on more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and key personnel as part of what it called "a sweeping effort to disrupt Iran's global shadow banking networks."

Iran's economy has come under growing pressure as Washington expanded sanctions under its so-called "Operation Economic Outcast" since August, aimed at crippling financial networks behind the country's oil revenue.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, in Monday's statement, described Iran's economy as "underwater," saying its shadow banking system was "the last financial lifeline of a bankrupt regime."

The conflict also appears to have extended to cyberspace, with Washington investigating Iran as a primary suspect behind dozens of cyberattacks targeting energy tankers, The Wall Street Journal reported Monday.

As many as 50 ships carrying oil, liquefied petroleum gas, chemicals and other potentially hazardous cargoes were believed to have been targeted, while some received warnings and bomb threats, the Journal reported, citing U.S. officials and others familiar with the matter.

U.S. authorities were also examining Russia as another possible culprit, according to the report, which described the widening attacks and occasional ransom messages as part of Iran's efforts to stoke fear among shipowners and further disrupt energy flows.

The foreign ministries of Iran and Russia did not respond to CNBC's requests for comment.

The conflict, triggered by the U.S. and Israeli strikes in late February, has seen Tehran close the Strait of Hormuz and the U.S. impose a blockade of Iranian ports in the Gulf, squeezing energy shipments through one of the world's most critical shipping conduits.

Oil futures inched lower Tuesday as investors looked past continued attacks on tankers to price in rising supplies from the region. On Tuesday, international benchmark Brent futures dipped to $89.2 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude fell to $80.1 per barrel, down from the October high of nearly $105 per barrel.

Pressure on oil prices has eased as regional crude supply recovered and Russian exports rebounded, loosening supply constraints ahead of winter, said Gregory Brew, senior analyst for Iran and energy at Eurasia Group.

Russian refined-product exports recovered in November, with diesel shipments rising back to 400,000 barrels per day, Brew said. But "intensifying Iranian attacks in the Strait of Hormuz could reverse that trend," he added, estimating Brent would remain in the lower half of an $80-100-per-barrel range.
