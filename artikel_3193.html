If a Bear Market Is Coming in 2027, History Says This 1 Simple Move Can Protect Your Investments

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bear markets are typically short-lived, but this investing behavior can have long-term consequences for building wealth.

By Jack Delaney – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than S&P 500 Index ›

Investor confidence can quickly turn to anxiety during a bear market, when a major index, such as the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.44%), falls 20% or more from recent highs. During that time, it can lead to knee-jerk investing decisions and even cause the most stoic individuals to question their decisions.

Fortunately, even as unnerving as it is to see a portfolio start to decline in value, history offers a few important stats to keep in mind. And it offers a roadmap to follow before a bear market even occurs.

As difficult as it is to watch a stock price do nothing other than seemingly fall, bear markets are natural. According to Hartford Funds, since 1928, there have been 27 bear markets compared with 28 bull markets in the S&P 500.

In bear markets, the S&P 500 lost an average of 35%, and those bear markets lasted roughly 10 months.

The good news is that bull markets are far more meaningful and reward long-term investors. Compared to the roughly 10-month duration of a bear market, bull markets last 2.7 years. Also, while the S&P lost an average of 35% in a bear market, it gained 112% on average during a bull market.

Additionally, some of the best days for the S&P 500 actually occurred during bear markets. From 1996 to 2025, 48% of the best days for that major index occurred during a bear market, 28% during the first two months of a bull market, and the remaining 24% during the rest of a bull market. That means missing out on stocks rebounding during a bear market can be costly.

Looking at a hypothetical example from Hartford Funds, a $10,000 investment in the S&P 500 in 1996 would have been worth $192,167 by 2025. And the only thing an investor would have had to do was leave it alone. In comparison, just missing the 10 best days for the index during that time would have resulted in a 56% lower return of $85,490.

Armed with that data, history shows there's one simple move to make to protect a portfolio.

If bear markets tend to be shorter-lived than bull markets and some of the best investing days are during bear markets, the solution is to fill a portfolio with investments you believe in as a long-term investment. Then, you simply leave them alone.

Picking stocks will look different for each individual. It could be owning Dividend Kings, which have increased their dividend payouts for 50 or more consecutive years, such as Coca-Cola (KO +1.32%) and Kimberly-Clark (KMB +2.00%). It could be owning Amazon (AMZN -0.63%), a leader in cloud infrastructure with a 28% global market share as of the first quarter of 2026. Or it could be owning leaders in the global payments market, such as Visa (V +1.31%) or Mastercard (MA +1.24%).

Again, which companies are high-conviction, long-term holdings will look different for each investor. But what everyone building a successful portfolio will have in common is that they didn't sell when things got rocky and plan to hold those stocks for multiple years, or even a decade or longer.
