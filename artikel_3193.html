Yelp: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Management is betting that a $250 million non-advertising revenue target can carry Yelp through a stalled local ad market before 2028.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 22, 2026 at 12:23AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Yelp (YELP -1.99%) announced a 1% year-over-year decline in net revenue to $371 million as weak consumer demand weighed on core advertising. While local ad budgets remain under pressure, rapid growth in artificial intelligence (AI) tools and data licensing offers a new path to long-term value creation. Three things management said on the call stand out.

Core ad revenue fell across both services and restaurant categories due to cautious business spending. To offset that drag, management is leaning into data licensing with technology partners and subscription software products.

Restaurant operators and home service professionals are adopting automated voice technology to handle calls and convert customer leads. Yelp Host, the company's internal call-answering tool, expanded rapidly after integrating advanced conversational models.

Management reduced diluted shares outstanding by 15% year over year after buying back $200 million of stock in 2026. It paused the repurchase program in the third quarter to pay down its revolving credit facility before resuming in 2027.

Local advertising headwinds show no signs of immediate relief, with persistent inflation and high labor costs weighing on business ad budgets. Yelp relies on structural advantages like high-intent search traffic and deep partnerships with leading technology platforms to preserve engagement.

Management is watching key operating themes, including pricing discipline in paid project acquisition and cost control around stock-based compensation (SBC).

The broader strategy hinges on transforming search interactions into automated lead conversions across services and dining. Long-term returns depend on whether software revenue can outpace macro softness in local advertising.
