The Best Pregnancy-Safe Sunscreens, According to a Dermatologist

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Mineral formulas are a first choice for pregnancy, especially if melasma is a concern.

Pregnancy often requires an overhaul of your skin care routine—mainly due to ingredients not being tested for safety during pregnancy and because hormonal shifts can cause skin changes that require a new set of products. One non-negotiable in any daily skin care line is sunscreen. Although the choice between chemical SPF and a mineral one is usually a personal preference, several considerations should be made during pregnancy, under the guidance of a medical professional. Dr. Brianna Olamiju, a board-certified dermatologist with Spring Street Dermatology in NYC, explains to ELLE which are the safest sunscreen formulas to use during pregnancy and which ingredients to avoid.

During pregnancy, Olamiju recommends mineral-based sunscreens as the first choice. Look for formulations with zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, or a combination of both. “Mineral sunscreens work by forming a protective layer on the skin and are particularly appealing during pregnancy because their active ingredients have minimal absorption through the skin,” she explains. They also tend to be well tolerated by individuals with sensitive skin, which can become more reactive during pregnancy.

Some of Olamiju’s top picks are EltaMD UV Physical Broad-Spectrum SPF 41, La Roche-Posay Anthelios Mineral Tinted Sunscreen SPF 50, CeraVe Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50, and Blue Lizard Sensitive Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50. “For patients experiencing melasma or pregnancy-related hyperpigmentation, I particularly like tinted mineral sunscreens containing iron oxides, as these provide additional protection against visible light, which can contribute to worsening pigmentation,” says Olamiju. Ultimately, she adds, the best sunscreen is one that feels comfortable on the skin and that you’ll want to use consistently.

According to Olamiju, there’s currently limited research specifically evaluating the safety of chemical sunscreen ingredients during pregnancy. However, she notes this does not mean all chemical sunscreens have been proven unsafe. “Some chemical sunscreen ingredients can be absorbed through the skin and detected in the bloodstream, but systemic absorption alone does not establish that an ingredient causes harm to a developing baby,” she explains. At this time, there is insufficient evidence to conclude that using chemical sunscreen as directed causes adverse pregnancy outcomes.

That said, as a precautionary approach, she recommends choosing mineral-based sunscreens containing zinc oxide and titanium dioxide during pregnancy. “It is important to emphasize that patients should not avoid sunscreen altogether out of concern about chemical ingredients, as protecting the skin from ultraviolet radiation remains extremely important during pregnancy.”

The sun protection factor (SPF) is important during pregnancy, just as it is any other time. Olamiju explains that pregnancy can increase the likelihood of developing hyperpigmentation, particularly melasma, commonly referred to as the pregnancy mask. “Hormonal changes can stimulate melanin production, making the skin more susceptible to discoloration, especially with sun exposure.” To get ahead of it, she recommends using a broad-spectrum sunscreen with an SPF of at least 30, although she adds that an SPF 50 is an excellent option for those who spend significant time outdoors or are particularly prone to pigmentation.

As a precaution, Olamiju recommends avoiding sunscreen formulations containing oxybenzone and octinoxate when practical, given research concerns raised about their potential endocrine-disrupting properties and the limited pregnancy-specific safety data. Beyond sunscreen filters, she says to also be mindful of other active ingredients like retinoids, including retinol, tretinoin, and adapalene, which should be avoided during pregnancy, as should hydroquinone. “When in doubt, patients should review their skincare regimen with their dermatologist and obstetrician.”

In addition to a mineral-based formula with zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, or both, and an SPF of 30 or higher, Olamiju also recommends a water-resistant sunscreen, particularly if you exercise outdoors, swim, or spend extended periods in the sun. For anyone experiencing melasma or increased pigmentation during pregnancy, she also says to consider a tinted mineral sunscreen containing iron oxides to provide additional protection against visible light. If you have sensitive or pregnancy-related reactive skin, you may also benefit from fragrance-free, noncomedogenic formulations. “I generally prefer lotions, creams, or sunscreen sticks over sprays to ensure adequate application and minimize the risk of inhaling airborne particles,” Olamiju adds.
