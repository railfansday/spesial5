Oil prices reverse course after Trump said U.S. doesn't need Iran deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Brent crude climbed as much as 1.8% to top $94 a barrel after falling on signs of higher Saudi exports

President Donald Trump said Thursday that Washington has no need to strike a bargain with Tehran, and crude prices recovered their earlier declines.

Trump made the comment in a Truth Social post. "We don't need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed. We've got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we've got their Strait!" he wrote.

In London, Brent crude futures rose by as much as 1.8%, briefly moving above $94 per barrel. The January Brent contract was 1.1% higher at $93.78 as of 8:28 a.m. ET. West Texas Intermediate futures advanced 1.3%, changing hands at $87.62 a barrel.

The post fed concern that the U.S.-Iran war may continue with no resolution on the horizon, according to CNBC.

Iran has taken a firm position as well. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, said the country will not send “a single atom” of its enriched nuclear stockpile abroad, according to Bloomberg.

Prices had fallen before Trump's post on signs that exports from the Persian Gulf were holding up. Saudi Arabia told OPEC that its output climbed 26% in October, reaching one of its highest monthly totals in years, according to Bloomberg. Iraq said its output hit a wartime peak, Bloomberg said.

Trump's remarks follow a week of swings in crude. On Sunday, he said additional U.S. strikes on Iran were improbable while negotiations to wind down the war advanced. Brent fell below $100 a barrel on Monday.

Oil also moved sharply in October. Brent rose 4% to around $104 a barrel on Oct. 8 after Trump said he did not want a deal with Iran to end the war. A tanker attack in the Persian Gulf coincided with that jump.
