Emirates saw its profits plunge as the Iran war drives up fuel costs

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The airline's fuel costs before hedging rose 60%, and it carried 22% fewer passengers than a year earlier

Emirates, the airline unit of Emirates Group, reported a 66% drop in profit over the first six months of its fiscal year as regional conflict disrupted its operations and raised jet fuel prices, the company said Thursday.

Emirates' profit after tax for the six months through Sept. 30 was AED 3.3 billion ($903 million). Profit before tax was AED 3.7 billion ($1.0 billion), compared with a record AED 11.4 billion ($3.1 billion) in the same period last year. Revenue fell 13% to AED 56.9 billion ($15.5 billion).

Emirates Group, which includes the services company dnata, posted profit after tax of AED 3.6 billion ($1.0 billion), down 66%. Group revenue fell 12% to AED 66.2 billion ($18.0 billion).

Emirates' fuel bill ahead of hedging climbed 60%, even though its fuel uplift fell 12% on reduced flying. Gains of AED 7.1 billion from its fuel price risk management strategy cut the net increase in fuel costs to 13%. Fuel makes up 34% of the airline's operating costs.

The airline carried 21.8 million passengers, down 22% from a year earlier. Capacity measured in available seat kilometers fell 17%, and the average passenger seat factor was 72.0%, compared with 79.5% a year earlier. At Sept. 30, Emirates was operating 91% of its pre-disruption network.

Cargo revenue rose 52% although tonnage carried fell 1%. The company said the conflict reduced available air freight capacity and pushed shippers toward air freight because of marine transport disruptions.

"It has been a demanding six months," Chairman and Chief Executive Sheikh Ahmed bin Saeed Al Maktoum said in a statement. He said global jet fuel prices doubled from the same period last year, which raised costs and cut profitability. Al Maktoum said the group enters the second half of its financial year with "measured optimism."

The Iran war began Feb. 28 and led to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Drones targeted Dubai International Airport in March, according to The Wall Street Journal. The war led Emirates to call off thousands of flights, and the airport halted operations temporarily, according to Bloomberg.

The International Air Transport Association forecast in June that Middle East carriers, including Emirates, would post a collective net loss of $4.3 billion in 2026, reversing a $7.2 billion profit a year earlier, after conflict-related airspace shutdowns disrupted their operations. The trade group projected global airline net profit of $23 billion this year, down from $45 billion in 2025.
