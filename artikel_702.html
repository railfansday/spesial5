Grammys Snubs and Surprises: Harry Styles Shut Out, Raye’s Huge Year and Noah Kahan Only Gets One Nomination

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The 2027 Grammy nominations have arrived, with some notable breakthroughs and reigning hot streaks from artists including Raye, Rosalía, Phoebe Bridgers, Miranda Lambert, the Olivias (Dean and Rodrigo), Bruno Mars and many more.

As with every year, there are plenty of surprises and just as many snubs among the nominees. Raye and Rosalía were among the artists who had the most unexpected success stories in the top categories, as were some best new artist nominees including Remi Wolf, Angine de Poitrine and the Womack Sisters. Women dominated across numerous categories, while artists like Ariana Grande and Stella Lefty had surprisingly lower-than-expected showings. Variety ticks down the nominees list to break down what was expected — and what wasn’t. SNUB: Harry Styles shut out entirely. Perhaps the artist with one of the biggest years is also one of the biggest snubs among the 2027 Grammy nominees. Styles had been on a red-hot run amid the release of his fourth studio album “Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally.” He announced the record in January and followed with its lead single “Aperture,” leading into the March release of the project. He then spent the bulk of the year on the road as part of his “Together, Together” world tour that brought him to cities including Amsterdam, London, Mexico City, São Paulo and New York City, where he staged a historic 30-night residency at the famed Madison Square Garden through the end of October. However, the album itself met a polarized reaction, even from hardcore fans.

Among the categories where Styles submitted “KATTDO” were album of the year, best pop vocal album, best engineered album and best album cover. He submitted “Aperture” for record of the year, song of the year, best pop solo performance and best music video; “Coming Up Roses” for best arrangement, instruments, and vocals; and “One Night Only Manchester” for best music film. It’s perplexing that the Recording Academy denied every submission, particularly considering that he has been a Grammy darling for years with nine nominations and three wins, including album of the year for “Harry’s House” in 2023.

SURPRISE: Best new artist category is an unpredictable mixed bag. No one should be surprised by many of these nominees — Ella Langley, Sienna Spiro, Don Toliver, Geese and Slayyyter were largely predicted to secure spots among the class of 2027 — yet it’s the remaining artists that may have some heads scratching for casual music fans, although all certainly have passionate fan bases. Canadian sibling duo Angine de Poitrine has built a very dedicated following that is equally mesmerized by their hypnotic strain of math rock as they are by their black-and-white polka dot costumes. The Womack Sisters, meanwhile, made waves among retro R&B fans with their self-titled debut album that released in August (and yes, they come from a formidable legacy: daughters of singers Cecil and Linda Womack, and nieces of the legendary Bobby). And Remi Wolf, who has been an indie-pop darling since she started putting out music in 2019, is something of a curveball, considering that she released her latest album “Mud” outside of the Grammy submission window and, like many nominees in years past (and some others this year), is far from a new artist.

It’s worth noting the slate of artists who were included in predictions lists from various publications and industry spectators that didn’t make the cut. Malcolm Todd, who had a huge year with his June album “Do That Again,” was left off the ballot, as was his sister Audrey Hobert, whose excellent debut “Who’s the Clown?” was released last year. Sam Fender, Role Model, Stella Lefty, Miles Caton and Bella Kay were also excluded from the category after making noise during the eligibility period.

SURPRISE: Raye leads with the most nominations…which is not entirely shocking, given the consistent goodwill towards Raye and critical praise for her sophomore album “This Music May Contain Hope,” which was released independently in March. The singer-songwriter, who was recently named Variety‘s 2026 Hitmaker of the Year, leads overall nominations with a staggering 11 nods, trailed by Rosalía with nine and Ella Langley, Miranda Lambert, Olivia Rodrigo and Phoebe Bridgers with seven. Raye is nominated in all three eligible categories of the Big Four, including album of the year, plus song and record of the year for “Where Is My Husband!” (She was already nominated for best new artist in 2025.) She’s also up for best pop solo performance for “Husband” and best pop duo/group performance for “Click Clack Symphony” featuring Hans Zimmer; best pop vocal album; best dance pop recording for “Life Boat”; best traditional pop vocal performance for “I Hate the Way I Look Today”; best music video for “Click Clack Symphony”; and best arrangement, instruments and vocals for “Click Clack Symphony.”

The only category where she’s nominated that may leave some Grammy scholars puzzled is her inclusion in songwriter of the year (non-classical), where she’s up against Amy Allen, Edgar Barrera, Theron Thomas and Joybeth Taylor — which is confusing because the honor was intended to recognize songwriters whose work was largely recorded by others. Among her credits are five of her own songs, plus four others where she has a songwriting credit but is not the main artist (Amma’s “God Be My Witness”; Halle and GloRilla’s “Know About Me”; Sonny Fodera and Libianca’s “Let Me Be in Your Arms”; and Say Now’s “Supermarket”). According to Academy rules, songwriters must submit a minimum of four songs in which they’re credited as a songwriter or co-writer, and songs where the songwriter was credited as a Primary or Featured Artist or Producer do not qualify to meet the minimum threshold. Translated to layman’s terms, Raye qualifies in this category because she met the threshold with credits as a songwriter for other artists’ songs.

SNUB: Noah Kahan receives only one nomination. It seemed as though the singer-songwriter may have been steeling himself — and prepping his fans — for Grammy disappointment on Sunday after he tweeted, “Whatever happens tomorrow I love you all and I have done everything I’ve ever needed to do in my career because of YOUR support. You guys are all I care about.” The reality may be even more sobering, considering that Kahan had a gargantuan year and was predicted to secure nominations in the top and genre categories. Instead, he only picks up a single nomination in best contemporary folk album for “The Great Divide: The Last of the Bugs,” the expanded edition of his fourth album that initially dropped in April. This really did seem to be his shot at Grammy glory, given he’s only gotten two nods before (best new artist in 2024 and best country duo/group performance in 2025), but it looks like it’s better luck next year for one Mr. Kahan.

SURPRISE-ISH: Major female dominance in top categories. Really not much of a surprise, considering the large swath of female artists who gave the industry a run for its money over the past year. Six out of eight record of the year nominees are songs by women (the seventh, Tame Impala’s “Dracula,” features Jennie). Bruno Mars is the only man to be nominated for album of the year with “The Romantic,” up against Phoebe Bridgers, Raye, Olivia Rodrigo, Olivia Dean, Rosalía, Ella Langley and Taylor Swift. Seven out of eight of the song of the year nominees are by women (Mars, again, offers a speck of diversity). And girl power isn’t just limited to the biggest categories: all of the nominees in best dance pop recording, best R&B solo performance, best traditional pop vocal performance and best contemporary country album are by women.

SNUB: Ariana Grande picks up fewer than expected. Admittedly, Grande hasn’t historically been a heavily nominated artist. The most nods she received in one year was in 2020, when she picked up five. But given how well her latest album “Petal” performed, as did its lead single “Hate That I Made You Love Me,” Grande was expected to have a stronger showing for the 2027 awards. Next year, she’ll be competing in four categories: song of the year for “Hate That I Made You Love Me,” best pop vocal album for “Petal,” best traditional pop vocal performance for “For Good” with Cynthia Erivo and best music video for “Hate That I Made You Love Me.” (“Petal” is up for a fifth award, in best immersive audio album, though Grande herself is not nominated.) This is certainly a snub for Grande who, despite picking up some substantial nods, was left out of album of the year, record of the year and all the other genre categories she submitted — surprising, considering that artists like Taylor Swift, Olivia Rodrigo and Rosalía got nominated in all three major categories where they were eligible.

SURPRISE: Speaking of, Rosalía’s “Berghain,” hardly a traditional mainstream single, gets nods for record and song of the year. You’d be hard-pressed to find anyone who doesn’t consider Rosalía’s “Lux” to be a pop masterpiece, yet many of those same folks would probably agree that “Berghain” is far from what you’d consider a “mainstream” single. The track, featuring Björk and Yves Tumor, is like a 21st-century cantata, replete with vamping strings and ominous choral chants. Even with a cursory glance at the song and record of the year nominees, “Berghain” sticks out like a sore thumb, showing that genuine musicians are voting and appreciate the artistry of Rosalía’s production and songwriting.

SNUB: Stella Lefty’s “Boston,” one of the biggest singles of the year, gets ignored. Following in Alex Warren’s “Ordinary” footsteps from earlier this year, Stella Lefty’s “Boston” may be one of the biggest songs of 2026, but that is of little concern to voting members. The track has been a commercial juggernaut, reaching No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100, yet has been consistently dogged by accusations of plagiarism, as fans have noted its striking similarities to Kahan’s “Stick Season.” Academy members fell in line with the critical consensus, it seems… And while Warren may have ended up with at least one nomination for best new artist, Lefty comes up empty-handed for next year.
