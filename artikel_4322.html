7 Soulslikes With the Most Nominations at The Game Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Ronald is a video game journalist with more than 10 years of experience in the industry, working on several of the most important Spanish websites.

Since 2009, FromSoftware has established an entire movement thanks to its work with the Soulsborne philosophy, not only giving us some of the best games of recent generations but also sparking the creativity of numerous developers worldwide.

The emergence of Soulslikes as a significant variation of ARPGs has been remarkable, influencing everyone from independent developers to major studios to follow in the footsteps of its challenging yet memorable journeys.

As more and more projects have joined the growing trend, the genre's average quality has increased considerably, which has been reflected in the ceremonies that celebrate each year's best titles.

Therefore, to remember which experiences have reached the pinnacle of recognition within the interactive industry, I present to you this list of the seven Soulslikes with the most nominations at The Game Awards.

Being not only the first great Soulslike game but also the first title to be nominated for a Game Awards category, Nioh is one of the genre's greatest representatives for many reasons.

Although it lost the Best Action Game award in 2017 to Wolfenstein II: The New Colossus, Team Ninja took the first step in establishing what would eventually become the finest and most successful series in this subgenre.

Team Ninja was the first studio to place as much faith in the original formula as in its talent and expertise to create something uniquely its own while remaining instantly recognizable. The result was a devilishly challenging title with a remarkable personality that resonated with the community thanks to its profound experience.

The sequels would improve upon what was already quite enjoyable, although it's clear that the first Nioh is the installment with the most historical significance and contribution.

After 2017, we would have to wait three years before another Soulslike was represented at The Game Awards, which finally happened with Mortal Shell's nomination for Best Debut Game.

Following Nioh's unfortunate trajectory, Cold Symmetry's project fell victim to the Phasmophobia phenomenon, though its achievement's magnitude was still considerable, not only given its independent status but also its significance in a niche genre.

Even without reaching its most refined version, which would come with the sequel, the title already possessed enough authority and ambition to be featured at the industry's most prestigious awards ceremony, a truly remarkable feat.

Currently, Mortal Shell might seem slightly outdated, more so considering the comprehensive improvements offered by its successor, yet this shouldn't detract from the fact that it was one of the best-received Soulslikes upon its release.

For the second time in a row, Team Ninja had to watch the Best Action Game award slip through their fingers, with the category going to Hades instead of Nioh 2.

Despite the comprehensive improvements the sequel added, it wasn't able to secure more submissions than the one it had received years earlier, partly because many preferred the original's darker, more focused, and less cluttered style.

Regardless of the reasons, and even though it was among 2020's standout titles, the studio had to settle for repeating the milestone, though it's more significant than it might seem considering that the IP now accounted for two-thirds of the genre's all-time nominations.

Certainly, two out of three is nominally a small number, but for a type of game that, by definition, will never achieve mainstream success, it's a clear demonstration of the IP's stature.

The genre suffered another defeat in 2021 with Death's Door, a practically 'Soulslite' approach that, nevertheless, remains one of the current decade’s best action-adventure titles.

In the Best Independent Game category, Acid Nerve's creation succumbed to Kena: Bridge of Spirits, but it did so surrounded by absolute marvels of the indie ecosystem, such as Inscryption and Loop Hero, demonstrating that the level of competition was extremely high.

Death's Door, like Hollow Knight in 2017, belongs to that lighter variant of the genre that focuses on concepts and ideas rather than strict mechanics, and the result was a hybrid between The Legend of Zelda and Dark Souls that feels truly majestic.

It may not have won, but Titan Souls' creators made a tremendous leap in quality by moving from the iconic boss rush to this fully-fledged adventure, which continues to be a regular fixture on lists of the best experiences of its kind.

From the indie ecosystem to productions approaching AAA measures, these are ten Soulslike franchises that should come back by 2027.

Remnant: From the Ashes surprised the world with its magnificent combination of Soulslike mechanics, third-person shooter gameplay, and multiplayer, while Remnant 2 refined the package and capitalized on that success.

In 2023, the Best Action Game category once again featured a genre standard-bearer, though it had to face the bittersweet reality of defeat, particularly at the hands of FromSoftware and Armored Core 6: Fires of Rubicon.

However, after a 2022 in which only FromSoftware made an appearance at the event, returning to the biggest stage in video games was important enough to maintain the genre's vitality.

Remnant 2's success is undeniable, but so are the frustrations of a genre that, after more than five years of accumulating nominations, had yet to secure even a single award that would elevate it to a new level.

Lies of P made Soulslike history in 2023 by becoming the first game in the subgenre to receive more than one nomination, an achievement unmatched until then.

Round8 Studio and NEOWIZ emerged from absolute obscurity and yet released what I consider the best Soulslike ever made, not only perfectly emulating FromSoftware's work but also imbuing it with a recognizable and well-developed identity.

It lost Best RPG to Baldur's Gate 3 and Best Art Direction to Alan Wake 2, but considering that both were Game of the Year nominees and consistently rank among the decade's best games, there's little to criticize about Lies of P.

Unfortunately, the game's quality and refinement remain an anomaly that exceeds the genre's actual average, though it continues to be a benchmark for future Soulslikes aspiring to such heights.

There's nothing more fitting for this season than being destroyed by the same boss fifty-seven times in a row.

However, the biggest historical event for Soulslikes at The Game Awards came in 2024 with Black Myth: Wukong, by far the most acclaimed game in the genre from the specialized press.

With the highest number of nominations ever recorded at The Game Awards, the genre's only Game of the Year nomination, and the sole winner of a medal (two, actually), Game Science shattered all previous standards for Soulsborne heirs.

Its nominations for GOTY, Best Game Direction, Best Art Direction, Best Action Game, and Players' Voice were historic, as were its conquests over the latter two categories, cementing its place in the annals of Soulslikes.

One of its victories was in the popular vote's realm, the particular category decided entirely by users and not the specialized media, although it doesn't diminish the merit of a production that paved the way for a whole country.

Since its resounding success, China has opened up beyond the mobile market to continue capitalizing on Black Myth: Wukong's prosperity, positioning it as a giant whose shoulders carry an entire ecosystem thanks to its distinctive excellence.
