Thanksgiving airfare has jumped 17%, but AAA says there’s a way to avoid some of the holiday travel pain

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Millions of Americans will take to the skies for the Thanksgiving holiday, even as travel costs are surging. But while soaring airfare prices seem inescapable, there may be some key ways to protect your bank account this year.

According to a new AAA report, slightly fewer people will travel for the holiday this year. Last year 81.6 million people hit the skies or the highway for Thanksgiving between Tuesday, Nov. 24, and Monday, Nov. 30. This year, the agency projects around 81.5 million Americans will do the same for a decline of 0.1% or 121,000 fewer travelers.  

AAA’s Vice President Stacey Barber says that the drop-off comes after a multi-year surge in the number of Thanksgiving travelers. “After three years of record highs, the number of travelers is slightly lower partly because of higher travel costs this year. But travelers won’t notice much of a difference on the roads and at the airports, since projected volumes remain near record levels,” Barber said.

In terms of air travel, the numbers drop off further. Around 5.96 million travelers are expected to travel domestically over the holiday for a 0.2% decrease over last year. The lower-than-average projection makes sense, given the cost of domestic flights is up around 17% this holiday season for an average of roughly $800 per ticket.

As the cost of plane tickets continues to climb, Americans are more invested than ever in finding deals. According to a new CheapOair survey, nearly half (49%) of travelers said they have delayed bookings in order to shop holiday travel deals. Another 60% say they are likely to consider doing so in the future.

You can’t exactly combat the cost of filling up your tank, but AAA says that driving is still the most cost-savvy way to get to your destination. That remains true even as the cost of gas has jumped from last year at this time. According to AAA’s fuel prices tracker, the national average is $3.78 per gallon. A year ago, it cost $3.07. In light of the cost of gas, around 200,000 fewer people will travel by car over Thanksgiving. While last year 73.1 million travelers hit the road, this year around 72.9 million will. 

While travelers are undoubtedly strained by travel costs, some travel categories are actually booming. AAA projects 2.6 million Americans will take buses, trains, and cruises this Thanksgiving. That’s an increase of 4.1% from last year alone. But even in 2025, train travel was ticking up. Amtrak saw record ridership that year and projected that it would have 6% more riders in 2026.

U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean P. Duffy attributed the boom to improvements to train travel as well as competitive pricing. “Amtrak’s growth is a preview of what’s possible when everyone is working together to help get the American people where they need to go,” Duffy said in a 2025 statement.

He added, “Faster trains, more affordable service, and extended routes are opening up a new era of American rail.”
