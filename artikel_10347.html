Here’s how Google’s Android XR glasses are handling the camera & privacy

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

It’s a shame, because a camera in smart glasses serves such a functional role, both for the user in just capturing pictures and for the AI assistant built into the experience. I recently talked about how the camera was my favorite part of smart glasses in an issue of our 9to5Google Weekender newsletter, but how it just feels a bit wrong now.

Anyway, with that context in mind, let’s look at how Google is handling this touchy subject.

Importantly, the light on each pair of Android XR glasses is also designed to avoid any tampering. Android XR glasses automatically perform a system check to ensure the light is working on a regular basis – you might catch the light blinking in white and blue while charging which is a part of the system check ensuring the hardware is fully functional. If the light is found not to be working, the camera is disabled.

Continuing on with how the camera interacts with Gemini, Google explained in a conversation with 9to5Google that Gemini is tuned not to use the camera in cases where it’s not obvious that the user wants the camera to be active. An interaction such as “what painting is this” would very clearly imply that the user wants the glasses to view the scene in front of them and identify the art piece, where “how much money is on the table,” Google explained, would ask for a follow-up before using the camera seeing as it’s not obvious that the user expects the camera to be used in that sort of phrase.

Another way in which Android XR glasses will avoid the recording stigma is with their limits. Google is limiting Intelligent Eyewear video recording to 90-second sessions. While that does limit what the user can do, Google said that most recordings fell within that window anyway. Meta, by comparison, offers recordings up to 5 minutes.
