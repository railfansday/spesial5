How Sheffield United's 'dinosaur' changed tactics to turn game against Southampton

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A game which began like a demonstration of how short the Blades were of the Championship elite ended with them proving they have what it takes to mix it with them.

For half an hour the Blades were outclassed by a Saints side who looked Premier League-ready. The Blades had only just over 20 per cent of the ball, which for them in a Championship home game is extremely rare.

When Hamza Choudhury gave it away and failed to stop Lewis Dobbin curling the ball in, it was no less than the Saints deserved.

But from that unpromising start, the hosts turned it around. The second half was pretty much all them, so when Daniel Peretz saved brilliantly from Tahith Chong in the 83rd minute, you wondered if it was going to be one of those nights.

But the drama was just beginning. Jordan Ayew, who had shown what mood he was in with a nutmeg in the middle of the pitch, produced another in the 90th minute as he beat two men along the byline and teed up Sydie Peck to equalise. It still could have all been for nothing had Blades goalkeeper Michael Cooper not made a crucial save one-on-one with Samuel Edozie in the third added minute.

“The concentration of Michael was absolutely outstanding and the save was great,” commented Wilder.

“It would have been incredibly cruel if we'd have not got a result.

"There would (still) have been a certain amount of pride - not enjoyment because when you play as well as that, you have to get something.

"But I think I'd have put my head on the pillow and I would have gone, whatever the result, I don't think they've been dealt with like we've dealt with them in the second half."

As for the other bit, Tonda Eckert played the Blades the compliment of moving the player who normally operates in the hole in front of James Ward-Prowse and Flynn Downes to sit between them and the back four.

"They've got really technical players, and we didn't get to grips with their shape," admitted Wilder. "They had control of the game, they had seven touches in our box in the first half.

"So the old man, the dinosaur, had to get to grips with their press, our press, which we did after half an hour.

"We just wanted the two wide players to jump a little bit earlier and we needed to get around their six (holding midfielder) because the six was dominating and dictating the game. So we stuck somebody a little bit higher, got around their six and had a little bit of a change at the back, just slid over and got more pressure on the ball. In the second half we've had 31 touches in the box, 16 shots, 71 per cent possession.

"We made really good footballers resort to just clearing the lines for the last 20, 25 minutes. Sometimes, when you look back on draws, it's bigger than just the draw, with the performance against a side of the quality that we played against."
