Gyokeres not entirely satisfied after firing Sweden to Nations League promotion

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Viktor Gyokeres thinks Sweden still have plenty to learn after he scored twice from the penalty spot to help down Bosnia and Herzegovina 3-2 and earn promotion in the Nations League.

Graham Potter's side went ahead after five minutes in Solna when Anthony Elanga's cross was met by a glancing header from Daniel Svensson, who scored his first international goal.

Gyokeres emphatically doubled the lead from the spot after he was pulled down by Stjepan Radeljic, and netted again from 12 yards before the break when Sead Kolasinac clumsily fouled Svensson.

Sweden were almost hit by a Bosnian comeback after the break, with Swedish-born midfielder Armin Gigovic heading home, and Kolasinac heading in from Kerim Alajbegovic's corner.

Sweden held on to secure top spot in Group B4 and earn promotion to League A, but Gyokeres felt they were almost punished for slacking off in the second half.

Gyokeres, who has scored in a record eight straight Nations League games, said in quotes reported by the Sweden Herald: "The first half was more fun to play. We scored three and almost got a fourth, and that was positive. Then in the second half it was the exact opposite.

"It's really just a mental issue, since we didn't change anything tactically and went out with the same eleven.

"Unconsciously, we didn't get up to the same pace as they do, and we relax a bit and think it will be easy. Then it gets tough, and they made it tough for us in the second half."

— Svensk Fotboll (@svenskfotboll) November 15, 2026

Gyokeres had set up an offside Alexander Isak goal in Sweden's electric first half, and the Arsenal striker also revealed how the Liverpool frontman had wanted to take the second penalty.

He added: "Yes, he was [wanting to take it]. But I was also keen and felt I wanted to take it this time.

"But we usually split the penalties. We have done that in the past and will do so in the future too."

Potter conceded his side had to "survive" when the momentum shifted in the second half.

In a video posted on the Swedish FA's website via Viaplay, Potter said: "We are happy to have won. The first half was very good, the 3-0 lead was well deserved.

"But then we start the second half too calmly and get punished. The momentum and the atmosphere changed there and then football becomes emotional."

"After their two goals we needed to survive, and we did."
