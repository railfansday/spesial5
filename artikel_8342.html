Videos Show Significant Flood Damage at Colorado Trail Southern Terminus

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Unconfirmed footage on social media appears to show extensive damage at the Colorado Trail’s southern terminus after two severe back-to-back flood events hit the area in the past two weeks.

One video shows much of the trail and its surroundings washed out at the Junction Creek trailhead, which doubles as the terminus of the 485-mile Colorado Trail. Once situated in a wide, flat area well back from the creek, the sign where Colorado Trail thru-hikers traditionally mark the start or end of their journeys now appears to stand at the edge of a badly eroded wash. A separate unconfirmed report on Facebook suggests that the Colorado Trail bridge over Junction Creek leading up to Gudy’s Rest has been completely wiped out.

Meanwhile, footage from Colorado Parks and Wildlife Durango shows “a 20 foot waterfall” taking the place of what was once a section of Junction Creek Road, the primary access road for the trailhead and local community, which sits several miles northeast of Durango proper.

In addition to housing the Junction Creek trailhead, the area is also residential. In recent days, posts from local residents have described scenes of chaos in the aftermath of the flood, with many seeking possessions carried off by the floodwaters.

A Trek blogger standing at the CT southern terminus at Junction Creek in late summer 2026. In the aftermath of the floods, footage appears to show that all the ground up to the sign has been washed away. Photo: Caroline Egener

Official information about the extent of damage in the backcountry is currently limited as officials wait for floodwaters to recede. Both San Juan National Forest, which encompasses the southern terminus, and the Colorado Trail Foundation have said they are waiting for conditions to become safe enough to assess the damage. A condition report on the US Forest Service website for the Junction Creek Trail says “huge rock and heavy debris is blocking the trail 1/2 mile from Junction Creek Trailhead (lower).”

Traveling in flood-damaged areas is currently strongly discouraged. Doing so is unsafe, and putting yourself in a situation where you might need rescue also directly pulls on resources that are urgently needed elsewhere in affected communities. “Record flooding across Southwest Colorado has left San Juan National Forest roads, trails, and other infrastructure inaccessible, severely damaged, and, in many cases, impassable. Don’t become a statistic — rescue will not be readily available due to the current demand for search and rescue services,” said San Juan National Forest on Facebook.

“We have received word of extensive damage to the CT in the Durango area but will not be able to assess the extent of the damage and next steps until it is safe to do so,” said the Colorado Trail Foundation (CTF) in a Facebook post.

“Like you, our hearts are broken by the pictures and videos coming from the communities impacted by the flooding along the southern terminus of the Colorado Trail. Right now our focus is on the people, families and communities who are affected by this devastating flooding,” the CTF post continues. “Please join us in supporting the communities of southwestern Colorado as they recover and heal from these devastating storms.”
