Joining the Military After High School – Weigh the Pay Against 8 Years of Service

Enlisting after high school can pay off. Besides your pay, you get housing, meals, and health care, and the GI Bill can later cover in-state tuition at a public college. But a first enlistment commits you to eight years, so compare the offer with college and a civilian job before you sign.

After 36 months on active duty, the Post-9/11 GI Bill pays full in-state tuition and fees at a public college for up to 36 months of school, the Department of Veterans Affairs says. It adds a monthly housing allowance while you’re enrolled more than half time, so enlisting can fund a degree for anyone willing to serve first.

In 2026, basic pay at the lowest enlisted grade, E-1, is $2,407.20 a month after your first four months, the Defense Finance and Accounting Service says. You also get housing and meals on base or tax-free allowances for both, and TRICARE, the military’s health plan, charges you nothing out of pocket for covered care. After 60 days, the military adds 1% of your basic pay to a Thrift Savings Plan account, the federal version of a 401(k), and after two years matches your contributions, up to another 4% of basic pay. It’s a strong offer, if you accept the cost.

The cost is time and control. A first enlistment commits you to eight years, generally four on active duty and four in the Individual Ready Reserve, where you can still be recalled in a national emergency. You can be deployed or injured, and you won’t know in advance where you’ll be stationed. Under federal law, with limited exceptions, you also repay the unearned part of an enlistment bonus if you don’t meet its terms. Enlist only if that trade is worth the money.

Run the offer through the Defense Department’s Regular Military Compensation calculator, which adds basic pay, allowances, and their tax advantage, then compares the total with a similar civilian salary. Next, use the VA’s GI Bill Comparison Tool to see what the benefit would cover at the schools you’re weighing. The military’s tuition assistance can cover off-duty classes while you serve, up to $250 a semester credit hour and $4,500 a year.

Before you sign, get your job, any bonus, and your enlistment length written into your enlistment contract, DD Form 4, or its attached annexes, since the form says no other promise will be honored.
