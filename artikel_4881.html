15 Best Doormats That Are Durable and Design-Forward (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Displaying a welcoming doormats at your home’s threshold is a crucial opportunity to make a strong first impression on visitors. But it’s not just about wooing your guests upon arrival—they should also have some staying power against the elements and an onslaught of muddy boots this winter.

Thankfully, we found plenty of heavy-duty, high-quality doormats that are both handsome and durable. Many of these options feature natural fibers made from coir, which is mildew-resistant, absorbent, and can survive any kind of weather conditions and foot traffic it endures. Eco-friendly jute is another natural material common among doormat designs—favored for its organic look and its ability to trap dirt while still looking clean. Our top picks include doormats we’ve tested in our own homes as well as a few exciting options from brands we trust, so we don’t suggest (figuratively) dragging your feet on these great designs.

Ruggable’s entire door mat catalog is machine washable, which feels like a non-negotiable for an item literally made to trap dirt. Senior commerce editor Nashia Baker found a kindred match in this pick, telling us that “the balance of whimsical lines and shapes makes walking through the door a harmonious experience.” She also noted that it’s “incredibly durable,” and after months of high-traffic it “can handle any wet or muddy shoes that land on it without getting waterlogged.” Non-slip backing keeps it (and you) from sliding around.

Lean into a modern nautical vibe (think: this contemporary Long Island escape) with Serena & Lily’s chunky mat that looks like it was woven from stolen sailboat rope. The natural/coastal blue colorway offers just a hint of color, while monochromatic options that weave in multiple shades of blue and white make a more vibrant statement.

Sometimes all you need to make a statement is a simple pattern. This honeycomb design is subtle, mildew-resistant, shed-free, and fade-safe. A rubber backing means there won’t be any slipping and it’s designed to hold water—the raised border design helps contain up to 1.5 gallons of water per square yard.

Quilt-like lines feed into a larger geometric pattern in this doormat design that still feels simple but very modern. The grid of black rectangular lines reads as a neutral, and the elongated shape covers more area than some of its peers on this list, so we’d recommend it for an entry hallway or wider doorsteps.

This Heymat doormat is a durable option for covered porches and entryways. Not suitable for true outdoor use (on an uncovered patio or anywhere susceptible to the elements), this Heymat doormat is highly absorbent and soft underfoot. Shopping market editor Kate McGregor’s family owns a few mats from this brand, which they use as an oversized entryway mat in the mudroom for muddy boots and dog paws, as well as a bathmat in the guest bathroom. “This multi-colored mat has held up so well against summer mud and dropped birdseed, despite its pastel grid design,” she says. “Even after five years, the colors have stayed vibrant, and the mat never gets soggy.” Plus, whether sprayed down or shaken, she notes that the cleanup is very easy. “I might protest if my mom ever decides to replace it.”

A departure from the traditional rectangular doormat shape, this Toast alternative offers colorful texture in one compact oval shape that’s fully reversible. This one is crafted from woven and braided jute strands that are ideal for trapping loose dirt and debris. For those looking for an alternative to standard brown jute styles, these alternating patterns are pretty mesmerizing.

If you’ve got more floor space to fill in your greeting room, smaller sizes of The Citizenry’s jute rugs could be exactly what you’ve been looking for. At 24” x 36,” this hand braided mat has a refined look thanks to the contrasting border design, and it’ll be a nice surface to station wet or damp shoes so you’re not damaging the floor.

In our doormat quest, we also found this woven gem from Matilda Goad’s MG&Co. with looped fringing that looks like scalloped edges. The deep burgundy tone will help hide visible mud stains (though the jute weave can also be easily hosed down when it’s time for a clean). It’s also available in a longer, thinner silhouette to help cover sprawling patios.

Floral without feeling overly whimsical (no shame if that’s your vibe, though), Anthropologie’s sweet, embossed doormat is a simple option for anyone with an already-decorated entryway. With an abundance of five-star ratings, lots of reviews praise the design's versatile look and durable material makeup.

The raised, slatted design of Crate & Barrel’s unfinished teakwood doormat keeps moisture, grime, and dirt away sans-bristles. It’s also great for indoor use, perhaps as a minimalist bath mat. Be mindful that teak patinas over time due to weather and regular wear and tear, shifting from a golden wood to a grayish color.

The bold tulip motif on this doormat has a cheerful Scandi-folk style that can brighten your front entry even in the dead of winter. When it starts to look a little worse for wear, the mat easily lifts off its non-slip base for machine washing. In a spot that receives near-constant foot traffic, the ability to refresh it from season to season will be a godsend.

Danish brand HAY is known for their lively color combinations, and their doormats are no exception. With interlocking strands of navy and pink rope, bookended by charming red trim, this pick welcomes guests in with a jolt of maximalist energy. The vegetable fiber strands offer a strong and durable natural alternative to plastic: Shake it out and spray it down with water to clean it and keep the colors fresh.

A stylish coir braid gives this under-$30 doormat a higher-end look. The thick weave creates an almost padded texture, designed to expertly track dirt as your shoe treads catch on the material. With its lattice-like appearance, we envision this doormat at the front door of your Cotswolds-inspired farmhouse, or perhaps a coastal home that could use another natural texture to contrast the white and blue color palette.

Leave it to Sarah Sherman Samuel to create a great doormat. The AD100 designer, arguably most known for Mandy Moore’s Altadena home, offers a twist on the traditional rectangle. Uneven stripes with cutouts give your entryway a modern edge. The coir is dependable, and the black accents help to disguise the inevitable mess.

CB2’s doormat selection is predominantly composed of versatile Chilewich designs—like this monochromatic pattern that feels like a fashionable take on television static. Designed with functionality first, the shag mat uses antimicrobial yarn to keep itself tidy. It also has a no-slip backing to keep it fixed near your doorstep, even when your shoes need a serious wipe down.

What should you look for in the best outdoor doormats?

Although design is important, you also want to make sure that your doormat is low profile enough to fit under your door and also has a non-slip backing to keep your guests from busting it before they make it into your home. (Talk about a first impression!) Materials like PVC with rubber backing are key for the latter, while 100% coir doormats give your entryway a more natural look and are more ideal for trapping dirt and debris.

Doormats are always made for outdoor use, but various climates will wear down the materials differently. Frequent rain and snow might soften jute while constant sunshine will fade most textiles. An easy solution, if you aren’t sure what your mat can withstand, is to drag your doormat into the mudroom or somewhere with a bit of shelter whenever there’s big weather on its way.
