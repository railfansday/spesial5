West Ham’s Former Celtic Starlet Gets Chance To Prove Himself

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

West Ham United striker Daniel Cummings is reportedly set to join National League side Aldershot Town on loan, according to transfer journalist Pete O’Rourke. The move has yet to be officially confirmed, but it would give the 20-year-old his first sustained opportunity to prove himself in senior football.

Interestingly, Cummings would be joining a club he scored against earlier this season. The young Scot was on target when West Ham’s Under-21s beat Aldershot 3-0 in the National League Cup back in September, so at least his prospective new employers have already had a decent look at him.

Much was made of West Ham signing Cummings from Celtic in the summer of 2025. The talented forward had been scoring goals for fun in the Bhoys’ academy set-up and had even been handed a Champions League appearance by Brendan Rodgers. In many respects, it seemed as though the youngster had the world at his feet.

Rodgers was understandably miffed that Cummings refused to sign a new deal at Celtic Park. Mark Noble, meanwhile, had spotted a potential bargain, and West Ham moved to secure the young forward for a compensation fee reportedly below £500,000.

Unfortunately, things haven’t quite worked out as hoped.

The breakthrough into West Ham’s first team has never materialised, and although Cummings has scored goals for the development squad, he hasn’t dominated at that level in quite the way many might have expected.

Rodgers was disappointed Cummings didn’t sign a Celtic deal

To be fair to him, his progress was disrupted by a shoulder injury shortly after joining the Hammers. He has subsequently returned to goalscoring form, including a brace against Braintree Town last month.

Nevertheless, he still looks a long way from establishing himself in the West Ham first team.

The move would at least give Cummings the opportunity to play regular senior football, something he has previously spoken about wanting.

Of course, there’s a significant difference between scoring goals in academy football and doing so against experienced professionals in the National League. Whether Aldershot is the ideal level for him remains to be seen, but it’s an opportunity to demonstrate what he can do.

Aldershot isn’t a million miles away from me, so maybe I’ll pop over and have a look at him myself.

I certainly wish him well and genuinely hope this could be the making of him as a footballer. Clearly there’s talent there, otherwise he wouldn’t have attracted so much attention at Celtic.

Hopefully, regular first-team football is exactly what he needs to start fulfilling that early promise.
