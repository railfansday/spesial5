West Ham’s Home Advantage Problem Won’t Disappear With 60,000 Fans

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Mark Noble has called on West Ham United supporters to help transform the London Stadium into an intimidating venue as Scott Parker prepares for his first home match in charge against Preston North End.

Speaking in an interview with the club’s official website, Noble praised the ‘extraordinary’ loyalty of West Ham fans following relegation and insisted that the club’s ambition remains an immediate return to the Premier League.

He also challenged supporters to make life uncomfortable for visiting teams, arguing that a crowd of 60,000 should give West Ham a significant advantage. But I think it’s important to point out that getting the fans onside and creating an intimidating atmosphere are probably two very different things.

There’s no doubt about it. When the London Stadium is loud, it is impressive, as anybody who attended that incredible night against Sevilla will testify.

But I do think it’s a long way from being intimidating.

For that to happen, I firmly believe supporters need to be close to the pitch.

Not just to cause opposition players discomfort, but to put pressure on referees and give West Ham the kind of home advantage that seems prevalent throughout the league.

The distance between the stands and the playing surface remains a fundamental problem, regardless of how many supporters are in attendance.

It’s something I’ve previously argued when discussing proposals to improve the stadium atmosphere.

Isolated tiers in the stadium and poor pitch proximity do little to help the atmosphere at the London Stadium

It was always my concern that relegation to the Championship would see visiting teams regard a trip to the London Stadium as a lovely day out in opulent surroundings. Opposition changing rooms are grand and palatial and their team coach can pull into the venue untroubled by home fans.

And our results in Stratford have certainly done little to disprove that theory. However, that doesn’t mean things can’t improve.

I genuinely believe that getting West Ham playing on the front foot will help eliminate some of the eerie silences we’ve heard so frequently at the London Stadium.

Noble wants supporters to see a team giving everything, and I think he’s absolutely right about that.

If Parker can produce an aggressive, attacking side that gives the crowd something to get excited about, the atmosphere should improve considerably.

But turning the London Stadium into a genuinely intimidating venue is another matter entirely.

And unfortunately, that’s a problem which can’t be solved simply by asking 60,000 people to make more noise.

Looking at all of the top games on TV, the stadia are considerably worse than London Stadium for proximity of fans.

Maybe we just lack the pyrotechnics in the crowd. The mad grabbing and jostling of inferior beings next to us. The joy of trying to intimidate anyone. We also lack a car park to show off our loutish best.

Whatever you think of the stadium, great and intimidating atmospheres can be choreographed to deliver at kick off, but if the team start sluggishly and without energy then that atmosphere will quickly deflate.

We were famously close to the pitch at Upton Park and won nothing for decades.

Blaming the stadium is just a distraction from the fact that we’re not playing well; when Andy Carroll scored that overhead kick against Palace, no one near me said they didn’t appreciate because they weren’t closer to the pitch.

If the ‘quality’ of the stadium is so important, explain Spurs to me…
