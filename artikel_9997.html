And once you DO get an "island" working again, you have to slowly add on other isolated segments. If you add too much at once, the whole thing comes crashing down and you have to start all over again.

Everything is near max capacity when something trips, causing load to fail over to another path, which then overloads it, causing the entire combined larger load to fail over to another segment and quickly trip it, and they go down like dominos. Basically what happens if you fix one gateway and bring it back up, it gets mobbed and immediately "falls over".

Isolation isn't the problem. The grid can be isolated automatically and remotely down to very fine individual components. The problem is the co-ordination. It's not one company starting up or shutting down their equipment. It's an effort involving detailed planning and steps involving many companies both on the supply and the demand side of the equation, each of which can have their own localised issues to deal with (e.g. generator not synchronising properly).
