Aston Villa Plot January Move For River Plate Keeper: Is A Deputy Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Aston Villa sit tenth in the Premier League with 12 points from nine matches, a record of three wins, three draws and three defeats that contrasts sharply with their form in Europe, where Unai Emery's side occupy sixth place in the Champions League league phase with nine points from four games.

Long-time number one Emiliano Martínez left this summer, and Villa responded by overhauling the goalkeeping unit rather than replacing one man.

Zion Suzuki became the new first choice in August, joining from Parma for £30m plus £5m in add-ons on a five-year contract that runs until 2031. The 24-year-old Japanese international has adapted instantly to English football, keeping two clean sheets and conceding nine goals in eight Premier League matches.

His Champions League performances have been just as strong, including five saves against Fenerbahçe and another five in the 3-0 defeat at Barcelona. Real Madrid and PSG have noticed, which makes Suzuki a potential record-breaking asset for Villa.

Marco Bizot, the 35-year-old Dutchman, provides experienced cover, while James Wright, a 21-year-old academy graduate, has joined the senior squad as third-choice depth. On paper, that gives Emery a settled hierarchy with a proven starter, a veteran backup and a youngster learning behind them.

Sport Witness, relaying El Intransigente, reports that Villa are preparing a formal January offer for River Plate goalkeeper Santiago Beltrán. The Argentina international caught European attention after former Villa star Martínez recommended him.

River Plate urgently want a contract extension carrying a release clause worth about £75m, yet talks remain unresolved. Emery's side intends to exploit that delay by opening official negotiations in the winter window, initially as a deputy to Suzuki.

Emery wants a sweeper-keeper who baits the press, passes with elite quality and commands a high line, with Pau Torres and Boubacar Kamara anchoring the build-up. Beltrán brings reflex-based shot-stopping, shown by 19 River Plate clean sheets, and the proactive attitude Emery demands.

His distribution lags with 20% long-passing accuracy (13 from 65) in the Copa Sudamericana and 60% overall completion, pointing to vulnerability under pressing, requiring significant coaching.

No. Suzuki has asserted himself as an immovable starter, and with Real Madrid and PSG watching, unsettling the number-one spot adds risk without reward. Paying anywhere near £75m for a secondary option would be financially reckless, more than double Suzuki's fee.

Villa should keep preliminary talks alive but refuse an inflated bid. They hold the leverage. Suzuki is tied down until 2031, and Bizot offers cover for the rest of the season. The trigger should be Beltrán rejecting River Plate's extension, which would force the Argentine club to sell below the release clause in 2027 and allow Olabe and Emery to secure a long-term understudy without overpaying.
