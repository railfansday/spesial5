Average Four Factor Index Power Rankings: 2026-2027 NBA Season Week 5

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

After another week of play (which has amounted to 3-4 additional games for most teams), the young season is rounding into form, and the aFFI rankings are coming into sharper focus. In particular, the data are beginning to suggest the emergence of clear tiers of contention, though there are also a handful of teams that are moving rapidly up or down the list. Let's dig in to the numbers!

Regarding the Silver and Black, the last week has seen some exceptional performances (e.g., against Golden State) balance against statistically unremarkable wins and one clunky loss to Detroit. Figure 1 (below) shows that this combination has resulted in the Spurs staying put in fourth place, though the margin separating the top five positions is razor thin (more on that later). San Antonio's overall aFFI value continues to be weighed down by an ∆EFG% value (54.72) that – while above average – is well below almost any other team that is considered a legitimate contender. Even so, the Spurs are doing quite well with respect to the other three differentials, with particularly excellent average percentiles in ∆ORB% (65.81) and ∆FTM/FGA (66). In summary, the Spurs excellent start to the season has leaned heavily on their ability to protect the ball, rebound, and get to the free throw line relative to their opponents.

Stepping back to review the league as a whole, one striking finding in Figure 1 is that there are a number of teams that have risen or fallen rapidly in the last week. Here are some exceptional standouts:

Memphis Grizzlies (↑8) – After Memphis started the season 6-0, they went on a three-game losing streak and seemed destined to be a promising young team that was ultimately unready to become a true contender. However, they have been on an absolute tear recently, riding exceptional effective field goal differentials (their average ∆EFG% percentile of 70.87 is by far the best in the league) to the top of the aFFI rankings. The Grizzlies continue to have some serious flaws (e.g., a dreadful average ∆TOV% percentile), but they are undoubtedly a team to watch.

Miami Heat (↑5) – Miami's 0-4 start was cringeworthy, and they have a long way to go to join this season's truly elite teams. Having said that, their current six-game win streak has included some impressive wins, and their aFFI value has risen above league average due to some excellent average ∆TOV% (60.24) and ∆FTM/FGA (61.58) percentiles.

Golden State Warriors (↓9) – The young season has already been quite a roller coaster ride for the Warriors. They started slow, but went on a hot streak driven in part by some exceptional performances by rookie Yaxel Lendeborg. Unfortunately, that magic has not continued with Steph Curry out of the lineup, and Golden State is currently on a five-game skid that includes four blowout losses.

Brooklyn Nets (↓8) – Let me be clear here: The Nets' aFFI value was never good; however, one week ago it would have been fair to say that Brooklyn was playing like a team that should win about 40% of it's games, even though they were actually winning about half as often. The games played since then suggest that this lukewarm initial assessment was likely a statistical quirk.

Looking at Figure 2 (below), we can see that teams are beginning to separate into distinct aFFI groupings, which provides us the opportunity to classify teams into tiers. At present, I see four tiers, though as time goes on teams that number is likely to rise:

Early Contenders (8 teams) – At present, there are eight teams that have an aFFI value of 56.28 or more, with the 9th-ranked Knicks (53.61) trailing the early contenders by a healthy margin of over 2.5 points. The first month of play suggests that any of these teams could emerge as true championship contenders as the season goes on, though if we're being honest this group could probably be subdivided further. In particular, the top five teams (Memphis, Minneapolis, Houston, San Antonio, and Boston) are noticeably ahead of everyone else and very close to each other, with the aFFI gap between the first-ranked Grizzlies and fifth-ranked Celtics (1.44 points) being just a bit larger than the gap between Boston and the sixth-ranked Lakers (1.21 points).

Likely Playoff/Play-in Participants (8 teams) – This is an interesting group for several reasons. First, it includes teams that are clearly heading in different directions. In particular, Dallas and Golden State appear to be at very high risk of falling to the tier below, whereas nearly every other member of this tier has seen their aFFI ranking rise in the last week. Philly remains by far the most interesting team in this set, as their stellar 13-3 record remains far out of step with a much more modest aFFI value (51.9). The 76ers' record will almost certainly move to 14-3 after they play the woeful Bucks tomorrow. However, after that they'll enter a difficult stretch of games that should reveal a lot about their true status.

Downslope to the Abyss (12 teams) – This is most crowded tier, with a correspondingly varied collection of outlooks. However, based on the first month of play, the best that most of these teams can hope for is a spot in the play-in (an opportunity that some of them will undoubtedly get, especially in the East). Within this group, the most interesting is clearly Atlanta, as its current record (9-6) is much more competitive that its 18th-ranked aFFI value (47.63) would suggest. Whether or not the Hawks can continue to beat the odds will be of particular interest to the Spurs, who are owed Atlanta's pick in the 2027 draft.

Kings' Corner (2 teams) – The Sacramento Kings and Milwaukee Bucks have collectively played 30 games, yet possess just two wins between them (one for each team). With their season over before the end of November, Bucks fans are already turning toward implausible draft and trade scenarios that will rescue them from obscurity. In stark contrast, Kings fans forgot long ago that any other state of being was possible.

Data Source: The underlying data used for this analysis was collected from Basketball Reference.
