Game Preview: Knicks at Timberwolves, November 19, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

It wasn't long ago that a Knicks vs. Timberwolves matchup almost felt like a mini-rivalry—one of the few the Knicks had against a team from the Western Conference.

Given the East Coast vs. West Coast of it all, and the LeBron vs. New York effect, the Lakers made for a fun matchup. The Spurs and Knicks have had some tough battles, including the 1999 Finals and the 2024 Christmas Day game, and that pseudo-rivalry was elevated to a new level with the 2026 Finals. But whenever the Knicks and Timberwolves played each other over the last two seasons, it felt like there was more on the line than just the game. It was a battle for narrative, not just bragging rights. Which team won the Karl-Anthony Towns trade? How would former Knicks Julius Randle and Donte DiVincenzo play against their former team? Now, with the Knicks having won a championship and Randle moving from Minnesota to Brooklyn, this matchup no longer feels as personal.

That doesn't mean that it'll be a bad game. Nor does it mean that the Timberwolves won't be a tough opponent. At 11-4, they enter Thursday night with a better record than the Knicks despite being without star guard Anthony Edwards since he underwent knee surgery on October 29. And contrary to expectations, their success hasn't been a result of LaMelo Ball putting up insane offensive numbers. Their scoring has been incredibly balanced, with Ayo Dosunmu averaging 18.6 PPG, Ball averaging 17.7 PPG, and Jaden McDaniels averaging 17.1 PPG. Even without a single player averaging more than 20 PPG, they've managed to have the 12th-highest offensive rating.

Where they've remained elite sans Edwards, though, is on the defensive end. The Timberwolves, thanks in large part to four-time Defensive Player of the Year winner Rudy Gobert, have been incredibly dominant defensively. As of today, they have the best defensive rating in the league at 104, two full points better than the second-place Celtics. They rank third in the league in opponents' field goal percentage, first in opponents' three-point percentage, and force the sixth-most turnovers per game.

The Knicks, who haven't completely gotten past their early-season shooting struggles, may have a tough time scoring against the Timberwolves. Even with the recent play of Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges, Landry Shamet, and Miles McBride supplementing Karl-Anthony Towns' dominant season, the Knicks still find themselves outside the top 10 in three-point percentage and near the bottom of the league in field goal percentage. That doesn't mean the Knicks won't be able to score, though. They have two very clear advantages heading into tonight.

One advantage is that they'll have the two best players on the court. The Knicks can say this in a lot of games, and that hasn't stopped them from getting off to a disappointing 8-6 start. And this matters less in the regular season than it does in the postseason. But this should still bode well for the Knicks. Brunson won Eastern Conference Player of the Week to kick off November and hasn't really looked back. Even against a tough Raptors defense, he came through and put together back-to-back quality games. And you can make the argument that Towns hasn't had a single game this season where he hasn't been great in one way or another. The big man has also had some monstrous games against his former team, averaging 29.0 PPG, 14.3 RPG, and 3.7 APG while shooting 61.7% from the field and 56.3% from three. And in two of those three games, Towns scored 32 or more points and grabbed 13 or more rebounds.

The Knicks' second advantage comes on the offensive boards. The Timberwolves give up the 12th-most offensive rebounds per game. Some of that is a direct result of their defense forcing so many misses. But with the Knicks being a top-five offensive rebounding team, Towns, as well as Andre Drummond and even Jordan Clarkson, could have big nights on the boards, making up for what could be an inefficient shooting night.

This will be a good test for a Knicks team that has earned seven of its eight wins against teams that are .500 or worse. The Timberwolves are 7-2 at home, deploy a dominant defense predicated on forcing players into the rim-protecting monster that is Gobert, and have a balanced offense that often scores just enough points. But expect the Knicks to lean on their two big horses. Brunson has also had some nice games against the Timberwolves and LaMelo Ball-led teams. And Towns has dominated just about everybody this season. We also can't forget that the Knicks themselves have a good defense, which could test a limited Timberwolves offense. The Knicks come away with a low-scoring, ugly win led by Towns to improve to 9-6.
