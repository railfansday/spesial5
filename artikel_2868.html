Lions vs. Buccaneers preview, prediction: On Paper

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

For the first time in several weeks, the Detroit Lions hold the top spot in the NFC North. But in order to hold it, they'll have to take down a sneaky-good Tampa Bay Buccaneers team. They may be 3-6 on the season, but as you'll soon see, they are doing a lot of things very well, and present a tough matchup for any team. There's a reason why they have a positive point differential, despite having two times the losses than wins.

I'll break it down further in my Detroit Lions vs. Tampa Bay Buccaneers preview and prediction: On Paper.

The Lions' passing attack remains pretty steady and reliable, despite the fact that Jared Goff has now thrown an interception in three straight games after not throwing a single one through his first six contests. For the year, the Lions rank:

In short, the Lions have been playing like a near top-five passing offense pretty much all season.

Goff is at his best when his offensive line is pass blocking for him, and the front has done a pretty good job this year. Detroit is allowing just a 30.8% pressure rate (fifth best) and 6.4% sack rate (15th), while ranking fourth in pass block win rate (58%) and second in PFF pass blocking grade (76.8).

Relevant this week is how Goff performs against the blitz, seeing as Tampa blitzes at the second-highest rate in the NFL. This year against the blitz, the Goff ranks:

The Bucs have shut down nearly every single opponent this year in the passing game, outside of—oddly—Deshaun Watson. For the year, they rank:

Obviously, these stats are pretty darn good, but with lower success rates and yards per attempt than EPA and passer rating, there's at least a little indication that some of these stats are being propped up by Tampa's ridiculous 16 interceptions (most in NFL) and 5.2 interception rate (highest). While the Bucs deserve credit for those interceptions, a 5.2% interception rate is unbelievably high. The highest interception rate for an entire season over the past 10 years is 4.7%. There's a good chance this is unsustainable.

Regardless, this is still a top-10 pass defense and shouldn't be overlooked.

As mentioned earlier, the Bucs have the second-highest blitz rate (43.9%), yet their pressure rate ranks just 24th (33.2%) and their sack rate is 25th (5.0%) per NFL Pro. That said, they are improving as the season goes on. Of their 17 sacks on the season, 11 have come in the last four games.

Really, their pass defense is led by a strong secondary. Cornerbacks Jacob Parish, Zyon McCullom, and rookie nickel Keionte Scott are having outstanding seasons.

Player to watch: Rueben Bain Jr. vs. Blake Miller. Who isn't excited about first-round rookie on rookie action? Bain and Miller were taken two spots apart, and both have had ups and downs in their rookie season. Bain is starting to put some things together after a slow start to his career, while Miller has seen more good than bad.

Advantage: Lions +0.5. With all due respect to DVOA, I think the Bucs' numbers are a little artificially inflated by high interception rates. I still think it's going to be a slog for the Lions to put up big numbers, but I won't be surprised if they can patiently dink and dunk their way down the field.

Every week, the Lions' run game gets a little more consistent and explosive. For the year, Detroit ranks:

What's even more promising is that the high stuffed rate that had been plaguing this rushing attack is finally starting to settle down. Right now it sits at 18.6%, which is still 10th-highest, but it's on a downward trajectory. Similarly, their power success rate is much better now. After Week 8, the Lions were converting on just 59% of short yardage situations (24th). Now they're at 68% (12th).

It's been a marriage between good blocking and Jahmyr Gibbs being outstanding. The Lions rank second in adjusted line yards (4.99), seventh in run block win rate (75%), and fourth in PFF run-blocking grade (77.0).

Meanwhile, Gibbs has forced 16 more missed tackles than any other player and is averaging 3.6 yards after contact per rush (11th).

Put it all together and Detroit is both extremely consistent down to down (see: success rate) and explosive (second-most 10+ yard runs in the NFL).

The Lions may have met their match this week. The Bucs haven't allowed over 100 rushing yards since Week 1, and only two opponents all year have managed 4.0 yards per carry or more against this unit.

It's hard to pick holes in the data here. Tampa is legit. They don't give up big plays (only 16 rushes of 10+ yards, third fewest). The defensive line ranks well in run-stop win rate (eight), adjusted line yards (seventh), and second in PFF run-defense grade.

Player to watch: Josiah Trotter. The second-round rookie linebacker has been outstanding for the Bucs' defense, ranking 19th in PFF run defense grade among qualifying linebackers. He leads the team in tackles (76) and PFF run stops (14).

Advantage: Draw. It's elite vs. elite, and both teams have shown they can win those kind of matchups. Detroit ran all over the elite Vikings' run defense, while the Bucs completely shut down both the Falcons and Bears strong rushing attacks. I can't pick a side here.

The Buccaneers' passing attack has been a disaster this year, and it's hard to figure out why. For the year, they rank:

It's been a disaster year for Baker Mayfield. Beyond just the injuries to his thumb and feet, Mayfield has just not looked the same as the game-changer he had been for the previous few years. Accuracy has been a problem, ranking 27th of 31 in completion percentage over expected (-3.3%). His PFF passing grade of 54.8 is 31st out of 37 qualifying QBs.

But his receiving corps hasn't been much help, either. There just doesn't seem to be any chemistry between quarterback and receiver.

Pass protection, however, has been acceptable. Tampa ranks 16th in pressure rate allowed (36.4%), 11th in pass-block win rate, and sixth in PFF pass-blocking grade (73.7). However, they have the second-highest sack rate (10.1%), suggesting both Mayfield and Daniels have struggled to escape pressure when it arrives.

For now, I'm assuming Mayfield plays in this game, but if Daniels has to go, it's good news for Detroit. The rookie quarterback has an even worse CPOE (-5.7%) and worse passer rating (74.9 vs. 63.4).

I want to believe that the Lions' pass defense is getting better, but it's really hard to know. They've faced some very mediocre quarterbacks all season, and the numbers are certainly improving, but I can't yet say it's good. Even the data from the last two weeks comes with significant asterisks because of Malik Willis' injury and Drake Maye exiting one series into the game.

Compiled on top of that are the injuries to Jack Campbell and Aidan Hutchinson, but the returns of Brian Branch and Kerby Joseph are a factor, too, and there is just so much noise with the data here that it's hard to make sense of anything.

Detroit has climbed out of the basement in a hurry, and if you were to just look at Detroit's stats out of the bye week (Weeks 7-10), they're even better:

But, again, how much is this impacted by strength of opponent?

Pass rush will be a big question moving forward without Hutchinson. He was responsible for 52 of the teams' 199 pressures—with Alim McNeill (39), Skyler Gill-Howard (19), DJ Wonnum (19), and Derrick Moore (15) next in line. Will the Lions just roll with those players, or increase their blitz rate, which currently just sits at 30.9% (16th)?

Player to watch: Derrick Moore. The Lions' second-round rookie defensive end has four sacks in his last two games, and is bound to have a ton more opportunities moving forward with Hutchinson out. In the past four weeks, Moore has posted a 68.5 PFF grade, ranking 37th out of 117 edge rushers.

Advantage: Lions +1. I still have a healthy amount of skepticism regarding the Lions' pass defense, but what they have proven without a doubt over the past month is that they can hold bad pass offenses down. That's what the Bucs are.

Tampa has been outstanding running the football this year, particularly as of late. They've rushed for over 100 yards in six straight contests and finished with at least 4.5 yards per carry in five of those six games. For the year, they rank:

The offensive line can be great at times, but their overall stats are more average than outstanding. They're 14th in adjusted line yards, 16th in run-block win rate, and 15th in PFF run-blocking grade.

The stars of the show, without a doubt, are the running backs. Both Kenny Gainwell and Bucky Irving are outstanding at creating yardage on their own.

Despite the very low DVOA ranking, the Lions' run defense has passed both the eye and statistical test over the past five games. For the year, Detroit ranks:

So what is dragging them down so much in DVOA? A few things. They haven't faced particularly good rushing attacks all season. By rushing yards per game, the Lions have faced just a single team (Bills) ranked 15th or higher this year. And while they own a great success rate, they've been terrible in short-yardage situations, allowing conversions on 86% of power runs (32nd). Those high-leverage plays are almost solely responsible for Detroit's low DVOA ranking. Because Detroit looks great in adjusted line yards (third), stuffed rate (sixth), and run-defense PFF grade (fourth).

Personally, I think they're a lot better than DVOA suggests, but will admit that the Bucs will present the second-biggest challenge of the season.

Player to watch: Irving. He's extremely capable of carrying defenders, but it's worth noting that the Lions are outstanding at tackling. They have PFF's best tackling grade in the league (81.6) and the fewest missed tackles, per PFR.

Advantage: Bucs +0.5. I think this could go in either direction, but because the Lions haven't been tested like this in months—and they'll be without Campbell and Hutchinson—I'll give the slight edge to the Buccaneers.

I'm tempted to throw out last week's prediction entirely because so much of my preview was about the huge advantage the Patriots' passing offense had. When Drake Maye was lost on the opening possession, that edge was completely erased. Now, the Lions still did a good job in coverage, but it's hard to believe that game would've looked anything like that if Maye had stayed in the game.

Still, it counts. I got the winner right, but missed on the Vegas line. On Paper is 7-2 overall and 4-5 against the spread.
