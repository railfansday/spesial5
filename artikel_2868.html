Nets get run over by Flagg, Irving, and Mavericks, losing 130-93

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

This time, the Net took a far more direct route to a loss.

Continuing their first legitimate road trip of the season, Brooklyn sure looked like weary travelers tonight in Dallas. They also played this one as the second half of a back-to-back, and that likely didn't help either.

In that prior game, the Nets let a contest they had complete command over slip away in its final moments. Rookie Mikel Brown Jr. kept his team in the driver's seat for as long as he could, dropping 13 points in the fourth quarter.

Held out for back injury management, the Nets were without him tonight, and any sense of control over whatsoever. It was in a lot of ways the worst loss of the season and that's saying something. Br00klyn is now 2-8.

Danny Wolf got the start once again for Egor Dëmin, who's also still out with a bone bruise in his right leg. But even with a seven-footer technically in their backcourt, Dallas looked like the bigger team early on. In the game's first few minutes, the Nets missed an abundance of attempts around the rim and even saw three of their shots get rejected. The Mavs nearly doubled Brooklyn up on the glass as well, out-rebounding them in the first 13-7. However, they did triple them in paint scoring, adding 18 points inside to Brooklyn's six.

Those statistical discrepancies would only swell as the game went on. By the end of it, the Nets were out-rebounded 46-30 and lost in the paint 64-40. The score was ugly too, but I'll save that score for later.

Keon Ellis also returned to the rotation in the first after not logging any minutes yesterday vs Atlanta. Still expected to be a spark plug kind of player for the Nets this year, Ellis' reappearance didn't give the Nets much juice, but nobody else outside of Michael Porter Jr. could either.

Brooklyn began the game 4-of-14 from the field. Kyrie Irving alone had the same amount of field goals as them through the 3:21 mark of the first, adding nine points on 4-of-7 shooting in the opening period. But after Porter Jr. got free in transition for a few triples, everything else started to fall for him. He scored 11 points in the period's final 2:51 and 15 in the quarter, just beating out Cooper Flagg, who had 14. That kept us out of blowout territory, with the Nets down 33-26 entering the second.

No, that's what the second quarter was for. The Mavericks started it with an extended 21-6 run in the period's first five minutes, quickly hurling the Nets into a 22-point hole. After a turnaround jumper from Kyrie with 4:06 to go in the quarter, it got as deep as 26 points.

"We were not connected, and we made a lot of mistakes," Fernández said. "And then a team like this, when they're in a rhythm with the players that they have, it's when you can get hurt. And I think they made some tough ones, and we also allowed some."

Were than any positives? Nolan Traoré continued to look like a confident, true point guard, navigating around the Dallas defense, directing traffic, and picking the right shooting spots. Turnovers, which were a major problem in the fumbled game vs the Hawks, also weren't as abundant. The Nets even won that battle in the first half, forcing Dallas into 12 and only committing eight themselves.

Still, Brooklyn posted .385/.287 splits going into the break, while the Mavericks shot .619/.400, and no silver linings or improvements around the margins could cover that up. The Mavs led at half 66-46. By that point, Flagg had 20 points, seven assists, and four rebounds on just 10 shots. Brooklyn's only man in double figures was Porter Jr., who had 16 points with seven boards on 3-of-7 downtown shooting.

If you expected the Nets to take that first-half embarrassment personally and respond accordingly, you were mistaken. By the 4:20 mark of the third, Dallas had gotten up by 30.

Things got borderline humiliating when Noah Clowney fouled out of the game with 2:23 to go in the third after shoving Morez Johnson Jr. in the chest away from the ball. They got into it earlier in the quarter too, where officials tagged Johnson Jr. with a technical foul after he knocked down Clowney on a box out. This time, however, Clowney took the guilty charge. He picked up the six fouls to disqualify himself in just eight minutes or burn.

"That's something that he's got to figure out," Fernández said. "He put us on the bonus right away and in one minute, and that's tough. And I think that it comes from a good place because I know he's extremely competitive guy. But then doing the war on your own, it's just tough at times.

"You got to choose the right way to do it, and channel all that energy and sometimes frustration the right way. I think the one person that you know is helping him and will help him is Juwan [Howard] because he's been there. I know that the intentions are great. It's just, keep doing what's best for the group. A nd we need Noah Clowney. L ike that first half against Charlotte, we don't win that game without him, so we need the best version of himself because we know how good he is."

And, at the same time, the Mavericks kept inflating the lead, just having fun out there. With two seconds left to go in the third period, Naji Marshal connected with Kyrie with a beautiful cross-court dime to set him up and-one…

Dallas also opened the fourth with a Cooper Flagg three after Wolf went under a screen for him, which extended their lead to a game-high 32 points. Flagg might've had a career night had the Nets been more competitive and gave Dusty May any reason to keep him in, but the star sophomore didn't play past the 8:28 mark of the fourth. He still finished with 25 points, five rebounds, and a career-high 12 assists while shooting 8-of-13 from the field and 3-of-5 from deep. Irving finished with 21 points, four assists, and three rebounds while shooting 9-15 from the field and 2-7 from three.

Around that point, Drake Powell and Josh Minott came in, giving us something fresh to watch, even if necessarily not fun.

Minott did hit two "Chris-Paul-hits-a-huge-three-to cut-the-lead-to-down…" shots on back-to-back possessions on near the quarter's midpoint. Leveraging the defense's newfound awareness of his shooting, he attacked a closeout and got up for a jam a few minutes later as well. He finished the game with 10 points, two steals, and two rebounds while shooting 4-of-10 from the field and 2-of-8 from deep.

Powell got through the defense for a rushed, but made floater that came back down and in after bouncing high off the back iron, but produced little outside of that.

With Brooklyn still down 25+, Dallas gave Jett Howard, son of Nets assistant coach Juwan Howard, some run as well. In front of his pops, he added five points and grabbed a rebound in three minutes of play.

That was cool. Everything else that happened on the court tonight was not. The Nets ended up losing by a whopping 37 points, where nobody on the team put up more than 20 points. Porter Jr. got up to 19 with nine rebounds while shooting 9-of-19 from the field and 4-of-11 from three. Randle contributed 13 points, four assists and four rebounds while shooting 5-of-10 from the field. He also had three turnovers and was a -33. Traoré got to 14 points and two dimes, shooting 6-15 from the field and 2-of-6 from deep.

Final: Dallas Mavericks 130, Brooklyn Nets 93

Brooklyn's road trip continues with a Wednesday evening game in New Orleans vs the Pelicans. The Pels are 4-5 at the time of writing. The Nets split their series against them last year. This one tips off at 8:00 p.m. EST.
