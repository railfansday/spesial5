Joe Biden Has a Lot of Unknown Unknowns

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Read Party Animals: A letter from the back rooms, barstools, and bacchanals of Washington, DC—with exclusive reporting, interviews, and analysis.

Good afternoon from Washington, where Party Animals has not forgotten the 5 a.m. wake-up required last Tuesday to attend Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth’s first Pentagon press conference in six months. This was a peculiar one, arranged so Hegseth could brief the public on his own personal battle for career survival.

The briefing kicked off at 8 a.m., but the press had to be at the Pentagon by 6 a.m. for an extensive security screening. I’d like to think that this cruel early start was a consequence of Hegseth’s self-proclaimed sobriety. What kind of drunk drags his sorry carcass before a crowd of jackals before noon? Not any I know.

But sources I spoke with for a new piece on Hegseth’s war inside the Pentagon told me there was a more Machiavellian motivation for the presser: Donald Trump loves to watch Fox & Friends at 8 a.m. And sure enough, soon after the conclusion of Hegseth’s performance, its intended audience of one offered a review on Truth Social.

Read on for my investigation into Hegseth’s turbulent tenure at the top of the Department of Defense, and what it means for his survival as Democrats take control of Congress in January.

The reviews are in for Joe Biden’s new book, Promise Me, America. The Atlantic calls the book “unrepentant.” The New York Times calls it a 400-page “cover letter.” The former president offers the fantastical opinion that he should have stayed in the race in 2024 and faced Trump, quite a position to maintain given he couldn’t voice his own audiobook and limited his press appearances for the release to a soft-focus People magazine story (his publisher did not respond to Party Animals’ multiple requests for an interview). According to the reviews, the former president dedicates scant attention to one of the biggest debacles of his presidency, the war in Gaza, which he abetted thanks to near-unwavering support for Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu as 2,000-pound bombs rained down on the densely packed enclave, killing more than 70,000 people. As president, Biden publicly questioned the death toll numbers. “Too many innocent Palestinians were killed,” he now writes, as if possessed by the passive-voice ghost of Donald Rumsfeld. Mistakes were made, indeed. In contrast with his remarkably clinical view of dead Gazans, he writes movingly about his friendship with Netanyahu, recalling his farewell to the prime minister days before leaving the White House: “I still love you, pal.”

Just five months ago, Elon Musk was promoting a movie in which a gunman goes on a mass murder rampage targeting supporters of immigration. That this was barely noticed is a sign of just how extreme Musk’s rhetoric has become over the years, as he devolved Xeet by Xeet into the online equivalent of a bum who yells at strangers on the street about race science. Then the midterms happened. After blowing nearly $500 million in an effort to get Republicans elected and falling short in key races, particularly in Texas, the octocentibillionaire seems to be seeking some kind of public rehabilitation. In a virtual appearance at The New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday, Musk told Andrew Ross Sorkin that he is “not MAGA”—despite the many photos of Elon wearing a certain conspicuous hat—and that he is actually “a moderate.” Don’t take the hat’s word for it, though, here’s Musk on the record in October 2024: “I’m not just MAGA, I’m Dark MAGA.” Ah, well, nevertheless. He expressed hope for incoming Texas governor Gina Hinojosa, a Democrat, and said he felt “foolish” for blowing so many millions to achieve very little. What’s the over/under on how long Moderate Elon will last?

Miller and Marco: The Wall Street Journal has a great report on the close relationship between Stephen Miller and Marco Rubio, who have forged an unlikely alliance despite their past clashes over policy like immigration. It’s a revealing look at how Rubio’s Republican Party lost to Miller’s Republican Party, and how Rubio then jettisoned his entire worldview to join the winning team.

Trump of Wall Street: The Washington Post reports that Trump’s financial disclosure revealed he held between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia stock as of the end of August. That’s considerably higher than the $615,000 to $1.3 million he held at the end of 2024.

French, Toast: No one should know who “Bo” French is. No one really did until the Republican candidate for Texas Railroad Commission went viral for complaining about a group of college kids celebrating a touchdown at a University of Texas football game. (Some of the kids were not white; French objected to this). He became whatever the opposite of a sensation is and ended up losing his election to a Democrat. Now Politico reports French has taken another blow: River Crest Country Club, the Fort Worth members institution he frequented, has kicked him out.

Garcia’s Hit List: Robert Garcia, the House Democrat who is expected to lead the Oversight Committee in the new Congress, has begun mapping out his targets. Per The Atlantic: “Garcia this morning sent a dozen letters to high-ranking Trump-administration officials and members of the president’s family, providing the clearest signal yet of which topics—and which people—will likely face the greatest scrutiny once Democrats take control of the House.”

Oversight O’clock: New York Magazine’s Ben Terris went deep on the new era of Democratic oversight, everything from the war with Iran to Trump’s big beautiful ballroom.

No Longer Satire: Read Joe Bernstein, writing for The New York Times Magazine, on how America is becoming more and more like Grand Theft Auto.

Bye-Bye Boebert: Annie Karni writes for The New York Times about the rise and fall of the MAGA Mean Girl Caucus, as Marjorie Taylor Greene, Lauren Boebert, and Nancy Mace all exit Congress.
