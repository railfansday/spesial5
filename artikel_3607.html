Christine Quinn Talks Divorce, New Love and Her ‘Selling Sunset’ Return

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

When we sit down to chat with Christine Quinn about her highly anticipated return to Selling Sunset, she’s everything we want her to be and more: whip-smart, gorgeous, funny and ready to spill the tea. She’s also surprisingly — and endearingly — anxious about doing her first big interview in some time. “I’m so sorry,” she tells Us a few questions in. “I’m a little nervous!”

Much has happened during the 38-year-old’s hiatus from the hit Netflix reality series, which follows real estate agents at the Oppenheim Group as they wheel and deal with L.A.’s rich and famous. After leaving the show in 2022 following a five-season run, Quinn split from her husband of four years, entrepreneur Christian Dumontet, in early 2024. Dumontet was arrested in connection with an alleged domestic violence incident involving their son, Christian, and was later arrested again for violating an emergency protective order. That April, he filed for divorce and requested legal custody of Christian. Amid the ordeal, Quinn moved back to her home state of Texas with their son, now 5, to be closer to her parents and sister. Looking back on that period, she tells Us, “I’m so glad I’m safe, and I’m so glad that my son is safe.”

Today, she’s ready for the spotlight again, and superfans couldn’t be more excited. The author of 2022’s How to Be a Boss Bitch is beloved for her no-nonsense attitude, quick wit and over-the-top style, but she insists she’s mellowed out — just a bit. “A lot has changed since viewers last saw me, and I think they’re going to be pleasantly surprised,” Quinn says. “I’ve grown so much. I’m still the same Christine, just a little softer and more vulnerable.”

Ahead of the season 10 premiere (all episodes available December 4 on Netflix), Quinn opens up to Us about where she stands with her Selling Sunset castmates, finding love again and why she insists she’s a lot more down to earth than people realize.

It’s been a while since we’ve seen you on our TV screens. Welcome back! I really never wanted to leave. But mentally, I had to for myself. I had my son, and my marriage was falling apart. It was a lot. Even though it’s [only] been four years, it feels like so long ago. And then when I got back into it, it was like no time had gone by. I’ve always had a special place in my heart for the Oppenheim Group and for real estate. So the timing was right. It felt good. I was thrilled.

What made you decide to return? Obviously a lot happened in my life; I got divorced and had some lessons I needed to learn. [Back when I was filming], I was married to someone who was not supportive, and there was a lot of tension between me and my work and the office. [After] my circumstances changed, I was like, “OK, what do I want? Who am I?” So when I got approached to do the show, I was like, “Absolutely.” I’ve always loved Selling Sunset. It’s my first love, my baby.

Did you have any reservations? Even though it was an obvious yes, it was not easy because I live in Texas, so dropping everything and coming out here for three months was a tough decision. But I knew it was the right one for me and my son. I missed him, but he was here every weekend with me. We wouldn’t go more than four days without being together. But I definitely had reservations. I worked so hard to have peace in my life.

What was it like to film again? I was nervous. I hadn’t met these girls; I didn’t have preexisting relationships with anyone except Heather [Rae El Moussa] and Amanza [Smith]… And there are a lot of big personalities. I was also nervous about getting back in the groove. I was like, “Am I going to be able to balance all of this? Can I handle it?” I don’t know how, but I did!

You’ve talked about being portrayed as the villain. Any concerns about that happening again? It’s hard because people are like, “She’s a villain.” I’m like, “Guys, you know we all have our levels, right?” We’re all villains in our own way. But [show producers] Adam DiVello and Jason [Oppenheim] made me feel comfortable. They were like, “We’re on season 10. It’s not gonna be dark or petty. We want it to be light and fun. Let’s bring that energy back.” That’s what got me because I love to make people laugh. Sure, I love to be a bit of a bitch sometimes, but there’s a balance.

Was anything off-limits for you? I wasn’t sure how much I was going to share about my marriage and my current relationship. But I realized it was best for me to talk about what I’ve been through and what life can be like on the other side. I’m comfortable and confident in who I am now. I really had a problem with vulnerability and talking about relationships and expressing myself. [Now] I just felt like the time was right to be myself and share my story. I think the audience is going to love it.

When you left the show in 2022, you’d fallen out with most castmates. Do you feel responsible for that in any way? It’s always difficult when you’re in an office with all girls. There’s gonna be drama. From day one, I’ve been really honest about my feelings and observations. Not everyone likes it, but I don’t tell a lie. I needed to be myself, and if in the process there was some collateral damage, I hate that that happened.

Looking back, is there anyone you feel you should apologize to? No. I don’t think there’s anything that I would’ve changed other than perhaps my delivery. Sometimes we get heated and we say things that we [don’t mean]. I wish things could’ve come across a little softer.

Does anyone owe you an apology? Everyone! I’m joking, but it is exhausting having the Cassandra complex. It’s just as damaging being too early to a situation. I’ve learned to be like, “Maybe I’ll shut my mouth and let this play out.”

Have you felt vindicated by a specific cast member’s behavior? I was definitely right about Chrishell [Stause]. People’s eyes were opened. I’m like a truffle pig. I can smell a biatch from a mile away.

In June, you said “the trash needed to be taken out” before you returned. Can you elaborate? Some of these girls are just bad leftovers, and if you leave fish out too long, it reeks up your house. It was time. We needed a mix-up. The energy was getting dark and vile, and it wasn’t fun for anyone. That’s what I’ve heard.

Would you have returned if Chrishell were still on the show? [Stause had accused Quinn of planting stories about her allegedly cheating on her ex-husband; Quinn claimed Stause got special treatment on the show and neglected to congratulate her on her pregnancy. Both women deny the other’s accusations.] I never had a problem with Chrishell. I don’t hold grudges. It’s not who I am. The animosity wasn’t from me. I was willing to be in rooms with her. Even though we had disagreements, I could still show up to work. So I wouldn’t have a problem filming with her. Would it be comfortable? Probably not for her. But she’s doing her own thing [now]. She was on for nine seasons. I don’t think she wants to be on the show anymore.

She’s going through her own split from her partner, G Flip. Perhaps you could bond over that common ground. I can bond and relate to anyone who’s going through a breakup. I never met G. I wish [Chrishell] all the best.

You also weren’t on great terms with Heather. Did you know she was coming back when you signed on? I don’t know the timeline, but I knew she was coming back, and I was excited. We need some familiar faces, and regardless of our situation and our relationship, it was a great decision.

Where do you two stand now? Both on our Louboutins, across the street from each other. I’m kidding. I like Heather. We used to be roommates. We have so much history. Of course we argue like sisters and we have our kerfuffles. But at the end of the day, if we didn’t get upset [it would mean] there’s not real love and feelings there.

Why do you think you guys disagree as much as you do? I’ve always been a lone wolf and done my own thing in terms of having opinions and hard conversations. That’s always kind of set me aside, and I do think there are people who feel more comfortable blending in and having a pack. Sometimes people choose comfort over their own identity.

How has she changed since you were last on the show together? She’s exactly the same — in the best way. Heather’s consistent. Maybe she’s a little hotter? She looks hot! But she’s been married a few years now and has a son as well, and we relate to having toddlers.

How are things with Mary Bonnet? The last time I saw Mary was probably right after my divorce. She was really sweet. I’m not sure what the future holds [about Mary coming back to Selling Sunset], but we all have our stuff we’re working on, and who knows? I haven’t talked to her in a while.

And Emma Hernan? [She and Quinn had tension in season 5 after Hernan alleged Quinn offered Hernan’s client $5,000 to work with Quinn instead; Quinn denied the claim.] I didn’t spend much time with Emma, so I never got the chance to know her. The cultural appropriation empanadas is really not how I roll. [Hernan faced backlash over her vegan empanadas line in 2021.] That’s her thing.

Did you guys work things out? We just moved on. When you’re new and you’re on a TV show, you’re like, “Hmm, how can I get creative?” We’ve all been there. The bribery thing [was the biggest lie about me]. I was just like, “No, I didn’t bribe a client.” They prefer me because I’m a more seasoned agent.

How difficult was it to open up about your marriage while filming season 10? It’s no surprise [that was] the hardest topic, and also how long it took me to get back on my feet. I was kicked to the floor with no life raft. I was hesitant [to open up about it], but I’m using it as a way to support women and to be like, “Listen, like, there’s another side. You can get out of this.” That was the most important lesson for me.

Where does the divorce stand now? It’s almost finalized. It took a really long time because he’s out of the country, and he was evading [his] responsibilities.

Do you remember when you knew the marriage wasn’t working? I was in a really dark place after I had my son. I was dealing with work and tension [with] my ex. I did everything I could to try to make it work. I don’t know if things changed [or] the signs were there all along and I didn’t pick up on it. Maybe. It was something that had been coming for a while.

What weren’t viewers seeing about your marriage at the time? Any marriage is hard. I’ve always had a hard time expressing myself, and I never wanted to look like I had a failed marriage. Was I putting on [an act]? Absolutely, sure. I didn’t want to be humiliated. That’s what women do. We just get through things. It’s not good for us. [Quinn tears up.] Sorry, I don’t talk about this stuff, really.

You don’t have to apologize. Do you think it was harder for you to reach out for help because you have such a strong persona? The dichotomy is fascinating. I built an entire brand on how to be a boss bitch — I literally wrote the book on it. I never wanted to admit failure. But the way that I look at it [now], I’m like, “Nothing can break me down.” This can and does happen to so many other women who aren’t strong enough to stand up for themselves.

You previously told Us you received legal papers from Christian while filming House of Villains. That must have been difficult. He served me intentionally when I couldn’t respond. It’s just nasty and unfair. But that’s what divorces are; you’re never divorcing the person you married. The timing was not by accident.

His arrest for domestic violence was in the news. How scary was that experience for you? It was a really, really dark time. I look back on it now… and I think it’s really important for women to know you don’t have to stick it out just to stick it out.

Did you hear from your former costars? Most people reached out, but not everyone. I wasn’t disappointed because I know these girls and nothing shocks me with them. It gets brought up on the show, so you’ll have to [wait and] see [who I’m talking about].

You were going to work on a crypto real estate venture with your ex. What’s happening with that? No clue. I have no update. [Going into business with a romantic partner] is not something I’d do again. It’s risky.

What advice do you have for other women dealing with similar relationship issues? Lean on your close friends and family. That’s why moving back to Texas was so important for me. You have a support group around you, and they’ll do anything for you. You just have to ask. It took a long time for me to admit that I was seeing some red flags and I needed help. There is a light on the other side, and there’s an amazing man waiting for you — I know because I have one.

Tell Us about this lucky guy! [Quinn’s been linked to business exec Thomas McGarrity since early 2025.] He’s great. He’s amazing. He’s everything my first husband wasn’t. [And he has] hair and abs.

Is he on the show? I didn’t know if I wanted to have my relationship on a show again. I’ve been burned by that formula. But I was comfortable, and everything felt right, and you can have a little sneak peek on the show.

What feels different about this relationship? The most important thing is having someone who’s supportive of you and your career. I didn’t have that, and now I do and it feels so good and like anything’s possible. Like, “Oh, wow, I can do this, and I don’t have to tiptoe around it. I don’t have to be scared.” That’s the best part.

Would you get married again? I’m like Elizabeth Taylor. I’ve always wanted to be married, like, 15 times. I’m not out of [my first one] fully yet, but I absolutely could [see myself getting married again]. If you asked me a year ago, I would have said, “No, definitely not.” But yes. I love hope. I’m a Libra. I love love.

What about more kids? Again, never say never. I’ve thought about it. But it depends on the day.

How’s Texas life? I’m a lot more down to earth than people realize. I’m a homebody. I take my son to school every day. I don’t have a nanny. It’s just me. It’s where I’m from and my sister and parents are there, so it feels great. I go back and forth between L.A. and Texas for work. I get the best of both worlds.

What’s your son like? I think at age 5 they really come online. [Christian] is booted up now; he’s conscious and remembers things and gets excited. Halloween was such a fun time, running around and getting candy… He started [Quinn tears up] — I’m sorry, he started kindergarten recently but before that I was able to fly him out here. He was playing Legos in the Oppenheim Group. I was with him constantly, and if I wasn’t, it was for two, three days [apart].

Will viewers see your son this season? I wasn’t able to have him on the show, and that was difficult because he’s the love of my life and I wanted people to see my relationship with him. Maybe soon. We’ll see.

What’s it like being a boy mom? He loves trains and cars and trucks and building things. He’s an engineer. He’s brilliant and so fun. The clothes are easier with a boy! If I had a girl, the outfits would need to be perfect, but I just throw shorts and a shirt on him, and he’s ready to go. He’s very low maintenance. But he’s still my version of casual, right? [Laughs.]

And what are you wearing to school drop-off? Oh, I look deranged. Definitely sweatpants in the morning. I [also] love jeans. I love a tank top. Scrunchie in my hair. That’s my go-to.

How is coparenting with your ex? There’s no coparenting. I’m the only parent. [I have] full custody. I’m a single mom, and it’s literally just me. There’s no financial help or relationship. In a weird way, I’m grateful.

Why? I’ve seen people coparent, and it’s difficult. It’s really hard with the back-and-forth.

What’s been the hardest part of parenting on your own? The questions around “What is a dad?” and “What is a father?” He hears these words, and he hears me call my dad “Dad.” He was asking questions and I didn’t know how to answer them. You’ll see me navigate it in the show.

How did you approach your wardrobe for your return? It was hard because I have tons of clothes and purses and shoes, but I live in Texas, so when it came time to film, I was like, “Oh, I don’t have earrings and shoes and purses and things to pull together.” My stylist and I had to work overtime. It’s an integral part of who I am on the show. Sometimes we’ll all be like, “We’re gonna go to a coffee shop,” and then it’s like, “Just kidding, we’re gonna have a night out.” I’m like, “I don’t have any clothes for that!”

Looking back at your previous seasons, are there any looks you regret? Honestly, no, because even the flops were still kind of fire. [But] me and Jason were at an open house, and I was wearing, like, a white cab driver hat. I was like, “Oh yeah, this is the best idea ever.” It looked like I had a taxi service. I see clips on TikTok now and I’m like, “Oh God, that was really a choice.”

Have you been happy diving back into the L.A. real estate scene? Being back here and seeing these beautiful views and amazing houses, I’m like, “Oh, my God, this is what I fell in love with.” I have clients in Texas, but L.A. is a different market.

What’s the craziest thing you’ve seen during a showing? I had a beautiful listing in Orange County, an $18 million all-white mansion. The woman came, and I’m showing her the garage and all of a sudden, there’s a person in the car. She’s like, “Oh, my God, there’s someone in the car!” And I’m like, “Did my client murder someone?” She was so freaked out she left. When I went back, I was like, “Oh, wait, it’s a mannequin so he can drive through the HOV lane.” I had to go back and say, “That’s not a real person. It’s a mannequin.”
