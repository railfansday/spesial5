Inside the mind games that will decide Arsenal v Man City

Mikel Arteta was punished by Pep Guardiola after a crucial selection in the Carabao Cup final, now, Miguel Delaney writes, he holds the experience and power to land a decisive blow on their bitter title rivals

Manchester City have a lot of bigger concerns right now but some of Enzo Maresca’s staff have naturally been conscious of the noise around David Raya. The goalkeeper was not wearing a sling at Tuesday’s Champions League match against Borussia Dortmund, after he had so conspicuously been photographed sporting one on London’s busiest street just two days before.

It’s certainly true the injury suffered at Newcastle United is nowhere near as bad as first feared, with contact on a nerve having apparently caused the initial issue.

Some on the City side have wondered whether Mikel Arteta is using it to play his usual games. The manager likes to use media and even official squad photos to keep opposition guessing.

It would be especially understandable here, since this position could go a long way to deciding Sunday’s game.

The entire decision on Raya, in fact, draws out so many of its strands and storylines.

Both sides are especially attuned to it since it was as recently as March's Carabao Cup final that Pep Guardiola’s response to the Kepa Arrizabalaga selection essentially decided that game.

It might even have been the Catalan’s last piece of genius in England. Arsenal couldn’t play out of the press without Raya's passing, and City gradually shut them down. The tactical effect of it was so pronounced that it framed the thinking of both clubs ahead of April’s suffocatingly tense Premier League showdown.

That 2-1 City win might well go down as one of those influential defeats, rather than decisive victories, because of the effect on Arsenal. They actually gleaned confidence and instruction, to win all of their remaining games.

And now they're champions, and intent on asserting exactly what that means - with Raya's injury currently the primary concern.

Except, aside from it looking 50-50 over whether Raya will be involved, Guardiola certainly won’t be. The man responsible for that tactical thinking isn't there. And even if there is now debate over Maresca’s exact quality as a coach, he’s not Guardiola.

The dysfunction of his own pressing system, and City’s newly profound susceptibility to counter-attacks, also means they may not be able to execute any such approach in the same way… but then very little about City is the same.

The very consequence of this match might even change.

It all adds to the feel of this being a defining game for this most peculiar of seasons.

Arsenal can win to emphatically kill City’s title challenge… but will that even be a title challenge? City can win to transform the entire dynamic around the top of the table, but will the outcome of their appeal skew it in a way the Premier League has never seen?

Or, what if they win and then win their appeal - however unlikely legal figures currently think that is - to then go on a run?

It’s an absurd context, and that for a fixture that has recently been freighted with greater meaning.

And it is because of that recent history that Arsenal do feel they owe City one.

Arteta is said to feel it was absolutely crucial they won the title before Guardiola left and the verdict of the long-running Premier League case came in.

It was about proving it on their own terms. And now, with City precarious, they want to fully prove their superiority; to emphasise this can be their era; to put City out of the picture.

Despite enduring two comebacks for draws in their last two matches, the Arsenal squad are in a good mood.

They feel they played excellently for 160 minutes of those games and that just continuing that will have them in good stead over an awkward run of fixtures.

Or, as one figure told the Independent: “We are due to hammer someone”.

And, as another said separately: “We need to hammer City on Sunday”.

Everyone at Arsenal knows that is much easier said than done.

Despite all of City’s recent problems, the rivalry of this fixture might just be the type of game Maresca’s side require. It might cause them to dig deep.

That of course may depend on how deep their problems actually are.

One recent argument coming out of City has been that this is all just tactical, and individual moments in individual games are going against them.

It’s just that the idea this slump is solely down to tactical/technical issues feels… a stretch.

Really, City are in a situation of remarkable extremes.

Even the departure of Guardiola would have been enough to distort an entire season. If City are obviously better structured than Manchester United after Sir Alex Ferguson or Arsenal themselves after Arsene Wenger, it is still adapting to the absence of one of the most psychologically imposing figures in football. His presence was immense.

That alone creates a vacuum, that is arguably manifesting in City's dysfunctional midfield.

That area also poses significant tactical questions about Guardiola's successor. Insiders familiar with both Chelsea and Leicester City can’t help notice familiar flaws in Maresca’s teams, especially as regards the balance. City are just so easy to get at.

You can even map previous Chelsea form onto this season. Maresca’s first season at Stamford Bridge in 2024-25 started well, to the point of being talked of as title challengers, only to endure a winter drop-off surrounded by the same tactical discussions.

The City squad are going through a situation no one in English football history has ever experienced. You can even extend that to European football, despite various controversies, given how long this entire process has been.

Another line from inside the club is that they are as adamant with the players as they are with everyone else: that they will overturn this verdict, and big questions will instead be posted to the rest of the Premier League.

Some at rival clubs even think that one element of City’s belligerent response, at least in public, has been as a message to the players.

They need to radiate a sense of business as usual so as to convince the squad, because they do need the actual football business to indeed be as it usually is. They know they need to keep them focused.

That’s just very difficult to do when the potential consequences could come this season, which recent results arguably show.

Because, even if the players themselves honestly insist they are genuinely fully focused, this is still a story that has major implications for their careers. They may have to take big decisions. Some might even be thinking this is a waste of a season in a short career.

That is going to at least have some effect, even if it is subconscious.

It creates a cloud of doubt, and this in a sport that demands more intensity of focus than ever before. The opposition manager, Arteta, demands it more than anyone else. Guardiola himself was a huge influence in this.

Both have abruptly discarded numerous players if they sensed that a necessary tunnel vision had been obscured by even 1 per cent.

And that is exactly what a situation like this does. It's too big to just completely shut out.

Without it, City might have even been better able to work around some of the tactical issues.

In short, even if performances have been OK, this does not look like a team playing to optimum level.

Neither do Arsenal but they don’t feel too far off. They're primed. City might well need that challenge to rouse themselves.

Either way, it is already a fixture that feels like it has the makings of a huge result. And also a result that might mean absolutely nothing by the end.
