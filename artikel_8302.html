These AI DCs are multiple buildings of blimp hangars costing a quarter of a trillion overall to perform mass data storage of redundant copies, data churns, glorified Google searches, and produce cute videos (Like the fake AI M* Chris ones). How many pop-ups and monetized ads will cover that?
I think a 'steel man' framing of AI data centre revenue generating workloads would be closer to this:

they're packed full of GPUs with enough RAM & compute to power LLM inference & agentic AI services -- services which companies like Apple find valuable enough to be willing to budget $200 per engineer per day to increase the productivity of their engineering teams, by offloading a lot of the software engineering grunt work. If we assume Apple has around 20k full time software engineers who on average consume half of their $200 / day budget on AI services for 45 weeks every year, that comes to $450m / yr in AI services revenue from one customer -- granted, a key large customer.

Think of it less as hardware to run low margin B2C ads & spam as a way to monetise clicks & attention, and more hardware for B2B services to replace/augment large chunks of technical jobs that historically have been done by employees paid salaries of hundreds of thousands per year.

On the other hand, $450m / yr in revenue is big number, but you gave a much bigger number of $0.25 trillion in capex! The big question is if the capex can achieve an adequate return on capital -- who knows. That depends on stuff like how fast competition drives down the price of AI services, market segmentation into routine tasks that can be performed by dumber AI running on cheaper hardware vs niches of high value tasks that benefit from throwing state of the art AI & compute at eye bleeding rental prices, vs successful AI adoption (or not) throughout new industry segments.
