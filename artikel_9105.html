MoMA to Host First US Ana Mendieta Retrospective in Over 20 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The 200-work exhibition will open in December 2027, almost a year after the closing of the Tate Modern’s current retrospective on the late Cuban-American artist.

The indelible impact of late Cuban-American artist Ana Mendieta's practice will be celebrated in a forthcoming retrospective in New York next year. Scheduled to open at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in Manhattan in December 2027, Ana Mendieta: A Retrospective marks the first presentation of Mendieta's overarching career in the United States in over 20 years.

MoMA's announcement comes just two months before Tate Modern's ongoing Mendieta retrospective closes in London. Comprising around 200 works, including rarely seen formative drawings and newly remastered films, the MoMA exhibition will present Mendieta's artistic process, signature developments, and career milestones in a chronological fashion. Starting with early figurative works completed during her undergraduate and graduate studies at the University of Iowa, the exhibition will follow the artist's subsequent expansion in making and documenting her performance art, as well as her foray into sculpture, installation, and site-specific land art.

Born in Cuba and raised in Iowa from age 12 as a child evacuee through Operation Peter Pan, Mendieta ruminated on themes of displacement, ancestral memory, ecology, and the politics of the female body, as well as its connection with the natural world. She's best known for her Silueta (1973–80) series, in which the outline of her body (either present or absent) is embedded, extruded, burned, or embellished into various natural environments as a physical and spiritual reunion with the earth. These “earth-body works,” as Mendieta called them, dealt with Mesoamerican and Afro-Caribbean divinity, natural reclamation and life cycles, and the deconstruction of borders.

Presaging Mendieta's groundbreaking Siluetas were transgressive performance pieces centering the use of blood as a signifier of violence, fertility, vitality, and ancestral ritual. Her 1973 performance “Untitled (Rape Scene)” and its photo documentation were created in response to the rape and murder of 20-year-old University of Iowa student Sarah Ann Ottens, whereas her filmed performance of “Untitled (Death of a Chicken)” (1972) graphically referenced animal sacrifice practices associated with Santería.

The retrospective also centers the artist's lens-based work, her sustained interest in organic material, and how her travels, reunion with Cuba, and art residencies sparked major evolutions in her practice. Mendieta's 1981 Esculturas Rupestres (Rupestrian Sculptures) series, 1983–85 fellowship at the American Academy in Rome, and a variety of later works, including a series of ink-wash drawings, are among the highlights mentioned by the museum.

“Ana Mendieta rigorously investigated the physical, conceptual, and emotional potential of materials like soil, sand, water, blood, leaves, wood, and fire to create images that speak across time and geography,” said Beverly Adams, MoMA's Latin American art curator and retrospective co-organizer, in a press statement. “In doing so, she redefined the boundaries between sculpture and action, object and image, and private and public.”

In celebrating Mendieta's punctuated career, the retrospective apparently strays away from the tragic circumstances of the 36-year-old artist's death and the unshakeable speculations that continue to follow. Such is the case with Mendieta's ongoing exhibition at the Tate Modern, which also omitted mentioning the artist's death and the resulting controversy surrounding her husband, artist Carl Andre.

Nevertheless, the artist's own poignant grapplings with loss and rebirth are staged independently of the discourse, underscoring her persisting critical impact in a visual arts landscape that has rarely offered credit to women artists.

Ana Mendieta: A Retrospective will be on view at MoMA from December 12, 2027 to April 15, 2028.
