UK Culture Secretary Faces Scrutiny After British Council’s Donation to Palestinian Museum

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The British Council came under fire from a pro-Israel lobbying group after providing public funding to the Palestinian Museum in Birzeit, West Bank, reports the Telegraph.

Lisa Nandy, the culture secretary, received a letter from UK Lawyers for Israel (UKLFI), saying that supporting such a cause “concerned that UK public money” and “glorifies terrorist activity.” ARTnews reached out to UKLFI at the time of press for the contents of the letter and whether Nandy’s office had responded. According to the Telegraph, the British Council’s Cultural Protection Fund has provided Taawon UK, a Palestinian-led charity linked to the Palestinian Museum, with numerous grants since 2018 with the aim of “support heritage projects in the West Bank and Jerusalem.” The museum was opened in 2016, and UKLFI is calling on the government to halt any fundings as its exhibitions “present people responsible for some of the deadliest terrorist attacks on Israeli civilians as heroes, martyrs and freedom fighters, without mentioning their victims.”

Among them objects exhibited there have been posters depicting Dalal al-Mughrabi, who led the 1978 Coastal Road massacre in which 38 Israelis were killed, as a martyr, alongside material celebrating Hamas bombmaker Yahya Ayyash.

In 2019, the Palestinian Museum received a grant of £152,208 through Taawon UK. The award notice stated that the museum conserve and digitize comments “complemented by a wide range of advocacy and education activities hosted by the Palestinian Museum.”

Taawon UK’s funds have come under further inspection, as UKLFI wrote to the Charity Commission to investigate whether the received funds have been appropriately used. “Following concerns raised about the Welfare Association and its trustees’ oversight of the end use of charitable funds, we have opened a regulatory compliance case to examine the matter further. We will be engaging with the charity’s trustees as we gather more information,” a Charity Commission spokesman said.

UKLFI’s lobbying has been powerful. In February, the British Museum stripped the word “Palestinian” from some of its displays about the Middle East after the group wrote a letter to Nicholas Cullinan, the museum’s director, seeking the removal of that word, specifically in texts that referred to certain peoples as being “of Palestinian descent.”
