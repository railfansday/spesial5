10 Subscription Box Gifts for Kids – Prepay a Set Term, Then Stop the Renewal

A subscription box turns one gift into a delivery every month, but only if the child opens each box and your charges end with the gift. Pick a box by the child’s age and what they already love, prepay a set number of months, and turn off any renewal before the last box ships.

Every box below renews automatically on its regular plans, and the renewal goes on the card that paid for it. On a gift, that card is yours, and the charges keep coming whether or not anyone opens the boxes.

Paying for six or 12 months up front doesn’t change that. When a prepaid term runs out, each of these companies renews it for the same length and bills you for it.

Prepay anyway, since a longer term lowers the price of each box. Then make sure the plan ends with the gift, either by buying a gift version that stops by itself or by turning the renewal off yourself.

Each price is for the plan named, with shipping in the contiguous U.S. included unless the entry adds it.

Six months of Little Passports World Adventures cost $162 paid up front, $30 less than the $192 the same six packages cost at the $32 monthly price. A grandparent who prepays and turns off the renewal right away gets all six packages, and the plan ends there.

Three of these sell a gift that ends by itself. KiwiCo’s non-renewing gift subscriptions run 3, 6, or 12 months, and Bookroo’s one-time gifts never renew. A CrunchLabs gift certificate for 3 or 12 boxes is set up by the child’s family and renews only if they add their own billing details.

Everywhere else, turn the renewal off in your account before the last box ships. KiwiCo, Little Passports, OwlCrate, and Raddish let you do it the day you order and still ship every box you paid for. Neither KiwiCo nor Raddish will cancel a prepaid term partway through, so a 12-month term can’t be cut short if the child loses interest.

A box that sits unopened is money spent for nothing, so choose by what the child already does, within the box’s age range. A kid who builds things fits CrunchLabs or KiwiCo’s Tinker Crate, a reader fits Bookroo or OwlCrate, and a kid who likes helping in the kitchen fits Raddish.

Check with the parents too, since some boxes ask something of them. Younger kids cook Raddish’s recipes alongside an adult, and Bitsbox’s projects need a keyboard to type on.

A short term costs more per box. Bookroo’s Picture Book Club costs $35.94 for one box as a gift, against $24.94 a box on its 12-month gift, both shipped.

When you can’t tell yet whether the child will keep opening the boxes, pay that extra for three months first, a term KiwiCo, Bookroo, and CrunchLabs all sell as a gift that ends by itself.
