School Lunch Can Cost 40 Cents or Nothing – Check Whether Your Kids Qualify

Before you pay for or pack another school lunch, find out whether your kids can get free or reduced-price meals. Your school or state may already feed every student free, and kids in families on SNAP food benefits get free meals automatically. Otherwise, fill out the school’s meal application each school year, or whenever your income drops.

A family of four earning $55,000 a year qualifies for reduced-price meals this school year, and a reduced-price lunch costs 40 cents at most. With two kids buying lunch every day of a 180-day school year, that family saves $936 by paying 40 cents instead of $3.00, the typical elementary lunch price in a School Nutrition Association survey.

The Agriculture Department publishes the income limits each year by household size, at 185% of the federal poverty line for reduced-price meals and 130% for free ones. Kids whose families get SNAP food benefits or TANF cash assistance qualify for free meals regardless of income, as do children in foster care, in Head Start, or experiencing homelessness.

Nine states make school meals free for all public school students: California, Colorado, Maine, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, New Mexico, New York, and Vermont. Elsewhere, many schools serve every student free breakfast and lunch, with no applications, under the federal Community Eligibility Provision.

Start by asking your kids’ school whether meals are free for everyone there this year. If they aren’t, look for a letter saying your kids were approved through SNAP or another benefit, which schools must send when no application is needed.

Otherwise, fill out the free and reduced-price meal application, one form for all your kids in the district. Turn it in any time during the school year, including right after a job loss or pay cut. File a new one every school year, since an approval expires no later than 30 school days into the next. Federal rules bar schools from posting or announcing approved kids’ names, or singling them out in the lunch line.

If you pay full price, add up what your packed lunches cost before assuming they’re cheaper. Deloitte’s 2026 school lunch index put its cheapest packed lunch, built around a peanut butter and jelly sandwich, at $4.89, against $3.00 for a typical elementary school lunch.
