On Episode 311 of The Menace Podcast, host Dennis James was joined by Milos Sarcev and Jose Raymond, and the panel tried to pick “The King,” Ronnie Coleman’s best year from a gigantic era in bodybuilding.

Former police officer, Ronnie Coleman burst onto the bodybuilding scene in 1990 when he became Mr. Texas in the heavyweight and overall and retired in 2007, with a total of 8 Mr. Olympia titles. He is now known as an icon and a legend, but with so many great competitions to choose from, which year did “The King” peak on stage?

Bodybuilding fans often refer to Coleman’s incredible muscle mass, but definition was also key to his success. In discussing the glutes, the panel noted that developing striations in this area, which typically carries a higher amount of body fat, is extremely difficult. “Ronnie was crazy, always,” remarked Milos Sarcev of The King’s winning physique. “Ronnie’s look changed in 96 a little bit, (and) 97 … but 98 is really when he delivered.”

When asked by host Dennis James to pinpoint Coleman’s best look in 1998, “The Mind” responded that it had to be the May 16, 1998, IFBB Night of Champions in New York City. On that show, Coleman beat the likes of Kevin Levrone, Mike Matarazzo, and Johnny Moya to win his first and only Night of Champions victory. It was a storied moment for Coleman, who had placed 14th during his first attempt in 1992.

Following his big win in New York, Coleman went on to capture his first of eight Mr. Olympia titles. “He beat Kevin (Levrone), but going into the Olympia, nobody counted on him winning,” explained Sarcev. “But (his) condition was spectacular.”

Jose Raymond put fourth Coleman’s presentation at the 2001 Arnold Classic as another contender for his prime time on stage. “That was peeled,” commented Raymond. “That was one of my favorite looks from him,” he added.

“Oh, crazy,” said host Dennis James in appreciation. “I would say so, too.”

Sarcev noted that Coleman was still a hot contender in 2003, but with his size now overtaking his conditioning, Dennis James suggested that his midsection was no longer at it’s best. Which was your favorite Ronnie Coleman performance?
