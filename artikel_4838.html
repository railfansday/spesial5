78 AD-Approved Interior Photographers in the Northeast

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Grand staircases. Cozy breakfast nooks. Color-drenched walls. You name it, these interior photographers in the Northeast have shot it, in daylight and darkness, maximizing views of the park or the coast, the backyard or the city street far below. Their images are portraits of homes, capturing a space’s personality and highlighting the talents of its designer. Read on to meet AD-approved interior photographers in the Northeast (and, in many cases, beyond).

A beachfront home in Santa Barbara, California.

Classical proportions and carefully composed details give William Abranowicz’s interior photos the poise of a still life. His background as a fine art printer shows in images that reward looking beyond the statement furniture to the smaller objects around it.

This vibrant San Francisco home designed by Noz Nozawa is sometimes called “the Rainbow House.”

Brittany Ambridge makes colorful rooms shine and white rooms look luminous without losing the details that give them dimension. Her lighting approach draws on techniques learned shooting film, translating a home’s atmosphere whether the mood is airy and expansive, or bold and colorful. She has worked with talents including Jeremiah Brent, Nate Berkus, Charlotte Moss, and Gachot Studios.

The living room of Kate Upton and Justin Verlander’s penthouse in New York

Dramatic natural lighting is often heightened and accentuated in Gieves Anderson’s photographs, in both moody, highly patterned rooms as well as light and airy spaces. He knows just how to make the most of a perfectly cast shadow.

An interior at the Maison Brummell Majorelle in Marrakech, Morocco.

Trained in classical and modern dance, New York City–based Emily Andrews parlays that craft’s contrasting principles of line and weight, positive and negative space, into her photography work. The beautifully captured balancing acts have garnered the attention of AD, WSJ Magazine, The New York Times, and others.

Glassworks offers a kaleidoscopic moment in a moody dining room.

If you love the enviable homes of Athena Calderone—a.k.a. EyeSwoon—Jessica Antola is a name you need to know. Antola has captured Calderone’s Instagram-famous spaces, as well as countless others.

The minimalist Brooklyn apartment of Coco Romack and Mars Hobrecker.

Winnie Au’s photography brings out the color and personality in each space she so smartly captures, whether it’s a playful Palm Springs hotel, a boldly designed New York apartment, or the creative studios of artists and makers.

A dining room in Greenwich, Connecticut, designed by Alison Hammatt Home.

From Nantucket antiques to contemporary interiors, Jane Beiles approaches a room as a still life, balancing textures, shapes, and scale rather than letting one trophy piece steal the show. Based in Fairfield, Connecticut, she has photographed work by Gil Schafer, Katie Ridder, Stonefox, and Bennett Leifer.

Chloe Fineman’s West Village home, designed by Tali Roth.

Andrew Bui loves dynamic, expressive lighting and strives to create images with a timeless quality to them. He comes from two generations of award-winning restaurateurs, and his food photography is just as delectable as his interior shots.

Filmmaker ShawnLevy’s dining room in Manhattan, designed by AD100 designer Jeremiah Brent

Raised in Texas and now based in Brooklyn, Max Burkhalter doesn’t shy away from assignments in the Lone Star State, having shot projects like David Adjaye’s crystalline Ruby City contemporary art center. Still, Burkhalter reps hard for Brooklyn and has collaborated with brands including In Common With and Roll & Hill.

Inside a 19th-century Brooklyn town house renovated by Hatchet Design & Build.

Nicholas Calcott has worked on a variety of editorial, commercial, and self-assigned projects across topics including home and belonging, creativity, and the future. He’s of Arabic, Jewish, and British heritage and also speaks French, so he brings an international sensibility to assignments.

The daughter of an architect and a photographer, Anita Calero brings an artistic eye to her still-life photography, and is equally adept at shooting nature and interiors. A celebrated fine-art photographer as well, Calero works between home bases in New York, Colombia, and Miami, living her philosophy that beauty is everywhere and “nothing is ugly.”

Photographer Ethan James Green’s apartment in Brooklyn Heights

A regular contributor to AD, New York, and other publications, Seth Caplan documents spaces with a keen sense of presence, emphasizing warm tones and moody shadows. He loves a good portrait and also shoots images for homeware brands including Bed Threads, Framebridge, and Caraway Home.

Sean Davidson beautifully captures the stillness of an organically neutral interior.

A favorite of many AD100 and AD PRO Directory studios, Sean Davidson has translated his industrial design background into a successful career in photography, frequently working with top fashion brands and design publications alike.

Polished yet raw, Blaine Davis’s style and compositions result in interior shots and portraits that are crisp and authentic—not unlike the glossy pages of the magazines in which they appear. (Including AD, of course.)

A historic Fifth Avenue mansion, built by Stanford White.

A documentary background shapes Hayley Ellen Day’s interest in the relationship between people and their spaces. Her interiors range from ornate salons to playful bathrooms, with small details offering clues to the lives unfolding beyond the frame.

Inside a reimagined 19th-century farmhouse in Southampton, New York, designer Ariel Okin animates a historic New York refuge using color and character.

Donna Dotan’s obsession with clean lines and natural light is evident across her catalog of interior shoots, in which spaces are portrayed as sun-kissed and in dazzling color. Her work has graced the pages of all major design publications, including AD.

A New York City apartment for Casameu New York, styled by Rebecca Bartoshesky

Combining strong compositional elements with a sense of warmth and sensitivity, Pippa Drummond—a Kiwi photographer now based in New York City—has been commissioned by the likes of Dwell and Herman Miller.

Bovina Farm and Fermentory in New York’s Catskill Mountains.

Soft daylight and tactile finishes lend Sarah Elliott’s interiors an easy, unhurried feeling. Her photographs for Upstate invite a closer look at country living, while her work with Elizabeth Roberts, Nune, and others brings that intimacy to many homes.

A bedroom on the Upper East Side of New York, designed by The Interiors Concierge and styled by Pablo Olguin

Whether a space is minimalist or maximalist, Claire Esparros is able to distill its essence by amplifying the serenity or vibrancy of her photos. Framing spatial sequences and highlighting vignettes are among her strengths.

A landing in Kacey Musgraves’s Nashville home.

Lelanie Foster’s portraits of Serena Williams, Kacey Musgraves, and Misty Copeland for AD make the home as compelling as the person. For designers seeking photographs that introduce the inhabitants as well as the interiors, her portfolio merits a look.

A bedroom designed by Mieke ten Have and photographed by Frank Frances in New York’s Hudson Valley

Trained in art practice at the School of Visual Arts, photographer Frank Frances has contributed his versatile eye to AD, British Vogue, West Elm, and others. His photography, which expands beyond interiors into beauty, food, and fashion, has appeared in exhibitions at galleries across the world, including Sasha Wolf Gallery, the Studio Museum of Harlem, and Werkstadt Graz.

Kirsten Francis captures a collectors’ trove.

An NYC–based lifestyle photographer specializing in interiors and portraits, Kirsten Francis has shot sun-drenched spaces by a roster of designers including Ashley Darryl, Alyssa Kapito, and Emily C. Butler.

A family room in Park City, Utah, designed by Wendy Labrum Interiors.

If Athena Calderone’s coffee table book Live Beautiful has earned a permanent spot on your coffee table, you’re already acquainted with Nicole Franzen’s photography. The New York talent’s portfolio also spans hospitality and cookbooks, giving homebodies and hosts alike something to savor.

Inside a private home on the Amalfi Coast.

The skill with which Patrick Fraser is able to portray history and character in his images is captivating. Born in the UK and recently relocated to Connecticut from the West Coast, he often takes inspiration from objects, landscapes, and people that have been forgotten or ignored.

The photographer shot his own kitchen on Long Island.

Douglas Friedman gives elaborate interiors a sense of drama, using natural light to bring out their materials. Along with photographing sumptuous rooms by Ken Fulk, Nicole Hollis, and Kelly Wearstler, the photographer has welcomed AD into his own Marfa retreat.

Alice Gao’s lens has been known to linger in shadows, resulting in a photograph that can romanticize even the most neutral, pared-back interiors. Her style brings minimalist, modern settings—such as those of clients Yabu Pushelberg and Toast UK, among others—beautifully to life.

This Brooklyn-based creative has brought an expert eye to shoots in countless stylish homes, restaurants like Altro Paradiso, and the studios of talents like Trueing and Christian Siriano.

The sunlit living room of a townhouse in New York City’s West Village

His training as a painter informs Gaut’s portrait-like images of interiors, imbuing each space with personality. AD has published his images of New York apartments, ski chalets in Wyoming, and farmhouses in Nova Scotia, but he’s also a fine art photographer whose prints have appeared in stores including Design Within Reach.

Inside celeb stylist Kate Young’s upstate New York home.

Family life in all its facets is a frequent muse for William Geddes—his own wife and daughters included—though he’s also keen on assignments across the editorial, advertising, and publishing industries. A graduate of The New School, Parsons School for Design, Geddes also offers both simple and complex video shoots among his professional services.

A vignette from Elsa Perretti’s home in Spain

Adrianna Glaviano finds visual wit in the smallest arrangements, such as jewelry on striped straws, or personal effects gathered around a sink. Her still-life sensibility offers designers a way to make the objects within a room part of its story. Working between New York and Milan, she counts AD France, HAY, FLOS, and Gohar World among her clients.

An Upstate New York couple painstakingly restored this geometric 1970s hideaway.

Golden hour appears to be Nick Glimenakis’s choice time to shoot, when he’s able to capture the warm light, long shadows, and exaggerated contrasts that up the drama in his imagery.

An indoor-outdoor space designed by Mike Moser Studio.

Residential design and hospitality meet in Christian Harder’s portfolio, which includes work with Jake Arnold, Ash, and AvroKO. His photographs of Darren Jett’s Brooklyn apartment for AD offer a useful introduction to his eye for rooms with personality. He’s also kept up a fine art photography practice and is repped by OneWay Gallery in Providence and Narragansett, Rhode Island.

In a Malibu home designed by Lala Reimagined, a dining area is filled with bentwood and natural fiber delights.

Ori Harpaz’s sharp editorial style has landed him multiple features in all of the top design magazines. His eye for color and penchant for strict angles mean his images are bold, striking, and arresting.

A marble bathroom in a Finger Lakes estate designed by Jeremiah Brent.

Ethan Herrington’s career began in commercial still-life photography, and places and things get equal billing in his portfolio—welcome news for those who obsess over both the room and its contents. Based in Cold Spring, New York, he has worked with Billy Cotton, Commune, and Studio Dorion.

A sunken living room oozes cool in this airy abode.

Formerly a fashion and textile print designer, Angela Hau—who operates out of San Francisco and NYC—has adapted her keen understanding of shape, scale, and balance to photography. Industry brands Thompson Street Studio, Egg Collective, and more have tapped Hau to beautifully capture interiors and product photography.

Studio Valle de Valle transformed a historic home in Toronto.

Jenni Kayne’s Los Angeles home and Neal Beckstedt’s wood-clad Chelsea apartment have both met Stephen Kent Johnson’s lens. He favors intimate views and revealing details over an all-encompassing wide angle, complete with soft light and carefully framed vignettes.

Inside a renovated California cabin with interiors designed by Fritz Haeg and Jeremy Schipper.

Recently relocated from LA, André Jones brings a sunny and warm approach to architecture and lifestyle photography. From rustic home interiors to sleek corporate buildings, his images are consistently well-framed and proportioned.

The living room in Walton Goggins’s home in New York’s Hudson Valley

The grand Victorian, simple Colonial, and traditional farmhouse-style homes scattered across upstate New York are a common subject for Kate Jordan’s lens. Her shots of Shaker-style kitchens and cozy living rooms depict all the warmth and familiarity they were designed to exude.

The city takes center stage in this New York apartment.

Long shadows and glimpses through windows give Joseph Kramm’s interiors a palpable sense of depth. His work with galleries including The Future Perfect and Hostler Burrows also makes him well versed in giving collectible furniture its moment.

One of the most versatile interior photographers in the Northeast, Jared Kuzia captures residential projects up and down the Eastern Seaboard for architects, interior designers, and publications. Whether shooting urban apartments or coastal cabins, he is motivated by “conveying the essence of home,” as he puts it.

A bedroom in Isabella Rosselini’s converted barn on Long Island by architect Pietro Cicognani.

Francesco Lagnese’s interiors work moves comfortably between a spare, sunlit corner and the layered rooms of Tom Scheerer, Williams Lawrence, and Veere Grenney. Working between New York, Palm Beach, and all over Europe from his base in Munich, his itinerary extends well beyond the living room, as he also shoots architecture, portraits, and travel.

With an instantly recognizable, highly atmospheric style that’s won favor with top designers and stylists, William Jess Laird is consistently in high demand. His calm, minimal photos appear regularly in AD and many other leading art and design publications.

People are part of the picture in David Land’s photographs, from children playing in a living room to a family gathered on the sofa. In his images of work by Danielle Colding, Nina Barnieh-Blair, and more, color and everyday activity make these interiors feel welcoming, with life happening inside them.

A high-contrast interior captured by Heami Lee

There’s something innately playful about the images Lee captures for clients. Whether shooting still lifes for Edie Parker or Design Within Reach, Brooklyn-based Lee creates work that is undeniably cool.

Tour a Josh Greene–designed Upper East Side family home with a tented room

Capturing lived-in interiors in an authentically stunning demeanor is Tim Lenz’s calling card. The talent has shot the work of AD100 studio Carrier and Company, Hendricks Churchill, and Augusta Hoffman.

A den on the top floor of a home in Provincetown, Massachusetts, designed by Stanton Schwartz Design Group.

Consider Sean Litchfield’s portfolio a love letter to New England. Maine lake houses, Cape Cod cottages, and Boston brownstones—his portfolio makes room for both rustic intimacy and a more polished setting with Nina Campbell, Patrick Ahearn, and Erin Gates included.

The dining room in Rachel Brosnahan’s apartment, which the actress designed with Zoë Feldman.

Pernille Loof’s restrained compositions give texture, color, and shadow room to register. A minimalist sensibility runs through her interiors and still lifes, bringing a crispness to the frame without stripping away the warmth of the subject.

A living room in Birmingham, Alabama, by Georgette Sturam Design

Adam Kane Macchia’s balanced room views keep architectural features clearly legible, highlighting everything from ceiling beams to the rhythm of tall windows. His portfolio is particularly useful for designers seeking photographs that show how furnishings and a home’s bones work together.

Meghan Marin photographs residential spaces as she finds them: unmade beds, displays of collected items, tables laid for dinner—all the idiosyncrasies of which bring rawness and authenticity to her shots, even if they’ve been styled to appear that way.

Step inside Nate Berkus and Jeremiah Brent’s freshly renovated Fifth Avenue family home

If the name Kelly Marshall isn’t immediately familiar, surely her contrast-rich photography—seen regularly in the pages of AD—will ring a bell. Marshall, who’s a member of the Female Design Council as well as the Black Artists + Designers Guild, is based in New York.

Designer Sara Story’s New York City living room

Joshua McHugh’s interests extend from private residences to museums and artists’ historic homes, including those of Carlo Mollino and Wharton Esherick. His portfolio is an appealing resource for designers whose interiors put architecture, art, and objects in conversation.

Creative dream team Murdock Solon Architects and designer David Cafiero lovingly injected whimsy and joy across all four floors of a New York City family home.

A cover star, Read McKendree’s lively interiors have fronted issues of Elle Decoration Germany, House Beautiful, and Luxe, as well as catalogs for Serena & Lily. We also love his portraits of artists at work in their studios.

Inside a Minneapolis home decadently designed by Prospect Refuge Studio.

Chris Mottalini’s aim each time he photographs a home is to create a portrait of the space and its occupants, revealing what’s hidden beneath the surface. His recent projects include the photography for interior designer Nina Freudenberger’s book Mountain House.

A loggia in the Moroccan home of writer and gardener Umberto Pasti.

Ngoc Minh Ngo’s images of gardens feel enveloping, with layered foliage and paths that draw the eye into the landscape. The New York photographer and author’s botanical photography brings the same attention to a single bloom, making her a natural fit for projects where planting matters. In her images of landscapes by Miranda Brooks and Dan Pearson, every leaf feels considered.

Tour fashion designer Adam Lippes’s bucolic Berkshires home

Growing up around creative matriarchs—her mother a weaver and her grandmother a painter—Isabel Parra understands the importance of an artisan’s tool kit. Her devices of choice? Natural light and its emotive shadow play and honest interior compositions. No wonder she’s caught the attention of fashion designer Adam Lippes, Oomph Home, House & Garden UK, AD, and others.

Natural light, analogue photography, and a sense of intimate nostalgia guide Clément Pascal’s work—with vintage cars making occasional cameos. French-born and now based in New York, his design photographs often keep people in the picture, conveying how a room is occupied as readily as the objects that furnish it.

A home in Sag Harbor Bay by BMA Architects with interior design by The Unionworks.

Trained in interior architecture, Eric Petschek pairs careful attention to design details with a notably calm atmosphere, as shown in his images of Worrell Yeung’s Hudson Valley home for AD. His softly lit, precise compositions give even a spare room emotional weight, without letting its architectural logic disappear.

The view from Kelly Behun’s Manhattan apartment.

Working across New York and Europe, Richard Powers has an eye for architecture’s graphic possibilities: a railing’s shadow, a curved volume, a building set against open sky. In his images, the relationship between form, light, and setting is central to a project.

Working out of New York and Paris, Arrow (Sharon) Radisch’s clients include luxury brands like Gucci and Chanel, as well as editorial outlets like Cereal, for whom she shot Le Corbusier’s Paris apartment.

Inside an AD digital editor’s light-filled Maine abode

Tara Rice takes a photo-journalistic approach to her work, whether in the forests or on the shorelines of Maine or across the far-reaching corners of the world. Her photography is fueled by curiosity about people and places, and the varied ways in which they overlap.

An alum of the School of Visual Arts’s photography program, Brooklyn-based Rayon Richards has 20 years of professional experience, with clients ranging from Def Jam to Fortune. Though accomplished in many arenas—he also paints and plays the drums—we’re especially taken by his eye for vibrant color photography.

Portrait of ceramicist and painter Woody De Othello

A creative producer, curator, and photographer, Kameron Richie has partnered with Nike, VSCO, Floyd, and Everlane on a range of projects. His interior shots—light-dappled and a tinge nostalgic—offer an inviting glimpse inside the lives of his subjects. Richie, who hails from Texas, now holds court in New York City.

An apartment in Casa Ramos, a 1906 apartment building in Barcelona.

New York. Los Angeles. Mexico City. Barcelona. Pia Riverola’s work locales read like a very appealing itinerary. Her sensitivity to color and passing light gives her photographs a dreamy, nostalgic pull. Interiors sit within a wider world of landscapes and everyday encounters, making her work especially appealing when a project’s sense of place matters.

Limewash paint brings emotion to an otherwise quiet interior

Life and movement appear in full focus throughout the varied portfolio of Julia Robbs, whose work in fashion and beauty photography naturally translates over to interiors. The lifestyle component of the home, and how its occupants use it, is key to her vibrant aesthetic.

A seating area in Brooklyn Decker and Andy Roddick’s home in North Carolina

A beloved portraitist, Reid Rolls started his career shooting album covers for record labels. The California native, who now lives in Brooklyn, also photographs fashion, travel, ad campaigns, and, of course, interiors, including his atmospheric images of Brooklyn Decker and Andy Roddick’s home in Charlotte for AD.

An aquatic-themed bathroom in an 1840s townhouse in Brooklyn, New York, designed by Charlap Hyman & Herrero with murals and mosaics by Lukas Geronimas Giniotis and Allison Eden Studios.

Jason Schmidt is as interested in creative people as the spaces they shape. His portraits of artists at work and photographs of buildings such as Frank Gehry’s Santa Monica home offer designers a perspective rooted in the arts.

Inside actor John Leguizamo’s New York City brownstone, on which he and his wife collaborated with myriad design creatives, namely, Barbara Batesko, Nathan Turner, Lucien Rees Roberts, Leslie Rylee, and Harry Gutfreund.

From established businesses to young brands, Tori Sikkema will not only photograph your projects, she’ll also support your branding through consultations that will tailor her shoots to meet specific needs and goals. Her aim is always to allow her clients’ design style to shine through while creating photographs that evoke a sense of place and feeling.

The Connecticut living room of Matthew Malin and Andrew Goetz

Having shot the likes of Maude Apatow, Malene Barnett, and Michael Imperioli for AD, Calcutta-born Ashok Sinha’s portfolio of architecture, interiors, and portraits also includes The New York Times, Bloomberg, and several other distinguished outlets.

A primary suite in Brooklyn designed by Young Huh.

Jacob Snavely’s interiors photographs give lofty ceilings and carefully planned sightlines their due, with clear views that explain a room’s layout. His still-life and portrait work offers designers a way to extend that story beyond the overall space.

A living room featuring a rug designed by Colin King for Beni Rugs.

Billal Taright’s eye gravitates toward sculptural objects, spare arrangements, and the space around them. Warm tones and considered framing lend even a shelf or mantel quiet presence. The New York photographer moves between interiors, fashion, and collectible design, with Ralph Lauren Home, Colin King, and Luke Edward Hall also among his clients.

The kitchen in Nate Berkus and Jeremiah Brent’s home in Portugal.

Trevor Tondro’s command of color and mood brings a lively quality to his interiors photography. With early experience assisting both Philip-Lorca diCorcia and Martyn Thompson, he maximizes both artificial and natural light, always finding more than one way into a room.

A kitchen by Jenna Chused of Chused and Co. for Isla Porter’s Tastemakers series.

Christian Torres makes interiors feel inviting and lived in, drawing on a practice that also spans hospitality and travel. He can participate from concept and location scouting through styling and lighting, helping designers carry a cohesive idea through the shoot.

Pattern and pigment freshen a traditional sitting room

No design detail is lost on Lesley Unruh, a Tennessee native and alum of Domino. Unruh, who now lives in New York City, has worked with Anna Burke Interiors, Malene Barnett, and CeCe Barfield Thompson, among others.

The grand staircase in a historic Georgian home in Pennsylvania designed by Haldon House.

Jason Varney brings a painterly handling of light and a strong feel for color to restaurants, hotels, and homes. His food photography makes him particularly useful for hospitality projects where the atmosphere extends from the dining room to the table, but homes are always on the menu too.

An indoor/outdoor living area in Sag Harbor, designed by Garnett.DePasquale Projects.

Nicholas Venezia brings architectural training and eight years at Selldorf Architects to his photography. His process connects a project’s underlying ideas with its visual story, a valuable trait when a designer wants the photographs to communicate more than appearances.

A 1909 mansion in New Jersey belonging to director Ruben Fleischer, designed by Studio DB.

If Brooklyn brownstones and beautifully considered hotels populate your design daydreams, spend some time with Matthew Williams’s portfolio. His interiors photography moves between whole-room views and studies of furniture, materials, and smaller objects. His portfolio includes homes by Elizabeth Roberts and Studio DB, giving designers examples of how a project can unfold across a sequence of images.

Tour a strikingly modern home in East Hampton

A resident of New York City but available for work worldwide, Tim Williams captures a sense of life in his photographs of commercial and residential interiors. His images recently graced AD PRO’s coverage of David Thomas’s new Madison Avenue outpost for Manolo Blahnik.

The powder room of a West Village townhouse designed by Starrett Ringbom.

A veteran of the fashion industry, Regan Wood shifted her focus to architecture and interior photography some time ago. Still, she sees a connection between them in that both form an “outward expression of one’s inner life.”
