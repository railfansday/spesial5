‘Hexed’ Review: Disney’s Bewitching Animation Is A Surprisingly Edgy Y.A. Fantasy

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

“Have you ever been persecuted, drowned or burned at the stake?” Conditions for entry to the city of Hexe are pretty rigorous and surprisingly dark in this engaging Disney animation. After playing things safe with 2023’s almost immediately forgettable, formulaic Wish, the studio has somehow turned the tanker round with a surprisingly edgy Y.A. fantasy that sometimes seems in danger of going too far in the opposite direction. Mention of warlocks in the stylish prologue is a little misleading; Hexed is a film about witches all the way, a hectic but mostly very entertaining fantasy that seems curiously beholden to Wicked, in ways that threaten to seriously undermine everything that’s good about it. Girl power is the central theme, introducing Billie Doe (Hailee Steinfeld) as an Ohio infant with special powers, whose mother Alice (Rashida Jones) watches over her with a cautious eye after almost losing her to a couple of crows that once appeared through a portal to another world. Fast-forward a few years, and Billie is now going through her difficult teenage years, borrowing her mother’s car without permission (or a licence) to set the school mascot — significantly, an owl — free into the wild. The headmaster is on the warpath, but, while hiding in the bathroom, Billie manifests a strange ability to move things with her mind. Watch on Deadline

Billie’s supernatural talents are somehow connected to a bracelet she has been forced to wear since childhood, and after she takes it off for a second time, the portal returns and sucks her into a parallel dimension. After being inducted by a talking book and a pen (Stephen Fry and Tracey Ullman respectively), Billie becomes a citizen of Hexe, a sanctuary city for practitioners of the occult. Hexe, though, is not quite the land of the free that it first appears to be; under the reign of Queen Celeste (Jodie Foster), it is very much a two-tier society, divided into those who indulge its narcissistic ruler and those who don’t.

For reasons that seem fairly obvious already, Alice follows her daughter to Hexe and tries to send her home in an enchanted cauldron while she distracts the local police chief. Instead, however, Billie follows the cryptic label on a magic potion that leads her to Beef Roger Crummchuk (Walton Goggins), a three-eyed cat and one-time familiar to Queen Celeste’s daughter. After an extended sequence in which Billie begs for his help — lots of scenes gone on far too long — Beef Roger decides to help Billie look for Alice, who is now locked up behind barred doors and windows in a tower at the royal palace.

Comparisons with Wicked were always going to be inevitable, but the extent to which Hexed leans into them goes way beyond homage, notably in Queen Celeste’s fleet of flying bird-women with creepy fluorescent eyes. Hexe itself even looks like Emerald City, and when Billie first embarks on her adventure, she tells Beef Roger that “We’re gonna yellow-brick-road it”, the only time The Wizard of Oz is consciously mentioned. And once again, the climax involves the unmasking of an impostor, as Queen Celeste manipulates the talents of others to perform a centuries-old moon spell and gain ultimate power.

There’s still a lot to like, however, especially in its use of witchcraft as a metaphor; much play is made of Billie’s teenage-emo awkwardness, with her purple hair, spindly legs and clunky goth boots (“You think there’s something wrong with me,” she wails to her long-suffering mother). Surprisingly, there is no father in the picture, and the story boils down to an interesting three-way dynamic between Alice, her daughter and the Queen, one that doesn’t quite take the direction you might be expecting. A great and very eclectic voice cast helps in this regard, with Goggins a surprisingly calming presence whenever things get hectic (and they do).

But while Foster’s frosty Queen Celeste offers a refreshing return to the unreconstructed Disney villainesses of old, the rest of Hexed can’t help but seem retrofitted to get us there. There are welcome shades of Zootopia in its world-building — notably a town of familiars, and a rowdy pirate bar — but the story takes some strange turns, especially when, as Queen Celeste’s plan is about to unfold, we go back, albeit briefly, to Ohio. Its message — the usual be-yourself, do-your-thing — is harmless enough, mind. It’s just disappointing that a film so high on intention, ignition and inwitchification should fall so short in the inspiration department. There’s still enough potential for a sequel, mind.
