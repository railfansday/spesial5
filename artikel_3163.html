Prediction: Micron Stock Is Going to Soar After Its December Earnings Report

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The report could show whether Micron's rough autumn was a gift.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 20, 2026 at 7:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

Colette Kress has had a whirlwind week. Last Thursday, she went from Nvidia's (NVDA -4.62%) longtime finance chief to its interim chief executive officer after CEO Jensen Huang abruptly resigned. On Nov. 18, after Nvidia posted results, she told CNBC something every memory investor should hear: "What we need in terms of chips, what we need in terms of advanced packaging or memory, all of that is also coming together."

Memory is Micron Technology's (MU -6.22%) whole world, and the Nvidia drama has helped drag Micron’s stock far below this year's high. That's why I'm circling Micron's next earnings report, due in December, on my calendar. Micron hasn't announced the exact day yet, but last year it reported its fiscal first-quarter results on Dec. 17.

The report will cover the first quarter of a year Micron's leaders have hyped for months. "As strong as fiscal 2026 was, we expect fiscal 2027 to be even better," CEO Sanjay Mehrotra said in September, adding that supply and demand should be "much tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026."

The bigger reason December matters is what comes after it. Micron has already locked in most of its 2027 supply of high bandwidth memory (HBM), the stacked chips that sit right beside artificial intelligence (AI) graphics processors, at significantly higher prices. Those prices "reset at the beginning of the calendar year," Micron President Manish Bhatia said. That means the guidance Micron gives in December will be the first to bake in those new prices. That forecast matters more to me than the quarter itself.

The quarter has a soft spot. Micron paid every employee a bigger bonus for 2026, and Chief Financial Officer Mark Murphy said much of that cost hits now, calling fiscal Q1 "the floor for gross margins in fiscal 2027." A dip is expected, and I'd only worry if the outlook for the next quarter doesn't improve.

I also want to hear how Micron's newest chips are selling. Micron is in high-volume production of HBM4 chips built for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform, and Nvidia said that Vera Rubin systems have started shipping. Next up is a custom version co-designed with Nvidia, which Micron technology chief Scott DeBoer called "the first major custom HBM product out in the market."

That closeness cuts both ways. If Nvidia's leadership shake-up slows its orders, Micron feels it fast. Micron also has the smallest market share of the three big HBM makers, and rivals SK Hynix (SKHY -4.82%) and Samsung (SSNLF +0.00%) are building huge new factories in South Korea.

Micron has spent the year locking customers into multiyear supply deals. It has signed 26, and customers have backed them with $32 billion in commitments, mostly cash deposits. Partners include General Motors (GM -0.72%) and Ford Motor Company (F +2.21%), and Micron struck a separate strategic deal with Claude maker Anthropic. "The AI revolution has permanently elevated the role of memory and storage solutions from the data center to the edge," said Chief Business Officer Sumit Sadana. New deals in December would tell me customers aren't flinching.

Shareholders could get more, too. Starting Dec. 9, limits on buybacks tied to Micron's federal CHIPS Act funding ease up. "Over time, we expect to return 100% of our excess cash to shareholders," Murphy said. December's call should show how hard Micron plans to lean in.

Then there are the factories, which is where I get a little nervous. In Taiwan, where most of Micron's DRAM came from in 2026, Micron has all but halted construction of a new memory plant amid a drawn-out labor dispute, according to Bloomberg.

Memory has always been a boom-and-bust business, and every new fabrication plant in the semiconductor industry brings the day when supply catches up to demand a little closer. For now, Mehrotra says, "We do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance."

If December shows 2027 prices sticking, customers still signing, and Nvidia's turmoil staying inside Nvidia, I think Micron's rough autumn will look like a buying opportunity.
