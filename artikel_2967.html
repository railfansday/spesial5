NextBoat (NXB) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Revenue more than doubled, but rising interest costs are now in focus.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

NextBoat Inc. (NXB -1.94%) reported record quarterly revenue that more than doubled compared to the prior year, supported by significant growth in pre-owned boat sales and the integration of new boat lines from recent acquisitions. Management indicated that while top-line expansion was strong, the focus has shifted toward margin improvement and operational efficiency to address rising interest and storage costs. The company is implementing new inventory management strategies to reduce carrying times and is integrating its financing arm into every transaction to capture higher-margin service and insurance revenue. Management maintained its full-year revenue outlook and indicated a path toward sustainable profitability through infrastructure development and cost reduction.

Chad Corbin: Thanks, John. Good afternoon, and thank you for joining us. Starting with revenue for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, we generated record third quarter revenue of $50.6 million, representing an increase of 111% compared to the $24 million in the same period of 2025. Revenue grew across all divisions this quarter. We put our floor plan capacity to work, expanded our broker networks at Off The Hook and Autograph Yacht Group and added service and new boat revenue from Apex Marine and Bellhart, both acquired in the second quarter of 2026. Our finance division, Azure, also contributed to revenue growth. The result, higher pre-owned boat sales, more completed brokerage transactions and broader revenue contributions across the business.

New boat sales increased by $5.3 million to $7.5 million for the 3 months ended September 30 compared to the $2.3 million in the same period 2025. The increase is attributable to the new boat lines, which were acquired through Apex Marine and Bellhart acquisitions. Pre-owned boat sales increased by $17 million or 81% to the $37.9 million for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the $20.9 million in the same period of 2025. For the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, we sold approximately 204 pre-owned units compared to approximately 109 pre-owned units for the same period 2025.

Our pre-owned boat sales encompass a broad range of brands and sizes and include inventory, trade-in, brokerage and consignment transactions. To get a sense of the scale of our brokerage sales, in addition to our company-owned boat sales, we sold $65.7 million worth of brokerage boats in the quarter versus $16 million for the same quarter in 2025. So that means our team sold a total of $103 million in pre-owned boat sales versus $36.9 million for the same quarter in 2025. The number of brokerage boats sold in the quarter was 126 versus 58 boats in the same quarter of 2025.

It is important to note that these brokerage boats can contribute service, financing, insurance and warranty contract revenue as well as brokerage commissions to the company. Revenue from arranging financial products, including financing, insurance, extended warranty contracts to customers through various third-party financial institutions and insurance companies increased to $1.2 million for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the $600,000 in the same period of 2025. The increase was driven by higher units delivered, incremental business generated by recent strategic hires and a greater focus on warranty sales for our pre-owned units.

Revenue from service parts and other sales increased to $3.9 million for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, compared to $164,000 in the same period of 2025. The increase is attributable to the acquisitions of Apex Marine and Bellhart Marine. These acquisitions have enhanced our in-house inventory services capabilities and supported the expansion of our retail service offerings. Gross profit increased by $3.8 million or 90% to $8 million for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the $4.2 million in the same period of 2025. The increase was driven by higher boat transaction volumes across our platform. Growth in our higher-margin service and financial product businesses also contributed to our gross profit expansion.

Selling, general and administrative expenses consist primarily of lease expense, insurance, utilities and other customary operating expenses. SG&A increased $1.9 million to $2.5 million for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the $600,000 in the same period of 2025. The increase was primarily driven by operating costs associated with Apex Marine, Autograph Yacht Group and Bellhart and the legal fees and transactional costs associated with those acquisitions, none of which were owned in 2025. These costs were consistent with our planned business expansion for 2026. Salaries and wages expenses increased $4.1 million to $6.9 million for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the $2.7 million in the same period of 2025.

The increase reflects the impact of acquisitions and investments into personnel to support growth, both leading up to and following our initial public offering. The company's floor plan interest expense increased $434,000 or 103% to $855,000 for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the $421,000 in the same period of 2025. We expect interest expense to remain elevated as we continue to utilize our expanded floor plan credit facility. Net loss attributable to NextBoat Inc. for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, was $4.6 million compared to a net loss of $100,000 in the prior year quarter. The increase in net loss reflected a $7 million year-over-year increase in total operating expenses.

Included in the third quarter results were $3 million in noncash nonrecurring expenses, of which $1.5 million related to stock-based compensation under the company's employee equity plan. After accounting for these items and the other adjustments detailed in our adjusted EBITDA reconciliation, adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $1.6 million for the quarter. We are maintaining our full year 2026 revenue guidance of $165 million to $170 million, representing approximately 43% year-over-year growth. Based on our performance to date, we expect to achieve the upper end of this range with the potential to exceed it.

Ross Tannenbaum: Thanks, Chad. I want to thank everyone for joining the call today, and I'm very excited to be part of the NextBoat team. We are in the process of fundamentally changing the economics of NextBoat, how we buy, how we sell and every touch point in between. I believe the progress made over the past 30 days represents an important turning point for the company. We have strengthened the leadership team, raised additional capital, renegotiated unfavorable economic arrangements and developed a much clearer strategy for the future. Our objective is to make NextBoat the most efficient buyer and seller of boats in the country.

I would like to spend a few minutes adding some color to the financial results before opening up to questions. I would like to make it very clear. We are now concentrating on margin expansion. We feel very comfortable that we can continue to grow this company. We've got great control over marketing, technology and all the things that we do. But now it's most important to work on all the costs associated with buying and selling boats. I would like to start with the pre-owned sales. As Chad pointed out, we saw 80% revenue growth on the pre-owned side and 98% unit growth.

I think it's important to understand that in the business we're in, revenue growth can be skewed by very large transactions. And so we look very closely at the unit growth. As we move forward, we've also identified that the best boats for us tend to be boats in the $500,000 range and below. We have the best margins in those boats. We have the most potential buyers of those boats, and we seem to have a much clearer understanding of what that market looks like. And so you will see us working very hard to grow that category over time.

And so I would call out that over the next several quarters, starting with our K, we're going to want to pay close attention to unit growth because we believe we can tremendously grow that space, which will add to margin contribution. So how are we going to grow sales? So the biggest part of our strategy is getting back to cutting costs. Today, how we buy boats, what we pay to transport a boat, to store a boat, the insurance, the survey, the selling costs, all are well over what they should be.

And for us to be able to close more transactions, we've got to cut those costs, which would allow us to push more margin, but to also allow us to bid more for a boat and increase our conversion rate on those transactions. Our goal is simple, to become the market for the wholesale side of the business when you want to sell a boat to know that NextBoat is a destination because you're going to get the best offer for your boat. To also accomplish that, that's going to require getting better on how we sell boats.

I truly believe that over the last 6 to 9 months, because of all the acquisitions and the growth since going public, the company got derailed somewhat from the management of its core business, which is buying and selling boats. The addition of Apex, the addition of Bellhart, the launching of AYG, all very important businesses moving forward. But all the effort it took to incorporate those into the company really had an impact on the management of the inventory of owned boats that we had and the process and how we liquidated those boats.

We have already implemented plans that manage that inventory on a daily basis and get much more aggressive in our markdown strategies to ultimately contribute the largest margin. I'll give you an example of how this can really impact the business going forward. In the first 9 months of the year, 6 of our boats made up the biggest losses we took in the entire company. And those losses added up to around $1.5 million. Of those losses, the boats that we purchased, the price of the purchase compared to the price of the sale was breakeven. The average days owned was approaching 1 year. It was over 250 days.

Had we been more aggressive in how we manage that inventory during that time, we could have cut that loss to either 0, potentially made a profit, but a worst-case scenario would have lost a couple of hundred grand instead of $1.5 million. So the impact of better managing inventory and having a better process and how we liquidate the boats will have a big impact on our long-term growth and profitability. I want to touch on Azure for a minute. I think you've heard in previous calls that Azure is an important part of the business. It's the part that is finance, insurance and warranty.

Without proper integration of that into your overall strategy, it's very hard to be a great successful business because we need those revenues and more importantly, we need the earnings and the margin from that. In the past, Azure has kind of run on its own. And although there has been some efforts to try to incorporate that into the overall company at NextBoat, it hasn't been that successful. Well, those days have changed as well. As of the last few weeks, we are now incorporating every transaction to go through Azure so that we can capitalize on finance insurance and warranty. It's part of our closing process and now should have a huge impact.

And even though it grew 97% year-over-year, we think that number could be much, much higher when it's properly integrated. And then finally, I want to touch on service. Service is a very important component of the business as well. It's a high-margin business. But for us, the key to service is actually controlling the work done on our own boats. It's shortening the time it takes to fix a boat. It's keeping the cost down on fixing those boats. The locations offer another place to store boats at a lower price. And it really gives us an opportunity to improve our pricing long term.

And then finally, as it relates to that, the service plays a key component in customer retention. We're going to make sure that every client that we deal with that buys a pre-owned boat for us gets a service opportunity, a service contract. And so we can stay in touch with them to try to make sure they come back to us for their next transaction. And so with that, I'm going to open it up to questions. And if anybody has a question, please raise your hand, and I'll try to address it the best I can.

With that being said, I believe today, that average ownership on the pre-owned boats is around 88 days. And as I keep touching on this whole, we've got to impact buying, selling and all the things in between, let me give you like one example of how we can impact that. So when we buy a boat, we contract with the client and then we go through a process of closing on that boat. Historically, we've made that transaction, made that deal, and then it's taken, we call it, a couple of weeks to close, and we're waiting until we close to start doing all the important things like taking photos and detailing and getting listed and selling that boat.

Internally, we've looked at and said, we believe that we can push out the closing date a little bit more, whether it's another week or 2. And during that time, be doing all the things that we would have done after close, which is photograph the boat, clean it again and get it marketed through our internal database. And so we think we can pick up weeks on the front end by just incorporating that. It's kind of the same thing on the back end. The company has also had so many transactions going on that the closing team has been somewhat strapped and it's pushed closings on the sell side out weeks longer than it was necessary.

And so every day that we go past the date that, that deal should close, it costs us money for storage. It costs us money for interest on all those things. And so -- we feel like we can take the 88 days down to some number even into the 60s just by that simple process on the closing side and the buy and sell side. And then the other thing is we had a great addition of Alex Dean to the team, which was announced this week, who's our COO. Alex worked very closely with me, both at my original company and Fanatics.

I don't know anybody stronger than Alex when it comes to operational efficiencies, and Alex has hit the ground running. And every single touch point, we see opportunity to improve that. We're analyzing all the offers that are coming in, and we're making sure that we're being aggressive in trying to turn boats.

We believe that we can -- by cutting all these costs, we can still increase our margins by reducing the price we sell if necessary sooner because it cuts down all the additional costs like I keep touching on, like interest and storage and everything else. And so of all the things that we're going to do over the next, call it, 120 days to 6 months, I believe you're going to see the biggest impact on the cost of carrying these boats, and you're going to see the turns come down and you're going to see a much better return on those boats because of that.

The other part of the floor plan comes from the new boats that we took on. And so the new boats, which are both at Apex and at Bellhart are going to be a little more seasonal. We're down here in Florida where I reside, it's not really a big part of the boat season in the summer as most of the snowbirds go away. But now through, let's call it, May is really boating season here.

And I think you'll see that floor plan come down pretty dramatically as we sell through those boats. At the Bellhart side, which is up in North Carolina, it's kind of the opposite. They just ended their boating season not too long ago, and their boat season will kick up again around, just after the first of the year. And so it will be a little slow now and that floor plan might increase or stay where it is, you'll see that come down pretty dramatically during the boating season up in the Northeast.

The bigger challenge right now that exists and the reason for the addition of an Alex Dean on the COO side and other additions we're making internally is we don't want to grow too fast before we can build the infrastructure below that revenue to support it and offer a good customer experience and make sure it's profitable.
