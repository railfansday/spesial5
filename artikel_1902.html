Tottenham to learn from mistake as Williams-Barnett lined up for 'progressive contract' - ex-Spurs employee

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Tottenham Hotspur will want to learn from the way Dele Alli's career unfolded when they hand Luca Williams-Barnett a new deal.

That is according to former senior Tottenham scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The teenager marked his full Premier League debut on Saturday with a goal and an assist as the Lilywhites beat Ipswich Town 2-0.

Williams-Barnett has rapidly emerged as one of the most exciting young talents in England, and will reportedly put pen to paper on a new long-term contract at Tottenham.

Clubs across Europe had been watching the teenager's contract situation progress, with his current terms set to expire in 2028.

But with Roberto De Zerbi now having made the youngster a key part of his team, it appears unlikely that he is going anywhere anytime fast.

Alli, 19 at the time, joined Tottenham from MK Dons in 2015 and was an instant success, enjoying three incredible seasons.

But troubles off the pitch eventually caught up with the midfielder, who struggled with being thrust into the limelight.

"If Spurs weren't in the contract negotiations with this kid, then there'd be something dramatically wrong at that football club, because you're going to want to tie him down until at least 2030," King told Football Insider when asked about Williams-Barnett.

"I think a progressive contract would certainly be an incentive for the club, and certainly the boy. It's no good giving him a massive contract now.

"I think that's what's been wrong with clubs and young players. I think one of the things to look at would be Dele Alli, who came in, a wonderful young player, and first-team player. Played for England. And look where he is now.

"He's struggling to find a football club. He's doing podcasts. He's obviously earning a lot of money, so unless he's been absolutely stupid, I would imagine he's got decent savings and whatever, but you've got to be very careful with young players.

"To give them the whole carrot is not a wise move. You've got to have an incentive in there for them. His future will be fine because if he does get offered a four-year contract, then it will be a situation where, as he develops, it will be relevant to his performances and he will be getting very well paid."

In a 2023 interview, Alli opened up on his struggles with mental health and addiction after being sexually abused as a young boy.

Football gave him an escape, but the 30-year-old was keen to emphasise just how difficult it can be.

"Don’t get me wrong, I love football, it saved my life, I owe everything to football – but it’s not just as easy as everyone thinks it is," he said. "

"It’s not this high life. Yes, you have money, you can do a lot of things that you wouldn’t be able to do without it, but mentally, I don’t think people will ever understand until you’re in it, what it can do to you.

"Rejection, just being told you’re not good enough, fighting every day. Even something like losing a game, it can affect you mentally, and you have to be ready, you have to be smiling the next day. When you’re not, it’s a problem.”
