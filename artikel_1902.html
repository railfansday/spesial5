Didi Hamann has been talking about Newcastle United.

Didi Hamann signed for Newcastle United in 1998 from Bayern Munich.

Kenny Dalglish the United manager who signed him for a reported £5.5m fee after the 1998 World Cup finals where he’d played for Germany.

Didi Hamann now giving his opinion on Newcastle United’s future stadium plans…

The former player turned pundit asked by Lottoland Live Casino: ‘Newcastle United are reportedly yet to decide whether to renovate or expand St James’ Park or build a completely new stadium. Do Newcastle United need a completely new stadium?’

Didi Hamann replying: “No, no, you can’t leave St James’ Park. Obviously, I was fortunate and lucky to be able to play there for one season. I think it’s one of the best grounds in the league. With the extension they had, I think they get 51,000 [ED: 52k+ capacity now], and they probably can fit a few more in. If they can extend it, I think they’ve got to do everything to keep it. It’s right in the middle of the city. I think it’s one of the best, one of the most iconic stadia in the world. And I think this is why it would be majorly important to keep St James’. Don’t leave St James’ Park, please.”

It is embarrassing that Didi Hamann is passing comment on this, considering how he was so desperate to leave St James’ Park less than a year after arriving and move to Liverpool.

The German international only started 20 Premier League matches for Newcastle United before forcing his way out of the club. In total he started 30 competitive games for United, plus one sub appearance. So fair to say he will have only started around 15 games in total at St James’ Park in a Newcastle United shirt.

The other thing when you are commenting on a club you don’t really care about, is that you also know precious little about the subject matter. If Didi Hamann had done his research then he would have known that there are zero plans to move the home of Newcastle United out of the city centre. That if indeed there is a new stadium built, then it will go up on a site no more than hundreds of yards away, very possibly even partly on the current St James’ Park site.

The former (very short-term!) United star also asked: ‘Newcastle and Liverpool are both known for their incredible atmospheres. Having played at both grounds, which stadium do you think has the better atmosphere and where did you enjoy playing more?’

Didi Hamann: “It’s very hard to compare because we didn’t play in Europe with Newcastle. Obviously, European nights at Anfield have always been special. But I’ve got to say, to walk out the first time at St James’ was just electric. When the music comes on, when you walk out and you see in my time, I think it was about 40, 45,000 Geordies [Ed: SJP capacity was 36k+ then] there. It was just fantastic. At the same time, I was in awe when I played with Newcastle the first time. I think it was Boxing Day that season in 1998 when we played Liverpool. When you stand in the tunnel there before it got redone. I think they are two of the best stadia in England. I’d go Premier League games at Newcastle, European nights at Anfield.”
