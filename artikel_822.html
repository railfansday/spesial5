French football head Philippe Diallo is shock contender to fight Infantino for Fifa job

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Diallo may have candidacy confirmed by Uefa on Tuesday

Uefa executive committee meeting to discuss matter

The French Football Federation president, Philippe Diallo, is a surprise late contender to run for the Fifa presidency and could have his candidacy confirmed by Tuesday. The search for a Uefa-backed candidate to run against Gianni Infantino has been taken to the wire, with Wednesday the deadline for those planning to contest the Fifa election in March.

Diallo has emerged as the favourite after plans to enlist a decorated former player or manager did not reach fruition, although other names are understood to remain under consideration. Uefa’s executive committee will discuss the matter on Monday and the approval of its 55 member associations would be sought.

Diallo strongly criticised Infantino in September, saying he had “seriously damaged the image and unity of football”. He continued: “His initiatives during and after the World Cup have seriously damaged the image and the unity of football. As a result, we believe that trust has been broken.”

It is unclear whether a move to back Diallo would spell the end of the campaign begun by Kozo Tashima, the honorary president of the Japanese Football Association, to challenge Infantino. Tashima, a Fifa council member, has influential backers within Uefa but its hierarchy may conclude Diallo has a greater chance of wooing voters in other areas of the world.

Diallo would be expected to win the backing of the vast majority of Uefa’s member associations and there are hopes he could pick up votes from Francophone members of the Confederation of African Football, which has been steadfast in its support for Infantino.

There are concerns that having multiple challengers to Infantino will strengthen his position and play into the hands of a campaign that will present the incumbent as the unity candidate after months of in-fighting.

Concacaf, the North and Central American and Caribbean confederation, has been reluctant to endorse an alternative candidate, despite opposing Infantino, with some federations arguing that a boycott of the election would send the strongest message regarding the need for governance reform.

Should a career administrator such as Diallo be granted Uefa’s backing it would be a marked departure from its original approach, in which it sought to coalesce behind a more high-profile figure who had made their name on the pitch or in the dugout. There had been late hope that Arsène Wenger, Fifa’s chief of global development, would throw put himself forward, but it would have required a major change of heart from him. Germany’s head coach, Jürgen Klopp, has said he would welcome a new president. “The general consensus is that a change might make sense. But what would interest me even more in this context, from a football perspective, is that we never thought things could get any worse after Sepp Blatter, and yet someone actually managed to make it worse.

“Right now, we’re thinking that if Mr Infantino were to leave, it would be cool if the next person could just do a better job. Because I believe it should be possible to do better.”
