Coachella Sues Walmart for Selling Knockoffs at Stores Near Festival: ‘Who Needs the Merch Tent?’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The lawsuit claims Walmart has been infringing Coachella's trademarks for years at stores near the Southern California festival grounds.

Coachella is suing Walmart over accusations that the retail giant has sold fake festival merchandise for years despite repeated demands to stop.

In a lawsuit Tuesday (Nov. 24), the owners of the festival accuse Walmart of infringing Coachella’s trademarks by plastering the name and logo on shirts, tumblers and other items, often at stores located near the Empire Polo Club grounds where the yearly festival takes place.

Coachella’s attorneys, representing the AEG unit Goldenvoice that runs the festival, say they’ve been asking Walmart to stop since as far back as 2017 to no avail, leaving the festival “with no option but to bring the present lawsuit.”

“Plaintiffs have made repeated requests and demands for Walmart to cease selling and advertising infringing goods,” Coachella’s lawyers write. “Plaintiffs’ requests have been of minimal benefit; Walmart typically stops for a period of time, but then resumes.”

The case claims Walmart is targeting festivalgoers at nearby stores, citing knockoff merch at locations in La Quinta, Calif., and North Indio, Calif., both of which are a short drive away. It cites the La Quinta Walmart selling coolers and folding chairs under a direct copy of the festival’s logo, as well as an in-store display featuring a miniature version of the Ferris wheel that had been created by Coachella sponsor Absolut Vodka.

Coachella’s lawyers say fans and others have clearly taken notice. In an Instagram post cited in the lawsuit, the local Palm Springs newspaper The Desert Sun posted a video of knockoff Coachella tumblers and tank tops with the caption “Who needs the merch tent when you have the North Indio Walmart?”

Goldenvoice is no stranger to trademark litigation. It sued Urban Outfitters in 2017 for using “Coachella” in apparel names and ecommerce metadata, and a Los Angeles festival called “Hoodchella” before that. In 2022, it filed another case against concert giant Live Nation for promoting a nearby event called “Coachella Day One 22.” And in 2024, it sued a local craft beer company called Coachella Valley Brewing Co. for using the Ferris wheel in branding.

Neither side immediately returned requests for comment on Wednesday (Nov. 25).
