Steelers could try to not pay Aaron Rodgers the balance of his $22 million salary

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

If/when the Steelers release quarterback Aaron Rodgers, they’ll have to decide whether to willingly pay the balance of his $22 million base salary, or whether to fight.

With his Week 12 paycheck already vested, the question becomes Week 13 through Week 18. Six weeks, totaling $7.33 million.

It won’t be easy to cut him off. First, the Steelers would have to release him for reasons unrelated to skill, injury, or salary cap. The argument, if the Steelers make it, would be that Rodgers’s appearance on Pat McAfee’s show constitutes personal conduct detrimental to the interests of the team.

A grievance would undoubtedly be filed, if the Steelers pursue that approach.

But even without the guarantee, Rodgers’s presence on the Week 1 roster qualifies him for the balance of his base salary as “termination pay” under the Collective Bargaining Agreement. To circumvent that, the Steelers would have to show that Rodgers has become ineligible for termination pay.

Here’s the key language, from Article 30, Section 3(b) of the CBA: “An otherwise qualified player will not be entitled to Termination Pay if (i) the Club terminated the player’s contract for Conduct Detrimental to the Club, and (ii) the Non-Injury Grievance Arbitrator determines that the player’s conduct was so severe that the maximum discipline for Conduct Detrimental of a fine equal to one week’s salary and suspension without pay for four weeks, pursuant to Article 42, Section 1(b)(xvii) of this Agreement (regardless of whether the Club imposed any such discipline prior to terminating the player’s contract), constitutes an inadequate remedy for the player’s conduct.”

It won’t be easy to satisfy that standard. But there’s language in the CBA that potentially applies to Rodgers’s public comments from Wednesday.

Here’s Article 6, titled “Public Statements": “The NFLPA and the Management Council agree that each will use reasonable efforts to curtail public comments by Club personnel or players which express criticism of any club, its coach, or its operation and policy, or which tend to cast discredit upon a Club, a player, or any other person involved in the operation of a Club, the NFL, the Management Council, or the NFLPA.”

That’s the same clause that fueled the NFL’s attack against the NFL Players Association’s report cards. The process constitutes comments from the NFLPA that express criticism of the teams, their coaches, the operations, and their policies.

None of this means the Steelers will attempt to cut off his final $7.33 million. But it’s an option. And it’s something to be considered as the Steelers try to figure out the next move.
