International Men’s Day: Anthropic IPOs, Trump Meets Putin and Xi, and GTA 6 Is Released

November 19, 2026. Three of the world’s most powerful men meet to discuss nuclear weapons. An artificial intelligence company raises more money in a single IPO than most countries spend on technology. And millions of men finally get to steal fictional cars in Vice City.

An ⁠excellent Venn diagram circulating on X captured the extraordinary coincidence of three events occurring on November 19, 2026.

Someone in our HackerNoon Slack channel pointed out the final coincidence: it’s International Men’s Day.

The joke is funny enough on its own. But the more you investigate the actual news, the stranger the connections become.

Elon Musk thinks AI might eventually generate games like GTA without traditional developers. Anthropic’s CEO believes advanced AI chips should be kept away from China. Trump thinks whoever wins the race to superintelligence wins everything. Meanwhile, the leaders of the world’s largest nuclear powers are discussing how not to destroy civilization.

And Rockstar Games has just released a video game about committing crimes.

Let’s examine this particularly remarkable day in the history of men.

On November 18, Anthropic announced that it had ⁠priced its initial public offering at $83 per share.

The offering comprised 980 million shares, representing approximately $81.34 billion in gross proceeds before expenses and excluding any additional shares sold through the underwriters’ option.

Not all of that money goes to Anthropic. Approximately 23.6 million shares were being sold by existing stockholders.

Nevertheless, it was an extraordinary amount of money.

When Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on November 19, shares opened at $111.50.

That’s approximately 34% above the IPO price.

As ⁠Barron’s reported when trading began, investors who received shares at the offering price immediately found themselves holding something worth considerably more.

The $83 price also happens to be almost identical to the price of GTA VI.

For approximately $80, Rockstar gives you a fictional world where you can do almost anything.

For $83, Anthropic gives eligible IPO investors a tiny ownership stake in a company trying to build artificial intelligence that might eventually do almost anything.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has spent years arguing that increasingly powerful artificial intelligence could fundamentally transform society.

Now public investors get to bet on that argument.

In an ⁠interview following the IPO, Amodei reflected on how rapidly the technology’s economic implications had become real:

“I don’t think we really believed it until it actually happened.”

His sister, Anthropic cofounder and president Daniela Amodei, characterized the IPO as another milestone rather than the destination:

“it’s like one stop on the journey, right?”

The company has also warned investors that its commitments to AI safety may sometimes conflict with maximizing profits.

What happens when shareholders want Anthropic to make more money, but Anthropic’s leadership believes making more money would create unacceptable risks?

Senator Elizabeth Warren has already raised concerns about exposing investors and retirement savings to the risks surrounding advanced AI.

In a statement ahead of the offering, she argued:

“The SEC must protect investors and Congress must act quickly to put in place safeguards on advanced AI development.”

The broader contradiction is difficult to ignore.

Anthropic was founded partly because its creators believed advanced AI required extraordinary caution.

Now it is participating in one of the largest financial spectacles in stock-market history.

Apparently, protecting humanity is extremely capital-intensive.

While Anthropic was making financial history in New York, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping, and Vladimir Putin were making diplomatic history in Shenzhen, China.

The three leaders held an unprecedented trilateral summit on November 19 during the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation gathering.

According to ⁠Reuters’ account of the meeting, nuclear weapons and the war in Ukraine were among the major subjects.

Xi described the gathering as a historic opportunity for cooperation.

Putin emphasized that the three countries shared interests in resolving regional conflicts, including Ukraine.

There is something extraordinary about three men collectively representing such enormous military and economic power sitting around a table to discuss whether they can trust one another.

Especially when you read what they actually said.

During the summit, Trump offered a remarkably straightforward explanation of why nuclear disarmament is difficult.

According to the ⁠published transcript of his remarks:

“Once you have the nuclear weapons, it’s very hard to not have them.”

“It’s very hard to give them up as the leader of a country.”

They are also the sort of statements that sound like a man explaining why he still has a garage full of things he purchased fifteen years ago.

Except the garage contains nuclear warheads.

Trump also praised Shenzhen, telling the gathering:

“They have a major city, one of the biggest cities anywhere in the world, and it’s flawless.”

The president of the United States was discussing global security while also delivering what sounded like a five-star hotel review.

Before the trilateral meeting, Trump greeted Putin warmly.

According to ⁠ABC News’ coverage, Trump referred to the Russian president as:

He also praised their longstanding relationship.

That friendship is politically consequential, given Russia’s continuing war against Ukraine and the enormous disagreements between Washington and Moscow.

Trump’s remarks illustrate the central tension of the summit: personal relationships between leaders are being presented as a potential route toward resolving conflicts involving millions of people.

Whether those relationships produce meaningful agreements remains another question.

The leaders endorsed a declaration emphasizing the importance of reducing nuclear risks.

“a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought”

It is also a principle the major nuclear powers have endorsed before.

The declaration did not establish a specific schedule for eliminating nuclear weapons, end the war in Ukraine, or resolve the major strategic disagreements among the three countries.

As ⁠the Associated Press observed in its analysis of the summit, the meeting’s symbolism appeared more substantial than its immediate policy achievements.

The world’s most powerful men agreed that peace is good.

Humanity will now wait to see what they do about it.

The overlap between Anthropic’s IPO and the international summit is arguably the most important part of the meme.

Advanced artificial intelligence requires enormous computing resources.

Those resources depend heavily on sophisticated semiconductors, including Nvidia’s most advanced chips.

And access to those chips has become a central dispute between the United States and China.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has been particularly outspoken about this issue.

In his ⁠essay about DeepSeek and American export controls, Amodei argued:

“Export controls serve a vital purpose: keeping democratic nations at the forefront of AI development.”

His argument is that restricting access to advanced computing hardware can help preserve America’s technological advantage.

China, unsurprisingly, sees restrictions on access to advanced technology rather differently.

During his visit to China, Trump discussed the competition to develop superintelligence.

His summary was characteristically direct:

SI refers to superintelligence: artificial intelligence that exceeds human capabilities across a broad range of intellectual tasks.

Trump’s statement captures the geopolitical stakes in six words.

The economy? Military superiority? Scientific progress? Control over the future?

At APEC, Trump also rejected international restrictions that could constrain American AI development.

As ⁠ABC News reported, Trump opposed what he called globalist regulation of advanced AI.

Meanwhile, Xi emphasized international cooperation and human oversight.

The Chinese government’s account of his position called for countries to:

“ensure that AI is always under human control”

These competing positions reveal a remarkable situation.

The United States wants to maintain its lead in artificial intelligence.

China wants greater access to advanced computing and a meaningful role in setting global rules.

Anthropic wants to develop increasingly capable AI while maintaining its safety commitments.

And investors want all of these companies to make money.

These objectives are not necessarily compatible.

Yet they are increasingly shaping the same global economy.

While world leaders were discussing nuclear weapons and investors were buying shares in Anthropic, Rockstar Games finally released Grand Theft Auto VI.

The company confirmed the launch in its ⁠official November 19 announcement.

The game is available on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

Its standard edition costs $79.99 in the United States.

That is $3.01 less than Anthropic’s IPO price.

Unlike an Anthropic share, however, the game comes with an entire fictional criminal economy.

Players return to Vice City and the fictional state of Leonida, following the story of Jason and Lucia.

It has been more than thirteen years since Grand Theft Auto V arrived in September 2013.

Rockstar delayed GTA VI several times before settling on November 19, 2026.

In its ⁠final major delay announcement, the company explained:

“these extra months will allow us to finish the game with the level of polish you have come to expect and deserve.”

Apparently, building a realistic simulation of American criminality takes longer than building some forms of artificial intelligence.

Rockstar’s parent company, Take-Two Interactive, is publicly traded.

Its CEO, Strauss Zelnick, has repeatedly emphasized the enormous commercial significance of GTA VI.

“a lot of people will be calling in sick on November 19”

That is a remarkable prediction from the CEO of a publicly traded company.

Imagine Anthropic’s CEO announcing that the company’s next AI model would cause millions of employees to stop showing up for work.

Actually, that’s not too far from what some people in the AI industry have been predicting.

Take-Two previously forecast approximately $8 billion to $8.2 billion in net bookings for fiscal 2027.

Those figures cover the company’s broader business, not just GTA VI.

Nevertheless, they demonstrate the scale of the expectations surrounding the launch.

The connections become even stranger when Elon Musk enters the story.

Musk has repeatedly argued that generative artificial intelligence will eventually transform video game development.

Instead of spending years and billions of dollars building a game, developers might eventually describe the world they want and let AI generate it.

In January 2026, Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch suggested:

“There’s definitely a non-zero chance we get ‘generate your own GTA 6 in a few minutes’ before GTA 6.”

Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney also entertained the possibility of AI-generated games.

The broader discussion concerned whether systems capable of generating images, video, and software could eventually generate entire interactive worlds.

That possibility would fundamentally change the economics of the video game industry.

Rockstar has spent more than a decade building GTA VI.

An AI system that could generate comparable experiences in minutes would represent an extraordinary technological achievement.

But generating a playable environment is not the same as creating a coherent, polished game with compelling characters, sophisticated systems, and a consistent artistic vision.

GTA VI is a particularly useful test case because its value depends on more than the number of buildings, vehicles, or characters an AI could generate.

It depends on how all those pieces work together.

Zelnick has also challenged sweeping claims about artificial intelligence replacing human workers.

Responding to Musk’s predictions about automation, Zelnick reportedly asked:

“If AI was going to take anyone’s job, wouldn’t it take his job?”

“Maybe Elon Musk is indeed a simulation, it’s possible.”

The exchange illustrates a disagreement that extends well beyond video games.

Musk believes increasingly capable AI could dramatically reduce the need for human labor.

Zelnick runs a company whose most important product depends on thousands of people coordinating their creative and technical work over many years.

Both men have enormous financial interests in their respective visions of the future.

And both are discussing a technology that could transform the businesses they operate.

On International Men’s Day, the argument is particularly appropriate.

One billionaire thinks AI might eventually generate the work of another billionaire’s employees.

The other billionaire wonders why the first billionaire still needs to work.

There is a deeper connection between Anthropic and GTA VI.

Both companies are building systems that simulate aspects of the world.

Rockstar builds a fictional environment with characters, vehicles, economies, police departments, and social systems.

Anthropic develops AI systems designed to understand and interact with the real world through language, software, and other tools.

GTA VI’s characters exist inside a fictional world.

Claude’s outputs can influence decisions outside the computer.

But both businesses depend on the ability to represent increasingly complex aspects of human behavior.

The comparison becomes more interesting when considering the economics.

Rockstar spends years developing a world that millions of people will pay to enter.

Anthropic spends billions developing intelligence that businesses hope will make work more efficient.

Rockstar wants people to spend more time inside its product.

Anthropic’s customers often want to spend less time completing tasks.

The other sells a tool for changing reality.

And on November 19, 2026, both are asking consumers and investors to pay approximately $80 for a piece of the future.

The final overlap in the Venn diagram reads:

It is attached to GTA VI and the Trump–Xi–Putin summit.

Grand Theft Auto is a video game built around criminal activity, reckless driving, violence, and increasingly elaborate ways to behave badly.

International diplomacy, meanwhile, involves leaders making decisions with consequences for millions or billions of people.

The difference is that GTA VI has a reset button.

There is also a revealing contrast in how the two events are evaluated.

Rockstar’s success will be measured through sales, reviews, player engagement, and the quality of its game.

The summit’s success will be measured through changes in international relations, nuclear risks, military conflicts, and economic cooperation.

Rockstar can release a patch when something goes wrong.

Perhaps the most absurd part of the day is that the fictional criminal world might offer a more predictable set of rules than the real geopolitical one.

The observance was established to recognize positive male role models, celebrate men’s contributions to families and communities, and raise awareness of issues affecting men’s health and well-being.

Its stated objectives include recognizing:

“everyday, working-class men who are living decent, honest lives.”

Those men are largely absent from the Venn diagram.

There are no circles for fathers taking their children to school.

No circles for men caring for aging parents.

No circles for teachers, nurses, construction workers, or friends helping one another through difficult times.

Instead, the world’s attention is focused on billionaires, presidents, nuclear weapons, artificial intelligence, and a video game about stealing cars.

There is something appropriate about that.

International Men’s Day is partly about recognizing the positive things men contribute to society.

The day’s biggest headlines offer an unusually vivid illustration of how much power men still exercise over its future.

Some of that power produces extraordinary technological achievements.

Some produces entertainment enjoyed by millions.

And some creates risks that everyone else must live with.

The International Men’s Day organizers have emphasized that improving men’s lives benefits society more broadly.

That is a more useful principle than treating masculinity as a competition to accumulate the most money, military power, or technological influence.

There are also important women in these stories.

Anthropic cofounder Daniela Amodei helped lead the company to its public-market debut.

GTA VI features Lucia Caminos as one of its two protagonists.

And the consequences of decisions made by the three presidents in Shenzhen will affect women and men alike.

The Venn diagram is funny because it compresses three enormous stories into one ridiculous coincidence.

But the stories are connected by something more consequential than their shared date.

They are all about who gets to control the future.

Anthropic wants to build increasingly powerful artificial intelligence.

Trump, Xi, and Putin want to shape the international rules under which that technology develops.

Rockstar wants to create a fictional world so compelling that millions of people will voluntarily disappear into it.

And ordinary people are left trying to figure out what all of this means for their lives.

And if he doesn’t answer, don’t rule out Vice City.
