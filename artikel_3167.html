Netflix Only Big Player Not Funding UK’s Independent Bullying & Harassment Complaints Body

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Netflix is the only major industry player not currently funding the UK’s anonymous whistleblowing service for complaints about bullying and harassment, it has been revealed.

The Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority (CIISA) has released its long-expected list of funders and the streamer’s absence is notable, along with a number of talent agencies.

Last year, a Deadline analysis found that Channel 4, Disney and Amazon were among those refusing to pay annual CIISA fees, while at the time Netflix and Skydance (then Paramount) declined to confirm if they were providing support. UK Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy, a vocal backer of CIISA, has also previously chided MasterChef producer Banijay for the same thing. Today’s register shows Channel 4, Disney, Amazon, Skydance and Banijay are now all paying, along with other production groups and major broadcasters plus Sky. All have pledged between one to three years of financial support from April 2026. The register, which is now publicly available and was unveiled at a CIISA event in parliament, will heap pressure on Netflix. Deadline understands the streamer did fund CIISA during its early phase, has shared best practice with the body and could provide funding in the future. “We are supportive of CIISA and we are actively exploring working together,” a Netflix spokeswoman said. The news comes just a few weeks after Netflix released a Russell Brand documentary titled Russell Brand: Open Secret, after the Forgetting Sarah Marshall star was sentenced to 10 years in prison for rape and sexual assault. The doc’s producer, 72 Films, is funding CIISA. Its premiere preceded the allegations of sexually exploitative behavior against David Walliams — allegations that Walliams vehemently denies — placing bad behavior in UK entertainment in the spotlight.

UK agencies like Curtis Brown, Casarotto Ramsay & Associates, United Agents and YMU are not on the CIISA list. YMU repped Walliams until the start of this year. Independent Talent Group, which repped Walliams until a few weeks ago, is one of the few talent agencies funding CIISA.

CIISA received 90 anonymous reports between its June launch and November 20, the body said today. Notably, we understand nearly half of these reports came during the two weeks after Brand was convicted and the Walliams allegations were published.

Emerging from the Time’s Up UK movement and Noel Clarke allegations, CIISA has taken years to get up and running but is now a fully-fledged indepedent whistleblowing service. As an officially designated whistleblowing body, people can anonymously report concerns to CIISA relating to harassment, discrimination, victimisation and other harmful practices. CIISA can then signpost help, make lawful refererals, generate reports and identify issues, themes and patterns.

In parliament today, CIISA gathered bigwigs like Nandy, BBC chair Samir Shah, ex-BBC Director General Tim Davie and Zelda Perkins, the Harvey Weinstein victim who campaigns on outlawing NDAs. In a discussion chaired by Sky News Presenter Cathy Newman, attendees discussed ongoing challenges within the creative industries and efforts to drive improvements in workplace culture.

CIISA boss Jen Smith said: “CIISA has a pivotal role to play in preventing harm and closing accountability gaps in the creative industry infrastructure, offering a unique opportunity to identify harmful patterns of behaviour across different projects. We thank the organisations who have already pledged their support for this important work and we will continue to work alongside industry and government to ensure that CIISA is adequately resourced to offer a safe, confidential route for people to seek independent advice and report concerns, and to embed the consistent cross-sector standards that will drive lasting change.”
