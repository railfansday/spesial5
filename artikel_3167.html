Tesla Just Started Selling Cybercabs, Here's What It Means for a SpaceX and Tesla Merger .

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Only a hefty cash premium is likely to satisfy most Tesla investors in the event of a merger, and that premium is likely to rise with a successful robotaxi ramp-up.

By Lee Samaha – Nov 22, 2026 at 2:05AM EST

The news that Tesla(TSLA -7.64%)is inviting select customers to buy Cybercabs isn't a game changer from a financial perspective (at least not immediately). Still, it is highly symbolic and highlights the case for Tesla shareholders holding fire on agreeing to a potential merger with Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%) (or SpaceX for short). Here's why.

In itself, buying a Cybercab right now is hardly an attractive option for most people. They are reported to cost $29,990, but they can't be used without a $299 monthly subscription, they lack manual driving controls (a steering wheel, pedals, etc.), and they can only operate in the limited geo-fenced areas where the robotaxi service currently operates.

However, they will obviously attract Tesla enthusiasts, social media influencers, and those confident in widespread regulatory approvals for Tesla's robotaxi service. To be clear, as management is, it's focused on becoming a transportation-as-a-service company with the "vast majority of the Cybercab stuff will be essentially Tesla-owned and Transport-as-a-Service," according to CEO Elon Musk.

The reality is their sale is more about generating promotional buzz around robotaxis as the company prepares for a widespread expansion in 2027. Fortunately, everything points to a quantum leap in the robotaxi rollout next year. The company continues to develop its next major upgrade of its full self-driving software, namely v15, which should be validated and released by early 2027.

Also, Cybercab production is ramping up, and Tesla says it's gathering data on unsupervised miles with its robotaxi at a double-digit weekly growth rate.

After years of delays and missed timelines, everything points to Tesla finally releasing value from its robotaxi service next year. At which point, its investors are entitled to ask whether it would make sense to agree to a merger with SpaceX before the robotaxi ramps up.

While an offer is not on the table, and investors don't know the terms of a deal, or whether there will even be one, it's hard not to think that a robotaxi ramp-up in 2027 would increase Tesla's share price.

As such, if you believe Tesla is undervalued right now, then anything other than a significant cash premium from SpaceX would make sense.
