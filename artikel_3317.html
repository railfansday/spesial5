How A “Mysterious” Message Kickstarted Aardman’s Gamechanging Pact With Pokémon

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It was a “mysterious” message that kickstarted Aardman’s once-in-a-generation team-up with Pokémon.

The celebrated stop motion outfit’s creative chief talked a London audience through the inception of Pokémon Tales: The Misadventures of Sirfetch’d & Pichu, which lands next year on Disney+. The pact represents Aardman’s first time taking its stop motion approach to a third party brand.

Pokémon gave Aardman carte blanche to do what it wanted with the characters. The result, Pokémon Tales, places its heroes front and centre in a region Trainers have explored before, Aardman says. Watch on Deadline

“The first conversation we had was not about the project or animation, it was about our values and the value of wanting to deliver really good meaningful content to our audiences,” said Cox. “We want strong characters, strong humor and a commitment to the quality of what we do. We talked for a while and the project emerged from those discussions.”

Pokémon Tales launches next year on Disney+ via the Mouse House’s partnership with Pokémon.

Aardman MD Sean Clarke said “taking on other people’s brands” will help the 50-year-old, employee-owned outfit navigate an independent production landscape that is struggling, especially when Aardman prides itself on protecting its “integrity” by only releasing Wallace & Gromit movies once every five years or so.

Another way in which Aardman is doing this is by embracing TikTok, with a bespoke Shaun the Sheep video going viral earlier this week that poked fun at James Bond.

“If we are here three years from now I would hope we have launched new characters doing funny things on TikTok so we can then go to market,” added Clarke. “At the moment you go to market to try and raise money and it’s all prequels, sequels and remakes. So this gives you a different ability to raise money.”

Aardman may be embracing TikTok but it is incredibly wary of AI. “When I look at AI it all looks the same because it’s learning from stuff that already exists,” added Clarke. “Why would we want to change what we do?”

Elsewhere, Clarke echoed concerns raised earlier today by culture minister Ian Murray that the proposed U.S. federal film and TV tax incentive will negatively impact UK production. According to Clarke, there were zero independent British animations launched last year, compared to around 10 in France and 15 in Germany.

Clarke added: “The bulk of the [UK] film and TV industry is making things for others and when you have Americans saying, ‘Maybe we should look at our own tax credit,’ then you have a real issue with less things being made in this country versus having an ecosystem to retain rights.”

Earlier, Murray said the UK government is taking the implications of the proposed credit “incredibly seriously.”

The Aardman bosses were speaking at the British Screen Forum Conference in London after Tilly Norwood creator Eline Van der Velden and Murray.
