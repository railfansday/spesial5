'I am not a magician' - what can Maresca's Man City learn from watertight Arsenal?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Manchester City have managed only five clean sheets this season

Manchester City's leaky defence faces one of its most difficult tasks of the season with a trip to watertight Arsenal, aiming to put right their patchy recent form.

Enzo Maresca's side began the season with eight straight victories but have won only one of their past seven in all competitions inside 90 minutes and head to Emirates Stadium trailing the champions by six points even at this early juncture.

A Premier League defeat on Sunday could well have long-term consequences for City's title aspirations, with Mikel Arteta's men clearly standing above any other top-flight side as the ones to topple.

Though the Gunners dropped points last weekend with a draw at Newcastle, they continue to set the standards with a miserly defence that has conceded only seven goals in the club's opening 11 league games.

Asked why he felt City have dropped off in recent weeks, Maresca - who replaced Pep Guardiola as manager in the summer - said: "For me it's because we've had four months together. Transition requires time.

"It's probably because we won seven games in a row, with Champions League eight in a row. Everyone was thinking at City there's no transition but I've said many times I am not a magician. There is transition, it's normal.

"There are eight or nine new players, the manager is new so you need time and you can see different clubs when they had transition how complicated it is. For me, it's completely normal, I'm completely relaxed, no panic at all."

"We are conceding too many goals and we need to avoid that," was an obvious statement made by Maresca following last Saturday's 1-1 draw against Fulham.

City followed that up with another home stalemate against Napoli in the Champions League on Tuesday and have now conceded in 10 successive games in all competitions for the first time in seven years.

"[It was] far, far too easy to play through [City]," said former England captain Alan Shearer of Rodrigo Muniz's goal for Fulham on Match of the Day.

"Fulham's throw-in was level with their own 18-yard box. For them to get through is not acceptable at any level, let alone when you are playing for Manchester City and Muniz ran past the entire midfield [to score]."

City have managed only five clean sheets all campaign, which is in stark contrast to Arsenal, who have kept double the amount so far, while Newcastle's opening goal ended a run off 445 minutes without the ball hitting their net.

Maresca's men also have an issue by conceding the opener, having done so seven times in the past 10 games, and of the 14 goals they have shipped, six have come inside the first 20 minutes.

An early Arsenal goal may well be an important factor in deciding Sunday's contest.

Asked how his side can improve defensively, Maresca said: "Video [analysis], meetings, [on] pitch coaching, It is a mix of that.

"Some of the goals we concede we can avoid, so hopefully we can do that."

The injury to key defender William Saliba has almost gone unnoticed for Arsenal as Ezri Konsa has stepped in to fill the void at centre-back seamlessly since joining from Aston Villa in the summer.

The England international has established a formidable partnership with Gabriel and the two have quickly formed an impressive understanding this season.

Maresca, meanwhile, admitted he was still "trying to find different" solutions to his team, which has been highlighted by the chopping and changing of his backline.

In 16 Premier League and Champions League games this season, the Italian has used eight different combinations in defence, which has given players little chance of fostering any sort of on-pitch relationships with each other.

City have also made seven errors leading to goals, compared to Arsenal's one.

"You are concerned in some way because that is a lot of errors leading to goals," said former City defender Nedum Onuoha. "With some of the best players you ask, 'what are they really good at?' For some it is the fact they don't make mistakes.

"They are ruthless and relentless in the way they play but at this moment in time City are being punished for those errors.

"A manager can have the tactics and do the team talks, but if people are going to continue to make those mistakes, they will suffer."

The chant of "1-0 to the Arsenal" has been synonymous with the Gunners over the years and it could not be more apt under Arteta.

Since the start of last season, his side have won 16 matches in all competitions by that particular scoreline, compared to six by City.

After beating Sunderland in a chaotic match in September, Maresca said he would "prefer to win 1-0 than 5-3", suggesting his side needed more control and he re-iterated that message after the Fulham draw.

"Sometimes you need to win games 1-0 for a small difference and to keep a clean sheet but in this moment we are not able to do that," he admitted.

City, though, have scored five more league goals than Arsenal in this campaign and Sunday's hosts have injury concerns over goalkeeper David Raya.

The Spaniard is a doubt with the shoulder problem he picked up at Newcastle, and compatriot Kepa Arrizabalaga may deputise again as he did in the 1-1 draw against Borussia Dortmund in the Champions League on Tuesday.

City have fond memories of coming up against Arsenal's stand-in keeper, after Kepa's howler for the opening goal contributed to their 2-0 Carabao Cup final triumph back in March.

A repeat result on Sunday will provide a much-needed morale boost for Maresca and his team.
