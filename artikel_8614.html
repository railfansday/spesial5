Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: Risks with autopay? Best practices?

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

No account used for autopay should have the ability to fill itself back up without your intervention, such as Vanguard's CashPlus. Fidelity's CMA I would never use because of this.

If you keep a large amount in an autopay account and aren't getting market rates on your funds, then you are throwing away money.

My CashPlus account finishes each month with a few hundred dollars in it. On the last weekend of the month I determine the next month's needs, and when the account would go negative, usually around the 10th to 15th. I then put on my calendar to refill the account via a Money Market sale 2 days prior, covering the upcoming expenditures and leaving a few hundred left. For those that complain this is too time consuming, do it quarterly.

CashPlus for me is currently paying 3.65%, or 3.49 after taxes. VUSXX is paying 3.97%. 0.32 on $100,000 is $320...

I have zero concern that a company will pull the incorrect funds. That said, I recently left Verizon after 28 years and went to GoogleFi. After porting out our lines, Verizon locked the account. We couldn't remove the autopay account, nor view the final bill, etc. Then they pulled a full month's payment for 3 days service. I pushed back, they kept saying a refund was coming. Finally went to the office of the CEO, they said no refund was coming, read paragraph 326 in the tiny print agreement. By the end of the call, a full pro-rated refund was granted, due to their reps putting in writing that a full refund was coming and the absurdity of treating a 28 year customer like this.

Lesson learned is to turn off autopay before ending the relationship.
