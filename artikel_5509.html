Ring is unveiling an AI-powered home security drone after six years of delays

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Amazon unit launched Ring Robotics with a $1,000 Rover Cam security robot for businesses that is due to ship Dec. 16

Amazon $AMZN's Ring unit introduced Ring Robotics on Tuesday, a line of autonomous security devices that starts with a flying indoor camera called the Ring Drone Cam and a security robot for businesses, the company said.

Ring first announced the drone more than six years before this week's debut, according to Bloomberg. The Drone Cam needs authorization from the Federal Communications Commission before it can be offered for sale or lease, the company said.

The Drone Cam will cost about $250 and ship in late January, according to Bloomberg and Engadget, which gave a Jan. 27 date. TechCrunch listed the price at $270.

The Drone Cam stays docked with its camera off until a flight begins, the company said. Users can set flight routes and schedules, which require a compatible Ring Protect plan, or send it out on demand. When a Ring camera, sensor or alarm is triggered, the drone flies to preset patrol points to investigate. It records 2K video with a 148-degree field of view and uses two built-in LED lights in low light.

Both devices run on a system called AI Pilot, which builds a floor plan as a user carries the drone through a space, suggests patrol points and steers around obstacles, the company said. Aside from getting airborne and touching down, the drone needs artificial intelligence and a working Wi-Fi link to handle almost all of its other functions, according to Bloomberg.

"That kind of invention is hard, and it takes time. But we kept at it until we got it right," Jamie Siminoff, founder and chief inventor of Ring, said in a statement.

Earlier versions struggled with a design problem, according to Bloomberg. Packing in more sensors and computing capability demanded a bulkier battery, and the extra heft called for sturdier rotors that undermined how steadily the drone flew. Siminoff called this "a vicious cycle" at a media briefing, according to Bloomberg. Ring dropped its first design and later shelved a follow-up effort.

The Ring Rover Cam is designed for businesses and patrols on its own, investigates disturbances and streams video in up to 4K, the company said. It has a periscope camera, a siren, a light tower and two-way audio. Ring said the robot was inspired by feedback from customers of Amazon's Astro robot.

The Rover Cam costs $1,000, according to Bloomberg and TechCrunch. It ships Dec. 16, according to TechCrunch.

Recorded video from both devices is protected by an encryption system called Throw Away the Key Encryption, according to Engadget. Ring said both devices have LED indicators that show when they are recording.
