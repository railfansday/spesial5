Mortgage industry expects CFPB servicing rule to narrow

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The CFPB has sent a revised version of its 2024 “Streamlining Mortgage Servicing for Borrowers Experiencing Payment Difficulties” rule to the Office of Management and Budget, moving closer to finalizing new loss-mitigation standards under Regulation X. Industry attorneys expect a significantly narrower rule than the original proposal, shaped by President Trump’s directive to modernize Dodd-Frank-era rules and broad stakeholder pushback against open-ended requirements.  AI Summary

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) last week took another step toward finalizing new loss-mitigation standards, sending a revised version of its 2024 mortgage servicing proposal to the White House Office of Management and Budget (OMB) for review.

The final rule, “Streamlining Mortgage Servicing for Borrowers Experiencing Payment Difficulties” under Regulation X, is expected to be narrower than the 2024 proposal advanced when the CFPB was led by Rohit Chopra, according to industry attorneys. The CFPB did not immediately reply to HousingWire‘s request for comments.

The move comes in the wake of President Donald Trump’s March 13 executive order, “Promoting Access to Mortgage Credit,” which directs the CFPB to modernize Dodd-Frank-era rules that lenders and servicers say increase compliance costs. The current standards were finalized in 2013.

“We won’t know the final rule’s content until it is published. But the comment record and the current Bureau’s priorities point toward a significantly narrower rule,”Joseph M. DeFazio and Joe Reilly, partners at Troutman Pepper Locke, wrote in a blog post. “Since President Trump’s second term began, the Bureau has also rescinded prior guidance and signaled that it prefers clear, fixed rules to open-ended frameworks like the one in the 2024 proposal.”

The CFPB received more than 80 comments on the proposal, with what the attorneys described as an “unusual degree of convergence” among groups that rarely agree.

The American Bankers Association (ABA) has urged the CFPB to finalize only a limited portion of the 2024 proposal, pressing the bureau to focus on targeted changes rather than a full rewrite.

“The Bureau’s 2024 proposal was founded on the misconception that servicers prioritize foreclosure over helping borrowers find a successful solution to resolve their payment disruptions,” Teshale Smith, the ABA’s senior counsel for regulatory compliance and policy, said in a letter to the CFPB in June. “In fact, sustained low foreclosure rates and high customer satisfaction rates demonstrate that servicers remain committed to helping struggling borrowers.”

The 2024 proposed rule aimed to make permanent several pandemic-era practices used when large numbers of borrowers needed fast assistance. Industry estimates showed that 8.8 million families were helped, with the re-default rate for loans exiting COVID-19 loss-mitigation programs at about 10%, ABA said in its letter.The proposal allows servicers to evaluate loss-mitigation options before borrowers complete an application, prohibits late fees and other charges while a loss-mitigation review is underway, and makes assistance possible in multiple languages.As it relates to foreclosure, the draft allows a loss-mitigation review cycle to start when the borrower asks for help, not only after a completed application, lasting until the loan is current or all options are ruled out. During that time, servicers could not begin or advance foreclosure, a protection that currently applies only after a complete application is on file. Foreclosure could resume only after options were exhausted or the borrower stopped communicating.

Attorneys at Troutman Pepper Locke expect the final rule to permit sequential review of loss-mitigation options instead of requiring servicers to evaluate all options at once; dropping the proposed ban on fee accrual during loss-mitigation reviews; and removal of the language access provisions that were part of the 2024 draft.

Isaac Boltansky, head of public policy at Pennymac, said the company “supports clear, consistent standards that give servicers the certainty they need to deliver the best possible outcomes for homeowners, particularly those facing financial hardship.”
