Nvidia Just Demolished Earnings, but the Stock Is Still Plunging. The Reason Is Obvious.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter earnings blew Wall Street's expectations out of the water.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 18, 2026 at 3:47PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -2.90%) just delivered another blockbuster earnings report. Despite the company's unprecedented growth, the semiconductor giant's stock is struggling to sustain its initial after-hours rally. As I write this (mid-morning on Nov. 18), Nvidia stock is trading at around $154. That's essentially in line with its closing price from yesterday and roughly 5% below its close from Friday, Nov. 13.

The disconnect between Nvidia's financial performance and its stock price suggests investors are more concerned about things beyond earnings growth. Following founder Jensen Huang's unexpected resignation, questions surrounding leadership, regulatory scrutiny, and the company's artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure partnerships are overshadowing its operating performance.

Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter earnings suggest the business remains exceptionally strong, but the company’s future direction is becoming much less predictable.

During the third quarter, Nvidia generated $115.8 billion in revenue, representing 103% growth year over year and exceeding Wall Street's estimate of $109.2 billion. Data center revenue reached $107.6 billion, increasing 110% from the prior year. Meanwhile, adjusted earnings per share (EPS) surged 111% to $2.68, comfortably exceeding Wall Street's estimate of $2.47.

The company generated $65.3 billion in net income during a single quarter, representing 105% growth year over year while also maintaining a gross margin of nearly 75%. These results are extraordinary given Nvidia's enormous size and scale.

Management's guidance was equally impressive, with Nvidia forecasting $130 billion in revenue for the fourth quarter, plus or minus 3%. This implies roughly 91% annual growth compared to the same period last year. Moreover, it also exceeds analysts' expectations of approximately $125 billion -- underscoring the insatiable demand for Nvidia's AI infrastructure.

Interim CEO Colette Kress reinforced the company's momentum during the earnings conference call. Management raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to 75% -- up from 70% -- suggesting the company expects robust demand to continue beyond the current quarter.

Huang was not just Nvidia's chief executive. He was the company's founder, technological visionary, and most public-facing representative. He is credited with transforming Nvidia from a graphics chip manufacturer for video games into a dominant supplier of AI computing products. As a result, his departure introduces uncertainty that even jaw-dropping financial results cannot immediately resolve.

During Tuesday's conference call, board chairman Harvey Jones made the company's position unusually clear, stating that Nvidia's board had unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." Nvidia has retained executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to identify a permanent successor, with both internal and external candidates under consideration.

The challenge now is determining whether another leader can replicate Huang's combination of technological expertise, strategic vision, and industry relationships. While Kress has extensive financial and operational experience, replacing a founder who shaped Nvidia's AI ecosystem is a fundamentally different set of challenges. Investors appear to be reassessing the premium valuation they previously assigned to Nvidia because of Huang's star power.

Leadership uncertainty isn't Nvidia's only problem. Federal authorities are investigating potential national security and export-control issues involving Nvidia and its customer Megaspeed, including concerns about whether the company's semiconductors reached restricted Chinese entities.

Investors are also focused on Nvidia's extensive network of AI infrastructure investments, financing arrangements, and commercial partnerships. During the earnings call, management disclosed approximately $118 billion in financial guarantees, including credit support associated with a planned data center campus. While these deals can help accelerate AI infrastructure build-outs, they also raise questions about financing dependencies and whether announced projects will actually translate into the revenue investors anticipate.

Adding to the uncertainty, Nvidia disclosed that its quarterly Form 10-Q would be filed in the coming weeks rather than published alongside earnings. The filing is not yet legally overdue, and the timing doesn't establish any accounting problems, per se. Nevertheless, investors likely want as much clarity as possible regarding legal proceedings, financial commitments, and risk disclosures before assigning Nvidia a higher valuation.

Ultimately, Nvidia's financial performance remains exceptional, but investors are increasingly evaluating intangible risks that quarterly earnings cannot distract from. Until Nvidia demonstrates that its leadership and strategic partnerships can thrive under new management, even spectacular earnings growth could struggle to restore investor confidence.
