Advanced Metrology for Defect Detection, Tool Health, and Semiconductor Yield Enhancement

Tanggal: 2026-11-22



In advanced semiconductor manufacturing, small defects can have a large effect on yield. Thin film deposition tools must stay stable and qualified. Process drift must be caught early. When a defect does appear, engineers need to know where it came from and how it affects device performance. This webinar shows how in-line inspection and offline characterization combine into one workflow, from tool monitoring to root-cause analysis.

In the first half, Dr. Bitan Chakraborty from Intel, explains how surface scan metrology fits into station monitors for thin film tools. The session shows how baseline signatures and real-time monitoring reveal hardware wear, particle generation, and process drift before they reach production wafers. It then moves to in-line scans on patterned wafers. These scans expose layer-level defects and show how they form and spread through multi-layer device stacks. Linking this defect data to electrical test results helps teams find root causes and focus their yield improvement work.

In the second half, Dr. Jacob Petersen from Evident Scientific, addresses a common question: what happens after an in-line tool flags a problem? In-line tools are fast, but they cannot always provide the details needed for a firm conclusion. Offline 3D optical profilometry fills that gap by combining three complementary inspection techniques in a single system. It delivers dimensional measurements and helps determine whether a defect lies within the sample or comes from foreign material. The session covers how several optical techniques support inspection from bare silicon to fully packaged chips. It also shows how automation enables fast measurement of features down to the sub-nanometer scale with minimal operator handling.

What You Will Learn:

  • How surface scan metrology in station monitors tracks tool health and supports the qualification of thin film deposition processes
  • How baseline signatures and real-time monitoring detect particle generation, hardware degradation, and process drift before production is affected
  • How in-line scans on patterned wafers reveal layer-level defects and show how they propagate through multi-layer device structures
  • When offline 3D optical profilometry should follow in-line inspection, and how it measures dimensions and separates sample defects from foreign material
  • How correlating tool health data, inspection results, and electrical performance links physical defects to yield loss and guides targeted improvement projects
