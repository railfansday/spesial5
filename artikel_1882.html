Worst-case scenario for Mikel Arteta as Arsenal lose their superpower at Newcastle

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Your weekly briefing on Arsenal, featuring team news and expert analysis from Matt Verri

Arsenal have had worse 45 minutes over the course of Mikel Arteta’s seven years in charge.

However, considering not just the context, but what his players should be capable of, how they let things slip, and what the consequences of one second-half moment in particular might be, this will be right up there as the most frustrating halves he has overseen.

It was all so unnecessary. For years now, a trip up to St James’ Park to take on Newcastle has meant an almighty battle. Brutal, draining encounters, played on the margins and very rarely delivering much joy for the Gunners.

Not here, at least not at first. In the opening 45 minutes, it was the Gunners who were the bullies, bringing an intensity and a level of physicality that Newcastle simply could not live with. By half-time, Arsenal had registered 18 touches in the opposition’s box. Newcastle had managed just one.

Arteta’s side led 1-0 through Eberechi Eze’s emphatic finish but it should have been more. Noni Madueke wasted two big chances. Bruno Guimaraes flung himself at a cross and turned it wide.

There was no reason for this encounter to follow the trend of previous showdowns. It was a Saturday evening stroll for Arsenal and it should have remained as much.

Instead, the Gunners fell apart. Alarmingly so. All it took was one shanked volley from Martin Odegaard, letting Newcastle away on the break to equalise, and the match flipped.

Like in the Champions League win over Slavia Prague last month, Arsenal were dominant in the first half but then let it slip away from them, whether it be complacency or something else. There was no sign of any game management.

In Prague, the opposition did not have the quality to punish them. At St James’ Park, Arsenal paid the price.

From the moment Harvey Barnes equalised, all composure from the visitors evaporated. Madueke swung at thin air attempting to clear. Eze slipped in his own box, Raya could not clear, and Gabriel had to quickly arrive to leather the ball behind.

Arteta looked shell-shocked on the touchline when Newcastle then took the lead from a mess of a corner but the worst was still to come.

David Raya had a collision with William Osula that looked fairly innocuous. When that then forced him off, looking in real pain with an apparent shoulder injury, it felt like a fever dream for Arsenal.

An injury to Raya is the worst-case scenario for the Gunners. There is no player more irreplaceable in this squad.

Arsenal face Manchester City next weekend and then Tottenham a week later. Real Madrid, Manchester United and Bournemouth, who are on a nine-match winning run, all visit north London before Christmas.

The drop-off from Raya to Kepa Arrizabalaga and Illan Meslier is stark. Kepa had his chance in an Arsenal shirt last season and was awful in the Carabao Cup final.

Madueke stepped up to make it 2-2 and ensure Arsenal left Newcastle with a point but there was the sense that the damage had been done.

At half-time, seemingly cruising to another win, Arsenal looked certain to go eight points clear at the top of the table. After City had dropped points to Fulham earlier in the day, there was already a sense that the title race was becoming a one-man show.

Less so now. Not because of a draw with Newcastle, even if a failure to kill off a vulnerable opponent is nothing new for this Arsenal side.

But because without Raya, Arsenal’s defensive strength, the foundations of this team, is far less of a superpower. The severity of that injury might well decide just how much of a title race we have on our hands.
