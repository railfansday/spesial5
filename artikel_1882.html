Celtic board working overtime on fan clashes ahead of AGM - Keith Wyness

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The Celtic hierarchy are working hard behind the scenes to try and ease the tension among the fanbase ahead of the club's upcoming AGM.

That is according to former Aberdeen chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the situation at Parkhead remains "toxic," despite the team being top of the league.

Celtic are due to host their next annual AGM on 1 December, when the club's shareholders will get the chance to make their feelings known at Celtic Park.

Wyness believes that the responsibility lies with the board to try and rebuild bridges with the fanbase, rather than continuing to drive a wedge between them.

He is clear that the club will be desperate to avoid a repeat of last year's AGM, which was ultimately abandoned after just 25 minutes.

Board members were heckled by fans present at the event, who used it as an opportunity to voice their frustration directly to the hierarchy.

Aberdeen's former chief Wyness - who served as CEO at Pittodrie between 2000 and 2004 and now runs a consultancy business advising elite clubs - believes that Celtic may ultimately need a change of ownership for their issues to correct themselves.

Current owner Dermot Desmond is hugely unpopular among the fans, and has been subject to regular protests throughout the last 12 months.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness believes that his presence is "harming" the club right now.

"There's a lot going on right now before this next AGM that's coming up, and it is just a toxic situation between the fans and the board right now and the number of weeks we've talked about this," he said.

"I do think it's incumbent on the board to try and somehow defuse the situation and get peace to break out in some way. It's got to come from those at the top.

"But I do think a refreshed start would be the right way for Celtic if there was a way to get some new owners in, because it's just harming everything about the club right now.

"There are bigger issues. But I'm afraid it's not a happy place at Celtic right now despite being top of the table."

Wyness told Football Insider earlier this week that Celtic will not face punishment from the Scottish FA after their minute's silence was interrupted last weekend.

The Bhoys held a Remembrance Day tribute ahead of their home victory over Kilmarnock, but referee John Beaton cut the minute's silence short amid jeers and whistles from some fans.

However, Wyness does not believe that Celtic have broken any rules with the botched tribute, and are only likely to receive a warning from the SFA.

The boardroom expert suggested that they can expect a "letter" from the governing body ahead of their next game, but no further punishment is likely.
