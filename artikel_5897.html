Universities, schools nationwide use Thanksgiving as chance to promote 'Day of Mourning' and decolonization

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Loudoun County parent Rachel Pisani joins 'Fox & Friends' to express outrage over school lesson plans teaching children that Thanksgiving is a 'Day of Mourning.' Pisani argues the 'woke' curriculum teaches children to hate their country.

Thanksgiving is a day of celebration and spending time with family for the vast majority of Americans, but for several students on college campuses across the country, the national holiday is a source of anguish that is being expressed in the form of protests and counter-programming events.

On Nov. 18, George Washington University hosted an event called "Breaking Bread: The Cost of Celebration" that was described as a chance for students to examine Thanksgiving, Christmas and Independence Day through an intersectional lens, including experiences of "belonging, exclusion, cultural erasure, and historical harm."

"Through facilitated dialogue and shared reflection, students will engage in meaningful conversations about historical memory, social responsibility, and the complexities of celebrating within a diverse society," the student life event description read.

At the University of Wisconsin at Milwaukee, the American Indian Student Center & Electa Quinney Institute for American Indian Education hosted a Nov. 17 event called "AlterNative Giving," which was described as "a community gathering that offers a meaningful alternative to the traditional Thanksgiving narrative. Instead of focusing on colonial histories, we come together to honor Indigenous ways of knowing, being, and giving."

Salem State University promoted an event that labeled Thanksgiving as a "National Day of Mourning" and told students the land they are being educated on was stolen from Indigenous people.

"The land occupied by Salem State University is part of the traditional and ancestral homeland of the Massachusett tribe," the school’s website posted. "We acknowledge the genocide and forced removal of the Massachusett tribe and their kin from their summer home in Nâmkek."

A review of university calendars found several examples lamenting the downsides of Thanksgiving, including Occidental College, which said the holiday is a "reminder of settler colonialism and its devastating impact on Indigenous individuals, communities and tribes."

The Pratt Institute lists "National Day of Mourning" as an occasion for Indigenous remembrance and reflection, and the University at Albany lists the holiday as "National Day of Mourning/Thanksgiving."

George Washington University hosted an event on Nov. 18 called "Breaking Bread: The Cost of Celebration." (iStock)

The colonial messaging around Thanksgiving isn't limited to universities but is also occurring at elementary and high schools, including in Loudoun County, Virginia, where a parent recently pushed back in an interview with Fox News about school lesson plans teaching children that Thanksgiving is a "Day of Mourning."

On its website, the San Diego County Office of Education explained that "November is Native American Heritage Month, a time to honor the diversity, history, and living cultures of Native peoples. It is also the month when Thanksgiving is observed in the United States, a holiday that carries very different meanings for different people, including many Native communities."

Oregon’s Bethel School District links to a "Decolonizing Thanksgiving: A Toolkit for Combatting Racism in Schools" on its official Equity, Access & Inclusion webpage. Listed under teaching resources, the toolkit appears alongside material about honoring Native peoples on Thanksgiving.
