Maine Senator Susan Collins Has Reportedly Conceded Race to Democratic Challenger Troy Jackson

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

One of the most closely watched states during the run-up to the 2026 midterm elections has been Maine, where incumbent US senator Susan Collins, a Republican, faced a significant threat from upstart candidate Graham Platner. Allegations of misconduct toppled the Democrat’s campaign, but his replacement, former logger and state senator Troy Jackson, has had a strong showing in the polls, and posed the first actual competition Collins has faced in nearly three decades. A little more than three hours after Maine's poll closure at 8 p.m. ET, Collins reportedly called Jackson to concede the race, even as most news outlets were reporting that the race was still ongoing.

Susan Collins was running for a sixth term, and was the only Republican representing a state President Donald Trump lost in 2024. As votes rolled in on Tuesday and into Wednesday morning, Jackson's lead continued to grow. According to outlets, including Maine broadcast station WMTW, Collins called Jackson shortly after midnight to congratulate him for his win. In an address to supporters early Wednesday, an ebullient Jackson celebrated his win. At the time of publication, the New York Times was forecasting that “Jackson is almost certain to win," and NBC had projected him the winner as well.

“20 minutes or so I spoke to Senator Collins and I thanked her for her service to Maine,” Jackson said. “I wished her and her family well.”

“To every Mainer who voted for her or supported her, you can count on me to be your senator now.”

Elsewhere in Maine, Democrats kept the governor’s office blue, as term-limited governor Janet Mills will be replaced by Democrat Hannah Pingree, the Associated Press said when they called the race Tuesday night. She bested Republican candidate Bobby Charles, according to the AP.

Maine only has two congressional districts, both of which are up for election every two years. The state’s First Congressional District is known as a Democratic stronghold, with Hannah Pingree’s mother, Chellie Pingree, expected to retain the seat she’s held since 2009. Its Second District is considered a battleground region, with former state secretary of state Matthew Dunlap, a Democrat, still locked in a tight race against Republican former governor Paul LePage as of publication time.
