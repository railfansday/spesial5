Genshin Impact cheater offering paid carry services reportedly arrested while meeting with customers

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A Genshin Impact player in China has reportedly been arrested after allegedly using cheats to help customers complete difficult in-game challenges in exchange for money.

The player is accused of running a paid boosting service for Stygian Onslaught, one of Genshin Impact’s toughest combat challenges, using unauthorized tools to clear content for other players.

However, the alleged operation came to an abrupt end when police reportedly detained the individual during a meeting with customers inside a clothing store.

According to Enduins, the player had been advertising their services through videos on the Chinese platform Bilibili, demonstrating how cheats could be used to complete Stygian Onslaught.

The challenge requires players to complete three timed battles, with higher difficulties offering significantly tougher encounters.

The allegations began circulating on Chinese gaming forum NGA before being shared by Seele Leaks on Telegram, alongside images documenting the incident.

One image appears to show several individuals gathered inside a clothing store in security camera footage dated November 3, 2026. Another shows a Chinese-language apology statement displayed on a phone.

The player subsequently apologized to HoYoverse and removed posts advertising their services.

“I used this account without authorization to publicly post videos of myself using cheats in Genshin Impact. I then directed viewers from Bilibili to my Xianyu secondhand marketplace store where I offered paid game boosting services,” they wrote.

“My actions seriously violated miHoYo’s and Genshin Impact’s official rules prohibiting cheating. They infringed upon miHoYo’s intellectual property rights and disrupted the game’s operations, fairness, and the gaming experience of other players.”

This wouldn’t be the first time HoYoverse has taken serious action against cheating. In March 2026, the developer revealed that it had removed more than one million illegal links and pieces of content during 2025, including cheat software and pirated game resources.

The company also previously threatened legal action against Genshin Impact cheaters in 2023 after malicious plug-ins were used to interfere with other players’ accounts.

China has also seen legal consequences for paid boosting operations. In September 2025, a Shanghai court ordered a Genshin Impact boosting studio to pay 3 million yuan in damages after finding that it had used cheats to provide services to customers.

For the latest alleged offender, what started as a way to make money clearing Genshin Impact challenges may now have become a much more serious legal matter.
