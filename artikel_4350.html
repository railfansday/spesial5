Call of Duty will always be a major force in the gaming zeitgeist; that much is certain, but the recent success of Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 was a much-needed win for the franchise. With the campaign receiving fantastic reception, the multiplayer rolling out, and the inclusion of the extraction mode DMZ, Activision is looking to keep racking up wins.

Former Skyrim lead designer Bruce Nesmith says Todd Howard knows when to make things simpler for players, and recalls a design decision that proved him wrong.

Their newest incentive was announced on the official Call of Duty Twitter: Modern Warfare 4’s DMZ is free to play for all until November 11th, giving new and returning players a taste of extraction action exclusive to the Modern Warfare reboot series. Serving as MW’s answer to Zombies, the mode has been totally revamped in preparation for new blood.

DMZ has stuck with the Modern Warfare reboot series since MW2, serving as the title’s unique mode alongside Black Ops Zombies. While Zombies leaned into the absurd paranoia and conspiracies that swirl around undercover operations, DMZ focuses on the realism and boots-on-the-ground feel that originally made Warzone work so well, playing off Modern Warfare’s thematic strengths.

MW4’s DMZ dropped only a few days ago, but has already hit the ground running, with story missions, PvE encounters, and plenty of incentives to play cool or callous when other players show. Packed with new weapons, gear, and locations, MW4’s DMZ is set on the Korean Peninsula, Hajin, where the devastation that rocked the campaign is still felt.

Stuck between squadrons of NPC soldiers, a dangerous map, and the X factor of players, the free trial will populate Hajin even more, sparking hijinks and carefully curated tactics in equal measure. Any progress made by players during their free playtime will translate to progression in the main game, giving prospective players a hit of the game’s action without asking for the full $69.99.

With the campaign receiving fantastic reception, the multiplayer rolling out, and the inclusion of the extraction mode DMZ, Activision is looking to keep racking up wins.

That price tag is honestly the biggest pain point for many players, but Modern Warfare 4’s new rewards for completing weapon progression, multiple modes with a variety of unlocks, and a phenomenal upgrade of the franchise’s gunplay make it a solid buy for anyone content with sticking to a single title for a while.

MW4’s reinventions were fully needed to pull the subseries out of the total rut that was Modern Warfare 3, and it’s incredibly exciting to see where the game will go both with its internal modes and as a guiding star for the franchise going forward. Black Ops might be up next on the rotation, but there’s no shame in taking notes from your neighbor.

Call of Duty stands alongside Grand Theft Auto as major gaming icons that have avoided aligning with any specific platform. While GTA 6 is releasing on PC at a later date, Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 dropped everywhere simultaneously, even on the Switch 2. The title’s sales give universal releases a good argument, even as console first-party titles are being tied back down.

With the gaming community finally back on its side, Modern Warfare 4 has some big opportunities to recover the franchise’s PR, which has been stumbling after questionable cosmetics and stuttering quality. Modern Warfare 4 is sticking to its aesthetic and goals well for now, but time will only tell if Warzone’s inclusion will make odd collabs start slinking in.

Infinity Ward has some time to rest on their laurels and focus on live service updates, with Season 1’s launch already bringing players back in. With a full DMZ trial, a good chunk of players are sure to stick around, even if those already in the game treat them like easy pickings for the duration.
