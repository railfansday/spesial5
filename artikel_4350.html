How much does charging an EV really cost? This Redditor says $7 per month

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

One of the most common questions asked by first-time EV buyers is about the cost of electricity. Whether they plan to charge at home or find charging in the wild, everyone wants to know what to expect when it comes time to top off their batteries. One EV owner did the math after two months of EV ownership and came up with a surprising number: $7 a month.

Whether we’re talking gasoline, diesel, or electricity, one driver’s fuel costs can look dramatically different from another’s. How far they drive, how often, and under what conditions can matter just as much as what they drive. And while EVs may offer the greatest benefits to rural drivers covering long distances, a Redditor going by Suitable-Truth-350 took a different approach, moving into a highly walkable city for the express purpose of driving as little as possible.

“My wife and I recently retired and moved from a car dependent, rural/suburban area to a large, walkable city with good light rail. Our goal was to drive as little as possible,” write Suitable-Truth-350. “We took delivery of an Ioniq5 in August. We still have a daily walking life, but we use the car to see friends in the country, shop or travel around the greater Chicagoland area. After 2 months, we finally charged it.”

The result? Suitable says it took less than 30 minutes to juice their Ioniq 5 back up to an 80% SOC, at a total cost of $13.

“Driving the car is a pleasure,” they continue. “The pickup and smooth operation so different from our last ICE vehicle. First impression, we love the car and could never imagine going back to (a gasoline-powered car).”

I’ve been saying it for years: drive an EV for a week and whatever range anxiety you might experience will disappear. And, while it seems like Suitable-Truth-350 didn’t have much range anxiety to begin with, it’s worth noting here that their situation isn’t that rare.

In their most recent national survey, AAA found that the average US driver covered less than 29 miles per day, with the vast majority weekly commuting totals falling well within the range of even the most affordable used EVs.

The same survey found that just 30% of Americans reported taking at least one road trip the previous summer, with an average one-way trip length of ~235 miles.

That 235 mile trip might be too much for a car like the Fiat 500e, but even that car – the new EV with the shortest EPA-estimated range you can buy in the US – can make a 235 mile trip with a single 30-minute stop to charge. And in 2026 and 2027, they’re sure to find plenty of places to charge, thanks to a public charging network that’s newer, faster, and far more built out than they probably think, with more fast-charging locations opening every week and modern charging speeds that make longer stops the exception instead of the norm.

Our two EVs raised our monthly electric bill by ~ $60/mo. and that's without solar. That wouldn't have paid for one gas fill-up for a week in our previous gas cars. I don't think this is even a question. If you can charge at home, it's cheaper. If you rely on public charging all the time then it's a mixed bag but still generally cheaper in most parts of the country. All said, we have three EVs now and my monthly electric bill for our entire house with six people is less than what we paid for fuel in one gas car.

In other words: your charging costs are probably going to be higher than Suitable-Truth-350’s seven-ish dollars per month, but with $4/gallon premium and $6/gallon diesel still very much the norm in 2026, it’ll probably be less than you think, too.
