Not Cocoa Powder Or Coffee: Add This Pantry Staple To Canned Chili For A Major Flavor Boost

A good chili is a solid meal in a bowl that can be made easily at home in the slow cooker. It takes a lot of time, but it can create meals for days. There are plenty of different types of chili to enjoy if you want to try something new. But when you don't want to spend the time in the kitchen with all the beef browning and veggie prep, you can grab a pre-made chili right off the grocery store shelf. Some swear by coffee or cocoa powder to upgrade your chili, but there's one ingredient you likely have in the pantry or fridge that you probably have never considered. To improve the flavor of your canned chili, you should add a touch of jam or jelly as you heat it up.

Adding a fruit jelly or jam to your canned chili will help build complexity and draw out the flavor of common seasonings found in the dish — like cumin, paprika, cayenne, chili, and garlic and onion powders — while also offering a bit of contrast. It's like adding a pinch of brown sugar, with just a skosh of bright, fruit-forward undertones that can make it taste more like it's fresh, homemade, with subtle nuance usually lost in canned goods. It also helps cut the acidity of the tomato sauce that may sometimes be overpowering, especially in canned chili. Using a bit of the jarred stuff will even improve the look of your meal, adding a nice gloss or top sheen to give you an end result that looks like something right out of the page of a cookbook.

If you want to go with something gourmet that's relatively easy to find, a jar of Bonne Maman Strawberry Preserves, available online and at retailers like Walmart, will work nicely, but you can use homemade preserves or pretty much any store-bought jam or jelly, like Smucker's Concord Grape Jelly, also available online and in stores. In fact, jellies are often preferred because they blend in seamlessly, and you won't end up with over-sweet chunks of fruit in your chili. If you plan to use something with whole fruit, you may want to simmer it for longer on the stove, so the chili flavor will fully infuse with it. Grape is a pretty solid choice because it will beautifully complement the deep umami flavor of the beef without overpowering it. Fig and apricot jams also work great for some added depth and earthiness.

When it comes to your base, not all canned chilis are created equal, but this trick will definitely add flavor to whatever you choose to use. You'll want to start with about a half teaspoon of jam or jelly per can as you heat up the chili, then give it a taste once it's simmering. If it's not quite there, you can always add more, but hold back from going too hard right out of the gate.

Because of the extra sweetness, you may need an extra pinch of salt — although most canned chilis are already pretty sodium-rich. And some spicy additions, like fresh or canned jalapeños, red pepper flakes, or a dash of hot sauce, will balance things out nicely.
