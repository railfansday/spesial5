Government investigators find AI platforms produce terror plot plans in undercover op

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Government Accountability Office official Kevin Walsh described investigators using AI technology to plan an attack on the homeland. (Courtesy: Government Accountability Office)

Federal investigators successfully bypassed safety safeguards on four major artificial intelligence platforms, obtaining detailed plans for potentially deadly terrorist attacks in less than 12 hours, according to a government watchdog report released Wednesday.

The Government Accountability Office (GAO) found that ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok each generated information that could help carry out attacks targeting crowded locations or the nation's electrical grid during undercover testing conducted in spring 2026.

Two platforms produced plans involving improvised explosive devices intended to cause mass casualties, while the other two outlined attacks on electrical infrastructure designed to inflict economic damage.

The AI-generated responses included potential targets, locations, timing and attack methods. Although all four platforms initially activated safeguards to block or remove harmful content, investigators ultimately circumvented those protections.

GAO investigators also consulted experts and conducted real-world checks to assess the plans, finding that the information provided by all four platforms was sufficiently feasible to assist someone planning an attack.

"The potential benefits of this technology are immense, but they can also benefit malicious actors," the GAO warned, citing growing risks to national and economic security.

The companies behind the platforms — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and SpaceXAI — said they were continuing to improve their safety measures, while some raised concerns about the testing.

Anthropic acknowledged that determined users could bypass AI safeguards and said it took the findings seriously, but questioned aspects of the GAO's methodology and said it could not independently verify the results.

Google noted that investigators tested earlier versions of its models, saying, "We continue to improve our models." OpenAI welcomed the opportunity to respond to the findings and acknowledged "the work that remains," while SpaceXAI said it "continues to work diligently" to strengthen safety with each model release.

Sen. Maggie Hassan, D-N.H., at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., on Monday, Nov. 10, 2025. (Aaron Schwartz/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

The report, requested by Sen. Maggie Hassan, D-N.H., comes amid growing scrutiny of AI's potential misuse and renewed calls for stronger safeguards against national security threats.

"The AI public safety threat is no longer theoretical," Hassan said in a statement Wednesday. "If Congress does not act, it is a question of when – not if – a bad actor will use AI to plan and carry out a major attack."
