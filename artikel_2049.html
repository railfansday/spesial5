West Ham can seal £200m jackpot and Sheffield United deal will help them get there

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

West Ham United are quickly learning that the Championship is no cakewalk. They brought in a promotion specialist, Scott Parker, to help oversee a change in fortunes and a run to the top, but he may need some top-level second-tier players to do so.

The Hammers have a lot of great individuals across the squad, whether that be Jarrod Bowen off the right, a centre-back pairing of Konstantinos Mavropanos and Max Kilman or a promotion-winning duo of Manor Solomon and Joel Piroe in their frontline.

In midfield, though, it's arguable that the current engine room doesn't have the quality to push towards the top two.

Divine Mukasa has been a promising, young, attacking option, but outside of that, record-breaking £22 million arrival Arne Engels hasn't lived up to the hype surrounding that price tag, and Tomas Soucek and Mohamadou Kante have been fine options, but it's not a midfield core with the experience at this level to help bring about promotion.

With the midfield options at the London Stadium, it's no surprise that West Ham were looking to add Sheffield United's Sydie Peck to their ranks following their relegation from the Premier League.

And while the record-breaking deal for Engels wasn't done at the time of these reports, it should still be something that the Hammers reconsider returning to as the January transfer window looms.

Peck had a great year last season, adding some goals to his game, captaining the side at times and being a bright spark in an otherwise dismal campaign for the Blades. This year, though, he's found a new level.

The 22-year-old is starting to influence games further forward under Chris Wilder. He's among the leading defensive midfielders in the division when it comes to creating chances, taking shots and scoring goals.

His hat-trick against Preston North End was an example of what he can do in an attacking sense now, taking his overall goal contributions this season to seven in 17 in all competitions, just three fewer than he managed last year.

Peck's value is arguably as high as it has ever been heading into this January. For a Sheffield United side that is well documented to be struggling financially due to the lack of Premier League parachute payments and the new SCR rules, cashing in could be something the higher-ups decide to do.

Especially given his current contract situation, too, with him entering the final 18 months of his deal. If a new contract can't be hashed out by the end of the year, the Blades face losing out on the amount of profit they could make on the 22-year-old.

We've seen in the past Sheffield United being forced to sell players at a cut-price once they enter the final years of their deals. The likes of Iliman Ndiaye, Anel Ahmedhodzic and, more recently, Gustavo Hamer, all left for less than their perceived market value in their final year.

Peck will be in the final year of his contract come the summer, unless a new deal can be reached. Last month, Blades boss Chris Wilder expressed optimism that these players could be tied down, but with the 22-year-old especially showing Premier League potential, it may be difficult to persuade him to stick around at a Sheffield United side that doesn't seem to be any closer to a top-flight return.

Unfortunately, West Ham weren't the only side to monitor Peck over the summer window. Premier League outfits Nottingham Forest and Brentford were interested, and in a recent report from TEAMtalk, so are now North-East rivals Sunderland and Newcastle United.

The fact that so many top-flight teams are being mentioned alongside the Hammers in their interest in Peck will make it difficult for the London Stadium-based side to strike a deal, as they could offer them Premier League football immediately.

At West Ham, all they can do is promise they'll get there for the start of next season. That could lend well to a deal, as it would allow Peck to progress into the Premier League with a new club, rather than being thrown in at the deep end midway through a season.

But on the flip side, if the Hammers don't secure promotion this year, it'll be another year in the Championship for a midfielder who is destined for the top flight.

The chance to take Peck back down to the capital should also put West Ham ahead of some other suitors, too. The 22-year-old came through at Arsenal before signing for Sheffield United. It may be a stretch, but it could work in their favour.

The Premier League is the most lucrative division in England. Promotion can earn a side £200 million in extra income from TV deals and commercial fees.

It's well-known that the longer a club stays in the Championship, the harder it is to get out of it. The likes of Norwich City, Watford and West Brom have flailed around in this division for a little while now; Sheffield United look to be becoming the next team to.

West Ham cannot afford for that to happen, especially given the size and stature of their club and the ground they play in. No side has a divine right to get promoted, but if the 62.500-capacity London Stadium remains a second-tier ground, that could end up being detrimental to the Hammers' revenue.

Promotion is a must this season, and with Scott Parker at the helm, they'll need to back him as much as they can in the winter transfer window at the turn of the new year. Adding Peck would be a real statement of intent.
