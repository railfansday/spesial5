Rangers icon Scott Arfield turns to Michael Beale after landing East Fife job

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

He broke through at Falkirk when John Hughes was in charge, played for five years under Sean Dyche at Burnley, helped Steven Gerrard to lift the Scottish title at Rangers, was a member of the Giovanni van Bronckhorst side that reached the Europa League final and starred as John McGlynn steered his first club back into the Premiership.

So Scott Arfield, who was officially unveiled as the new player-manager of William Hill League One outfit East Fife at New Bayview this afternoon, has no shortage of top-class coaches to turn to for advice about how to succeed in the dugout in the months ahead.

It was something of a surprise, then, when the midfielder revealed who he has contacted since agreeing to take over from the inimitable Dick Campbell in the Methil hotseat and who he hopes to emulate going forward.

“I have spoken to previous managers,” he said. “The one I have spoken to the most and as recently as last night is John Hughes. He has worked in part-time football before so he knows everything about it. I have known him since I was 15 and he has been giving me great pointers.

“But Michael Beale is definitely another one. He was the first person I spoke to when I got the job at Livingston (Arfield was appointed caretaker at Almondvale towards the end of last season). He is the same as John. He worked in academy football at Liverpool and Chelsea. He gave me an analysis.”

Beale, despite being first-team coach under Gerrard when Rangers were crowned Scottish champions for the first time in 10 years in 2021, is hardly held in high regard by fans of the Ibrox club these days.

The Englishman, who is now at Zulte Waregem in Belgium, struggled when he was manager in Govan and was sacked after less than a year in the role. However, he left a lasting impression on one player who became a firm favourite with supporters during his time with his boyhood heroes.

So what was it that Arfield liked about working with him? “His variety,” he said. “I was down at Burnley for five years, where they had a brilliant management team. But it was very structured in terms of Monday to Friday and how you prepared for games and debriefed a game.

“Mick perhaps didn’t have the reputation at that time. Steven was the authority figure. I wouldn’t say he was in the background as such. He was looking over things from a vantage point. But Mick was hands-on.

“His variability and ability to make everyone enthused and involved in the drill was just incredible. His knowledge of football and the variety of his training drills was amazing. It didn’t matter if you were a left-back or a centre-forward, everyone was all in, switched on and really focused on the training drills. That was my lasting impression.”

New East Fife player-manager Scott Arfield at New Bayview today (Image: Mark Runnacles / Shutterstock)

Arfield added, “He was absolutely delighted when I phoned him. He knows you cannot just go in at the top. There’s so many players who have gone from my position and even higher (into lower-tier teams). There’s very few players who can go straight in (at a high level).

“Look at Steven Gerrard, one of the most iconic footballers there’s ever been. He had to go into an academy at first. He was obviously willing to go into an academy and actually sit there looking at kids progressing. He got experience and then came up and won a league in Scotland with the help of Mick.

“Mick was delighted when I called him. He completely and utterly understands Scottish football at all levels because of the cup games we were involved in down the years. He knows it is a great situation. I think I am one of the first to get into management from that core group, so he is delighted to see it happen.”

Arfield also intends to get in touch with Gerrard, who took to Instagram yesterday to congratulate his former charge on his appointment at East Fife, in the coming days.

He admitted there are aspects of the legendary Liverpool and England captain’s management style that he will be borrowing in the weeks and months ahead.

"I certainly will be picking his brains,” he said. “I loved Steven's calmness as a manager. I absolutely loved it. When he spoke, people listened. I thought his demeanour was (fantastic).

“There were so many times he could have lost his head, but I think if you do that, if you're too emotional, players sometimes switch off, especially in this day and age. So the calm, authoritative figure is something that I'll be trying to keep.

“It does not matter at what level you are at, you need to get the best out of players. It is an important trait. It could be the most important thing you have got. I think I can get people on board and progress their football career and their ability.”

Arfield got a brief taste of management last season when he took over from Marvin Bartley at Livingston on an interim basis for the final three games of a difficult season. He was pleased to discover that he revelled in the position.

“I just loved everything about it,” he said. “I loved the chaos of it. I loved getting thrown into it. I have spoken to so many ex-players who have told me they thought they would want it and then when they got thrown into it found they didn’t want it.

Scott Arfield is unveiled as the new player-manager of East Fife (Image: Mark Runnacles / Shutterstock)

“It went the opposite way with me. It just ignited something in me that I knew was in there. But I didn’t know how big the fire was. When I was looking back over the summer at all these valuable experiences - dictating things, coming in at half-time and trying to assess the problems, seeing it from a completely different side from playing – I recognised that it ignited something in me.

“I was just waiting for the right opportunity. I didn’t want to rush into any opportunity, hence the reason why I am here in November. It is the perfect scenario and hopefully I can repay the faith that East Fife showed in me.”

Following in the footsteps of Campbell, one of the most successful lower-league managers in Scottish football history, will not be easy. But Arfield, who intends to play when he gets his match fitness and sharpness up to the required level, is undaunted by the prospect.

“I have never crossed paths with Dick,” he said. “But I have met loads of players throughout my career who have great stories about him. Listen, we have probably got different ideas about how the game should be played. But there is one ball and two goals.

“Belief in players is a big thing. You see it everywhere in Scottish football. If somebody can come in and give you a different dimension it is a good thing. There will be players here who will probably not be able to do what I am asking. They will find it tough. But I need to assess the team. Everybody is on a blank canvas.

“Whether they are good enough, whether I will need to bring players in in January to get to where I am wanting to go, we will see. The previous management team thought the players were good enough for their style. But it will be a different style.

“Obviously I will be telling the players what I demand and expect. But I will still tell them to play here how they would play with their mates – with that freedom. Football is an entertainment business and I want players to enjoy it.”

Arfield is optimistic that his contacts in the Scottish and English game, including at Rangers, will be invaluable to him if he feels that he has to strengthen his squad at East Fife during the January transfer window.

“I remember when I was at Falkirk first time around,” he said. “Yogi [Hughes] and Brian Rice at Falkirk travelled all about the country with not a big budget whatsoever. They were ducking and diving a bit like Del Boy to try and get the best deals. So that'll be my thought process, to recruit the best players.

“The best thing you can give young players is a platform to perform and get eyes on them. Under-23 football down the road and reserve team and B team football just doesn't quite give you what men's football gives you.

Scott Arfield is unveiled as the new player-manager of East Fife by chairman Liam Anderson (Image: Mark Runnacles / Shutterstock)

“So, that's obviously the scenario. That's why teams with high hopes for their prospects give them to teams like this - to put eyes on them and to get their talents growing at a faster rate than what they would get. I'll certainly be trying to use my contacts for the best players.”

Arfield continued, “We've got these Hearts boys (Connor Dow and Liam Walker) here, but they’re not getting enough eyes on them and they’re not growing. So Hearts are obviously going to be thinking, ‘Why are we giving you these players where we could just keep them training here?’.

“There's no obligation on me to play them, but they're at Hearts for a reason. They'll certainly have a point to prove with me. As I say, they're obviously there for a reason. Whether they can get up to a physicality to play in this league is a different matter.

“But the technical ability is there. That's another thing I need to look at - to see if they're good enough to put the current team out.”
