Morgan Rogers 'endangered opponent' in 'unacceptable' Chelsea vs Sunderland incident - ex-PGMOL chief

Morgan Rogers was fortunate not to see at least a yellow card after catching Trai Hume with his knee as Chelsea lost 1-0 to Sunderland on Sunday.

That is the opinion of former head of PGMOL and FIFA referee, Keith Hackett, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

Enzo Le Fee's first-half penalty ultimately separated the sides after what was a game packed with controversial decisions.

Sunderland's penalty was heavily disputed after Brian Brobbey looked to have taken hold of Levi Colwill's shirt before the Chelsea defender was penalised.

The Blues felt that they should have had a penalty just minutes before, when Cole Palmer was taken out by Luke O'Nien on the edge of the box.

Rogers has enjoyed a superb start to life at Chelsea since completing the £117million move, but he was perhaps fortunate to complete the 90 minutes on Sunday.

Chelsea's frustration grew as the second-half went on, with Sunderland holding strong and continuing to dominate much of the action.

Some of those frustrations appeared to boil over when Rogers walked past a downed Hume during a break in play.

The defender was on his haunches when the Chelsea player walked past, knocking his knee into his opponent's head.

Rogers was immediately apologetic, and referee Jarred Gillett did not dish out any punishment.

"This is unacceptable behaviour which is clearly endangering the safety of an opponent," Hackett told Football Insider.

"This should have been brought to the attention of the referee because it certainly required a sanction.

"The minimum sanction should be a yellow card, because of a lack of excessive force."

Interestingly, Rogers and Hume have come to blows before at the Stadium of Light.

Last season, the Sunderland defender launched the then-Aston Villa man into the air with a crunching challenge that did not even draw a foul.

Replays showed that the tackle had certainly been excessive, with the England international unlikely to have forgotten it.

The Black Cats would later post a video of the tackle on social media, drawing a response from Rogers.

"Great message to send out. Dangerous tackles getting highlight reels," he wrote.
