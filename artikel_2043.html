On 18 July 2025, Sean Longstaff moved from Newcastle United to Leeds United.

The Geordie midfielder had been a mainstay under Eddie Howe and across the Premier League seasons of 2022-23 and 2023-24 seasons he had featured in 68 of the 76 league games, starting 58 of them.

However, in what proved to be his final Newcastle United season, Sean Longstaff only started eight Premier League matches across the 2024-25 campaign and made 17 mostly brief sub appearances.

In total, Sean Longstaff only played 788 Premier League minutes in that final NUFC season, the equivalent of less than nine full matches of minutes.

So at the age of 27, the midfielder left Newcastle United to get more regular first team football with Leeds United, the Elland Road club paying a reported £12m.

Having turned 29 last month, Sean Longstaff has been reflecting on how things have gone so far in the 16 months since he moved to Leeds United.

The former Newcastle player has only started 11 of the 48 Leeds matches in the Premier League since his move, including only one of 10 this season, spending most of his time watching from the bench…

Sean Longstaff speaking to The Official Leeds United Podcast: “I would be lying if I said it has been the easiest year I’ve ever had. It’s been a tough year. There has probably been times, at home especially, where you are not that nice to be around. Ultimately, I made the decision to come to Leeds. I took my family away from the comforts of being at their home. I have taken a newborn baby away from his grandparents to bring him down here for me to enhance my career. Obviously, at the minute, it’s not gone probably to the perfect plan. But I don’t think life ever does.”

Sean Longstaff is not giving up though: “I take training as serious as possible. Whether that’s the day after a game, whether it is during the week, you have got to train as if you’re going to play. Then ultimately, if you don’t play, that’s not in my control. I can’t control it. All I can control is making sure that I’m fit enough to play when I get a chance, make sure I’m sharp enough, make sure my mentality is right. If I get a chance I want to try and show what I can do, whether that’s for 30 seconds, come on at half-time, or whatever the case may be, I can’t control other people’s decisions, all I can control is the controllables.”

Sean Longstaff comparing and contrasting the two clubs: “It is not until you probably get down here, everyone says Leeds is a massive club, but until you get here, you don’t realise how big it is. I came with an expectation of ‘it’s going to be Newcastle’s this and Leeds is that’….when you get in, you think ‘oh actually, Leeds is so similar. Apart from the kit, everything else felt exactly the same. Obviously the only difference is me being from Newcastle and not from Leeds, but actually got to know a lot of people from the city and…the way they speak about Leeds is the way I felt about Newcastle. The more you meet people, the more you realise it’s massive.”
