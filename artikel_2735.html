The ‘Madden’ movie is a steaming pile of turducken

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Madden released Wednesday on Amazon Prime, and it's one of the most confusingly stupid entertainment products to come out in 2026. It's a movie completely confused about what it wants to be, unsure of basic details, and feels thrown together in such a cursory manner that it's as if it was only created as a payday for those involved.

The movie opens on the tail end of John Madden's run as head coach of the Oakland Raiders in 1977 at a time when pressure was on to win a Super Bowl after flaming out in the playoffs in four consecutive seasons. It's established quickly that Madden is a brilliant coach, but viewed as incapable of winning the big one.

Considering the significant setup this moment is given, it's almost alarming how quickly the movie then skips through the AFC Championship game against Terry Bradshaw and the Steelers, before giving approximately 90 seconds of screen time to Madden's victory in Super Bowl XI over the Vikings. It's a blink-and-you'll-miss-it moment, that moves from the pre-game, to the celebration — with almost no football in between.

Madden is not a football movie, though the sport dominates so much of the narrative. It's also not really a character study of John Madden himself. It's not a drama, nor is it a comedy — it just … exists. There is an astonishing amount of whiplash taking place throughout the movie, in which one second John will be having a dramatic scene with his wife Virginia over failing his family in service of Al Davis and the Raiders, then the next will be a moment of comedy between Madden and his fictional neighbor Vince, who is played by Shane Gillis, who does absolutely nothing to be anything but Shane Gillis.

This back-and-forth between drama and comedy happens through the movie in such a jarring way that it becomes difficult to even focus on the narrative. None of that is helped by Nicolas Cage's performance, which is so horrible it defies belief. It's undoubtedly difficult to play a real person, but Cage's rendition of John Madden is more akin to Jimmy Stewart after oral surgery than John Madden. This is a cast full of incredible actors, but the only one who seemed to take the assignment seriously was Christian Bale, who legitimately turns in a brilliant performance as Al Davis, in spite of the script being a total mess.

In its almost two-hour runtime Madden takes us from John's time as a coach, through being lost in retirement, beginning his broadcast career, and into becoming the face of the iconic Madden NFL video game franchise. The issue is that it gets basic details completely wrong, in a rather alarming way.

Comedian John Mulaney plays Trip Hawkins, the founder of Electronic Arts. We first see him on the field at Super Bowl XI, before he introduces himself to John Madden and Al Davis at a celebration afterwards to pitch them on his idea for Madden Football. Not only does this meeting make no sense, but it didn't happen. Hawkins was fresh out of college in 1977, he didn't have any influence to make it onto the field of the Super Bowl, and he sure as hell didn't meet Madden and Davis at the time. It wasn't until 1978 that Hawkins began a career in computing with Apple, and he didn't leave the company until 1982. It wasn't until 1983 that Hawkins first met John Madden, a full six years after the movie suggests.

The artistic license of this moment is critical because it's not a side note of the story, but one of the primary conceits of the film. Getting these details wrong is astonishing, and calls into doubt most of the events shown. When Hawkins is rejected in 1977, Madden suggests he "ask Joe Montana" to be the namesake for the game. Montana did not take his first snap in the NFL until 1979.

A technique the movie goes back to again, and again, and again is John Madden drawing up plays on the screen, as if he's performing a telestrator routine just for the viewer. None of these work. They're either overly simplistic for football fans, or confusing to non-football fans. They offer nothing outside of allowing Nicolas Cage to chew scenery and riff, never being good at either.

We're left with a hollow, vacuous movie that seemingly exists only as a cash grab. There is no other explanation for how a director of David O. Russell's caliber (Silver Linings Playbook, The Fighter, American Hustle) turned in what feels like a rushed, lackluster, mediocre film devoid of any redeeming elements. If you want to waste two hours of your time just to laugh at Nicolas Cage trying to be John Madden, go for it — but there is nothing else about this movie that makes it worth your time.

Madden is available on Amazon Prime Video, if you hate yourself enough to watch it.
