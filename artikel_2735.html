Chase Home Lending CEO says he plans to 'go on offense' against non-bank lenders

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A version of this article first appeared in the CNBC Property Play newsletter with Diana Olick. Mortgage rates shot higher again last week, after four straight weeks of declines. A hotter-than-expected monthly inflation report, coupled with new headlines from the Iran war, had the average rate on the 30-year fixed mortgage up 33 basis points to 7.5%, according to Mortgage News Daily.

Total mortgage demand, including both refinancing and new purchase loans, is now down just over 39% from a year ago, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association. Despite all that, Sean Grzebin, the CEO of Chase Home Lending, the nation's largest bank mortgage lender, said he's hiring and bent on increasing market share.

"For the last several years, we haven't really added a lot of salespeople," said Grzebin, who admits plainly that this is the worst market in decades. "We're going to hire a bunch of home lending advisors across the United States. We're going to focus on areas where we haven't had a lot of coverage, but there's a lot of mortgage activity and transactions taking place, and it really matches the expansion that we're having within the branch network on the consumer bank side."

Grzebin said he is looking to take back market share from non-bank lenders, who flourished after the financial crisis, when banks were cleaning up from thousands of failed home loans. In August, JPMorganChase announced a 40% increase in home lending capital through 2035 to $750 billion. It will also hire 850 new lending agents.

"I think the non-banks are all very formidable competitors. I think they've had an advantage while the banks were distracted through several decades," he said. "I think now you're starting to see the resurgence of the banks, and I think a lot of banks are actually going to grow their mortgage business through this."

He said he expects to see continued consolidation in the mortgage market, especially as artificial intelligence penetrates the origination part of the business. Chase is investing heavily in AI for underwriting, he said.

"We're hiring mainly in our sales functions because we think we can grow by adding people that have relationships with existing customers today, as well as them taking advantage of our branch network to help grow their business on one side," explained Grzebin. "And we think we can achieve all this growth without adding any incremental headcount in our operation because of the streamlining of the process that's happening with AI."

Chase just ranked highest in mortgage origination satisfaction in J.D. Power's annual U.S. Mortgage Origination Satisfaction Study. While overall satisfaction was unchanged from 2025, just 58% of borrowers said they receive same-day responses from their lender when they ask questions, down from 62% a year ago. Fewer borrowers also say they have a main point of contact, down to 88% from 91% a year ago.

"Borrowers appreciate being able to handle more of the mortgage process online, but they still want someone they can turn to when they have questions," said Bruce Gehrke, senior director of wealth and lending intelligence at J.D. Power in a release. "J.D. Power is seeing less recognition of updates and guidance that are important to keeping borrowers informed. Lenders have an opportunity to make the process more convenient while ensuring borrowers feel supported, especially as they approach closing."

That appears to be what Chase is leaning into as so much of the process now moves to agentic AI. Grzebin said they focused first on the closing process, which customers said was most frustrating. Then they moved to digital tools.

"There's a lot of opportunity there that we're going to be able to take advantage of in the next 12 to 24 months, just making it easier for customers to do more self-service through AI capabilities that we're going to also roll out over the next couple quarters, and so we are invested heavily in AI," he said. "We're going to see some real efficiency from it, and then on the sales side, adding salespeople, but then also figuring out a way to make them more productive."

Servicing is also becoming a bigger factor again, as mortgage delinquencies slowly rise, especially in the FHA space. These low-down-payment loans generally carry higher risk, and as rates stay stubbornly high, those risks are starting to emerge. Chase does do FHA lending, but it is a smaller part of its business.

"I think others are going to have other problems to deal with, particularly if there's any unemployment, and we're just going to be in good shape for that entire cycle. So we look at it as a chance to really go on offense," he said, specifically in FHA lending.

"The non-banks stepped into it and served that market, and that business is being challenged right now, and so we're trying to get back into it," Grzebin said. "We'd love to get back into it. We're priced competitively to get back into it."
