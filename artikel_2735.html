Predicting Where Every Clippers Veteran Could Land Before The NBA Trade Deadline

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

After everything that happened with Kawhi Leonard over the summer, we knew this was going to be a difficult season for the Los Angeles Clippers, but I don't think many people expected them to be this bad. They're 4-12 and 13th in the Western Conference, have lost four straight games, and haven't won a single game on the road.

Even Brandon Ingram's return on Monday couldn't change much, as the Clippers lost 116-104 to the Rockets despite Darius Garland going for 26 points and eight assists, while Ingram had 16 points and seven rebounds in his season debut.

And now, it looks like almost everyone on this roster could be playing somewhere else by February. ESPN's Tim MacMahon said on Monday's episode of The Hoop Collective that rookie Keaton Wagler might be the only player the Clippers aren't willing to move, while Marc Stein reported that the team has been giving minutes to veterans like Rui Hachimura, Derrick Jones Jr., Brook Lopez, and Max Strus with the trade market in mind.

It makes a lot of sense when we consider that the Clippers lost five first-round picks from 2029 through 2033 as part of their punishment for the Kawhi situation. They desperately need draft capital, and there are quite a few players on this roster who could help playoff teams right now.

But I also don't think trading everyone would be the best idea. Some players are still young enough to build around, others have very little trade value right now, and Wagler will need some talent next to him as he develops.

So, let's predict which players will still be on the Clippers after the February trade deadline and where the rest of their veterans could end up.

Staying: Keaton Wagler, Brandon Ingram, Bradley Beal, Isaiah Jackson, Gradey Dick, Kobe Sanders, Jordan Miller, Yanic Konan Niederhauser, Baba Miller

Leaving: Darius Garland, Rui Hachimura, Derrick Jones Jr., Brook Lopez, Max Strus, Kris Dunn

I'd keep almost all of the young guys for now. Gradey Dick is still 23, Isaiah Jackson is 24, and Kobe Sanders has been giving the Clippers 7.8 points and 2.3 assists in limited minutes. With the way this season is going, why not give them 25 minutes a night and see what happens?

And the same goes for Wagler, who's averaging 11.2 points, 3.5 rebounds, and 2.9 assists in just 21.8 minutes per game. The No. 5 pick in the 2026 NBA Draft should probably be starting and running a good part of the offense once the Clippers make some trades. They don't have many chances to draft someone with his potential over the next few years, so getting his development right should be their biggest priority.

Brandon Ingram is probably the hardest decision here. He averaged 21.5 points, 5.6 rebounds, and 3.7 assists with the Raptors last season and made his second All-Star team, but he's literally played one game since coming back from a partially torn Achilles. Who's going to give the Clippers a good package for him right now?

He's making $40.0 million this season and has a $41.9 million player option for next year. I'd much rather give him a few months to get back to playing at an All-Star level and see what his market looks like in the summer. And at 29, he could still be part of the team for a few more seasons if things work out.

Then there's Bradley Beal, who I'm not even sure has a trade market at this point. He's 33, hasn't played this season due to a right knee injury, and played only six games last year with averages of 8.2 points. His $6.4 million salary isn't a problem, but his health is. I could see a buyout eventually happening, although I don't expect anyone to give up much to get him before the deadline.

That leaves six veterans I'd be looking to move, and I think there are some very good destinations for all of them.

If there's one team I'd love to see Darius Garland on, it's the Hornets. I've watched enough of their offense this season to know that they desperately need a point guard, and I think Garland would be a great player to put next to Brandon Miller and Kon Knueppel for the next few years.

The Hornets are 4-12 and 13th in the Eastern Conference, and losing LaMelo Ball has made their offense so much worse. Coby White is a good scorer, but asking him to run the team every night has been a problem, and Miller has had to create way too much by himself. Knueppel's hamstring injury hasn't helped either, although the bigger problem will still be there when he returns.

Garland is averaging 21.3 points, 2.1 rebounds, and 6.6 assists while making 2.6 threes per game for the Clippers. He's also played all 16 games, which is great to see after some of the injuries he's had over the years. And he's only 26, so it's not like the Hornets would be trading for someone who's going to be past his prime by the time their young players are ready to compete.

Just imagine how much easier scoring would become for Miller and Knueppel with Garland running the pick-and-roll. Both wings could get more catch-and-shoot opportunities instead of having to create everything off the dribble, and the Hornets would have three young perimeter players who can shoot.

The $42.2 million salary is where things get complicated, with another $44.9 million coming next season. I'd also be very careful with Charlotte's own 2027 first-round pick, because with how badly this season has started, that could end up being a very valuable selection. But they have other contracts and picks they could use to put together an offer.

For the Clippers, Garland is probably their best chance to get multiple valuable assets at this deadline. Trading him would also open up the starting point guard spot for Wagler, so if the Hornets are willing to pay a good price, I'd do it.

If I'm the Spurs, I'm already looking at potential playoff matchups rather than worrying too much about the regular season. They're 15-3, have the second-best defensive rating in the NBA, and have enough offensive talent to win games against almost anyone. But how are they going to defend guys like Luka Doncic, Kevin Durant, or Anthony Edwards over a seven-game series?

That's where I'd love to have Derrick Jones Jr. He's 6-foot-6 with a 7-foot wingspan, can switch onto guards and forwards, and has enough athleticism to recover when someone gets past him. Having Jones available would also allow Stephon Castle to focus more on defending opposing point guards instead of constantly having to deal with bigger wings.

And Jones has been having a good offensive season too, with 10.1 points, 4.5 rebounds, and 1.3 assists per game. Between De'Aaron Fox's speed in transition and Victor Wembanyama drawing so much attention inside, I could see Jones getting quite a few easy baskets with the Spurs.

Now, I wouldn't go crazy with the trade package. Jones is 29 and has never been a consistent three-point threat throughout his career, which could become a problem against teams that are comfortable leaving him open. But for $10.5 million this season, getting someone who could close out playoff games depending on the matchup is worth exploring.

The Clippers desperately need draft picks, and the Spurs have plenty of them. I'd try to get Jones without giving up a first-round pick, but I also wouldn't mind offering some decent draft compensation for a player who could help them win a championship during Wembanyama's rookie contract.

Where did this version of Brook Lopez come from? Because going from 8.5 points per game last season to nearly 15 at 38 years old is something I definitely didn't see coming.

Lopez is averaging 14.9 points, 5.2 rebounds, 1.8 assists, and 1.3 blocks in just 24.4 minutes per game, shooting 54.8% from the field and 43.1% from three. Between November 4 and November 21, he had six 20-point games in eight appearances, including 23 points on five made threes against Wembanyama and the Spurs.

And while he's probably not going to keep shooting over 43.0% from three all year, I think there will be quite a few contenders trying to get him before February.

My favorite one would be the Lakers. They're 15-3 and second in the Western Conference, and Rob Pelinka already said on Friday that they'll be active at the trade deadline. One of their biggest problems has been the center rotation, with Kevon Looney barely playing and Walker Kessler having to do a lot of the work inside.

According to CBS Sports' Sam Quinn, the Lakers have been almost 12 points per 100 possessions worse without Kessler on the floor, so getting another good center should probably be one of Pelinka's priorities.

And just think about how different those minutes could look with Lopez playing next to Luka Doncic or Austin Reaves. Instead of having another center standing near the basket, they'd have someone who can pull the opposing big man out to the three-point line, while still giving them some rim protection on defense.

I'd even try a few lineups with Kessler and Lopez together against bigger teams, although getting 20 good minutes from Brook off the bench would already be enough.

Lopez is making $9.2 million on an expiring contract, and the Lakers still have three second-round picks they could use. If I'm the Clippers, I'm taking advantage of this unexpected run from Brook and seeing what the Lakers would be willing to give me for him.

Rui Hachimura has been one of the few players actually having a good season with the Clippers, and I wouldn't be surprised if several playoff teams called about him.

He's putting up 14.4 points, 5.9 rebounds, and 1.6 assists on 43.6% from the field and 41.3% from three. And the part I like most is that he's taking 5.4 threes per game, so that percentage isn't coming from making one open corner three every other night.

I'd send him to the Pistons, who are 10-6 and fifth in the Eastern Conference. They've had some injuries in the frontcourt with Jalen Duren missing time, and I still think they could use another forward who can shoot next to Cade Cunningham.

The Pistons rank 19th in the NBA with 13.0 made threes per game, and we've seen how difficult spacing can get when Ausar Thompson and Duren share the floor. Having someone like Hachimura at the four would give Cunningham another option in the corners, while Rui could also score against smaller guards when defenses switch.

And I wouldn't even need him to start over John Collins to make this trade. Having both together would give J.B. Bickerstaff some different options at the small ball with Collins playing center, and there will be nights when Rui's shooting is exactly what Detroit needs.

His contract is also one of the reasons I like this move so much. He's making $14.0 million this season with a $14.0 million team option for 2027-28, so the Pistons could keep him for another year at the same price if everything works out.

Detroit is at a point where it should be thinking about how to win a playoff series or two, and Hachimura is the kind of player who could help without costing the franchise a ridiculous amount of money or picks.

I'm still waiting for the Magic to become a consistently good offensive team, because with the talent they have, being 7-9 and eighth in the Eastern Conference after one month is a little disappointing.

They're shooting 35.4% from three, ranking 15th in the NBA, while Franz Wagner is making just 25.0% of his outside shots. Franz has been incredible so far this season, though, and I'd love to give him another shooter who can move without the ball instead of just standing in the corner.

Max Strus is having a somewhat strange season with the Clippers, averaging 9.0 points, 3.4 rebounds, and 3.3 assists in 24.3 minutes per game. He's shooting only 37.7% from the field, but he's still making 2.1 threes per night at a 36.2% clip.

I don't think Orlando would need much more than that from him. Strus could run around screens, get open shots from Wagner and Paolo Banchero once he returns, and help the offense when one of them sits. He can also make the extra pass when defenses close out on him, and those 3.3 assists per game show he can do a little more than just shoot.

And we already saw him start 22 playoff games for the Heat during their 2023 NBA Finals run, so I'd have no problem trusting him in a playoff rotation instead of throwing younger, unproven draftees as they did in recent seasons.

The $16.7 million expiring salary is a little high for his current production, and I'd definitely avoid giving up a first-round pick to get him. But if the Magic can get him for matching salary and a couple of second-round picks, I think he'd be a good addition.

The Clippers would also have more minutes available for Gradey Dick, Kobe Sanders, and some of their other young wings, which should be a priority for them anyway.

Okay, this one might be a little strange considering the Jazz are 3-16 and 14th in the Western Conference, but there's a reason I'd send Kris Dunn there instead of another playoff team.

According to The Salt Lake Tribune, the Jazz have already had some trade discussions with the Clippers involving their big men and wings, so we know the two front offices have been talking. And while Dunn hasn't been directly linked to those discussions, I think he's someone the Jazz should ask about.

He's averaging 7.1 points, 3.9 rebounds, 4.1 assists, and 1.9 steals per game, and Utah could use someone like him next to Keyonte George and Darryn Peterson. Both young guards are taking a lot of shots and still have plenty to learn defensively, while Dunn could take the tougher guard matchups and help organize the offense.

We also shouldn't forget that he spent parts of two seasons with the Jazz before joining the Clippers in 2024, so he'd be going back to a franchise he already knows.

Now, why would a 3-16 team trade for a 32-year-old guard? Well, the Jazz have some complicated rights involving their 2027 first-round pick, and the new lottery rules also limit what they can get after drafting inside the top five in each of the last two years. Losing another 50 games won't necessarily give them the draft position they'd want.

I'm not saying Dunn is going to save their season, but having a good defensive guard around Peterson and George could help their development, and at $5.7 million on an expiring deal, he wouldn't cost much.

I'd offer a second-round pick and matching salary at most, which could be enough for the Clippers if they're trying to open up minutes for Wagler.

And if the Clippers manage to pull off most of these moves, they could come out of the deadline with more draft picks, a few younger players, and a much better opportunity to develop Wagler. After losing five first-round selections and starting the season 4-12, I'd take that over trying to save this roster any day.

One thing I'd love to see from the Clippers is how creative they can get as a third team in some of the bigger trades around the league. Not every move needs to involve one of their veterans going directly to a contender. There could be opportunities to take on a bad contract from another team and get a first-round pick for doing so, which I'd have no problem with as long as that money comes off the books within the next two seasons.

I'd also be careful about making too many trades right away. Once we get closer to February, there will be playoff teams dealing with injuries, others trying to get below the luxury tax, and contenders realizing they're a player or two away from having a much better rotation. That's when someone like Derrick Jones Jr. could become more expensive than he is today.

And for once, I'd like to see the Clippers go into an offseason without trying to put together another team full of former All-Stars. Give the younger guys a chance, get the financial situation under control, and see what this franchise looks like when it isn't trying to compete for a championship every single season.
