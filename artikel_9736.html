ProWritingAid VS Grammarly: Which Grammar Checker is Better in (2022) ?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Grammarly is a tool that checks for grammatical errors, spelling, and punctuation.it gives you comprehensive feedback on your writing. You can use this tool to proofread and edit articles, blog posts, emails, etc.

Grammarly also detects all types of mistakes, including sentence structure issues and misused words. It also gives you suggestions on style changes, punctuation, spelling, and grammar all are in real-time. The free version covers the basics like identifying grammar and spelling mistakes

whereas the Premium version offers a lot more functionality, it detects plagiarism in your content, suggests word choice, or adds fluency to it.

ProWritingAid is a style and grammar checker for content creators and writers. It helps to optimize word choice, punctuation errors, and common grammar mistakes, providing detailed reports to help you improve your writing.

ProWritingAid can be used as an add-on to WordPress, Gmail, and Google Docs. The software also offers helpful articles, videos, quizzes, and explanations to help improve your writing.

Sounds like you're biased for Grammarly. Not really that informative if you're already leaning towards one to begin with. Still, it was an interesting read. Tell me, do you use Grammarly with your day-to-day writing/blogging?

I use the paid versions of both Grammarly and Pro Writing Aid. Both of them have issues:- Grammarly IS more focused on nonfiction than fiction. If you're writing fiction, you may find Grammarly's insistence on "Conciseness" annoying. They seem to want to take the soul out of your writing, which is fine for a corporate report or technical manual, but not for your Great American Novel.- Grammarly pushes features on users that are not ready for prime time. It doesn't matter that you never agreed to be a beta tester for them; you get to do it anyway.- Pro Writing Aid actually counts the occurrences of words in your document and tells you when a word has been overused compared to published literature. Grammarly, on the other hand, seems hell-bent to remove certain words that it has deemed overused from the language, whether or not they're actually overused in your document.- Grammarly's Clarity function is simply horrible. It mangles sentences and changes their meaning. Sometimes the result of its suggestion is unintelligible garbage.- Grammarly allows you to turn off some checks, but not all of them. Unfortunately, the ones you cannot turn off are some of the most irritating (such as that horrible "Clarity"). On the other hand, Pro Writing Aid lets you choose which checks you use.- Pro Writing Aid has a mediocre command of American English (it's a British product). The app insists on rules of grammar and spelling that are outright wrong, per Merriam-Webster, the Chicago Manual of Style, and modern usage as shown by Google Books Ngram. At one point, Pro Writing Aid insisted that American English did not include the words "holiday" and "autumn," that "vacation" and "fall" must be used instead.- Pro Writing Aid's rules for commas are 100 percent consistent with the way I learned them in high school--50 years ago! They do not reflect modern usage. Grammarly is much better at this.- Pro Writing Aid seems to waste their limited resources on tweaking their UI. Their UI most definitely could use improvement, but these changes are not great improvements; they only serve to confuse users. Their resources would be better spent fixing the many functional problems in their app.- Some of Pro Writing Aid's checks are completely useless, as they do not take context into consideration. For example, an ordinary word would not be capitalized as part of a narrative, but would be as part of a title (for example, "professor." But if you capitalize correctly for context, Pro Writing Aid dings you for "Inconsistent capitalization." Similar issues exist with hyphenation consistency check.Each of these applications has its strengths as well as considerable weaknesses. I will say that both of them are light-years better than the execrable grammar checker in Microsoft Word, but that's damning with faint praise.
