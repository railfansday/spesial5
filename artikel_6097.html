There are six United credit cards, ranging from a no-annual-fee card to premium personal and business cards with United Club access. Fortunately, figuring out which ones you're eligible for is straightforward.

Holding one United card generally doesn't prevent you from opening another or earning its welcome bonus. Instead, welcome-offer restrictions are largely card-specific: You can’t apply for a card you already hold or earn its welcome bonus if you've received a bonus on that same card in the last 24 months.

There's one exception: If you are an existing United Club Visa Signature cardmember (no longer available to new applicants), you are not eligible for the current United Club card.

You'll need to close the old card and apply for a new Club card 24 months after you received the initial bonus. You won't qualify if you currently hold the business card you're applying for or received its new-cardmember bonus within the past 24 months.

You don't need an LLC or a company with employees to apply. You may qualify with a legitimate sole proprietorship or side hustle, such as freelance work, consulting, pet sitting or reselling.

Chase generally requires business card applicants to be under 5/24. However, once you're approved, a Chase business card typically won't appear on your personal credit report or add another account to your 5/24 total.

Yes, you can have multiple United credit cards at one time. Unlike some other airline credit cards, United's cards don't have a broad restriction that prevents you from holding multiple cobranded cards.

You could hold a for its TravelBank Cash and 10,000-mile award discount, the for lounge access and a for business expenses.

There's also an incentive to pair a personal and business United card: Both United business cards offer 5,000 bonus miles each account anniversary if you're also the primary cardmember on a personal United card.

Holding multiple United cards won't make sense for infrequent flyers, since benefits like free checked bags and priority boarding overlap on multiple cards. However, for loyal customers, the collection of slightly different perks and cardholder PQP bonuses could justify the cost of paying multiple annual fees.

Yes, you can get the same United credit card more than once. However, you will only earn another welcome offer if you no longer hold that card and at least 24 months have passed since you received the new-cardmember bonus.

Remember, Chase counts from the date you receive the bonus, not from when you open or close the account.

If you are an authorized user on someone else's United card, Chase does not consider that being a current cardholder for the purposes of that card's bonus restrictions. In other words, you can still apply for your own version of that card and earn its welcome bonus.

However, an authorized-user account may appear on your credit report and increase your 5/24 count.

If you're denied because of an authorized-user account, you may be able to explain the situation to Chase by calling the reconsideration line, although approval isn't guaranteed.

If you're interested in both a personal and business United card, applying for the business card first often makes sense. That's because the business card won't add to your 5/24 limit and tip you over the edge.

However, if you're applying for multiple personal United cards, the order you apply shouldn't matter. Pick whichever card is your highest priority first, considering its benefits and how its current offer compares with past bonuses.

The best choice depends on how often you fly United and which benefits you'll actually use.
