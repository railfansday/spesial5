Here are the 6 plays that defined NY Giants' 6th win in Jaguars upset

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

EAST RUTHERFORD - The New York Giants are a team, and it's the best explanation as to why they lead the NFC East.

The Dallas Cowboys have the high-powered offense with Dak Prescott throwing the ball all over the lot. Their defense remains the Achilles' heel that has prevented them from winning big games against good teams when it counts.

The Washington Commanders had the momentum and the tiebreaker over the Giants that gives them hope of an improbable comeback in the division, but their coaching staff has made head scratching decisions that have proven costly.

And the Philadelphia Eagles don't dominate the trenches the way they used to.

So the Giants might not be about style points, but the substance of this team matters.

"This is where the season starts," Giants coach John Harbaugh said following Sunday's 23-18 victory over the Jaguars, who lost for just the fourth time in their last 20 regular season games. And 10 of those 16 wins for Jacksonville have come by margins of 14 or more.

"A lot of things we've got to improve on, but I'm proud of our guys. I just love the way they found a way to win the game," Harbaugh said. "You're going to be happy with these things and you're going to be upset with those other things, but you're going to be really ticked off about certain things. But, you work past them because we found a way to win the football game and that's what you've got to do, especially at this point in time."

Here are the six plays that defined the Giants' sixth victory of the season:

Cam Skattebo's fumble could have buried the Giants. J.J. McCarthy would not let it.

As Jaguars safety Antonio Johnson scooped up the loose ball and raced in the opposite direction, a veritable hush fell over what was an outstanding home crowd inside MetLife Stadium. That's when McCarthy met the moment and made a textbook tackle on Johnson, stopping him cold at the Giants' 31.

Phillips' coverage has been inconsistent in his career, but he was outstanding against the Jaguars. He had one pass defensed in the end zone later that was big, but one of the biggest of his career came on fourth-and-1 from the 11 following Skattebo's fumble.

The Jaguars opted to go for it, and Trevor Lawrence threw to his left at the sticks, intended for Jacobi Meyers. Paulson Adebo was tight over the top, which allowed Phillips to break underneath and punch the ball away for a turnover on downs with 1:03 left in the third quarter.

The Giants' offense had not been able to close out the game with the lead since Week 1. There was criticism that they were not aggressive enough down the stretch of the game against Washington, but not this time. Harbaugh put the trust in McCarthy and the passing game, and he threw a 19-yard strike to the rookie Malachi Fields for the first down.

McCarthy's pass just got over Jaguars linebacker Foyesade Oluokun, and that completion set up Skattebo and the offense line a short time later.

The Jaguars scored to pull within eight on Trevor Lawrence's touchdown pass to Brian Thomas Jr. with 6:39 left, leading their coach, Liam Coen, to go for two. Lawrence went to the air again and Kayvon Thibodeaux intercepted the conversion attempt, as he continued to make plays that don't show up on the stat sheet.

"I know it's not a real interception," Thibodeaux said with a laugh. "But it's an interception to me."

When rookie Dominic Zvada pushed a 32-yard field goal attempt wide left, the groans were understandable. It was a "here we go again" scenario brewing for the young kicker, who had rewarded Harbaugh's faith with a pair of 50-yard-plus field goals early in the game.

So when the Giants gave Zvada another chance to redeem himself with a 43-yard field goal early in the fourth quarter, he made the most of it for a 23-9 lead. A 14-point deficit led the Jaguars to several decisions that backfired down the stretch.

Cam Skattebo could not help himself. He atoned for an earlier fumble with consecutive 5-yard runs to put the game away just before the two-minute warning, and after the second one that moved the sticks, Skattebo went to what he considers old reliable as his celebration: a back flip.

That comes just days after Skattebo told reporters that he would likely retire the backflip, at least for now, after Lions star Aidan Hutchinson blew out his knee when he jumped in the air and landed awkwardly celebrating a sack last week.

"When energy calls for it, it calls for it," said Skattebo, whose game-sealing run went behind blocks from center Bryan Hudson and right guard Francis "Sisi" Mauigoa. "My teammates, I do whatever I can to bring the energy for the team. I play this game with my heart and soul, so when that happens, it's second nature and it's hard to hold back from."

Added John Harbaugh of Skattebo, who he trusted to close the game out after his earlier fumble: "He couldn't help himself."
