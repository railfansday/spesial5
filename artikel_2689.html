PGA Tour Boards approve changes to competitive structure, eligibility

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The PGA Tour Boards approved on Nov. 16 the Future Competition Committee's final set of recommendations for a new competitive structure beginning in 2028 and also signed off on a player development program that the Tour said will "strengthen the meritocracy and opportunity at each level."

The Policy Board and the Enterprises Board met in Boca Raton.

The Future Competition Committee was formed in August of 2025 to evaluate the PGA Tour's existing model and strengthen its long-term competitive and commercial future. The Committee's initial set of recommendations were announced in June with the goal of getting the top players competing in the same tournaments more frequently, with a "true promotion and relegation" system.

"Having been on this Tour for almost 30 years, I can confidently say the PGA Tour is in the best position it has ever been," said Future Competition Committee Chairman Tiger Woods in a statement. "We set out last year to build the strongest possible version of the PGA Tour, and by listening to our fans, players and partners, we created a competitive model grounded in meritocracy, built to last and that offers the best opportunity for the next generation of players."

PGA Tour Commissioner Brian Rolapp said during a virtual news conference with the media on Nov. 16 that the 18-month work of the FCC was officially at an end.

"The FCC's process was inclusive, rigorous and highly collaborate," he said. "We held 16 formal FCC meetings in addition to working groups, players meetings, Players Advisory Council discussions and board sessions ... had more than 40 conversations with PGA Tour corporate sponsors, engaged extensively with current and prospective media partners and retained experienced outside experts to help us evaluate competitive models and long-term economics."

"As we go into next season every player now knows what the are playing for and will have the opportunity to earn their spot for 2028," he said.

The two boards approved the model Rolapp laid out on Sept. 15: A Championship Series, with around two dozen events and at least $20 million purses, and a Challenger Series of around 20 tournaments with purses of at least $4 million. The two series will co-exist throughout the season, with the Championship Series culminating in a two-week Tour Championship that will end with a match play event to determine the season champion.

The Championship Series will start with the four major championships (Masters, U.S. Open, PGA Championship, British Open) and The Players Championship, along with familiar stops such as the Arnold Palmer Invitational in Orlando, Cadillac Championship at Doral, The Memorial in Dublin, Ohio, RBC Heritage on Hilton Head Island, S.C., The Travelers in Hartford, Conn., the Truist Championship in Charlotte, N.C., GO by Raymond James in Greensboro, N.C., and The Sentry at Torrey Pines.

Rolapp said bidding for title sponsorship of Championship Series event has ended and he said the Tour has "multiple bids under consideration for the final open PGA Tour Championship Series slots."

He said the Challenger Series "is similarly off to a fast start commercial," with signed agreements for four events and a "line of sight" for venues and partners for half the schedule."

The Tour will unveil its full schedule for the 2028 PGA Tour Championship Series, including sites for the 2028 Tour Championship, on Feb. 22, 2027, during a live Golf Channel broadcast event at PGA Tour headquarters in Ponte Vedra Beach.

In addition to giving players access off the Korn Ferry, the PGA Tour University Rankings of the top college players, and PGA Tour Americas, the Tour will partner with third-party operators on a network of affiliated events with the top players rewarded with competitive benefits throughout the year.

Those events could be domestic or international, with a list developed by next year.

The Tour also will have a unified ranking system for Korn Ferry membership, with the rankings updated on a weekly basis. The rankings will include non-PGA Tour or Korn Ferry Tour members and the top 75 will be exempt into each Korn Ferry Tour field.

The national qualifying tournament, currently held in Ponte Vedra Beach, will continue to provide more opportunties. The top five players in the qualifier will advance to the PGA Tour Challenge Series and the next 25 will earn Korn Ferry Tour membership.

Among the eligible players for the first season will be PGA Tour winners in 2026 and 2027. Those who won majors and The Players will be eligible through 2029; the top 30 players from the 2026 FedEx Cup points list; and the top 70 players from the 2027 FedEx Cup points list.

The members of the Challenger Series includes winners of FedEx Cup Fall and opposite events (held during the weeks of majors or Signature events) in 2027; Nos. 96-175 on the 2027 FedEx Cup Fall points list; Nos. 11-40 on the 2027 Korn Ferry Tour points list; and the top 10 players from the 2027 PGA Tour Q-School.

After 2028, the top 90 on the Championship Series points list will retain their status for 2029, joined by the top 20 on the Championship Series points list Going forward, winners of The Players and majors will get a three-year exemption (reduced from the current five years).

Rolapp also announced that Player Advisory Chairman Lucas Glover will join the PGA Tour Boards, with Adam Scott taking over the seat held by Patrick Cantlay, who is leaving the board. Scott's term will be three years.

He also took note of the end of Jay Monahan's tenure as PGA Tour Commissioner. Monahan had been the Commissioner since 2017 and guided the Tour through rock moments such as the COVID-19 pandemic and LIV Golf making its failed but expensive venture into the global golf landscape.

"His leadership has been guided by his love of the game, and by his love of the Tour," Rolapp said of Monahan. "During the most consequential moments in our sport's history, he never lost sight of what mattered most: strengthening the Tour and positioning it for the future."

Rolapp also paid tribute to NBC golf anchor Dan Hicks, who passed away last week.

"His passion for the game and his warm spirit left a warm impact on everyone who watched," Rolapp said.
