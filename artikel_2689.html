Is this the best cornerbacks room in BYU history?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

This article was first published in the Cougar Insiders newsletter Tuesday. Sign up to receive the newsletter in your inbox each week.

BYU has never defeated Kansas and will make its final regular-season road trip of the season to Lawrence to take on the Jayhawks. On the line is a chance at the conference title game and a perch in the CFP rankings.

The Cougars need star running back LJ Martin to fully return as a weapon. He's slowly returned to carry bigger loads the past two games. His backup Sione Moa, banged up in a win over Baylor needs to heal and get back on the field — but it won't be this week — as Preston Rex showed he has returned and is ready to contribute.

Question of the Week: Is this the best cornerback room in BYU history? Predict BYU at Kansas and give a score.

Jay Drew: I mentioned in an article after the Cougars beat Baylor 28-7 that BYU's defensive performance was even more impressive than it appears because it was without star linebacker Isaiah Glasker and star cornerback Evan Johnson.

The Cougars have been without Johnson since the UCF game, but have only lost once since then — at Utah. Coincidentally, one of the most pivotal plays in that game was the defensive pass interference penalty called on Johnson's replacement, Jayven Williams. I spoke to cornerbacks coach Lewis Walker last week, and he said it was not a good call.

Williams, in my opinion, has twice been the victim of poor calls, including one in the Arizona State game. However, the Mississippi State transfer has been a godsend for BYU, combining with Tre Alexander and Johnson (when he's healthy) to give the Cougars one of the best cornerback rooms they've ever had.

Arizona State Sun Devils wide receiver Omarion Miller (4) taunts BYU Cougars cornerback Jayven Williams (15) after making a catch for a touchdown during a game at LaVell Edwards Stadium in Provo on Saturday, October 31, 2026. | Lukas Katilius, Deseret News

It is the best ever? Possibly. If there's a knock on the Williams-Alexander tandem, it is that they don't come up with interceptions as frequently as some of their predecessors.

They are both good tacklers, though. That skill goes a long way for a cornerback. It should also be mentioned that Cannon DeVries has been solid as a nickel back, and that Matthias Leach and Jordyn Criss have contributed well in Johnson's absence.

Obviously, the corners will be needed to play well Saturday, as Kansas quarterback Isaiah Marshall is a very good dual-threat QB, and has hurt teams with his arm and his legs. This will be a tough test for the Cougars, on the road in Lawrence, Kansas.

Dick Harmon: A good indication of how good BYU's cornerback room is this year is how Jayven Williams is playing. He's a hard-hitter and can cover. After Evan Johnson went down in the overtime loss to UCF on the road, ASU went right after Williams and time and again he had pass breakups and outstanding coverage. Alexander is an elite cover guy and can bring threats to the ground.

I covered the 1996 Cotton Bowl team with Omarr Morgan and Tim McTyer. They were both athletes from inner Los Angeles City and for years, perhaps decades, were the standard for pass cover corners at BYU. But this group is just as good, even better.

They have the speed, they are taller, have greater reach, and are well-coached. They are well-drilled to make great decisions. They are asked to do a lot in Kelly Poppinga's defense. That Williams has come in for Johnson and you've seen very little change at all in the effectiveness of what Poppinga runs, is a testament to their talent.

I like the swagger and confidence of this group. I like that they can easily go four or five deep and still play at near-elite level. I'd like to see more interceptions out of them and they've had their chances, the latest an Alexander ball through his hands against Baylor. He was in perfect position.

BYU has never beaten Kansas. This won't be an easy game. The Cougars' two losses have both come on the road at Orlando and Salt Lake City. But a team that can play defense like we saw in Provo against Baylor last week always has a chance.

Kansas has a great run game; a top-10 rushing attack with a mobile quarterback. BYU has had more total yards than Kansas in all three losses. The difference has been BYU turning the ball over (-5). It will take a great effort, even if nobody is picking Kansas to pull off an upset. This is the kind of November game where outstanding teams make their mark and put down the challenger. BYU needs that kind of outing.

It's easy to fix BYU's third-quarter droughts.

Don't take your foot off the gas pedal on not to lose play calling until/unless the other mounts a comeback. Football is all about momentum and by then it might be too late to get it back. That is the pattern of BYU play calling and player mentality that is evident in every game, including Baylor, and the reason for the third-quarter letdowns. — Periloustimes

Baylor pressures the QB at a high level. When BYU started cooking it was because they saw that pressure and attacked the holes they left.

The offense sputtered when the run game slowed down.

Bear beat them with his arm, aided by a strong running game. When the running game goes dry the passing game slows also because it's expected and the defense can focus on it more.

That's a very good defense we beat. — JoshfromLondon
