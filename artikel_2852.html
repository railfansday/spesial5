Milwaukee Bucks vs. Portland Trail Blazers Preview & Game Thread: Staying the course

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Milwaukee Bucks are fresh off the front end of a back-to-back as they host the Portland Trail Blazers—who have been waiting in Milwaukee—tonight. In a word, the Bucks are struggling, currently on an 11-game losing streak. Of their last ten losses, only three games saw Milwaukee lose by single digits. They lack consistent center play, are one of the league's worst defensive teams, and lack a viable, consistent first option on offense. Those issues showed up in a particularly grim loss to Miami last night, where Miami led by as much as 30 before the Bucks' garbage-time comeback. In any event, Milwaukee gets another chance to right its wrongs tonight against a Blazers team that has just lost two straight.

The Trail Blazers have started the NBA season well. In their first 14 games, they rank eighth in scoring (117 ppg), fueled by strong offense from Deni Avdija (24.2 PPG) and Damian Lillard (20.1 PPG). They've also been elite on defense, partly because of their premier shot-blocker Donovan Clingan and defensive Swiss Army knife Toumani Camara, but also because of their emphasis on defending the perimeter and cleaning the glass. Portland has held opposing teams to just 33.1% shooting from the field, good for fourth in the league. Portland could really give Milwaukee, which has relied on spurts of momentum rather than consistent production, a real headache if they don't start hot.

Portland is fresh off a two-game losing slide, both on the road in Minnesota. In the latter game, LaMelo Ball stole Clingan's inbound pass and finished it with a fast-break layup to take a one-point lead with less than three seconds left. Portland has slowed down due to injuries—missing star guards Ja Morant and Shaedon Sharpe—and their hot-shooting start has slowed. Although it will be an uphill battle, Milwaukee could certainly upset Portland and earn its second win of the season if a few things go their way.

Deni Avdija: Questionable (foot inflammation)

Despite serving as an elite three-and-D wing for first-year coach Micah Nori and the Trail Blazers, Toumani Camara has struggled with streaky shooting to start the season. Camara shot just 5/26 from the field and 2/15 from three in Portland's back-to-back road losses to Minnesota. Lamelo Ball also denied Camara's go-ahead layup on Tuesday as time expired. He's been having a rough time on offense recently, and when Camara struggles, Portland struggles too because it loses the spacing and offensive versatility he provides. If Camara starts out shooting well, Milwaukee could have a much harder time stopping Portland's dynamic offense.

Who: Milwaukee Bucks (1-14) vs. Portland Trail Blazers (9-5).

Kasparas Jakucionis' hot streak stops, and he's stifled against the Trail Blazers' backcourt. Portland's defense is especially tough. The Bucks' backcourt will have to deal with All-Defensive Second Team member Toumani Camara, defense-first wing Sidy Cissoko, and prolific shot-blocker Donovan "Cling Kong" Clingan. Jakucionis has been playing very well, but I expect him to take a step back against Portland's elite defense.
