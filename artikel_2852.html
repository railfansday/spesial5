Buzz in their ears: Hatton Hornets block out noise as historic run continues

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Nov. 13—HATTON — If a team wants to make a deep playoff run, there's no better place to look than one of the greatest college football coaches of all time.

Following Alabama's 27-19 win over Texas A&M in 2017, Crimson Tide coach Nick Saban was talking to reporters when he said the line that might inspire a Hatton team almost 10 years later.

"All that stuff you write about how good we are, and all that stuff they hear on ESPN. It's like poison," Saban said. "It's like taking poison, rat poison."

Last week, Hatton made a lot of history in a 46-8 first-round beating of New Hope in what the Hornets say was one of the largest home crowds they have seen.

"It was just like I've never seen before," Hatton senior lineman Levi Liles said. "Coming from 2A ball, you don't really get crowds like that."

It was Hatton's first playoff win since 2002 and the largest playoff victory in school history. The Hornets (11-0) have also set school records for wins and points scored (426) in a season.

"Everybody's been talking about it ever since we went undefeated, about how we made history," linebacker Chance Campbell said. "it's been all over social media, around the community, everything."

"Our coaches preach don't eat the rat poison, don't drink the Kool-Aid," Campbell said. "It's OK to be proud of yourself. But at the same time, we know what's ahead of us. We're trying to get to the state championship."

When Hatton got ranked for the first time in 31 years in late September, Hatton coach Nikita Stover, a former Alabama receiver, didn't give it a second thought.

"I played under Coach Saban," he said. "That's rat poison."

To block it all out, Stover said the Hornets emphasize taking things one game at a time.

"We don't overanalyze things or micromanage them," he said. "We're going to have fun."

Said Liles: "If you're not keeping it fun, what's the point of being out here?"

But that hasn't affected their focus. On Wednesdays, they travel 10 minutes to Town Creek and practice on Hazlewood's former home field.

Hazlewood won 11 football state championships from 1970 to 2000 before closing in 2009. Most of that area's students now go to Hatton.

"These guys see those numbers on the wall, they know they're fighting for another one," Stover said Wednesday. "We want a state championship."

On Friday, the Hornets face the Lauderdale County Tigers (8-3) in their first home second-round playoff game since 1993. Campbell joined Hatton from Lauderdale County ahead of this season and now leads a Hatton defense that has allowed just 112 points.

The junior, in 10 games played this season, is averaging 12.4 tackles with a team-leading 124 total tackles, including 22 for loss and three sacks.

"They've been talking all week," Campbell said. "I already know it's going to be a big game for me. It's pretty personal."

Liles said they just need to keep doing the same thing they've done all season.

"We just need to keep doing our thing," he said, "and shouldn't have any problem with them."
