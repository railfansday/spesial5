Advanced Micro Devices vs. Dell Technologies: Comparing Revenue Trends Amid the Artificial Intelligence Boom

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Dell's revenue has consistently outpaced AMD's over the past two years, even as AMD's sales have climbed steadily since late 2024.

By Robert Izquierdo – Nov 23, 2026 at 12:02PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Advanced Micro Devices ›

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +8.32%) makes computer processors and graphics chips. It closed the World Labs acquisition on Nov. 16, and announced a partnership with Meta Platforms on Oct. 27.

AMD's year-over-year quarterly revenue growth rate has stepped up from the low-to-mid 30% range throughout 2025 to an average of 44% across the first three quarters of 2026, with management projecting further acceleration in Q4.

Dell Technologies (DELL +22.61%) makes personal computers, servers, and storage solutions. It partnered with Rackspace on Nov. 17, and collaborated with Mistral AI on Oct. 21.

Dell has seen an even sharper inflection in top-line growth across calendar 2026 as its massive AI server backlog converts into shipments. Its higher absolute revenue total is due to the company selling complete, high-ticket systems -- such as servers, data center hardware, and PCs -- whereas AMD sells individual semiconductor components that go into those systems.

Tracking revenue helps investors measure a company's ability to generate baseline sales before accounting for expenses. This metric reveals whether an organization is successfully attracting customers and growing its overall business volume over time.

A look at the revenue trends for AMD and Dell reveal both are enjoying massive revenue boosts as the artificial intelligence boom accelerates in 2026. With the rise of AI, these companies quickly pivoted away from relying on cyclical PC markets to being anchored by enterprise and hyperscale AI infrastructure expansion.

Thanks to a record $95 billion server backlog, Dell raised its full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to $192 billion, implying approximately 69% annual growth over fiscal 2026. AMD demonstrated a steady, compounding staircase, climbing 74.7% from $7.7 billion in Q4 2024 to $13.4 billion in Q3 2026, with management guiding for roughly $17 billion in Q4 2026.

While Dell generates more than triple AMD's trailing twelve-month revenue (about $151.2 billion vs. $45.4 billion), the revenue trends highlight the difference between a systems integrator, such as Dell, and AMD's fabless chip design approach. Dell converts massive AI server backlog into high-volume revenue at roughly 21% gross margins, exposing it to component cost inflation. Conversely, AMD captures upstream silicon pricing power with 54% gross margins, meaning each incremental dollar of data center revenue flows through to operating income at a much higher rate.
