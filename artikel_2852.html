4 takeaways from Michigan’s upset win vs UCLA

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Michigan Wolverines defeated the UCLA Bruins 18-16 in triple overtime.

Here are key takeaways from Michigan's win.

True freshman running back Savion Hiter is starting to come into his own, with Jordan Marshall receiving fewer carries in recent weeks and zero against UCLA. Hiter had 26 carries for 104 yards and is a bruising runner who is displaying better vision.

Underwood was 11-of-24 for 127 yards and made plenty of errant throws, fumbled the ball away, and took unnecessary sacks, but also made two huge plays, including a red zone touchdown pass to Mason Bonner and an overtime-winning two-point conversion throw to Jalen Hoffman. The overtime throw was a laser on the move and was a pinpoint pass. While Underwood's performance in totality was underwhelming, he did enough to win the game.

Michigan's defense continues to keep the team in games. Michigan's offense only mustered three points through the first three quarters, and Michigan's defense was often ferocious. They tallied four sacks; they intercepted quarterback Ty Diffenbach, and Dieffenbach also fumbled twice — one of his fumbles was particularly fortuitous as he coughed it up while winding up to throw.

When I say Michigan's defense received good fortune, it came in the form of Dieffenbach suffering a serious lower body injury in overtime that resulted in him being carted off the field.

Madden Iamaleava came into the game, and he was downright awful. He was shell-shocked and didn't get rid of the ball during a two-point conversion attempt, and on a 3rd & 6 in overtime, he could have run for a first down or thrown for one, but hesitated mightily and gained just one yard. It's fair to say that if Dieffenbach didn't get hurt, the final outcome may have been different — but that's football.

After an uninspiring effort against Oregon, Michigan defensive coordinator Jay Hill had the defense fired.

Congratulations to Kyle Whittingham for getting a win on his 67th birthday. It was an ugly game, and Michigan's offense has shown little improvement since firing Jason Beck and moving to interim OC Jim Harding. This wasn't a statement for Whittingham or Michigan; it was merely a win against a wounded UCLA team. Fans have stormed the field against Western Michigan and No. 4 UCLA this season — while it may be fun for the fans who were there, it's not a good sign of where the program is currently at. Storming the field against Western Michigan or UCLA would not have happened during the Jim Harbaugh era. Those standards, those expectations, are what Michigan must get back. It took Michigan until Nov. 21 to be bowl-eligible; that kind of stat should always be inexcusable.

Trev Alberts is Michigan's new athletic director, and there's every reason to believe he's going to want his own pick at head coach and not attach himself to a head coach who is clearly losing steam. This isn't an operation that's going to compete for championships. After the game Whittingham said talk about a new athletic director is above his pay grade and noted he gave a game ball to departing AD Warde Manuel. Manuel receiving a game ball seems symbolic, a thank you for hiring him and perhaps a sign that Whittingham and Manuel will be riding off into the sunset together.
