Buccaneers Week 11 Top Performers at Detroit Lions

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

After a week of prep time to tweak an anemic passing offense and get healthy for a second-half desperation push, the Buccaneers still suffered from familiar issues in a 31-21 loss to the Detroit Lions.

Dropping to 3-7, the Bucs face a déjà vu moment next week facing a must-win against the division rival (and leading) Carolina Panthers on Monday Night Football. The last time this season it was truly "must-win," they pulverized Carolina to the tune of 43-13 in Week 7. It's probably not reasonable to expect a similar result, but it's certainly a winnable game and the opener of the team's easiest stretch of the year.

Ultimately, it's still a deeply flawed team that's going to live and die week-to-week on the defense being the best version of itself and the run game driving the offense. Hard way to consistently win in the modern NFL.

We'll give some shoutouts and move onto the next one.

Even on a down day, Irving still served as the primary driver for the Bucs' offense. He finished with 21 touches for 100 total yards and a touchdown, but it should've been much more if a needless holding penalty from Luke Goedeke didn't erase an excellent 80-yard home run. Nevertheless, Irving continued his Pro Bowl worthy campaign, and he'll need to mirror his dominance against the Panthers from last month (106 yards, 2 TDs) to give the Bucs a chance next week.

Kenny Gainwell continued to be useful in his change-of-pace role, totaling 8 touches for 59 yards. Against a tough run defense in Detroit, both of the team's top options delivered.

Much fewer positive notes for the passing game (unsurprisingly).

Baker Mayfield is a complete liability. You can blame the injuries or the coordinator or the receivers, and they all share some blame, but ultimately your $165 million QB shouldn't be terrified at any remote sign of pressure and be able to execute some basic throws under duress.

Mayfield's pressure-to-sack ratio, which attempts to demonstrate how a quarterback's sack percentage relates to an offensive line's pressure allowed rate, was an egregious 36% Sunday. Out of 44 dropbacks, he was pressured only 11 times yet sacked 4 times. Too often the eyes are dropping when the pocket muddies and he attempts to turn into a fullback, and it simply isn't winning football.

He got the ball to rookie Ted Hurst quite a bit, and that was nice to see since Hurst has struggled mightily with drops. He reeled in 5 catches for 56 yards and 2 touchdowns, and hopefully this performance will do well to build the third-rounder's confidence and take a stronger role in the receiver room.

Not many highlights to speak of on defense, which held tough for three quarters but ultimately couldn't remain sturdy against one of the league's best offenses.

Most notably, David Walker continued to be the team's most reliable pass rusher. Toting one of the highest pressure rates in the entire league despite never starting, Walker recorded his team-leading 5th sack, the only one of the afternoon, when running the hoop on rookie tackle Blake Miller to take down Jared Goff. Perhaps the next coaching staff will let him be a starter next year.

Losing Jacob Parrish early to a back injury crippled the secondary, but Zyon McCollum continued to play very well and kept top target Amon-Ra St. Brown quiet most of the afternoon — the latter netted just 5 catches for 37 yards and a touchdown, and his score came on the opposite side of the field with Benjamin Morrison in coverage. Speaking of whom, Morrison got absolutely dogwalked all afternoon long and then left with what must be his 5th 0r 6th hamstring issue since arriving in Tampa last year. If he and Parrish are both out next week, the Bucs could be facing a very dire situation at cornerback.

Josiah Trotter continued to be a tackle machine, recording 14 total tackles on the afternoon. The coverage reps can be shaky, but he's not a liability at least and that's encouraging to see. A phantom facemask call put a damper on things at the end of the game, but such is life with NFL officiating sometimes.

McLaughlin remained perfect on his kicks for the year, the only kicker in the league to do so. He attempted no field goals but made all three of his extra points, so hurray!

McCollum made a nice play on Detroit's first field goal attempt, jumping over the line to seemingly affect Lions kicker Jake Bates on a missed attempt. After his other excellent special teams play against the Bears, McCollum is making an impact everywhere.
