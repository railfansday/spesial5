Family offices move more money into stocks, hedge AI risk, according to CNBC Family Office Portfolio Tracker

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A version of this article appeared in CNBC's Inside Wealth newsletter, a weekly guide to the high-net-worth investor and consumer. Sign up to receive future editions, straight to your inbox.

Family offices continued to boost their stock holdings in the third quarter, although they hedged the artificial intelligence trade with a larger bet on financials, according to the CNBC Family Office Portfolio Tracker.

Family offices increased their allocation to public stocks to 36.4% of their portfolios in the third quarter, up from 35.6% in the second quarter, according to the Tracker.  Their stock holdings have jumped from 33.6% in 2024, a substantial increase for family offices, which rarely deviate from their long-term financial allocations.

The CNBC Family Office Portfolio Tracker is powered by data from Addepar, the foundational data and artificial intelligence platform used by financial professionals globally that now has over $10 trillion on its platform. Unlike surveys, Addepar's data provides a real-time look into the portfolios of hundreds of single-family offices — aggregated and anonymized —representing a total of more than $1.4 trillion in family office assets.

The increased allocation to stocks, whether from passive market gains or active buying, shows that family offices aren't pulling back from the market despite growing fears of an AI bubble and weakness in tech stocks.

"As we've studied family offices for more than 15 years, they've demonstrated time and again that they take the long view," said Eric Poirier, CEO of Addepar. "But they do like staying invested. So with the run-up in AI thematically, they're staying exposed, they're risk on."

At the same time, family offices are hedging their tech bets. According to Addepar, family offices have lower exposure to AI stocks when compared to the broader S&P 500. While tech stocks account for 40% of the S&P market cap, family offices have an average allocation of 28% of their equity portfolios in tech stocks, according to the Tracker.

They are offsetting the tech risk in part with financials. Financial services account for 11% of the S&P, but the average allocation for family offices is 18%. Poirier said the lower tech weighting and higher financials exposure suggests that family offices see financials as a potential offset to AI risk.

"They're fading the AI trade to some extent, but still staying quite exposed," he said.

Family offices are also continuing to dial back their exposure to alternative investments. Alts account for 47% of their portfolios, down from 48.5% a year ago. The biggest decliner was real estate, down to 7.2% from 9.4% in the third quarter of 2025. Higher interest rates have driven down valuations in many real-estate portfolios and funds, likely accounting for much of the drop.

Hedge funds also saw a decline, from 8.9% a year ago to 7.5%, as many funds grapple with more volatile stock markets and declining tech stocks. Their investments in private credit declined slightly, to 1% of their portfolios. Their private equity holdings fell for the second straight quarter, to 6.4% of their portfolios.

They also saw a decline in their allocation to private companies — companies held directly rather than through a private equity fund. Family office holdings of private companies fell 0.3% to 15.7%, likely due to slight declines in valuations. Still, private companies remain by far their largest segment among alts, as family offices continue to focus on direct investing.

Cash piles grew slightly for family offices — to 8.6% — suggesting they want to keep adequate dry powder on hand in case of a market correction or crisis.

The big question for family offices this year and next is how they manage rising yields and inflation fears. Some were positioning for inflation at the start of the year with bets on infrastructure and real estate, yet the surge in the yields driven by oil prices was faster than many expected.

Family office holdings of fixed income have steadily increased for three straight quarters, to 8.1% in the third quarter.

"Family offices tend to be able to withstand market volatility more so than other investors," Poirier said. "That said, their response to the historic rise in fixed income and rates has been more muted relative to even five-year averages."
