Preview: Can the Utah Jazz finally get their second win vs. the LA Clippers?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

There aren't many NBA franchises that have ever felt more hopeless than the Los Angeles Clippers in the aftermath of the Kawhi Leonard-Steve Balmer saga. They went all-in on the concept of Leonard alongside All-Star wing Paul George, mortgaging the house, the dog, and even undergoing construction of a state-of-the-art arena in which they could showcase their prosperous future. Instead, that core imploded. Leonard and George have both departed, and the Clippers were stripped of five first-round picks, with bans, suspensions, and fines abound.

It's a team without a future. Without hope for organic improvement. Without allure for potential free agent signings. So yeah, times are feeling a bit shallow in this shadowy corner of LA.

If anyone can relate to such a feeling of hopelessness, it's the Utah Jazz.

After four long years in the tanking pressure cooker, Utah's rebuilt product still came out half-baked. Losers of 12 straight, a veteran (expensive) frontcourt spending more time on the injury report than the basketball court, and good for at least one baffling implosion per game, Will Hardy's team is 1-13, tied for the worst record in the entire NBA, and light-years removed from the playoff hunt that had been promised pre-season.

Utah's young core has been underwhelming outside of Darryn Peterson, Keyonte George, and Ace Bailey. In all truth, Peterson's is the only name in that trio whose development has taken a clear and obvious upward trajectory. George's scoring efficiency and decision-making have fluctuated in quality, and his defensive impact remains minimal. Bailey's mastered the Warriors-era Shaun Livingston mid-range pull-up, but can't seem to hit his target from beyond the arc, shooting just over 26% on the young season.

Despite keeping the score tight against some of the best teams in the West, Utah still has no winning experience. When the game clock hits winning time, the Jazz's watches are unsynchronized.

The host Clippers, currently 4-8, are 6.5-point favorites at the time of this article. If the Jazz can't find a way to win this one, it's difficult to imagine where they might collect their second win of the season.

Calvin Barrett is the Associate Editor for SLC Dunk and a Utah Jazz columnist for the Standard-Examiner. Originally from Springville, Utah, he currently writes from his desk in Japan.
