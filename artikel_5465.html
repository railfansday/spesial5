Pending Sales Down Over 16% as 2026 Market Craters

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Pending home sales have slowed significantly according to the latest data from the National Association of Realtors® (NAR), sparking questions about the long-term health of the housing market as activity falls to levels seen three decades ago.

NAR’s Pending Home Sales report for October reveals that sales dropped by 4.7% month-over-month, and fell 16.7% on an annual basis. The monthly decrease is not as severe as September’s drop of 7.3%, but pendings are now far below the same point in the housing cycle last year as economists expect an even slower fall and winter market.

“October could mark the low point in contract signings for this housing cycle, as mortgage rates appear to have topped out,” NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun said.

Prior to the report’s release, Realtor.com® Senior Economist Hannah Jones expected pending sales to fall, attributing high mortgage rates as the cause.

“Mortgage rates climbed nearly 75 basis points from late August to mid-October, adding roughly $200 a month to the typical mortgage payment on a median-priced home, assuming a 10% down payment. That is more than many already-strained buyers can easily absorb,” she stated.

The latest data from Freddie Mac showed the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage at 7.11%, down from mid-October’s high around 7.40%. Despite rates reducing, Jones noted that she expects affordability problems to persist.

“Markets now expect that a Fed rate hike is coming in December,” she added. “Until inflation cools enough to put mortgage rates on a downward path, affordability problems are likely to persist, keeping contract signings and home sales subdued.”

Although Jones predicts one final rate hike before the end of the year, Federal Reserve Open Market Committee Chairman Kevin Warsh did not allude to housing or rate hikes in his Nov. 16 remarks. Rather, he honed in on the impact of “superintelligence”—an alternative term for AI preferred by President Donald Trump—on the economy at large.

In line with September’s data, October saw all four regions decline in pending home sales, both monthly and annually.

Western states experienced the greatest drop month-over-month, with a 6.9% decrease. Following the West was the South, Midwest and Northeast at declines of 6.3%, 2.7% and 0.8%, respectively.

Annually, the rankings are the same. The West’s pending home sales fell by 20.3%, South by 18.7%, Midwest by 14% and the Northeast at 11.1%.

On a local level, the report notes that pending home sales fell annually even in the strongest markets. Using data from Realtor.com, NAR highlighted the top 10 strongest markets for pending home sales, and found that they still experienced declines.

The slowing in pending home sales coupled with lackluster homebuilder confidence—which was last reported at a new 14-year low—leaves many questioning whether 2027 will see a turn-around for the housing market, as 2026 will likely end on a low note.

“It is somewhat puzzling that home sales would fall to levels last seen three decades ago, even as the U.S. population has grown by roughly 75 million,” Yun concluded. “Either sizable housing demand is building in the background, or the dream of homeownership is increasingly out of reach for many Americans.”
