Court Report: Compass Scatters Legal Battles; Bronx Real Estate Scammer Arrested

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Editor’s note: The COURT REPORT is RISMedia’s weekly look at current and upcoming lawsuits, investigations and other legal developments around real estate.

In the ongoing legal battle among Compass and several large MLSs across the country, both sides are maneuvering to control how and where the cases are tried.

The Arizona Regional MLS (ARMLS), one of 15 MLSs sued by Compass last week, filed a letter with the Southern District of New York on Nov. 18. The letter was in response to Compass’s Nov. 13 motion to dismiss a suit filed against the mega brokerage by seven MLSs in October. In lieu of dismissal, Compass petitioned the court to sever and transfer the case to the individual plaintiffs’ regions. The ARMLS letter argues that Compass’s motion to “splinter one dispute across multiple courts” should be denied.

The filing is the latest in a series of back-and-forth litigation between Compass and MLSs regarding broker cooperation rules that prevent pre-marketed or office-exclusive listings from being shown on the MLS. On Nov. 12, Compass sued 15 MLSs in 15 separate actions in 11 districts, claiming their longstanding broker cooperation rules violated antitrust law. The en-masse filing was preceded by a letter Compass sent to several MLSs in September, warning them to cease enforcement of their broker cooperation rules or face federal antitrust claims.

In response, six MLSs, later joined by one more, filed a lawsuit in New York seeking to prevent Compass from suing them along with a declaration that their cooperation rules are lawful under Section 1 of the Sherman Act and state-law analogues. In its Nov. 13 motion, Compass claimed that the action filed by the MLSs should either be dismissed or handled individually in the jurisdictions where each MLS resides.

ARMLS counsel Gregory S. Skidmore argues in his letter that trying the cases individually would create “massive inefficiencies and duplication of effort.”

“The MLS Parties sued Compass where it is headquartered, and where Compass itself has chosen to litigate its own Cooperation Rules-related suit,” wrote Skidmore, referring to the brokerage’s injunction hearing against Zillow in November of 2025. “Compass has not shown any ‘indication that nonparty witnesses would refuse to appear’…and Compass’s executives reside in New York,” he continued. “Nor is any other district the locus of operative facts: Compass’s campaign originated at its New York headquarters and reached nationwide.”

“Transfer would create seven suits in five districts, each replicating Compass’s evidence,” he added. “Courts need not indulge an ‘onslaught of litigation.'”

On Nov. 19, New York Attorney General Letitia James announced the arrest and indictment of a Bronx-based real estate broker named Marcia Campbell.

Campbell—the 64-year-old owner of Extreme Realty Associates—was originally arrested in 2024 for committing deed theft and conducting a series of real estate scams in the Bronx, stealing more than $250,000, according to a release. Later, she pleaded guilty to two felonies for “falsifying business records and failing to file corporate taxes after being charged for her role in a separate deed theft scheme involving three stolen Westchester homes.”

In June of this year, Campbell had applied to renew her real estate broker license but did not disclose that she had been convicted of two felonies in Westchester County and had 18 charges pending against her in Bronx County. As such, she was charged in Albany County Supreme Court with “offering a false instrument for filing and making a punishable false written statement,” the release says.

“New Yorkers depend on real estate brokers to help them make some of the most important purchases of their lives,” James stated. “Marcia Campbell abused her role as a licensed real estate broker to steal New Yorkers’ homes and savings, then tried to hide her crimes to maintain her license. New Yorkers should be able to trust the professionals who guide them through major life decisions, and my office will not hesitate to take action against anyone who puts that trust at risk.”

Currently, the charges against Campbell for the $250,000 worth of real estate scams is ongoing with no trial date set. But the release notes that Campbell had “tricked clients into sending her money for sham real estate transactions, only to keep the money for herself and refuse to return the vast majority of funds.”

The latest charges against Campbell with offering a false instrument for filing is a class E felony, coupled with a publishable false written statement as a class A misdemeanor. Should she be convicted, Campbell faces up to four years in prison.

“Instead of helping families find a home, this defendant is accused of using her real estate background to exploit them out of money, and in one instance, the deed to a house,” Bronx District Attorney Darcel D. Clark said. “It is not only egregious, but illegal for her to attempt to renew her broker license without disclosing her criminal past, especially as she actively faces a slew of charges being prosecuted by my office. I commend Attorney General James for pursuing these latest charges and protecting New Yorkers from future scams.”

The Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) continues its efforts to avoid funding fair housing initiatives, as the agency awaits a judge’s decision on an appeal.

The lawsuit involves the Massachusetts Fair Housing Center and National Fair Housing Alliance (NFHA) suing HUD for its restructuring of the Fair Housing Initiatives Program (FHIP) and eliminating funding for over 100 private nonprofit fair housing enforcement organizations, according to the original complaint.

The residing judge—U.S. District Judge Myong J. Joun—who has sided with Massachusetts and the NFHA several times throughout court proceedings, ruled that HUD had failed to justify a significant restructuring of a landmark fair housing program earlier in late August.

The latest appeal by HUD challenges two of the judge’s decisions. One is a denial for HUD’s request for a briefing schedule, and the second is for the agency’s motion to cancel the Oct. 30 deadline, in which HUD had to publish the revised FY2025 Notices of Funding Opportunity (NOFOs) and identify where they were publicly available.

Initially in its motion to cancel the Oct. 30 deadline, HUD argued that the court lacked the authority to order the agency to “publish new notices of funding opportunity,” the original motion stated.

“This court does not have jurisdiction to order specific relief, even on a purportedly temporary basis, because a court reviewing agency action effectively sits as an appellate tribunal only, merely to determine whether an agency made an error of law,” HUD’s motion stated.

HUD’s official appeal is not yet available for viewing, but marks another barrier for fair housing funding. Joun has ruled in favor of the NFHA and Fair Housing Center on several occasions throughout the lawsuit, but it appears HUD has been successful in stalling.

Pennsylvania is once again seeing a new lawsuit in regard to broker fees, as one first-time homebuyer filed a complaint against Howard Hanna Real Estate Services over a “Realtor Transaction Fee.”

Dena Bergamasco filed the suit against Howard Hanna on Nov. 10, claiming she was charged a $395 “Realtor Transaction Fee” on top of the buyer-broker commission for her October 2021 home purchase in Pittsburgh.

Her attorneys—the law firms Kopelowitz Ostrow P.A. and Kaliegold PLLC—are the same firms representing another Pennsylvania homebuyer, Gregory Deivert, in a similar case against Keller Williams Elite.

Filed in the Court of Common Pleas of Allegheny County, Bergamasco’s complaint alleges that Howard Hanna breached fiduciary duty and is in violation of the Pennsylvania Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law.

“The fee had no service behind it, no cost behind it, and no name that accurately describes it. It is money taken from a client by the broker who was supposed to be looking out for her,” the complaint states.

The issue surrounding broker fees is prevalent in many brokerages, as Deivert’s case against Keller Williams Elite alleges that the franchise charged him a $650 fee that is “essentially for nothing.”

In 2024, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) launched a formal inquiry into the issue of extra fees tacked onto home mortgage costs.

The lawsuit also seeks class-action status on behalf of all buyers, who were represented by Howard Hanna when buying a Pennsylvania home and paid a “Transaction Fee” or “Realtor Transaction Fee.”
