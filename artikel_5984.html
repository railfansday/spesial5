Is Norwegian Luna the best big new ship sailing out of Miami?

For someone looking for a vessel that feels more refined and upscale than what is typical among big Miami-based ships, it just might be. (Its sister ship, Norwegian Aqua, also sails from Miami.)

As I saw during a week on board in October, Norwegian Luna has an elevated design compared to many of the big new ships sailing out of Miami, giving it an elegance that stands out among the competition.

That said, for someone who just wants the widest array of onboard amusements, entertainment and nightlife that they can find on a cruise ship, the choice gets harder.

Although its large, the eight-month-old Norwegian Luna — the latest in Norwegian Cruise Line's Prima Plus class — is notably smaller than the other big new ships that it is up against in Miami. That means there's less room for the wide range of attractions you'll find on those vessels.

Unlike two of its biggest rivals in the market, Royal Caribbean's Icon of the Seas and MSC Cruises' MSC World America, for instance, it lacks a water park area with multiple waterslides on its top deck (though it does have one large water attraction, the Aqua Slidecoaster). It also has fewer and smaller main pool areas. That's a real strike against it for families with kids, in particular.

As someone who generally prefers more intimate and elegant ships, I know which side of the trade-off I'm on. If it were my money, I'd lean toward Norwegian Luna. It's the perfect big ship for me.

But read on to see if it's right for you.

Unveiled in March, Norwegian Luna is the second ship in Norwegian's new Prima Plus Class of vessels, following the year-old Norwegian Aqua.

These two ships, along with two similar but slightly smaller Prima Class vessels that debuted in 2022 and 2023, are smaller than the ships that Norwegian launched just before them, which is a bit unusual. Most of Norwegian's competitors, such as Royal Caribbean and MSC Cruises, have been rolling out ever bigger ships, loaded with ever more onboard attractions and venues.

For now, Norwegian Luna mostly operates seven-night voyages to the Caribbean out of Miami on Saturdays, competing head-to-head with new ships from Royal Caribbean and MSC Cruises (Icon of the Seas and MSC World America) that are much bigger.

Those two ships, notably, are 59% and 38% bigger than Luna, respectively, allowing for a lot more onboard venues of all kinds, from decktop attractions to showrooms, restaurants and bars.

On the flip side, Norwegian Luna's smaller size, coupled with its elevated design, generally contributes to a more intimate and elegant feel — a significant differentiator.

I say "generally," as Norwegian Luna's more elegant feel is sometimes punctured by aggressive upselling and extra charges that are incongruous with the ship's overall vibe.

It's not a dealbreaker, but it's off-putting at times and something you need to be prepared for going into a sailing.

Cabins are a strong point on Norwegian Luna. The majority of balcony cabins (the biggest category of cabins on the ship) are surprisingly spacious at 358 square feet, with a clean-lined, modern design and solid storage. I particularly love the bathrooms, which offer larger showers than you sometimes get on mass-market ships as well as a clever pivot door that makes them easy to enter.

Balcony cabins account for about 57% of Norwegian Luna's 1,813 cabins. But there are also 140 oceanview cabins without balconies of various sizes and 424 windowless "inside" cabins that offer a modern design with solid storage.

The latter category includes 73 very small but perfect-for-one solo cabins, known as Studios, in a keycard-access-only solo area with its own lounge — a Norwegian signature. Norwegian has leaned into the solo cruise market in recent years with these exclusive solo areas, and the cabins are in high demand. Book one early if you want one.

Norwegian Luna is also home to one of the line's signature The Haven suite complexes. These are separate, keycard-access-only enclaves found on many Norwegian ships that not only have a wide range of lovely suites but also their own private restaurants, lounges and bars, and outdoor sun and pool decks.

On Norwegian Luna, this complex (tied with the one on Norwegian Aqua as the biggest in the fleet) fills the top four decks of the back of the ship, accessible via its own private elevator. It's truly wonderful. Staying in one of its suites — there are 123, including 25 Owner's Suites that measure up to an astounding 2,100 square feet — feels like staying in a completely different, far more upscale vessel.

Among my favorite features of Norwegian Luna's Haven area is its cozy bar, which offers a much-upgraded bar experience to what you'll find anywhere else on the ship. There are higher-end spirits and creative craft cocktails made by some of the best bartenders at sea.

In addition to the suites in The Haven, you'll find additional suites and penthouses outside the area that also come with extra privileges but not all the perks that Haven guests receive.

Pro tip: If you are sensitive to noise, steer clear of cabins with connecting doors to adjacent cabins (you'll find them noted on Norwegian Luna's deck plans). Even when closed, their imperfect seals will cause you to hear noise from the cabin next door.

Norwegian ships have always been known for a wide range of restaurant choices, and Norwegian Luna is no exception. It has more than a dozen sit-down eateries, ranging from a super-casual food hall to a high-end steakhouse — some quite good for a big, mass-market ship.

Just be warned that the restaurant scene on Norwegian Luna is one of the areas where you'll encounter a lot of upcharges, with both high cover charges for some restaurants and additional charges for individual menu items that can really push up the bill.

Among the priciest eateries on board are Norwegian's two signature restaurants, Cagney's Steakhouse (found on every Norwegian ship) and the French-focused Le Bistro (found on nearly every Norwegian ship). Both have cover charges of $60 per person that rise to $72 per person with a mandatory 20% gratuity; that price can climb even higher if you order an item with an upcharge.

Both are elegant and upscale, and serve quality dishes, as does Palomar, which serves seafood-focused Mediterranean cuisine for the same $60 cover charge. I highly recommend Palomar's whole fish-for-two baked in salt, which is well worth its additional $25 charge ($30 with the mandatory gratuity) — not just because it's a showy dish that tastes as good as it looks, but because it's deboned tableside, adding some tableside theatrics to the evening.

Other extra-charge eateries include Los Lobos ($40 per person), which dishes out Mexican cuisine; Nama Sushi & Sashimi ($50 per person), for its namesake Japanese cuisine; Japanese hibachi restaurant Hasuki ($60 per person); and Thai-centered Sukothai ($50 per person).

All of these have mandatory 20% gratuity charges on top of their cover charges, as does Onda by Scarpetta, which is an Italian eatery ($40 per person) with ties to New York City's high-end Scarpetta restaurant. The latter is one eatery I'd skip, as the cuisine is spotty and the service more rushed than relaxing. (Despite ordering four courses, we were barely there an hour as the dishes were raced out to us one on top of the other.)

For those keeping to a tight budget, there are fortunately plenty of solid included-in-the-fare options on Norwegian Luna, too.

The real standout here is Indulge Food Hall, a casual, multi-outlet venue with separate stands and counters serving tapas, Indian dishes, noodles, vegetarian bowls, barbecue, Latin and Mexican food, and rotisserie items. Some of these items, particularly the Indian dishes (which include chicken tikka made in an actual tandoor oven), are quite good for a free-on-a-ship casual eatery.

Pro tip: If you're sitting at one of the many tables scattered around Indulge Food Hall, you can order from any of the stands and counters via a tabletop tablet. But if you sit at the counter for any particular food outlet, you can only order from that outlet's menu. Pick your seats with that in mind.

A sleeper hit with no extra charge is The Local Bar and Grill, which has both indoor and outdoor seating with a casual diner vibe. It's open around the clock for table-served American comfort food (think: burgers, chicken wings and nachos). But most importantly, for me, it's a calmer and less crowded place for breakfast than the seemingly always-jammed buffet at the top of the ship (more on that in a moment). I loved sitting in its outdoor area on sea days, overlooking the water, for a leisurely eggs-bacon-and-toast breakfast served by a friendly waiter — a much more civilized experience than the buffet.

By contrast, the shiptop buffet called Surfside is an included-in-the-fare outlet to avoid. While a convenient quick-bite option for passengers lounging at the pool (it's right next door), it's too small for a ship the size of Norwegian Luna. It has too few tables, too few food stations and too narrow a walkway along the food stations to be a pleasant experience much of the time it's open.

Rounding out the included-in-the-fare options are two larger complimentary sit-down restaurants, The Commodore Room and Hudson's. Both offer the same dinner menu, with decent, if not always consistent, offerings and pleasant service. Hudson's is also open for breakfast and lunch.

Among the upcharges at both eateries are $20-$30 fees for signature dishes from the ship's Cagney's Steakhouse, such as its ribeye steak ($25). While I'm generally not a fan of the trend toward more upcharges at supposedly included-in-the-fare cruise ship eateries, this does allow for what is one of my favorite dining hacks when sailing on a Norwegian ship: creating an almost-as-good-as-the-real-thing Cagney's steakhouse experience at less than half the price.

I order that ribeye and the always-available Caesar salad (nearly identical to the one at Cagney's except you don't get anchovies on top) and a couple of glasses of the included-in-Free-at-Sea-packages Malbec, which is the very same Malbec offered at Cagney's, and pretend I'm at the latter eatery. The total cost, including the mandatory gratuity, is $30 versus $72 per person at Cagney's.

No matter where you are on Norwegian Luna, you are never far from a bar. There are more than a dozen of them in the interior parts of the ship alone, plus more on the outside decks.

Among the liveliest (and my personal favorite) is the Metropolitan Bar, which stands out for its "zero waste" cocktails featuring such ingredients as syrup made from discarded banana peels. It's bustling every night with live music that ranges from Latin beats to classic '80s rock.

The Belvedere Bar at the top level of the atrium is also hopping with live music nightly, as is the Penrose Bar two decks below. (Musicians play at the base of the atrium not far from it.)

All three of these bars line high-traffic areas and can get busy. For something more tucked away, quieter and usually less crowded, the side-by-side Whiskey Bar and Swirl Wine Bar offer intimate spaces dedicated strictly to their respective namesake pours.

There's also the bar at The Local Bar & Grill mentioned in the restaurant section above, which serves as the casual go-to spot to catch a game over bar bites.

Beyond these, you'll find multiple bars servicing the pool and activity areas at the top of the ship as well as every interior entertainment venue, including the main theater and comedy club. Up at the very front of the ship, the Observation Lounge pairs a full bar with sweeping daytime ocean views.

What you won't find on Norwegian Luna, unlike on some competitor ships sailing from Miami, is a true craft cocktail venue. The Metropolitan Bar comes the closest with its "zero waste" cocktails. But even here, most drinks are quick-made affairs lacking the house-made ingredients, specialty clear ice and precision construction that has become commonplace at craft bars on land.

The Whiskey Bar also has craft elements, including a wide range of whiskeys and knowledgeable bartenders mixing old fashioneds, Boulevardiers and other whiskey drinks with precision. But the venue falls down on things like proper ice for drinks. (They oddly have just 20 large cubes available each night, which disappear fast, resulting in most cocktails coming out with the same small ice cubes found at the basic bars on the pool deck.)

For coffee drinkers, a full-size Starbucks in the atrium rounds out the beverage options.

Norwegian is no longer the leader in big ship entertainment, following cutbacks over the past three years. All the big-name Broadway shows, from "Jersey Boys" to "Kinky Boots," are gone. But it still offers solid entertainment in its main theaters, including the one on Norwegian Luna.

The big crowdpleaser on Norwegian Luna is "Elton: A Celebration of Elton John," a flashy tribute show that strings together John's biggest hits with visual pizzazz that includes elaborate costumes and choreography, special effects and archival footage projected on a back screen. If you're an Elton John fan like me, you'll love it.

The other big show in the Luna Theater, "HIKO: Innovation Meets Wonder," is a moody, sci-fi-themed mashup of custom-scored music, dance, showy costumes, 3D projections, lasers and acrobatics (think high-flying trampoline acts and aerial silk routines). It starts out slow but picks up toward the end.

There's also a rocking Eagles tribute show in the main theater with a full band, which is another crowd-pleaser. There are also more tribute shows focused on Billy Joel and Fleetwood Mac (the former in the smaller Syd Norman Pour House; the latter at the main theater).

And yes, you'll see a theme among the performers Norwegian has chosen for these tribute shows. These are all musical groups that are popular with Baby Boomers and Generation Xers who grew up with them, but have also had a resurgence among younger generations — perfect for Norwegian's wide demographic.

One other notable entertainment option is LunaTique Pop Circus, a bawdy, adults-only, cabaret-style nightlife experience set to pop music, with plenty of audience participation. (If the idea of being pulled up on stage terrifies you, don't sit in the front rows.) It's the only show on Norwegian Luna with an extra charge ($45 per person, including four drinks that get served to everyone as part of the show).

LunaTique Pop Circus takes place in Improv at Sea, an intimate comedy club that also is the place for stand-up comedy on most nights.

While not quite as elaborate as the entertainment on the larger Royal Caribbean and MSC Cruises ships that sail out of Miami on weekends, the above shows have been earning high marks from cruisers since Norwegian Luna debuted and are among the most popular activities on the ship.

Just be warned that, because of that popularity, it can sometimes be hard to get a seat for these shows, which is a common complaint among passengers. With just 752 seats, the Luna Theater is small for a ship the size of Norwegian Luna and fills up long before showtime. Ditto for the smaller Syd Norman's Pour House and Improv at Sea venues, which each can hold just 100 or so people.

If you want a seat for any of the shows, particularly a good seat near the front, head to the venue at least 45 minutes in advance.

Norwegian Luna has a lot to offer on its top decks, particularly in the "family fun" department.

The standout attraction is its giant Aqua Slidecoaster water ride. It uses the same magnetic accelerator system found in modern roller coasters to blast you skyward in a raft that then takes you winding back down a meandering waterslide course. It's both tons of fun and completely free.

You'll find several more fun family attractions tucked under and around the Aqua Slidecoaster, including a 12-hole, high-tech minigolf course where each stroke of your ball is electronically tracked and you get bonus points for hitting certain targets. It comes with a per-person charge of $5 but is well worth it.

Also in this area is The Stadium (free outdoor games including table tennis, bean bag toss and foosball) and the Bull's Eye Bar, which features tech-enhanced darts games.

You'll also find the entrance to The Drop, a terrifying-looking dry slide that spirals 10 decks down the side of the ship. It was a hard pass for me, but try it if you're daring.

Rounding out this area are a pickleball court and Glow Court — a high-tech sports court with an LED wall that lets you play challenging ball-throwing games (just be careful not to hit your partner with a ball, as I did; you might find yourself in the doghouse).

While great fun, these attractions eat up a lot of real estate on the top decks, leaving less room for pool areas. The ship's main pool area, just in front of the Aqua Slidecoaster, feels small for a ship the size of Norwegian Luna (and, indeed, was uncomfortably jammed on sea days during my sailing).

One deck up from the pool is the often-crowded Kids' Aqua Park, where little ones can enjoy water sprayers and mini waterslides.

The solution to escape the crowds (if you're kid-free, at least) is to spend $289 per person for a weekly pass to the adults-only Vibe Beach Club — a separate, keycard-access-only deck-top lounge area that is roughly the same size as the main pool area but limited to just 166 passengers. It is blissfully uncrowded and quiet compared to the main pool area and has its own bar and hot tubs facing the sea.

Pro tip: Splurge for one of the 12 private cabanas at the Vibe Beach Club, which cost a seemingly high $619 per week but come with access for two people, so they're really only about $310 per person. That's just $21 more per person than buying two Vibe passes, and it's a much nicer experience.

Alternatively, you can book a cabin in The Haven, which, as noted above, also has its own decktop lounge area (at the back of the ship) with its own pool and bar.

Between these two areas (Vibe and The Haven), Norwegian Luna notably has a lot more of its decktop real estate devoted to exclusive upcharge areas than other big ships sailing from Miami. What this effectively means is that the ship offers a bifurcated decktop experience. If you pay up, you will experience something more relaxing and exclusive than you will find on many other big ships. If you don't, the top deck experience might feel less appealing.

That said, if you're willing to head down nine decks, you'll find additional outdoor lounge space with lounge chairs and small plunge pools along an outdoor promenade that wraps around the sides of the ships. With a cool South Beach vibe that includes plush circular seats for two surrounded by wading pools, it's an innovative additional outdoor area that is often less crowded than the top-deck pool area.

For indoor fun, the family-focused Luna Game Zone near the Observation Lounge offers an array of video, virtual reality and other games (some for free and some for a fee). Plus, for adults, there's a sizable casino with VIP and smoking rooms, as well as slots and table games.

Norwegian Luna also has a massive, multilevel spa (the Mandara Spa) with one of the loveliest and most relaxing thermal suites at sea. Accessible for an extra charge, it has a stunning, two-deck-high room with water therapy pools; a separate area that is home to all sorts of therapeutic rooms including Finnish, charcoal and clay saunas, a steam room and a salt room; and a separate, blissfully relaxing room full of heated loungers overlooking the sea.

Just be warned that access to the thermal suite costs a sky-high $630 per person for the week (though discounts are sometimes available onboard at the start of the cruise).

The spa area is also where you'll find the ship's fitness room, which is free to use and offers plenty of cardio equipment and free weights, but lacks a full range of weight machines.

The ship's entertainment staff also programs all sorts of daily activities throughout the ship, including trivia games, karaoke, art and dance classes, and family events.

Through at least April 2028, Norwegian Luna will mostly sail seven-night cruises from Miami to the Caribbean, with departures on Saturdays. The exception is during the summer of 2027, when it will reposition to New York for four-, five- and seven-night cruises to Bermuda.

When sailing from Miami, the ship operates both Eastern and Western Caribbean itineraries. Eastern Caribbean itineraries typically include stops at Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic; St. Thomas, U.S. Virgin Islands; Tortola, British Virgin Islands; and Great Stirrup Cay, Norwegian's private island in the Bahamas. Western Caribbean itineraries typically bring calls in Roatan, Honduras; Harvest Caye, Belize; and Costa Maya and Cozumel, Mexico.

Related: Eastern Caribbean vs. Western Caribbean cruises

Fares for seven-night Caribbean sailings start at $549 per person, based on double occupancy. Fares for seven-night Bermuda sailings start at $1,229 per person, based on double occupancy.

Such pricing is for inside cabins on the most offseason dates. You'll pay more for oceanview and balcony cabins and suites, as well as for sailings on more in-demand weeks.

Norwegian Luna is a big new ship that feels more refined and upscale than many of the other big new ships sailing out of Miami. That's in part because of its more elegant design and its (relatively) smaller size. The downside of that smaller size is that the ship doesn't have quite as many onboard attractions — like decktop features, showrooms, restaurants and bars — as some of its competitors.
