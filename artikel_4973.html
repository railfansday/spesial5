UNLESS YOU'RE SHAVING with a top-notch wet or dry electric razor, you’ll need to first apply a product to prep the skin. There are really only two options: pre-shave oil and shaving cream. For the smoothest, most irritation-free shave, opt for both in order. However, the latter is a non-negotiable unless a red, sliced-up face is the look you’re going for. No judgment?

The truth is, your shave is only as good as the cream you use because the right shaving cream cushions the skin, reduces irritation, and locks in moisture for added comfort afterwards. It also helps the razor glide smoothly, preventing nicks and tugging.

Most importantly, it turns a tedious routine into a clean, refreshing experience. But not all shaving creams are created equal—some barely protect or soothe at all. To save your skin the abuse (and potential scars), I’ve rounded up the best options worth trying.

Shaving cream is often used as a catch-all term for several products that prep and protect your skin while you shave. There are three main types, and the right one depends on your razor, your skin type, and your personal preference.

If your shave is only as good as the cream you use, then the cream is only as good as its ingredients. Shaving creams often include soothing ingredients like aloe vera, which calms irritation and helps the skin stay hydrated before and after shaving. Shea butter is another popular one, as it provides rich moisture that softens hair and improves razor glide, reducing friction during the shave.

Many formulas also contain vitamin E, an antioxidant that nourishes the skin and supports repair after shaving. Then there is a slew of natural oils and other vitamins to keep an eye on, like vitamin B5, vitamin C, argan oil, and coconut oil that hydrate, soothe, and prevent post-shave irritation.

How often do you shave? That’s the most important question to ask yourself when considering the size of your cream. A larger tube offers better value and lasts longer, especially for daily shavers. Smaller sizes, on the other hand, are ideal for travel or testing a new formula before committing. You’ll also want to consider what you shave.

I rock a beard most of the time, so I only shave for touch-ups on my neck and cheeks. Plus, I rarely shave body hair and stick to trimming. Because of this, I can often get away with a smaller tube. But if you’re clean-cut or shave large parts of your body, you’ll want to look to larger options.
