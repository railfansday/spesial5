Man Utd Icon Cost Sir Alex Ferguson £32,000 on Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Sir Alex Ferguson might be among the most knowledgeable people to ever work in football, but the Manchester United icon was left stumped by a general knowledge question in 2004.

Appearing on the famous quiz show 'Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?' alongside TV presenter Eamonn Holmes, Ferguson even called upon one of his most reliable Man Utd players for help, to no avail.

The game show presents participants with multiple-choice questions that get harder each time. Players' prize fund increases with every correct answer, with some checkpoints making the money safe. However, a wrong answer ends the game immediately.

Ferguson and Holmes were playing for the charities of their choice, and everything started very well. The football manager and presenter reached the £32,000 checkpoint, meaning that money was safe.

However, they were left stumped by the £64,000 question. They were asked: "What was the name of Tom and Barbara's cockerel in the TV sitcom 'The Good Life'?"

True English football fans would be able to answer it without any help.

It left them so unsure that they ended up using all three lifelines. First, they asked the audience, but the results didn't provide a clear consensus.

The next port of call was the 'Phone a Friend' option. Ferguson called upon Brian McClair, who played 471 games for Man Utd, scoring 127 goals.

Unfortunately, the former striker didn't have a clue between the four options: Trotsky, Rasputin, Lenin and Stalin. It led to the pair using '50/50', but their final answer of Trotsky was incorrect. Watch the moment below.

Recalling his appearance on the game show, Ferguson told the Manchester United website that McClair was 'useless'. He said: "Well, I was on it with Eamonn Holmes in 2004 and we phoned Brian McClair – but he was useless!"

The 61-year-old was the ultimate system player under Sir Alex Ferguson, but he is even more undetectable these days.

It proves that the greatest manager in Premier League history had faith in his compatriot's intelligence. He gave McClair credit as a 'bright lad', as he continued:

"We had to give the name of the rooster in The Good Life. Now, I'd never seen it, so I had no idea, but the options were Stalin, Trotsky, Lenin and Rasputin.

"We were going for £64,000, so we asked the audience and there was no decisive answer. Then I phoned Brian but he had no idea either, which surprised me because, in fairness, he's a bright lad. So we used our 50-50 and it was either Trotsky or Lenin."

He wasn't too happy with his teammate that night either, putting the blame on Holmes. Ferguson claimed: "We went for Trotsky and, just as we locked it in, Eamonn tells me the family in The Good Life were socialist farmers.

"Well, if he'd have said that earlier, I could have worked out it was Lenin! Although both were socialists, Trotsky was a bit of a dissident and became a bit of a radical. He was eventually assassinated in Mexico under Stalin's orders."
