Man United Confidential: Carrington crash adds to Mauricio Pochettino's woes, as new boss set to cut players' days off... and why the wait goes on for coach Silvia Tuya

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER and NATHAN SALT - FOOTBALL REPORTER

Published: 12:00 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 12:16 EST, 24 November 2026

As if having his first training session as Manchester United head coach sabotaged by a drone wasn’t dramatic enough, Mauricio Pochettino’s second day at work saw access to the club’s training base blocked by a serious road accident.

The collision between a vehicle and a cyclist on one of the two approaches to Carrington, where United’s players and staff are regularly seen driving through a security checkpoint, meant it was closed off for several hours on Friday while an ambulance and police cars attended the scene.

Greater Manchester Police say the cyclist was taken to hospital to receive treatment for injuries that were not life-threatening and no arrests were made.

However, anyone leaving Carrington had to take an alternative route in the opposite direction. That included the players after training and Pochettino and his staff following his first pre-match press conference.

The accident on Friday completed an eventful 48 hours at Carrington after a rogue drone operator filmed 12 minutes of Pochettino’s first training session and posted it on YouTube. He later took the video down and apologised for the intrusion, which United sources said was the right thing to do – even though they had not contacted the uploader directly and do not intend to take legal action against him.

They insisted there was not a breach of security at Carrington, but claimed a number of laws had been broken. Insiders promised a robust response to any further incidents to protect the rights of the club and its staff, and warned that those responsible would be ‘exposing themselves to significant legal and financial risk’.

It's been a testing first few days for Mauricio Pochettino as Manchester United boss

One of the unexpected issues to hamper the Argentine was a drone filming his first training session

Confidential understands Pochettino ordered the United players into Carrington an hour early on Monday morning to flush the Liverpool defeat out of their systems as soon as possible.

The squad were pictured arriving around 8.30am as they looked to regroup after the 4-0 hammering at Anfield and begin their preparations for the Champions League tie against Sporting Lisbon in Portugal on Wednesday night.

The Argentine is eager to spend as much time as possible with the players on the training ground, having only taken over as United boss last Wednesday – four days before the Liverpool clash.

United now face 11 games in 43 days and sources believe there may not be too many days off as Pochettino tries to haul his team out of the relegation zone and ensure qualification for the knockout stage of the Champions League.

At least the indications are that he will allow the players to stay at home the night before games at Old Trafford, rather than having them cooped up in a hotel like some previous United managers.

Matheus Cunha won’t play any part against Sporting following his red card in United's last Champions League tie against Roma three weeks ago.

Confidential has learned the Brazilian was set to claim the player of the match award after scoring two late goals in the 3-2 win over Roma, only for UEFA to step in.

UEFA rules prevent a player who has been sent off from receiving the award and so the selector – in this case Steve Cooper, the former Leicester, Nottingham Forest and Swansea boss who is now one of the governing body's technical observers – was forced into a late switch, picking Bruno Fernandes instead.

Cunha’s absence will be keenly felt in Portugal. He played no part in Tuesday’s training session at Carrington, instead working on his own in the gym.

At a time when United have the most porous defence in Europe’s top five leagues and Matthijs de Ligt, Leny Yoro, Lisandro Martinez and Ayden Heaven are all struggling for form, Pochettino is going to need Harry Maguire.

Pochettino overlooked the experienced Englishman from the start for the trip to Anfield but ended the match with Maguire on the pitch and he was United’s best defender on another desperate afternoon.

Maguire is walking the tightrope of a ban as he remains on four yellow cards after 11 league games. Players are hit with a one-match ban once they receive five or more yellows prior to the conclusion of the 19th league game of the season – eight matches from now.

Even after Yoro's red card at Anfield was overturned on appeal, Pochettino can ill-afford to be without Maguire going forward.

Pochettino is going to need the experience of Harry Maguire to hold his defence together but he faces a ban if he is booked again

United are still waiting to see if Pochettino’s strength and conditioning specialist Silvia Tuya can join his staff at Carrington despite hiring new fitness coach Sam Wilson.

Wilson has joined United following his departure from Parma in October, having only moved to the Italian club as head of performance in the summer after 12 years at Arsenal.

It is understood Tuya has not yet been confirmed as part of Pochettino’s backroom team because she is waiting to see if a work permit will be approved.

She joined the US national team staff in October 2024 shortly after Pochettino, and travelled with him, first-team coach Miguel D’Agostino and analyst Alec Scott when they flew from Cincinnati to Manchester by private jet last week.

Some of the early changes under Pochettino have been more subtle than others. Observant fans may have noticed that when the club announced the team to face Liverpool at 3.15pm on Sunday, it was in positional order.

Previously, United’s line-up has been listed numerically, leaving fans to deliberate over the formation. But Pochettino liked to name his team positionally at Tottenham and Chelsea, and admitted in a Q&A on MUTV last week that he wants to do the same at United.

It was clear from the club’s social media post on Sunday that Pochettino would be sticking to the 4-3-2-1 formation favoured by Michael Carrick, with Patrick Dorgu returning on the left wing and Cunha leading the attack.

Not that it lasted very long, as Pochettino admitted he was forced to put his game plan ‘in the bin’ inside eight minutes when Yoro was sent off.

This weekend’s match-up between United and Brentford brings together the most ill-disciplined English team in all competitions and the dirtiest team in the Premier League.

No English team has had more players sent off this season than United’s four – Yoro twice, Cunha against Roma and Martinez against Chelsea.

Meanwhile, Brentford are bottom of the Premier League’s disciplinary 'points table' thanks to their three red cards and 22 yellow cards. So expect referee Rob Jones to be a busy man on Pochettino’s home debut.

Richard Arnold, who preceded Omar Berrada as United's chief executive, pocketed a £5.53m pay-off to stand down after Ineos moved into the club

While Omar Berrada found himself back in the firing line last weekend, the man he succeeded as United’s chief executive was cutting a much more relaxed figure.

Richard Arnold, who led United between February 2022 and December 2023, was spotted enjoying himself on Chester’s popular Eastgate Street on Saturday night – 24 hours before a beleaguered Berrada watched United’s 4-0 thrashing 30 miles down the road at Anfield.

After stepping up to replace Ed Woodward, Arnold was in charge of United for the period spanning United’s strategic review which led to Sir Jim Ratcliffe acquiring 29 per cent of the club. Once it became clear that Ineos would take over the day-to-day running of United, he accepted a £5.53million pay-off to stand down, with the club’s general counsel Patrick Stewart stepping in on an interim basis.

Arnold is now enjoying a quieter life as an executive director at Norton Motorcycles and, it would appear, nights out close to his home near Chester.

It was there that he met a group of United protesters outside his local pub in June 2022 and admitted that the last 12 months had been ‘a f****** nightmare’ and United had ‘f****** burned through cash’. Nothing much seems to have changed since then.
