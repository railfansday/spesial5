NBC Pulls Ad Criticizing U.S. Military Aid to Israel After Backlash, Reviews Approval Process

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The ad aired during Wednesday's 'SNL' Thanksgiving special without senior NBC management's knowledge, a source familiar with the situation tells The Hollywood Reporter.

NBC will not air a controversial anti-Israel advertisement again after it drew backlash for airing during Wednesday night’s Saturday Night Live Thanksgiving special.

A person familiar with the situation told The Hollywood Reporter that senior management was unaware of the advertisement until after it aired. NBCUniversal will now evaluate its advertising approval process to ensure appropriate levels of review.

The 30-second advertisement, titled “Every Three Seconds,” shows a young girl saving money for a year by doing yard work, including mowing the lawn, blowing leaves and shoveling snow, until she raises $400 to buy a bicycle. The ad then cuts to a girl in Gaza, standing on crutches and missing a leg amid rubble, as the narrator draws a comparison between the two scenarios and U.S. military aid to Israel. “Over a year, she saved $400 for a new bicycle,” the voiceover says. It then cuts to the girl in Gaza. “Every three seconds, our government sends $400 of taxpayer money to Israel to do this.”

The advertisement was paid for by the Institute for Middle East Understanding Policy Project, or IMEU Policy Project. This pro-Palestinian advocacy nonprofit supports ending U.S. military aid to Israel. The group’s campaign, titled “No More for Israel,” encourages viewers to contact their congressional representatives to oppose continued military assistance to the country.

After the advertisement aired, several Jewish and pro-Israel organizations criticized NBC for broadcasting the spot.

Anti-Defamation League CEO Jonathan Greenblatt criticized the network in a post on X, writing, “Again and again, why do people in positions of authority fail to demonstrate basic decency, let alone respect, for the Jewish community? At a moment of escalating antisemitism, there is no rationale to justify fanning the flames with such a twisted narrative.”

B’nai B’rith International also condemned the advertisement in a Facebook post.

“Shame on NBC,” the nonprofit wrote. “The suffering of innocent civilians is a tragedy. But using a child’s pain to spread a twisted, one-sided story that blames the Jewish state, and by extension Jews everywhere, is disgusting.”

Jim Berk, CEO of the Simon Wiesenthal Center, also shared a statement with THR criticizing the network for airing the advertisement.

“The advertisement’s imagery, an American child’s hardship set against the anguish of a child in Gaza, offers viewers a seductive but unsubstantiated narrative: that the struggles of American families are somehow bound up with U.S. support for Israel,” the statement reads in part. “In collapsing a complex foreign policy into a single, emotionally charged imagery, erases the deeper realities, including the imperative to protect Israeli lives and the very existence of the country itself. By manipulating emotion and stripping it of context, this ad seeks to become a catalyst for hostility.”
