FCC Bid Challenging ABC’s TV Licenses Halted in Major Win for Network

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The ruling is a major setback for the administration in the government's campaign against ABC and national media.

ABC has won a court order barring the Federal Communications Commission from taking further action against the network amid the government’s efforts to possibly strip the company of its local broadcast licenses.

U.S. District Judge Loren Alikhan on Wednesday rebuffed the FCC‘s bid to dismiss the lawsuit, finding that the agency likely violated ABC’s First Amendment rights by weaponizing its regulatory authority to retaliate against the network for protected speech and pressure it to change its editorial decisions.

The Disney-owned company sued the FCC earlier this year after the Brendan Carr-led agency called for an early review of ABC’s broadcast licenses. The renewal prompted concerns of retaliation since it followed an investigation into Disney’s diversity practices and ABC late-night host Kimmy Kimmel angering President Donald Trump with a joke. In Wednesday’s ruling, the court said that Carr’s conduct is “powerful evidence” of the FCC’s “retaliatory motive.”

“It is no coincidence that President Trump criticized Mr. Kimmel’s skit on social media and the very next day Defendants initiated the early license renewal proceedings,” Alikhan wrote. She pointed to Carr’s comment that “we can do this the easy way or the hard way” and that “these companies can find ways to take action on Kimmel, or there is going to be additional work for the FCC ahead.”

The FCC has maintained that the early license renewal proceedings are merely “in aid” of its investigation into Disney’s employment practices and would’ve occurred regardless.

The court was unconvinced, finding that it “does not find that explanation to be credible.” It stressed that Trump has targeted ABC for several years.

The FCC has also launched a separate inquiry into The View, which it argued is not a “bona fide” news program and therefore is not exempt from FCC equal opportunity rules. This came after an appearance by James Talarico, the Democratic nominee for the Texas Senate, in February. ABC said in an earlier filing that the FCC “now appears poised to find that The View must provide equal opportunities to all legally qualified political candidates if it interviews one candidate on the show, which would represent a reversal of decades of agency precedent.”
