It's more different priorities. The US (and Canada) has safety laws (and indeed a whole policy on roads) oriented towards protecting the driver and passengers. While the rest of the world tends to consider the lives of those outside of a car to be more important.

Of course, some times those policies will mesh and vehicles can be made that match both priorities. Other times, they won't. And other times, there's no reason why a vehicle can't be built that considers both priorities to be important, but for whatever reason, the designer decides to focus on one market at the expense of the others.

The Cybertruck is probably not the only design that can't be exported to Europe for the same reasons, and there are reasons why most car manufacturers have "US" and "non-US" portfolios so different they'll slap the same name onto two entirely different vehicle designs knowing that there's no confusion because one is intended for Americans, and the other not.
