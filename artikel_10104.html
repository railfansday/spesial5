It's been long proven that outrage gets more traction on the internet than positivity...really, it predates the internet; nightly news stories have led with rage bait for decades.

The existing social media platforms have gotten to the point where everyone is outraged about something - whether it's The Orange Man or The Illegals or The Guns or The Abortions or The Terrible Men or The Terrible Women...and even if we somehow managed to make a new social media platform, tomorrow, that was 100% bot/AI free and exclusively human-made content with no import of content from existing social networks, all the Chronically Online humans would bring their rage about those issues with them to the platform.

The solution, then, is highly restrictive moderation to keep the outrage out of the space. Many vBulletin-style bulletin boards did exactly that to keep users on topic throughout the 2000s and 2010s...but such a heavy-handed moderation system requires dedicated moderators and a group of people who generally agree with the moderation for the sake of group cohesion on the topic. A general-purpose social network would find the required amount of moderation prohibitive. Reddit's moderation requirements basically meant that anyone who was successful at being a moderator, was commonly a Chronically Online person who isn't great at separating disagreement from justified moderation.

So, yes, any new social network, save for maybe a Mastodon server of known friends or an extension of an in-real-life social club, is going to speedrun itself into the flame wars and spam that made Usenet fall out of favor. Personally, I think that it's a result of the removal of the online/offline paradigm of dial-up internet - when there were limits on time and connectivity, it helped keep some distance between our Internet Selves and our Real Selves. Once the internet was always online and in our pocket all the time, there was no stop-and-think, it was just an outlet for every impulse. Any new social network will inherit that problem.
