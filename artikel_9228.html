Native Artist Receives $10K Grant to Revive Visor Craftmaking

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Dustin Newman (Unangax̂/Deg Hit’an Athabascan) will use the Walker Youngbird Foundation grant to revive the qayaatx̂ux, or chief visor.

The Walker Youngbird Foundation named Native artist Dustin Newman (Unangax̂/Deg Hit’an Athabascan) the annual recipient of its Núhi grant today, November 19. The Alaskan artist will use the $10,000 award to revive and pass down the endangered craftsmanship behind qayaatx̂ux, traditional closed crown visors worn by Unangax̂ chiefs.

Through technical research with the Anchorage Museum and collaborations with regional hat-makers, Newman will produce a small series of visors to gift to cultural leaders. An intricately decorated hunting hat historically worn by chiefs and high-ranking whaling captains, the qayaatx̂ux began fading into obscurity in the early 20th century due to factors including American colonization. After completing the series, Newman will share the tradition with community members through Camp Qungaayux on the island of Unalaska and the Agdaagux Culture Camp in King Cove, the fishing village where the artist was raised.

"I first started bending visors at 17 with my mentor Okalena Patricia Lekanoff-Gregory at our regional culture camp in Anchorage and Unalaska," Newman told Hyperallergic. "Why I chose to pursue this project through the Núhi Grant was to uplift the status of these visors not as a wall hanging but as a symbol within our communities honoring our culture bearers and community leaders."
