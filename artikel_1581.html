Gasly takes shock Italian GP pole and denies Russell

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Sometimes inspiration can come from the unlikeliest of sources. After claiming the first pole of his Formula One career, an absolutely stunning effort in his 192nd attempt – edging out Mercedes’ George Russell right at the death – Alpine’s Pierre Gasly revealed that a harmless jibe from his performance coach Ben Thorpe just before the session started had riled him up.

It turned out Gasly and Russell had been playing online chess until about 15 minutes before qualifying.

“Since the [drivers’] briefing last night, I figured out that he [Russell] is obsessed with chess, and so am I,” Gasly explained later in his press conference. “There are a few of us in the paddock who are. So we have exchanged our IDs on Chess.com and we have had a little game going on.

“I won yesterday, and another one that carried on into today,” the Frenchman added. “I was feeling confident and I was picking [taking] piece after piece. Then Ben told me: ‘You are getting George here but he is going to get you in a few minutes!’. And I thought ‘F--- off!’.”

What a way to break your duck. And what a day on which to do it. On Friday, Gasly lost his only podium of the season when the International Court of Appeal reinstated two five-second penalties he received in Monaco, dropping him from third to seventh in that race. The ruling produced fireworks when Flavio Briatore, Alpine’s executive adviser, then accused one of the judges on the ICA panel of being biased towards McLaren, one of two teams who had brought the appeal.

Another 24 hours later, the fireworks were for all the right reasons. And there was no appealing the result; the start of Russell’s final lap in Q3 was slightly compromised by Red Bull’s Max Verstappen, but the Englishman could have no real complaints, losing out by 0.060secs in the end, prompting wild celebrations in the Alpine garage.

To say the result was a surprise would be an understatement. This was the Enstone team’s first pole in 17 years, since Fernando Alonso took P1 Renault at the 2009 Hungarian Grand Prix.

The warning signs were there. Alpine had been quick all weekend. But the Tifosi packing Monza’s grandstands fully expected it to be a battle between Mercedes and their beloved Ferrari for pole.

The Scuderia’s performance was underwhelming. Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton were only ninth and 10th quickest in Q2, the latter squeaking into Q3 by one thousandth of a second from Audi’s Gabriel Bortoleto. “F--- me,” Hamilton said after dodging that bullet.

And although they improved a bit in Q3, Hamilton managing to claim P5, just behind Leclerc in P4, they never threatened the front row. In fact, they later got lucky as McLaren’s Oscar Piastri, who took P3, was later hit with a three-place grid drop post-qualifying for impeding Liam Lawson, meaning both Ferrari drivers rose one place.

It could prove significant. Hamilton, like Russell, knows this weekend represents a golden opportunity to close the gap to Kimi Antonelli in the title race. The two Britons are tied second in the championship heading into the weekend, 59 points behind the Italian. But Antonelli, who sacrificed his qualifying to help Russell, must start from the back of the grid on Sunday due to exceeding the permitted number of engines in a season.

It promises to be a fascinating race, with Antonelli fighting his way through the pack and Russell and Hamilton trying to make hay while they can. Russell sounded confident. “I think they [Alpine] have got a car that’s got some good straight-line speed, which will be nice for them in the race. But, yeah, confident I’ve got the pace to go for the win.”

Could he be underestimating Gasly again? The Frenchman, who lives locally in Milan and speaks fluent Italian, has won here before. He claimed the only victory of his F1 career at Monza for AlphaTauri in 2020.

“Anything is possible tomorrow,” insisted Steve Nielsen, Alpine’s managing director. “We didn’t expect to be in pole position, we are. Tomorrow, good starts, good strategy, good pit stops – who knows? Let’s try to win it.”

Gasly himself said he had no idea what he was capable of. “My plan is to stay first as long as I can. How long is that going to be? I have no idea. My focus is going to be on my driving and making no mistakes and making the best out of what I have.” Perhaps that will be enough for another checkmate.

Antonelli acknowledged that his engine-parts grid penalty meant he had no reason not to assist his teamate Russell by offering towing assistance.

He seemed a little peeved at times on the radio, but he did his job. With Russell likely to need to return the favour for him later this season, there is no point causing bother.

He told Sky Sports: “It’s what I have to do. The team asked me and of course I did what I had to do, I tried to help as much as possible. It’s a bit weird going in two, even the lap I did alone was a bit of a weird feeling. Overall with the penalty today I had to be a good teammate and I had to help as much as I could. I think I did a good job with that and happy it worked out.”

Here’s Oscar Piastri, asked if a pole had been possible: “I don’t think so, no. I think the first lap felt good and I tried to see if anyone wanted to go in front out of the pit lane but no one did and I knew it was always going to be difficult and misjudged the braking a little bit and locked up. That was the session but good to have a decent result, it has been a while. I’ll take it.”

Alpine managing director Steve Nielsen said to Sky Sports that they were aiming high: “Pierre is a class act, as you say, if you give him the machine, he will deliver. We failed to do that a few times last year. We haven’t always given him the most competitive machinery this year, but when we have he has delivered. Particularly earlier in the season so to see the team bounce back, to see him deliver on the track he knows well, he lives locally, he lives in Milan. It was wonderful.

“We mustn’t forget Franco either, he’s done a great job today. I really feel we have given them a good car, and who knows? Anything is possible tomorrow.”

Here’s what he said immediately after the session: “Massive congrats to Pierre because that’s an incredible performance. We were actually playing a game of chess about 15 minutes before qualifying and he beat me there and I thought ‘well I think I’ll beat you in qualifying’ and here we are - he’s ahead of me again! I was disappointed not to get pole. It was a bit messy with Verstappen opening the lap. But Pierre and Alpine have been super fast, even from Q1. Happy to see them get a good result.”

Gasly’s advantage, it appears, seems to be over Russell’s car understeering and lacking grip in turbulent air.

Welcome to Telegraph Sport’s live coverage of and qualifying for the Monza Grand Prix.

Before the weekend’s racing, George Russell was, at least publicly, less than bullish about his chances in Italy.

He did, though, believe he would deliver a better showing than he had in recent weeks.

“I don’t know if we can say we’re favourites this weekend, but we probably have a greater opportunity than we’ve had in the last two races where it’s been less about the power unit and more about the chassis,” he said.

“It’s so close right now, that last tenth in qualifying can be the difference of a couple of positions, which can change the podium. Right now, I don’t know who is going to be the team to beat.”

Russell was almost a minute off winner Lando Norris of McLaren in Hungary, finishing seventh in his Mercedes. Two weeks ago, in the Netherlands, he took the last spot on the podium, with Norris winning again.

However, in the second practice session on Friday and FP3 today, Russell was quickest. He led his former colleague, Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton, who was second fastest.

Red Bull’s Max Verstappen hinted at a rare good weekend as he was third fastest, about a third of a second behind Russell.

Russell, of course, needs a win soon, or he is in danger of seeing the championship run away from him entirely, as Kimi Antonelli already leads him by 59 points in the world title chase.

Perhaps psychologically just as pressing is that erstwhile teammate Hamilton is tied with Russell on 183 points, in third because he has one victory compared to Russell’s two. Slipping down the rankings could knock his confidence.

Russell has not secured pole for the last four races, so a strong showing in qualifying might be the boost he needs ahead of Sunday’s race, while Norris is looking for a pole position hat-trick.
