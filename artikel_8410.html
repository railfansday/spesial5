The Dog Days Aren’t Over – Stage 4 of the Hexatrek Part 2

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A Thru-hike in France involves coming across not just natural wonders but also beautiful architecture and centuries-old villages. While we are crossing Southern France, the Hexatrek route takes us through a multitude of these picturesque municipalities. We walk on cobbled stones, past ivy-covered walls, and through narrow, cozy passages. Many of these villages have been built on hills, with the houses sheltered by the natural surrounding boulders. Other communities tell the story of the Middle Ages with their castles and their bourg ruins within the old village walls.

Bigger towns along the way have often preserved their beautiful 18th-century townhouses and colourful window shutters, like here in Viviers.

And some of these small communities have even set aside a space for hikers to pitch their tents in the heart of the village. There are bathrooms and water taps available as well as communal picnic grounds. It makes one feel incredibly welcome. One of these places is, for example, in St. Genies de Varensal at Kilometer 381 of Stage 4.

Staying in or walking through these places is impressive, calming, and often brings one huge advantage in particular to our hike. That is access to…

and we have found THE best one. In the village of Nant, there is a shop called ‘L’enfariné du Roc’. It is an absolute must for hungry hikers like us. We almost stayed an extra day just for their pain suisse. Another great advantage of these artisan bakeries is that they give us a chance to wait out some of the extreme midday heat, so we are killing two birds with one stone. We delight in all of these different baguettes, flans, tartlets, croissants, and sweet buns.

Thankfully, there is a seemingly never-ending supply of delicious baked goods here in France.

On the other hand, the southern regions also offer an endless supply of something else… horseflies. They really drive me mad, to the point of crying on some days. They are a different breed here, practically indestructible. Hitting them doesn’t seem to do them any harm and they keep coming back for more. It can be absolute torture. Once the dusk settles in, the horseflies give way to the mosquitos, which are just as relentless in southern France as they were in previous stages. I really miss the mountains in these moments and look forward to starting the Pyrenees soon.

But first, we have an animal encounter of a different, much more welcome type. Let me introduce the story of…

After one early lunch, we get unexpected company. Having just left the outskirts of a village, walking over a bridge and standing at the base of a small mountain, we are joined by a small black dog that comes running out of the bushes. The little one keeps right by our side without needing any encouragement. He seems confident around these paths so we assume that he knows the area and that he is going to run off back to where he came from soon. So we start ascending the mountain and the dog stays in close vicinity, never more than 10 meters away. After a while, we realise that he will simply not leave of his own account and we backtrack down the mountain in the hopes that this will encourage him to return home. He follows us without fuss, but still stays close and there’s no owner in sight. Having no other choice but to keep hiking this late in the day, we start back up the mountain, still hoping this curious and confident dog will decide on his own when to head back. No such luck. Kilometer after kilometer, he stays at our side. At this point, we are starting to ask ourselves what we should do with our little friend. We decide to go to the Mairie (the town hall) in the next municipality and ask what we should do. If they can’t help directly,  we will get in touch with a local vet and try to find the dog’s owners. To be quite frank, at this point I am silently hoping that he doesn’t have any. I am getting attached with lightning speed and would seriously consider adopting him. I have even gone so far as to name him ‘Coffee’ already. He is just the sweetest. At some point Mark and Coffee have a silent staring contest, and Coffee wins by jumping on Mark’s leg and making himself comfortable without a care in the world.

It takes around 10 kilometers before we reach the next community. Just as we walk onto the street turning into town, a car comes up behind us. It comes to a screeching stop and a man jumps out, yelling in delight ‘Johnny! Johnny!’. Coffee, or Johnny I suppose, runs up to the man and both are delighted at being reunited. We are quite happy for the two of them, especially since it was so lucky that the man just now happened to drive into town. Waving Coffee/Johnny and his owner goodbye, we can’t help feeling a little sad at his departure, but it is undoubtedly for the best and we are relieved that he has someone who clearly loves him. It also realistically means we get to continue our hike as planned, as it might have looked very different with a new companion. Nevertheless, it was an adorable and unforgettable encounter.

The last day of this section has us walking into the city of Carcassonne (yes, the inspiration behind the board game) with its impressive 12th century landmarks and beautifully preserved walled city. A wonderful place to take a zero before continuing onto the next big adventure – the crossing of the Pyrenees.
