Instagram creators can now earn Amazon commissions right from their posts

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Instagram just gave creators one less reason to say "link in bio."

Starting Nov. 16, eligible U.S. creators can now add Amazon affiliate links right to their Reels and feed posts, according to head of Instagram Adam Mosseri, with room for up to 30 products in a Reel and 20 in a feed post.

"A lot of creators make money with affiliate links, but you've mostly had to share them in stories or bio," Mosseri wrote in a post. He added that the feature is "free to add, and we don't take any cut of what you earn."

Creators have long sent followers to a Story, a DM, or their profile to find those links. Now the recommendation and the link can live in the same post. If someone makes a qualifying purchase through the link, Amazon may pay the creator a commission. (Disclosure: Mashable participates in the Amazon affiliate program.)

The rollout is open to U.S. adults with a professional Instagram account and more than 1,000 followers, WIRED reported. To get started, eligible creators can connect their Amazon Influencer account under Monetization in Instagram's Professional Dashboard. From there, tagging an Amazon item automatically adds an affiliate link and an image preview. Creators who connect an Amazon Associates account can also paste a regular Amazon product URL and still get credit for qualifying sales. Before adding an item, creators can see the estimated commission rate for that product. Shoppers who tap a tag can see the product and its price in Instagram, then head to Amazon to check out.

Moving the link closer to the recommendation could make a real difference for creators who earn a commission when someone buys. Instagram told TechCrunch that product links on Reels and feed posts have drawn about twice as many clicks as links in creators' bios. The timing gives creators a chance to see whether those extra clicks turn into sales during Black Friday.

The announcement comes as Instagram's parent company, Meta, was at odds with Amazon over shopping just two months ago. In September, Amazon blocked Muse, Meta's AI assistant, from shopping on its site on behalf of customers.

So will there be more shopping posts in the feed? "We don't expect a sudden influx of shopping content from this launch," Instagram spokesperson Megan Buckley told WIRED, saying the app will keep recommending posts based on what people are likely to find interesting or useful.

It's important to note that YouTube has been doing this for years. Eligible creators have already been able to tag products and earn commissions through YouTube Shopping in 2023, and in August, YouTube added Amazon to the program, giving U.S. creators a way to tag Amazon products in Shorts, longer videos, and livestreams.

The holiday shopping rush will be an early test for Instagram's version of this feature, as your favorite creator's next gift guide in Reels could have a shopping link for every item on the list.
