Sam Retford, who is known to millions of Outlander: Blood of my Blood fans as Dougal MacKenzie, looks very comfortable on a show that requires him to navigate the unpredictable Scottish terrain, and there’s a good reason why. Not only does Retford love the great outdoors, but he’s also vastly experienced in a variety of skills. From riding a horse to cave diving, or rucking to offroad bike racing, this actor is as able as they come. So, staying still for just a second, Retford tells M&F why Outlander is such a personal and professional high, and how his favorite pastimes informed the role and his physique.

Now living in Glasgow, Retford’s love of adventure was passed down through blood. “My mum was a proper ranch cowgirl,” says Retford, who was born in Australia. “She has ridden horses all around the world, so she taught me to ride.” The star says that he loves nothing more than to get a bunch of pals together and go exploring, and has recently biked in The Himalayas, crossed the rivers of Amazonia, and cave dived around the United Kingdom. When the news came down that Retford would take up the role of Dougal MacKenzie and battle it out in 18th century Scotland, he was understandably all-in.

While most actors stay within the confines of a gym to ready for their roles, Retford added to his indoor workouts by acclimatizing to his new filming environment. As War Chieftain Dougal MacKenzie, Retford wanted to get a feel for the rugged highland landscape. “I wanted this guy to be physically capable,” he tells M&F, noting that he practiced rucking up and down the hills with a weighted backpack. “Even going and sleeping in the heather. I really wanted to build a physique capable of doing these long winter treks, but remain flexible enough to be agile in battle, to be strong enough to carry heavy loads—so everything had to be functional.”

The type of physique required to hunt and survive in the wilds of Scotland would not require a six-pack or be akin to an underwear model, so Retford wanted to build his character with authenticity. “I wanted him to have a big strong back and strong legs,” says the actor. “Okay, he can, lift but can he swing a sword around as well?”

Sam Retford earned a second-degree black belt in Taekwondo in his youth and understands the importance of exercise for agility and injury prevention. He also likes to swing the Indian Mace Bells for balance. But in the gym, his workouts include plyometric moves that require explosive power, such as box jumps. When lifting weights, the star focuses on time under tension and replicating proper form while squatting or making an overhead press.

The star also introduced instability into his lifting elements, for example with elevated platforms or unilateral movements in order to prepare him for the uneven ground outside. Still, the actor says that ultimately, the best way to prepare for his sprinting and fighting scenes was to run in the woods and climb trees. “Just moving about on set and staying warm,” he recalls.

Fans of Outlander: Blood of My Blood will have noticed that the physicality has leveled up in Season 2. For example, Dougal MacKenzie becomes embroiled in a serious battle in Episode 5 and must be rescued from being trapped underwater in Episode 6. Fortunately, the enthusiastic adventurer is more than comfortable with holding his breath. In Episode 2, Dougal also found himself in a fistfight with Brian Fraser (played by Jamie Roy).

Retford wants people to know, however, that progress is individual, takes time, and is not as easy as some social media accounts would like you to believe. He is also keen to point out that it takes a team of stunt coordinators, special effects wizards, lighting, and audio technicians to make any actor appear at their best. “You’ll notice I do quite a lot of shield work and headbutting, as opposed to sword fighting,” he explains. “That’s because I’m a lefty, and my right-handed combat is not very good. So, I have to keep the sword well and truly sheathed for a lot of it, to not cut anyone’s fingers off!”

Blood of My Blood no doubt the dream job for this thrill seeker. “Just to be out there in the cold, in the thick of it, running around, swinging swords. I mean, it’s a beautiful type of physicality, and it’s an amazing thing to call your job. So, I try never to complain. I’m always grateful.”

With Retford more than open to battling through a third season of Outlander: Blood of My Blood, the talented performer is also producing an upcoming documentary in Uganda and also has an eye on traveling to Mexico. “The key is to just say yes to crazy things,” he beams.
