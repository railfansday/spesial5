Te Araroa day 10: My first zero day

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

0km, Amelia and Nathan’s house in Kerikeri

It was delightful to wake up in a bed – a double bed no less. I think the last time I slept in a double bed was in Vietnam in March! The sleep-out isn’t fully finished so there weren’t any curtains in my room, which was fine by me. I woke up before the sunrise anyway. The others had said they wanted a slow morning, so I spent quite a while in bed writing and uploading blog posts before anyone else was up. I also called my parents, who I hadn’t spoken to since before I started hiking. We had bought stuff for a shared breakfast the night before but eventually I was too hungry and couldn’t wait anymore, and ended up grabbing some toast.

When the others were up, we made breakfast and had a guided tour around Amelia and Nathan’s large garden. Seeing Nicole’s reaction to the house and garden remided me that I felt the same when I first arrived in NZ. I was really taken aback by the amount of land a lot of people have over here. In our small, densely populated bits of Europe (Nicole is Swiss) land is very expensive, and you would assume anyone with a garden that size was rather wealthy. But over here, I guess land is relatively cheap (outside of the main population centres anyway). So having big gardens or a paddock or two is much more common.

Then we all spent some time trying to plan the next section. Ruby was trying desperately to organise kayaking from Paihia to Waikare, but the person who would drive the kayaks back was away until Thursday. Nicole and I were trying to figure out how many days of food we would need, and where to stay. It was a challenge because the accommodation options aren’t all shown on the map, some are only in the ‘accomodation’ section of the trail notes, and some are even buried in the trail description. We were getting a bit frustrated at the lack of clarity from the TA app. Based on the mileage though, it was clear it would be quite a long stretch to the next ‘proper’ supermarket, a Four Square in Ruakaka. Four Squares are small local supermarkets usually found in smaller towns in NZ. They can be highly variable in terms of how big they are and what they sell, but TA hikers have to rely on them for resupply in quite a few places. After Paihia, we wouldn’t be hitting another big supermarket until Mangawhai, in over 200km.

Eventually we admitted defeat and decided to take about 5 days of food and plan the rest on the fly. There are enough small shops and dairies in between that we figured we would make it work. It was getting on for lunchtime and we needed to go to the gear shop and the supermarket.Ruby drove us into town and we went into Hunting and Fishing. I got a new headlamp, and I think Ruby needed gas. Then we headed across the road to Woolworths. I managed to get most of the things I wanted, although I find the Woolworths bulk section very lacking compared to New World and Pak’N Save. I got some food for lunch too, and Nicole and I bought some alcohol for Amelia and Nathan as a thank you.

We picked Zoe up in town, where she had been staying with some friends of friends, and headed down towards the Stone Store where we had finished our hike the previous day. Nicole had seen a slice she wanted but hadn’t been able to get it as the cafe was closing. We had a nice lunch by the river, then dropped Zoe off and headed ‘home’ to organise our resupplies.I was quite pleased with the amount of food I had. Actually, I started wondering if I should have got more!

Later in the afternoon, the others all went for a swim but I wanted to continue doing my blog posts. I’d been so far behind and it was feeling good to catch up. I got a couple of posts done by the time the others came back, and when they were done showering we headed over to the main house to make pizzas. It was a nice evening and cool to get to know Amelia and Nathan a bit – and hear some of their stories from outdoor adventures with Ruby and Matt! After dinner I spoke to a friend on the phone, and eventually went to bed.
