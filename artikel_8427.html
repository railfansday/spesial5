When an emergency leaves you short on money, call 211 to find local help with rent, utility bills, and food. Then apply directly to the program that matches each need, since each one has its own rules and approves its own aid.

Coming up short is common. In the Federal Reserve’s survey for 2025, only 63% of adults said they would pay a surprise $400 expense entirely with cash or savings, or with a credit card they’d pay off at the next statement.

211 is the one number built for this. Local specialists answer it around the clock, every call is confidential, and they know the help in your area for housing, food, bills, and health care. Dial 211, or find your local 211’s website through 211.org.

Starting January 1, 2027, adults ages 19 to 64 who get Medicaid on income alone may need to show 80 hours a month of work, school, or community service to qualify or stay covered.

Apply the day you find a program that fits, before every document is in hand. SNAP, for one, pays benefits back to the date you apply, and you finish its interview and send proof during the 30 days the state has to decide.
