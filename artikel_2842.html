Are you confident in the Lions defense without Aidan Hutchinson?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Pyrrhic victories are unfortunately mounting for the Detroit Lions.

Despite winning five games in a row, they have lost two irreplaceable parts of their team. The Lions have now lost two cornerstone pieces of their defense over the last three weeks. Jack Campbell suffered a scary neck injury and will be sidelined for an unknown period of time. Against the New England Patriots in Munich, the Lions lost yet another heartbeat of their defense. Aidan Hutchinson suffered a serious knee injury that will likely sideline him for the rest of the 2026 season—and perhaps into the 2027 season as well.

The impact Hutchinson has on this team is difficult to quantify. For a team built on grit, Hutchinson exemplified that to a tee. Without Hutchinson, can the Lions defense continue to play at a high level?

Few teams boast a pass rusher like Hutchinson, and he was by far the most potent edge on the Lions' roster. Hutchinson will finish the season with 52 pressures per PFF, while the next-closest defender is Alim McNeill (36) and the next-closest edge is DJ Wonnum (18)—that's a significant gap. Without question, the Lions are in a far worse position than they were entering the week. The ceiling for the Lions to be a great defense is gone, in my opinion.

However, I think the floor is still intact.

When the Lions lost Hutchinson back in 2024, their defense was ill-equipped to handle it. The pass rushing depth was largely just Josh Paschal and Al-Quadin Muhammad and The Lions had to make a deadline deal to acquire Za'Darius Smith. They lost Alim McNeill to a torn ACL in Week 15. By the time they rolled into the postseason, their pass rush was limited. This limitation put a lot of pressure on the secondary, and the defense wound up allowing 45 points to the Commanders. This, in turn, forced the offense to be perfect, and on a day when Jared Goff threw three interceptions, it was not.

I think the rest of 2026 will be tough sledding for the defense, but the Lions have some options. For one, Wonnum has been playing very well this season. He is not the game-wrecker that Hutchinson is, but he is a steady presence capable of sound run defense and pass rush. Secondly, rookie Derrick Moore is coming into his own of late, now having four sacks in two games. Refining his skillset is still a priority for him, but it's not hard to see the potential with him. It might be earlier than expected, but he now has to step into a sizable role on defense.

The Lions also have McNeill playing at an All-Pro level, while Tyleik Williams is quietly entrenching himself as a menace up the middle. Sprinkle in guys like Tyler Lacy (another underappreciated player) and Skyler Gill-Howard (the pass-rushing rookie tackle), and the Lions not only have more talent on the defensive line than in 2024, but also more flexibility.

The loss of Hutchinson will put more pressure on the secondary to step up. The secondary has been balling as of late, but there will be questions about whether that is a mirage or not. Over the last five games, the defense has played the hapless Arizona Cardinals, the inconsistent Green Bay Packers, the formerly-hapless Minnesota Vikings, the hapless Miami Dolphins, and the Drake Maye-less New England Patriots. You can only play the opponent on the schedule, but concerns about the authenticity of this defensive turnaround are certainly justified—are they actually good now or just benefitting from weak and misfortunate opponents? If the Lions can put up a great defensive showing versus the Chicago Bears on Thanksgiving, then you can squash some of my doubts.

What makes me encouraged about the secondary is that they look invigorated. They are intercepting passes. They are batting away balls. They are (mostly) limiting the explosive plays. Whereas the defense looked lost to start the season, they are playing with far more confidence at this point. Getting Brian Branch was certainly a catalyst, and if the Lions can add Kerby Joseph to the mix as well, then the defensive back group suddenly looks far more capable

You cannot replace a player like Hutchinson. You will struggle to even recreate him in the aggregate. He is a rarity amongst NFL players, a player with a high level of talent and the motor to back it up. However, I think that the Lions have enough pieces on their defense to weather the storm. If they can be a good defense, that might be enough when paired with this potent offense.
