Greg Olsen calls out New York Post for ‘making up quotes’ about Malik Nabers commentary

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Fox Sports NFL analyst Greg Olsen did what any analyst worth his salt is paid to do on Sunday afternoon: he offered his perspective on one of the key storylines emerging during the New York Giants-Philadelphia Eagles game. That storyline involved the Giants' star wide out Malik Nabers, who grew frustrated throughout the game as the Giants struggled to get him the ball, and later, after he dropped a touchdown pass.

Olsen and play-by-play partner Joe Davis discussed Nabers' body language after he was seen slamming his helmet after a frustrating sequence in the fourth quarter.

"His body language has been terrible today," Davis said.

"You brought up Malik Nabers. You never know exactly what's being said. It's easy for us to sit here and interpret body language," Olsen said. "When we asked [Giants OC] Matt Nagy about him, he said 'Listen, he's very vocal. When he didn't get the ball to start the year, he let everyone know it.' He said 'Me and Malik have a good understanding right now. What's perceived as being negative, what's perceived as being a distraction, I think we both have an understanding of each other that allows it to coexist.' Just certainly is something to keep an eye on."

Later in the game, Davis again brought up Nabers' body language after the wide receiver still appeared sour after a teammate scored a go-ahead touchdown. Olsen, once again, offered a measured response.

"I have played with a lot of really good wide receivers, a lot really good players. There's never been a great wide receiver who hasn't demanded the ball. That kind of comes with the territory," Olsen said. "But there is a very fine line between being demanding, being a competitor and all those great things. That's great. It just can never cross that it feels like it takes away from the success of your teammates. So when your teammate catches a wide open touchdown, you need to be the happiest guy on the field. You can't still be worried about your targets. That's the fine line. He is a talented, young, really good player. You've just got to keep it in check."

Then came the most critical portion of Olsen's commentary. Nabers would drop a difficult, but catchable, pass in the end zone and Olsen asserted that those are the types of plays Nabers needed to make if he was going to demand more of the ball.

"Listen, you want to demand the ball, you can't throw that ball any better," Olsen said. "That's a touchdown. You're a premier player in this league. You've got an opportunity there, you've got to catch it."

Nabers was apparently tipped off to the analysis from the Fox booth after the game, and the wide receiver went on social media to criticize Olsen.

"Y'all be trying create a narrative on me soo bad it's actually crazy!!" Nabers wrote. "If you [don't] like the way I play the game keep the camera off me!! SIMPLE!

"But @gregolsen88 callin me a bad teammate when every team I've been on, there's NOBODY I've played with had said dat bout me is crazy! My face the same way if I'm havin a good game, bad, winning or losing. If it's a problem 1. Keep the camera off me. 2. Pray bout it! Simple."

The problem with Nabers' retort is that Olsen never said, nor implied, that the star wide out is a "bad teammate." Olsen, in fact, seemed to go out of his way to normalize Nabers' behavior as something that competitive players, especially at the wide receiver position, tend to do. Olsen's main critique was that if Nabers is going to behave like that, he needs to have the play to back it up.

But it seems that some news outlets took Nabers' social media post at face value and have attributed the words "bad teammate" to Greg Olsen. The Fox Sports analyst took to social media to offer a retort of his own on Monday morning.

"Since people are going to just make up quotes and put worlds [sic] in my mouth, here were my comments on Malik Nabers," Olsen wrote. "At no point did we ever say he was a bad teammate."

Olsen then singled out the New York Post and USA Today as needing to "adjust their stories." The Fox Sports analyst posted a screenshot of the Post quoting him as saying Nabers was a "bad teammate" when that is never something he said.

Awful Announcing subsequently reviewed Sunday's broadcast and did not find any instances of Olsen or Davis using the phrase "bad teammate." That suggestion appears to have originated from Nabers' social media post.

The Post has subsequently adjusted its story to include Olsen's response and no longer attributes him the "bad teammate" quote. However, the USA Today story still attributes the quote to Olsen. Both the Post and USA Today seem to have gotten their original, inaccurate transcription from a Heavy.com story about Nabers' remarks towards Olsen. At the time of this publication, Heavy.com has also not updated its story.

There is a certain irony here that Olsen needed to call out the Post in particular, considering both Fox and the New York Post are Murdoch-owned enterprises. It really be your own people sometimes.
