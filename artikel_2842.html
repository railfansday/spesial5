Sean Payton gives curious answer on Broncos’ vision for Jahdae Barron

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

With the recent extension of cornerback Riley Moss and the desire for the Denver Broncos to revisit contract negotiations with Ja'Quan McMillian, many fans across Broncos Country have wondered what the vision is for second-year cornerback Jahdae Barron.

The Broncos have locked up both their boundary corners long-term in Moss and Patrick Surtain II. Knowing that, it's a given that Barron isn't going to see any significant reps on the outside in the near future unless an injury were to occur. If McMillian departs in free agency after this season, kicking him inside to the nickel spot to replace Ja'Quan makes a lot of sense. Though that's a bridge the team will have to wait and cross in due time.

Yesterday after practice, Head Coach Sean Payton was asked how Barron fit in with the defense. I thought Payton gave a curious answer. Your mileage may vary, but this is what he had to say on the issue.

"It's all year-to-year. In other words, relative to Jahdae, he's got that flexibility to play in the dime stuff, play outside," stated Payton.

With Denver's cornerbacks now healthy, it seems likely, based off the aforementioned, that Barron's only chance of seeing the field this year [barring injury] is going to be in Defensive Coordinator Vance Joseph's dime packages. That's not necessarily the news Broncos Country wants to hear, with many of them longing for the 2025 first-round pick to have a larger role in the defense.

Moving forward, as noted, things do remain fluid. On multiple occasions, Payton and Joseph have touted Barron's outside and inside flexibility. One thought that has rolled through my mind, as well as many of yours, is whether or not Jahdae could have a future at the safety position—potentially replacing Brandon Jones, who is a free agent at the end of this season.

Back when Payton was coaching the New Orleans Saints, they drafted Ohio State standout Malcolm Jenkins in the first round of the 2009 NFL Draft. Like Barron, Jenkins was also a Jim Thorpe Award winner. During his rookie season, he spent time on the boundary but had most of his snaps inside at the nickel in Gregg Williams' defense.

The following year, he would move to free safety, a position he would man for the Saints and Philadelphia Eagles for the next twelve years of his career. Asked as a follow up to the first question, Payton gave his answer on Barron's ability to play safety. He kept it short and sweet.

"That's not something we've thought about or discussed."

All things considered, I am a bit surprised by Payton's answer. Though the fluidity with the year-to-year comment does make a bit of sense. Given Barron's versatility and ability to play multiple roles, he's a good player to have in the defense. But the commentary given by Payton makes it a little ambiguous as to what the future has in store for Barron.

For now, apparently Barron will be relegated to dime package looks. Beyond this season? That'll be determined with whatever moves they make in the offseason.

What do you think, Broncos Country? Give me your thoughts on Payton's comments and where you see Barron fitting in with the team moving forward.
