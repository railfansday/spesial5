Bye Week Roundtable: The Falcons are stuck in the mud

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Entering the bye week on a losing streak was likely for the Falcons, given their recent opponents. Facing the Bengals and Chiefs in consecutive weeks will leave most teams scrambling for answers. What is alarming in Atlanta is how lifeless and helpless the recent performances have been, including in a matchup against a far lesser team than Kansas City or Cincinnati put on the field. From being manhandled in the trenches against Tampa Bay to having no answers against Cincinnati in Madrid to never posing a threat against the Chiefs, it's hard to find positives about the Falcons right now.

The bye week couldn't be coming at a better time for Kevin Stefanski. It's also an ideal time to put together a roundtable with Tre'Shon Diaz and Kevin Knight. We'll dive into some of the most pressing topics surrounding the franchise's current state.

Allen Strk: Where to begin? The lack of play-action efficiency is glaring, but the frequent stubbornness in calling predictable screens and ineffective jumbo packages has been just as jarring. The coaching staff isn't learning much from previous game tape. Opposing defenses are consistently breaking on screens before the ball is even released. Several of these failures occur on third downs, which completely neutralizes them. In short-yardage scenarios, they attempt to use extra tight ends and offensive linemen to generate extra push. That has rarely worked, yet those packages continue to be consistently used.

They struggle to function playing from behind and operate with a total lack of confidence on third-and-longs. They also aren't recognizing where the unit is falling short, and they fail to effectively utilize their premium talent. Bijan Robinson's extraordinary skill set hasn't come close to being maximized. He is rarely featured as a pass-catcher outside of a swing pass or checkdown. For all the effort put into Michael Penix Jr.'s development, the coaching staff has lost sight of featuring the team's best players not named Drake London. That includes Kyle Pitts, who is largely an afterthought until the team is down by three possessions in the second half.

Tre'Shon Diaz: Rees' offense lacks balance, discipline, and rhythm. The offensive line was already declining as an outside zone unit. They've struggled to adjust to the newly implemented gap-scheme concepts. They also can't stop committing penalties. Jawaan Taylor's false start troubles are no surprise, but that sickness is spreading.

Holding penalties have cost the team lately. It's hard to get into a rhythm when you're shooting yourself in the foot. It doesn't help that Michael Penix is missing open receivers throughout the game. Rees has struggled to establish game flow, as his attempts to get Penix comfortable aren't working.

Kevin Knight: I think the simplest explanation is that Rees/Stefanski came in prepared to run a certain offense, and the Falcons do not have the personnel to execute it. We've talked about the transition to more diverse run schemes and more emphasis on the screen game as an answer to pressure.

The offensive line is sort of trapped between schemes, the screen game is sloppy, and Michael Penix is not able to hit the short throws with consistency. It's sort of a toxic stew that is really exposing Rees/Stefanski's inability to adapt the scheme to their talent.

Allen Strk: There has to be genuine concern when all the positive attributes are non-existent. Terrell isn't physical at the line of scrimmage, disrupting the receiver's timing and rhythm. His route recognition hasn't been there to jump on targets in his direction. His tackling has been disastrous, as he missed four against Tampa Bay. Worst of all, the simplest double moves are baiting him out of position.

It's one thing for Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins to have their way with you. For Cyrus Allen to win on a stop-and-go to torch you for a highlight-reel touchdown is troubling. Quarterbacks aren't hesitant to target Terrell. It happened on opening day against the Steelers, when Aaron Rodgers found Roman Wilson and D.K. Metcalf open downfield after beating Terrell at the line of scrimmage. The veteran corner was forced to interfere before the ball arrived, albeit somehow the one on Metcalf wasn't penalized. If things don't improve, the front office will have to assess his future, because you don't want a liability on the roster who is being paid like a top-tier player.

Tre'Shon Diaz: I do think there's cause for concern if the team expects him to regain All-Pro status. Corners rarely sustain their peaks for more than five years. That's how long it's been since the corner was named an All-Pro. Terrell is losing reps to everyone right now, not just Chase.

It's fair to suspect that his groin injury is still hampering him, but that's another reason his longevity could be called into question. He's missed six games in the last two seasons and will be 30 at the start of 2028. The hope is that he can recover and be viable in 2027 before the regime has to make a tough call.

Kevin Knight: You kind of have to be after his play this season. I still think a fair amount of it is health-related, but even so, Terrell is making way too much money to be such a liability. He'll have the rest of the season to get healthier and prove that this first half was an aberration.

If he doesn't, there aren't really many good options for the Falcons. They still owe Terrell a ton of money in 2027. There isn't an easy way out of the contract. Most likely, he'll be with the team through next season regardless of his play this year.

Allen Strk: As much as the Falcons need to revamp their offensive line, replacing two tackles in the same off-season is extremely difficult. The troubling state of offensive line play across the league has been a talking point for years. Look no further than the potential market. With the best potential available tackle options being Warren McClendon Jr. and Ikem Ekwonu, with the latter coming off a severe injury, the front office will likely be forced to go in another direction.

They should absolutely draft a later-round prospect to develop under and compete with Matthews, who is an ineffective run blocker and becoming more penalty-prone. The group desperately needs more of an anchor on outside runs. Jawaan Taylor is too sluggish getting out of his stance and gives far too many easy tells, as J.J. Watt brilliantly broke down. Despite needing a new right tackle and Matthews being serviceable at best, they won't have the bandwidth to add two surefire starters next year.

Tre'Shon Diaz: You can argue that the Falcons should do what they can to upgrade both, but that's easier said than done. Franchise tackles rarely hit the market. The team would need to invest a high draft pick to acquire one. Pulling off both moves in the same offseason while also trying to upgrade the center position may prove too difficult.

Matthews has been reliable as a pass protector after a slow start and an early-season injury. The run game would suffer on the left side for one more year, but if the team fills the other holes on the line, they should be able to compensate.

Kevin Knight: I think Jake Matthews can hold it down for one more season. He'll also probably have to. It does help contract-wise. The Falcons can come up with one tackle via free agency or (more likely) a high pick in the 2027 NFL Draft. Adding two new tackles is almost impossible. Best-case scenario would be a top tackle addition and someone like Ethan Onianwa stepping up and claiming the other spot. We'll hope for the best.

Allen Strk: This can be a tricky assessment considering how the game went against Tampa Bay. The entire defense took a one-sided mauling on the ground. Daniels and Perkins Jr. took their share of punishment, with the gap-scheme runs leaving them isolated at the second level against an imposing running team. Both linebackers are understandably a work in progress with gap discipline and play awareness. They still have shown encouraging signs of developing into true three-down starters.

The way Daniels takes on blocks and delivers punishing hits can become a real tone-setter over time. Perkins Jr.'s range in coverage is impressive. Overcoming an ugly lapse on Emmitt Johnson's first receiving touchdown was important in Sunday's loss to the Chiefs. After missing badly in the open field, he played with real urgency and made several plays across the field. Both linebackers have been thrown into the fire recently. They have responded well to the challenges, gaining valuable experience as a potential long-term starting tandem.

Tre'Shon Diaz: I would give both a C+. Rookie linebackers typically make a lot of mistakes; Sonny Styles-es don't grow on trees. Still, both are making enough good plays to get a positive rating from me. Daniels is at his best moving forward; he has a nose for screens and can slip past blockers in the box. He needs to beat blockers through their shoulder and get stronger at the point of attack. He also needs to process a bit faster, but that will come with reps. His ability to handle green dot duties gives me confidence he'll get there.

Perkins excels at moving backward and has been their best coverage linebacker. He struggles with contact more than Daniels, however, and hasn't been as effective as a blitzer as some predicted. If he focuses on building up his play strength this offseason, it'll help him stay on the field for all 3 downs.

Kevin Knight: Kendal Daniels has been everything you could ask for. He's worn the green dot as a rookie. Despite some struggles, he has largely looked like a good starting linebacker. Harold Perkins has been more up and down, but he was also the bigger project (and later pick) between the two.

I think both have more than justified their draft selections thus far. Am I confident that Atlanta has locked down two long-term starting linebackers? Not yet. I think Daniels is a full-time starter, but Perkins is still a work in progress. We'll see how he progresses over the remainder of 2027.
