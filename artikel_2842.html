An Eagles rookie progress report at the midway point of 2026 season

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

An Eagles rookie progress report at the midway point of 2026 season originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

The Eagles had high expectations entering this season but are sitting at 2-7 at the bye week.

Now it's time to see what the young players can do.

The Eagles drafted eight players in April and seven of them have seen game action over the first half of the season, but to varying degrees.

With the Eagles on their Week 10 bye week, let's recap the first half of the season for all eight of their draft picks:

After some disappointing moments early in his rookie season, we have seen a ton of growth from Makai Lemon recently. Without DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks for three weeks, Lemon has had the three best games of his rookie season against the Cowboys (7 catches for 67 yards, 1 TD), Commanders (6 catches for 57 yards) and Giants (6 catches for 61 yards). While Lemon was tough on himself for a couple drops in Week 9, he has been one of the few bright spots recently.

Through nine games, Lemon has been targeted 54 times and has 37 catches for 312 yards and a touchdown. He's on pace for 102 targets, 70 catches, 589 yards and 2 touchdowns. But it's also worth noting that his numbers have been improving as the season goes on.

Here's a look at the Eagles' rookie records for receptions and receiving yards. Lemon is on pace to finish his season second on the receptions list and ninth in receiving yards. But hopefully the Eagles have scrapped all those behind-the-line passes to him.

Through nine weeks, Lemon is tied with KC Conception for the lead among rookies in receptions and is fourth in receiving yards among rookies behind Denzel Boston (507), Concepcion (437) and Carnell Tate (413).

After a rough training camp, Eli Stowers dealt with a quad injury that kept him on IR for the first six weeks of the season. He made his NFL debut a few weeks ago against the Cowboys, but hasn't played a ton on offense yet. Here's a look at his offensive snap counts for these last three weeks.

Week 7 vs. DAL: 12 snapsWeek 8 at WAS: 13 snapsWeek 9 vs. NYG: 7 snaps

Overall, Stowers has been targeted 5 times and has 3 catches for 53 yards. All three of those catches came in the loss to Washington and two of them came very late in the fourth quarter. His long of 34 yards came at the very end of the game.

But Stowers hasn't looked out of place in limited action. He has been doing some good things away from the football and has shown a real willingness to do some dirty work and block. While blocking is probably never going to be his strength, he needed to improve in that area and is clearly working on it. But in this lost season, it would make sense to see more of Stowers in the second half.

In addition to his work on offense, Stowers has also played 27 special teams snaps. That's something new for him because he began his college career as a quarterback and then became a star tight end. But he's working hard at that too.

The plan was for Markel Bell to sit all season and learn behind Jordan Mailata and Lane Johnson. But that plan got tossed in the garbage can early this season when Johnson abruptly retired. Bell was thrust into the starting lineup in Week 4 against the Rams and has started the last six games with plenty of ups and downs.

Early in his time as a starter, Bell looked really promising and looked to be settling into that role. But the last couple of games have been much rougher for the 6-foot-9 rookie. In six starts this season, according to ProFootballFocus, Bell has given up 5 sacks and 19 pressures, but 3 of those sacks came in his late-notice start against the Rams in Week 4.

There's a really good chance that veteran Fred Johnson would be a better option right now, but the Eagles should ride this out with Bell to figure out if he really has the potential to be a long-term replacement for Johnson. We'll probably learn a lot about him over the second half of this season.

Round 5-178: Cole Payton, QB, North Dakota State

The Eagles made sure to give Cole Payton plenty of reps in training camp despite having three quarterbacks on the depth chart ahead of him. But Payton has been inactive five times this season and has been active but hasn't played three times. Back in Week 5 against the Jaguars, Payton played 10 snaps on special teams and that's the only time he has seen the field.

The idea of Payton on special teams is fun, especially in a lost season like this. Maybe the Eagles will get him some more reps in that role or perhaps find a way to get him on the field on offense. Joe Milton and Tanner McKee are both still ahead of Payton on the depth chart but the way this season is going, would you be surprised to see Payton eventually get on the field as a starter?

When the Eagles drafted Micah Morris in the sixth round, he was expected to be a developmental pick and that has held true. Through 9 games, Morris has played one snap on offense and 19 on special teams. Morris has been active the last seven weeks but hasn't needed to play. If this season continues to spiral, maybe we'll get to see what Morris can do.

Round 7-244: Cole Wisniewski, S, Texas Tech

Because Cole Wisniewski missed most of training camp with injuries (hamstring and concussion), he never really had much of a shot to make the Eagles' 53-man roster. But once Wisniewski cleared waivers, the Eagles signed him to the practice squad and he has been there all season. Through nine games, Wisniewski has been elevated twice and has played 36 snaps on special teams. He has one elevation remaining.

The Eagles knew Uar Bernard was a long-term project when they drafted him and that is playing out. Bernard has next-level athleticism but is extremely raw so he needs to continue to learn the game. After the Eagles waived him at final cuts, they signed him to the practice squad. Unfortunately, Bernard suffered an injury in practice and has been on the Practice Squad Injured list back on Oct. 9. So this project is on hold for now.

Round 7-252: Keyshawn James-Newby, Edge, New Mexico

There was a lot of hand-wringing from fans in the summer when the Eagles attempted to get both Bernard and Josh Weru through waivers to the practice squad. The Eagles were probably more concerned about Keyshawn James-Newby. Because even though the seventh-round pick missed most of training camp with an ankle injury, he did flash in his preseason action. But James-Newby cleared waivers and has been on the practice squad all season.

The Eagles have used up two of his three elevations this season. James-Newby played 48 defensive snaps in his NFL debut against the Panthers and then 4 the following week against the Panthers. He has a couple of combined tackles in his defensive action. He has also played 40 special teams snaps.

The Eagles signed the 24-year-old long snapper after the draft and he has played in every game this season — totaling 77 special teams snaps. He has 3 combined tackles and a fumble recovery.
