NBA: Top 5 Wolves Games I’ve Attended

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Over the years, I've been very blessed to attend a litany of exciting Timberwolves games, many in the playoffs. Now being a Gen-Z'er, these all came in the Anthony Edwards era. I wish I could've seen Kevin Garnett live, but unfortunately his prime came before my time as a serious basketball watcher.

Here are my top five games that I've attended and my point-of-view, in reverse order.

The entire 2023-24 season was an absolute whirlwind and the lead up to this game was no different. I was entirely confident the Wolves were going to blitz the Dallas Mavericks as we'd advance to the NBA Finals, but alas that was not the case. I cannot begin the immense joy I felt walking into Target Center that night, with pregame drinks at Cowboy Jack's across the street, of course. I hit the team store to get some Conference Finals merch, took some pictures of the TNT crew (including Draymond Green, ew), and even got to my seat damn near in the rafters.

It was pure bliss, only to get my heart ripped out two and a half hours later. Even though Minnesota lost, it was still a close game with high stakes and as just a fan of the sport, I was enthralled. Had the Wolves won this game it would've been higher, but it lands on my list at number five.

The story of my time at this game begins at Blue Door Pub on Como Avenue in Minneapolis. Only living a couple blocks away, I had never been and my time there was spent nervously eating a Juicy Lucy and worrying about the game. If you may remember, Phoenix was widely thought of as the prohibitive favorite. Guys like Devin Booker and Kevin Durant terrified me, as to that point the Timberwolves hadn't won a playoff series since 2004.

The Wolves absolutely took over. Anthony Edwards was hitting step-back threes over Durant while jawing at him. Naz Reid was hitting "jelly" reverse layups on the fastbreak. I almost couldn't believe it. Watching your team win like that, especially live…there's nothing like it. Because I attended this game and it went well, a tradition was sparked with my roommates and I: Since we had watched the game apart, I now had to watch the games alone and away from them to preserve those good vibes. I can't say it didn't work, either, as I watched games two, three, and four from my room as Minnesota swept Phoenix.

Man, this was fun. For the first time in my basketball watching life, a Timberwolves team enjoyed success in a playoff setting. Riding some irrational confidence, I didn't think that there was any way that a LA Clippers team led by Paul George (still called "Pandemic P" at the time) was going to stop the Wolves from setting a date with the Memphis Grizzlies in the first round.

George had a great game, hitting big shot after big shot. Things weren't exactly going great on the Timberwolves side of things because if you remember, Karl-Anthony Towns was in major foul trouble for most of the game. Then D'Angelo Russell started making shots. Anthony Edwards made a couple big plays. Before I knew it, I was watching Patrick Beverly throw the ball up in the air as the buzzer sounds and jump up onto the scorer's table like the Wolves had just won the NBA Finals.

To all the NBA fans who clown us Wolves fans for that reaction and our appreciation of it: take a hike. You never had to experience the suck that was a Luke Ridnour-Alexey Shved backcourt. As far as meaningful games I've attended, this may take the top spot. However, this list is the best games, so it lands at number three. I say meaningful because I attended this game with just my dad. He is the one who raised me to be a basketball fan and taught me most of what I know about the game, and to share the moment of our favorite team advancing for the first time since I was two years old with him was absolutely special. I'll never forget it.

The most recent game on my list slides in at number two. Anthony Edwards was out. Donte DiVincenzo had torn his Achille's. Then just hours before the game, it was announced that Ayo Dosunmu was going to be out. I was convinced that Jaden McDaniels and Julius Randle were not going to be enough to finish the Nuggets with most of their cast of characters. Denver would be able to focus their efforts on Jaden, who earlier in the series had dropped the "They're all bad defenders" line.

In the building, it felt like most Timberwolves fans felt the same. Minnesota blitzed Denver early as Julius made some tough buckets flanked by TJ Shannon being a menace in transition. Then as the game went on and the Wolves remained neck and neck with the Nuggets, even earning a lead, belief started to creep in. Denver wasn't going away though. I really thought they were going to pull some voodoo magic out of Jokić's horse racing hat and push this series to a seventh game. I was pushed further down this line of thinking when Jokić and Jaylen Clark got into a scuffle, resulting in two Minnesota technical fouls while Denver only received one.

Then, Jaden McDaniels showed everyone the player he truly is.

A couple made threes, a couple mir-range shots, and plenty of drives to the rim where he proved himself right: the Nuggets were all bad defenders. 32 points and 10 rebounds for Jaden that night, and a massive statement to the rest of the league that even though the Wolves were down their best three guards, their pack was not left defenseless.

Yes, the best game I have ever attended in person is a 45-point blowout victory.

Everyone remembers this series, but they remember it for the epic Game 7 comeback. What gets lost in the shuffle is that the Timberwolves blitzed Denver to the tune of a 2-0 series lead before the Nuggets came back with a Balboa-level haymaker to take a series lead of 3-2. This Game 6 was an elimination game for Minnesota, and it didn't start very well. The Nuggets jumped out to a 9-2 lead after just three minutes of play.

What followed that opening stanza of that game can only be described as a waxing.

Blink and you would've missed a 24-2 Timberwolves run. The Nuggets made a couple runs, sure, but it was never all that close again. Hell, even Jordan McLaughlin and Luka Garza got minutes on the floor in a Game 6 in the playoffs. That's how lopsided this victory was. Euphoria is the only feeling I can describe from the crowd at Target Center that night. Every Wolves fan wearing the "I <3 Wolves" t-shirt left happy that night. The team we absolutely hate had a chance to knock us off our Cinderella ride early, on our floor even. Saying "Hell No" and forcing a Game 7 was the only thing we could do, and we did it in crushing fashion.
