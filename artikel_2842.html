Milan Momcilovic’s 22-point first half shows Kentucky’s ceiling and 1 big PG problem

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Things didn't end that great Friday against Indiana, but the first half was at least fun. It showed just how good Kentucky Basketball can be, and then the second half revealed the biggest problem with this roster: Point guard play.

Everyone has been waiting for a true Milan Momcilovic eruption, and against Indiana at Lucas Oil Stadium, it finally arrived.

Early on, the Hoosiers looked like they couldn't miss if they tried, opening the game red-hot from beyond the arc and burying Kentucky in an 11-point first-half hole.

The Wildcats needed someone to stop the bleeding before the game slipped away completely. That's when Momcilovic decided to take over.

Once he found his rhythm, the ball was barely grazing the net. Momcilovic torched Indiana for 22 points in the first half on 8-of-10 shooting from the floor and 5-of-6 from three-point range.

Single-handedly flipping the momentum of the building, he helped Kentucky erase that 11-point deficit and walk into the locker room with a 47-45 halftime lead.

As electric as that first-half outburst was, it left behind a glaring question: how does a shooter that hot go minutes at a time without even touching the ball?

Momcilovic came out firing, scoring seven of Kentucky's first nine points in the opening three minutes. Yet somehow, the Wildcats followed that up with a three-minute stretch, and then another nearly five-minute stretch, in the first half where he didn't get a single shot attempt.

Even more maddening was what happened coming out of the locker room.

After catching fire to drag Kentucky back into the lead, Momcilovic didn't get a single shot off during the first five minutes of the second half.

With 7:54 remaining in the game and Indiana in front 74-66, Milan had attempted just four total second-half shots.

When your best shooter, and maybe the best shooter in college basketball, disappears for long stretches, it rarely falls on just one person.

Off-ball movement: Part of it is on Momcilovic himself. When defenses start hugging him off screens, he has to keep cutting with the same urgency he showed during his first-half flurry.

Play-calling: Part of it falls on Mark Pope and the coaching staff. When a guy has 22 points at the break, the playbook shouldn't get cute; you keep running actions designed to free him up until the defense proves it can stop it.

Point guard play: The biggest share of the blame, in my opinion, belongs to Kentucky's backcourt.

Zoom Diallo had 15 points, but it took him 14 shots to get there. Even worse, he and Alex Wilkins stopped moving the ball when Kentucky needed it the most.

Both of Kentucky's lead guards, Diallo and Wilkins, get tunnel vision. They only see the rim, and the ball is going up no matter what the score is, who is hot, or what the situation dictates.

Rule No. 1 of playing point guard is pretty simple: find the hot hand. Too many times on Friday night, Kentucky's guards dribbled the ball like they got paid by the bounce while a scorching-hot shooter stood waiting on the perimeter.

Your primary ball handlers both can't be score first options, and right now that is where Kentucky is. That gets us into a whole new topic of why Kentucky decided on grabbing both, and whether they can play together in a system designed for movement.

None of that takes away from how special Momcilovic's first half was.

He would finish the night with 28 points on 10-of-16 shooting to go along with four rebounds and four assists. But it came in a loss.

Now, the Wildcats just need to figure out how to keep feeding him so some of these losses turn into wins.
