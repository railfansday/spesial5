Cannavaro: Zidane’s headbutt helped me win the Ballon d’Or, Barcelona criticism makes me ‘smile’

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Italy legend Fabio Cannavaro admits Zinedine Zidane’s headbutt in the 2006 World Cup Final helped him win the Ballon d’Or: ‘It’s worth three times more now; I don’t see how a defender can win it again.’

The legendary defender won the Ballon d’Or 20 years ago today. Cannavaro, currently the Uzbekistan national team coach, remains the last defender and the last Italian player to have won the coveted award.

“Now it’s worth three times more. No defender has won it since, and I don’t see how it can happen again,” he told Gazzetta.

When the journalist argued that Pau Cubarsì might be the closest, Cannavaro replied.

“A Barcelona defender—the city that criticised my victory the most.”

Catalan newspapers strongly criticised Cannavaro’s Ballon d’Or win 20 years ago, arguing that Barcelona striker Samuel Eto’o deserved it more.

“Criticism has never annoyed me. Now I smile. It seems that it is my fault if Maldini, Baresi or Cubarsì have never won it,” Cannavaro replied.

The former defender revealed how he learned he had won the Ballon d’Or.

“I remember a France Football journalist stopped me in front of the Real Madrid training centre. He got into my car and said: ‘I must tell you something: Buffon can’t reach you; you have won, and we must do an interview.’ I thought: ‘Is this a joke?’ When I watch games of that season, I think: ‘Mamma mia! I was playing so well.’ But I knew, as I know today, that if Zidane had not given that headbutt, it would have been much more complicated.”

Cannavaro was named the best player in the world in 2006 after helping Italy win the World Cup that year.

The Final against France was one of the most epic games ever and saw Zidane sent off in extra time for headbutting Italy defender Marco Materazzi.

That Final remains Italy’s last World Cup knockout game. What should Italian football do, especially defensively, to return to greatness?

“We’ve only stopped working on individual technique, on running, and on the habit of defending with the ball frontally and laterally,” Cannavaro replied.

“Each role has its characteristics, and sometimes they prefer a goalkeeper who can make a 70-meter pass to one who actually makes saves. It’s okay, but then don’t complain if he concedes at an important moment.”

When asked whether his mentality has changed now that he has become a coach, Cannavaro replied: “For sure. When you start a coaching career, you think your ideas are the most important in the world; then you understand you have to adapt. I’ve seen coaches sacked because they wanted to change the team’s menu or train in the morning instead of the afternoon. Let’s say that I’ve become wiser and more experienced.”

During the same interview, the former Juventus, Napoli, Parma and Inter defender said Liverpool defender Virgil Van Dijk told him during the 2026 World Cup that he’d like to play in Serie A one day.
