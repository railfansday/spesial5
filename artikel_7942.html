Winter Container Designs from Ellen and Tracy

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Hi everyone! I hope you’re enjoying the cool weather and a nice cup of coffee—or seven. With winter right around the corner, I’ve been absolutely gushing over these container designs from New Jersey garden designers Ellen Goldfarb and Tracy Buttrick of What’s Blooming. The wheels are turning! Here’s another round of their winter container designs to inspire us.

These sturdy, no-nonsense planters stand guard on either side of an impressive entry in Saddle River, New Jersey. The challenge here is matching the weight of the pots and doors. We had to carefully avoid the glass lamps just above them.

We carefully placed birch logs and then filled around them with some golden curly willow (Salix matsudana ‘Tortuosa’, Zones 4–8) and berried Chinese juniper (Juniperus chinensis, Zones 4–9). The red berries are winterberry (Ilex verticillata, Zones 3–9). Greens are a bit of desert juniper (Juniperus osteosperma, Zones 3–9), noble fir (Abies procera, Zones 5–6), and incense cedar (Calocedrus decurrens, Zones 5–8). We also used frosted pine cones, some lotus pods (Nelumbo cvs., Zones 4–10), and silver brunia.

Close-up of one of the planters in the previous picture

Another pot at the same house in Saddle River: This one sits at the front door, where we had the same challenge. We were working with chunky planters, limited porch space, impressive doors, and breakable lamps above.

We opted for thick bunches of redtwig dogwood (Cornus sericea cvs., Zones 2–7) against the off-white stone walls. We added lots of desert juniper, red winterberries, natural brown pine cones, some silver bell eucalyptus, noble fir, and incense cedar.

This pot and its twin on the other side of the door and landing stand in front of the one in the previous picture, but down one step. You can see a peek of the red branches in the background.

For contrast with the red planter in back, we used cooler colors: yellow twig dogwood (Cornus sericea ‘Flaviramea’, Zones 3–7), artificial white berries, and frosted pine cones. Finally, we added clusters of silver brunia, noble fir branches, and incense cedar.

A small planter on a covered porch in Saddle River, New Jersey, is crowned with southern magnolia (Magnolia grandiflora, Zones 6–10) leaves. Pieces of incense cedar peek through here and there. We love using seeded eucalyptus, and that year we found really pretty purple branches of it. Some natural brown pine cones and clusters of silver brunia complete the planting.

The tall branches are white-painted “sweet huck” branches (huckleberry) mixed with dark redtwig dogwood. Under them are branches of seeded eucalyptus and red winterberries, some frosted pine cones, a little noble fir, and incense cedar.

Thanks for another dose of winter container inspiration, Ellen and Tracy! It’s been such a joy to see your designs—you are both incredible artists.

And we’ll be back tomorrow for another round of winter containers. Hopefully you’ve spotted something you’d like to try or re-create in your own containers this season!
