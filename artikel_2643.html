Sean McVay shapeshifts the Rams once again

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

In another in transformation form, the Los Angeles Rams morphed into a different era to grind down the Green Bay Packers 26-13 on Thanksgiving Eve. L.A. coach Sean McVay took his team back in time to the early 70's. The days of three yards and a puff of poly turf. The Rams ran the ball, played tough defense, and were nattily attired in old-school blue on white uniforms.

But McVay is not a paunchy flat topper with a chaw of tobacco, he's a modern-day shaman, buffed out with a boy band coiff. He's a shapeshifter, continuously transforming the Rams since taking the reins in 2017. It's a sorcerer's alchemy of motion, formational subterfuge, and a heavy dose of traditional football thinking.

In his eleven years, there's been constant metamorphosis, but basically L.A. has gone from a fairly traditional West Coast offense and bend, but don't break defense to duo/gap downhill run with multiple tight ends and an attacking defense that relies heavily on sub-packages. Last night, the Rams almost completely shelved their three wide receiver formations, shapeshifting into multiple tight ends and/or an extra offensive lineman on 83 percent of reps. Another 24 percent of snaps had two running back sets.

It didn't start out dominating with 12 carries for 46 yards in the first half, but the Rams stayed with the run game and were eventually able to impose their will in the second half. The blitz gave them some trouble, but in the end Green Bay only got one sack. Mostly bully ball work, only a couple of outside zone runs and a jet sweep, the big uglies were in road grade mode, between the tackles. Murray went out late and Dedich got work on his first game back and the offense didn't miss a beat. Love the Rams use of six offensive linemen, a goal line offense at any point on the field. Fans were holding their collective breath when McClendon went down, but it was only cramps. It diid allow Trost to get his feet wet, if only a toe.

How 'bout them apples? All units, return, coverage, and FG/PAT served up a solid all-around performance. Biggest takeaway? Kicker Josh Karty getting all his corkscrew kickoffs into the landing zone. Of his seven kickoffs, only once were the Packers able to start across the 30 yard line and just one yard past it at that. I hate to be a wet blanket, but it should be noted that the Pack's Special team Points Over Expected ranking is 31st while the Rams hover at 28th.

The Rams come through in a "must win" game. With the margins so thin, grabbing a wildcard seed may end up being a back door effort, knocking off the Packers and thus gaining a head-to-head advantage over a wild card competitor is the first step in the playoff journey.was important on so many levels. As of today, L.A. holds the #7 seed and NFL.com gives L.A. a 57 percent probability to make the playoffs. That number will rise, if or when the Rams can stack a few wins. Take care of business and allow the NFC North to cannibalize itself with inter-division games and NFL parity to rear its head and regress things toward the mean.

As inconsistent as the Rams played, their path to one of the wildcard playoff berths is still wide. There is indeed plenty of traffic, but 10 wins would likely be enough for a wildcard seed and 11 victories should assuredly lock one down. L.A. sits at 7-4 and to get to 11 wins must find four victories in the last seven games. It won't be easy with tilts against the Kansas City Chiefs, hated San Francisco 49ers, Dallas Cowboys, Tampa Bay Buccaneers and two with two the Seattle Seahawks. Coming out flat or inspired in even one outing could be the difference.

The big "must win" is now the race to 11 wins.
