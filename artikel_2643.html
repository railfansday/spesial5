Terrion Arnold's lawyer seeks sanctions for discovery violation

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Seahawks cornerback Terrion Arnold remains on paid leave with eight felony charges pending in Florida. And the case against him is moving forward.

On Monday, attorney Harvey Steinberg filed a motion for sanctions, arising from the alleged failure of the prosecution to timely disclose as discovery material.

The gist of the 13-page filing is that the prosecution withheld a statement from defendant Boakai Hilton until after another defendant, Arianna Del Valle, sat for a sworn, pre-trial deposition.

Hilton’s statement, per the filing, conflicts with statements made by Del Valle. By not having the Hilton statement, Steinberg was unable to properly question Del Valle about the discrepancies.

On the surface, it’s an easy fix. Steinberg should get another chance to question Del Valle under oath. At a deeper level, the situation (if it unfolded the way Steinberg alleges) speaks to the integrity of the process.

Prosecutors have immense power and discretion, which should be exercised at all times with the goal of doing justice. Sometimes, a blind desire to “win” can overcome the mandate to do justice, thereby corrupting the system.

The Hillsborough County prosecutor’s office is already dealing with an unrelated case in which “winning” may have trumped justice. A prosecutor recently resigned for failing to disclose during jury selection that the sister of a fellow prosecutor was in the jury pool for a rape trial. The woman ultimately served as the foreperson of the jury, which returned a conviction.

A new trial has been ordered, and the prosecutor who failed to disclose the relationship has resigned.

It all comes back to integrity. Are the prosecutors playing games in order get a conviction or a favorable plea deal, or are the prosecutors committed to justice in whatever form it may take?

If Steinberg’s contentions are accurate, it looks like someone may have been trying to sandbag Arnold’s defense in an effort to make it harder to draw helpful testimony out of a key witness. In a case like this, the cards should always be on the table — with no one trying to keep anything up their sleeve.
