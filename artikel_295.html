Nvidia hardware worth $11.4M vanished between Oakland and Texas

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

6 pallets of hardware vanished en route to Texas, with no arrests 2 months later.

A shipment of Nvidia hardware valued at $11.4 million vanished in transport between Oakland and Texas over the summer, and more than two months later, police have announced no arrests or recovery of the cargo.

As reported by The Mercury News, six pallets of unknown Nvidia equipment left Oakland on August 29 on a truck bound for Fort Worth, Texas. The hardware was reportedly picked up as scheduled by a truck bearing the logo of a food transportation company before vanishing off the grid. The theft wasn’t noticed until early September, when the buyers realized that the shipment had never been received. Police attempting to trace the truck’s route found it had made several multi-hour stops after picking up the hardware before losing the truck somewhere in San Bernardino County, California.

Oakland police confirmed that there was a grand theft report filed on September 13 and that the investigation remains ongoing. Authorities have not said what Nvidia hardware was stolen or exactly how the theft was carried out.

The theft in Oakland is the latest in a wave of increasingly bold hardware heists. As Ars’ Ashley Belanger reported recently, thieves have made off with more than $100 million in similar shipments since June. According to investigators cited by NBC News, the criminals have shown planning and sophistication by employing techniques like GPS location spoofing and even arranging collisions with police escort vehicles. Stolen components can be stripped down and sold in gray-market transactions or shipped overseas where access to US tech hardware may be restricted.

Nvidia’s hardware has been a specific target. In 2025, police investigated burglaries of Nvidia hardware, valued at more than $7.5 million, around Fremont and Union City, California. As reported by The Mercury News, police believe the thefts were carried out by a group that obtained vehicles for the heist in Southern California before traveling north to the Bay Area.

Professor Olaf Groth of UC Berkeley’s Haas School of Business told ABC News that “if a chip costs about $550,000, then they go for about $1 million or more abroad.” Those margins, he said, may be incentivizing criminals: “Because cargoes are getting bigger. The heists are getting more complex and that will point toward a professionalization of the teams that are hijacking these.”

Not every would-be heist crew gets it right, though. A theft in September, reported by Wired, saw two trailers marked with Nvidia and PlusAI logos stolen from outside of a warehouse facility in California. The trailers were recovered by police nearby, presumably after the criminals cut the locks to reveal they were each loaded with 20,000 pounds of sand. PlusAI is a company working on autonomous trucking solutions with Nvidia, and the sand was in the trailers to simulate freight loads.
