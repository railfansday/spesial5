Won the Lottery? Stay Quiet and Hire a Team Before You Claim

Before you claim a big lottery prize, lock up the ticket, tell almost no one, and hire a tax attorney, a CPA, and a fee-only financial planner, one paid only by you. Choose the lump sum or annuity with them, and expect to owe more federal tax than the 24% withheld.

That 24% is only the IRS withholding rate on a prize over $5,000. A jackpot is taxed almost entirely at the top 37% rate, so roughly another 13 cents of every dollar comes due at tax time.

Put the ticket in a safe deposit box, and tell no one but your spouse or partner until your team is hired. Ask the attorney before you sign the back, since the lottery pays whoever’s name is written there.

In Ohio, a trust can claim the prize, and the lottery’s announcement names the trust instead of you. Powerball tickets typically expire 90 days to a year after the drawing, depending on the state, so check your state’s deadline and whether it lets winners stay anonymous.

Then comes the payout. Mega Millions and Powerball pay the annuity in 30 installments over 29 years, each 5% bigger than the last. The lump sum is the cash it takes to fund them, such as $450.5 million for a $1.04 billion Powerball jackpot won in August 2026.

Taking the annuity saves little federal tax, since even Mega Millions’ $50 million starting jackpot pays about $752,000 the first year, past where the top rate starts for a single filer. State tax depends on where you bought the ticket and where you live, and California doesn’t tax its own lottery’s prizes. So choose the payout by how you handle money. The annuity keeps you from spending it all early, and the lump sum suits you only if you’ll invest it and leave it alone.

While you plan, park the money and wait before any big purchase. Then pay off high-interest debt, fill an emergency reserve, fund retirement accounts, and set a yearly gift budget before the requests start. Keep working until the plan is written down.

Even a prize of a few thousand dollars is taxable income, and no federal tax is withheld at $5,000 or less, so set aside your tax bracket’s percentage of it the day you cash it.
