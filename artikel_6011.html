6 Airlines, 1 App: Why Lufthansa Group Wants To Build a Superbrand

Lufthansa Group has spent years bringing its airlines closer together behind the scenes. This latest move suggests Frankfurt increasingly wants to own the customer relationship, too.

Lufthansa Group is taking another step toward becoming a consumer brand in its own right with the launch of a new app that brings together six of its airlines under a single digital identity.

Unveiled on Monday, the app combines the digital services of Lufthansa, Swiss, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, ITA Airways, and Discover Airlines, alongside elements of the group’s Miles & More loyalty program. Passengers can book flights, purchase additional services, check in, access boarding passes, and track their baggage.

While much of the functionality will be familiar to users of the existing airline apps, Lufthansa Group chief commercial officer Dieter Vranckx described it as a logical step in developing the parent company’s brand and “making it more tangible” to passengers.

His comments this week echo an earlier observation that the firm “is evolving from a group of airlines into an integrated airline group.”

Lufthansa Group launched a single app on Monday that brings together the digital services of Lufthansa, Swiss, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, ITA Airways, and Discover Airlines, along with elements of the Miles & More loyalty program. Passengers can book flights, buy extras, check in, access boarding passes, and track bags. Chief Commercial Officer Dieter Vranckx described the app as a logical step in developing the parent brand and "making it more tangible" to passengers, echoing his earlier comment that the company is evolving from a group of airlines into an integrated airline group. The app follows a sequence of centralizing moves: an internal shake-up pulling network planning, finance, technology, and human resources closer to Frankfurt; a new corporate identity in December; the rebranding of the loyalty program as Lufthansa Group Miles & More in July; a first consumer advertising campaign in October; and a January 1 merger of loyalty and marketing functions under Clemens Strauss. The timing matters because Lufthansa has just signed a €1 billion agreement for a 44.9% stake in TAP Air Portugal after promising to preserve its Portuguese identity, and because Swiss media have reported debate over the growing prominence of group branding on Swiss. Lufthansa says individual airline apps will remain available and continue to be enhanced.
