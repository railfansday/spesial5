Lampard: Decisions went against Coventry in defeat to Everton

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Frank Lampard was still left questioning the decisions made during Coventry City's 3-1 defeat to Everton as they prepare to bounce back against Crystal Palace on Saturday.

Coventry signed off for the international break with a loss at Hill Dickinson Stadium, despite taking the lead through Yann Gboho's 52nd-minute strike past Jordan Pickford.

However, Thierno Barry led Everton's fightback with two goals before teeing up Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall, though Lampard expressed his confusion about some of the decisions.

And on Wednesday, the independent Key Match Incidents panel concluded that Coventry should have been awarded a penalty when James Tarkowski fouled Caleb Yirenkyi.

There was also wide-scale debate over Thierno Barry's second goal and whether it was handball, with the Everton forward squeezing the ball in at Carl Rushworth's near post.

"It's a game we lost because of decisions, in my opinion," Lampard told reporters of those findings. "There were decisions that went wrongly against us by the referee.

"I appreciate they were honest after the event, but that doesn't help us. Going away from home is not easy, but I felt like we deserved a result. We must keep going.

"These things happen in football so we have to keep positive and keep going."

There will be a new face in the Palace dugout at the Coventry Building Society Arena this weekend, with Thomas Frank set to take charge of his first game with the Eagles.

Frank was appointed as Palace's new boss following the sacking of Pierre Sage, who left the club sitting bottom of the Premier League after 10 matches.

Only two of Palace's 13 previous permanent managers in the Premier League have won their first game in charge (D4 L7 – first spells only), with Alan Pardew beating Tottenham in January 2015 and Oliver Glasner beating Burnley in February 2024.

And when asked about his hopes for the rest of the season, Frank outlined his ambition to help Palace compete on all fronts, including the EFL Cup and Europa League.

"Of course, I've looked at the team and done a lot of analysis on the team. I think there's a lot of positives, definitely," Frank said.

"There's no doubt, to state the obvious, that we need to stabilise things. That's one thing. We need to work incredibly hard.

"Obviously, we're conceding too many goals that we need to address, so we need to work on the defensive side of things with principles, attitude, everything regarding that.

"Then, of course, it's one game at a time. We need to balance a lot of things with aiming high in Europe. There are two fantastic cup tournaments, but, of course, the Premier League is the most important, if we need to prioritise. But we would like to compete on all fronts."

He is also the only Coventry City player to score more than once in the Premier League this season (three).

Despite falling to a 3-1 defeat against Liverpool ahead of the international break, one positive for Palace was to see Nketiah get himself on the scoresheet.

Nketiah scored his first goal in the Premier League this season, netting for the first time since December 2025 against Fulham, and with Jean-Philippe Mateta still serving a suspension, Frank could give him the nod up front ahead of Jorgen Strand Larsen.

This is the first meeting between Coventry and Crystal in any competition since March 2012, a 1-1 draw in the Championship.

Coventry have lost just one of their six Premier League games against Palace (W2 D3), with this the first meeting since a 3-0 win at Selhurst Park in February 1998.

The Sky Blues have, however, lost seven or more of their opening 10 games of a league campaign for only the third time ever, also doing so in their first two Football League seasons in 1919-20 (nine) and 1920-21 (seven). It is already as many defeats as they suffered in last season's Championship.

But they face a Palace side that have conceded 24 goals in 10 Premier League games this season, their most at this stage of a season since 1980-81 under Terry Venables (also 24).

Indeed, 10 of the 15 sides to have conceded that many goals in their first 10 Premier League games have been relegated, though Wolves did it most recently in 2024-25 (27 conceded) and ended the season in 16th.

Palace won their first four visits to the Coventry Building Society Arena between 2005 and 2008 but are winless in their last four in all competitions between 2009 and 2012 (D2 L2), making Frank's debut all the more tricky.
