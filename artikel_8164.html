The Best Fitness Gifts for People Who Love to Sweat

Fitness gear makes a particularly tricky gift because the people who use it most need it to cater to their (often intensely) personal needs. The good stuff works for many varied athletes, disappears during a workout, and keeps annoying little problems from becoming big ones, or makes recovery suck a bit less.

From blister-fighting socks to a $40 bandana that earns its hefty price tag, here are 13 gifts for people who would rather be sweating.

Giving socks for the holidays feels unimaginative—until those socks eliminate the hot spot that usually appears around mile 12. Smartwool uses 53 percent merino wool blended with synthetic fibers for durability and stretch, then reinforces high-wear areas with its Indestructawool construction. The 4 Degree Elite Fit System has targeted stretch zones to keep everything locked in place, while mesh zones improve breathability, and a nearly seamless toe reduces friction, making these absolute blister-mitigating machines. These are the best socks you could give.

The highest compliment I can give a running shirt is that I completely forgot about it on the run. Adidas built the ADI365 Climacool Iconic Tee around that goal, using lightweight performance material and targeted ventilation to move heat and sweat away from your skin during hard efforts. The athletic shape keeps excess fabric under control without squeezing you like a race singlet. At $35, it’s also refreshingly reasonable in a category where shirts now often cost twice that.

I have sacrificed dozens of reusable bottles to protein shakes with smells that became structurally bonded to the plastic and ruined everything poured into them after. RTIC attacks that problem with a ceramic-lined 18/8 stainless-steel interior designed to reduce lingering tastes and odors. Double-wall vacuum insulation keeps drinks cold for up to 24 hours, while a removable shaker helps mix powders. The chug lid gets a magnetic cap that stays out of your face while drinking. The whole setup is built for athletes who want one bottle to handle everything from water to protein shakes.

Forty dollars for a bandana sounds ridiculous until you use it every single day of a two-week climbing trip. Arc’teryx cuts the Sinsolo from ultralight 20-denier recycled nylon and adds breathable mesh sections, creating a one-ounce triangle that dries quickly and takes up basically no room. This makes the Sinsolo a utility player that can be worn as a headband, cover a burning neck from the sun, or be dunked in a creek to make an on-the-go swamp cooler.

Consuming a recovery drink should not feel like the final unpleasant interval of a workout. Tailwind’s mix wins because it goes down easily while still delivering meaningful nutrition with 20 grams of complete plant protein from fava and brown rice sources, enough carbohydrates to replenish glycogen, plus sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium. Just as importantly, it actually tastes good mixed with plain water, so recovery doesn’t require dragging out a blender when you’re already cooked.

Ciele helped transform running hats from disposable race swag into something runners happily collect. The GOCap SC earns the brand obsession with lightweight, fast-drying material, strategic ventilation, reflective details, and a pliable brim you can crush into luggage without ruining it. The soft design disappears on long runs and absolutely sucks sweat off foreheads. It also looks good enough that the recipient might keep wearing it after the run instead of immediately stuffing it into the bottom of a gym bag.

A 2.5-inch short leaves very little room for mediocre fabric or poor fit. Vuori uses its smooth BlissBlend Form knit, a 74 percent polyamide and 26 percent elastane blend with four-way stretch and moisture-wicking capability. The high-rise waistband gets subtle V shaping at the front and back, while the short inseam stays completely out of the way during yoga or Pilates. This short is minimal without feeling flimsy, and it’s polished enough to continue wearing after class.

A sports bra shouldn’t require a warmup just to pull over your head. Beyond Yoga’s Glowzone aims for support without sacrificing the incredibly soft, stretchy feel the brand is known for. Medium-impact construction makes it useful for less bouncy activities like studio work and lifting, while the streamlined shape layers easily under a tank. The Electric Fuchsia color injects joy into any workout.

The Moab XC’s large shield provides plenty of coverage, and Tifosi offers lens options designed to maintain effective contrast as light changes. I particularly like the pink-tinted lens for variable trail conditions because it makes terrain pop without turning shady woods into nighttime. The broad coverage also works beautifully on a mountain bike. They’re sport sunglasses, but they don’t make you look like you’re trying to win the Tour.

The Orosi leggings are the most organic-feeling leggings we’ve tested, and they still completely hold their own in the gym. Credit the substantial 10-ounce jersey made of 27 percent hemp, 61 percent organic cotton, and 12 percent spandex. That magic amalgam of fibers creates a softer, more natural hand while preserving enough stretch for yoga and strength work. A triangular gusset improves mobility, and flatlock seams help reduce irritation.

RENPHO’s Active Mini Thermacool shrinks percussion massage into a compact package, and adds hot and cold treatment options for another layer of recovery. Dialing up the heat while hammering tired quads adds a soothing element to an otherwise aggressive massage. It’s small enough that it doesn’t monopolize luggage space, which makes me much more likely to actually bring it when traveling.

Nathan takes a refreshingly straightforward approach to an often overcomplicated category with the AeroZip Hydration Vest. Four-way-stretch perforated fabric hugs the body, a full front zipper makes getting it on simple, and adjustable side cords dial in fit without leaving webbing or bungees flapping everywhere. Two 17-ounce HydraPak flasks ride on the chest, and the vest itself weighs just 4.8 ounces.

From warm up to recovery to everything else the rest of the day brings, this is a staple that every active guy needs in his closet. The soft performance four-way stretch knit that’s moisture-wicking and maximizes comfort all the same while the tapered leg and cuffed ankle create a clean, athletic look. The two inseam options make it a great gift, and the zippered pockets keep belongings secure from the gym to afternoon errands and beyond.

These feel a little ridiculous underfoot because of how well they buoy each stride. The Cloudmonster 3 Hyper combines On’s oversized CloudTec geometry with highly responsive Helion HF PEBA-based foam and a more durable, stable layer beneath it. There’s no rigid plate, so your stride rolls smoothly off the aggressive rocker rather than feeling controlled by mechanical stiffness. At around 9.6 ounces (men’s) and 7.5 ounces (women’s), with a 6-millimeter drop, it’s a maximal shoe that feels refreshingly light and agile underfoot.
