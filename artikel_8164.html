Avoid California’s Crowded Trails With These 5 Quiet Coastline Hikes

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

There’s no shortage of spectacular hiking in California. Big Sur, Yosemite, and Redwoods have achieved international renown––and for good reason. But if you want to escape the crowds, the less congested San Luis Obispo coast offers scores of trails, uncrowded wilderness, and stellar vistas along Highway 1.

These routes invite you to hike on hills turned gold by spring flowers, spot seals in coastal reserves, and explore both long and short routes near cozy coastal towns—all without fighting for a parking spot at the trailhead. Integrate these hikes into a road trip along the iconic Highway 1, or just visit for an active getaway. San Luis Obispo’s landscapes are beautiful, the food is delicious, and, for the nights you’re not camping, there’s great lodging.

Named for the abundant yellow wildflowers that paint the hills gold every spring—namely bright yellow poppies, Deerbrush, and wild mustard—this unique park along the Morro Bay estuary is home to riparian environments, coastal scrub, and pines.

Scores of trails weave across 8,000 acres of wilderness and offer views of Morro Bay. But for epic views of the coast and a solid taste of all the park has to offer, start at the Hazard Peak Trailhead on Pecho Valley Road and hike out on Hazard Trail to the peak. From there, you can retrace your steps back to the junction with Canyon View Trail. Take that and descend a series of short switchbacks to Islay Creek Road, which will spit you out just down the street from the Hazard Peak Trailhead. You can also extend the hike by doing the full Hazard Peak loop, which is about 11 miles.

Located in the Lopez Lake Recreation Area outside Arroyo Grande, this trail climbs up and along the western ridge of the county park. Wildlife sightings of deer, birds, even black bears, aren’t uncommon. The last section of trail involves a steep climb to the peak, but the 360-degree views of the Oceano Dunes and coast to the west and surrounding Santa Lucia, Caliente, and Sierra Madre Mountains are worth the effort.

Start on Wittenberg Trail at the end of Lopez Dr. and branch off onto Duna Vista Loop Trail. Duna Vista Spur Trail will lead you to the peak. Hike directly back on the southernmost part of the loop for a slightly shorter route (8 miles) or split off onto the northern section for the full 8.8-mile trek. A day-use fee of $12 per vehicle is required.

This beautiful coastal walk isn’t a challenging one, but give yourself plenty of time to complete it: You’ll want to stop and soak in the wide ocean views, sea stacks, and wetlands. Most of the hike is along bluffs, but opportunities to descend and explore intertidal areas at low tide abound. Expect to see plenty of sea lions and bring your binoculars to try to spot an endangered snowy plover.

Start at the North Ocean parking lot on the edge of Cayucos and take Estero Bluffs Trail along the coast to the end of the park and back.

There’s more to this hike than classic California coastal scrub. In fact, as you climb over rolling hills, you’ll score views of Morro Bay and jagged Morro Rock, a volcanic plug in the estuary, and even drop into shady live oak groves. Volcanic peaks pepper the periphery, and opportunities to spot wildlife abound, including any of hundreds of bird species that follow the Pacific Flyway.

The state park’s trail system is extensive, but this route is a nice loop that touches a lot of the trails on the east side. Start at the parking lot on S Bay Blvd. and take Quarry Trail to Canet Trail, followed by Chumash, Crespi, Park Ridge, and Live Oak Trails. That leads you right back to the parking lot you started in. You can customize the length by combining various side trails, including to the top of Portola Trail, a small summit, which adds about a mile. A state park pass or $8 day-use fee is required.

This rugged trail leads into the Santa Lucia Wilderness within Los Padres National Forest, finally reaching an 80-foot waterfall that streams into an azure pool. Much of the trail slowly climbs through the woods alongside a creek and lush riparian zone in Big Falls Canyon. However, if you continue onward past the trio of falls, your efforts will be rewarded with sunny views of the surrounding Coast Ranges.

Start at Big Falls Trailhead on Upper Lopez Canyon Road and hike to the junction with Hi Mountain Trail. Return the same way. The last section of Lopez Canyon Road to the trailhead can be rough, so 4WD is recommended.

Eat: Gardens of Avila, located within Sycamore Mineral Springs Resort and Spa in San Luis Obispo, offers elegant dining, homegrown ingredients, and garden paths for after-dinner strolls.

Sip: Wineries abound. Oenophiles who appreciate organic and regenerative farming should visit Chamisal Vineyards in Edna Valley.

Explore Town: Los Osos offers welcoming coffee shops, local art galleries, and fine boutiques. Avila Beach is worth exploring for sunny waterfront vibes. Cayucos is home to classic seaside tourist scenes complete with souvenir shops and antique stores.

Campgrounds are in no short supply along Highway 1, and the San Luis Obispo area is no exception. Most of the state parks, like Montaña de Oro, Morro Bay, and Estero Bluffs, have their own developed campgrounds. Morro Beach also has a campground along the coast. Those can be booked online or in person, but they fill up quickly, especially on weekends. During the winter, these campgrounds are closed, so you’ll need to seek out dispersed camping or other lodging options.

Dispersed camping is strictly prohibited along Highway 1. However, some nearby mountain areas allow it. Check out TV Tower Road, Carrizo Plain National Monument, or Williams Hill Recreation Area.

Want to break up your nights outside? You have options. San Luis Obispo and nearby towns have plenty of hotels, BnB’s, and more luxurious accommodations. For a splurge, check out La Lomita Ranch, a chic and colorful former stable with painted tiles, terracotta, and horses in San Luis Obispo.
