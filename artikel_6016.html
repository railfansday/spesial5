Rep. Ken Calvert wins all-Republican race in California

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Rep. Ken Calvert defeated Rep. Young Kim in a redrawn Southern California district that pitted the two Republicans against one another.

Calvert was leading his rival by 7 points Monday night when The Associated Press called the race for California’s 40th District with more than 70 percent of the vote in.

Calvert, who chairs the powerful House Defense Appropriations Subcommittee, is the longest-serving Republican in the California delegation, first winning election to Congress in 1992.

The race was reshaped by the Golden State’s new voter-approved House map, drawn by state Democrats in response to mid-decade redistricting by Texas Republicans. The new map dismantled Calvert’s 41st District in Riverside County, transforming it into a safely blue Democratic seat in the Los Angeles area. That prompted him to run for the redrawn 40th District, which included a sizable percentage of his current constituents.

The rare intraparty general election was possible because Calvert and Kim finished in the top two slots in the state’s June primary. Under the California system, all candidates run on the same primary ballot, with the top two vote-getters, regardless of party, advancing to November.

Calvert had already declared victory last week, saying on social media that there was “no greater honor than serving my neighbors and our communities in Congress.”

Kim’s defeat is the second of her congressional career — she lost her initial bid during the 2018 blue wave before winning election to the House two years later.

In her concession message, Kim singled out California Democratic Gov. Gavin Newsom, who led the redistricting effort last year that targeted five Republican-held districts. Democrats flipped all five seats last week.

“It’s unfortunate that Gavin Newsom’s political redistricting scheme forced two Republican members from Congress into this race,’’ she said in a statement.

The race between was marked by rancor, with Calvert alleging that Kim was not a true conservative and supported higher taxes. Kim accused Calvert of growing rich off stock trades over his decades in Washington.

Calvert will serve in a delegation with a dramatically smaller GOP presence next year, with Democrats likely to hold 48 of California’s 52 House seats. Just two other California Republicans – Reps. Vince Fong and Tom McClintock – have been reelected so far, while a third, Rep. Jay Obernolte, narrowly leads his Democratic challenger in an as-yet-uncalled race.

The Calvert-Kim battle was not the only all-party November clash in California, with several districts featuring matchups between two Democrats.

One incumbent has already been unseated, with Rep. Jimmy Gomez losing to fellow Democrat Angela Gonzales-Torres in the Los Angeles-area 34th District, according to The Associated Press.

Two other blue-on-blue contests in Northern California remained uncalled as of Tuesday afternoon: Rep. Mike Thompson was trailing venture capitalist Eric Jones in the 4th District, while Rep. Doris Matsui led Sacramento City Councilmember Mai Vang in the 7th District.
