Bitter Fetterman-El-Sayed feud threatens to complicate Schumer's Senate unity push

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Michigan Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed was questioned by "The View" co-host Sara Haines on why he was so slow to apologize for a past comment about an attack on a Jewish synagogue.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called for Democratic Party unity Wednesday following the midterm elections, but a bitter feud between Sen. John Fetterman and Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed could complicate efforts to unite the caucus in the 120th Congress.

The two Democrats traded personal insults throughout the 2026 election cycle, clashing over U.S. military assistance to Israel, Medicare for All and the ideological direction of the Democratic Party.

"You put one ogre on a pike, and then everyone else gets the message," El-Sayed, D-Mich., reportedly told progressive donors during a private Zoom call, arguing that challenging moderate Democrats in primary elections would send a message to the party's establishment.

Fetterman, D-Pa., has been an outspoken supporter of U.S. military assistance to Israel, including funding for its Iron Dome missile defense system, and he has backed military action against Hezbollah and Iran. El-Sayed, meanwhile, campaigned against the war in Iran and opposed taxpayer-funded military operations and U.S. aid to Israel.

A longtime Democratic Senate staffer told Fox News Digital that they would "not be surprised" if tensions emerged between the two lawmakers but expressed confidence that their loyalty to the Democratic Party would outweigh their personal disagreements.

The staffer added that Fetterman has faced criticism from other members of the Senate Democratic caucus, though those disagreements have not necessarily prevented lawmakers from working together.

During a televised primary debate, El-Sayed confirmed that his "ogre on a pike" remark was directed at Fetterman, accusing the Pennsylvania senator of having "abandoned his elected values" through his support for U.S. military assistance to Israel.

El-Sayed later doubled down on his criticism, arguing against funding foreign defense systems such as Israel's Iron Dome while American schools struggle with limited resources.

Fetterman fired back, referring to El-Sayed as the "Pontiac Platner" in an apparent jab at the Maine Democrat Graham Platner, who dropped out of his Senate race this election season under the weight of multiple scandals.

FETTERMAN 'DARES' EL-SAYED TO STICK WITH PIKER, CLAIMS MICHIGAN NOMINEE HAS 'LITTLE MAN SYNDROME ENERGY'

The Pennsylvania senator also accused El-Sayed of seeking to become the "head of our emerging pro-Hamas wing here in the party," criticizing his positions on Israel.

El-Sayed's campaign appearances with left-wing streamer Hasan Piker, who previously said "America deserved 9/11," further fueled the dispute, prompting Fetterman to publicly condemn Piker's remarks.

The tensions escalated after El-Sayed narrowly defeated Rep. Haley Stevens, D-Mich., in the Democratic primary by less than one percentage point.

Days before the general election, reports emerged that El-Sayed's campaign had barred Stevens from attending its final campaign events, drawing another sharp rebuke from Fetterman.

"Rep. Haley Stevens simply held Abdul El-Sayed accountable for his views on Israel and fixation with Jewish money. El-Sayed is going after a woman and banning her from campaign events for having an opinion," Fetterman told Jewish Insider.

"He has the worst case of little man syndrome I've ever seen," Fetterman added.

Despite the public hostility, the Democratic Senate staffer's assessment suggests the two lawmakers could ultimately set aside their differences to advance their party's legislative priorities.

The feud could nevertheless present an early test for Schumer, D-N.Y., as he seeks to maintain unity among Senate Democrats, particularly on divisive issues such as U.S. foreign policy and the party's progressive agenda.

El-Sayed, Fetterman and Schumer did not respond to Fox News Digital's requests for comment.
