GTA 6 Is A Tragedy Because You Cannot Sit Down And Eat Spaghetti

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Everyone, including me, has been making much (rightfully) of how incredible the level of detail in GTA VI is. This game is ridiculous, man! The sheer amount of incidental and specific dialogue, the clothing, the way characters move, the weather, the TV and radio programs, I could go on all day. But there’s a single aspect I’ve found where the level of detail genuinely felt short of what I’d hoped for going in: the food.

You cannot sit down and enjoy a slow, relaxing meal. There is nowhere to order a plate of spaghetti or other food to be eaten explicitly there, in the establishment. There is no cooking. There is only fast food and takeout. And that’s how I know this game is going to be a tragedy.

I love video game food. I love cooking through video game cookbooks, I love beautifully designed and rendered meals within game worlds, and I love creative food crafting systems. So I’m always on the lookout for such things! And in one respect, I actually think GTA VIÂ does food beautifully. It isÂ freaking incredible that you can hold a food and a drink (or two foods, or two drinks) in each hand simultaneously, and swap between consuming each of them at will. Last night, I took Lucia out to a food truck and bought an empanada and a mojito. I had her stand there and take bites of the empanada, and watched with wonder as the empanada disappeared, bite by bite in her hand. (I tried to take a sip of the mojito but accidentally pulled my gun instead and someone called the cops, but I’m sure it would have been equally impressive).

Much has been made of this system, and deservedly. Jason takes bites of apples, and the apples having those bites visible in them until only the core is left! The pair will drink liquids, and you can see the liquids in the containers slowly recede! All of these items have unique models, too: burgers, tacos, croissants, the grilled fish sandwiches made in an early mission that have different levels of char as Jason cooks them…God, it’s incredible! Hats off!

I don’t want anything about that system to change. I love being able to eat handheld foods like this. But what bothers me is that this is…all that Jason and Lucia ever eat.

I complained about this earlier, after a glimpse in the pair’s fridge in an early trailer. Mostly alcohol and protein shakes. Nothing substantial whatsoever to keep two active crime-doers properly fed. But at the time, I thought that was just…their state at that moment in the game. I assumed eventually the pair would end up in a situation where they ate food like normal human beings.

Yet this never happens. I’m in chapter 3, and I’ve consulted with our reviewer who has finished the game, and my understanding is that the pair will never evolve beyond eating on the go. You can’t order a plate of spaghetti or some lo mein and sit comfortably and lazily consume it. The pair never cooks food at home, either as a mechanic or even as a visual suggestion, as their cabinets remain full of nothing but plain bread and cereal and unused pots and pans right up until the end of the game. Every restaurant that looks like it could be a proper sit-down dining establishment is closed and locked to you, and everything that’s open serves like, five things: water, soda, beer, and roughly two handheld food items. And those same 10-20 food items are repeated ad nauesum across all the game’s restaurants, regardless of what’s on the posted menu. In a small handful of establishments, you can sit down to eat those items, but it still doesn’t look like the two are relaxing over a meal. Again, I understood this early in the gameâthe two were broke, Jason was living a bachelor life, and they were on the run. Hell, the motel room in Chapter 2 doesn’t even have its own coffee pot. I get it.

But as the two move to Vice City, build wealth, and start living at a higher standard, I really just thought there’d be some option, somewhere, for one of them to take the other out on a nice dinner date. Maybe just a little cutscene showing a decent plate of food with the two all dolled up. Or perhaps, if that was too risky, the barest suggestion that either of them had ever cooked at home: TV trays, or a dirty pot in the sink, or more than like four spices sitting in the window for some reason. Even like, aÂ discussion about having eaten a meal?

So on one level, I’m disappointed. I love seeing gorgeous food renders in games, and I can only imagine Rockstar’s would be stellar. I think a lot about how the Dragon’s Dogma team bought expensive steak just so they could film themselves cooking it for the game. Or the absurd feasting scenes in Monster Hunter with gorgeous cheese naan. Or even the very simple scenes in the Yakuza games, where you couldn’t see the food, but you could hear the characters discussing how it tasted and what their thoughts were. Impressive as the handheld food is, I’m not really excited at making Jason eat another croissant after I’ve seen the animation a few times. He doesn’t talk about it, it doesn’t seem to do anything for him, he doesn’t even really seem to derive any pleasure from it. Just a health boost maybe.

But even as I’m disappointed by all the storefronts I can’t enter and the menus I read but can never get Jason or Lucia to order from and eat, I’m starting to get what GTA VI is trying to say here. It’s not a budget or a time issue, I know that. If Rockstar devs had wanted to make gorgeous food or record discussions about eating it, they would have. I really do think GTA VI wants to convey in every possible way that this pair will never, ever be safe. They will not be able to slow down or stop or rest for the kind of luxurious meal I want them to have. And that’s luxury not even just in terms of spending lots of money on nice food, but the luxury of time and safety: spending the time to sit, in public, and eat it regardless of where that is. The kind of luxury I feel when my husband and I go to our favorite local Indian joint and, instead of ordering takeout, sit and eat out of the metal trays and sip chai and share naan. If Jason and Lucia sit to eat at all, it will always be in chairs that are easily pushed out, in empty greasy spoons where they can make a quick getaway.

I suspect that wherever the story is going, it is not going to a place where they will be able to comfortably enjoy a slow glass of wine and a fine risotto. It’s not even that it’s an option I can see on the horizon, out of reach due to price or other limitations. It is just, permanently and forever, not for them. Maybe I’ll be proven wrong, and there will be a perfect, peaceful, happy ending where the pair get to live a normal life with their son in a safe place with minimal crime, and where they can go out for a big plate of spaghetti that all three can sit down to enjoy over a wonderful hour of laughter and merriment. Maybe there’s a world where someone could reasonably imagine the three enjoying a multi-hour, homecooked Thanksgiving together. But given all the crime I’ve been doing, somehow I feel that a happy and normal future will, like the many, many restaurants of Leonida, be forever closed to them.
