Bloodthirsty Brighton feast on rattled Arsenal

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Overpowered and overrun, Arsenal were subjected to the sort of gruelling treatment they usually inflict on their opponents. Mikel Arteta’s side did not look anything like Premier League champions here, as Brighton and Hove Albion produced a stunning performance full of swarming energy and breathless bravery.

In the biggest test of their season so far, Arsenal fell woefully short of the standards they have set themselves over the past few campaigns. This sort of thing simply does not happen to Arteta’s side. Well, it did not happen. Not before this monstering on the south coast, where the Brighton fans were whipped into a frenzy by the brilliance of Fabian Hürzeler’s players.

Brighton were everything that Arsenal are expected to be: aggressive, inventive and dominant across the field. They even scored with a set-piece, as one of Arsenal’s great strengths became a sudden weakness. Everywhere Arteta looked, the foundational truths of his team were being shaken by a Brighton storm. It was their first defeat by a three-goal margin since May 2023, also against Brighton.

Remarkably, this is now the third consecutive season in which Brighton have beaten the reigning Premier League champions. They are rarely happier than when they are on the hunt against more illustrious opponents, working as a team to snap at heels and then attacking in numbers. They are known as the Seagulls, but they played more like a pack of bloodthirsty wolves. The ferocity of their off-the-ball work, especially in the first half, was extraordinary.

Not for a long time have Arsenal been as rattled as they were here. David Raya misplaced passes, Gabriel Magalhães lost duels and Bruno Guimarães chased the ball in vain. It was Brighton’s Pascal Gross, rather than Arsenal’s Martin Odegaard, who shaped the game in midfield. It was Brighton’s Charalampos Kostoulas, rather than Arsenal’s Kai Havertz, who offered incisive class in attack.

Gross and Kostoulas both struck from range in a one-sided first half, before Chema Andrés added the third after the break. At half-time, Arteta had sprinted down the tunnel towards the dressing room. Whatever messages he gave to his players, they either did not sink in or did not work. By the end, Arsenal were lucky the scoreline was not even more humiliating.

Arteta’s players had come through some tricky assignments in winning their first seven matches of this season, but this was their toughest test yet and they failed it badly. They did not handle the emotions of the occasion particularly well, either: in stoppage time, a frustrated Declan Rice kicked out at Malick Yalcouyé. He was perhaps fortunate to avoid a red card.

It was the first time that Rice and Guimarães had started alongside each other for Arsenal and it quickly became clear that their £180m partnership will need some work. Arsenal would have hoped their experience in the Premier League would allow for an instant connection but, evidently, that is not the case. Guimarães was hooked after 60 minutes, but might easily have been removed at the break.

Both managers had a similar assessment of the match and how it was won: Brighton did the basics far better than Arsenal. “How we wanted to approach this game was getting into it through small wins,” Hürzeler said. “Everyone speaks about a goal or chance as a win, but a small win can be the first throw-in, the first corner, win the first header, win the first tackle. These small things. If you win these small steps, and celebrate them as a win, then you can gain bigger and bigger wins.”

It was typical of Brighton that one of their new signings produced an excellent performance in midfield. Andrés, a 21-year-old Spaniard signed from Stuttgart at the end of the transfer window, looks to be the latest gem identified by their data-led recruitment model. No one inside the Amex Stadium was thinking about Carlos Baleba, sold to Manchester United, when Andrés made his tackles and won his headers.

Another new signing, centre-back Luka Vuskovic, was similarly impressive at the back. Signed for £46m from Tottenham Hotspur, Vuskovic looks more than capable of going to the top of the game.

This was perhaps the first occasion this season in which Arsenal missed William Saliba, the injured centre-back who has been so fundamental to their success in recent years. The Frenchman can always be relied on to provide calmness and composure at the back, even under intense pressure. Without him, Arsenal were uncharacteristically jittery.

“The amount of technical errors we did in our own half...” Arteta bemoaned. “We didn’t respect any principle of play that you have to get out of those man-to-man situations. That put us always on the back foot.”

Gross, who at 35 is playing as well as ever, opened the scoring with a low shot from range after 31 minutes. Kostoulas then struck the second, from a similar distance, after some end-to-end action resulted in Arsenal’s midfield and defence melting away. The third was the most simple, as Andrés headed in at the back post from an inswinging corner.

Arsenal did have some chances, with Havertz missing the best of them, but there was never a sense that Brighton were vulnerable. The pressure on the home side was never sustained. For Arteta, who now has an extended international break in which to ruminate on this defeat, it all made for an unfamiliar and unexpected pain.

Mikel Arteta said his Arsenal players failed to “respect the game” as their perfect start to the season was ended in stunning style by a brilliant Brighton performance.

Arsenal’s capitulation on the south coast led to their first defeat of the campaign and Arteta admitted that Brighton had a different “edge” and “bite” throughout the match. The Arsenal manager also said the 3-0 loss represented a “big lesson” for his players.

“There are certain basics you need to accomplish in football, especially in the Premier League, that we were very far from,” Arteta said. “From the first throw-in you could sense it, it was so clear.

“It was a really disappointing result and performance. Brighton were another gear up from us. We were extremely poor technically and were not respecting the game.

“It is not going to take away what the team has done in the last six or seven weeks since coming back from the World Cup. What we have done and the way we have competed, and the amount of games we have won, has been remarkable. But this is a big lesson for all of us to understand what it takes to win at this level.”

Arteta added that his team did not “respect the principles of play” that were required against Brighton’s intense pressing, and also said his players were guilty of numerous technical errors.

“Credit to Brighton for what they have done because they have overcome us and they had another edge,” Arteta said. “There are a lot of moments when the ball is 50/50 and it changes hands and most of the time it was for them.”

Brighton are the Premier League’s top scorers this season with 16 goals in five matches so far, and this emphatic victory took them up to third place in the table.

“It was a special win,” Fabian Hürzeler, the Brighton head coach, said. “Not only because we played the best team in England but because of the performance. I am really proud of my team. It’s always important to enjoy these moments. We were horrible to play against. My players really trust each other on the pitch.”
