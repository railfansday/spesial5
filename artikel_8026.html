Beyond the Kennel: Giving Long-Stay Shelter Dogs a Chance to Be Seen

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Their body starts to relax. Their nose hits the ground. They start sniffing everything, noticing people, wanting to explore. Sometimes they start playing. Sometimes they give me the biggest zoomies I've ever seen. Suddenly, I'm looking at a completely different dog.

I've watched it happen more times than I can count â the exact moment a long-stay shelter dog stops being "a shelter dog" and starts just being a dog. It usually happens somewhere in the middle of an adventure.

That moment is what inspired me to start Ariana's Doggos. Because when a dog has spent months living in a kennel, the version of them you see through the bars isn't necessarily their true self.

Dogs have always grounded me. They bring joy, companionship, and a sense of home, often asking for nothing in return. I started Arianaâs Doggos because I believe every shelter dog deserves to be seen for their potential, not just as an animal waiting behind bars. I know that every dog sitting in a kennel could be a companion, adventure buddy, couch potato, comedian, walking partner, and family member. They just haven't met their person yet.

I started Ariana's Doggos because I wanted to give long-stay shelter dogs the opportunity to simply be dogs outside the shelter environment, while capturing those experiences for potential adopters see them too. I don't just want to show people a dog's face and tell them that they're sweet. I want to show them what that dog could actually look like in their life.

What does this dog look like walking through a park? Do they love sniffing trails? Do they want to chase a toy around a pet store? Are they goofy? Are they cuddly? Do they want to say hello to every person they meet?

Those are the moments that can help someone imagine, "I could see this dog living with me."

Before Ariana's Doggos, I worked in fundraising, media, and storytelling. I spent three years working for a fundraising app, seeing firsthand how vital community support can be. I've always been drawn to production and storytelling, and previously worked as a radio producer. Eventually, I realized that audio alone wasn't filling my creative cup. I wanted to create something deeply visual and emotionally impactful.

At the same time, I became increasingly interested in rescue dogs and saw firsthand how the stress of a long shelter stay can affect them. I knew some rescues already gave dogs opportunities to leave the facility for walks or special outings, but I kept thinking about what could happen if those experiences became continuous storiesâthe difference between someone scrolling past a static photo and stopping to think, "Maybe that's my dog."

Ariana's Doggos started as a personal media passion project about two years ago, based in Calgary, Alberta. This year, I've begun turning it into a more structured community initiative involving shelters, local businesses, sponsors, and people who simply want to see these dogs get a little more attention.

Something important to understand about shelter dogs is that a kennel is an extremely specific, highly stressful environment. A dog may be overwhelmed, overstimulated, shut down, or frustrated by everything happening around them. Rightfully so â they don't understand why they're there.

Shelter staff and volunteers who care for these animals daily see glimpses of their true nature, but an adopter on the other side of the kennel door rarely sees beyond the first impression.

A dog sitting quietly in a corner or barking like mad can be easy to overlook. That same dog walking beside you through a park, stopping to sniff a tree, looking up at you with a goofy expression, or happily carrying around a new toy tells an entirely different story.

And sometimes that story is what gets them noticed.

As rewarding as the outings are, there is also a side of this work that breaks my heart.

The first time you take a dog out, they can be absolutely ecstatic. Everything is new. They're sniffing, exploring, and soaking everything in.

But when a dog has been waiting for a home for a long time, that excitement can start to change. They seem to understand the routine now. We're going somewhere, we're going to have fun, and eventually, we're going back to the kennel.

That's also why I believe so strongly in the importance of foster homes and other individualized resources for long-stay dogs. Foster homes are an amazing temporary solution for many dogs, but foster networks are always limited, and more dogs will always be waiting for the right foster environment or a spot to open up.

Not every dog has an immediate foster match; some need a very specific environment, routine, or space. When those resources aren't available, dogs can remain in shelters for months. And the longer they wait, the easier it can be for people to overlook them. That's exactly why I keep showing up with a camera and a leash â every video is one more chance for the right person to finally notice them.

Right now, my focus is simple: one dog at a time. Taking shelter dogs out, creating meaningful experiences and exposure for them, and turning those adventures into story-driven videos that reach people far beyond the shelter walls.

With Ariana's Doggos, the outing doesn't end when the dog goes back. The digital story keeps working long after the day is over, putting dogs in front of potential adopters.

I also have a bigger, long-term dream. Eventually, I would love to build a dedicated, foster-scale facility that could work alongside existing shelters and foster networks.

It wouldn't replace shelters or traditional foster homes. Instead, I envision it as a centralized resource for shelter dogs who need extra exposure to find a home, basic training to help them fit into the right home, or a more individualized setting for dogs struggling with the shelter environment.

Most importantly, I want that environment to combine professional care with maximum public visibility through media and storytelling, preparing them fully for their future families.

I don't know exactly what Ariana's Doggos will look like years from now. Right now, I'm still building the foundation through these individual adventures and learning what works.

Every time I take a dog out, I'm reminded that these animals are so much more than their shelter profile.

They're goofy,  complicated, affectionate. They're curious and occasionally chaotic. They're individuals. They can become such an important part of someone's life, and they deserve to be seen that way.

I may not be able to find a home for every shelter dog, but I can give them a day where they aren't a shelter dog. They're just a dog.

Sniffing a trail, choosing a toy, meeting a stranger, or getting the zoomies. A dog discovering something new.

And I can take those moments and put them in front of potential adopters who may never have looked twice at them in a kennel.

Because sometimes finding a dog a home starts with being seen.

You don't have to adopt to make a difference. Just by sharing these videos, you help potential adopters find their new best friend. Whether you can donate to support these adventures or simply follow along on Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube, every bit helps. Local businesses and organizations can also get involved by sponsoring an adventure, hosting a shelter dog for an outing, or exploring other collaborations. Iâd love to hear from you.

The ultimate goal is to build a vibrant community around these dogs â to get more people seeing them, talking about them, commenting, and giving them the chance to be seen for who they really are.

Every shelter dog has a story. They just need someone to tell it.
