Time to Break Down That Major Twist at the End of ‘Campus Drivers’ and How It Differs From the Books

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Prime Video series takes a major turn from C.S. Quill’s series.

If—after watching the sixth episode of Campus Drivers—you wondered whether there were still more episodes to come, I’ve got bad news: That really was the finale. Season 1 of the French series from Prime Video ends with a twist that leaves plenty of questions, especially about Lane and Lois’s future. It’s a choice that even surprised those familiar with the book series by C.S. Quill, because the TV ending takes a different direction from the novel’s.

But what exactly happens in the final episode? And, above all, why does Lane decide to leave for Los Angeles right when his relationship with Lois finally seemed destined to take off? Let’s get into it.

In the season 1 finale, we discover that Lane leaves with his mother to move to L.A. and attend film school—a decision that, at first glance, seems to come out of nowhere, especially considering the bond that has formed with Lois over the course of the episodes. Behind this choice, however, there is a secret motivation: Lane’s mother does not approve of the path her son wants to take and believes his career choice is not the right one, but she accepts it on the condition that he leave Bellerose and end his relationship with Lois; otherwise, she is prepared to interfere with Lois’s scholarship, since she considers her an unsuitable partner for Lane.

At that point, Lane is faced with an impossible decision: stay beside the person he is falling in love with, or sacrifice his own happiness to allow her to continue studying medicine at a prestigious university.

Lane chooses the second option. He agrees to leave with his mother, thereby giving up the healthiest relationship of his life just when the two of them were finally beginning to truly grow closer.

The season’s final scene makes the separation even more concrete. In fact, we see movers carrying the furniture out of Lane’s apartment: an image that confirms his departure and the fact that the move to the United States is not just a threat or a possibility, but a decision that has now been made.

What’s more, Lois does not know the real reason for Lane’s departure. She does not know that he agreed to leave only to protect her future and allow her to continue her studies. In her eyes, that separation therefore risks seeming like a personal choice, a renunciation of their story just when it could have begun. At this point, the series departs considerably from the novel: the TV ending changes the protagonists’ journey and chooses to interrupt their story at a particularly delicate point, leaving the most important issue unresolved: the two protagonists did not get their romantic happy ending.

The ending seems designed precisely to leave the door open to a possible sequel (with the two of them still as major protagonists) and to heighten the drama—although for now, an official renewal has yet to be announced. Here’s hoping we will see Lane and Lois again in a second season!
