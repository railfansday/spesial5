Chipotle Mexican Grill vs. Hershey: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Hershey trades at a lower forward P/E than Chipotle, but the gap raises a bigger question about which stock is the better buy.

By Jack Delaney – Updated Nov 20, 2026 at 8:45PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Chipotle Mexican Grill ›

Choosing between Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG -2.07%) and Hershey (HSY +0.82%) depends on whether you value aggressive expansion or reliable brand power.

Chipotle leads the fast-casual space with its focus on fresh ingredients and digital efficiency. Hershey dominates the confectionery market with a massive portfolio of snacks and sweets. This comparison helps you decide which of these consumer giants better aligns with your financial goals heading into 2027.

Chipotle Mexican Grill focuses on its mission of serving real ingredients across more than 4,000 restaurants in the United States, Canada, Europe, and the Middle East. The company relies on a robust digital ordering platform that accounts for over 16% of food and beverage revenue, utilizing third-party delivery aggregators to reach more customers. It also expands its global presence through key partnerships, such as the one with Alshaya Group for international restaurant operations.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $12.7 billion, representing growth of approximately 7.9% compared to the prior period. The company reported net income of close to $1.4 billion, which reflects a net margin of approximately 10.8% for the period.

Hershey operates as a dominant force among consumer discretionary stocks through its massive portfolio of chocolate, sweets, and mints. The company sells its products through wholesale distributors and mass merchandisers, with McLane Company accounting for approximately 27% of consolidated net sales in 2025. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business since the loss of such a large partner could significantly impact overall sales.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached close to $12.3 billion, an increase of roughly 6.7% over the previous 12 months. The company achieved net income of approximately $1.8 billion, which contributed to a net margin of nearly 14.6% during this timeframe.

Chipotle Mexican Grill faces significant risks related to food safety and potential food-borne illness incidents, which can severely damage its brand reputation and increase operating costs. The company also deals with intense competition from traditional restaurants and newer entities like ghost kitchens or delivery services such as Starbucks (SBUX -1.28%). Furthermore, the business must navigate legal and regulatory challenges regarding labor practices.

Hershey is sensitive to the availability and pricing volatility of key raw materials such as cocoa, sugar, and dairy. Supply chain disruptions caused by climate change or natural disasters could negatively impact its manufacturing operations across its global facilities. The company also faces pressure to successfully implement digital transformation and strategic business realignment programs to stay competitive against other snack manufacturers in a rapidly evolving retail environment.

Hershey appears cheaper than Chipotle Mexican Grill based on traditional metrics.

Chipotle is a story about growth and Hershey is about rebounding profitability, and knowing that can help find what the best fit for a portfolio is. For Chipotle, revenue increased 9.6% in the third quarter of 2026 to $3.3 billion. Transactions increased by 0.8%, comparable sales rose 2.1%, and digital sales jumped 38.8%. On top of that, Chipotle opened 84 new locations and expects to open up to 370 new restaurants by the end of 2026. That said, its operating margin dropped from 15.9% to 13.7%, and earnings per share dipped 6.9% despite the noticeable increase in revenue growth. Overall, while revenue growth and location expansion look impressive, shareholders will want to make sure higher costs aren't cutting too deeply into profitability.

For Hershey in its third quarter, while revenue increased only 3%, adjusted earnings per share (EPS) rose 103% and net income climbed 112%. Gross margin also increased from 32.6% to 46.1%. That said, organic sales increased only 0.8%, while international sales fell 7.5%. What Hershey is seeing appears to be improved results driven by better margins and lower costs, rather than pure consumer demand.

Between the two, if you prioritize more long-term sales growth potential, Chipotle may be the better fit for a portfolio. For anyone looking for a more mature business model with improving profitability, Hershey may be the better option.
