If the Stock Market Crashes, Warren Buffett Says This is the Best Move Investors Can Make

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Market downturns have historically been ideal times for patient investors to buy stocks.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.40%) has advanced 10% year to date amid remarkably strong earnings growth driven by the artificial intelligence infrastructure build-out. Yet, investors have become increasingly pessimistic throughout the year, raising a familiar question: What should you do if the market takes a turn for the worse?

Warren Buffett has addressed this question repeatedly. His advice starts with accepting that steep declines are part of stock ownership. No one can reliably predict when the next crash will happen, but investors can decide in advance how they will respond.

Warren Buffett. Image source: Getty Images.

Weekly surveys conducted by the American Association of Individual Investors (AAII) show that bearish sentiment -- the percentage of investors who expect the stock market to decline over the next six months -- increased from 27% in January to 51% in November.

Possible explanations include doubts about the durability of the artificial intelligence boom and whether the profits it generates will justify massive infrastructure spending. Midterms may have added to those concerns, as several incoming governors and members of Congress support data center restrictions, including moratoriums on construction projects.

Meanwhile, high energy prices tied to the Iran conflict and tariffs imposed by President Trump have contributed to inflation. The Federal Reserve raised interest rates in September, and the market expects another hike in December, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool. Higher rates make bonds more attractive, which can pull money away from stocks.

Any of those pressures could deepen the recent sell-off. But even if the stock market recovers in the near term, the next severe downturn is inevitable. So, it makes sense to have a plan in place before it arrives.

Stock market downturns can make selling seem like the safest move. But Warren Buffett has repeatedly explained that falling prices are actually an opportunity for patient investors to purchase stock in their favorite companies at a discount.

"Widespread fear is your friend as an investor, because it serves up bargain purchases," he wrote in his 2016 shareholder letter. That sentiment is particularly relevant today given that investors have become increasingly bearish throughout the year. That hard part is deciding where to invest your money.

Buffett answered that question in his 1996 letter. "Your goal as an investor should simply be to purchase, at a rational price, a part interest in an easily-understandable business whose earnings are virtually certain to be materially higher five, ten, and twenty years from now."

That quote is jam-packed with good advice. First, Buffett makes it clear that price matters. Second, investors must understand a company before buying shares. Third, investors should only consider stocks whose earnings are likely to be much higher in the future.

The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.91%) recently entered correction territory, and similar downturns in the past reveal an encouraging pattern. During the last decade, the Nasdaq returned an average of 21% over the 12-month period following its first close in correction territory.

The S&P 500 is not currently in correction territory, but it has displayed a similar resilience during past downturns. Specifically, during the last decade, the S&P 500 has returned an average of 18% over the 12-month period following its first close in correction territory.

Here's the bottom line: Market downturns are part of the investing experience. But history says stocks will eventually recover and Buffett's advice provides a clear plan: Focus on understandable businesses with strong long-term growth prospects, and buy when shares trade at sensible prices.

If the stock market crashes tomorrow, investors should put that advice into action. And they should plan to hold any stocks they buy for at least five years. Buffett once said, "All there is to investing is picking good stocks at good times and staying with them as long as they remain good companies.
