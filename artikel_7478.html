YouTube’s biggest creators join forces to support the Susan Wojcicki Foundation’s critical mission: changing the conversation about lung cancer

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

In August 2024, YouTube‘s former longtime CEO, Susan Wojcicki, passed away at age 56. She battled non-small-cell lung cancer for two years and left behind one heck of a legacy.

Now Susan’s husband Dennis Troper and her sisters, Dr. Janet Wojcicki and Anne Wojcicki, have teamed up with professionals in clinical research, AI, diagnostics, and early detection to launch the Susan Wojcicki Foundation, which wants to “change the conversation” around lung cancer and help more people access screening, early detection, and prevention.

And, fittingly, the SWF is marking Lung Cancer Awareness Month by telling people about its mission on YouTube–with the help of creators.

MrBeast, Mark Rober, Hank Green, Lilly Singh, Cassey Ho, Marina Mogilko, Cleo Abram, Mark Vins, Jake Roper, sWooZie, Matthew & Stephanie Patrick, Dr. Mike, Stephanie Chuang, and Rhett & Link all joined the SWF’s first YouTube video, offering their advice about how it can most effectively connect with viewers and share its message.

“The channel and its contents will address the fact that anyone with lungs can get lung cancer, and nobody deserves it,” Audrey Eatherly, the SWF’s Head of Creator and Community Partnerships, tells Tubefilter. “Early detection and prevention are key. But stigma and confusion keep too many people from talking about it or asking about a screening.”

The widespread assumption that only smokers can get lung cancer and the societal propensity to blame people who get it is a critical challenge to helping them, she adds. Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer deaths worldwide, killing more people each year than leukemia, lymphoma, and breast and prostate cancers combined.

“And yet, it receives the least amount of funding per death,” she says. “The stigma and misunderstanding have been barriers to addressing this critical challenge.”

Yvonne Diaz, a co-founder & Chair @ Oncogene Cancer Research and Board Member @ ALK Positive who has stage 4 ALK+ lung cancer, puts it well: “No matter how you wound up with lung cancer, you deserve the best outcomes, the best care, the best support, and empathy.”

Eatherly says the SWF wanted to bring its mission home to YouTube because the platform has the power to “transform lung cancer research into meaningful human conversations, bringing experts, research, real stories, shared experiences, and understanding to the forefront.”

The channel will share “credible, science-backed information and recent advancements” through things like deep-dive interviews with experts, informative Shorts, FAQs, and creator collaborations, she says.

Speaking of creators, they aren’t just helping launch the SWF’s YouTube channel. Jake Roper, Mark Vins (aka Brave Wilderness), sWooZie, and Matthew & Stephanie Patrick are all on the foundation’s official Creator Advisory Council.

“As longtime creators on YouTube, Matthew and I recognize the massive impact the platform has had on the world and how Susan Wojcicki’s leadership shaped its growth for almost a decade,” the Patricks tell Tubefilter. “The Susan Wojcicki Foundation carries Susan’s legacy forward through the very platform she helped build, and the creators whose careers she made possible are here to help spread its message. While YouTube is an entertainment platform, for many people it’s also their primary source of information and education online, making it a powerful place for the Susan Wojcicki Foundation to share a global message about lung cancer awareness and treatment.”

Stephanie also notes that she has a master’s degree in cancer pharmacology, and has seen firsthand “the massive scientific advancements made in the last decade in cancers that were previously thought to be untreatable.”

“The research the Susan Wojcicki Foundation is supporting is incredibly promising and could lead to significant improvements in patient outcomes while accelerating progress in lung cancer care,” she says.

Vins adds that he’s dedicated to the SWF because he wants to honor Susan’s lasting presence in the creator space.

“Susan was revered as a business leader who helped shape the technology and media landscape, but to creators, she was revered for something even more personal—her unwavering belief in our potential,” he says. “She empowered our voices at a time when the possibilities of YouTube were only beginning to emerge. She understood that creators weren’t simply making videos, we were building communities and creating relationships that would make a meaningful impact in the world.

“Ultimately, the greatest way I can honor Susan’s legacy is to do exactly what she always encouraged us to do: speak from the heart, create with integrity, and use our platforms to make a positive difference. Susan believed in the power of creators to change the world. I hope we can continue proving her right to bring greater awareness to lung cancer and support the pursuit of early detection that will save lives.”
