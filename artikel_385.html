The 12 best phones of 2026, tested by experts

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

After extensive testing, we're ready to name the best smartphones of 2026, and we don't take these recommendations lightly.

For most of us, our phone is the most important device we own. It's a lifeline to friends and family, and our primary gateway to the internet. And unless you're still carrying around a separate camera, your phone is also your means of documenting the world around you. There's a lot riding on this pocket-sized gadget!

Mashable has been covering smartphones since 2011, and we've tested all of this year's most exciting releases so far, including the latest flagships and foldables from Apple, Samsung, and Google. Phones have definitely gotten more expensive this year, but we have recommendations for every budget across both iOS and Android operating systems.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

If you're in the market for a new phone right now, you should know that it'll cost you more than usual. The best phones were never cheap, but smartphone prices surged in 2026 amid a global RAM shortage driven by the AI boom (commonly known as "RAMageddon"). New AI data centers are hogging supplies of memory and SSD storage chips, driving up their prices. Consequently, consumer tech devices with memory components are also getting more expensive. (RAMageddon price hikes have also taken a toll on laptops and gaming consoles.)

So far, we've seen Apple, Google, and Samsung all raise the prices of their new smartphones at launch. This fall, Samsung implemented another $100 to $200 price hike across its Galaxy S26 lineup, while Apple increased the prices of some older iPhones by $100. The average iOS user: Most people will be perfectly happy with the regular iPhone. Fast, long-lasting, pretty, and equipped with nice cameras, it's the Goldilocks of Apple handsets — not too basic, but not overkill.

Impatient folks: The Pro-level iPhone just got an upgrade, but the iPhone 18 probably isn't launching until March 2027. If you can't wait until then, last year's iPhone 17 is still a rock-solid buy.

Read Mashable's full review of the Apple iPhone 17.

Apple didn't reinvent the wheel with this one — we might get a more dramatic redesign next year — but the iPhone 17 is an awesome smartphone nonetheless. In fact, "It's easily the most compelling standard iPhone in years," per Mashable senior editor Stan Schroeder. As he pointed out in his review, it's basically an iPhone 17 Pro with no telephoto lens, vapor chamber cooling system, or RAW video support. (That's the extent of their tangible differences that we noticed in testing, but I'll add that the regular iPhone also lacks a 1TB storage option.) If you're not a specialty user in dire need of those features, it just makes sense to go with Apple's mid-range phone. For a couple of hundred dollars less than a Pro model, you still get a buttery 120Hz display, stellar performance and battery life, and very capable main cameras with the iPhone 17. Its wider range of colors and less bulky, less polarizing design seals the deal.

The average Android user: If you're happily part of team Android, then Google makes some of the best phones in the mobile market. Google's latest flagship phone is largely the same device as its Pro-tier models (save for some even better cameras and an underwhelming LED notification system), but it comes in safely under $1,000. Out of all the Android phones we've tried, this one strikes the happiest medium between value and features.

Parents: The Pixel 11 has a new Magic Capture camera feature that automatically takes and edits photos when you're recording video. Instead of trying to capture both at once or wrangle fast-moving kiddos for a picture, you can stay in the moment.

Read Mashable's full review of the Google Pixel 11.

The new Google Pixel 11 proves that you can get everything you need in an Android phone for under $1,000.

Google fine-tuned its flagship phone with a thinner design and a newer, faster chipset for 2026. Like Apple's regular iPhone, it's still a very slick smartphone even though it's not an exciting gen-over-gen upgrade. And also like Apple's regular iPhone, it has a lot in common with its pricier Pro-tier counterpart. Better cameras and a sharper display with an anti-scratch coating are the biggest things you get for splurging. (The two phones have the same processor, form factor, and battery life.)

Mashable tech reporter Alex Perry approved of the Pixel 11's performance, writing: "Apps load quickly and smoothly, on-device AI processing happens in a fairly snappy fashion, and the phone doesn't run all that hot, even without a case." Perry also called out the Pixel 11's new AI-powered Magic Capture image capture feature and Rambler text-to-speech tool, which wowed him even as a self-proclaimed AI skeptic. Unavailable on older Pixel phones, they alone make a decent case for upgrading to the Pixel 11.

iOS users on a budget: The iPhone 17e is the cheapest, most accessible, most low-stakes entry point into the world of Apple phones.

People who stash their phones in their pockets: The 6.1-inch iPhone 17e is shorter than the iPhone 17 as well as the ultra-thin, ultralight iPhone Air. It's not as light as the Air, but it's damn near close. It's the most compact traditional (non-flip or foldable) phone on this list.

Read Mashable's full review of the Apple iPhone 17e.

This spring, Apple refreshed its budget phone with MagSafe charging, more starting storage, and a more powerful chip (the same one inside the regular iPhone). The resulting iPhone 17e is a more convenient, future-proofed, and faster handset than before. It also takes slightly better photos in low-light settings.

While the standard iPhone 17 is a nicer device overall with vastly better cameras and a smoother display, the iPhone 17e is still a good, reliable phone for penny-pinchers who like iOS. It's a full $200 cheaper than its mid-tier counterpart, which is a significant difference for many shoppers.

People who want to venture beyond Apple, Samsung, and Google: Nothing is a British tech company that also makes stylish and affordable headphones and smartwatches. This affordable handset proves that the best smartphones don't have to cost you $1,000 and up.

Design enthusiasts: The Phone (4a) Pro sports the funky, retro-inspired look that Nothing has come to be known for.

People with the strictest budgets: Starting at $499 with 128GB of storage, this is the least expensive phone we recommend — now even more so after the iPhone 17e's recent price bump.

Read Mashable's full review of the Nothing (4a) Pro.

"If you want an under-$500 phone with flair," said Mashable contributor Adam Doud, "it doesn't get more flair-ish than" the Nothing Phone (4a) Pro. It has a striking retro-industrial look with a cool Glyph Matrix display on the back, which shows you the time, battery life, and other status indicators and notifications when it's face down. (Google just copied this feature, kind of.)

On the front, you'll find a surprisingly sharp AMOLED display with a top-tier 144Hz refresh rate. Its mid-range processor isn't fast enough to handle games, but it's apt for everyday usage scenarios and easy on its battery: "I regularly got just under two days of light-medium use on a single charge." Doud said. Google's identically priced Pixel 10a takes better pictures, but we think this is the better, snazzier budget Android phone overall.

Mobile photographers who prefer iOS: The iPhone 18 Pro takes fantastic photos in almost any environment, and it has much more customizable camera settings than lesser iPhones — including a physical variable aperture. (The iPhone 18 Pro Max has the same shooters, but it's much less portable.)

Folks who really like Apple's new burgundy color: The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max are both available in the "dangerously chic" new hue, as Mashable's Shannon Connellan put it. This model will appeal to more people because it's slightly cheaper and more pocket-friendly.

Read Mashable's full review of the Apple iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max.

Apple's latest Pro-tier handset has upgraded cameras with a new variable aperture on its main lens and new creator-friendly Pro controls. There are new Photographic Styles, new Cinematic video effects, and a new Focus Tracking tool, to name a few. All of these upgrades make the iPhone 18 Pro best-suited for serious shutterbugs who really like to fine-tune their camera settings, said Mashable senior editor Schroeder. "That doesn’t mean the regular user won't benefit," he wrote, "but I do bet that the majority of people will try out the manual aperture setting once and then leave it on auto."

Plus, the iPhone 18 Pro is configurable with up to 2TB of storage — it has plenty of room for all of your shots. Apple rounded out its spec sheet with a long battery life and a turbocharged A20 chip that gives it ample processing power for Siri AI.

Heads up: Early reviewers have reported some fading on the back of the burgundy and black iPhone 18 Pros, similar to the scratching problem with last year's cosmic orange iPhone 17 Pro. If that concerns you, either get a nice case or opt for the pretty new glacier blue iPhone 18 Pro instead. It doesn't seem to be having the same issue.

Mobile photographers who prefer Android: The Pixel 11 Pro takes the best photos of any Android phone we've tested. Not only are its cameras spectacular, but it also supports some great software features that make sure its pictures turn out great every time — Magic Capture, for one. (The standard Pixel 11 also has these features, though its cameras aren't quite as nice.)

Android users who want a high-end flagship with magnetic charging: Unlike the Samsung Galaxy S26 series, including the S26 Ultra, the Pixel 11 has built-in magnets. In other words, it supports magnetic charging by default. You don't need to buy a separate case to make sure it's securely attached to wireless chargers or accessories. (The regular Pixel 11 also has magnets, FWIW.)

Read Mashable's full review of the Google Pixel 11 Pro.

Admittedly, this category was a tight race between the Pixel 11 Pro and the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra. So, why did we pick the former? Take it from Mashable's Alex Perry: "Photography is fun on the Pixel 11 Pro, which is not something I feel about many handsets these days," he wrote. "A combination of hardware and software makes your images consistently look nice, even in challenging lighting conditions." Daytime shots, nighttime snaps, macro images, landscapes — in our experience, they all turn out great on the Pixel 11 Pro. Also worth noting: Google upgraded its digital zoom to a whopping 120x. (The Galaxy S26 Ultra's digital zoom maxes out at 100x.)

Cameras aside, we also gave high marks to this phone for its capable everyday performance, multi-day battery life, brighter display, and attractive color options. Its HiLight feature is kind of a flop, but the rest of it makes up for that.

Multitaskers on the move: The new iPhone Duo is thinner and lighter than Apple's iPads, and it has a huge, gorgeous inner display with a glare-reducing "nano-texture" finish. Its design makes it great for on-the-go multitasking and entertainment (think reading, watching movies, or playing games); it can replace an e-reader or tablet in a pinch.

Stylus users: The Duo is the only foldable we've tested that supports stylus input on its inner and outer displays. It works with the Apple Pencil (USB-C), which is sold separately for $79.

Early adopters: Foldable phones aren't in their infancy anymore, but they're still pretty niche devices. And as Apple's very first foldable, the Duo has some quirks — namely, many apps aren't optimized for it yet. Its ideal buyer is a tech enthusiast who wants to check out the latest and greatest in smartphone design and is willing to put up with some bugs for a bit.

People with cash to burn: The Duo starts at an eye-watering $1,999, making it Apple's most expensive phone ever — not to mention, the priciest phone on this list. (It maxes out at $3,199 with 2TB of storage.)

Patient people: The Duo was backordered for months at the time of writing. If you order it through the Apple Store right now, you probably won't get it until the opening weeks of 2027.

Read Mashable's full review of the Apple iPhone Duo.

The iPhone Duo is the first and only foldable that Apple's made so far, but it's quite impressive for a first-generation device. It's basically an iPhone that doubles as an iPad when unfolded — you can look at apps side by side, enjoy e-books and games in greater immersion, or use it as a MacBook's secondary display. (We just wouldn't recommend turning it into an alarm clock as Apple suggests.) It certainly helps that the Duo's matte inner screen is "the nicest phone display I’ve ever seen," said Senior Editor Stan Schroeder, who described it as "crisp, sharp, [and] beautiful to look at; it’s downright addictive."

The Duo has a passport-style design like the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8, our favorite Android foldable, just with rounder corners for a more polished look. It runs on the same powerful A20 chip as the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, so it's zippy enough for all the multitasking your heart desires, and optimized apps work incredibly well. While the Duo doesn't last as long as those regular iPhones, its battery life is fine for a foldable.

You'll have to temper your expectations with the Duo's cameras — it lacks a telephoto lens, and the hidden FaceTime camera on its inner screen is straight-up "bad," Schroeder said. That said, this is the case with most foldables.

On a final note, some inaugural Duo owners reported that their device's inner screen cracked shortly after they started using it (a problem that's since become known as "crackgate"). The good news is that the issue doesn't seem to be as serious as social media might have you believe. In a statement, Apple told us that it's isolated to "a small number" of Duos, and that impacted buyers should contact AppleCare for a replacement.

If it helps, Schroeder has been using the Duo for nearly a month and hasn't noticed any hinge or display defects with his review unit. What's more, another Duo has withstood more than 200,000 folds (and counting) inside the "Fold-a-Phone" machine built by our sister site, CNET; they're folding it 900 times an hour until it breaks. You can catch the livestream daily on CNET's YouTube channel.

Commuters and frequent travelers: Like Apple's iPhone Duo, the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 has a compact and versatile passport-style design that's tailor-made for multitasking on the go.

Reluctant guinea pigs: Unlike Apple, Samsung has been making foldable phones for many years now. Our reviewer noticed some "very minor bugs" with certain apps on the Galaxy Z Fold 8, but "most of the time, the phone just works," he said. And its construction is pretty much rock solid at this point. (We think the iPhone Duo's "crackgate" issue is largely overblown, but it's still something to be aware of.)

People who like a lightweight foldable: This is the lightest folding phone we've tested. It's 21 percent lighter than the iPhone Duo, 16 percent lighter than the Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold, and six percent lighter than Samsung's taller, book-style Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra.

Folks who want an "affordable" foldable: The Galaxy Z Fold 8 is $100 cheaper than Apple's iPhone Duo. If we're really splitting hairs, it's technically the thriftier choice.

Read Mashable's full review of the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8.

Google's Pixel 11 Pro Fold is also a great Android foldable, but ultimately, we prefer the wider, passport-style design of Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 — it's sleeker and better for entertainment. Plus, its crease isn't as visible. (We also prefer it over the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra for similar reasons.)

The Galaxy Z Fold 8's spacious inner AMOLED screen is "far and away" its biggest asset, said Mashable's Timothy Beck Werth. It's smooth and so bright. "I generally avoid streaming TV shows and movies on my phone, but not with the Z Fold 8," he wrote. "Likewise, while I've never been a huge fan of mobile gaming, the mobile version of Mario Kart looks and plays awesome here."

The display has a glare-free finish that makes it easy to view in direct sunlight, as well as Samsung's new "Flex Titanium" tech that keeps its crease subtle and hinge strong. Other highlights of the Galaxy Z Fold 8 include peppy multitasking performance and all-day battery life. While its cameras aren't spectacular — most foldables' shooters aren't — they're sufficient for most non-pro users.

The most advanced iPhone ever built is here, and it doesn't disappoint. So, who should buy the iPhone 18 Pro Max instead of the smaller iPhone 18 Pro?

Battery life maximizers: The iPhone 18 Pro Max is rated for up to 45 hours of video playback. It's the longest-lasting phone we've ever tested.

Mobile gamers who prefer regular (non-foldable) phones: This handset features a blazing-fast processor and a huge vapor chamber thermal system, plus a smooth, crisp, and massive display.

People with big hands: At 8.78 ounces, the iPhone 18 Pro Max is the heaviest regular phone we recommend. It's the same size as the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra, but weighs 16 percent more.

Read Mashable's full review of the Apple iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max.

Apple reorganized the components inside the iPhone 18 Pro Max to squeeze in a giant new battery that can run for nearly two days between charges. It's so big that the company had to add a special setting that keeps it within shipping limits on battery sizes. It lasts up to nine hours longer than the smaller iPhone 18 Pro, no "Max."

The iPhone 18 Pro Max's A20 Pro chip ran laps around the competition in our benchmarking, and the new thermal management system that fills its chassis keeps it cool while you're running demanding apps. Mashable's Stan Schroeder "tested this out by playing a few games of PUBG: Battlegrounds on the highest settings for a full hour," and "The phone barely got warm," he wrote. Its battery life only fell by nine percent during that stress test, FWIW.

The regular iPhone 18 Pro has the same hardware, but we think mobile gamers and movie-watchers will appreciate the bigger, more immersive screen on the "Max" version. The two phones are basically identical otherwise, including their shooter setup. Opt for this model if you want the best iPhone for battery life and cameras.

"If there's ever been a phone to make you ditch Apple, this is it," said Mashable tech editor Timothy Beck Werth. The question is, who should make the splurge?

Advanced users: The Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra is a premium device with a zippy custom processor, a large, high-end display with stylus support, and elite cameras. It's "too much phone" for the average person, to quote our review; only professional creatives and developers will be able to use it to its full potential.

AI enthusiasts: This handheld comes crammed with powerful AI tools, including pre-loaded Gemini, Perplexity, and Bixby AI assistants.

Read Mashable's full review of the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra.

In October, Samsung raised the prices of the Galaxy S26 phones it released back in March — and they were already more expensive than last year's S25 models. A new S26 Ultra will now cost you $1,400, while the 1TB version is a whopping $2,000. But if you want the finest, most future-proofed Android smartphone available, this is it.

Teens with deep-pocketed parents: The trendy little Galaxy Z Flip 8 makes a great starter phone for a younger person (though it doesn't have a "starter" price). It's cute, durable, and easy to hold in one hand.

People who don't obsess over photo quality: Like foldables, flip phones aren't ideal for serious photographers. The Galaxy Z Flip 8's cameras underwhelm compared to those on regular phones that cost the same or slightly less.

Nostalgic Millennials: If you haven't used a flip phone since the early 2000s, this is a great entry point into the modern market.

Read Mashable's full review of the Samsung Galaxy Z Flip 8.

In addition to your usual next-gen upgrades — a faster processor, a slightly thinner, lighter design — the Galaxy Z Flip 8 also features improved durability compared to last year's Galaxy Z Flip 7. Like the Galaxy Z Fold 8, above, its new Flex Titanium display tech equates to a stronger hinge and a more subtle crease on its inner screen. Mashable tech editor Timothy Beck Werth was particularly enamored by its small size and "charming good looks," calling it "a smartphone with good vibes."

Its color options are few but cute; the pink one won over our newsroom full of Apple users. While its cameras aren't much to write home about as a whole, it takes great selfies (as long as you don't use the selfie camera, ironically), and its AI image editing tools work really well. Regarding battery life, you should get over a day of use out of the Z Flip 8 before needing to charge it.

People who want a flip phone with great cameras: Flip phones aren't known for taking amazing pictures — Samsung's Galaxy Z Flip 8, above, is pretty representative of the market — but this model is an exception.

Folks who want a long-lasting flip phone: The latest Razr Ultra can last up to a day and a half away from an outlet, depending on your usage.

Fashionistas: This flip phone comes in two chic, Pantone-approved colorways featuring unique back covers.

Read Mashable's full review of the Motorola Razr Ultra.

If you can spend a little more on your flip phone, we really like the 2026 Motorola Razr Ultra, which Mashable tech editor Timothy Beck Werth deemed "a conversation-starting beauty." Both of its pretty AMOLED displays (inner and outer) are bigger, smoother, and brighter than the ones on last year's model.

Its external design is equally eye-catching: The Pantone Orient Blue model's cover is finished in Alcantara, a suede-like microfiber, while the Pantone Cocoa one has a wood veneer on the back. It takes "surprisingly good photos for a foldable," Werth said — better than the Galaxy Z Flip 8 for sure — capturing natural-looking colors and handling bright scenes well. Its 36-hour battery life is also an improvement over its Samsung counterpart, which taps out at 31 hours (same with the mid-tier Razr+, which also underwhelmed in the photography department).

Mashable has been covering phones for over a decade and a half, since the launch of the iPhone 4S, Samsung Galaxy S II, and Motorola Droid Razr in 2011. As of 2026, our team uses the following metrics to test and evaluate the best smartphones:

We spend several days to weeks with every phone we review. We stress test them by playing games on them, opening multiple apps at once, and trying any unique software features they support. We quantify their performance using Primate Labs' Geekbench CPU benchmark.

Throughout our testing periods, we measure how much a phone's battery drains with heavy usage. We also see how fast it charges. In more recent phone reviews, we've started conducted an additional video rundown test using "Tears of Steel," an open-source Blender movie.

We assess the overall design, construction, and ergonomics of each phone as we use it. Is it comfortable to hold? Can it fit in a pocket? How sharp is its display? Is it waterproof? What colors does it come in?

We test each of the phone's cameras in a variety of lighting conditions and settings. We take photos indoors and outdoors, in low and bright light; we get macro, zoom, and night shots, and we snap a bunch of selfies. We include the photos in our reviews for reference.
