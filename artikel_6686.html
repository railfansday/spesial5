Bond: The Business of James Bond

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

This week, the internet is abuzz with news of the much-anticipated casting of the new James Bond: Robert Pattinson, one of this year’s favorite leading men.

Pattinson’s arrival to the Bond franchise also marks the most luxury fashion-entrenched 007 actor yet. Pattinson has been a Dior ambassador for over 10 years, and has remained a key face for the brand since Jonathan Anderson took the helm in June last year. And in September — just two months before his Bond casting announcement — Pattinson was announced as Swiss watchmaker Jaeger-LeCoultre’s new global ambassador. The announcement generated $1 million in media impact value (MIV) within 48 hours for the brand, according to Launchmetrics.

Pattinson’s fashion affiliates were quick to stake their claim on the actor around the news of his casting. Dior’s Anderson posted an image of Pattinson and ex-Bond Daniel Craig on his personal Instagram story, taken at Anderson’s first Dior men’s 2026 show last summer, complete with a heart emoji. (Anderson had previously cast Craig in Loewe’s Fall/Winter 2024 campaign, around the same time he worked with the actor while doing the costumes for Luca Guadagnino’s Queer.) Richemont-owned Jaeger-LeCoultre, meanwhile, has posted two stills of the newly minted global ambassador to its Instagram account since Monday morning’s announcement.

Pattinson may have the most pre-existing off-screen deals of any Bond, but on-screen, the franchise is a blueprint for mega-deals between brands and movies. Partnerships have been part of the 007 world since Sean Connery stepped into the role of Bond in the inaugural Bond movie Dr. No in 1962, which featured hatmaker Lock & Co. Bond has driven an Aston Martin since 1964’s Goldfinger, Bollinger has been Bond’s champagne of choice since 1979’s Moonraker, and Heineken has been a partner since 1997. The beer brand has reportedly spent over $100 million on Bond sponsorship. Swiss watchmaker Omega is Bond’s longest-running sponsor and has decorated the wrists of Bonds since 1995, while also developing Bond watches for sale. (Prior to that, Bond wore a Rolex.) Past-era Bonds have cycled through different suit brands: Pierce Brosnan wore Brioni suits (and later starred in a campaign for the brand), as did Craig in Casino Royale. For the rest of Craig’s tenure, though, he wore Tom Ford.

The value of a Bond film has only grown. Spectre was estimated to feature $45 million in product placement; while the most recent Bond film — No Time to Die, Craig’s last — reportedly featured almost $100 million in brand placements and partnerships. In 2026, Bond’s on-screen value is immense. So much so that, last year, Amazon MGM Studios was said to have paid $20 million for the franchise, plus additional payouts that could total up to $1 billion to take full creative control over the franchise.

Brand deals have evolved dramatically since the early days of Bond — and even since the last movie. “Five years ago, an ambassadorship primarily meant red carpets and campaigns, while the film was kept separate,” says Krista Corrigan, senior retail analyst at EDITED. Now, they’re bleeding into the films themselves, whether by financially supporting films (and getting their clothes on-screen in the process), or inking deals with studios, as Dior recently did with A24. “Luxury brands give studios the aspirational quality that Bond depends on in return for a global marketing moment.”

Daniel Craig celebrating 60 years of James Bond in 2022.

The landscape into which this new Bond is releasing is dramatically different from the No Time to Die days of 2021. That was pre-Barbiecore, well before the number of brand-film-product collaborations for a given movie surpassed the hundreds, and was a different era of film promotion, before A24’s embrace of fashion and merch developed into a playbook in and of itself.

“What’s interesting about Bond is that there has always been that one iconic fashion moment or item everyone wants. Think Halle Berry’s orange bikini in Die Another Day,” says Adee Drexler, founder and CEO of communications agency ICA, who regularly brokers brand deals for clients. “These moments become part of pop culture and often live far beyond the film itself.”

The difference today, she argues, is that these moments can go viral overnight — which means the pressure to convert cultural relevance into commercial impact is heightened. “By aligning costume design, brand partnerships, and retail, the franchise can ensure that when an item captures the public’s attention, there’s already a way for consumers to buy into that moment,” Drexler says.

Will Pattinson’s be the most luxury-heavy Bond era yet? And how will the actor’s brand deals come into play?

Robert Pattinson in Dior for the Primetime screening in Venice.

Already, online chatter is bubbling about the impact of Pattinson’s current brand ambassadorships on the films. Puck’s Matt Belloni reported in his newsletter on Tuesday that Pattinson’s test deal didn’t address the actor’s current brand deals with Dior and Jaeger-LeCoultre, and that these will now need to be ironed out. One Redditor worried that Pattinson’s existing brand deals could delay filming. Most respondents expected not.

Still, it raises questions about how an actor’s off-screen brand ambassadorships influence their on-screen outfitting. “This is the first time in recent memory that a new Bond has been cast with a major luxury relationship already in place,” Corrigan says. “While brands have historically attached themselves to the role, this time it went to an actor already tied to Dior. With release expected in 2028, Dior —if involved — has plenty of time to design menswear around the film, which I’d expect to see over the next few seasons.”

Corrigan wouldn’t be surprised to see Dior on-screen come 2028, particularly given the brand’s recent embrace of the film industry and Anderson’s many costume designer credits. On November 12, Dior sent an email to US customers presenting Anderson’s cruise 2027 collection — shown in Los Angeles — as an exploration of the house’s Hollywood history, she notes.

Even if Dior isn’t integrated into the films themselves, the brand still stands to gain, Drexler says. “The press tour, premieres and cultural conversation create an enormous opportunity for Dior to own those fashion moments and translate that visibility into real commercial impact.”

Robert Pattinson in Dior, again, at the New York premiere of Die My Love.

Bond’s watch may be a different story. Jaeger-LeCoultre may have been posting Pattinson, but Omega wasted no time in staking its own claim on the Bond franchise. The brand got ahead of Pattinson’s casting by launching new models in its Seamaster diver’s watch collection, the James Bond and Spectre, in October. Omega will host an Art Basel Miami dinner to celebrate the Bond-themed timepieces. And on Tuesday morning, less than a day after Pattinson’s announcement, the watchmaker posted an image of its Seamaster Diver 300M James Bond Edition watch. Commenters were quick to pick up on what they expect to be a “battle” between the two Swiss watchmakers.

It may be a case of the highest bidder, and Omega is one of the largest watchmakers in the world (though Richemont CEO Johann Rupert recently told investors that Jaeger-LeCoultre was up 17% in the most recent quarter). Or it may be an on-screen, off-screen split. Some brand ambassadorship contracts include non-exclusivity clauses for filming. That said, when films know an actor is an ambassador for a given brand, producers will sometimes request to borrow clothing from that brand, given the existing relationship. For a moneyed franchise like Bond, though, no lending will be necessary. Instead, it’s a question of which brand will be Pattinson’s official outfitter.

Plus, for a role like Bond, the character the actor plays on-screen does become synonymous with the actor off-screen, particularly given how long the actor remains in the role. (Pattinson has a three-film deal.) One couldn’t imagine Craig donning Omega watches on-screen, while shilling non-Omega watches off-screen. In fact, the actor was — and still is — an official Omega ambassador.

Whichever brands do fill out this next iteration of the Bond world, they’ll have even more work cut out for them than in eras past, Drexler says. “It’s about moving beyond traditional product placement and creating partnerships that can capitalize on cultural moments in real time, without compromising the authenticity of the Bond universe.”
