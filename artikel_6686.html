Culture Watch: What to See and Where to Go in November

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Culture Watch is a series spotlighting the intersection of fashion and art, bringing you the latest must-see exhibitions, pop-ups, installations, and more.

November is a good month to make a little room in your calendar. As the holiday season starts to gather pace, galleries, brands, and creative spaces are offering plenty of reasons to get out and look around. In New York, Salon Art + Design returns for its 15th year, while Loro Piana presents its “Master of Cashmere” installation at The Brant Foundation; farther afield, international exhibitions offer even more to explore. Check out all of this month’s best cultural happenings below.

Loro Piana Names Its Cashmere of the Year and Unveils a ‘Master of Cashmere’ Installation

Loro Piana put cashmere in the spotlight at The Brant Foundation in New York, where it marked the 11th edition of its Cashmere of the Year Award. Established in 2015 and held stateside for the first time, the prize recognizes the herders who produce the finest cashmere fiber. Coinciding with the honor, which went to three couples of cashmere herders from Inner Mongolia, is “Master of Cashmere: The Quest for Fiber Excellence,” an installation that traces the material from its origins to the Maison’s work with it. The multi-room experience features archival sketches, explanatory timelines, and, of course, soft-to-the-touch cashmere.

Taylor Swift, take note: famously hard-to-get-into New York City hotspot The Corner Store has officially landed at The Cosmopolitan of Las Vegas, marking its first-ever location outside Manhattan. Expect its adventurous martinis, signature wood-paneled interiors by Rockwell Group, and a handful of Vegas-exclusive menu additions, including a Butcher’s Counter serving up wagyu tomahawk and porterhouse steaks.

Salon Art + Design Returns to the Park Avenue Armory for Its 15th Year

The 15th edition of Salon Art + Design is now on view at the Park Avenue Armory—and it’s one of its most immersive, expansive editions yet. As is tradition, the event brings together collectible design, fine art, and jewelry. The event is open through November 8. Standouts include Jean Dunand’s 1929 La Forêt lacquered screen, Michel Dufet’s 1918 daybeds, and Jean Royère’s illuminated desk. Fernando Mastrangelo’s totem lamps add a sculptural note, while the jewelry lineup includes pieces from Fernando Jorge, Mellerio, Cora Sheibani, and Viltier.

Lara Saget Debuts Her First New York Solo Show, ‘And the World Came Apart’

Lara Saget brings more than a decade of material experimentation to her first solo exhibition in New York. At Trotter&Sholer’s new Elizabeth Street space, her sculptures and wearable works fuse glass, stone, and metal through heat, pressure, and time. Drawing on geological processes and NASA data, the show moves from ancient natural formations to humanity’s rapid impact on the planet.

The Phillips Collection Opens ‘Avery, Gottlieb & Rothko: By the Sea’

Milton Avery went to Gloucester, Massachusetts, to “live like an artist,” then brought Adolph Gottlieb and Mark Rothko along for summers of creative exchange. In Washington, D.C., The Phillips Collection’s “Avery, Gottlieb & Rothko: By the Sea” brings together watercolors and color-drenched canvases, tracing what those Cape Ann days set in motion.

Tate St Ives Spotlights Wilhelmina Barns-Graham

What does a landscape feel like in the body? Scottish artist Wilhelmina Barns-Graham spent eight decades exploring that question through art shaped by sensation and memory. The retrospective at Tate St Ives in Cornwall, England, is the most comprehensive survey of her work, moving from early landscapes and studio interiors to later works alive with bold color and sweeping gestures.
