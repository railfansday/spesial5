The all-time most-watched channel in YouTube history returned to the #1 spot in the Global Top 50. T-Series was the only channel that hauled in more than one billion views during the first week of November — it topped out at 1.09 billion weekly views, bringing its lifetime view count above 362 billion.

T-Series was not the only channel from India to crack the Global Top 50. In fact, more than half of the channels in this week’s chart hail from the world’s most populous nation. In India, a spate of film releases and national celebrations have directed more traffic toward channels that were already thriving on YouTube.

YouTube is fixated on the holiday season — and no, I’m not talking about American celebrations. India recently rejoiced for Navratri and Unity Day, and a more famous festival is up next on the calendar. It’s time to celebrate Diwali.

The Hindu festival of lights falls on November 8 this year, and many of the traditional celebrations are taking place on YouTube. As an example, we can take a look at Baccha Mat Bolna, a channel that describes itself as a celebration of the “freedom of childhood.”

The latest Baccha Mat Bolna videos embody that youthful spirit, but with more Diwali. A new vlog “with the boys” takes place at a local holiday gathering, and a Short featuring the classic childhood game “the floor is lava” adds in some bright lighting to deliver appropriate festival flair.

Those videos have been key for Baccha Mat Bolna this week. The channel got 415.4 million weekly views to reach 28th place in the Global Top 50. That was the most seven-day viewership Baccha Mat Bolna has received since early August.

Diwali might not be quite as colorful as Holi, but bright displays are nevertheless an important part of the ongoing celebrations. That’s good news for the channels that teach young viewers how to paint and draw.

One such channel belongs to Paintellectual PriyA, a creator who just returned to the Global Top 50. Priya put herself in 40th place in the ranking by picking up 359.6 million weekly views. Her videos typically include painting tutorials, but for the Diwali season, she expanded her focus to include crafting projects, unboxing videos, and (of course) colorful lamps that put her technicolor sensibilities to good use.

Previously, we talked about how drawing and painting channels are well-equipped to harness the power of YouTube Shorts, because they can post tutorials that teach viewers how to depict whatever character is hot in the zeitgeist at any particular moment. Priya’s success shows how to extend that strategy beyond the realm of pop culture characters.

Diwali isn’t just a celebration that takes place throughout the South Asian diaspora. It’s an opportunity for South Asian creators to connect with local viewers by delivering the craft and game ideas that turn their celebrations into the talk of the town. Clearly, Priya’s videos are generating plenty of attention — that’s why hundreds of millions of viewers have decided to tune in.
