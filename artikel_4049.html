Xbox's first chief operating officer Helen Chiang leaves Microsoft just three months into the job

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

UPDATE: Chiang responds with statement denying claims of "policy violations" as "not accurate".

UPDATE 2 - 9.00pm (UK) 3rd November: Helen Chiang has responded to the below claims with the following statement, which she shared publicly via her LinkedIn profile:

There has been some speculation about my abrupt departure from Microsoft, including claims that I was "fired for a policy violation". That's not accurate and while I won't turn this into a play-by-play, silence should not be mistaken for agreement. What has been said about my departure, and what has been left unsaid, has created a picture of me that I hardly recognize. It's important to me to set the record straight.

First, I did not leak confidential company information to the press. I have never communicated with any journalist outside the limited, company-authorized interviews I've done as part of my job. That includes every journalist who reached out this week seeking my side of the story.

Second, I did not leave XBOX to join a competitor. You don't spend two decades somewhere without developing a deep loyalty to the people, teams, and businesses you've helped build. XBOX was never just another stop in my career. My end game was always to keep building here.

Third, I did not engage in discrimination, bullying, harassment, insider trading, embezzlement, or other fraudulent activity. I stand by my record: both the results I delivered and the way I have treated people throughout my career. I won't let speculation define my character or erase that record.

Lastly, I did not leave because I was passed over for a bigger role. Since having children, I've been intentional about how I define ambition and success. I haven't actively sought out bigger roles or increased responsibilities, and the last time I interviewed for a job was in 2011. My reputation and results paved the way for the opportunities that followed. I went where the company believed I was needed and where I could have the greatest impact. I did not ask for my most recent role, either. I was promoted into it in July.

I believe in fairness, accountability, and holding ourselves to the standards we expect of others. Those standards only mean something when they're applied consistently. I have serious concerns about how they were applied in my case, and I believe those concerns deserve to be taken seriously.

I share this because I don't want my departure reduced to an inaccurate story about incompetence, ambition for the next big job, or a Nelson-level scandal. My story is about staying true to your core values, especially when it is hard. That's the legacy I hope to leave behind.

To everyone who has reached out with hugs, love, and support: thank you. So many of you have reminded me of the wonderful times we've shared over the past 20 years and the impact we've had on one another. Some of you I haven't been in touch with for several years, yet you still took the time to check on me. Others are people I've never even met, but you reached out anyway. That speaks volumes about the care and humanity in our industry and the connections that transcend where we work.

This is not how I ever imagined my story here would end. And it is not the whole story.

Previous update and original story follows.

UPDATE 6.00pm (UK), 24th October: In a formal statement, Xbox told Eurogamer: "We don't discuss individual personnel matters. We're grateful for Helen's years of service and wish her well".

A verified source familiar with the situation separately told Eurogamer that Helen Chiang had allegedly been "terminated for policy violations". Eurogamer has not been able to independently verify this claim.

Eurogamer has contacted Helen Chiang for comment.

ORIGINAL STORY 2.00pm (UK), 24th October: Helen Chiang, Xbox's first-ever chief operating officer (COO), is leaving Microsoft just three months after taking the job.

According to The Verge, Xbox CEO Asha Sharma confirmed Chiang's departure in an internal memo circulated on 23rd October.

"Helen Chiang is leaving Microsoft today. I'm grateful for her years of service and wish her well," Sharma wrote.

Sharma provided no details on why Chiang - who'd been at Microsoft for 21 years and had previously served as head of Minecraft - was leaving the company, only adding, "we'll share more on COO leadership as plans are finalised". It's thought her duties will be shared between other Xbox execs.

Sharma created the role in July as part of Xbox's massive "reset", saying it was needed to "work toward a shared goal, make the right tradeoffs, and get things done". It also came with 3200 layoffs and four studio departures in "most significant" Xbox restructuring in history.

Microsoft revealed that Psychonauts developer Double Fine, State of Decay studio Undead Labs, and South of Midnight developer Compulsion Games would go independent following negotiations, while Hellblade developer Ninja Theory is now likely to close after two acquisition deals fell through.

Arkane Lyon - the beloved immersive sim studio behind the likes of Dishonored and Deathloop - is the one still in limbo, and yesterday we learned it reportedly only has until November to find a buyer.
