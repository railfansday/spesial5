League One clubs aim for further curbs on spending as budgets continue to spiral

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Wage bills in third tier have increased by 15% this season

‘Luxury tax’ and three-year limits on losses considered

League One clubs want to introduce new spending restrictions to reduce losses by limiting investment from owners.

Clubs in the division voted to tighten the rules last summer by reducing spending limits from 60% to 50% of turnover, and removed a staggered approach to owner investment, so that only 50% of equity injections can be spent on squad costs, with the rest assigned to infrastructure projects. Less than six months later many want to go further.

The Guardian has learned that two proposed models are being ­con­sidered: a fixed cap on owner investment or introducing a version of the Premier League’s squad cost rules, with a £1 levy on clubs for every £1 spent above 50% of their revenue. Sporting sanctions would be applied if spending reached 60%, with the money raised from the levy redistri­buted to other clubs in the division.

The move to introduce more spending curbs comes despite a legal challenge to the existing rules from the Professional ­Footballers’ Association, which launched proceedings in August on the grounds that the clubs had failed to abide by the terms of their membership of the ­professional negotiating and consultative committee.

An attempt by League One and League Two to introduce salary caps of £2.5m and £1.5m respectively was withdrawn in 2021 after the PFA took them to an independent arbitration panel.

Discussions over stricter controls have been taking place for some time, but have been given fresh impetus by the publication last month of the State of the Game report. The first piece of work by the ­Independent Football Regulator showed that 94% of clubs in the EFL and National League are loss-making, and that 32 of the 96 would not survive for a month without owner funding.

The changes last summer reduced the percentage of clubs’ income that can be spent on wages, with mana­gers’ salaries included for the first time, but there is a renewed move to reduce losses, which averaged £7.5m per club in League One last season, up from £5.3m the previous season.

One owner said that further regulation was ­necessary because the clubs had shown ­repeatedly that they could not restrain themselves, pointing out that League One wage bills have increased by 15% this season, and that a top-six budget in the division five years ago would now rank in the bottom three.

Reading and Peterborough have long campaigned for tighter spending controls, but face opposition from clubs such as MK Dons and Wycombe, and it is unclear whether any proposal would gain the two-thirds majority required to be introduced.

A source at another League One club warned that an unintended consequence would be to create a cliff edge in terms of financial resources with the Championship, which loosened its regulations last season by permitting owners an inject up to £33m over three years, an equity allowance that does not count towards the 85% wage spending/revenue limit.

League Two clubs are also involved in the discussions, although it is unclear whether they will choose to align with League One.

The EFL confirmed discussions were ongoing, although formal proposals have yet to be put to the clubs.
