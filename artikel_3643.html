What Happened to Detroit Lions’ Jack Campbell? Injury Explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Detroit Lions linebacker Jack Campbell faced a scary on-field injury as the team faced off against the Minnesota Vikings.

Campbell was seemingly “immobilized” after a tackle against Vikings tight end T.J. Hockenson during the game at Detroit’s Ford Field on Sunday, November 1. (The Lions ultimately won 19-15 over the Vikings.)

The Lions’ athletic training staff swarmed Campbell, who did not appear to be able to move after the fall. He was then placed onto a backboard and transported to a local hospital.

“Heavy emotions in Detroit,” Michigan sportscaster Brad Galli wrote via X. “All the Lions players and coaches surrounded Jack Campbell before he was carted off at Ford Field.”

Campbell, an All-American college athlete during his time on the Iowa Hawkeyes, was selected by the Lions in the first round of the 2023 NFL Draft.

The linebacker broke his silence about the incident in a statement released by the Lions on Wednesday, November 4.

“My family and I want to sincerely thank everyone for the overwhelming amount of love, prayers, and support we’ve received over the past few days,” Campbell, 26, said. “It means more to us than words can express.”

Campbell explained he was “incredibly encouraged by the progress I’ve made in such a short period of time.”

“I have full movement in my lower body, and I’m continuing to make progress with movement in my upper body,” he revealed. “I know there’s still work ahead, but I remain very optimistic. I’m taking things one day at a time, staying positive, and focusing on getting better.”

Campbell continued, “My faith in God has never been stronger. I know He has a plan for me, and I trust in that plan. I know there may be challenges ahead, but with my faith, my family, and so many people in my corner, I know we’ll get through this.”

The Lions shared in a statement on Sunday that Campbell was staying at the hospital overnight.

“Jack Campbell is resting and will remain in a local hospital tonight, for continued evaluation and monitoring,” the message shared via their official X account read. “The team will provide updates as we have them.”

In a postgame press conference, Lions head coach Dan Campbell shared that the linebacker was “being taken care of,” adding, “Thoughts and prayers are with Jack and his family.”

Dan spoke to the media again on Wednesday, November 4, right after Jack broke his silence about the injury.

“Being able to hear it from him, that means a lot,” the head coach said. “That’s powerful in itself. More than hearing it from me or anybody else, those are his and his family’s words. That’s powerful. I know that always helps, to know he’s improving. Because at this point, that’s all you’re looking for. Just to know that he’s improving.”

He added, “The status is changing every eight hours, 10 hours in a good way.”

Lions quarterback Jared Goff shared that he was thinking about his teammate after the injury. “Jack’s deal was definitely scary,” Goff said on Sunday. “It just brings you back to reality for a moment that he’s a husband and a father. Scary. We’re praying for him and hope he’s going to be alright. If anyone is tough enough to get through something like that it’s going to be him.”

Tom Brady, who was calling the game on Fox, got candid about how the Lions could return to the field after the linebacker’s injury.

“It’s very difficult for everybody, obviously, but it’s also part of what you’ve been doing your whole life,” the former quarterback said. “He’s under great medical care and attention and you have to go back out there and do your job.”

Sideline commentator Erin Andrews also weighed in on Jack’s impact on the Lions organization while relaying a conversation with one of his teammates.

“He said, ‘This is Jack Campbell’s defense.’ I don’t hesitate to say that it goes through him and everyone in that locker room knows that,” Andrews said on Sunday. “Point blank period.”

Jack’s wife, Megan Campbell, has not spoken about his injury, but she was seen meeting him at field level on Sunday before he was taken to the hospital. The pair tied the knot in 2024 and welcomed their first baby, son Cooper, in July.
