Trump orders DOJ probe into Democrats over alleged threats against admin officials

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Ron Johnson, R-Wisc., joins 'Saturday in America' to discuss President Donald Trump's comments to Senate Republicans and a new bid for Senate minority leader.

President Donald Trump said he has ordered the Department of Justice to open an investigation into instances of Democratic lawmakers "threatening" to impeach Attorney General Todd Blanche, among others.

The announcement, which was made via a Truth Social post Saturday afternoon, came after the Democrats retook control of the House and Senate in the midterm elections, winning strong majorities in both chambers of Congress.

"The Democrats are going around threatening a lot of people, including the Attorney General of the United States, and others, with Impeachment and many other forms of punishment, even though they admit they have no reason to do so," Trump wrote.

Since Election Day, two House Democrats — Reps. Hank Johnson, D-Ga., and Deborah Ross, D-N.C. — have publicly said they would support impeaching Blanche.

"I personally believe that the offenses of Todd Blanche demand that he be impeached and convicted and removed from office," Johnson said Thursday in an interview on SiriusXM’s "The Dan Abrams Show."

Ross went on host Dan Abrams' radio show shortly after Johnson and said she supported impeaching Blanche as well, but thought Democrats "should start with Hegseth."

In his post, Trump denied that any "impeachable acts" had been committed by Blanche or any other administration officials. He didn't mention Johnson or Ross by name, nor did he mention the fact that Thomas Massie, R-Ky., will likely reintroduce impeachment articles against Secretary of War Pete Hegseth on Monday.

Trump named only one Democratic politician, Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan, in his post. He accused her of saying she would "go after all young people that believe in Republican Values."

Sen. Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich., votes during a session in the U.S. Capitol on Thursday, Oct. 23, 2025. (Tom Williams/Getty Images)

TRUMP CALLS OUT SENATE REPUBLICANS AFTER MIDTERM LOSSES

Trump appears to have been referring to comments Slotkin made on Oct. 13 at the University of Chicago about wanting to pursue "signature cases" against people who "enabled" Trump.

"I want young people coming out of school to say, 'Should I go work for the Trump administration? Like, that sounds fun, I might get an opportunity.' I want them to be like, 'Lord, that might be buying myself a ton of problems that I don't want, and so let me avoid participating in that,'" Slotkin said. "I want that deterrence message sent."

Trump also said that Democrats have been threatening Republican donors "with imprisonment" because of their views.

President Donald Trump speaks on the day the final steel beam goes into the ballroom at the White House in Washington, D.C., on Nov. 4, 2026. (REUTERS/Evan Vucci)

The president did not identify the donors that have allegedly been threatened. Democrats have, however, signaled they plan to investigate some major Republican donors and corporations when they assume control of Congress, Reuters reported this week. This could include donors to the White House ballroom.

Trump concluded his post by saying "these EXTORTIONISTS" should be imprisoned and that he wanted the DOJ to investigate the alleged actions as a "criminal act."
