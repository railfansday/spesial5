Some hotels are designed solely as places to sleep at night while you are traveling. Others, however, are meant to be a part of your travel experience. Unless I am truly checking in for a quick overnight stay, I always seek out the latter. That mindset is exactly what led me to Palihotel Hollywood on a recent visit to Los Angeles.

Palihotel Hollywood is one of more than 20 boutique properties under the Palisociety umbrella; a collection of independently branded hotels that prioritize design, connection and individuality. These include Palihouse, Palihotel, Le Petit Pali, ARRIVE by Palisociety and a handful of independently branded properties. Earlier this year, several Palisociety properties joined the Marriott Bonvoy ecosystem as part of a larger partnership between Design Hotels and Marriott.

"Design Hotels and Marriott Bonvoy give us the best of both worlds," Palisociety founder Avi Brosh told me in an interview about the partnership. "We stay fully independent in how our hotels look, feel and operate, and we gain access to one of the largest and most loyal traveler communities in the world. For Bonvoy members, it means they can earn and use their points at a hotel that feels genuinely personal and local. For us, it means introducing Palisociety to travelers who might never have found us otherwise."

Palihotel Hollywood is located just two blocks from iconic Hollywood landmarks such as the Chinese Theatre and the Hollywood Walk of Fame. The hotel's walkability and quick access to the highway made it an easy choice for our California home base, but it was the way I felt while I was there that made it memorable.

The hotel is a fully renovated 1958 motor lodge that retains its original appeal, making it feel right at home on Sunset Boulevard.

"[At Palihotel Hollywood], we leaned into that history rather than erasing it. The dark green facade, striped awnings, the checkerboard pool deck and Benny’s on the Blvd all are meant to belong to this hotel and this stretch of Hollywood. You won’t find them anywhere else," Brosh said.

As soon as I stepped into the lobby, I felt transported to Hollywood's golden age. I'm about the furthest thing from a celebrity looking for a secluded hideaway, but if I were, I would have found it here.

Through the lobby's back doors, I found the hotel's secluded courtyard. Green and white striped loungers with peach-hued umbrellas for shade surrounded the pool. The pool deck was bursting with greenery, which made it feel even more homelike.

Back inside, there is a cozy bar behind the front desk for afternoon and evening drinks, a seating area for guests to commune and Benny's on the Blvd, the hotel's restaurant that serves breakfast, lunch, afternoon snacks, coffee, beer, wine and cocktails. You can dine at the counter or by the pool to soak up the California sunshine.

Each of the hotel's small, intimate spaces adds to the neighborhood feel, a quality consistent across the brand. All Palisociety hotels have fewer than 150 rooms, with Palihotel Hollywood clocking in at 74 rooms and suites.

"Each of our hotels is completely unique, inspired by the individual spaces we inhabit and the communities we call home," Brosh said. "What stays consistent across every Palisociety property is the sensibility: warm, layered interiors that feel collected over time, thoughtful in-room touches, a social heart where guests actually want to gather, and hospitality that feels relaxed and personal rather than scripted. Every hotel should feel like it could only exist where it is, while still feeling unmistakably ours," he added.

Each Palisociety property has its own personality, with custom details and design touches that reflect its locale. Palihotel Hollywood, for example, leans into that vintage Hollywood charm throughout the public spaces and guest rooms.

The hotel offers king, queen and two double-bed rooms, pool-view rooms with patios or balconies overlooking the courtyard and interior "night owl" rooms with no exterior windows.

My room had plush fabrics, light-colored walls and furnishings with jewel-toned accent pieces like the headboard and loveseat, marble bathrooms and retro touches like a Smeg minifridge. From a practical perspective, I appreciated the Nespresso coffee machine, nightstands on both sides of the bed, in-room steamer — and loved the touch of Diptyque bath products.

Marriott Bonvoy members get many of the same benefits at Palisociety properties as they would elsewhere. These include the ability to earn and redeem points and redeem free night awards. Where Palisociety and other Design Hotels differ is in the elite benefits.

Elite members still earn bonus points on stays and can receive a welcome gift of points. Room upgrades are subject to availability, as is late checkout. Benefits like lounge access, nightly upgrade awards, discounted member rates, mobile check-in and key and similar perks are excluded for Design Hotels.

A one-night stay at Palihotel Hollywood starts at around $185 or 43,000 Marriott Bonvoy points per night.

When I asked Brosh how Palihotels keeps nightly rates reasonable while offering a bespoke experience, he told me, "We spend money where guests feel it and save it where they don’t. That means investing in the things you touch and use every day, like great linens, good lighting, Diptyque in the bathroom, a real coffee setup, and being disciplined about everything else. We also love working with existing buildings that already have soul, which lets us put our effort into character instead of square footage. Good design doesn’t have to be expensive. It has to be considered."

Palihotel Hollywood perfectly encapsulates the Palisociety ethos to "enhance the experiences of our guests and our communities by creating one-of-a-kind places filled with interesting people, friendly staff and trusty service."

If you are looking for an exceptional stay that feels comfortable and welcoming, where you can earn or redeem Marriott Bonvoy points and enjoy some of your elite status benefits, you'll feel right at home with Palisociety.
