5 Spots for a Water Leak You Might Totally Miss—But Can Cost You

Plumbing lines run throughout the home and the yard, bringing in fresh water for you to bathe, wash, water, and drink. However, when there is an issue with one of these plumbing lines or the attached appliances and fixtures, what seems like a small leak can quickly become a major problem as the growing accumulation of water causes water damage, encourages mold growth, and slowly ruins existing plumbing connections.

According to the EPA, the average household’s leaks can account for more than 9,300 gallons of wasted water per year, while some homes have leaks that waste 50 gallons or more per day. As a plumber and restoration expert, I have seen many issues that were easy enough to fix turn into major restoration projects simply because they were unseen or neglected for so long.

Even a single leak can destroy insulation, ruin drywall, and cause mold to spread behind your walls. Before your plumbing becomes a problem, use this guide to discover five water leak sources you might miss so that they don’t cost you in the long run.

Water-carrying appliance hoses, like those on refrigerators, dishwashers, and washing machines, are notorious for causing catastrophic, hidden water damage. Since these appliances are often pushed tight against walls or tucked inside cabinetry, a slow leak can be difficult to find as it quietly rots the subfloor for months before a puddle ever creeps out into the open room.

The wax ring or plastic flange connecting the toilet base to the waste pipe can degrade over years. Instead of leaking onto the bathroom floor, the water can seep straight down through the subfloor, rotting the ceiling framing of the room directly underneath, which is why you need to be aware of any potential signs of a water leak, especially in the bathroom.

Water leaks caused by frozen pipes hidden behind walls or under concrete slabs can go unnoticed for a long time, causing extensive water damage in the walls if you aren’t careful. Water lines running beneath a concrete slab foundation can corrode or shift, causing a slow leak. These are highly hidden and usually only identified by a warm spot on the floor, a spike in the water bill, or eventual foundation cracking.

Underground irrigation lines are one of the most common culprits behind massive, hidden water loss. Because irrigation systems are buried across a wide outdoor area, an underground leak can dump thousands of gallons directly into the soil without ever creating a surface puddle.

Like roof leaks, outdoor spigots can lead to unexpected damage over a long period of time. These spigots should be turned off during the winter to prevent them from bursting internally during winter freezes. However, they may also develop leaks during the warmer months of the year, and the leak can remain completely hidden inside the wall cavity or crawlspace, only letting water escape whenever the valve is opened.
