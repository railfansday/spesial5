Microsoft lifts Indian cloud prices by 15 percent, cuts costs in China

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

PLUS: SEGA Japan suffers outage and data leak; India bans dead battery exports; Alibaba chip unit to float; and more

Microsoft has announced massive price rises for commercial cloud services in India and New Zealand, but a substantial reduction in China.

The software giant sells its cloudy services in many local currencies, at an equivalent rate to the prices it charges in the USA.

As exchange rates shift, so do the prices Microsoft charges around the world.

Microsoft last week announced the new charges that will come into effect from January 1, 2027, and the news isn’t pleasant for customers in India, who face an increase of 15.7 percent for cloud services and up to 20 percent for some on-prem offerings. New Zealand customers face a ten percent price hike.

Customers in China, where Microsoft relies on a local partner called 21Vianet to deliver its cloud, will see costs fall by up to 8.5 percent.

Australian buyers will see costs fall by 4.5 percent. The only nation not in Asia on Microsoft’s list is Norway, where prices will ease 5.9 percent.

Sega has just had a rotten week that saw it battle a data leak and an outage.

The outage impacted the company’s ALL.Net and Aime services, which connect arcade games across different locations. The services include administrative features for arcade operators, plus services for gamers including pre-payment to play, high score and profile recording, and networked gaming.

Sega suspended some ALL.Net and Aime services on November 1 after discovering unauthorized access to servers. At least thirteen games and services were then unavailable until around November 15.

The company hasn’t explained what went wrong, but assured customers the incident was not a data breach and that payment information was safe.

Chinese tech giant Alibaba last week announced its intention to float its T-Head chip design biz on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.

The filing announcing the spinoff describes T-Head as “a leading provider of full-stack data center chips which enable system-level compute solutions in China,” suggesting Alibaba doesn’t think buyers in the rest of the world are keen to acquire its chips.

T-Head going public “allows a more efficient deployment of Alibaba Group’s financial resources,” the filing states, and should also mean the chip design outfit can more easily find third-party customers.

The government of India last week banned the export of “Lithium-Ion battery Black Mass” – a powdery substance created during the process of recycling batteries.

“It contains recoverable quantities of critical minerals, including lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese,” according to the government’s announcement of the ban, which argues the measure “will increase domestic recovery of lithium, cobalt, nickel and other critical minerals, thereby reducing import dependence and the associated foreign exchange outgo. It will also strengthen supply chains for battery manufacturing, electric vehicles, energy storage and clean-energy technologies.”

“Further, the measure will advance the circular economy and ‘urban mining’, reducing reliance on primary mining, while supporting environmentally sound recycling of this hazardous material within the country in accordance with applicable environmental regulations.”

Five giant Japanese companies – NTT, Mitsubishi Electric, MUFG Bank, the Development Bank of Japan, and telco NTT DOCOMO – last week signed a memorandum of understanding that will see them jointly explore development and operation of a mega-constellation of satellites.

The five “will examine the technical and business feasibility of commercializing a diverse range of services enabled by mega-constellations, including communications, Earth observation, satellite-based positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT), and space data centers.”

Mitsubishi already works with the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) to jointly develop and manufacture rockets. The two banks regularly back big infrastructure projects, while the two NTT entities have advanced comms expertise.

The five companies didn’t set a deadline for delivery of a plan.

If they do decide there’s a business opportunity, they’ll likely need to look beyond Japan for launch capacity as JAXA does not currently possess a re-usable launch vehicle and operates at a modest launch cadence compared to private launch operators. ®
