‘This seems like an extreme privacy violation.’ Surprising messages from the Muse AI agent are unnerving Meta’s own employees, report says

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Meta employees have raised concerns about the company’s Muse AI agent after receiving unprompted notifications that tapped into private information, according to internal posts seen by Reuters.

In one example, a Meta staffer reported that Muse sent her a reminder on her personal phone to respond to a message from her sister on the encrypted messaging service Signal.

A Meta colleague also shared that he got unsolicited chats related to apps he had not connected and discovered Muse was set up by default with permission to access push notifications on Android phones, Reuters reported.

Another employee posted that during his chats about fishing-related weather and tide information, Muse said, “BTW, two bank updates, your Capital One balance is $2722.58, your Charles Schwab balance is $5899.57.”

Other random Muse messages also took Meta workers aback, including a question about parental control over access to their child’s data and a conversation about picking up a coffee table that raised concern over “possible cross-account context leak.”

In response to the internal posts about Muse, a member of Meta’s Superintelligence team said the agent appeared to be hallucinating, owing to built-in prompts that were designed to engage users between sessions, according to Reuters.

“These (hallucinations) unfortunately bias towards the most uncomfortable topics, like finances/security/safety, which sucks,” he said, adding that other cases involved “crypto-related things too, like giving people updates on their Bitcoin balances.”

The random, out-of-context messages were confirmed as hallucinations.

As for the Muse messages that referred to encrypted Signal chats, the spokesperson pointed out that Android users have the option to grant apps notification access, which they must opt in to and is not specific to Muse.

“With Mac, to allow Muse to work on your behalf, you not only need to click allow for accessibility and screen recording, but after that it takes you to a settings screen where you once again need to confirm and turn this on,” Meta explained.

Since Muse was launched in September, it’s been downloaded more than 10 million times, becoming one of the fastest-growing apps ever. It’s also key to the social media giant’s plan to generate billions of dollars in revenue from AI.

But other companies are blocking Muse and other AI agents. For example, JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon said the bank won’t allow agents to access customer accounts and act on their behalf.

Similarly, Amazon won’t let Muse shop on its e-commerce platform and is already fighting to keep out Perplexity’s AI agents.

The process of raising those barriers, however, also entails network upgrades that may be contributing to a sudden surge in outages.

In recent weeks, companies like Chase, Wells Fargo, United Airlines, Apple, and Microsoft saw temporary service disruptions. U.S. officials have said there’s no evidence of cyberattacks, suggesting software patches were deployed before all the bugs were worked out.

Because AI tools can find vulnerabilities faster than companies can safely fix them, the pressure to fix them right away is straining systems that were never designed for that speed, Sen. Mark Warner, vice chair of the Senate Intelligence Committee, told the Financial Times.

In an interview with Fortune’s Catherina Gioino, Mark Giles, director of Downdetector parent company Ookla Research, said there’s nothing linking the outages.

“This is a big blip, but it’s kind of on par for the course for what we’re seeing in terms of those longer-term trends,” he said. “Most of the time they’re fairly self-contained and remedied quite quickly. It’s the ones where you can’t withdraw money or you can’t access your emails that really get the headlines.”

Jason Ma is the weekend editor at Fortune, where he covers markets, the economy, finance, and housing.
