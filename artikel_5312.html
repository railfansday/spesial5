‘Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ opens to $120 million despite GTA VI—and its audience explains why

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping opened to an estimated $120 million in North America this weekend, nearly three times the $44 million debut of its 2023 predecessor, The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes. The Lionsgate prequel added $105 million overseas for a $225 million global launch, returning the franchise to the three-figure heights of its original run.

It did so in the shadow of Grand Theft Auto VI, the long-awaited Rockstar Games release that arrived one day earlier. Fans and industry insiders alike speculated that the game, which topped $1 billion in pre-sales ahead of its Nov. 19 launch, would monopolize the attention economy and drain an already thin November box office.

In hindsight, releasing a Hunger Games movie the day after GTA VI may have been a clever piece of counterprogramming. “I think the marketplace has already spoken,” Rentrak’s Paul Dergarabedian told the Associated Press.

Both GTA and The Hunger Games are now multigenerational action franchises, but the former has always targeted men, mostly young-adult to 40-year-old gamers drawn to its edgy depictions of criminal violence. The Hunger Games, meanwhile, has its roots in teen readers and women.

Like the five films before it, Sunrise on the Reaping is adapted from Suzanne Collins’s young-adult novels. It centers on 16-year-old Haymitch Abernathy, played by Joseph Zada, but the series was built around its heroine, Katniss Everdeen. Jennifer Lawrence, who led the original four films as Katniss, returns for a brief epilogue cameo.

The opening-weekend audience bears out the split. Women made up 67% of ticket buyers, and 74% were between ages 18 and 34, according to the AP. Lionsgate studied the risks of opening against GTA from the earliest stage of dating the film and concluded the two could coexist.

While the Mature-rated GTA VI may have kept men of a certain age glued to their consoles this weekend, the PG-13 Sunrise on the Reaping held on to the core Hunger Games crowd: young women, along with teens too young to play the game.

That base may also have come out of thirst for a big-screen, four-quadrant blockbuster. This fall has been a sleepy season for PG-13 action movies, with dramas, documentaries, and R-rated fare dominating the box office and the zeitgeist. Audiences liked what they found: The film earned an A- CinemaScore, up from the B+ that greeted Songbirds & Snakes.

Overall, the weekend was a surprisingly strong one for theaters. Total domestic grosses topped $160 million, nearly double the prior weekend, though still about 10% below the same frame last year, when Wicked: For Good opened.

Sunrise on the Reaping accounted for most of that. Behind it on the weekend chart came I Play Rocky with $9.3 million in its first weekend of wide release, Ebenezer at $7.7 million, and The Cat in the Hat at $6.9 million. You Can See Everything is still hanging in the top 10, adding another $2.5 million.

These titles are diverse, but most target an audience different from GTA VI. Beyond The Hunger Games locking up young women, I Play Rocky is for the boomer dads, and The Cat in the Hat is for kids.

None of it came close to GTA VI. Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar’s parent company, says the game’s day-one retail sales, including preorders, reached $3.79 billion worldwide, more than 20 times the weekend’s entire domestic box office.

Still, Hollywood held its own heading into Thanksgiving week, suggesting that with the right strategy, audiences will still show up for the movies, and that GTA VI may not dominate every corner of the entertainment economy this season. The next test comes this weekend, when Sunrise on the Reaping faces a crowded holiday slate that includes Disney’s animated Hexed.
