‘Tracker’ Officially Switches Protagonists After 4 Seasons

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Editor's Note: The following contains spoilers for Tracker Season 4, Episode 7.

Although Tracker has a strong, confident, and irreplaceable leading man in Justin Hartley's Colter Shaw — seriously, we wouldn't want our favorite reward seeker to ride into the sunset any time soon — this week's "That's The Job" suggests that perhaps the CBS drama could share its top billing. In this case, the episode introduced us to Luke Kelso, a bounty hunter played by Josh Holloway, who offers some real charisma and potential for narrative growth and development. Could Tracker be hoping to introduce another rewardist with a heart of gold? If "That's The Job" proved anything, it's that there's certainly room for such a recurring force.

Like Colter Shaw, Luke Kelso has a somewhat tragic past that has led him to the world of bounty hunting. He may not have had a father who trained him to be a lone wolf survivalist, but his time in the foster system taught him resilience, and he acquired other skills in the following years that led to becoming a bail bondsman before venturing into his seedier chosen profession. But perhaps the most interesting thing about Luke is that he has often found himself on the wrong side of not just the law, but his own moral code — and it takes meeting Colter and seeing a job through to finally realize that. It's not until Luke sees those errors in his ways, the outcome of his "take the money and run" mentality, that he then understands the value in what Colter does. By the time we leave him in "That's The Job," Luke is a changed man — one who could easily find himself back on Tracker at any given time.

Tracker works because the case changes every week and the four people solving it never do. Here is which of them you are, what it says about you, and the case for yours being the best of the four.

You would rather handle it yourself and you are usually capable of doing so. Colter is a survivalist who lives in an Airstream and takes reward money for finding missing people, which is a premise that only works because he is clearly running from something of his own.

You know somebody, and that somebody owes you a favour. Reenie is the lawyer Colter calls when a case needs a door opened legally, and she is the only person on the show who can get further with a phone call than he can on foot.

You are two steps ahead and you rarely mention it. Bobby is the one who finds the thing that unlocks the case, usually from a desk, usually about ninety seconds before anybody realises they needed it.

You turn up, change the temperature of the room, and leave people arguing about you. Russell is Colter's brother, ex-military, and the show has never once let the audience settle on whether he is a help or a problem, which is exactly why he keeps coming back.

Tracker has had a good track record with recurring allies that come back into Colter's life at just the right times. Jensen Ackles has been a presence as Colter's brother Russell (who remains perfect spin-off material) since the first season, as has rival reward-collector and thief Billie Matalon (Sofia Pernas). Likewise, former detective John Keaton (played by Brett Sexton) has made himself a welcome recurring presence since the second season. But unlike the rest of these leading characters, who all have their own things going, John Holloway's Luke Kelso feels so seamless in Colter's world that we could easily see him as either a co-lead or a rotating leading man who tackles select cases throughout the season. Indeed, we've suggested previously that Russell could fill that role in Tracker if the CBS program were to take notes from the classic Western series Maverick, which famously alternated its leading men and occasionally brought them together.

Again, this isn't to say we'd ever want Colter totally replaced with someone else — far from it. Arguably the show's biggest draw is Justin Hartley himself, who continues to use his on-screen charisma to charm his way into the hearts and minds of viewers each week. If not for the Yellowstone-adjacent Marshals, he may very well still be the most popular leading man currently on network television. However, the show is called Tracker, not Colter Shaw (or even Shaw, for that matter). It could be interesting if the occasional episode or two were headlined now and then by someone like Luke. Now that Colter has inspired him to look at the job differently, his weekly routine may not look all too different from our main hero's, and it might give Hartley the chance to enjoy some R&R while not sacrificing what makes the show engaging in the first place, namely the weekly caseload and twisty one-and-done mysteries.

At the very least, it's clear that Holloway (billed as a "special guest star," like Ackles) deserves some additional Tracker screen time. Not only would we adore a team-up between Luke and Russell, but finding ways to further challenge this character as he now seeks a more righteous path would be a great way to bring him back. Perhaps there are other cases that haunt Luke that he now cannot get out of his head? Maybe he looks back on previous jobs and discovers he has been on the wrong side of the conflict on other occasions as well? Finding ways for Luke to further explore his failures and seek to make them right would certainly be enough cause for his return — or even another idea for a spin-off.

Not only does "That's The Job" wrestle with Luke's failures, but Colter's as well. Because of Luke's interference, Colter failed at his own task and was unable to bring a good man home to those he loved. It's the tragedy of the work that, sometimes, things don't all turn out happy in the end. While this is not the first time that Tracker has explored such failures, the arrival of Luke gives Colter (and the audience) an interesting glimpse of the type of man he might have become if he had any less integrity than he does now.

Tracker airs on Sundays on CBS and is available for streaming the next day on Paramount+.
