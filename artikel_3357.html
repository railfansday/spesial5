Chicago Fire Quietly Sets Up A Much-Needed Firehouse 51 Overhaul

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Caution: This post contains spoilers from Chicago Fire season 15 episode 7, "No Love Lost."Chicago Fire season 15, episode 7 brought us an hour without Squad 3. Kelly Severide (Taylor Kinney) was away to train Marcus Burke (Da'Vinchi) for Squad on Chicago Fire season 15, leaving Stella Kidd (Miranda Rae Mayo) in charge, and it quickly set the tone for how things could be with a complete overhaul at the firehouse in more ways than just the soft Chicago Fire reboot with the new probies.

Like the majority of Dick Wolf's procedural on NBC, cast rotation is the norm in Chicago Fire. Over the years, viewers have gotten used to missing one or two main characters throughout the cycle. However, Chicago Fire season 15 fully takes advantage of Severide's absence this time to emphasize a major change that needs to happen in Firehouse 51.

Kidd quickly stepped into the running of the firehouse, taking command at every call to ensure people were safe. Nobody questioned her decisions, even the more veteran firefighters like Mouch and Herrmann. When things got personal for Vasquez, she listened to him with respect and trusted his gut, something that we've seen Boden (Eamonn Walker), Pascal (Dermot Mulroney), and Severide do over the years.

Not only was Kidd able to get to the bottom of the gas tanks exploding, but she did her own research into credit card information. When she got stuck, she leaned on the rest of the team, showing her belief in them, and together, they were able to prevent a dangerous situation from having any major casualties.

Throughout Chicago Fire season 15, episode 7, Kidd didn't just step into Severide's role, but she also stepped into his office. Not only did everyone respect the chain of command, but Kidd proved herself capable and confident. At no point did she doubt whether she was acting as the leader because she was Severide's wife, but knew it was because she had the most experience as a lieutenant.

It all harkens back to the words Van Meter had said to her during season 15, episode 4, when Chicago Fire effectively admitted its mistake. Firehouse 51 already had a leader who could step in if Severide chose OFI. While Severide did an honorable thing by staying with Firehouse 51 after everything that happened to Cruz (Joe Minoso), Kidd knew that his heart wasn't there.

Chicago Fire's 300th episode was more than a final goodbye to Cruz; it also confirmed the departures of three other integral characters.

There isn't a doubt that Kidd can lead, and it's clear that she likes the job. At the end of the hour, Mouch mentioned that she'd probably be happy stepping away from the office during the next shift. Severide is set to be back, and that means he'll get that spot again. However, there was a look on Kidd's face that made it clear she was ready to take over as leader at Firehouse 51. She just needed the experience to see what it was like and whether the entire firehouse would follow her.

While Chicago Fire used Severide as a scapegoat when it came to Cruz's exit and his decision to choose Firehouse 51 over OFI, an entire episode without him proves that he doesn't need to be. Severide needed someone to step up and say that they could run the show because he would feel guilty if he walked away without someone there. It's an honorable part of his character, but he shouldn't have to sacrifice his career dreams for others.

Now that Kidd has had a taste of leadership, she can speak with her husband about alternate options. Not only did she command respect, but she didn't seem to care about the paperwork that was involved before the end of the shift, something that Severide hates doing. In fact, she's never once complained about the paperwork that's involved as an officer, and it's rare to see that. She also offers a listening ear and trusts her team, while also being strict when it comes to teaching them lessons.

Chicago Fire delivered a meaningful 300th episode, but one character's absence was a glaring error in judgment, and not just because of the milestone.

Chicago Fire needs changes to the dynamics at the firehouse to give it fresh breath. This is exactly the way to do it, and it doesn't even mean Severide needs to leave the show, as it could open the door for more OFI episodes, showing more of how an arson investigation works.

Chicago Fire follows the lives of firefighters, rescue squad members, and paramedics of Firehouse 51 in Chicago as they face professional and personal challenges, showcasing their dedication, teamwork, and resilience.
