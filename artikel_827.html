How two Welshmen delivered 'unbelievable' England win in Australia

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

England's all-Welsh opening partnership of Aneurin Donald and Phil Salt delivered a 151-run opening partnership as Harry Brook's side raced to an eight-wicket victory; Donald fired 85 from just 32 deliveries to help England race to their target

England captain Harry Brook praised the ‘unbelievable’ batting of his two Welsh openers after they sparked a demolition of Australia to level their T20I series on the Gold Coast.

Aneurin Donald and Phil Salt combined for a 151-run opening stand in just 61 deliveries, firing 13 sixes along the way, as England raced to their victory target of 185 inside just 13.1 overs.

Donald declared himself "quite good at slogging" after smashing 85 in just 32 balls, where he threatened to break Salt's record as England's fastest T20 centurion before hitting Riley Meredith to long-on.

Salt scored four sixes in one 31-run Joel Davies over and was on 58 when he was out caught-and-bowled to Ben Dwarshuis, with the Welsh duo laying the firm foundations to close out an eight-wicket win.

"Speechless, just unbelievable," Brook said of his power-hitting opening pair. "That opening partnership was explosive to say the least.

"The pitch played a bit better than we thought and Salt and Donald put on an absolute clinic. They should be very proud."

Australia 184-8 in 20 overs (won toss, batted first): Nikhil Chaudhary (45 from 35), Joel Davies (39 from 25), Jake Fraser-McGuirk (27 from 13); Sam Curran (2-29), Henry Crocombe (2-40)

England 188-2 in 13.1 overs: Aneurin Donald (85 from 32), Phil Salt (58 from 30), Tom Banton (26no from eight); Riley Meredith (1-18), Ben Dwarshuis (1-19)

Donald only scored 56 runs from his first four England outings but ended that disappointing run in spectacular fashion in Carrara, with his strike-rate of 216.37 making him the fastest run-scorer in all T20 cricket since the start of 2024.

"It was pretty special doing that with Salty, two proud Welshmen ticking the box for the England and Wales Cricket Board. We'll mention it a lot more now," Donald admitted, having been named player of the match.

"There's not many sports where you play as two nations together. Wales probably hasn't produced the guys they'd have liked to after having some good youngsters over the years. It's special and it's testament to the system back home.

"Welsh cricket and the support I've had in my career has been incredible and it feels like this is a night where I can give back a little bit."

Donald's free-flowing innings was further proof that he could deliver on the big stage, with Australian captain Mitch Marsh looking forward to playing with the Welshman when he features for Perth Scorchers in the Big Bash after this series.

"Donald is my opening partner at the Perth Scorchers this season so I am looking forward to watching that from the other end!" Marsh admitted.

"I thought we were well in the game posting 184 but hats off to England - we tried everything and some of our good balls were going 40 rows back. We were under extreme pressure."

Donald's 18-ball fifty was the joint third-fastest for England in the format, with his latest knock likely to attract further interest from Indian Premier League franchises ahead of the auction next month for the 2027 season.

"In the last few years I've realised I'm not great at batting but I'm quite good at slogging," Donald added. "In modern cricket I'm not sure there's too much (of a difference), especially in T20, but there might be a career in it now.

"It's just about staying true to myself and trusting that bat swing. I'm sure I'm going to make myself look like an idiot over the next few years batting the way I do, but hopefully it's going to come off more often than not.

"I'm not the most skilful, I'm not the most powerful but my intent and the way I play has probably separated me out in the last few months. There's going to be periods where it's not quite firing but it's about staying as true as I can."
