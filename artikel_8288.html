A Night in a Camino Ghost Town

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The streets of Arzúa were completely deserted again as we set out this morning. It felt almost apocalyptic—a good-sized, modern city with well-kept buildings that were clearly occupied, yet not a single person was in sight. I kept expecting zombies to come out of an alleyway.

On our way out of town, we ran into our tramily and ended up hiking together for the rest of the day. Good conversation flowed easily, making the miles fly by. Surprisingly, the terrain kept us on our toes with several sharp climbs and steep descents as we passed through a handful of ancient hamlets.

The Camino was busy with walkers again today, giving the trek more of a touristy atmosphere than a quiet pilgrimage. Still, it was a pleasant hike overall. Most of the route guided us through lush woodland paths, past sprawling farm fields, and along sections framed by towering stone walls.

As we crossed an interstate highway via an overpass bridge, a light rain began to fall. It was our first rain in weeks, but it was brief and refreshing, keeping temperatures cool in the mid-60s. The chain-link fence lining the bridge was covered in thousands of wooden crosses, notes, and memorials left behind by passing pilgrims.

The remaining miles melted away as we entered our final destination for the day: O Pedrouzo (also known as Arca). Like Arzúa, O Pedrouzo is a modern town built primarily to service the influx of pilgrims on the final stretch to Santiago, so there wasn’t a whole lot of sightseeing to do.

For dinner, we stumbled upon a great Mexican restaurant where I ordered seafood paella. The dish was cooked with squid ink (paella negra), which turned the rice jet black while imparting a rich, savory flavor. Knockerz opted for an authentic Mexican burrito, which hit the spot and tasted just like the ones back home.

After dinner, we relaxed on the small patio outside our albergue room. We spent the evening sipping wine and soaking in sweeping views of the valley, forests, and distant mountains—knowing tomorrow would be our final push into Santiago de Compostela.
