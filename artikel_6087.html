Western Global Boeing 747 Stuck In Soft Ground Closes Tokyo Runway For 14 Hours

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

On Wednesday evening, a Western Global Airlines Boeing 747-400 Freighter suffered a runway excursion while turning around ahead of its planned departure from Yokota Air Base (OKO) in Western Tokyo. The cargo-carrying quadjet was set to fly to Naha Airport (OKA) in Okinawa Prefecture, but, as a result of getting stuck in the mud, it has remained on the ground ever since the unfortunate incident.

With Yokota Air Base only having one runway, and the stranded 747 being stuck in close proximity to one end of this landing strip, the facility subsequently had to close for more than 14 hours, only reopening again on Thursday morning. While the Boeing 747-400F is a popular choice among cargo operators today, Western Global only has four examples, so the incident has grounded 25% of its 747 fleet.

According to the Aviation Herald, the aircraft involved in the incident bore the registration N356KD. The jet was preparing to fly from Yokota to Naha as flight KD188, but, before it could take off, it had to 'backtrack' along runway 36 and turn around. According to Aero Corner, runway 18/36 at the base is 11,001 feet (3,353 m) long and 200 feet (61 m) wide, with the latter measurement not proving quite enough.

Indeed, during its 180-degree turn, N356KD's nose gear exited the runway and got caught in the adjacent soft ground before the jet had finished lining up for takeoff. This prompted a temporary closure of the landing strip, which lasted from 6:28 PM local time on Wednesday to 8:59 AM on Thursday, a duration of more than 14 hours. While operations have resumed, the 747 is yet to leave Yokota Air Base.

Simple Flying has reached out to Western Global Airlines for futher information on this incident. We will update our coverage upon receiving a statement from the carrier.

According to data from Planespotters.net, N356KD is one of four examples of the Boeing 747-400F that Western Global Airlines currently has at its disposal. These are accompanied by 14 examples of the McDonnell Douglas MD-11F, although only three of those trijets are listed as being active at present. As for Western Global Airlines' Boeing 747-400F fleet, these quadjets are 32.4 years old on average.

N356KD itself clocks in at 32.7 years old, having first been delivered to Japan Airlines back in March of 1994 as JA8911. It was initially configured to carry passengers, having been fitted with 74 business class and 346 economy class seats. After 13 years in this setup, JAL converted the jet to carry cargo in December 2007, after which it spent three years ferrying airfreight around for the Japanese flag carrier.

JA8911 eventually left JAL in November 2010 for Western Global Airlines, which re-registered it as N356NA. Almost immediately, it leased the jet out to Atlas Air, with the plane being re-registered again as N458MC in March 2011. It returned from its lease in August 2015, by which time, according to ch-aviation, it had amassed almost 67,000 flight hours. That October, it gained its current registration of N356KD.

While Western Global Airlines is a US-based operator, flight tracking data shows that N356KD often spends time in Asia. Its most recent deployment began on November 23, when, after flying for an hour up the Californian coast from Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), it crossed the Pacific Ocean from Travis Air Force Base (SUU) in Fairfield. The flight, as seen above, took it to Yokota, and took 11 hours.

Thereafter, on November 24, it flew from Yokota to Andersen Air Force Base (UAM) in Guam, before returning to the West Tokyo air base in the early hours of the morning on November 25. It remains to be seen what its next steps will be following the cancelation of its planned flight to Naha on Wednesday evening. Yokota Air Base itself is a shared facility, with both the Japanese and US militaries present there.
