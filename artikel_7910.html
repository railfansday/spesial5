Hyperactivity is common, and the good news is that knowing what causes it makes it much easier to help a puppy calm down. The reasons range from ordinary puppy exuberance to, on rare occasions, a health problem.

Puppies burn energy by exploring and playing, but they can run out of things to do. A mix of toys, activities, and walks helps manage their energy. Without it, boredom and frustration can lead to destructive behavior or aggression, or to zooming around the house. A puppy with no good outlet, such as a sturdy chew toy, will release that pent-up energy by barking, running around, or jumping on people.

If a puppy gets attention every time it becomes overexcited during play, even when the owner is trying to say stop, the puppy sees it as a reward, and the next time it feels neglected, hyper behavior may seem like a good way to get noticed. The fix is to interact when the puppy is gentle or relaxed, and to become less interesting when it gets overexcited, by dropping the tug toy and stepping away.

Border Collies, bred to herd sheep all day, require lots of physical and mental stimulation to keep up with their boundless energy. A more sedate breed, like the French Bulldog or King Charles Spaniel, tends to have far less energy to burn. A Border Collie puppy has less stamina than an adult, but it still has plenty of energy to manage.

Most of the time, hyperactivity is easily corrected and comes down to diet, breed, or limited exercise. Health problems are a rare cause. If a puppy is still hyper after changes to diet, activity, and training, a vet check to rule out any health issues is always a good idea. The earlier a problem is diagnosed, the easier it is to treat.

Puppies are still learning what their bodies are telling them. One of the most common signs is becoming hyper or restless when they need to go. An older puppy getting used to house training may be asking to go out, while a younger one may not understand the signal and burst into a case of the zoomies until it figures it out.

Most hyperactivity simply comes from being a puppy. Younger dogs are more excitable, and they need lots of energy to explore the world without yet knowing how to manage it. Puppies steadily grow out of it. They may still get the zoomies later on, but they settle and learn better control. Rewarding calm behavior, working on obedience, and giving a working breed a job to do all help.

Researching a breed before bringing a puppy home matters, since a high-energy herding breed can be too much for some households. Time matters too, because a long workday followed by evening chores may leave little room for the exercise a puppy needs.

Training helps too, because some hyper behavior is really bad manners learned along the way, such as jumping up or racing around when the leash comes out.

Most puppies grow out of their boundless energy as they reach adulthood and learn self-control, which can take between one and two years depending on breed. Some breeds are naturally quieter and happier to laze around, while more active breeds never lose their energy.
