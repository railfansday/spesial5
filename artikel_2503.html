NWSL announces roster-building mechanisms for 2028 expansion teams

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

With Atlanta and Columbus joining league play in 2028, NWSL announced the return of additional allocation funds

Two expansion teams will play their inaugural National Women’s Soccer League seasons in 2028: Atlanta City FC and the to-be-named Columbus club. With the teams able to start building their rosters in 2027, the league announced Friday the mechanisms they can use to build their inaugural rosters, since they got rid of the expansion draft ahead of Boston Legacy FC and Denver Summit FC joining the league.

The options available to Atlanta and Columbus are relatively the same as the mechanisms Boston and Denver had but adjusted for the consistently increasing salary cap.

Each expansion club will have access to $1,430,000 in allocation money, roughly 30% of the 2028 base salary cap according to the announcement. Allocation money must be funded by the clubs and may be used to reduce salary cap charges, pay transfer and loan fees, and be used in trades with all other clubs. Unused, funded allocation money will remain available through December 31, 2030, including any funds subsequently traded to another club.

Comparatively, Denver and Boston had access to the same percentage of allocation money, but 50% of the funds were provided by the league and the rest funded by the teams at their discretion. They have until December 31, 2027, to use all of their funded allocation money. Allocation money purchased before 2024 via the mechanism’s original iteration will be phased out by the end of 2026, so it now appears that allocation money will live on as a unicorn available to expansion teams in the short term.

Atlanta and Columbus will also have access to an increased transfer fee threshold of $1.9 million over two years, compared to the threshold of $665,500 for non-expansion clubs in 2027. Starting on the first day of 2027 and through the conclusion of the 2028 secondary transfer window, the expansion teams have $1,167,950 available, then an additional $732,050 during the 2028 calendar year. Spending above the applicable threshold will incur a salary cap charge equal to 25 percent of the excess amount, same as the rest of the league.

For a more in-depth explanation of allocation money and transfer fee thresholds, check out this explainer from Jeff Kassouf.

In order to allow the expansion clubs to start signing players in 2027 ahead of their inaugural seasons, they will be subject to a limited salary cap of $628,320, equivalent to 14.28 percent of the 2027 base salary cap. High Impact Player (HIP) rule spending is not available until January 1, 2028.

The previous pair of expansion teams had a 6-month period during which they could sign players without being subject to the salary cap as long as they had a net-zero salary cap impact by being: loaned out to a team that picks up the full cost of salary and bonuses; qualified to be placed on SEI; or allocation money was used to cover the salary cap charge. Then they had a limited salary cap of $250,00 after the second transfer window opened.

Once again, expansion clubs will not be required to meet the league’s minimum roster size requirements in 2027 but must remain within the maximum roster size. So the players signed in 2027 can keep playing expansion clubs may loan them to clubs within or outside the NWSL. A new wrinkle for intra-league loans during 2027: the expansion clubs are permitted to retain up to 25% of the salary cap charge. Standard loan accounting rules and required roster compliance will resume in 2028.
