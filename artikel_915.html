Record-breaker Robertson speaks out after Scotland's bad loss to Switzerland

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Switzerland vs Scotland. UEFA Nations League Group B1.

Match report as Scotland lose to Switzerland in their final Nations League Group B1 match; Lewis Ferguson's own-goal put the hosts ahead; Winsley Boteli, Alvyn Sanches and Zeki Amdouni scored in the second half; Scotland will remain in League B for the next Nations League tournament

Football Journalist @AlisonGConroy

Andy Robertson created Scotland history, then delivered a scathing assessment of their Nations League collapse against Switzerland.

On the night he became the men's record appearance holder with 103 caps, Sebastien Pocognoli came under pressure with a bruising 4-0 defeat in St Gallen.

"That cannot happen in international football," he told BBC Scotland after the match.

"I don't think you've got time in international football, you have to do it. These supporters who travel everywhere, are they going to give us time? No. Are the people inside Hampden going to give us time? No. Are the people outside Hampden, who are going to be critical and rightly so? No.

"We don't have time, the players do not have time, we have got to step up.

"We have a long break now to March, which is not a good thing, and it is going to be a tough one to swallow."

The Scots were comfortable against the much-changed Group B1 winners until the 22nd minute when midfielder Lewis Ferguson put through his own goal.

The unforced error deflated the visitors, who lost wing-back Aaron Hickey to injury before the break, and there was more damage in the 47th minute when Samuel Ballet's drive was touched in by Winsley Boteli before Alvyn Sanches hammered in a third just four minutes later, with substitute Zeki Amdouni adding a late fourth.

The Scots finished third in the group behind Switzerland and Slovenia, with seven points from 18, missing out on a promotion play-off spot next year and a more favourable seeding for the European Championship qualifying draw next month.

The Belgian boss' first campaign as Steve Clarke's successor has been underwhelming to say the least with just two wins - both against bottom side North Macedonia - from six fixtures and Pocognoli now finds himself under the spotlight just months into his tenure.

Almost a year on from the elation of Scotland beating Denmark to reach the World Cup, it now looks like a struggle to reach Euro 2028.

Scotland will be in Pot 3 when the draw is made next month, and there is a lot of improvement needed before the qualifying campaign then kicks off in March.

In his first six games as Steve Clarke's successor, Sebastien Pocognoli has won just two. Both against North Macedonia.

They had a decent first 20 minutes in St Gallen, but this was not Switzerland's best team.

Murat Yakin was without Granit Xhaka, Dan Ndoye, Manuel Akanji and Johan Manzambi and they still made it look easy against the Scots who managed just one shot on target. A tame one.

How much time should the new Scotland head coach bet given? Following the man who led the nation to three major tournaments was always going to be a hard act to follow, but the team appears to have regressed.

Scotland head coach Sebastien Pocognoli speaking to BBC Scotland:

"The score is heavy with the 4-0, but I don't think it's a reflection of the game, if I'm really honest and objective.

"I think we had some very good moments in the game, we started the game like we wanted, aggressive away from home.

"I think if we were in confidence, we would not produce the moments we produce in the game.

"I think we create good moments on the ball, on pure bravery decisions to play forward through the lines or the pressure.

"We create chances also for pressing, sometimes we should have to manage better the transition from pressing, but it is all the things we want to see.

"So the next step is to have the quality to just finish this action, and the more we have games together, the more we will try to fix these things.

"We have a lot of good response from this camp and it's good for the future as well."

Scotland captain Andy Robertson speaking to BBC Scotland:

"First half we were in the game but lost a stupid goal. But I thought we had chances and should have made it one each at half-time.

"We talked about it at half-time, we told ourselves to keep going and the time you get to 50 minutes, you're 3-0 down? That cannot happen in international football.

"That's what happened tonight. We were playing against a good team. But while we were in the game in the first half, you have to do it for 90 minutes. Then you're 3-0 down, it's a tough place to come and that. A really tough night for us."
