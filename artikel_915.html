James Bowen latest jockey to reveal death threat incident

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bowen brothers were given security protection last year after a threat was made; fellow jockey Nico de Boinville revealed a similar incident earlier this week after threat made to Nicky Henderson's yard. Warning: Readers may find some of the language in this story distressing

James Bowen has become the latest jockey to reveal he has been subjected to death threats from disgruntled punters, with the 25-year-old disclosing that he and champion jockey brother Sean required security protection following an incident last year.

Nico de Boinville was in the news earlier this week when a threat against his life had been phoned in to Nicky Henderson's yard following the defeat of an odds-on favourite at Plumpton.

That was far from the first incident of its kind, and Bowen, speaking in his DragonSports blog while he recovers from a broken wrist, said both he and his brother regularly receive abuse on social media - but one incident in particular stood out.

He said: "Last season, at Ffos Las on October 16, 2025, we were both the subject of death threats during a race meeting, to the extent that we had security accompanying us between the paddock and weighing room for the next three or four days.

"It was something we kept largely to ourselves at the time, but recent events involving Nico de Boinville have brought the subject back into sharp focus.

"In our case, somebody made an anonymous telephone call, which I believe went to either the racecourse or the British Horseracing Authority, threatening to kill both me and Sean.

"It happened while we were both racing at Ffos Las. We were informed about it and, understandably, the authorities had to take it seriously.

"After our final race, we spoke to the police and explained everything we knew. There was security around us for the following few days, with people accompanying us to and from the paddock.

"Nothing much came of it afterwards, as far as I know, and we never heard anything further about who had made the call."

He went on: "You can perhaps understand why somebody might get frustrated if a horse they have backed gets beaten, but there is absolutely no justification for taking it to that sort of extreme.

"I hope the person who made the threat had no intention of carrying it out, but you cannot simply dismiss something like that.

"What is perhaps even more worrying is that receiving unpleasant messages has become such a regular occurrence that you almost become accustomed to it.

"I have had plenty of messages telling me they hope I break my legs or my neck. They tend to come through private messages on social media, particularly Instagram and X.

"It is not something that surprises me any more, which is probably quite sad when you think about it.

"You get hardened to a certain extent, and there is a bit of dark humour among jockeys about some of the things people send. Sometimes you have to laugh about it because otherwise you would cry."
