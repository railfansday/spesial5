A shock departure and key results - inside Wrexham's seismic week

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Wrexham sit 15th in the Championship table after 15 matches of the 2026-27 season

In the midst of a pivotal week for Wrexham, the club announced a shock shake-up within the boardroom.

Gone was chief executive officer Michael Williamson, back came director Shaun Harvey as acting CEO.

While change occurred off the pitch, Phil Parkinson steadied the ship on it.

Watching over were co-owners Ryan Reynolds and Rob Mac, who were in attendance for the 1-1 draw with Wolverhampton Wanderers, which came just shy of 24 hours after Williamson's exit.

Make no mistake, as Wrexham bid to achieve more success under their star owners, there is no doubt that recent changes at the Racecourse Ground have been immensely significant.

From afar, Williamson's exit came as a surprise.

Williamson was appointed to the role of CEO just 34 days after Wrexham sealed promotion - at the time their second under Parkinson - into League One by thumping Forest Green Rovers 6-0 in north Wales.

The American - who previously worked at clubs including Inter Milan, DC United and Miami FC - oversaw huge growth as Wrexham evolved during their rise.

Overall staff numbers increased from 255 to 313 in his first year at the club, while Wrexham's iconic Racecourse Ground is also expanding.

Williamson had been a key figure in the development of Wrexham's new Â£69m Kop Stand, which is due to be completed next year.

He oversaw what was Wrexham's third successive promotion into the Championship - where the Red Dragons earned a highest-ever finish of seventh in 2025-26.

In November 2025, Williamson was named Football Industry Leader of the Year at the inaugural Global Football Industry Awards.

The club lauded Williamson for his pivotal role in the creation of the new academy facility at Darland High School, along with the subsequent approval of their English Football League (EFL) academy category three license.

The finances have continued to be boosted too.

Wrexham are expected to report a turnover of between Â£46m and Â£50m for the year ending June 2026, a huge jump on the Â£33.3m club record posted for their 2024-25 League One season, Williamson's first in north Wales.

But there has been growing discontent behind-the-scenes of late.

Wrexham made a key alteration to their transfer set-up in the summer.

Williamson took a prominent role in the club's transfer operations as director Harvey stepped down from day-to-day operations in early July.

Earlier this season, former Newcastle United chief Steve Nickson was appointed to the newly-created role of sporting director, a move welcomed by boss Parkinson. That said, he did not begin the role until after the summer window.

The transfer market proved difficult, as evidenced by the fact Wrexham were the last of the 24 Championship clubs to make a signing in the summer.

Two of their seven senior recruits - Callum O'Hare and Joe Worrall - joined on deadline day.

Only Danny Imray and Ben Whiteman arrived prior to the season opener against Middlesbrough in the League Cup on 7 August.

They were recently the subject of a bombshell announcement from the EFL regarding the registrations of summer signings O'Hare and Issa Kabore.

The club was fined Â£75,000 - of which Â£25,000 was suspended - after the EFL ruled that Kabore and O'Hare were ineligible to feature against Birmingham City and Millwall respectively earlier this season.

It led to the embarrassing situation of Southampton - who Wrexham mocked in the aftermath of the Spygate scandal - poking fun at the Red Dragons when they announced their line-up for the Sheffield United fixture in late October.

It was a game Wrexham went on to lose 1-0 following a lacklustre display, which led to Parkinson raising concerns over the performance of his players.

In the aftermath of the debacle, it was made clear the club want to recruit a new secretary - a vacancy that has existed since current club historian Geraint Parry stepped down from the role a year ago.

But the issues weren't simply behind-the-scenes. There have also been some concerns that the club has shown signs of losing its connection with supporters.

Changes to ticket policies in addition to the Â£10 surcharge - which was later scrapped due to a backlash from fans - required for supporters to use the Fanzone behind the temporary Kop stand in Williamson's first season at the club left the Wrexham faithful angered.

The club were also criticised in the summer for advertising merchandise featuring a white dragon, typically associated with England as opposed to the red dragon that features on the national flag of Wales.

It was seen as further evidence that the club's strong bond with those who have followed Wrexham through thick and thin has waned.

One beloved figure connected to the Red Dragons even told BBC Sport Wales that the achievements of some of the key members involved in Wrexham's rise have not been sufficiently recognised by some at the club.

Parkinson 'pleased' with point against Wolves

In a 139-word statement issued by Mac and Reynolds announcing Williamson's exit, the Hollywood pair wrote: "We remain focused on our most important task: to make the Wrexham community proud."

The word community was twice used in the brief statement. Plenty have made their own interpretations of the words chosen.

The announcement undoubtedly came out of the blue, with many staff on the ground in Wrexham only being made aware of the news shortly before the club's official announcement.

Parkinson himself was only informed on Friday morning prior to his pre-Wolves press conference, held at the club's Colliers Park training ground in the afternoon.

It was perhaps telling that Williamson's usual notes to fans were included in the Wolves matchday programme, despite his exit from the club a day earlier.

Only three days earlier, ahead of an impressive 2-0 victory against Middlesbrough, Williamson had utilised his programme notes to address the fiasco of Wrexham's breaches of player eligibility rules, incidents that occurred prior to sporting director Nickson's arrival.

"We co-operated with the EFL, carried out an internal review and have already taken steps to strengthen our processes and help prevent a recurrence," he wrote.

"As Wrexham AFC grows, our operations and infrastructure must keep pace with our ambitions on the pitch.

"Growth brings responsibility. We are proud of the progress being made, but we must be open and accountable when we fall short of our own standards. That is how we learn and build a stronger club for the long term."

Wrexham have largely operated in an efficient but ruthless fashion since being taken over by Reynolds and Mac in February 2021.

Icons including Paul Mullin, Elliot Lee, Andy Cannon, James McClean, Steven Fletcher and Ollie Palmer have all been moved on during the club's surge up the football pyramid.

Parkinson himself has been the recipient of some criticism during various stages of his five-and-a-half year tenure as boss.

The experienced manager rewarded the faith shown in him this week as his side collected four points in fixtures against two sides in Wolves and Boro that will likely be automatic promotion contenders come the latter stages of the campaign.

Williamson has not been as fortunate, and some of the reaction to his departure spoke volumes.

Former players Mullin, Lee, Cannon and Anthony Forde, along with Humphrey Ker - who recently stepped down as a director after five years with the club - were among those who liked a BBC Sport Wales post confirming Williamson's exit.

When one fan commented that those likes "says it all", former England midfielder Rob Lee - father of ex-Wrexham midfielder Elliot Lee - responded: "Yes it does."

Wrexham's acting CEO Shaun Harvey (right) is a long-time advisor to co-owners Ryan Reynolds and Rob Mac

In Harvey, Wrexham feel they have someone who is more than just a safe pair of hands.

The 56-year-old has extensive knowledge of both the Welsh club and the English football structure following previous roles with Leeds United and Bradford City. He was also chief executive of the EFL between 2013 and 2019.

It is a huge part of the reason he has acted as an advisor to Reynolds and Mac ever since the duo became involved in the club.

Following his side's draw with Wolves, Parkinson said: "I had a brief chat with Shaun Harvey this morning.

"Shaun is back in a more official capacity, but he's always been around, he's been here since day one.

"He's an important figure. He understands everything about the club because he was one of the key people in starting off this journey with the owners."

There is clearly a strong bond between those at Wrexham and Harvey.

In the second minute of the contest with Wolves, Harvey headed to his seat in the Racecourse Ground's Macron Stand, known as the Mold Road Stand - the same stand in which Reynolds and Mac are situated for home games - and was roundly applauded by home fans.

The hope is that supporters will be continue to be applauding over the remaining months of the campaign.

Mac and Reynolds themselves stated Harvey has been an "integral part" of the club's journey.

Harvey could potentially land the role on a permanent basis, although an appointment would require approval from the Independent Football Regulator.

Nevertheless, it proved to be a seismic week for Wrexham.

The two-week international window will give those at the club a chance to take stock.

But the Championship grind will soon kick back in as Wrexham aim to build on their encouraging week on the field and continue their quest to clinch a play-off spot in just shy of six months' time.

"There's togetherness in the stands and with the boys on the pitch - but more than anything, there's just something about this place," the Deadpool star wrote on Instagram after the Wolves draw.

"Some of the best days of my life have been spent in this stadium, standing next to Rob Mac. Today was one of them. Up the Town."
