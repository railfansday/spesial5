Xabi Alonso will hope his Chelsea side can finally find some consistency now the final international break of 2026 is over.

The Blues are fifth in the Premier League, but a record of five wins and four defeats — with 19 goals scored but also 16 conceded — paints a picture of a team who are yet to reach their potential.

Chelsea's Carabao Cup clash with Leeds in September was arguably the best illustration of their volatility, as they went 2-0 down before scoring six times in the second half to eventually triumph 6-3. It was one of five victories in their opening seven home games in all competitions this season.

Joao Pedro, Maxence Lacroix, Jorrel Hato, Reece James and Jamie Gittens remain sidelined through injury, but Josh Acheampong, Moises Caicedo and Emi Martinez are all available.

Leeds earned a crucial 1-0 victory over Tottenham Hotspur a fortnight ago to end their five-game winless run and ensure they entered the international break in the top half of the table.

They will now aim to follow that up with the club's first win at Stamford Bridge since 1999, when Stephen McPhail's second-half double secured a 2-0 triumph.

Goalkeeper James Trafford will surely be full of confidence after his excellent performance in England's thrilling 3-2 victory over Spain in Madrid last Sunday, while defender Joe Rodon got valuable minutes for Wales following his comeback from injury.

Winger Noah Okafor could also feature after coming off the bench to score the winning goal against Spurs, although he has been managing an adductor issue.

Games involving Leeds have generally been low-scoring this season, so expect this to be another tight contest.
