Lil’ Kim Takes The Spotlight—With A Symphony

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

When you hear the name Lil’ Kim, a variety of images come to mind. She’s forever known as the force of nature from Brooklyn who turned the music industry on its head when she entered the scene, and her bodacious lyrics and unique fashion sense have kept her as a source of inspiration on your favorite designer’s mood board ever since. On November 18 and 19, to celebrate the music that cemented her icon status, Lil’ Kim will perform in front of a symphony orchestra at the Kings Theatre in Brooklyn, not far from her humble beginnings.

Today she’s less interested in being in the spotlight and more focused on raising her 12-year-old daughter, Royal Reign. In between practices for her performances, Lil’ Kim spoke with Vanity Fair to reflect on her career, how she spends her days, and her standout looks.

This interview has been edited and condensed for clarity.

Vanity Fair: What do you remember about putting your debut album, Hard Core, together?

Lil’ Kim: I remember Biggie and Un [Lance Rivera], who was the label owner that we had. I watched Un build a record label called Undeas, which [stood] for “Un ideas,” and then he finally sold it to Atlantic Records. At the time, we didn’t know the process, but we became Undeas-Atlantic. I remember Biggie having the idea of how he wanted my album to be red, and he explained it to me and Un. I was able to write according to how his idea was to present me, because when they met me, I was who I was—in high heels, jeans, motorcycle jackets, and furs at 13.

Portrait of members of R&B group 112 and rappers Notorious BIG and Lil’ Kim circa 1996

That’s incredible. It sounds like your fashion-icon status started very early. Was there anyone that you looked to back then? What inspired you to dress that way?

My mom was a big inspiration to me when it came to fashion, and so I looked to her for a lot of things. I used to wear her mink coat. She used to wear big sunglasses with her fur coats, with the diamonds and jewelry. She was always taking vacations and I would see her pack her stuff, and she would always have the cutest outfits. They were all matchy-matchy. It’s funny because she would have her outfit to go play tennis in and her outfit would match her tennis racket. So when I bought my big house, I bought a Chanel tennis outfit and I bought the racket to match it, because of my mom.

I love that. Do you keep up with tennis or specifically the ladies in tennis?

No, I haven’t in a while, but, of course, you know, Serena and Venus were like my heroes.

Well, maybe we need to see you on the court one day.

I know. I’m pretty sure I’m rusty, but I like to get out there, though. I used to beat my cousin’s butt back then.

How did the symphony performances come about?

My agents called me and they’re like, “Kim, what do you think?” And I was like, “That’s a freaking awesome idea.” I love performing with bands. And so I imagine that it could be very similar, if not very close. And I thought this could be something really different for me. Sometimes I even listen to classical music. I don’t have to know who the artist is, but I just put on a station and let it flow.

Do you have a favorite song from Hard Core?

I love “Queen Bitch”; it’s my favorite song. It’s still my favorite to perform. And “Crush on You.”

Salma Hayek, Donatella Versace, Pamela Anderson, and Lil’ Kim at Versace’s spring/summer 2001 fashion show

When it comes to fashion, you’re obviously on everyone’s mood board, from stylists to designers, so how important was fashion for you as part of the Lil’ Kim persona?

I just was being myself, honestly. When I first met Biggie, I was coming from my after-school job at Bloomingdale’s. I was probably 13 or 14 at the time, and they would let the teenagers work there.

But I had on a motorcycle jacket and high heels. And one of my best friends told Biggie, “You know, my girl can rap,” because we knew he was rapping around the way. And he was like, “I heard you can rap.” And I just laughed. He was like, “Yeah, see, I knew you didn’t know how to rap.” He was very condescending. He knew how to get you to do what you wanted to do, because I was really shy.

And then I started rapping, and he was blown away because I had on high heels. No one had ever seen someone just bust a rap in high heels. When Biggie, Un, and Jacob York started really talking to me, they didn’t want to change me because that was just what they were so blown away about. That was just who I was.

Do you think people ever misunderstood the way you presented yourself?

To be honest, I never really felt misunderstood by my hardcore fans—the kids my age and the younger kids, they totally got it. I think that’s why I had such a cult following and there was such pandemonium about me when I came out. They understood me. I think it was the older people and the politicians, those are the people who I think misunderstood me.

What did you think of the midterm elections and all of the women who broke barriers in the Senate and gubernatorial offices?

I’m going to be honest with you, I stay away from politics.

Are you keeping up with all of the changes within fashion today? When I listen to your music, you’ve mentioned so many designers and houses, and since then the creative directors have changed. Are you following all of the changes within fashion and seeing how you’re still a part of the conversation?

That’s what I’m trying to say. As a fashion icon, if you really her, and I’m considering myself really her, that’s never gonna change. That’s never going to change. I might put on a fur coat that my stylist or myself will get asked about, because I style myself a lot. And I was styling myself then.

Lil’ Kim and daughter, Royal Reign, on June 16, 2016, in New York City

Extremely rewarding. My daughter’s with me all the time. This world is so cruel and so evil; it’s so different. She’s my safe haven. I feel the extra need to keep her safe.

What does a regular day look like for you?

Lately, I’ve just been really getting into myself. I get up and go have a little spa day. I might take my daughter to go do whatever she wants.

Singer Mary J. Blige with Diana Ross and Lil’ Kim at the 1999 MTV Video Music Awards on September 9, 1999

How do you feel that we’re still talking about your MTV VMAs look in 1999?

I think it’s iconic and I love it. I love when they bring that up, because it was a fun day and it was so good to see people’s faces when they saw my outfit.

Are there any other looks that don’t get as much love as you wish that they did?

I don’t know, because most of the looks that I love, they get the love. There’s a look when I was on the cover of Nylon magazine and I had the teddy bear dress on; that’s one of my favorite looks. The Louis Vuitton look is one of my favorite looks; I love that look. A lot of the Chanel looks and the Versace and the Chanel in the hair.
