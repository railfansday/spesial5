'Ocarina of Time' for Switch 2 Might Already Have a Weird Glitch

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Thursday is a big day for Zelda fans. That's when the remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time officially launches on Switch 2, marking the first major linear (i.e., non-open-world) 3D Zelda game since the 2016 Twilight Princess remake on Wii U. I, for one, have been waiting for this day for years. I loved the N64 original and the 3DS remake, so to have the chance to play a modern take on the definitive Zelda is so exciting—that is, if it actually runs as it should.

I stumbled upon this interesting post on X from Austin John Plays, a YouTuber with a host of Zelda coverage. According to Austin John Plays, Ocarina of Time already ships with a weird glitch that won't necessarily break your experience, but which may slow it down for a while, and perhaps even confuse players into thinking something is wrong with their game or their console.

As his post explains, if you're playing the physical copy of Ocarina of Time, and it's the first game you've ever played on your Switch 2—maybe you got one of those fancy new Zelda anniversary consoles and want to break it in properly?—you may run into glaring performance issues: The game may freeze, your controller may stop working, and the FPS may drop to single digits.

Luckily, Austin John Plays says the fix is easy: just wait. Eventually, your game will boot back to the title screen. When it does, don't try to play right away. Instead, close the game, restart your Switch 2, then try relaunching Ocarina of Time. Austin John Plays advises you may need to repeat this process two or three times, but after that, you shouldn't continue to run into issues. I wouldn't be surprised if Nintendo releases a patch right away for this glitch if it turns out to be as widespread as this post implies.

However, seeing as the game doesn't come out until tomorrow, there's something you can do to prevent it. Austin John Plays thinks the bug only impacts Switch 2 consoles that haven't run other games before. If you've had your console a while, you're likely set. But if you just bought the 40th anniversary Zelda edition of the Switch 2, you might not have had a chance to run a game on it yet. As such, download a game from the e-shop—even a free demo—just to get the system running. Hopefully, that will be enough to circumvent the glitch, and prevent issues when Ocarina of Time launches tomorrow.
