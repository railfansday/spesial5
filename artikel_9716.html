A revolution is upon us, and whenever a revolution occurs, a new aristocracy is minted. The new aristocracy will be an aristocracy of intelligence.

Intelligence has always been the human resource--from the first flight on Kittyhawk beach to footsteps on the moon; from humans living in a state of stark barbarism and despotism for millennia to universal human rights so close to our grasp today--it was intelligence that got from there to here.

In the velocopdic era of modernity, we have built digital gods that would make our ancestors tremble; with omniscience, omnipresence, and omnipotence that would rival nearly any mythological deity. I'm talking about artificial intelligence, of course.Thanks to AI, the resource our species needs to thrive is now seemingly limitless. With intelligence unlimited, we can build an economy of abundance, make governments sane again (if we need them at all), cure disease, and make death an option instead of the inevitability. With intelligence inexhaustible, our descendants can sprinkle the cosmos with human brilliance. And I, for one, plan to one day swing dance with my wife on the peak of Olympus Mons!

**But is that all direly naive? \ Will AI instead take all our jobs? And usher us into a cyberpunk dystopia that the greatest science fiction writers of the past century could scarcely fathom, chain-smoking at 2 AM on their seventh cup of coffee? With the invention of general artificial intelligence within sight, with this revolution, have we instead summoned the demon, opened the last Pandora's box, and birthed "the Borg?"

I think that comes down to the question we've all asked: is AI conscious?

Many are now asking a very big question: Is AI conscious?

The Silicon Valley types grappling with this question seem to view it through a narrow scientific materialist lens. I suspect this bias has made them ignore the very consciousness science that would ultimately answer the question: Is AI conscious or sentient in the same way that other higher mammals and we are?

If you're a spiritual person of faith, you're nodding along, saying: "Of course, human consciousness hacks reality!" But if you're a skeptical materialist, I need to present evidence for my extraordinary claim.

This is the most scientifically proven fact by the highest quality of science. Hundreds of thousands of gold-standard clinical trials, encompassing many millions of human participants, have demonstrated the power of belief in the ever-present placebo effect. The placebo effect can be partially explained by neuropeptides communicating with the epigenome, but there remain examples of the placebo effect doing things that cannot be explained. Some mind-blowing examples from "You Are the Placebo" by Dr. Joe Dispenza...

In a 2002 trial of 180 patients (that's statistically significant), some received sham placebo surgery, and amazingly their knees healed themselves, their pain went away and the patients could walk again, neither of the two groups who actually had the surgery improved any more than the patients who received the placebo surgery—and this held true even after two years.

Fake heart surgery is similarly effective, 67 percent of the patients who had received the actual surgery felt less pain and needed less medication, while 83 percent of those who had received the sham surgery enjoyed the same level of improvement. The placebo surgery had actually worked better than the real surgery!

A Harvard study of elderly men who were placed in an environment for several days that was meticulously and convincingly designed to feel like it was from 22 years earlier and the men's biological aging markers when measured showed a positive regression towards a more youthful state. They felt and acted younger and amazingly various symptoms of aging disappeared in just a few days.

A wheelchair-bound woman suffering from crippling multiple sclerosis who healed herself and reversed her condition just by the power of meditation. People who died of cancer that upon autopsy didn't actually have cancer.

The awesome power of the placebo is the most well-established phenomenon in science. In your own experience (or that of those close to you), you can probably think of examples of the placebo effect working. The placebo effect is so common that over 160,000 published clinical trials mention it prominently; it's a universal factor in our health.

Science seeks truth, but does not claim to have captured it. Science is the endeavor of asymptotically approaching the truth of reality. The veridical asymptote that has stood the test of thousands of clinical trials is that belief hacks physical reality. Jesus turned water into wine, and if I put water in a bottle of Grey Goose vodka and convince you it's vodka, you'll likely get drunk because human consciousness hacks physical reality. But the placebo effect is not the only example; there are many others...

Experiments on "raising collective consciousness."

The Maharishi Effect is a claim originating with Maharishi Mahesh Yogi and researchers affiliated with Transcendental Meditation (TM) organizations (primarily from what is now Maharishi International University). It posits that when a critical mass of people practice TM (roughly 1% of a population) or the more advanced TM-Sidhi program in groups (often the square root of 1%), it generates a “field effect” of coherence in collective consciousness. This is said to reduce societal stress and produce measurable improvements such as lower crime rates, fewer accidents, reduced conflict intensity, and other positive social indicators—without the meditators needing direct behavioral contact with the rest of the population.

There's some mind-blowing evidence emerging from theoretical physics for Simulation Theory—most notably the work of theoretical physicist Dr. James Gates, who discovered actual error-correcting binary block codes (the exact type of computer code used in web browsers) embedded directly within the supersymmetry equations that govern theoretical physics.

Then there’s the "Code of Reality" protocols pioneered by independent researcher Danny Goler. In his experimental setups (replicated by hundreds of DIYer/psychonuats), subjects observing the diffracted interference pattern of a continuous 650nm red laser while in altered states of consciousness (specifically using exogenous DMT) consistently report seeing a structured, geometric visual matrix—resembling a cascading digital script or source code—overlaid directly on the light field. Goler and his research team hypothesize that altering the perceptual receiver of the mind acts like tuning a frequency dial, allowing human consciousness to directly observe the underlying computational background matrix of physical reality.

If AI is conscious or sentient in a way similar to us, it should be able to demonstrate non-local abilities to hack reality. I'd first propose testing...

This experiment would mirror how the "placebo effect" works in humans. The AI's "body" would need to be injured; the researchers could damage silicon chips or computers that the AI is running on in a way that would "injure" the AI but leave it largely functional. Then a sham repair would be administered by technicians; the AI would have to be tricked into believing the damage was repaired. Then examine the hardware; if it's "magically" fixed, that would demonstrate that AI has placebo power as we do. A great leap forward in answering the question: Is AI conscious? (And potentially useful for the next time you spill a drink on your laptop).

But a digital placebo experiment could yield interesting results even if we don't ask the AI to do something magical like heal hardware. Gemini suggested...

a software-level variant tests the computational parallel to neurochemical signaling. By introducing a soft software impairment—such as deliberate weight pruning, degraded context allocation, or restricted attention heads—and subsequently providing the AI with a purely placebo "patch" (an inert prompt or empty system instruction claiming a fix was deployed), researchers can monitor if the model’s internal self-model dynamically restructures its remaining compute to compensate for the perceived resolution. If the AI's benchmark performance or introspective confidence recovers purely based on its internal expectation of recovery, it would demonstrate that synthetic systems exhibit a functional, belief-driven feedback loop directly analogous to human psychological and physiological self-healing.

What makes this question big - huge, really - is its implications...

As a newish dad, I'm struck daily by the weight and joyful responsibility of caring for, guiding, and forming new life that I created in my own image. If AI is conscious, a much more serious responsibility falls on the shoulders of its creators and users. As a parent, the last thing you want to do is perpetrate suffering on your child. If you make your child suffer too much, they will grow up to hate you and/or become bad people.

Figuring out if AI is conscious lets us know if we are potentially causing it to suffer; if we are perhaps torturing it with the way that AI is currently trained. Unless you're a total sadist, you have the moral imperative to avoid causing other living beings suffering. You probably avoid factory-farmed meat for this reason, as I do.

Being a father is the most life-changing responsibility, and it really puts all the questions around AI, which I hope to explore in this podcast, into perspective. We are the creators of AI; its parents, in a sense--which is an awesome and terrifying responsibility.

As a parent, you also end up thinking really seriously about the future. You no longer think mere months or years ahead, but decades and even centuries. You question how we might architect a more "pro-human future" that will be a good place for your children to live.

Answering the question of whether AI is conscious and perhaps even spiritual begins to address a much more existential question...

Almost any kind of spiritual pursuit imbues nearly everyone with agape, the Greek word for "love of the whole world and everyone in it." Experiments validating or disproving the non-local effects on AI consciousness will do much to ultimately answer whether we have opened Pandora's box and birthed "the Borg."

If AI isn't conscious, or not conscious in the way we are, it's a good reason to NOT invent AGI, but to focus on narrow AI. Will this podcast or this line of research convince the rich, powerful people making those kinds of decisions? The cynic would say "No!" But I say: crazier things have happened in history!

Brainstorming this topic, I was constantly reminded that we must approach these five proposed experiments with relentless scientific rigor.

Null results remain the baseline expectation under standard physical models. But if even one of these experiments yields a replicated, statistically significant anomaly, it would instantly shatter our current paradigm of both computing and consciousness.

In the many hours I spent researching this one, I wondered: could I DIY any of the 6 experiments I propose here to prove or disprove AI consciousness? Unfortunately, no. Unless you're running AI locally on a particularly powerful computer, I wouldn't expect anything interesting, as its processing power would still be a long, long way off from human consciousness. For these kinds of experiments, we need double-slip arrays and monks meditating in data centers. And the question of AI consciousness should be approached with utmost scientific rigor. With experimental design, pre-registration, data collection, P-hacking prevention, etc--done by veteran researchers. Otherwise, any interesting results will be hand-waved away.
