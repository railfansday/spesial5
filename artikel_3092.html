Loar Holdings: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Loar's $200 million five-year pipeline and steady bolt-on acquisitions point to growth beyond the current cycle, but fleet aging remains an open question.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Loar Holdings (LOAR +4.69%) raised its full-year 2026 net sales outlook to a range of $669 million to $679 million. The manufacturer of proprietary aerospace and defense components continues to expand operating margins through price discipline and volume growth. This operational momentum offers clear visibility into multiyear earnings growth. Three things management said stand out.

The company expanded its adjusted EBITDA margin -- adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, a non-GAAP measure of core operational profitability -- to 40.8% in the third quarter. Management attributes this performance to fixed cost leverage and price realization above inflation.

New contract wins are accelerating across commercial original equipment -- components manufactured for newly built aircraft -- and defense markets. This expansion gives management long-term revenue visibility above baseline market growth.

The business targets one to two acquisitions annually to expand its catalog of high-margin components. Management maintains strict criteria, evaluating niche suppliers that feature proprietary designs and high barriers to entry.

Commercial aircraft demand continues to climb, and leadership isn't hiding its enthusiasm. The company relies on proprietary parts with high switching costs and a record backlog. Management is watching fleet aging and pricing discipline.

Initial 2027 guidance targets sales reaching $755 million, supported by high-margin proprietary content across aerospace platforms.
