VSCO might be popular for iPhone photography, but its Studio Pro has rival Adobe firmly in its sights.

Editor at Large; Lead Photographer, Europe

VSCO is best known these days for its iPhone and Android editing app that makes pics look all cool and analog. But the company is launching its first stand-alone desktop photo editor for Mac, called VSCO Studio Pro, bringing its famous filmic presets—and a wealth of editing tools. I’ve used it, and while it’s not yet my sole image-editing solution, it’s great for getting those dreamy vintage vibes out of your photos.

The desktop app will be immediately familiar to any photographer who’s spent any time in Lightroom or Capture One. There are exposure, contrast, highlight, and shadow sliders along with color sliders for white balance or individual color adjustments. It’s par for the course for any raw image editor.

VSCO’s wide range of color presets has always been where it shines the brightest. Numbering just north of 200, Studio Pro packs in all the presets, from color classics like the company’s A1-10 range to a wealth of black-and-white options and a host of presets designed to emulate real film stocks like Kodak Gold or Fuji Velvia. VSCO says these are modeled on real scans of these film stocks to make them as authentic as possible.

It’s the presets that I love the most in VSCO’s app. Years ago, the company sold its presets as a downloadable Lightroom preset pack, which I have used in my photography workflow almost daily for the past 13 years, and their look is identical here. While it hasn’t sold the Lightroom preset pack in about a decade, VSCO’s Studio Pro app finally lets photographers achieve those same looks in desktop editing.

With Studio Pro, VSCO clearly has its sights on Lightroom. While Adobe is increasingly filling its services with various generative AI tools that radically alter your image’s content, VSCO is taking a more stripped-back approach that focuses on the creative side of color and exposure adjustments.

It’s still quite bare-bones at launch, with several features I need for my own photography missing, like masking. I can’t make selective edits to my images—like using a gradient to tone down the highlights only in the sky. Apparently this is coming soon, as are bloom and halation tools, which are already in the app, but grayed out with a “soon” marker next to them. Ditto the spot removal tool for cleaning up dust or sensor spots.

The big thing for me, though, comes down to cataloging and archiving of my images. I have several terabytes of images cataloged in my Lightroom library, available to edit on any device I sign in on — including phone, tablet or desktop. While VSCO will have some basic organization options within the Studio Pro app (grouping images into “Projects”—again, coming soon), it’s not as full-featured as Lightroom’s archiving system. I worry that once I’m working with thousands of images across multiple photoshoots, things will quickly become cluttered and difficult to navigate. Time will tell.

I don’t imagine VSCO’s app will necessarily be the first choice for working pros who need strict cataloging, backups and remote access. I count myself in that category, and for me Lightroom’s cloud storage remains the biggest reason to stay with Adobe, while others swear by Capture One for its Sessions-based filing system and its tethered shooting workflow.

By contrast, I see VSCO Studio Pro appealing more to enthusiast photographers who want to efficiently add real creative flair to their images and don’t necessarily want to spend on the commercial tools, cloud storage, or AI options offered by Adobe.
