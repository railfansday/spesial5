Not SpaceX. Not Blue Origin. The Space Stock That Just Raised Its Launch Price to $70 Million -- and Still Sold Out.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Rocket Lab raised its prices on a rocket that hasn’t even flown, and customers are still lining up.

Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -0.59%), better known as SpaceX, dominates the headlines. Amazon (AMZN -0.29%) founder Jeff Bezos' Blue Origin is chasing its shadow. But a third launch provider just quietly demonstrated something neither of them has managed for a rocket that hasn't even flown yet: genuine, documented pricing power.

Rocket Lab (RKLB -3.58%) announced a record quarter of Neutron launch contracts -- eight missions across five constellation operators totaling more than $515 million, including several missions sold at an elevated base price of $70 million per launch, up from $55 million. That price increase arrived before Neutron's first flight. And it was driven entirely by demand.

The $70 million price point isn't a marketing claim. It's a contract price already reflected in a signed backlog. Rocket Lab even deliberately left money on the table to protect it.

"We could have actually booked more launches in Q3 for Neutron than we did, but we actually chose to go for higher ASP and lower volume," CFO Adam Spice said. A company that turns away customers to defend its pricing isn't struggling to compete. It is managing scarcity -- and scarcity, in the launch business, is the rarest competitive advantage of all.

Spice clarified on the earnings call that Neutron pricing isn't going to $70 million -- it's already at $70 million, with contracts already booked into backlog at that price. Prices will logically rise from that new baseline. New buyers looking for Neutron capacity are now eyeing availability in the 2030s.

Neutron hasn't flown yet. However, Rocket Lab's operational credibility isn’t theoretical. On Nov. 14, 2026, the company completed its 100th Electron launch -- a milestone reached faster than any other commercially developed rocket. Customers aren't betting on potential. They're contracting with a proven operator.

The financial results reflect that credibility. Rocket Lab delivered record revenue of $267.8 million in Q3, up 73% year-over-year -- the sixth consecutive record quarter -- while backlog reached a record $3.57 billion, up 52% sequentially. Total contracts signed in Q3 reached approximately $1 billion, lifting the launch backlog to 117 missions.

Neutron's debut has been pushed back to 2027. A first-flight failure would damage the commercial backlog and the stock regardless of how compelling the pre-launch order book looks. That risk is real and deserves position sizing that reflects it.

But the investment case doesn't rest on Neutron alone. Rocket Lab is producing one Electron rocket every eight workdays, the backlog is at a record $3.57 billion, and the next available Neutron slots fall in the 2030s.

When Neutron does fly, the $70 million price already locked into those contracts will tell the market exactly what the milestone is worth. Investors who waited for the first flight to buy this space stock could be left in the stardust.
