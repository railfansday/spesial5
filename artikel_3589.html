Things are moving fast. Now that an illicit Xbox build of GTA 6 is out in the wild, anonymous modders are racing to port it to PC, and the work is progressing faster than many expected. At this rate, it's possible that a static recompilation of GTA 6 will run on a PC in a matter of days. It's also possible it will take much longer to produce a playable build that's actually on par with the console versions.

What we do know for sure is that Rockstar is taking notice and has already begun cracking down on the highest-profile projects. As always, downloading pirated software is illegal and can infect your PC with malware, and that last bit is doubly true in the dubious AI era we now live in.

For now, the only thing to do is observe and report. Here's where things stand with GTA 6 PC ports.

Several projects have made it as far as booting the game and using menus, and at least one has seemingly managed to move around in-game with bugged out lighting, but it's hard to verify clips making the rounds when so many are obvious fakes.

Dozens of groups of anonymous modders and vibecoders. So many, in fact, that several websites dedicated to tracking the progress of GTA 6 recomp efforts have also appeared. One of the tracker sites, Portwatch VI, has a list of projects ranked by their "playable" completion percentage—best not to take those made up figures as an accurate estimate of when a port will be complete. Every GTA 6 recomp project is based on the same thing: An illicit Xbox build of GTA 6 released online by Cyberleek, the notorious leaker who previously shared video of a GTA 6 development build months before its release.

With GTA 6 being the biggest game in the world at the moment and not available on PC, there is an outsized motivation to get it running.

A recomp refers to static recompilation, which is the result of reverse-engineering a game's code to make it run on other hardware (usually a PC). If you want to read further on how that's achieved, PCG alum Wes Fenlon's excellent Read Only Memo newsletter has what you're looking for.

As for how GTA 6 ports (and ports for other new games) are coming together so quickly, the answer is AI. The advent of AI coding tools like Claude have greatly expedited the work required to create a static recompilation of games on another platform. What used to take months to years now takes hours to days. Recomps that heavily use AI are often buggier, messier, and have fewer features than human-made fan projects, but they are fast and functional.

It's way too early to speak in absolutes, but I'm comfortable assuming that the first "playable" recomps will require an exceptionally powerful PC.

That guess is based on GTA 6 being an obviously demanding game and also some recent precedent: The earliest Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake recomps that've appeared online have high system requirements to perform as well as it does natively on Switch 2—and, well, GTA 6 has a lot more happening under the hood than a 28-year-old Zelda game.

That's the big question. My money is on no, it won't. To switch up plans on a dime would assume that Rockstar is sitting on a polished PC version that it could put out any day, and I'm not convinced that's the case. The current rumor, preceding all of this leak business, is that the PC version of GTA 6 is planned for Q1 2027 (so anytime before April).

That's not to say Rockstar couldn't expedite the process if it wanted to—they certainly have the resources to achieve the unlikely.
