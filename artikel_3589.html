Lady Eliza and Lady Amelia Spencer Support Cousin Prince William In Style

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Lady Eliza and Lady Amelia Spencer stepped out in style at the 2026 Tusk Conservation Awards on Wednesday. The annual awards ceremony recognizes and celebrates the leaders, researchers, and rangers working on the front lines to protect wildlife and ecosystems in Africa.

It’s a cause close to the sisters’ hearts, as they have spent much of their lives in South Africa. It is no surprise, then, that they attended the London event and showed their support for their cousin, Prince William, who has supported the charity for many years.

For the occasion, the twin sisters pulled two of the most dazzling dresses from their collection and, in true sisterly fashion, opted for the same designer: Oscar de la Renta.

Lady Eliza Spencer chose a strapless silver-toned midi dress with cutout details designed to create a sophisticated play of see-through effects. The shimmering texture makes it the perfect dress for the holidays, while the floral pattern, with its sculpted leaves, lends the whole look a romantic flair. In short, a harmonious blend of inspirations.

To amplify the outfit’s radiance, a handbag studded with cubic crystals—a true crystal box also by Oscar de la Renta—and a pair of elegant nude-toned, satin-finish pumps by Roger Vivier. The precious touch? A pair of bling-bling comet-shaped earrings to illuminate the face.

Her sister Amelia, by contrast, went all out in gold. She wore a refined ankle-length dress studded with sequins and fringes at the hem, giving the whole look a 1920s flair. The elegant, sophisticated look was completed with a gold clutch and a pair of pumps, also by Roger Vivier, featuring a precious buckle.

For jewelry, Amelia opted for a pair of diamond hoop earrings to sparkle in the light.

Charles Spencer’s daughters were not the only cousins supporting Prince William on this prestigious evening. Zara Tindall—daughter of Princess Anne—also attended. For the occasion, she wore a midnight-blue dress with short puff sleeves by Rebecca Wallace, featuring an exquisite collar and equally striking buttons, as if they were brooches borrowed from the royal jewelry box.
