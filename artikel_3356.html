Nicolas Cage Officially Confirms Why He’s Happy His Marvel Series Was Cancelled

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Nicolas Cage had already played a Spider-Man who sounded like he’d walked out of an old detective movie in the genre-defining Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse. Giving that idea eight hours of live action was a different proposition. In Spider-Noir, Cage plays Ben Reilly as a down-on-his-luck private investigator in Depression-era New York, with a past he’d rather leave alone. Prime Video even let viewers choose between color and black-and-white versions. The actor had plenty of room to see how far he could take the performance, and it turns out eight episodes was exactly as far as he wanted to go.

Prime Video canceled the series in September, a little over three months after it released. Spider-Noir drew 2.6 billion U.S. viewing minutes in its first six weeks, according to Nielsen, and earned 11 Emmy nominations. There were reasons for fans to expect another season. Cage, who’d already called one season enough in a statement after the cancellation, went a little further when he was asked about it on the Madden red carpet.

Cage told IGN in a recent interview that he was relieved Prime Video passed on Spider-Noir Season 2 because he worried that repeating his performance as Ben Reilly would wear out its welcome. He described the role as “pretty high-risk, tightrope stuff” and said the choices he made could lose their appeal over a second or third season. His reaction to the cancellation was simple: “Whew, thank you!”

Four powers, and four completely different kinds of person behind them. Here is what you would wake up with, what it would cost you, and why the MCU keeps handing it to somebody exactly like you.

You get the power that is really about taking the hit so that nobody else has to. Strength in the MCU always lands on people who were already carrying too much, which is exactly why it suits you.

You get the power that never switches off. Spider-sense is a gift and a nervous system on permanent alert, and the people who get it in these films are always the ones who were already worrying about everybody.

You get the quietest power in the building, and quietly the most useful. Invisibility comes with force fields for a reason, because the people who prefer not to be seen are usually the ones holding a shield around everybody else.

You get the biggest and least comfortable power on the list, the one that runs directly on feeling. Chaos magic is not really a superpower in the MCU so much as an emotional state with consequences attached.

Cage brought up True Detective in the same answer. He’s set to star alongside Antonio Banderas in the HBO anthology’s fifth season, where a single season is the deal going in. That gives him another detective to play and a defined stretch of time to figure out how he wants to play him. It also gives some context to what he meant by calling Spider-Noir a “perfect season.” He got to make the version of Ben Reilly he wanted to make, and he likes where he left him.

Cage has separately said he refused to sign an AI waiver for Spider-Noir and doubts he’ll work with Amazon again. In the IGN interview, his explanation for welcoming the end was about the performance itself. Plus, there’s something fitting about Cage getting a whole season to play a man who’s reluctant to put the suit back on and then deciding he’s happy to leave it hanging.

Season 1, which will be the only season Prime Video releases of Spider-Noir, is available to stream.
