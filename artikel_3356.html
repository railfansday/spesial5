How Sauron’s Bold Rings Of Power Decision Sets Up Season 4 Explained By Creators

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Warning: There are spoilers ahead for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3, episode 8, "The Doom of Men."Sauron (Charlie Vickers) has a bold new plan heading into The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 4.

Instead of going into battle against Ar-Pharazôn (Trystan Gravelle) and his Númenórean army in The Rings of Power season 3 finale, Sauron surrenders and bends the knee. After successfully forging the One Ring in the previous episode and nearly being on the cusp of dominating Middle-earth, the move comes as a surprise to Pharazôn and the Elves, with the former believing he has now defeated the enemy.

In an interview with TV Insider, The Rings of Power creators and showrunners Patrick McKay and J.D. Payne explain that Sauron seemingly surrenders because defeating Pharazôn and his army would have been a hard-won, short-term victory, and he is more interested in figuring out how to win the long game. They tease that Sauron is already thinking about how to manipulate Pharazôn and his empire, which would be a more effective way to win the war. Check out McKay and Payne's comments below:

Patrick McKay: He calculates that even with, he has a force of Orcs, he has the One Ring, he’s Sauron, but maybe he’d be looking at a very bloody Pyrrhic victory or maybe a stalemate and it would be a short-term thing that would potentially leave him weakened. Whereas he calculates that maybe I can’t win this battle, but I can win the war. I’m going to play a longer game.

What’s great about Sauron, and writing about that character, is he has so many moves. He’s such a great improviser that when he’s on his back foot, he’s sometimes the most dangerous because he’s going to wait you out. He’s going to be patient, and he’s going to find a way to… Maybe he can’t beat you in this moment, but he’s going to destroy you forever. And I think those are the kind of emotions that he’s working his way through in that last 15 minutes or so.

J.D. Payne: And also why destroy your enemy when you can co-opt and potentially corrupt your enemy? There’s all kinds of things that Sauron can do rather than just fight to a bloody eventual maybe stalemate or prepared victory. In the moment, he’s looking at Pharazôn laughing and laughing and laughing. He’s already met him once in our show, and he’s seeing how eminently corruptible Pharazôn is. There’s all kinds of things you can do with a corrupt empire that you can twist it to your will.

When asked about the in-universe reason why Pharazôn and the Elves don't kill Sauron after he surrenders, McKay provides the reminder that "you can’t kill him." Payne builds on this by emphasizing that "that’s the true thing about Maiar. We’ve shown in our show that the Orcs tried that, and he came back as a black goo monster and ultimately re-instantiated."

McKay also hints that season 4 will begin with the characters wrestling with the question of what to do with such a powerful being in their custody: "But maybe we start the next season with that exact question. What the hell are we supposed to do with him? How do you actually handle a demigod who has surrendered to you? I think that’s a question worthy of exploration."

Earlier in the season 3 finale, Sauron did have the numbers to easily destroy Pharazôn and his soldiers, but that was no longer the case after the death of Pallando (Ciarán Hinds), which led to the Wizard's acolytes abandoning the fight, along with the legions of men from Rhûn. Even with the One Ring and an army of Orcs, victory in this battle would not have been worth the cost, so the villain turns back to the deceit and manipulation that were integral to his resurgence in seasons 1 and 2.

As Payne points out, season 1 also plays an important role in Sauron's decision because it is when the character first met Pharazôn in Númenor, allowing him to see how both the politician and the empire itself can be corrupted and destroyed from within. Being able to control them can give Sauron a greater advantage than simply defeating them on the battlefield of Middle-earth.

Only hours ahead of season 3's final episodes premiering, Prime Video announced that The Rings of Power season 4 would be moving forward. This ensures that viewers will get to see how Sauron's plan unfolds after kneeling before Pharazôn rather than ending the series on a frustrating cliffhanger. McKay and Payne have been open about having a five-season plan, meaning season 4 is expected to be the penultimate chapter of the overarching story.

While Pharazôn is fooled by Sauron's bold move, the Elves who were present for the moment are rightfully skeptical of it, with those who witnessed it including Galadriel (Morfydd Clark), Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower), Gil-galad (Benjamin Walker), and Elrond (Robert Aramayo). They will provide further complications for Sauron to contend with after turning himself over in The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3's final scene.

All episodes of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power seasons 1-3 are streaming on Prime Video.
