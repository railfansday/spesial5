Surprise, Surprise: Short-Sighted Automakers Back Trump's Fuel Economy Standards Rollback

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Good morning! It's Tuesday, November 3, 2026, and this is The Morning Shift, your daily roundup of the top automotive headlines from around the world, in one place. This is where you'll find the most important stories that are shaping the way Americans drive and get around.

In this morning's edition, car companies are all in favor of the Trump administration's rollback of fuel economy rules while they simultaneously urge Congress to pass the permanent ban on Chinese vehicle imports they've been whining about. Also, Europe has all but forgiven Tesla CEO Elon Musk and a chip shortage at Stellantis could hamper Fiat output in Italy.

The Alliance for Automotive Innovation, a group representing some of the biggest automakers in the world, including General Motors, Toyota, Hyundai, Ford, Stellantis and Volkswagen, said it was backing the Trump administration in a legal challenge to its sharply lower vehicle fuel economy standards that had been filed by California, New York and two dozen other states, cities and counties around the country. I'll tell ya what, I'm not surprised, but I'm sure those automakers will be once those fuel economy rollbacks are changed in the next administration (whenever that comes).

Last month, the U.S. Department of Transportation said it was finalizing a fleet-wide fuel economy average of 34.9 mpg by 2031. That's down from the 50.4 mpg mark that was set by the Biden administration in 2024. The group argued that automakers require "certainty and stability" in standards. From Reuters:

It added that if the standards were set aside, it "would affect automakers' ​business, endangering the capital investment planning, product development, and performance validation for hundreds of ​new vehicle models intended for the US market."

The states said the Trump administration was ignoring the presence ‌of millions ⁠of electric vehicles, "leading to a flawed, dramatically distorted analysis of the 'maximum feasible' fuel economy level." The automaker group said the court should reject those arguments.

The revised Trump administration standards seek to reverse Biden's push toward greater fuel efficiency and electric vehicles. A coalition of environmental ​and consumer groups ​also filed a separate ⁠lawsuit challenging the new rules.

The Transportation Department said the regulation reduces manufacturers' average cost per vehicle to comply by $1,289 but increases fuel ​costs by more than $1,600 over the life of the vehicles.

The department ​said the ⁠rule would increase US gasoline consumption through 2050 by 4.6%, or about 121 billion gallons, compared with the Biden rules but also boost new car sales.

The American Petroleum Institute, an oil ⁠and natural ​gas industry trade group, also filed in support of ​the Trump administration's rules, saying they ensure the government sets standards that "do not force consumers to purchase vehicles that ​consume less fuel than they would otherwise choose."

Automakers can talk all they want about being environmentally sustainable and eco-friendly, but at the end of the day, it's very clear that what it all comes down to is the bottom line. It'll certainly be interesting to see what the fallout will be once a new, more eco-conscious administration enters the White House in a few years' time.

That same group, the Alliance for Automotive Innovation, is currently on a heater. Not only are they going after fuel economy standards. They're also continuing their push to get Congress to ban the import of Chinese EVs to the U.S. Automakers have been whining about this for years at this point, and now they'd like the bill to pass when lawmakers return next week for a quick session following today's midterm elections.

Back in September, the Senate sponsors of a bipartisan bill to effectively ban Chinese-made vehicles from entering the U.S. agreed to delay the effort to fast-track the bill until November, despite the fact it had overwhelming support from other congresspeople who apparently aren't a fan of a free market or freedom of choice for the consumer. There are some officials who believe that if Dems win control of Congress, there may not be enough time to approve the ban before the session ends in December. Whatever will these OEMs do??? "We need to ​protect the US market from a flood of Chinese imports that carry ​surveillance risk and disadvantage current manufacturers that have made generational investments in ⁠American communities," said John Bozzella, who heads the Alliance for Automotive Innovation.

"Given the ​obvious national security risk and China's pattern of unfair behavior, Congress should protect ​our defense and advanced manufacturing base and stop Chinese automakers from doing here what they've gotten away ‌with ⁠in Europe," Bozzella said.

Bozzella said in the first eight months of 2026, the EU imported more than 1.1 million vehicles from China — more than all of 2025.

President Trump, wildcard that he is, sparked a hell of a lot of worry among key stakeholders when he said he would accept Chinese automakers building cars in the U.S., but so far no one has taken him up on the offer.

It would seem that Europe has all but forgiven Tesla CEO Elon Musk for all of the nasty crap he's pulled over the past few years, as registrations for his vehicles rose sharply across several European countries in October. It's an extension of the Austin, Texas-based automaker's recovery in the region, but the gains were admittedly a bit uneven as Chinese rivals continued to expand their market share. From Reuters:

Tesla registrations soared 248% in Sweden, 131% ​in Norway and 34% in Denmark, data from Mobility Sweden, Norway's OFV and Denmark's bilstatistik.dk showed.

The ⁠automaker also recorded strong growth elsewhere in Europe, with registrations rising 271% in Spain, 186% in Portugal and 94% in Italy, according to data from ANFAC, ​ACAP and Italy's Transport Ministry.

The rebound has been aided by easier year-ago comparisons, higher fuel prices, government incentives ​and continued growth in EV demand. But the pace of recovery varied significantly between markets, underlining the increasingly fragmented nature of Europe's EV sector as Chinese brands broaden their presence.

Tesla remains one of Europe's strongest EV brands, though its reliance on the ​Model 3 and Model Y has left it facing growing competition from rivals with broader lineups.

The automaker ​said in October it had ended the third quarter with its largest passenger vehicle order backlog in four years.

Norway remained a bright ‌spot ⁠for Tesla. The carmaker recorded its best October on record and was the country's top-selling car brand with a 9.8% market share, according to OFV. Chinese brands continued to expand their footprint, accounting for 22.5% of new passenger car registrations, their highest monthly share on record.

"Northern Europe is quite open to the new players as long as they ⁠are efficient ​EVs. That's what allowed Tesla to get to a leading position ​and it's what is allowing the Chinese to gain ground fast," industry analyst Felipe Munoz, who runs the Car Industry Analysis platform, ​told Reuters.

I guess time really does heal all wounds. Well, that, and staying out of the spotlight as best as Elon really can.

Stellantis is partying like it's 2021 again, because it's going through its own chip shortage in Italy. It says a shortfall for an electronic component used in an engine control unit is causing "significant" disruption to production of some Fiat models in Italy, and the issue could persist through the end of the year.

The shortage from a supplier has already weighed on October sales, especially for the Italy-made Fiat Pandina, one of the brand's best-selling models. There's a possibility that the shortage could lead to further issues, including production halts in the coming months at its plants in Pomigliano and Mirafiori — where it builds the Fiat 500. From Reuters:

"Stellantis and Fiat ​are working with the highest priority to identify alternative solutions and gradually restore normal operations," the group ​said.

The disruption could also impact Stellantis plants in Italy making ​engine components, including ​Termoli, ⁠where the N3 GSE engine used in both the Pandina and ​the 500 is assembled.

Mirafiori is currently ​undergoing ⁠a three-week production halt that started in mid-October due to a separate component shortage, with unions ⁠pointing ​at weak demand for the ​Fiat 500 as one of the factors behind the ​stoppage.

Mama mia! That's a spicy production nightmare — one an ailing company like Stellantis can hardly afford right now.
