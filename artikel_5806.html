$1199 — Anyland Rev Pro: An Electric Dirt Bike With a Wheelie Assist Built for Beginners

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

When I was a kid, I loved to ride. Riding a bike, for me, is synonymous with childhood, in fact. We used them to get ourselves places, but we also just used them to entertain ourselves. Inevitably, this led to popping wheelies, building ramps and trying jumps, and doing all sorts of other stuff my Mom just didn’t want to know about, no matter how much I wanted to brag. As an adult, I still love to ride — as the famous saying from Animal Farm goes, “four wheels good, two wheels mo’ bettah!” (I’m paraphrasing, a lot, but you get the point).

So when Anyland hit us up to do a review of their new Anyland Rev Pro, an all-electric dirt bike capable of 34 mph and assisted wheelies, the inner child in me jumped at the chance.

Wheelies look effortless until you try one. Anyone who has learned on a dirt bike knows the usual lesson plan: too little throttle and the front wheel never lifts, too much and you’re flat on your back. Anyland, a startup electric dirt bike brand, created the Rev Pro as an entry-level electric dirt bike with a controller that lifts the front wheel to a preset angle and holds it there, so a new rider can focus on balance instead of juggling throttle and brake.

The system, which Anyland sells as WELIXON and says it co-engineered with controller maker Moxin, offers three angle settings: 25°, 35°, and 45°. Riders start at the lowest angle and move up as their balance improves. According to Anyland, the controller delivers continuous, smooth power to keep the front end at the chosen angle, which is designed to reduce the risk of a “loop out,” where the bike flips backward, one of the most common ways wheelie learners get hurt. Once you’re ready, you can switch the assist off, and the Rev Pro behaves like a regular throttle-controlled electric dirt bike for practicing manual wheelies.

The speed modes set the first speed caps as low as 12 mph, and matter for the audience Anyland is targeting: teenagers and adults who are new to dirt bikes. Starting in the lowest mode lets a rider build skills before moving up, and gives parents a way to keep a first-timer in a gentler setting.

In short, the ride is really fun. I found the suspension incredibly responsive and comfortable, the acceleration smooth and quick, and the riding position much more comfortable than I anticipated. It’s been raining non-stop where I live, so while I got this bike a few weeks ago in the mail, I haven’t had a chance to get it up onto the trails yet, so stay tuned for that. Testing it on the dirt sidewalks and running over curbs shows me it will likely be a great addition to my weekends.

A wheelie assist doesn’t make any dirt bike risk-free. Wear a helmet, gloves, and protective gear every time, and start in the lowest speed mode at the 25° setting. Practice using the rear brake, supervise younger riders, and stick to private land or designated off-road areas. Check your local rules before riding anywhere else, since many electric dirt bikes aren’t legal on public roads.

Anyland’s Black Friday and Cyber Monday price starts as low as $1,199, and prices have varied, so check the current price before you buy.

For a startup brand in a crowded field of lookalikes, the Rev Pro’s pitch is simple: a beginner-friendly bike built around one clever feature. If you’ve always wanted to learn a wheelie without the bruises, it’s worth a look.
