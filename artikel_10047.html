Dell and HPE both say they’ve become ‘different companies’

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Still slinging hardware – now with added AI and attendant risks or opportunities

Dell and HPE this week both told investors they’ve become “different companies.”

The two hardware giants made that assertion on their respective earnings calls this week. HPE went first, on Monday, when CEO Antonio Neri declared “We are now a fundamentally different company” after acquiring Juniper Networks and creating products “built to capitalize on the next phase of AI.”

On Tuesday, Dell’s chief operating officer and vice chairman Jeff Clarke declared “We are now a different company” because the Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG) has become the source of “the vast majority of our profit and growth.”

HPE’s claim to having changed is perhaps more credible on the face of it, given that its acquisition of Juniper means it has become more of a networking player.

Dell hasn’t made significant additions to its hardware portfolio, but there’s a big difference in its sources of revenue, which three years ago was just about evenly split between its infrastructure and client devices. In its Tuesday announcement, Dell revealed ISG revenue grew 198 percent year over year to $42 billion, while CSG racked up three percent growth to $12.9 billion – meaning Dell is now predominantly an infrastructure company.

Clarke said he’s seen some companies deferring their PC purchases to buy AI hardware instead, and said Dell is re-assigning parts it ordered to build PCs and using them for servers instead.

Dell exited its Q3 with a backlog of $32 billion worth of AI servers and is clearly aware that investors might worry that’s not a useful metric because some buyers’ dreams of operating neoclouds have struck financial or regulatory barriers. Clarke assured investors that Dell is “highly selective about the partners we work with”

“Every AI order is vetted for access to capital, end customer quality, trade and export compliance, and access to land and power.”

HPE also has wrinkles in its AI business, because buyers won’t stand for fat margins and that’s dented the company’s profits a little. Neri was able to point to AI-related revenue also rising sharply, up 68.6 percent year over year to $11.3 billion, while overall revenue climbed 50 percent to reach $14.5 billion. Networking grew at a more relaxed 12 percent.

The CEO said HPE expects better growth ahead, because new server purchases create demand for networks and storage. He also pointed to HPE having the opportunity to sell its networking kit to other OEMs who build AMD’s Helios rack-scale systems. HPE predicted its next quarter will see revenue reach $14.0 billion to $14.6 billion, up 51 to 57 percent year over year.

Dell is also optimistic that AI buyers will spend on kit beyond servers and GPUs and updated its full-year revenue outlook to $202 billion, $10 billion above its previous forecasts and up 78 percent year over year. ®
