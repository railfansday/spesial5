Chase, Visa, Mastercard confirm third-party cyberattacks that may have exposed customers' info

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

First service outages, then data breaches…what's next?!

JPMorgan Chase, Visa, and Mastercard have all confirmed that they are investigating third-party breaches that may involve customer information after a data-theft-and-extortion gang claimed to have compromised the financial giants.

“Visa is aware of these claims,” a spokesperson told The Register on Thursday. “They are associated with an incident involving a third-party service provider in Asia Pacific. Visa’s systems were not involved and were not impacted. No payments data such as card numbers were affected. This is unrelated to any of the system disruptions across different industries this past week.”

Mastercard also cited a third-party provider in an email sent to The Register following our questions about the alleged breach.

“We were recently informed of an incident at a third-party provider that resulted in the exposure of a limited amount of email addresses - non-payments information - from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program,” Mastercard SVP Seth Eisen said. “There is no impact to the Mastercard payment network or the ability for people to use their cards. At this time, the exposure has been contained and our teams are working with the provider to support the impacted cardholders as needed.”

JPMorgan Chase did not respond to The Register’s request for comment, but confirmed it was investigating a cyberattack on social media.

“We have learned that third parties who provide services to the firm were the target of a cyber attack,” America’s largest bank posted on X. “Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses.”

According to Chase, the third-party breach didn’t disrupt any of its services, and it’s not connected to the outages earlier in the week attributed to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems.

“There is no connection to service disruption some customers experienced earlier this week,” the bank said on Thursday.

None of the three financial firms would comment on who the third-party providers are, when the breaches occurred, and what specific data was stolen. The Register will update this story as we learn more details about the intrusions.

Extortion crew n0n claimed responsibility for all three alleged hacks, posting the financial giants to its data-leak site on Monday. The group, believed to be based in Russia, emerged in September and posted 108 victims over its first month in operations. ®
