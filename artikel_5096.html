UK to ban crypto exchanges from making markets on their own platforms as Bitcoin rules change

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Firms can make markets in spot Bitcoin on qualifying venues without principal-dealing permission for that activity. UK exchanges face separate rules.

The UK is carving out a role for third-party firms that make markets in spot Bitcoin. From Oct. 25, 2027, they can buy and sell on qualifying crypto trading platforms without permission to deal as principal for that activity. The Financial Conduct Authority’s final rules bar UK platform operators from making markets on their own venues.

HM Treasury made the amending regulations on Nov. 18, 2026, adding Article 9UA to the UK’s regulated activities framework. The provision excludes market making on a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform from Article 9T, which covers dealing in qualifying cryptoassets as principal. A separate part of Article 9UA excludes trading strictly for a firm’s own purposes when it provides no service to anyone else.

A qualifying platform brings together multiple third-party buyers and sellers so their orders can result in trades in qualifying cryptoassets. The exclusion covers market making in those assets, including spot Bitcoin.

In its draft guidance, the FCA describes a market maker as a firm that stands ready to buy and sell listed assets on a continuing basis at prices it sets. The regulator proposes that responding to requests for quotes on the platform can also qualify.

CryptoSlate previously covered how the FCA’s final crypto rules govern venue access and authorization when the new regime begins.

Operating a UK qualifying cryptoasset trading platform requires FCA authorization. The FCA’s final venue rules require operators to identify, document and monitor market-making users and disclose the terms of relevant incentives. A separate affiliate may trade on the venue under the same access rules as other users.

An operator can step between buyer and seller to execute client orders on its own platform as a matched principal when it holds the appropriate additional permission and follows venue rules that give it no discretion over how orders are matched. An operator that executes client orders against its own capital away from its own venue needs principal-dealing permission and must disclose its role and manage conflicts.

Firms that take orders from customers have to assess their permissions separately. Dealing as an agent or arranging deals can require permission. Firms executing or forwarding orders covered by the FCA’s rules for UK retail and elective professional clients must use a UK qualifying cryptoasset execution venue.

The FCA’s application window closes Feb. 28, 2027, for eligible firms with an existing UK cryptoasset business that want transitional protection for regulated activities. Market making that falls within Article 9UA carries no principal-dealing application requirement for that activity.

The FCA’s consultation on the carve-out closed Nov. 18, 2026. Its consultation page targets January 2027 for final guidance, while the consultation paper gives spring 2027.
