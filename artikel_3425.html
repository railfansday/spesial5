Avengers: Doomsday's PG-13 Rating Explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

We are a month away from potentially the biggest box office weekend of 2026, with Dunesday set to blow Barbenheimer out of the water as both "Dune: Part Three" and "Avengers: Doomsday" make their way into theaters. These movies are aimed at similar audiences, yet they're vastly different when it comes to their tones and approaches to adapting popular characters for the big screen. Indeed, where Denis Villeneuve's cinematic takes on the "Dune" books have so far been somber and introspective, the road to "Doomsday" has been paved almost entirely with nostalgia.

To be sure, between Robert Downey Jr. returning to the Marvel Cinematic Universe (this time to play Doctor Doom), the latest Avengers roster featuring multiple beloved characters from the MCU's past, and several actors reprising their roles from 20th Century Fox's "X-Men" films, "Doomsday" has had no problem catching the attention and interest of virtually every kind of moviegoer out there. After all, where "Dune: Part Three" can afford to target older audiences given the mature themes of its source material, "Doomsday" is aimed at everyone with a pulse and a heartbeat.

Hence, it's no surprise to learn that the new "Avengers" movie is now officially rated PG-13, just like almost every other previous film entry in the MCU since 2008 (save for "Deadpool & Wolverine"). Per FilmRatings.com, the film earned this rating from the Motion Picture Association (MPA) based on its "violence/action, bloody images, and language."

Just in case it needs to be said, "Avengers: Doomsday" was never going to be rated anything other than PG-13. The MCU's movies, in general, tend to feature too much intense action for any of them to garner a mere PG rating, yet they also tend to avoid going overboard into full-blown adult territory. At the same time, the explanation for its rating suggests "Doomsday" will make good on its promise of being one of the darker installments in the franchise.

Keeping that in mind, Marvel Studios and Disney were never going to allow a movie as costly as this one to get slapped with an R rating either, lest it severely limit the number of people who can pay to see it in a theater. And yes, "Deadpool & Wolverine" managed to become a massive success with an R-rating, but that was a different situation. Since the first two "Deadpool" films had been R-rated, it might've actually hurt the movie financially if it had tried to take the larger "Deadpool" property in a more family-friendly direction.

That's not to suggest that "Doomsday" won't be sufficiently dark or violent, though. Just too long ago, in fact, Sam Raimi pushed the PG-13 rating as far as humanly possible with 2022's "Doctor Strange In the Multiverse of Madness," the most gruesome Marvel movie yet. As you may recall, that film was filled with incredibly brutal death scenes, legitimate jump scares, and upsetting imagery that's not suitable for younger audiences. On the other hand, we can probably safely assume "Doomsday" won't push the envelope that far, given everything that's riding on it.

You can see what "Avengers: Doomsday" has to offer for yourself when it opens in theaters on December 18, 2026.
