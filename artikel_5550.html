Chicago Fed president won't rule out a larger rate hike for December

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Austan Goolsbee said how steep the path of rate increases needs to be depends on what inflation does, after months of high readings

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee did not rule out a larger interest rate increase at the Federal Reserve's December meeting, according to Bloomberg.

Goolsbee took part in a moderated Q&A at the Economic Club of Indiana luncheon in Indianapolis on Thursday, according to the Chicago Fed.

"We've now had multiple months in a row of borderline disturbingly high inflation rates with little progress in the sectors where we would expect to be seeing some progress," Goolsbee told reporters on Thursday, according to Bloomberg. "So everything's always on the table, in my view, and how steep that path needs to be is determined by what we think is happening with inflation."

Goolsbee said he is concerned about seasonal factors. Inflation has been running hot during a stretch of the calendar that usually brings cooler prices, he said, and upward price pressure has typically been strongest early in the year. That makes it "that much more relevant that as we roll into next year, do we think that the through line is going to be another jump back up in the inflation rate?" he said, according to Bloomberg.

He said officials had expected the price effects of tariffs and costlier energy to fade sooner than they have. He described the construction of infrastructure that runs artificial intelligence as a "second component, which to me looks like the beginnings of just ordinary demand overheating."

Fed Governor Christopher Waller said again on Wednesday that policymakers could need to weigh a half-point hike in December, according to Bloomberg. Minutes of the October meeting, released Wednesday, show another unidentified official joined Waller in calling for that discussion. Goolsbee is not a voting member of the policy committee this year, but he will be next year.

Goolsbee called the October consumer price report "borderline disturbing" in an interview last week. Asked then about a bigger hike in December, he said he would rather not box in policymakers before they meet. The consumer price index rose 0.4% in October, and core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3%.

Waller said in a London speech last week that a half-point increase should be considered if October core CPI came in around 0.3% or higher. The Fed raised rates by a quarter point in September and held its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4% in October. Its next meeting is Dec. 8-9.

Inflation rose 3.6% in September from a year earlier by the Fed's preferred gauge. Core CPI has come in above expectations for three consecutive months, according to Bloomberg.
