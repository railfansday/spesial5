Indianapolis, IN Struggles with Low Starter Home Availability Compared to Other Cities

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

For many prospective homeowners, the dream of purchasing a first home can feel increasingly out of reach. A recent analysis by Realtor.com® explores the availability of starter home options across the country, revealing a challenging landscape for many would-be buyers.

Nationally, the share of starter homes in active inventory has seen a decline, falling from 38.1% in August 2019 to 36.2% in August 2026. This scarcity is compounded by rising prices and climbing mortgage rates. Over the same period, starter home prices increased by 30.8%, from approximately $260,000 to $340,000.

In Indianapolis, IN, the market reflects this national trend, with the availability of starter homes experiencing a downturn in recent years. This shift presents new hurdles for those looking to enter the housing market in the area.

A starter home is defined as a property priced at roughly 80% of a metro's median list price, making it a more accessible option for a first-time buyer. These homes are typically smaller and less expensive, aligning with the budget of a household earning at or below the area's median Income and offering a gateway to homeownership and improved affordability.

In Indianapolis, the share of starter homes has decreased over the past several years. The market saw its starter home share at 36.2% in 2019, which slightly decreased to 35.9% in 2022.

By 2026, the share of starter homes in Indianapolis had fallen to 33.0%. This represents a 3.2 point change since 2019, highlighting a growing challenge for buyers seeking entry-level properties in the metro area.

For those weighing the decision between renting or buying, current data suggests that renting may be the more financially prudent choice for now. According to the Realtor.com® July Rent Report, the cost of buying a starter home was $858 higher per month than renting one across the 50 largest metros.
