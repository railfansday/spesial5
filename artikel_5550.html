Vodacom will seek partners to build AI data centers across Africa

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

CEO Shameel Joosub names Egypt, Kenya and South Africa as bigger markets; half-year headline earnings rose 25.6%

Vodacom Group will seek partners for the construction of AI data centers across Africa, splitting the costs and drawing on outside know-how, CEO Shameel Joosub told Bloomberg. The Johannesburg-based company, majority-owned by Vodafone Group, made the comments as it reported half-year results.

Vodacom is co-investing with Elsewedy Electric and Cassava Technologies in an AI data center in Egypt, the company said in its results presentation. Joosub said in the interview that Vodacom will look at other markets, including South Africa and Kenya.

"Egypt, Kenya, South Africa are our bigger markets," Joosub said, according to Bloomberg. "It will be a big investment, and that is why we're bringing on partners."

The data centers, held in joint ventures, will draw their funding from both equity and debt, Joosub said. Vodacom will hold a stake in the facilities and probably handle most of the sales to customers, he said.

For the six months ended Sept. 30, Vodacom’s headline earnings attributable to equity holders rose 25.6% to R11.3 billion (about $646 million). Headline earnings per share rose 25.7% to 587 cents. Dividend per share rose 7.6% to 355 cents, and customers grew 11.8% to 250 million.

Service revenue rose 29.4% to R85.1 billion (about $4.9 billion), and EBITDA rose 40.0% to R42.7 billion (about $2.4 billion). Reported growth rates reflect the consolidation of Safaricom from the second quarter, the company said. On a pro-forma basis, service revenue rose 13.3% and EBITDA rose 18.0%.

Vodacom's stake in Safaricom rose to 55% from 35% effective June 30, 2026, and the transaction remains subject to appeal, the company said.

Africa represents under 1% of data-center capacity, while South Africa is home to most of the computing power on the continent, according to Bloomberg. Rival MTN Group has teamed up with an investment platform based in the Middle East to begin work on AI data centers in both Nigeria and South Africa.

Vodacom stock gained close to 5% in Tuesday trading on the Johannesburg exchange.
