CFPB narrows enforcement practices, closes 86% of supervisory actions

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The CFPB said it is narrowing supervisory and enforcement activity, closing 86% of open supervisory actions and cutting scheduled exams by about 50%. It is also reviewing ability-to-repay and mortgage servicing rules, along with revised fair lending and Section 1071 requirements.  AI Summary

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) said that it is narrowing its supervisory and enforcement activities and closing most of its outstanding supervisory actions, as well as pursuing changes to mortgage lending and servicing rules, according to its latest semi-annual report, released Oct. 27.

The report, which covers the first half of 2026, outlines the bureau’s efforts to align its work with the Trump administration’s priorities.

During the broader reporting period of July 2025 through June 2026, the CFPB closed 1,327 of the 1,552 supervisory actions that were open as of July 1, 2025, or approximately 86%. The bureau said the closures reflected either institutions sufficiently addressing the issues or findings that no longer aligned with its priorities.

The CFPB also reassessed all 80 open examinations and closed 66 that did not align with its revised priorities. The remaining 14 continued with a focus on correcting violations, addressing compliance weaknesses and providing relief to affected consumers.

The bureau also reduced the number of scheduled examinations by approximately 50%, shifting its attention toward depository institutions, activities it deemed actual consumer fraud and areas within its statutory authority. Mortgage origination and servicing remain among its supervisory priorities.

Under its new approach, the CFPB said examinations will focus on clearly identified products and potential violations tied to specific laws and regulations. The bureau also plans to avoid duplicating oversight already conducted by state or other federal regulators.

The CFPB is pursuing several mortgage-related regulatory changes under President Donald Trump’s executive order on promoting access to mortgage credit. Among its priorities is a review of the ability-to-repay requirements and qualified mortgage definitions under Regulation Z. The bureau is evaluating whether to modify the requirements, which apply to most residential mortgages.

The CFPB is also considering a final rule to streamline mortgage servicing requirements under Regulation X. The proposal, initially issued in July 2024, is intended to simplify the loss-mitigation review process and make it easier for servicers to assist borrowers who experience payment difficulties.

In July 2026, after the report’s primary reporting period, the bureau also issued a request for information seeking feedback on ways to promote access to mortgage credit.

The CFPB additionally finalized changes to its fair lending and small-business lending rules. In April, the bureau amended Regulation B, which implements the Equal Credit Opportunity Act. The rule removed longstanding language supporting disparate-impact liability and revised restrictions on discouraging prospective credit applicants.

It also prohibited for-profit creditors from using race, color, national origin or sex as eligibility criteria for Special Purpose Credit Programs.

A separate May rule revised requirements for small-business lending data collection under Section 1071 of the Dodd-Frank Act. Among other changes, it lowered the annual revenue threshold for businesses covered by the rule from $5 million to $1 million, removed certain data collection requirements and extended the compliance date to Jan. 1, 2028.

The bureau said it did not cite any fair lending violations during the reporting period, and that while it continued investigations, it instead focused its efforts on cases involving intentional discrimination with identifiable victims. It also made no referrals to the Department of Justice for potential patterns or practices of lending discrimination.

Overall, the CFPB said it closed approximately 40% of its pending investigations as part of its effort to align enforcement with its revised priorities.

The bureau reported receiving approximately 8.27 million consumer complaints between July 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026. It sent approximately 7.5 million complaints to companies for review, and companies responded to 99.6% of those complaints.

The report’s complaint figures come as the CFPB has moved to limit the public availability of consumer complaints. In August, the bureau said it would stop publishing unverified complaint narratives and related data visualizations in its public database, arguing that the accounts present only one side of a dispute and may not reflect violations of law.

The bureau also said it would continue collecting complaints, reviewing company responses and sharing information with regulators. Previously published narratives will remain available through its Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) reading room.
