Spurs will be surprised but Leicester's won't by Oliver Skipp at Charlton

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Having shone when coming through the academy at Tottenham Hotspur and then experienced a bright start to his career in the EFL, Oliver Skipp is again struggling this season, now at Charlton Athletic after a tough time of it at Leicester City.

Following their promotion back to the Championship via the 2024-25 League One play-offs, Charlton have looked to assert themselves as a part of the furniture again in the second-tier, under the management of Nathan Jones.

A young squad under Jones as they gained promotion, Charlton did have a fair bit of experience dotted around their squad but, as ever with a team heading into a campaign seeking to avoid ‘second season syndrome’, they have again sought to add more pedigree to their squad.

That may well have even been with a view to kicking on into the middle-of-the-table and, albeit as an outsider, looked at the top eight and the expanded play-off spots to eight teams in the Championship this season.

One player that was brought in with good pedigree to provide extra quality in the middle of the park was Oliver Skipp – but the 26-year-old is enduring another struggle this season down at The Valley.

Over the summer, Charlton added a fair bit of experience to the middle of the park with the signing of Nathaniel Chalobah on a free transfer from Sheffield Wednesday and Timothy Ouma from Slavia Prague.

Both defensive midfielders, it was somewhat surprising that The Addicks then signed Oliver Skipp on loan from Leicester City on the final day of the campaign – albeit in a Nathan Jones-coached side, perhaps you can’t have too many conservative and defensive-minded midfielders.

Skipp, Ouma and Chalobah joined a squad that already boasted other key midfielders such as Conor Coventry, Greg Docherty and the slightly more advanced Sonny Carey, while the versatile Joe Rankin-Costello is also capable of playing in a central midfield role.

Whether it be a 4-2-3-1 system or even a three-at-the-back with either one or two so-called ‘number tens’; there tends to only be room for two out and out box-to-box or defensive central midfielders – and with the quality Skipp has previously shown and the experience he already has in his mid-20’s; Skipp was viewed as a floor-raising addition to a Charlton side that had won their opening three games of the campaign before his arrival.

However, Charlton have since gone on a ten-match winless run of form after the arrival of Skipp and his own individual performance have perhaps been reflective of the malaise and regression of the team as a whole.

The former England U21 international came into the side immediately and played the full 90 minutes as Charlton endured their first loss of the season – a 4-0 hammering at the hands of Stoke City.

He has gone on to start seven of the next eight matches, too, only not involved against Norwich City after being hooked at half-time in a 2-0 defeat to Watford a week earlier.

He came back into the eleven against Middlesbrough but found himself in a midfield that was completely overrun by the league leaders in a 3-0 defeat at the Riverside Stadium in that one.

Keeping out Coventry in midfield, as well as even Chalobah, who had started life in South London well, being moved to the periphery of things seems now to be a surprising inevitability for Skipp.

Once a metronomic figure in terms of his passing and tempo setting when coming through Tottenham youth teams and then on loan at Norwich; Skipp has badly struggled to adapt to the more conservative nature of Jones’ style.

Skipp has remained tidy enough on the ball with an 83.1% pass success rate but he is averaging too few passes per game and allowing games to pass him by, averaging more than 12 passes per 90 fewer than Conor Coventry, as an example.

Previously better known for his ability on the ball, it is his defensive work that has been his more impressive suit thus far this season but, with ball-winners such as Chalobah at the club, more quality was to be expected of the Leicester loanee.

That quality was shown when coming through at Spurs as he made the fringes of the first-team picture in North London and even began to emerge with more regular minutes when coached by Antonio Conte – who he once suggested he could help become a ‘top midfielder’.

His breakthrough into more regular minutes at Spurs had come after an extremely impressive loan stint at Norwich during the 2020-21 campaign – named in the PFA Team of the Year for the second division that season as The Canaries won the Championship title under Daniel Farke.

That season, Skipp was that metronome in Norwich’s proactive and entertaining side, averaging 51.5 passes per game with an 88% pass success rate – dwarfing his figures at Charlton so far this season and highlighting his drop off.

That drop off being seen by Charlton supporters this season may come as a surprise to Spurs and Norwich supporters but it won’t be much of a shock for Leicester fans.

Purchased by The Foxes in the summer of 2024 for a fee believed to be in the region of £20 million, following Leicester’s Championship title-winning campaign under Enzo Maresca, Skipp linked up with Steve Cooper at the King Power Stadium.

A good coach but a pragmatic one, the passing and ability to retain possession that had been qualities of Skipp weren’t necessarily going to be used too much for a Cooper-led relegation battler.

However, his fierceness in the tackle and general industry also diminished in a Leicester squad that succumbed to back-to-back relegations from the Premier League and into League One.

Perhaps a very good example of Leicester’s issues in the last half a decade, being a big money signing brought in without too much sense in terms of exactly what they wanted; Skipp badly struggled at the King Power Stadium.

Having been one of the best players in the Championship and then someone seemingly coming close to England recognition in the years before his arrival at Leicester; a loss of confidence and inability to rediscover his quality on the ball led to Skipp sticking out as a fairly hapless figure in Leicester’s badly underperforming side.

Brought in for such a big fee, he managed just ten starts in his debut campaign as they were relegated from the Premier League before being a peripheral figure in the opening few months of last season.

Skipp became a key man for a Leicester side that became enemies of the fanbase and his failure to rediscover any sort of form at Charlton thus far will not come as much of a surprise to Foxes supporters.
