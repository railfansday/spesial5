8 Elden Ring Details That Suggest Some of the Biggest Mysteries Still Aren’t Solved

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

She specializes in lists, reviews, and features, with additional experience writing guides for Hardcore Gamer. Elden Ring is weird, and I mean that in the best way. FromSoftware builds elaborate worlds in each game the developers make, and the effort and attention to detail that goes into those worlds mean that you can spend dozens of hours digging into the lore and still not have a clear idea of it.

Bored with the way you go about your journey in The Lands Between? Here are the 10 best ways to make your second Elden Ring playthrough different.

This design for lore also means that you likely won't find an answer to every question. If you're like me, then the mystery is part of the charm. It keeps you wondering, and that keeps you playing as you search for answers that might not fully exist in-game in as straightforward of a way as you’d wish.

My favorite mystery is the Gloam-Eyed Queen. You never get to see her, but multiple items refer to her, and you learn that she was the main rival of Marika. It's also heavily implied that she still has some followers in the Lands Between.

However, we don't know her real identity, what happened to her, or where she is if she's alive. One interesting theory is that Melina is the Gloam-Eyed Queen due to her one eye seeming sealed shut, but when it's open in the Frenzied Flame ending, it has a gloam color to it.

This is a bit interesting because we know why the Land of Shadow was separated from the Lands Between. Marika effectively sealed away her past in the Land of Shadow, but more importantly, she was able to block access to the exact gate that let her ascend to godhood. Of course, she would put measures in place that prevented others from getting power like that.

The mystery is how she separated it and how Miquella was able to create an entrance that others could follow him through. However, the methods aren't exactly important enough to impact the lore much, and we just don't know the extent of Marika’s abilities.

Aside from its name, there are multiple mentions of locations beyond the Lands Between. We also know that the Tarnished, when they had their Grace removed by Marika, were sent away and returned later. So, that means that the Lands Between is a place that can be accessed from other lands.

It also raises the question as to why the Lands Between is so unique and has the Erdtree and, previously, the Crucible. Are the other lands not worth the attention of the Outer Gods, or is it just that the gods we know of aren't interested, but others might be? There's just not enough information to find an answer.

The Greater Will is well-known, and a few gods like the God of Rot, Formless Mother, and Frenzied Flame are mentioned enough that we know they exist and have some interest in the Lands Between. However, we really don't know much about the gods or what they want from this land.

These Elden Ring bosses are underrated and deserve more praise.

It's obvious that the Erdtree is what they're after, but the Erdtree is almost certainly built on the Crucible, which could be another goal or the main goal. But the Greater Will apparently has the ability to create and defeat gods, so it seems strange that it would want power from the Lands Between unless it's to keep it out of the hands of other gods.

Nokstella alone is filled with so much mystery that it's difficult to condense into a summary when you need a ton of context and explanations. Honestly, you could write a novella on the many unanswered questions that come from this location.

The Nox, who built Nokstella after rebelling against the Greater Will unsuccessfully, were originally thought to be connected to the Numen and Marika, but the DLC puts Marika in a completely different location. But then you have the connections between the Black Knives and Marika, along with the Black Knives being connected to the Nox. There are too many threads and almost no answers here.

Placidusax has two heads when you encounter him for his boss fight, but he also has a few stumps where more heads once were. The story is that his missing heads were cut off by Bayle, and you can find that two of his heads are with Bayle when you see him in the Shadow of the Erdtree DLC.

Except that's not accounting for every head. Placidusax has two remaining. Bayle has two of the missing ones, but if you look at Placidusax’s model and the Old Lord's Talisman, it shows that he should have five heads. The problem is that we have no idea what happened to the fifth one.

There are seven walking mausoleums in Elden Ring, and we can assume that they were made by the Nox because of their design, which matches the buildings in Nokstella, a style found nowhere else. The interesting part is that we're told they house soulless demigods, so those in the same state as Godwyn.

If that's true, then the bodies in them must be Marika’s children. A ghost even tells you when talking about one that it has Marika's unwanted child in it. When you start Elden Ring, you feel like you know who Marika's children are, but the details of the world let you know that she apparently had far more children than you think as you dig into the lore.

I'm fascinated by the Gate of Divinity, and I think it's one of the most well-known mysteries if you played the DLC or even watched the trailer for it. This is where Marika ascended to godhood, and Miquella later followed in her footsteps, albeit with a much shorter reign since you end it immediately.

Yet we know nothing about this gate other than the fact that it grants divinity. There seem to be requirements for being able to use it successfully, but beyond that we don't know how or why it works. We don't even know where it connects to.

FromSoftware makes such fascinating games that leave you thinking about them even years after they were released, encouraging you to explore every bit of content. Unfortunately, like most of their games, we're probably not going to get answers for every mystery in Elden Ring.
