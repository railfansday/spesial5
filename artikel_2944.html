Coca-Cola vs. Monster Beverage: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Coca-Cola's global scale and steady bottler network go head-to-head with Monster's 20% revenue surge, but the valuation gap may settle the debate.

By Jake Lerch – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 9:45AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Coca-Cola ›

Investors often choose between established dividend stability and high-octane growth. Choosing between Coca-Cola (KO -0.49%)and Monster Beverage (MNST +1.11%)involves weighing a massive global powerhouse against a rapidly expanding energy drink leader.

While both companies operate in the same broader industry, they serve very different market needs. Coca-Cola offers a diversified portfolio of sodas and juices, while Monster Beverage specializes in the high-growth energy category. This comparison examines their financial health and risk profiles to help you decide which is better.

Coca-Cola sells brands like Sprite, Fanta, and its namesake cola through a vast network of independent bottling partners and retailers in over 200 countries. One significant bottler accounts for nearly 10% of net operating revenues. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business. The company also manages the dairy subsidiary, fairlife, LLC, and maintains an equity investment in Monster Beverage to expand its reach.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 2, 2026, revenue reached approximately $51.1 billion, representing growth of nearly 7.1% over the prior year. Net income for the same period was roughly $14.6 billion. This growth reflects a steady recovery and pricing power in global markets despite varying economic conditions across its geographic segments.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 1.5x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio is roughly 1.5x, indicating its ability to cover short-term liabilities with current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $5.3 billion, reinforcing its position among beverage stocks.

Monster Beverage develops and sells energy drinks, including Reign and NOS, and relies heavily on the Coca-Cola distribution network to reach 158 countries. Its primary customers include Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP -0.05%)and Coca-Cola Consolidated (COKE -0.86%), which account for roughly 15% and 10% of net sales, respectively. Such concentration adds a layer of risk to the business, as these two partners account for a significant share of distribution.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $9.6 billion, a significant growth of nearly 20.2% compared to the previous year. Net income for this period was roughly $2.2 billion. This expansion highlights the increasing consumer appetite for energy beverages and the company's ability to successfully scale its brands globally.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carries a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0x, indicating it has no total debt relative to its equity. The current ratio is approximately 3.7x, suggesting a very strong ability to meet short-term financial obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached nearly $2.0 billion. This financial flexibility allows for continued reinvestment into new product lines.

Coca-Cola faces pressure from global economic volatility and geopolitical instability that can disrupt its complex supply chain. The company must navigate intense competition from rivals such as PepsiCo (PEP -0.11%)and Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP +0.53%). Additionally, it manages ongoing tax litigation with the IRS and regulatory scrutiny regarding sugar-sweetened beverage taxes and environmental impacts of packaging.

Monster Beverage is vulnerable to category-specific regulations, such as potential age restrictions or limits on caffeine content in energy drinks. The company relies heavily on the Coca-Cola distribution system, meaning any strategic shift by its partner could impact market access. Furthermore, it faces increasing competition from nimble competitors such as Celsius Holdings (CELH +3.08%)and rising costs for raw materials like aluminum.

Monster Beverage carries a higher Forward P/E and P/S ratio, which divides market cap by sales over the past twelve months, than Coca-Cola.

When comparing Coca-Cola and Monster Beverage, investors should consider a few key factors. Let's have a look at them and see what that tells us about each stock.

Firstly, we should consider revenue growth. In this category, Monster gains a real edge. Over the last three years, it has averaged 11.8% year-over-year revenue growth, while Coca-Cola has averaged about 4.9%. Moreover, in recent quarters, Monster's revenue growth has surged well above its average, clocking in at 16.7% in its most recent quarter. Coca-Cola has also delivered above-average revenue growth, at 7% in its most recent report.

Another factor to consider is each company's profitability. Over the last 10 years, Coca-Cola has had an average operating margin of 27.1%, while Monster's was 31.7%, seemingly giving Monster the edge here. However, in recent years, Monster's profitability has drifted lower, while Coca-Cola's has increased. In fact, Coca-Cola has now overtaken Monster. Coca-Cola's operating margin is now 32.1%, while Monster's has fallen to 28.5%, giving Coca-Cola an advantage in this category.

Finally, there's valuation. Since both companies are profitable, it makes sense to compare their price-to-earnings (P/E) multiples. Coca-Cola has a P/E multiple of 29x, while Monster's is 40x. That gives Coca-Cola another edge, as its stock is more affordable.

To sum up, investors may want to consider both of these consumer stocks. However, given its more reasonable valuation and its higher profitability, Coca-Cola wins the battle between these two beverage giants, in my opinion. Nevertheless, given its impressive revenue growth, solid profitability, and reasonable valuation, Monster also remains a stock worth considering.
