curved concrete extension by belén ilarri studio updates 1970s house near valencia

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

removing a suspended hallway ceiling establishes a cross-shaped, skylight-lit plan paired with a curved concrete kitchen extension and hand-mixed lime finishes.

BI STD edited by christina vergopoulou November 21, 2026 4 min

Located on the outskirts of Valencia, Spain, Casa Lucerna is the renovation and extension of a 1970s residence by Belén Ilarri Studio. The intervention begins with the removal of a dropped hallway ceiling, uncovering the house’s concealed original ceiling height and structural proportions. Using this vertical recovery as its anchor, the studio reorganizes the floor plan around a cross-shaped circulation layout that links multiple orientations, frames sightlines toward the garden, and promotes cross-ventilation. New openings cut into the south and west facades introduce natural daylight, while a central skylight placed at the intersection of the cross brings zenithal light into the core of the plan.

a curved concrete volume accommodates the wine cellar and pantry, then extends to form a porch, discreetly blending into the existing geometry and material palette | all images ©Belen Ilarri Studio

At the rear of the house, the studio extends the kitchen onto the outdoor patio through large glazed openings, anchored by a cast-in-place concrete addition that preserves the recognizable street-facing character of the 1970s structure. The curved concrete volume houses a wine cellar and pantry before projecting outward to form a sheltered patio porch. Across the interior and exterior, raw construction textures and tactile finishes distinguish the new interventions without obscuring the existing fabric: the concrete extension retains its formwork irregularities, paired with hand-mixed on-site lime renders, custom natural timber joinery, and honed, lightly bush-hammered natural marble in the kitchen.

a concrete extension integrates with the existing architecture, extending the kitchen towards the patio without altering the house’s exterior appearance

outside, the preservation of original elements ensures that the house remains recognisable

this discovery becomes the starting point for a new layout, organised around a cross-shaped configuration that redefines circulation

this sculptural architectural feature transforms the perception of a modest house without resorting to unnecessary gestures

a skylight introduces zenithal light, emphasising the geometry of the cross, which becomes more than a mere spatial planning device

the relationship between interior and exterior finds one of its strongest expressions in the kitchen

large openings extend the room towards the patio, blurring the boundaries between the two and making everyday life feel more connected to the outdoors

custom-designed and crafted natural timber joinery brings warmth and precision, while the honed, lightly bush-hammered natural marble introduces a mineral texture into the kitchen

the choice of materials follows a shared intention: to introduce a new architectural layer without concealing those that came before; natural materials and handcrafted finishes highlight the texture
