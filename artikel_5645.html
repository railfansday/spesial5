FirstEnergy utilities get approval for $2.5B gas plant in West Virginia

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Consumer advocates and environmental groups opposed the project, citing concerns over load growth projections and shifting of project risk to ratepayers.

The project was prompted by an unnamed data center customer that would require an initial capacity of 100 MW in 2028, increasing incrementally to 1,012 MW by 2033, according to the commission staff.

A commission analysis found that Mon Power had projected a 2,997 MW summer peak in 2026. In 2028, the company could see a 60 MW capacity shortfall.

That grows to 2,194 MW in 2034 with the addition of new large loads, including data centers. That shortfall comes amid surging capacity prices in the PJM Interconnection has seen in recent years, the commission noted.

Groups like the Sierra Club argued that the utility’s $4 billion rate base would increase by 67%as a result of the project. Mon Power should create large load tariffs to ensure recovery from the customers prompting the electric infrastructure upgrades, the group added.

Anna Sommer, a principal at Energy Futures Group, testified on behalf of the West Virginia Citizen Action Group, Solar United Neighbors and Energy Efficient West Virginia, raising questions on the project’s levelized cost of energy.

“The underdevelopment of the Companies’ CC proposal introduces significant risk of cost increases and schedule delays,” Sommers wrote. The approved gas-fired power plant, called the Maidsville Energy Center, is expected to cost $2.48 billion.

The commission approved the construction surcharge, stating the mechanism had been used before – including for the canceled Mid Atlantic Power Pathway by Pepco Holdings, which ultimately was allowed to recover more than $80 million from ratepayers for the uncompleted project.

The surcharge mechanism would prevent any credit downgrading because of a lack of funds from operations flowing in, Mon Power had argued in its case.

In the “unlikely event that construction ceases or experiences extraordinary delays,” the commission will “initiate an appropriate proceeding to determine the applicable accounting and ratemaking treatment, surcharge or base rate recovery and the timing of such recovery,” the order says.
