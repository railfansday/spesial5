Wendy's rejected a $1.4 billion takeover offer as its stock rose 37% in 11 trading days

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Trian-led investor group offered $7.35 a share in cash, and the company adopted a one-year poison pill

Wendy's $WEN rejected a $1.4 billion takeover offer from an investor group led by Nelson Peltz's Trian Fund Management on Nov. 5, according to CNBC. On Oct. 28, the group proposed paying $7.35 in cash for each share, a bid that would take the burger chain private.

Wendy's stock has gained 37% in the past 11 sessions and now sits near $8. It did not post a losing session in that stretch, according to MarketWatch. The gain has nearly cleared the stock's decline for this year, but the shares still trade far under the highs north of $20 they hit in the early part of the 2020s.

Besides Trian, the investor group includes BlueFive Capital, Sixth Street Partners and Flynn Group, one of Wendy's largest franchisees, according to CNBC.

Wendy's board formed a committee of independent directors in February to assess any bid from Trian after the firm made a regulatory filing, according to CNBC. The committee concluded that the offer "substantially undervalues" Wendy's while overlooking the benefits the chain could see from its turnaround strategy, according to CNBC.

"Our confidence rests firmly in [CEO] Bob Wright's proven track record of seasoned industry leadership, including restaurant business turnarounds," said Art Winkleblack, who chairs Wendy's board and sits on its independent committee, in a statement, according to CNBC. Trian declined to comment on the decision.

As protection against an unwanted hostile bid, Wendy's put a poison pill, formally a shareholder rights plan, in place on Nov. 4 that runs for one year, according to CNBC. The Trian-led group rebuffed a proposal from Wendy's to sign a standstill agreement freezing further stock buying, and that refusal contributed to the decision to adopt the pill.

Wendy's stock dipped by under 1% during early trading on Nov. 5. The stock, which has shed roughly 60% of its value across three years, got a lift from the takeover offer, according to CNBC.

Wendy's named Peltz chairman emeritus in 2024 after he spent 17 years on its board, according to CNBC. Trian executive Peter May and Peltz's son, Bradley Peltz, remain on the board.
