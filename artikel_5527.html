<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/catching-new-ceo-unitedhealthcares-surest" hreflang="en">Catching up with the new CEO of UnitedHealthcare's Surest</a>

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Catching up with the new CEO of UnitedHealthcare's Surest" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Joe Clark is stepping into the CEO chair at UnitedHealthcare's Surest unit with an eye on affordability.

Clark was appointed to lead the division earlier this month, after serving as the chief financial officer for the insurer's employer and individual segment. In an interview with Fierce Healthcare, he said that the new role comes at "an exciting time" to be a part of a plan like Surest.

Under the model, Surest eliminates deductibles for a copayment-only approach, where members' costs are tied to the quality and value for their provider. Members connect to the Surest app or website to compare the cost between different providers.

"Affordability is a challenge every year, but especially right now, for employers," Clark said. "We've seen demand for Surest and other sort of alternative health plans just skyrocket over the last year. And being part of being able to deliver that and offer solutions to employers is incredibly exciting."

UnitedHealthcare first launched the unit in 2016 as Bind before rebranding as Surest in 2022 with an eye on growth. In a 2023 report, the company said that Surest members had 6% fewer emergency department visits and 13% fewer inpatient admissions compared to people enrolled in traditional commercial plans at the same company.

The plan also saw a 20% increase in visits to a physician and 9% increase in preventative physical exams compared to the members in traditional plans.

In a 2024 analysis, conducted by Aon, the company found that Surest enrollees generated $400 or more in cost claims savings per year. That study also identified improvements around spending and utilization across all age groups and accounting for several conditions.

Clark said that in a traditional health plan, only about 15% of people hit their deductible for the year, meaning they're ultimately not using their insurance during that time frame. Surest removes that problem by eliminating the deductible and puts the power in the consumers' hands to make choices for their own care.

He added that the Net Promoter Score for Surest is higher than its other commercial plan offerings. Consumers report satisfaction with the digital experience and the cost certainty that the plan presents, Clark said.

"It's very much like the Amazon shopping experience, but for healthcare, which hasn't happened before," Clark said.

Surest also aims to personalize the member experience by leaning on AI and other digital tools to present data on providers and costs that may be of use to the user, while leaving the ultimate decision in their hands.

"The consumer is in charge with their doctor in making those decisions," Clark said. "But we want to make sure that we are giving you, similar to what you would see with any shopping app, a personalized recommendation of what is a good fit."

Clark said that starting his career on the actuarial side of healthcare made plain how much of a challenge affordability can be, and that drew him to pursue his current path. Prior to UnitedHealthcare, he served as an associate partner at McKinsey and as an actuary at Cigna.

Through Surest, he believes he can help lead the charge toward a more modern healthcare system, he said.

As for the future of Surest, the first piece is continuing to make sure the team delivers on a personalized and simple-to-understand experience for consumers. The next level is building on that consumer-focused experience to add a provider layer, and work with them on the quality and value scores that are surfaced to members.

For example, a provider may consistently recommend a patient seek an MRI scan at a hospital rather than at a lower-cost lab. And Surest is gaining the "critical mass" of members where providers actively want to have these conversations, Clark said.

"I see that as sort of the next evolution beyond working with consumers and members: moving to providers as well," he said.
