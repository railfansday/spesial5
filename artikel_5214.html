70.5 Million POL Heist Triggers Polygon’s Hard Fork Proposal

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Home » Altcoins » 70.5 Million POL Heist Triggers Polygon’s Hard Fork Proposal

A controversial governance proposal requires a hard fork to retrieve the looted balance in two separate incidents.

Two previous hacking incidents on Polygon’s Proof Of Stake (PoS) chain have looted nearly 70.50 million tokens, worth approximately $6.5 million at the current rates. A fresh hard-fork proposal from a member of the Polygon Labs Validator Support Team is looking to retrieve the sum via new amendments.

The idea is to move them from the attackers’ addresses into recovery wallets designated and verified by Binance & Polymarket, the platforms where the Polygon (POL) related accidents took place. On 29th and 30th October 2026, Polymarket and Binance each dealt with separate POL-tied hacking instances.

The block-producing POL nodes are presently restricting these funds from traveling across the blockchain. However, if this Polygon (POL) dev proposal gets granted, an extreme measure is expected to be taken: the frozen funds are sent back to a recovery address that Polymarket & Binance designated & verified.

The 70.50 million proposal is scheduled for discussion during the Polygon Protocol Governance Call on November 5. If approved, developers expect to complete client development and testnet rehearsals within approximately two to three weeks. The proposal guarantees there’s not one new POL token created & supply stays fixed.

During the Polymarket-related incident on October 29, 2026, two malicious POL transfers were indicated, moving a combined 3,282,275.71 POL between addresses. The PoS network’s node validators managed to block four addresses containing roughly 24 million POL. This POL returning coordinated transfer needs to be done at a particular block size.

That’s what makes the Polygon developer (POL) offer rather difficult to carry out. Intriguingly,  the attacker also reportedly contacted validators and offered cash payments in exchange for help removing the restrictions. Whichever way this plays out, the proposal raises a crucial question about how far a decentralized network should go to recover stolen coins.

With cyberattacks getting more common, it’s important to implement procedures to retrieve the stolen funds along with precautionary steps to prevent cybercrime.
