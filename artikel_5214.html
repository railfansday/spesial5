Bitcoin surged above $81,000 after the U.S. midterm elections before quickly reversing lower.

Hot October inflation data added fresh pressure as Bitcoin slipped below $74,000.

Historical midterm data suggests Bitcoin could still recover after its initial weakness.

After the November 2026 U.S. midterm elections, Bitcoin briefly climbed above $81,000, but the rally quickly faded. While it initially held up well after Democrats sweep of Congress, but the tech stock sell-off  quickly wiped out its gainsBTC has since dropped to $73,700, leaving traders focused on what the election outcome really means for Bitcoin and crypto

On the day of the US midterm election, BTC closed near $77,435, but the initial reaction was not bullish. As early results pointed toward Democrats taking control of both the House and Senate, Bitcoin slipped to an intraday low near $75,649.

The selling did not last long. Once the election result became clearer, traders moved back into Bitcoin calls, helping BTC stage a sharp V-shaped recovery. The buying pushed Bitcoin to a post-election high of $81,196.

By November 5, Bitcoin had fallen back toward $75,942 as U.S. technology stocks suffered a sharp sell-off. Over the weekend and into November 9, BTC continued trading between $74,500 and $76,100.

The latest decline came after October U.S. CPI data showed inflation rising 0.4% month over month, above the 0.3% forecast. Core CPI also came in at 0.3% against expectations of 0.2%. Bitcoin fell toward $73,400 after the report.

The biggest problem for Bitcoin is that the election removed one source of uncertainty, but it did not remove the overall pressure.

A Democratic majority in Congress could make it harder for major pro-crypto proposals to move forward, including renewed efforts around the CLARITY Act and a potential Strategic Bitcoin Reserve.

The election outcome may also affect Bitcoin miners moving into AI. According to CoinGeek, new Democratic governors in Texas, Georgia, and New Mexico have pledged to pause or stop data center projects. This could make it harder for miners to expand their AI businesses.

However, Core Scientific plans to exit mining by Q4 2026, while IREN expects mining to become a small part of its business in 2027

That leaves Bitcoin in a difficult position. Now, the focus has shifted from the election result to whether Bitcoin can reclaim the levels it lost after the rally.

The fading rally does not necessarily mean Bitcoin’s post-midterm cycle is over. Coinpedia news reports that Bitcoin gained during the 12 months following the previous three U.S. midterm elections. BTC rose 24.5% after the 2014 election, 44.9% after 2018, and 92.3% after 2022.

Previous cycles often saw Bitcoin find a major bottom after the midterm election rather than immediately before it.

Historical data shows bottoms came 71 days after the 2014 election, 39 days after 2018, and 13 days after 2022. In 2026, however, Bitcoin already reached around $57,718 in July, well before the November election.

For now, Bitcoin’s post-election rally has lost steam. However, historical data shows that Bitcoin has often found a bottom 13 to 71 days after midterm elections before starting to recover. This suggests the market’s next move may not be clear in the first few days
