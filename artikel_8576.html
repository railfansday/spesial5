TurboTax Experts Review – In-Person Tax Prep at the Online Price, Plus Per-Form Fees

Book TurboTax Experts if you want a tax expert to prepare and sign your return face to face and one of its 500-plus offices is near you. It costs what TurboTax’s experts charge online, but the starting price covers one W-2 and a Form 1040, so get every form priced before the expert starts.

TurboTax built its name on software you fill in yourself. Its offices work the other way around. You sit down with a TurboTax expert and hand over your documents, and the expert prepares the return, reviews it with you, signs it as the preparer, and files it.

Intuit, founded in 1983 and based in Mountain View, California, added 200 TurboTax offices for the 2026 filing season, and its office finder now lists more than 500. TurboTax customers gave Expert Full Service 4.8 out of 5 in more than 108,000 reviews from 2026, posted on TurboTax’s own site. The Better Business Bureau gives Intuit an F, citing 35 complaints the company didn’t answer and 32 it didn’t resolve, out of more than 4,600 filed against Intuit in the past three years.

TurboTax sells this as Expert Full Service, the product it also offers online, with the meeting moved to an office.

TurboTax Expert offices take appointments only and stay open year-round. The office finder lists more than 500, from 72 in California to one each in states such as Alaska, Maine, and Oregon, and none in Vermont or Wyoming. TurboTax counts an in-person expert as local when the office is within 50 miles of your ZIP code, and if you’d rather meet online, it matches you with an expert in your state.

TurboTax stores take walk-ins as well as appointments. You can drop in with a question, start your return yourself with an expert’s guidance, or hand it off. Intuit’s February 2026 announcement listed stores in six metro areas: New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, Seattle, Dallas, and Phoenix. Hours vary by location, and TurboTax says its local service runs nights and weekends during tax season.

Every return starts at a federal base price that covers one W-2 and one Form 1040, and in person you pay from the same price list as online. TurboTax’s Full Service price list sets that base at $129 late in the season and after April 1, and at $89 early in the season, without saying when early season ends. Each state return adds $69, or $49 early. Most other forms carry a fee of their own.

By our math, a homeowner with a mortgage and one W-2 who itemizes deductions and files one state return pays $318 late in the season. That’s the $129 base plus $40 for the mortgage interest form, $80 for Schedule A, and $69 for the state. Early in the season, the same return costs $258.

Your expert gives you an estimate before starting, based on what you report and the documents you upload, and confirms any change with a final price before filing. You pay nothing until the expert files, and you can leave at any point before you pay. Paying the fee out of your refund can add $40 more.

TurboTax doesn’t publish the credentials of the expert you’ll get. Its blog says its expert community includes CPAs, enrolled agents (tax specialists the IRS licenses), and other experienced tax experts, so the person preparing your return may hold no credential at all. The years of experience on its pages are self-reported.

The credential matters if the IRS questions your return. Under IRS rules, CPAs, enrolled agents, and attorneys can represent you on any matter, while other preparers can do so only in limited cases or not at all. You can look up a preparer’s credential in the IRS’s Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers.

TurboTax matches you with an expert by your location and tax situation. You can’t pick one by name, but you can ask for a new match at any time, and you can request the same expert next year if they still work for Intuit and are available.

TurboTax’s Expert Full Service Guarantee covers the expert’s work. If your expert’s error as the signed preparer leads to a penalty or interest charge from the IRS or your state, TurboTax pays it, though you still owe any extra tax. If another preparer later files an amended return that gets you a bigger refund or a smaller tax bill, TurboTax refunds what you paid for Full Service.

TurboTax also guarantees its calculations, your maximum refund, and audit support for the seven-year life of the return. Audit support means one-on-one answers from a tax professional, and TurboTax won’t represent you before the IRS. For representation, you’d buy Audit Defense, a separate add-on from TaxResources, Inc.

The first meeting is a free consultation, in person or online, where an expert reviews your situation and answers your questions with no commitment to file. Filing with TurboTax then takes five steps.

Bring a government-issued ID for each person on the return if you plan to file at the appointment. Same-day filing needs every document uploaded first and depends on the expert’s schedule.

After you file, the office stays your tax contact all year, for consultations, prior-year returns, amended returns, and IRS notices. An amended return on Form 1040-X carries no form fee.

TurboTax sells the same service online, where your expert works with you by phone, message, or one-way video, in which you see the expert but the expert can’t see you. The price list and guarantees don’t change, so the office is worth the trip only when a face-to-face meeting matters to you.

TurboTax’s own software lets you file a simple return free or fill in a harder one yourself, and its Expert Assist tiers add an expert who answers questions and reviews the return before you file.

H&R Block starts in-office preparation at $99 plus a state fee, against TurboTax’s $129 late-season base plus its own. Its offices also take walk-ins, which TurboTax leaves to its stores. Choosing between TurboTax and H&R Block also comes down to their software and online help, not only their offices.

IRS-certified volunteers at Volunteer Income Tax Assistance sites prepare returns for free, generally for people who make about $70,244 or less, though each site may set its own limit. Every return they prepare gets a quality review before it’s filed.

Sole proprietors can use TurboTax Experts, since Schedule C is part of the personal return, but an S corporation’s or a partnership’s own return goes to a separate product, TurboTax Experts for Business.

TurboTax Experts puts a tax preparer across the desk for what TurboTax charges for the same work online. You see a quote before the expert starts, pay only once the return is filed, and have TurboTax cover the penalties its expert’s errors cause, from an office that stays open after April.

It’s a poor fit for a simple return that free software or a volunteer site would handle, and for anyone who wants a CPA or an enrolled agent by name. Book the free consultation if an office is near you, and ask for the price with every form you’ll file before the expert starts.
