FAA Lifts Multi-Hour Nationwide Ground Stop Linked To "Instability" Of Radar & ATC Communications

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

* Last Updated: 10:05 PM EST November 13, 2026 by Channing Reid

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) lifted a rare nationwide ground stop that halted operations at all airports late Friday. The ground stop was implemented by the agency due to communication failures affecting "multiple" air traffic control centers. Citing extraordinary circumstances and safety concerns, the FAA suspended all departures in the multi-hour ground stop. International arrivals were handled individually while technicians investigated the disruption.

The FAA said "communication issues" impacted air traffic control (ATC) centers around the country on Friday. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said late Friday that the cause of the ground stop was related to “instability" regarding radar and communications in the "TRACONS throughout the country.” Aviation watchdog JonNYC reported that the halt was not a “9/11 type of event,” which required flights to be forced to land at the closest airport available.

Simple Flying's Flight Tracker captured ATC first communicating to pilots that all departures had been stopped.

The FAA lifted the nationwide ground stop at around 8:30 PM EST. All flights are allowed to resume, but some warn it could take days before airlines can restore their normal schedules. The agency confirmed the lift in a statement around 10:00 PM EST.

"The safety of the flying public is always the FAA’s highest priority," it said. "The FAA lifted the ground stop and some flight departures are resuming. Before clearing flights to resume, we thoroughly tested our systems to confirm they were stable and that operations remain reliable."

Multiple sources have reported that the FAA's telecommunication infrastructure rebooted on Friday due to an update to a private communications network that handles connections between the US' nationwide ATC system. Amid the reboot, the system crashed, resulting in a communications outage, according to CBS News Political Reporter Olivia Rinaldi. The system is reportedly operated by L3Harris – the same company responsible for the security overhaul of the Air Force One bridge Boeing 747-8.

International flights are slowly resuming. According to Flightradar24, some eastbound transatlantic flights have been cleared to depart from airports across the Northeast.

The first flight from Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD) also departed around 7:15 PM EST. Despite the gradual resumption, the ground stop still remains in effect, meaning all domestic flights have not been cleared to resume.

There is no evidence that the Microsoft outage that affected millions of people worldwide on Thursday is related to the ground stop.

"Microsoft has no indication that the FAA nationwide ground stop is related to the recent service disruption," an FAA spokesperson said in a statement obtained by NBC News.

Several major airports around the country issued statements and notices to passengers about the ground stop. Officials at Miami International Airport (MIA) told passengers to stay away from the airport until they confirm their flight's status with their airline.

"MIA is coordinating closely with airlines and safety and security partners to monitor evolving conditions, support affected passengers, and maintain operational readiness," the airport said in a statement. "Passengers are strongly urged NOT to come to MIA until they have confirmed directly with their airline that their flight is operating and have received updated travel instructions."

Friday's ground stop is the second nationwide halt since the September 11, 2001 attacks. The last time such action occurred was in early 2023 when the FAA's Notice to Air Missions (NOTAM) system failed. All departures were grounded for about an hour and a half before resuming. Investigators said that damage to a database file may have been the cause of the system failure.

Law enforcement officials say the ground stop is not the result of a cyberattack. However, personnel from the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) around the country are monitoring the situation, according to CNN.

Friday's ground halt comes on the heels of the FAA rolling out a new AI system to manage air traffic. While it is not clear that the telecommunications outage was directly related, the agency has been criticized for implementing the system.

It is known as the Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories (SMART), and "analyzes airline schedules, weather, airport capacity, airspace conditions, and operational constraints to predict traffic airflows and identify potential conflicts before they occur," according to the FAA.

All airborne aircraft were allowed to continue flying until further notice. According to FlightAware, more than 6,500 flights are currently halted.

The FAA confirmed that the ground stop was implemented "out of an abundance of caution" in a statement.

"There has been a temporary telecommunications outage - out of an abundance of caution all flights have been halted. Every flight has been accounted for and international flights will be handled on a case by case basis."

Flightradar24 is reporting that more than 1,000 flights had already been canceled by 6:00 PM. Cirium data indicates that more than 11,000 flights were still set to depart across the US on Friday after the ground stop was put in place, so cancellations are expected to increase significantly as the evening progresses.

Ground controllers will also face growing difficulty as aircraft continue to land and none depart, putting pressure on already stressed infrastructure and increasingly scarce gate availability.
