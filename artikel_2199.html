Celtic AGM latest as O'Neill talks long-term future and tense CSC visit

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

For Martin O'Neill, it has already been a hectic week. On Monday evening, he attended a meeting at Greenock Celtic Supporters' Club alongside interim chairman Brian Wilson, chief executive Michael Nicholson, and chief financial officer Chris McKay. Over 120 fans were present as the Celtic manager and board faced, according to the club, 'hard but fair' questioning.

Less than 24 hours later, O'Neill found himself in Dingwall with assistant coaches Mark Fotheringham and Stephen McManus watching Celtic B lose 2-0 to Ross County in the Scottish Challenge Cup.

That's all before two crucial home fixtures against Besiktas in the Europa League and Kilmarnock in the William Hill Premiership come into the fray.

The 74-year-old sat down ahead of the tie with Besiktas to discuss Monday's meeting, his long-term future, and the club's upcoming Annual General Meeting (AGM).

"First of all, I wasn't really aware of the meeting, so it was kind of an ad hoc thing," O'Neill explained. "But I obviously was around. I spoke to Michael [Nicholson] and he said that they were going to meet.

"And I said, well, I wouldn't mind going down to it if that was the case, obviously to experience what the fans would be asking.

"The meeting itself was, I think you could use the word robust, yes, firm, but very orderly questioning.

"And trying to give the answers that, not necessarily what people are looking for, because I'm not so sure that will ever reach a satisfactory stage for a while anyway. But, yeah, it was really interesting and an experience.

"I probably didn't really need to go to a meeting, but these people were big Celtic fans. And naturally, I think both board and supporters, I thought there was a wanting the best for the football club.

"At the end of the day, there are bad times and I think that you should face up to that. Was everything answered by the board and myself to their satisfaction? One, you'd have to ask the people there at the time what was there.

"There was no shouting. It wasn't like the AGM that appeared [last year]. The shouting had taken place before the meeting."

Indeed, as O'Neill and company arrived in Greenock, a number of Celtic supporters wearing balaclavas were outside the supporters' club, voicing their own concerns.

"To me, I think that was the saddest thing," O'Neill continued. "Would I have ever, in any day, ever thought that Celtic fans would be clothed in balaclavas before a meeting? Even if they were disapproving of what was happening. I think that's really disappointing.

"I do find it hard to get my head round that kind of stuff.

"I think if you've got a dispute or something, I think you should be brave enough to show your face doing it.

"[It was] just on the way in. I don't know whether they expected me to be there or not, but it doesn't really matter. It was just regardless of who was there."

The hope is that this year's AGM doesn't entail similar hostilities. The last iteration was adjourned early following animosity between the hierarchy and supporters.

It's understood the 2026 AGM will take place in early December in person.

"I would expect to be at it as a manager," O'Neill said. "So if given an opportunity to answer some of the things and if they want to hear from the manager, I would have no problem.

"I hope it's similar to what Monday night was in terms of the questions and fairness and not falling into what it was last year."

As O'Neill discusses myriad off-pitch matters, it's easy to forget he's Celtic's football manager.

"People are asking me now if it’s something I’m going to continue to do," he added. "No, my main job depends on results and winning some football matches. That’s the most important thing."

Contracted at Celtic Park until the end of this season, O'Neill's deal has the option of a further 12 months.

This time last year, he was two games into what was meant to be the most fleeting of interim spells.

Wilfried Nancy's disastrous reign came and went and O'Neill returned in January, winning the double and staying on as permanent manager in the summer.

When asked if this was his final season in charge, O'Neill responded: "I can’t imagine anybody asking me back. Seriously, I can’t imagine that happening.

"Listen, I didn’t come in here today to give a final word at this minute."

If the coming months are to be his last, how important is it to go out on a high?

"It would be nice to see the club that I knew intimately from 2000 – having followed from a distance since my school days – be successful.

"Did I ever think I’d see a day when 75,000 people would descend on a UEFA Cup final day, with no arrests? There are still pictures on the wall here and all you see is a sea of green and white. To see that again, that would be your dream."

Celtic, though, could not be further away from reaching another European final. Sunday's defeat to Falkirk was their sixth in 10 matches, with Besiktas up next in the Europa League.

From their first three fixtures in the competition, Celtic are pointless, heightening criticism on O'Neill and his coaches.

"Everyone is a tactician now, that’s the whole point," he said. "Everybody is a football manager. There are people who have got jobs in football now – at football clubs – because they played PlayStation Manager.

"Honestly, it’s extraordinary. But it does not bother me, honestly, it doesn’t. People talk about tactics but in my semi-defence, no-one talked about tactics when we won the seven games to win the league [last season]. Or the 14 games we won before we got beat.

"And you know what, you’re right to think – if you got beaten in a game – that maybe somebody has out-thought you. Absolutely. You’re right to think that.

"[Falkirk manager] John McGlynn has every right to be proud of his side after beating us and to have had the upper hand."

Despite recent form, O'Neill remains more determined than ever to turn things around.

"I'm supposed to be the driver of the club," he quipped.

That said, the 74-year-old is well aware of the threat Besiktas, who sit eighth in the 36-team Europa League table, pose.

"I think these games are really hard for us," O'Neill said. "I think there are teams in the Europa League who are capable of playing in the Champions League and getting results in the Champions League at this minute. However, we're in the Europa League.

"We haven't got a point on the board. We're going to try and do something about it if we can on Thursday and then ask the question.

"But I think that no-one has ever lost the first three and qualified for the competition. So, we’ll be up against it. We're definitely up against it. But are we capable? Yeah, I have to believe that."
