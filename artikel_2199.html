Celtic's 'Che Guevaras' should show their fellow fans some respect at this year's AGM

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

There is about as much chance of Dermot Desmond taking a seat amongst the Union Bears ultras group the next time Celtic play Rangers at Ibrox in January as there is of the billionaire Irish financier rocking up at their AGM at Parkhead next month.

Desmond, who has been the Glasgow club’s principal shareholder for more than two decades, has not attended the gathering since way back in 2006 and he is not, with disaffection about the stewardship of the Scottish champions still rife in the stands, about to break with tradition.

The greeting which chief executive Michael Nicholson, chief financial officer Chris McKay, interim chairman Brian Wilson and manager Martin O’Neill received from some protesters when they attended a question-and-answer session at the Greenock Supporters’ Club this week gave an indication of the sort of treatment he would receive if he did.

Speaking at his pre-match press conference ahead of the Europa League encounter with Besiktas on Thursday night, O’Neill expressed his dismay at the fact that around 25 supporters appeared wearing face coverings. “That was the saddest thing,” he said. “I would never have thought that fans would be clothed in balaclavas at a meeting.”

The frustration in the statement which the Celtic Fans Collective, an umbrella coalition of supporters’ clubs and organisations, published this week demanding that Desmond make a rare appearance at the meeting was palpable and understandable.

That the man who, rightly or wrongly but unquestionably, wields the most power at Parkhead is an annual no-show highlights everything that is wrong with how the former European Cup winners are being run. Are accountability and dialogue too much to ask for?

Is the famously publicity-shy man from Macroom in County Cork, though, really going to trap when the same individuals who hurled tennis balls at him when he was playing golf in the Alfred Dunhill Championship at St Andrews last month are likely to be in attendance?

There has been fevered speculation about when Celtic directors will face their shareholders in recent weeks. Supporters are desperate to know what their long-term football strategy is, who has been driving recruitment, how governance is going to be modernised, why tens of millions of pounds continue to sit in the bank and much else besides.

But are they likely to get answers to those questions now the AGM date has been set for Tuesday, December 1? The actions of a certain sub-section of their support in Inverclyde in midweek suggested that it could well be another bad-tempered, ugly and ultimately futile get-together.

Celtic interim chairman Brian Wilson, left, chief executive Michael Nicholson and chief financial officer Chris McKay at Parkhead (Image: Stuart Wallace / Shutterstock)

Ill-feeling about then-chairman Peter Lawwell bringing last year's annual meeting to a premature end amid persistent disorder still lingers. It has been suggested once again in recent days that the move was a cynical ploy by the besieged board to avoid public scrutiny.

However, David Low, the financial analyst who was a driving force behind the Fergus McCann takeover back in 1994 and is now associated with the Celtic Supporters Limited pressure group, rejected that interpretation of events when he appeared on the A Celtic State of Mind podcast last week.

“You are allowed to hold the people who shareholders elected to account once a year,” he said. “But it is a professional environment. Last year’s meeting was a bun fight. Nobody came out of that looking well. It is a professional forum and you have to respect the forum you are in.

“Give them red cards if you have to if they give an unsatisfactory answer. But listen to what is said and then react. That is the way it has always been at Celtic, even when it was a private company.

“That was a horrible meeting last year. People go there who want to learn. That is why they are there. You turn up, you ask questions and you try to learn something. If you don’t like it you can boo and do whatever within reason. But you have got to listen.

“It disintegrated into a shambles last year. It was over-the-top. There was a lot of unacceptable behaviour from the hardcore. It was not on. It was the biggest AGM in 20 years.”

There has been widespread unhappiness about the course Celtic are on for some time and a substantial section of the support wants meaningful change in how the Parkhead club is run. But some of the more vocal and militant members of the rebel alliance can be arrogant and selfish.

They did not merely show disrespect to the board last year. They showed disrespect to the hundreds of fellow shareholders who had turned up, many from far afield, wanting to exercise the same right to scrutinise those running their club.

The club later alleged that its head of safety, security and operations, Mark Hargreaves, had been threatened and intimidated by two prominent members of the Green Brigade. Whatever the truth of those allegations, it was a sorry affair from start to premature finish.

Celtic fans hold up banners referencing their teams failures against teams in Europe (Image: Andrew Milligan)

One lifelong fan voiced his exasperation at the leaders of the civil war that was raging at his beloved club in these pages as he attended the Scottish Gas Scottish Cup game against Dundee back in February.

“We don't need modern-day Che Guevaras,” he said before he ignored the boycott that had been called. “We want people who love Celtic.” He is not alone in taking that view.

The Celtic Fans Collective appealed for the “focus to be firmly on scrutiny rather than protest” at the AGM in their latest missive. They are right. The board should be made to answer searching questions. Those who shout directors down before their answers can be heard are serving neither their cause nor their fellow supporters.

It would perhaps help the Parkhead club to move forward on and off the park if those words were heeded and there were no disruptions from “aggressive and irrational factions” hellbent on “stirring up toxicity”.
