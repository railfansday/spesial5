Bitcoin dropped to $61,291 on November 20, extending a slide that started when US stocks peaked on October 6. Since that day, the largest crypto has lost 28%, while the Nasdaq Composite is down 13.5% and the S&P 500 8.2% through Thursday’s close.

On Thursday alone, the day Anthropic listed on Nasdaq, the S&P 500 fell 2% for its sixth straight loss, and BTC slipped under $62,000.

The two markets are falling in step, and that wasn’t always the case. We measured how closely BTC and the Nasdaq have moved day to day since 2015. For its first years, Bitcoin went its own way. Since 2020, it has traded more and more like a tech stock, and over the past 40 trading days the link has been stronger than in any full calendar year.

So which stocks are hurting most, and why does crypto now fall with them?

The biggest names held up far better. Microsoft is down 6%, Tesla 2%, Amazon is flat and Apple is up 5%.

Nvidia is at the center of the AI slide. CEO Jensen Huang resigned last week amid a personal scandal, and the chipmaker has lost $735 billion in market value since it broke, the Associated Press reported. Worries that AI stocks had run too high added to the pressure. So did new US limits on remote access to advanced chips and talk of curbs on new data centers after this month’s midterm elections. “The market was already worried about this risk before what happened at Nvidia,” Zhiwei Zhang, chief economist at Pinpoint Asset Management, said.

Rates aren’t helping either. The 10-year Treasury yield sits above 5%, and inflation is running at 3.1%.

To answer whether crypto and stocks always moved together, we compared BTC’s daily price changes with those of the Nasdaq Composite, year by year since 2015. The measure, called correlation, runs from 1 (always moving together) to 0 (no link) and -1 (always moving in opposite directions).

So the answer is no. Crypto and stocks didn’t always move together. It’s a habit that started in 2020 and has grown stronger this year.

On the daily chart, BTC trades at $61,859. That is below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $74,655, and its 200-day EMA at $75,470.

As long as Bitcoin keeps moving with the Nasdaq, the next clue for BTC may come from tech stocks, starting with Nvidia. A calmer week for AI shares would give $58,500 a better chance to hold. Another leg down in tech would likely drag crypto with it.
