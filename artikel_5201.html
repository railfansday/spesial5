Ethereum Foundation Sounds Alarm Over AI Threats

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Ethereum Foundation has been working for years to secure the network against a future in which quantum computing breaks the cryptography underpinning Ethereum, but it is now facing a new and more immediate concern around artificial intelligence.

AI is identifying bugs in live software more quickly than human reviewers can keep up with, and the Ethereum Foundation has now doubled the size of its protocol security team, according to a recent announcement.

As Kev Wedderburn wrote in a post from Devcon, AI models are now identifying thousands of bugs in the Linux kernel, one of the most scrutinized codebases in the world.

For Ethereum developers, the immediate challenge is ensuring that bugs are not present in their software before an upgrade.

The longer-term challenge is mathematical. Ethereum uses digital signatures to identify who can spend funds and which validators have approved the state of the chain.

Wedderburn says that AI's advances in analyzing decades-old algorithms raise questions about the mathematical assumptions underlying those signatures that researchers should consider.

It is not clear whether AI could break Ethereum's existing cryptography. However,  the Ethereum Foundation wants work on new cryptography to be underway.

After more than a year of considering various signature schemes, the Ethereum Foundation has decided to replace elliptic-curve signatures with hash-based cryptography.

The argument is that Ethereum already relies on cryptographic hash security, and by using hashes for signatures too, it can remove an additional assumption about the difficulty of solving mathematical problems associated with elliptic curves.

The proposal affects three areas of Ethereum.

It would see validators use hash-based keys for their signatures, which in turn changes how block finality is determined on the network.

Wallet developers would need to provide users with a way to transition their accounts to hash-based signatures.

And Ethereum's change to the cryptography used for commitments to data (blobs) that rollups publish to the network would require adjustments from rollups too.

One challenge is the size of the signatures. Hash-based signatures are much larger, so developers need to consider how that affects the hardware needed to run a node at home.

Staking pools and multisig wallets would need adjustments too. So would smart contracts that rely on existing cryptography.

There will also be a need to adjust tools that rely on the signature scheme currently used by Ethereum validators (BLS).
