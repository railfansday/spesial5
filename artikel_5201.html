The AI Compute Race report prices GPU compute outside the biggest buyers: a three-year commitment cuts the median price by 48%, while 17 of 22 tracked GPU models rose in price over the past year

DALLAS, Texas – November 13, 2026 – Fluence has published The AI Compute Race, a report that prices GPU cloud compute for every company that is not a frontier lab or a hyperscaler. It was produced with research partners NEAR AI, io.net, Boundless, Phala, Gensyn, Fortytwo, Theta, Compute Labs and GAIB. Its central finding: where a company buys a GPU-hour sets the price. Across H100, H200 and B200 models, hyperscalers list GPUs at 1.98 times the GPU-cloud median, from 1.4 times on the B200 to 2.8 times on the H100. The same H200 lists from $4.08 to $10.60 an hour.

Commitment length matters almost as much as provider choice. Against each provider’s own on-demand rate, a one-year reservation cuts the median price by 21% and a three-year term by 48%. Spot capacity lists at 53% of on-demand, for workloads that can be interrupted.

Buyers who expect GPU prices to fall may need to recheck their budgets. Seventeen of the 22 GPU models tracked by GetDeploying cost more than a year earlier, and the median H100 rents for 13% to 30% more, depending on the window measured.

The report also traces why prices are rapidly rising:

“Open-weight models are close behind the best closed ones, and companies are switching to them. Any company with GPUs can now serve a near-frontier model on hardware it controls. The GPUs are the hard part,” said Evgeny Ponomarev, Co-founder of Fluence.

The report closes with four ways to pay less for the same compute: compare the same chip across provider types, commit when the workload is steady, match the GPU generation to the model, and look beyond the hyperscalers, since much of the newest neocloud capacity is contracted to labs years ahead.

The full report is available at fluence.ai/compute-race-2026.
