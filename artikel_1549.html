How Arsenal are evolving their build-up play

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Arsenal have evolved how they attack this season. I think there have been quite a few improvements in the way Arsenal seek to create danger that haven’t necessarily shown up on the score board so far. This is an especially sensitive subject this week after draws with Newcastle and Borussia Dortmund- games where Arsenal took deserved one goal leads but failed to build on them.

However, the failure to add true ceiling raising talent in attack this summer- despite heavy interest in Morgan Rogers, Vinicius Junior and Julian Alvarez- has led to Mikel Arteta needing to coach his way out of a tight spot. The manager has made some alterations to his materials to make Arsenal’s final third play less austere this season. Here are some ways Arteta has evolved the team’s approach play.

Rotations and fluidity Under Mikel Arteta we are accustomed to seeing the left-back enjoy a role based on improvisation and freedom. Last season, we began to see Jurrien Timber take up some very advanced roles from right-back. This stands to reason because full-backs are not typically assigned a marker by the opposition, so it’s easier for full-backs to pop up hither and thither in advanced areas without detection.

That license has been extended this season. Let’s look at some examples just from the Newcastle game. David Raya launches a long pass here and *squints* who is that playing the role of target man and bringing the long ball down at centre-forward? By God THAT’S EBERECHI EZE’S MUSIC?!

And look who that is sprinting up beside him in the number 10 position but right-back Jurrien Timber. Madueke has a shot and hits the post from this build-up. Look at where Timber is when Madueke takes the shot.

Then we see Odegaard at left-centre-back here (sure, what the hell?) and whose buccaneering forward run is that he is picking out?

Why it’s Riccardo Calafiori. Spotting where Jurrien Timber is when Arsenal have shots on goal has become a favourite pastime of mine. Where’s Jurrien in this shot?

Here he is running down the left-wing to tee Havertz up for a shot.

This is how the move starts for the Eze goal. Konsa at right-back, Timber at centre-back, Calafiori in central midfield.

When Konsa clips the ball forwards, Madueke has shown for the ball centrally like a number 9 while Havertz has pulled into Madueke’s position. Calafiori continues his run like a horse trotting along without a jockey. You get the message by now, Madueke’s goal in the second half sees Timber make a pass to Havertz in left central midfield.

One of Arsenal’s first shots of the season is created by Tzolis drifting over to the right wing and we had seen Saka manipulated into all sorts of shooting positions prior to his injury. Tzolis was also frustratingly caught offside at St. James’ Park when he had drifted over to the right wing. Hincapie spent a lot of time in central midfield against Borussia Dortmund. Arsenal are a far more fluid outfit this season.

This sense of shapeshifting is reflected in midfield. Rice and Odegaard pretty much always start, the identity of the third midfielder can be any one of Bruno, Lewis Skelly, Zubimendi and Merino, all of whom are high level players with very different qualities. Just the identity of those names gives you face value options.

Declan Rice leads the Premier League for assists this season and all of them have been from open play. That alone tells you something different is happening. Roles in midfield are less defined. There were probably times last season when the Rice and Zubimendi combination became a little too ‘side to side’ and not cutting enough. Spending lots of money on 28-year-old Bruno tells you what Arteta felt his midfield was missing last season- risk and thrust.

Bruno expanded on this in the pre-Dortmund press conference, he spoke about the midfield at Arsenal being far less defined positionally, where Rice, Odegaard and AN Other (usually Bruno) will freelance between the 6, 8 and 10 positions. ‘It’s very different. When I was at Newcastle, you play more positional, keep more your position. Here it’s a little bit more free.

‘If you see the game against Newcastle, they were marking us man to man. You see JT [Jurrien Timber] plays a six, an eight and a number nine sometimes, so you have to move a lot. Of course, I was not used to it. So now I understand a little bit more what he wants for the midfield.’ I expanded on midfield a little more in my column from two weeks ago.

Arsenal have not been able to land a genuinely elite attacker in what feels like years now. Fortunately, Arsenal have a manager who might be the best in the trade at this moment in time. As well as moving to a more freestyle approach all over the pitch, there have been signs of improvement in individuals.

Firstly, Noni Madueke seems to be thriving on the opportunities afforded him by Saka’s recent injury. He looks more confident, his actions have greater clarity and Arsenal have treated Madueke as if he is Saka, that is to say, they give him the ball at every opportunity and rely on Noni’s capacity for repetition.

He is averaging 3.28 shots per 90 in the Premier League this season compared to 1.85 per 90 last season. (He is also averaging fewer crosses per 90 this season which implies an increasing willingness to vary his play and drive at goal as often as he does the by-line). His pass completion rate has jumped by 10% on last season.

The goal against Dortmund saw Madueke cushion a long pass, kill it perfectly and shift it to Gyokeres, who turns and lashes the ball home from 25 yards. That is not a goal description I could have envisaged in September. Gyokeres started the season very badly indeed.

Lewis touched on Gyokeres’ increased creativity this season during the international break. It looks to me as though there has been a focus on him being allowed to offload the ball after one or two touches. You don’t really want the ball getting bogged down with Gyokeres, the more touches he requires the more his technical limitations will show.

Gyokeres gets the ball from a Raya kick here and Madueke is nice and close to him and running in behind.

It means Gyokeres can take one touch and then play in behind. We see something very similar for the chance he creates for Havertz away at Brighton, Havertz is already moving when the ball comes to Gyokeres and it means he can take one touch and hook it in behind.

Then there is his lovely one-touch assist for Declan Rice in Munich where, again, he has a runner very close to him and it allows him to play the ball first time.

Arsenal have also been working on getting Eze in behind and through on goal, look at his position here when Odegaard plays a through ball in Munich.

And look at where he is when he slots Havertz’s lovely slide-rule pass at St. James’ Park.

Getting Eze in on the shoulder of the opposition right-back is another move we are seeing bear fruit sporadically. This fits into a wider theme I touched on a fortnight ago about Arsenal going in behind more often and more quickly this season.
