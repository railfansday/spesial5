I keep reading that it's difficult for the seller to legally keep the earnest money, even if you really do just walk away. With that much money it would be worth a quick call to a real estate lawyer to find out if you're really likely to lose the money.
OP, I think there is something to this approach.

Quick story, a friend was selling their home (in central NJ) a couple of years ago. Like you, the buyers waived inspections and appraisal. However, the buyer got cold feet and notified their intention to back out. Our friends talked to their lawyer about keeping the deposit (a similar amount to what you put down and a similar house cost). As I recall, their lawyer said something along the lines of "I've been doing this for 30 years and never seen any seller successfully keep a deposit even under similar circumstances...Do you really want to be in litigation for a long time and stuck holding the house while this plays out in court or do you want/need to sell? If you want/need to sell, give them their money back and move on". They let the buyers out and moved on to another buyer.

IANAL, while these things seem cut and dry - you waived all logical ways to walk - they rarely are. I think the option of tying this up in court for a long time while the sellers have decided they want to sell is going to be a significant motivator for the sellers to move on.
