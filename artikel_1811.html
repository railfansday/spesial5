Man City got ‘deserved’ draw, but leave Allegri with ‘bitter taste’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Massimiliano Allegri admitted Napoli left the Etihad Stadium "with a bitter taste in our mouths" after twice giving up a lead against Manchester City in the Champions League.

Napoli have won just once in Europe this season but hit the front through former Manchester United striker Rasmus Hojlund before Ayyoub Bouaddi levelled things shortly after.

Noa Lang restored Napoli's advantage with a volley at the end of a lovely flowing move, but Rayan Cherki found a way through in the 86th minute, meaning the Partenopei had to settle for a point.

Napoli have won just one of their last five Champions League games in which they have led (D2 L2), while they have failed to win nine successive away matches in the competition for the very first time (D2 L7).

The point means that Napoli remain 27th in the league-phase table, outside the play-off places on goal difference after five games.

Allegri was largely impressed by their performance, particularly what they showed in defence, even if they were unable to leave with all three points.

"This match was important, because if we win at least one of the last three, we should be in a good position for qualification," Allegri told Sky Sport Italia, via Football Italia.

"It was a very difficult game; the lads did well, and obviously there were moments when we had to suffer under pressure, but for the first time we really did well out of possession to slow Manchester City down. They did really well in that.

"It was a deserved draw; ultimately, we leave here with a bitter taste in our mouths, but I told the lads that the belief, the courage is necessary in Europe, as there are spaces to play in.

"After this, we must prepare in the best way for Sassuolo; [this] was a good training session above all for our defence."

Allegri was only able to name seven substitutes for the trip to Manchester, leaving them short-handed.

"We should be happy with the point, which is the most important thing, but also the performance," he added.

"There were moments when we should've made more of their high line, and had a couple of good situations, but we were against a very strong opponent. The lads applied themselves brilliantly.

"I am above all happy for the lads. When you play in the Champions League, you need that extra belief, to even be arrogant and trust your abilities."
