Chainflip founder admits heavy losses after second hack in 10 weeks drains $12 million

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Simon Harman says the exchange can recover, but frozen withdrawals, a 37% token decline and an unresolved compensation plan threaten its comeback.

Chainflip founder Simon Harman expressed confidence that the decentralized exchange could recover from a $12 million hack despite significant losses to liquidity providers.

The cross-chain trading platform suffered its second exploit in 10 weeks on Nov. 21, losing Bitcoin, Ethereum and stablecoins from its vaults. The attack forced it to suspend deposits, withdrawals and swaps.

The incident also battered Chainflip's native FLIP token, which lost roughly 37% of its value after the breach, according to CoinGecko.

In a Nov. 22 statement, Harman said he had been working with law enforcement, investigators and other stakeholders to secure the network and assess the damage.

“Although I was initially unsure about the future, I'm now relatively confident we can get back and running despite the significant losses to LPs.”

The attacker drained 122.86 BTC from Chainflip's Bitcoin vault, alongside 520.22 ETH, approximately 3.25 million USDC and 758,141 USDT from its Ethereum vault.

Blockchain investigators traced the Ethereum-based assets into approximately 2,909 ETH, which subsequently entered the Tornado Cash mixing protocol.

Chainflip has offered the attacker a 10% bounty to return 110.575 BTC and 2,618.1 ETH by 18:38 UTC on Nov. 23.

The company pledged to withdraw its law enforcement complaints if the funds are returned under the agreed terms.

The latest breach comes barely two months after Chainflip lost approximately $736,000 in USDT through a vulnerability affecting its Tron integration in September.

After that incident, validators approved a compensation proposal to use surplus assets held in the protocol's vaults to reimburse affected liquidity providers.

At the time, Chainflip reported approximately $1.09 million in excess USDC in its Ethereum vault and projected a network-wide surplus of about $585,000 after covering the earlier shortfall.

The November attack has now drained $3.25 million in USDC from the Ethereum vault, adding another substantial demand on the protocol's resources.

Harman said Chainflip remained a profitable business with strong distribution, but acknowledged that restoring confidence would require recovering assets and strengthening security.

He said the team was developing compensation options for affected liquidity providers, with an incident report expected on Nov. 23.

The recovery offer could replenish the vaults, but Chainflip has yet to disclose how it intends to cover outstanding liquidity-provider balances or when withdrawals will resume.
