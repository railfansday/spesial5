'Significant number of customer records' pwned in Flight Centre Travel Group breach

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The latest attack in spate of similar incidents targeting booking systems globally

Travel giant Flight Centre Travel Group (FLT) says "a significant number of customer records" may have been accessed by "unknown persons" following a cyberattack.

The Brisbane-headquartered company said the incident affected a third-party accommodation booking system used by Flight Centre, Travel Associates, Cruiseabout, and some independent travel agents.

Its disclosure [PDF], filed with the Australian Securities Exchange today, added that it was aware that records were accessed, but cannot definitively say how many were exfiltrated.

Flight Centre is working with the supplier and incident response specialist Mandiant to establish the scope of the incident.

An initial review suggests most accessed records contained only customer names, some with basic travel information. A "very small percentage" may also include birth dates, email addresses, limited passport details, phone numbers, or, in rare cases, health or mobility information.

Flight bookings and corporate travel use separate systems and are unaffected, the company said.

Investigations remain ongoing, but FLT has already contained the attack, rotated keys and credentials, and increased system monitoring, it said.

The company said it would contact affected customers directly. Its brands continue to operate normally, and travelers should proceed with their plans.

Customers should independently verify unexpected messages about their bookings before clicking links, opening attachments, or sharing personal information.

The attack on FLT follows others that have hit booking systems in the past year, including those on Booking.com and BWH Hotels.

More recently, another Australian business, RMS Cloud, which provides booking software to travel and leisure companies, also acknowledged a breach that led to WhatsApp scams in late October.

Numerous reports of travelers being targeted with messages that contained their names and booking details hit local news outlets at the time.

Customers were sent convincing messages seemingly from the hotels they had booked, and according to Australia's National Anti-Scam Centre, the scams appeared to be financially motivated.

Muhammad Yahya Patel, vCISO and cybersecurity advisor for EMEA at Huntress, said attackers targeting booking systems is a "familiar supply chain story" that has a greater impact than simple data theft.

Speaking generally about the risks of exposed travel records, Patel said: "Unlike a compromised email address or phone number, a passport can't be changed easily or quickly. For affected customers, the practical risk extends well beyond the immediate breach.

"Passport details combined with names, addresses, and travel history create a comprehensive identity profile that enables document fraud, identity theft, and targeted social engineering that could persist for years." ®
