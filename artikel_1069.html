Red card for football chiefs as they announce ex-Premier League referee is dead aged 78 - when he is still alive and kicking

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Published: 13:01 EST, 14 November 2026 | Updated: 20:45 EST, 14 November 2026

A former Premier League referee was mistakenly declared dead by football chiefs this week.

On Tuesday, the Football Association of Wales (FAW) announced Keith Cooper had died aged 78. But he is alive and well.

The news reached the ears of the Wembley Cup final referee when he went out to his local sports centre for a flu jab only to be told: 'You were dead.'

The Football Association of Wales posted a statement online and a tribute on social media to announce his death, but then withdrew it within an hour.

Mr Cooper, who is the father of former Nottingham Forest and Leicester boss Steve Cooper, told a Welsh sports website: 'I walked into the sports centre yesterday to have my jab and the fellow went, "You were dead!"

'Well, I'm not dead, I'm here and I'm OK!'

The blunder appears to have emerged after the Wiltshire FA announced the death of another referee called Keith Cooper, who the football chiefs in Wales believed was the other Keith Cooper.

The FAW statement said: 'Keith was a well-known figure who enjoyed an exceptional career at both domestic and international level and would later hold the position of FAW Referees' Officer following his retirement in 1996.

'Born and raised in Pontypridd, he is considered one of Wales' best match officials through his highly-regarded status in the game.

'In addition to his work with FAW, Keith was also a valued member of the Wiltshire FA Council who recognised his 'integrity, passion and commitment' in their tribute to his services to the game.

'Everyone at the FAW sends their condolences to his family and friends at this sad time.'

Mr Cooper oversaw the 1994 League Cup final between Aston Villa and Manchester United, won by Villa 3-1, and worked in radio punditry after hanging up his whistle.

The FAW has not issued an explanation over the posted tribute. His family has not yet commented on the error.

But the unfortunate mistake has not spared the FAW from ridicule by other football fans.

One said: 'We always said there was only one Keith Cooper - turns out there are two.'

Another said: 'Glad the whistle wasn't blown too early on Keith and much respect to the family of the other one.'
