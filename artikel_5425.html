The first price is the one nobody scores

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Industry metrics focus on sale price vs final list price, but that can hide multiple reductions. With 21.2% of listings showing a price cut, the more useful CMA score is sale price vs the first asking price.   AI Summary

Agents are more confident in the comparative market analysis than they have been in years. In the 2026 Survey of Best Practices for CMAs and Listing Presentations, from Giant Steps Advisors in partnership with Lone Wolf Technologies, 89.6% of 2,165 real estate professionals said they expect CMAs to become more relevant, up from 67% in 2020. HousingWire reported on October 22 that the same respondents are ready to bring AI into the work, with 83.2% very or somewhat likely to use AI-assisted CMA tools, and suggested that the lasting value of the document may lie in explaining a price rather than producing one.

That is a fair reading of what a CMA does at the kitchen table. It is also an incomplete one, because it judges the document on a single day. The CMA is scored on whether it persuades the seller to sign. Whether the price it argued for held up once buyers began making offers is a question the industry has no standard way to answer.

The pressure on that single day is rising. Roughly two-thirds of agents in the survey named pricing as the leading seller objection, up from 52.7% in 2020, and 33.6% said they had lost a listing because they and the seller could not agree on a price.

At the same time, the survey found that 90.8% of respondents trust the MLS above any other source for comparable sales. Similar sources, increasingly similar tools and familiar formatting tend toward similar documents. When two agents arrive with reports that look alike, the seller is left choosing between two persuasions with nothing to say which one was right last time.

The industry does keep a score, but it is the wrong one for this purpose. In the National Association of Realtors 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, the final sale price was a median of 99% of the final listing price. The final listing price is the one left standing after any reductions. A home listed too high, cut twice and then sold sits in that figure looking almost exact, because the comparison begins after the correction has already been made. Reductions are not unusual.

Redfin reported that 21.2% of listings carried a price cut in the four weeks to November 1, 2026. The figure that tells a seller how well a CMA priced their home is the sale price compared with the first asking price, but that is not the figure the industry reports.

Even the first asking price is not always the number the CMA stood behind. HousingWire’s October report noted research showing that about half of agents put a listing price in their CMA and about half do not. Where the price is left off the page, the CMA can never be checked against the outcome, because it never committed to a number in the first place.

The gap widens when agents move. HousingWire reported in March that roughly 230,000 agents moved to a new brokerage in 2025, according to Courted’s 2025 State of Brokerage Recruiting and Retention Report. The sale prices stay in the closed files after an agent leaves. The judgment that set the first prices, and the sense of where that judgment ran high or low, leaves with the agent.

None of this is an argument against AI in the CMA, or against the CMA as a persuasion tool. Persuasion is part of the job, and a seller deserves an agent who can explain a price clearly and defend it under pressure. The difficulty is that the explanation is examined closely on the day it is given, while the outcome arrives months later and is measured against a different number.

The industry has spent years making the valuation faster to produce and easier to present, and the survey suggests the next generation of tools will make it faster still. The question it has not yet asked is the one a seller would ask if they knew to: When this agent’s first prices met real buyers, how close did they come? Until someone asks it, every CMA will be judged on the day it persuades and never on the day the house sells.
