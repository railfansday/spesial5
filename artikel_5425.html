Bitcoin Price Holds $64K Ahead of Nvidia Earnings: AI Bubble Finally Pops?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The CryptoNews editorial team is composed of seasoned writers specializing in cryptocurrency and blockchain technology. Their expertise ensures comprehensive, accurate, and insightful content for...

Bitcoin price is struggling to find direction just below $65,000 as we brace for Nvidia earnings report. BTC USD has recovered from its weekend low near $62,000, but remains down 13% over seven days. Its modest rebound comes as technology stocks face yet another selling pressure.

This timing puts the Bitcoin price in an uncomfortable position. Nvidia’s results could determine if investors return to risk assets or abandon another crowded trade. With market sentiment still deep in extreme fear, we still have little conviction that the worst is over.

Meanwhile, Michael Burry is increasing his bearish bets against technology stocks. The investor made famous by The Big Short is targeting the Nasdaq-100 and semiconductor sector. His positioning raises an ugly question: Is the AI boom approaching its own dot-com moment?

Burry doubled his Nasdaq-100 put position on November 12, targeting early 2027 options 20% below the index’s market level. He also bought back part of his previously reduced bearish position in the VanEck Semiconductor ETF.

His argument goes beyond stretched stock valuations. Burry believes conventional accounting earnings can exaggerate technology companies’ underlying profitability. Depreciation schedules, particularly across expensive data-center infrastructure, sit at the center of his concerns.

Consider a hyperscaler investing billions in GPUs and AI infrastructure. Those purchases become capital assets rather than immediate operating expenses. Companies subsequently recognize depreciation across their estimated useful lives.

This accounting treatment can make current earnings look stronger than the underlying cash economics. If hardware becomes obsolete faster than expected, reported profitability could overstate actual investment returns.

The concern becomes significant when companies must continuously replace expensive accelerators. Their infrastructure spending needs to generate sufficient revenue before the equipment loses economic value.

Burry isn’t alone in questioning the investment cycle. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman previously acknowledged excessive enthusiasm surrounding artificial intelligence.

“Are we in a phase where investors as a whole are overexcited about AI? My opinion is yes,” Altman said.

Jeff Bezos offered another interpretation, describing the spending cycle as an “industrial bubble.” He argued that excessive investment could still produce valuable infrastructure, even when individual investors lose money.

Nvidia’s own Jensen Huang previously defended the industry’s economics. According to him, the AI infrastructure was already generating productive revenue, rather than merely attracting speculative capital.

The disagreement exposes the central tension surrounding the AI bubble. Demand can be genuine while valuations and infrastructure commitments become unsustainable.

The dot-com crash offers an important historical comparison. The Nasdaq Composite lost 78% between March 2000 and October 2002. Internet technology survived, but countless businesses and investors did not.

Today’s AI infrastructure market has similarities, particularly aggressive capital expenditure and expectations of exponential demand. However, established hyperscalers now finance much of the expansion through enormous operating businesses.

Now, can future AI revenue justify today’s investment?

Bitcoin once traded with relatively little correlation to traditional equities. That relationship changed dramatically as institutional participation expanded.

CME Group’s historical correlation research illustrates the shift. Between 2014 and 2016, Bitcoin’s correlation with the S&P 500 stood near -0.01. Its correlation with the Nasdaq-100 was effectively zero. Between 2020 and 2022, those correlations climbed to 0.40 and 0.42, respectively. They remained positive through April 2025, reaching 0.30 against both indices.

The shift matters because institutional investors increasingly treat Bitcoin as another liquid risk asset. Portfolio rebalancing, leveraged positions, and macroeconomic conditions can influence its price alongside technology stocks.

An AI sell-off could create another liquidity shock across crypto. Investors facing losses elsewhere might sell Bitcoin to raise cash or reduce overall exposure. But an AI correction doesn’t automatically invalidate Bitcoin’s monetary thesis. A technology valuation reset could eventually redirect capital toward alternative assets.

The gap lies between an isolated technology correction and a financial liquidity crisis. Bitcoin could eventually decouple from semiconductor stocks under the first scenario. The second could pressure both markets simultaneously. For now, Bitcoin price remains exposed to the same risk appetite driving technology shares.

Nvidia’s third-quarter fiscal 2027 results arrive at a particularly sensitive moment. Wall Street expects $109 billion in revenue and $2.47 in adjusted earnings per share.

Investors will focus heavily on Data Center revenue, gross margins, and forward guidance. The company’s Vera Rubin platform also faces scrutiny as customers expand their AI infrastructure commitments.

A headline earnings beat might not satisfy investors demanding another acceleration in growth. Strong revenue accompanied by cautious guidance could trigger another sell-the-news reaction.

The opposite scenario could restore confidence across technology stocks. Strong guidance would reinforce expectations that hyperscaler spending remains sustainable. Bitcoin could benefit if investors regain their appetite for speculative assets.

Technically, Bitcoin needs to stabilize above its recent $62,000 weekend low. A break below that level would expose the psychologically important $60,000 threshold.

A stronger recovery would put $68,000 back into view as an important technical resistance area. Reclaiming that level could help establish if buyers are returning.

Bitcoin price now faces a test extending beyond crypto markets. Nvidia must demonstrate that AI infrastructure spending can translate into durable earnings growth.

Burry is betting that expectations have run too far ahead. Huang’s former company built the infrastructure powering the AI revolution, but investors now need proof of its economic returns.

If the AI bubble starts deflating, Bitcoin must answer another question: Can it survive without Wall Street’s appetite for technology risk?
