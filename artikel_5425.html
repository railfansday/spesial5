AI Use is Widespread, but Only 10% of Organizations are Reinventing Work at Enterprise Scale

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

i4cp's 2027 Priorities & Predictions report warns that AI could remove experiences that develop future leaders; separate i4cp research finds only 40% of organizations are effective at building leadership bench strength.

BOSTON, Nov. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Despite the enormous attention surrounding AI, new research from the Institute for Corporate Productivity (i4cp) finds that only 10% of organizations have focused on continuously redesigning work. This same small subset is also reinventing traditional roles, skills, structures, and workforce deployment to develop new enterprise capabilities and increase future readiness.

i4cp's highly anticipated Priorities & Predictions report annually explores the human capital agenda for the coming year, informed by the executives responsible for delivering it. It reveals what the CHRO - and the leaders across HR's major functional areas - are prioritizing, how they anticipate their disciplines will evolve, and what it will take to better prepare their organizations for the future.

The result is a uniquely comprehensive view of the forces shaping the future of work and the actions human capital leaders must take to adapt and drive business performance. It draws on the collective insights of several hundred members of i4cp's executive boards in North America and abroad (CHRO, Future of Work, People Analytics, Chief Learning & Talent Officer, Total Rewards, Impact & Belonging, and Talent Acquisition), i4cp's Global Advisory Council, and thousands of HR professionals worldwide who regularly take part in i4cp's practitioner-only communities.

"Everyone's asking how fast to adopt AI, but I think that's the wrong question," said Kevin Martin, Chief Research Officer, i4cp. "The companies that come out ahead in 2027 won't be the ones that change the most. They'll be the ones that are better at the art and science of changing."

Beyond slow enterprise-wide AI adoption, the report points to a critical hidden risk: as AI takes over routine analysis, coordination and entry-level work, companies may be removing the tasks that provide valuable developmental experience in judgment, critical thinking, and other skills that are essential for long-term success. i4cp predicts this will result in "a talent shortage created from within."

"The task may disappear, but the capability it helped build remains essential," said Martin. "Organizations need to be as intentional about designing how people develop expertise as they are about redesigning the work."

"AI is reshaping how companies create business value and high performance, which puts work design, agility and workforce capability at the center of business strategy," said Terry Waters, CEO of i4cp. "The opportunity for CHROs and business leaders is to align technology, talent, and operating models around the outcomes the business needs."

i4cp's 2027 Priorities & Predictions report details how human capital leaders are preparing their organizations for this shift. Its four enterprise predictions for 2027 point to major changes in how companies operate: building repeatable ways to execute change; preserving experiences that build expertise as AI reshapes work; rethinking management as spans widen; and moving from system-by-system HR integration toward AI orchestration. Function-specific predictions point to an evolving HR business partner role and closer collaboration between talent acquisition and cybersecurity to counter AI-enabled candidate fraud.

AI enablement is the most commonly anticipated human capital investment for 2027, cited by 58% of all respondents to i4cp's 2027 Priorities survey. As organizations seek business value from AI, i4cp expects CEOs to scrutinize whether technology-led transformation efforts are redesigning work, building organizational capability, and delivering measurable results. That scrutiny will elevate the CHRO's role in orchestrating adoption and demonstrating business impact.
