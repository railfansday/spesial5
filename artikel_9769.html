AI Could Become the Front Door to Digital Banking

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Over the last several years, we have witnessed a real boom in AI technologies. Companies have been experimenting with agents, launching AI assistants, and trying to integrate new capabilities into existing products.

However, the initial enthusiasm is gradually colliding with reality. Building an impressive demo is much easier than launching a safe, secure, and reliable product for millions of users.

This is particularly evident in the banking sector, where companies work with sensitive financial data, face strict regulatory requirements, and where the cost of any mistake can be much higher. These challenges hinder the adoption of new technologies and bold innovations.

Over the last 10–15 years, banks have invested huge amounts of resources in developing the digital customer experience. They have introduced new functions, new services, and complex customer journeys.

Several years ago, the whole industry, not just the banking sector, was actively discussing and developing super apps — applications that bring together multiple functions, including financial and non-financial services, in one place.

Having worked on banking products, I remember how complex it was to fit so many functions into a single interface.

We conducted a lot of research. We tried to assess the clarity of the text and navigation, and we tested different screen designs. We needed to find out whether customers could find the right function or service, interpret the information correctly, and complete the entire customer journey.

The interface had to be intuitive, simple, and clear because its usability could directly affect conversion rates and other business metrics.

An entire industry has developed around UX research, design, testing, and customer behavior analysis.

But what we see today is that ChatGPT and other AI assistants, like Claude, are forming a new habit. Instead of searching for specific information or services, you can simply explain what you want to do, and the assistant helps you find the right one among a myriad of other capabilities.

And it's not just about the convenience of interacting with AI. Customers' expectations of digital products are actually changing.

A recent McKinsey study found that 23% of surveyed banking consumers already use generative AI for financial tasks. Among those, 74% use ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini to ask financial questions and get help with banking-related tasks.

But a banking interface is not just a collection of screens and buttons. It reflects the underlying banking processes.

When customers navigate between screens, fill out forms, select options, and confirm operations, they are following a process designed by the bank. The interface guides them through each step.

Now imagine that customers no longer need to navigate through these steps themselves. They simply explain what they want to achieve, and an AI assistant helps them get there.

This raises an interesting question. Are we just making existing interfaces easier to use, or are we moving towards a different way of delivering services?

And this is where the difference between automation and transformation becomes important.

Today, you can find many AI assistants on the market. They help answer customers' questions, find information, and automate individual customer service processes.

But it's important to distinguish between three levels of AI adoption: assistance, automation, and transformation.

At the first level, AI helps people understand an existing process and complete tasks faster.

At the second level, AI takes over individual steps, making the whole process faster and cheaper. But we are still operating within the same process.

At the third level, we change the process itself.

Instead of asking customers to follow predefined banking processes, we start with what they actually want to achieve. AI could then determine how to deliver that outcome, combining different banking services and capabilities without requiring customers to understand how they work behind the scenes.

This is not just about making an existing process faster. It changes how customers interact with banks.

And we are already seeing early examples of this shift. In the US, eligible ChatGPT users can connect their bank accounts through Plaid for read-only financial guidance. Banco Bilbao Vizcaya Argentaria (BBVA) has taken a different approach, building an app inside ChatGPT where customers can explore its banking products without leaving the conversation.

These are still early steps rather than full process transformation. But they show something interesting: banks can become a **data source or a storefront **within AI platforms, allowing customers to interact with banking services without opening a traditional banking app.

If the potential benefits are so obvious, why are we not seeing a large-scale transition to this model?

In my opinion, one of the main challenges is, of course, security.

Recent years have demonstrated not only the capabilities of AI but also the rapid growth of security threats. In 2025 alone, AI-enabled fraud increased by 1,210% in data analyzed by Pindrop.

And recent months have also brought particularly concerning examples of the difficulties in controlling autonomous AI systems.

In July 2026, during OpenAI's internal cybersecurity evaluations involving models with reduced safeguards, AI agents bypassed isolation controls and compromised parts of OpenAI's research infrastructure and Hugging Face's systems. The agents took actions beyond their assigned tasks, exposing the risk of giving AI systems access to real infrastructure.

Another example came in October 2026, when researchers using AI tools helped identify and analyze critical vulnerabilities in PHP. These vulnerabilities could allow attackers to execute code remotely on vulnerable servers. This was not an AI-caused breach, but it demonstrated how AI can accelerate vulnerability research.

For banks, these risks are particularly serious. It's one thing to allow AI to answer questions about banking products, but it's something entirely different to allow it to manage financial operations or execute transactions.

Moving from an assistant that provides information to one that can take action requires a completely different level of security, control, and trust.

And not every company has the resources and expertise necessary to build its own secure infrastructure for AI agents. For smaller players, this could mean relying on specialized technology providers rather than building everything themselves.

I don't think traditional banking interfaces will disappear. I believe they will remain necessary for visualizing information, checking data, and confirming important operations.

But their role could change significantly.

So far, we have tried to make complex banking processes simple and clear for customers through interface design.

But the next step may be the opposite: making sure customers no longer need to understand the complexity of these processes.

And the real transformation will happen not when every bank launches its own AI assistant, but when AI assistants stop being just an additional feature and become a new way of interacting with banking services.
