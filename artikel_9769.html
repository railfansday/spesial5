In addition to a global uncertain financial climate, 2026 has challenged the viability of DeFi with more effective, often AI-assisted hacks and exploits. In Q2 2026, Solana-based perpetual DEX Drift Protocol was drained of $285 million and KelpDAO was hacked allowing bad actors to mint $292 million in fraudulent rsETH to borrow real assets on Aave.

The dual pressures of more sophisticated hackers and increasing economic pressures are driving users to look for more reassurances. Specifically, they want the advantage of DeFi combined with the institutional assurances of Traditional Finance.

Onchain cover providers like Nexus Mutual are meeting the need for these new levels of certainty working with projects like Saffron Finance to protect depositors against accidental losses due to technical error or bad actors.

The upshot for DeFi users greater confidence that their money won’t be swept away by consequence of fate or malfeasance and fraud.

Since its launch, Nexus Mutual has lived up to its promises consistently, most notably in several prominent cases:

Ralf Taner, Head of Business Development at Nexus Mutual, feels that the trend toward protecting users is growing with the mainstreaming of AI technology.

“The trend is growing after major announcements from leaders like Aave and Robinhood about integrated insurance. With an increase in hacks driven by AI, users are more worried about onchain security than ever. That’s why exploits remain a significant reason people hesitate to put money into DeFi. Protocols increasingly treat coverage as a requirement to onboard capital from high-net worth individuals and institutions at scale.”

But security is not the only driver – buyers are more conservative in 2026 and demanding more from DeFi projects.

“Our team has noticed three main trends driving coverage adoption: users actively choose protocols where they can protect their deposits; institutions and treasuries often require coverage before allocating capital at all; and forward-thinking teams use coverage as a differentiator, signaling they take security seriously and are committed to protect their users’ funds.”

Saffron Building a Three-Layer DeFi Security Stack

“Beyond static code audits, there's two other things you can do to make your on-chain smart contracts more secure. One is active monitoring. For Saffron, as a permissionless protocol, the active monitoring is more about making sure that nothing on the front end goes wrong. Anotherline of defense that you have is an onchain surety that ensures users get paid back if something goes wrong,” Psy, Founder of Saffron Finance said.

“There's only really one example of a good, solid, established protocol that does this, and that's Nexus Mutual. We've been aware of them since our launch in early 2020, and you can see on-chain that they paid out all of their coverage. It's all verifiable. So, we decided this was a way to ensure that we have everything that we need to make everything as secure as possible.”

Saffron has undergone nine audits, uses active monitoring from Hypernative, and has operated on mainnet for roughly six months during its pre-beta phase, accumulating nearly $3 million in TVL before its vault launch. Since it’s built on Uniswap v3, Saffron has immutable code, no pause function, and allows no admin controls over user funds, so there are fewer ways the protocol can be compromised.

Nexus Mutual's risk team priced Saffron's Native Protocol Cover at 1.99%, which Nexus characterized as competitive for a young protocol. Nexus pointed to the protocol's immutable contracts, lack of external price feeds, and security reviews among the factors that drove the favorable rate.

Saffron’s Native Protocol Cover protects user funds against risks including smart contract exploits, governance takeover, oracle and liquidation failure, and other covered events on the Saffron protocol. It does not protect funds on other protocols, economic losses, or other non-covered events. The project plans to purchase $2.5 million in protocol-owned coverage for Saffron Vaults.

Odds are we are seeing the development of new, higher standards in DeFi – which makes a great deal of sense as we move toward a world with more DeFi/TradFi crossover users who want the best of both sides.

Has the reception by early users lived up to the expectations?

“The Nexus Mutual engagement led to a $1 million deposit from a depositor who wanted to use our product, but even after audits and active monitoring, he wasn't convinced that a new DeFi protocol was sufficiently safe. When I told him that there was coverage for all deposits up to $2 million for the first month of beta he decided to deposit. So, early signs show that this reassurance that their money won’t be lost even in the case of bad actors really means a lot to users.”

This is just an anecdote about one depositor, but it offers an early indicator that third-party coverage can affect how users assess risks for new DeFi protocols. The Saffron-Nexus deal is forward-thinking for now, but in the near future a third-party insurer may be table stakes for staking protocols.
