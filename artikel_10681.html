Viral app lets strangers vote to decide if you can buy something to stop impulse spending

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

A bizarre new app is going viral in China for letting complete strangers decide whether people should be allowed to spend their own money.

The free WeChat mini-program, called ‘Wallet, Please Wait,’ is designed to help users avoid impulse purchases by requiring them to seek approval before buying something.

As reported by the South China Morning Post, shoppers submit requests detailing what they want, how much it costs, why they need it, and whether cheaper alternatives exist.

They can then share their requests with friends and family or post them to a public forum where complete strangers vote to approve or reject the purchase.

Some of the requests have produced surprisingly wholesome results, including one unemployed user who asked for permission to spend 60 yuan ($9) on a barbecue dinner.

Strangers approved the meal, with one voter encouraging the user not to be so hard on themselves during a difficult period.

One person asked whether they should spend 15,000 yuan ($2,240) on a new iPhone, resulting in a six-to-six deadlock between those supporting the upgrade and others suggesting a cheaper screen repair.

Meanwhile, a 40-yuan ($6) carbon monoxide detector for elderly relatives was approved within minutes.

According to Chinese outlet Sichuan Online, a reporter also tested the app by requesting a 20-yuan milk tea. Five of seven voters rejected it within a minute, arguing that cheaper alternatives were available.

Despite its unusual concept, the app has become a massive success.

Developed in just eight days with AI assistance by a Shanghai product manager using the pseudonym ‘Huoshan,’ the program reportedly attracted 10,000 users within nine hours of launching.

It has since surpassed 300,000 participants, generating roughly 370,000 purchase requests.

The developer explained that the idea came from repeatedly regretting unnecessary purchases and wanting a way to reconsider spending decisions.

It’s not the first unusual Chinese app to become an overnight sensation. Earlier in 2026, an app called ‘Are You Dead?’ surged to number one in China, requiring people living alone to regularly confirm they were still alive.

As for Wallet, Please Wait, its popularity comes during China’s massive Singles’ Day shopping festival, which runs through November 13 this year.

While supporters argue that seeking outside opinions helps prevent unnecessary spending, critics warn that relying too heavily on strangers could undermine people’s confidence in making their own financial decisions.
