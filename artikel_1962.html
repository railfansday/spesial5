Scotland year that started with promise ends in another setback amid fears over one player’s career

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A year that started so full of promise for Scotland ground to a humiliating halt in St Gallen. The 13th match of a World Cup dominated 12 months always threatened to be the toughest of tough tasks.

Not perhaps as challenging as taking on Brazil in Miami or Morocco in Boston. But Switzerland, World Cup quarter-finalists themselves, are no pushovers, as they proved in a match of supposedly little consequence for them. Having already secured top place in Group B1, they enjoyed the luxury of making changes and still eased to a deserved 4-0 victory in the university city.

What did Sebastien Pocognoli learn? Not too much as he watched his side picked apart by Murat Yakin’s exciting team. Two goals just after the break – a tap in from Winsley Boteli and a rocket from Alvyn Sanches – rendered the second half little more than a training exercise.

Substitute Zeki Amdouni wrapped things up in the final minute with a shot from the edge of the of the box that took a wicked deflection off Luke Graham. It was that kind of night. Lewis Ferguson had put through his own goal to set the Swiss on their way after 22 minutes.

It was anything but the much-needed statement result for Pocognoli some had surmised might be possible against hosts who could afford to coast. Instead, there were fears that Switzerland – 3-0 up after just 51 minutes – would pile on the pain.

Scotland suffered a 7-0 defeat against Uruguay 80 or so miles away in Basel at the 1954 World Cup and something as embarrassing felt possible, particularly when the "oles" from the home fans were ringing around the Sitterstadion early in the second half.

This wasn’t how Andy Robertson, who had 103 stitched into the tongues of his boots, wanted to become Scotland’s most-capped ever men’s player. Scotland were completely licked.

Sanches missed an open goal with eight minutes left after Angus Gunn miscued a pass, although the Swiss forward was around 50 yards out. The keeper did well to block a later shot from the same player but was helpless when Amdouni’s deflected effort looped into the net for 4-0.

Of course, it helps when the opposition scores the all-important first goal for you. Ferguson inadvertently put through his own net as he attempted to cut out the danger following Zachary Athekame’s cross from the right. Not too long afterwards, about 900 miles away in Skopje, Slovenia scored their opener against North Macedonia to further put the Scots behind the black ball in their quest to finish in second place. The Slovenians ended up winning 3-0, leaving Scotland in a very distant third place in the group and wondering what the future holds. At least there are no more games until March. It’s been a tough old year. The memorable moments have almost all occurred off the pitch. It isn’t all on Pocognoli, granted.

What was underlined here is that the Belgian could do with a bit of fortune. He could do with the stars aligning in the way they did for Steve Clarke a year ago this week, when everything seemed to fall into place eventually on a night of nights against Denmark in Glasgow.

Pocognoli, by contrast, is struggling to get going. His Hampden bow against Switzerland was ruined by Jack Hendry’s red card just ten minutes in. Here he saw Scott McTominay blaze over from six yards and watched John McGinn place a header straight into the hands of keeper Yvon Mvogo.

More serious woe arrived in the form of the latest injury misery to befall Aaron Hickey. The former Hearts player’s left hamstring has proved troublesome for a few managers, including Clarke, and obviously for the player himself.

It was heartbreaking to see the player stumble having performed a typically diligent defensive duty, in this case mopping up by directing a header into Gunn’s arms. He also pulled something in the process and made a sad exit down the tunnel after 36 minutes, with Nathan Patterson replacing him. Brentford will be furious although it’s not as if Pocognoli hadn’t sought to manage the player’s minutes.

He left him out of the first game of the window against North Macedonia. This latest setback feels bigger than just what it means for Pocognoli and Scotland. It raises real fears about a brilliant young player’s career.

So the outcome of the match, as dispiriting as the defeat was, needs to be placed in context. It might have proved a happier evening had Hickey’s header from Andy Robertson’s cross 24 minutes sneaked in rather than past the post just minutes after Ferguson’s own goal horror.

Hickey was also in the thick of things after a clever touch from Oli McBurnie saw him burst into the Swiss half. Kieran Tierney was to his left and was screaming to be played in but the wing-back just could not get the ball out of his feet and the chance was gone.

Ferguson’s misery was all the more frustrating because it was so unnecessary. Ryan Porteous was waiting to clear but the Bologna midfielder got in before him and steered past Gunn while attempting to clear the danger.

The game was over six minutes into the second half. Ballet exchanged passes with Remo Feuler and shot towards goal. Although Boteli probably didn’t need to help the ball over the line, he did anyway at the risk of seeing the goal chalked off for offside.

He was just onside and was credited with the strike. There was no doubt who scored the third for the hosts, with Sanches latching on to a loose ball after Patterson had been dispossessed before rifling into the corner past Gunn.

The loss of a fourth goal seemed inconsequential but did seem to sum everything up. These are very different times to 12 months ago and more hurt may lie ahead.
