Marvel’s Post-Credit Scenes Have to Go

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Hey, did you see the post-credit scenes in VisionQuest? Of course you did. Did you see the eight pre-credit installments, aka VisionQuest's actual episodes? The 421 million minutes streamed over one week would suggest that you certainly did. Now, be honest: are you talking to any of your fellow MCU fans about the series itself? Or are you talking about the Doctor Doom/Scarlet Witch post credit scene, and what it means for the upcoming Avengers: Doomsday? If you said the former, you either clearly missed the "be honest" part of the question, or your pants are on fire. That post-credit scene is the only thing anyone wants to talk about, and that's a problem. So, at the risk of being labeled a heretic for even suggesting it, here goes: Marvel, the post-credit scenes have to go.

It didn't have to come to this. It all began innocently enough with the stinger that came at the end of 2008's Iron Man, where S.H.I.E.L.D. director Nick Fury (Samuel L. Jackson) told Tony Stark (Robert Downey Jr.) he was "putting together a team." If that didn't pop your bottle, shop your K-mart, or blossom your bloomers, then you had no soul. It was a brilliant, touchstone moment that foreshadowed something truly special.

And it only got better from there. Agent Coulson (Clark Gregg) finding Thor's hammer in Iron Man 2. Captain America (Chris Evans) waking up in the present in Captain America: The First Avenger. The introduction of the imposing Thanos — Than-freakin'-os (Josh Brolin), for crying out loud — in The Avengers. And who could forget the collective, worldwide "awwww" upon seeing Dancing Baby Groot in Guardians of the Galaxy. Scenes that either teased what was to come, or were simply there to entertain; it didn't really matter. They were good, sometimes very good, and became must-see moments.

Then, somewhere along the way, they began to suck. Hard. Ant-Man & The Wasp: Quantumania introduced a threat in Jonathan Majors' Kang that, even if it had come to fruition, had the feel of "been there, done that" with Thanos. Films like Eternals and Thor: Love and Thunder introduced characters — Starfox (Harry Styles) and Hercules (Brett Goldstein), respectively — who have never reappeared. It's like someone realized that the movie didn't have an end-credit scene yet, so they said, "Hey, let's come up with something, even if it never goes anywhere." The MCU had created its own monster, and now had to feed it. Worse, the expectations for post-credit scenes expanded to films that weren't even in the MCU, prompting websites that specifically address whether there are after-credit scenes or not. Let me save you the bother: Titanic does not have an after-credits scene.

Then they re-released Avengers: Endgame with four additional scenes. If you paid for "Infinity Vision," that is. Common folk who saw it in "Normal Vision" got screwed out of one scene, which arguably wasn't even worth the extra $8 to see. In fact, none of them were truly must-see, but you had to see them, because, dammit, it might be important to know for Avengers: Doomsday. Guess what? It'll all be disclosed in Avengers: Doomsday, so you paid money to find out something you were going to find out anyway.

And then came VisionQuest and its two post-credit scenes. One has Ultron (James Spader) holding Jocasta's flash drive, which likely falls into the Starfox/Hercules camp of something that will never come up again. Another has Doctor Doom lifting rocks off of Scarlet Witch after the events of Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness and awaking her. At least, we assume it's her. She looks as much like Elizabeth Olsen as the Scorpion King looked like Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson in The Mummy Returns (hint: he doesn't). With one scene that likely means nothing, and another that at least appears to have not even bothered including Olsen whatsoever, overshadowing a very good series in VisionQuest by changing the conversation, the post-credit scenes have crossed the line.

What is the point in having a post-credit scene you throw together, if it's going to negate the work you put into the series leading into it? Here's an idea: have a series and/or movie that is just post-credit scenes, and put the movie proper after it. "Sure, the post-credit scenes were good, but did you see the post-post-credit movie after it? Dude, it's epic!"

And now, they're re-releasing Spider-Man: Brand New Day on December 4th with "a few surprises that weren't in the original cut." What are those few surprises? It's hard not to believe there isn't at least one additional post-credit scene that ties into Avengers: Doomsday, at least if you see it in "Infinity Vision." Otherwise, you have to wait two weeks. Two measly weeks. Enough already. If there isn't any artistic merit to adding a post-credit scene — and forgive the cynicism, but it smells like "cash grab" — then don't bother.
