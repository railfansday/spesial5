Stacy Keach, ‘Mike Hammer’ and ‘Fat City’ Star, Dead at 85

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Stacy Keach, the actor whose turns as Hamlet on stage and hard-bitten detective Mike Hammer on television captivated audiences, died on Tuesday in Magdalenka, Poland, at 85.

His death was confirmed by his wife, Malgosia Tomassi Keach, who said he died at his family home. “After a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure, Stacy peacefully departed this world,” she said in a statement to The Hollywood Reporter. “He fought for so long, with extraordinary courage, determination and strength. He was a true warrior, facing his illness with the same remarkable spirit and resilience that defined his life.”

She continued, “Stacy was not only a legendary actor, admired and loved by audiences the world over, but also my beloved husband, my companion and the heart of our family. His passing leaves an immeasurable emptiness in our lives. But his extraordinary spirit, his unforgettable voice and the love he gave so generously will remain with us forever.”

Walter Stacy Keach Jr. was born on June 2, 1941, in Savannah, Georgia. His father was veteran character actor Stacy Keach Sr. and his mother was Mary Cain Peckham, the daughter of Texas oilman William H. Peckham. Along with his wife and their son, Shannon, and daughter, Karolina, he is also survived by his brother, actor James Keach. The siblings portrayed outlaw James brothers and also served as their own co-producers on the 1980 western The Long Riders.

Keach received a Tony nomination for portraying Buffalo Bill in the 1969 Broadway production Indians and went on to gain further acclaim as the fedora-wearing Hammer on the CBS series that ran from 1984 to 1987. He reprised the role in the 1989 TV movie Mike Hammer: Murder Takes All and another show, Mike Hammer, Private Eye, from 1997 to 1998.

Throughout his prolific career, Keach co-starred in a wide range of projects from movies The New Centurions, the John Huston-directed Fat City, and two Cheech and Chong films, Up in Smoke (1978) and Nice Dreams (1981). His depiction of Ernest Hemingway in the 1988 made-for-TV movie Hemingway earned him a Golden Globe Award.

He also won many stage accolades including Helen Hayes Awards in 1991 and 1996 for his work in Richard III and Macbeth with the Shakespeare Theatre Company. In 2015, he was inducted into the American Theatre Hall of Fame and honored with a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2019. While Keach never reached superstardom, his career reflected his profound passion for both his craft and life. “If a script interests me, or a character intrigues me, or the cast features someone I’ve always wanted to work with, or the location is somewhere exotic, then sign me up,” Keach penned in his memoir All in All: An Actor’s Life On and Off the Stage. “I’m not going to wait for a call that may or may not come.”
