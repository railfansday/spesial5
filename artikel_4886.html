Hästens 2000T Mattress, Tested and Reviewed (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

From the moment I touched down in Sweden, the focus was on sleep. Coming off a red-eye flight from New York with only two hours of rest (three if we’re being generous), there was nothing more I wanted to do than lie down. Luckily, a Hästens mattress—the reason for my trip—awaited me at the hotel and soon provided what was perhaps the most luxurious nap I’d ever experienced.

The Swedish manufacturer, which has been making beds since 1852, sits atop the luxury market for mattresses. The brand—whose name is Swedish for “the horse’s”—has a consuming commitment to craftsmanship, and its horsehair-filled beds are handmade. That level of focus also comes with a hefty price tag, which is why the brand is perhaps best known by the general public for its presence in celebrity homes (or possibly its cameo in an Emily in Paris episode as the subject of the titular character’s latest marketing campaign). But reputation aside, Hästens knows how to make a damn good bed.

But what earns a mattress luxury status? And what is it actually like to sleep on one from Hästens? After touring the brand’s main factory in Köping, Sweden, I’ve laid my head on one of its most popular mattresses, the 2000T, for the last few months, all in the name of trying to understand how much money a good night’s sleep is worth.

To the average person, horsehair is likely not the first mattress fill material that comes to mind—if it comes to mind at all. Gel, innerspring, memory foam, cotton, or some combination of those are way more common. But horsehair has a historic precedent, having been used in beds for many periods of human history, particularly when horses were the primary mode of transportation.

Much of the present-day reason for using the material comes from a scientific point of view: Horsehair strands are naturally hollow, which Hästens claims “transports any excess heat and moisture from your body during deep sleep.” Additionally, when cut and treated, the strands form a helix shape, which works in conjunction with the metal springs in a bed for a strong base of support.

Exterior of the Hastens factory in Köping, Sweden, designed by British architect Ralph Erskine.

An hour and a half train ride northwest from Stockholm, the small town of Köping is so quiet, you might expect to see a horse roaming the streets—maybe having recently received a haircut to donate to the mattress cause. When thinking of the word factory, one may expect a loud, gigantic warehouse filled with whirring machines and assembly lines moving so quickly you can hardly see the product before it whizzes past. But the Hästens factory possessed a quite different vibe. Sure, there is still machinery and expansive production areas, but it was fairly quiet, and I felt that the energy was more akin to a pottery studio than an industrial plant: craft-forward, methodical, and with a focus on handmade artistry. In fact, my host told me that only around 100 people work in the main factory, most of whom are from the area, highlighting the company as a point of pride for the local community.

Walking into the processing room of the factory, I was greeted by a small mountain of horsehair on the floor. It looked much more like a soft version of steel wool than the shiny mane and tail I was envisioning. That’s because the hair is cut down to small strands prior to being integrated into the mattresses. The hair also goes through several disinfecting and processing treatments before it even arrives at the factory. Taking a clump between my hands, I was surprised by its springiness. (And horse girls need not fear about animal welfare: My host for the trip confirmed that their suppliers from farms around the world aren’t killing horses for their hair, the mane and tail cuttings they receive are ethically sourced.)

The blue check pattern, developed by fourth-generation company leader Jack Ryde in 1978, is synonymous with the brand. So much so that a check-laden Mercedes was waiting to drive us from the train station to the factory.

Each layer of horsehair in the mattress is fluffed by hand and placed between sheets of cotton and wool.

Every aspect of the bed creation process happens by hand: prepping the layers of wool and horsehair for the filling, arranging and securing steel springs, framing the Swedish pine base. I watched workers build each part of the bed with a devoted level of care. The only fully automated aspect of the bed manufacturing that I witnessed on my tour was stitching a pattern onto the top mattress to give it a signature tufted texture. But even that is hand-sewn on certain bed models.

Quality is tested at every step, and if something isn’t up to par, production restarts. For some models, including the Grand Vividus (at the top of Hästens range), there are only a select few craftspeople who are trained in the production process. Much of the brand’s higher price tag comes down to that heightened level of labor and care. If someone on the team is sick or is simply just working slower on a given day, the production timeline increases to accommodate the quality level that Hästens demands: perfection.

My loaned mattress also included a full set of Hästens bedding.

The 2000T is one of the brand’s best sellers, falling around the middle of their price range.

When it comes to my sleep setup, my mattress was the element that I’d been putting off upgrading for years. I only leveled up my years-old box spring with a mattress topper a few years ago. I tend to prioritize bedding when buying new duds for the bedroom, and because I’m consistently testing various sets of sheets for AD, my focus tends to be on how linens can visually transform a space rather than shift my physical experience. All to say, upgrading to a Hästens was like going from a hand-me-down sedan to a Lamborghini.

Before receiving the loaned mattress for testing, I tried out different models in person at the Manhattan flagship store in NoMad, an experience Hästens coins the Sleep Spa. As a staff member talked me through proper spinal alignment and important pressure points, I lay on various models in a Goldilocks-esque journey to see which mattress was the right fit. True to Hästens’ superlative level of service, even this part of the process felt curated. Ambient music played, lights dimmed, and I was given two pillows and a duvet for snuggling. The meditative process made me focus on how my body responded to each mattress. As a combination stomach-and-side sleeper—and someone who likes to sit on her bed—I selected the 2000T (in firm) after around an hour of sampling.

Despite being a firmer model, the 2000T still contours to my body in a way that I can only describe as cushy. The horsehair filling provides a unique springiness that the compression of memory foam or cotton can’t quite replicate. It sinks in just enough to give my shoulders enough space when I sleep on my side, but is supportive enough to keep my hips from dropping out of alignment for the nights I’m on my stomach.

As I mentioned, I often lounge on bed to read, play video games, or what have you. While facilitating sleep is the primary function of a mattress, I was keen to see how its edge control and support would stand up to this alternative usage. I was surprised by how little back pain I sustained after reading sessions that extended beyond two hours. Because of its reinforced springs at the corners and edges, I could also sit right on the edge of the mattress without feeling like I was going to roll off.

The aspect of the horsehair fill I was most intrigued to test was its claim of moisture and heat-wicking properties. I like to be fully swaddled underneath my duvet, top sheet, and sometimes even another blanket when I sleep, which can be a recipe for waking up sweaty. That hasn’t happened once with the 2000T. The foam topper on my prior bed often trapped heat, and I’d wake up in the middle of the night to rip off some of the layers of bedding in order to cool down. With the Hästens, though, I can be as greedy with my bedding as I want to be without overheating.

The ‘massage’ portion lasted about 10 minutes.

As part of their high bar of customer service, the company offers a free recalibration program from select stores to help maintain the longevity of the mattress and mitigate any compression issues. The product care program involves Hästens staffers visiting your home (typically once a year) to flip and massage the mattress—the latter consisting of a person marching back and forth across the mattress to redistribute the fill materials. I can safely say having a grown man in booties walk in circles on top of my bed for 10 minutes was one of the oddest moments of my life, but sleeping on the bed that night, I could feel the difference. While there weren’t any severe lumps formed in my two-month testing period, the process smoothed out any compression in the areas I most often lay.

Although I’ve only slept on the 2000T for around two months, I can see a noticeable change in how my body feels after a night’s rest. I struggle with hypermobility and have a family history of arthritis, so I’m on high alert about how my joints feel upon waking up in the morning. On my old bed, I typically awoke with some pain in my hips and a stiff lower back. While sleeping on the Hästens hasn’t fully eliminated any achiness, I essentially ceased needing to do the typical stretching routine I used to perform in the morning in order to get my body ready for the day.

Down pillows were provided by the brand for my loaner bed.

The structure of Hästens beds includes a top mattress and middle mattress, the former mimicking a thick topper.

As someone who has notoriously bad nighttime habits (like plentiful screen time), I had accepted I’d only be able to expect a low level of restfulness from my sleep. I was content with a solid six hours and low-level of back achiness in the morning, thinking I couldn’t consistently adjust my routine to improve my experience. Sleeping on the Hästens 2000T has changed that mindset drastically. It’s without a doubt the best mattress I’ve ever slept on.

However, it’s easy for me to say this bed is worth it because I didn’t have to pay the steep price tag of $74,780 to try it out in my home. Yes, Hästens exceeded my expectations when it came to sleep, but after the testing period ends, I don’t anticipate a sudden change in my finances that would allow me to invest in my own 2000T. Although the brand’s beds are built to last a lifetime, and we spend a third of our lives asleep, the question of how much a good night’s sleep is really worth is highly individualized.

This experience more broadly made me consider where we are most willing to invest in when it comes to our home spaces. Sure, a covetable mattress like one from Hästens may not be as flashy as other big-ticket purchases, like a giant flat screen, but I’d posit a splurge has few better use cases than on something you return to each night.
