'Doomed From the Start': President Trump's Meme Coin Hits New Lows After Midterms

President Trump's official meme coin has reached new lows since he suffered a major blow in the U.S. midterm elections. The token has dropped almost 40% since election day, and analysts warn that new congressional scrutiny could push it lower.

Over the weekend, the TRUMP coin touched $0.896, marking the lowest point the token had ever stooped. It has since bumped 8%, sitting at $0.965, which is still down almost 40% from election day and 98.7% from its all-time high of $73.43, set the day after it launched, per Decrypt data.

Luke Nolan, senior researcher at CoinShares, told Decrypt the midterms "added pressure to an already struggling token." Trading volume increased around the vote, and he believes some traders had bet on a Republican surprise win. He said there was a "visible unwind" that led to a "sharp drawdown" after the election results rolled out.

"Democrats take both chambers in January and plan to use their subpoena power on his crypto ventures. His net approval rating has almost never been lower," Max Shannon, senior associate at Bitwise Europe, told Decrypt. "It is still possible this continues to decline and, therefore, has a negative effect on these Trump-affiliated assets."

Other Trump-linked assets have also taken a hit. Since the election, World Liberty Financial's WLFI token has dropped 7.5%, per Decrypt data, and shares of the Trump Media & Technology Group have dropped 32%.

Trump launched TRUMP on January 17, 2025, three days before his second inauguration, which Shannon described as "close to the peak of his popularity." Since then, the coin has faced significant scrutiny over how much the President and his family have profited from it. (The First Lady following suit and launching her own meme coin didn't help matters.)

The president’s crypto ventures also became a sticking point for the Clarity Act, a bill that aimed to provide a formal legal structure for most cryptocurrency activity. Democrats scrutinized the Trump family's involvement in the crypto industry, which profited them more than $1 billion in a year.

A July draft would have barred public officials and employees, along with their spouses, from issuing or sponsoring digital assets while in office. That draft didn’t extend to officials’ children, including Don Jr. and Eric Trump, and the provision would have expired in January 2029.

The bill failed a key Senate procedural vote in September. Patrick Witt, former executive director of the President’s Council of Advisers for Digital Assets, later said the “political issue of ethics really consumed everything else.”

TRUMP has "no value accrual, and it has been left in the dust of this narrative," Shannon said. "Google Trends shows searches for $TRUMP essentially flatlining since June 2025."

Nolan agreed, saying the coin’s appeal was "largely speculative," with no clear link between holding it and sharing in any underlying business success. The most it offered, he said, was the exclusive dinner to the top holders, which was both popular and controversial, depending on who you asked.

"In my view, that was just an attempt to prop up the token without actually creating any long-term demand for it," he said.

Interest in these dinners has dried up too. Ahead of the third gala scheduled for the event of the month, 185 holders of the token were invited to register, but only 130 wallets showed on the final leaderboard. Those wallets held a combined $8.6 billion worth of TRUMP, according to Reuters, which is a 94% decline from the first dinner.

Then there's the "unappealing" token supply structure, Nolan said. The majority of tokens were allocated to Trump-linked entities and are being slowly unlocked, which means demand needs to keep growing to soak up newly injected supply.

"That is a difficult position for an asset whose main attraction is attention around one individual," he said.

And what happens when Trump loses power? He has said he "would love" to serve a third term, but that remains unconstitutional—so the clock is ticking on his presidency.

"I think overall Trump leaving office probably means the token continues its gradual decline," Nolan told Decrypt. "Any big Trump headlines probably make it experience the same dead cat bounces (sharp rallies) we have seen it have during short periods of time."

Shannon thinks Trump doesn't even need to lose power for the token to keep going down. He said that it is "incredibly unlikely" that the token reclaims its highs, as it would require it to boost 82x, too many investors have been burned, and the majority of the supply is still to be unlocked.

"I think the meme coin was doomed from the start, and like with most meme coins, creating long-term demand is a Sisyphean task," Nolan told Decrypt.
