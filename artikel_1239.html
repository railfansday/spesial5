Rules Expert Casts Verdict Over Decision to Disallow Man Utd Goal vs Sporting

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A former referee and current rules analyst has provided a verdict on Manchester United's disallowed winning goal against Sporting CP in the Champions League on Wednesday night.

With the game at the Jose Alvalade Stadium poised at 2-2, Bruno Fernandes' corner was palmed into his own net by Sporting goalkeeper Kaique Pereira. Mauricio Pochettino and his players celebrated wildly, believing they'd secured their first victory under the Argentine.

However, after VAR recommended an on-field review, referee Szymon Marciniak ruled the goal out for a foul on Kaique.

Patrick Dorgu was adjudged to have impeded the shot-stopper, and United subsequently had to settle for a point, leaving them 19th in the Champions League table after five matches.

As United pushed for a winning goal in Lisbon, they opted to run a trendy corner-kick routine which has increasingly become known as the 'meatwall'.

Several United players were stationed inside Sporting's six-yard box as Fernandes delivered the corner, with Dorgu, Lisandro Martinez and Leny Yoro ultimately surrounding Kaique as the ball landed in the danger zone.

Officials are supposedly attempting to clamp down on grappling and general contact from corners, in an attempt to reduce teams' reliance on scoring from set-pieces, and this policy ultimately cost United in Portugal.

Speaking on CBS Sports, former referee Christina Unkel, who is a rules expert and analyst for the American broadcaster, provided her verdict on the incident.

The ex-official claimed that those overseeing the Champions League clash at the Jose Alvalade Stadium came to the correct decision.

"Yeah, this is the right decision," she said.

"And, you know, in this match — and you guys said there are multiple different incidents that have occurred — but this one's correct. And if you really take a look at it, there's going to be a good comparison of this one.

"You have number 13 kind of coming through and making more of the impact on that goalkeeper on the body side of it. The decision on the field, just for a reminder, was a goal, but the VAR recommended an overturn.

"I think this is an appropriate overturn. Too much impact. It's coming through the side of the player, and so I think this is a good decision by the officials to take away the goal and recommend it for a foul coming out."

It's safe to say that former Watford striker Troy Deeney, who was also on CBS Sports' panel, did not agree with Unkel's verdict.

Many felt Kaique made contact with the ball before there was any infringement from Dorgu. Deeney went a step further than this, arguing that United should have been awarded a penalty for the incident, rather than a goal.

"Goalkeepers are always protected, aren't they?" he said.

"So, for me, again, he gets the advantage of being able to punch — he actually pushed it into his own goal. But the fact that he's going to get contact... it's from a corner; you're going to get contact. It's factually going to happen. So for me, that's a penalty, not a goal. The penalties were all over the place.

"The referee was terrible. Christina, I don't know what you thought, but watching the game, [he] kept making so many mistakes, and I thought that epitomised it. That should be a goal. Again, maybe I'm just an old fart, but that should be a goal."
