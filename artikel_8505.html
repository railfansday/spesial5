Amend Your Tax Return With Form 1040-X – Claim Refunds Within Three Years

If a mistake on a tax return you already filed changes what you owe or get back, fix it with Form 1040-X. Amend within three years of filing to claim a refund, and pay any tax you owe when you amend, since interest runs until you pay. Skip it for math errors, which the IRS corrects itself.

Say you’re in the 22% tax bracket and forgot to deduct $1,500 of student loan interest. A 1040-X gets you 22% of that back, about $330.

Amend when the mistake changes your income, deductions, credits, dependents, or filing status. Two kinds of mistakes usually don’t need an amendment. The IRS corrects a math mistake while processing your return and tells you by mail. If you forgot to attach a form or schedule, the IRS either accepts your return without it or sends a letter asking for it.

A refund has a deadline. You have three years after filing the original return to claim one, or two years after paying the tax if that gives you longer. If you filed early, the three years start at the April deadline, and a federally declared disaster or service in a combat zone can add time.

Income you left off, such as freelance pay on a 1099 that arrived after you filed, means you owe more instead. The IRS charges interest on that tax from the April due date until you pay it, so amend and pay as soon as you can. Leave interest and penalties off the 1040-X, since the IRS figures them itself.

If you’re expecting a refund on the original return, wait until that return has been processed. Then e-file the 1040-X through tax software that handles amended returns, including every form or schedule the change affects. E-filing works for recent tax years and lets you take any refund by direct deposit. Amend older years on paper.

Your state tax may change too, so check your state tax agency’s rules for correcting your state return.

Allow 8 to 12 weeks for the IRS to process your 1040-X, or up to 16 in some cases, and check its status in Where’s My Amended Return starting three weeks after you file.
