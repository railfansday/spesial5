Thinking About Buying Anthropic’s IPO on Nov. 19? Here’s an AI Stock I’d Consider First (Hint: It’s Not Nvidia or Broadcom).

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

ASML’s impeccable fundamentals allow investors to rest easy amid AI stock volatility.

By Daniel Foelber – Nov 18, 2026 at 6:15PM EST

Artificial intelligence (AI) stock investors have been on a roller coaster in recent weeks, with far more drops than pops.

The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +0.42%) is now in a correction -- which is a drop of more than 10% from the highs. The iShares Semiconductor ETF -- which closely tracks the semiconductor industry -- is in a 35.3% drawdown, and leading chip stocks Broadcom (AVGO +3.65%) and Nvidia (NVDA -2.90%) are both down over 30% from their all-time highs.

Red-hot AI spending has pushed once-cash-cow businesses like Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta Platforms into negative free-cash-flow territory. Rising interest rates are making capital more expensive. And Jensen Huang's sudden resignation as CEO of Nvidia leaves the industry without one of its biggest champions.

The timing is bad for Anthropic, which is set to hold its initial public offering (IPO) on Thursday, Nov. 19. Reports indicate Anthropic was eyeing a valuation as high as $2 trillion. But a Nov. 10 press release by Anthropic prices its 825 million shares between $70 and $78 per share. At the midpoint of $74 per share and based on its total share count of 13.09 billion, Anthropic would have a valuation around $968 billion.

Given the downward pressure on AI stocks, some investors may be eyeing Anthropic's IPO as a great buying opportunity for the company behind one of the leading frontier models. But I think semiconductor equipment manufacturer ASML (ASML +0.88%) is an even better way to buy the dip on AI stocks.

Huang's resignation exposed Nvidia's key-person risk. So much of the company's identity, strategy, and even its organizational structure centered around Huang. And his sudden departure is undoubtedly challenging the investment thesis.

Similarly, the frontier models like Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT, Alphabet's Gemini, and xAI's Grok, owned by Space Exploration Technologies, have shaky moats, as a single upgrade can challenge one model's competitive advantages in a heartbeat.

Meanwhile, hyperscalers like Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet are spending so much on AI so quickly that it's difficult to discern whether those investments will pay off in the long run or prove wasteful.

Uncertain profitability road maps, paired with Nvidia's scandal, have made me even more confident that ASML is the best all-around AI stock to buy for the long term.

Ultimately, ASML doesn't really care if Advanced Micro Devices takes market share from Nvidia or Anthropic grows its lead over OpenAI. Rather, its long-term growth runway depends on increasing demand for next-generation AI chips that must be manufactured on ASML's machines.

ASML has a virtual monopoly on extreme ultraviolet (EUV) machines that are used in the lithography step of chip manufacturing. These machines are needed to mass-produce both logic and memory chips at commercial scale. And the most advanced version of these machines, called high-numerical-aperture (high-NA) EUV, will be needed to make next-generation AI chips.

EUV is making up a larger share of ASML's revenue, but the tech company remains heavily reliant on its deep ultraviolet (DUV) lithography systems and aftermarket servicing on existing machines. In its latest quarter, EUV system sales accounted for 35% of net sales -- which puts into context the sheer size of its non-EUV and servicing segments.

ASML being in the relatively early stages of its EUV and high-NA system rollout is music to the ears of long-term investors. ASML spent over 6 billion euros researching and developing EUV over a 17-year period. But many chip fabs have yet to widely adopt high-NA. On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, ASML said that Samsung Electronics will be using high-NA in high-volume manufacturing starting in 2028 -- joining Intel. ASML is also working with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing to unlock the full efficiency benefits of high-NA EUV by 2033.

In the meantime, ASML can't keep up with 2027 or 2028 demand. On its recent third-quarter 2026 earnings call, the company said it is now taking orders for 2029 and 2030 and increasing production to meet customer needs.

If there is an AI spending slowdown, ASML's near-term results will definitely cool off. But it's still incredibly well positioned to capitalize on AI innovation over the long term because chip fabs will need to upgrade their equipment to EUV and high-NA to support advanced-node manufacturing. In the meantime, ASML generates steady revenue from its DUV business, and high-margin cash flow from services for its existing installed base. What's more, ASML isn't dependent on an expensive research and development (R&D) budget because it has already cracked the code on high-NA and is now in the stage of fine-tuning the commercial manufacturing process with chip fabs. Its operating costs are reasonable, and its balance sheet is in impeccable shape -- with ASML exiting its latest quarter with 8.03 billion euros in cash compared to just 1.96 billion euros in long-term debt.

ASML is the exact company long-term investors can rest easy holding during an AI slowdown. The business is extremely high-quality and will produce strong earnings even during a downturn -- albeit at a slower growth rate. ASML can also generate high cash flow to buy back its stock, even if the price gets discounted. And with the growth stock down 23.7% in the last three months, ASML no longer commands as lofty a valuation -- with the price-to-earnings ratio now sitting at a premium but more reasonable 34.7 compared to over 50 a couple of months ago.

ASML is a company built around the strength of its technology, rather than a single key feature or product upgrade. It sits upstream of the AI value chain, making it the perfect bet for investors who believe in a sustainable, gradual AI innovation cycle rather than those looking to capitalize on a boom in a particular bottleneck, such as memory chips.

ASML presents a far more attractive risk-and-reward profile than an Anthropic IPO or setting sail into uncharted waters aboard Nvidia -- a ship that suddenly finds itself without its veteran captain.
