3 Reasons I Own More Disney Than Nvidia Stock Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Disney and Nvidia are two of my larger holdings.

By Rick Munarriz – Nov 19, 2026 at 8:47AM EST

I've been a Disney (DIS -0.70%) shareholder for decades. I've been an Nvidia (NVDA +2.66%) investor for only months. I naturally believe in both companies. They wouldn't be in my portfolio if I didn't have faith in them, though my stake in the media giant is larger than my position in the AI bellwether.

The two companies do have some things in common. They both reported respectable quarterly results this week. They have new CEOs this year. They're also both historically cheap.

There are some pretty good reasons I own more of the slower-growing Disney than a classic growth stock like Nvidia. I want to break down some of those reasons.

Jensen Huang. Image source: Getty Images.

The five-year stock charts are very different. Even after its recent drawdown, Nvidia has more than quadrupled over the past five years. Disney peaked in 2021, so it's not surprising to see the House of Mouse trading more than 20% lower over the same five years.

They have been passing ships in 2026. Disney is up a modest 8% this year, but that's considerably better than Nvidia, with its 19% year-to-date slide.

Disney is trading for 16 times its new fiscal year's earnings, and 14 times next year's bottom-line results. Buying Disney stock at a forward earnings multiple in the mid-teens isn't something you see often for a blue-chip stock that routinely commands a market premium.

Nvidia stock is also historically cheap. It's also trading for 16 times this fiscal year's analyst profit forecast. A big difference here is that Nvidia's multiple drops to 9 if you look out to next year's profit forecast.

Both stocks are inexpensive, and Nvidia is even cheaper heading into next year. I still like Disney's opportunity here, since the downside appears more limited with the family entertainment behemoth.

I know it's a light knock, but Disney had a traditional transfer of power this year. Josh D'Amaro was outgoing CEO Bob Iger's choice to lead the company. It was a peaceful transition of power, generally applauded by investors. The stock is up 24% since D'Amaro took the helm in mid-March.

Nvidia's succession strategy, in contrast, is messy and controversial. The cp-founder and CEO was let go for having at least two high-profile affairs, including one with a direct report, a violation of Nvidia's code of conduct. There is no doubting what Jensen Huang did to make Nvidia the market's third most valuable company, but he can't come back. The board would be powerless, and Huang would have no reason to believe that any of the other restrictions in the company's code of conduct would apply to him.

You may see executive defections at Nvidia in response. Interim CEO Colette Kress will face unfair distractions. That's not fair to her. It's also not fair to us Nvidia shareholders.

Disney and Nvidia are leaders in their industries, but there are some big differences. Nvidia is the top dog in AI chips and related solutions, but its dominance and the sustainability of the AI boom itself aren't long-term locks.

Disney may have a lower ceiling to its upside, but a clearer one. It operates the world's most popular theme parks, it owns many of the top theatrical franchises, and folks pay a premium for a Disney gated-attraction experience. They will show up in large numbers when the next installment in a popular intellectual property comes out.

In Nvidia's favor, Disney will never sport the kind of profit margins Nvidia is producing right now. Then again, the same can probably be said about Nvidia.

It's OK to believe in both companies. I certainly do. However, my Disney position is more than double the value of my Nvidia stake. I might add to Nvidia once I think the negative market sentiment is bottoming out. The optimist in me prefers to dwell on the positives, and Disney is a perpetual provider of optimism.
