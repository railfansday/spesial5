Israel launches new ground operation in Gaza

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Israel has said its military is targeting Hamas infrastructure in central Gaza and urged civilians to avoid the area. The fight is unfolding against the backdrop of a dire food and shelter situation for Palestinians.

Israel says its military is involved in operations on Wednesday in central Gaza in an effort to dislodge Hamas militants.

France's AFP news agency reported "intense Israeli artillery shelling amid ongoing gunfire and airstrikes."

A statement from the Israeli military (IDF) said, "The IDF launched a precise operation within Gaza earlier today west of the Yellow Line to remove a threat posed to IDF troops operating in the Gaza Strip."

The Yellow Line in Gaza was established in an October 2025 ceasefire between Israel and Hamas, and separates Israeli and Hamas-controlled territory.

On Wednesday, COGAT, an Israeli military body responsible for civilian matters in the Palestinian territories warned international aid agencies and Palestinian civilians to avoid areas near the Al-Bureij and Nuseirat refugee camps and Gaza's main thoroughfare, the Salah al-Din road.

Palestinian medics told Reuters news agency that one person had been killed in an Israeli airstrike in central Gaza and that two more had been killed in separate incidents unfolding in Deir al-Balah and Gaza City.

Hamas — which is listed as a terrorist organization by Israel, the US, Germany, the EU and others — blasted the Israeli operation, calling it "a flagrant violation of the ceasefire agreement and confirmation that the occupation has reneged on its obligations."

Israel's military countered that it was reacting to Hamas ceasefire violations and the group's attempts to rearm.

The October 2025 ceasefire halted major operations, but both sides have continued to carry out limited attacks. Gaza health authorities report at least 1,600 Palestinian deaths and Israel reports the loss of fewer than 10 soldiers since the ceasefire went into effect.

In all, Palestinian authorities say more than 74,000 people have been killed inside Gaza during Israel's retaliation for Hamas' October 7, 2023, assault — which killed roughly 1,200 Israelis, most of whom were civilians.

Beyond the threat of violent gunfire and airstrikes, civilians in Gaza continue to face ongoing food insecurity and lack of adequate shelter as winter approaches.

On Tuesday, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) and the UN's World Food Programme (WFP) warned that funding shortfalls and access restrictions put untold numbers of Palestinian lives at risk.

Currently, more than 1.7 million Gazans are displaced, with IOM and WFP saying 1.4 million face acute hunger and 1.98 million require shelter assistance.

With Gaza's infrastructure decimated by years of Hamas mismanagement and pounding Israeli attacks, more than 80% of Palestinians are unemployed, meaning they cannot afford food and have been forced to burn garbage to try to keep warm.

"Families in Gaza are facing their first sleepless nights of a very long winter ahead," said WFP Palestine Country Director Shaun Hughes. "Unless we get immediate support to scale up the humanitarian response, we risk this becoming not only a shelter crisis but also a deeper food security crisis."
