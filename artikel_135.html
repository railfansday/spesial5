Violent protests and killings in Bangladesh ahead of exiled Sheikh Hasina’s planned return

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Awami League party called for ⁠nationwide protest on Thursday after the government rejected Hasina’s request for passport

Parts of Bangladesh were shut down Thursday as protests by supporters of former prime minister Sheikh Hasina sparked clashes, bomb blasts and arson attacks, with the ancestral home ⁠of former leader Muhammad Yunus set ablaze in the violence.

Hasina’s Awami League party had called for a ​week-long programme of protests and rallies to mobilise support ‌for Hasina’s planned return in December, culminating in a ‌nationwide lockdown on Thursday.

Hasina, who has lived in exile in neighbouring India since she fled Dhaka following violent ⁠protests demanding her resignation in August 2024, has requested a new passport and identity card to facilitate her return. Her supporters are protesting against the government’s rejection of that request.

To deter ⁠violence, 97 platoons of the Bangladesh Border Guard fanned out across seven districts, including Gopalganj and Faridpur, considered to have many Hasina supporters, a spokesperson ⁠said.

Police said former interim leader Yunus’ ancestral home in Chattogram, about 300km (186 miles) south-east of Dhaka, ​was set on fire late on ‌Wednesday.

It is likely that flammable ‌substances were thrown into an unused ground-floor room, police told Reuters, adding that Yunus’ paternal family members, who live on the upper floors, are safe.

Reuters could not immediately reach the Yunus Centre thinktank that speaks on his behalf to seek comment. Yunus and his government, which led Bangladesh after Hasina’s ouster, have faced hostility from her supporters.

During the last 10 days of protests, police have reported dozens of crude bomb blasts and incidents of ‌vehicles being set afire in at least 28 districts, injuring several police officials and members of the public, media have said.

Late on Tuesday, an activist of the ruling Bangladesh Nationalist party (BNP) was allegedly killed by unidentified people during a protest near Hasina’s home district of Gopalganj, police said.

On the same night, a crude bomb exploded outside the Dhaka residence of cultural affairs minister Nitai Roy Chowdhury, and the house of a BNP lawmaker and shops ‌belonging to BNP supporters in Gopalganj were set on fire, police added.

On Wednesday morning, a bus company helper was burnt to death in the southern district of Pirojpur after two buses ​were set on fire, authorities said.

Tuesday marks a year since Hasina was ​sentenced to death by a local court that found her guilty of ordering a deadly crackdown on a student-led uprising against her in 2024. She has lived in exile in neighbouring India since fleeing Dhaka.

On Thursday, authorities detained former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid, who faces charges ⁠over several killings during the 2024 protests, from his house in Dhaka, police told Reuters.

At ​least 37 branches and offices of ​Grameen Bank, set up by Yunus, have been targeted in arson attacks or ​attempted fires since 10 November, ‌with 14 more ​attacked since midnight ​on Tuesday, the bank said.

On Tuesday, the home minister Salahuddin Ahmed accused Awami League leaders abroad of inciting supporters to destabilise Bangladesh and torch property, adding that those involved in violence would be identified and prosecuted.

Awami League leaders, most of them in hiding, could not be reached for comment.
