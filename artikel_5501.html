How GTA Online makes money: free updates, Shark Cards and 10 moments

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

How GTA Online makes money comes down to Shark Cards, free updates and console and PC re-releases, traced here through 10 moments from 2013 to 2022

GTA Online gives away its new content and sells the currency players spend inside it. New missions, vehicles, properties and game modes arrive as free updates. Many of the biggest items in those updates carry steep prices. Players pay those prices in GTA$, an in-game currency they can either earn through play or buy with real money.

That arrangement explains how GTA Online makes money. It turned a multiplayer mode into a business built on content that players never pay for directly. Each new update gives players something else to spend on. Each purchase helps fund the next round of free updates.

The mode came from Rockstar Games, the studio that developed Grand Theft Auto V, and Take-Two Interactive $TTWO, its publisher and parent company. GTA V went on sale Sept. 17, 2013. Take-Two reported that the game passed $1 billion in sales within its first three days. That figure covers GTA V as a whole, not GTA Online on its own. The online mode opened on Oct. 1, two weeks later, inside a game that had already reached a large audience.

The start was rough. Connection failures and characters that would not save marked the first weeks. Rockstar gave affected players GTA$500,000 as an apology. From there the model took shape one piece at a time. Shark Cards, added in the weeks after launch, gave players a direct way to buy GTA$. Heists, businesses and a casino added new things to do and new things to buy. Releases on newer consoles and PC kept bringing fresh players into the same online world.

The 10 slides that follow are a selection of those moments and mechanisms, arranged in chronological order. They run from the October 2013 launch to the March 2022 release on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X $TWTR|S. That release arrived alongside a new subscription called GTA+. These slides are not a ranking, and they are not the complete story. Each one covers a specific update, release or business decision in the mode's history.

GTA Online opened on Oct. 1, 2013, two weeks after Grand Theft Auto V went on sale on Sept. 17. The online mode was not sold separately. It arrived as part of a game that players had already bought.

Rockstar Games developed both the story campaign and the online mode. Rockstar North, the studio's team in Edinburgh, Scotland, led the work. Take-Two Interactive $TTWO published GTA V and is Rockstar's parent company.

GTA V first went on sale for PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360. GTA Online opened on those same two consoles. On those consoles, a single session held up to 16 players.

The story campaign follows three playable leads: Michael, Franklin and Trevor. Their story plays out in Los Santos, a fictional city modeled on Los Angeles, and in the countryside of Blaine County. GTA Online uses the same map. Players build their own character instead of controlling the three story leads.

That shared setting tied the two halves of the package together. Players who spent the first two weeks in the campaign already knew the streets, the vehicles and the layout of the city. The multiplayer side did not have to introduce a new world.

Some faces carried over as well. Characters from the campaign, including Lamar Davis and Lester Crest, hand out jobs to online players. Those jobs sit alongside races, deathmatches and other modes that pit players against each other or put them on the same team.

Progress in the online mode runs on two tracks. Reputation points, known as RP, raise a character's rank and unlock weapons, clothing and activities. GTA$, the in-game currency, pays for cars, apartments and gear. Completing jobs earns both.

The release order also shaped how the mode found its audience. GTA Online did not need to attract buyers on its own. Its potential player base was everyone who already owned GTA V on PlayStation 3 or Xbox 360.

GTA Online launched in October 2013 with serious connection failures and lost character saves. Rockstar Games answered by giving affected players GTA$500,000 in in-game currency as an apology.

The trouble began on day one. GTA Online went live on Oct. 1, 2013, on PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360. Those were the same consoles Grand Theft Auto V had reached two weeks earlier. Players opened the online mode from inside GTA V and did not buy it separately. Many could not connect at all. Others got in, only to find the game had failed to save the characters they had built.

Lost saves struck at the core of the mode. GTA Online characters are created by players and are separate from Michael, Franklin and Trevor, the three protagonists of the GTA V story mode. Each one is a persistent character who earns money, buys property and climbs in rank over time. A failed save erased that progress. A player who lost a character in the first week had to start again from the beginning.

Rockstar released patches over the following weeks to address the connection and save problems. The compensation was part of that recovery. Instead of cash refunds, the studio paid players in GTA$, the fictional currency used throughout GTA Online. GTA$ exists only inside the game and cannot be exchanged back into real money.

The payment fit the way the mode works. Players spend GTA$ on cars, weapons, apartments and other items in Los Santos, the game's fictional version of Los Angeles. Apartments also serve as a base, with garage space for the cars a player collects. A payment of GTA$500,000 gave affected players a head start on those purchases.

The broken start did not end GTA Online. The mode recovered from its October 2013 failures and kept its players. Rockstar went on to support it with new content for years.

Shark Cards let GTA Online players spend real money to receive GTA$, the in-game currency. That currency buys the cars, weapons, apartments and clothing sold inside the game. The cards form the direct link between GTA Online's virtual economy and real-world revenue.

Rockstar Games added Shark Cards in the weeks after GTA Online opened on Oct. 1, 2013. The online mode came included with Grand Theft Auto V at no extra charge. The cards gave Rockstar a way to earn money from players after the initial game sale.

The cards are sold in tiers named after sharks, including Red Shark, Great White Shark and Megalodon. Larger tiers deliver more GTA$ for a higher price. Megalodon takes its name from an extinct prehistoric shark and sits at the top of the range.

The purchase itself is simple. A player picks a card, pays in real currency and sees the GTA$ added to their in-game balance. Cards can be bought through a platform's digital storefront or through the store menu inside the game.

The GTA$ is then spent at in-game shops and on the game's fictional websites. Legendary Motorsport, the in-game site for high-end cars, is one example. Property and vehicle purchases go through these same menus whether the money was earned or bought.

Shark Cards do not unlock a separate catalog. GTA$ bought with a card is the same currency players earn by completing jobs, missions and races. A player who never spends real money can reach the same items by playing longer.

That earn-or-buy choice defines how the cards work. Players with limited time can skip hours of repeated jobs. Players with more time can earn the same currency without paying.

Take-Two Interactive $TTWO, Rockstar's publisher and parent company, reports this kind of ongoing in-game spending under a category it calls recurrent consumer spending. The cards turned GTA Online into a product that could keep earning money long after a player bought the base game.

The Heists update arrived in GTA Online on March 10, 2015, about a year and a half after the mode opened. It added cooperative, multi-stage robberies that a crew plans and carries out together. Each stage feeds into a final job.

Heists were not new to the Grand Theft Auto series. The single-player campaign of Grand Theft Auto V is built around them, with its three protagonists planning and pulling off large scores. Online players waited until 2015 for a version of that structure designed for groups.

The update shipped with five heists: The Fleeca Job, The Prison Break, The Humane Labs Raid, Series A Funding and The Pacific Standard Job. Each one follows the same basic pattern. A crew completes a set of setup missions, then attempts a finale that carries the main payout. Setup missions range from stealing vehicles to collecting equipment the finale depends on.

Most of the heists call for four players. The Fleeca Job, the first in the sequence, needs only two. Lester Crest, a character from the story mode, offers the jobs.

Planning happens on a board inside a high-end apartment. A player has to own one of these properties to host a heist. Apartments are bought with GTA$. The host also pays the setup costs before any missions begin.

That host acts as heist leader. The leader assigns players to roles and decides what share of the final payout each crew member receives. Roles change from one job to the next. One player might fly or drive while another hacks a security system or provides cover.

The update came out on PlayStation 3, Xbox 360, PlayStation 4 and Xbox One at the same time. It gave every console version of the online mode the same new long-form content on the same day. Grand Theft Auto V had not yet reached PC at that point.

Rockstar Games releases new GTA Online content as free updates instead of selling it as paid expansions. Revenue comes afterward, when players spend GTA$ on the vehicles, weapons, properties and other items those updates introduce.

That arrangement differs from a paid add-on. A player downloads an update at no cost and gets access to its new missions and activities. Many of the items inside it carry an in-game price tag, though. To own them, the player needs enough GTA$ in the bank.

There are two ways to get that money. Players can earn GTA$ by completing missions, races and other jobs inside the game. They can also buy it with real money through Shark Cards, the currency packs Rockstar sells for GTA Online. Both routes lead to the same GTA$ balance.

This structure links each content release to Shark Card sales. A new car or property gives players a specific GTA$ target. A player short of that total can keep earning through jobs or buy the difference with real money. The update costs nothing, but what it unlocks can lead to a real-money purchase.

Free updates also keep the online player base together. Paid expansions divide players into those who bought the add-on and those who did not. When every player receives the same content, new missions and items reach the whole audience at once. A new vehicle or activity is open to everyone who plays GTA Online, not only to buyers of a specific pack.

Rockstar used the paid model with its previous game. Grand Theft Auto IV received two paid episodic expansions in 2009, The Lost and Damned and The Ballad of Gay Tony. GTA Online took a different path, offering its additions as downloads with no separate charge.

The approach has carried through the mode's major releases. Large cooperative missions, property and business updates and later content drops all arrived without a purchase price of their own. Each one added new items with GTA$ prices attached.

Three GTA Online updates pushed players toward buying property and running criminal businesses inside the game. They were Executives and Other Criminals in 2015, Gunrunning in 2017 and After Hours in 2018.

Each update added expensive properties and businesses. Players paid for them with GTA$, the currency used throughout the mode. The updates themselves came free. The price sat inside the game rather than at the point of release.

Before these releases, a GTA Online player mostly took on jobs and finished them. The business updates added ownership to the mode. A player now had property to buy and an operation to run.

Executives and Other Criminals came first. As its name signals, the update cast the player as the head of a criminal enterprise. The player was no longer only a lone operator picking up work.

Gunrunning followed in 2017. Its theme was arms dealing. It carried the ownership idea into a second line of illegal work.

After Hours arrived in 2018 and became known as the nightclub update. Its new content revolved around a nightclub that players could own. That made it the third business type in the series, after the criminal enterprise and the arms trade.

Price links the three releases. Each one put a large GTA$ cost on the property or business at its center. Those costs gave players a concrete target for the currency. A player saving for a business had a specific item in mind, not just a growing balance.

The updates also built on one another. Each arrived as a new business type rather than a replacement for the last one. A player who bought into an earlier business did not lose it when the next update arrived. Each release instead added another option to spend GTA$ on.

From 2015 to 2018, owning an in-game business became a recurring theme of new GTA Online content rather than the subject of one release.

Grand Theft Auto V and GTA Online were re-released for PlayStation 4 and Xbox One in November 2014, then for PC on April 14, 2015. Each new version brought another group of players into the same online mode.

The console re-release came about 14 months after the original game went on sale. The PC version followed roughly 19 months after that first launch.

None of these releases created a separate online game. Players on the new platforms entered the same GTA Online that had opened in October 2013. They joined its missions, its in-game economy and its GTA$ currency.

That meant newcomers did not arrive at a blank start. By November 2014, the mode had been running for more than a year. Its missions, prices and GTA$ economy were already in place when the new versions went on sale.

That structure ties each re-release to the mode's revenue model. GTA Online earns money when players use real money to buy GTA$ through Shark Cards. Those cards were already on sale when the new versions arrived. A new platform adds more people who can make those purchases. Rockstar Games did not have to build a new multiplayer game to reach them.

The PC release also followed a major content drop. The Heists update came out on March 10, 2015. The PC version arrived about five weeks later. PC players joined with the cooperative heist missions already live. They could plan and carry out those multi-stage missions with other players from their first weeks. The newest content and the newest platform reached players within weeks of each other.

Rockstar Games developed each version, and Take-Two Interactive $TTWO published them. The same online mode carried on across every format. Players on every system bought the same GTA$ and spent it on the same kinds of in-game items. Each release widened its audience without changing how it made money.

The Diamond Casino & Resort update arrived in GTA Online in July 2019. It added an in-game casino that served two roles: a social space where players could gather, and a new source of content inside the mode.

Rockstar Games, which develops GTA Online, released the update nearly six years after the multiplayer mode opened on Oct. 1, 2013. Take-Two Interactive $TTWO publishes the game and is Rockstar's parent company.

Many earlier updates centered on missions, properties and businesses. The casino offered a different kind of content. It was not another job to finish or another business to manage. It was a destination inside the game world where players could meet and spend time together.

The update also sits at a specific point in the mode's timeline. It came the year after After Hours, the 2018 nightclub update. It arrived before the Cayo Perico Heist, which followed in December 2020.

Like other GTA Online content, the casino reached players as a free update to the existing mode. It was not sold as a separately priced expansion.

The Cayo Perico Heist launched in GTA Online on Dec. 15, 2020. It was set on a private island, and players could complete it alone or with a group.

That solo option broke from how GTA Online heists had worked before. The Heists update of 2015 was built around a crew. Its missions were cooperative jobs that players planned and carried out together. Cayo Perico made those other players optional rather than required.

The change removed a practical hurdle for players without a regular crew. A heist no longer depended on finding teammates, matching schedules or waiting for others to join. Players who preferred company could still bring a group along.

The design widened the audience for a major piece of content. A crew-only heist served players who already had organized groups. A heist one person could finish was open to anyone who logged in to the mode.

The private island also gave GTA Online a new setting for a heist. Earlier updates had expanded the mode with businesses, properties and a casino. Cayo Perico added a destination of its own.

The release date shows how long the mode had stayed active. The heist arrived more than seven years after GTA Online opened on Oct. 1, 2013. It came about five years and nine months after the original Heists update of March 10, 2015. It followed the Diamond Casino & Resort update of July 2019 by about 17 months.

Those gaps point to a mode that kept growing rather than being replaced. Rockstar Games added a new heist to the same online world that had opened in 2013, instead of moving players to a new game.

Cayo Perico reached players the same way as other GTA Online content. Rockstar Games delivered it as a free update. Players did not buy the heist as a separate expansion. Like other GTA Online activities, the heist paid out in GTA$, giving solo players a way to earn the currency through play.

GTA V and GTA Online arrived on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X $TWTR|S on March 15, 2022. Rockstar Games paired the release with two changes to how players could get into the online mode and pay for it. The first was a standalone GTA Online download on PlayStation 5. The second was a subscription called GTA+.

The release moved GTA Online onto a newer generation of home consoles than the PS4 and Xbox One versions of November 2014. The mode had opened on Oct. 1, 2013, so the new versions came about eight and a half years into its life. Rockstar Games developed the game, and Take-Two Interactive $TTWO publishes it.

The standalone version split the multiplayer mode off from the GTA V story campaign. On PS5, a player could download GTA Online by itself instead of buying the full game. For the first three months after release, that download was free.

The free entry point fit a game that earns its money from spending after the download. Every new player joins the same online world, where GTA$ pays for vehicles, properties and businesses. Players can earn that currency through play or buy it with real money through Shark Cards. Under that model, the revenue comes from what players buy once they arrive, not from the download itself.

Rockstar launched GTA+ the same month. It is a paid subscription service for GTA Online that bills players on a recurring basis. It added another way to pay, alongside buying the game and buying Shark Cards.

Both changes kept the core of the model intact. New content stays free to play, and spending happens inside the game. The standalone download widened the way in, and GTA+ added a recurring way to pay once there.
