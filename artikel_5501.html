ULA's Vulcan rocket failed mid-flight, destroying 40 Amazon internet satellites

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The failure on Amazon Leo's first Vulcan mission is the first for ULA and is expected to lead to a lengthy investigation

United Launch Alliance, the Boeing $BA-Lockheed Martin $LMT venture, lost 40 Amazon $AMZN Leo internet satellites on Sunday when its Vulcan rocket broke down roughly 10 minutes into its climb from Cape Canaveral, Florida, according to Reuters. The failure is the first in the company's history.

The rocket lifted off at 12:40 p.m. ET, according to Bloomberg.

Amazon had targeted a Nov. 15 liftoff for the mission, called Leo Vulcan 1 (LV-01), the company said. It was the first of 38 launches Amazon has contracted with ULA, and it was to carry 40 satellites to low Earth orbit.

ULA said the vehicle "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit," according to Reuters. The cause was unclear, and ULA said it had no specific data yet on what occurred.

"There is risk with any launch, and that's especially true for inaugural missions like LV-01," Amazon said in a statement, according to Reuters. Amazon said it believes ULA will take lessons from the flight and follow through on its longer-range goals for Vulcan.

The failure is expected to trigger a long technical probe that would sideline the rocket from commercial service for months, according to Reuters. Blue Origin, which supplies Vulcan's BE-4 engines, is working with ULA on the investigation. Reuters said it was unclear whether the engines played a role.

Sunday's flight was only the fifth for Vulcan since it debuted in 2024, and it followed a nine-month gap between launches, according to Reuters. Amazon has placed 398 Leo satellites in orbit over 14 launches since 2025, roughly 12% of the 3,236 required to offer service worldwide.

Before the launch, Amazon said its second Vulcan mission, LV-02, was being prepared and was expected to launch within weeks of a nominal LV-01 mission. The company also said its next Arianespace mission, Leo Europe 4, was targeting no earlier than Nov. 23 from Kourou, French Guiana.
