India’s crypto industry is asking the government for a major tax reset ahead of the Union Budget 2027. The Bharat Web3 Association (BWA) has submitted five key recommendations to the Finance Ministry, including a lower 1% TDS, loss relief, and changes to the 30% tax on crypto gains.

The Bharat Web3 Association shared its recommendations with the Finance Ministry ahead of the Budget. It said India needs a tax system that encourages people to trade on Indian platforms instead of moving their activity to offshore exchanges.

The demand comes as India’s current VDA tax structure continues to impose a 30% flat tax on gains, along with a 1% TDS on eligible transfers.

Under the Income Tax Act, 2025, traders can deduct only the cost of buying crypto from their taxable gains. They cannot use crypto losses to reduce other gains or carry those losses forward.

This makes the 1% TDS a major concern for active traders.

Therefore, BWA believes the current framework needs to be reviewed to make compliance more practical while keeping the government’s tax and reporting controls in place.

However, BWA has proposed five major changes to India’s crypto tax and regulatory framework.

The impact of India’s current crypto tax structure can also be seen in where users are trading.

According to a TIOL Knowledge Foundation report, around 95% of India’s crypto trading volume moved to offshore exchanges in FY 2025–26, while only 5.4% took place on Indian platforms.

The report estimates that India has around 30 million active crypto users, with roughly 3 million to 5 million users moving to offshore platforms after the government introduced the 1% TDS on crypto transactions.

The shift has also appeared in user activity. Between July 2025 and April 2026, visits by Indian users to a major offshore peer-to-peer platform increased 73%, while purchases grew 3.6 times.

This migration could also have a major impact on government tax collections. The report estimates that the government may have missed around ₹19,049 crore ($2 billion) in TDS and ₹57,147 crore ($6.18 billion) in capital gains tax through FY 2025–26 as trading activity moved to offshore platforms.

The numbers explain why the offshore-exchange issue has become a central part of BWA’s recommendations.

BWA believes the current framework needs to be reviewed to make compliance more practical while keeping the government’s tax and reporting controls in place.

The association says these changes could encourage more users to participate through compliant Indian platforms rather than shifting activity elsewhere.

The proposals go beyond changing taxes for domestic users.

BWA wants Indian tax and TDS requirements to apply more clearly to offshore crypto exchanges serving Indian users. The association argues that Indian platforms face significant compliance obligations while offshore competitors can attract the same users without facing an equivalent regulatory burden.

It has also called for stronger enforcement of registration and reporting requirements for offshore entities operating in the Indian market.

Another major issue is access to global liquidity.

BWA wants clear rules for FIU-IND-registered Indian VDA platforms that access liquidity through offshore exchanges. It has called for clearer guidance under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), particularly where different interpretations have created uncertainty for compliant platforms.

The broader regulatory framework is also becoming more demanding. Reporting entities face penalties for delayed or inaccurate transaction statements, while cross-border crypto-asset reporting under the OECD’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) is scheduled to begin in April 2027.

The industry’s argument is becoming harder to separate from the numbers: India has a 30% tax on VDA gains, a 1% TDS and restrictions on using crypto losses, while reporting and compliance requirements are also expanding.

These rules were left unchanged in the 2026–27 Union Budget. BWA is now asking the Finance Ministry to reconsider the framework before Budget 2027.

Its five main demands are clear: reduce the 1% TDS, allow loss set-off and carry-forward, review the 30% tax, strengthen rules for offshore exchanges and clarify FEMA requirements for global liquidity access.

For BWA, the goal is not simply lower taxes. It is to create a system where compliant Indian platforms can compete, users have clearer rules and the government can strengthen oversight and tax collection without driving more crypto activity offshore.

Budget 2027 will show whether the government is willing to make that shift.

Bharat Web3 Association (BWA) presented the Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) industry’s recommendations to the Ministry of Finance during the pre-budget consultations ahead of Union Budget 2027.

The BWA submission focused on the need for a fair, balanced and effective taxation framework that encourages compliance, strengthens investor protection, enhances tax revenues and supports the growth of India’s domestic Web3 ecosystem.

BWA highlighted several key recommendations, including:

1. Ensuring a level playing field: Clarifying and strengthening the applicability of Indian tax provisions, particularly TDS obligations, to offshore VDA platforms serving Indian users, so that compliant domestic platforms are not placed at a competitive disadvantage.

2. Rationalising VDA taxation: Reconsidering the existing 1% TDS, permitting the set-off and carry-forward of losses, and reviewing the 30% tax rate to encourage greater participation through compliant Indian platforms.

3. Strengthening compliance and accountability: Improving the enforcement of registration and reporting obligations and ensuring that offshore entities serving Indian users are subject to appropriate Indian legal and regulatory requirements.

We also drew attention to the need for regulatory clarity regarding access to global liquidity by FIU-IND-registered Indian VDA platforms through offshore exchanges, particularly in the context of ongoing enquiries and differing interpretations under FEMA.

Clear and consistent guidelines, developed in consultation with the relevant authorities, would provide much-needed certainty to compliant Indian platforms, strengthen the domestic ecosystem and help ensure that Indian users continue to access transparent, accountable and compliant channels.

BWA’s central message was that effective taxation, regulatory certainty and a level playing field can reinforce one another.

A framework that encourages activity within compliant Indian platforms can strengthen oversight, improve tax compliance and support innovation and investment in India.

BWA remains committed to working constructively with the Government and regulatory authorities towards a responsible, transparent and globally competitive VDA ecosystem.
