Hyperliquid Hits Hong Kong Alert List as HYPE Erases 5% Drop

On Nov. 18, Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) added the decentralized exchange Hyperliquid to its list of suspicious virtual asset trading platforms. The listing covers the web interface of the Hyperliquid trading terminal at app.hyperliquid.xyz/trade. The HYPE token dropped to around $65.50 on the evening of Nov. 18, and it recovered to $68.79 by midday Nov. 19, according to TradingView data.

According to the entry, the company claims to provide digital asset trading services on the website, and it has no address on record. Hyperliquid is not licensed by the SFC, and it is suspected of operating a virtual asset trading platform in Hong Kong or actively promoting its services to the public, the regulator said.

In an accompanying warning, the SFC reminded investors that such platforms often lure users with "incredible investment opportunities." The SFC also reminded investors that clients of unlicensed platforms have no legal protection if a platform is hacked or misappropriates their assets.

On the same day, the Hyperliquid Policy Center (HPC) sent a letter to the U.K. Financial Conduct Authority with seven recommendations on the country's new crypto rules. "Regulatory obligations belong with persons who have unilateral control over a function that is a regulated activity," the group wrote.

On Nov. 16, two days before the SFC added Hyperliquid to the list, Hong Kong Financial Secretary Paul Chan spoke at the Bloomberg Global Regulatory Forum. "Before the end of this year, we will introduce a bill to complete the regulatory perimeter," Chan said.

According to Chan, the bill will cover crypto dealers, custodians, advisers and asset managers. "Decentralisation does not mean less accountability," he said.

In April, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority licensed HSBC and Anchorpoint Financial as the first stablecoin issuers in the city, out of 36 applicants in the first round.
