Eagles notebook: Nolan Smith reacts to the Jordan Davis trade

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Eagles notebook: Nolan Smith reacts to the Jordan Davis trade originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Nolan Smith and Jordan Davis were teammates at Georgia and then became teammates again in the NFL. They have been friends for a long time.

"Man, he's a great guy," Smith said on Friday. "That's part of the business. That's how it goes. I'm pretty sure Jacksonville is picking up a good player. But we have to focus on us. We have leaders in this locker room. We've got guys who can lead and push guys."

Davis was traded to the Jaguars ahead of the trade deadline last week and this was the first chance to catch up with Smith since the trade. So it has been over a week since it all went down. Jalen Carter, who also played with Davis at Georgia before becoming teammates in the NFL, was again not available to reporters this week.

The return on the Davis trade for the Eagles was undeniable. Getting a first-round pick and second-round pick for Davis represented incredible value. But it doesn't mean they won't miss Davis.

"Man, it was special," Smith said of their time together in Philly. "But that time come and go, man. I'm still playing with guys that I played with at Georgia. But that was a real special moment, though."

The Eagles are back from their bye week and Sean Mannion is still their offensive coordinator and play-caller. Mannion this week even said he got a vote of confidence from Nick Sirianni.

"We're in conversation all the time," Mannion said. "Nick's been totally supportive since I got here in, what was it? I guess February, January. We remain in lockstep and it's great getting to work with him every day.

"Like I said, it's the same things I'm saying about the joy I get working with the players in here. The same can be said about our coaching staff, whether it be with Nick, with our assistants, everybody across the board, it's been totally positive."

If Sirianni decided to hand over play-calling to a different coach during the bye week, it would have been understandable. After all, the Eagles are 32nd out of 32 teams in yards this season and are 30th in points. But this is Mannion's offense and going to someone else (like Josh Grizzard) likely wouldn't fix the problems.

Mannion, 34, is new at this. It's his first year as an NFL offensive coordinator and his first time as a play caller. There was always likely going to be a learning curve; but it has been steeper than many envisioned.

But the fact that Sirianni has been asked multiple times this season if he's sticking with Mannion is telling. A lot of the outside criticisms of Mannion are warranted. Mannion tries to block out that discussion.

"I don't pay attention to any of that stuff," Mannion said. "I'm just totally focused on the role I'm in and doing everything I possibly can to help our players be successful, help our team be successful. You mentioned it, but just kind of blocking out the outside noise. I'm doing everything I can every day to help the Eagles and to help us win and be a successful offense. If there's things that are kind of orbiting outside that, it doesn't pertain to that task and that focus that I have on what's going on here."

Saquon Barkley made it clear that he doesn't know exactly what T.J. Paganetti's role is going to be with the coaching staff now that Paganetti was brought back as a consultant during the bye week.

But Barkley on Friday realized he never really publicly praised Paganetti for his contributions during Barkley's 2,000-yard season. So he took the chance to do that.

"I think having T.J. back is great," Barkley said. "I don't really know to the extent of how involved he's going to be. I'll leave that up to the coaches. I will say in '24. I'm not saying what happened in '24 is going to happen (again). I'm just saying, giving him props. In '24 he probably was like the secret sauce that no one really knew or talked about. He played a key role in the way we were able to play that year and run the ball at a high level.

"Does that mean bringing him in right now that we're going to …? I can't speak on that. To be honest, that's not where my mind's going. But also giving credit to him. There's a reason why Kellen took him when he went. Behind the scenes, fans and people know it's a lot of work that was put in but he definitely played a major part. So it was just good to see his face and have a conversation with him."

Vic Fangio has been coaching in the NFL for decades and is known for his sometimes gruff demeanor. But even he has a soft spot for the recently traded Jordan Davis.

"Yeah, it's just part of the NFL business," Fangio said. "We're going to miss Jordan. I'm going to miss him personally. Got a little emotional with him when we talked about it after it happened.

"I've got a lot of players that over the years I've remained in touch with and had a good relationship with, some 40 years ago, 30, 20, 10 years ago. He's one of them. So, I'll miss him personally, but he'll do good and we'll be ready to roll."

The Eagles late last week waived veteran tackle Fred Johnson, who played an important role with the team for several years.

From 2023-26, Johnson played in 58 games with 14 starts. He resurrected his career in Philly, which led to this unforgettable moment in the 2024 season when Johnson had to enter the game for Lane Johnson in a 15-12 win in the Superdome.

This was Johnson's first real playing time since the 2021 season. And all of his teammates were thrilled for him.

"After that touchdown to Saquon, I almost cried on the sideline," Johnson said. "I ain't going to lie. I got emotional. I got really emotional because I thought I'd never be back in action. Everybody knows some of my story. It's just been a long time coming. When I seen him running to score, I got hype, real hype. I was just grateful for the moment. I'm ready to go back out and do it again."

Here are some streaks to keep in mind as the Eagles go into this game on Sunday:

• After winning their first two games of the season, the Eagles have lost seven straight. That's the longest active losing streak in the NFL after the Chargers were able to get a win on Monday Night Football. With a loss on Sunday, this would be the longest Eagles losing streak since they lost eight in a row in 2012.

There's a chance this is something that interests only me. But sometimes when there aren't a ton of obvious questions for Michael Clay, I like to get into the weeds with some special teams topics. This week, I asked him about how the Eagles coach the defense in situations where they stay on the field for a punt return.

A couple of these have come up this year. One happened against the Giants in Week 9 when the defense stayed on the field for a 4th-and-1 in the first quarter. Riq Woolen — not normally in as a jammer — ended up getting called for an illegal block.

Here was Clay's answer about getting the defense ready for those situations:

"I talk about it a lot on Fridays in our meetings right here. Obviously, I have all the defense on Saturdays in our walk-through, where we're able to go through a lot of it and talk about different things. What does this coordinator like to do in fourth-and-short scenarios? Who's their personnel? Is it a running back? Can this punter throw? Just little tidbits in there so they know, hey, alert. Something very similar to last year with Detroit. We knew (Dave) Fipp and Detroit ran a lot of fakes in that 4th-and-2, 4th-and-3 area, but it's just my job to give these guys the information. Hopefully, they can retain it, and a lot of the time they do, so they keep themselves, one, from getting hurt and, two, alert for something, a team trying to steal an extra possession, especially in a defensive back-and-forth."
