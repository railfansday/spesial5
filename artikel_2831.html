Why extending Riley Moss was a smart decision for the Denver Broncos

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Denver Broncos continued their tradition of rewarding their homegrown talent. Yesterday, news broke that the Broncos had agreed to terms with fourth-year cornerback Riley Moss on a four-year contract worth $67 million. As of now, the guarantees have not been made available. However, the average annual value makes him the NFL's 23rd highest paid corner. In my opinion, that's quite the bargain.

This summer, I asked Broncos Country who they felt deserved extension priority—Moss or Ja'Quan McMillian. I was under the impression that Denver would have to just choose one. It was a close battle, but Moss came ahead with 56-percent of the votes.

Flash forward to recent memory and fans seemed to have changed their tune. A little over a month ago, Scotty Payne wrote an article and put a poll up on Mile High Report's X account asking the same question. The result? McMillian in a landslide with 94-percent of the votes.

Would I love for McMillian to come back too? Absolutely. He has been an underrated playmaker in their secondary and has a penchant for big plays in crunch time. It appears Denver will revisit negotiations after the season, but I have a feeling the Broncos' standout nickelback may test the open market. Though I'd like to offer some thoughts on why extending Moss was a good decision.

The Broncos' defense under Vance Joseph is known for their aggressive man-to-man coverage assignments. This season due to injuries in the secondary, they have utilized more zone coverage than prior years. However, their man numbers are still higher compared to many teams in the NFL.

In fact, I'd argue that Joseph would like to be running more man than he has this season. Every team uses a mixture of both, but the difference compared to past season is notable. With a healthy Moss and Surtain, I expect Denver's man coverage numbers to go up quite significantly in the second half of the season.

The main critique on Moss has been those pesky pass interference penalties. That's fair. However, I'd just say it's also a part of the process considering how often he is targeted and tasked with man-to-man assignments. Last year, Moss was targeted an astounding 118 times. However, he allowed just a 58-percent completion rate and had the most pass breakups of any corner in the league.

This season, the targets have been significantly less. The former Iowa prospect has been thrown at only 35 times. Through eight games, Moss has allowed 18 receptions for 223 yards. That's only a 51.4 completion percentage allowed—certainly one of the best marks in the NFL for any defensive back. Even with those penalties, he has proven to be a great player for Denver.

Only time will tell if the Broncos can come to terms with McMillian too. As I wrote in my article this summer, I felt that they would likely only choose one for cap purposes. Truth be told, they have the cap flexibility to accommodate a deal. Additionally, there are also various contracts that may be moved on to bolster their salary cap space.

However, I question whether or not General Manager George Paton and the rest of the Broncos' brass would want to invest such a monumental number of guarantees at the cornerback position. McMillian will likely get a lucrative deal in free agency and have no shortage of suitors. In the event he embarks on greener pastures, the Broncos do have his replacement already in house.

That's second-year cornerback Jahdae Barron. The former Jim Thorpe Award winner was a versatile defender for the Texas Longhorns. Barron played just about every spot imaginable in their secondary. His cross-training across the backend of the defense has continued into the pros in Joseph's scheme.

While many fans have already written him off as a potential bust due to lack of playing time, I still remain confident he has the ability to contribute at a high level. He seems like a more natural fit on the inside anyway. I'm not sure Barron had the length or athleticism to routinely hold his own in press and go head-to-head against bigger wide receivers in man coverage.

It's also important to note that Kris Abrams-Draine will be a free agent at the end of the 2027 season. Abrams-Draine has primarily been a boundary backup for Denver in his first three seasons. He has played well when the opportunity presented itself.

Quite frankly, I think he is a starting caliber player and there is a chance he could wind up receiving a nice free agent deal a few years down the line. With Moss extended, that's a little bit less of a worry at the boundary position moving forward, though they will still have to find depth at some point in time beyond the '27 season.
