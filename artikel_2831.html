Inside the Cowboys historic defensive collapse against the 49ers

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Dallas Cowboys lost a game to the San Francisco 49ers. That's not a shocker to anyone, as this Cowboys team has struggled all season, and the 49ers are the best team they've played so far, and by a good margin. Looking at their opponents, San Francisco is the only offense the Cowboys have faced this year that is ranked in the top eight in scoring, now averaging over 31 points a game. In fact, the Cowboys haven't fared well against offenses that average over 22 points a game.

They've played five teams that average more than 22 points/game. Their record against those teams is 1-4.

They've played five teams that average fewer than 22 points/game. Their record against those teams is 3-2.

Clearly, playing decent offenses is not ideal for this Cowboys defense, so when they faced the NFC's highest-scoring offense, it was a huge problem for a unit that has struggled immensely all season. Even so, it shouldn't have been that bad. The defense gave up 584 total yards on the day. It was the most yards they have ever given up at home during their 67-year existence. The most ever is never good.

The frustrating part about this game is that when you take a closer look, it's easy to tell that the Cowboys never had a shot. The defense was just outmatched. It was like playing one of those games where you play against an older sibling, where they might toy around with you for a bit, only to flatten you when it's time to get serious.

The 49ers did that to the Cowboys' defense. That doesn't mean they were playing opossum at any point during the game. It's just that whenever the 49ers found themselves in a crunch, they always had a go-to play where they knew they could take advantage of the Cowboys' vulnerabilities.

The Cowboys had several defensive breakdowns in the game, with multiple third-down letdowns and explosive plays. One of the most embarrassing plays came on the Deebo Samuel reverse play in the third quarter. When fullback Kyle Juszczyk went in motion, the entire defense was lured into a trance. Follow the Juice because that's where the action goes most of the time. Only this time, the Juice wasn't worth the squeeze.

Sadly, the entire defense took the bait. It was crazy to see how many defenders continued chasing running back Kaelon Black. That left an entourage of blockers out in front of Samuel, leaving very little chance for the Cowboys to make a stop. Malik Hooker and Shemar James were the first to figure it out, but they were so far out of position that by the time they caught up, they were left gambling and taking poor angles. Cornerback Joey Porter Jr. was the last hope. He's not the greatest tackler as it is, but he's even worse when he's washed out by multiple blockers.

In our game preview article, we mentioned that "Shanahanigans" would be an issue due to the Cowboys' over-pursuing nature, and right on cue, they obliged. It was one of the poorest-defended reverse plays we've seen.

Mental lapses are common for this defense, but more frustrating is when their effort is so lackadaisical. The touchdown run by the rookie Black was horrible for different reasons. It starts with Juice looping around with banana motion. He and wide receiver Jordan Watkins take on the outside defenders, leaving a nice hole for Black to run through. With no defenders to impede his path, Black gets a full head of steam and commits some terrible Big 10 rookie-on-rookie crime by running over Caleb Downs.

Yes, that's the Cowboys' top draft pick being leveled, but in fairness to Downs, he squared up well. It's just hard to defy physics when the running back comes plowing through with that much force.

The rest of the Cowboys' defense is sealed off from clever run-blocking that creeps to the second level. Cornerback Cobie Durant has one final chance to bring him down, but he gets run over like he's not even there. It wouldn't be a Cowboys game without a humiliating run-stop attempt against a team's backup running back.

Getting their playmakers in space was the name of the game for the 49ers, and they did it often. They just meandered off to one side, got the defense to cha-cha-cha in that direction, and then boom, sealed off! On the quick pass to Samuel, the Cowboys' defense was so out of position that their lone, helpless cornerback wasn't even able to get a hand on him.

And then there were the two long touchdown passes to Christian McCaffrey. To be fair, McCaffrey is a handful all of the time, so game planning for him isn't easy. The first touchdown catch was especially hard because the defense actually played well for 90% of the play. It was just hanging on for that last 10% that got them in trouble. McCaffrey was a sneaky sneakerton and, at the last second, leaked out into the secondary, and as soon as Brock Purdy saw that, it was over.

The second touchdown was a harder pill to swallow for a couple of reasons. First, it essentially iced the game. Any hopes of a comeback were crushed. Second, in the defense's biggest moment, they were once again escorted out of the play. It was a simple play for the 49ers, with all their receivers basically running away from where McCaffrey was, taking the defense with them. It was like Moses parting the Red Sea, only Kyle Shanahan was holding the staff, and it was the Cowboys' defense that opened up for the easiest pitch-and-catch you'll ever see.

The Cowboys' defense has been a problem all season, and the 49ers exploited every element of it. The same things keep happening, and they aren't showing any sign of improvement. And it's not going to get any easier with four of their final five games against offenses averaging over 22 points a game. This defense has fallen into troubling times with very little chance of escape.
