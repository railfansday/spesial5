Install WeWard for steps you’d take anyway, since it pays more per step on days you walk farther, but treat any step app as a bonus, never income. For real money from walking, walk dogs through Rover, where you keep most of the rate you set after its fee.

The CDC recommends at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity, such as brisk walking, so a step app can pay you for walking you’d do anyway. To compare the apps, we count an hour of brisk walking as about 6,000 steps, since the CADENCE-Adults study found that about 100 steps a minute marks moderate intensity in adults from 21 to 85.

Most step apps pay pennies for that hour, in points or coins you trade for cash, gift cards, or brand offers, so treat them as a bonus, not income. Real money comes from apps that pay for work done on foot, such as walking dogs, delivering food, and checking store shelves.

An hour’s walk of about 6,000 steps clears its 5,000-step milestone for 3 Wards, while a 20,000-step day earns 23, so longer days pay more per step.

Every 100 steps earns a coin, so an hour’s walk of about 6,000 steps adds 60 coins toward gift cards from well-known brands.

For more information, read our full CashWalk Review.

Sweatcoin turns an hour’s walk of about 6,000 verified steps into 5.7 sweatcoins, which buy offers inside its app but can’t be withdrawn as cash.

For more information, read our full Sweatcoin Review.

Walking earns the same 8 points a day, under a penny, whether you walk 10 minutes or an hour, so its paid surveys and research studies are where the money is.

No single walk pays on StepBet. Winners of its games, usually six weeks long, split the pot after a 15% cut, so it suits walkers who need money at stake to keep going.

For more information, read our full StepBet Review.

Rover’s walkers averaged $21.45 for a half-hour walk in September 2025, and its 20% fee in most of the U.S. leaves you about $17 of that, by our math.

Pet parents book its walks on demand or ahead, and since they pay 1.67 times your rate, two of its typical $24 half-hour walks pay you about $29, by our math.

Uber Eats still signs up couriers on foot in some cities, the only delivery app here that does, so no gas or car insurance comes out of each offer’s pay.

Its jobs at stores and other locations generally pay $3 to $20 each, so they pay best on walks or errands that already take you past them.
