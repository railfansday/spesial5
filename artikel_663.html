Wilshere v Ramsey - Arsenal legends treading similar managerial paths

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Jack Wilshere and Aaron Ramsey are the third and fourth youngest managers across the 92 Premier League and EFL clubs

Aaron Ramsey and Jack Wilshere have a lot in common.

Both were midfield prodigies. Both won the FA Cup playing for Arsenal under Arsene Wenger. Both represented their countries.

The image of the pair celebrating Ramsey's extra-time winner together in the 2014 FA Cup final against Hull City to end Arsenal's nine-year trophy drought at the time will forever be ingrained in the north London club's history.

They were teenagers when they made their Premier League debuts for the Gunners on the same day just two minutes apart as substitutes in a 4-0 win at Blackburn in September 2008.

Aged just 16 and 17, each were considered hot prospects who would go on to have excellent careers - and so it proved in many ways, even if both were restricted by injuries along the way.

Now 34 and 35, their paths cross again under different circumstances but many similarities very much still remain.

They are both in League One in their first permanent managerial jobs. And, guess what? Both their sides are in fine form and vying for promotion to the Championship.

It will be the first time they have met as permanent managers when Wilshere's Luton Town host Ramsey's Oxford United at Kenilworth Road on Saturday (15:00 GMT).

Jack Wilshere won two FA Cups with Arsenal and Aaron Ramsey won three

Ramsey and Wilshere have both tried to play down their reunion.

"Obviously it will be nice to see Jack again, but it's not about us," Ramsey told BBC Radio Oxford.

Except it is about them a little bit, given their 101 appearances in 10 years together in the Arsenal midfield - and as two young managers who are following almost identical paths.

Immediately after retiring from playing in July 2022, Wilshere was appointed the under-18s head coach at Arsenal, the club where he had risen from the youth academy to the first team.

Meanwhile, former Wales international Ramsey gained coaching experience towards the end of his playing career, also working with the under-18s at boyhood club Cardiff City.

Wilshere joined Norwich City as a coach for the 2024-25 season and it was at the end of that campaign he would first meet Ramsey as a manager in a remarkable twist of fate after they were both placed in interim charge of the Canaries and Bluebirds respectively.

The bragging rights went to Wilshere on that day in May last year as Norwich beat Cardiff 4-2.

Despite their close relationship and working towards the same objectives in the same league, they have not spoken "for a while".

"We are in a similar position at the moment, but I haven't had much dialogue with him over the last few months," said Ramsey.

"We're both fighting for the same things but, for me, it's just going out there to try and win a game of football."

"It doesn't change anything for how we prepare," Wilshere told BBC Three Counties Radio on Friday.

"Of course, when I see Aaron, that'll be a nice moment. We spent a lot of good times together.

"He's had a really good start and credit to him. He's got a football brain, he's got a good career in the game and he's clearly taking that into his coaching.

Only joint-leaders Stockport and Huddersfield have scored more goals than Luton (29) this season, while Oxford boast the third-best defensive record

Since taking over at Luton in October 2025, Wilshere has already tasted silverware, lifting the EFL Trophy at Wembley in April - a feeling he said he enjoyed more as a manager than when he was a player.

The 34-year-old guided the Hatters to seventh in League One last season, narrowly missing out on a play-off spot by a point.

Wilshere slightly edges his counterpart in terms of managerial experience, with 64 games in all competitions as Luton boss, while Ramsey has taken charge of 18 matches in his first season with the U's.

Neither could have their side in much better shape heading into Saturday's showdown.

After a stuttering start to 2026-27, Luton have won five of their past six games (D1) to climb from 16th up to fifth.

As for Ramsey, he is doing a fine job with Oxford considering the club have been under a transfer embargo since he arrived - and says he is "loving every second of it".

His side are on an 11-game unbeaten run - the second-longest across the entire Football League behind only Salford City (13).

With just one defeat so far, Oxford are third and three points behind Stockport and Huddersfield with two games in hand.

Oxford could go top of League One if they win their two games in hand on Stockport and Huddersfield

The influences on Wilshere and Ramsey are clear to see, both in the styles of play they adopt and the way they conduct themselves.

They have both learnt from the same mentors, particularly Wenger and current Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta, who they also played alongside in midfield.

It is perhaps no surprise the pair have gone into coaching - a common theme among Wenger's disciples.

Arteta, Patrick Vieira, Thierry Henry, Ashley Cole, Robin van Persie, Kolo Toure and Cesc Fabregas have also done so.

Wilshere and Ramsey have both spoken openly about the huge impact Wenger has had on them.

Ramsey scored 58 goals and made 329 appearances for Arsenal under Arsene Wenger

They also give a lot of credit to their role model Arteta.

Watching from afar, Ramsey previously said it was inspiring to see his former team-mate guide Arsenal to the Premier League title and Champions League final.

It was also Arteta who advised Ramsey to take his coaching badges and has continued to help him along the way.

But for Wilshere, he got to learn first hand from the Spaniard on the training ground.

"I got to see Mikel and how he coached and the influence he was having," Wilshere said last month.

"I would ask him questions, why he's doing certain things, and I started to fall in love with it and how he started to prepare training sessions.

"I was influenced by Mikel, but then I had to learn how to teach. I had to learn how to get that across, how to plan a session and how to get the outcomes that you wanted."

Mikel Arteta and Wilshere played together 53 times for Arsenal

There are some key ideologies Wilshere and Ramsey share which might sound familiar - an emphasis on a high-technical level, possession-based football, hard work and intensity.

Wilshere encourages his team to get the ball to his best attacking players but is maybe Arteta-coded with his focus on "winning duels, second balls and competing physically".

He also sometimes allows his full-backs freedom - something else Arteta is renowned for at Arsenal to create unpredictability.

It is not just on the pitch where Wilshere takes care of the details.

When he walked into his post-match press conference at Wembley after winning his first trophy as a boss last season, he shook the hand of every reporter in the room - a small insight into his warm nature.

The ex-England international has also spoken about how he expresses both empathy and honesty with his players.

Wilshere started his career at Luton's youth academy before moving to Arsenal

Ramsey made no bones about his plans when he joined Oxford in June either.

"I have a really clear way of playing, an identity that I want to create," he said at the time. "But that is built on clear principles first, underpinned by elite habits and behaviours."

That identity so far has been a team that likes to play out from the back, dominate possession and attack with verve.

Only Leicester (66%) and Stockport (62%) have had a higher average share of possession than Oxford (57%) in League One so far this season.

"I think it's evident to see why we're doing it," said Ramsey, who wants his team to take risks.

"It's not always going to be perfect. We're fully aware of that but those bits in the game where we can carve teams open - that outweighs the rest."

That approach sounds very Wenger-esque, with a sprinkling of Arteta when it comes to mindset and tweaking their tactics to suit the game on offer.

"Every week is different," he said. "But what I've liked so much about us is that we've always been able to adapt and figure out what's going on and ultimately win games of football."

Ramsey also exudes calm on the touchline, another trait perhaps borrowed from his former French boss.

Back to the immediate matters in hand this weekend, and it could be a pivotal one.

"Some are talking about this game, when you put aside the Arsenal connections, as season-defining in League One," said BBC Radio Oxford sports presenter Jerome Sale.

Ramsey was keen to reiterate that this game was not about him and Wilshere.

"Far from it," he added. "It's about the players. Two good teams going head-to-head and hopefully everything on the pitch does the talking."
