3 Jumbo Jets, 1 President: Every Air Force One Boeing 747 Flies To Asia For Trump’s Trip

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Yesterday, US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin met for a trilateral summit in Shenzhen, China. In addition to this unprecedented summit, often referred to as the second Yalta, the meeting is also notable because the United States flew all three Air Force One aircraft to China to accommodate Trump and his entourage.

Trump initially landed in China on November 18, using the Boeing 747-8 that he controversially received as a gift to the American government, alongside the older Boeing 747-200. The other Air Force One 747-200 was also seen in Asia.

It was in Manila, not China, where the presidential fleet made a stopover, that the three Air Force One jumbo jets were spotted together. The 747-8 is by far the newest and bears a white, red, and blue livery that Trump has demanded for presidential aircraft. It is called VC-25B Bridge. The two older jets are painted in the simpler blue-and-white livery, which has been traditional for presidential aircraft. These aircraft are designated VC-25A and have the registration numbers 82-8000 and 92-9000.

The sight of the two jets in Shenzhen was to be expected, as a second Air Force One often accompanies the jet that the president is actually traveling on. It serves as a backup in case mechanical issues strike. However, it is strange that all three aircraft were flying together at least as far as Manila. It is not known for certain whether the third jet also made its way to China or was employed on other duties in Asia.

All of these presidential aircraft completed their journeys, allowing the historic summit to go ahead. Stock and News has reported that Trump said of the meeting:

“I’m delighted that this trip has also given us the opportunity for this first-ever trilateral meeting between the leaders of the United States, Russia and China….so in that sense, and I think in lots of other ways, we are really making history,”

The chances to see the aging 747-200 Air Force One jets in operation are limited because the planes are approaching 40 years old. The jets have been used by American presidents since the 1990s. They are set to be replaced in 2028 by two 747-8 aircraft, each of which is under 15 years old, following an ongoing conversion program.

It is formally called VC-25B and is expected to cost $5.3 billion. The expense is required to install technology to withstand nuclear electromagnetic pulses, evade surface-to-air missiles, and provide uninterrupted global communications on a civilian aircraft. Air Force One jets feature over 200 miles (321 km) of insulated, military-grade wiring, roughly double what a standard 747 uses.

The unexpected move is raising questions about why the President would make such a decision.

The arrival of the VC-25B will not see the immediate retirement of VC-25A, which is expected to come a year later. In June 2026, White House Communications Director Steven Cheung and Deputy Chief of Staff Dan Scavino posted farewell messages after what was thought to be the final time the president rode on the jets.

There was previously speculation from analysts that the interim 747-8 provided by the Qatari state would not be appropriate for international missions due to its limited modifications and defense systems. It is clear from the plane's use in the China summit that this is not the case.

The 747-200s remain in the fleet in case of mechanical issues, and will serve as Air Force Two until their final retirement in 2029. What will happen to them after is not known, but given their advanced age and the amount of government technology that would need to be removed, they will likely be scrapped or donated to museums.
