Air quality in northern India has plummeted as the so-called pollution season gets underway. It is now a regular feature of traveling to the region in winter. Masks have emerged as a solution — a Band-Aid to a structural problem fueled by emissions.

Smog fills Delhi and northern India every winter, casting a shadow on Diwali celebrations and hitting peak tourism season.

The region’s position on the Indo-Gangetic Plain, where the Himalayas block air from flowing north, leads to trapped pollution from late October to February. Air quality in Delhi is then often made worse by firecrackers used in Diwali celebrations.

October to March is also the busiest window for foreign visitors to India, and Delhi is often used as an entry point.

On Saturday morning, November 7, Delhi had a U.S. Air Quality Index (AQI) value of 581, according to reports citing data from Swiss air monitoring company IQAir. That made it the most polluted major city in the world at that moment.

Delhi's winter smog is again colliding with India's peak inbound tourism season, and hotel operators are starting to see it in demand. On November 7, Delhi recorded a U.S. Air Quality Index value of 581 — above the 301 threshold considered hazardous — making it the most polluted major city in the world at that moment, according to reports citing IQAir data. October to March is the busiest window for foreign visitors to India, and Delhi is the common entry point. Radisson's VP of operations in India, Ashwani Kumar Goela, said some guests defer or postpone visits until conditions improve, that air quality concerns are more pronounced than the same period last year, and that they are likely contributing to softer demand. Intrepid Travel said its operations are continuing as planned with no cancellations, though its tour leaders now carry N95 masks and cut back outdoor time on the worst days. India's Supreme Court limited Diwali firecrackers to "green" versions within set hours, but pollution still spiked: PM2.5 hourly concentrations peaked at around 10 times India's daily limit of 60 micrograms per cubic meter on Diwali night, according to the Center for Study of Science, Technology & Policy. The backdrop is 9.2 million foreign tourists in 2025, down 9.4% year over year, against a government target of 100 million international arrivals by 2047.
