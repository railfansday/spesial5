Steven Hack, Broadway Star and American Horror Story Actor, Dead at 68

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Broadway star Steven Hack—also known for roles on shows like Seinfeld, General Hospital and American Horror Story—has died at age 68.

A bright light in Hollywood has gone dim.

Broadway star Steven Hack, who also had minor roles in American Horror Story and Seinfeld, has died after suffering a medical emergency, Los Angeles’ Interact Theatre Company confirmed. He was 68.

“It is with great sadness that we share the news that Steven Hack, a founding member of Interact Theatre Company, recently passed away at home following a sudden and unexpected cardiac event,” the company wrote in a recent statement shared to Instagram. “Steven was a gifted and versatile performer, a superb graphic artist, and a cherished member of our artistic family.”

Hack died of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, according to the County of Los Angeles Medical Examiner, a condition caused by plaque buildup in the arterial walls, per the America Heart Association.

“His creativity and talent helped shape Interact in its earliest years,” the statement continued, “and his artistry, humor, warmth, and generous spirit enriched everything he touched.”

Hack was born in St. Louis in 1958 before going on to study at the California Institute of the Arts and the American Academy of Dramatic Arts.

“Steven took great pride in his early career as a Broadway dancer, which included performing in the original production of CATS and touring with A Chorus Line,” Interact Theatre Company noted. “Here in Los Angeles, he went on to work extensively in film, television, and commercials.”

In fact, he made his screen debut as one of the teenage dancers in the 1978 film Grease before landing roles in other films like Fantastic Four, Thor: The Dark World and The Happytime Murders.

He also dominated the small screen with credits in American Horror Story: 1984, Marvel’s Agent Carter, Criminal Minds, The Newsroom, Days of Our Lives, The West Wing, Scrubs, General Hospital, Seinfeld and so many more.

“As we remember Steven, we celebrate not only his many contributions to our company, but also the joy of knowing and working with him,” Interact Theatre Company wrote. “He will always hold a special place in Interact’s history and in the hearts of those who knew him.”

They expressed hope that Hack’s "memory be a blessing,” adding, "We extend our heartfelt condolences to his family, friends, and all who loved him.”
