Morocco vow further legal action after Senegal reinstated Afcon champions

Morocco’s football federation has vowed to take further legal action to appeal the Court of Arbitration for Sport’s (Cas) verdict to restore Senegal as Africa Cup of Nations champions.

Senegal contested the Confederation of African Football's (Caf) decision to declare Morocco champions after the team staged an injury-time walk-off in protest to a penalty awarded against them, wiping the Teranga Lions’ title from the records despite winning January’s Afcon final 1-0 after extra time in Rabat.

Cas, sport’s highest court, confirmed on Tuesday that Senegal’s appeal had been upheld, with it found that referee Jean-Jacques Ndala “did not commit any error of law” when he decided to resume the match after Senegal eventually returned to the pitch, which fell within his sole authority.

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation (RMFF), however, claims Cas “did not take into account” the new tougher approach of global refereeing body Ifab and Fifa in dealing with team walk-offs and says it will “pursue all available legal avenues” to appeal the decision, which strips them of their first Afcon title since 1976.

The RMFF has also questioned “the integrity and credibility of the arbitration process” after it reported an alleged phone call involving Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) president Abdoulaye Fall and a member of the judicial panel, which was vehemently denied by the FSF.

A statement read: “The Royal Moroccan Football Federation expresses its surprise and protest at the failure to take into account its demand to arrange the necessary legal consequences for practices that would compromise the integrity and credibility of the arbitration procedure, especially what has been confirmed regarding communication between the German referee Ulrich Haas and the president of the Senegalese Football Federation, 48 hours before the decision was issued.

“The Royal Moroccan Football Federation also notes that the decision did not take into account the approach recently adopted by both the International Football Association Board (Ifab) and Fifa, regarding players leaving the field of play without the referee’s permission, and the resulting disciplinary responsibilities and penalties.

“Accordingly, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation confirms its intention to pursue all available legal avenues to appeal the decision of the Court of Arbitration for Sport.”

Under amendments to Law 12 of Ifab’s regulations earlier this year, players or coaches who leave the field in protest to a referee’s decision, or instruct other individuals to leave, are to be sent off.

Had this approach been taken during the final, several Senegal players would have been dismissed, likely taking their number of active players on the pitch below the minimum of seven, which would have resulted in the game being forfeited.

Senegal’s federation, meanwhile, have welcomed Cas’ verdict to restore their title.

“The Senegalese Football Federation welcomes this sentence with deep satisfaction,” a statement read.

“Far from consecrating the preeminence of one party over another, this decision affirms above all the triumph of sporting fairness and recalls a cardinal principle: the truth of a match is established exclusively on the field, under the protection of sports law.

“On this solemn occasion, the FSF wishes to express its deep gratitude to the Lions of Teranga and their management, to its lawyers and advisors, to the President of the Republic and the Senegalese Government for their support, as well as to all supporters, both in Senegal and in the diaspora.”

Federation president Fall added: "The Court of Arbitration for Sport has delivered justice to sport.

“I wish to strongly reaffirm the bonds of fraternity that unite us with the Royal Moroccan Football Federation and the Moroccan people. Senegalese football now resolutely looks to the future, with confidence and enthusiasm, in an Africa united around the values ​​of sport."
