Levski Sofia fans hospitalised after violent clashes with Marseille supporters

Marseille head coach Bruno Genesio said “we are living through violent times” after five Levski Sofia fans were hospitalised following violent clashes before their Europa League encounter with the French side.

Groups of supporters threw projectiles and flares and French outlet L’Equipe reported that there were Nazi salutes observed in the away section.

Ten minutes before kick-off at the Stade Velodrome a group of Marseille fans broke through into the away stand, which housed around 500 fans.

In the ensuing clashes five people, all Bulgarian, were injured and taken to hospital “with eye injuries, head trauma and a dislocated shoulder”, according to the Marseille police prefecture, while six others were treated on-site for more superficial injuries.

The police force said there was “no information at this stage regarding any arrests”.

Security were able to restore order and the game kicked off on time, although there were further incidents later on, particularly at half-time.

Genesio said: “We are living through violent times in our society, so this is very, very regrettable.” Julio Velazquez, head coach of the Bulgarian domestic champions, said: “It's a very regrettable episode. These are incidents which are unacceptable for football.”

The Bulgarian Football Union condemned the violence and said it had referred the incident to UEFA, calling for the harshest possible sanctions. It also demanded full cooperation and transparency from French authorities in investigating the attacks and identifying those responsible.

Levski said the club's priority is the safety and well-being of its fans. A statement read: “What happened tonight is particularly alarming given the extraordinary security measures put in place by the French authorities, as well as the restrictions imposed on our travelling supporters—restrictions that the club had already formally contested with UEFA.

“Despite the risks identified by the club prior to the match, the measures taken failed to provide adequate protection for our fans. This is completely unacceptable.”

Marseille also condemned the violence and said it is conducting “a thorough analysis of the circumstances in which these incidents took place.”

Bulgarian President Iliana Yotova called for those responsible to be held accountable and Prime Minister Rumen Radev said he had spoken to French president Emmanuel Macron about the violence at a European summit in Brussels.

A spokesperson for Macron’s office said: “He condemned in the strongest possible terms these acts of violence targeting Bulgarian supporters, which have no place in sport or in public spaces, and assured the Prime Minister that these acts would not go unpunished.”

Marseille won the game 1-0, with Amine Gouiri scoring a 28th-minute penalty.
