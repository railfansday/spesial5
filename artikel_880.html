Sunderland ‘disappointed’ after banned Jagiellonia Bialystok fans enter Stadium of Light

Sunderland have expressed their disappointment after a number of Jagiellonia Bialystok fans gained access to home sections at the Stadium of Light during Thursday night’s 1-1 draw.

UEFA had issued an away supporter ban against Jagiellonia for Thursday’s match on Wearside.

However, police and stewards were called to handle a disturbance within a home area of the ground during the early minutes of the game.

A club statement said: “Sunderland AFC is disappointed that a number of Jagiellonia Bialystok supporters were present in home areas of the Stadium of Light during tonight’s UEFA Europa League fixture.

“Jagiellonia Bialystok were subject to a UEFA away supporter ban for tonight’s Matchday 5 fixture, following a disciplinary sanction imposed by European football’s governing body.

“In the build-up to the match, the club had put measures in place in line with this sanction and maintained regular communication with our supporters, Jagiellonia Bialystok and UEFA. Unfortunately, Jagiellonia Bialystok failed to engage and cooperate on this matter.

“We are disappointed that these measures and communications were disregarded by some visiting supporters and will review the circumstances surrounding their entry to the stadium.”

Sunderland struck early in the second half through Timur Tutierov’s header, his second goal in the competition, but Kamil Jozwiak levelled only minutes later after capitalising on a mistake at the back.

A point means the Wearsiders stay in the knockout phase play-off places after being held to their second draw from five games in the competition.

Sunderland remain without the injured Brian Brobbey and Granit Xhaka, and Regis Le Bris made six changes from their goalless Premier League draw with Aston Villa on Saturday.

Jagiellonia showed an early flash of danger when Nik Prelec weaved into the box and played a low cross into the area, but Roefs held the resulting clearance.

Sunderland caused problems when Angulo found Enzo Le Fee and the midfielder had his strike deflected narrowly behind.

Slawomir Abramowicz was forced to push away Fofana’s menacing shot before the half evolved into a scrappy affair.

A sudden burst of energy from the hosts saw Trai Hume chip over and Le Fee was at the heart of another solid attack.

The Frenchman went around Abramowicz before playing a low ball across the box towards Angulo lingering in front of an open goal, but his strike from close range was blocked by Yuki Kobayashi.

An enforced change six minutes before the break saw Luke O’Nien introduced for Omar Alderete and moments later Roefs was called into action to palm away Szmyt’s free-kick just outside the box.

On the stroke of half-time, Jagiellonia struggled to clear their lines and Hume blasted wide of the upright.

Sunderland brought on Jules Ahoka and Thomas Meunier at half-time and just two minutes after the restart they went ahead.

A good team move stemming from Chris Rigg’s break down the middle resulted in the ball reaching Le Fee and his cross from the right was deflected by Taras Romanczuk in to Tutierov to nod home at the back post.

However, their lead was short-lived when Jozwiak pounced on a tame attempted back pass from Reinildo Mandava and rolled past Roefs in the 52nd minute.

Another sloppy defensive error allowed Jozwiak to again latch on to the ball, but Roefs tipped the resulting shot behind.

Sunderland’s frustrations continued when Fofana’s half-volley was stopped and Angulo blasted a free-kick straight into the wall before Rigg’s low shot was comfortably scooped up by Abramowicz.

Both sides went close in the 90th minute when Reinildo’s header was saved by Abramowicz, and Jeremy Agbonifo broke for the visitors but was denied by Roefs as the game fizzled into a draw.
