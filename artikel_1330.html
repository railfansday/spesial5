Robert Pires Named the ‘Toughest Opponent’ He’s Ever Played Against

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Former Arsenal and France winger Robert Pires did not need a second to think when asked who the toughest opponent of his career was, before picking an iconic English defender.

Of course, based off that description, one would usually assume he was on about either Rio Ferdinand - whom he had many battles with during the Gunners' intense Premier League title rivalry with Manchester United in the mid-2000s - or legendary Chelsea captain John Terry.

But having played against so many defenders during his illustrious career, it was another Three Lions star that got his flowers off of Pires, who would get the better of his marker more often than not, to rack up 180 goals and 119 assists during his playing days, where he won two league titles under Arsene Wenger alongside a World Cup for France.

Alongside Terry and Ferdinand - both named among the top three greatest centre-backs in Premier League history - Pires also snubbed the likes of Jaap Stam, Marcel Desailly, and Ashley Cole.

Instead, when faced with the question about his toughest opponent, he explained to Sky Sports that Gary Neville would usually get the better of their duels. He said:

"He kicked me all game! Old Trafford, Highbury, he was always shouting. It was normal because of the rivalry between Arsenal and Manchester United."

A wind-up merchant of the highest order, it would seem that the now-pundit's tactics of goading his opponents worked on some players, including Pires, and that's what made Neville stand out, even beyond his own defensive brilliance.

One of Jamie Carragher's funniest quotes is when he once remarked 'nobody wants to grow up to be a Gary Neville.' But as the modern full-back role evolves from its defensive foundations, and Pires gave his former Red Devils' adversary some well-deserved praise, it's easy to forget just how good Sir Alex Ferguson's right-back was during a period of unprecedented success.

These two had plenty of memorable battles.

Better known more as a pundit these days - and one of the highest-paid at that - Neville made 602 appearances for United, winning two Champions League titles and an incredible 10 Premier League crowns, all while tirelessly running up and down the right flank.

His era came during a time when the traditional winger - like Pires - was thriving, not afraid to run at the defence and make a direct attacking impact. It was by no means an easy time to defend in English football, but a time that Neville grabbed by the scruff of the neck.
