Giants’ defense came up one interception short of securing a win

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Two should-be game-sealing fourth-quarter interceptions weren't enough for the Giants defense to hold a four-point lead.

Given a third life because the Giants offense couldn't get enough first downs to run out the clock or the points to put the game away, Jayden Daniels connected on a 32-yard touchdown pass to Stefon Diggs with 57 seconds remaining as the Commanders stole a 24-21 victory at MetLife Stadium.

"We just have to finish the game — it's not over until it's over," linebacker Micah McFadden told The Post. "The defensive side of the ball is always ready to go win the game. We want to be in that position."

It looked for a moment like it was going to be a hat trick of victory-clinching interceptions near their own end zone over the first nine games for the horseshoe Giants.

Jevón Holland turned around late at the 2-yard line and found a pass from Daniels in his stomach for an interception that completed a defensive stand with under six minutes to go.

When the defense needed to come up big again a few minutes later, Ar'Darius Washington jumped a route just shy of midfield for an interception on fourth-and-2. He then curled into a ball on the ground to make sure that he didn't lose possession and hopped up after the whistle to celebrate with teammates in the end zone as if the game was over.

Jevon Holland of the Giants makes a pick on a ball intended for Chig Okonkwo of the Commanders. Charles Wenzelberg/NY Post

"Embarrassing," left tackle Andrew Thomas said of the four-minute offense after the interceptions.

Washington, who is one of three safeties to start next to Holland this season, played the best game of any of the three (Jason Pinnock, Tyler Nubin) so far. He almost singlehandedly forced a turnover on downs earlier in the game when he made a wrap-up, push-back tackle to set up a fourth-and-1 and then had a pass breakup to force a turnover.

"When we make plays on defense, we always think we're in a good spot," Washington said. "Our confidence was high."

Rasul Douglas of the Washington Commanders intercepted a pass intended for Malik Nabers of the New York Giants. Getty Images

It was Holland who came up with the end zone interception in the final 20 seconds of the Week 3 win against the Titans.

It was teammate Deonte Banks who came up with the game-sealing 98-yard pick-6 in Week 4 against the Cardinals.

The third time was no charm. The Commanders' biggest play on the game-winning drive was a 33-yard pass interference penalty on Deonte Banks that was a point of frustration in the postgame locker room.

"It's a passing league," Washington said. "You have to get your head around, but the receiver pulled him. You can't do anything about that."

It turned out the biggest interception of the game came from New Jersey native Rasul Douglas, who wrestled a jump ball away from Malik Nabers to clinch a victory for the Commanders before the Giants could reach field goal range.

The Giants wasted not just the two interceptions but also a 13-tackle game from rookie Arvell Reese.
