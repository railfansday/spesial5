Jensen Huang returns to Nvidia. And, Rep. Gomez resigns amid alleged sexual misconduct

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO yesterday, causing the company's stock to soar more than 30%. President Trump claimed credit for facilitating Huang's return. The CEO's comeback follows his resignation just two weeks earlier due to reports of extramarital affairs, including involvement with a woman U.S. intelligence agencies suspected of being a Chinese spy. Huang, a close ally of the president, had previously donated $25 million to the new White House ballroom and security complex. His return boosted Nvidia's stock and triggered a rally in the entire stock market, revealing that one man's comeback is worth about a trillion dollars. Some individuals advocating for diversity and inclusion in Silicon Valley argue that Huang's return signals that profit is the only priority.

Ukraine recently sank a Russian warship in Libya, while Russian missiles struck Poland. There has been no comment from the Kremlin on the strikes. Depending on perspective, Russia's war in Ukraine appears to be either escalating or de-escalating. This month, the Trump administration said it is making progress towards a negotiated peace. However, fighting has expanded into Eastern and Northern Europe and the Mediterranean.

Rep. Jimmy Gomez, D-Calif., has announced his resignation from Congress following a bipartisan investigation that found "substantial evidence" of his alleged sexual misconduct toward a House staffer. The House Ethics Committee determined that Gomez violated House rules by "making unwelcome sexual advances" and disregarding the power imbalance in his relationship with staff. The report says his actions could amount to criminal sexual assault. Gomez, who has represented parts of Los Angeles since 2017, lost his reelection bid this fall to Democrat Angela Gonzales-Torres. Gomez says he has strong disagreements with the report and its conclusions.

Move over, violins and pianos. The Seattle Symphony is giving the timpani its moment in the spotlight with a world premiere concerto. Principal timpanist Eric Schweikert, who says he is drawn to the back-of-the-classroom vibe in the orchestra, is stepping up to showcase the versatility of the percussion instrument. The piece offers listeners a look at the timpani's range, including rumbling rolls produced with clever mallet work to imitate the effect of a long note. While Schweikert promises to deliver plenty of the wild, Muppets-style Animal energy audiences crave, he's also ready to keep his fellow musicians on their toes — and show the crowd that kettle drums can sing, too.
