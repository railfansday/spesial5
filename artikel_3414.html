It has been a tough year for Warner Bros. In the months before its merger with Paramount was finalized, the studio had been struggling with a string of box-office underperformers. The streak seems to be continuing, with yet another WB movie having failed to recoup its budget at the box office. The movie in question has been in theaters for two weeks and will see its theater count drop for the first time this Friday. Things are expected to turn around for WB this December, with Denis Villeneuve's hotly anticipated Dune: Part Three tracking to break franchise records.

However, the box-office performances of this year's Supergirl, Mortal Kombat II, Lee Cronin's The Mummy, They Will Kill You, Practical Magic 2, The Bride!, The End of Oak Street, and most recently, Digger, all fell short of expectations. Even though Clayface, the new DC Studios release, has grossed three times its reported budget of $40 million at the global box office, it's still one of the lowest-grossing superhero tentpoles of the decade. Digger, on the other hand, is one of the biggest bombs of all time.

This slate was designed to appeal to every type of moviegoer, and now, another WB film aimed at an entirely different demographic is struggling to break out theatrically. We're talking, of course, about The Cat in the Hat. The new version features Bill Hader as the voice of the titular Cat, with Xochitl Gomez, America Ferrera, Giancarlo Esposito, and Matt Berry in supporting roles. The previous live-action version, released in 2003, featured Mike Myers in the central role and grossed around $135 million worldwide against a reported budget of $110 million.

The new movie has so far generated just under $60 million worldwide against a reported budget of $90 million. It received mixed reviews and is now sitting at a 47% score on Rotten Tomatoes. The aggregator website's consensus reads, "A high-energy sprint through the world imagined by Dr. Seuss, The Cat in the Hat's eagerness to please has some charm but could have benefited from smaller stakes and a less frantic pace."
