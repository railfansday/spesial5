Cher’s Victory in Royalties War Under Fire as Mary Bono Pushes for Reversal

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Sonny Bono’s widow, Mary Bono, is appealing her defeat in a four-year royalties war with Cher, arguing in a brief filed Monday that a federal judge erred in ruling that Cher’s 1978 divorce agreement with Sonny Bono took precedence over federal copyright law. Mary Bono contends that the federal Copyright Act entitles her and Sonny’s children to reclaim the 50 percent share of his songwriting royalties awarded to Cher in the divorce.

In his prior ruling, U.S. District Court Judge John Kronstadt found that Cher’s marital settlement agreement entitled her to a 50 percent share of Sonny’s composition royalties, rather than an interest in the copyrights themselves. As such, he found that her claim was protected by state contract law and not subject to the termination rules of the Copyright Act.

In the divorce settlement, Sonny awarded Cher the 50 percent cut of his composition royalties for hit songs including “I Got You Babe” and “The Beat Goes On.” Cher also got a 50 percent share of the musical recordings they made together. In court filings, Cher’s lawyers said Mary had “secretly” begun diverting Cher’s royalties to herself and Sonny’s other heirs after using the termination provisions of the Copyright Act. Cher sued, and the court determined her grant was untouchable.

In the new 33-page appellate brief obtained by Rolling Stone, Mary and her lawyers sharply criticize the ruling. They argue the lower court “struggled with the concept that copyright terminations preempted Sonny’s right to contract freely and bargain away his heirs’ rights.” They say copyright termination “trumps the common law’s freedom to contract.”

“Sonny could not have alienated Mary’s right, as his widow, to the post-termination copyright interests when he entered into the MSA in 1978. Despite any language to the contrary in the MSA, all of the reverted rights, including the right to receive royalties, belong to the heirs,” the brief, filed in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, states. “The District Court focused extensively on family law questions and, in particular, spousal support, even though the parties agree that the MSA is a contract like any other,” the filing reads. “This unfortunately led the District Court to err by failing to acknowledge that the federal Copyright Act preempts state law on the issue of copyright terminations.”

Attempts to reach Cher’s lead lawyer on the lawsuit were not immediately successful Tuesday. The lawyer, Peter J. Anderson, previously argued in court that the Copyright Act was clear, and that by the time Sonny and Cher signed their divorce settlement in August of 1978, they were “sophisticated people” represented by “sophisticated lawyers.”

He said Sonny and Cher knew during negotiations that “I Got You Babe” and “Bang Bang” were big hits. “They knew this. They also knew that the 1976 Copyright Act was in effect and that on January 1, 1978, it had a termination right. So, when they said Cher gets 50 percent of all royalties from all sources, that includes under the replacement publishing agreements when they terminate the initial publishing agreements,” Anderson argued to the court. “These songs were already tested by the marketplace. Cher’s side will have a chance to respond before the appeals court issues any ruling.

Cher, 80, rose to fame alongside Sonny in the 1960s before building a decades-long solo career that brought her Grammy, Oscar, and Emmy wins. Sonny died in a skiing accident in 1998, leaving Mary in charge of his estate. His music publishing grants became eligible for termination beginning in 2018.
