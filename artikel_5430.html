Consumer Confidence Surges in November

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

With the election uncertainty in the rearview mirror and gas prices coming down, Americans’ confidence in the economy surged this month, according to the latest report from The Conference Board.

The nonprofit research firm’s Consumer Confidence Index, which measures American opinions of current economic conditions and their outlook for the next six months, rose by 11.4 points to 103.3 in November, the second consecutive monthly increase after months of glum reports.

The organization’s other monthly indicators—the Present Situation Index, based on consumers’ assessment of current business and labor market conditions, and the Expectations Index, based on consumers’ short-term outlook for income, business and labor market conditions—also both increased, by 13.7 points to 132.9 and by 9.9 points to 83.6, respectively. The survey period for this month’s preliminary results was November 1-17.

“Consumer confidence rallied again in November, potentially due to lower uncertainty following the midterm elections,” said Fergus McCormick, senior economist, Global Indicators, The Conference Board. “After two months of consecutive increases, the headline index is back to levels of confidence last seen in January 2025. The gain in confidence was led by stronger perceptions of present and future labor market conditions. Perceptions of current and future business conditions also improved sharply. Along with the gain in headline confidence, consumers planned slightly higher spending on goods and vacations.”

McCormick said write-in responses in November were slightly less negative than previous months, noting that while consumers continued to express frustration with high prices and rising costs, some comments cited hope for the general economy. He said comments about war and conflict ticked up slightly, along with mixed feelings about elections, politics and the direction of the labor market.

A host of macroeconomic factors continue to hinder consumers who are looking to buy a home. The 30-year fixed-rate jumped to 7.32% as of Nov. 24, fueled by hotter-than-expected inflation in October with a rate hike expected by the Fed in December, as well as homebuilder confidence slumping to a new 14-year low, existing-home sales sinking to a 16-year low and muted new home sales this year have weighed on homebuyers and added more pressure to a typical late-fall slowdown in the housing market.

Yet, despite these indicators, the November Consumer Confidence report also shows on a month-over-month as well as on a six-month moving average basis, plans to purchase autos and homes both improving slightly this month. Planned spending on durable goods in the month generally increased across the board.
