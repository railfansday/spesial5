Recent spate of outages attributed to technical issues amid accelerated AI bug discovery

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

U.S. authorities don’t believe that the recent outages at federal agencies and top tech firms were caused by cyberattacks.

The Federal Aviation Administration, United Airlines, Apple Inc. and Microsoft Corp. were among the affected organizations. All four said that their systems were disrupted by technical issues rather than hackers. Experts noted that artificial intelligence models’ ability to find software bugs has made patching more complicated, which may increase the risk of outages.

The first major service disruption hit Exchange Online on Thursday. The Microsoft-operated email service went offline for nearly a day, which left 450 million users without access to their inboxes. The company attributed the downtime to two separate bugs in the software that powers its network. The first one went undetected for three months, while the other cropped up a day before the outage.

The FAA experienced a network disruption of its own on Friday. An issue with a router at the agency’s El Dorado, Arkansas campus disrupted aircraft communications and radar data feeds. The outage led to more than 12,000 flight cancellations.

United Airlines reported a fairly extensive, but brief infrastructure disruption a few days later. The issue took several of the carrier’s pilot-facing applications offline for 30 minutes. Fewer than 150 flights were affected.

Yet another high-profile network outage occurred on Tuesday, this time at the U.S. National Security Agency. A technical problem cropped up in NSANet, an internal network that agency staff use to exchange classified information. The cause of the outage and its duration were not specified. However, officials did tell NBC the disruption wasn’t caused by a cyberattack.

U.S. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton reiterated that point in a Tuesday interview. He added that officials have “looked very closely” into the other outages that occurred over the past week and ruled out cyberattacks as the cause. According to Clayton, authorities believe that the disruptions occurred because companies are patching vulnerabilities “on much larger scale” than before.

Frontier AI models have found thousands of software vulnerabilities over the past few months. That accelerated pace of discovery is creating more work for administrators, who have to roll out patches more frequently than before. The less time there is to prepare a patch, the greater the risk of human error.

“As AI accelerates vulnerability discovery and development cycles, pressure is mounting on companies to deploy fixes quickly, often without fully understanding how those changes will affect interconnected systems, “ said Prince Kohli, the chief executive officer of software testing provider Sauce Labs Inc. “The only way to prevent these issues is continuous testing and release validation in their development processes, even more so for critical software updates.”

Apple and Workday Inc. are also among the companies that have experienced outages in recent days. Separately, several financial services temporarily became unavailable because of an issue with one of JPMorgan Chase’s on-premises systems. All the incidents have been resolved.
