Nvidia is pausing AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Dell and Super Micro were told to halt server deliveries to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia for an unspecified period

Nvidia $NVDA has told Dell $DELL, Super Micro Computer and other manufacturing partners to pause server deliveries bound for Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The instruction comes on the heels of Washington's effort to stop Nvidia's advanced processors from reaching Chinese firms. A portion of the computing power Chinese companies rely on has come from reaching Nvidia chips remotely, through data centers located in the four countries, according to The Journal.

The pause has no set length. Nvidia is examining its partners in the region more closely to satisfy American export rules, and there is a mechanism under which held shipments can be cleared at a later date.

Remotely accessed data centers in Malaysia and other countries have accounted for about 24% of the computing capacity China uses for artificial intelligence, according to The Wall Street Journal, which cited the think tank Institute for Progress.

The Commerce Department issued an interim final rule earlier this month requiring data center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil to obtain U.S. licenses before buying advanced AI processors from Nvidia and other American companies. The Bureau of Industry and Security said the rule is meant to stop companies from sidestepping limits on sensitive American technology by tapping chips remotely through data centers in other countries. Licenses will be issued case by case.

Malaysia's Trade Minister Johari Abdul Ghani said Kuala Lumpur does not intend to negotiate with Washington over the rule and expects data center operators to abide by it, according to The Wall Street Journal. Singapore said it is studying the measure. China's Foreign Ministry called the rules "economic coercion."

The pause could hit cloud-computing providers, along with American companies counting on Nvidia chips hosted in the four countries, according to The Journal. Companies that are hunting for project funding could encounter lenders inclined to hold off on committing capital until the pause ends.

Firmus Grid, a cloud provider that counts Nvidia among its backers, disclosed in September that it is constructing a pair of Malaysian data centers for OpenAI, according to The Wall Street Journal. The company is also developing facilities in Indonesia and Singapore.
