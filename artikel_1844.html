Premier League predictions: Standard Sport names title winners, relegation race, Golden Boot and more

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Your weekly briefing on Arsenal, featuring team news and expert analysis from Matt Verri

The 2026-27 Premier League season is just days away from resumption following the final international break of the year, and with it the return of some familiar faces.

Mauricio Pochettino is back in English football after a spell in international management, he has succeeded Michael Carrick as Manchester United head coach. Meanwhile, Thomas Frank has replaced Pierre Sage at Crystal Palace.

After 10 games of the campaign, both Man United and Palace are in the relegation zone. So will these appointment help to drag them out of the bottom three?

At the other end of the table, Arsenal have made a solid start to the defence of their Premier League crown, and appear to have a surprise challenger as confusion has set in around Manchester City.

Plenty of questions to be answered over the next six months, and the Standard Sport football reporters, Matt Verri, Dom Smith and Sam Tabuteau, have had a go at predicting what is in store for the remainder of the season.

MV: Arsenal continue to look ruthlessly efficient and should be confident of defending their title. The defensive unit is absurdly good, the midfield depth is ridiculous, and it means the forwards only need to deliver a moment or two per match.

Even whey they are not at their best, Arsenal are just so difficult to beat. That is the case over 90 minutes and it should be the case over 38 matches again.

DS: No reason to change what I predicted at the beginning of the season, which was for Arsenal to win the Premier League title. If anything, the opening 10 rounds of games have made me more convinced the Gunners won’t be caught and will defend last season’s crown.

Arsenal fans may continue to worry about a lack of goals from their forwards, but their defence is on track to perform even better than last season’s, and that gives other challengers a daunting task to catch them. Arsenal could genuinely win the title by as many as 15 points.

ST: Arsenal were favourites to retain the Premier League title before the season started and that has not changed 10 games in.

Manchester City started the season with a perfect record, but their performances have begun to suffer, perhaps impacted by the looming threat of a potential points deduction.

Brighton will surely fall away, while the likes of Liverpool and Chelsea are capable but lacking consistency.

MV: Now it gets harder. Liverpool have not quite put it all together yet but there are signs that they will. I’ll go for them to just edge second ahead of Brighton, who have been superb this season but could fade slightly if they go deep in Europe as expected.

Chelsea have the firepower in attack to secure fourth and Unai Emery can work his magic to get Aston Villa into fifth.

DS: My top five, in order, back in August was Arsenal, Manchester City, Manchester United, Chelsea, then Liverpool. That needs some revising.

Man City are difficult to predict because of the unknowns involved in their impending punishment for financial rule breaches, but they may just about hold on to second (pre-punishment). In third, there are signs Liverpool could come good, particularly if Alexander Isak can stay fit.

Brighton will drop from second but stay strong enough to finish in the Champions League in fourth. Chelsea in fifth — though their performances must improve.

ST: I think the teams who currently occupy the top five spots will all remain there, it is more a case of which position each team will finish. Arsenal will be champions, while I believe Brighton will slip to fifth when the going gets tough in the spring.

Manchester City, Liverpool and Chelsea are all interchangeable. But I think it will end up being City second, Liverpool third and Chelsea fourth.

MV: Assuming a resolution is reached this season, it is hard to see how Manchester City can avoid a sanction that leads to relegation. I predicted in the summer that Sunderland would go down and that has been reinforced over the first ten games. That squad looks stretched and sorely lacking in goals.

Thomas Frank was abysmal at Tottenham but can do enough to solidify things at Crystal Palace. Instead, Coventry will not quite have enough to stay up.

DS: Along with many others at the time, I predicted the three promoted teams to go straight back down. Even though Hull made a good start, Ipswich have been decent and Coventry seem to have turned a corner, I still expect those three to be weaker than Sunderland, Fulham, Crystal Palace and other strugglers as the many months progress. Coventry, Hull and Ipswich still.

ST: With so much time left in the season, it really is anyone’s guess who will get relegated. Just two points separate the Premier League’s bottom club Crystal Palace and 17th place Coventry, so if Thomas Frank gets it right, the Eagles can quickly pull away from danger.

I think Fulham, Nottingham Forest and Coventry are all capable of getting dragged into the relegation zone.

Sunderland, Forest and Coventry will be the three to go down.

MV: Thierno Barry gets enough chances in front of goal to challenge, while Alexander Isak is starting to show why Liverpool were so determined to sign him. However, it is hard not to be boring. Even with the circus surrounding City, Erling Haaland will almost certainly secure another Golden Boot.

Joao Pedro would be the one I might have backed to challenge him, but injury has stalled his challenge.

DS: Why would it be anyone other than Erling Haaland? It says a lot that even after the Norwegian was criticised in some circles for failing to score in City’s last two league games, the defeats to Brighton and Nottingham Forest, he is still sitting pretty on top of the leaderboard with nine goals in 10 league games.

The Golden Boot simply won’t be won by anyone else.

ST: It’s the boring answer but in a league lacking natural goalscsorers, Erling Haaland is the obvious pick. The Norwegian has been off the boil in recent weeks, but he always finds his way back into form.

No one knows where the back of the net is quite like Haaland. It’s that simple.
