England winners and losers: Arsenal star rebuilding as Chelsea man suffers further setback

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A terrific week for England ended with an away victory over Spain which Thomas Tuchel described as “like the Azteca reloaded”.

The Three Lions closed out their 2026 campaign by finishing top of their Nations League group thanks to a 2-1 win over Croatia last Thursday and then a heroic 10-man win in Madrid, 3-2, on Sunday.

While plenty of Tuchel’s players pushed their cases for further England involvement down the line, there were others who will feel less positive about the past week in terms of what it means for them.

Here, Standard Sport considers the winners and losers from England’s impressive autumn week.

Ben White has scored only 12 goals in his senior career but two have been for England, including the eventual winner in a famous win in Madrid.

Five years after his England debut, his first competitive start for his country came in Spain, and he didn’t disappoint.

The Arsenal man followed up a cameo against Croatia with one of England’s standout performances at the Estadio Metropolitano, defending robustly against Spain’s attacks down the left and then chipping in with that overlapping run and finish to make it 3-0.

Has had to field booing from sections of England fans since his return to the international fold in March after he walked away from their World Cup squad in 2022, but is rebuilding his England career successfully and will be thought of by Tuchel as one of his most well-balanced right-back options now.

Hopes to climb above Reece James and become first choice.

England’s left-back position changed hands a few times during the World Cup as Nico O’Reilly and Djed Spence battled it out mid-tournament, but that only served to make Tuchel’s decision to leave Hall behind appear all the more strange.

Hall has come back stronger and this month backed up his encouraging displays from the last international break.

Solid in the 2-1 win over Croatia, he was on another level entirely in Madrid, taking care of the threat of Lamine Yamal and finding a perfect balance between defence and attack as he helped England to victory in the world champions’ own backyard.

At the end of the last international break, when Trafford and Jordan Pickford split the four games two each, Pickford could no longer claim to be uncontested as England No1.

And after his heroics in Spain, even more so there is a debate to be had over whether it is time for England’s first-choice goalkeeper to be Leeds United’s Trafford rather than his Everton counterpart.

Pickford has never let England down in 94 caps, but Trafford made a string of stellar saves in Madrid and is eight years his junior.

Tuchel will likely continue to share the game time between them until he is pulled strongly enough in either direction. Trafford is making a very strong case for Pickford’s nine years without a genuine challenger to end with the shirt changing hands.

Quansah was solid for the most part but switched off in both games. And both errors were punished.

First he attempted a header back to Pickford from a Croatia corner that gifted the visitors a leveller at Wembley, then he dawdled on the ball long enough for Spain to intercept and race through; Elliot Anderson’s last-man red card and a Spain penalty followed.

Tuchel will hope he is stronger for the mistakes he made, but 23-year-old Quansah will be disappointed as he heads back to Germany.

Arguably it is harsh to include a player who was not even involved in the two matches, but the reason for Cole Palmer’s absence this month spoke volumes.

After dropping out of the last camp due to load management and being told by Tuchel that he “misses too many opportunities” to play for his country, this time the Chelsea talisman was not even selected.

The 28-year-old is approaching 40 caps but his England career has been a strange old saga, really. Only Lee Carsley has ever appeared to truly trust him; Gareth Southgate and now Tuchel, less so.

Not in favour at Real Madrid under Jose Mourinho, his England game time across the autumn has felt a result of Reece James’s injuries and evidence right-back is a problem position more than it has an opportunity earned through strong club form.

Categorising Alexander-Arnold as a sublime passer who is suspect defensively became cliché long ago — yet being pinched of the ball in a poor area by Croatia was just the latest confirmation.

England managers have lacked the faith required to start him in England’s toughest matches for years now, and so it continued as he remained an unused substitute in Madrid.
