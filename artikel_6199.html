Allegiant Air Drops 34 Airports In 10 Years: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Allegiant Air carried more than 157 million scheduled passengers in the past decade, all of whom were domestic. Some context is needed. In this period, all US airlines had eight billion domestic passengers. As such, Allegiant only transported around 2% of the market.

Despite carrying one in 50 passengers, Allegiant continues to play a major role in leisure markets, particularly involving small and medium-sized cities, just like Volotea in Europe. Allegiant's dominance will increase slightly following the acquisition of Sun Country by the Allegiant Travel Company.

All airlines have route and airport churn, and this ultra-low-cost carrier is no exception. ULCCs are particularly renowned for greater churn. Some say this is because of their dartboard approach to network planning, while others believe it is because they are more entrepreneurial in what they try. They may also have less tolerance for what does not work.

Between January 2016 and October 2026, Cirium Diio data shows that Allegiant stopped flying to all the following places. The list does not include Minneapolis, as Allegiant is returning there in 2027. Chicago Midway, which the airline is ditching in 2027, is also not featured. Allegiant itself will no longer serve Chicago (Rockford is far away), but Sun Country will, just at O'Hare.

The cuts mean that Allegiant has pulled out of some states entirely. They include Alaska, Hawaii (for which the short-lived Boeing 757 was used), New Mexico, Vermont, and Wyoming, along with the US territory of Puerto Rico. Naturally, the airline no longer serves most areas at all. For example, it pulled out of the San Francisco Bay after ditching Oakland. Stockton, which remains part of Allegiant's network, is not part of the Bay Area.

The ULCC's debut in Alaska took place in May 2019, exactly seven years before the arrival of Southwest Airlines in the Last Frontier. Allegiant operated from Bellingham, located between Seattle and Vancouver, to Anchorage for just one season. Departures existed between May 22 and September 2, 2019, with just 31 takeoffs, all on the Airbus A319. It was the first and only time that Bellingham had scheduled flights to Alaska.

US Department of Transportation data shows that Allegiant carried 6,272 round-trip passengers on what was a brand-new route. As usual, this is just a number, and says little in itself. When related to seats for sale, the load factor was just 67.2%. Given the poor result, even for a new market, it's no wonder it was cut so quickly. While fare information is unavailable, whatever was achieved was clearly insufficient.

Between January 2016 and October 2026 only, Allegiant had a total of 49,955 from the 34 airports that are no longer part of its network. Of course, the number would be even higher if the time frame were expanded beyond a decade. With 13,391 of them, Los Angeles International was utterly critical. The airport accounted for more than a quarter of the total departures.

Los Angeles International joined Allegiant's network in 2009. But focusing on 2016-2026, 40 routes were served, with more flights to Bellingham than anywhere else. The carrier pulled out in January 2026. In the first ten months of 2027, analysis shows that 23 of the 40 destinations will have nonstop flights by at least one carrier. Moreover, some of them are now served by Allegiant from the other LA Basin airports that it serves, whether Burbank, Orange County, or both.
