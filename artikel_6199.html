7 Airlines With The Largest Dreamliner Fleets In 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

An aviation writer, pilot, and flight operations professional who sees aviation from both sides of the cockpit door. With a background in aeronautics, safety, and human factors, she brings curiosity, firsthand experience, and a love of the details that turn aircraft and airports into stories worth telling.

The Boeing 787 Dreamliner has become an important aircraft for airlines operating long-haul services, offering a family of twin-engine widebodies designed for different passenger capacities and route requirements. Since entering commercial service in 2011, the aircraft has been adopted by carriers around the world, with the 787-8, 787-9, and 787-10 offering different combinations of capacity and range. These three variants let airlines choose aircraft that better match their network strategies without relying on a single widebody configuration.

The world's largest Dreamliner operators demonstrate how extensively the aircraft family has been adopted across Asia, Europe, the Middle East, and North America. This ranking compares seven airlines by their reported active Boeing 787 fleets, rather than combining aircraft already in service with future orders or scheduled flight volumes. Fleet registers can differ because of reporting dates and aircraft status, so the figures below should be read as reported late-2026 snapshots rather than permanently fixed totals.

Japan Airlines operates a substantial Boeing 787 Dreamliner fleet, with approximately 42 aircraft listed in the available register snapshot. The Japanese carrier uses the 787-8 and 787-9 across its network, giving it two capacity options within the same aircraft family. This combination supports different passenger needs without requiring the airline to operate every Dreamliner variant.

The 787-8 offers a smaller-capacity widebody option, while the 787-9 provides additional space for passengers and cargo. Operating both models gives Japan Airlines flexibility when planning services with different levels of demand, although aircraft assignments also depend on route length, scheduling, and operational requirements. The shared aircraft family provides commonality while retaining meaningful differences in passenger capacity.

Japan Airlines ranks seventh in this comparison because its reported fleet is smaller than those of the other six operators featured here. Its Dreamliners remain an important part of the airline's widebody operation, particularly as the carrier balances capacity across its network. The reported total represents a fleet-register snapshot, and should not be interpreted as the total number of Dreamliners Japan Airlines has operated throughout the aircraft program's history.

The airlines operating the most Boeing 787 flights in 2026 are based in the United States, Middle East, and Japan.

British Airways operates 43 active Boeing 787 Dreamliners, placing it sixth in this ranking. The aircraft form part of its long-haul fleet alongside other widebodies, including the Boeing 777 and Airbus A350. British Airways operates the 787-8, 787-9, and 787-10, giving it access to all three main production variants of the Dreamliner family.

The 787-8 is the smallest member of the family, while the 787-9 offers additional passenger capacity and the 787-10 accommodates even more passengers. These differences give British Airways several aircraft configurations to consider when planning long-haul services with varying demand. Operating three variants allows the airline to select among different capacity options without depending exclusively on one Dreamliner model.

British Airways' reported fleet is slightly larger than Japan Airlines' in this comparison, but smaller than those of Etihad Airways, Qatar Airways, American Airlines, ANA, and United Airlines. The Dreamliner complements the airline's other widebodies, each with its own role in the wider fleet. Its position here reflects reported active aircraft rather than additional Dreamliners that remain on order.

Etihad Airways operates 47 active Boeing 787 Dreamliners from its base in Abu Dhabi, placing it fifth among these seven airlines. Its fleet comprises the 787-9 and 787-10, which support international services across the carrier's network. The Dreamliner operates alongside other widebody aircraft, giving Etihad several aircraft options for routes with different passenger volumes and operating requirements.

The 787-9 makes up most of Etihad's Dreamliner fleet, with 37 active aircraft reported alongside ten examples of the larger 787-10. The 787-9 combines long-range capability with passenger capacity suited to a variety of international routes, while the 787-10 offers additional seating capacity. Together, these variants give the airline different options for matching aircraft size to route demand.

Etihad's 47 active Dreamliners put it ahead of Japan Airlines and British Airways, but behind Qatar Airways and the three largest fleets in this ranking. Its fleet composition shows a clear preference for the 787-9, while the 787-10 provides a higher-capacity option within the same family. The reported active-aircraft count is separate from scheduled flight totals, which measure planned operations rather than the number of aircraft operated.

Qatar Airways operates 61 active Boeing 787 Dreamliners as part of its international network from Doha. The airline uses the 787-8 and 787-9, providing two capacity options within its widebody fleet. These aircraft complement other types, including the Boeing 777 and Airbus A350, giving Qatar Airways a broader range of aircraft for different routes and passenger requirements.

The 787-8 provides a smaller-capacity configuration, while the 787-9 offers additional space for passengers and cargo within the same aircraft family. Qatar Airways' reported fleet includes 32 active 787-8s and 29 active 787-9s, with another 787-9 listed as on order in the available register. This mix gives the airline flexibility when assigning aircraft across its international network.

In May 2025, Qatar Airways announced an agreement covering up to 210 widebody aircraft, including 130 Boeing 787 Dreamliners. Boeing described the 787 portion as the largest Dreamliner order in its history, underlining the scale of the airline's future fleet plans. These aircraft are not part of the current active fleet total, however, and should only be counted as they are delivered and enter service.

In 2025, Dreamliners flew over 16,000 flights from the airline’s five busiest Boeing 787 hubs.

American Airlines operates a large Boeing 787 Dreamliner fleet, with 67 active aircraft listed in the available register snapshot. The aircraft support the carrier's international network and form part of its wider fleet of Boeing widebodies. American operates the 787-8 and 787-9, giving it two capacity options within the Dreamliner family for routes with different passenger and cargo requirements.

The 787-8 provides a lower-capacity widebody option, while the 787-9 offers additional room for passengers and cargo. Operating both variants gives American Airlines flexibility when planning long-haul services with different passenger demand and operational requirements. The airline does not operate the 787-10, so its Dreamliner fleet is built around these two models.

American's 67 active Dreamliners place it third in this ranking, behind ANA and United Airlines but ahead of Qatar Airways and the other operators listed below it. Cirium schedule data for 2026 also listed 33,192 scheduled Dreamliner round trips for American, equivalent to 66,384 one-way flight sectors. Those figures describe planned services rather than flights confirmed as completed, making them a separate measure from the airline's active aircraft total.

All Nippon Airways, widely known as ANA, was the launch customer for the Boeing 787 Dreamliner, placing an order for 50 aircraft in April 2004. The airline received its first Dreamliner in 2011, helping introduce the aircraft into commercial service. ANA's early involvement in the program and its continued use of the type make it one of the most prominent Dreamliner operators worldwide.

ANA operates the 787-8, 787-9, and 787-10, giving it a range of passenger capacities within the same aircraft family. The 787-9 forms a substantial part of the fleet, while the 787-8 and 787-10 provide additional options for different capacity requirements. This combination allows the airline to deploy the Dreamliner across its network without relying on a single aircraft configuration.

ANA was long associated with the position of the world's largest Dreamliner operator, but recent reporting in October 2026 puts its fleet at 92 aircraft, behind United Airlines' 96. The Boeing 787 Register's general operator table has displayed a lower figure, illustrating how reported totals can differ by source and update date. For this ranking, the 92-aircraft figure follows the late-October report, while the discrepancy should be checked against the publication's chosen fleet-data source before the article goes live.

United Airlines has overtaken ANA as the world's largest Boeing 787 operator, with further Dreamliner expansion planned.

United Airlines leads this ranking with 96 active Boeing 787 Dreamliners, according to its individual fleet listing in the Boeing 787 Register. The aircraft operate across the carrier's extensive network, providing widebody capacity for domestic and international services. United also has additional Dreamliners on order, but those aircraft are separate from the active fleet total used here.

United operates all three main Dreamliner variants, the 787-8, 787-9, and 787-10, giving it several aircraft configurations for different capacity requirements. The 787-9 is the largest part of its fleet, with 63 active aircraft, while the airline also operates 12 787-8s and 21 787-10s. This mix gives United a range of passenger capacities within a common aircraft family.

United's 96 active aircraft put it ahead of ANA, American Airlines, Qatar Airways, Etihad Airways, British Airways, and Japan Airlines in this comparison. Its leading position reflects aircraft currently listed as active, rather than the number of Dreamliners ordered or expected in future deliveries. As United receives additional aircraft and other airlines expand their fleets, the ranking may change, making it important to distinguish active aircraft from future orders.
