KASPER HOGH - THE £11M ENIGMA: Until such time as Celtic can cut their losses on the misfit striker, Martin O'Neill has a problem on his hands

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 14:08 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 17:47 EST, 27 November 2026

On the website of Pluto Data Analytics, prospective clients are given a taste of what they can expect if they form a partnership with the London-based company.

Under a section headed ‘Transferability You Can Trust,’ football clubs are told: ‘At Pluto, we use advanced machine learning to validate how a player might adapt in a new league.

‘For example, how would a defender in Japan’s J1 League perform in the MLS in America? This kind of insight supports clubs to make smarter, more confident recruitment decisions across borders.’

We’ll never know, of course, how different Celtic’s summer transfer dealings might have been had they got into bed with the company before this week.

But on one question, in particular, the Parkhead club would have been likely to have seen more red flags raised than a communist parade.

‘How would a forward who’s so clearly tailor-made for a unique environment north of the Arctic circle fare in the Scottish Premiership?’ Well, not too great, as it turns out.

Hogh endured another tough night in Portugal as his goalless run extended to 17 games

Listen, you didn’t need to have a PhD in mathematics to question the wisdom of Celtic paying a record £11million to sign Kasper Hogh from Bodo/Glimt in the summer. Just an appreciation that so many players who’ve thrived at that extraordinary club have found success hard to come by elsewhere.

This observation is hardly new. It’s been the subject of discussions throughout Europe since the impressive Kjetil Knutsen began establishing a culture at the Norwegian outpost in 2018, one which would pave the way to four titles, a cup and a run to the semi-finals of the Europa League.

Knutsen and his sizeable entourage have created a world of their own 520 miles north of Oslo. And the dogs in the street know that those who venture outside it rarely do well.

Sure, Victor Boniface, now of Bayer Leverkusen, hasn’t really looked back since leaving for Union Saint-Gilloise. And Hugo Vetlesen has done well since moving to Club Brugge two years ago.

But they are outnumbered by those who looked lost the minute Bodo/Glimt sold them. Patrick Berg, Fredrik Bjorkan and Jens Petter Hauge, to name but three.

Were Celtic not aware of this curious trend as Bodo/Glimt somehow convinced them to part with a club-record fee? Or did their research only go as far as the five goals Hogh scored in the Champions League last season?

Provided this Tuesday’s annual general meeting does not descend into disarray, an explanation from someone involved in the deal would be fitting.

Hogh wasn’t some six-figure punt, after all. He was an eight-figure sure thing. Or, at least, that’s what the Celtic hierarchy believed him to be.

Who first flagged him up and who agreed that he was worthy of such a gargantuan investment? The trouble with Celtic these days is that no one is quite sure about who’s responsible for that kind of thing.

Despite Paul Tisdale leaving his head of football operations post on January 5, the club has yet to appoint a replacement. Nor has the vacant role been reclassified to that of director of football or sporting director.

Two months previously, chief scout Jay Lefevre left for Swansea. Despite Mark Cooper effectively filling that void and former Chelsea operative James Bell-Walker joining the team in January, officially, there is still no head of recruitment.

O'Neill turned to Columbian hitman Duran to get Celtic out of a hole against Torreense

That’s just not a good look when the collective decision in June was to go all-in on a player who’s now gone 17 games without a goal.

Celtic’s partnership with the analytics company is all well and good and may indeed sharpen up the club’s future recruitment. It certainly needs it.

But it can’t undo what’s already done. And until such time as the club can cut its losses on Hogh and sign a replacement, Martin O’Neill has a serious problem on his hands.

Starting at Tynecastle tomorrow and up to the visit to Ibrox on January 3, Celtic have eight games to play.

That run includes a trip to Cyprus to face Omonia Nicosia, which now carries added significance after Thursday’s narrow win over Torreense revived the club’s Europa League hopes.

The trouble is that Hogh can no longer be trusted to deliver in any of those crucial matches. That much became abundantly clear across his dismal 59-minute contribution in Portugal.

That old cliché about strikers always being judged on goals has never actually rang true. Provided they hold the ball up and facilitate their team-mates to score, there will always be a degree of forgiveness.

Hogh’s epic goalless run stretching back to the hat-trick he hit at Kilmarnock on August 9 is only one part of the story now. It’s his inability to do the fundamentals of football — securing possession and finding a man — which have taken him to the point of no return.

This is no longer about one man needing a scruffy goal to get going. That’s just not going to happen. He’s demonstrably the wrong fit for Celtic. He has been a scandalous waste of money.

His display against Torreense was that of a player who is getting worse by the week, not better. He touched the ball just 13 times, misplaced three passes and only found a team-mate on seven occasions.

Hogh’s one moment of promise came when he robbed veteran defender Stopira in the right channel just before half-time. Having done the hard part and got to the line, his cut-back went straight into the goalkeeper’s arms.

Hogh's confidence already looks shot after making a miserable start to his Celtic career

‘It’s poor. It’s really poor. He is really lacking conviction,’ said former Celtic manager Neil Lennon.

Hogh’s withdrawal came not a moment too soon for the visiting fans. With approximately half as much time on the pitch, his replacement, Camilo Duran, managed just one touch fewer than the Dane, including the header for the winning goal which came from James Forrest’s cross.

Now with 15 goals for the season after signing from Qarabag for an initial £4.5m, the Colombian is continuing to prove that spending money is one thing. It’s how you spend it which is crucial.

O’Neill is hardly without options across the front area as a defining spell begins in Gorgie.

After being sidelined for 18 months with a serious knee injury, Jota’s display against Torreense was scintillating. There should be no concerns now over the £8m which was spent to bring him back to the club.

While Yang Hyun-jun had a poor night in Portugal, it was a rarity in recent times. The Korean is a different animal to the one which arrived from Gangwon FC three years back for £2.2m.

Haissem Hassan, Yang’s main rival on the right, hasn’t kicked a ball in Celtic’s last three matches. The fact that Forrest and Seb Tounekti stepped off the bench on Thursday before the £6m Egyptian was instructive.

Intriguingly, Callum Osmand remained there throughout. It won’t have escaped anyone’s notice that the 20-year-old arrived from Fulham ostensibly as a centre-forward.

He was recently afforded opportunities on the wing as Jota got back up to speed, but with the Portuguese returning to top form, that door is closing.

Osmand shouldn’t lose much sleep over that recent development, though. Without even shedding his tracksuit in Leiria, he jumped ahead of the hapless Hogh in the pecking order.
