Every year, MIT Technology Review honors 35 of the world's brightest young scientists, entrepreneurs, and inventors in our Innovators Under 35 list. And starting today, our 2027 competition is officially open for nominations.

We’re on the hunt for researchers and founders who are engineering solutions to society’s most formidable problems, from human disease to climate change. We also recognize those who have made clever and useful inventions, important new scientific discoveries, or technological breakthroughs early in their careers.

Nominees can be based anywhere in the world, but must be under 35 on October 1, 2027. Have someone in mind? Or think you’d be a great candidate and want to put yourself forward? Here's how to make a nomination for our 2027 list.

There are a lot of remarkable things about Avi Davidson. A horrific injury as a teenager left him paralyzed and without a left hand. Today, his experience informs his work as a trauma therapist. He's also taking part in a clinical trial of new tech that allows him to move—and feel with—a prosthetic hand.Before the trial started, researchers scanned his brain to see how his missing left hand was represented in his brain. Will that change? And what might that mean for people who are born without limbs?
