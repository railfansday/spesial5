Joel Embiid has huge night, but Sixers still have plenty of work to do

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Sixers' 135-123 win over the Brooklyn Nets was powered by Joel Embiid's best game of the season. The big fella dropped 37 points, shooting 13-of-22 from the field while getting to the free throw line 10 times. That included knocking down a pair of threes after only making one shot from behind the arc in his previous nine games.

Embiid built off his previous outing with continued aggression. He took 14 of his 22 shots in the paint, the consistent attack of the rim helped him get to the line so frequently. The difference, according to him, was being ready to attack from tip-off.

"It kind of feels like I've been going out there and letting myself go a bit," Embiid said, "so that was just the one adjustment we made, because I feel like we felt that impacted everything. The more force I play with, the more aggressive I am, the better the shot is going to be or is going to look."

In the walk off interview with NBC Sports Philly's Amy Fadool, Embiid said hitting a couple three balls felt good, especially because his shot's been "so broke."

It wasn't hard to see how that was translating into confidence for Embiid. For the second game in a row, he attempted a self-lob off the backboard to throw down a massive slam. Thankfully, for the second game in a row, the attempt got broken up before he could attempt the dunk. Despite the unsuccessful attempt, Embiid thinks that is a sign that he is playing the right way.

"I'm so used to playing hard that when I don't do that it affects everything else," he said. "I just wanted to go back to playing hard. That's the main thing. It's a long season, we're all gonna have these moments, so it's about being balanced."

Embiid's night was nearly overshadowed by the team blowing a 24-point lead to one of the worst teams in the league. They let the Nets shoot 55% in the second half as they cut that lead down to four points multiple times late.

What's held the Sixers back from their first true blowout win of the season has been the offense falling into place. Even after the 135-point performance, they're still 17th in the league in points scored per 100 possessions. As guys like Embiid start to find their footing, they do feel like their process is getting better.

"We're far from perfect, far from where we want to be," said VJ Edgecombe, "but I think we are trending in the right direction when it comes to offensively."

Jaylen Brown also seems to have found a groove over his past two games, scoring 31 points in each of those outings. He continues to preach that more time is what's going to allow him to find comfort with this group. As the chemistry develops on the floor, the camaraderie continues to show itself off the floor. Brown happened to drop a "trust the process" when detailing his acclimation right when Embiid was walking by, something the big man took great pleasure in.

The night was also nearly overshadowed by a couple injury scares for Brown. He had a scary-looking collision with Julius Randle with a cut on his head, and checked out of the game with 1:44 left as he felt some hamstring tightness.

Brown said he'll have to see how he feels in the morning, but wasn't too concerned about it compared to hamstring injuries he's dealt with in the past. As for the collision with Randle, the cut made him have to wear a headband, which led to more joking around from his teammates.

Edgecombe gave him some grief for donning a headband, something Brown said he has never done before in his career but a look Edgecombe sports quite often.

"I tell him he don't even know how to swag out the headband, he just looked nasty," Edgecombe said, "but you gotta protect, gotta cover up that little boo-boo."

As teammates continued to give him a hard time over the headband, Brown obliged his teammates by hitting some ninja poses. He joked that it might be something he needs to lean into even more.

"It might be a thing going forward," Brown said. "I do martial arts and shit, maybe it's time."

On a serious note, the Sixers know they still haven't picked up a dominant, blowout win on the season, despite having that chance in Brooklyn. As their stars start to figure things out offensively, they do feel like it's only a matter of time.

"We're putting together a bunch of games where we're winning. So I just think when I say we haven't even had a like a dominant win yet that you know we're capable of," Edgecombe said, "but that's where we got to continue to just keep getting better."

The good news for the Sixers is they've got themselves at the top of the Eastern Conference while still searching for that.
