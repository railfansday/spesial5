Art Rooney II expresses support for Mike McCarthy, calls Aaron Rodgers not a "good teammate"

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

After Wednesday’s unprecedented chaos, it had to happen. And it did.

Steelers owner Art Rooney II has addressed the events that resulted in the benching and then the firing of quarterback Aaron Rodgers.

“A change had to be made, and I certainly agree with the change and the way it was handled,” Rooney told Gerry Dulac of the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette in a Thursday interview.

The change was sparked by the notion that Rodgers gave coach Mike McCarthy an ultimatum: “Either let me call all the plays or just release me.” Instead, McCarthy benched him.

It went to the next level when, as many expected, Rodgers scurried to the McAfee show to take shots at McCarthy for missing meetings, showing up late to meetings, and falling asleep in meetings — among other things, including the ultra-embarrassing “1-2-3 Packers!” moment from halftime of Sunday’s loss to the Eagles.

“There’s maybe a time and place after the season if he wanted to kind of unload,” Rooney said. “He talks about wanting to be a good teammate, but you don’t do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that’s for sure. . . .

“In general, I think, historically, what’s in the locker room stays in the locker room. That’s the way it’s always been. That’s the way it should be as far as I’m concerned.”

Rooney also confirmed that the team supports McCarthy, despite Rodgers’s rant and reports confirming the facts underpinning the petty delivery of them.

“I just want to say that I have 100 [percent] confidence in Mike McCarthy and I expect he’ll be our coach this season, next season and beyond,” Rooney told Dulac. “The big thing, what happened this week, is insubordination can’t be tolerated in any organization. When you start getting ultimatums, you can’t live with that.”

Rooney also was asked whether Rodgers raised with the owner supposed concerns about McCarthy’s health.

“Nothing,” Rooney said. “Mike’s as healthy as the rest of us. Health was not an issue. I’m not sure where that part comes [from].”

Rooney also addressed something that has been largely overlooked during the fast-moving drama: Rodgers’s subpar performance.

“The sad thing is Mike took some of the bullets for Aaron in terms of some of the plays that were called, particularly in the red-zone troubles that we had,” Rooney said. “Mike took some of the bullets for things that were going wrong down there. He tried to protect him, and it just wasn’t working.”

They should have known it wouldn’t work between Rodgers and McCarthy. It’s still unclear why either one of them thought it would. McCarthy shouldn’t have wanted a reunion with Rodgers. And Rodgers shouldn’t have wanted a second act with McCarthy.

Then again, it’s not as if Rodgers had many choices. In 2025, he had few takers. In 2026, he had fewer. If he was going to ensure that his final NFL throw wasn’t a pick-six by playing another year, Pittsburgh wasn’t just the best place.
