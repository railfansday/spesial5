Week 11 NFL Power Rankings: Each Team’s Second-Half Outlook

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Although it might be hard to believe, we've not only crossed the halfway point of the 2026 NFL regular season, but the trade deadline has come and gone, too. While the NFC playoff picture is muddied with nine very good teams fighting for seven playoff spots, the AFC is a little easier to decipher. With the stretch run upon us, it's time to take a deeper dive into every team's second-half outlook. While many are still competing for a playoff spot, others will need to find meaning amid lost seasons. We'll dive into all of that and more in our Week 11 installment of WCG NFL Power Rankings.

Second-Half Outlook: Get healthy and continue their push for the No. 1 seed in the NFC

The first nine games of the season have been nothing short of impressive for the 49ers. Looking back to the preseason, nobody had the 49ers running away with a loaded NFC West, especially with the defending Super Bowl Champions and the overwhelming 2026 Super Bowl favorites. Instead, Kyle Shanahan has taken one of the league's oldest rosters and survived multiple impact injuries. We'll see if they can keep the pace up over the back half of the year, but this is one of the more well-rounded rosters in the league this season.

Second-Half Outlook: Hope that the defense can round into form and support one of the league's elite offenses

The addition of Amani Hooker should be a big one for this Buffalo defense, but it's fair to wonder if one player will be enough to turn the tide for a struggling defense. Some might point to their Week 8 and 9 performances as hope, and while that might be the case, the opponents need to be noted. The good news is that this is an experienced roster with an MVP-caliber quarterback. We'll see if they finally have enough to get them over the hump in the AFC. Like last year, it's wide open and theirs for the taking.

Second-Half Outlook: Ride a favorable schedule and find a way to earn the No. 1 seed down the stretch

The Bills might have better odds to win the Super Bowl, but the Jaguars have a strong argument for being the more well-rounded team. Simply put, Jacksonville has been dominant for the majority of the season, and the additions of Jordan Davis and Devin Godchaux should help stabilize the middle of their defense. If quarterback Trevor Lawrence can continue his ascension, this is going to be the toughest out for anybody in the AFC.

Second-Half Outlook: Survive a brutal schedule down the stretch and hope that Jeffery Simmons continues to be a difference-maker

Once dubbed a prime regression candidate heading into the regular season, the Bears have survived without Caleb Williams for three games and somehow continue to play much better defense than the numbers suggest. Williams' Year 3 jump has been notable, even with a four-interception game in Week 9. The talent on offense is evident, and their ceiling feels safe with Ben Johnson calling plays. How far they go this season will come down to the defense, especially the secondary. We'll see if not making another move at the deadline comes back to bite them.

Second-Half Outlook: Continue to build continuity on defense and hope that they'll be good enough to get them over the hump down the stretch and into January

When quarterback Joe Burrow is healthy, this offense is hard to stop. Their running game has been spotty at best, but with Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins on the field, it really hasn't mattered. Their defense is much improved from previous seasons, but there are still some holes that could expose them against better teams. The AFC North appears to be coming down to the Bengals and Steelers, and at full strength, I'd take Cincinnati without much thought. Similar to the Bills, their defense will dictate how far they can go this season. So far, so good.

Second-Half Outlook: Hope that Patrick Mahomes is healthy enough to enjoy his new offensive weapon and push the Broncos for the AFC West title

Despite their 5-0 start to the season, I'm still not ready to say the Chiefs are all the way "back". They've continued to ignore the wide receiver position, and while the addition of Josh Downs is a step in the right direction, there's still a lot to be desired on the offensive side of the ball when it comes to personnel. Keeping Mahomes healthy will be key, and their Week 9 scare could have been season-altering. They aren't out of the race in the AFC West, but the offensive production is going to need to jump in the second half for them to win the division.

Second-Half Outlook: Rely on a weak schedule down the stretch and hope that the defense can continue to play at the same level

In many ways, the start of this season has been eerily similar to how they looked last season. So far, the biggest difference is that they are winning those close games that they couldn't pull off last season. The offense has looked great under Drew Petzing, but their season will come down to how much their defense can sustain over the final eight games. Following a rough start, they've been playing much better as of late, but there are still plenty of questions on that side of the ball. In a loaded NFC North, they must take advantage of their favorable schedule. Missing Jack Campbell and Aidan Hutchinson will hurt, especially over time.

Second-Half Outlook: Hope that the presence of Kirk Cousins is enough to support an elite defense

Defensively, there isn't a better unit in the league. Frankly, with as poor as their offense has played to this point, Brian Flores' group is the only thing separating them from a losing record. With that in mind, the addition of Cousins comes at a great time. Not only are they in the midst of their toughest stretch of the season, but the veteran quarterback knows the offense and should be able to hit the ground running. Even if he's still the fourth-best quarterback in his own division, the ceiling for the offense is considerably higher than it was just a week ago. We saw that on Sunday in Green Bay. Now, we'll see if that's good enough to separate themselves in a loaded NFC North.

Second-Half Outlook: Hope that Bo Nix can take his play up a notch (or two) to make them legitimate Super Bowl contenders in January

Very rarely does a team's fortune in one-score games carry over (good or bad) from season to season the way that the Broncos have. Even with Nix struggling for the majority of the season, they are a seven-win team in full control of their destiny. The defense remains elite, and if Nix can figure out a way to start challenging downfield more, there's no reason they can't find their way back to the AFC Championship game again this season. Even with the Daiyan Henley deal falling through, there's little reason to doubt this defense.

Second-Half Outlook: Get healthy on both sides of the ball and hope that their slow start doesn't keep them out of the playoffs come January

Despite coming into the season as the overwhelming Super Bowl favorites, the Rams haven't been healthy enough to reach their ceiling. While there's still time for that to happen, Puka Nacua's trip to Injured Reserve is a tough blow to absorb, especially with Davante Adams still a few weeks away from returning. The NFC is loaded, and even for as talented as Los Angeles is, they face an uphill battle to make the playoffs. We'll see if quarterback Matthew Stafford can elevate his play without his two best receivers. Their season might depend on it.

Second-Half Outlook: Get healthy at quarterback and hope their slow start doesn't keep them out of the playoffs come January (like the Rams)

If you're comparing the Rams' and Seahawks' schedules down the stretch, Seattle has the clear advantage, especially with both teams holding the same record. As noted before, the NFC is loaded, and barring a second-half collapse from the 49ers, the division is all but out of reach. Quarterback play has been an issue for most of the season, so the Seahawks have to hope that when Sam Darnold returns, he's playing multiple levels above what he was when he went out.

Second-Half Outlook: Fight off the Bengals in the AFC North and hope that their older roster doesn't wear down in December

In my estimation, the Steelers have looked considerably better than expected. Aaron Rodgers is clearly a 43-year-old quarterback on the back end of his career, but the defense has not seen a drop-off with the departure of former head coach Mike Tomlin. On paper, this is a fringe playoff team, but following a slow start to the season, they've rounded into form and have looked like a true playoff contender. The bigger question is about their ceiling, but for now, they're locked into a battle to repeat as AFC North champs.

Second-Half Outlook: Wait for Micah Parsons' return and hope that their weaknesses don't cost them in the end

Following a brutal start to the season, the Packers have rounded into form. The scary part- it's been without their star pass rusher. The offensive line might still be leaning "makeshift," but with Jordan Love playing the way he has, it hasn't mattered until Sunday. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden have been the league's most explosive receiving tandem, on top of it. Parsons' return will be big, but following a loss to the Vikings, their backs are going to be against the wall over these final seven games. "Thanksgiving Eve" becomes as close to a "must-win" game as the Packers have had this year.

Second-Half Outlook: Continue to build on a surprising season and hope to hang around in the Wild Card race into late December

Even if their chances of making the playoffs shrink with every loss, the Browns have been one of the better stories in football so far in 2026. First-year head coach Todd Monken has done a nice job, and their high-round rookies are looking like hits. There's plenty of young talent on this team, and even if they can't finish the job with a Wild Card berth, it's hard not to see this season as a success. The future is bright in Cleveland, especially if they can find a quarterback.

Second-Half Outlook: Continue to do exactly what they are doing and hold onto first place

Without any other context to the season, it would be easy to say that the Giants have arrived ahead of schedule. Add in some key injuries to players like quarterback Jaxson Dart and edge rusher Brian Burns, and their hot start is even more impressive. New York has started three quarterbacks this season, and now has J.J. McCarthy playing the best football of his career. All things considered, this season should be considered a success no matter what, but even after a disappointing loss on Thursday night, they are well on pace to win the NFC East for the first time in over a decade.

Second-Half Outlook: Survive long enough to get back some defensive reinforcements and win the division

It hasn't always been pretty, and despite a rollercoaster ride for the offense, the Panthers held onto the division lead on Sunday with a big win in New Orleans. Bryce Young's productivity and efficiency have fallen off a cliff since late October, but this offense still has plenty of weapons. Getting back both starting cornerbacks and keeping Jaelan Phillips will be key to getting this defense back on track. Even if they don't look improved over last season, the rest of the division might be worse. At this point, the goal should be finding a way to finish over .500.

Second-Half Outlook: Survive without Drake Maye and hope that his ankle injury isn't season-ending

Originally, I would have said that they simply need to put these last two games behind them and figure out how to win the Wild Card. The Maye injury changes everything, and the early reports are that he might miss the rest of the regular season. If that's the case, the Patriots are in serious trouble. Even if he misses the next four-to-five games, New England might be in trouble. Injuries have plagued them all year, but Maye was one of the only good things going for this group.

Second-Half Outlook: Cling to their slim playoff hopes and pray that Christian Parker can fix the defense

The Cowboys remain on the fringe of the playoff picture, but the Giants' loss on Thursday night opened the door for Dallas to get back into the race. The offense has been somewhat inconsistent, but the defense will ultimately dictate their season. The talent is there, but so far, the entire product simply hasn't come together as hoped. Following a quiet trade deadline, it feels like Brian Schottenheimer and his staff are quietly fighting for their jobs, especially with a desperate owner in Jerry Jones. Sunday's 46-40 loss at home was not a great step in the right direction.

Second-Half Outlook: Enjoy the ride with Fernando Mendoza over the final eight games of the season

With last Tuesday's deadline trade of Kirk Cousins, it's Mendoza's team down the stretch. Following a 3-0 start, it was easy for Raiders fans to believe that maybe they could be a "surprise" playoff team in 2026, but as the season has played out, reality has struck. This can still be a successful season, and assuming Mendonza is ready, I would already classify the year as a success. That said, how Mendonza develops on the field will matter heading into the offseason and 2027.

Second-Half Outlook: Figure out the passing game and ride a top-tier defense to a division title

News broke on Sunday that quarterback Baker Mayfield is dealing with a leg injury, and his status for Week 11 is in doubt. Considering they are facing one of the hottest teams in football, in Detroit, that's a tough ask for an undrafted free agent. The Bucs have a Super Bowl-caliber defense, but unless they can marry the passing game with a dangerous rushing attack, it's hard to envision many scenarios where they can string wins together. Although they are just one game back, they'll need to find more consistency down the stretch.

Second-Half Outlook: Hope that it's not too late to get back into the Wild Card picture

On one side, the Browns' last-minute win might have saved their season (at least for the time being). For the Texans, their season is on life support with a limited ceiling, unless they win out. The AFC's final Wild Card spot is still gettable, but with the Browns leading that race and Houston's head-to-head loss, DeMeco Ryans and company have their backs against the wall. It'll likely take a 7-1 finish, with games remaining against the Steelers, Jaguars, and Packers over the final two months of the season. Good luck.

Second-Half Outlook: Hope that a second-half run is possible and avoid big changes in the offseason

It's been back-to-back one-score games for the Saints coming out of their bye week. Unfortunately, the more important of the two games is the one they lost. Although they'll have another crack at the Falcons next month, they are 0-2 within the division and 1-4 in the conference. Barring a successful run against the Bears, Bengals, and Packers coming up, the outlook for the remainder of their season appears bleak. Ownership has already turned up the heat following a slow start, so things better improve soon or changes could be coming.

Second-Half Outlook: Hope that Jacoby Brissett can get you back in the Wild Card race

The back end of the AFC Wild Card picture is still wide open, especially with the Patriots struggling over the last few games. That said, the Ravens are banged up and on their fourth quarterback of the season. Despite injuries to players like Lamar Jackson, the Ravens are still surviving. If all goes well, he can make it back before the end of the season, but a lot of that will depend on how well the Ravens can do until the middle-to-end of December.

Second-Half Outlook: Evaluate the coaching staff and keep quarterback Jayden Daniels healthy

Thursday night's win mathematically keeps them in the divisional race, but barring a serious collapse down the stretch, Washington's season is still all but over. At this point, figuring out who is worth keeping around for the future should be the primary goal. Head coach Dan Quinn and his coaching staff are fighting for their coaching lives, so we'll see how it goes. Outside of being in the NFC South, the Commanders might be in the right division to make a comeback happen, but the margin for error will be extremely small from here on out.

Second-Half Outlook: Get back to Weeks 3 through 5's offensive production and take a run at the division

Each loss makes that more difficult, but in the weakest division in football, nothing is impossible. Really, though, the offense struggling has been a big issue for this entire team. Michael Penix looked good after returning from injury, but his last few games have left a lot to be desired. This has always been the concern with this roster. Although they are far from out of the playoff race, it's going to take quite a run for them to make that happen.

Second-Half Outlook: Just survive with an eye towards April's quarterback class

Injuries have completely derailed what has otherwise been a quality rebuilding season. With Jacoby Brissett in Baltimore and Gardner Minshew done for the season, the offense's ceiling is bleak, especially without Michael Wilson. This team needs a long-term solution at quarterback, and unless rookie Carson Beck takes the world by storm, that'll be the case come March and April. First-year head coach Mike LaFleur has done a nice job, but there's still plenty of work to be done in the future.

Second-Half Outlook: Evaluate the coaching staff and prepare for plenty of changes ahead

One thing you learn over time during Black Monday is that surprises happen every year. Typically, when a team fires a general manager midseason with a regime already in question, the head coach and his staff don't survive past the regular season. My gut tells me that's what happens here, but only time will tell. The Colts have needed a rebuild for a few years, and firing Chris Ballard should jumpstart the process. Like most teams at the bottom of our rankings, it's a wait-and-see approach heading into the offseason.

Second-Half Outlook: Prove enough down the stretch to give the coaching staff a third season

Barring an unexpected turnaround over the second half of the season, it feels unlikely that the Jets will continue to hang with the same coaching staff. Head coach Aaron Glenn is a good leader on paper, but this group has not melded the way most expected. Although they might not have a Top 3 pick when all is said and done, they'll likely have three selections in the Top 10. That will be extremely attractive if the Jets decide to make a change. For now, trying to save jobs is all that's going to be worth watching.

Second-Half Outlook: Survive the season and wait for the fallout

Although things didn't fall apart last season as they should have, the bottom has completely collapsed in 2026. It started with a few losses in a row after a 2-0 start, but once Jalen Hurts was placed on Injured Reserve with blood clots in his lungs, the ceiling on this season has totally disappeared. This group has plenty of questions moving forward, but general manager Howie Roseman is one of the best. Right now, it's just about surviving the final stretch of games and making some tough decisions when the offseason arrives.

Second-Half Outlook: Can the Chargers avoid a regime change after a disastrous winless start to the season

For a while, it felt like everyone was waiting for the Chargers to figure things out one week and hit their stride. September and October came and went without a win, and now, heading into mid-November, their season is over. Despite that, they didn't make a single (official) trade, which was confounding, to say the least. Considering what they invested in Jim Harbaugh when he was hired three offseasons ago, cutting bait won't be easy, but it also won't be easy to excuse how poorly this season has gone. There's plenty to monitor heading into January.

Second-Half Outlook: Figure out if Cam Ward is the quarterback of the future

2026 has been nothing short of a disappointment for the Titans. What was once just a slow start turned into serious questions about the future. Now that Jeffery Simmons is in Chicago, the focus is solely on the future. The first question they must answer is whether Ward is their quarterback of the future. Through nine weeks, it hasn't been pretty, but there's simply too much talent for him to be this bad. The final eight games of the season will go a long way in determining whether or not they'll be in the quarterback market come March and April.

Second-Half Outlook: Keep on building, even without key veteran pieces

Last week was a rough one on the injury front for the Dolphins. Their playoff hopes are all but dead, but this season has never realistically been about anything more than building for the future. Quarterback Malik Willis is down for at least the next four games, and veteran defensive tackle Zach Sieler is done for the year. At this point, Miami must keep looking toward the future, and all of that depends on the young core they are trying to build.
