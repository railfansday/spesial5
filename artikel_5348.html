Utah is moving to drop criminal charges against Kalshi after it exited the state

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The state's motion to dismiss is without prejudice, and Utah says seven prediction market companies have agreed to stop operating there

The Utah Attorney General's Office filed a motion on Monday to dismiss the 14 criminal charges against Kalshi and the individual defendants after the prediction market company blocked access to its platform in the state.

The motion, filed in Third District Court, seeks dismissal without prejudice, so the office can refile the charges if Kalshi resumes offering its markets in Utah. The office said its position has not changed: Utah law prohibits gambling, and it considers prediction markets gambling.

"Utah does not allow gambling, and prediction markets are gambling. Kalshi has pulled out of Utah, and if it returns, so will the charges. Every company in this space should take note. If they promote, support, or enable gambling, we will hold them accountable," Utah Attorney General Derek Brown said in a statement.

The office said it opened a criminal investigation into Kalshi in September and filed the charges on Oct. 28. After that, Kalshi said it would geofence Utah and monitor for Utah-based IP addresses trying to reach its site. The office reevaluated its position and moved to dismiss the case.

The charges named CEO Tarek Mansour and Chief Operating Officer Luana Lopes Lara as defendants, according to The Hill. They consist of 13 felonies and a single misdemeanor. A Utah district court had set Dec. 4 as the date for Mansour and Lopes Lara to appear, according to CNBC.

Kalshi halted its Utah business on Monday, pointing to an unsettled legal picture, according to CNBC. Kalshi General Counsel Yaakov Roth said in a post on X $TWTR that "good faith jurisdictional disagreements should never be criminalized," according to CNBC. He added that Kalshi is willing to cooperate with state attorneys general on temporary arrangements while the courts sort out which authority governs prediction markets.

The attorney general's office has sent cease-and-desist letters to several Kalshi competitors. Including Kalshi, seven companies have agreed to stop operating in Utah, the office said. It said it will keep enforcing the state's gambling laws against other prediction market companies and may bring civil or criminal cases against any company that promotes sports gambling to Utah residents.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission filed suit against Utah one day ahead of the state's announcement of the charges, according to CNBC. The federal regulator maintains that it alone has the authority to set rules for prediction markets. In August, a federal judge ruled in Utah's favor and allowed the state to enforce its antigambling rules, according to The Hill.

The Utah case is part of a wider dispute. States and lawmakers have stepped up their fight against Kalshi's sports trading business, and multiple states contend that its sports contracts are illegal gambling that falls under state oversight.
