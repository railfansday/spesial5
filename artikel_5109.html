Bitcoin reclaims $61,000 before giving back a third of the weekend rally

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Perpetual futures led aggressive buying in two early Sunday rallies. The next check is whether spot buying broadens across major exchanges.

A Bitcoin rebound took the price back above $60,000 on Saturday while sellers initiated more volume than buyers across Binance’s BTC/USDT spot session. The pair fell to 55,130 USDT before closing the Nov. 21 UTC session at 60,216 USDT, a 9.2% rebound from the low.

Binance’s daily trading data show that seller-initiated trades accounted for 201 million USDT more in volume than buyer-initiated trades. Turnover reached 2.82 billion USDT, about 2.3 times the 1.23 billion USDT median Whalio reported across 358 days.

Every trade has a buyer and a seller. Whalio defines volume delta by assigning each trade’s volume to the side that crossed the spread to trade immediately, then subtracting seller-initiated volume from buyer-initiated volume. Saturday’s total covers both the selloff and the rebound. Sunday’s early rally windows offer a closer look at how buying and selling shifted during the Bitcoin rebound.

Order-flow tracker CryptoDOM reported two early advances on Sunday, Nov. 22. The first ran from 05:45 to 08:37 UTC. The second began at 08:48 UTC, with Bitcoin at $59,532, and reached $61,343 by 09:56 UTC. CryptoDOM attributed the larger share of aggressive buying in both moves to perpetual futures across the markets it tracks. Bitcoin gave back about a third of the later advance by 10:34 UTC on its feed.

Binance’s one-minute candles show aggressive buyers were also active in its cash market during that second move. In the candles that opened from 08:48 through 09:56 UTC, Binance spot data show that buyers outpaced sellers by 15 million USDT. Data for its BTC/USDT perpetual, a futures contract with no expiry, put the net buying imbalance at 144.3 million USDT. The net imbalance was almost ten times as large in perpetuals. Binance spot was back below 61,000 USDT at the end of the 10:00 UTC minute.

Binance recorded 1.27 billion USDT of turnover in the perpetual during those minutes and 119.1 million USDT in spot. The spot imbalance was slightly larger relative to its own turnover, at 12.4% against 11.4% on the perpetual. The larger contract market generated the bigger net volume, even with a similar split between aggressive buyers and sellers.

CryptoDOM recorded about $25 million in forced closures of short positions in the first Sunday rally and $10 million in the second. It reported that open interest, a measure of outstanding contracts, fell 0.5% in the first rally and rose 0.5% in the second. The event cards give percentage changes without specifying contract quantity or dollar value. Open interest measured in dollars can rise as Bitcoin’s price climbs, even if the number of contracts stays flat.

The NYSE opens at 9:30 a.m. ET on Monday. CryptoDOM’s partial Sunday tally showed net spot buying on Coinbase and Bitstamp alongside net selling on Bybit Spot. Comparing spot trade flow at those venues and Binance in the same intervals before and after the U.S. open would show whether more cash markets participate in the next move in Bitcoin’s price.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis has scheduled the October report containing the personal consumption expenditures price index and the second estimate of third-quarter GDP for 8:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday, Nov. 25. Changes in the two-year Treasury yield and the dollar can put Bitcoin’s initial reaction around either event in context. With both releases due at once, the direction of that reaction can reflect a mix of growth and inflation news.

Net buying on several spot venues over the same interval would show wider cash-market participation, even if prices later falter. A check of Bitcoin’s price half an hour later would show whether the market held the initial gain.
