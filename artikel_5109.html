MegaETH Cuts Staff as AI Ambitions Complicate Its Ethereum Scaling Story

Ethereum scaling project MegaETH is reducing its workforce while developing a new artificial-intelligence business, a combination that raises an awkward question for investors and users: what exactly should they expect the project to prioritise? The company has reportedly cut around one-fifth of its staff and is directing substantial resources toward an AI marketplace, even as its existing on-chain activity remains under scrutiny.

A strategic pivot is not necessarily a bad decision. Many technology businesses adjust their product mix when customer demand changes. But in crypto, where teams often raise funds on a particular network vision, a change in emphasis deserves careful explanation.

The Block reported that MegaETH had reduced staffing by about 20%. Additional reporting described plans to allocate roughly $30 million from treasury resources to an AI-related marketplace scheduled for December. These are plans and reported allocations, not proof that the proposed marketplace is already operating at scale or generating revenue.

The layoff percentage matters, but so does which capabilities remain. An Ethereum scaling network needs engineers working on performance, security, developer tools and operational reliability. If a new AI product draws from the same management attention and treasury, token holders and users need to understand how the two efforts fit together rather than assuming one automatically supports the other.

Reported data placed value locked in the MegaETH ecosystem at about $8.8 million after an earlier period above $240 million. Total value locked can change for many reasons, including token prices, rewards programmes and the movement of a small number of large liquidity providers. It is therefore a useful indicator of deployed capital, but not a complete measure of developer adoption, revenue or user retention.

Even with those caveats, a large decline makes execution questions unavoidable. Are applications attracting repeat users without incentives? Is the underlying network delivering the speed and reliability promised? Can independent developers deploy products that people want to use? Those measures would say more about the scaling thesis than publicity around a new business category.

There are plausible connections between blockchain infrastructure and AI services, including machine-to-machine settlement, verifiable payments and coordination of agents. NewsBTC's examination of Circle's approach to AI-agent payments illustrates why companies are exploring this intersection. Yet a broad trend is not a commercial plan in itself.

An AI marketplace would need to answer ordinary product questions: who are the customers, what service are they buying, what is the business model and why does this specific blockchain make the experience better? If the answer is primarily that both AI and crypto are fashionable, investors should be sceptical. If the product solves a measurable problem, the new direction could make sense.

Reporting has also referenced a planned decentralisation milestone in March 2027. Timelines like that are significant for a scaling network because they speak to who controls upgrades and critical infrastructure. A pivot cannot quietly replace the need to explain governance, operational resilience and user protections.

For Ethereum more broadly, the ecosystem is already navigating difficult market conditions, including the ETF outflows covered in NewsBTC's recent Ether fund report. MegaETH's situation adds a separate company-specific test. The next meaningful update will be evidence of both product execution and the original network commitments, not simply a new roadmap graphic.
