New ‘The Prisoner’ Series in the Works as Amazon Picks Up Rights (Exclusive)

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Don Murphy and Susan Montford of Angry Films and Philippe Maigret and Lucianne Papon of ITV will executive produce a modern update based on the British cult classic from the 1960s.

Hollywood has spent a good many years trying to adapt British cult classic TV series The Prisoner into a feature film.

Now, however, a producer and a streamer are taking a ride on the penny-farthing back to the original medium.

Amazon Studios has picked up the rights to The Prisoner for a modern take on the series. Angry Films’ Susan Montford and Don Murphy, lifelong fans of the 1967 series who secured the rights, will executive produce, as will Philippe Maigret and Lucianne Papon of ITV, the company that originally distributed the show. The new potential series is a priority for the streamer, which is already out to showrunners. The new story will be set in modern times.

The original series was a blend of sci-fi, spycraft and psychological drama set against the paranoia and existential angst of the Cold War. Patrick McGoohan starred as an intelligence agent who is kidnapped and awakens in a remote town known as the Village. Like all the residents, his name is stripped from him and he becomes known only as Number Six. McGoohan spends the series squaring off against Number Two and Number One, not knowing whom he can trust in the town as he tries to escape.

The show only lasted one season, but its reach was long, thanks in part to some avant-garde, swinging ’60s production values and an ending that left the audience hanging. It has been cited as an influence on shows such as Twin Peaks, X-Files and Lost. And it has mesmerized filmmakers such as Christopher Nolan, who mulled a movie adaptation. In fact, Ridley Scott and Simon West have also been associated with feature adaptations that Hollywood tried, and failed, to mount over the decades.

There was, however, a poorly received TV remake. In 2009, AMC aired a six-part miniseries that starred Jim Caviezel and Ian McKellan.

Murphy is one of the producers of the long-running Transformers franchise, the latest of which will gear up next year. He and Montford produced the update of horror movie Faces of Death, released earlier this year, and are developing feature adaptations of classic pulp hero Buck Rogers, set up at Legendary, and Don Pendelton’s The Executioner book series, the latter set up at Sony, with Shane Black writing and an eye toward directing.
