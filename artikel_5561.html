Greenville, SC, Struggles With Low Starter Home Availability Compared to Other Cities

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

In a recent metro-by-metro analysis, Realtor.com® examined the state of the American starter home. For many would-be buyers, these entry-level homes have felt increasingly scarce as rising prices and climbing mortgage rates create significant financial hurdles.

Furthermore, research supports this feeling of a shrinking market. The national share of starter homes in active inventory fell from 38.1% in August 2019 to 36.2% in August 2026. During that same period, starter home prices rose 30.8%, from approximately $260,000 to $340,000.

In Greenville, SC, the market has experienced a downturn in starter home availability in recent years.

A starter home is defined as a property priced at roughly 80% of a metro's median list price, making it an accessible option for a first-time buyer or a household with an Income at or below the area median. These homes are typically smaller and offer a more manageable entry point into homeownership, boosting overall market affordability.

In the Greenville-Anderson-Greer, SC, metro area, the share of starter homes was 32.2% in 2019. This figure fell to 26.6% in 2022 before recovering slightly to 31.3% in 2026.

However, this still represents a -0.9 point change since 2019, indicating a slight tightening of the market for entry-level buyers.

Greenville trailed neighboring South Carolina and Georgia markets like Charleston (36.7%), Augusta (35.1%), and Columbia (34.7%) in starter home inventory.

However, between 2019 and 2026, Greenville experienced a relatively mild drop of just 0.9 percentage points, reaching a 2026 starter home share of 31.3%. This stability allowed Greenville to pull ahead of nearby regional peers that suffered steep losses over the same period, including Columbia (-8.3 points to 26.4%) and Augusta (-6.5 points to 28.6%).

After dipping to 26.6% in 2022, Greenville rebounded strongly by 2026, nearly matching Charleston, which saw its share contract by 5.0 percentage points to 31.7%.

By 2026, Greenville’s 31.3% starter home share closely aligns with major adjacent hubs like Atlanta (31.5%) and Charlotte (31.1%), though Charlotte achieved a 1.0 point gain while Atlanta fell by 3.3 points.
