Numbers tell a story. Are you uncovering the right ones?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Surface conversion rates can mislead sales managers when lead mix skews to harder credit buckets. Reviewing credit-bucket conversion and PQ-to-lock rates can pinpoint coaching opportunities and process leaks.   AI Summary

Helping loan originators get clarity on their business is important, especially when the headwinds can easily cause us to lose focus. How can we help our loan originators get more out of what they already have?

While analyzing some data recently, I dug for clarity I could share with a few LOs to help them better leverage what was already working at the top of the funnel.

The first case study was a branch that, on paper, showed a conversion problem. You know the story. How many of our at-bats are making it to the closing table? When I examined this branch, I found they were converting far below the company average.

Easy one. I can simply have a conversation with them about conversion and coach them through some strategies to help them convert better. Let’s take a deeper dive, though, and look at conversion based on credit buckets.

Looking at five different credit buckets, the story started to change. Yes, conversion was still weaker, but the reason why started to become clear.

The at-bats this branch was getting were coming from credit buckets that are harder to convert. Comparing the credit buckets, we found that they were converting higher-credit-score borrowers at the same pace as the rest of the company. Lower-credit-score borrowers and borrowers without a qualifying credit score were a large part of their top of funnel.

This was the reason their conversion was off.

Had I not dug deeper, a coaching conversation could have gone wrong, and we could have focused on some of the wrong things.  The layers of data are important, and we shouldn’t look at just what the surface tells us.

That led me even deeper, where I started to analyze credit-bucket conversion to see what was being left on the table. Yes, there were MLOs who had left significant dollars on the table because they weren’t converting the best borrowers at the same pace as the company average. This coaching conversation becomes about showing up as a vendor or as an advisor.

After all, a high-credit-score borrower knows who they are, and they also know they can get a loan anywhere. If you are just going to point them to a URL, you are teaching them they can get the same level of service and expertise at any URL.

If you’re a data nerd like me, you’ve stuck around to see what’s next.

One of the harder conversations in our business is giving clarity to a top producer about possible leaks in their business. After all, they are in the top 1% in the country among all originators, perennial President’s Club winners and have 5-star reviews in the thousands on lock.

Now imagine that top producer having an absolute banger of a year. Year-over-year volume and units are crushing the year before. The top-line numbers are fun. It’s sexy. It’s what everyone is looking at. Sometimes, though, it doesn’t hurt to take a bit of a deeper look.

In this case, you wouldn’t be surprised to find that this producer is also getting way more at-bats at the top of the funnel than they did the year prior.

If numbers tell a story and the numbers help us get clarity, where else should we look? In this case, I looked at conversion.

PQ-to-funding conversion is important. I looked at a different number, and that was PQ-to-lock conversion. It’s an earlier indicator than PQ-to-funding, and when the closings are coming in much hotter than a year before, you would think those numbers would be moving in parallel all the way through.

What we uncovered was that, compared with the same period the year before, lock conversion was down. Sometimes one of the byproducts of success is that we don’t see the whole field.

Think back to 2020 and 2021. When business was coming at us that fast, it was easy for inefficiencies to hide underneath production. Even today, when winning at a high level, it’s easy to overlook the tiny leaks.

The numbers underneath production told us there was still an opportunity to improve even more. The hard work of creating additional opportunities was already being done. A few adjustments in how those opportunities were handled could create an even greater increase in closed business.
