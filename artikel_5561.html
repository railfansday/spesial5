Alibaba stock falls as much as 7% on fears of tighter U.S. AI chip scrutiny

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Z.AI and MiniMax fell at least 8% as an Nvidia scandal fueled worries about Chinese cloud access to advanced chips

Alibaba Group Holding stock sank by up to 7.3% on Thursday, weighing on the Hang Seng Tech Index as worries grew that Washington could look more closely at Chinese access to cutting-edge AI chips.

Peers in China’s artificial intelligence industry were hit as well. Model developers Z.ai and MiniMax Group each slid at least 8%. Investors fear that Washington could clamp down harder on how cloud providers obtain high-end chips as the uproar involving Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang unfolds, according to Bloomberg.

Alibaba rents Nvidia chips through Singapore-based Megaspeed International, according to Bloomberg. Jensen Huang acknowledged having cheated on his wife after reports tied him to Alice Huang, who has links to Megaspeed, according to CNBC. According to CNBC, American intelligence officials suspect she is working as an asset for Chinese intelligence. China has denied the allegation, according to The Japan Times. Megaspeed has rejected claims that it unlawfully rerouted chips to China.

Alice Huang held investments in, and did business with, a number of top Chinese tech firms such as Alibaba and Tencent, according to The Japan Times. Singapore corporate records indicate that Apex Enterprise Solutions, a unit of Alibaba, put money into Speedmatrix, a Malaysian business with ties to Megaspeed.

Chinese AI firms are seeking to lease computing capacity powered by Nvidia chips in data centers located abroad, according to The Japan Times. One large Chinese cloud provider has internally debated securing capacity abroad in advance, while smaller AI labs are considering comparable moves.

The companies are concerned that the affair may give fresh impetus to the Remote Access Security Act, according to The Japan Times. Under the proposed legislation, the U.S. Commerce Department would be empowered to oversee how restricted technology is accessed remotely through the cloud. The House approved it in January, 369 to 22, and the Senate has yet to act on it.

Julia Pan, an analyst at UOB Kay Hian, said Chinese cloud providers have been building up chip inventories since January, but research and development timelines will "inevitably be postponed," according to Bloomberg.

Alibaba and Tencent Holdings are scheduled to release their results for the September quarter next week, according to Bloomberg. Beijing on Thursday called on the U.S. to refrain from citing the spying allegations as grounds for technology curbs and said the two governments remain in touch as their leaders prepare to meet.

Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang, is alleged to have assembled a web of firms spanning China and Singapore that moved roughly 240,000 Nvidia chips into China, according to The Information, and part of that hardware supported Alibaba, Tencent and several smaller AI labs. Reuters said it could not independently verify the claims.

Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation, and House committee leaders have asked Jensen Huang to attend a Nov. 18 briefing, according to CNBC.
