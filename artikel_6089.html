Beach access on the UAE’s east coast and northern emirates now depends on local notices that can change with each tanker strike. That’s a risk Dubai and Abu Dhabi don’t carry yet.

Oil from tankers attacked in the Strait of Hormuz is washing onto beaches in the UAE and threatening some of Oman’s best-known dive sites, adding an environmental crisis to a Gulf tourism downturn now into its ninth month. For example, Dubai hotel occupancy fell to 68.4% in October, down 16.5 points from 84.9% a year earlier, according to CoStar’s STR.

More than 600 oil spills have been identified in and around the Gulf since the U.S.-Iran war began on Feb. 28, according to data released by environmental group Greenpeace and maritime tracking firm Kpler. The data is current to Nov. 19 and Greenpeace counts more than 160 attacks on oil tankers in that period, with some resulting in spills.

An interactive map of the data shows slicks along much of the UAE’s Gulf coast, through the Strait of Hormuz and down Oman’s coast, as far as Muscat.

Oil from tankers attacked in the Strait of Hormuz is washing onto UAE beaches and threatening dive sites along Oman's Musandam peninsula, layering an environmental crisis onto a Gulf tourism downturn now in its ninth month. Greenpeace and maritime tracking firm Kpler have identified more than 600 oil spills in and around the Gulf since the U.S.-Iran war began on Feb. 28, along with more than 160 attacks on oil tankers through Nov. 19. The most damaging incident for the UAE so far, a strike on the tanker Gem No. 2 in early October, produced a 22-mile slick and led authorities to close all public beaches in Ras Al Khaimah, Umm Al Quwain, and on Sharjah's Gulf coast on Oct. 23. Ras Al Khaimah reopened seven public and resort beaches on Nov. 11, including those at Anantara Mina Al Arab, InterContinental Ras Al Khaimah Mina Al Arab, and The Cove Rotana. The closures hit an emirate already short of visitors: Ras Al Khaimah hotel occupancy fell 16.4 points to 71.5% in October, and Dubai's fell to 68.4% from 84.9%. Experts quoted by Skift said there is no fixed number of days after clean-up when a beach becomes safe, and that heavily affected habitats could take decades to recover. With tanker strikes continuing, including an LNG carrier and two oil tankers that caught fire on Nov. 17, each new strike restarts the clock for resorts on the coasts nearest the strait.
