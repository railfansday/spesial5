Battery incidents are rising and airlines are tightening restrictions, with cabin crews the last line of defense. Is the industry doing enough to prevent the next fire, or simply getting better at putting them out?

The culprit was small enough to fit in a pocket, but the consequences were big enough to turn a Boeing 777 around over the Atlantic.

On September 17, a portable charger caught fire aboard KLM flight 735 from Amsterdam to Curaçao. The crew extinguished the flames and the jet returned safely to the Dutch capital. One person was injured and the incident led to knock-on cancellations, overnight accommodation for hundreds of passengers, and the plane being out of service until September 20.

An intriguing twist soon followed when Dutch media reported that the device allegedly belonged to Camiel Eurlings, KLM's former chief executive. The airline has declined to identify the passenger and Eurlings did not respond to Skift’s request for comment. On November 4, the Dutch Safety Board opened an independent investigation into the incident.

A portable charger fire aboard KLM flight 735 from Amsterdam to Curaçao on September 17 forced the Boeing 777 to turn back, injured one person, triggered cancellations, and kept the aircraft out of service until September 20. Dutch media reported the device allegedly belonged to former KLM CEO Camiel Eurlings; the airline declined to identify the passenger, and the Dutch Safety Board opened an investigation on November 4. The incident sits inside a sharply worsening trend. The UK Civil Aviation Authority estimates the average passenger now carries four lithium-powered devices, and its reports of passenger devices overheating rose 98% between 2024 and 2025. The FAA logged 97 lithium-battery incidents involving smoke, fire, or extreme heat in 2025, against 47 in 2017, and deputy director Paul Serafin said the agency has recently seen roughly two incidents a week on passenger aircraft. UL Standards & Engagement estimates an average cost of about $96,000 per event, with about 12% of incidents in its dataset resulting in a diversion or emergency landing. New ICAO provisions that took effect at the end of March cap passengers at two power banks and prohibit recharging them in flight, but implementation varies by jurisdiction and airlines can set stricter limits. Battery safety expert Paul Christensen said the airline industry needs to wake up as a matter of extreme urgency; IATA's Jonathan Jasper cautioned that most reports involve overheating rather than full thermal runaway.
