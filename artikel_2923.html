Giants midseason 2027 NFL mock draft

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

We're officially in the second half of the New York Giants 2026 season with eight games remaining this year.

The Giants were very active at the trade deadline, though while they went "big game hunting" they weren't able to land any impact players. They were hoping to add another difference maker before they got to the meat of their schedule, but it wasn't to be. The upside of that is that the Giants still have all draft capital that they came into the season with.

All this year we've been using the NFL draft as a lens through which to evaluate the state of the Giants as well as context for a chaotic season of college football. Now the College Football Playoffs are taking shape while the NFL playoff race is heating up.

The Giants are in the unfamiliar position of being in control of the NFC East and making a run to the playoffs, but this is still a good time to take stock of the Giants' wants and needs as we head into the home stretch.

I used the simulator from Pro Football and Sports Network to do this draft.

The Giants were hot after a defensive tackle at the trade deadline, chasing after every impact lineman they thought they could pry loose. It didn't work out, so now they're diving into a deep draft class to add talent and depth to their defensive line.

My choices here were between David Stone of Oklahoma and A'Mauri Washington from Oregon. Both were a bit disappointing to start the year, but have come on in recent weeks. I opted for Washington because I believe he fits what the Giants want in a tackle more closely than does stone. He's an enormous young man at 6-foot-3, 330 pounds, but also has legitimately freakish power and athleticism. Washington ranked fourth on Bruce Feldman's annual Freaks List in 2025 and moved up to third on the 2026 list. He not only has elite first step explosiveness, but also has rare long speed for his size and clocked over 20 mph in a sprint.

Washington is an inconsistent defender at this stage of his development, but he flashes the ability to be an absolute rock in the run game as well as a real factor as a pass rusher thanks to his mass, strength, and explosiveness.

He's similar to a young Dexter Lawrence in that his athletic tools are overwhelming in one-on-one situations, but his technique is the limiting factor at this stage of his development. His freakish strength could make him a force on the defensive line if he can become a technician.

Also considered: David Stone (iDL, Oklahoma), KJ Bolden (S, Georgia), Trey'Dez Green (TE, LSU), Jackson Harris (WR, LSU)

Note: I'd like to shout out the Dallas Cowboys for taking Florida RB Jadan Baugh one pick before me, saving the Giants from themselves. I fully believe that Baugh will be high on the Giants' draft board, and he'll be in heavy consideration for their first-round pick if he's on the board when they pick. I'm not sure John Harbaugh will be able to help himself, even if drafting a running back in the first never works out for the drafting team.

I've been consistent over the last year (plus) in viewing wide receiver as a significant need. The Giants only have two receivers under contract beyond this year — Malik Nabers and Malachi Fields. The Giants might prefer to run heavy personnel packages, but a good 11-personnel package is still important to any and every scheme in the NFL.

I chose Duff over Alabama's Ryan Coleman-Williams for three reasons. First, while Coleman-Williams is as much of an explosive threat as Nabers, he has struggled with consistency. Second, I want a second big-bodied outside receiver to play the X opposite Fields.

Secondly, Malik Nabers has been the best slot receiver in the NFL, and much better from the slot than out wide. The Giants need to build the kind of receiving corps that will allow him to play where he's so devastatingly effective.

And finally, Duff himself has the potential to be special in his own right. He has great size at 6-foot-6, 225 pounds (listed), and appears to have good explosiveness, long speed, and agility for his size. He excels at the catch point, with a catch rate over expected of 17.7% and a miniscule 1.3% drop rate.

Duff has an impressive 1,858 yards (105 catches, 17.6 per catch) and 16 touchdowns over the last two years, despite playing on a Rutgers team that's probably most charitably described as "unfortunate". It's exciting to think of what he could do in the same offense as Nabers, Fields, Isaiah Likely, and Jaxson Dart.

Also Considered: Reed Harris (WR, Arizona State), Ryan Coleman-Williams (WR, Alabama), Ty Benefield (S, LSU)

Back to the defensive side of the ball we go.

I was hunting for an offensive lineman or running back at this spot, but the board didn't fall my way. Instead, I picked up Alabama's versatile STAR to help take the Giants' defense to the next level.

Dennard Wilson has taken the Ravens 2.0 defensive model and remixed it with a healthy dose of "Ryan style" aggression. The "Ravens style" defense loves to use a versatile safety hybrid as a force player to dictate terms to the offense and adapt to different personnel packages and looks. The Giants don't really have that, but Hubbard brings an excellent blend of size (6-foot-2, 217 pounds) and athleticism (estimated 4.4 speed). He has plenty of experience in Alabama's sophisticated defense and playing against elite competition in the SEC, which should allow him to step in and contribute right away.

He brings great run defense, solid pass coverage, physicality, and ball-hawking ability. Hubbard is a tackling machine with 193 tackles to go with 9 interceptions and 10 passes defensed over the last 3 years.

Also considered: Vicari Swain (CB, South Carolina), LJ Martin (RB, BYU)
