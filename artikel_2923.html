Is there concern about Nick Bosa upon his return from injury?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The San Francisco 49ers defense struggled on Sunday against one of the best offenses in the league, as they gave up 40 points to the Dallas Cowboys.

Following the game, there were two main talking points about the defense: the cornerback struggles and the up-and-down pass rush. A lot of the pass rush focus centered on Nick Bosa, who has been a bit quieter statistically since returning from injury.

Bosa has yet to record a sack this season in four appearances, while having eight tackles. Is there any concern with the star pass rusher coming back from the nagging injury that kept him out for nearly two months?

"I see Nick working his way back," head coach Kyle Shanahan said on Monday. "I think that he has a number of plays that look just like Nick, and I think he's had a few plays that doesn't. I think Nick's ready to go, and I think he's definitely affecting the quarterback and doing some good things out there. But Nick's going to get better the more he gets back with us. He's taken a lot of time off with his injury.

"He had a lot of time off before that, going into Australia with his injury over the summer and everything like that, or from last year, so he hasn't gotten to play a lot of football. But I still see a guy out there who's a top pass rusher, who does a lot of good things. He's got to handle the majority of the chips, which doesn't give him as many opportunities on the edge. But I'm not worried about Nick. Nick's going to get better each week, and he'll eventually get there."

As a whole, the 49ers sacked Dak Prescott just once on Sunday after the Indianapolis Colts and Arizona Cardinals sacked him three and four times, respectively, over the two weeks prior. How did Shanahan see his unit as a whole?

"I thought we definitely rushed the passer better earlier on," Shanahan said. "Obviously, we didn't get any sacks, but I thought we affected the quarterback. I thought we hit him. I thought we wore down as the game went. I wouldn't have said it was a bad pass rush game by any means if I never watched the fourth quarter. But, by the time we got to the fourth quarter, I thought we had a chance, it was 38-19 with like nine-and-a-half minutes to go, and I thought we had a number of chances to get them off the field. And they stayed on for whatever the reason.

"Everyone had their moments in that, but then I thought we really died out and the pass rush disappeared. I thought all 11 did, but mainly our D-Line. A lot of them hold on it way too long. That was too much time with those wideouts running around. We've got to be able to finish better. We've got to clean some stuff up. I thought there were some times that we did rush them real well and Dak [Prescott] did a good job getting rid of it. Sometimes they had some chippers that we need to rush better on because we didn't get there at all. But for the whole part of the game, we've definitely got to do better."

When you look back on the film with Bosa's pass-rushing snaps, it's evident he's constantly getting double-teamed and chipped. That's more an indictment of the rest of the room than Bosa himself, as teams aren't fearing the pass rush elsewhere and instead are focusing game plans on San Francisco's star.

There have been a couple of seasons where Bosa's sack production hasn't told the full picture. Bosa had 69 pressures in 2024 with an elite 20 percent pass-rush win rate, but had only (I know, only!) nine sacks that season while playing 14 games.

In 2023, playing a full 17-game season, Bosa had 10.5 sacks, but an incredible 122 pressures with a 21.8 percent pass-rush win rate. Now, this season, in four games, Bosa has 13 pressures with a 16.7 percent pass-rush win rate despite the zero sacks. That's still a top-15 rate among edge rushers this year.

Bosa is still coming back from his injury, and the 49ers pass rush is still middling as a whole. Not one of their rotational defensive linemen has a pass-rush win rate over 12 percent outside of Bosa, which has allowed teams to chip him quite a bit.

Even with the doubles and the chips, Bosa still had five pressures on Sunday while getting his biggest workload of the year. Additionally, as I said in the three takeaways, yesterday's troubles felt more against the secondary than the pass rush as well.

There should be brighter days ahead for Bosa's sack production, but the underlying metrics and film are painting a more positive picture in his return from injury, at least from Sunday's game.
