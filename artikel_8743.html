Ryan ‘Koup’ Kuperus Shares How the Reason Outdoors is Saving Lives and Building a Community

The mission for The Reason Outdoors is to take veterans, first responders, and their children on outdoor adventures such as hunting or fishing as a positive experience that can help enhance their quality of life.

The organization was founded by three veterans, Ryan Kuperus, Tony Cowden, and Andrew Somplasky. Kuperus is also President of The Reason Outdoors, and he feels that what he does now benefits himself as much as it does the people they take on these adventures.

“I really take pride in seeing veterans and first responders and especially their kids harvest their first animal to see that joy and happiness. It’s just fantastic.”

The connections between the members of The Reason Outdoors team and the over 300 people they work with go beyond the benefits of hunting and fishing. They also understand the pressures that come with service, the search for a purpose when the uniform comes off, and the struggles that go along with them. Kuperus doesn’t need to question whether their efforts make a difference. They get told first-hand about the impact they have had.

“We have had five guys tell us, ‘If it wasn’t for this hunting trip or this fishing trip, I would have put a pistol in my mouth and pulled the trigger.”

Kuperus could have gone into farming like his father before him, but he felt the move for him was to join the military. He joined the Marines in August 2000. By the end of 2001, he was a part of the first deployment following the September 11th terrorist attacks. In 2003, the then 20-year-old was a squad leader during the invasion of Iraq, which he considered a career highlight.

“As a squad leader, that is a very respected position to be able to lead men in combat,” he said. “I had a guy in my squad who was 37 years old. That was pretty big for me.”

Throughout his 17-plus-year career, he would travel to Iraq, Japan, and the Philippines as well as Afghanistan. At one point in his career, he broke his femur and fractured his forehead in a Humvee rollover. Nonetheless, he rehabbed and eventually returned to action, including a return to Afghanistan.

Gunnery Sergeant Kuperus’s career ended via medical retirement in 2017. The Purple Heart and Navy Commendation Medal with Valor recipient was looking for another way to make a difference. While he was recovering from his rollover accident, he took up bow hunting and said he found healing while he was in the woods. He never let go of that connection and leaned into it even more after his career ended by going on organized hunts with other groups.

Kuperus recalled, “I had the opportunity to go on a couple of Wounded Warrior hunts and Purple Heart hunts and stuff and had a great time with some awesome organizations.”

Kuperus went on to start Hunting Carolinas, a part-time guide service. In 2022, Kuperus, Cowden, and Somplasky then started The Reason Outdoors. This wasn’t just another job for “Koup,” it was helping him as much as it was those they took on trips.

“We were off to the races,” he said. “I found healing in the outdoors, in nature, and through hunting.”

Kuperus found personal healing in the woods, but his experience is far from anecdotal. Science increasingly backs what veterans discover around the campfire. A pilot study published in 2013 found that combat veterans suffering from post-traumatic stress disorder experienced noticeable reductions in stress and PTSD symptoms after participating in group-based outdoor recreation programs. There are physical benefits of the outdoors as well. A report in 2018 confirmed that increased exposure to natural outdoor environments was statistically associated with significant reductions in diastolic blood pressure, heart rate, and salivary cortisol levels.

Kuperus and his teammates not only take their clients out on trips. They teach them the skills needed for harvesting so they can apply them later on. Cowden provides workouts on his social media for others to use, and they are actively engaged with their followers. They are looking to provide more than a one-off experience. They have been planting the seeds for a community, and that community is growing. Kuperus sees the results of their efforts for himself every time he looks at a fellow veteran or first responder that they go out with.

Kuperus stated, “Somebody there was feeling down before that hunt. Now they’ve expanded their network. The camaraderie brought the light back to their eyes, so to speak.”

Kuperus and everyone with The Reason Outdoors will continue their mission because they know how important and supportive their work can be. He hopes that other veterans will find their own ways to continue serving or reach out for help if they are in a dark place like he and others he knows have been in. Focusing on mental, physical, and spiritual fitness are key for veterans and their families, and Kuperus advises that if any or all of those aspects are not optimal, addressing it in a positive way is crucial.

“That’s probably the hardest thing for a veteran to do is to admit how dark a path they’re on. So, don’t let yourself get to that point. Start by talking to somebody and get it out. That alone is a start and helps a lot.”
