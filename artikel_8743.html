The Runner’s World Guide to Recovery

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Your guide to smarter recovery—from managing soreness and fatigue to easing back into running after a race, offseason, or injury.

What you do to recover between runs matters just as much as the miles themselves. But knowing what your body needs isn’t always straightforward. Should you head out for an easy run or take the day off? Is that soreness normal, or a sign of an injury about to derail everything? And which recovery tools are actually worth the hype?

This guide helps you navigate those decisions, starting with the fundamentals. Plus, we’ll help you choose a training plan to ease back into running after a race, your offseason, or an injury (both minor and burdensome).

Whether you’re bouncing back from a tough workout or gearing up for your next training block, start planning your recovery here.

Recovery doesn’t have to be complicated, but it does require giving the basics as much attention as your workouts. Easy runs, rest days, postrun fueling, and sleep all help your body manage the work you’re putting in. That means keeping most of your miles at a pace where you can comfortably hold a conversation, rather than turning every run into another hard effort.

It also means taking at least one full day off from training each week—and more if you’re new to running or need extra recovery.

What you do after a run matters, too: Carbohydrates replenish energy stores, protein supports muscle repair, and sufficient sleep gives your body time to recover and rebuild.
