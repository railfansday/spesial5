If Staying in Nvidia Stock Scares You, You Might Want To Consider This ETF

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Nvidia's troubles may suggest that ETF investing is more suitable for some investors.

The controversy surrounding Nvidia’s leadership is undoubtedly a sucker punch to many investors. Its dominance in AI chips and rapid growth made it appear unstoppable, attracting numerous investors. Now, these same investors may have become more concerned about the stock as scandals have ended co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's leadership.

However, AI is likely here to stay, a factor that may leave scared investors reluctant to stop investing in chip stocks altogether. Fortunately, the ETF world offers a solution that minimizes exposure to Nvidia while allowing investors to still profit from the AI trade.

Instead of Nvidia, risk-averse investors can turn to the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX -4.93%). SOXX is a semiconductor industry ETF that invests in 30 stocks across the sector, offering easy diversification. Moreover, it caps the weight of any one stock in the fund at 8%.

Consequently, its Nvidia exposure is only around 7.1%. For those concerned about Nvidia, this may compare favorably to its most prominent competitor, the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH -4.25%). The VanEck ETF sets its cap at 20% and, as a result, holds about 18% in Nvidia.

Additionally, Nvidia is the third-largest holding in SOXX, not the first. Instead, it holds larger stakes in both AMD and Broadcom, with AMD accounting for 10.1% of its holdings and Broadcom making up about 8.3%. That approach arguably makes SOXX a trade-off between tactical concentration and low-cost breadth.

Furthermore, SOXX achieves this with minimal effect on returns. Over the last 10 years, average total returns exceed 32% annually. In comparison, the iShares Core S&P 500 ETF (IVV -0.93%) averaged 14.9% per year over the same period, indicating that SOXX typically outperforms the market.

Also, if measured since the fund's inception in 2001, total returns are almost 15% yearly. Since that includes the 2008-2009 financial crisis, it suggests to investors that they can still earn positive returns over the long term no matter what happens in the market.

To achieve such gains, investors pay an annual management fee of 0.33%, which is low or on par with other semiconductor-focused ETFs. That means for every $10,000 investment, shareholders pay the fund only $33 in fees. That makes it an economical way to safely benefit from the growth of the chip industry with far less risk and with far less time commitment than is required to properly manage individual stocks.

Given the fund's track record and comparatively low fees, SOXX may be an excellent choice for chip-stock investors who have been burned by Nvidia's recent price action.

Indeed, owning a stock like Nvidia carries both reward and risk, and some investors may feel that the growth potential is worth the risk of holding an individual stock.

However, for investors with less risk tolerance, SOXX offers the potential for market-beating returns with considerably less direct exposure to a stock like Nvidia. When also considering the low management fees, such an investment may be a more suitable choice for many chip stock investors.
