$200 a Month in Realty Income Could Generate $150 in Monthly Dividends by 2036

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Steady investment in this REIT's shares could turn it into a three-figure monthly income stream in 10 years.

By Thomas Niel – Nov 26, 2026 at 5:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Realty Income ›

Besides its reputation as one of the best monthly dividend stocks, Realty Income (O +1.18%) often increases its dividend. In the recent past, the real estate investment trust (REIT) has raised its monthly payout quarterly.

Although these payout increases are relatively slight, they do provide a bit of a boost if one's objective is to build a steady income-generating machine out of a Realty Income position. For instance, just investing $200 per month in this REIT, starting now, could create an asset that generates $150 in monthly dividend income by 2036.

Currently, Realty Income pays a monthly dividend of $0.274 per share. Annualized, that's about $3.29 in cash dividends. With this REIT recently trading for about $60 per share, this works out to a forward annual dividend yield of about 5.5%.

Let's say you invested $200 per month in Realty Income. Making several assumptions, such as 1% annualized share price appreciation, in line with historic performance, plus 3.5% average annual dividend growth, after 10 years you would own about 515 shares, worth roughly $30,900. With estimated monthly dividends of $0.30 per share by year 10, this position would generate an estimated $154.50 per month in dividend income.

The good news about this strategy is the high likelihood that Realty Income's slow-and-steady growth trend will persist for yet another decade. Since going public in 1994, Realty Income has raised its monthly dividend 117 times. As it sticks to its strategy of geographic and property diversification, the REIT appears well-positioned to maintain its dividend growth streak.

The key downside of this plan is the opportunity cost. Realty Income may sport a high yield, but it has underperformed in terms of share price growth relative to the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%)index. Even if you are willing to trade potential returns for lower volatility, consider weighing it against other options, such as dollar-cost averaging into dividend-focused index funds.
