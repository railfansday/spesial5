He played for West Brom & Bolton Wanderers - Then he was jailed over match-fixing plot

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

For 16 years, Delroy Facey was a familiar face in the EFL, but shortly after his playing career came to an end, shocking revelations were made about him which ended up with the former player in prison.

Over the course of a career that lasted for almost two decades, Delroy Facey made over 450 appearances in the senior game and was largely considered a stalwart player of the EFL's three divisions. He also represented Grenada twelve times at an international level, and even made ten Premier League appearances for Bolton Wanderers.

But shortly after his career ended, it became clear that he had been involved in a match-fixing scandal which tarnished his entire legacy as a player, and which resulted in him being sent to prison for two and a half years, along with a number of others in a scandal which had involved the National Crime Agency, the Gambling Commission and the Football Association, following two separate newspaper investigations.

Born in Huddersfield in 1980, Delroy Facey joined his hometown team as an apprentice at 16-years-old and signed his first senior contract with the club the following year. By the 1998-99 season, he was breaking into their first team.

His playing career became somewhat nomadic. In total, he played for ten different clubs and spent time on loan at three others, meaning that, although he ended up making 451 senior appearances in the EFL and Premier League - where he played ten times for Bolton Wanderers between 2002 and 2004 - between 1997 and 2012, the 85 times he played for Huddersfield was the most that he played for any one club.

The only silverware of his career came at the end of the 2009-10 season. He'd joined Notts County in 2008 and became something of a fan favourite, and in 2010 he was part of their team which won the League Two title, although he was released by the club at the end of that season.

He also found a route international game as a result of his success at club level. He qualified to play for Grenada, Trinidad & Tobago and Jamaica through his parents and was called up to the Grenada squad in June 2009. Between then and 2011, he made twelve appearances for the Grenada national team.

The scandal broke in December 2013. Separate investigations carried out by The Sunday Times and The Sun on Sunday had revealed a spot-fixing scandal within the EFL, leading to the arrest of six people, including Facey. Spot-fixing - the practice of gambling on any aspect of a match apart from the final result - had become a signficant issue in line with the growth of online gambling, which allowed a wider range of options for betting than had ever been seen before.

It was reported that Sam Sodje, a former player for Reading and Portsmouth, claimed that he could arrange for footballers in the Football League Championship to get themselves booked in exchange for cash payments. It was also reported that Sodje "was filmed describing how he had twice punched an opponent, Oldham Athletic's Jose Baxter, while playing for Portsmouth in a League One match last February as he sought to receive a red card in exchange for £70,000."

Facey was charged with conspiracy to bribe alongside Moses Swaibu, a non-league player who'd played for a number of clubs including Lincoln City and Bromley. The case went to trial in the middle of April 2015. The court heard that Facey had told a footballer at Hyde FC that he could make some "easy money" by fixing the result of a match, and had also told a contact that some Football Conference - now the National League - teams would "do" a game in return for payment.

And on the 29th April 2015, the verdict came in. Delroy Facey was convicted of conspiracy to bribe and sentenced to two and a half years in prison. The BBC reported at the time that, after the sentencing, "Andy Young, from the CPS, said Facey had acted as the "middleman", sourcing players who would be willing to fix matches." Swaibu was also convicted and sentenced to 16 months in prison.

The temptation to lower division and non-league footballers is clear. The very nature of spot-fixing can make it difficult to prove, and the very existence of it is a direct result of the proliferation of online gambling in the first decade of the 21st century. The FA tightened their rules regarding players and gambling in August 2014, but for Delroy Facey the damage had been done. His reputation was in tatters, with the memories of him as a player fundamentally tarnished by the knowledge that he'd been involved in such disgraceful behaviour at the time.
