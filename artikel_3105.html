Who Will Replace Jensen Huang? Here's What Prediction Markets Think.

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Jensen Huang has resigned as CEO of Nvidia.

Throughout the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution, Jensen Huang has been synonymous with Nvidia (NVDA -4.34%). As the company's co-founder and longtime CEO, Huang helped transform Nvidia from a graphics chip manufacturer into a dominant supplier of AI computing infrastructure.

Recent reports concerning Huang's personal relationships and potential exposure to foreign intelligence have prompted an independent board investigation and raised questions about his future. Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on Nov. 12. As investors focus on leadership succession plans, prediction market platform Kalshi offers an interesting glimpse into who might lead Nvidia next.

According to Kalshi’s trading odds, Ian Buck is currently in the lead, with an implied probability of approximately 36% of becoming Nvidia's next CEO. Buck joined Nvidia in 2004 and helped develop CUDA, the software platform that allows developers to program GPUs for applications beyond graphics processing.

That's particularly relevant because hyperscalers like Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, and Oracle account for most of the investments in AI infrastructure. During Nvidia's fiscal second quarter, hyperscalers accounted for $48.7 billion of the company's total data center revenue.

Nvidia's chief financial officer (CFO), Colette Kress, currently has a 19% chance to emerge as Nvidia's next CEO. For now, Kress has been named interim CEO.

Kalshi traders are also considering candidates from outside Nvidia. Rene Haas, CEO of semiconductor designer Arm Holdings, currently has 10% odds. Haas previously spent seven years at Nvidia, giving him familiarity with the company's operations and semiconductor strategy. His experience running Arm, whose chip processor designs are used across various devices, makes him a logical candidate.

Former Apple CEO Tim Cook is also among the possibilities, with recent odds around 4%. Of note, Cook's name may only be among the top ranks because President Trump floated the idea of Nvidia appointing him as its CEO.

While Cook's experience managing Apple's global supply chain and navigating international business relationships could be valuable, his candidacy seems speculative, as there's no confirmed indication Nvidia is pursuing him.

Prediction markets can offer useful insights into investor expectations, but they are hardly infallible. Kalshi's probabilities change in real time as they reflect trading activity rather than inside knowledge. Limited liquidity in niche markets can also cause outsize swings in the odds.

Nvidia's next CEO will inherit a business with enormous competitive advantages. The company's Blackwell and Vera Rubin computing platforms, proprietary networking technology, and CUDA software ecosystem have established robust barriers to competition.

These assets are not going to disappear because of a leadership change. Ultimately, investors should pay more attention to Nvidia's ability to execute its technology roadmap than to the latest fluctuations in prediction market odds or rumors.
