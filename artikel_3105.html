Woodward: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Shifting production to Spartanburg and closing two sites targets $50 million in annual margin savings, a cost lever that could matter more than top-line growth into 2028.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:47PM EST

On its fourth quarter of 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 18, 2026, Woodward (WWD +1.64%) announced full-year net sales of $4.4 billion, up 22% year over year. Deep backlogs in commercial aviation alongside demand for data center power generation give the company multiyear revenue visibility across both operating segments. Three things management said stand out.

Woodward is closing two facilities and shifting production to higher-performing sites to improve operational productivity. Management expects these work transfers and footprint consolidations to deliver a $50 million annual run-rate margin benefit starting in fiscal 2028.

Demand for maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) services on modern narrow-body aircraft engines reached a key milestone. As initial build rates expand and legacy fleets age out, growth in service orders for current-generation engines provides a steady stream of recurring aftermarket revenue.

Woodward finished winding down its China On-Highway bus and truck fuel systems business, eliminating an unprofitable drag on the segment. The industrial division now focuses on high-margin power generation, data center infrastructure, and marine transportation.

Commercial aircraft production rates and data center infrastructure demand continue to drive higher orders across key end markets.

Long-term maintenance contracts and a lean balance sheet protect earnings power while broader production rates expand. Management is tracking factory automation milestones and work transfer schedules to drive structural cost reductions into 2028.
