Prediction: Nvidia Will Hit a New All-Time High in 2027 (Hint: Watch This Metric)

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Nvidia has lost nearly 37% of its market value in recent weeks, but an epic recovery could be in store next year.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 20, 2026 at 8:30AM EST

It's been a turbulent November for Nvidia (NVDA -3.81%) as the company deals with the unexpected resignation of founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang. Uncertainty surrounding the company's leadership transition, investigations involving ties to China, and questions about its artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure partnerships are weighing heavily on investor sentiment.

Nevertheless, Nvidia's latest financial results suggest the company's business is expanding at a record pace. During its fiscal third quarter (ended Oct. 25), Nvidia generated $115.8 billion in revenue, representing 103% year-over-year growth. Meanwhile, adjusted earnings per share (EPS) climbed 111% to $2.68. Despite this impressive performance, Nvidia stock is trading roughly 37% below its all-time high.

With that said, one financial metric could ultimately drive the stock to new highs: earnings per share (EPS). Nvidia has two opportunities to expand EPS over the coming year, combining organic profit growth with an enormous share repurchase program.

Nvidia's first opportunity to increase EPS comes directly from its underlying business. The company is a key beneficiary of AI-driven demand from hyperscalers expanding their data center build-outs. Nvidia's data center division generated $107.6 billion in revenue during the third quarter, increasing 110% year over year.

What's particularly impressive is Nvidia's ability to convert its top-line growth into substantial profits. During the third quarter, the company reported a gross margin of 74.8%. This reflects the pricing power associated with Nvidia's GPUs, networking products, and CUDA software ecosystem. These economics allow Nvidia to generate enormous profits alongside an accelerating revenue base.

Although management expects gross margin to moderate to between 71% and 72% in the fourth quarter, it anticipates a recovery to between 72% and 73% in fiscal 2028. More importantly, interim CEO Colette Kress raised the company's fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to approximately 75% year over year, up from its previous projection of 70%.

Even if gross margin remains below its peak, Nvidia can still produce higher earnings through increased sales volumes and operating leverage. In other words, as revenue grows, certain operating expenses may increase at a slower rate relative to sales. This would allow additional revenue to contribute disproportionately to operating profits. The idea here is that Nvidia doesn't necessarily need to maintain record gross margins to achieve record earnings.

Nvidia's second earnings catalyst is its share repurchase authorization. In September, Nvidia's board of directors approved a $150 billion increase to its existing repurchase program. As of the end of the fiscal third quarter, Nvidia had $227.3 billion remaining under its authorization.

Management affirmed its intention to execute the remaining portion of the repurchase authorization by the end of fiscal 2028. This makes stock buybacks an important, albeit underrated, component of Nvidia's capital allocation strategy. The significance of Nvidia's share repurchases becomes clearer once you understand how EPS is calculated.

Earnings per share equals a company's net income divided by its shares outstanding. When Nvidia repurchases and retires shares, the company effectively reduces the denominator in that equation. This means EPS can increase even if net income remains unchanged.

For example, suppose a company generates $100 billion in annual net income across 25 billion shares. That would translate into EPS of $4.00. If the same company reduced its share count by 5%, while net income remained unchanged, EPS would increase to $4.21 without any additional revenue or profit growth.

Stock prices are influenced by two interconnected variables: earnings per share and the valuation multiple investors are willing to pay for those earnings. A company's share price can rise even when its price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio remains unchanged, provided EPS increases. This unique relationship makes the mechanics around Nvidia's potential earnings expansion particularly interesting.

Nvidia's combination of projected revenue growth, compounding profitability, and aggressive share repurchases creates a compelling outlook around next year's EPS. These catalysts provide the foundation for a bounceback in Nvidia stock even without assuming a dramatic increase in the company's valuation multiple.

I think Nvidia stock could soar next year, driven by accelerating EPS growth rather than speculative enthusiasm. Against this backdrop, investors should monitor Nvidia's net income growth and changes in shares outstanding over the coming quarters. Taken together, these metrics could reveal whether Nvidia's financial performance is strong enough to overcome its recent challenges and propel the stock to new highs.
