Project Jupiter report returns online after developers’ legal threat

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Environmental groups stand by their findings on the $165bn Oracle and OpenAI data centre in New Mexico.

A rendering of the planned Project Jupiter data centre campus in Santa Teresa, New Mexico, updated August 2026

The New Mexico Environmental Law Centre and the Tech Initiative have put their investigative report on Project Jupiter back online. A legal threat from a law firm tied to the data centre’s developers had kept it offline for seven weeks.

The two groups first published the report on September 24. A letter demanding its retraction arrived within 24 hours, and the report came down while lawyers checked the claims.

An updated version went back up on November 12. After a review with outside advisers, the groups said in a statement that they stand by what the report says and the sources it rests on.

Project Jupiter is a roughly 1,400-acre data centre campus rising in Santa Teresa, a border town in Doña Ana County. Oracle will lease the site for 18 years, and OpenAI will be its main customer.

The campus is one of nine sites in Stargate, the Trump-backed $500bn AI venture that has sent data centre energy demand soaring.

Doña Ana County commissioners approved up to $165bn in industrial revenue bonds for the project in September 2025. The report calls the sum record-setting, and says backroom political dealing shaped the vote.

County officials had signed NDAs. A firm run by a former developer executive, meanwhile, won a state contract that let it scan millions of environmental records while a key permit was under review.

If built, the campus could become New Mexico’s top corporate polluter, the report finds. Its emissions could exceed those of Albuquerque, Las Cruces, and Santa Fe combined. A loophole written into state law in 2025 also frees the project from the state’s emission cuts until at least 2045.

The developers have changed their power plans twice. Gas turbines gave way to a methane-powered fuel cell microgrid, and a redesign filed in October would cut annual emissions to 5.7 million tons. Even then, the report notes, the microgrid would emit more than PNM, the state’s largest electric utility.

Water is the sharpest local concern. The site sits in a drought-hit basin where a US Supreme Court settlement already forces cuts in groundwater pumping. Commissioners believed the campus would use an average of 20,000 gallons of water a day, the report says. Developers later told state officials the original turbine design needed an extra 1 million gallons daily.

Oracle pushed back in a January statement. Drinking water use is capped at 20,000 gallons a day, the company said, and cooling water comes from non-potable commercial sources.

The report also questions what the county gets back. Instead of property taxes, the developers will pay $360m over 30 years. Their only binding jobs pledge is 750 full-time and 50 part-time roles within three years of opening. They have already missed two required job reports, and the county issued a breach notice on November 11.

“The development of Project Jupiter will serve the few and sacrifice the many, but its development is not inevitable, and community opposition works,” said Maslyn Locke, senior staff attorney at the law centre.

The courts have pulled the project both ways. In August, the state Supreme Court paused the air permit process and halted pumping from a construction well. In September, it rejected both challenges and let permitting resume, Source NM reported.

New Mexico’s attorney general has since opposed the federal permit for the gas pipeline that would feed the microgrid. His office is also probing allegedly fraudulent letters sent in support of the air permit. A hearing set for December 7 was vacated this month after the developers revised their application. No new date has been set.

The report also lands amid a wider backlash. US voters backed nearly all of the roughly 40 ballot measures to curb data centres this November, Route Fifty reported. The industry’s appetite for grid capacity and water is stirring opposition in Europe too. Two New Mexico counties have passed moratoriums on future data centres.

For now, the revised air permit application sits with the Environment Department. Phase one of the project, including its microgrid, is due to finish in late 2028.
