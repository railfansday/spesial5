Nvidia and Jensen Huang sued by shareholders over alleged Chinese spy affair

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The class-action complaint seeks unspecified damages for investors who bought Nvidia stock between Oct. 9 and Nov. 8

David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images

Nvidia $NVDA and CEO Jensen Huang face a shareholder lawsuit alleging they misled investors regarding Huang's affair with a woman believed to be an agent of Chinese intelligence, according to The Associated Press.

The complaint, which retail investor Mark Kistenmacher submitted to a California court on Monday, alleges that the company and Jensen Huang never revealed the affair, according to The Associated Press. It also accuses them of making false statements about Nvidia's relationship with the company of Alice Huang, the woman at the center of the reports. The complaint says this posed a "material risk" to Nvidia's reputation and business prospects.

The suit is meant to cover all purchasers of Nvidia stock between Oct. 9, the day news of the affair surfaced, and Nov. 8, the day Nvidia announced it was looking into reports that Jensen Huang was the "target of a foreign intelligence effort," according to The Associated Press. It demands a jury trial and damages in an amount it does not specify.

"As a result of Defendants' wrongful acts and omissions, and the precipitous decline in the market value of the Company's securities, Plaintiff and other Class members have suffered significant losses and damages," the complaint said, according to The Associated Press. It adds that investors would not have bought Nvidia stock at "artificially inflated prices" had they known the truth.

The suit was filed in federal court in San Jose, California, by the Rosen Law Firm, according to CBS News.

Nvidia stock fell $7.97 on Monday, erasing about $190 billion in market value. The stock was down before the opening bell on Tuesday.

Jensen Huang has acknowledged the affair and apologized, according to The Associated Press. He told Nvidia employees in a message released Sunday that he had been "unfaithful" to his wife, according to CBS News.

The New York Times reported that American intelligence agencies had concluded Alice Huang, a onetime executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed International, was collecting information on behalf of the Chinese government, according to The Associated Press. Megaspeed purchased Nvidia chips valued at over $4.8 billion during the last two years, according to The New York Times. The two Huangs are unrelated.

President Donald Trump said Monday that Jensen Huang told him he did not know the woman was an alleged spy. Trump said he believes Huang and that the FBI is reviewing the issue.

The Justice Department is conducting a criminal probe of Megaspeed, which is suspected of illegally funneling Nvidia AI chips to China. China's Foreign Ministry has called the allegation against Alice Huang unsubstantiated. Nvidia's board is conducting its own scrutiny of the affair.
