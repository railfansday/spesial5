Sen. Cantwell said Lutnick failed to stop U.S. AI chip exports to China

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The top Democrat on the Senate commerce committee urged the Commerce secretary to enforce export controls before a planned meeting with Xi Jinping

Sen. Maria Cantwell, a Washington Democrat and the top Democrat on the Senate Commerce Committee, wrote to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Sunday that under his leadership the Commerce Department has not stopped China from getting hold of the most sophisticated American AI chips, according to CNBC.

Cantwell pressed Lutnick to put export controls into force and halt shipments of AI chips to barred companies, according to CNBC. She sent the letter ahead of a planned meeting, set for later this week in China, in which U.S. officials will meet Chinese President Xi Jinping at the margins of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation gathering.

"The Department of Commerce has resisted efforts to restrict companies flagged as national security threats, weakened safeguards, and left open pathways for the PRC to train advanced AI models using sensitive American technology," Cantwell wrote, according to CNBC. "These failures to carry out the law help give away America's technology advantage in AI."

She called on Lutnick to use his authority under the 2018 Export Control Reform Act, a law that grants the Commerce Department wide discretion to carry out export controls and safeguard sensitive American technology.

Cantwell also raised possible conflicts of interest tied to the finances of Lutnick's sons, who control Cantor Fitzgerald. She said the firm held $1.2 billion in Nvidia $NVDA stock when Lutnick was nominated. She cited the Trump administration's 25% fee on certain Nvidia chips sold to China as a potential "perverse financial incentive to approve exports of sensitive American technology."

"America's national security and AI leadership cannot be compromised by family business interests or a policy that generates federal revenue from exporting technology critical to our competitive edge," Cantwell wrote.

The Commerce Department had not responded to a request for comment on Monday, according to CNBC.

Lutnick has agreed to appear before the Senate Banking Committee to answer questions about Nvidia's chip exports to China. Committee Chair Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, asked Jensen Huang to testify on Dec. 3 and Lutnick on Dec. 10. Huang stepped down as Nvidia CEO last week and has acknowledged a relationship with a former Megaspeed executive suspected of spying for China.

That executive, Alice Huang, worked at the Singapore-based company, which is accused of illegally shipping export-controlled chips into China, according to CNBC. Lutnick has drawn criticism for dragging his feet on limiting chip sales to China, and for his close personal ties to Jensen Huang, a vocal opponent of export controls.

Lawmakers in both parties have increased pressure on Commerce officials since The New York Times reported on Nov. 9 that the department may have dragged its feet on a probe of Megaspeed.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren, a Massachusetts Democrat, and Sen. Chris Coons, a Delaware Democrat, sent a letter on Wednesday to Lutnick, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller. It told them to hold onto every document and message connected with Nvidia’s shipments of advanced AI chips, whether bound for China or for entities that might reroute them there. It sets a Nov. 17 deadline for the three officials to confirm that preservation directives have gone out.
