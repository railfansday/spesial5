Commerce media networks: 6 questions agencies should ask before investing

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Commerce media networks (CMNs) use first-party data, media, and audience relationships across platforms to help brands reach new groups and measure outcomes across the shopping journey. Retail media networks (RMNs) are a familiar, retail-focused part of this broader landscape, and many agencies still use RMN as shorthand for the category.

“The biggest benefit of a commerce media network is that it connects the brand to their consumers at that moment of purchase when there is already intent,” said Deb Bhattacherjee, industry manager of agency partnerships at DoorDash.

As the number of commerce media networks expands and agencies play a larger role in planning and executing brand campaigns, evaluating potential partners requires looking beyond the scale of the audience and available formats to determine which networks can deliver significant growth.

Here are six questions agencies should ask when assessing the next generation of network options.

A commerce network’s greatest opportunity lies in influencing what shoppers buy, even without a specific product or brand in mind. It’s an area where DoorDash Ads shines, Bhattacherjee said.

“About two-thirds of DoorDash users[1] haven’t decided what they’re going to order,” she said. “That offers an ideal moment for brands to use storytelling to help someone discover a brand or product they may not otherwise have purchased.”

For example, a stretched parent who opens the app to order art supplies for a class project may also remember they need beauty essentials for an upcoming business trip, then add on a fun dessert for that evening — all additional purchase occasions they hadn’t originally planned.

“You want to make sure the platform is friendly for discoverability across the browsing and purchasing journey, inviting shoppers to consider your brand when they might not have originally done so both in the moment and for purchases down the road,” Bhattacherjee said.

Given the plethora of options, the question is whether the network is contributing to the existing media plan or able to offer distinct, incremental reach. Agencies should take into account not only the scale of a network’s audience but also whether it reaches unique consumers their clients aren’t already engaging through other channels.

DoorDash combines broad platform scale with the potential for incremental reach. At year-end 2025, it reported more than 56 million monthly active users[2], more than half of which are estimated to be millennials and Gen Z[3]. As of June 2026, across DoorDash, Wolt, and Deliveroo, its platforms support more than 400,000 advertisers. DoorDash also cited a June 2026 LiveRamp analysis in which more than 80% of consumers reached through its campaigns were new to advertisers’ customer bases, suggesting the platform can help brands reach beyond their existing customers into a differentiated audience.

Today’s marketer needs to prove the value of every dollar, making incremental return on ad spend (iROAS) more critical than ever. Yet according to a report from eMarketer and TransUnion, just 52% of brand and agency marketers measure their campaigns with incrementality testing.

While closed-loop attribution can link ad exposure to purchases, incrementality testing goes further by identifying sales that only happened because of the advertising. That distinction helps agencies demonstrate the true contribution of their campaigns.

Bhattacherjee said agencies should look for partners that are working toward a standardized, transparent approach to measuring incrementality. “We pursued Incrementality Certification from the Alliance for Audited Media because standardization is essential to building trust in measurement. Independent review helps establish a clear, credible foundation for how incrementality is evaluated, so agencies can better understand how metrics are calculated and what’s included. It also gives us a stronger basis for building shared learning frameworks with agency partners across first- and third-party measurement, and clean room measurement where feasible.”

Even the most promising media opportunity can lose its appeal if activating and managing campaigns adds unnecessary complexity, which is why a network should integrate into existing processes and buying strategies.

Flexibility is essential because different agencies and brands have different requirements. A network might offer managed-service options, a self-serve platform that allows buyers to directly activate and manage campaigns, or demand-side platform (DSP) that support programmatic buying. API connectivity with third-party commerce media platforms such as Pacvue and Skai can also simplify campaign management by allowing agencies to work within tools they already use.

The goal is to reduce the operational burden so agency teams can spend more time optimizing performance and developing strategy.

Current capabilities are only part of the equation. Agencies should also consider how a network is investing in technologies that can improve both the consumer shopping experience and streamline campaign management to deliver stronger results over time.

Bhattacherjee pointed to DoorDash’s AI assistant, which she said among consumers who placed grocery orders both with and without using Ask DoorDash, their average Ask grocery order had nearly 50% higher basket value than their average non-Ask grocery order, according to orders placed in August through early September 2026.

On the advertising side, DoorDash Ads continues to expand its automated bidding capabilities on sponsored products, using machine learning to optimize bids in real time. Newer enhancements allow advertisers to set minimum ROAS targets and optimize for sales while maintaining control over their campaign strategies — leading to averages of +30% attributed sales and +20% ROAS, according to internal DoorDash Ads data[4].

Agencies should also prioritize having a voice in how those tools evolve. Bhattacherjee shared that her team recently worked with an agency representing a major CPG brand that wanted to learn more about its auto-bidding solution. During the call, the agency identified what was working and suggested potential improvements, feedback that DoorDash Ads brought directly to its engineering team.

“With agencies’ eyes on our solutions, we can build our AI optimizations together to ensure they are user-friendly and easy for them to adopt, while, of course, driving performance and connecting back to broader measurement goals,” Bhattacherjee said.

Technology can make campaign execution easier, but agencies also need partners who bring strategic thinking to the table. That means looking for networks that offer proactive recommendations and opportunities for joint business planning, rather than just providing access to advertising inventory.

Bhattacherjee said the most effective partnerships allow a commerce media network to function as an extension of the agency’s team, combining proprietary insights with the agency’s understanding of the brand and its goals.

“DoorDash makes fantastic insights available, from audience-level to retailer-level data, but our clients appreciate that we synthesize that data and collaborate to convert those insights into storytelling moments,” Bhattacherjee said. “Technology alone isn’t the answer anymore, with the human touch becoming more valuable than ever. We see that agencies prioritize access to a team equipped to bring big ideas and brainstorm together.”
