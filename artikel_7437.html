Meta expands content protection to Instagram

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Let Social Media Today's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The company is expanding its Facebook content protection systems, which use automatic detection features and alert creators when people repost their content.

Meta is expanding its content protection tools to Instagram, which will alert creators when other users repost their content, and enable them to take action on IP violations.

Meta added content protection on Facebook last year, which enables creators to activate protections for their original posts. Meta’s system then scans uploads as they come in, and alerts users to any copies of their content.

Now, the feature is coming to Instagram, with eligible creators able to enroll and protect their future uploads.

As per Instagram: “For eligible creators who enroll, content protection is a tool that automatically looks out for visual matches of your original videos, alerts you when we find a match across Instagram and Facebook, and lets you decide what happens next.”

That could offer peace of mind for creators who often see their content copied by aggregators and spammers.

Though it will be interesting to see how the system handles artificial intelligence-generated copies, which have become a much bigger issue of late.

With generative AI, users can easily create a replicant, or re-skinned version, of an original video in a completely different setting, or with completely different characters. As highlighted by the BBC News, one of the most popular examples of this trend has seen users creating alternate versions of the “Hotel Lobby” video by musicians Quavo and Takeoff.

Last month, Instagram Chief Adam Mosseri said that many creators have raised this trend as a concern, and added that he was also “very worried” about it.

Though he said that taking action will be tricky.

As Mosseri posted on Threads: “We are actively working out what to do, it’s just a tricky problem. We already let people make copyright claims against content, and take stolen copyrighted material. That’s not enough though, as assume many comedians and dancers haven’t gone through the trouble to copyright their material. We need a way to help those creatives without letting bad actors make more fake claims (happens all the time unfortunately).”

Instagram is experimenting with the addition of transcript detection for this exact purpose, which will alert creators when it detects a video that matches the spoken words in their content, even if the visuals have been altered by AI.

Instagram said that it’s testing this element with a small group of creators.

AI replicants will likely become a bigger problem moving forward, as more viral videos are re-framed, re-cast and re-created. Maybe, through expanded reporting, and new tactics like transcript detection, as well as the resulting copyright claims from those processes, Meta will be able to gain more training data to enhance its detection systems.

Though it’s unclear how keen Meta will actually be keen to crack down on AI use, given that its business has gone all-in on AI.
