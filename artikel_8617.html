Pool your money with coworkers, friends, or siblings for one group gift instead of each buying a small present. Have one person choose it, set a share that fits the tightest budget in the group, and collect everyone’s money through a payment app before buying. Then give it with one card everyone signs.

Six coworkers who each spend $25 on a baby gift hand the new parents six small presents. Pooled, the same $150 buys one registry item that costs six times as much as each small present.

Together, a group can afford a gift none of its members would buy alone, like good luggage for a retiring coworker or a family photo session for grandparents, while each person pays only a share of the price.

A group gift works best when one person runs it and collects every share before buying anything. An organizer who buys first and collects later can spend weeks chasing repayment, or end up covering a share or two.

If you’re organizing, check for a registry or ask someone close to the recipient what they’d like, then set a fixed share or a suggested range, such as $20 to $30. Make it an amount the person with the least to spare can manage, keep what each person gives private, and tell everyone they can sign the card without chipping in.

Collect every share through one payment app by a deadline a few days before you need to shop. If the group doesn’t all use the same app, start a Cash App pool, a fund people pay into through a link you share. Anyone who doesn’t live in New York can chip in with Apple Pay or Google Pay, no Cash App account needed. Everyone in a pool can see what each person paid, so use a pool only with a fixed share.

Then spend what came in. If two of the six coworkers drop out, buy a $100 gift, not a $150 one. Give it with one card everyone signs, and keep the receipt for an exchange or return.

Ask for the money the day you announce the gift, and buy nothing until the deadline passes.
