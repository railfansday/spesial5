Wide shoulders are a staple for anyone looking for a fit physique, but if you take the wrong approach to training them, you could be growing your trapezius instead of your deltoids. Fortunately, the coach they call “The Pro Creator” has shared one of the secrets that helped him win 25 Olympia’s for his legendary clients, and it’s a lot simpler than you might think.

Whether you are a competitive bodybuilder, or just want to look more “alpha,” then wider shoulders are a much sought after physical attribute. The shoulders contain the deltoid muscle group and often work in combination with the trapezius muscle towards the neck, and the triceps in the arms. To help isolate those gains, shoulder training requires specific exercises such as the overhead press, lateral raises, rear delt flies, and front delt raises, but the way that you execute these movements is paramount to your success.

“The key for shoulders is not to train too heavy,” explained Rambod, who has guided the likes of Phil Heath, Jay Cutler, Hadi Choopan, and Derek Lunsford towards bodybuilding gold. “Because if you go heavy, your traps kick in.” In other words, putting too much strain on your shoulders can lead to the recruitment of the trapezius muscles that are located in the upper back and neck. “If you’re really trying to build your delts, you really want to make sure that you’re not going light, but you’re using medium to heavier weight, without swinging.”

While we are often told that “more is more” as relates to lifting weights for muscle growth, scientific research has shown that significant muscle gains can occur at just 40% of your one rep max, particularly if a higher volume of reps is added. While you may want to go heavier than that, or stick within the 8- to 12-rep range, the truth is that whatever weight you engage, any exercise will be inefficient if proper form is not practiced.

“Once you swing, you engage the traps,” said Rambod, explaining why moving with the momentum could cause you to miss the muscle building potential inside the shoulders. “And, if you’re traps get too big, it actually can make you look more narrow.” Of course, he’s right again. A thick neck can make the shoulders look out of proportion, and appear slimmer, and this is the opposite of the effect you should be aiming for.

For competitive bodybuilders, failing to get the balance correct will result in a lesser score from the judges. “If you’re gonna be bodybuilding and you’re trying to get on stage at some point, you really want to make sure you balance the trap size with the thickness of the delts, so therefore you maximize your width.”

Take home message? “It’s not about how much weight you move, it’s about where you’re putting the tension,” wrote Rambod in his caption. “Keep the traps out of the movement, focus on your delts, and make every rep counts.”
