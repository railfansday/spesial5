Justice Cody Hiland Publicly Criticizes The President For Nominating Him To District Court Instead of Eighth Circuit

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Apparently, the Arkansas Senators are blocking Judge Lee Rudofsky's nomination because Hiland didn't get the nomination.

We are coming into the endgame for President Trump's judicial nominees. Since the election, it seems the President has made (at least) two nominations to the federal bench in Texas: Justice James Sullivan of the Texas Supreme Court for the WDTX seat in Austin and OLC Head Elliot Gaiser for the NDTX seat in Dallas. I say "seems" because the President has not posted these nominations to Truth Social, as that makes it official. But the Senate Judiciary Committee has received questionnaires for both candidates. I have known both of these sharp legal minds for some time, and would welcome their addition to the bench. Will Sullivan and Gaiser get votes? I sure hope so. Over the next six weeks, Republican Senators will need to be motived to vote, while Democrat Senators will pull out every stop to leave these seats open. Plus, there is an entire line of other nominees who are still waiting for votes.

Beyond the district court nominees, there is the question of circuit court nominees. Judge Anna St. John's nomination to the Fifth Circuit and Judge Daniel Domenico's nomination to the Tenth Circuit are pending. They had their hearings some time ago, and should get through. Todd Butler has not even had his hearing yet, so he is down the pecking order. Then there is Judge Lee Rudofksy, a nominee to the Eighth Circuit, who had his hearing back in September. Yet, both of the Arkansas Senators are blocking Rudofsky's nomination. Why? They prefer Justice Cody Hiland, a member of the Arkansas Supreme Court.

There's a problem with Hiland. He has demonstrated absolutely terrible judgment which should permanently disqualify him from holding any public office.

In August, he decided to disclose to a room full of law students private communications between him and the White House Counsel. Worse, rather than teaching students about the value of grace, he promoted belligerence and vindictiveness. In short, the White House approached Hiland about a nomination to the Eighth Circuit. After the vetting process, the White House changed course, and offered Hiland a seat on the federal district court. Hiland refused, and told the White House Counsel to tell President Trump about it. Talking to law students is my profession. I can't imagine a judge making such a huge blunder. What lesson are these students taking away here? That you should be so stubborn about getting one position that you refuse another lifetime commission? That you should reveal private communications with the White House in which you admit you were "belligerent"? He is comparing not getting an Eighth Circuit appointment to not getting on law review? No wonder the White House didn't pick him. In my view, making a bad nomination is worse than making no nomination at all (see Butler).  The Eighth Circuit is at a turning point. Judge Stras writes far too many dissents. One more vote could flip the circuit. Arkansas is but one state in the circuit. This stubbornness will spite the good people of those states.

Here's the bottom line. Senators pick Judges who are their friends. Those are the worst possible reasons to pick a judge. We need to reward the people who advance the rule of law, not those who are chummy with Senators. And yes, this applies to Todd Butler too. The Junior Senator from Mississippi told people to choose a different protein. Well, they should have chosen a different judge. But here we are.
