Aaron Rodgers’ brother Jordan goes off on Mike McCarthy following shocking accusations

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Jordan Rodgers knows not to take sides against the family.

Aaron Rodgers' brother teed off on Steelers coach Mike McCarthy following the former Pittsburgh quarterback's ugly divorce from the franchise earlier this week after a play-calling spat that led to the future Hall of Famer alleging that McCarthy sleeps during meetings.

"I think what's getting lost in this is just how unacceptable and unprecedented some of the behavior that McCarthy has displayed and how unique that is," Jordan said on "Good Morning America" on Friday before the Steelers host the Broncos in their first game since cutting Aaron.

"I think I would be surprised if he continued to be the head coach moving forward, whether that's short term, long term, whatever it is. I think having been in locker rooms, having been a third-string quarterback on a practice squad, somebody who knew if I even looked like I was nodding off, I'd be cut in a second and to have that happen from the head coach, the play-caller, you can't play football that way and there's too many jobs at stake to put up with that type of behavior. I think the Steelers are looking into this, but seemingly the accusations, allegations are confirmed in a lot of ways, and he didn't rebut them. Crazy world we're living in but, yeah, weird way to see it end."

Jordan Rodgers on "Good Morning America." ABC

Jordan, who calls college football games for ESPN/ABC, has not always been close with his brother, with the two only coming closer earlier this year after Aaron reconvened with his family.

After the Steelers benched Aaron late Tuesday night via a team statement, Jordan appeared on ESPN's "Get Up" the next day and pointed the finger at Pittsburgh general manager Omar Khan.

Jordan said McCarthy and Rodgers had spent 14 years together — 13 in Green Bay and this one season in Pittsburgh — so he believed there were other factors involved.

Aaron then went scorched earth the next day on McCarthy, alleging the coach snored during meetings, skipped sessions entirely and even claiming that McCarthy said "1-2-3 Packers" instead of "1-2-3 Steelers" during last Sunday's loss to the Eagles, a mistake McCarthy has since owned up to.

When Aaron challenged McCarthy to make changes in the play-calling operation or to release him, the team ultimately moved on — but only after his explosive interview on the "Pat McAfee Show."

"That was something we all weren't preparing for when we were getting ready for our tryptophan slumbers that we all got into yesterday," Jordan said. "It was a crazy couple days for the Steelers and Aaron. I think the first and foremost — it was kind of sad to see it end this way in Pittsburgh here with so much good vibes and hope about the reuniting of McCarthy and Rodgers and the potential to make a playoff run, one last dance. Unfortunately, it didn't happen that way."

As Jordan pointed the light toward McCarthy instead of his brother, plenty of others have ripped Aaron.

Tom Brady mentioned the "sanctity" of an NFL locker room, while stating that certain things should not be made public.

Several ex-Packers teammates have also made it clear they disagree with the man who led the franchise to its last Super Bowl title during the 2010 season.

Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers, left, chats with head coach Mike McCarthy before a game against the Philadelphia Eagles in Philadelphia on Nov. 22, 2026. Getty Images

McCarthy and the Steelers now have to try to rebound from all the turmoil before Friday's critical home clash with the Broncos in a clash of two current AFC playoff teams.

The Steelers (6-4), losers of two straight, are tied with the Browns for the final two AFC playoff spots and are now turning to longtime backup Mason Rudolph.
