Mikal Bridges’ buzzer-beater saves Knicks from brutal choke

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

For most of this season, Mikal Bridges had been benched in these moments.

With the Knicks missing Karl-Anthony Towns (illness), OG Anunoby (left calf tightness) and Jordan Clarkson (right plantar fasciitis) on Saturday, Bridges found himself on the court in the closing moments, and the ball ended up in his hands with the game on the line.

Mikal Bridges hits the game-winning, buzzer-beating 3-pointer in the Knicks' 97-96 win over the Trail Blazers on Nov. 21, 2026, at Madison Square Garden. Getty Images

He promptly delivered what just might be his coming out party for this season, drilling a buzzer-beating 3-pointer as the shorthanded Knicks staved off the Trail Blazers 97-96 at Madison Square Garden to extend their winning streak to four games.

The Knicks saw a 23-point halftime lead disappear.

After Deni Avdija's free throws broke a tie to give the Blazers a two-point lead with 3.2 seconds left, the Knicks needed a hero.

A leaping Josh Hart celebrates with Mikal Bridges as Landry Shamet (left) looks to join in on the celebration after Bridges' buzzer-beating 3-pointer in the Knicks' win over the Trail Blazers at the Garden. Jason Szenes for New York Post

Bridges — amid a season of intense criticism and struggles — became one.

The play was, as expected, supposed to be for Jalen Brunson. But he was covered. So Bridges instead got the inbounds from Josh Hart, pump-faked Toumani Camara out of the way and drilled the game-winning 25-footer from the left wing.

"Knowing that any good and bad stretch in the NBA can change very quickly," Landry Shamet, one of Bridges' closest friends on the team, told The Post, "he's always in a position to put himself in a good situation."

With the game tied at 94-94 and the Knicks in the penalty, Andre Drummond committed a boneheaded foul on Avdija, who was dribbling in the opposite direction of the basket near halfcourt. Drummond later admitted he thought the Knicks had a foul to give.

Avdija's free throws looked like they would seal what would have been the Knicks' worst loss of the season.

Before long, though, the Garden was erupting, and Bridges' teammates surrounded him in celebration.

Jalen Brunson, who scored 30 points, celebrates after hitting a 3-pointer in the Knicks' buzzer-beating win over the Trail Blazers at the Garden. Jason Szenes for New York Post

"Shoutout to Mikal for making the big-time 3," Drummond said, "to kind of mask the nonsense that happened."

Nobody has come under more scrutiny than Bridges this season, and nobody needed a win more.

"That's just who he is," Brunson said. "I don't know how many times I've said this before, but when you continuously work on your game every single day, and things may not be falling in your favor, but you keep doing the right thing every single day, you keep chipping away, chipping away, eventually things are gonna turn for you. And over the past week or two, things have been turning for him."

Landry Shamet, who was in the starting lineup and scored 20 points, grabs a rebound during the first half of the Knicks' buzzer-beating win over the Trail Blazers at the Garden. Jason Szenes for New York Post

It was Bridges' second game-winner against the Blazers. He did it two years ago as well.

"I like the city of Portland," Bridges joked.

The Knicks are making a habit of seeing big leads dissipate. It hasn't come back to bite them yet, but they are treading in dangerous waters. Saturday's letup came on the heels of watching a 16-point fourth-quarter lead cut down to three points in the final moments of their win over the Timberwolves on Thursday.

Andre Drummond, who started in place of Karl-Anthony Towns (illness), looks to block Jayson Kent's layup attempt during the Knicks' buzzer-beating win over the Trail Blazers at the Garden. Jason Szenes for New York Post

They drilled their first eight 3-pointers and erupted for 40 points in the first quarter to jump out to a 17-point lead. Brunson, who made his first five 3-pointers, scored 19 points in that period and 23 by halftime. Shamet, continuing his red-hot start to the season, followed suit with 14 first-half points. The Knicks shot 53.2 percent from the field and 48.1 percent from deep in the first half and took a 23-point lead into the break.

They held Portland to a brutal 34.9 percent shooting from the field and 21.7 percent shooting from behind the arc in that first half.

Considering the quality of opponent and who the Knicks were missing, it was two of the best quarters the Knicks have played this season.

But the Knicks' red-hot shooting suddenly went ice-cold in the second half. They were outscored 29-15 in the third quarter and 24-16 in the fourth.

Their 31 second-half points were a dramatic drop-off from the 66 they erupted for in the first half. It was a complete role reversal, as the Blazers caught fire offensively — until Bridges bailed them out.

"He's been there and done that," head coach Mike Brown said. "So for him to have the ball in his hands at that point in the game, there's a lot of confidence not only coming from him but from his teammates and this staff."

The three absences meant a trio of usually end-of-bench reserves saw action. James Wiseman, Pacôme Dadiet and Dalton Knecht — who was called up from the G-League on Saturday — all featured in non-garbage-time minutes.

Dadiet actually was back on the floor to start the fourth quarter. In total, 11 Knicks saw the court — Mohamed Diawara was the only active player not to enter the game.

It meant a pretty uneven scoring output. Brunson and Shamet combined for 50, more than half of the team's points.

But it was Bridges who provided the most important ones.
