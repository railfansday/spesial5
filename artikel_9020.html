Raúl Sánchez Architects transforms laundry into light-filled Barcelona home

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Spanish studio Raúl Sánchez Architects turned a former washhouse into Casa de las Lavanderas, with a lightwell that is a “modern translation of the old wells” used by local laundry workers.

The home is located on Barcelona’s picturesque Calle Aiguafreda street, historically an area where washerwomen used wells to handwash their clients’ clothes.

Raúl Sánchez Architects, which won interiors project of the year at this year’s Dezeen Awards, turned one of the old laundry houses on the street into the two-storey Casa de las Lavanderas.

The house is now home to a couple and their newborn baby.

The lower level of the 80-square-metre building was partly underground and very dark, leading the studio to look for new solutions to add more light.

“Since the ground floor is semi-basement, the area furthest from Aiguafreda Street was darker, so to bring light into it, I incorporated this lightwell topped with a skylight,” studio founder Rául Sánchez told Dezeen.

The lightwell also functions as a “modern translation of the old wells” that used to provide water for the laundry workers.

Casa de las Lavanderas faces two streets – Calle Aiguafreda and Calle Granollers – and has entrances on both sides, with the Calle Aiguafreda entrance sitting 1.5 metres lower than that on Calle Granollers.

From here, one flight of stairs leads down to the lower level and the Calle Aiguafreda entrance, and another up to the house’s upper level.

The 40-square-metre lower level houses an open-plan living, dining and kitchen area, a bathroom and a small study.

This can be partitioned off from the living space using folding doors made from wood and rice paper, inspired by the clients’ time living in Japan.

In the kitchen, Sánchez said he added a mirror-clad island to “introduce a sense of fragility without disrupting the open feel of the space”.

The upper level, meanwhile, houses the home’s bedrooms and a bathroom. The two floors are connected by the Klein-blue lightwell, which has oculi on all four sides to help distribute the illumination from its skylight throughout the space.

Sánchez wanted to “bring that picturesque atmosphere of Aiguafreda Street into the house, making it part of its surroundings by reclaiming that well – now translated into a lightwell that also helps regulate the home’s temperature.”

This was one of many tailor-made solutions to turn the former laundry house into a home.

“There were many challenges, both technical – access to the mezzanine level, the new staircase, the structural reinforcements – and functional, like fitting the program into just 80 square metres,” Sánchez said.

His studio also worked with materials that would complement the existing ones, using terracotta tiles on the ground floor to extend the traditional exterior clay paving and adding colored glazed tiles to the washbasins and bathroom walls.

“Ultimately, the goal is always to create a cohesive, overarching project that addresses all these challenges and offers something extra – something almost hard to put into words, but that translates into simply feeling good in the space,” Sánchez concluded.

Rául Sánchez Architects is currently working on the final phase of the project, the design of its orchard and garden, which will begin at the start of 2027.

The studio also recently designed a concrete home with a sunken courtyard in Cala Tamarit.
