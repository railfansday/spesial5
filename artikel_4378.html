Mercedes-Benz’s New Driving-Assist Tech Puts Tesla on Notice

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The German brand's MB.Drive Assist Pro is Mercedes’s answer to Full Self-Driving (Supervised).

If Tesla operates under the Silicon Valley mantra of "move fast and break things," then Mercedes, like most automotive players outside China, runs on "move steadily and avoid unnecessary liability." To wit, Mercedes took nearly half a dozen years to roll out a competing system to Tesla’s point-to-point automated driving system better known as Full Self-Driving (Supervised).

Dubbed MB.Drive Assist Pro, the automated driving technology allows so-equipped Mercedes models to accelerate, brake, steer, and respond to dynamic environmental features such as traffic lights, road signs, and nearby vehicles and pedestrians. With the aid of the automaker's aptly named Hands-Free Highway Assist, Mercedes models equipped with MB.Drive Assist Pro can handle the majority—if not all—of the tasks of driving with minimal input from the person behind the steering wheel.

Mercedes is blunt about the capabilities of both systems and does not attempt to obfuscate the active role the driver plays when using these subscription-based services. Though it didn’t dive into specifics, the company stated it plans to give eligible customers access to the features for an undetermined trial period.

The automaker plans to initially launch these driver-assist tools in December 2026 to a limited number of California customers with select Mercedes models running the brand's MB.OS software via an over-the-air (OTA) update. Nationwide rollout follows in 2027 through an OTA update for current owners of compatible Mercedes models, new versions of which will be able to leave the lot with one or both features active.

Mercedes vehicles capable of supporting both MB.Drive Assist Pro and Hands-Free Highway Assist for 2027 include CLA-class (gas and electric), GLC-class EV, GLE-class, GLS-class, S-class, and 2026 CLA-class EV models equipped with the brand’s MB.Drive Assist suite of driver-assist features. Meanwhile, MB.Drive Assist-equipped 2027 GLB-class, C-class, gas-powered GLC-class, EQS-class Sedan, and AMG GT 4-Door models will support Hands-Free Highway Assist but not MB.Drive Assist Pro.

Earlier this year, Mercedes invited us to San Francisco to experience MB.Drive Assist Pro automated driving system from the passenger seat. Less than 12 months later, we returned to the Bay Area to experience the tech from the driver's seat of an all-wheel-drive GLC400 EV.

Once activated, MB.Drive Assist Pro puttered the battery-powered Benz around north San Francisco with smooth but cautious inputs. If early iterations of Full Self-Driving (Supervised) reacted with the timidity and hesitancy of a new driver, MB.Drive Assist Pro approached the dense urban environment of San Fran like an experienced driver playing the part of a diffident tourist visiting the area for the first time.

For instance, after successfully stopping for a red light at an intersection with a busy crosswalk, MB.Drive Assist Pro struggled to take off after the light turned green. Instead, it ever so slowly crept forward before applying the brakes again the moment its various cameras and sensors detected movement from a pedestrian standing at either end of the crosswalk ahead, seemingly interpreting such motions as an intent by the individual to enter the crosswalk. Getting across the intersection ultimately required us to temporarily override the system with a quick boot of the right pedal.

A second such incident occurred a few minutes later when the battery-powered Benz encountered a truck standing in our lane. MB.Drive Assist Pro navigated around this obstacle by swiftly entering and exiting the opposing lane of this narrow two-way road. Once clear of the truck, the dual-motor Mercedes spotted a stop sign ahead. The electric SUV slowed to a halt before fully reentering its lane. Again, we overrode MB.Drive Assist Pro with a nudge of the accelerator, which brought every bit of the Benz out of the way of oncoming traffic.

Between its occasional need for overrides and its inability to operate completely hands-free, MB.Drive Assist Pro certainly has room to improve. Even so, the system’s actions never left us feeling in danger or uncomfortable. Likewise, we quickly adapted to keeping a hand on the wheel while MB.Drive Assist Pro took charge of steering duties. Ideal? No. Acceptable? Yes.

Mercedes will let drivers go hands-free on the highway, though. We got a feel for the brand’s Hands-Free Driving Assist system from behind the wheel of a plug-in hybrid S580e, which we took across the San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge toward the East Bay and back.

Operating Hands-Free Driving Assist is by way of the brand’s usual cruise control layout. An instrument cluster graphic of a steering wheel lets drivers know whether or not hands-free driving is available.

As with other competing systems, Hands-Free Highway Assist can perform automatic lane changes. The Mercedes setup pulls these off with little drama, and will do so with or without the presence of the driver’s hands. Put a destination into the in-dash navigation system, and the lane-change function will guide you to your exit.

Stay vigilant around toll areas, though. We almost ran one after the plug-in Benz failed to clock the red stoplight overhead. Likewise, be ready to take over driving duty even on mildly curving stretches of highway. It’s arguably the most irritating characteristic of Hands-Free Driving Assist.

Neither Hands-Free Driving Assist nor MB.Drive Assist Pro are static services. Real-world driving data from these systems will allow Mercedes to regularly improve the performance of these driving-assist features. These updated parameters can then be sent directly to vehicles OTA. How quickly such software updates deploy will ultimately depend on how comfortable Mercedes is with breaking things.
