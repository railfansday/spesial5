Over 1 Million Hondas Are Being Investigated for Start-Stop Failures

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration has gotten hundreds of complaints that engines are not restarting after coming to a stop at traffic lights.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) has launched an investigation into Honda’s automatic engine start-stop system. The inquiry comes after NHTSA received hundreds of complaints, with owners stating that their engines were failing to automatically restart after coming to a full stop at a traffic light.

The investigation was announced on Friday, November 13, and covers 1,033,632 total vehicles. The affected models are the Honda Passport, Odyssey, Pilot, and Ridgeline, along with the Acura MDX, all from between the 2024 and 2026 model years.

In total, NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation has been sent 701 complaints about the automatic start-stop feature. The system is supposed to automatically turn off the engine when the vehicle comes to a complete stop, and it’s supposed to automatically restart the engine when the driver takes their foot off the brake pedal. However, “the complaints allege that the engines are failing to restart once they have been shut off by this system.” NHTSA says that there have been no injuries related to the stop-start system problems.

This isn’t the first time that this issue has arisen with Honda’s automatic start-stop system, which is meant to improve fuel economy by limiting idle time. In 2025, the federal government investigated the system, looking at 2.2 million units of the Pilot, Odyssey, Passport, Ridgeline, MDX, and Acura TLX. The previous investigation covered all of the vehicles in the current investigation, except for the 2026 model year.

Although the system failure is similar in both populations, “the vehicles in this current investigation appear to have different root causes and respond differently to repairs,” NHTSA says. More information should continue to emerge as NHTSA continues its investigation.
