Stan Collymore reveals the 'lifeline' from Danny Kelly that saved him as ex-footballer pays emotional tribute to broadcasting titan following his death at 69

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Published: 05:59 EST, 11 November 2026 | Updated: 10:52 EST, 11 November 2026

Stan Collymore has paid an emotional and heartfelt tribute to talkSPORT presenter Danny Kelly after he passed away at the age of 69.

Kelly had been undergoing treatment after being diagnosed with cancer of the oesophagus, which led to him stepping away from his talkSPORT role in March.

In an update to fans in August, Kelly informed fans his cancer treatment had 'definitely not gone to plan' after revealing he had been hospitalised on seven occasions, including spending time in intensive care.

On Wednesday morning, talkSPORT then heartbreakingly confirmed the legendary broadcaster's death in a statement.

Kelly was known to be a mentor for many younger journalists and pundits, one of whom was former England, Liverpool and Nottingham Forest star Collymore.

And, shortly after his tragic passing was confirmed, Collymore took to social media to pay his respects to Kelly and thank him for the role he had played in his life and career.

talkSPORT presenter Danny Kelly has died at the age of 69, the broadcaster announced on Wednesday

Stan Collymore was one of many individuals to share an extremely heartfelt tribute to Kelly

Kelly had updated fans throughout his treatment, which he admitted in August had 'not gone to plan'. Source: X/dannykellywords

'This morning I was told of the passing of my good friend and colleague, Danny Kelly,' he wrote. 'The tributes to him in the coming days from those who knew him and worked with him will give you an idea of how much he was loved.

'I loved him too. Very much so. A man who I called "Dad" on air because I wished he was. I hope you give me a few minutes to read my thoughts, and I hope they do you justice, Dan.

'By 2008 I was a mess. I would sleep 18 hours a day, the other 6 mostly wanting to die. Once or twice a week I would open my laptop, organise football tabs, gossip and news as prep for jobs in broadcasting that I knew would never come, "just in case". When hope dies, we die, after all.

'Then Danny Kelly came into my life. I was driving out of London having been to see a psychiatrist about the state I was in and had BBC London on the radio. Danny was presenting with a guest, reviewing books. My name came up in the conversation, so I called in.

'We hit it off straight away, talked about football for what seemed like forever but was probably a standard 10-12 minute section. But for me it sparked a temporary respite from sleep and chronic mental health issues and even for that I was grateful.'

Collymore continued: 'A couple of years later Danny joined talkSPORT to do the 7-10pm slot through the week. He told me he was offered a few co-presenters but he remembered that conversation we had and he wanted me to do it.

'A lifeline of work, passionately talking about our sport, having fun and feeling a value as a human again. Something lost for 7 long years. Danny gifted me that. Without that I know I wouldn't be writing this today.'

Collymore went on to highlight some of Kelly's many qualities and provided further insight into their close relationship and how he was supported on his broadcasting journey.

'"Always fresh, never frozen" was the tagline in a Domino's read we had to do in the show, something we'd instinctively greet each other with until our last meeting, in a pub, broadcasting for talkSPORT around 3 years ago,' he added. 'I didn't know that time would be the last, but perhaps fittingly we hugged as I left, I'm glad I did.

'The shows were loved. They were funny, honest, insightful and I hope, along with Saggers after that, proof that Danny's belief in me was worthy. But to say my love for him was only thanks for a job doesn't even scratch the surface.

'His kindness, his phone calls late at night (Dan was a notorious night owl) of support, his man hugs, his indomitable spirit, his big brain on anything from football to music to geopolitics, his love, as I know many others will testify, came from the biggest of hearts and the biggest of souls housed in a giant of a man in every sense.

'Dan, thank you, thank you for the kindness, the fun, the hope, the support and for being someone to look up to and aspire to be. I love you very much, I know I'll hug you again some day. Thank you for everything.

Collymore was one of many to pay tribute to Kelly, with former talkSPORT Breakfast host Laura Woods also highlighting the role he had played in her career.

She wrote: 'I had so many great memories with him, he was a guidance to me. We had production meetings, sitting around Danny, it was like kids at school, great stories. The level of knowledge he had, about anything and everything. He was so pure and genuine, that’s so unique.

'He would say it how it was, I loved that. I was in awe of him. He challenged me in the right way. He’d give you so much knowledge, I credit him with my early development. I will miss him.'

Kelly joined talkSPORT permanently in 2008 and presented the Tottenham-focused 'View From The Lane' podcast for The Athletic

Kelly previously edited NME and Q magazine, while he also launched the Football365 website

When announcing the news on Wednesday morning, talkSPORT said: 'We are heartbroken to share that our much-loved friend and colleague Danny Kelly has died, aged 69.

'A cherished part of talkSPORT for so many years, Danny brought extraordinary wit, warmth and knowledge to our airwaves.

'His passion for football, music and life was infectious, and his kindness touched everyone around him.

'“I love football, Stan. I love it.” We loved you, Danny.

'We’ll miss you dearly. Our thoughts are with Danny’s family, friends and everyone who loved listening to him.'

Kelly had provided updates following his diagnosis and thanked fans for their support during his treatment.

'KELLY CANCER KRONIKLES. Cos so many of you kindly asked for an update... My treatment has most definitely NOT gone to plan. SEVEN hospitalisations have turned an already tough journey into a messy stagger. I spent last week in the Intensive Care Unit. Scary stuff,' Kelly shared in August.

'Some of this is just terrible luck. The syndrome that had me in ICU is SO rare that nobody in Ireland is an expert. My tests were sent to LONDON. Several brain scans, multiple infusions and a lumbar puncture later, I’m a bit “better”. Maybe even going home (again) today..

'One medic commented that I’ve “the constitution of an ox.” Which made me feel very sad for an ox, alone and weedy in a field somewhere, with MY constitution...

'I have to say this, cos it’s true. The ICU nurses - half locals, half from the south Indian seaside state of Kerala - are amazing. Their diligence, compassion and humour pulled me through. I have a list of their names; I will never forget them.

'As always, I thank you all for your support. Especially from bands I haven’t worked with for years. Members of Dexys, the Waterboys, The Fall, Primal Scream, The Loft, The Coral and the mighty Manics, among others, have all wished me luck. Note; only great bands; im humbled.

Kelly had been part of talkSPORT since 2007, joining the broadcaster permanently the following year.

In a famous moment, the ardent Tottenham fan was heard shouting in delight when they rescued a 4-4 draw with a last gasp equaliser in the North London derby against Arsenal in 2008, telling co-host Stan Collymore 'I love football, Stan, I love it.'

Kelly hosted the Trans Europe Express show on talkSPORT, as well as appearing as a regular on Hawksbee & Jacobs.

He also presented the Tottenham focused 'View From The Lane' podcast for The Athletic, while he previously edited NME and Q magazine.

'The entire Tottenham family mourns the loss of one of our own, broadcasting legend Danny Kelly,' a Tottenham statement read on social media following the news.

'Our thoughts and love are with his family and friends at this incredibly sad time. Rest in peace, Danny.'

Kelly's close friend and long-time broadcast collaborator Danny Baker was among the others to offer a tribute, writing: 'My great friend, supreme broadcaster and funniest man I ever knew, Danny Kelly, died yesterday afternoon.

'I cannot believe I will never hear his voice again.'

talkSPORT reporter Ian Abrahams wrote: 'Absolutely heartbroken to hear Danny Kelly has passed away. One of the best broadcasters I’ve worked with.

'One of the most amazing, kindest, funny, passionate people I knew. Danny, we’ll always have “I love Football”. I will miss you my friend, sleep well.'

'We are absolutely devastated at the death of our host, our leader, the great Danny Kelly,' the View From The Lane' podcast shared. 'We all loved him as much as he loved Spurs.'
