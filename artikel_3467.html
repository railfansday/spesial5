Taika Waititi’s Sci-Fi Disaster Officially Removed From Cinemas After Earning Just $15 Per Theater

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

He has covered everything from Marvel to the Oscars, and Marvel at the Oscars. He also writes obsessively about the box office, charting the many hits and misses that are released weekly, and how their commercial performance shapes public perception. In his time at Collider, he has also helped drive diversity by writing stories about the multiple Indian film industries, with a goal of introducing audiences to a whole new world of cinema.

Director Taika Waititi was the toast of the town after resurrecting the stagnant Thor series with his trademark take on the po-faced superhero in Thor: Ragnarok. The movie is still considered among the finest achievements in Marvel Cinematic Universe history. It afforded him significant creative freedom not just on its immediate follow-up, Thor: Love and Thunder, but also on his prestige play, Jojo Rabbit. Waititi ended up winning a Best Adapted Screenplay Oscar for Jojo Rabbit, but his career curiously took a hit almost immediately afterward with two major critical and commercial underperformers.

Besides Thor: Love and Thunder, which fell short of its predecessor's box-office haul and is generally considered rather self-indulgent, Waititi directed two would-be awards contenders in the same vein as Jojo Rabbit. While that movie ended up grossing more than $90 million worldwide against a reported budget of $14 million, Waititi's two follow-ups suffered troubled productions and tanked commercially. First out of the gate was the sports drama Next Goal Wins, which languished on the shelf for over a year before grossing $18 million worldwide against a reported budget of $14 million.

Waititi's latest film, on the other hand, has delivered such a poor performance at the box office that its distributor, Sony, stopped reporting its grosses after two weeks. The movie in question is based on a sci-fi novel by the Nobel Prize-winning Kazuo Ishiguro. It features the six-time Oscar nominee Amy Adams and the ingénue Jenna Ortega, the star of Netflix's most-watched original series, Wednesday. Despite the talent involved, the film opened to poor reviews, which reportedly prompted Sony to cancel its wide release and global rollout. The movie eventually debuted in a little under 900 theaters on October 23.

We're talking, of course, about Klara and the Sun, which made $701,000 in its opening weekend and $157,000 in its second. By November 5, Sony stopped reporting the film's daily grosses. The movie's total global haul stands at less than $3 million against a budget that has been reported to be as high as $44 million. Its final daily haul was reported to be just over $13,000, which represents a per-theater average of just $15.

Klara and the Sun received poor reviews and is now sitting at a 26% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes. The site's consensus reads, "Flattening the nuanced ideas of its acclaimed source material with a tonally broad approach, Klara and the Sun aims to be endearing and inspiring but winds up cloying and twee." In a rare instance of a filmmaker publicly lashing out at critics, Waititi posted a note on Instagram slamming those who've disliked his movie.

You'll have to wait for Klara and the Sun to hit the PVOD market to check it out. Stay tuned to Collider for more updates.
