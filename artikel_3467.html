Vanity Fair Announces New Hires and Promotions Across Departments

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Vanity Fair Global Editorial Director Mark Guiducci today announced 11 new hires and appointments to the magazine’s masthead, deepening the magazine’s coverage across technology and politics, while adding talent to its operations and creative teams.

Vanity Fair’s new hires bring fresh perspectives to the magazine’s reporting on power and influence, alongside new creative voices to help shape its visual storytelling.

Maggie Coughlan is promoted to global managing editor. Since joining the team in 2021, she has held several positions at Vanity Fair, most recently as executive editor. She previously held editorial roles at the New York Post and People.com. She holds a master’s degree in digital publishing from NYU.

Emily Shugerman joins Vanity Fair as a senior staff writer reporting on wealth and politics on the West Coast. Emily comes from The San Francisco Standard, where she covered the shifting political allegiances of Silicon Valley’s richest, the fight over California’s proposed billionaire tax, and the growing power of AI lab workers. Before that, she was a reporter at The Daily Beast, where her investigations spurred leadership changes at national nonprofits and policy shifts at major corporations. Her work has also appeared in The Cut and The Guardian. She lives in San Francisco.

Jack Holmes joins as a senior editor for Vanity Fair, where he’s been editing features and political coverage since January. He will continue that work and expand into society and technology. Previously he was a senior staff writer and political editor for Esquire. He’s also the founder and publisher of The Football Weekend, a publication covering world soccer. Jack graduated from Vanderbilt University with a degree in philosophy and political science.

Nina Joyce is vice president of communications, based in New York, leading Vanity Fair’s communications globally. With 17 years of experience, she previously oversaw media relations for Condé Nast’s UK business and portfolio of brands, managing corporate reputation, brand strategy and major cultural events. Prior to joining Condé Nast, Nina spent a decade at Vice Media Group, holding senior roles across VICE, Refinery29, and i-D, and ultimately leading corporate PR across EMEA.

Roxanne Doucet joins as global visuals director. She has worked across New York and Paris, and collaborated with renowned artists and luxury houses including Dior, Loewe, and Balenciaga, building a practice rooted in fine art, documentary photography, and editorial storytelling. She was previously visual director at Vogue US and has consulted for Vogue France.

Dondre Stuetley joins Vanity Fair as senior visuals editor. Previously he was the photo editor at WSJ Magazine, commissioning stories on art, fashion, entertainment, design, food, and architecture.

Sameet Sharma joins as senior visuals editor. He was most recently the visuals director at V Magazine, and prior to V, he oversaw visual production at G-III, working on brands such as DKNY and Karl Lagerfeld. He also brings experience from Hearst magazines, where he worked on the photo teams at ELLE, Marie Claire, and Esquire.

Christian Zelaya joins as a senior designer. He brings more than five years of experience shaping editorial reporting into creative and clever visual stories for digital and social platforms and attracting readers for global brands like NBC, Forbes, and Scholastic.

Michael Kanyongolo joins Vanity Fair as audience development manager. He was most recently the news producer for Quanta Magazine, where he carried out web development, visual design, and production. He previously worked on web production at the New York Post and has a background in media and psychology.

Nazanin Hakim joins Vanity Fair as a creative producer on the video team. She brings a background in documentary filmmaking, having worked on a range of projects exploring politics, culture, and technology, including the Emmy-nominated series Turning Point: 9/11 and the War on Terror and What’s Next? The Future With Bill Gates.

Chris Black joins Vanity Fair as a contributing editor. He is a writer, photographer, and cohost of How Long Gone. He lives in New York and Los Angeles.
