Holiday Spending Cap – Set It at What You Can Pay Off in January

Holiday shopping on a credit card stays interest-free only if you can pay the whole balance when January’s statement comes due. So before you shop, add your holiday savings to what your next few paychecks leave after regular bills, use that total as your spending cap, and track every purchase against it.

In ICSC’s 2026 Holiday Intentions Survey, 23% of the adults surveyed said they expect to still be paying for this season’s purchases in 2027.

Most credit cards charge no interest on purchases when you pay the full statement balance, the total your bill shows, by the due date each month. Whatever part of the holiday bill you leave unpaid in January becomes debt at the card’s interest rate.

In August, bank credit card accounts that were charged interest paid an average rate of 22.36%, according to the Federal Reserve. Say $1,000 is still on the card after January’s payment, you pay $100 a month from then on, and you charge nothing new. At that rate, clearing it takes 12 payments and about $117 in interest, and the last payment is due in January 2028, after next year’s holidays.

So set the cap before Black Friday, Nov. 27, from money that will be in your checking account by the January due date. Start with what your holiday savings hold now, and add whatever each paycheck before then leaves after rent, utilities, and other regular bills. That total is the cap, not what you’d like to spend or your card’s credit limit.

Count every holiday cost against it, not just gifts, since food, travel, decorations, and year-end tips for a sitter or house cleaner come out of the same money. Then put every holiday purchase on one card, keep everyday spending off it, and check its balance once a week. When the total gets close to the cap, trim the gift list rather than raise the cap.

Two things mean the cap was set too high: a balance you won’t be able to clear in January, or a gift you’d need to split into buy now, pay later installments to afford. Both mean the season costs more than the money you’ll have by January, so lower the cap or drop the purchase.

Write the cap at the top of your gift list this week, beside the date January’s payment is due.
