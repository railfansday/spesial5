In 1977, the Vantage was the first Brooks shoe to top our charts. The running renaissance began several years prior, when Frank Shorter won the 1972 Olympic marathon in Munich. Two years later, the shoe industry would be changed forever with the introduction of ethylene vinyl acetate (EVA) foam, which was lighter, softer, and had more rebound than the rubbers used in midsoles around that time. Our editors knew Brooks had a winner when we tested the Vantage.

Almost half a century later, the brand is still dazzling us with its innovations and earning prizes in the 2026 Runner’s World Shoe Awards. And to think the company started out more than a hundred years ago manufacturing ballet slippers and bath shoes.

Since the 1970s, Brooks has solely focused on upping the ante in shoe tech. The Vantage also introduced the varus wedge, which was basically a medial post used to help with instability caused by overpronation. Now the varus wedge is dated and has been replaced with Brooks’s “GuideRails” system. EVA is also being phased out with improved foams that are bouncier and even softer.

Here’s a breakdown of everything you need to know—foams, stability tech—about Brooks’s footwear.

One helpful way to choose Brooks shoes is by knowing Brooks’s foams. The kind of foam determines the type of running experience the shoe is meant for.

Through the decades, Brooks revved up its EVA foam, developing MoGo foam in 2006, and then DNA foam in 2013. Known for its adaptive midsole cushioning, DNA has undergone an evolution of its own, breaking into its own subcategories in types of shoes.

Runners looking for a more cushioned shoe will want to try trainers with DNA Loft midsoles. DNA Loft v3, for example, which is featured in the Ghost 18 and the Adrenaline GTS 25, is nitrogen-infused EVA and rubber. It has large cells of air to pack in cushioning without adding extra weight.

At the beginning of 2025, Brooks released the Glycerin Max which showcased DNA Tuned. In developing DNA Tuned, the brand’s team found they could manipulate the cells creating larger cells in the heel and smaller cells in the forefoot. In an interview with Runner’s World, Brooks Chief Product Officer Carson Caprara explained how this dual-cell cushioning softens landings and becomes a springboard for responsive toe-off.

For runners looking for shoes for speedwork or racing, they should look for shoes with DNA Flash or DNA Gold; the latter is a pure PEBA midsole. DNA Hype is a combination of EVA and PEBA. We found in our testing its good for speedwork.

Before the Adrenaline GTS launched in 1999, Brooks had set out to build off of the success of the Addiction. Runners wanted a more flexible version of the stability shoe, and so, the Adrenaline GTS—Go-to-Shoe—was born.

In 2018, Brooks adapted a more holistic approach with stability, implementing a guide rails system into its shoes. The guide rails system has two dense foam pieces in the midsole, one on the medial side and one on the lateral side. These foam pieces reduce your foot from rolling outward and inward when your form becomes erratic due to overpronation or late onset overpronation.

Now redefined as Go-to-Support, GTS Brooks shoes are the stability counterpart of their neutral models. In this way, you can easily find your favorite Brooks trainer with just a little extra support in the midsole and not much else changed with the shoe.

What’s the best Brooks shoe for beginning runners?

We always recommend the Ghost because it provides plenty of supportive cushioning without being too bulky. It’s an ideal daily trainer for beginners to start with as an offshoot.

How does the Hyperion Elite 6 compare to other carbon shoes?

According to testers, the Hyperion Elite 6 feels less softer and more accommodating compared to other super shoes they’ve tested. The new DNA Gold midsole adds more edge compared to the Hyperion Elite’s DNA Flash foams, with testers saying the rebound is comparable to the Adidas Adizero Adios Pro 4 or Saucony Endorphin Pro 5.

The Launch 12, included on this list, has a fit and feel almost on par with more expensive models. It’s not as responsive compared to $140 to $160 trainers due to its EVA cushioning, but it delivers a smooth ride.
