What does Arteta do with Konsa once Saliba returns?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ezri Konsa has starred for Arsenal at centre-back following his £55m move from Aston Villa this summer, but William Saliba has now returned to take back his position in the Arsenal team; the Gunners have important games against Man City, Real Madrid and Spurs after the break

Football Journalist @SamBIitz

Arsenal have a big set of fixtures coming up after the November international break. Big games require big team selections - and Mikel Arteta has a huge decision to make in his defence.

William Saliba is coming back, which is a major boost for the Premier League champions, but to say Arsenal have missed him in recent weeks has been an understatement. Ezri Konsa has stepped up.

Not only has Konsa stepped in brilliantly for the injured Frenchman, but he has even proven to be just as good as Saliba was in Arsenal's title-winning season. Some areas of the data show Konsa has performed at higher levels.

But with Saliba now back in training and stepping up his fitness, Arteta has a task to balance the reintegration of one of his most important players, whilst keeping arguably their most in-form player happy.

And given the likes of Borussia Dortmund, Manchester City, Tottenham, Real Madrid and Manchester United face the Gunners before Christmas, these calls are important.

Konsa came to Arsenal with the reputation of being excellent at two key areas: accurate passing and duel-winning abilities. Since making the move from Villa to north London, he has continued that trend.

He ranks second in the Premier League this season for both passing accuracy and the percentage of ground duels won. These numbers make him one of the best defenders in the league this season in terms of an all-round game.

This form would normally make a player like Konsa undroppable at any level. The only issue is that one of the best centre-backs in the world is returning, who has been one of the centrepieces of the immense defensive unit Arteta has formed at the club.

The area that shows the importance of Saliba is the average height of Arsenal's defensive line. This season, without Saliba, the Gunners backline has dropped by at least one metre, no matter whether it is attacking or defending. It is a marginal shift, but this level has fine margins.

When Saliba broke into Arsenal's team in the 2022/23 season, Arsenal's defensive line was transformed. Their defensive line went higher by two metres compared to the previous season - partly due to the Frenchman's pace, with Saliba regularly described as a 'cheat code' in the way he can snuff out transitions and counter attacks with ease.

That higher line keeps opponents further away from their goal, leading to a better press and subsequently more Arsenal pressure and possession. Arteta regularly says one of the key aims for his team is "not allowing the opponent to breathe."

Konsa has done his fair share of sweeping up in the Arsenal high line this season. He has made three successful last-man tackles this season, with some of the highlights being result-defining challenges to deny the likes of Chelsea's Morgan Rogers and Nottingham Forest's Liam Delap as Arsenal protected one goal leads to claim victory.

But on that note, the centre-back partnership of Konsa-Gabriel has made more last-man tackles between them this season (seven), than Saliba-Gabriel needed in the whole of last season (five). Opponents are not just breathing a little, they are threatening in open spaces a bit more.

In a system where Arsenal pride themselves on giving next to nothing away in terms of attacking chances to their opposition, there is value in bringing back the 'cheat code'.

One scenario where Konsa and Saliba could play together is by moving the England international out to right-back, where he has played before in his career.

That would also suit Arteta's full-back plans - with the Arsenal manager almost exclusively playing natural full-backs in his full-back roles in recent years. Jurrien Timber, Ben White, Riccardo Calafiori and Piero Hincapie were all centre-backs by trade before moving to north London and being retrained as wider options.

However, the last couple of years has seen Konsa specialise centrally - to the point where he hasn't played a Premier League match at right-back for nearly two years.

His last league start as a full-back came in December 2024 when playing for Aston Villa, with his last 5,813 league minutes - the equivalent of nearly 65 games - coming as a centre-back. "Ezri, yeah, he can play as a full-back," said Arteta earlier this month. "I think his main position is as a centre-back."

And of course, Konsa and Saliba could play at centre-back together, giving Gabriel a bit of a rest when needed. It would mean moving one to a left centre-back role - and taking a left-footed centre-back out of the team, but the Arsenal manager is confident in that.

"He can play right or left," said Arteta of Konsa. "Willy has done the same with the national team and here at Arsenal. That's going to be something really positive for the team."

And while this could be a headache of Arteta, this has emerged as a positive problem. Arsenal have felt the impact of an injury to Saliba and Gabriel in the past - it has sometimes cost them Premier League and Champions League titles in recent years.

Now they're in a better place, but Arteta still has to get these big calls right. The Arsenal manager has sometimes made questionable selection calls this season in terms of the profiles he has in his team.

In the 3-0 loss at Brighton, he gave Bruno Guimaraes a first start in midfield when perhaps the more press-resistant Myles Lewis-Skelly was a better option against the high-intensity Seagulls in the press.

And in the recent win over Hull, Arteta went for Kai Havertz up front when the game was screaming out for Viktor Gyokeres' bullish nature against the three Tigers centre-backs.

Now Arteta has the a similar dilemma, only in defence.
