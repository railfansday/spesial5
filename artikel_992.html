Liverpool head coach Andoni Iraola bemoaned his side’s poor defending of set-pieces leading to “cheap, messy” goals which cost them a 3-2 defeat and the loss of their 100 per cent Champions League record against Fenerbahce.

On a chaotic night in Istanbul, the visitors twice conceded the lead given to them by Trey Nyoni – scoring his first goal for the club – and Alexander Isak, with goals from Englishmen Mason Greenwood and Archie Brown before Vedat Muriqi scored a 79th-minute winner.

It was the first time they had conceded three in three successive European games, extending their woeful record in Turkey to just one win, one draw and seven defeats and they have yet to keep a clean sheet in the country, which also extends to Champions League and Super Cup finals.

After five successive wins in all competitions they have now lost back-to-back games, but this defeat was far more damaging to morale than Sunday’s reverse to Arsenal.

“Cheap goals. Messy goals,” said Iraola. “The free-kick is quite far out, through the wall. The last goal was a long throw, the second one Cody (Gakpo) touched the ball just in front of Ali (goalkeeper Alisson). In the stadium it was almost impossible to communicate, it was difficult for me to give instructions and the communication was difficult between Cody and Ali.”

Andoni Iraola defended making changes to his team but a late collapse in Istanbul highlighted the challenge of leading such a lopsided squad across multiple fronts.

The last thoughts from the Liverpool boss were on his team conceding from set pieces.

“It's something that we were very aware of in pre-season,” he said.

“We've reinforced a lot the defensive set-play situation. We are doing very well in the league but today it has cost us.

“We know they are strong there, especially with Muriqi, with [Romelu] Lukaku, with the centre-backs, but maybe the last goal is the only one that is because they are strong in there, they are tall.

“The others are situations that we have to control better.”

Iraola also addressed his decision to start Bradley Barcola as the main striker.

“We thought it would work well because I think Bradley, in terms of speed [and] the match-ups, it could be good for him,” he explained.

“He hasn't found the position too hard... all season he has been better when he has played outside wide, and then obviously with Cody, with Alex, I think we've been more dangerous.”

Andoni Iraola spoke during his post match press conference about why he made the changes he did.

“Yeah, I think we put in Victor [Munoz] and Rio [Ngumoha], they were more fresh,” Iraola said.

“We thought also that for Alex [Isak] it was maybe good to play 30 minutes, not more. We wanted to try Bradley. We tried Cody [Gakpo] in the past week against Chelsea. We wanted to play Bradley.”

Andoni Iraola will be hoping to send his players off to the international break with a win when the Reds face Crystal Palace in the Premier League this weekend.

Another defeat would raise the pressure on Liverpool and make their first match after the break, versus Manchester United at Anfield, more important that it already is.

In the Champions League, the Reds will face Club Brugge at the end of November before fixtures against Porto, Inter Milan and Lens round out the league phase.

Fenerbahce’s interim manager, and former Liverpool midfielder, Dirk Kuyt gave his reaction to the result.

Tonight’s match was his second and final fixture as interim boss and Kuyt described the win as ‘a miracle’.

“A very special night for everyone who loves Fenerbahce,” he said.

“We told each other to stick together and be united. That’s something I learned from Liverpool.

“We made a miracle tonight, not many expected us to win.”

Andoni Iraola made five changes to the Liverpool starting XI for this evening’s game but admitted that he does not think switching out the personnel was to blame for the defeat.

“We have done this not just today, we have done it all season. All the games midweek we have been changing four or five as it’s impossible to retain the starting XI,” Iraola added.

“I trust the squad to sustain the level and I don’t think it is the main reason why we lost.

“We have to look more at how we conceded the goals as the reason we didn’t win.”

“We conceded from a free-kick, a corner, a long throw and that has hurt us,” Iraola continued.

“We have gone in front twice, we conceded in situations we should have definitely done better (with).

“It is frustrating because it is a difficult place to play and for the most part of the game we were thinking we could win this one.

“The first one the wall we didn’t set properly, the last one when he jumps he wins the ball against three defenders.

“I think before scoring the goals we had very good moments on the ball, very good offensive situations. The thing is we conceded almost straight away.

“We have been dealing well with these situations up to today. It has cost us. We have to do better.”

More reaction from Liverpool’s manager who is not happen with the manner in which his team conceded.

He called Fenerbahce’s goals ‘cheap and messy’ amid the atmosphere inside the stadium.

“The free-kick is quite far out, through the wall. The last goal was a long throw, the second one Cody (Gakpo) touched the ball just in front of Ali (goalkeeper Alisson).

“In the stadium it was almost impossible to communicate, it was difficult for me to give instructions and the communication was difficult between Cody and Ali.”

The Fenerbahce striker added: "Just that we were all going to suffer together because we know Liverpool are one of the best teams in Europe. They've got great players at the top of the pitch.

"We knew if we stuck together in the difficult moments, in the low block, we'd be able to create chances, catch them on the break like we did in the first half.

“Then we knew we'd be very good in the air, would be able to create some chances, we managed to take them and get the win."
