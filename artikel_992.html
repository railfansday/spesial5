How does Euro 2028 qualifying work? Confirmed pots, format, and rules for co-hosts

Co-hosted on home soil by England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland, the road to Euro 2028 is fast approaching as the draw for qualifying takes place in Belfast in early December.

But the four co-hosts will enter qualifying for the tournament as normal, even though there is a ‘safety net’ that can be used by two of the teams should they fail to qualify automatically.

The United Kingdom hosted some games during the Covid-delayed Euro 2020 tournament in 2021, although many matches were played with limited capacities due to Covid restrictions.

Wembley hosted the final and will do so again in 2028, with Cardiff, Glasgow, Dublin, Birmingham, Liverpool, Manchester, Newcastle also staging games during the 24-team tournament.

Here’s everything you need to know ahead of the Euro 2028 qualifying draw, which takes place in Northern Ireland on Sunday 6 December.

Unlike previous major tournaments held in multiple countries, England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland do not qualify automatically just because they are co-hosts. Although this is a tournament for the UK and Ireland, Northern Ireland are not considered co-hosts, as they are not staging any games.

But while England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland all enter qualifying as usual, there are two reserved places to be used by co-hosts if they fail to qualify automatically, either by winning their group or finishing as one of the eight-best runners-up.

The four co-hosts, as well as Northern Ireland for that matter, will all be kept apart in the qualifying draw to boost their qualification chances, so if they all go on to their win their groups then happy days – the safety net is not required.

But if disaster strikes and three or more of the co-hosts fail to qualify on merit, then there are just the two reserved places to fall back on: they will go to the two teams who have the best record in qualifying and the others will have to watch on.

The Euro 2028 qualifying draw takes place in Belfast on Sunday 6 December. Qualifying then begins in March 2027, but only for the teams not involved in the Nations League quarter-finals or Nations League play-offs who were drawn into Groups G-L. Within those groups of five, the four other teams will play against each other across March and June 2027, with everyone else then beginning their qualifying campaign from September 2027.
