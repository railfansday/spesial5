Why Did MARA Sell $3B in Bitcoin in 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Home » Bitcoin (BTC) » Why Did MARA Sell $3B in Bitcoin in 2026?

On-chain data and Q3 filings reveal a major shift in the mining giant’s treasury playbook.

On-chain alerts lit up after Bitcoin mining giant MARA Holdings moved another 366 BTC ($23.33 million), capping off a massive 2026 sell-off totaling 40,581 BTC worth $3.03 billion.

While a multi-billion-dollar miner sell-off sounds like a bearish warning sign for retail investors, MARA’s third-quarter 2026 financial disclosures reveal a calculated financial reset: the company sold near market highs to wipe out $1.4 billion in debt and unlock 17,477 BTC in collateral.

According to on-chain tracking data shared by Lookonchain, MARA transferred 366 BTC worth roughly $23.33 million out of its wallets while Bitcoin traded near the $63,700 level. The transfer immediately drew market scrutiny toward MARA’s broader 2026 treasury activity.

Per company disclosures and on-chain tallies, MARA has offloaded 40,581 BTC in 2026 for approximately $3.03 billion. The miner executed those sales at an average price of $74,446 per Bitcoin.

For years, publicly traded Bitcoin miners adhered to a strict “HODL everything” playbook, borrowing cash against their Bitcoin reserves to fund operations and hardware expansion. In 2026, MARA aggressively reversed that strategy.

In an official statement on X accompanying its Q3 2026 earnings release, MARA confirmed that it deployed $750 million from post-quarter Bitcoin sales to fully repay its Bitcoin-backed debt.

Clearing those credit facilities released all 17,477 BTC that had been pledged as collateral, bringing MARA’s total debt reduction since the beginning of 2026 to $1.4 billion—a 37% cut to its overall debt load.

The company also highlighted its ambition to evolve from a pure-play Bitcoin miner into an integrated, energy-backed AI and digital infrastructure platform.

According to MARA, Bitcoin mining built the physical footprint, access to low-cost power created the opportunity, and AI/HPC and cloud services offer higher-margin, longer-duration, and more predictable returns. The company is now shifting from building capacity to commercializing it as an integrated “energy-to-compute” operator.

MARA’s energized hashrate reached 71.2 EH/s in Q3 (up 18% year-over-year from 60.4 EH/s), cementing its position as the largest publicly traded Bitcoin miner by computing capacity.

The company captures roughly 5.4% of all global Bitcoin miner rewards, producing 2,247 BTC and winning 644 blocks during the quarter—a slight dip from 5.9% in Q2 as overall network difficulty grew faster than MARA’s deployments.

At the end of Q3 2026, MARA held $3.8 billion in total liquidity, consisting of 34,012 BTC (valued at ~$2.8 billion) and $946.5 million in unrestricted cash.

MARA’s Bitcoin sales could add further downward pressure on the price in the short term, particularly as demand remains weak and U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded nearly $240 million in outflows yesterday.

However, the move could be positive from a longer-term perspective. By repaying $750 million in debt, MARA freed up 17,477 BTC previously pledged as collateral, significantly reducing the risk of forced liquidations if Bitcoin falls further.

MARA’s move reflects a broader shift across the Bitcoin mining industry. Publicly traded miners are no longer simply holding Bitcoin; they are actively managing their treasuries, reducing debt and expanding into energy and AI infrastructure. This could make the sector more resilient during market downturns and reduce the risk of forced Bitcoin selling.
