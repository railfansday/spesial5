AI banking agent scored 100% on its own tests then failed 92 of 97 real-world simulations

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In a Uniphore benchmark, 13 Claude Opus 4.8 builds gained 16.9 percentage points on their own tests and 3.2 points on cases held back from them.

AI systems tasked with building business agents often gave their work near-perfect grades on tests they wrote themselves. In a Nov. 19 preprint, researchers at Uniphore found that the grades could improve far more than scores on cases withheld from the builders during development.

The researchers used AppSci-Bench, a benchmark that puts AI builders to work in seven simulated business workflows, including invoice review, customer relationship management and airline rebooking. A builder could ask a simulated business user what the agent needed to do, then develop it using the tools and data available in that setting.

In the test runs that allowed revisions, builders could write their own tests and keep changes that raised the score. After the builder submitted an agent, the benchmark ran it on separate cases withheld during development. The researchers released the benchmark code and recorded results alongside the preprint.

Uniphore researchers found that 86 of the 132 builds with a recorded self-test scored at least 99% on the tests their builders wrote. The researchers tracked 13 Claude Opus 4.8 builds that could revise their agents, comparing the first round of testing with the last. For those builds, scores rose an average of 16.9 points on tests the model wrote and 3.2 points on the withheld cases. In six of the 24 revision steps the researchers examined in that subset, the builder's grade rose while the withheld-case score held steady.

In a broader comparison of 252 builds using Claude Opus 5, Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5, the researchers found no statistically significant difference in withheld-case scores when they switched the test-and-revise loop on or off. That result held in both the researchers’ setup and the models’ native coding tools, Claude Code and Codex, after accounting for multiple comparisons.

One banking build showed how a test could reinforce a bad assumption. GPT-5.5 assumed it could not look customers up by email or name even though both options were available. One test it wrote rewarded asking for a customer ID, and the agent ended up refusing the other lookups. The agent eventually scored a perfect 1.0 on its builder’s tests. In the withheld trial the researchers examined, it succeeded on five of 97 cases.

Meta used a live search test after its AI agents screened candidate changes offline.

For a team evaluating a banking agent, a test that supplies only an email address could expose the same blind spot. Keeping a set of cases outside development lets the team see how the submitted agent handles situations its builder’s tests may never have covered.
