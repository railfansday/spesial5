Man who killed three during psychotic episode ‘had been released after catalogue of failures’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Alexander Lewis-Ranwell was arrested and let go twice by police in the 48 hours before he killed three men in their 80s in Exeter

A man with paranoid schizophrenia who killed three people while experiencing a psychotic episode had been released from custody after a “catalogue of failures” by police and medical professionals, an investigation has found.

Alexander Lewis-Ranwell was arrested and released twice by police in the 48 hours before he killed Anthony Payne, 80, and the twin brothers Roger and Dick Carter, 84, in their homes in Exeter in 2019.

Officers were found to have ignored warnings from a national database as well as a mental health assessor, according to a joint investigation by the Sunday Times and BBC Radio 4’s File On 4 Investigates.

Numerous failings were found in hours of police video, detention logs and witness testimony. The medical professional who was sent to examine Lewis-Ranwell, 27, did not have expertise in mental health and dismissed his unpredictable behaviour.

There were also failures to record and share key details that would have built up a picture of the severity of Lewis-Ranwell’s mental illness. Officers did not know that by law they had the power to detain him under the Mental Health Act without a medical assessment. Warnings from Lewis-Ranwell’s mother, that he was having a psychotic episode and should be kept in custody, were also ignored.

The families of the three men have called on the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) to open an investigation into Devon and Cornwall police, but they are still waiting for answers and an apology. The watchdog has said that the force should investigate itself, but they have heard nothing for two years.

Two days before the murders took place, Lewis-Ranwell was arrested after breaking into a farm. After his release he was arrested a second time for attacking a farmer with a 1.2-metre (4ft) logging saw. Despite exhibiting disturbing behaviour in custody – police bodycam footage shows him semi-naked, hissing, spitting and punching at the hatch window to his cell, while ranting about paedophiles and seeing animals – he was released again.

Following a rampage that lasted hours and in which he threatened multiple people, he killed the three men, bludgeoning Payne with a hammer and killing the twins with a spade.

A trial at Exeter crown court in December 2019 found Lewis-Ranwell not guilty by reason of insanity. He was given a hospital restriction order, where he will stay until agencies are confident he could be released back into the community.

One psychiatrist who studied the case told the court he believed Lewis-Ranwell, a former public schoolboy from Croyde, north Devon, was “one of the illest people” he had come across.

Last month an inquest found failings by police led to his release after a doctor missed “clear signs of delusion and disordered thinking”.

Amanda Taylor told the Sunday Times that her family was still seeking justice for her uncles the Carter brothers and that they want the IOPC to revisit the case.

“All I want is for just one of the agencies involved to stand up and say: ‘We are so sorry. Serious mistakes were made. We recognise the devastating consequences of those failures and we will do everything in our power to make sure this never happens again,’” she said.

Lewis-Ranwell’s case is one of several where failures from the police and mental health services to manage risk have resulted in tragedy.

A review by the Care Quality Commission of the treatment of Valdo Calocane, who has paranoid schizophrenia and killed three people in Nottingham in 2023, concluded that a “series of errors and misjudgments” led to him being discharged, despite repeatedly not taking medication and showing signs of aggression.

The report concluded that there are “systemic issues with community mental health care which, without immediate action, will continue to pose an inherent risk to patient and public safety”.

NHS England has since strengthened guidance around community mental health support for people with severe mental illnesses who face high risk or struggle to engage with services.

Noting that there are an estimated 100 to 120 mental health-related killings in the UK every year, Julian Hendy, of the victims’ charity Hundred Families, said: “In my view, had the lessons in the Lewis-Ranwell case been learnt earlier, and effective action taken, there is a real chance that the Nottingham attacks could well have been prevented. Services aren’t learning and totally innocent people are losing their lives as a result.”

A police spokesperson said the force was addressing “areas of learning” and would reflect on the recommendations of the inquest jury, adding: “Our thoughts, as always, remain with the loved ones of Anthony Payne and Dick and Roger Carter.”

The IOPC said the family had a right to ask for a review of the internal investigation and that it had not yet received a request.
