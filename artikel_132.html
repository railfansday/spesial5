Leave.EU and Vote Leave veterans launch rival campaigns to defend Brexit

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Andy Wigmore says there will be no animosity this time between his Enough Official group and Michael Gove-backed Stay Out movement

The Brexit wars were a bitter period in political history, fraught with tension and badmouthing – and that was just between the different leave campaigns.

Now as a new incarnation of Dominic Cummings’ Vote Leave looks set to re-enter the political fray, so too does a rival anti-EU campaign from the team behind Leave.EU, who ran their own unofficial campaign backed by Nigel Farage.

This time, both have vowed to be cordial with each other, but in 2016 there were significant creative differences.

Many of the names are the same. Oliver Lewis, a veteran of Vote Leave who was a Brexit negotiator under Boris Johnson, will chair Stay Out, a new campaign group backed by the former cabinet minister Michael Gove and under the interested eye of Cummings, though he has no formal role.

The launch of the campaign video – which claimed the costs of rejoining the EU would be £750m a week, and featured footage of recent riots in France and Spain and warnings about unchecked migration – caught No 10 off-guard.

On Monday they were joined by the launch of a parallel campaign from Andy Wigmore’s Leave.EU, which will be called Enough Official. Wigmore told the Telegraph he wanted to work with Lewis’s group. “There’s no split camps this time,” he said. “We’re all going to support each other, and we will support anyone who’s going to fight this.”

A number of high-profile political figures seem to be willing to re-enter the fray from retirement, including Johnson. The former prime minister and leave campaigner told the Telegraph it would be “madness to escalate the Brexit issue as your political priority … [Andy Burnham] won’t win – because the reality is that people are not fools.”

Johnson hinted to the paper that he would be willing to be part of a campaign to stay out of the bloc. “There are plenty of people who are only too happy to make the points I’ve made – but I will of course make the points that I’ve made,” he said.

One senior campaigner said it was an irresistible opportunity for many former political advisers who were now out of Westminster politics. “They’re very effective guys that have been sitting around waiting for a chance to make themselves useful. A lot of talent on the right is currently off making money or kicking their feet. Rejoining is the one thing that will bring them back,” they said.

Veterans of both Vote Leave and Leave.EU are split between those who are willing to campaign to stay out and others who believe the political landscape is now far more uncertain.

One senior figure said they “had PTSD” from the Brexit period and vowed to steer clear. “I think that is exactly the reason why the prime minister should not be reopening this debate, but I don’t think anyone should be under any illusion it would be an easy win for either side.”

Another campaign veteran said both pro- and anti-EU campaigns would find it more difficult to get businesses to pick a side or to get public figures involved. “I think that many are scarred from previous involvement, but this would work in both directions,” they said.

Several of those involved in the campaigns said they expected serving politicians to be kept at arm’s length – including Farage.

At a press conference on Monday, the Conservative leader, Kemi Badenoch, said she would not engage with campaigning on Brexit. “We need to stop obsessing about Europe and whether to be in or out, and actually get on with doing the things that we are elected to do,” she said.

“Sort out the economy, fund our defence, cut welfare, get jobs for people, make sure that businesses can create those jobs. We don’t need to be in the EU to do any of that.”

Badenoch said she believed that voters would decide to stay out of the EU, but added: “I’m not interested in engaging in this debate. They [Labour] want to distract us by making us talk about Brexit, because if we’re talking about that then we’re not talking about the government’s failures. I’m not falling for that trick.”

Launching the campaign over the weekend, Lewis said Stay Out would make the argument on the economics and on immigration – and on the risk of reopening divisions.

“The bloc is also getting bigger, with Albania, Moldova and the western Balkans all in the queue to join, and Brussels wants Britain back in as one of the few countries rich enough to pay for it,” he said. “The country has had enough of arguing about Europe, and it is time to move on and spend our money on our own priorities.”

Gove, who was a co-convener of Vote Leave, said a move towards rejoining would “commit the country to another decade of fractious negotiations and a return to the divisions we have spent years putting behind us, at the very moment people are desperate for Westminster to get on with delivering on their priorities.”

Speaking on Monday before a crunch EU summit where Burnham hopes to clinch a new deal with the EU on food and drink and emissions trading, as well as a youth mobility deal, the foreign secretary, Ed Miliband, said the government respected the 2016 result.

“What we’re very focused on is actually what’s in front of us, which is this summit and how we can just help the British people in the situation we face,” he told BBC Breakfast.
