Chipotle Mexican Grill vs. e.l.f. Beauty: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Chipotle's fresh-ingredient model and e.l.f. Beauty's value-driven innovation pull in very different directions for investors weighing growth against profitability.

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 2:27AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Chipotle Mexican Grill ›

Investors seeking resilient growth among consumer stocks often weigh the merits of dominant restaurant leaders against rising retail disruptors. Choosing between Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG -2.07%) and e.l.f. Beauty (ELF -5.01%) requires understanding how each manages scale and profitability.

Chipotle relies on its massive physical footprint and premium brand perception to drive consistent returns. Meanwhile, e.l.f. Beauty uses a lean manufacturing model and digital prowess to gain market share from established legacy brands. This comparison explores which business model offers the most compelling path forward.

Chipotle operates over 4,000 company-owned restaurants across North America and Europe, focusing on a limited menu of burritos and bowls made from fresh ingredients. The business strategy centers on high-volume throughput, particularly through its Chipotlane drive-thru format, which enhances convenience for digital orders. While the company owns its locations, it relies heavily on third-party delivery services, which accounted for roughly 16% of food and beverage revenue in the latest reporting year.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $12.7 billion, representing a growth rate of 7.9% compared to the prior period. The company remains highly profitable, generating net income of $1.4 billion during those twelve months. This performance reflects a steady net margin of 10.8%, as the company successfully balanced higher ingredient costs with incremental menu price increases to maintain its bottom line.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio stood at 1.2x, which measures the ability to cover short-term obligations with assets that can be converted to cash within a year. The debt-to-equity ratio was 1.8x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity to measure financial leverage. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was $1.4 billion, providing ample capital for the company to continue expanding its restaurant count without seeking external financing.

e.l.f. Beauty is a multi-brand powerhouse in the beauty and retail stocks space, offering clean and vegan cosmetics at price points significantly lower than prestige competitors. The company sells its products through major retail partners including Target (TGT -0.97%), Walmart (WMT +1.51%), and Amazon (AMZN -4.10%). By maintaining a rapid innovation cycle, the company can bring trending beauty products to market much faster than traditional industry giants.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $1.9 billion, a substantial increase of 39.9% over the previous year. This rapid expansion was accompanied by net income of $86.6 million, resulting in a net margin of about 4.5%. While the company has prioritized market share gains and brand acquisitions, it has remained consistently profitable during this period of high-velocity growth.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio was strong at 2.3x, indicating a robust liquidity position. The debt-to-equity ratio was 0.8x, suggesting the company maintains a conservative level of financial leverage. In the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, free cash flow was $190.1 million. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented 40.9% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Chipotle faces significant risks related to food safety and food-borne illnesses, which are exacerbated by its reliance on fresh produce and raw ingredients. It operates in a highly competitive restaurant industry, facing pressure from traditional chains like Starbucks (SBUX -1.28%), meal kits, and delivery aggregators. Additionally, labor shortages and the need to comply with complex employment laws, including fair workweek and just cause legislation, pose risks to operational consistency and cost management.

e.l.f. Beauty trades at a lower earnings multiple than Chipotle, although both companies currently command similar valuations when measured against their trailing sales.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Chipotle and e.l.f. Beauty represent two very different consumer stocks. The former is an established player with higher margins while e.l.f. Beauty is a rising star, producing outsized sales growth. This makes choosing between them a tough decision. Ultimately, I would recommend Chipotle.

The restaurant chain's robust net margin of 10.8% compared to e.l.f. Beauty's 4.5% is hard to ignore, as is its return on equity of 52.5% versus e.l.f. Beauty's 7.4% over the trailing 12 months. These factors contribute to its higher share price valuation.

Moreover, Chipotle expects to end 2026 with 350 to 370 new restaurants with 80% of them including a Chipotlane, contributing to the company's throughput. It forecasts 340 to 360 new restaurants in 2027, which provides a clear runway to revenue expansion next year.

e.l.f. Beauty is seeing spectacular sales growth. In its fiscal second quarter, ended Sept. 30, it posted an impressive 51% year-over-year increase to $520.8 million, representing the 31st consecutive quarter of net sales growth. However, its slimmer margins, heavy marketing spend, tough year-over-year comparisons, and supply-chain execution could weigh on earnings momentum, further tilting toward Chipotle as the better stock for 2027.
