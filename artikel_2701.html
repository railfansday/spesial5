Raptors Injury Updates: Latest On RJ Barrett, Collin Murray-Boyles, And Kawhi Leonard

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

It hasn't been easy-going for the Toronto Raptors in 2026-27. At ninth in the East (5-10), they are barely above the play-in line and far from where they expected to be at the start of the season. Now, as they endure a growing list of injured players, the task of climbing out of their early hole is looking increasingly daunting.

With star forward Kawhi Leonard already out and sidelined for months, they were leaning on former Knicks swingman RJ Barrett to help carry the load, but he's set to miss at least the next two weeks with a right hamstring strain.

"Forward RJ Barrett has been diagnosed with a mild strain of the right hamstring," wrote the Raptors in a statement. "An update will be provided in two weeks."

Barrett, 26, is averaging 20.9 points, 6.4 rebounds, 4.3 assists, 0.4 steals, and 0.5 blocks per game on 47.3% shooting from the field this season. His play on both ends has been huge for the Raptors, and his absence will be felt over the next few weeks.

Young big man Collin Murray-Boyles has been another critical piece for Toronto. The 6'7″ athlete has started in eight games this season, but he's now in concussion protocol, with his timeline contingent on when he can complete the return-to-participation process.

"After reassessment Wednesday evening, forward Collin Murray-Boyles has been placed in the NBA's concussion protocol as a precaution," the Raptors added. "He will complete a return-to-participation process designed to ensure he is symptom-free before resuming full basketball activities."

With averages of 9.3 points, 4.6 rebounds, 1.8 assists, 1.0 steals, and 1.1 blocks per game on 46.7% shooting this season, Murray-Boyles is an important part of the formula in Toronto, and his return will be one to watch over the coming days.

Of course, the biggest story involves Kawhi Leonard, whose mysterious injury continues to baffle critics and fans alike. Despite having a stated goal of playing 65 games this season, Leonard has already missed all but two of their first 15 games. The injury, which has been identified as nagging knee inflammation, has been a career-long struggle for Kawhi, and the Raptors are taking an extra-cautious approach given his extensive injury history.

"Flare-ups are generally triggered by overuse and the only way for it to heal is through treatment and rest, which is why the process has been slow," reported Josh Lewenberg. "What Toronto doesn't want is for him to come back and suffer another setback. The hope is that once he's back, he's back for good. However, given the nature of the condition and Leonard's overall medical history, they can't be sure of that. To believe they're getting anything close to 65 games of peak Kawhi is wishful thinking."

Leonard, 35, was rusty in his first two games for the Raptors this season, averaging 19.5 points, 7.5 rebounds, 1.5 assists, 3.5 steals, and 0.5 blocks per game on 32.4% shooting. Still, there were high hopes for where he could lead this team alongside Scottie Barnes and RJ Barrett.

Now, it seems the Raptors may not even make the playoffs as Leonard's return remains a total mystery. Between him, Barrett, and Boyles, the Raptors are facing a tough reality, and if something doesn't change, this could turn into another lost season for everyone involved.
