Fantasy Football Rankings: Hayden Winks' RB Blueprint for Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Blueprint is my weekly column and basically everything that I use to build out the most accurate rankings I can. There will be this RB column, a WR column and a combination column on the onesie positions. You will find film notes and endless charts. Maybe enough charts to stress test the Yahoo website. I kid.

It's impossible to get every ranking thoroughly early in the week, so be sure to keep checking in on these columns through Sunday morning. As always, my full rankings at every position are available.

1. Jahmyr Gibbs (vs. TB) - 127.5 proj. total yards

Gibbs is the 1.01 in fantasy points over replacement per game (+15.9) and only trails Jaxon Smith-Njigba in winning percentage on Yahoo. Seems good.

Tampa Bay has been stout against the position, ranking 32nd in half PPR points allowed to opposing backfields and holding recent primary rushers like Bijan Robinson (RB28), D'Andre Swift (RB50), Chuba Hubbard (RB34) and Jaylen Warren (RB23) in check. Detroit heads into the home matchup with an implied total of 25.25 points (ninth) as 7-point home favorites. It's a difficult spot, but it's getting nearly impossible to not have him as the RB1 overall every week.

The Chiefs seem to be planning for Kenneth Walker III (groin) to rest another week after Johnson more than held his own in last week's blowout win. The rookie, who reminds me of a Bucky Irving type on film, handled 22 carries and six receptions, leading to 32.1 half PPR points on 20.6 expected points. Those are obviously elite RB1 numbers. Kansas City simply doesn't have another RB it trusts for the moment, and the offense is firing on all cylinders against mediocre opponents.

The Cardinals are just that. They've allowed the fifth-most rush YPG (130) and were just schooled by Blake Corum (119 total yards) last week. A huge 28.0-point team total as 15.5-point favorites (!!!!!!) awaits.

3. James Cook III (vs. MIA) - 110.5 proj. total yards

Cook ranks as the RB6 in half PPR points per game on RB8 usage across his last four contests, averaging 23 carries (second) and 109.8 rushing yards per game. He has scored a rushing touchdown in each of his last four games (what's new?), including a season-high 157 rushing yards on 18 carries in Week 10.

The Bills are expected to beat the Dolphins by 16.5 points with a bonkers 31-point team total. Miami just lost DTs Jordan Phillips and Zeek Biggers to injured reserve. I don't have to tell you this is a great spot.

4. Christian McCaffrey (vs. MIN) - 87.5 proj. total yards

When McCaffrey is in the United States of America this year, he averages 22.7 half PPR points (RB2) on 16.3 expected half PPR points (RB6). Turns out, Kaelon Black is a fine role player but nothing more than that to CMC. Remember to go outside in early September while the weather is nice.

Anyways, this will be a great test for the 49ers as they face the Vikings in Mexico City. They've been run on at times and won't have LB Blake Cashman, but that's about as negative as I can get on what otherwise is an elite defense. They've been fifth best against fantasy RBs this year (16.7 half PPR per game), though fantasy RB1s have held the line; James Cook III (RB7), Jahmyr Gibbs (RB5) and Jonathan Taylor (RB8). We can ding CMC's projection with the Niners projected for 24.25 points versus their current 31.75 points per game, but it can only go so far. He's a mid-range RB1.

5. Chuba Hubbard (vs. BAL) - 109.5 proj. total yards

The Ravens defense has been one of the more underwhelming units. Their secondary has been better than their run defense, to the point that even Omarion Hampton ran well up the gut against them last Monday night. It's a shorter week for Baltimore, who is now trending out of the playoff picture. We'll see how much fight it has left. The Panthers have a 23.0-point team total (12th) as near-field-goal favorites.

6. Javonte Williams (vs. TEN) - 94.5 proj. total yards

Let's keep this simple: Williams is an upside RB1 whenever the Cowboys have a 31.5-point team total, as they do here.

The Titans have allowed nearly 1,500 yards over their last three games, the most in a 3-game stretch in their franchise history. Trading away mega-star DT Jeffery Simmons and Pro Bowl-caliber S Amani Hooker won't help. The starting RB against Tennessee has cleared 100 total yards in four straight contests.

7. Bucky Irving (at DET) - 85.5 proj. total yards

This assumes Jalon Daniels is back at QB with Baker Mayfield (foot) picking up a new injury during the bye week. The Bucs are likely going to be extremely run-heavy, as they've been for the past two months with the OL smoking defenses up front. Irving has been the RB7 on RB11 usage this season, with 21.0+ half PPR points in 4 of 5 recent games. He's averaged 22.2 carries during that stretch, with games against pretty easy opponents. That's too big to fail, especially given how slippery he is on tape.

The Lions are a pass funnel typically, but the losses of LB Jack Campbell, EDGE Aidan Hutchinson and CB D.J. Reed (questionable) make them fairly bad in both phases. They still have some DTs to worry about, but the Bucs should be able to run it here, despite the spreadsheets suggesting otherwise. Tampa Bay will attempt to bleed out the clock as 7-point road dogs with a team total of 18.25 points (21st). Irving should absorb a lot of that team total.

8. Derrick Henry (at CAR) - 78.5 proj. total yards

The Ravens are going to pass more with Jacoby Brissett at QB and with the OL struggling, especially when first-round rookie LG Vega Ioane isn't on the field. Henry can still score goal-line touchdowns as he did on Monday night, but a real reset is warranted in terms of efficiency and rushing volume if the Ravens aren't expected to be crushing teams as often as they would've with Lamar Jackson, who is hoping to return around Week 16 if things go to plan. In the meantime, it's possible Henry catches an extra target or two based on the scheme changing, but that won't even out the loss of projected touchdowns. He'll settle in as a boom-bust RB2 the rest of the way.

The Panthers have cleaned things up with better health and a new DC recently, but Carolina has bled fantasy points to RBs (24.8, third) across the season. It was Jonah Coleman (RB6) two weeks ago and Josh Jacobs (RB11) and Bucky Irving (RB2) before that. A 21.0-point team total isn't the worst thing this week, with six teams on bye and five others projected for fewer than 17.0.

9. Jonah Coleman (vs. LV) - 99.5 proj. total yards

With R.J. Harvey (knee) on IR, the backfield has been down to just two. Coleman leapfrogged J.K. Dobbins over the last pair of games, finishing as the RB7 and RB9 in expected half PPR points. Dobbins won't play on passing downs, so Coleman is grabbing some receptions alongside his rushing role. He has been just fine on tape in a good situation.

Speaking of, the Raiders are generous when it comes to fantasy points for RBs. They've allowed, oh, just 176 rushing yards per game this month. Both Seahawks RBs played well behind three backup linemen, and CMC (RB4), Braelon Allen (RB7) and Kyren Williams (RB12) were fantasy RBs this month. Denver is listed as 10-point home favorites with an implied team total of 25.25 points (eighth).

10. Tony Pollard (at DAL) - 85.5 proj. total yards

Pollard has been the RB8, RB3 and RB1 most recently, and it's simply been because he's breaking a ton of tackles. It's hard to believe it'll be sustainable, as star LG Peter Skoronski is on injured reserve, but Pollard is also the RB10 in expected points this month, with Cam Ward taking his game up a tier. It's still a painful offense on the whole and the floor can fall out at any point. It's best to stay tethered to his RB15 on RB16 usage standing across the entire year sample. Hopefully Carnell Tate (shoulder) can return to make the offense more potent.

The Cowboys are pathetic on defense. They've removed their sitting DC from play-calling duties, while their starters have missed a ton of tackles in crucial spots. They also don't play sound against the run, which is why they've allowed the most fantasy points to RBs (27.0) and most rushing yards per game (154) this season. Last week's 44.3 eruption from Christian McCaffrey against them was the best fantasy outing of the year by any player at any position. Beyond that, opponents facing Dallas have passed at a 47% rate in neutral situations, which is the third-lowest neutral pass rate allowed in the league. Tennessee enters as 8.5-point road underdogs with an implied team total of 23 points (13th).

11. Chase Brown (at WAS) - 74.5 proj. total yards

The Bengals have largely abandoned the run, which arguably isn't even a bad thing for Brown. Cincy is up to 30.9 points per game because it's first in passing success rate. Brown has a better chance of lucking into touchdowns if the team passes its way down to the goal line. We saw that happen last week when each of his scores came through the air, both out of structure.

Aside from the scores, Brown tallied eight receptions as a check-down option with space to operate, as defenses are going to have to play softer and softer coverage against these alien WRs. Brown can be forgettable as a talent on a severely pass-heavy offense, and still finish as a low-end RB1 in fantasy due to the scoring bonanza. He's the RB1 in receptions per game (5.2).

The next victim of the Bengals' offense will be the Commanders, who don't have two of their top DTs and were struggling through the air before anyway. The Bengals are up to a 30.5-point team total.

12. D'Andre Swift (vs. NO) - 83.5 proj. total yards

Kyle Monangai (toe) is back. Here are Swift's splits with him since Week 5 of last year, when the offense became the version we have today.

For context, 13.9 half PPR points is the RB16 right now, while 13.0 expected points is the RB17. Those are probably closer to his per-game floor the rest of the way, but it is a real reset for Swift's baseline projection. He can still have RB1 moments with Monangai. They will just happen less often.

The Saints are a great spot to sneak in a fantasy RB1 moment! They've allowed the second-most fantasy points to RBs on the sixth-most rushing yards per game (130). Chuba Hubbard ran right through tackles last week, something that's happened way too often this year. Chicago has a monster 30.0-point team total, so both backs are obvious starts.

13. Jaylen Warren (at PHI) - 95.5 proj. total yards

Warren has settled in as an upside RB2 with Rico Dowdle back in the mix. He's averaged 14.0 carries and 2.8 receptions this month, leading to RB17 on RB19 usage. The Steelers haven't played great ball on offense over that stretch and just watched LG Mason McCormick dislocate his ankle last week. The OL being great this season has certainly helped Warren, who has been a top-5 RB to my eyes this year beyond that.

The matchup is reassuring. This is an eruption spot, looking at the EPA numbers on the ground. The Eagles have struggled all year, then traded away run-stuffing DT Jordan Davis at the deadline. There are fewer reasons to be afraid of what used to be an impossible opponent, and the lines reflect that. Pittsburgh enters as 4.5-point road favorites with an implied team total of 21.5 points (15th).

14. Bhayshul Tuten (at NYG) - 92.5 proj. total yards

Tuten set a career high with 28.1 expected half PPR points last week, with 26 carries and three receptions. Many of those were failed goal-line attempts, but Tuten did score late en route to an RB10 on RB1 usage outing. He's now the RB10 on RB17 usage this season as a low-end RB1 with weekly upside. Chris Rodriguez Jr. will vulture the occasional touchdown, but otherwise, it's all Tuten now. The only marginal issue is that the already suspect Jaguars OL is now missing C Robert Hainsey (knee) for the next couple of weeks.

The Giants rank 25th in half PPR points allowed to opposing RBs at 17.1 points per game. That includes ranking 23rd in points allowed on RB rushing plays (11.3 per game) and 20th on RB receiving plays (5.8 per game). Opposing lead rushers have routinely been held in check against them recently, with no RB clearly the top-24 over their past three games. DROY favorite Arvell Reese has a lot to do with that. Jacksonville heads on the road as 6-point favorites with an implied team total of 26.25 points (seventh). It's more of a mediocre matchup for Tuten.

15. David Montgomery (vs. IND) - 67.5 proj. total yards

Montgomery got in a full practice last Friday before being ruled out, but has practiced in full leading up to Thursday Night Football this week. Woody Marks (concussion) won't play, leaving Monty with Emari Demercado, who missed some lanes in the second half of last game. It won't be a surprise if the desperate Texans use Montgomery as a bell cow despite him being out since Week 5. They don't have a real choice. Of course, the Texans' bell cow RB hasn't been very valuable so far. Since Week 6, the Texans RBs are averaging 16.8 expected half PPR points per game with very mid efficiency. Montgomery finishing with 15-20 expected points shouldn't come as a surprise this week, considering the opponent.

The Colts haven't been competitive all year, especially without their best linemen on both sides of the ball. This month, Indy has allowed the sixth-most rushing yards per game (144). This season, they've allowed the sixth-most points to fantasy RBs (23.0). There really are no excuses for Houston this time around. They own a 28.5-point team total at home as 12.5-point favorites.

16. Omarion Hampton (vs. NYJ) - 71.5 proj. total yards

Hampton set season highs in fantasy points (17.0 half PPR) and usage (15.9 expected) last week, in their first win of the year. Hampton handled 22 carries in a run-heavy game plan that continued because the scoreboard finally allowed for it. The Chargers are first in neutral run rate (61%), so don't be surprised if Hampton's fantasy performances tick up if they can stay more competitive after a brutal schedule. You can see the extra carries in the second half of last week here:

On tape, Hampton was used slightly differently last week. He handled nine pitches, which is the second-most by a player in a single game all year. Instead of being pointed to the perimeter, many of them got him going straight downhill, where his attributes are maximized. They also ran more man concepts instead of the wide zone stuff OC Mike McDaniel typically calls for. It's a meeting in the middle between player and coach, one that was long overdue.

This week, the Chargers are favored. I repeat, the Chargers are favored against the Jets. They don't have their best run-stopping DT right now either, which has helped opposing teams rack up 154 rushing yards per game this month (fourth). James Cook III (RB7) and Kenneth Walker III (RB1) just went nuclear against them, but even Ollie Gordon II had 103 total yards and a score in Week 7, too. This is easily the best it's looked for Hampton since the games started this year.

17. Saquon Barkley (vs. PIT) - 75.5 proj. total yards

Barkley is impossible to get a grasp on due to his injuries, the moving parts on the OL and now Joe Milton at QB. In a part-time role on a lousy offense in three games before the bye, Barkley was the RB25, RB21 and RB20 on RB26 usage. He looked good when he was on the field, and the OL did seem to be in a bit of a better spot on tape. I can see him out-kicking those marks after the bye, which hopefully has allowed him to get healthier. The Eagles' offense is in a spot where they can fully clunker at any time, so the floor doesn't exist.

The matchup will be a challenge, of course. Milton versus the Steelers' pressure will put the team in bad spots. Pittsburgh is only allowing 83 rushing yards per game (30th), as teams have essentially stopped trying to run on them in neutral situations (61% pass, first). Philly can't afford to do that, so it'll be running into brick walls for Barkley, with the hopes he springs a few of them for explosives. A 17.0-point team total works against those odds. Barkley is a volume-based RB2 in the most optimistic of projections.

18. Cam Skattebo (vs. JAX) - 70.5 proj. total yards

Skattebo is back to bell-cow work with Tyrone Tracy Jr. on IR and Najee Harris released. Devin Singletary failed in that role when Skattebo missed in Week 9, then Skattebo delivered an RB5 on RB6 usage outing in Week 10 against the Commanders. They've had extended rest to get Skattebo prepared for another heavy workload in a balanced offense. It won't be surprising to see Skattebo emerge as a fantasy RB2 for the rest of the way, especially if his three grabs from Week 10 hold up.

The Jaguars defense has been stingy to opposing backfields, ranking 29th in half PPR points allowed to RBs. They did concede an RB8 weekly finish to Tony Pollard in Week 10 without DT Jordan Davis and safety Jessie Bates active, though Jonathan Taylor managed just 4.7 points (RB37) against them in Week 8 when they did have a star run-stuffing DT. Opposing offenses have leaned heavily into the pass against Jacksonville with a 59% neutral pass rate (second highest). I doubt the Giants will do that too much given their coaches and personnel, but it's a difficult matchup regardless. New York enters as 6-point home underdogs with an implied team total of 20.25 points (18th).

19. Breece Hall (at LAC) - 70.5 proj. total yards

Hall has been the RB16 and RB21 in usage since returning from a quad injury. He's easily paced Braelon Allen in touches and played more receiving snaps last week after being eased in the game prior. Hall will stick in the 14-18 touch range most outings, while splitting the goal line role with the more downhill Allen. That'll put a cap on Hall's projections each week, but as long as Geno Smith is healthy, the OL is in good enough shape for them to remain balanced on offense. Hall is a fine RB2, while Allen is a shakier FLEX, even during the byes.

The Chargers represent an above-average matchup overall (duh), ranking 11th in half PPR points allowed to opposing RBs at 21.1 per game. Los Angeles is sixth in points allowed on the ground, though they rank 27th against backs via the air. They're still without Derwin James. New York travels out west as 3.5-point road underdogs with an implied total of 19.0 points (19th).

20. Kyle Monangai (vs. NO) - 55.5 proj. total yards

In 18 games with D'Andre Swift since Week 5 of last year, when the Bears offense ascended to the spot they are today, Monangai has averaged 9.9 half PPR points on 10.0 expected points. That's closer to his lower-end outcome at this point, and the Bears seem antsy to get Monangai back so they can begin spelling Swift after a stretch of too much volume. In four games this year, he's the RB16 (14.2) on RB21 usage (12.6 expected).

Fresh off the bye, the Bears should be at the peak of their powers. A date with a Saints team that can't tackle awaits. Chicago projects for 30.0 points. Monangai is an upside FLEX, possibly a straight-up RB2 given the constraints of the week.

21. Ollie Gordon II (at BUF) - 74.5 proj. total yards

Gordon is now playing with fire after ripping off explosives as a new starter back in September. He's down to RB25 on RB13 usage this past month, thanks to 20 carries and just 1.5 receptions per game. His efficiency could crater without Malik Willis, as it did last week when he was the RB18 on RB10 usage. Trickier defenses than the Colts will give Kyle McCord more trouble.

The Bills are waking up on defense with a new DC. They've seen multiple true breakouts from their DL, and they're more shored up at S with Amani Hooker in the mix and moving Cole Bishop more into the box where he's at his best. Even while going through some pains, the Bills are still only allowing 93.6 rushing yards per game (25th). It's a bad matchup for Ollie with a team total of just 14.5 points.

22. Jonathan Taylor (at HOU) - 76.5 proj. total yards

Taylor is no longer a fantasy RB1. Over the past month, with Seth McGowan earning more change-of-pace work, Taylor has dropped to RB16 in usage at 12.9 expected half PPR points per game. This matches the historical splits I mentioned last week based on how many points the Colts finish with. When Indy is held to under 21 points, Taylor is largely a fantasy RB2. Last week, McGowan converted a goal-line score on a drive Taylor was playing on. Taylor was stuffed on another drive's goal-line attempt, but it's no longer true bell-cow usage for Taylor.

Beyond that, the Colts ruled out both of their starting OTs and obviously traded away star LG Quenton Nelson the weeks prior. Having all of this backup talent on a short week against the Texans' DL is beyond concerning. The Colts have a 16.0-point team total as 12.5-point dogs. With a real receiving corps and a healthy OL in Week 3, Taylor was held to 68 scoreless yards against Houston. He also had 39 scoreless yards against them in Week 18 with a backup QB.
