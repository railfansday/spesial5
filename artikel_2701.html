NBA Game Preview: Timberwolves vs Blazers Thread

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Well hello there, Blazers. Good to see you again. The Wolves welcome back the team from the Rose City on the second night of a double-header bout. In the "Brains of the Operation Bowl" between Wolves head coach Chris Finch and former Wolves assistant coach, now Blazer's head coach Micah Nori, Finch holds the lead with Micah looking to close the gap.

For more on that game, go and check out Troy Asseln's marvelous piece.

I have been thoroughly impressed with Nori's work thus far in coaching this Blazers team up to a top 10 offense. Damian Lillard looks like the Dame of old. They have not minimized Deni Avdija though how he's able to get the whistle he does while the Wolves are begging for a call is beyond me. And though currently injured, even Ja Morant is looking primed for redemption in this new system.

The Blazers will be looking for redemption of their own in Target Center tonight, lets see how the Wolves can rain on their parade.

Who: Minnesota Timberwolves (10-4) vs. Portland Trailblazers (9-4)

When: Tuesday, November 17th at 7:00 PM CT

Local TV: DAZN-MN, KATU, KUNP, BlazerVision

These back-to-back matchups against the same team thing has been around for a bit now. Since 2021, the Wolves and Blazers have played in two such matchups. The first they drew in 2021. The second in 2024, Portland pantsed the Wolves twice. With one game over in this series, Minnesota can even the score.

As simple as it sounds, a thing that is very different from most regular season play where a team is preparing for a whole new opponent in their next matchup, these same team back-to-backs allow for more of a playoff atmosphere. Counter punches are planned.

Basic schemes that were scouted, film that was watched, all of the preparation has now been tested. As such, each team has a better sense of how to take away the game plan of their familiar opponent.

More than that, there is pride on the line. No one likes losing. At the board game table or at the park basketball courts, a new game is called for by the losing side to avenge the defeat. This is a rare moment where it happens in professional basketball.

The Blazers are one of the most efficient shooting teams in basketball, ranking ninth overall in field goal percentage (46.3%). They take the fourth most three-point attempts (43.4) and third most free throw attempts (29.5), the latter stat being mostly due to Avdija.

Last night the Wolves kept the foul sheet mostly clean with 21 fouls to the Blazer's 28. Portland attempted seven more threes, but made only two more than Minnesota. Meanwhile the Wolves strong rebounding continued as the swallowed up 70 boards. No one could hit a consistent shot last night save for Terrance Shannon Jr., Donovan Clingan, and Avdija.

Both teams shot under 40% from the field and under 30% from three. This is to the Wolves advantage to junk up the game. In a hopefully more common occurrence, Minnesota shot over 70% from the free throw line to the tune of 30 extra points, almost half of them from TJ.

The Wolves played this game sans Anthony Edwards, Donte DiVincenzo and Jaylen Clark for only his second missed game of the season, this time on account of a sore hamstring. Towards the end of the game just before the starters were pulled, Jonathan Kuminga fell down and took an accidental knee in the back from a Blazers player, sustaining a shoulder injury. Minnesota had to call a timeout as the medical staff gently helped him to the bench before he exited the game into the locker room.

This afternoon, the Wolves announced he would be out due to a right shoulder contusion. Hopefully this is just a short-term absence and utilizing caution on the end of a back-to-back.

One the great storylines through this season has been Kuminga taking on primary point of attack defensive duties allowing Jaden McDaniels to roam. The painted area will still be patrolled by "Walking Top 10 Defense" Rudy Gobert, but with McDaniels more zeroed in on someone like Avdija, Portland will be able to puncture the Wolves shell and put the defense in the blender.

Championships and basketball immortality are now built on endurance and the nicks and bumps sustained during the regular season echo out all the louder when teams are asked to suit back up less than 24 hours from their last game. Those Wolves healthy enough to take the court need to rub some dirt in, and hold the line for much needed reinforcements.

The Timberwolves continue to junk things up, pressing on Avdija just far enough off his preferred spot without allowing him to hear the chirp chirp of the whistle he loves so dearly. The game stays knotted through the first half and the Wolves unlock their offense while locking up the Blazers in the second. TJ blazes trails of his own all the way to the cup for enough three point plays the old fashioned way that a bunch of hipsters start ordering Terrance Shannon's at the Loon and the barkeep gives back a knowing smile as they grab the rye, syrup, and bitters.

Our blogging buddies are over at Blazer's Edge. Be kind to our blazing buddies, the last thing we need between friends is a wedge.
