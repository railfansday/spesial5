Warriors rookie takes dramatic step in the barbershop; earns eye-popping comparison

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

NEW ORLEANS — You know the saying: Look good, feel good.

The bold and brash Warriors rookie slogged through the worst game of his young career the last time he took the court. He went 1-for-13 from the field and the Warriors, in their first game without Steph Curry, were handed nearly a 50-point loss by the red-hot Spurs.

Yaxel Lendeborg #1 of the Golden State Warriors and Stephon Castle #5 of the San Antonio Spurs play during the NBA Cup in San Antonio, Nov. 13, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

"He actually told me before he took his hood off and showed me his haircut," Golden State forward Georges Niang said. Or else, he may not have even recognized the rookie.

"Ain't nothing wrong with a low Caesar, man. He looks professional."

Lendeborg was so distraught over the loss that he needed a new hairdo that showed he meant business. Mission accomplished. Somewhere on a barbershop floor here in the Big Easy is the big hair that Lendeborg once embodied.

"He chopped all his hair off. You'll see," coach Steve Kerr said. "I didn't even recognize him. Apparently that's his response to getting beat by a hundred – to go right to the barber."

Yaxel Lendeborg of the Golden State Warriors arrives at the Frost Bank Center before a game against the San Antonio Spurs in San Antonio, Friday, Nov. 13, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

It wasn't quite a hundred, but it felt like it to Lendeborg, who used the same terminology in his customarily honest comments after the loss. After all, on his run to a national championship last season with Michigan, the Wolverines lost three games — by a combined 16 points.

"We just lost by like 100," Lendeborg said Saturday night. "So you know I'm f—ed up mentally right now. … I was flustered the entire game. Couldn't make a damn layup."

Kerr saw the comments after the game and used Lendeborg to make a point to the rest of the team when they regrouped for practice Sunday at Tulane University's gym.

"I loved how honest he is, how vulnerable he is. There's no pretense. He's just telling you exactly how he feels," Kerr said. "Our message today was how you respond."

Even in his short NBA career, Lendeborg hasn't had many tough nights personally. Over his first 12 games before Saturday night, he had scored in double figures 11 times, averaging 21.7.

Yaxel Lendeborg of the Golden State Warriors ties his shoes during a game against the San Antonio Spurs in San Antonio, Friday, Nov. 13, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Lendeborg and the Warriors get their first chance to put the blowout behind them Monday night in New Orleans against a Pelicans team they beat by 51 last week.

Of course, that was with a healthy Curry and the Warriors rolling amid an eight-game winning streak.

Without Curry, who will miss at least the next two weeks with a tear in his oblique, the Warriors' fate largely hinges on Lendeborg's ability to move on from his bad game.

"I'm excited for Yaxel," Niang said before tipoff. "He has raw emotion, which is great. It's like the purity of what's going on. Yaxel's just gonna say how he actually feels. And you're like, Oh, well I do feel that. Maybe I don't say it. But that's the best thing about him."

Niang, a veteran in his 10th season playing for his sixth different team, has shared a locker room with lots of different personalities. To find a comparison for Lendeborg, though, he had to go across sports — to the gridiron.

"I don't know if you find that [honesty] from any other player in the league of his stature," Niang said. "I'm not gonna do him a disservice and put these two in the same category, but you think about someone in football who's raw with his emotions, like Jameis Winston, right?

"Yaxel's probably a little more serious than some of the stuff you get from Jameis Winston, no disrespect, but that raw emotion is awesome to see."

Golden State Warriors player Yaxel Lendeborg looks on during a game against the San Antonio Spurs in San Antonio, Texas, Friday, Nov. 13, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Niang reminded Lendeborg that it was just one game of 82, and in November, without Curry, to boot. But, he said, "I think he likes to feel his emotions and kind of go through it."

Everyone processes losses differently. Kerr, a five-time champion as a player, joked that it took him until the final of his 15 seasons before he was able to

"My last year I was finally fine," he said. "My first 14 years I was a mess. I'm kind of kidding. It's one of the hardest things to do when you're a competitor — to get over a bad loss and a bad personal game. … It's not necessarily learning how to lose; it's about learning how to respond to a loss."
