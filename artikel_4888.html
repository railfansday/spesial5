🏛️ Backpack and tZERO Win the First Onchain Derivatives Approvals

For years, perps were the product American traders couldn’t legally touch. On Monday, that officially changed.

Chairman Michael Selig approved applications from Backpack and tZERO to run regulated derivatives exchanges and clearinghouses that execute and settle on blockchain rails. Selig called it “an important milestone in the evolution of American financial markets.”

Backpack got both pieces at once, a Designated Contract Market designation to run the exchange and a Derivatives Clearing Organization registration to clear its own trades. It plans to offer Americans round-the-clock leveraged crypto perps on a single global order book, accepting both fiat and crypto as collateral with cross-margin across positions. tZERO received its exchange approval a week earlier, after already holding clearing registration, and is building it into a derivatives vertical inside its infrastructure business.

Backpack US president Mark Wetjen, himself a former CFTC commissioner, framed it as “moving price discovery from offshore unregulated venues to the United States.” CEO Armani Ferrante said the firm enters markets “fully regulated from day one.” Backpack already runs licensed platforms in Europe, Asia and the UAE.

The CFTC has certainly been busy this month. They cleared single-stock perpetual futures on November 3. It issued guidance on November 5 telling federally regulated exchanges they generally cannot restrict access by state. Now it has approved blockchain-native exchanges and clearinghouses.

Selig is building an onshore home for every product the offshore venues were built to serve, and he is doing it through staff action rather than legislation, with one sitting commissioner and nomination hearings for two more this week.

Hyperliquid is the obvious candidate and also the one that has publicly said it doesn’t want this. Jeff Yan spent his Bloomberg interview this month arguing Hyperliquid is infrastructure rather than an exchange, and that regulators should oversee the firms deploying markets on top rather than the chain underneath. His US route is HIP-3 partners taking on the compliance burden, with ICE investing in Trade.xyz and Kraken’s parent reportedly in talks. Backpack getting a DCM and a DCO makes that argument harder, because it demonstrates a crypto-native venue can simply register.

Then there’s Lighter. It has run Robinhood Wallet’s perps since July on a dedicated instance with a 50/50 revenue split, which is the partner model rather than the registration model. The registered model opens up with a CFTC license, and many think Lighter could very well be next.

One thing’s certain—the CFTC is serious about bringing perps onshore. From Selig himself, “I look forward to building on that milestone.” And we will find out soon enough who’s next…
