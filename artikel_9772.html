Sitemap strategy for an 18-page tool site: what to include and what to leave out

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

My downloader site is small — eighteen pages, one tool, no blog archive the size of a library. My sitemap is not a discovery problem; it is an editing problem. Every URL I add is a promise to Google that the page is worth crawling, and on a tool site a lot of URLs are not. Here is how I decide what goes in and what stays out.

The sitemap is a static XML file I edit by hand. That sounds old-fashioned, but for eighteen pages it is the right call: an auto-generated sitemap would happily include /index.php?ref=twitter or the service worker file, and I would have to bolt on exclusion rules anyway. Hand-editing takes two minutes per new page and forces the real question: is this page worth having crawled?

Each entry carries a lastmod I update only when I actually change the page. I never fake it — a sitemap where every page claims yesterday's date teaches crawlers to ignore your dates, and then the one time you need them noticed they will not be.

I set priority sparingly — homepage on top, tool pages in the middle, trust pages at the bottom. Google treats these as mild hints at best, and I write them mostly for my own clarity: ranking my own pages is a useful exercise the next time I consider adding one. I skip changefreq entirely; for a tool site that changes when I ship, not on a schedule, guessing 'weekly' for a page I touched twice this year is fiction.

Once the file exists, getting it noticed is the easy part: I submitted it in Search Console and ping IndexNow from a small PHP script whenever a genuinely new page goes live, so Bing and Yandex learn about it within hours. But submission only matters because the contents are deliberate — a perfect sitemap of forty junk URLs is worse than a plain sitemap of eighteen good ones. Discovery is cheap; relevance is the actual work.

The sitemap on ramrom.io is twenty-one URLs today (it grew since I first counted). Every one is a page I would be happy to see in a search result. That is the whole strategy: the sitemap is a list of pages I am proud of, and anything that does not clear that bar does not get a slot.
