ZimaBlade NAS + Router Combo: PCIe Build Guide

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Posted on Nov 9 • Originally published at valtersit.com

A client called me at 11pm because their "NAS" had been silently corrupting backups for six weeks. The culprit wasn't ZFS, wasn't the drives, and wasn't the filesystem. It was a USB 3.0 enclosure with a UAS controller that reset itself under sustained load, dropped the pool offline, and re-imported it without complaint. They found out only when a restore failed during an actual incident. Forty terabytes of "backups" that were never tested, on a transport layer nobody should trust with a zpool.

That story matters here because the ZimaBlade is exactly the kind of board people buy to do everything at once — NAS, router, DNS, containers — on a single x86 SBC with a real PCIe slot. Done right, it's a beautiful little combo box. Done the way most forum posts suggest, it's a single point of failure with a cheerful front panel.

This guide is for sysadmins and security engineers who already know their way around zpool, iptables/nft, and a hypervisor, and want a build that won't embarrass them in six months. By the end you'll have a Proxmox host with PCIe passthrough, a ZFS NAS layer with sanoid snapshots, an OPNsense router VM on its own NIC, VLAN segmentation that doesn't loop, and a backup and monitoring story that actually proves itself.

This section exists because half the internet treats the ZimaBlade as either a Raspberry Pi killer or a datacenter replacement. It's neither. It's an x86 SBC with one PCIe lane, and that lane is what makes the NAS+router combo viable at all.

The board ships in configurations based on Intel Celeron N-series silicon (N3450/J4125-class parts depending on SKU — check the vendor spec sheet for the exact model you buy). The critical detail is the PCIe slot: physically x4, but on some board revisions it's electrically wired as x1. That distinction is the difference between "a quad-NIC works fine" and "a quad-NIC negotiates down and drops links under load."
