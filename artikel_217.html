NVIDIA reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO and president, effective immediately

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

So much for the board not intending to bring him back.

NVIDIA has reversed course and reinstated Jensen Huang as its CEO and president, effective immediately. The board said in a statement it held a series of meetings and discussions with Huang, who resigned 11 days ago following reports of multiple affairs. It added that it had "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and determined that reinstating Huang posed no risk to national security.

"I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution," Huang said. "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment. I want to thank the NVIDIA board for its confidence and deliberation through this process. I am especially grateful to Harvey Jones for his leadership as chair, and to Colette Kress and our executive staff for leading NVIDIA during this period. I am also deeply thankful to our employees, shareholders, leaders across the industry and partners around the world for their extraordinary support. Above all, I am grateful for the love and support of my family. I can't wait to get back to work."

NVIDIA said the board members voted unanimously to reappoint Huang, just a few days after Jones said on an earnings call that they did "not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." In his first public statement since stepping down on November 12, Huang said last week he "would be honored" to return to the helm of the company he cofounded. He said he'd "seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA's employees, investors, business partners and customers" for him step back into the CEO position.

Huang's reinstatement follows two weeks of turmoil at NVIDIA. The board said on November 8 it had opened an investigation into reports that Huang was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort." He admitted to having an affair following reports linking him to Alice Huang, a tech executive suspected of being a Chinese spy. On November 11, The New York Times reported Huang also had an affair with one of his subordinates at NVIDIA. By the following morning, he had departed the company. The board appointed Jones as board chair and Kress — NVIDIA's Chief Financial Officer and Executive Vice President — as interim CEO.

The board met on Sunday night to vote on Huang's reinstatement, according to Semafor, which previously reported that several directors had pushed for him to return. President Donald Trump was among the external figures who wanted Huang to be reappointed. Amid reports that he called into the meeting, Trump confirmed to CNBC he spoke to the board. "And I convinced them," he said. "I made the deal." He added that he told Huang to "work hard" in a conversation they had after the meeting.

"The country needs Jensen's leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the success and prosperity of not only the company, but the American economy. The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen's return to NVIDIA poses any risk to national security," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "Separately, NVIDIA should continue its thorough internal investigation of the company's compliance with US export controls in order to ensure that it is fulfilling its legal obligations and patriotic duty to protect US national security."

Last week, CNBC reported that Trump's investment accounts have traded between $27 million and $97.5 million of NVIDIA stock since he returned to office in January 2025, including a sale of between $500,000 and $1 million after he was reportedly briefed on Huang's affair in May. Trump's latest financial disclosure showed that, in September, he bought up to $265,000 worth of NVIDIA stock and sold up to $15,000 worth of his shares in the company. The White House told Reuters that third-party financial institutions manage Trump's portfolio. NVIDIA's share price skyrocketed by around 33 percent after Huang's reinstatement.

NVIDIA's board "believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Jones said in a statement. "We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the company. We are grateful to Colette Kress for her outstanding service as interim CEO, in addition to her continuing responsibilities as NVIDIA's Chief Financial Officer."

NVIDIA's investigation is ongoing and Huang said he'll fully cooperate. He's also set to testify before Congress on December 1. Huang will appear before the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Intelligence Committee. NVIDIA General Counsel Tim Teter will do the same the following day. It appears they'll testify behind closed doors, though their remarks will at least be transcribed.

Meanwhile, Alice Huang is reportedly under federal investigation over her alleged ties to Chinese companies that are said to have smuggled in chips from the likes of NVIDIA and Super Micro Computer in the face of US export restrictions. In 2023, she became a director of Singaporean cloud computing company Megaspeed, which is said to have since bought over $4 billion worth of NVIDIA chips and equipment. NVIDIA placed Megaspeed on its "no sell" list in 2025. Megaspeed said Alice Huang left the company two months later. The US Commerce and Justice departments said last week they'd been investigating Megaspeed over potential export control violations since 2024.

Last week, NVIDIA set up a committee to ensure the company's compliance with US export controls. It said on Monday it was recruiting a Chief Compliance Officer as well. NVIDIA added that it will "continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries."
