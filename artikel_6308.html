Flagship Dining: American Expands A La Carte Lounge Dining To Business Class

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Several weeks back, American announced plans to completely change the concept for its Flagship First Dining facilities, as the airline eliminates international first class. With this, we’re seeing Flagship First Dining simply rebranded as Flagship Dining, with business class passengers also getting access.

While these changes went “live” as of September 15, 2026, there’s now an additional update, as a third Flagship Dining facility has just opened, so you can now enjoy this experience in Dallas (DFW), Los Angeles (LAX), and Miami (MIA). Let’s cover all the details.

For years, American had Flagship First Dining, which are facilities inside select Flagship Lounges. The idea was that this offers an a la carte dining experience for those traveling in international or premium transcontinental first class.

American is currently in the process of eliminating “premium” first class — Boeing 777-300ERs are being reconfigured without first class, while Airbus A321XLRs (without first class) are replacing A321Ts. First class is no longer being sold on the wide bodies as of November 19, 2026, or on the narrow bodies as of March 28, 2027. So there has been a big question as to what American would do with these facilities in the future. Would they just close, or what? Well, it’s much better than that.

As of September 15, 2026, Flagship First Dining has simply become Flagship Dining, and the idea is that this is now open to all Flagship Business passengers traveling on qualifying long haul international, transcontinental, and Hawaii flights.

In other words, if you have access to a Flagship Lounge on account of your American business class ticket, you’re also eligible for Flagship Dining, on a space available basis. However, if you have Flagship Lounge access on account of elite status, or through traveling in a premium cabin on a partner airline, you wouldn’t have access to this facility.

American notes how guests interested in dining should check in with the host team inside the Flagship Lounge upon arrival. If a table is immediately available, guests will be seated at that time. If all tables are occupied, guests may be added to an electronic waitlist and will be notified when their table is ready. Tables will be offered on a first come, first served basis, and as I understand it, there’s no priority for first class passengers, Concierge Key members, etc.

I would expect that demand will greatly exceed supply, especially during peak hours. However, the same is true for the a la carte dining in United Polaris Lounges and (to a lesser extent) at some Delta One Lounges. This is really a great way for American to become more competitive with Delta and United. I’m really, really impressed, and this seems like a sincere effort to improve the premium experience.

Launching alongside the expanded dining facility access is an all-new four-course Flagship Dining menu. The airline promotes that “the seasonal offerings blend regional flavors, thoughtfully sourced ingredients and refined culinary techniques to create an elevated dining experience inspired by some of the world’s most celebrated restaurants.”

American is sharing some of the dishes that will be on offer, including a Texas pear and burrata salad…

In addition to that, American is introducing a wine and chocolate pairing, featuring KHK Wines and K+M Extravirgin Chocolate. Chef Thomas Keller, who co-founded K+M Extravirgin Chocolate with Italian olive oil producer Armando Manni, curated the pairing.

American has launched Flagship Dining, essentially a rebranding and repurposing of the Flagship First Dining facilities. This is being done because American is eliminating “premium” first class in the coming months.

This means American is now offering proper a la carte dining in its business class lounges, increasingly matching the competition. What an awesome change from American! You can find these facilities in Dallas, Los Angeles, and Miami.
