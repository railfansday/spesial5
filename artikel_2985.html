DiamondRock: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Low leverage gives DiamondRock room to pursue deals without straining its balance sheet, while each hotel gets its own value playbook.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 27, 2026 at 9:28AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 4, 2026, DiamondRock (DRH +0.73%) announced raised full-year guidance as third-quarter hotel adjusted EBITDA increased 12.5% on broad demand across its resort and urban properties. Long-term real estate investors care because the hotel owner is expanding margins and converting top-line revenue into cash flow faster than its industry peers. Three things management said stand out.

DiamondRock expanded operating margins by controlling expenses, converting a 6.5% increase in total revenue per available room (RevPAR) into a 27% surge in trailing 12-month free cash flow per share. Productivity gains in labor hours per occupied room helped offset industrywide wage pressure, so earnings grew faster than top-line revenue.

Management avoids a uniform playbook for its properties, choosing instead between manager swaps, brand renegotiations, targeted renovations, or going independent. At The Dagny, which went independent in 2023, group revenue has more than doubled since 2019, while targeted renovations at a Sedona resort pushed hotel earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) up 58%.

With net debt at three times EBITDA and no debt maturing until 2029, DiamondRock maintains a conservative balance sheet relative to real estate investment trust (REIT) peers. Management estimates each additional turn of leverage provides roughly $500 million in deal capacity, so it doesn't have to strain its balance sheet to make acquisitions.

Booking momentum across leisure and business travel is accelerating into 2027. Constrained lodging supply gives DiamondRock room to raise rates, while management focuses on pricing discipline, labor efficiency, and direct booking channels to defend margins.

Stabilizing renovated assets and lower contract fees at key hotels position the company to capture profitable demand without requiring aggressive market growth assumptions.
