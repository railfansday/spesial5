Southwest Airlines' New 6-Hour Alaska Route Is Flopping: Here's Why

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Southwest Airlines has not flown to Alaska for long. The increasingly evolving hybrid operator's first service to the Last Frontier was on May 15, 2026. Two routes were available from the get-go, with flights from Las Vegas and Denver, both of which are major cities for Southwest.

Many routes take time to develop, while some perform worse than anticipated despite strong enough expectations. An airline will often be able to tell how well a new link will do within the first couple of days of tickets going on sale. Initial data for Southwest's two markets suggests that one, in particular, might not be doing particularly well.

It is important to note that data from the US Department of Transportation (DOT) is currently only available until the end of August 2026, although that was only half-a-month before the first season's flights from Las Vegas and Anchorage ended. As it does not consider the full season, it is not fully comprehensive and is, therefore, perhaps somewhat unfair, especially given Alaska is a brand-new state for the airline. Treat it for what it is: a simple snapshot of initial performance.

Covering 2,002 nautical miles (3,708 km) each way, Southwest ran daily between Las Vegas and Anchorage in the first season. The DOT shows that 26,097 round-trip passengers were transported between May and August. However, the load factor was only 68.7%. That was over ten percentage points lower than the carrier's average load at Las Vegas in the same period.

This is just one part of the pie. What about fares? Cirium's Route Grader Report is currently only available until June 2026, so data provides even less of a view. In the first month-and-a-half of life, this new route had an average fare that was 17% higher than the airline's overall network from Las Vegas, but the distance was virtually double the length.

When this imbalance is considered, the new route to Alaska had distance-adjusted total revenue per available seat mile of 9.23 US cents, which was by far Southwest's worst result from Sin City. More than just this, it was the airline's fourth-lowest result across its entire network.

Despite only limited initial data, the DOT indicates that just under a quarter of Southwest's traffic only flew between Las Vegas and Anchorage. They did not fly elsewhere. Despite the carrier's daily service and 'relatively' low fares, it only captured 11% of the local market. In contrast, Alaska Airlines had 79% of it.

Southwest was massively reliant on those who connected to another flight at Nevada's busiest airport. The overnight service from Anchorage, leaving just before midnight and arriving at about 6:00 AM, is designed for that transfer traffic. Despite this and Southwest's considerable network from Sin City, the early overall load was not good (68.7%).

The low result was perhaps influenced by launching two routes to Alaska simultaneously, both of which have a similar schedule and both of which focus on connectivity. Analysis shows that the top ten markets from Anchorage via Las Vegas were Dallas Love, Phoenix, Austin, San Antonio, Sacramento, Salt Lake City, San Diego, Orlando, Ontario, and Albuquerque.

In 2026, Southwest operated nonstop between May 15 and September 14, with 123 round-trip flights available. In 2027, the first departure of the season will now be on June 3, three weeks later. The final service of the year will be on September 13. In total, 103 movements will exist, down by 16% year-over-year.

What is more notable is that the equipment has changed, although it is possible that it'll change again. For now, at least, the 175-seat 737 MAX 8 has been replaced by the 137-seat 737-700, contributing to a 34% reduction in seats for sale. Perhaps the MAX 7 will take over. The downgauge means that 14,000+ seats have been removed. Clearly, Southwest is taking big action to achieve stronger fare, yield, and load factor performance next year.
