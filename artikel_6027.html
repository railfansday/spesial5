Trump's Nvidia power play opens Pandora's box Democrats could weaponize, analyst warns

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Eric Schiffer, CEO of Reputation Management Consultants Inc., believes Democrats are engaging in double-sided messaging by attacking Trump’s efforts to reinstate Jensen Huang.

The Trump administration’s efforts to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO of Nvidia have set a new bar for shaping a private corporation, according to Eric Schiffer, chairman and CEO of Reputation Management Consultants, a crisis management and brand protection firm.

"Trump came in to really give a second life to Jensen, where he would have been dead," Schiffer told Fox News Digital Tuesday.

Schiffer said the intervention has opened a kind of Pandora’s box that Democrats may seek to exploit to benefit ideological allies in the private sector. Still, he said the national security interests underpinning Trump’s efforts to intervene on behalf of Huang fall within the responsibilities of the presidency.

Asked whether he believes Democrats may use the moment as a precedent to tip the scales in favor of ideological allies down the road, Schiffer said he sees that possibility in the cards.

"I think that, of course, they would have that capability. I think that that is a risk," Schiffer said.

But Schiffer said the justification would likely depend on the circumstances.

"If the Democrats had a similar situation or it's a leading organization that is so critical to both national security and the economy, then it would make sense," Schiffer said.

"However, if the Democrats — and perhaps the future president that might be a Democrat — is empowering a crony … then I think they're going to be open to significant criticism. And I think that there would be pushback on behalf of the American public," he added.

"This is very unique and different in my opinion."

Nvidia's board decided to bring back Huang as CEO after he resigned earlier this month following reports of an extramarital relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive. The New York Times reported U.S. intelligence identified Alice Huang as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset operating under corporate cover.

A source familiar with the matter told Fox News that President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance worked over the weekend to reinstate Huang after they received security briefings and became convinced that Huang did not present a national security threat in his role as CEO.

In recent days, Vance worked with figures in Silicon Valley and executives at Nvidia to urge the board to reinstate him, the source said.

Democrats have blasted Trump's involvement, calling it presidential overreach. Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif., the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, noted that Trump owns Nvidia stock. Trump holds between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia stock, according to reporting from The Washington Post and its review of Trump's disclosures.

"We must investigate these actions. No president who has invested in a private company should be involved in their board decisions," Garcia said in a statement.

U.S. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-Calif., echoed Garcia's criticisms.

"We will not allow America’s national security to be sold out by the Trump Administration for personal financial gain," Jeffries wrote in a press release on Monday.

Despite those criticisms from Democrats, Schiffer believes the nation’s security interests prevailed through Trump’s decision, arguing that Nvidia's central role in U.S. competition with China made Huang’s return a matter the president could not ignore.

"[In] the office of the presidency, you know, there's the duty to ensure that the United States continues to be strong geopolitically and certainly from a national security perspective. The move to help Jensen accomplishes those," Schiffer said.

"That's part of what a good leader and a strong leader should be doing."

Apart from its reasoning for the administration's intervention, Schiffer pointed out that Trump had only used soft power to encourage Huang's return, a far cry from forcing Nvidia into its decision through any formal use of the presidency.

"This was not a situation where the government came in under some military policy and tried to take control of Nvidia," Schiffer said. "This was the lightest form of power."

The White House and the Office of the Vice President did not immediately respond to requests for comment about whether they shared concerns about Democrats using political power to intervene in the private sector in future administrations or whether the administration had created a new precedent for corporate intervention.
