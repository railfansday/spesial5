Arsenal play like the champions they are as title defence starts with a bang

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Mentally liberated and physically rested, Arsenal began the new Premier League campaign with a performance befitting champions. Gone are the anxieties of last season, when the title race gnawed away at their minds and bodies. Gone are the pressures of chasing a first Premier League trophy in more than two decades. Arsenal look slick, refreshed and hungry for more.

“Champions of England, we know what we are,” sang the home supporters in this thrashing of promoted Coventry City. The players moved with the same energy and confidence, as they dominated every aspect of this match against Frank Lampard’s side.

There will, of course, be many tougher games than this one over the course of the season. But there can be no doubt that Arsenal are settled and strong. Everywhere Coventry looked, they saw world-class opponents who knew exactly what to do and where to be. This was Coventry’s first Premier League match since 2001 and it was a reminder, as if one was needed, of the size of the gulf between England’s top two divisions. Thankfully for Lampard, his season will not be decided by matches against teams of Arsenal’s quality.

As it was in last weekend’s Community Shield victory over Manchester City, Mikel Arteta’s side purred and flowed in attack. The rejuvenated Martin Odegaard scored again, as did his close friend Kai Havertz. Bukayo Saka also struck, easing any lingering concerns about his fitness. Both Saka and Declan Rice, who endured such draining campaigns last year, looked sharp.

"The champions are on their way!" Kai Havertz sweeps home from the edge of the box to give Arsenal an early lead against Coventry 👏 pic.twitter.com/SNtrMSPkYJ

There was also another promising performance by new signing Christos Tzolis, while midfield dynamo Myles Lewis-Skelly continued where he left off at the end of last season: winning tackles, spinning past opponents and firing up the home crowd. New face Bruno Guimarães, absent through injury for a few days, was certainly not needed here.

“They know how to do it,” Arteta said of his players. “They know what they have to do. There was a lot to like in the performance. The manner we started the game, with such domination, clarity, understanding and flow, it was really impressive. At this level the players have to be decisive. They were certainly decisive today.”

Coventry had attempted to irritate Arsenal with physicality and direct passes, but it never looked likely to work, despite the best efforts of strikers Ellis Sims and Taiwo Awoniyi. They will not be the first team to struggle against Arsenal’s defenders, and this will not be the first occasion in which their goalkeeper Carl Rushworth is kept busy.

“Arsenal were the best team in the league last year and they showed it,” Lampard said. “They played like it. The noise here at the start of the game, it was different. I remember that noise. It’s different to the Championship.”

Before this opening match, Arteta had objected to the idea that Arsenal were planning to “defend” their title. Instead, he said, they are going to “attack” the Premier League once again. It was a classic Arteta-ism, but the manager’s meaning was clear: he wants his team to be aggressive, inventive and courageous. Perhaps more so than in the second half of last season, when their defending was a greater strength than their attacking.

If that was a case of Arteta talking the talk, then his players happily followed it up by walking the walk here. Arsenal played with an unmistakable swagger, as if they simply knew there would only be one outcome against a group of Coventry players with limited experience at this level.

The energy of the home supporters, as buoyant as you might expect following last season’s triumph, undoubtedly helped Arsenal. There were none of the nerves that defined the run-in in the spring, with the fans instead in a partying mood from the moment new signing Ezri Konsa was introduced to the Emirates Stadium before kick-off.

The opening goal took only 15 minutes to arrive, as Havertz swept in an accurate finish from Riccardo Calafiori’s low cross. It has been a good summer for Havertz, who missed so much of last season with injury but has found some rhythm over the past few months. Including friendlies and his performances for Germany at the World Cup, this was his 10th goal in his last 17 appearances.

Coventry have invested in some powerful, commanding players this summer, but there were signs that the physical battle was proving difficult for Lampard’s side. Caleb Yirenkyi is their new club-record signing in midfield, but he was brutally thrown to the floor by Lewis-Skelly in the first half.

Saka’s goal was the result of a quick cross by Tzolis, the new £34m winger who has made such an exciting start to his Arsenal career. The Greek forward was the star of the first half, constantly making things happen, and his delivery was diverted into Saka’s path after 23 minutes.

Meanwhile, Arteta has said he and his players should relish being Premier League title favourites and the role of the hunted rather than the hunter.

“In the last three seasons, at some point we were first and at some point we were chasing. And you never know what is better,” Arteta said. “But if you give me the choice, I want to be first. I want to be hunted, and I want to be in control of our future, rather than depending on somebody else.”

Arteta called on his players to retain the hunger they had last season.

“We start from scratch, from zero, and we’re going to have to prove we are at the level to go back to that position again,” he said. “We need to show that desire and the ambition to be better than what we were last season, a month ago, a week ago.”

This is Coventry’s first Premier League campaign since they suffered relegation in 2000-01, and they were led to the Championship title by Frank Lampard. They scored the most goals in the Championship and conceded the fewest, and could face one of their former players tonight in Viktor Gyokeres.
