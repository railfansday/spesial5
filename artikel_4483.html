I use my Breville smart oven every single day. In fact, it’s one of the few products that I reviewed here for the site, then bought for myself, then bought again when it was time for a new one. It has totally replaced my full-size oven and dispenses chicken fingers with unprecedented expertise. Right now, it’s on sale and I’m thinking about upgrading again. Breville’s entire line is basically discounted for Black Friday, so if you want to upgrade your kitchen with stainless steel appliances that work as well as they look, now is the time.

The countertop oven that handles weeknight dinners

Sous vide that leans on some serious math

Sous vide is mostly a math problem, and Breville treats it like one. The Breville Joule Turbo Sous Vide is down to $149.95 (was $249.95), 40 percent off. Breville claims its Turbo mode cooks select recipes in as little as half the time of standard sous vide using thermal-modeling algorithms. The 1,100-watt circulator snaps onto steel pots with a magnetic foot and is small enough to stash in a kitchen drawer. The one trade-off: there are no physical buttons or screen on the stick itself, so you control everything through the Joule app on your phone.

Espresso machine and burr grinder in one box

A good espresso machine needs a good grinder, and buying both is where a lot of home espresso budgets fall apart. The Breville Barista Express builds a half-pound bean hopper and conical burr grinder (for much more consistent grounds than a blade grinder) right into the body for $494.95 (was $699.95). It pairs a 15-bar pump with low-pressure pre-infusion before extraction, and the 67-ounce water tank is removable. Expect a learning curve, since you dial in grind size and dose yourself. It’s also on sale in black and Black Truffle at the same price. Our espresso machine guide has more picks.

That half bottle of flat prosecco has a second life here. The Breville InFizz Fusion is $174.95 (was $249.95), and it carbonates juice, tea, cocktails, and wine, plus re-fizzes flat soda. Breville’s FusionCap controls how the CO2 releases so sugary drinks don’t foam over, and it needs no power or batteries. Note that this standalone model does not include a CO2 canister, so you’ll need a standard 60L screw-in cylinder to get started. If you only drink plain seltzer, the water-only Breville InFizz Aqua is also on sale for $159.95 (was $199.95).

Stepping up from the Barista Express gets you Breville’s 3-second ThermoJet heating system instead of a slower thermocoil, along with more automated milk frothing and shot guidance as you climb the lineup.

Beyond the Smart Oven Pro above, Breville’s countertop ovens split by footprint and whether you want high-speed convection for air frying. The Air Fryer Pro jumps from six slices of toast to nine, while the Mini and Compact models save counter space in tight kitchens.

Breville’s centrifugal juicers scale up by motor power, jug capacity, and noise reduction. The Cold series uses Breville’s Cold Spin tech to limit heat transfer during extraction if you’re prepping larger batches to store in the fridge.

If you mostly blend daily smoothies, the Fresh and Furious handles ice and frozen fruit without taking over your counter. Stepping up to the Q and Super Q gets you commercial-grade motor power for hot soups and nut butters.

The Paradice models are Sous Chef processors with a dicing kit included in the box. At $299.95, the Paradice 9 is only $20 more than the standard Sous Chef 9, which makes it the easy upgrade if you chop a lot of vegetables.
