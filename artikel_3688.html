How WoW: Forever Is Overhauling Classic WoW’s Infamous PvP Honor Grind

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

World of Warcraft: Forever's player vs. player systems will work much differently than in previous versions of old-school WoW, developer Blizzard has revealed.

While Forever is now out in the wild and breaking records from the MMORPG, how its PvP rank system would work remained largely a mystery prior to launch. Now, Blizzard has shared more information about how players will earn PvP ranks and honor, with the main takeaway being that players won't have to commit to months of grinding or competing with players on their own faction for top ranks.

Honor, a currency earned from killing enemy players and completing battlegrounds and PvP activities, will now exclusively be used to purchase PvP gear and doesn't impact progress towards ranks. Instead, players will rank points by completing daily and weekly PvP quests, fighting in open-world PvP activities like the Gurabashi Arena, and competing in the weekly battleground.

In a video detailing the new system, Blizzard said the rank climb will now feel "more varied and predictable" than in the past, when players needed to farm varying amounts of Honor to rank up based on how other players on their same faction were performing. It will also encourage players to engage in different PvP activities each week, instead of simply doing the most efficient one in terms of Honor gained. 

Though it isn't central to ranking up, Honor still remains as a currency and is used to purchase  PvP gear. Players can start purchasing PvP gear at lower ranks, and then use Honor to upgrade those items into epics once they've reached higher ranks. At the max rank of 14, players unlock access to epic weapons equal in power to gear from the MMO's endgame raids. 

Blizzard said the biggest way to progress the ranks is by completing weekly season journey quests. For those who might be unable to play for a few weeks and worry about falling behind, each weekly quest doesn't disappear and can be completed later in the season. 

Forever's PvP rank grind will still take several months of dedicated play (according to Blizzard, players can expect to hit max rank by week eight or nine of a season), but the amount of actual time required will be far less. That's thanks to players on the same faction no longer competing to farm the most Honor in order to achieve top ranks, something that resulted in players of original WoW and WoW Classic grinding battlegrounds for 12 or more hours a day for months on end.

"WoW: Forever's Honor system keeps you coming back each week, rather than grinding every day," Blizzard said. "We hope this is a more approachable system that still provides a healthy challenge as you climb to max rank."

This isn't the first time Blizzard has revamped classic WoW's Honor system. Though it kept the infamous old system in place for WoW Classic's launch in 2019, it finally made changes in 2023. It kept the Honor grind somewhat intact, but removed the competition between players on the same faction, instead requiring players meet certain Honor thresholds each week to rank up.

Forever looks to be a hit for Blizzard so far, with more than 15 million characters created and a record breaking number of concurrent, returning, and weekly players. Despite the game's massive launch and a banner year for WoW in general, it wasn't enough to snag the MMO a nomination for Best Ongoing Game at The Game Awards. 

In other Forever news, Blizzard recently hotfixed a major dungeon exploit that saw some players, including prominent streamers, earn thousands of gold, only for Blizzard to then remove the ill-gotten gains and issue temporary account suspensions to the worst offenders.
