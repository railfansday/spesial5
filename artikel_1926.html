England versus Spain becoming a new 'clásico' as Leeds United keeper James Trafford announces himself on world stage

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

That's how widely-respected Spanish sports newspaper Marca described La Roja's rivalry with England following the Three Lions' stunning Sunday-best victory at the Metropolitano. High praise indeed.

That it is becoming one of the most absorbing and significant in world football is surely beyond doubt now.

In terms of beating the world champions, England have been here before. Back in November 2011, a screamer from Frank Lampard secured a Wembley victory against the feted Spanish, who were also European Championship holders at the same time.

This felt different. On that aforesaid occasion, England prevailed following a backs-to-the-wall performance which saw them pinned back in their own half for long spells against dominant opponents. They converted one of just two chances that came their way.

By contrast, Sunday saw those in white subjugate Spain with an elite showing.

Certainly for 65 to 70 minutes, the hosts' much-vaunted midfield—Arsenal duo Martin Zubimendi and Mikel Merino, alongside Fabián Ruiz and Alex Baena—were suffocated by England's high press.

It was an intense game plan executed 'with the precision of a master tailor' in the words of Marca. Ruiz and Merino both got the hook at half-time.

The narrative only changed when Elliot Anderson received his marching orders.

The hosts roared back and things became undeniably hairy in the final quarter. But unlike against Argentina in Atlanta in July, Thomas Tuchel's substitutions did not see England adopt a defensive straitjacket.

Leeds United's Dominic Calvert-Lewin came on as the visitors focused on staying higher up the pitch, if at all possible, amid serious late pressure from Spain.

It ended in a merited 3-2 win for the English. In three of the last four meetings between these two, a team has triumphed by the odd goal in five.

England triumphed by the same scoreline and in similar fashion to their previous visit to Spain back in October 2018 - in another Nations League encounter played out in Seville, this time. Again, they achieved success by being proactive as opposed to parking the bus.

Fabulous counter-attacking strikes from Raheem Sterling (2) and Marcus Rashford put the visitors 3-0 up to silence the Spanish hordes. It was the prelude to a strong home fightback in the second half, but no victory.

On this occasion, England's physical and tactical dominance was even more sustained and their third goal, scored by Ben White on 57 minutes, finished off a move of the highest order.

For too long in the past, certainly before that 2018 occasion in Seville, England had been cowed on their visits to Spain. In their previous four matches, all defeats, they failed to score. Ever since Gary Lineker, then at Barcelona, struck all four goals in a stunner at Madrid's Bernebeu Stadium in February 1987.

England have now regained the full respect of international football's undoubted success story of the current century. The team who, alongside France, are the current benchmark for all other nations.

Sunday night was undeniably sweetened by the fact that Spain relinquished their magnificent 44-game unbeaten sequence in the process.

That run included a 2-1 win over England in the Euro 2024 final in Berlin and this summer’s World Cup.

Spain's previous defeat was back in March 2023 against Scotland in Glasgow.

On an evening when the usual suspects came to the party in the shape of Jude Bellingham, whose pass for White's goal was delectable and Harry Kane, others also accepted their invites.

What a night for Leeds's James Trafford for instance. His fantastic double save late on to deny Fer Lopez and Aymeric Laporte announced his arrival on the world stage.

It presents Tuchel with a genuine headache - a welcome one, that said - regarding who will be his bonafide No 1 in 2027.

The battle between Trafford and Jordan Pickford is now a proper fight. It's not quite Peter Shilton versus Ray Clemence levels in the 1970s and early 80s just yet, but it might be heading in that direction. Tellingly, Trafford started both Nations League games against Spain, as opposed to his Everton rival.

Remember, remember the month of November? England will.

Sunday's success was a statement win of the ilk of 2015 when they beat the French 2-0 at Wembley and a decade earlier, in the same month, when a late brace from Michael Owen saw them triumph 3-2 against bitter foes Argentina, in Geneva of all places. Let's hope on this occasion, they build on it.
