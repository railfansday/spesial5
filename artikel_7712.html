If you’ve weighed the benefits of curtains, shutters, and blinds in your search for the right window coverings for your home and you’ve settled on blinds, you’re in good company. According to a study by research firm Market Research Future, the size of the window blind market was an estimated $75.48 billion in 2025, and it’s expected to grow to $136.35 billion by 2035. Another research firm, Global Market Insights, found that blinds and shades dominated the window treatment industry in 2025.

With so many different types of blinds on the market, how do you decide which will suit your home best? Whether you’re looking for something sleek and modern or traditional and classic, this guide will help you figure out which ones might be the right pick for your windows.

Blinds are a type of window covering composed of numerous slats, vertical or horizontal, that can be tilted or rotated to adjust the amount of light entering a space. Blinds made of aluminum or vinyl are typically the most cost-efficient types, but there also are blinds crafted of fabric and wood.

They can be installed inside the window frame or above it. Window blinds are typically mounted with specially designed brackets that are sold with the blinds.

Blinds consist of many parts. The headrail, for example, is the top part of the blind that contains the operating mechanisms such as the cords, pulleys, or tilt wands. The headrail provides structural support for the blind. The valance is the decorative cover that hides the headrail and its workings.

Depending on the style of the blinds, there may be a cord that draws the slats up and down. A wand adjusts the tilt of the louvers, which are the collection of narrow horizontal slats that tilt to adjust the amount of light that passes into the space.

The slats are suspended from a track that runs along the top of a window frame. This style of blinds collects less dust than other types of blinds and is easy to clean. The slats just need to be wiped down with a damp cloth.

Best For: Affordability and minimal maintenance

Mini blinds are like Venetian blinds, with some minor differences. Like Venetian blinds, they are typically made from aluminum or vinyl instead of wood or bamboo. Mini blinds have smaller slats than Venetian blinds, though: Mini-blind slats are usually an inch wide or smaller. While these two types of blinds perform the same way, mini blinds have a sleeker design and thus tend to look more modern and contemporary.

With a minimalist design and cordless operation, the Achim mini blinds are safe and stylish.

Wood blinds are a popular choice for those looking to add an element of luxury to their window treatments since wood blinds offer a natural, timeless look that never goes out of style. Not only do they exude sophistication, but they are also incredibly durable and can last for many years with proper care and maintenance. They can be painted or stained to match any color palette, and different slat sizes are available to accommodate shoppers’ specific needs. Wooden blinds tend to be horizontal—or Venetian—style, though vertical wooden blinds are also available.

Best For: Homeowners who are in it for the long haul—these are built to last

Levolor’s 2-inch wood blinds have cordless lift and wand tilt and come in a variety of wood stains.

Aluminum blinds are one of the most popular types of window blinds because of their affordable price and lightweight yet durable design. A sleek, modern look that can complement many decor styles, they are easy to install and incredibly long-lasting. Aluminum blinds also come in a variety of colors, sizes, and textures, allowing homeowners to customize the look of their windows to suit many different aesthetics. In terms of energy efficiency, aluminum blinds help keep a home’s interior cooler by reflecting the sun’s rays away from the window. This helps reduce air conditioning costs since less energy is required to maintain steady temperatures inside the house.

Best For: Easy installation and durability

Sleek and affordable, Blinds.com’s 8-gauge aluminum blinds are available in a variety of neutral colors.

If you’re looking for blinds to cover sliding doors or large windows, consider panel blinds. These window treatments are composed of several panels affixed to a headrail at the top of the window. Each panel is attached to a carrier that slides along a track in order to open or close the blinds. They offer a unique look that differs from standard vertical blinds, making them a great choice for design-forward shoppers.

Roller blinds are technically a type of window shade, making them different from many of the other types of blinds on our list. An alternative to curtains, these shades are made up of a single piece of fabric material that rolls up and down to control the amount of sunlight that enters the space. Roller shades can be made from a variety of fabrics, ranging from sheer materials to opaque options. While roller blinds might have a reputation for being low quality and not performing well, their design has actually come a long way in recent years.

Chicology’s blackout roller shade has a cordless design and is made from hypoallergenic polyester that won’t attract dust.

UNISHASDES bamboo blinds  partially filter sunlight and come in a variety of sizes and finishes.

Motorized blinds are a modern window covering solution that allows users to open and close their blinds using a remote control, phone app, or voice command. Some models come with sun sensors that automate the opening and closing of your blinds at certain times of the day. Unlike traditional blinds that must be adjusted manually, motorized blinds use either electric or battery-powered motors to raise and lower them. This makes it easier to control the lighting in a room without ever having to leave your seat. Today, many window blind types can be motorized.

Sometimes called layered or dual shades, zebra blinds consist of two layers of fabric, each with alternating horizontal bands of sheer and opaque fabric. You can adjust, or roll, the fabric to line up the stripes on the two layers of fabric, meaning opaque is lined up with opaque, and sheer with sheer; this allows light to enter. If you roll the shade such that the opaque stripes on one layer are lined up with the sheer strips on the other layer, the effect is that of an opaque shade, and no light comes through.

Also called cellular blinds or cellular shades, honeycomb blinds are constructed of two sheets of fabric that are seamed together horizontally at regular intervals. When looking at these blinds from the side, the resulting pattern somewhat resembles that of a honeycomb. Highly insulating, the design is considered one of the most energy-efficient, and the cellular construction is also good at noise cancellation

Best For: Energy-efficiency; they also offer a modern vibe

Pleated blinds are made from a single length of woven fabric that is pressed into sharp, horizontal pleats at regular intervals, yielding a zig-zag appearance. The blind is raised or lowered to adjust the amount of light entering the room. While they’re often compared to honeycomb blinds, pleated blinds don’t offer the same insulating properties as cellular blinds. Pleated blinds offer a clean, modern look.

Best For: Decorators in search of an elegant, minimalist  look.

“Blackout” is a descriptor applied to a number of types of blinds and shades that are designed to block out all light. Not only do they work to block light from entering a room, but they’re also typically made of thick, dark-colored materials that absorb light. Blackout window coverings might be cellular blinds or roller shades.

Best For: Rooms in which someone needs to sleep during the day.
