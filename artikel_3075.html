Follow the Smart Money: 4 AI Stocks Billionaire Dan Loeb Just Bought

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Hedge fund manager Dan Loeb was buying shares of Nvidia, Space Exploration Technologies, Microsoft, and Meta Platforms in Q3 2026.

By Geoffrey Seiler – Nov 21, 2026 at 12:00AM EST

Dan Loeb is widely considered one of the world's top investors, starting his Third Point hedge fund with a few million dollars in 1995 and turning it into an over-$28 billion behemoth. In Q3 2026, he was busy scooping up several artificial intelligence (AI) stocks, including Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%), Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%), and Meta Platforms (META -5.93%).

Let's look at what may have attracted Loeb to these names.

Loeb's buy of Nvidia shares came before CEO Jensen Huang stepped down, but the company remains in the pole position in the AI infrastructure build-out. Nvidia today is more than just a graphics processing unit (GPU) provider, offering a complete AI factory solution that includes networking, central processing units (CPUs), language processing units (LPUs) for the decode phase of inference, its Nemotron open-weight models, and an expanding software layer.

That package has helped Nvidia increase its revenue per gigawatt from around $18 billion when it introduced its Hopper architecture a few years ago to around $40 billion today with Vera Rubin.

With future memory and manufacturing capacity locked in, along with its strong ecosystem, expect Nvidia to remain the AI infrastructure leader over the coming years.

SpaceX is one of the most exciting companies on the planet as it looks to disrupt industries and conquer space. The company recently achieved a major milestone by catching the upper stage of its Starship rocket, setting it up to fundamentally change the economics of space launches as it moves toward rapid reuse. This is also a stepping stone for SpaceX to hit some of its loftier goals, such as orbital data centers, Earth-to-Earth transport, and asteroid mining.

Back on Earth, SpaceX has become one of the world's largest neocloud companies and plans to rapidly grow its AI infrastructure-as-a-service business. Right now, it is getting great rates on its AI infrastructure through short-term rentals, allowing it to see a payback on its investments within a year. The company also plans to disrupt the U.S. mobile market, and its Cursor AI coding platform is growing quickly.

Microsoft continues to see strong growth in both its Azure cloud computing unit and its enterprise software business. Azure has been a persistent 30%-plus revenue grower, with its revenue growth hitting 48% last quarter. With significant commitments from OpenAI and an overall $677 billion backlog, Azure should remain a strong growth driver in the years ahead.

At the same time, the company has been gaining significant traction with its AI Copilot assistants across Microsoft 365 and its GitHub code repository. The shift to a seat-plus-consumption model has been paying off, and Copilot usage has been surging, with it seeing an eightfold year-over-year increase in seats last quarter. Given how embedded the company's software and governance tools are in the enterprise space, it looks well positioned to emerge as an AI software-as-a-service (SaaS) winner.

Meta Platforms has been one of the best companies at using AI to drive growth in an already established, strong business. It's employing AI to both keep users on its apps longer by displaying more content they are interested in and to feed them more relevant ads. This is leading to higher ad loads, stronger conversions, and better ad pricing.

Last quarter, its revenue surged 26%, with a 14% increase in average price per ad and a 10% jump in ad impressions.

Perhaps more exciting, though, is that Meta is jumping headfirst into consumer AI agents. These agents can help users with a multitude of tasks, but Meta will benefit most when they make purchases on a user's behalf. Here, Meta will get a cut of the transaction fee, giving it another potential new large revenue stream and putting it at the forefront of agentic commerce.
