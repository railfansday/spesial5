Advanced Micro Devices vs. Broadcom: One Stock Has a $27 Billion Advantage Investors Shouldn't Ignore

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Both semiconductor giants are benefiting from accelerating AI infrastructure investment, but substantial differences in profitability, cash generation, and valuation could shape their returns.

The artificial intelligence (AI) buildout has created enormous growth opportunities for semiconductor companies, but not all chipmakers are benefiting equally. Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -8.75%) and Broadcom (AVGO -4.76%) are two major beneficiaries of surging AI infrastructure investment, yet their financial performance and valuations tell very different stories.

AMD is aggressively expanding its presence in AI accelerators and high-performance computing, while Broadcom has established a strong position in custom AI chips, networking, and infrastructure software. Despite their exposure to the same industry trends, substantial differences in profitability, cash flow, and valuation raise an important question about which company offers investors the stronger opportunity heading into 2027.

AMD is a major player in the semiconductor sector, providing processors and graphics units for data centers, gaming, and personal computers. The company relies on a small number of customers for a large portion of its business, including Microsoft (MSFT +0.17%) and Sony. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business. Recent strategic partnerships, such as working with OpenAI to deploy data center GPUs, highlight its focus on the high-performance computing market.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 26, 2026, revenue reached nearly $45.4 billion. This represents revenue growth of approximately 41.9% compared to the prior year. The company reported net income of roughly $7.6 billion during this period, resulting in a net margin of close to 16.8%. These figures reflect its successful push into the server market and the rising demand for AI-related hardware.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.1x. This ratio, which compares total debt to shareholder equity, suggests the company is not heavily reliant on borrowed money. The current ratio, which measures the ability to pay short-term debts with short-term assets, is roughly 2.9x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 27, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $6.7 billion. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 21.2% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Broadcom has established itself as a major beneficiary of the AI boom through its custom accelerators, data center networking equipment, and infrastructure software. Its acquisition of VMware has also expanded its enterprise software business, providing an additional revenue stream beyond semiconductors. However, customer concentration remains a risk, with its five largest end customers accounting for approximately 40% of revenue in fiscal 2025.

Over the 12 months ended August 2, 2026, Broadcom generated approximately $89.1 billion in revenue, up 48.7% year over year, and $38.3 billion in net income, representing a profit margin of 42.9%. Growth has accelerated alongside demand for custom AI chips and networking solutions, with third-quarter AI semiconductor revenue surging 221% to $16.7 billion. The company's combination of high-margin infrastructure software and rapidly expanding semiconductor sales has translated into exceptional profitability.

Broadcom's financial position also supports its growth ambitions, although its acquisition strategy has left it with more leverage than AMD. At the end of fiscal 2025, the company maintained a debt-to-equity ratio near 0.8 and a current ratio of approximately 1.7. Free cash flow reached $26.9 billion, roughly four times AMD's reported figure, demonstrating its substantial cash-generating capacity. However, stock-based compensation represented approximately 27.5% of operating cash flow, an important consideration when evaluating the quality of that cash generation.

AMD faces intense competition from Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%) in AI accelerators and Intel (INTC -2.52%) in server and personal computer processors. Although AMD is gaining ground in data centers, Nvidia's established software ecosystem and customer relationships present significant barriers to market share gains. AMD also relies heavily on Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -4.69%) for advanced chip production, exposing it to geopolitical tensions and potential supply chain disruptions. U.S. export restrictions on advanced chips to China and execution risks surrounding its expanding AI partnerships could further complicate its growth outlook.

Broadcom faces significant customer concentration risk, particularly in its custom AI chip business, where demand depends heavily on capital spending by a handful of hyperscale customers. Like AMD, Broadcom relies on TSMC for advanced manufacturing, leaving it vulnerable to supply disruptions and geopolitical uncertainty. Its substantial debt burden, largely stemming from acquisitions, also creates greater financial exposure if borrowing costs remain elevated.

Broadcom appears to be the more affordable option based on both earnings and sales multiples, while Advanced Micro Devices carries a significantly higher premium from investors.

AI-related stocks have come under renewed selling pressure as investors reassess elevated valuations and the sustainability of infrastructure spending. Broadcom and AMD have both experienced significant volatility, reflecting growing uncertainty surrounding the industry's growth outlook. As the AI trade matures, investors may become increasingly selective, favoring companies that can translate strong demand into sustainable earnings and free cash flow.

For this reason, Broadcom looks like the better buy. The company generated nearly $27 billion in annual free cash flow, roughly four times AMD's $6.7 billion, providing substantial financial flexibility to invest in new technologies, reduce debt, and return capital to shareholders. Its combination of custom AI accelerators, networking equipment, and infrastructure software also provides multiple sources of revenue as AI adoption expands.

Valuation further strengthens Broadcom's appeal. At 18.9 times forward earnings, it trades at a substantial discount to AMD's 72.8 multiple, despite generating significantly higher profits and cash flow. AMD offers considerable upside as it challenges Nvidia's dominance in AI accelerators, but its premium valuation leaves little room for disappointing growth.

While both companies are positioned to benefit from continued AI investment, I favor Broadcom's superior cash generation, diversified business model, and more attractive valuation.
