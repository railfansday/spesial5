Week 11 Game Recap: Bucs fall to 3-7; Behind coaching woes

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Knee deep in the midst of one of the most surreal playoff races that imaginable, the Buccaneers would be looking to collect a win against the Detroit Lions in week 11. Can Bucky Irving, Baker Mayfield, and the Bucs' bodacious defense pull together to upset Dan Campbell's bunch on the road?

It was Baker Mayfield's ball first on Sunday and it was his chance to get sacked first— One play, three defenders getting off their blocks, and one sack of Baker Mayfield with a forced fumble to boot. The Bucs stayed in possession only to punt and play defense. Jared Goff's second pass and first completion of the day took Detroit across midfield, but two batted balls in a three play sequence forced the Lions into a long field attempt and long field goal miss.

The Buccaneers' offense found a few completions, a few third down conversions, and more importantly a bit of their stride. Tampa Bay moved nearly 50 yards to the Detroit 10-yard line before having to make a decision. Facing a fourth and one, Todd Bowles neglected a short field goal opportunity and chose to leave his offense on the field. Zac Robinson opted for Bucky Irving running into traffic and that traffic did more than slow him down, it stopped him. Tampa's failed fourth down attempt jump started Detroit's next drive.

50% of Tampa's star cornerback tandem would leave the field on Detroit's second drive and for Todd Bowles' defense, no Parrish = no good. The Lions tagged Benjamin Morrison for a big play and tagged him again for a touchdown. While Morrison did struggle to fill the Jacob Parrish's shoes, one of the early death nails for Tampa's defense though the early moments has been thorough inability to keep the Lions out of third down and one and a subsequent inability to stop Detroit from converting those third downs into first downs. 7-0, Lions.

Tampa's next start was kicked off by a false start— No doubt they had been holding that in, going an entire quarter without one. The Bucs' next drive no longer featured third down as an unconquerable foe third downs— First down Cade Otton, first down Chris Godwin, however, old habits die hard— All out blitz, sack, punt.

With the seems as open as a 7/11 in New York City, Jared Goff picked apart Tampa Bay's zones all the way into the final two minutes of the half and field goal range. 10-0, Lions.

Rocking a bad thumb, a bad foot, and a bad offense Baker Mayfield had a 1:30 from the 35-yard line to cut into the Lions' lead. One more big scramble from the Buccaneers' injured quarterback and rare down the field throw to Bucky Irving planted Tampa Bay squarely in Chase McLaughlin's range. The Buccaneers did not choose to settle, they took a shot at the end zone, trying for six. Baker Mayfield reared back and lofted a beautiful drop into the bucket to Ted Hurst III for a 23-yard touchdown strike. Hurst III's second grab of the drive gave the Bucs' a feeling of what could be, in a season where deep passes or a passing game with any consistent at all has been a needle in hay stack, Ted Hurst III's score before half flashed a glimmer of what it could be if it all ever clicked. 10-7, Detroit.

Following a Detroit punt, the Buccaneers' offense had the chance to capitalize on their end of half success— They did not. Their drive began with an 80-yard touchdown scamper by Bucky Irving (called back for a Luke Goedeke hold) and ended with a punt.

After coming mere moments away from holding a lead over the Detroit Lions, the Bucs were back on defense and still trailing. Once again, no Parrish = no good. Jameson Williams ran by Benjamin Morrison like Ferrari passing a Hyundai at a stop light. Williams' score gave the Lions their largest lead of the day (10 points)— 17-7, home town team.

8:56 left in the third, down 10, plenty of time to rally— Also plenty of time for a close game to become a rout. In a pinch, the Buccaneers' offense turned to their backs. Bucky Irving on the ground and Kenny Gainwell through the air maneuvered Zac Robinson's offense down the field and into the end zone for a second touchdown of the day— Bucky Irving's score brought the Buccaneers back to whin three. 17-14, team awkwardly dressed like a Carolina Panthers spin off.

The game's final 15 minutes were Detroit's to start. Tampa's defense notched their first sack of the day in order to force Detroit into a punt. Pinned deep at the nine, the Bucs needed some Mayfield magic to flip the scoreboard— Somebody might've miscalculated the formula on the magic because the Buccaneers finished their drive with a punt, a bad punt at that. Easy does it for the Lions' offense, smooth sailing into the end zone for another touchdown— 24-14, Lions.

With 5:24 remaining in the game, the Bucs found Emeka Egbuka to pick up a first down. That would be the only first down they would earn because Tampa Bay's offense (in 2026) is to sacks like sand is to a beach. Tampa Bay's turnover on downs segued into a nice undisciplined brawl (discipline falls on the coach) headed by Vita Vea and the Buccaneer's weekly collapse was complete— Seven more points for Jahmyr Gibbs, 31-14, not Todd Bowles' team.

A hurt, beat down Baker Mayfield rallied the troops for one last gasp of air. Just under two minutes to go and the Bucs road penalty flags down to the red zone and Ted Hurst III across the goal line. 31-21, Detroit.
