Future Hogs Week 11: College football players who could help the Commanders

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The college football games from Week 11 are unlikely to change the College Football Playoff landscape much because 11 of the top 12 teams in the playoff rankings won. Georgia easily dispatched of Missouri, Utah barely beat Arizona in one of the year's most exciting games, Alabama and its defense smothered Vanderbilt, and UCLA remained undefeated by beating Illinois. LSU's playoff hopes are over after Texas scored a last-minute touchdown to escape Baton Rouge with a win. The top team in the ACC, Miami, needed the second half to pull away from Duke. The only team in the top 12 to lose was Kentucky. The Wildcats just couldn't make enough plays to beat Florida. It's likely Texas Tech will replace Kentucky in playoff rankings.

After having their playoff hopes dashed, LSU could play spoiler in Week 12 by knocking Tennessee out of contention. The top team, Georgia, will go on the road to face South Carolina. Utah will also be on the road to take on TCU. UCLA will leave sunny southern California for Ann Arbor to face Michigan. SMU, who got blown out in Week 11 by Wake Forest, will look to bounce back against Notre Dame.

Here are a few players who had a good week and might be able to help out the Commanders. Let me know what you think about these prospects and feel free to post players that stood out to you:

While Boise State has been able to rely on its run game much of the year, Broncos' quarterback Maddux Madsen had his best game of the season. While Maddux is short for an NFL QB at only 5'10", he weighs 210 pounds. His height doesn't appear to affect his vision as he is able to find receivers all over the field. Madsen has enough arm to push the ball down the field, but it looks like he has to put a lot of air under his deeper passes. His passes are accurate with good ball placement. While he's not a dual threat, Madsen can scramble effectively and gain positive yardage with well-timed QB runs. The Broncos won a shootout with Oregon State thanks to Madsen's 371 yards and four touchdowns on 23 of 32 passes.

If you long for the days when the Commanders had a big back that was tough to bring down, Micah Ford might interest you as a prospect. After watching a few plays where he bounced off defenders or ran through them, I had to look up his measurables. Ford is 6', 225 pounds. That's a large running back that can dole out damage, but he is also a big target. He could remedy that by running less upright. At that size, Ford has good burst. He is most effective when he can get downhill immediately as his lateral quickness appears to be average at best. While he's not a huge receiving threat, it is a part of his game as he has 27 receptions on the year. Ford helped Stanford beat Virginia Tech with 27 carries for 149 yards and three touchdowns.

It is possible that Aidan Mizell is the fastest wide receiver in college football. He's topped over 22 miles per hour this season according Reel Analytics. His only competition might be his younger brother, Arizona receiver Isaiah Mizell. Back to Aidan, he is slight at 6'1" but only weighs 177 pounds. You can't hit what you can't see, right? Mizell uses his speed to get open. He regularly beats defenders deep downfield and tracks the ball well. UCLA will also manufacture touches for him because once the ball is in Mizell's hands, he can take it the distance. He has to show that he's more than just a speed merchant by running a greater variety of routes and using his route running to get open. The Bruins beat Illinois, and Mizell contributed six catches for 176 yards and a touchdown.

After Ben Sinnott's performance against the New York Giants, I'm not sure we need another tight end. Heck, just trade John Bates and Chig Okonkwo in the offseason and use those roster spots for other positions. Okay, maybe that's going too far…maybe. Adding a player like Eli Finley to the tight end room would make it better. He's got good size at 6'4", 246 pounds. Finley is a good route runner and has good athleticism with that frame. That athleticism makes him effective on and off the line of scrimmage. Once he makes a catch, Finley is a good runner and can be hard to tackle. As a blocker, Finley latches onto defenders with strong hands and drives them off the ball. Louisiana Tech scored 63 points in a win over Southern Missouri, and Finley had six catches for 104 yards and a touchdown.

Arizona suffered a close loss to Utah despite putting up 39 points. Although there was a stretch of the game where Utah put a ton of pressure on Wildcats QB Noah Fifita, Arizona still had a ton of explosive plays. Zach Henning was a big reason Fifita had time to get the ball downfield. Henning is stout in the passing game at 6'4", 310 pounds. He plays with a strong base and strong core that allow him to anchor against power up the middle, something the Commanders need considering the defensive tackles in the NFC East. He's a little inconsistent in the run game. Arizona uses a lot of inside zone runs. What he does best is work to the second level to find blockers, but he can struggle to generate movement at the point of attack. Henning wears #58 in the clips below.

I wanted to highlight Myles Bradley earlier this season. In Week 4, he had a game with 4.5 sacks. It just so happens that Colin Simmons from Texas had five sacks that week. Well in Week 11, Bradley had five sacks, bringing his NCAA-leading total to 14.5. One thing that we have to get out of the way. Bradley is a small edge rusher at only 6', 230 pounds. That's so small that he may only be a pass-rush specialist in the NFL. The other thing is Bradley plays defensive end in a 4-3. Can he drop to play in space as required by a 3-4 OLB? What he definitely can do is use his speed to beat tackles around the corner and bring down the quarterback. He uses that speed to set up an effective spin move. While his hand usage is good, you won't see many power rushes. To go along with his five sacks, Bradley had nine total tackles, a forced fumble, and two deflections in Bowling Green's win over Kent State.

It's been a tough year for Virginia, but they were able to get a comfortable win against California. The Cavaliers' defense only allowed seven points with Kam Robinson leading the way. For the second year in a row, Robinson got a pick-six against the Golden Bears. It's because Robinson is a smart player that can quickly react to an offense. He uses that skill in coverage. In zone, Robinson drops to the right depth quickly and closes space on receivers to knock the ball away or make a pick. His quickness helps him beat blockers to make plays in the backfield. At 6'2", 239 pounds, it'd be nice to see Robinson play to his size by taking on blockers instead of running around them. To go with that pick-six, the fourth of his career which ties a school record, Robinson had 12 total tackles.

The matchup between Utah and Arizona was an exciting affair that went down to the wire. With the game in the balance on a key fourth down, it was Jackson Bennee who made the deciding play. Although Utah gave up their fair number of passes to Arizona and their QB Noah Fifita, Jackson Bennee stood out in a major way. Utah plays Bennee in the slot and he's superb playing man coverage from this position. That's because Bennee is incredibly quick and changes directions with ease. It is surprising to see him do this at 6'2", 190 pounds. The blend of quickness, agility, and length allows him to stay attached to receivers throughout their routes. Utah lists Bennee as a safety, but he looks like a slot cornerback at the next level. That's largely because Bennee lacks stopping power as a hitter and tackler. The effort and willingness is there, but he'll have to be stronger in this area to play the slot in the NFL. Bennee had three tackles and two PBUs in the Utes big win over the Wildcats.

Texas needed all 60 minutes to beat LSU, but there's no way they would've been in a position to win without Jelani McDonald. Last week, mistamoe2 commented that the Commanders could use a top-notch free safety. McDonald might be that player. He stands 6'2" and weighs 201 pounds. The Longhorns trust McDonald to play numerous alignments from single-high safety to split-safety looks, to man coverage. In zone coverage, McDonald is incredibly aware. He trusts his eyes to take him to where the ball is going and is rarely wrong. In man coverage, he moves fluidly to stay with receivers throughout their routes and will look back for the ball to avoid penalties. His run support and tackling is another attribute of his. Although he's not the biggest safety, he is physical. McDonald had 11 total tackles, a PBU, and three interceptions to help the Longhorns take down the Tigers.
