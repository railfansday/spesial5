Tarik Skubal agrees to record nine-year, $450 million deal with Mets

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The New York Mets reached an agreement Sunday with left-hander Tarik Skubal on a nine-year, $450 million contract, bringing the two-time Cy Young Award winner to Queens after a brief stay with the Los Angeles Dodgers. The deal is pending a physical, according to the New York Post, which first reported the agreement. The Mets had yet to announce the signing Sunday afternoon.

The contract would pay Skubal an average of $50 million a season and set a major league record for a full-time pitcher. It tops the previous mark held by his Dodgers teammate Yoshinobu Yamamoto by $125 million; Yamamoto signed for $325 million over 12 years before the 2024 season. Skubal's deal reportedly includes a $45 million signing bonus and no opt-outs, tying him to New York through 2035.

For the Mets, the agreement addresses the first order of business after a 74-win season and a second consecutive October at home. President of baseball operations David Stearns identified starting pitching as a priority at the general managers meetings in Las Vegas. When the Mets pitched to Skubal in Southern California, owner Steve Cohen, manager Andy Green and pitching coach Justin Willard joined Stearns for the meeting.

Skubal, who turned 30 on Friday, won consecutive American League Cy Young Awards with Detroit in 2024 and 2025. From 2023 through 2026, he posted a 2.47 ERA and struck out nearly seven batters for every walk he allowed. Even in a season interrupted by May surgery to remove loose bodies from his left elbow, he went 12-7 with a 2.72 ERA in 26 starts between Detroit and Los Angeles.

The Dodgers sent three prospects to Detroit at the trade deadline to add Skubal to their postseason rotation. He delivered a 2.45 ERA in 11 October innings for Los Angeles, bringing his career postseason ERA to 2.13 over 50⅓ innings. The Dodgers met with him about returning, and the San Diego Padres and Toronto Blue Jays were also among the clubs that pursued him.

The bidding moved quickly in its final days. On Friday, former Mets general manager Jim Duquette reported that New York had offered Skubal nearly $400 million over nine years. By Sunday, the length of the reported deal was the same, but the guarantee had reached $450 million. Skubal and agent Scott Boras reached the agreement more than a week before baseball's collective bargaining agreement was set to expire Dec. 1.

Two winters after committing $765 million over 15 years to outfielder Juan Soto, the Mets have now committed a combined $1.215 billion to two players. Soto was already under contract through 2039. If Skubal passes his physical, New York will have its ace and its lineup centerpiece under contract together for the next nine seasons.
