Circle M8 Land & Cattle / High Card Ranch Claims WRCA World Championship

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The road to Amarillo ended with a world championship for Circle M8 Land & Cattle / High Card Ranch.

The team topped the 2026 Working Ranch Cowboys Association World Championship Ranch Rodeo, with Rye Reynolds adding the prestigious Top Hand award. Representing the championship team were Phillip Reynolds, Rye Reynolds, Jayde Smith, Chance Holcombe, Ky Brownlee and Kelby Brownlee. The crew also captured the team branding event title.

Held November 12–15 in Amarillo, the championship brought together 23 teams for four days of competition. The format divided the opening round between Thursday and Friday, followed by a second round on Saturday and Sunday. The final performance of the 31st World Championship Ranch Rodeo concluded with awards recognizing the standout teams, cowboys and horses.

For Circle M8 Land & Cattle / High Card Ranch, the national championship followed a qualification earned at the Rail 3 Ranch Rodeo in Florence, Arizona. Its journey illustrated the reach of WRCA's qualifying circuit, which brought teams to Amarillo from sanctioned competitions across ranch country.

Meier Cattle Co. / 4E Livestock claimed reserve world champion honors and won wild cow milking. Rocking T Cattle / Flowers Ranch finished third overall while capturing both stray gathering and team penning. Wilson Cattle Co. placed fourth, followed by Barrow Ranch in fifth. Pettigrew / White Ranches earned the ranch bronc riding title.

The individual awards also recognized the horses and hands behind the performances. Rustyn Gilmore of Wilson Cattle earned Reserve Top Hand. Casas Hickory Cat, ridden by Cody Kendall for CK Cattle / Hyer & Son Cattle Co., received Top Horse honors. Goose, ridden by Tallan Miller for Lochhead Cattle Co., was Reserve Top Horse.

The weekend also celebrated the next generation. C3 / Myers Cattle won the 2026 WRCA Youth World Championship Ranch Rodeo with Reece Myers, Bonnar Barns, Chance Anderson, Tristan Satterfield and Stetson Duncan. Anderson earned youth Top Hand honors, while the team won sorting and the wild steer race. Baca Float 3 / Antelope finished reserve, and Nail Ranch placed third.

The competition serves a purpose beyond determining champions. Founded in 1995, WRCA works to promote ranching and preserve the working cowboy's lifestyle. Its annual championship showcases ranch skills while raising funds for the WRCA Foundation, extending the event's impact beyond the arena.

This year, Circle M8 Land & Cattle / High Card Ranch left Amarillo with the world championship, and a place in that continuing ranch rodeo tradition.
