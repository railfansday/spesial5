Top-5 Thanksgiving Day plays in Miami Dolphins history

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Thanksgiving Day means a few things to most Americans: food, family and football. And while the Miami Dolphins won't be playing on this year's Turkey Day, they've had some very memorable moments for the franchise on Thanksgiving in the past.

The Dolphins have played eight times on Thanksgiving — winning five of those contests. And in those games, they've had their fair share of incredible plays, including one of the most infamous blunders in the history of the National Football League.

So before Mom busts out the gravy and stuffing, before Dad cracks open his first cold one of the day, and before the kids break out the ol' pigskin in the backyard, let's rank the top-5 plays in Miami Dolphins' Thanksgiving Day game history.

On November 22nd, 1973, the defending Super Bowl Champion Miami Dolphins faced a very good Dallas Cowboys team on Thanksgiving Day. Early on in the contest, future Hall-of-Fame quarterback Bob Griese hit future Hall-of-Fame wide receiver Paul Warfield for a 45-yard touchdown in a game that would eventually end in a 14-7 Dolphins victory.

On a day that saw the Dolphins' defense sack Cowboys' quarterback Quincy Carter five times and intercept three of his passes, Sammy Knight's pick and subsequent 70-yard return was one for the highlight reels. Knight joined Patrick Surtain and Zach Thomas as beneficiaries of errant Cowboy Carter throws that afternoon, and the Dolphins went back to South Florida with a 40-21 victory.

Sammy Knight wasn't the only Dolphins defender with a signature play during Miami's win over Dallas in 2003. Hall-of-Fame defensive end Jason Taylor also thrilled fans of the aqua and orange that Thanksgiving with a 34-yard scoop and score after a Quincy Carter fumble. Dressed in throwback uniforms that day, the Dolphins defense dominated Dallas en route to a big Turkey Day victory.

One of the most memorable plays in NFL history would come later in this contest, however, without Keith Byars' 77-yard touchdown scamper through the Texas snow earlier in the game, we may have never seen it. After Byars crossed the goalline in Dallas, he would celebrate by making a snow angel on the Texas Stadium turf in Irving.

The most famous play in Thanksgiving Day history came in the Dallas snow in 1993. With Miami trailing 14-13 in the closing seconds of the tilt, Dolphins kicker Pete Stoyanovich attempted a 40-yard field goal for the win. The Cowboys blocked the kick — seemingly securing the victory for the home crowd. However, in a blunder for the ages, Cowboys defensive lineman Leon Lett inexplicably attempted to recover the loose football, but slipped on the slick turf, knocking the ball forward. Dolphins center Jeff Dellenbach would jump on the fumble near the Cowboys' goalline, giving Stoyanovich another opportunity to make a field goal from a much shorter distance. He would nail the kick and Miami would win the game by a score of 16-14.
