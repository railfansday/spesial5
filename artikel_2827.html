Good and Bad News for Lightning’s Brayden Point; Team Recalls Prospect

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

It's bad news for the Tampa Bay Lightning, but it's not the worst-case scenario.

The team that is quietly in the mix atop the loaded Eastern Conference and especially brutal Atlantic Division may have to navigate the coming weeks without one of their most important offensive weapons. Brayden Point exited Sunday's 4-3 loss to the Winnipeg Jets after just 5:35 of ice time, leaving with an undisclosed injury that could keep him sidelined for an undetermined amount of time. Head coach Jon Cooper acknowledged after the game that Point might miss some time, and Tampa Bay placed the 30-year-old center on injured reserve Tuesday.

Being placed on IR means the player must miss at least seven days. What is notably absent is a long-term injured reserve designation, which carries a minimum of a 24-day absence. So, the bad news is that Point will be out for at least the next week, but the good news is that the Lightning expect his return within the next couple of weeks.

Regardless of severity, the timing is particularly unfortunate for the Lightning as Point had begun to find his offensive rhythm. After a relatively quiet opening stretch, Point notched 10 points in seven games. Overall, he began Sunday's game with five goals and 11 assists in 17 games, putting him on pace for something close to another point-per-game season.

Point's recent production also came as he approached a significant career milestone. Earlier in the week, he became the sixth player in Lightning history to reach 700 career points, joining an exclusive group of franchise greats.

There is an additional reason for concern surrounding his latest injury. Point missed 19 games last season with a right knee injury, which also kept him out of the 2026 Winter Olympics. Although the Lightning have not disclosed the nature of his current injury, another extended absence would be an unwelcome development for a player whose game relies heavily on acceleration, agility and quick changes of direction.

Point's explosiveness is particularly important when considering what the Lightning stand to lose beyond Point's immediate production. He is not simply another top-six forward who can be replaced by shifting a few players around. His chemistry with Nikita Kucherov and Jake Guentzel on the power play, combined with his ability to operate as a high-end playmaking center, gives the Lightning a dimension that is difficult to replicate internally.

Cooper will have to make some adjustments in his absence. Anthony Cirelli is an obvious candidate to take on additional responsibilities down the middle, while Yanni Gourde's return to the lineup provides another option. Zemgus Girgensons could also see an expanded role, although his strengths are better suited to defensive assignments.

The Lightning have also recalled 20-year-old Sam O'Reilly from the Syracuse Crunch. The former first-round pick, acquired from Edmonton in July 2025, has four points in 12 AHL games and could make his NHL debut in Point's absence. O'Reilly's promotion also presents an opportunity to inject some youth into the lineup, but expecting a rookie who was not putting up big points in the AHL to immediately compensate for the absence of a player like Point is unrealistic. His presence on the top power-play unit during Tuesday's practice, however, suggests Cooper is at least willing to experiment.

The encouraging part is that Point had begun looking like himself again before Sunday's injury. His recent scoring streak demonstrated that his offensive instincts and ability to generate chances remain intact. The priority now should be ensuring that he can sustain that level of play once he returns, rather than simply getting him back into the lineup as quickly as possible.
