Vancouver Whitecaps & Thomas Müller look to &quot;finish the job&quot;

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Any dose of sunshine is a welcome development at this gray, chilly time of year in Cascadia. So it was a natural discussion topic when a certain celestial body broke through British Columbia's famously formidable cloud cover at a Vancouver Whitecaps FC training session earlier this week.

"It's always great to have the sun in 'Raincouver,'" deadpanned 'Caps winger Emmanuel Sabbi during a media availability on Tuesday.

It became even more difficult to avoid the metaphorical angles when head coach Jesper Sørensen confirmed that, should his side's delegation of national teamers return unharmed from international duty, a squad ravaged over the past two years by a relentless schedule and the injuries that tend to accompany it would be almost entirely healthy heading into their Western Conference Round One Best-of-3 Series opener vs. Real Salt Lake on Sunday at BC Place (8:30 pm ET | Apple TV; TSN, RDS).

"It's quite amazing," said the Danish manager. "As a coach, you just want to have the ability to choose between available players, and we have that.

"The players have looked energetic and ready, and also very competitive and wanting to improve, to show that they need to play and want to play, and that's the most important thing for me."

Every match matters. Don't miss a moment of the Audi 2026 MLS Cup Playoffs.

That's bad news for the rest of the West.

The Whitecaps just produced the two best – and probably most dramatic – seasons in their 16-year MLS existence, back to back, along the way riding a roller coaster of highs and lows, among them the headline-grabbing recruitment of German legend Thomas Müller, a landmark fourth consecutive Canadian Championship trophy (and falling short of defending that title this September), exhilarating runs to the 2025 Concacaf Champions Cup and MLS Cup finals, and agonizing losses therein.

Vancouver weathered the physical and psychological demands imposed by their own success, the uncertainty of the club being up for sale, the exciting but nevertheless disruptive presence of the 2026 FIFA World Cup at their BC Place home, the sale of influential starters Ali Ahmed and Sebastian Berhalter to English Championship outfits Norwich City and Middlesbrough, respectively… and survived. Thrived, even.

With a Decision Day win at CF Montréal, they managed to surpass last season's remarkable points total (63 then, 64 now) and finish atop the West with a 19W-8L-7D record and impressive +40 goal differential, ensuring that the left side of the Audi 2026 MLS Cup Playoffs bracket will run through their house.

"Axel [Schuster, VWFC's CEO and sporting director] talked to us one day, and then he was right when he said, 'OK, it's a big achievement for this club to be at the top of the league two years in a row," Müller revealed in a one-on-one interview with Global News, in which he also said he's sorted out his recent hamstring issues.

"When I think about [the LA] Galaxy, they won the [2024 MLS] Cup, and didn't qualify for the playoffs two years in a row now. So, in this American sports system, it's not that easy to stay on top year in and year out. That's what we achieved."

How did these cardiac 'Caps shake off the haunting pain of those high-profile heartbreaks, the 2025 Concacaf Champions Cup final rout against Cruz Azul in the lung-burning altitude of Mexico City and the narrow 3-1 defeat at Lionel Messi's Inter Miami in MLS Cup? The ConcaChampions hangover that challenges MLSers who venture deep into that continental competition is a proven phenomenon, one that has wrecked entire seasons.

"Losing finals is never easy," striker Brian White told MLSoccer.com this week. "Then to go through two of them and to lose both is obviously a very difficult thing to process and deal with. But as players and as professionals, I think it's even more motivating to want to get back to those spots and, in a sense, redeem yourselves and finish the job.

"That's what everyone was focused on going into this season, was getting back to MLS Cup, and winning it."

What's more, Vancouver kept on course with a target on their backs as the reigning conference champs, becoming one of MLS's prime barometers for both style and success alongside Miami.

"Last year was really a year that, definitely no one from the outside expected," noted playmaker and captain Ryan Gauld, who's been with the club since 2021. "The runs and stuff we went on, that probably wasn't as much pressure.

"This year, there was a little bit more expectation that this is how good we can be, and this is the standard that we set for ourselves, and it was just about picking it all back up again and going again. … I think we've come out stronger from that."

Gauld's long-awaited return from a nagging knee problem in July – despite taking part in last year's playoff run, he went an incredible 501 days between league starts – was a vital facet of coping with Berhalter's departure, even if his role is generally higher up the pitch than the US international's was, with Oliver Larraz and U22 Initiative signing Aleksa Cvetković usually sitting deeper.

After so many delays and setbacks, Vancouver's Scotsman says he's now "back to 100%, and it's not even a thought in the back of my head – I can play free again." His 6g/5a in 20 league appearances down the stretch earned him a place among the finalists for the MLS Comeback Player of the Year award, and rallied his teammates to boot.

"As much as it's been a hard process for him, it's been hard for us as well – not only not having him, but seeing everything that he's had to go through with the injury," said White of his 'White-Gauld' attacking mate.

"It's inspiring to all the fans and inspiring to all the players just to see how hard he works and the way he's able to lead us out on that field, and to have him back playing fit and healthy and back to the Ryan Gauld we all saw the years before."

Sørensen, too, is in the running for an end-of-season honor: Sigi Schmid MLS Coach of the Year. The Dane pushed the right buttons again in his second campaign in charge, tweaking his tactics and mixing in a flurry of new arrivals without fundamentally compromising results or the ethos of his fluid, front-footed press-and-possess game model.

Even at their low point, an early-autumn winless skid coinciding with their CanChamp semifinal elimination against Montréal, Vancouver's worst stretch in league play was a 2W-3L-3D span. Yet those on the inside don't deny that adjustments, and a measure of recentering, were required.

"We had ups and downs, especially after the World Cup. We struggled a bit with our results," said Müller. "In the beginning, I had the feeling we didn't struggle with our playing style. We were kind of dominant but not really effective. In between, after that, we also struggled sometimes with the games we played. But now we are on the up slope.

"And maybe teams adapted a bit to us. We were at the top of the table almost the whole season, and they look a little bit more into it: How can we hurt them? How can we maybe avoid their strengths? And maybe that's also what happened in one or two games. But we are always a team that kind of fights back."

The Whitecaps now have ample collective experience to reference as they embark on this '26 postseason adventure. Much of their squad is already battle-tested, and the rest have demonstrated their capacity to contribute. Few of the 18 playoff participants boast a deeper roster, or a clearer, sharper philosophical identity.

Heavy is the head that wears the crown, it's said. Yet the Whitecaps have come to embrace outsized ambitions, and an inside track on their pursuit.

"I would rather be favorites than underdogs," said Sørensen, "because that means that people have your probability of winning higher than the others. So that's always something that you like. And also, that's what we wanted all along.

"We know this is knockout games; we know how much can happen. But we would rather welcome that we are favorites than have to surprise everybody."
