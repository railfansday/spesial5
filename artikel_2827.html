Michigan basketball snaps skid, pummels Kansas State in Vegas finale

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

LAS VEGAS — After taking a beating in the first two rounds of the Players Era 16, the Wolverines delivered a beatdown in the finale.

Michigan snapped a three-game skid and salvaged the three-game Thanksgiving week trip with a 98-69 get-right win over Kansas State at T-Mobile Arena on Friday, Nov. 27.

Elliot Cadeau had 18 points and eight assists, Trey McKenney scored 18, and Moustapha Thiam added 16 points and 16 rebounds for Michigan (4-4), which left with something to feel good about following a pair of blowout losses to Creighton and Miami (Fla.) in its first two games of the Vegas tournament.

The fact Michigan was down a starter and a bench player hardly mattered. Roddy Gayle Jr. (ankle) had his left foot in a boot after rolling his ankle against Miami (Fla.) and Oscar Goodman missed his second straight game with a medical issue.

Despite being shorthanded, the Wolverines were in control throughout. They led by as much as 18 in the first half, by 17 at halftime and by double digits the final 25 minutes. They blew it open with an 18-2 spurt that was sparked by Kenny Noland.

Noland dished two assists in the span of five seconds, the first coming after he saved the ball from going out of bounds and the second coming after he stole the ensuing inbounds pass. He drained a corner 3 and made two free throws. He grabbed two offensive rebounds that led to points. Michigan's lead swelled to 73-40 with 12:33 to play and never dipped below 25 points the rest of the way.

J.P. Estrella added 12 points, Noland had 11 points and seven assists, and Joseph Hartman scored 10 for Michigan, which shot a season-best 53.1% from the field, had 27 assists and committed a season-low nine turnovers.

Brock Vice scored 16, Dez Lindsay 15 and Jaden Schutt 12 for Kansas State (3-5), which shot 36.5% for the game, finished 10-for-39 from 3-point range and never led.

Cadeau was a man on a mission and the Wolverines' primary source of offense out the gate. He scored the first eight points of the game and Michigan's first 10 points by the first media timeout. He hit a 3-pointer and a driving layup late in the shot clock. He had an and-1 layup and another finish in the paint.

Cadeau wasn't done. He found Thiam for a finish inside. He buried another 3 from the top of the key as a trailer on a fast break. He fed Hartman for another deep ball. Michigan jumped out to a 25-10 lead with 10:11 left in the first half.

Kansas State, meanwhile, hoisted one 3-pointer after another with little success. The Wildcats missed nine of their first 11 shots — nine coming from beyond the arc — and didn't hit double digits until nine minutes into the contest.

But after Kansas State finally got some shots to drop and used a 13-2 run to cut Michigan's lead to four, the Wolverines responded with a 12-0 burst to separate. It started with a five-point possession where Thiam had an and-1 bucket and grabbed his missed free throw, leading to a second-chance 3 by McKenney. A fallaway jumper from McKenney capped the run and made it 39-23 at the 3:18 mark.

Michigan pushed the margin to 18 in one of the better halves it's played this season before taking a 47-30 lead into the break that it wouldn't relinquish.
