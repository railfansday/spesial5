LeBron James sues startup over viral AI ad allegedly exploiting his likeness

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

NBA superstar LeBron James sued artificial intelligence-powered sports betting startup Bron and its two founders, accusing them of using his name, likeness and AI-generated voice without permission to promote their app.

In a lawsuit filed Tuesday in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, James accused PlayVision, which operates the Bron app, and co-founders Anish Gupta and Marc Zoghby of deliberately exploiting his "Bron" nickname to mislead consumers into believing he endorsed or was affiliated with the app.

The complaint alleges that the startup released an AI-generated advertisement earlier this month depicting James in a basketball locker room, wearing a jersey bearing his nickname and the iconic number 23, along with his tattoos and a simulated version of his voice.

The video showed the AI-generated figure scrolling through NBA betting options on the app.

James alleged that the defendants deliberately sought to provoke controversy to generate publicity for their app, with the advertisement attracting about one million views on X within a day.

The lawsuit claims the founders calculated that the publicity would outweigh any potential legal consequences, effectively treating the alleged violation of James' rights as a marketing expense.

It argues that James' endorsements are "uniquely valuable," adding, "That is why defendants decided to steal it for themselves."

The NBA star's lawyers sent a cease-and-desist letter on Nov. 5, after which the startup removed the ad from its X and Instagram accounts and said it was working to change the app's name, according to the complaint.

However, the ad remained available through third-party reposts, and the company had not changed the app's name as of the lawsuit's filing.

James is seeking damages and a court order preventing the defendants from using his name, image, likeness, and voice, as well as the Bron name.

Bron, operated by PlayVision, is described in Apple's App Store as a "sports analytics and betting research app with live odds comparisons, data-backed picks, player and team trends, a multi-leg Slip and bet tracking." The listing says it does not accept wagers.

PlayVision is headquartered in California and participated in Y Combinator's fall 2025 batch.

— CNBC's Dan Mangan contributed to this story.
