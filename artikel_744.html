Watford appoint Iordanescu as head coach

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Edward Iordanescu took Romania to the last 16 at Euro 2024

Watford have appointed former Romania boss Edward Iordanescu as the Championship club's new head coach.

The 48-year-old replaces Alessio Dionisi, who was sacked on Sunday after only five months in charge following a run of seven defeats in their past 10 league games, culminating in Saturday's 4-0 loss to Sheffield United.

"I am very proud and happy to be at this football club," Iordanescu told the club website., external

"I can see the team is in a difficult moment but I have already watched many of the games over the last few days and I'm very confident about the future."

He becomes Watford's sixth new boss in less than 18 months and is the 25th manager since the Pozzo family took over the club in 2012.

The former CFR Cluj and Legia Warsaw manager will be joined at Watford by backroom staff Alexandru Radu, Grzegorz Mokry and Michele Iannucci, but will watch Wednesday's Championship match at home to Blackburn Rovers from the stands before taking charge for Saturday's game at Derby.

Adrian Mariappa will oversee Wednesday's match at Vicarage Road (19:45 GMT), alongside Paul Furlong and Lloyd Doyley.

The Watford job is Iordanescu's first since leaving Legia Warsaw in October 2025, where he won the Super Cup last season.

It is the 10th managerial post of his 15-year coaching career, which has taken in Bulgarian club CSKA Sofia as well as Steaua Bucharest and Cluj - who he guided to the Romanian title and two domestic cups after joining midway through the 2020-21 season.

He also spent two-and-a-half years in charge of Romania, guiding them to the last 16 of Euro 2024, where they were beaten by the Netherlands.

Iordanescu takes over a Watford side currently three points above the relegation zone, having won only four of their 16 games so far.

Watford will be Iordanescu's first job in English football

Although untried in English football, Watford are getting a trophy-winning head coach with a wide-ranging CV spanning spells in his homeland, Bulgaria and Poland, as well as elite-level international experience with Romania.

His connections with his national side run deep as the son of his country's legendary former head coach Anghel Iordanescu, who famously took them to the quarter-finals of the 1994 World Cup during a reign of more than 100 games.

It is likely he will need every bit of that nous and inspiration as he becomes the latest boss to try to revive a club which continues to plough through managers at an alarming rate.

Dionisi was Watford's 12th head coach, not including interim bosses, since the end of the 2020-21 season, with Iordanescu becoming the sixth permanent boss since Tom Cleverley was sacked in May 2025.

The Hornets' slow start to the season - with only four home wins and one point out of 24 away - has played out against the backdrop of uncertainty and unrest off the pitch.

Watford's owner, Gino Pozzo, is currently standing trial in Spain as one of a number of defendants accused of tax offences relating to Spanish team Granada, a club he used to be associated with.

He denies the allegations, which are related to player trading at Granada.

The case continues a tumultuous period for the club, with supporter and former owner Elton John voicing his frustration after a 4-0 home defeat by Burnley last month.

Fans have also questioned the running of the club, with one supporters' group declaring a loss of confidence in the ownership.

Iordanescu's immediate concern will be improving results, particularly Watford's form on the road, where they have yet to win and have conceded 18 goals in eight matches - with only Burnley letting in more.

He arrives with a reputation for defensive organisation and stability, of which the Hornets are in desperate need.

Geoff Doyle, BBC Three Counties Radio sports editor

So Watford try again with a new head coach - it is a regular occurrence at Vicarage Road.

Iordanescu has a decent CV with his work overseas in Romania, Bulgaria and Poland and is well thought of as a coach and manager.

But a lack of Championship experience is a concern and he is thrown in at the deep end with his new team hovering just above the relegation zone and confidence and morale low.

Iordanescu needs to hit the ground running in a critical week with the Hornets up against mid-table Blackburn and then Derby who are in the relegation zone.
