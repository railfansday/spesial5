Reese's Heir Brad Reese Reveals Dad's Eerie Deathbed Confession

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Reese's heir Brad Reese shared that his dad Dick Reese's 2025 deathbed confession is the reason he's publicly called out  Hershey for allegedly replacing the candy's ingredients.

Brad Reese isn't sugarcoating a recent family revelation.

Nine months after the grandson of Reese's Peanut Butter Cups inventor H.B. Reese accused The Hershey Company of "quietly replacing" the candy's signature chocolate and peanut with different ingredients, he revealed how dad Charles Richard "Dick" Reese's biggest regret led to his new mission.

"'I don't wanna die because I don't wanna go to heaven,'" Brad revealed Dick told him before his 2005 death in an interview with The Daily Mail published Nov. 22. His dad added that he was afraid to reunite with H.B. because he "double-crossed him" by selling the company outside the family.

Though Brad—who noted that he still holds shares in Hershey—has no formal role in, or ownership of, Reese's since his dad and five uncles sold the rights to produce the candy to the company in 1963, no amount of sweet-talking would keep him from advocating for the peanut butter cups' legacy.

"He made me promise if he gave me my shares I would never sell them," he said of their conversation. "There's no amount of money that would send me packing. I'm not doing this for money, and I'm not doing this for a position on the board."

Brad's decision to unwrap his feud with Hershey—which went on to introduce other products like Reese's Pieces in 1978 and Reese's Puffs in 1994—turned a deeply personal situation public when he criticized the company online earlier this year.

"I'm doing it because I believe in it, that's the most important thing," he clarified. "There's nothing that the Hershey company can do to financially hurt me."

The U.S. Army veteran couldn't resist publicly criticizing the major chocolate manufacturer, alleging in a February LinkedIn post that Hershey replaced "milk chocolate with compound coatings and peanut butter with peanut-butter-style crèmes across multiple Reese's products" in recent years.

"Right now, the Reese's story is diverging from what's inside Reese's products," he continued. "I'm not asking for nostalgia. I'm asking for alignment. For truth in Reese's brand stewardship. For a corporate narrative from The Hershey Company that reflects the Reese's product consumers are actually receiving."

Hershey disputed Brad's claims, telling E! News in a statement at the time that the peanut butter cups are still "made the same way they always have been, starting with roasting fresh peanuts to make our unique, one-of-a-kind peanut butter that is then combined with milk chocolate."

However, the company noted that other Reese's products may have different ingredients.

"As we've grown and expanded the Reese's product line, we make product recipe adjustments that allow us to make new shapes, sizes and innovations that Reese's fans have come to love and ask for," Hershey continued, "while always protecting the essence of what makes Reese's unique and special: the perfect combination of chocolate and peanut butter."
