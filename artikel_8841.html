Picture this: you've just landed in Honolulu after a long transcontinental flight. You've got four days on Maui planned, and then a few nights on Kauai before heading home. The problem? Those interisland flights add up fast — Hawaiian Airlines will gladly charge you $150–$250 *per person* for a 20-to-40-minute hop between islands. For a family of four, that's easily $600–$1,000 in airfare just to island-hop, on top of everything else you've already spent to get to Hawaii. Now imagine paying 7,500 HawaiianMiles per person instead — miles you transferred from your American Express Membership Rewards account at a 1:1 ratio just a day or two before booking. Suddenly, that $150–$250 ticket costs you points worth less than a nice dinner. At 3.3 cents per point on the high end, you're extracting extraordinary value from miles that most people leave sitting in an account doing nothing. This is what the ChooseFI community means when we talk about finding pricing inefficiencies in loyalty programs. Hawaiian Airlines prices these interisland segments on a dynamic award chart — but because these are short, regional hops operated almost entirely on 717s and A321s between islands most people aren't cross-shopping for redemptions, the program hasn't inflated the price the way major international carriers have. The sweet spot holds. The math is simple and satisfying: a cash ticket from Honolulu (HNL) to Kahului, Maui (OGG) or Lihue, Kauai (LIH) typically runs $100–$250. You're redeeming 7,500 miles to cover that cost, which means you're getting **1.3 to 3.3 cents per point** in value — above what most programs deliver on domestic redemptions. And because Hawaiian charges **zero fuel surcharges** on its own-operated flights, every mile goes toward the ticket price, not toward fees. For the FI-minded traveler who planned a multi-island Hawaii trip anyway, this redemption doesn't just save money — it reframes how you think about points. These aren't bonuses. They're a currency, and knowing where to spend them is the skill.

The core insight here is deceptively simple: HawaiianMiles prices interisland flights at a flat 7,500 miles in economy, and that price is shockingly stable relative to what these tickets actually cost in cash.

Here's why this works as a pricing inefficiency. Hawaiian Airlines operates a near-monopoly on interisland travel in Hawaii. There's limited true competition for these routes (go! Mokulele operates turboprops on some routes, but Hawaiian dominates the jet service). Because cash demand is inelastic — visitors need to get between islands — Hawaiian charges a premium in cash. But the HawaiianMiles award program hasn't kept pace with those cash price increases in the same aggressive way that programs like United MileagePlus or Delta SkyMiles have. The result: 7,500 miles per segment, consistently, while cash prices climb.

Now let's talk about how you actually get those miles into your account.

The primary transfer path the ChooseFI community uses is American Express Membership Rewards → HawaiianMiles at a 1:1 ratio. If you have the Amex Gold, Platinum, or a no-annual-fee Amex Everyday card earning MR points, you can transfer directly to Hawaiian. Transfer speed is 1–2 days — not instant, so plan accordingly and don't wait until the last minute.

Two other paths exist if you're working with different currencies:

One critical nuance: HawaiianMiles are earned primarily through flying, not credit card spend. Hawaiian does have a co-branded credit card, but it's not a top earner for most people's daily spend. This means your primary accumulation strategy is transfer partners — Amex MR and Bilt are your best levers.

Another key detail: Hawaiian miles never expire. This matters because you can slowly accumulate miles across multiple statement cycles, transfer when you're ready to book, and not feel rushed. There's no expiration clock running against you.

Finally, the no fuel surcharges policy is a genuine differentiator. Many programs — particularly those involving European carriers — tack on $300–$600 in surcharges that gut your redemption's net value. HawaiianMiles charges no fuel surcharges on Hawaiian-operated flights. Your 7,500 miles covers the flight. Taxes and fees on interisland tickets are minimal, often just a few dollars. What you see is what you get.

Go to hawaiianairlines.com and use the 'Book' tool, selecting 'Reward Travel' as your travel type. Enter your route — for example, HNL to OGG (Honolulu to Kahului, Maui) or HNL to LIH (Honolulu to Lihue, Kauai). Plug in your desired travel date and search. You're looking for economy award space priced at 7,500 miles. Availability on interisland routes is almost always wide open — this is one of the best availability scores (5/5) of any sweet spot in this guide series, but it costs nothing to confirm before you transfer.

If you don't have a HawaiianMiles account, create one at hawaiianairlines.com — it takes about two minutes and is free. Note your HawaiianMiles number. You'll need it for the transfer step.

From Amex Membership Rewards: Log into your Amex account, navigate to 'Membership Rewards,' select 'Transfer Points,' and choose Hawaiian Airlines HawaiianMiles as the destination. Enter your HawaiianMiles number and the number of miles you want to transfer (at minimum, 7,500 per person you're booking). Transfers process in 1–2 days.

From Bilt Rewards: Log into your Bilt app or biltrewards.com, navigate to 'Redeem,' select 'Transfer to Airline Partner,' and choose Hawaiian Airlines at 1:1. Same 1–2 day transfer window.

From Marriott Bonvoy: Use this only as a last resort due to the 3:1 ratio. Navigate to Bonvoy's transfer portal and select HawaiianMiles. Allow up to 5 days.

Once your miles land (check your HawaiianMiles account balance), return to hawaiianairlines.com, log in, and re-run your award search. Select the flight you want, confirm the 7,500-mile price, and proceed through checkout. Taxes on interisland awards are typically just a few dollars — no fuel surcharges apply.

HawaiianMiles opens its booking window 330 days out. If you're planning a Hawaii trip far in advance and have specific flight times in mind, you can search and book early. That said, given the excellent availability on these routes, booking 2–4 weeks out is usually fine.

This is rare for interisland routes, but if it happens, try: searching adjacent dates, looking at different departure times on the same route, or checking alternate routing (e.g., OGG to LIH instead of HNL to LIH directly). Interisland availability doesn't fluctuate the way transatlantic business class does.

Let's make this concrete. You're planning a two-week Hawaii trip for two people. You've booked your Honolulu arrival flight separately, and now you need to connect the dots between islands: Honolulu (HNL) to Kahului, Maui (OGG) mid-trip, and then Maui (OGG) to Lihue, Kauai (LIH) before heading home.

The cash price check: Pull up hawaiianairlines.com and look at what these tickets retail for. On a typical mid-trip travel day, the HNL→OGG segment is priced at $189 per person. The OGG→LIH hop runs $149 per person. For two travelers, that's $676 in cash — before you've bought a single plate of shave ice.

The points price: Each segment costs 7,500 HawaiianMiles per person in economy. Two passengers, two flights: 30,000 HawaiianMiles total, plus roughly $10–$20 in taxes and fees across both bookings. No fuel surcharges.

The CPP calculation: You're replacing $676 in cash spending with 30,000 miles. That's 2.25 cents per point — well above the 0.8–1.2 cpp you'd expect on typical HawaiianMiles redemptions, and solidly within the 1.3–3.3 cpp sweet spot range for this redemption.

What the experience looks like: Hawaiian's interisland product is no-frills — these are 20-to-40-minute flights on Boeing 717s. You won't get a meal or premium service. But you will get a reliable, on-time carrier, clean aircraft, and the undeniable satisfaction of hopping between two of the most beautiful island destinations on Earth without opening your wallet at the gate.

For the ChooseFI traveler, the win isn't the in-flight experience — it's the mental accounting shift. You budgeted for interisland flights; now that budget goes to a sunset sailing charter or a snorkeling excursion instead. The points paid for the planes. The cash funds the memories.

Do: Transfer Points in the Right Batch Size HawaiianMiles doesn't allow you to transfer partial miles back out, so calculate exactly how many you need before initiating a transfer. For two passengers on two flights, that's 30,000 miles. Transfer exactly that amount — or a small buffer — rather than dumping your entire MR balance into a program you may not use frequently.

Do: Use Amex MR or Bilt as Your Primary Earning Strategy Since HawaiianMiles are earned primarily through flying, your accumulation plan should center on transferable currencies. The Amex Gold Card (4x on dining and groceries) and Amex Platinum (5x on flights) are elite MR earners. Bilt's no-annual-fee card earning on rent is underused by the ChooseFI community for exactly this kind of transfer.

Do: Search for Availability Before You Transfer This is worth repeating as a pro tip because new points travelers make this mistake constantly. Transfer speeds are 1–2 days for Amex MR and Bilt. Don't transfer points and then discover the flight you wanted is sold out. Always confirm availability first, then transfer, then book immediately once miles land.

Avoid: Using Marriott Bonvoy for This Transfer At a 3:1 ratio, you'd spend 22,500 Bonvoy points for a 7,500-mile award. Bonvoy points are worth approximately 0.7–0.9 cpp on their own best redemptions. The math doesn't hold here — save your Bonvoy points for hotel redemptions.

Do: Consider the Merger Context Hawaiian Airlines is in the process of merging with Alaska Airlines, and HawaiianMiles may eventually transition into Alaska Mileage Plan. Pay attention to program announcements. This could change transfer partners, award pricing, or program structure. The current sweet spot exists now — book it while the program operates under its current rules.

Avoid: Waiting for a 'Better' Redemption on These Miles HawaiianMiles are a limited-use currency. They're excellent for interisland and mainland-to-Hawaii flights, but they don't transfer to other airlines and don't unlock international business class sweet spots. If you've got miles in this program, use them for exactly what they're designed for — Hawaiian flights — rather than letting them sit hoping for a better opportunity that may never come.

Do: Book Multiple Segments in a Single Trip If you're doing a true multi-island itinerary, every interisland hop is 7,500 miles. Three islands means two hops — 15,000 miles per person. Plan your island sequence to minimize backtracking, and book all segments at once once your miles transfer.

Make multi-island trips affordable with the right points strategy

Most visitors to Hawaii stick to one island because interisland flights add $100-$400+ per person to the trip cost. By using points for these short hops, you unlock a fundamentally different Hawaii experience — island-hopping from Oahu to Maui to the Big Island to Kauai without the airfare costs stacking up. This is a multiplier strategy: you are not just saving on one flight, you are enabling an entirely different type of trip. Combining a points-funded mainland-to-Hawaii flight with points-funded interisland hops creates a nearly free (in cash) multi-island Hawaiian vacation.

Decide which islands you want to visit and in what order. The most common combinations are Oahu + Maui, Oahu + Big Island, or a three-island itinerary. Map out how many interisland hops you need so you know the total points required.

Check Hawaiian Airlines (7,500 miles each way), Southwest (variable, typically 4,000-8,000 points), and United (5,000-7,500 miles) for each interisland segment. Southwest often offers the best flexibility with no change fees and no blackout dates.

For Hawaiian Airlines, transfer Amex Membership Rewards. For Southwest, earn Rapid Rewards directly or transfer from Chase (transfer to Southwest is currently limited). For United segments, transfer from Chase or use Turkish Miles&Smiles for even lower pricing.

Book each interisland hop as a separate one-way award. This gives you maximum flexibility to mix airlines and optimize pricing per segment. One-way awards have no penalty compared to round trips on these short routes.

Align your interisland hops with your arrival and departure flights. Arrive on one island and depart from a different one (open-jaw booking) to avoid backtracking. For example, arrive Honolulu, hop to Maui, depart Maui to the mainland.

Hawaiian Airlines has the most extensive interisland network and offers the most daily frequencies. Southwest Airlines also operates key interisland routes (Honolulu-Maui, Honolulu-Kona, Honolulu-Lihue). United flies select interisland segments as well. Each airline has different award pricing and transfer partner access.

Hawaiian Airlines interisland awards typically price at 7,500 HawaiianMiles each way. Southwest varies by cash fare but generally costs 4,000-8,000 Rapid Rewards points each way for interisland hops. United interisland segments can be booked for 5,000-7,500 miles using various Star Alliance partner programs.

Yes, especially when booking multiple hops. A family of four making two interisland hops at $150 each way would spend $1,200 in cash. Converting that to points frees up significant cash for accommodations, activities, and dining. The per-point value (1.5-2.5 cents) is reasonable, and the trip flexibility gained is substantial.

A popular strategy is to fly into Honolulu (Oahu), hop to Maui for beaches and Haleakala, then continue to the Big Island (Kona) for volcanoes and snorkeling, and optionally finish on Kauai for dramatic scenery. Each hop is 30-60 minutes. Allow 2-3 days minimum per island.

This redemption was built for families and couples planning a multi-island Hawaii vacation — full stop. If you're visiting multiple islands (which most Hawaii first-timers and repeat visitors do), you're already mentally budgeting for interisland flights. This sweet spot simply redirects that cash budget back into your pocket and replaces it with points.

Families get a 5/5 Family Friendly score here for good reason: availability is wide open, booking is simple, and the economics scale beautifully. Four people, two hops = 60,000 miles to cover potentially $1,200+ in interisland airfare. That's an extraordinary displacement of vacation cash.

Beginners are explicitly welcomed by this redemption's design. There's no partner airline complexity, no fuel surcharge math to calculate, no routing rules to decode. You search on Hawaiian's website, you transfer points, you book. The 5/5 Beginner Friendly and Easy to Book scores reflect that this is genuinely one of the simplest award redemptions you can execute.

Minimum points needed: 7,500 HawaiianMiles per person per segment. For a solo traveler doing two hops, that's 15,000 miles. For a family of four doing two hops, that's 60,000 miles.

This is NOT for: Travelers who don't have an Amex MR or Bilt account and no path to earning HawaiianMiles quickly. It's also not ideal for travelers who only need a single interisland flight — the opportunity cost of setting up the transfer relationship for 7,500 miles may not be worth it unless you're already accumulating MR points for other redemptions.

This redemption earns a **Beginner** rating — and it genuinely deserves it. In a world of waitlists, fuel surcharges, routing rules, and phone-only bookings, Hawaiian interisland awards are refreshingly clean. **What makes it easy:** Award availability is consistently excellent (5/5). The booking portal is functional and user-friendly. There are no fuel surcharges to calculate. Miles never expire, so there's no pressure. You can complete the entire redemption — from transfer initiation to confirmed booking — in about 48 hours. **The one genuine friction point:** Earning HawaiianMiles isn't as straightforward as earning Chase Ultimate Rewards or Amex MR. Hawaiian doesn't have a widely-used co-branded credit card for most people's everyday spend. This means you're dependent on transfer partners, and the Amex MR and Bilt paths are your primary options. If you don't have either, this redemption becomes harder to access. **Tip 1:** Start with an Amex card that earns Membership Rewards — the Amex Gold is the ChooseFI community favorite for everyday earning. Once you're accumulating MR points, you have a direct path to Hawaiian via a 1:1 transfer. **Tip 2:** Always search award availability before transferring. It takes 30 seconds and protects you from the one mistake that could derail an otherwise smooth booking. **Tip 3:** Don't overthink the merger situation. Hawaiian and Alaska remain separate programs today. Book what's in front of you. If you can earn Amex MR points, you can execute this redemption. It's the perfect first award booking for a ChooseFI member who wants to see how the system works without risking complexity.

Interisland awards are the key that unlocks multi-island Hawaii vacations without the stacking airfare costs that keep most travelers on a single island. At 7,500-10,000 miles per hop, the points cost is modest, and the trip transformation is significant. Whether you use Hawaiian Airlines miles, Southwest Rapid Rewards, or United partner programs, the strategy is the same: let points handle the short hops so your cash stays in your pocket for experiences. Combined with a points-funded mainland-to-Hawaii flight, interisland awards make a multi-island Hawaiian vacation one of the most achievable aspirational trips in travel rewards.
