Zwift’s Dedicated Indoor Bike Makes Your Winter Riding Better—and Protects Your Road Bike

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Zwift Ride V2 makes indoor training easier with quick fit tweaks and low upkeep.

Gear-obsessed editors choose every product we review. We may earn commission if you buy from a link. How we test gear.

I’ve used a handful of indoor trainers over the years, but a few years ago I picked up a Wahoo Kickr Core and I thought that would be my trainer for the foreseeable future. It’s easy to use and reliable. It did exactly what I needed it to do—turn my bike into a trainer for offseason training.

When I ran a shop, we would sell through the Wahoo Kickr Core because it has always been a product I personally love—it is easier to sell products that I genuinely believe in and use. More than that, every shop team I’ve worked with would use the Wahoo Kickr Core as their trainer of choice. Then I was introduced to the Zwift Ride and realized I was not done buying trainers just yet.

As the shop’s service manager, I got firsthand experience assessing bikes after a season of sitting on the trainer; let alone season after season, year after year. The level of maintenance needed for those bikes was initially shocking to me as well as to the customer. Most of us are aware we need to do maintenance on our bikes that we ride outside, but it’s easy to forget about our unsung hero—our trainer bike sitting in the basement constantly covered in sweat, spilled drink mix, and getting the watts cranked into it day in and day out.

There’s a better way than just throwing your bike on the trainer for your offseason. The Zwift Ride V2 with the Wahoo Kickr Core is now my top recommended way to use a bike trainer. It’s easy to see why.

For those who have multiple riders in one house, this may be one of the biggest benefits. When I had my bike set up on the Kickr Core it was harder for my wife to just hop on the trainer. Since my old trainer bike was carbon, even a simple saddle adjustment required a torque wrench. I’ve even gone so far as to change out stems to change the reach too—not the hardest thing to do, but by no means convenient. On the Zwift Ride, most fit adjustments are super easy and take just seconds.

The Zwift Ride 2 I’ve been on even has multiple crank length options set up in the cranks, so you just swap the pedals to a different hole in the crank to change the effective crank length. Taking out one more friction point between me and being able to start my trainer ride means I’m even more likely to get my winter trainer miles in.

The other day I was a few minutes into my warmup and I needed to adjust my reach a bit. I pulled the integrated 6mm Allen key out from its place under the top tube, easily adjusted the reach at the handlebars, tightened everything down, and reinstalled the tool all without interrupting my cadence. Having the tool I need to adjust just about anything on the bike is a new level of convenience I was not used to.

As Matt Phillips pointed out in his Zwift Ride 2 review, “Mechanically, the Ride is almost primitive.” For clarity, this is by no means a negative—when it comes to a trainer bike, mechanically primitive but extremely functional is a best-case scenario. This bike has a chain that goes from the chainring, through a chain tensioner, around the Zwift Cog attached to the Wahoo Kickr, and back up to the chainring. There’s no derailleur, no front tire that needs air, no brakes to knock out of alignment. The handlebars don’t even turn, so there’s no headset to replace in the spring because it was sitting in a pool with sweat all winter.

On the other side, you’re saving your daily driver from a lot of unseen mechanical damage. As the snow thaws out on the roads, road riders are ready to get back in the saddle under the sun, but if you use your bike on the trainer all winter, it may need A LOT of love before it’s road-worthy.

A bike that sits on the trainer all winter collects significant corrosion in headset and bottom bracket bearings. The sweat that comes off your head drops straight onto your headset and sweat from your body (and any of those sticky drink mixes) drop straight down toward the bottom bracket. Then we get to the drivetrain. I’ve had bikes come in around March that need full drivetrain replacement: chainrings, cassette, and chain after a good season of indoor miles. Finally, we get to the handlebar—at a minimum you should replace that bar tape after a season riding on the trainer. This also gives you a chance to get the handlebar looked at to make sure all that corrosion (sweat) hasn’t eaten into the them.

I’ve seen service tickets range anywhere from $100 to almost a thousand dollars just to get a bike off the trainer and ready to ride outside. For some people one season of trainer riding on the Zwift Ride 2 could be more cost-effective than using your road bike on a trainer.

Before an activity becomes a habit people are good at finding reasons not to do something. It doesn’t take long to setup a bike on the Wahoo Kickr Core, but even that little bit of set up time to pop your bike on the trainer is one more hurdle for you not to ride today. If you have to swap your cassette over from your bike to the trainer—you can almost forget about it.

It doesn’t just go for getting your bike on the trainer. This week in Pennsylvania we’re having a stretch of perfect riding weather—relatively low humidity, sunny, and temperatures between 55 degrees and 60 degrees. Next week’s forecast calls for colder temperatures and rain, also known as great indoor-riding weather. But this week, if I have a window of time to ride, I want to be able to just hop on my bike and go; I don’t want to have to take the bike off the trainer and reinstall my cassette before I can even start pedaling outside. With a dedicated trainer setup, I can just take my bike off the hook on the wall and roll away. Next week, when the weather goes south, I can just hop back on the trainer.

The Zwift Ride makes it easier to do things like use power-ups, steer the avatar, and toggle through screens, but those features are just an added bonus compared to the other reasons to pick one up. It is a burly, simple, near-flawless machine for riding on the trainer. It’s stable when putting out your max watts. The built-in tool makes it easy to accommodate different riders—including adjusting the reach. Even the bottle cages are designed to fit almost any bottle. The bike’s handlebar tray fits an iPad or other tablet well, but I pair it with a rolling trainer table if I’m using my laptop. You can buy the Zwift Ride itself, or as a package deal with Wahoo’s excellent Kickr Core 2, which gives you everything you need for a stress-free indoor riding season.
