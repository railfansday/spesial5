Qatar’s visitor numbers are recovering on road traffic across the Saudi border, while air arrivals keep falling. Drive-in visitors tend to make shorter trips, which limits how much they can do for hotels.

Qatar’s tourism recovery from the U.S.-Iran war is running on road traffic across the Saudi border, a Skift analysis of Qatar Tourism data shows. .

International visitor arrivals in October totaled 336,000, down 10% from the same time last year, according to Qatar Tourism’s monthly arrivals data. September’s arrivals were up 1%. Both are a sharp turnaround from March, when arrivals fell 82% year over year to 63,000.

Qatar received 2.95 million visitors in the first 10 months of 2026, down 24% from 3.9 million in the same period last year.

Road is the only part of Qatar’s arrivals that is growing. Land arrivals, which all come through Qatar’s sole land border with Saudi Arabia, rose 18% year over year in October. Air arrivals fell 19%, registering an eighth straight month of decline.

Qatar's tourism recovery from the U.S.-Iran war is being carried by road traffic from Saudi Arabia, a Skift analysis of Qatar Tourism data shows. International arrivals reached 336,000 in October, down 10% year over year, and 2.95 million in the first 10 months of 2026, down 24% from 3.9 million a year earlier. Land arrivals through Qatar's only land border rose 18% in October and now make up 31% of all arrivals, up from 24% a year earlier. Air arrivals fell 19%, an eighth straight monthly decline, after collapsing 90% in March. Shaza Doha general manager Siddhartha Chowdhry said several pre-war source markets have yet to return, citing travel advisories, reduced airline seat capacity, higher travel insurance costs, and weaker traveler confidence. Hotel occupancy in September was 61.5%, down three percentage points year over year but well above April's 44.5% low, with RevPAR down 4%. Sea arrivals have been near zero since March, and the new cruise season is a test of whether ships return. Qatar is again leaning on events — MotoGP in early November and F1 from November 27 to 29 — after last year's F1 weekend drove ADR of about $241 and roughly 92% occupancy on its opening Friday. ValuStrat's Saad Lodhi expects cooler weather and F1 to lift performance but warned against counting on a repeat of last year's peaks.
