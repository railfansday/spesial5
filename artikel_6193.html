American Airlines Pilot Arrested At Heathrow For Being Unfit To Fly And Having Cocaine

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Every so often we’ll hear stories of pilots being arrested for showing up at the airport intoxicated. Well, here’s an even wilder story, about an American Airlines pilot who not only showed up at the airport unfit to fly, but also had cocaine on him.

View from the Wing flags a story about how on November 1, 2026, a 64-year-old pilot was arrested at London Heathrow Airport (LHR):

So this is sort of wild — not only was he under the influence of something (drugs or alcohol), but then he also had cocaine in his possession. That’s just next level irresponsibility.

It’s not clear how exactly all of this was discovered, in terms of the sequence of events. Was there just a random check, or was he acting erratically, authorities were alerted, and that’s when this all came to light? If so, it sounds like the first clue was how he was acting (which made him unfit to fly), and then presumably he was searched, which is when the cocaine may have been discovered.

This is just so beyond irresponsible. If he was already unfit to fly during boarding and had cocaine in his possession, was he going to consume that during the flight, or just take it home with him?

Keep in mind that in the United States, commercial pilots have to retire by their 65th birthday. So this guy was just weeks or months from retirement… what a sad way to end a career, as you can bet he won’t be flying again. One wonders if he was just trying to party a little before retirement, if this had been going on for some time, or what.

While the authorities haven’t identified the airline that this pilot works for (other than to say he works for a US airline), it’s not too hard to figure out. Based on his name (which I won’t post here, because he’s in enough trouble as is), it’s clear he’s an American Airlines pilot.

American had two flight cancelations out of Heathrow that day — one for flight AA85 to Chicago (ORD), and one for flight AA91 to Miami (MIA). It’s not clear which of those he was scheduled to fly on.

Under UK261 regulations, any passenger who ended up arriving at their final destination four or more hours late would be entitled to £520 ($690) in cash compensation. That’s on top of covering any reasonable expenses incurred. Admittedly some people may have been rebooked on other flights once this flight was axed, but at least a large percentage of passengers would presumably be eligible for this.

An American Airlines pilot who was close to retirement decided to end his career in a rather unfortunate way, as he was arrested at Heathrow for being unfit to fly, while also having cocaine in his possession. The lack of decent judgment shown here is beyond irresponsible, and I hope he gets the help he needs so he doesn’t ever endanger others in this way again. The good news for him is that he was within months of retirement anyway, so at least his “career” impact is pretty minimal.
