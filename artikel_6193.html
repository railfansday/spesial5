Singapore Airlines Becomes The 1st Airline To Reveal A Full Boeing 777-9 Seat Map

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

As the Boeing 777X series of widebody twinjets edges ever closer to finally receiving its long-awaited certification, one of the main questions surrounding the aircraft is how airlines will configure it. With the popular stretched 777-9 model in particular being longer and wider than most other airliners today, there is ample space to install luxurious premium products. Singapore Airlines plans to do just this.

When the carrier revealed its new first and business class suites for the Boeing 777-9 at an event in Singapore last week, it also shared its planned seat map for the aircraft, becoming the first airline anywhere in the world to do so. This finally gave a concrete indication of the premium-heavy nature of how airlines might configure the 777X, with almost a third of the seats being non-economy ones.

Singapore Airlines plans to seat 300 passengers on its Boeing 777-9s. According to ch-aviation, the Star Alliance member has 31 of these new widebody twinjets on order, having bought 20 in June 2017 and another 11 in February 2021. The most luxurious seats on these aircraft will be the six first class suites: of these, four will be located at the windows while the other two are an exclusive center pair.

Behind the six first class suites are 54 business class flatbeds that are laid out in a 1-2-1 setup, with every seat offering direct aisle access. The rear of the second section has 36 premium economy recliners in a 2-4-2 layout, meaning that there are 96 non-economy seats (32%) overall. The rear section features 204 economy seats in a 3-4-3 layout that narrows to 2-4-2 in the last three rows. The airline says that:

"While each cabin has been designed individually, they are united by the same ambition to make the experience more comfortable, flexible, and rewarding for our customers."

With the arrival of the Boeing 777-9 at Singapore Airlines heralding a new era of long-haul travel for the Star Alliance member, the carrier is using the jet as a means of showcasing various luxurious new cabin products. As previously noted, the most notable of these is the center pair of suites in the first class cabin, offering a private and exclusive retreat for couples traveling together in the best seats in the house.

Regardless of whether first class passengers are seated in the center pair or one of the four outer solo suites, they can expect 89.5 inches of pitch (227 cm) and 23 inches (58 cm)of width, with the latter figure increasing to 30 (76 cm) when the armrests are lowered. When converted to a bed, the seat stretches to 80 inches (203 cm), making for a good night's sleep for even the tallest of passengers. Entertainment comes from a 32-inch (81 cm) 4K screen.

Singapore Airlines is also set to roll out a new business class product as part of this initiative in the second half of 2027, with Airbus A350s also included. These spacious flatbeds will offer 50 inches (127 cm) of pitch and 24 inches (61 cm) of width on the 777-9 when upright, with their bed length being 79 inches (201 cm). However, the silver and yellow color scheme has drawn criticism online for not being very Singapore Airlines-coded.

Singapore Airlines is making a surprising choice for its next-generation premium economy cabin. The reason involves a much-delayed aircraft.

As well as replacing older Airbus A380s, Singapore Airlines also plans to use its Boeing 777-9s to replace older examples of the 777-300ER. With this model being the 777-9's most natural predecessor, it is worth examining how Singapore Airlines configures these aircraft as a point of comparison. On the first class side, capacity is lower, with the carrier's seat map showing just one row of four spacious suites.

These are laid out in a 1-2-1 configuration, as are the 777-300ER's 48 business class flatbeds, which are split between the rear of the first cabin section and the whole of the second. Behind these are 28 premium economy recliners in a 2-4-2 layout, which occupy the front of the third section, followed by 184 economy seats in the rear of the third and the whole of the fourth section. A key reason for the lower capacity is the less dense 3-3-3 configuration in the economy class cabin.
