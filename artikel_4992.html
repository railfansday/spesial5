Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Bitcoin fell below $76,000 as US stocks reversed their post-election gains, France’s National Assembly rejected a budget revenue bill containing proposed crypto tax measures and Democrats threatened investigations into President Donald Trump’s crypto ventures.

Bitcoin (BTC) fell below $76,000 on Thursday as risk assets abruptly reversed their US midterm elections rally. After making swift gains on the back of a Democrat sweep of the US House of Representatives and Senate, equities saw sharp selling on Thursday as traders rotated out of tech.

Crypto markets liquidated nearly $500 million in positioning in the first hour of the US trading session, per data from CoinGlass, with 24-hour total liquidations nearing $850 million.

Now targeting $76,000, BTC/USD approached the bottom of its one-month trading range. The pair risked losing support at the 50-week simple moving average (SMA), currently at $75,733.

In his latest analysis, crypto trader Rekt Capital noted that Bitcoin was repeating behavior from May, when a period of rangebound moves around $80,000 subsequently gave way to a breakdown and new macro lows of $57,000 in late June.

The French National Assembly rejected the 2027 state budget’s revenue section late Wednesday, removing crypto-related amendments from the version of the bill sent to the Senate.

The Assembly rejected the revenue section by a vote of 458–0 at first reading, with two abstentions, according to the official voting record. The revenue section covers government income, such as taxes.

After the rejection, the government will send the Senate the original budget text, without the crypto-related amendments adopted by lawmakers.

The proposals could still be reintroduced in the Senate and would need to survive the remaining budget process to become law.

Faustine Fleuret, global head of public affairs at lending protocol Morpho, told Cointelegraph before the vote that rejection of the revenue section could “result in the rejection of the entire bill in the National Assembly, including provisions adopted by way of amendments.”

Democratic lawmakers said Big Tech executives should expect a wave of subpoenas, along with investigations into US President Donald Trump’s business circle when the new Congress takes office in January.

As Democrats secured control of Congress in a sweeping victory on Tuesday, Democratic lawmakers were already firing warning shots at the Trump family and tech executives close to the US president.

“They are going to need to plant new forests for all of the subpoenas we’ll be printing,” said US Senator Ed Markey, who sits on the Senate Commerce Committee, referring to Big Tech executives.

US Representative Robert Garcia, the expected incoming House Oversight Committee chairman, said he plans to take on “the Trump Crime Family, corporate corruption, DHS and ICE abuses, and the cover-up of the Epstein files,” adding a two-word warning: “Lawyer up.”

The warnings follow a mid-term election campaign in which Democrats challenged Big Tech’s growing political influence and the rapid expansion of AI, raising concerns about data centers’ demands on electricity, safeguards for AI products and tech executives’ ties to Trump.
