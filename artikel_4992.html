Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Trump Backs Jensen Huang’s Return to Nvidia as Sam Altman Says 'Heroes Have Flaws'

President Donald Trump joined Elon Musk on Monday in supporting Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia CEO, while OpenAI chief Sam Altman expressed empathy for the ousted co-founder without endorsing his reinstatement.

“I would certainly not mind seeing that,” Trump told reporters Monday as he left the White House for Asia, according to Reuters. “He could return. There’s a big group of people that would like him to return.”

Trump said Huang had “learned a big lesson,” and questioned claims that Alice Huang was a Chinese spy. “Nobody’s proven that she’s a spy,” he said, per The Guardian.

Trump also dismissed concerns that his ties with Huang threatened national security and defended Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick amid questions over enforcement of AI-chip export controls.

Musk also said Friday he was signing a petition demanding Huang’s reinstatement.

“A petition is underway to reinstate Jensen and I’m signing it too,” Musk wrote on X. Epic Games boss Tim Sweeney and Replit CEO Amjad Masad are also listed among the supporters.

More than 182,000 people had signed the open letter by Monday. It seeks Huang’s return during an independent investigation, although signatories’ identities and affiliations are not independently verified.

Speaking at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World conference Monday, Altman said Huang had “long been a hero of mine. Heroes have flaws.” He described feeling “personal strong empathy” for the former Nvidia boss.

He has reason to understand a boardroom revolt. OpenAI fired Altman in November 2023, only to reinstate him less than five days later after employee and investor backlash. Still, asked whether Huang should return, Altman said: “I don’t think I know enough to answer that question.”

Altman said he struggled to imagine Nvidia without Huang, but argued the giant chipmaker was big enough to retain investor trust. That is sympathy—not an endorsement.

Huang, who founded Nvidia in 1993, stepped down Thursday after admitting infidelity. Reports later linked him to Alice Huang and two Nvidia employees, one a subordinate.

Alice Huang previously worked at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer facing scrutiny over possible diversion of advanced chips to China. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing; espionage allegations remain unproven.

Washington is tightening export controls on advanced AI chips, including new licensing requirements for shipments to Singapore and Malaysia, as officials seek to prevent diversion to China.

Trump’s backing also puts him at odds with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who said Sunday that Nvidia should look outside its ranks for a replacement. “They need a dramatic shake-up,” Bessent told Fox News.

Nvidia’s board has made no decision to reinstate Huang. CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO, while the Financial Times reports Huang’s allies are lobbying for his return and Arm CEO Rene Haas is a potential successor.

Employees have also pushed for reinstatement. An internal discussion drew more than 1,600 comments, including calls for Huang’s return.

Nvidia shares opened 2.7% lower Monday, wiping out roughly $100 billion in market value at the open.

Huang could face congressional questions on China chip exports in early December. Nvidia reports earnings Tuesday, its first financial update under Kress.
