Is SK Hynix the Better Artificial Intelligence (AI) Memory Stock Compared With Micron?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Both memory chipmakers are growing like weeds.

SK Hynix (SKHY +0.35%) and Micron (MU -0.92%) are two of the world's largest memory chipmakers. Both stocks soared as the AI boom fueled the market's demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips, but they pulled back sharply over the past two months. Let's see why they cooled off -- and if either chipmaker is still worth buying as a long-term AI play.

SK Hynix, based in South Korea, is the world's second-largest producer of DRAM and NAND memory chips after Samsung. Micron, based in the U.S., ranks third in both markets. Both companies generate most of their revenue from DRAM chips rather than NAND chips.

Both companies are also relying on HBM, a specialized high-density DRAM chip that supports Nvidia's (NVDA -0.44%) data center GPUs in AI clusters, to drive their near-term growth.

SK Hynix became the first memory chipmaker to mass-produce HBM3 chips in 2022. That early-mover advantage gave it an edge over Samsung and Micron in the nascent market and put it in a prime position to profit from the AI boom over the following four years. It subsequently started producing HBM3E chips in 2024 and HBM4 chips in early 2026. SK Hynix still leads the global HBM market, while Samsung and Micron rank second and third, respectively.

Micron completely skipped the HBM3 generation, but it started mass-producing its first HBM3E chips in 2024 and made the leap to HBM4 chips in early 2026. It's also growing rapidly as the global demand for HBM chips outstrips its available supply.

As SK Hynix and Micron allocate more of their manufacturing capacity to HBM chips, they have less capacity to produce conventional DRAM and NAND chips for other markets. In other words, the accelerating production of HBM chips is causing a shortage of other memory chip types.

In 2025, SK Hynix's revenue surged 47%, its gross margin expanded year over year from 48.1% to 60.3%, and its net income soared 117%. For 2026, analysts expect its revenue to jump 275% and its net income to rise more than sixfold.

In fiscal 2026 (which ended in September), Micron's revenue more than tripled, its gross margin more than doubled to 80.7%, and its net income rose nearly tenfold. Those growth rates are incredible, but both chipmakers should see growth slow over the next two years.

Looking ahead, analysts expect SK Hynix's revenue to rise 60% in 2027 and 17% in 2028. They expect Micron's revenue to soar 107% in fiscal 2027, followed by just 16% growth in fiscal 2028 and nearly flat growth in fiscal 2029. Those longer-term estimates suggest that even though the current AI growth cycle is robust, it will inevitably cool off as the market matures.

That's why both stocks are still valued as cyclical chipmakers rather than high-growth AI stocks. SK Hynix trades at just three times this year's earnings, while Micron trades at four times this year's earnings. Several other major challenges are compressing their valuations.

First, inflation and elevated interest rates could curb AI spending and make it more difficult for smaller neocloud companies to expand. Second, the construction of new data centers in the U.S. could be more tightly regulated in several Tier-1 data center states -- including Texas, Ohio, and Georgia -- after the Democrats swept the midterm elections in early November.

SK Hynix and Micron could both experience significant volatility over the next 12 months. But if I had to pick one over the other, I'd stick with SK Hynix instead of Micron for three simple reasons: it's larger, it's growing faster, and it will remain the market leader in HBM chips.
