1 Reason Target Could Outperform in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Target just beat analysts' earnings estimates, with strong growth in high-margin, non-retail categories.

By John Ballard – Nov 24, 2026 at 9:30AM EST

Target (TGT +4.31%) had a lot to prove heading into its third-quarter earnings report released on Wednesday, Nov. 18. The stock had already climbed 63% in the year through Nov. 17, when it closed at $159.53.

But the company showed that its turnaround is accelerating, and the shares jumped about 6% in early trading on Nov. 18. Improving margins and earnings growth could lead to more gains next year.

Third-quarter net sales rose 7.1% year over year, and comparable sales, which exclude new stores, grew 5.6% driven by higher customer traffic. Adjusted earnings beat Wall Street's estimates, and management raised its full-year sales growth outlook to about 6%.

There's a catch: The adjusted earnings per share of $2.65 includes $0.50 per share of tariff refunds, and the full-year adjusted earnings guidance of $10.65 to $11.40 per share includes about $2.14. Without refunds, the range would be roughly $8.51 to $9.26, up from $7.57 in the prior year.

The reason Target could keep winning in 2027 is a faster-growing, higher-margin part of the business. Non-merchandise sales, which include ads that Target sells on its own sites, credit card profit-sharing, paid memberships, and its online marketplace, jumped 19.3% last quarter, almost three times the growth in merchandise. Management partly credited that growth with helping lift gross margin.

The prospect of further earnings growth, driven by higher margins, could bolster the stock in 2027. As of the Nov. 17 close, the stock traded at about 17 times analysts' estimate for next year. Analysts see long-term growth of only about 6% a year, and it also offers an attractive 2.8% yield.

I believe Target's higher-margin growth could fuel additional earnings surprises, supporting the stock and potentially helping it outpace the market averages.
