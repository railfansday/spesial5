How Washington Could Exploit Qatar’s LNG Shopping Spree

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The U.S. may have a chance to drive a wedge between Doha and Tehran.

A tanker sails past ExxonMobil and QatarEnergy’s Golden Pass LNG facility in Port Arthur, Texas, April 2, 2026.

Brett Coomer/Houston Chronicle via Getty Images

America’s leading natural gas competitor is on track to become its biggest customer. With traffic down through the Strait of Hormuz, Qatar is betting on U.S. liquefied natural gas (LNG). The state’s energy company, QatarEnergy, signed two purchase agreements for American LNG in late October and is reportedly in talks with U.S. supplier Venture Global for a third. There may yet be more deals coming down the pike, including one with Houston-based NextDecade. QatarEnergy already owns 70 percent of Golden Pass LNG, a joint venture with ExxonMobil that shipped its first cargo out of Sabine Pass, Texas, in April.

Doha’s ventures are adding up. So much so that, according to an early November Financial Times report, Qatar may become “the biggest U.S. LNG buyer” by 2033. This presents the United States with opportunities to affirm market leadership and drive a wedge between Doha and Tehran.

Natalie Ecanow is a research analyst for the Foundation for the Defense of Democracies. Her work focuses on the Middle East.
