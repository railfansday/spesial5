Trump brings two divided Cabinet heavyweights to China as controversy hangs over high-stakes talks

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

NVIDIA chip investigations escalate as President Donald Trump prepares to meet with three world leaders, including Chinese President Xi Jinping. Heritage Foundation chief economist E.J. Antoni joins co-hosts Charlie Hurt, Ainsley Earhardt and Lawrence Jones on 'Fox & Friends' to analyze the national security stakes.

President Donald Trump is bringing two powerful Cabinet officials divided over restrictions on China's access to advanced American AI chips on his high-stakes Asia trip, as controversy over the issue follows them into talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick are both accompanying Trump on the four-day trip despite reports of a dispute over whether to blacklist a company suspected of helping China obtain restricted American AI technology. Trump acknowledged their competing views Monday and suggested Lutnick may have delayed action to avoid worsening relations with Beijing.

"He might have delayed it, but he was doing that because you could have if you didn't delay it. If you didn't do something, you could have a blow up with China. We're having a very good relationship with China right now, and I understand both sides of it, and I'll make a decision. But I understand very well both sides," the president said.

The dispute follows reports that Lutnick resisted efforts to place Singapore-based Megaspeed on a U.S. trade blacklist, despite Trump reportedly supporting action against the company on three occasions this year, according to Axios. Megaspeed has faced allegations that its operations helped Chinese companies gain access to restricted Nvidia chips.

The Commerce Department announced a new rule this week aimed at preventing Chinese companies and other restricted foreign entities from accessing advanced American computing technology through data centers in third countries. The department said the rule was written at the direction of the Treasury Department and National Security Council.

Trump told reporters Monday he is not considering replacing Lutnick, saying "he's done a very good job."

Bessent publicly confirmed Friday that several administration departments had supported adding Megaspeed to the Commerce Department's Entity List, which would restrict the company's access to U.S. technology.

"Treasury, War, USTR and State — they were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the Entity List for months, but Commerce has the pen," Bessent said.

The Commerce Department denied Axios' account of a confrontation between Bessent and Lutnick in the White House Situation Room, saying the two officials had no such conversation.

Trump is bringing several of his administration's most powerful officials on the four-day Asia trip, which includes stops in the Philippines and China.

The delegation includes Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Bessent, Lutnick, White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett, among other officials, a White House official confirmed to Fox News Digital.

Trump is expected to meet with world leaders, participate in regional economic and security summits and hold high-stakes talks with Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin, and possibly North Korea leader Kim Jong Un.

The controversy has also drawn attention to Nvidia's leadership. Nvidia announced Thursday that CEO Jensen Huang had resigned following reports about his relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive suspected by U.S. intelligence of spying for China.

Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing and said Sunday that it retained outside counsel to investigate allegations surrounding its operations.

Trump said Monday it has not been confirmed she is a spy.

Lutnick, the former chairman and CEO of investment firm Cantor Fitzgerald, is one of the wealthiest officials to serve in a presidential administration. A longtime Trump ally, he co-chaired the president's 2024 transition team, and the two have known each other for decades through New York's business and social circles.

Lutnick has also agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 10 amid growing scrutiny of the Commerce Department's handling of Nvidia chip exports to China, CBS News reported.

Sens. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., and Chris Coons, D-Del., jointly called on the administration to preserve records related to Nvidia's advanced AI chip exports and potential national security risks. The senators set a Nov. 17 deadline for written confirmation that the records would be preserved and requested a congressional briefing.

Fox News Digital reached out to the Departments of Commerce and Treasury for comment.
