Mark Speakman resigns and says Liberals face ‘existential threat’ as NSW exodus continues

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Former NSW opposition leader warns party against becoming ‘Labor lite’ or ‘One Nation lite’ in late-night speech

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

Former New South Wales opposition leader Mark Speakman will not contest his seat at the March state election, the latest in a mass exodus of MPs under the Liberal leader who replaced him, Kellie Sloane.

In a late-night speech on Thursday, the final sitting day of NSW’s legislative assembly before the vote, Speakman said he would not contest his south Sydney seat of Cronulla, which he has held since 2011.

He is the eighth out of 24 current Liberal MPs to announce they will not re-contest their seat in March, following Jordan Lane, Mark Coure, Tanya Davies, Ray Williams, James Griffin, Alister Henskens and Mark Taylor.

The announcement comes almost a year to the day after Speakman resigned from the leadership of the opposition in a spill in favour of Sloane, who was elected unopposed.

In that time the Coalition has gone from commanding 28% of the primary vote in NSW to 23%, Sydney Morning Herald Resolve polling suggests.

Labor has gone from 37% to 30%, while One Nation, which Resolve did not start polling separately until March, is currently at 26%.

Last week, Sloane said her party would not form government with One Nation, but she did not rule out a confidence and supply arrangement. The Australian Financial Review has reported many of her MPs privately expect the Liberals to go into a coalition with One Nation.

In his speech in the dying minutes of the parliamentary term, Speakman, a member of the party’s moderate faction, warned the Liberals against becoming “Labor lite” or “One Nation lite”, saying they should stick to the values of “Menzian Liberalism”.

Referencing Pauline Hanson’s notion of “monoculture”, Speakman said Australia was “the most successful multicultural society on the planet” and was “so much better for it”.

“You know, we hear certain people say we want a monoculture, and that people who don’t support that monoculture somehow are enemies of Australia, hate Australia,” he said.

“I love Australia for its multiculturalism. Diversity is our strength. It is our strength, and we should build on that.”

Speakman, a barrister, served as environment minister between 2015 and 2017, and was attorney general between 2017 and the Perrottet government’s defeat in the 2023 election.

He was chosen as the Liberal and opposition leader after Dominic Perrottet’s resignation, a position he held until November last year.

On Thursday, he said being opposition leader “is often said to be the toughest job in politics” and, while it had “ultimately unravelled” for him, he had mostly led an opposition “which was competitive with, or even ahead of, the government in the polls”.

He said his record as attorney general included legislating for NSW to be the first Australian jurisdiction to criminalise coercive control, introducing affirmative consent tests in criminal sexual assault trials and reforming criminal and civil child sexual abuse cases.

Speakman’s resignation, ending speculation that bubbled up again when he spoke during question time on Thursday, is the latest blow for Sloane, who he said in his leaving speech had the “attributes to be a great premier”.

In a statement on Friday morning, Sloane thanked Speakman for his “extraordinary contribution to the people of Cronulla and to NSW”.

“Since his election in 2011, Mark has been a dedicated local member, a thoughtful colleague and a principled leader of the NSW Liberal Party,” she said.

“Mark has been a generous and supportive colleague. I’m grateful for the confidence he placed in me when I became leader, and for the contribution he continues to make to our team.”

Earlier on Thursday, the NSW premier, Chris Minns, announced former Liberal councillor and parliamentary staffer Tanya Raffoul would contest the Liberal-held seat of Epping as a Labor candidate.
