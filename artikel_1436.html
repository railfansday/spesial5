Next USA manager: 7 options to replace Pochettino after Man Utd hire

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Mauricio Pochettino is preparing for his USA farewell as he gets set to start his Manchester United reign. But who’s going to step into his shoes next?

US Soccer have already dreamt about Pep Guardiola becoming the next manager of their men’s national team, but the latest reports say the answer will be a thanks, but no thanks.

Who else could they turn to, then? Pochettino could be a tough act to follow, after all, even if he didn’t get them as far in the World Cup as they’d have liked.

They’ll be losing a high-profile coach capable of generating international attention and now have a void to fill.

Here are some potential contenders for the USMNT job.

Not as big a name internationally as Pochettino, sure, but Cherundolo might be the obvious option from within.

A former right-back who earned more than 80 caps for the USA, he took charge of their under-23 national team earlier this year in view of the 2028 Olympics.

The former Las Vegas Lights and Los Angeles FC coach will also serve as interim manager of the senior side after Pochettino’s exit.

Could he get the permanent job? It might depend on how the interview process goes with other candidates. Pochettino will still oversee the last USA fixture of the November international break and you’d imagine they’d want to have a permanent successor chosen before the next one in March.

But Cherundolo, 47, might have some backers as someone who knows the system.

At this stage, it seems unlikely Southgate will return to management after leaving the England job in 2024.

“Towards the end of my time with England, I stopped enjoying the wins that keep you going. By the end, I didn’t enjoy the wins for any longer than a couple of minutes,” he said earlier this year, before adding: “My desire to be involved in football has changed.”

Fair enough if that’s what you want, Sir Gareth, but we think it’d be a shame for him to leave management behind completely.

Going back into club football doesn’t interest him at all, but could a different international job in an English-speaking country? Who knows.

The USA job might be the kind of project that would have floated his boat when his hunger for management was bigger. There’s an appetite to build something there and Southgate knows what it takes to go far in tournaments, even if he wasn’t quite the man to get England over the line.

We equally think it’d be a shame if the last sight of Bielsa as a manager was his early exit from the World Cup with a drab Uruguay side.

At 71, he has every right to put his feet up and reflect on an influential career. But is there room for a more fitting end?

Bielsa has plenty of experience managing at international level. He’s had spells with Argentina, Chile and Uruguay, but it’s fair to say some of his better work was in the club game.

A meticulous coach if ever there was one, Bielsa showed at Leeds United his capability for building a project from the ground up and getting almost universal buy-in.

He even once managed a club called America. If that isn’t a sign…

Perhaps a more realistic contender from the pool of managers last employed by a country instead of a club, Martinez resigned from the Portugal job after the World Cup.

It’s been a decade since he started out in international management with Belgium. Judging his time there is a bit tricky. Did he underachieve with the talent at his disposal? Probably. Did he still do pretty well at some tournaments? You could say so.

But to protect his reputation, he needed to do a better job with Portugal than he eventually managed.

Now Martinez faces having to rebuild his managerial career. Doing so outside of Europe entirely might be what he needs.

It’d be a risky choice for the USA but it’s not an unimaginable one.

From the current crop of MLS managers, the most likely to become a candidate for the USMNT job is probably Callaghan.

Currently in charge of Nashville SC, he was the USA’s interim manager in 2023, in between Gregg Berhalter’s spells at the helm.

Callaghan had been an assistant to Berhalter but has since forged his own path in coaching, taking charge of Nashville in 2024.

There was a brief time when it felt like Nagelsmann might be a rival to Pochettino for the Man Utd job. What if he replaced him for the USA instead?

Once one of the Bundesliga’s most promising young coaches, he turned his hand to international management for a three-year spell in charge of Germany.

But he’s been out of work since leaving that role in the summer and needs to find the next step.

Nagelsmann can communicate in English and has shown serious potential from the touchline before. His next move will be crucial and he’ll have to weigh up whether to stay in the international game or go back to club football, but he could bring some innovative ideas.

It’s doubtful if Henry will ever return to management, but he hasn’t ruled it out.

He last held a management role in 2024, leading France at the Olympics and somehow managing not to win gold.

Henry is a familiar face to American soccer fans. He used to play for New York Red Bulls, once managed Montreal Impact and is a regular pundit on CBS Sports.

He’s suggested a willingness to take charge of the USMNT before, saying a couple of years ago: “Do I know the players? Yeah, I know the players. Do I know the league? Yeah, I know the league. It’s a different ballgame, I cannot talk about it now like that, but would I want to be a manager at any level? Yes, of course. That’s one of the things I’d like to do.”

Come to think of it, this one could actually tick a few boxes. But it’d be a bit of a gamble in the first place.
