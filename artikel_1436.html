Mitochondrial protein import defects enhance tumour immunogenicity

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Tumour cells evade detection and elimination by the immune system through a suite of adaptations1, including the repression of antigen presentation, which is required for the recognition of tumour cells by CD8+ T cells. Mitochondria have emerged as targets for enhancing antitumour immunity; however, the extensive mitochondrial proteome has remained largely unstudied for its ability to modulate immune responses. Here we show that defects in the mitochondrial protein import machinery promote tumour immunogenicity and synergize with immune checkpoint blockade treatment in melanoma models. Mitochondrial protein import perturbation, specifically through depletion of the TIM23 import complex subunit TIMM50, enhances tumour immunogenicity through a pathway involving mitochondrial DNA escape, cGAS–STING pathway activation and interferon-β (IFNβ) secretion. Secreted IFNβ signals through the type I IFN receptor to activate the JAK–STAT pathway, resulting in increased antigen presentation on MHC class I complexes. Mitochondrial DNA escape in Timm50-depleted cells is linked to the formation of mitochondrial-derived vesicles. Depletion of SNX9 and RAB9, involved in mitochondrial-derived vesicle formation2, suppresses mitochondrial DNA-dependent type I IFN production and signalling. By disrupting mitochondrial proteostasis and membrane architecture, defective protein import in Timm50-depleted cells drives the immune phenotype. This study shows that mitochondrial protein import inhibition causes tumour immunogenicity, including sensitization to immune checkpoint blockade, by promoting mitochondrial DNA escape and IFNβ-driven MHC class I antigen presentation.
