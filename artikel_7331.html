What Verified Purchase Data Unlocks for Streaming Advertisers

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

This post was created in partnership with Magnite

Connecting the dots between an ad exposure and a sale has rarely been easy. Today’s fragmented buying behavior and growing number of shopping touchpoints, including CTV, are adding to the challenge.

During an ADWEEK House Commerce Summit fireside chat co-hosted with Magnite, Sabir Muhammad, lead, brand partnerships at Magnite, sat down with Jim Prandato, global head of ads partnerships at PayPal Ads, to discuss how PayPal is helping brands close the measurement loop, providing other valuable insights, and thinking about shoppable storefronts.

Prandato began the conversation with a bold statement: “Connecting CTV, or the biggest screen in the house, with actual true product purchase data through closed-loop measurement is the greatest untapped opportunity in our industry.”

He subsequently outlined how PayPal, which also operates Venmo and PayPal Honey, is helping advertisers capitalize on that opportunity with purchase data it gathers across millions of merchants.

The workflow PayPal enables starts with pre-campaign insights: Brands can analyze product purchases to understand consumer behavior, which can inform messaging. For example, it’s helpful to know transactions relating to pickleball have significantly tapered off—running creative about the sport could make an ad look passé, Prandato warned. Next, brands can build unique audience segments, activate campaigns, and access closed-loop measurement based on financial-grade data.

Muhammad shared that he and his team partner with PayPal to support advertisers that are activating programmatically.

“Brands are really leaning into the PayPal data because you can find verified data that is scaled in areas where traditionally it’s been really hard—business-to-business being one of those areas,” he said. For instance, now that many people use Venmo to pay for their haircuts, Magnite can leverage Venmo data to help advertisers find hairdressers.

That example prompted Prandato to highlight that Venmo is increasingly becoming more of a point-of-sale tool, not just a peer-to-peer payment option. “There are really unique audiences that we can build off of that,” he noted, including emoji-based targeting.

When the conversation shifted to shoppable storefronts on streaming services, Prandato said PayPal thinks it can have a big impact in the space because it owns the payment rails and consumers are familiar with the company.

PayPal has already rolled out storefront ads on the open web for display and online video that enable consumers to purchase within the ad, he explained. The next opportunity will come from figuring out the right consumer experience for CTV.

“The ad formats like pause ads, squeeze-back ads, native display ads—the foundation is there,” Prandato explained. “It’s really just building on top of that.”

Muhammad weighed in on pause ads, noting that the number of advertisers transacting with them is up dramatically this year. “Advertisers are really leaning in, and they’re finding value in terms of the returns,” he said.
