Andrew Rannells Was Told to ‘Stay Away’ From Kevin Spacey After Meeting Backstage at ‘The Book of Mormon’; He Then Sent a ‘Handwritten Letter’ ‘Asking Me on a Date’

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Andrew Rannells recently revealed that Kevin Spacey asked him out while the “Girls” actor was starring in “The Book of Mormon” on Broadway.

“I’ve never told this story before. He asked me on a date once,” Rannells said while guesting on “The Downside” podcast hosted by Gianmarco Soresi. “He sent me a letter to ‘The Book of Mormon.’ Handwritten.” The actors went on to explain that the Oscar winner came to see the musical and “came backstage” to meet him after the show. “There were always a lot of really fun people there, but sometimes they would bring them up into our dressing rooms, but with no notice,” he said. “So after the show, we’re getting changed. There’s a knock on my door. It was Goldie Hawn, Kevin Spacey, and Joel Schumacher. They were not together, but they all walked up together. So then we were all crammed in my dressing room.”

Rannells continued: “Kevin Spacey was sort of rubbing my neck as we were standing there… Joel Schumacher, who was very close to me, he said, ‘Stay away from that man.’” That prompted Spacey to respond, “Hey! C’mon, c’mon,” he recalled. “And then like a week later, I got a handwritten letter from him asking me on a date.” Rannells concluded by noting he “never responded” to the note.

Rannells originated the role of Elder Price in the long-running musical, performing from the show’s opening in 2011 through June 2012. The part earned him a Tony nomination for best leading actor in a musical.

Spacey’s sexual assault allegations came years later in 2017, when fellow Broadway actor Anthony Rapp said that the actor assaulted him in his New York apartment while Rapp was a teenager. Rapp later filed a $40 million civil lawsuit for sexual assault and battery in 2022, but a New York jury found Spacey not liable on all counts. Earlier this year, Spacey reached a settlement with three men who sued him over sexual assault claims, avoiding a civil trial in London that was set for October.
