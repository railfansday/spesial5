Sheffield Wednesday must deal with Stevenage’s ageless danger man to secure a third consecutive win under Dave Challinor

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Sheffield Wednesday have rediscovered winning form in League One since the arrival of Dave Challinor. The new SWFC boss oversaw a 3-0 win over Plymouth Argyle before a battling triumph at Oakwell against Barnsley.

Next up for Challinor and the Owls is Stevenage on Tuesday night. Boro have had an inconsistent season, leaving them in 14th as they look to build on last term’s play-off finish. Alex Revell’s side are a tough one, with physical players across the pitch.

It will be a tricky test for Sheffield Wednesday, and the key to picking up a third League One win on the trot will be keeping Boro striker Josh Magennis quiet. At 36, the Northern Irishman has been one of the standout performers at Broadhall Way this season.

Magennis moved to Stevenage in the summer after the expiry of his contract at relegated Exeter City. He maintained a consistent output with the Grecians, recording 21 goals in 89 games for the club as he plays well into his 30s.

And, he’s been a hugely effective player for Revell’s Boro too. Magennis has always been a physical presence at the top of the pitch, and the 6ft 2in striker is perfect for Stevenage. His strength and ability in the air make him a real outlet at the top of the pitch and from crossing or set-piece situations.

Not only do these attributes make him a goalscoring threat, but Magennis’ ability to win knock-downs or hold up the ball for his teammates makes him a creative asset too. If Sheffield Wednesday can’t contain him, the Bangor-born striker will be key in tonight’s game.

Compared with his fellow League One strikers, no one has created more goalscoring opportunities than Magennis’ 19 – six of them big chances. He wins 48% of his aerial duels and 44.4% of his duels overall (stats via FotMob).

Magennis is in some of the most productive form of his career. He is a perfect fit for Stevenage’s style of play, and it is helping him age like a fine wine and contribute in the twilight of his career.

Dealing with Magennis’ presence is not easy – his recent League One opponents will tell you that.

However, Sheffield Wednesday do have the personnel to limit his impact. In Liam Cooper, Challinor has a vastly experienced centre-back who will be wise to Magennis’ tricks and style, and he can be key in preventing the Stevenage man from having his way while guiding his fellow defenders on how to deal with him too.

Of the potential starters, it may be Cooper and Sil Swinkels tasked with challenging Magennis in the air. They have aerial duel win rates of 72.6% and 73.3% respectively this season, while Di’Shon Bernard has a lower but respectable 68% (FotMob).

Magennis is usually a lone striker, but with on-rushing attackers in tandem. By suffocating the Boro striker at the source, they can limit his aerial duel wins and prevent him from bringing the ball down to create chances.

It is a task easier said than done, but SWFC boss Challinor must have a plan to deal with Magennis if he is to maintain his perfect League One record.
