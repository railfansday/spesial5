No case for defence if Neil McCann can't turn around kamikaze Kilmarnock quickly

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

When Gary Neville’s Valencia side conceded a seventh goal to Barcelona, the camera cut to the Manchester United legend standing in his technical area looking ashen faced, utterly bereft.

As you took in his befuddled expression, helpless to prevent the car crash unfolding before him, you couldn’t help but wonder if he would have the gall to soon be back on the Sky Sports couch, telling others how to do the job he had just so miserably failed in.

Of course he did. And that spell in charge of what, admittedly, at the time, was an absolute basket case of a club, has been used as a stick with which to beat him ever since.

If things ultimately don’t work out for Neil McCann at Rugby Park – and it is starting to look as though that may be the case – I am sure he too will be back in the BBC Sportsound studio before you know it. And rightly so. McCann is an engaging and insightful pundit. The qualities that make a good, entertaining football analyst don’t necessarily make a top-class manager, though.

But then, I would say that. I too am paid to give my opinions on the beautiful game, and the highlight of my own managerial career – about half a season as player/manager of Motherwell Phoenix in the Carluke & District Sunday league – was a solitary 3-2 win amid a barrage of hungover batterings. Take that, Lanark Youth.

It would be massively unfair too and disrespectful in fact to dismiss McCann as a pundit turned manager, given his vast experience in the game as a hugely talented player and his previous managerial stints at Dundee, Inverness on an interim basis, as well as his time at Rangers as part of Barry Ferguson’s backroom team.

However, as McCann himself will know having been part of the Scottish football media set, his performance as manager of Kilmarnock will justifiably be subject to the same sort of scrutiny he used to apply to others, and at the risk of drawing a similarly icy stare to the one he fixed on former BBC colleague Chick Young at the weekend when he asked if McCann was feeling the pressure, any objective analysis would have to conclude that things are not going so peachily at present.

Killie currently sit in 11th place in the table, a point ahead of Hibs, who have a game in hand. Most concerningly, they are shipping goals for fun, conceding 2.3 per game so far this term, comfortably the worst record in the division. They have lost eight goals more than anyone else, letting in 28 across their 12 games to Motherwell and St Mirren’s 20. Their goal difference of -15 puts them seven worse off than the closest side, the Saints again, in that column.

McCann has been round the block often enough to know that the team who ships the most goals over the course of a campaign usually goes down. On only four occasions in the Premiership era have the side who conceded the most goals survived, with two of those doing so via the playoffs after finishing 11th. Never has the team with the worst defence in the league finished higher than 10th, which has also happened just twice.

Surely, stumbling to safety, whether that be through the playoffs or by scraping into tenth place, isn’t the limit of Kilmarnock’s ambitions this term? If they continue on their current trajectory, though, and carry on defending so poorly, then not only will that be their ceiling, it also seems a bit of a reach. Across McCann’s time at the club, in fact, Killie have conceded 53 goals in 22 league games, a record that is surely unsustainable.

It would be remiss not to mention though that many of those games came after he walked into an absolutely dire predicament in January following a run of 10 games without a victory under predecessor Stuart Kettlewell. That extended to 14 games under interim boss Kris Doolan before club owner Billy Bowie sent out the SOS to McCann and assistant Billy Dodds in early January.

Kilmarnock were also 11th at that time, just three points ahead of bottom side Livingston, who had a game in hand. Had you offered any Killie fan the playoff spot at that stage, they might have bitten your hand off like a, well, hungry squirrel, but McCann managed to turn things around despite the make-up of the squad not being suited to his preferred 4-2-3-1 formation, and he secured the Rugby Park side’s safety with a game to spare.

It is fair to say then that the many sceptics who gave his appointment in the winter window a frosty reception were mainly won over by the fine job he did in the remainder of the campaign.

There was a great deal of optimism around in the summer, in fact, that having had the opportunity to build a squad in his image, that the issues such as the leaky defence that had plagued Killie would be ironed out, and all the while, he would retain the front-footed approach that had been so refreshing during his first months in charge.

The balance, though, is still way off kilter. While Killie still look decent going forward at times and play some nice stuff, there is no way of dressing up their persistent issues at the back. Their defending at times is absolutely criminal, and you can't be in a position where you need to score three times in a game just to have a chance of winning.

McCann held his hands up to a naïve approach when Celtic came and plundered four first half goals in an eventual 5-1 win at Rugby Park back in August, but naïve barely covers the way that his team twice gave up a lead against 10-man St Johnstone on Saturday.

The second goal in particular had to be seen to be believed. A Killie corner somehow led, within seconds, to an opposition player running through on goal, with the set-up behind the ball leaving the hosts wide open. Even then, in fairness to the Killie manager, his defenders should have done much better when they got back at Joel Cotterill, but three retreating Killie men allowed him to waltz through on Nico Mantl unhindered before finding the net to howls of derision from the home stands.

And therein lies the growing issue for McCann. The boos are getting louder week upon week. While it could be argued that he deserves a bit of time to correct the defensive issues his team are facing after the escape act he pulled off last season, the more that his side display the same consistent weaknesses, the less faith there is from the Killie support that he can pull their team out of another mess. This time, one of his own making.

In the end, it won’t be his erstwhile colleagues in the media that seal his fate, but those paying – and increasingly, braying – punters. If he doesn’t want to be back at Pacific Quay any time soon, he has to find a way to bolt the back door.

There are no guarantees of course that changing manager mid-season would help pull Kilmarnock out of this death spiral they are currently in. Though, admittedly, it did work for them the last time they tried it.

Time is surely running out for McCann to avoid being on the other side of that scenario.
