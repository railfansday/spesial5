Where Does Caroline Kennedy Live Now? Unpacking the Former First Daughter’s Real Estate Portfolio

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

She entered the public eye as a little girl living in the White House, but where does Caroline Kennedy live now? The only surviving child of President John F. Kennedy and Jacqueline Kennedy, the diplomat and author has lived in many prominent homes throughout her life, the Executive Mansion being just one of them. Below, we’re unpacking all of the places that Caroline has called home.

An aerial view of the Kennedy Compound in Hyannisport, Massachusetts

In 1956, the year before Caroline was born, her parents paid $45,948 for a home at the Kennedy Compound in Hyannis Port, Massachusetts, where JFK’s parents and brother, Robert F. Kennedy, also owned homes. The 4,500-square-foot residence was built in the mid-20s, which is also the period when the famed family first put roots in the area. Born on November 27, 1957, Caroline spent childhood summers at the Cape Cod enclave. It was also where, on July 19, 1986, she married museum exhibit designer Ed Schlossberg.

Caroline Kennedy and Edwin Schlossberg on their wedding day in Hyannis Port, Massachusetts

When Jacqueline died in 1994, the property went to Caroline and her brother, John F. Kennedy, Jr., but reportedly, only John spent his summers there. After he and his wife, Carolyn Bessette Kennedy, tragically died in a 1999 plane crash, the home’s ownership went to Caroline and her children. In 2004, Caroline sold the dwelling to her uncle, Senator Edward Kennedy, for $3 million. “It was sold to the senator to keep it in the family,” family friend Dick Gallagher told the Cape Cod Times. “It will always be the president’s house.”

Caroline spent her first few years primarily living in Washington, DC’s Georgetown neighborhood. In late 1957, her parents paid $82,000 for a brick Federal-style home and tapped designer Sister Parish to decorate its interiors. The Kennedys lived at the circa 1811 property, known as the William Marbury House for the Federalist financier who first lived there, until JFK’s 1961 presidential inauguration. John Jr. was also born during this period. The 5,215-square-foot residence was most recently sold in May 2026 for $6.125 million.

US President John F Kennedy in the Oval Office of the White House with John Kennedy Jr and Caroline Kennedy in 1962

Of course, the next residence that the Kennedys took up was the White House, where they lived from 1961 until President Kennedy’s 1963 assassination. Caroline attended kindergarten in the White House’s solarium along with about 20 other children. She was photographed riding her pony named Macaroni, a gift from Lyndon B. Johnson, on the White House lawn for Life magazine in 1962. The image inspired Neil Diamond to write his hit song, Sweet Caroline. In 2011, Caroline recalled celebrating childhood Halloweens in America’s most famous dwelling: “One of my favorite memories of living here is dressing up in my witch costume and going over to scare my father in his office,” she said.

After JFK’s death, Jacqueline, Caroline, and John moved back to Georgetown, in a $175,000 home known as the Newton D. Baker House, on the same street as their former home in the neighborhood. After less than a year of being hounded by tour buses at the residence, Jackie put the 1794-built Federal-style dwelling up for sale and headed for the Big Apple.

Jackie Kennedy Onassis outside of her 1040 Fifth Avenue home

The family of three moved into a 14-room penthouse apartment on Manhattan’s Upper East Side, which Jacqueline bought in July 1964 for $200,000. The 5,300-square-foot home had five bedrooms, five-and-a-half baths, and views of Central Park and the Metropolitan Museum of Art, where Caroline later worked and met her future husband. The 18-story limestone building was built in 1930 by prominent architect Rosario Candela. The penthouse unit had a library, conservatory, three fireplaces, and a balcony overlooking the park. Caroline lived there until 1972, when she left to attend a boarding school in Massachusetts. Jacqueline called the apartment home until her 1994 death. A year later, billionaire David Koch bought the unit for $9.5 million.

An aerial view of Jackie Kennedy’s Martha’s Vineyard property

In 1979, Jacqueline bought Red Gate Farm, a 340-acre property on Martha’s Vineyard, for $1.1 million. At the time, only a small hunting cabin sat on the grounds. The former First Lady soon built an impressive compound comprised of a Cape Cod–style main house with five bedrooms, a four-bedroom guesthouse, a three-bedroom caretaker’s house, a boathouse, a storage building, two garages, a pool, a tennis court, and a treehouse for her grandchildren.

The property stayed in the family long after Jacqueline’s death. Caroline and Schlossberg’s daughter, Tatiana, was married at the residence in 2017. Two years later, Caroline listed Red Gate Farm for $65 million. “Forty years ago, my mother fell in love with Martha’s Vineyard. When she found Red Gate Farm, it was a perfect expression of her romantic and adventurous spirit,” she said in a statement. “The dunes and ponds and rolling hills of Aquinnah gave her the chance to create a world where she could be so close to nature, close to her family and friends, and, most importantly, close to her beloved books.”

In a letter printed in The Vineyard Gazette, Caroline explained her rationale for the sale. “Being able to raise our children in a place that meant so much to my mother and my brother has kept them close to our hearts. Now that our children are grown, it is time for them to spread their wings, and for us to explore new places.” The diplomat also recalled her pleasant memories at the property over the years: “Together we wove the traditions of summer across three generations — setting lobster pots in Menemsha Pond, entering the Fair and never winning, growing a vegetable garden, bringing home the best shell from the beach every day, getting stuck at the airport when the fog rolled in.”

In 2020, 304 acres of the property sold for $27 million to two nonprofits: the Martha’s Vineyard Land Bank Commission and the Sheriff’s Meadow Foundation. In 2025, it opened to the public as the Squibnocket Pond Reservation. A year later, the Land Bank Commission paid an additional $10 million for a 32-acre portion.

Caroline and Schlossberg still own 95 acres of the property, including the main house and guesthouse.

Caroline and Schlossberg primarily raised their children, Rose, Tatiana, and Jack, in an apartment on Park Avenue on Manhattan’s Upper East Side. The dwelling has six rooms and was described in a 2009 NBC News report as “a low-key place covered with books and decorated with slip-covered sofas.” The couple seems to still own the residence.

Following Tatiana’s tragic death to cancer in June 2026, Jack told People that his parents had moved in with Tatiana’s husband to help take care of their grandchildren, aged two and four years old. “My parents are grandparents, but they’re really playing the role of new parents right now,” Jack told the outlet. “They live with my niece and nephew and take care of them every single day… they’re all living in the same apartment.” Three months before her death, Tatiana and her husband bought an Upper East Side co-op unit near her parents’ apartment.
