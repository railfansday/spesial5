Victor Wembanyama throws down two self-alley-oop dunks off backboard in first quarter vs. Pistons

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Victor Wembanyama's combination of height, length, athleticism and ingenuity allows him to not only do things on the basketball court that few other players could even dream of — to consistently, and at the NBA level, make competing against the best opposition in the world look like a dad lording over his elementary schooler in the driveway.

On Tuesday, against the Detroit Pistons — a team that won 60 games last season, a team that entered their matchup at 8-4, with the NBA's No. 6 defense — the San Antonio Spurs superstar center did that twice.

Just over a minute into the game, the 7-foot-4 MVP candidate crossed half-court, trailing the play as teammate Stephon Castle surveyed the floor. Wembanyama took a pass from Castle, and then a screen from point guard De'Aaron Fox, and ran an inverted pick-and-roll, driving to his right toward the rim.

Pistons guard Daniss Jenkins swiped at the ball as Wembanyama approached. There was just one problem: Jenkins is 6-foot-4, and Wembanyama is a literal foot taller than that. The Frenchman blithely windmill-gathered the ball over Jenkins' head without breaking his stride and got two feet in the paint before getting airborne.

At that point, Detroit defensive ace Ausar Thompson — who finished third last season in Defensive Player of the Year voting, behind winner Wembanyama and runner-up Chet Holmgren — soared in from the weak side, intent on protecting the rim from encroaching alien threats. This is the kind of thing that might unnerve most drivers.

Wembanyama, however, is not most drivers:

He floated the ball over the outstretched arm of Thompson and off the backboard with his right hand, landed in the restricted area, collected the carom with his left hand, and tomahawked it home. Two points, with a little bit of panache.

Well, evidently, Big Vic liked the feeling of self-containment inherent in that sort of farm-to-table approach to bucket-getting … because about five minutes later, he decided to do it again:

Wembanyama drove from the perimeter on John Collins, who's been starting at center for the Pistons with All-NBA pivot Jalen Duren sidelined with an injury. Wemby planted his left foot, spun back to his right into the paint, and once again tossed the ball off the backboard. The 6-foot-9 Collins, with inside position, went up to try to collect the rebound. Wembanyama, wielding the dual benefits of knowing what he had in mind before he'd ever let the ball go and significantly longer arms than his opponent, just reached up over the top of him, grabbed the ball, and dunked it. Again.

Once upon a time, an NBA player throwing an alley-oop to himself off the backboard was an exceedingly rare and remarkable thing. For the exceedingly rare and remarkable Wembanyama, though, the self-lob dunk has become downright common — to the point that, when he does it twice in a six-minute span, the only appropriate response is, "It's ridiculous that that's no longer ridiculous." (Other reasonable responses are inappropriate, because they feature expletives.)
