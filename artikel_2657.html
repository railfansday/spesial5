Kyle Larson Never Thought He’d Race for Hendrick Because of Its Stance on Dirt Racing

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Kyle Larson says he never pictured himself driving for Hendrick Motorsports before 2020 because the team would not let its drivers run dirt races.

He has since won three Cup championships with the team. The reason for his doubt was dirt racing, and Larson explained it on the Nov. 25 episode of SPEED With Harvick and Buxton.

"Like, so I mean before I lost my job in 2020, I never thought that I would race for Hendrick Motorsports because I didn't really want to … I could tell that they wouldn't let anybody run dirt stuff and that was always important to me," Larson said.

Larson was close with Kasey Kahne, who drove the No. 5 for Hendrick from 2012 to 2017. That friendship gave him a look at how the team handled outside racing.

EssentiallySports reported that Larson said he would have turned to dirt racing full time if Hendrick had refused to accommodate his schedule.

Dirt has been part of Larson's racing career since he was a kid in California. He has won the Chili Bowl Nationals three times and the Knoxville Nationals four times, and he co-owns the High Limit sprint car series. In 2011, he swept all three divisions of the 4-Crown Nationals at Eldora Speedway in one night.

Larson lost his Chip Ganassi Racing ride in April 2020 after using a racial slur during an iRacing event. NASCAR suspended him indefinitely, and Ganassi fired him the next day.

He spent the rest of that year racing sprint cars, midgets and late models across the country. He won 46 of the 97 dirt features he entered in 2020. NASCAR reinstated him in October 2020, after he completed sensitivity training and other requirements.

Rick Hendrick signed him on Oct. 28, 2020 to drive the No. 5 the following season. Hendrick also agreed to let him keep his dirt schedule.

The deal paid off right away. Larson won 10 races and his first Cup title in 2021 while running dirt races on many of his off nights. He also led 2,581 laps that season.

Hendrick has stood by that arrangement. In August, a rain delay at Richmond pushed Larson's Cup race late into the night. He still reached Knoxville Raceway in time to win the 65th Knoxville Nationals early the next morning.

It was his fourth Knoxville Nationals title in six years, and he thanked Hendrick for helping make the trip possible.

Larson signed a five-year contract extension with Hendrick in February that runs through 2031. He won his third Cup championship this month, after titles in 2021 and 2025.
