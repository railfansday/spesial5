Knicks 113, Pacers 106: “Knicks handled their business”

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

They say a big part of being a good golfer is not how you play when you pump drives 300 yards down the middle of every fairway, see the rough only from a distance, or sink putts from anywhere on the premises. Your quality is rather defined by the afternoons when you do not have your A game in the bag. When you spray it left and right, when you have to grind around the greens. Karl-Anthony Towns apparently has enjoyed golf since childhood, and Josh Hart likes to tee it up a bit as well. And while they might be just like most mere mortals when their swings get a bit shaky, they sure know how to play basketball when they do not have their A game going.

The poor and pummeled Indiana Pacers might argue that KAT was feeling damn fine and fit since he poured a nasty 30/13/5 line over their scalps. Those who understand the Knicks—and a tiny bit of basketball—of course can recognize that it's not normal for the big Meowth to hit the side of the backboard with an open corner jumper or turn into Chris Dudley at the free throw line. Fuming, the Pacers might then argue that Josh Hart always plays like that, bereft of a shot or any kind of functioning shooting motion, yet hell-bent on doing everything else from rebounding to pushing the pace to cleaning up the MSG concession stands. And they'd be wrong here as well. The soul of the team has been hurting lately, both mentally and physically, and was hurting in the early stages of the game, too.

In the end, it doesn't matter what the Pacers think because "the Knicks handled their business" yesterday, as the wonderfully named 90sRback pointed out in the Postgame Thread. They didn't flinch when Julian Slawson turned into a reincarnation of the great Bobby Jones or Jalen Brunson got bumped numerous times by that third-string guard who looks like he could play a cringe-worthy Harfoot in that abomination of a TV show called Rings of Power. They also did not worry when the Pacers answered every quick power surge with a little, ugly run of their own and just could not be shaken off entirely, kind of like a film of sweat on a hot summer's day. The Knicks didn't care about any of that. They just plodded along, hitting mid-iron after mid-iron to the middle of the green. And then sprinkled in a few big monster drives as well.

Most of those came from their big man Karl-Anthony Towns. Sick the night before, he needed a bit of time to get going but then reminded everyone watching in the third quarter why quite a few people consider him one of the most skilled bigs of our generation. Crossovers between the legs, passes that Magic Johnson would be proud of, nasty step-back jumpers, the old Mark Jackson shimmy—KAT had it all going for about ten minutes. The younger Larry Nance probably is still wondering where that behemoth went that he thought he had cornered a few seats away from Spike Lee and how something that big can disappear that quickly. Or glide into the paint like he was Jean Ratelle skating over the blue line, for that matter. People sometimes say that KAT is not Nikola Jokic, and they are right. But man, Nikola Jokic or anybody else isn't KAT either.

Josh Hart might actually even up the Big Bodega in the uniqueness department. It is astonishing how much the 31-year-old does while seemingly lacking most of the skills that modern analytics consider indispensable for an impactful NBA wing. Yeah, Hart is having an impact alright, you guys sitting in front of a computer. Now, it's not always pretty, and we all could do without some of the mental lapses or lazy passes. But in the end, you just cannot argue that the New York Knicks need Josh Hart. Maybe not in as big a role as he has right now, maybe not even as a frontline starter. But they need him. Jalen Brunson definitely knows it, and that gifted dunk he let Hart have on a two-against-none fast break was a clear sign of that.

Maybe he can do the same for Mikal Bridges next game. A day after hitting the most spectacular shot of the young Knicks season so far, he fell back into the hole that he had dragged himself out of painfully slowly over the last few games. He clanked shot after shot, he had people running by his jogging self in transition, and he just looked like he was upset the game took him off his couch on Sunday night. Now, give him credit: he battled Pascal Siakam late in a matchup that you really do not want to see as a New York fan, but all in all, it just is not enough right now from a starter on a team with ambitions.

Pacome Dadiet has those as well, we believe, but that's probably the best you could say about his night. The young guy goes all out and fights hard but frankly doesn't look skilled enough to really have an impact now or in the future. You almost have to feel for him when you see his eyes after a big or clumsy mistake. Deep down inside, he seems to know it himself. In the end, he is just a kid trying to live his dream, but no matter how badly you want it, sometimes you have to realize that things might not work out. Nobody thought it would be something you could even remotely think about with Mo Diawara as early as this season, but the fact that Dadiet is seeing minutes in these games before him should sound the alarm. And last season's biggest surprise should send all the fire trucks he can find to deal with this emergency.

Diawara won't lack for prime examples of how to turn a career around in his very own locker room. Landry Shamet (again), Jose Alvarado (amazing response to losing his primary backup spot and earning it back recently), and Andre Drummond (weird but true) all played like consummate professionals yesterday, impacting a game in which their team needed more from them than they are usually accustomed to giving. That's another hallmark of a good team. Role players step up when they are counted on, guys fight back when they lose their spot in the rotation. They deal with adversity.
