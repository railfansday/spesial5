Nvidia heads for a sixth straight weekly loss as its market value sinks by about $1.6 trillion

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Thursday after reports of two affairs, and Nvidia stock fell 4.3% that day

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia $NVDA CEO on Thursday after reports of two extramarital affairs, and the chipmaker's stock is on track for a sixth straight weekly loss.

Nvidia stock dropped 4.3% during Thursday’s session, leaving it down 11% across the week. The stock is down 29% across those six weeks, which erased roughly $1.6 trillion in market value and pushed Nvidia beneath Apple $AAPL. It is the stock’s longest weekly losing streak since 2012, according to CNBC.

The selling picked up in recent days, according to CNBC. Huang sent employees a letter on Sunday apologizing for being "unfaithful to my wife," according to CNBC. The second affair, with an employee, was reported on Wednesday, and the board met late that night.

Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO while the board looks for a permanent successor. Nvidia is due to post fiscal third-quarter earnings on Tuesday, Nov. 17.

Huang, 63, stepped down as CEO and from the board, Nvidia said in a statement. Harvey C. Jones, who previously led Synopsys $SNPS, becomes the company's first board chair and will direct the search, which will consider candidates from inside and outside Nvidia, the company said. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993.

The affair Huang disclosed on Sunday was with a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. U.S. intelligence officials suspect the woman is an agent of Beijing's spy agencies.

Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation on Sunday. The board said nothing it has seen suggests Nvidia's trade secrets or U.S. national security were put at risk. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the allegation unsubstantiated.

Three House Republican committee chairs have asked Huang to appear for a private session with lawmakers on Nov. 18.
