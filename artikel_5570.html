Why Offerpad is getting more selective on home buys

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Offerpad said faster rising mortgage rates and affordability pressures slowed home sales in Q3 2026. The iBuyer tightened acquisition criteria, leaned more on brokerage and its marketplace and guided to positive adjusted EBITDA run rate by Q2 2027.  AI Summary

Heading into the second half of 2026, Offerpad was laser-focused on “turning its buying engine back on,” however, housing market headwinds caused by rapidly rising mortgage rates and continued affordability challenges, have led the iBuyer to increase the intentionality behind its strategy.

In a call with investors and analysts to discuss the company’s third quarter 2026 earnings, Offerpad executives said that with homes taking longer to sell, the firm adjusted its acquisition criteria to require higher expected returns on a property. In addition, they said the company has become more selective about new purchases and that it adjusted its underwriting to build wider expected margins into each purchase.

Instead of providing home sellers with a direct cash offer, executives said Offerpad was looking to serve more sellers through its Cash Offer Marketplace and Brokerage Services offerings.

During the quarter, Offerpad recorded 324 total transactions, up from 295 transactions a quarter prior, but down 32% annually. Executives attributed the lower-than-anticipated number of transactions for the quarter to the overall slower housing market.

“We’ve operated with mortgage rates above 7% before,” Brian Bair, the CEO of Offerpad, said during the earnings call Monday evening. “What was different this time was the speed of the move late in our quarter and the attention it received. Housing affordability has become a national story. With headlines describing it as a buyer’s market. Buyers weighed higher monthly payments against the possibility of a better deal. While sellers faced uncertainty about how long their homes would take to sell and what price they could achieve. We believe that combination contributed to a more cautious market as the quarter came to a close.”

In total, Offerpad’s Brokerage Services and Cash Offer Marketplace represented approximately 35% of Q3 closed transactions, with the company noting that brokerage listing signings more than doubled from Q2 2026.

In total, Offerpad acquired 605 homes during the quarter, up 198% annually. According to executives, these purchases were concentrated in markets where homes were still selling at a pace that met the company’s requirements. On the sales side, Offerpad sold 210 homes during the quarter, down 43% annually, but up slightly from Q2 2026.

“Last quarter, we said we were increasing acquisitions and we did by acquiring 605 homes in Q3. We also had additional homes under contract that could have increased that total. As market conditions shifted, we reevaluated them against our wider margin requirements and chose not to proceed with homes that no longer met our criteria,” Bair, a HousingWire Vanguard award winner, said. “Where possible, we offered those sellers an alternative through cash offer or broker services. Buying well is only half the process.”

At the end of the quarter aged inventory represented just 2.6% of all homes on the balance sheet, well below Offerpad’s 10% aged inventory threshold. The company said that this inventory is expected to provide a pipeline of dispositions through the rest of 2026 and into early 2027. So far in Q4, leaders said 200 homes have sold or are under contract for sale.

Bair noted on the call that Offerpad’s current portfolio of homes is “healthy” and that most were bought within the last few months.

Looking ahead, Offerpad executives said that returning to profitability is the company’s primary focus. During the third quarter of 2026, Offerpad recorded $78.1 million in revenue, down 41% annually, while the company’s net loss of $11.5 million for the quarter was a 1% improvement compared to a year prior. As the iBuyer looks to get back into the black, executives said they are focused on reducing operating expenses and growing Cash Offer Marketplace and brokerage services.

While the company has cut operating expenses by more than $140 million since 2022, during Q3, executives said they identified an additional $8 million to $10 million in annualized operating expense reductions, which they expect to be implemented in 2027.

“Profitability remains our No. 1 priority. And nothing about that has changed. What has changed is the timeline. The shift in the market has pushed our path out, and we now expect to exit the second quarter of 2027 at a positive adjusted EBITDA run rate,” Bair said. “Together, these actions give us the opportunity to reach positive adjusted EBITDA with fewer transactions than before.”

Offerpad executives have noted that they believe positive adjusted EBITDA will come with roughly 1,000 transactions per quarter. In Q4 2026, the company said it anticipated 400-475 transactions, but executives are optimistic the firm will reach that 1,000 transaction threshold. On the call, executives noted that the firm is using lenders to fund a larger share of each home’s cost, meaning that less of Offerpad’s capital is tied up in the homes the firm buys.

“That makes our capital go further and it helps it return to the business faster as homes sell. At quarter end, we had approximately $251 million of undrawn capacity under our inventory financing facilities, the majority of which is uncommitted,” Andrew Piper, who was appointed interim chief financial officer, replacing Peter Knag, said. “We believe our cash, the capital we expect to return as homes sell, and our inventory financing are sufficient as we execute our 2027 plan. We also remain open to opportunities to strengthen our balance sheet, including strategic capital partnerships, and we will be disciplined about the structure and cost of any capital we add.”
