The housing laws that could derail a sale — and why Realtors need to pay attention

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

At NAR NXT 2026, industry speakers said DSA-aligned housing policies are influencing local tenant laws. They highlighted tenant right of first refusal, just cause eviction rules, and source of income protections as issues affecting rentals, investors, and some mortgage underwriting.  AI Summary

This past week’s midterm elections saw over 100 candidates endorsed by the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) win their respective races at the federal, state, county and municipal level. While the National Association of Realtors (NAR) does not solely support one political party, instead endorsing candidates that support housing policy positions that align with NAR’s goals, Realtors are raising the alarm when it comes to some of the policies being enacted by elected DSA lawmakers.

“I am Chicken Little and I’m here to tell you that the sky is falling. But it is our responsibility to lift it up and make sure we’re aware of what’s going on so that we’re prepared,” Stacey Johnson-Cosby, an agent at ReeceNichols, a RealTrends Verified firm, said at a session during NAR’s 2026 NXT conference on Sunday. “In 40 years in the business, I’m seeing stuff now — laws and regulations that I would never have imagined and to this day can’t imagine that they’re in our marketplaces today.”

As Johnson-Cosby noted, the DSA does not believe in private property rights. Instead the group believes that housing is a human right and that properties should be owned by either the government or non-profit organizations.

“Their North Star is true social housing — housing that’s backed by public dollars. They feel housing is a human right and they’re going to make sure there is housing for everyone, whether you can afford it or not,” Johnson-Cosby said.

The things the DSA is advocating for beyond social housing, include universal rent control, tenants right to counsel and tenant organizations to organize things like rent strikes.

“Their goal is to end land and real estate speculation and decommodify housing — housing should not be for sale,” Johnson-Crosby said. “Anybody in here agree with that?”

Ending private property rights in America would not only be a drastic change for the U.S. economy, but it would also be a massive disruption to real estate and housing professionals nationwide, who are in the business of helping consumers buy and sell homes.

“They want to get rid of the concept of the white picket fence. To us, that’s the American dream. And we need the white picket fence for our clients to build wealth,” Johnson-Crosby said. “That’s our business.”

As of right now, things have not progressed this far, but Johnson-Crosby and Monica Gilroy, a founding member and managing partner at Gilroy Law Firm, said that where they are seeing an impact from DSA lawmakers and advocates is in the investor and rentals space.

Over the past decade Gilroy and Johnson-Crosby said they have seen the rise of what have been termed “tenant protection laws,” a term Johnson-Crosby does not love.

“I’m going to push back against the term tenant protection laws if the tenants are our customers. Nobody will protect our customers better than we will, right?” Johnson-Crosby, who is also a property manager, said. “We don’t need laws and elected officials and all of these groups to come in and protect our customers from us. We need them as much as they need us. We’re two sides of the same coin.”

Some of these tenant protection laws include rent control, source of income protections, just cause eviction rules, eviction moratoriums, tenant right to counsel, tenant right of first refusal and tenant bill of rights.

“These are the laws that are coming. If they’re not in your neighborhood or your jurisdiction or your city now, at some point the goal is that they would be,” Johnson-Crosby said, noting that it is important for not only property managers, but also Realtors to be aware of them if they are working with clients looking to buy or sell an investment property. “They’re spreading across the country.”

The laws Johnson-Crosby and Gilroy said agents and property managers should be most concerned about are tenant right of first refusal, just cause eviction rules and source of income protections.

Under a tenant right of first refusal, if a landlord wishes to sell a property, they must first provide the tenant with the option to purchase the home.

“Every real estate agent needs to know this. If your property that you’re listing has a renter in it, in some jurisdictions, you have to check with the renter first and give them the opportunity to determine if they’re going to buy the property,” Johnson-Crosby said. “It could be 30 days. It could be 90 days. It could be six months. It depends where they’re holding up the ability to put that house on the market until they determine if they can buy it and in some cases, they can sell the rights to a nonprofit. If this is in your marketplace, you need to know that so that your clients won’t put a house on the market, skip the tenant and end up losing money.”

With just cause evictions, renters are allowed to stay in a property as long as they want.

“If they’re not violating the law or the lease, you can’t remove them,” Johnson-Crosby said. “They are in your property for as long as they want, in perpetuity.”

If an agent is working with an investor, Johnson-Crosby and Gilroy said they need to be aware of these laws, so their client knows the risks they may be facing in venturing into this business.

While these laws can certainly have an impact, the one Johnson-Crosby and Gilroy said agents need to be especially aware of is source of income protection. In some jurisdictions, source of income has become a protected class under fair housing laws, meaning that a person cannot be denied housing based on their source of income, which may be alimony, gig work, rental assistance programs or a housing choice voucher. The denial of housing due to source of income has become the subject of multiple lawsuits over the past few years tied to rentals, but this is also a concern for lenders as it is pushing into the mortgage space when looking at someone’s ability to obtain a mortgage.

“Fair Housing is obviously something that is crucially important in our industry and we want to always be sure we’re doing everything we can to stay as compliant as possible with it,” Gilroy said.

While many of these laws are currently happening at the local level, Johnson-Crosby and Gilroy said agents need to keep an eye on these things as they may be coming to their market next.

“This is going on throughout the United States, and one of the worst things that can happen — it spreads to other states. If it happens at a national level, you’re going to be impacted,” Johnson-Crosby said. “And it can happen at the White House through regulatory agencies.”

This is why Johnson-Crosby said Realtor members need to be in contact with their local, state and federal lawmakers.

“If [lawmakers] want to create more housing, have us at the table so we can tell you what really works — from the people who actually create the housing,” Johnson-Crosby said. “We met with the state reps and we got a law put into place that pre-empted source of income in our state and we killed it through advocacy.”
