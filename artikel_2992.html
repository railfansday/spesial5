13 Ways Self-Tuning Quantum Computers Could Transform Business

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Dr. Pravir Malik is the founder and scientist of QIQuantum and the Forbes Technology Council Community leader for Quantum Computing.

Quantum computing holds significant potential for solving complex business problems, but maintaining these highly sensitive systems requires frequent calibration and specialized expertise. Agentic AI could change that equation by continuously monitoring hardware performance, detecting errors, and adjusting operating parameters with minimal human intervention. From reducing operational costs to accelerating experimentation, self-tuning quantum computers could make the technology more practical for enterprise adoption.

To help leaders leverage these potential benefits, I asked members of the Quantum Computing Group, a community that I lead through Forbes Technology Council, to share what they believe this emerging capability could unlock.

Self-tuning quantum computers eliminate system downtime by using agentic AI to autonomously calibrate and suppress operational environmental noise. Businesses gain continuous, high-fidelity execution for complex live computations such as dynamic supply chain logistics, real-time risk modeling, and complex chemical simulations, without requiring costly manual intervention by quantum physicists. - Ambika Saklani Bhardwaj, Walmart Inc.

One benefit is getting more consistent results without so much hands-on tuning. If AI can keep the machine dialed in, your specialists can spend less time babysitting the hardware and more time working on the problems you bought it to solve. - Ed Fox, MetTel

Today, quantum systems are extremely sensitive to noise, calibration drift and environmental changes, requiring constant specialist intervention. Agentic AI could continuously monitor system performance, detect degradation and autonomously adjust parameters to keep the machine operating near its optimal state. For businesses, this could turn quantum computing from a highly experimental technology into a more dependable computing resource. Self-tuning lowers the operational barrier to adoption. - Joydeep Mukherjee, Akamai Technologies

I would focus on utilization. Quantum processors need repeated calibration as their operating parameters drift. AI agents could shorten that cycle by detecting changes and adjusting controls, reducing both downtime and specialist effort. For a business running quantum pilots, the benefit is more time testing applications on expensive hardware it is already paying for. That could lower the cost of determining whether a particular use case merits further investment. - Dr. Aditya Vikram Kashyap, Morgan Stanley

Today’s quantum systems require frequent calibration because qubits drift over time. Agentic AI can continuously monitor error-correction signals, detect performance degradation, and automatically adjust operating parameters while the quantum computer is running. This reduces interruptions, improves stability and lowers the need for scarce quantum engineering expertise. So the practical benefit is higher uptime and more reliable quantum computations with less human intervention. - Peter Smulovics, Morgan Stanley
