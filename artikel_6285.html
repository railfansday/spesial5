All the Republican House members ousted from Congress

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Former House Speaker Kevin McCarthy analyzes the midterm election results during 'America's Newsroom.'

At least 30 House Republicans lost during the 2026 midterm elections as the GOP suffered an electoral rout that will result in Democrats wielding the majorities in both chambers of Congress next year.

Because the results of multiple House contests remain unclear as of Monday, the tally of House GOP lawmakers who failed to secure another term could rise even higher.

Based on the results so far, here is the list of House Republicans who lost in the midterms:

Rep. Juan Ciscomani, R-Ariz., arrives for the House Republican Conference caucus meeting in the U.S. Capitol on Tuesday, Sept. 1, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Juan Ciscomani, R-Ariz., who has served in the House since early 2023, lost to Democratic rival JoAnna Mendoza in the Grand Canyon State's 6th Congressional District.

Rep. James Gallagher, R-Calif., arrives for the House Republican Conference caucus meeting in the U.S. Capitol on Tuesday, Sept. 1, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. James Gallagher, R-Calif., who took office earlier this year, lost the election in the Golden State's 1st Congressional District to Democratic candidate Mike McGuire, a member of the California state Senate.

Gallagher won election in June to fill the vacancy that arose due to the death of Republican Rep. Doug LaMalfa of California, who passed away in early January.

Rep. David Valadao, R-Calif., speaks to the media at a campaign event as Speaker of the House Mike Johnson, R-La., stands by on Sept. 30, 2026 in Bakersfield, Calif. (Mario Tama/Getty Images)

Rep. David Valadao, R-Calif. lost the race in California's 22nd Congressional District to Democratic challenger Randy Villegas.

Valadao previously served in the House for three terms, but departed office in early 2019 after losing a 2018 re-election bid. He later won an election in 2020 and returned to the House in early 2021.

Rep. Jeff Crank, R-Colo., leaves the House Republican Conference caucus meeting at the Capitol Hill Club in Washington on Wednesday, May 13, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Jeff Crank, R-Colo., who just joined Congress last year, failed to clinch a second term, losing the contest in Colorado's 5th Congressional District to Democratic challenger Jessica Killin.

Rep. Gabe Evans, R-Colo., arrives for the House Republican Conference caucus meeting in the U.S. Capitol on Tuesday, July 21, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Gabe Evans, R-Colo., who took office last year, failed to win reelection, losing the race in Colorado's 8th Congressional District to Democratic challenger Manny Rutinel, a former member of the state's House of Representatives.

Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks, R-Iowa, attends an announcement made by President Donald Trump in the Oval Office at the White House on Sept. 28, 2026 in Washington, D.C. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images)

In Iowa's 1st Congressional District, Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks, R-Iowa, who joined Congress in early 2021, lost to Democratic candidate Christina Bohannan, a former Iowa state House member.

Rep. Zach Nunn, R-Iowa, arrives for the House Republican Conference caucus meeting in the U.S. Capitol on Tuesday, July 21, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Zach Nunn, R-Iowa, who is currently serving his second term in Congress, lost his reelection bid to Democratic Iowa State Sen. Sarah Trone Garriott in Iowa's 3rd Congressional District.

Rep. Victoria Spartz, R-Ind., departs from a House Republican Conference meeting in the U.S. Capitol Building on July 21, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Anna Moneymaker/Getty Images)

In Indiana's 5th Congressional District, Rep. Victoria Spartz lost to Democratic candidate J.D. Ford, who was a state senator until departing office last week.

The congresswoman, who is an immigrant from Ukraine, has served in the U.S. House of Representatives since early 2021.

Rep. Bill Huizenga, R-Mich., leaves a House Republican Conference meeting at the Cannon House Office Building on May 20, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images)

Rep. Bill Huizenga, R-Mich., who has served in the U.S. legislature's lower chamber since early 2011, lost to Democratic state Sen. Sean McCann in Michigan's 4th Congressional District.

Rep. Tom Barrett, R-Mich., who took office last year, fell short in Michigan's 7th Congressional District, losing his reelection bid to Democratic opponent William Lawrence.

In Missouri's 2nd Congressional District, Rep. Ann Wagner, R-Mo., who has served since early 2013, lost to Democratic candidate Fred Wellman.

Rep. Addison McDowell, R-N.C., arrives for the House Republican Conference caucus meeting at the Capitol Hill Club in Washington on Wednesday, May 13, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Addison McDowell, R-N.C., who joined Congress last year, failed to secure a second term in office.

He lost to Democratic challenger Cyril Jefferson in North Carolina's 6th Congressional District. Jefferson is the mayor of High Point, North Carolina.

Rep. Richard Hudson, R-N.C., arrives for the House Republican Conference caucus meeting in the U.S. Capitol on Wednesday, March 4, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

National Republican Congressional Committee (NRCC) chair, Rep. Richard Hudson, R-N.C., who has served in the House since early 2013, lost the election in North Carolina's 9th Congressional District to Democratic challenger Richard N. Ojeda II, a former West Virginia state senator.

Rep. Brad Knott, R-N.C., leaves the U.S. Capitol after the last votes of the week on Thursday, September 4, 2025. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Brad Knott, R-N.C., a first-term congressman, lost his re-election bid in North Carolina's 13th Congressional District to Democratic candidate Paul Barringer.

Rep. Tim Moore, R-N.C., leaves the House Republican Conference caucus meeting in the U.S. Capitol on Tuesday, Sept. 1, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

In North Carolina's 14th Congressional District, Rep. Tim Moore, R-N.C., who took office early last year, lost to Democratic rival LaKesha Womack.

Rep. Jeff Van Drew, R-N.J., during the Republican National Convention (RNC) at the Fiserv Forum in Milwaukee, Wisc., on Tuesday, July 16, 2024. (Eva Marie Uzcategui/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

Rep. Jeff Van Drew, R-N.J., lost to Democratic challenger Zack Mullock, the mayor of Cape May, New Jersey, in the Garden State's 2nd Congressional District.

The congressman, who took office in 2019 as a Democrat, switched to Republican during his first term and has remained a Republican since.

Rep. Thomas Kean Jr., R-N.J., arrives for the House Republican Conference caucus meeting in the U.S. Capitol on Wednesday, March 4, 2026. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Thomas Kean Jr., R-N.J., who has served in the House since early 2023, lost his reelection bid to Democratic challenger Rebecca Bennett in New Jersey's 7th Congressional District.

2026/10/25: Rep. Mike Lawler attends a Get Out The Vote Rally in Yorktown Heights. (Michael Nigro/Pacific Press/LightRocket via Getty Images)

In the Empire State's 17th Congressional District, second-term Rep. Mike Lawler, R-N.Y., lost to Democratic candidate Cait Conley.

Conley previously served in roles including director for counterterrorism on the White House National Security Council and as senior advisor to the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) director.

Rep. Max Miller, R-Ohio, is seen after House votes in the U.S. Capitol on Tuesday, Sept. 1, 2026. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Second-term Rep. Max Miller, R-Ohio, lost to Democratic candidate Brian Poindexter, a former Brook Park city council member, in the Buckeye State's 7th Congressional District.

Rep. Mike Turner, R-Ohio, during a campaign event at Butler-Vandalia Student Activity Center in Vandalia, Ohio, on Saturday, Oct. 3, 2026. (Jessica Phelps/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

Veteran lawmaker Rep. Mike Turner, R-Ohio, lost to Democratic challenger Kristina Knickerbocker in Ohio's 10th Congressional District.

Turner has served in the U.S. House of Representatives since early 2003.

Rep. Rob Bresnahan Jr., R-Pa., a first-term lawmaker who took office in early 2025, lost his race in Pennsylvania's 8th Congressional District to Democratic opponent Paige Cognetti, the mayor of Scranton.

Rep. Scott Perry, R-Pa., arrives at a House Republican Conference meeting in the U.S. Capitol Building on July 21, 2026 in Washington, D.C. (Anna Moneymaker/Getty Images)

Rep. Scott Perry, a former chair of the House Freedom Caucus, lost the race in Pennsylvania's 10th Congressional District to Democratic challenger Janelle Stelson.

The congressman has served in the House since early 2013.

Rep. Monica De La Cruz, R-Texas, who has served in Congress since early 2023, lost her reelection bid in Texas's 15th Congressional District to Democratic challenger Bobby Pulido.

Long-serving Rep. Rob Wittman, R-Va., who has been a House lawmaker since late 2007, lost his reelection bid in Virginia's 1st Congressional District to Democratic rival Shannon Taylor, Henrico County's Commonwealth's Attorney.

Rep. Jen Kiggans, R-Va., attends the House Armed Services Committee hearing titled "Department of Defense FY2027 Budget Request," in Rayburn building on Wednesday, April 29, 2026. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images)

Rep. Jen Kiggans, R-Va., lost her re-election bid to former Rep. Elaine Luria in Virginia's 2nd Congressional District.

Kiggans, who initially won the House seat by defeating then-incumbent Luria in 2022, has served in the lower congressional chamber since early 2023.

Four-term Rep. Bryan Steil, R-Wisc., who took office in early 2019, lost his re-election bid in Wisconsin's 1st Congressional District to Democratic candidate Mitchell Berman.
