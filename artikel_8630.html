Make your savings goal hard to quit with a commitment device, a choice you lock in now so that backing out later takes effort or costs money. Use the least restrictive one that keeps you saving, and give the goal a dollar amount and a deadline so the device has something to hold you to.

You set a savings goal on a day when saving feels easy. You keep it, or don’t, on harder days, when a sale or a short paycheck makes skipping one deposit look harmless.

A commitment device makes the decision for those days in advance. Ulysses had his crew tie him to the mast so he could hear the Sirens’ song without steering toward them. With money, the device removes the tempting option, or puts a price on it, before temptation arrives.

Economists Richard Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi built one into a retirement plan. In their Save More Tomorrow program, workers agreed in advance to save part of each future raise. At the first company to offer it, participants’ average saving rate rose from 3.5% of pay to 13.6% in 40 months.

Start with the least restrictive device that keeps you saving. A penalty so steep that you hesitate to put money in leaves you saving nothing.

Give the goal an amount and a date, such as $2,400 by Oct. 1, so you and any referee know what counts as keeping it.

Set up one device this week, and keep the stricter ones for goals you’ve already broken once.
