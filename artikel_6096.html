For years, the travel industry has been embracing artificial intelligence, using it to make websites easier to digest — and even generate personalized recommendations. Now, it has become an invaluable tool for travelers searching for the best Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals.

Experts told TPG that using AI can make the start of trip planning feel less daunting and more natural. In fact, travelers can ask for a list of hotels within a certain budget range, and that happen to be close to things like public transport or have perks like free breakfast, said Melanie Fish, a travel expert at Expedia.

“Instead of starting with a long list of filters, travelers can tell [sites like] Expedia or Hotels.com what they have in mind, in their own words,” Fish said. “AI can help them explore hotel options, compare what matters and narrow the choices.”

At Hotels.com, the company offers a “Natural Language Search” for Black Friday travel deals, allowing travelers to search for things like “‘Four nights in Barcelona in May on sale for Black Friday or Travel Tuesday,’ and get hotels offering up to 50 [percent] off,” Fish said.

Ian Ackland, the managing director for the Americas at Booking.com, recommended travelers start their prompt with the kind of trip they want, rather than just searching by the discounts that pop up post-Thanksgiving. That’s because what constitutes a “good deal” may mean more than simply a cheap rate.

“Share your destination, dates, budget and the details that matter, like breakfast, family space or walkable restaurants,” Ackland said. “The more specific you are, the more useful the results can be. A good deal is one that fits the trip, not one that’s simply the lowest price.”

It can also be used for side-by-side comparisons of different hotel options, said Google spokesperson Sarah Armstrong. At Google, the AI assistant Gemini can pull everything from base rates to estimated taxes, resort fees, cancellation policies and more.

The AI tool can also save time by continuing to work in the background with things like price alerts while travelers focus on other things.

“When you're ready to secure your room, Auto Browse in Gemini in Chrome can take the wheel,” Armstrong said. “Working right from the Chrome side panel, Gemini can navigate travel sites, fill in your dates and select your room options then pause safely at the checkout screen so you can review the final details before paying.”

That said, AI isn’t full proof. That’s especially true with Black Friday deals since prices can change quickly and promotional terms vary, Fish said.

“Tools like Claude and ChatGPT can be a useful shortcut, but travelers should treat its recommendations as a starting point, not the final source of truth,” Fish said. “Potential pitfalls include outdated or incomplete pricing, missed taxes and fees, limited availability, incorrect details about amenities or cancellation policies, and recommendations that don’t reflect the traveler’s priorities.”

Ackland said Booking.com has worked with AI for over a decade, and ultimately, it “should help you make a decision, not leave you guessing about what you’ve booked.”

It is important to note while AI tools do “occasionally get things wrong,” Armstrong said, it won’t make purchases without permission. Nonetheless, “it's still smart to review the key details like dates, prices, and cancellation rules before hitting confirm.”

Thankfully, Fish said picking whether travelers use AI or a traditional search engine doesn't have to be “an either-or choice.”

“AI is particularly useful for inspiration and discovery,” Fish said, “while traditional search and trusted travel platforms… remain the best places to confirm live prices, availability, sale terms, guest reviews and cancellation policies — and to complete the booking.”
