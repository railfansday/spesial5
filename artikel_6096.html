Singapore Airlines’ India bet is costly. Losses are shrinking, but the stake only pays off if Air India fixes its costs. Until then, the profitable core airline is footing the bill.

Singapore Airlines (SIA) is sticking with its 25.1% stake in Air India, even as the investment continues to weigh heavily on its financial results.

For the six months to September 30, SIA on Wednesday reported a net profit of S$134 million ($104 million), down 44% from a year earlier, on record revenue of S$11.3 billion ($8.8 billion). Its share of Air India’s losses was S$361.7 million ($281.5 million), nearly 2.7 times the group’s net profit. That was still a marked improvement on the S$429.5 million ($334 million) loss recorded a year earlier.

The improvement came entirely in the second quarter. SIA said its share of losses from associated companies, mainly Air India, was S$110 million ($85.6 million) lower than a year earlier in the three months to September. Singapore Airlines is keeping its 25.1% stake in Air India even as the investment cuts deeply into group earnings. For the six months to September 30, SIA reported net profit of S$134 million ($104 million), down 44% from a year earlier, on record revenue of S$11.3 billion ($8.8 billion). Its share of Air India's losses came to S$361.7 million ($281.5 million) — nearly 2.7 times the group's net profit — though that was an improvement on the S$429.5 million loss a year earlier, with the entire gain landing in the second quarter. SIA's own airline operations stayed profitable, with first-half operating profit of S$569 million ($443 million) despite a 56% rise in net fuel costs after hedging. The carrying value of the Air India investment fell to S$800 million ($622 million) from S$1.135 billion at the end of March. SIA described Air India as a key part of its multi-hub strategy and said a transformation of this scale "will take time," giving no timeline for a return to profitability. Air India Group reported a combined loss of about $2.3 billion in fiscal 2026, more than double the prior year, after the June 2025 crash, the closure of Pakistani airspace, aircraft delivery delays, and higher fuel costs. Tata Sons and SIA are preparing to inject INR 100 billion ($1.1 billion) in installments tied to performance milestones, with SIA's share at about $276 million, Skift reported in September.
