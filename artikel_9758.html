The day after the Microsoft 365 outage: what we learned and what you can do now

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Posted on Nov 14 • Originally published at daniele-michelin.com

On Thursday 12 November 2026, Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Defender XDR failed or ran degraded worldwide. This post sorts it out: what is documented, what is still open, what we learn from it, and what you can do today without rebuilding your whole infrastructure.

The question of how dependent you want to be on a single vendor is covered in Microsoft 365 outage and dependency. This post is about the analysis and the concrete steps afterwards.

State of the information: Microsoft has not published a final post-incident review yet. Everything below rests on Microsoft's status updates as summarised by BleepingComputer and on a technical analysis by Control Plane. The analysis is an interpretation, not a confirmation by Microsoft.

The description fits a known pattern, the cascading failure: health checks take sick servers out of rotation, their load lands on the remaining ones, and those fail the checks too. The load per server keeps rising although traffic stays flat. Servers that were returned to service are said to have failed again under load. That explains why recovery took hours and not minutes. It does not explain what caused the first failure; that is the question for the final report.

Once Microsoft publishes its final report I will update this post. Until then: the outage was a test you did not plan. Use the result.
