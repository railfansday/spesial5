Compress to exactly 200 KB: how exact-target image compression works in your browser

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Most image compressors give you a quality slider and wish you luck. You pick 80%, export, check the size, and repeat until the file fits under the limit. ToolsByUs Compress Image does it the other way round: you state the target size, and the tool finds the settings that land on it.

The whole thing runs in the browser. The file's contents never leave the tab — there is no upload step at all.

I took a 4032x3024 JPEG photo and asked the tool for exactly 200 KB:

The preview the tool shows is the real encoded result, not a simulation — what you see is the file you download.

A quality slider controls one variable; file size is the outcome of quality, dimensions, format, and image content together. Hitting an exact size means searching that space: try settings, encode, measure, adjust, encode again. Doing that search locally, in the browser, is the interesting engineering part — every encode is real work on your machine, and the tool has to converge without making you wait through dozens of visible attempts.

That is also why the preview matters. If the tool only estimated the size, you would still be guessing. Showing the actual encoded output means the number on screen and the file you download agree.

If you have ever re-exported the same image four times to get under an upload limit, stating the target size up front is the saner workflow. One tool that does it, free, with no upload: https://toolsbyus.com
