Demand For $1,589 In Rental Car Damage Dropped After Pointing Out Illegal Fees

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Sixt demanded $1,589 from a customer for splatter and scuffs on a rental car, including nearly $500 in illegal fees. The customer disputed the claim, complained to consumer agencies, and says it went to collections anyway. The bill disappeared once the practice came under scrutiny. Christopher Nienhuis, a Colorado resident, rented a Buick at Milwaukee airport in October while visiting Marquette University. Days after returning it, he received a damage bill for black splatter around the license plate and near the tires, along with white scuffing around the rear windshield. He says he didn’t cause the damage and that the black material could simply be washed off. Sixt dropped the claim a week after being contacted by a news reporter. Four Renters Were Billed Fees Wisconsin Doesn’t Allow Four customers with disputed claims from the same airport location complained of illegal charges. Todd Lofgren: $6,265 for “graffiti.” He described white specks near a tire and along the bumper. The photos and invoice were dated August 18, three days after he returned the car. The bill included $1,161 in prohibited fees. Sixt dropped it September 28 after he challenged it through multiple channels. Brenda Bassett: nearly $600 for a dent already documented. The rear dent was listed in her rental agreement before she took the 2025 Toyota. About $137 consisted of diminished value and appraisal fees. A September 15 letter came from Strala Group, which handles claims for Sixt. The claim was dropped. Autumn Wojciechowski: $872 for a scratch underneath the car. She’d photographed the vehicle before driving away, but not its underside. Nearly $200 of her October 2025 damage invoice consisted of prohibited fees. This claim was dropped, too. All four claims have been withdrawn. Wisconsin law limits damage liability to reasonable repair costs, reflecting discounts available to the rental company, or the vehicle’s value before the damage, whichever is lower. Loss of use, administrative charges and appraisal fees are prohibited. Diminished value charges (loss in resale value) aren’t allowed either. Sixt Already Settled A Damage Billing Lawsuit Sixt’s 2025 damage billing settlement addressed allegations involving estimated repairs, diminished value, loss of use and administrative charges. The $11 million package included about $2.87 million in potential refunds and $6.6 million in abandoned collection claims, along with legal and administrative costs. The incentives are lousy. A customer can pay to make a collection demand go away, turn it over to insurance, or spend hours fighting. If the company withdraws a bad claim only when someone gets enough attention, everyone else still has to decide whether contesting it is worth the trouble. I’ve called rental car fees the scammiest in travel. These damage add-ons are especially difficult to comparison shop because you don’t discover them until after you’ve returned the car. Sixt Isn’t Great, But You Expect Charges To Be Correct I’ve found Sixt offers an inferior experience in the U.S. market. My Miami rental years ago involved a Mercedes they didn’t and a proposed alternative of a $30 a day upgrade for a lesser car. I canceled and went to National. The Milwaukee airport said its lease requires Sixt to follow the law and allows termination for an uncured violation after notice. But the airport said it was waiting for the state consumer protection agency to determine that a violation occurred. It’s not yet clear whether they’ve stopped charging these fees in Wisconsin. This is common practice in Europe. Hertz sent me an invoice and charged my credit card almost a year after a rental in Germany for $460.00 for “damages.” I disputed the charges as I still had my return showing that they signed off with no damage. AMEX refunded my money, so Hertz turned it over to a collection agency in PA. They called me and I told them to go to hell and sue me. The collection dude said, “No one is going to sue you for $460.00,” so I sent them a letter telling them to cease and desist contacting me about the charge and if they wanted to sue, I was willing to counter sue as I had paperwork saying the car was returned undamaged. Last I heard of it and it never turned up on my credit report. Every time I rent in Europe, I get dinged for a toll, or in Italy it was for driving in a “congested area.” You figure they steal $100 to $200 from every renter, that is a good chunk of change a year.

Sixt got me for some damage that I know I didn’t cause. I didn’t think I needed to record video of the car after I brought it back. Credit card CDW paid for 90% of it except for a lost depreciation fee of $200. Snakey.

Im not going to no collections over a damage dispute. Ruining my credit? Pay for it and sue later.

I don’t rent cars very often, but when I do, I video the entire exterior and interior at pickup and again when I return the car along with the guy/girl I hand the keys to. I had one of these BS damage incidents several years ago and I got dinged for something I didn’t do and it was a couple months after I rented. Fortunately I was still in possession of the turn in receipt so I didn’t have to pay. All that being said, they are scam artists of the 10th degree and I don’t trust any of them.. Video and photograph your rentals.

Avis is notorious for ‘cleaning fees’. 9 times out of 10 the claim is dropped. It is important to take pics before AND after.

Maybe I’ve just been lucky, but this is (one of the reasons) why I like National. They don’t seem to want to nickel and dime you over everything. The “allowed” amount of damage is pretty generous. I’ve never had them hassle me over anything. And the refill costs if you bring a car back not full of fuel aren’t totally ridiculous either.

Wisconsin law also requires rental car companies to provide you with independent damage appraisals and a right to inspect before they can just charge you for damages. I once got billed by Avis for repairs for damage that I didn’t cause weeks after my rental. I challenged it to the Avis General Counsel’s office, pointing out that they didn’t follow the law, and they dropped the charges.

It really amazes me Congress doesn’t take on Hertz and now Sixt. This is literally a 99.9% issue. You would get so much publicity and public support.

I had one management job where I had a lot of my team traveli to UK. There was a span where my company was between.major rental contracts in Europe, and in the interim they were pointing us to Sixt, and unlike in the US, we self-insured. SIxt was really good at finding (plastic) wheel cover scuffs and paint scratches, consistently $700 to 800 GBP. Fortunately, they were not selected for the new contract. My company has about 4000 people on travel at any given time.
