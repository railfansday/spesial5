Spirit Airlines Shut Down—Did It Forget To Cancel Its Internet? Comcast Wants $1.25 Million

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Spirit Airlines stopped flying in May, but Comcast and its subsidiaries say it still owes $1.25 million in internet bills. Some charges are for locations Spirit says it left in 2024—but the providers say they haven’t produced records showing the service was canceled. Always keep proof when dealing with Comcast! Part of this sounds like Spirit forgot to turn off its internet when it moved out. According to the providers, on October 22 the airline authorized them to disconnect every location except Dania Pointe in Dania Beach, Florida. That was nearly six months after the airline stopped flying. Their November 19 court filing asks the bankruptcy judge to order payment and make Spirit decide whether to keep or reject the contracts. Comcast and its Masergy subsidiary say some services were still running when they filed. Spirit shut down on May 2 after it couldn’t obtain the money to keep operating. No one would continue to fund its massive losses. But the bankruptcy process to unwind its business remains ongoing. There are assets to sell, contracts to resolve, records to maintain and creditors arguing over who gets paid. I’ve covered the lawsuit from workers seeking 60 days of pay over the abrupt shutdown and the court-approved $668 million sale of a 27-aircraft portfolio. Much of that aircraft price represented lenders applying what Spirit owed them toward their purchase. The four internet providers list these unpaid balances: Provider Amount Claimed Comcast Cable Communications Management $219,743.41 Masergy Cloud Communications $164,626.99 Masergy Communications $849,646.45 Nitel $11,990.73 Total $1,246,007.58 Comcast acquired Masergy in 2021 and Nitel in 2025. This is for internet service at Spirit locations, not inflight wifi (which was provided by Thales). The providers say they’ve repeatedly sought payment. Spirit responded that some locations had been vacated in 2024, and that disconnection requests should have been submitted for others. You might expect the internet companies simply to get in line with Spirit’s other unpaid creditors. For bills dating to before the bankruptcy, that’s probably what happens. But these companies say they continued providing services after Spirit filed on August 29, 2025. Necessary expenses of running a company in bankruptcy can qualify as administrative expenses, which get priority over other unsecured debts, or else no one would keep supplying a bankrupt business trying to turn itself around. And services needed to sell its assets and administer the remaining business can continue to qualify. It’s unclear what treatment will apply to internet Spirit was no longer using, even if it hadn’t cancelled the contracts. Comcast needs to establish the benefit their services supplied to the bankruptcy estate. In fact, their motion cites In re Enron but that denied administrative priority for unused pipeline capacity that hadn’t benefited the estate. Internet connections supporting the Spirit Airlines liquidation are likely to get repayment priority But defunct services that hadn’t merely been cancelled might not. A hearing is scheduled for December 16, with responses due December 9. And even a successful priority claim doesn’t necessarily mean Comcast gets immediate payment.
