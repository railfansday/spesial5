Alaska Airlines Eyes New Long-Haul Boeing 787 Route: But There's A Major Twist

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Alaska Airlines is keen to fly to Lisbon, which is the capital of Portugal and an important destination for tourism. The oneworld member has previously expressed interest in flying there. A major ongoing stumbling block is the lack of slots, at least until, and if, Lisbon's new airport opens in many years.

As Ishrion Aviation has pointed out, Alaska Airlines applied for slots for a daily service to Lisbon. Unsurprisingly, it was not granted any of them. Slots do exist, but only in limited numbers and at particular times. It is likely that the growing US operator would either have to reduce its request substantially (say, to a logical three weekly flights) or accept inferior times, as WestJet did. Given Alaska Airlines' hub structure, a poor schedule would not be sensible.

The carrier has big plans for its long-haul network, although it is finding the going tough for now. Recently, it called its long-haul routes 'a drag', but they'd always take time to develop, even before the rise in the price of fuel. Against this backdrop, it'd be reasonable to question the appeal of potential future flights between Seattle, obviously Alaska Airlines' primary hub, and the Portuguese capital.

In the 12 months to September 2026, booking data via OAG shows that 22,700 round-trip passengers flew indirectly between the two cities. Obviously, that volume is small, although nonstop flights, along with hopefully strong promotions, would easily grow that by 40% to 50% in the first season.

Nonetheless, the supposed daily frequency would be highly excessive (three weekly flights would make much more sense). As such, it would therefore rely even more than usual on lower-yielding transfer traffic via Seattle.

Despite the low existing traffic, Lisbon is Seattle's third-largest unserved European market, excluding places like Athens, which Alaska Airlines has already announced. Zurich was the top unserved market (36,100 passengers), which stopped having nonstop flights with the Swiss leisure operator Edelweiss in 2025, followed by the oneworld hub of Madrid (25,500).

Of course, traffic (both existing and likely stimulated) is just one part of it. Across all airlines, cabins, and passengers, the average fare to Lisbon was $641 one-way (excluding a fuel surcharge, taxes, and add-ons). That was similar to Barcelona, to which Delta Air Lines began flying this year, almost certainly to get in before Alaska Airlines announced the route. However, Lisbon flights would cover less distance and be less expensive to serve.

The airline's international expansion continues.

More notably, and perhaps worryingly, Seattle-Athens had an average one-way fare of $646, which was less than 1% higher than Lisbon's. However, flights to the capital of Greece, which Alaska Airlines will begin on May 12, 2027, will cover 19% more distance and be much more expensive to operate. Of course, in these instances, and elsewhere where it is the only operator, it should get a reasonable price premium for the convenience of flying nonstop.

The carrier's debut to Europe took place in April 2026, when the first flight from Seattle to Rome took off. The launch came after Alaska Airlines began its first-ever long-haul service, which was to Asia. The first route was from Washington State to Tokyo Narita in May 2025, followed by Seoul Incheon four months later.

Alaska Airlines' Planned Operations To Europe: July 2027

Faces British Airways, Delta, and Virgin Atlantic

After Rome came London Heathrow (May 2026), followed by Reykjavík (May 2026). Next year, Alaska Airlines will begin flying to Athens (May 2027) and Paris CDG (May 2027). When writing, the carrier currently plans five transatlantic routes, with a big emphasis on leisure markets, just like WestJet from Canada to Europe. This is perhaps why Alaska Airlines has not announced flights to Madrid, in what would be a logical tie-up with fellow oneworld member Iberia.

Let's narrow the focus to mid-July 2027. In that week, Alaska Airlines will be one of 12 airlines flying from Seattle to Europe, which will collectively have 147 weekly departures (21 daily). Discover Airlines will be there, as the Lufthansa Group's leisure unit is replacing Lufthansa itself. Alaska Airlines will have 28 weekly departures (four daily) and rank second behind Delta (35 weekly; five daily).
