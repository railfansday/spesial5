OK, that photo is a bit crazy, but I do have to praise airline mobility assistance.

I fractured my ankle last month, two weeks before a family vacation to Roatan. I wasn’t going to make my family suffer for that, and I had a business meeting in Dallas the week after anyway. I was in a boot and crutches, so I could get around, but airports are really big and we had a 45 minute connection in Miami, so I begrudgingly requested wheelchair assistance.

If you’ve done this before, they ask you if you need help on-and-off the plane or just in the terminal. If you don’t need help on the plane they still have your name on the list and you (and your family) can board early. They’re not pushy, but they are extremely helpful and will encourage you to take advantage of the services offered.

In retrospect, I do not think I would have been able to do this trip without assistance. On both trips (there and back) we got to the departure airport early so I walked/hobbled myself through security to the gate, but this was essential for our connections each way (MIA down on AA, IAH back on UA). The assistance staff are shockingly polite and competent, and understanding of your situation, whatever it may be.

This was especially helpful at IAH, where we had to clear customs and immigration on the way into the country. We all have Global Entry so the line isn’t the problem, but the distances would have taken me an hour to cover. We had enough time that they dropped us off as the Centurion Lounge, and I had to reassure them multiple times I really didn’t need help connecting after. (This was somewhat foolish, as our flight was delayed and then moved to a farther gate, and it was a significant pain to get there.)

Going back to the photo, the wheelchair assistant at IAH said that he often sees assistance booked for older relatives from foreign countries specifically for language assistance — if you are navigating US customs and immigration and do not speak English well (or at all) it can be a complete nightmare. This is perhaps indication of a different missing service, but he did not begrudge these people asking for help at all.

Overall the whole experience was eye-opening on how inaccessible airports are unless you have good mobility, and was really an example of the sort of considerations we need in other everyday situations. I assume the only reason this is free-of-charge in airports is because of the immense costs of delaying a flight for someone hobbling across the huge airport to make a connection, but it’s a good example of things we can just do to improve the lives of others.
