Singapore Airlines Cancels 9 Airbus A350 Routes: See Full List Now

Singapore Airlines has trimmed up to nine Airbus A350-900 routes, deploying alternative aircraft to key international destinations. The Star Alliance carrier operates 64 A350-900s, according to data from ch-aviation, the largest of any type in the airline's fleet.

Beginning in January, the airline began adjusting its network, dropping the A350-900 from its nonstop services across Asia, while later in the year services to Australasia and Europe also saw network changes. This article's data is from Cirium, an aviation analytics company.

In January, Chengdu lost its nonstop A350-900 services from Singapore Changi Airport (SIN), as the airline opted to deploy the Boeing 787-10 Dreamliner on the service to China. That same month, Singapore dropped the A350 on its route to Penang, instead assigning the narrowbody Boeing 737 MAX 8 to serve the Malaysian state.

In February, the popular tourist destination of Phuket in Thailand lost its widebody services with the Singaporean carrier, as the airline opted for the MAX 8 on its nonstop services to the Thai destination.

In March, Singapore Airlines decided to change its Bali services from the A350 to a mix of the 787-10 and MAX 8. This came as the airline adjusted its operations on the route to meet ongoing demand.

This was followed in April, when SQ adjusted its services to Auckland, dropping the A350 in lieu of the Airbus A380 and Boeing 777-300ER. The resumption of the double-decker on flights to Auckland marks just the second airline to operate regular A380 services to New Zealand's largest city, after Emirates.

Taipei became the next destination in April to see aircraft changes by Singapore Airlines, as the carrier adjusted its schedule to Taipei to the 787-10. Colombo, Sri Lanka's largest city, has seen the A350 absent from its network since April, as SQ also opted to deploy the 787-10 to meet market demand.

From October, Singapore Airlines adjusted its network to the Dutch capital. The airline has since removed the A350 from its service to the city, instead placing the 777-300ER on the route. Singapore is connected to Amsterdam by two airlines, Singapore and KLM. Singapore Airlines operates the route once a day.

Cairns AIrport (CNS), the gateway to Tropical North Queensland and the Great Barrier Reef, has been the latest in the airline's network to see the A350 removed. From November, the airline has opted to change the service to be served with the smaller MAX 8. Cairns is one of eight Australian gateways for Singapore Airlines, as the airline also welcomes its inaugural service to Western Sydney (WSI) on November 23.

These schedule changes reflect the airline's major fleet reallocation and are driven by its assessment of market demand, the ongoing A350-900 cabin retrofit program, and other network expansions. While the aircraft will be missed on some routes, it opens new opportunities for others, such as the First Class cabin additions on routes to Amsterdam and Auckland; however, there will be major cabin downgrades for those on MAX services to Penang, Phuket, Bali, and Cairns.
