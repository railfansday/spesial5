Moving Abroad? Qualify for a Visa, Then Price the Move Against What You’ll Save

Before you move abroad, confirm you qualify for a residence visa and plan for the U.S. tax return you’ll still file. Price what the move adds, including health insurance that covers you there, deposits, and trips home, against what you’ll save each month. Then rent there for a month or two before you sell your home.

The visa comes first, since Americans can visit Portugal and the rest of Europe’s open-border Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without one, the State Department says. The costs that follow decide whether the move saves money.

Staying longer takes a residence visa, and each kind sets its own requirements. A work visa usually starts with a job offer there, a family visa with a spouse or parent who’s a citizen or legal resident, and an investor visa with a large investment. Portugal’s D7, a visa for retirees and others living on their own income, asks for steady income of at least the Portuguese minimum wage, now €920 a month. Costa Rica’s digital nomad visa asks remote workers to show at least $3,000 a month in net income, or $4,000 with dependents.

Both of those visas also require health insurance, and Medicare won’t pay for care outside the U.S. in most situations. So price that coverage first.

Say a retired couple heading to Portugal budgets about $12,000 for the move, visas, and rental deposits. They expect to spend about $2,800 a month there, including private health insurance and a trip home each year, against $4,500 for the same life at home. The $1,700 a month they save covers the $12,000 in about seven months.

Some U.S. ties stay. You still file a federal tax return on your worldwide income, and foreign accounts that top $10,000 combined at any point in the year go on an FBAR, a yearly report filed online. Social Security pays U.S. citizens abroad everywhere but a few countries, and its Payments Abroad Screening Tool tells you whether yours is one. Keep a U.S. checking account that refunds ATM fees abroad and a credit card with no foreign transaction fees.

Before you sell your home, rent in your first-choice city for a month or two on a visitor’s stay, and track every expense against your monthly budget.
