You Don’t Have to Do Everything Yourself

Posted November 3, 2026 | Reviewed by Michelle Quirk

When was the last time you were on a rollercoaster? Honestly, I can't remember the last time I was on one, although lately life itself has felt like a rollercoaster ride. Riding the highs, lows, and everything in between certainly keeps things interesting, even if some days it feels pretty challenging. But it's reminded me of something important: You don't have to do everything yourself.

This is not an easy thing for me to admit. I’ve always been fiercely independent. I've always been determined to figure things out myself. I struggle to ask for help because I hate the idea of inconveniencing anyone and, honestly, because it makes me feel weak and incapable. Yet, I know that's not true, not for me or for anyone else. Sometimes, we just need help.

That doesn't mean someone else has to completely take over and solve our problems for us. It could mean hearing a different perspective, getting some new ideas, or just being listened to. Or it could mean someone holds our hand and stands there with us. Whatever shape that help takes, it's OK to ask for it, even if it feels hard.

Most of our cultures place a lot of value on independence. Self-sufficiency, resilience, and the ability to keep going are all celebrated. We’re encouraged to take responsibility for our lives, solve our own problems, and not rely too heavily on anyone else. These are all useful qualities, but like with most things, they can be taken too far. That’s why asking for help can leave us feeling like failures. Yet, when I think about the people I love, I would never want them to feel they couldn't ask for my help. I would want them to tell me when things were difficult so I could show up for them. So why is it so hard to offer ourselves that same kindness?

When I think about it, I believe asking for help is a sign of strength. It means we're vulnerable enough to admit that we don't have everything figured out. It means we're brave enough to acknowledge that perhaps we don’t want to do something alone. None of this makes us any less capable; it simply makes us human. And, if we look at research, accepting help can actually make us stronger humans. A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis found that feeling socially supported is associated with better mental and physical health. Maybe that's because knowing we're not facing a difficult situation alone can already make it feel more manageable.

To add to this, we can look at the perspective of the person offering the help. Think about how it feels when someone you care about lets you help them. You lend them a hand when they're overwhelmed, and you can see how much it helps them. It feels good to be there for them. So refusing help can actually deny someone who cares for you the opportunity to show they care.

Research has also explored this side of social support, with a 2017 review suggesting that providing care and support can benefit the person giving it as well as the person receiving it. This doesn’t mean we have to accept every offer of help, especially if we really don't feel like we need it. But maybe occasionally we underestimate how much people want to be there for us. I know I do. When we let someone help, we’re giving them the opportunity to do something meaningful, both for them and for us.

Being independent doesn’t mean never needing anyone. It means recognising our limits and allowing others to help. After all, there’s no award for making life harder by insisting on doing everything alone (and even if there was, how silly would that be?!). Asking for help doesn’t make us any weaker. It simply means that while we can stand on our own two feet, sometimes we’d like someone to stand there with us.

I'm going to leave you with an excerpt from one of my favourite books, The Boy, the Mole, the Fox, and the Horse by Charlie Mackesy:

“What is the bravest thing you’ve ever said?” asked the boy.

“Asking for help isn’t giving up,” said the horse. “It’s refusing to give up.”
