The 7 Best Microcurrent Devices of 2026 Tested by Women’s Health Editors

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Women's Health may earn commission from the links on this page, but we only feature products we believe in. Why Trust Us?

We’re living in the golden age of high-tech skincare devices—and as our beauty editor, I couldn’t be happier. Red light therapy devices, including LED face masks, provide excellent anti-aging benefits from the comfort of our couch, while face steamers have replaced my need for pricey spa facials. Laser hair removal devices are becoming more inclusive by offering technology safe enough for deeper complexions. Face massagers also help de-puff skin after a hangover. However, when it comes to lifting saggy skin, microcurrent devices are the ultimate MVP skincare tool deserving of a spot in your beauty arsenal.

Think of them as a sculpting workout for your face without the sweat. “Microcurrent devices use low levels of electrical current to stimulate facial muscles, helping to boost collagen and sculpt and lift the face,” says dermatologist Marisa Garshick, MD. And don’t worry: These aren’t painful. You’ll notice more of a temporary tingling sensation that’ll disappear within minutes of using the device.

While these at-home machines aren’t as powerful as the ones used by professionals, they’re a more cost-effective option for achieving subtle improvements, says dermatologist Bradley Glodny, MD. You’ll just need some patience to find a weekly cadence and intensity level that works for you. (More on that in a bit.) And if you’re getting seasonal in-office microcurrent treatments, these at-home machines help enhance and sustain your professional results over time, says Dr. Garshick.

What was once a closely guarded secret among my fellow beauty editors and plastic surgeons has now become increasingly popular and readily accessible across our most trusted sites, such as Sephora, Ulta, and Amazon. But an influx of hundreds of options can be overwhelming, which is where I come in. My years of reporting and research have informed this shopping guide. I also consulted the pros about what to look out for when buying at-home microcurrent machines. Ahead are a few non-negotiables to keep in mind, which helped inform the devices we tested and ultimately featured in this story.

Most devices on this list have a range of microcurrent levels—some, like Foreo, even go up to 10. The higher you go, the more intense the microcurrent will feel. If you're accustomed to microcurrent and prefer a device with more power and customization options, opt for one that offers multiple microcurrent levels.

On the other hand, if you're a beginner, you might only need to purchase a device with a few microcurrent levels, such as the NuFace Trinity.

Dr. Garshick advises using your microcurrent device at least three times a week for four to six weeks before you can expect to see results, although some devices recommend daily use. “While much of the initial benefit may only be noticed for hours to days, with consistent use, the results will continue as collagen production can take six or more months,” she says.

After the initial treatment period, you can decrease the frequency of treatments to two to three times per week to maintain your results. Always refer to the specific instructions for use of your microcurrent device.

Many microcurrent devices come with additional features, such as interchangeable attachments, smartphone app connectivity, and even instructional guides.

An added bonus for your microcurrent device is FDA approval, which means that the government organization has determined that the device is safe and effective for its intended use. This is not a necessity when purchasing a microcurrent device, but it's definitely a solid addition if you're looking for something that has been rigorously tested.

You’ll also want to order a microcurrent device that either includes a priming gel or purchase one separately. They’re conductive and allow the electrical currents to be transmitted from the device to the skin. They also help the device glide easily across the skin and help prevent irritation.

Unlike bulky in-office machines, at-home microcurrent devices are about the size of a closed fist. Someone who travels often might prefer the Solawave Facial Wand, a slim option that resembles a T-shaped salad fork. Regardless of which of these featured options you order, they won’t take up too much space in your medicine cabinet or cosmetic bag.

At-home microcurrent machines aren’t exactly cheap. Entry-level models cost around $150, while high-end options can easily go for upwards of $500. However, you’ll save money over time because you get multiple uses out of a single device. And if it helps ease the burden, I spent $275 for a pro microcurrent facial, which costs $60 more than the WH Skincare Award-winning Laduora Lumeo and conductive gel.

With the above dermatological guidance in mind, I began whittling down our list of contenders. The Women’s Health beauty closet was my first stop, along with my at-home collection of skincare devices. Of course, because I’m inquisitive and love knowing what the pros actually use IRL and recommend to their patients, I asked both Dr. Garshick and Dr. Glodny for their fail-safe picks. Next, I tapped our team of skin-obsessive editors and writers, including Brian Underwood, Lily Wohlner, Maridel Reyes, and Cristina Montemayor. And because we prefer only spotlighting products we actually tested, we put them all through a vetting process, which includes:

I eliminated anything that was cumbersome. For example, anything with a short cord or an extra-long one that easily tangled was cut. Were the cordless devices easy to charge quickly if I was in a crunch? Were there any other buttons or features that are easy to accidentally hit while rolling the device across my face?

These aren’t exactly short treatments. We’ll need at least a few minutes per treatment session, so caring for an entire face can easily take 15 minutes or more. I prioritized lightweight, but durable devices that easily slide across my skin. Anything that made my wrist tired after a few minutes was docked points. Was the tingling too intense for beginners or best suited for novices? Are there enough levels for both beginners and experienced users?

This was particularly important because everyone wants a lifted appearance. I evaluated if I noticed any improvements after the first usage, and kept checking in over the last six months. How do the results improve over time?

This was determined based on how each device scores in relation to the above factors. Plus, I asked myself and our team if these are products they’d be willing to repurchase or gift to loved ones because they stand behind the quality of the devices. Ultimately, each of these featured picks met those requirements; here are the results of our labor.
