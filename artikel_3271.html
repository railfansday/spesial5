Here are the best Samsung phones based on our testing

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

Finding the best Samsung phone is a tall order — there are so many different options available, all with the same name. It's confusing, to say the least, and finding out what sets each model apart from the others is, at best, annoying.

That's why we've created this guide, so that you can more easily tell which phone is the best for you, highlighting the strengths and weaknesses of each. We've also made a quiz that you can use to help you find the phone that's going to fit your unique use case.

All you need to do is answer the questions, and you'll be recommended a device that fits what you want out of your next Samsung phone. It couldn't be easier. So let's get started — which is the best Samsung phone for you?

Want to learn more about the current Samsung lineup? Here's how we rank them after thoroughly testing each model.

The S26 Ultra pushes the boundaries of software with its new AI powers. Samsung has added predictive tools like "Now Brief" and automated app actions that can handle complex background tasks, making it a much more capable productivity tool.

In the photography department, the S26 Ultra remains far more advanced than any other Galaxy phone, with its 200MP main camera and 50MP telephoto lens, which have both been upgraded with wider apertures for superior low-light performance. Samsung has also introduced Horizon Lock for video, providing a level of professional-grade stabilization that the cameras on older and cheaper models cannot match.

While the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra is Samsung's thinnest phone, the Galaxy S26 Ultra still managed to be thinner and lighter than its predecessor without sacrificing its 6.9-inch screen size.

Regarding battery performance, the S26 Ultra’s 16 hours and 10 minutes of life easily outclasses Samsung’s foldables like the Z Flip 7 (12:24) and Z Fold 7 (10:55). It also powers up faster thanks to newly added 60W wired charging support.

Ultimately, the S26 Ultra remains a steep $1,299 investment, especially compared to the $899 Galaxy S26. But the Ultra's combination of top-tier zoom, innovative security features, and AI-driven efficiency makes it the definitive choice for power users who find other phones simply too limited.

While it's still over six inches in size (what would have been called a "phablet" not too long ago), and technically a foldable would be smaller in your pocket, the Galaxy S26 is as compact as you can get with a modern Samsung phone. And fortunately, as a flagship Galaxy S model, it's got everything you could want it to have despite its size.

The new 6.3-inch AMOLED display is significantly brighter than Samsung's older models, reaching up to 1,950 nits, which makes it far more legible in direct sunlight than its predecessors or main competitors like the iPhone 17. This makes it an ideal choice for users who prioritize media consumption and outdoor visibility in a device that still fits comfortably in one hand.

The S26 maintains an easy-to-carry form factor while still housing the same powerful Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset found in the more expensive Ultra model (at least in the U.S.). This ensures that average users get top-tier speed and gaming capabilities without the physical or financial burden of the largest devices.

The introduction of new and upgraded AI features is a major selling point too. These features allow the phone to become more aware of user habits and offer sophisticated photo editing capabilities—such as generating new elements or removing reflections—that were previously limited to more niche devices. For the everyday user, these AI tools simplify complex tasks like photo editing and device management, making the smartphone experience more intuitive and efficient.

Finally, while the S26 saw a price increase to $899, it remains the most practical choice due to its high-end software experience and innovative camera features like "Horizon Lock." This video stabilization tool keeps footage perfectly level even during significant rotation, a feature the reviewer noted as being far more useful for the average person than high-resolution 8K recording. By combining professional-grade internals with user-friendly software and a manageable size, the Galaxy S26 secures its spot as the most well-rounded option in Samsung’s 2026 line-up.

The Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra is, currently the most expensive phone you can buy from Samsung. Apart from the ill-fated Trifold. What do you get for a $2,099 purchase? The best folding phone that you can buy right now, as you might expect. Samsung has stepped up its game with this model, after other manufacturers started to catch up and land higher places in best lists.

The screen on the inside is the most noticeable improvement. For one, it’s incredibly bright and clear. This is one of the brightest AMOLED screens that Samsung has packed into a foldable yet, and it looks phenomenal. Movies are immersive, and games look epic. The crease is almost gone now too — you can still feel it with your finger, but it’s all but entirely invisible this time around.

Going back to games quickly, the Z Fold 8 Ultra is a powerhouse. We threw some very graphically intensive games at the Fold 8 Ultra, and they all ran incredibly smoothly. Gamers will love it. Productivity heads will get a kick out of it too, as they pan around and multitask without any kind of slowdown at all. This is a very powerful handset.

We only wish that the cameras were better, especially considering how much the phone costs. The main camera is fine, and the selfie camera takes a decent image, but the telephoto camera is straight-up bad. It might as well not be there at all — everything looks blurry and awful. If you want the best camera around, you’re better off with the non-folding Galaxy S26 Ultra.

Battery life is good, with 14 and a half hours of staying power. That’s excellent, although not quite as good as the Motorola Razr Fold.

And we need to talk about that price. On a phone contract, it might not be something you think about initially, but it will add up month by month. $2,099 is a lot of money for a phone, whether it's excellent or not.

“Big screen” and “easily pocketable” aren’t usually things that you’d assume could be a part of the same conversation. And yet, thanks to a very clever folding screen and square shape, the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 is just that. A pocketable phone with a massive screen inside. It’s about the same size and shape as a passport when it's folded.

There’s not much missing here, beyond perhaps a telephoto lens. But given how bad the telephoto lens on the more expensive Fold 8 Ultra is, it's perhaps no great loss. We’d also like it if more apps were suited to the larger screen inside, but that’s not really the fault of the phone itself. Just app developers. Still, worth thinking about if you’re going to buy one.

Everything else is great. The large display inside is super bright and clear, and the crease in the middle has been handled with some very cool new screen tech to make it almost invisible.

The outer screen is similarly bright and clear, and easy to use with one hand thanks to that squarer shape. The whole thing is mind-blowingly thin when unfolded, as well. We really shouldn’t be wowed by stuff like this anymore, but it's hard not to be impressed by a knife-like phone in the hand.

It all runs extremely smoothly as well, with the Elite chip inside handling multitasking and heavy games with ease. Gamers will love that extra screen real estate too. Productivity folks will find a lot to like too, as they hop quickly from app to app on the same screen. Very cool.

Cameras are fine, although by no means class-leading. If you’re looking for a camera to take loads of snaps, you’ll want one of the non-folding models. If you just want to take the occasional picture, you’ll be fine, mind you. Battery life is good too, with more than an extra hour of battery over the Fold 7. It’s likely that most users will be charging by the time they go to bed every evening.

The Fold 8 is a great phone, and this wider form factor is a master stroke that sets it apart from the larger and more expensive Fold 8 Ultra. It’s still expensive, but it hasn’t broken the $2,000 mark yet. That honor is for the flagship.

The Samsung Galaxy Z Flip 8 is a very similar phone to the 7th generation that came before it. In essence, not a necessary upgrade if you’ve got the old one. But. If you’re looking for a good flip phone to buy right now, and you’re upgrading a 3-year-old phone that’s coming up to the end of its device plan, then there’s little better right now than the Z Flip 8.

The screens this time around are brighter than ever, making sure that you can see them no matter the outside lighting conditions. Bright sun? No problem for either display in the Flip 8. There’s also some fancy new tech in the folding display that makes the crease inside far less noticeable. Like, it’s there, but it’s far less noticeable than before.

Battery life is fine, although nothing to write home about. During our lab testing, we found that the phone lasted for 12 hours and 50 minutes — that’s pretty good for a folding phone, but not that much more than the previous model. Expect to be plugging in every night, regardless.

It's smooth and slick to use thanks to a very powerful chip inside. We threw a range of different games at the phone during testing, and that larger interior display looked great the whole time. The phone had little problem running everything at high frame rates. Just don’t expect to use the outer screen. You can even use the Flip 8 as a computer with DeX, if you want. You should probably stick to your laptop.

This is an expensive phone, mind you. It comes in at around $1,099, which is more than other phones that have a similarly sized screen when it's open. For those with small pockets who sometimes want a larger screen, though, you’ll love the Flip 8.

The Samsung Galaxy A57 stands out as Samsung’s best value phone this year, delivering an expansive 6.7-inch display at a midrange price that’s especially compelling for buyers who want big-screen utility without overspending. The screen size brings benefits for streaming and multitasking, and the price means you don’t have to compromise on core features.

Battery life is a clear highlight, with the A57 using a battery as large as the S26 Ultra. Long-term value is another strength. Samsung guarantees six years of security updates and four years of major Android upgrades, so buyers can expect their investment to stay secure and current well past the average upgrade cycle. It's not as good as the seven-year offer you get with a flagship Samsung, but still plenty of time.

Compared to Samsung’s upmarket S26 Ultra and S26, the A57 lacks some luxuries. It doesn’t come with the S26 Ultra’s brighter display, ultra-fast performance, or advanced AI camera features. For hundreds less, you still get a roomy screen and reliable everyday camera performance — essentials most users genuinely need.

The Galaxy A57 is our pick for buyers who want a long-lasting phone with a big screen and great battery life but don’t need the latest in AI tricks or flagship camera prowess. If you want a future-proof everyday workhorse at a sensible price, this is the Samsung to get. Those chasing cutting-edge AI or the brightest, fastest display may want to look at the S26 Ultra instead.

In previous years, the cheaper FE edition of the flagship would easily outperform the A-edition. But this time around, the S26 FE isn't quite a step up from the S25 FE, and the A57 is a significant improvement on the A56. The S26 FE is still almost twice the price of the A57, but the gap between them is shrinking.

One thing the S26 FE does have is a lot of AI features. Some are good, most are novelties, but if you need a more affordable entry-point to Samsung's tools, this is the phone to go for. Not that it always feels it (and I mean that literally). The materials in the 2026 model don't have the same premium quality.

Then again, it doesn't feel like a cheap phone, which often are quite plastic-y in your hand. Whether this is an appropriate compromise when this isn't actually a cheap-cheap phone depends on how far your budget will stretch to get access to Samsung's latest features, when the A57 isn't far behind, performance-wise.

At the time of writing, we've not been able to perform our full battery tests, so can't accurately compare it against the other models. But it seems about in line with past FE performance; good, not great. Enough to see you through a day, but you'll still need a charger handy by the evening or a few hours of heavy use.

If I seem a little down on the Galaxy S26 FE, I'm not. It's a very good phone with a lot of what we liked about the mainline S26 at a cheaper price. But the A57 isn't a whole lot different and is quite a bit cheaper, so I have a hard time recommending this phone when there's a more bank-balance-friendly option available.

Picking which Samsung phone to buy is primarily driven by budget considerations. You won't want to look at the latest Galaxy S Ultra or Z Fold if you're not prepared to pay for it. But other factors need to be taken into consideration as well.

Focusing on battery life will mean ruling out Samsung's foldables. Most other phones on this list have strong longevity on a single charge, but unfortunately, you'll probably have to pay more to get the best available battery life.

For photography, the best will normally be the latest Galaxy S Ultra model. That goes for productivity too, due to Samsung bundling the S Pen in with its Ultra models, unless you'd prefer the extra screen space and multitasking options that a Galaxy Z Fold can provide. And if you're a gamer, any Galaxy S phone will offer the best possible performance — it's just up to you to decide how much you want to pay for other features on top of that.

For a smartphone to make our best phone list, it needs to excel on several tests that we run on every handset. We perform some of these tests in our labs and some in the real world. See our detailed explanation on how Tom's Guide tests and reviews smartphones for more information.

When it comes to performance, we rely on such synthetic benchmarks as Geekbench 6 and 3DMark to measure graphics performance. These tests allow us to compare performance across iPhones and Android devices.

We also run a real-world video transcoding test on each phone using the Adobe Premiere Rush app and time the result. (We unfortunately have to skip this test on some phones due to app compatibility issues, but we attempt this benchmark with each device we get in to review.)

To measure the quality of a phone's display, we perform lab tests to determine the brightness of the panel (in nits), as well as how colorful each screen is (DCI-P3 color gamut). In these cases, higher numbers are better. We also measure the color accuracy of each panel with a Delta-E rating, where lower numbers are better, and a score of 0 is perfect.

One of the most important tests we run is the Tom's Guide battery test. We run a web surfing test over 5G (or 4G if the phone doesn't have 5G support) at 150 nits of screen brightness until the battery gives out. In general, a phone that lasts 10 hours or more is good, and anything above 11 hours makes our list of the best phone battery life.

Last but not least, we take the best phones out in the field to take photos outdoors, indoors, and at night in low light to see how they perform versus their closest competitors.
