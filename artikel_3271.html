David Ellison Goes Personal: Raised By Single Mom, Bonded With Dad As Teen, Massive VHS Collection

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

“I was raised by a single mother, by my mom. My parents got divorced when I was 3, and I really started building my relationship with my father when I was 13 years old through aviation,” said David Ellison, getting a bit more personal than usual at the New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday.

His father is Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison.

“You didn’t have a relationship with your father till you were 13?” asked moderator Andrew Ross Sorkin.

“We saw each other, you know, once a year, every other year. We couldn’t be closer now. I want to be clear. We really built a phenomenal relationship through aviation. We started flying model airplanes” then graduated to real planes.

“Basically, we learned how to fly together, and kind of the weekend ritual was every Saturday, every Sunday, I’d go do an hour of flight training. He’d be on the ground and study. He’d go do an hour of flight training, I’d be on the ground and study.” Watch on Deadline

Ellison said he traveled to air shows across the country, including Oshkosh [Air Show] – “which, for those who don’t know, is kind of like the Super Bowl of aviation events … It was an incredible thing to get to do in early life.”

He was aware of his tech lord father’s status but “my parents definitely made a concerted effort to make sure that we had a really grounded and normal childhood.”

The younger Ellison fell in love with movies and storytelling as a kid going to the cinema every single weekend.

“By the way, my father loves film as well. But you know, it was really something the three of us kind of all loved growing up,” he said, referring to his mother and his sister Megan Ellison, founder and CEO of Annapurna Pictures.

“Literally, we had, I still have it, a VHS collection that was like 3,000 [tapes]. We used to joke it was like Blockbuster at home. And Sunday was the one day, when you were a kid, when you can kind of do whatever you wanted. And me and Megan would be like, let’s watch all five Rocky movies, or, you know, let’s watch the Star Wars trilogy.”

David Ellison founded Skydance in 2006, grew it substantially, then acquired Paramount in August 2025. He said he and former Paramount owner Shari Redstone “used to have a standing breakfast where I would ask her if she would ever be open to selling the company. For several years, the answer was always no. And one day the answer was yes.”

Then came the Warner Bros Discovery deal, which closed last month.

Sorkin pressed him on the hypothetical “dinner table conversation” where he told Larry Ellison he wanted to buy the company and needed the financial backing. “How did you convince him?”

“Look, one of the things I think people have to understand … is my father is an incredible partner … But he demands an incredible performance, an incredible result [from] this investment,” Ellison said.

So, “When you’re doing this, and he’s got to put in $40 billion, is he giving you a hard time if something’s not right?” asked Sorkin.

“The answer is, of course,”Ellison responded. “I mean, come on.”

“We talked throughout the deal process, literally every day. We probably talk on average four days a week, and we talk about strategy. We talk about where we want to creatively take the company. Yesterday, we were talking about artificial intelligence and how that’s going to impact our business.”

Inevitably, Ellison was asked about Digger, the loss-making Tom Cruise-starring Warner Bros film released this fall.

“I’ve made eight movies with Tom. I think he’s one of the seminal artists of our time. We’re literally working on a sequel to Days of Thunder right now, and then we want to try to roll right into [the next] Top Gun after that,” he said.

“I spent the last 16 years of my life on the filmmaking side of this business. I have lived on set. I’ve slept on Hans Zimmer’s couch. I’ve been in editing rooms for 20 hours nonstop. I do understand the creative process of this business. And the goal has to be, in any film that you make, is how do you reach the broadest possible audience for that particular story. And there is no one-size-fits-all approach. We will make franchise movies. We will make original stories. We will make genre pictures. We will tell stories aimed at audiences from every single walk of life. What is important is that you basically size the budget to the potential addressable audience.”

He said Skydance co-film head Dana Goldberg is someone who can absolutely say no to him.

“Do you think she has better judgment than you do when it comes to relighting bills?” asked Sorkin.

“She has been my partner at Skydance for 16 years, and … this is a team sport,” Ellison said.

Back to family, Sorkin noted that the Skydance portfolio includes Succession, Yellowstone, The Godfather and Billions, asking Ellison if his family was closest to the Roys, the Duttons, the Corleones or the Axelrods.

“Is none of the above an option?” asked the Skydance chief.

“You could try to pick one,” urged Sorkin.

“There’s nothing more important to me. My kids, my wife and I. We’ve been married for 15 years. We’ve been together for 20 years. And you know family cannot be more important to me. And apologies if [it’s] a little boring because I pretty much work and I hang out with my family, my kids, and those are the things that really matter to me.”
