Breakfast News: A Boulder Amid a Rocky Market

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Berkshire Hathaway announces big buyback, a full week of Fool rec earnings, and more…

Berkshire Hathaway’s (BRKB -0.46%) latest quarterly updates highlight a strategic pivot under CEO Greg Abel. The conglomerate is more than ready to aggressively deploy capital even when headline profits soften. Berkshire bought back $9.6 billion of its own stock in the third quarter, more than double from Q2. It lifts 2026 buybacks under Greg Abel to about $14.4 billion. The stock, recommended in Stock Advisor by Team Hidden Gems, gained 0.5% in pre-market trading.

Despite cyclical headwinds in insurance, Berkshire's diversified business keeps throwing off massive free cash flows, and that’s one reason the recent sell-off has barely touched the stock. This company itself is a diversified portfolio packed into a single stock.

Thursday’s 5.5% drop in the Nasdaq Composite was hard on every investor. But if you’re retired or a few years away, a day like that can hit differently. And the money in your account is your future paycheck.

That worry makes sense. As TMF Financial Planning Expert Stephanie Marini puts it, “After decades of watching the balance grow, spending from it can feel like failure.” In her words, “Selling for that purpose is the plan working.”

If stocks fall early on and you sell shares to pay your bills, you sell them at low prices. Those shares aren’t around when the market recovers. Financial planners call this sequence-of-returns risk. So the aim is to avoid being forced to sell stocks at a bad time.

One common approach is to keep a year or two of planned withdrawals in cash or short-term bonds. When stocks fall, you live off that cushion instead of selling. When they recover, you refill it.

Everyone’s situation is different, and this isn’t personal advice. Decide before the next drop, so your plan does the work instead of your nerves.

Stocks ended lower last week, as heavy selling hit the tech sector. The S&P 500 fell 2.68%, and the Nasdaq dropped 3.37%.

Futures point to a higher open. Both S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures rose roughly 0.50% in pre-market trading.

Sticky inflation readings this week could give the tech pullback more room to run. Investors should stay anchored with their long-term goals and not panic.

A number of stocks recommended across Motley Fool services report earnings this week, from rockets and video games to sneakers and networking gear. Stocks are coming off a rough week. Management views on the broader markets and the company’s prospects may move shares as much as the results do.

Team Hidden Gems has a 5-year price prediction of $1,184.29 for Deere & Company (DE +2.49%). That’s an annualized return of 12.1%, plus a 1% dividend yield. Deere’s shares barely budged last Thursday, even as fears of slower artificial intelligence spending gave the S&P 500 its worst day since April 2025. We first recommended Deere in August 2024 at $370.35, during a downturn in farm spending, and shares are up about 80% since. All seven of our Team Hidden Gems recs sit in the green, so our target reflects how far Deere has already climbed.

Market routs like last week’s show why we pair high-flying tech with steady category leaders, and we plan to stick with Deere for years to come.
