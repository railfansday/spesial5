Through 10 weeks, the AFC playoff field is basically set

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The 2021 expansion of the regular season to 17 games has given the bad teams more time to separate from the good teams. Which makes it easier for the eventual playoff teams to declare themselves.

This year, the seven AFC playoff teams have essentially declared themselves after only 10 weeks of games.

The most important developments in that regard were these: (1) the Browns beating the Texans; (2) the Ravens losing to the Chargers; and (3) the Patriots losing badly to the Lions — and losing quarterback Drake Maye, possibly for the rest of the season.

Already, we know that the Bills, Jaguars, Bengals, Broncos, and Chiefs are essentially locks to win tickets to the postseason party. The Steelers, despite being outclassed by the Bengals, have enough winnable games remaining to land a wild-card berth.

The only spot up for grabs is the seven seed. And the Browns are now in the driver’s seat.

Already, four AFC teams are dead in the water: the 2-8 Jets, the 1-8 Dolphins, the 1-8 Titans, and the 1-8 Chargers. The 3-7 Colts barely have a pulse, and it won’t last long. That’s five of 16 teams, with a fork already stuck in a spot where forks aren’t supposed to go. And the 3-6 Texans aren’t far behind.

The remaining contenders for the last spot in the playoff tree are the 6-4 Browns, the 5-5 Patriots, the 4-6 Raiders, and the 4-6 Ravens. New England could be in the process of imploding. The Raiders are 1-6 over their last seven games, and they’re now starting a rookie quarterback. The Ravens have lost five in a row.

As a result, there’s currently not much drama as to who the seven AFC playoff teams will be.

The good news is that the top of the seven-team hierarchy remains undecided — and it could stay that way for a while. The Bills, Jaguars, and Bengals stand apart from the rest. The Broncos and Chiefs remain in the mix. (The Steelers seem to be destined for another one-and-done postseason appearance, with a road game looming to Buffalo, Jacksonville, Cincinnati, or Denver/Kansas City.)

The bad news, and the inescapable reality, is that the have-nots have become crystal clear. With eight weeks of the season left.

For a league that loves parity, there isn’t much of it when it comes to the line separating the playoff teams from the wait-til-next-year franchises.
