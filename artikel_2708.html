Maryland men’s basketball overwhelms Alcorn State in 117-57 beatdown

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Through three games, it appeared Maryland men's basketball had a 3-point problem. The Terps had shot 21.5% from beyond the arc, the 357th-best mark in Division I.

Against Alcorn State on Monday, the Terps eased those fears, draining 11 first-half 3-pointers — including seven in a row — en route to a 117-57 blowout of the Braves. Maryland's 117 points were the most in a game since at least 2002.

Against Virginia, it took until the first-half clock expired for Maryland's second 3-pointer to land. Against Alcorn State, it took 68 seconds.

Mike McNair Jr., restored to the starting lineup, opened the game with a make. Freshman Austin Brown, starting in his first game of the season, splashed another on the Terps' second offensive possession.

Both Brown and McNair were recalled into the starting lineup in favor of Andre Mills, who had struggled in the Terps' two losses. The guard's 6-of-28 slump visibly impacted his body language and confidence on the court.

But even Mills got in on Monday's fun. Just under eight minutes into the game, he peeled into the corner late in the shot clock, caught a pass from Baba Oladotun and heaved up a shot. It bounced off the front end of the rim and up, floating down through the net to break a streak of 10 consecutive misses from beyond the arc.

The redshirt sophomore made another 3-pointer on the Terps' next possession, too. He finished the game with 15 points on 4-of-6 shooting and three assists.

From the opening tip, head coach Buzz Williams took the opportunity to rotate his team, presenting a variety of combinations. That left the Terps out of position defensively, which Alcorn State used to its advantage by drawing fouls.

While the Braves went just 3-of-15 from the field to open the game, 7-of-8 free throw shooting kept the game within seven points just before the midpoint of the first half.

From there, though, the Terps exhausted their opponents. Alcorn State switched from man to zone defense, and Maryland capitalized, firing the ball around the perimeter until someone got open. That left DJ Wagner, who had 14 points, wide open at the top of the key to drain Maryland's seventh successive 3-point attempt.

The Terps finished the first half with 60 points, their most in an opening frame since 2000.

Maryland's deep shooting slowed after the break, but the scoring continued thanks to steady interior pressure. Pharrel Payne finished with 12 points against an Alcorn State frontcourt that logged 12 fouls trying to contain him and the rest of the Terps.

However, fouls were a frequent occurrence for both teams. The Braves finished the game with 29, but the Terps' 23 infractions were not far behind.

Kaden House's turnaround fadeaway near the free-throw line marked Maryland's 98th point, setting a new record for its highest total under Williams. Mills made it 100 on the fast break, collecting an outlet pass and rising high above the defender for an easy layup.

In a late cameo, Guillermo Del Pino finished with 11 points and four rebounds in eight minutes. His late snipe also made it 15 3-pointers on the evening for the Terps, which tied for the third-most in a game in Maryland history..

1. Sharing the scoring. Maryland's 117 points came despite no one player scoring more than 16 points. Six Terps finished in double figures on the day, and the only Terp to play and not score were Maban Jabriel and Alex K'Medehouto, who both entered the game late on.

2. Quick on the switches. Across the entire game, Maryland made 49 substitutions. Twice, Williams opted for a full hockey line change, bringing five fresh faces back into the fold.

3. Faulty on free throws. While the Terps earned several opportunities from the charity stripe, they made just 67% of them as a team against Alcorn State. Some players have shown a greater cause for concern this year than others: this year, Oladotun is 1-of-7, and Wagner is 4-of-8.
