Genoa's Jaxon Magnone consummate target at tight end

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Jaxon Magnone is like an African safari rolled into one football player.

Initially, Magnone was like a wobbly baby giraffe for Genoa. All height; very limited coordination.

He finished his career as a rhinoceros at defensive end. In between, he matured to 6-foot-4 and 215 pounds. He learned to leverage and block like a gorilla might. He's strong and fast like the cats. He can be graceful and agile, like a gazelle at tight end, with the vertical jump to overcome crocodiles in any crowd.

He didn't play varsity as a giraffe, err, 14-year old freshman linebacker. He was a cornerback as a sophomore and safety as a junior. He played defensive end and safety in the same game as a senior. As a freshman, Magnone was basically too tall, too soon. He grew only about another inch the next three years.

"He just needed that one year to grow into his body and understand how to use his body to dominate," Comets coach Bill Fisher said. "He worked on the right things; footwork, coordination. You see the results."

He was twice recognized offensive player of the year in the Northern Buckeye Conference. He was first-team on defense both seasons, at different positions. He was first-team on offense as a sophomore.

"Even in pictures, how little I looked," he said. "I'm the same height, but I'm surprised how far I came physically. I was a big baseball kid. As a freshman, I thought I'd just be a receiver, a tight end in our offense. But a skinny kid, running decent routes. Block when needed."

Magnone embraced the transitions and was inquisitive while buying in. He enjoyed the opportunity afforded by multiple position groups to get to know more teammates. He wanted to be coached: "What do I do in this scenario?"

"A talent like that, you want him in the best position to help the team," Fisher said. "He has the size and ability to play anywhere. That was just a thing that was. But to want to be good and play at another level — he initiated coaching points. That was a breath of fresh air."

Magnone wasn't running from any blocks prior, but defensive end was a physical adjustment. He had to learn to move and strike with his hands, his head and his shoulders. It made him a better tight end.

Fisher believes players are supposed to make plays. That makes him difficult to impress. He remembers three of Magnone's receptions, even though none went for a score. He split defenders and tracked a long pass against Triway in the postseason as a junior.

He had a one-handed snare and concentrated for another hands-only catch in heavy traffic against Eastwood.

Magnone and Genoa quarterback Myles Mollenhauer have been friends since before they started playing football. Their bond was forged on the playground in elementary school. Then, it was mutually honed for football specifics.

"I couldn't ask for a better guy to throw me the ball," Magnone said. "The love and trust we have. 'It's a slant.' With a look, that's what we were running and what we were throwing. That was the trust built over the years."

Magnone also had an impact when he didn't touch the ball. He occasionally played wildcat quarterback or tailback in I-formation. He could line up out wide as a receiver. He was used off the line for jet sweeps and motion. As a decoy. He liked to think he was a threat to a defense because his teammates were threats. And vice-versa.

"Alex Materni, Tommy Braun, Hunter Bickel, Gavin Grigson," he said. "We have such great players. It allows me to do what I do. I wouldn't be who I am or the player I am without my family, coaches and friends."

Magnone continues his career at Valparaiso, a Division I FCS program.

"They believe in me as a player, as a person, academics," he said. "I fell in love with the atmosphere and the faith they have in me to come in and play. I'm excited to go to the next level."

Magnone established Genoa records for touchdown receptions in a career (28), receiving yards in a career (3,077), receiving yards in a season (1,030), receptions in a career (189) and catches in a season (72). He was third-team all-state as a junior and second-team as a sophomore.

"He can make any kind of catch from any kind of body position," Fisher said. "He's the consummate target. He's the real deal in the game of football."

You don't spot many players like Magnone in the high school habitat. He's rare to see.
