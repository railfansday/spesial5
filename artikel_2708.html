Nations Championship: Fixtures and standings

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

France ended South Africa's 100% record in the Nations Championship with a sensational 21-19 victory over the world champions at the Stade de France on Friday.

Les Bleus have now won four of their five pool matches and extended their lead in the northern hemisphere standings to six points with one game to play.

South Africa remain top of the standings in the southern hemisphere but will be overtaken by New Zealand if the All Blacks beat Wales in Cardiff on Saturday.

England, who face Japan at Allianz Stadium on Saturday, are in second place on points difference in the northern pool after an emphatic 45-22 victory over Australia last weekend.

Ireland secured a 42-12 win over Argentina and are third, with Fiji the visitors in Dublin on Saturday.

Scotland are in fourth place with two wins and two defeats and face Australia at Murrayfield on Sunday.

Wales overcame Japan 28-21 in their first home match of the championship, but remain in fifth place.

France will face Argentina in the last round of pool matches on 21 November, while England will play New Zealand and Ireland host South Africa.

The finals weekend takes place from 27-29 November, with all six matches at Allianz Stadium.

The Nations Championship pits the northern hemisphere teams who compete in the Six Nations - England, Ireland, Scotland, Wales, France and Italy - against southern hemisphere giants South Africa, New Zealand, Australia and Argentina, plus invited sides Fiji and Japan.

All teams will play each of the six in the opposing hemisphere once, with three rounds of fixtures staged in July and another three in November.

Their results will rank the teams within their own hemisphere, from one to six.

On the final weekend in November, there is a three-day play-off event staged at Allianz Stadium in Twickenham. The sixth-placed team in the northern hemisphere plays off against the equivalent in the southern hemisphere standings and so on, culminating in the two top-ranked sides taking each other on.

The winner of that final match is crowned the inaugural Nations Championship winner.

There is also a parallel, Ryder-Cup style hemisphere title. The winner of each of the matches on the play-offs weekend will earn one point for their hemisphere, except for the contest between the two top-ranked teams which delivers two.

The first hemisphere to earn four points over the weekend will be crowned winners.
