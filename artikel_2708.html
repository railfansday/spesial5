Fantasy Football Waiver Wire Week 12: Chris Rodriguez and Fernando Mendoza step up

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Week 11 is nearly in the books and we don't have much time to make moves leading into Thanksgiving week. These are the biggest waiver wire adds as we continue to push toward the fantasy football postseason.

Week 11 Stock Up, Stock Down: Can you still trust your Steelers?

Rivers McCown highlights four fantasy players whose fantasy football stock rose in Week 11 of the NFL season and four whose stock fell.

Mendoza is the only breakout bet at quarterback you'll find on waivers now that Cam Ward has crossed the 50 percent threshold. Mendoza had an up-and-down Week 11 with three touchdowns but five sacks and a pick-six. The arm looks lively, but the game is still too fast for him too often. Mendoza faced top-tier defenses in the Seahawks and Broncos in his first two starts. The Browns have also been a good unit this year, but they're not quite on the level of Mendoza's previous opponents. With a slightly easier matchup on tap, Mendoza is finally in the streaming mix.

Watson is the QB20 by points per game and is riding a three-game streak of throwing for at least 300 yards. Watson has also been extremely effective as a runner. He's second among all quarterbacks in attempts (59) and fourth in yards (266). Watson gets a date with a terrible Raiders defense in Week 12. He's a solid, high-floor streaming option this week.

Smith isn't the most exciting option, but he's the highest-scoring quarterback this year who is also available in over half of Yahoo leagues. He's 16th in EPA per play and fifth in CPOE. Now he gets a weak Miami defense that ranks 25th in EPA per dropback allowed.

Liam Coen's "grind Bhayshul Tuten to dust" strategy has in fact reduced the Jags' lead back to a fine, flour-like consistency. He appeared to get hurt on his first carry of the game, played through it, and got hurt again later in the contest. The Jags have needed to give Tuten a game off for a while and a date with the Titans is just the spot. Even if they don't make Tuten inactive, expect more C-Rod as they try to protect him for the postseason. If he does miss Week 11, Rodriguez will be a high-end RB2.

In two games with Jacoby Brissett under center, Hill has a dozen targets for nine catches and 61 receiving yards. Even his rushing output is up with a 6-69-1 line on the ground last week. The part is likely a mirage, but his 14 percent target share in two Brissett starts is here to stay. He's on the RB3/4 border headed into Week 12.

Johnson is a similar bet, though with a much shorter track record. He popped up with seven catches for 55 yards on eight targets in Week 11. It was his first game of the season with more than four targets. I would normally write that off as a one-game blip, but the Bills take on the Chiefs in a game with a total north of 50 this week. There's nothing your family wants to see on Thanksgiving more than you tilting every non-check-down from Josh Allen.

Washington has settled into a change-of-pace role, seeing 17 carries with just one target over the past two weeks. It looks like he missed his shot at forcing a meaningful committee, but there's still enough work for him to see almost 10 touches a game. He'll be in the RB4 mix this week.

Saquon Barkley left Week 11 with an arm injury in the fourth quarter. Barkley told reporters after the game that he's looking forward to rehabbing the injury ahead of Thursday's game against the Cowboys. It sounds like he'll play, but we'll know more after Philly's first two injury reports. Shipley would be an RB2 if Barkley can't go. In a potential shootout with the Cowboys, he'd still be a desperation FLEX option if Barkley does play.

Hurst broke out with two touchdowns on a team-high seven targets in Week 11. He now leads the team's trio of starting receivers in yards per route run, albeit at a measly 1.1. Hurst has been playing a sacrificial X role this year, but it might be time for him to take on a more traditional WR1 job. It's just not happening with Emeka Egbuka or Chris Godwin, and the Bucs have tried to get both going all year. Hurst is a risky play for Week 12, but one that could pay off beyond just a single start.

Dike was the next man up with Wan'Dale Robinson on IR. He ran a route on 75 percent of Cam Ward's dropbacks and led the Titans with seven targets, six of which he brought down for 69 yards. Dike is second on the team in yards per route run and second among the team's wideouts in YAC per catch. He's earned a shot at the WR2 gig and the Titans are giving him that chance now. That might only be good for WR4 fantasy production going forward, but this is as good as it gets on waivers this week.

Bech's role will likely be vaporized by the return of Brock Bowers this week, but it's a good gig while it lasts. Bowers got banged up early in last week's game and Bech earned a 24 percent target share with 34 percent of the Raiders' air yards. Bowers missed Week 11 entirely and Bech stepped up with a team-high eight targets. He caught all of them for 88 yards and a touchdown.

Vele popped up with his best yardage total of the year in Week 11. He caught all seven of his targets for 76 yards. The Saints ran less 11-personnel on Sunday and subbed in other WR3s more often, cratering Jordyn Tyson's route share. With Tyson on the field less, Vele's role became far more valuable. I don't expect this to happen every week, but it's proof of concept that Vele still has some WR4 juice.

Hurleman was a practice squad call-up for the Steelers this week and he did not disappoint with six catches for 61 yards and a score. He finished second on the team in targets with seven and led the way in all receiving categories. Hurleman is also an Aaron Rodgers favorite, with the grizzled veteran asking for him to see more work (on special teams) ahead of Week 11. The bad news is that Michael Pittman Jr. should be back for Week 12, so Hurleman's role is likely a one-week thing. He's only worth a stash in the deepest of leagues.

Don't chase his two-touchdown performance. Shepard ran 13 routes and was targeted twice, but both went to the end zone. Shepard stepped in as the Jets' WR3 with both Omar Cooper Jr. and Isaiah Williams out. He will hold that role until at least one of the two is healthy, but I don't expect any more fireworks from Shepard, even as the de facto WR3.

Freiermuth is the TE1 among players rostered in less than half of Yahoo leagues. He has at least 30 yards in three straight games and has quietly taken on tush push duties for the Steelers this year. Freiermuth gets a great matchup with the Broncos this week. Only two teams have allowed more tight end targets than Denver.

The Rams ditched the TE-heavy offense in Week 10, opting to replace their tight end with a running back on the majority of their passing plays. That limited Higbee to a measly 32 percent route rate, but he managed a serviceable 15 percent target share anyway. Higbee turned that role into three catches for 17 yards and a touchdown. With Davante Adams unlikely to play in Week 12, Higbee will be a boom/bust TE2 for the Rams' game against the Packers.

Engram led the Broncos with eight targets in Week 11. His six catches and 74 yards also led the squad. Engram hasn't been all that productive this year, but it at least felt notable that his best game came directly after the team's bye. He'll hold a spot in the TE2 ranks for one more week.
