Fernando Mendoza gave Raiders a glimpse of their future with ownership after loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Alex Singleton had spent Sunday afternoon trying to make Fernando Mendoza's first NFL road start as miserable as possible. By the time it was over, the Broncos linebacker sounded like a man who understood Denver had better enjoy days like this against the Raiders quarterback while it could.

"He's a first-round pick, first overall for a reason," Singleton said. "He's going to be a good player."

And that was after the Broncos tagged Mendoza for five sacks. Imagine what Singleton might say someday when the kid has time to throw.

Las Vegas Raiders quarterback Fernando Mendoza (15) throws against the Denver Broncos in Denver, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. AP Photo/Jack Dempsey

Mendoza earned the compliment. Playing without Brock Bowers, his best receiver, in a stadium loud enough to cost the Raiders an early timeout, he threw three touchdown passes.

Patrick Mahomes is the only other visiting quarterback to do that in Denver this season. Matthew Stafford managed two. Sam Darnold and Trevor Lawrence left town without one between them.

Mendoza also joined Lawrence and Joe Burrow as the only quarterbacks drafted first overall to throw for at least 250 yards and three touchdowns in a game within their first two career starts.

Somewhere down the line, maybe Mendoza will appreciate the company he kept Sunday. In Denver, he seemed more interested in a cornerback named Ja'Quan McMillian.

And that might be the best news the Raiders brought home.

"Don't get me wrong, although I believe it was an improvement, there are a lot of things I need to improve on for next week," Mendoza said.

Denver Broncos' Alex Singleton (49) sacks Las Vegas Raiders' Fernando Mendoza (15) during a game in Denver, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. Getty Images

Take the throw that McMillian returned for a touchdown. If you want to understand the distance Mendoza traveled in a week and how much ground he still has to cover, listen to him explain what happened in the instant before he let that ball go.

"I saw him take a shuffle inside – thought he was walling another slant – and I let it go with anticipation, and he made a fantastic play," Mendoza said.

A week earlier against Seattle, Mendoza was trying to see the entire field. In Denver, he picked up a cornerback shuffling inside, understood what that movement usually meant for a different receiver, and threw to the spot he expected to come open. McMillian got a hand on the ball. All that processing, and the guy who wound up in the end zone played for Denver.

The easy thing would have been to tip his cap and leave it there. Mendoza was considering what a checkdown over the middle might have bought him. A few yards. Another down. A chance for his defense to catch its breath. Modest ambitions until you consider what the alternative cost.

Denver Broncos defensive tackle D.J. Jones (93) sacks Las Vegas Raiders quarterback Fernando Mendoza (15) on Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026, in Denver. AP Photo/David Zalubowski

Raiders coach Klint Kubiak saw the progress in how Mendoza took charge as the afternoon wore on.

"I think that every day he gets a little bit better," Kubiak said. "I think his leadership of the whole operation, I saw that improve."

A day later, and more importantly, after watching the tape, Kubiak was just as impressed.

"Number one is just the environment. That is a very hostile environment, and I thought overall, I thought he did a good job of getting everybody on the same page," Kubiak said. "I thought he used his legs extremely well, extended some plays for his second start in the NFL against two elite defenses. I've seen improvement."

Las Vegas Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak speaks during a news conference after an NFL football game against the Denver Broncos in Denver, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. AP Photo/David Zalubowski

Center Tyler Linderbaum had a good vantage point for that. On a day the Raiders fell behind 17-0, with passes getting knocked down at the line and rushers getting home, the guy behind him sounded like someone who had put in enough work to trust what he knew.

"I thought he was poised," Linderbaum said.

Mendoza felt it in his feet. He stepped into throws he had drifted away from against Seattle. He felt more certain about when he had to plant and deliver. Broncos coach Sean Payton came away impressed by what happened when Mendoza had to improvise, an element of his game Payton said he hadn't appreciated on film.

Yet the most revealing adjustment Mendoza made Sunday might have come after he took his last hit. Asked how the Raiders handled falling behind, he was talking about how long they had managed to protect the football when he caught himself.

"At the end, we didn't protect the ball. I mean, I didn't protect the ball," Mendoza said.

One pronoun, corrected on the spot. His linemen could answer for the sacks. His receivers could speak for themselves. Mendoza knew whose hand the ball was in when it left for McMillian's.

Singleton thinks he's going to be a good player. The Raiders have to like how clearly their quarterback sees the work ahead.
