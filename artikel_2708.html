Rockets Scurry Over Suns To Another 116-104 Victory

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Another game, another 12pt victory that never felt comfortable, but never seemed much in doubt, either. While the previous opponent, the LA Clippers are not a good team, the Suns are good, or at least decent. They beat the Rockets in Phoenix not that long ago, in a game that has been described as strange, or one where the Rockets seemed asleep.

Because I've been dealing with an household electrical problem (that took out the refrigerator) which, of course, cropped up on Thanksgiving Eve; this might be a shorter recap. (I fixed the problem BTW, Thanksgiving is saved, hooray for me.) It's also a shorter recap because it's hard to pin much of a narrative on the past two Rockets game. They look like easy wins, but weren't. The Rockets didn't play their best, but won. The defense didn't seem great, but was. No one player stood out as the star, or the goat, of the team. The offense didn't look great, but it was above NBA average.

In both of the games, Clippers and Suns, it seemed as though the Rockets knew they were more talented than their opponent, turned on the afterburners for a short time, and then just played keep away.

Here's what I mean – quarter by quarter scoring tonight:

The Rockets scored a lot for the first half, then just kind of added slightly to a lead that spent the time from around the middle of the second quarter onward fluctuating between about 10 to 6 points.

The Suns obviously came out in this game with a plan to play physically with the Rockets. This lead to a whopping 26 free throws in the first half for the Rockets. In troubling news, the Rockets made only 14 of those 26 free throws, or this could have been a substantially more comfortable win. Sengun went 2-6, Thompson 4-9, Jabari 3-5, though Durant was more consistent, at 5-6. Where the good free throw shooting from the earliest part of the season has gone is anyone's guess. It hasn't cost the Rockets as it did in Denver, but neither the Suns nor the Clippers are a strong a team as the Nuggets. If it doesn't improve, though, it might cost them as soon as Saturday, when the Rockets face the 15-4 Philadelphia 76ers.

The Suns changed tack in the second half, apparently, and the Rockets only shot six more free throws in the second half.

The Rockets continue not to have a single fulcrum in terms of scoring or usage, but it's clear the engine remains Alperen Sengun. Sengun is averaging 22pts/11rbs/7ast/1stl/1blk/3TO on 55%FG shooting, 63TS%, 50%3pt on 26% usage in 33mpg. If you asked most NBA fans who was playing better, Sengun or Luka Doncic, they'd all say Doncic. But Doncic is shooting worse overall at 46%, his True Shooting Percentage is lower, despite him taking over 10FT per game, his assists are about 3/4 of an assist higher, than Sengun, his rebounds 4 lower, and his usage is sitting at a staggering 41% compared to Sengun's 26%. The argument can be made that Doncic's gaudy points total is largely driven by usage, while Sengun is the more effective player. Yes, Doncic is a heliocentric player, so perhaps he gets less help (perhaps), but that's by design.

Anyhow, Sengun has been fantastic, those are excellent numbers, and his effect is similarly profound for the Rockets. He remains, despite everything, 24 1/3 years old. He's also making 50% of his threes.

At some point we will have to have a serious conversation about Ime Udoka's truest soulmate, Marcus Smart. He's an intense player, and an incredibly streaky shooter. He also can simply lose his mind in a game, and throw a punch, or beef with a fan, or any of a variety of undesirable things. Tonight he almost single-handedly lead a Phoenix comeback tonight by committing a shooting foul on three consecutive plays before he was thankfully removed. Were they all fouls? Pretty much, yes.

We also got a waning seconds, late game stare down standoff, between Dillon Brooks and Kevin Durant. It looked for an instant like Durant was contemplating throwing a punch. If he was Marcus Smart, he probably would have done it. Instead he settled for an unnecessary dunk with seconds remaining. This marked another good game for Durant with 20pts on 7-15 shooting but a woeful 1-7 from three. Extrapolating from that he was almost perfect with his mid range shot, and it looking exactly as great as 7-8.

The next highest scorer was Amen Thompson at 18 points, and it could have been better if he made more free throws. Amen has returned to form, and he had a lot of space to work with near the rim tonight as the Suns seemed extremely focused on Sengun.

The Rockets looked like they might give this one away in the fourth quarter, but the man the Rockets missed last season, Fred VanVleet did indeed ride to the rescue, steadied the ship, toed the line, and took the wheel. So for a second game things might look a bit better on the scoreboard than the Rockets played. At least their mental fragility didn't wreck them. Dillon Brooks would not have let them hear the end of it.

The Rockets and Suns overall played a pretty feisty, chippy game with a lot of the player chosen with the Rockets pick last year Khaman Maluach trying to molest Alperen Sengun in a basketball sense. This was not successful, but he's a powerful player with a nice close shooting touch who might one day be excellent. Today was not that day.

Tomorrow, though, is Thanksgiving! On behalf of TDS, happy Thanksgiving to all who celebrate, and our best wishes to those who don't.
