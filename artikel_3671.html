Nintendo Fans Are Crashing Out After Amazon Suddenly Delays Tons Of Zelda: Ocarina Of Time Remake Pre-Orders

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

It’s November 5, the day The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake officially launches on Switch 2. Diehard fans had an important decision to make prior to the game’s release. Buy it digitally and start playing last night or wait for the physical copy on launch day. And if they did opt for a physical copy, should they schlep out to a brick-and-mortar store or just order it online? Amazon has some people who opted for the latter now wishing they hadn’t.

If you pre-ordered Ocarina of Time remake through Amazon or another online retailer, you’ve probably spent the last couple of days anxiously checking the shipment info to see when it will be delivered. Many retailers promise launch-day delivery on orders of big games. It might not arrive first thing, but fans can be assured a copy will be waiting for them when they’re done work or school that day.

But last night lots of people who ordered from Amazon were greeted by a delay notification showing their copy wouldn’t be arriving on November 5 after all. Some wouldn’t even be arriving until next week.

The Amazon screw-up is especially notable for two reasons. One, the Bezos founded online retailer has a chippy history with Nintendo. The gaming giant has at times withheld its products from being sold on the platform, with Amazon having to opt for third-party resellers or overseas inventory to stock popular games instead. Former Nintendo of America President Reggie Fils-AimÃ© accused Amazon of once asking him to effectively break the law.

But fans continue to buy through Amazon, in part because the company has made a point of granting discounts on all new physical Switch 2 games. Physical copies now cost $10 more than their digital counterparts, but Amazon still honors the digital price, meaning you can get a physical copy of Ocarina of Time for just $60, whereas places like GameStop sell it for $70. For pre-order copies that come with special foil art covers, it’s very tempting, though perhaps not anymore.

Hell hath no fury like a Nintendo fan scorned. “Jeff Bezos is gona have to pay,” one wrote. He could certainly afford to. Giving every person receiving a late copy their game for free wouldn’t even appear as a rounding error on his net worth.
