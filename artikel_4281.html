Spied! Ford May Finally Be Working on a Two-Door F-150 Lobo Street Truck

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

New spy photos show a lowered regular-cab F-150 with a mix of styling elements from the Lobo and Ford Performance FP700S.

When Ford revealed the new F-150 Lobo, it was billed as a successor to the beloved gas-powered Lightning street trucks of yore. While the new Lobo packs a rumbling V-8 engine and (slightly) lowered ride height like its predecessors, it deviates from the original recipe by only being offered with four doors. However, new spy photos hint at the possibility of Ford building a two-door version of its factory street truck.

The camouflaged prototype that’s pictured in these photos is based on the F-150’s base-level XL trim. How do we know that? Because that’s the only model that’s available with the regular-cab body style. What stands out is that it appears to be outfitted with the square-jawed front bumper that’s otherwise only found on the F-150 Lobo. Interestingly, his test mule also has halogen headlights, but unlike the Lobo, they feature amber daytime running lights instead of white units.

The 22-inch six-spoke wheels also look like the same ones that are bolted to Ford’s full-size street truck. But eagle-eyed readers might also notice that while the 275/50-sized rubber matches the Lobo’s specs, the brand is completely different. Whereas the Lobo wears Bridgestone Alenza A/S 02s, the Michelin Primacy LTX tires seen here are typically reserved for the Ford Performance FP700S. The latter is also powered by a 5.0-liter V-8 with a dealer-installed supercharger that helps make 700 horsepower, and earlier this year, Ford offered the same kit on the Lobo. Our spy photographer couldn’t confirm the engine in this prototype, but it does feature a dual-exit exhaust.

Looking at all the separate clues, it’s possible that this mule could preview an updated version of the Ford Performance FP700S, judging by the tires and the amber DRLs. Of course, we’d prefer it’s an early look at a more faithful recreation of the original F-150 Lightning formula, featuring two doors instead of four. It’d also be appealing if the Lobo treatment were available on the XL model because it would be more affordable than the SuperCrew truck, which currently starts at nearly $60K. Either way, with Ram’s family of Rumble Bee “muscle trucks” preparing to hit the streets, this could be the first clue that Ford is working on a way to respond.
