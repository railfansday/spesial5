While it would be a dream to get to every new hotel within days of opening to be among the first to experience special properties around the world, the reality is, that’s not always possible. When it is, though, you can bet we jump on the opportunity. Especially when it’s a flagship new points hotel.

That’s how I ended up staying at the gorgeous new Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur within a week of its opening on Oct. 30, 2026. To be clear, I understood that not all the hotel’s restaurants would be open, nor would its spa, so this is not a full review, rather a first glimpse at what’s sure to be a favorite Hilton points redemption option soon.

Although it’s the Malaysian capital’s newest hotel, the Waldorf Astoria actually occupies the site of the storied former Hotel Istana in the heart of city’s Golden Triangle district — so called because it is the capital’s main financial and shopping district, with luxury megamalls galore — which means it’s centrally located, but tucked onto a quiet stretch of Jalan Raja Chulan.

Because of that, the hotel feels secluded and quiet, but plugged into the rest of the city, and easily accessible to most of the activities international visitors are likely to seek out while in town.

The hotel feels like a destination unto itself, too, though, thanks to its palatial suites (no mere rooms here) and an impressive slate of bars and restaurants, including outposts from celebrity chef Jean-Georges Vongerichten and Bangkok-based Garima Arora. The hotel will also be home to Malaysia’s first and only La Prairie spa, and the outdoor pool is the perfect place to while away a tropical afternoon.

Here’s what else you should plan to do during your stay at the new Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur.

During my stay, only a fraction of the accommodations were ready for guests, but the hotel should be at full strength soon. There are no rooms here, just suites, which start at an impressive 80 square meters (861 square feet).

Deboss Collective is behind the decor, and sought inspiration from traditional Malay construction and crafts, incorporating lots of natural stone and timber, woven textiles and wooden furnishings that feel like contemporary urban updates to what you might find in a traditional rumah kampung, or village house.

To wit, there are huge windows overlooking the hotel grounds and the city skyline, letting in plenty of natural light. The carpet patterns and hardwood are meant to evoke traditional Malaysian marquetry while colorful accent walls call to mind vibrant Peranakan shophouses you can still find in parts of the city.

My suite’s footprint was enormous, with a huge, welcoming living area that included a 65-inch flatscreen television, a breakfast table which could double as a workdesk thanks to a panel of plugs, a rounded sofa and armchair and a low-slung wooden chair that blended colonial and midcentury influences.

In the separate bedroom, a king-size bed was dressed with crisp white sheets embroidered with a “WA,” just in case you forgot where you were. To either side were walls with a terete theme hearkening to the bamboo stalks that grow in gardens around the city. Another 55-inch flatscreen hung on the wall in there.

The bathroom was mostly white marble, including geometric floor tiling, but the countertops were a handsome darker hue. Anchoring the entire space was an elliptical standalone tub with a scalloped shell, while a water closet and separate walk-in shower stood close by. To lend a sense of place, the bath products are from Jo Malone’s Jo Loves line and scented with citrusy-fresh pomelo, which is a staple in Malaysian cuisine.

Considering this was a standard category room, it felt positively palatial, with a huge amount of real estate that provided a variety of distinct spaces to enjoy within the suite at different times.

The Waldorf Astoria isn’t the biggest hotel in town, but it fields an outsize roster of restaurants that should be a draw for guests and Kuala Lumpur locals alike.

You can’t call your hotel a Waldorf Astoria if it doesn’t have a version of Peacock Alley, the famous lobby lounge from the New York City original. The one here sits smack dab at the hotel entrance and has soaring ceilings, sculptural marble columns, dramatic overhead chandeliers and a variety of seating vignettes that feel homey yet upscale.

The all-day menu offers items like chicken skewers with lemongrass and coriander ($10.50), a Waldorf salad ($14.50), a smash burger ($22) and Malaysian nasi goreng ($17) among other options.

Named after the previous hotel to occupy this spot, Istana is the hotel’s all-day dining restaurant and is adjacent to the lobby. Breakfast includes an extensive buffet and both Western and Asian dishes. For lunch and dinner, you’ll find specialties like spiced Borneo chicken wings with a honey glaze ($10.50), chargrilled red snapper with wild ginger chili sambal ($23.75) and fresh fiddlehead ferns from the Cameron Highlands sauteed in coconut milk with tongue-torching bird’s eye chili ($8.75).

If you’re up for it, the durian dessert with coconut cream and a palm sugar compote and served with toasted buttery buns is a rich way to end the evening ($12).

This is Vongerichten’s farm-to-table concept, so you’ll find a menu focused on seasonal fruits and vegetables. The signature eggplant dip is smoky and smooth with tangy coconut yogurt and tomato chutney served with fluffy pita ($9.75). There are also tartines like a king crab crostini with tart lemon aioli and dill ($16.50), various pizzas and pastas, and an array of plant-based dishes including pistachio-crusted broccoli with hazelnut vinaigrette ($14.50).

At the entrance to ABC Kitchens KL, there is a bright, casual area with a cocktail bar and small plates.

Jean-Georges Vongerichten has a longstanding relationship with the Waldorf Astoria brand, so it should be no surprise that the signature restaurant here is a French-Malaysian fine-dining fusion restaurant courtesy of the chef. It is only open for dinner Tuesday-Saturday. I did not have the opportunity to dine here, but the menu features an entire caviar section, starters like diver scallops with caramelized cauliflower and caper-raisin emulsion ($29.50) and spice-charred duck breast with roasted cabbage and chili-lime broth ($39.50).

It was not yet open during my stay, but Yaari will be the hotel’s sophisticated Indian restaurant and will only be open for dinner from Wednesday-Sunday. The chef is Garima Arora, whose Bangkok restaurant, Gaa, holds two Michelin stars.

Located on the fourth floor, Coda feels like a swanky hideaway for those in the know. Glossy dark stone surfaces and emerald-green velvet chairs and banquettes give the space a swanky vibe, as does the menu of libations inspired by various vinyl albums, one of which will likely be spinning on the record player during your visit.

Even the classics get a Malaysian twist, like an old fashioned crafted with jerky-like beef bak kwa-washed Michter’s bourbon, cardamom, clove and cascara distillate and mandarin-infused jaggery for a sweet finish ($20.50).

Nightly rates at the Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur start at around $520 or 100,000 Hilton Honors points.

The Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur is part of  American Express Fine Hotels + Resorts, so if you have or , you can make a booking through amextravel.com and receive perks like availability-based upgrades, up to a $100 credit to use on-property during your stay for things like drinks or meals, complimentary daily breakfast for two and guaranteed 4 p.m. late checkout.

Both cards now offer a biannual $300 (up to $600 per calendar year) statement credit on prepaid stays at Amex Fine Hotels + Resorts, so you could save $300 on a reservation at the hotel if you are able to take advantage of one of these cards’ credits. All public areas at the Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur are wheelchair-accessible thanks to elevators (with wheelchair-height buttons), ramps and wide pathways. The hotel has accessible rooms in the King Urban Deluxe category. These accommodations feature audio and visual alarms, an automatic door closer, and wheelchair-height door viewer.

The Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur is an exciting new points option in the Malaysian capital, and one with plenty of award availability. While 100,000 points is a lot to pay, considering the sheer size of the suites here and the relatively high (for Kuala Lumpur) price point, it might be well worth it. Once the hotel’s restaurants and spa are all open and operational, this should become one of the city’s most popular luxury hotels.
