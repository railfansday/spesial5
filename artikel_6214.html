Well-Traveled Women on Where They Plan to Visit in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A bucket list item is one thing—turning it into an itinerary is another. The difference often lies in deciding that the places we dream about are worth pursuing amid limited vacation days and financial constraints. It’s a sentiment captured by avid traveler and content creator, Lucrenzia Worthington: “If there's one thing I've learned, it's that it’s never the right time, only the time you finally decide to go.”

With 2027 in sight, we’re taking inspiration from well-traveled women who are already thinking about the possibilities of the fresh, new year ahead and the trips they hope to pull off. Spanning photographers, artisans, interior designers, rock climbers, divers, and cookbook authors, their wanderlust is as varied as it is far-reaching. Read on for how they plan to travel next, from diving Antarctica’s crystalline waters to cooking in the fertile valleys of the Peloponnese Peninsula.

“For as long as I can remember, I’ve dreamed of diving in Antarctica. Maybe it’s growing up in Bangkok’s tropical heat, but the polar regions endlessly fascinate me: the icebergs, the seals, and penguins. I’ve visited the White Continent before, but seeing it underwater is something else entirely. In 2027, I’m joining Aurora Expeditions, a pioneer in polar diving for over 20 years. Even as an experienced diver and underwater photographer, it takes serious preparation: I’m logging drysuit dives off [the coast of] Cape Town with specialized gear to get ready for one of the most extraordinary dive sites on earth.” —Pier Nirandara, travel writer, underwater photographer, and founder of Immersiv Expeditions

“You know, I've been lucky enough to see some extraordinary corners of the world, yet one place keeps slipping down my list. It's always ’not the right time,’ always ’too complicated to book.’ But 2027 is the year: Lapland, in February. There are direct flights from Milan to Rovaniemi, Finland, and I would plan two nights there and two in Levi. I'm dreaming of a night in those super cool glass cabins where you fall asleep under the northern lights and endless starry skies, and booking experiences like visiting a reindeer farm, husky sledding, and snowshoe hikes through frosted pines—but also cozy up indoors by a crackling fire, with a warm blanket, and soak up the winter wonderland around me. If there's one thing I've learned, it's that it’s never the right time, only the time you finally decide to go.” —Lucrezia Worthington, content creator and freelance journalist

“I've always wanted to visit Morocco, and I think 2027 is the year. My dream is to stay in a riad in Marrakech with however many friends I can persuade to join me. I want to find tagine and wander through the [medina]. I also hope to venture out to the Atlas Mountains for a weekend.” —Ríoghnach Robinson, author of Bad Words

“In 2027, I'm planning to return to Antalya, Turkey. I spent a week there this year and fell in love with the food, the people, and the climbing. I'll be based around Geyikbayırı and Çitdibi, where there are limestone crags in the hills with views out to the sea. I usually find a place to stay on Airbnb and lean on local recommendations for where to eat. One spot I'm definitely returning to is Coffee Roaster 404.” —Sasha DiGiulian, professional climber and founder of SEND Bars

“Most flock to Greece for the idyllic beaches and all things Mediterranean, and while I completely understand that desire, my curiosity has landed me in the Peloponnese Peninsula, in particular the central region of Arcadia. Its fertile valleys are known for strong culinary and agricultural traditions; apples, sour cherries, slender eggplants, cheeses from mountain sheep and goats, walnuts, chestnuts, and mushroom foraging. I have been obsessing over a beautiful hotel that caught my eye: Manna, located on Mount Mainalo among hiking trails and dense forests. The mountain is also known for a very special high altitude honey cultivated from fir trees. Honestly, the honey alone is enough to get me on a plane.” —Noreen Wasti, cook, food creative, and author of Feast Your Eyes: Beautiful Recipes to Garnish, Share, and Savor

“In 2027, I’d love to finally visit Peru and see Machu Picchu. South America is completely new territory for me, and the landscapes, craftsmanship, and food are all calling. I’m imagining local guides, time in the Sacred Valley, and plenty of exploring. I’d also love to go home to Mauritius, but this time be a tourist. A beautiful hotel, all the food I miss, and time to enjoy the island and its lovely hospitality. Usually, my visits revolve around family and catching up. In 2027, for once, I’d like my biggest responsibility to be deciding what to have for lunch.” —Nashmita Rajiah, interior designer

“I’m perpetually scouting [destinations] for In Hand’s newsletter and group trips by exploring culturally rich regions through the lens of traditional craft. I'm looking forward to an upcoming trip to the Atlas Mountains to visit the female Amazigh weavers in their homes, and learning about the by-hand process of weaving Berber rugs at the source rather than the point of sale, focusing on the culture and traditions around this generation-to-generation craft. I’m staying at Kasbah du Toubkal in Imlil, a small mountain town grounded in real Amazigh village life. It’s a trip I’ve wanted to take for years. ” —Meghan McEwen, writer and founder of group trip planner In Hand

“What I look forward to most is traveling with friends and family. Mornings in the sea, a simple lunch in the hot sun. Afternoons are for slow wandering, and at night we'll cook big meals from our market finds. In 2027, my family is heading to Sicily for two weeks in shoulder season, before the [summer] tourist wave. The first half we'll explore the main island, from the salt flats of Trapani to the bone white cliffs of Scala dei Turchi on the south coast. Then Favignana in the Egadi Islands, off Sicily's west coast, will hopefully be home for a week or so. The water is crystal blue, the coves are plentiful, and you can bike around the entire island easily. Tuna is still king there, though the old canning factory has long since closed. I'm dreaming of spaghetti with bottarga. At the moment I have my eyes on Villa Zu Nillu from The Thinking Traveller, perched above its own labyrinthine Roman stone quarry, into which a beautiful pool and a Mediterranean garden have been set. And I will document it all.” —Andrea Gentl, photographer and author of Cooking with Mushrooms

“After last summer’s European heatwave, we’re planning a cooler 2027 Finnish escape with my decorative arts grad school friend and her family. We always mix design with fun for the kids, so we’ll visit Helsinki’s Aalto House, the design district, and Hvitträsk museum before renting a lakeside mökki for swimming, hiking, and archipelago adventures. I want to experience the “midnight sun,” but I might regret it if the kids never sleep. Truthfully, I’m more of a lazy reptile than an outdoor adventurer, so afterward, our family of four will retreat to a less popular Italian beach for pasta, sunshine, and absolutely no obligation to do anything adventurous at all.” —Ajiri Aki, author of JOIE: A Parisian’s Guide to Celebrating The Good Life and The Joie Journal

“Long the secret destination of all the chic Italians I know, the low-key vibe of Maremma [in Tuscany and Lazio] is starting to also draw an international crowd looking for relaxed summers featuring all the best of Italy—raw nature, excellent food—minus the crowds and bling. For families, the Terre di Sacra Estate is on my wish list, either “glamping” on the beach or in one of their villas. And speaking of villas, Maremma doyenne Amanda Sposito is behind one of the most beautiful private rentals, the Villa Tre Querce, in town.” —Philomena Schurer Merckoll, founder of Riad Mena and hospitality group Egypt Beyond

“I would love to visit Japan. Surprisingly, I have never been to Asia before, so it feels like a whole new world waiting to be explored. As a designer working with raffia and traditional weaving techniques, I am fascinated by Japan’s appreciation for craftsmanship and handmade objects. I would love to explore Kyoto’s historic temples, meet local artisans, and learn about their weaving traditions and use of natural materials. Seeing Mount Fuji in person would also be incredible: I find so much inspiration in the colors, textures, and everyday details of a place, and I would love to bring those experiences into my designs.” —Akosua Afriyie-Kumi, founder of AAKS
