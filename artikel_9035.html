kanal-centre pompidou opens in brussels inside restored 1930s citroën garage

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

the new museum combines contemporary art, architecture, performance and more than 20,000 square meters of free public space, proposing a more open relationship between cultural institutions and the city.

Designed by Atelier Kanal, a consortium comprising EM2N, noAarchitecten and Sergison Bates architects, Kanal opens in Brussels on November 28th, 2026, transforming a former Citroën garage into a 40,000-square-meter museum and public cultural center (find designboom’s previous coverage here). The project houses contemporary art, architecture, performance, film and community activities under one roof, with over 20,000 square meters of freely accessible public space.

Kanal’s artistic program develops through a long-term partnership with Centre Pompidou, which provides access to one of the world’s leading collections of modern and contemporary art. The collaboration, initiated in 2017, also involves institutional support, exhibition production and joint curatorial work.

Built in 1933–34 on the banks of the Charleroi-Brussels Canal, the former Citroën complex was conceived as a monumental setting for the automobile. Its defining features include a vast glass-and-steel showroom facing the street and an expansive industrial structure behind it. Atelier Kanal restores the existing building and introduces three new structures within it, creating spaces for exhibitions, performances, research and public activities.

The former showroom remains central to the identity of the building, as it retains the scale and openness of its original architecture, taking on a different cultural role. Across its five floors, the museum accommodates exhibition galleries, performance and film auditoriums, workshops, a library, a research center and community spaces. A basement gallery, reaching seven meters in height, adds another dimension to the exhibition program.

A 700-square-meter indoor playground designed by Turner Prize-winning architecture collective Assemble brings another dimension to Kanal’s public program. Conceived as an imaginary landscape, the installation draws inspiration from the piles of coal once transported along the Charleroi-Brussels Canal, reinterpreting this industrial history as a terrain of hills and volcano-like forms. Ropes, tires, masks and other loose elements invite children aged four and above to explore and play.

The playground extends into a dedicated workshop space for movement and encounters between generations. Alongside the museum’s library, bookshop, rooftop terrace and community spaces, it reinforces Kanal’s ambition to function as an everyday public destination.

The opening exhibition, A truly immense journey, brings together more than 350 works dating from the early 20th century to today. Artists include Lygia Clark, Sonia Delaunay, Alberto Giacometti, Natalia Goncharova, Wifredo Lam, Henri Matisse and Pablo Picasso, alongside contemporary artists such as Sammy Baloji, Edith Dekyndt, Aglaia Konrad and Hana Miletić. Drawn from the collections of Centre Pompidou and Kanal, as well as Belgian and international public and private collections, the exhibition considers how people, ideas and artistic movements have shaped the modern world.

Other opening presentations establish a more locally engaged and multidisciplinary direction. An infinite woman: Black archives in two acts examines how artists of African descent have reclaimed and reinterpreted colonial imagery associated with Mangbetu women from the Congo. Belgian artist Otobong Nkanga’s With These Hands invites visitors to participate in a collective weaving installation, where trained workers and members of the public work side by side, exchanging skills and experiences. The museum also presents new commissions by Belgian artists, including Joëlle Tuerlinckx and Manon de Boer, alongside a free presentation by Banu Cennetoğlu.

Architecture has its own dedicated presence through Kanal Architecture, the new home of the institution formerly known as CIVA, Brussels’ museum and archive of architecture, landscape architecture and urbanism. With its own library and research center, the institution develops a program focused on the relationship between the built environment, society and the ecosystems that shape cities.

Its inaugural exhibition, Right to the City – Right to the Future, looks at Brussels as a testing ground for alternative spatial practices and addresses its complicated relationship with modernization, including the large-scale demolitions associated with the Manhattan Plan and their lasting impact on surrounding neighborhoods. Another initiative, Yser Print Coop by WERKER Collective, will establish a cooperative print room inside the museum until the end of 2028, providing space for risography, woodcut and screen printing.
