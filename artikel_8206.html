Giant November Waves Threaten Surfers, Swimmers, and Spectators Along California’s Coast

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Powerful waves crash against Pacifica Pier in Pacifica, California, on November 14, 2026, as large Pacific swells batter the state's coastline. (Photo: Stephen Lam/San Francisco Chronicle via Getty Images)

Lifeguards along California’s coast have had their hands full over the past several days, as powerful Pacific swells stranded surfers, swept a teenager photographing the waves off rocks, and even overturned a rescue jet ski during an operation near Santa Cruz.

In San Diego alone, lifeguards conducted 175 water rescues between November 9 and 16, according to San Diego Fire-Rescue figures reported by FOX 5/KUSI.

“I’ve seen seasons start off with a bang like this, but we’ve never had this type of impact so immediate,” Ernst Bauen, operations division chief for the Central Fire District of Santa Cruz County, told Outside on November 17.

The enormous swells were generated by powerful low-pressure systems over the northeastern Pacific, where hurricane-force winds pushed waves toward the California coast, according to the National Weather Service. On November 14, forecasters predicted breaking waves of 25 to 35 feet, with occasional sets reaching 40 to 50 feet at big-wave surfing spot Mavericks, near Half Moon Bay.

Bauen, who spent about 20 years as a rescue swimmer, said waves are reaching roads, cliffs, and beach access points earlier in the season than usual.

“The ocean is extremely dynamic, and where there used to be a safe amount of space to recreate even on the beach, those spaces are becoming fewer and further between because of the erosion along our coastline,” he said.

On November 14, rescue crews pulled three surfers from the water near Pleasure Point in Santa Cruz County. A fire department rescuer had picked up two surfers on a jet ski when a large wave overturned the watercraft, throwing all three into the ocean.

The rescuer, a part of Bauen’s team, managed to right the jet ski and continue the operation, returning about ten minutes later to help a third surfer.

Hundreds of miles south that same day, a San Diego lifeguard swam through powerful breaking waves to reach a struggling swimmer at La Jolla Cove. Another lifeguard eventually arrived on a jet ski and brought both men to safety.

Also in San Diego on November 14, at nearby Windansea Beach, a teenager photographing the waves from nearby rocks was swept into the ocean. A surfer jumped into the water and briefly helped keep the teenager’s head above the surface before waves carried him toward the rocky shoreline, where several bystanders helped pull him out.

The close call underscored warnings from San Diego Lifeguard Chief Daniel Orloff that even spectators watching from dry land were at risk. “If you are close, at any moment, a wave could take you out. It can breach a bluff, a berm, even the boardwalk,” he told ABC 10News.

In Orange County, a surfer helped rescue three people struggling in strong currents at Crystal Cove State Beach on November 15, according to a witness who shared video of the aftermath with TV station 10 News San Diego.

Not every incident ended in a rescue. On November 14, a man disappeared while swimming near Zuma Beach in Malibu. Lifeguards responded to the scene around 1:40 p.m., and Coast Guard helicopters and boat crews searched for the missing swimmer into the following day. The Coast Guard suspended its search on November 15 without locating him. The man has not publicly been identified.

Bauen said the ocean can also lull people into a false sense of security. During these swells, waves can stay relatively small for 15 or 20 minutes at a time, making conditions look calm before a surge of much larger waves suddenly arrives and catches surfers off guard.

Rising tides can also leave surfers stranded when the beach access points they used to enter the water become submerged.

While the largest waves have eased, forecasters say hazardous surf will linger through November 19. In its November 17 forecast, the National Weather Service predicted breaking waves of 13 to 17 feet along portions of the Bay Area and Central Coast through November 18, with dangerous rip currents and sneaker waves.

Bauen urged surfers and beachgoers to check the forecast, pay attention to changing conditions, and identify a safe route back to shore before entering the water.

“Ultimately, it comes down to the individual,” Bauen said. “You really need to know before you go. You need to tune into the ocean conditions, look up forecasts, and do a little bit of research before you explore a secluded beach, decide to go for a swim, or paddle out.”
