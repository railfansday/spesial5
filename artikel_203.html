Friedrich Christian "Mick" Flick, an art collector, investor and multimillionaire who belonged to one of Germany's richest business dynasties, has died aged 82 in Rosenheim in the southern state of Bavaria, his family has said.

The family said in a statement issued on Saturday that Flick left behind "the memory of a life shaped by significant acts of generosity as a donor and patron, particularly in contemporary art and in promoting tolerance."

Flick was the grandson of Friedrich Flick, who made a huge fortune in the Nazi era with his coal and steel business, partly by using forced labor.

This past came back to haunt the grandson in 2003 when he pledged to loan his world-famous collection of modern art to Berlin, with the Central Council of Jews in Germany calling the Flick Collection a "Göring Collection" and saying the loan was intended to wash clean the "blood money" from the Nazi era.

The collection, which contains some 2,500 works by 150 artists, was exhibited in parts in the German capital from 2004 to 2021.

Flick later said in an interview that the widespread outrage, while hurtful, had forced him to look more closely at his family's history.

According to his family, Flick once said: "I have no guilt for my forefathers' conduct, but I feel a responsibility, a very special responsibility. Every German bears this responsibility. As a Flick, I bear it twice."

Among other things, he collaborated with the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation, which oversees Berlin's museums, to research his family's Nazi past.

He also set up a foundation in Potsdam, near Berlin, that aimed to combat xenophobia and racism and contributed €5 million ($5.4 million) to the foundation to compensate former forced laborers.

The family was also involved in a highly publicized party donations scandal in the 1980s that became known as the "Flick Affair."

"Mick" Flick married Countess Maya von Schönburg (1958-2019), the older sister of well-known socialite, businesswoman, art collector and artist Gloria von Thurn und Taxis, in 1985. The couple had three children.

German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier is setting off on a five-day trip to Africa that will take him to Ivory Coast, Ghana and Kenya.

Steinmeier will be accompanied by a business delegation and is expected to focus on economic cooperation and the training of skilled workers during the trip.

On Monday, Steinmeier is to be received by Ivorian President Allassane Outtara in Abidjan, Ivory Coast's largest city and seat of government.

The next day, in the Ghanaian capital, Accra, he is set to open the German-African Business Summit, which, according to its website, "brings together senior business executives, policymakers, investors, and industry leaders from across Germany and Africa."

On Wednesday, he will be received by Kenyan President William Ruto in Nairobi.

In Kenya, Steinmeier will also visit the Crescent Island Game Sanctuary on Thursday, the last day of his trip.

The Federal Institute for Hydrology (BfG) has said it expects water levels in large rivers in western Germany, including the Rhine, to return to average or above in the next few days amid sustained rainfall.

It said the water level at the key gauge in Kaub near Koblenz, located at an important bottleneck on the Middle Rhine, could reach some 3.3 meters (10.8 feet), more than 3 meters higher than the last recorded low-water readings.

The BfG said it also expected rising water levels in the Weser and Danube rivers, with smaller increases in the Elbe and Oder.

The rising water level in the Rhine, an important shipping route, means that conditions for water-borne cargo transport have improved, according to Germany's inland navigation association BDB.

"The more abundant rainfall in the past days has caused the water level in the Rhine to rise considerably, and they will keep rising a great deal in the next few days as well, meaning that each ship has much more transport capacity again," said Fabian Spiess, the deputy manager at BDB.

In the middle of the year, many ships were forced to drastically reduce the loads they carried, leading to economic losses that will not be recuperated any time soon, according to Spiess.

He cited the year 2018, in which shipping on the Rhine was also hindered by extremely low water levels, saying that the months of improved conditions at the end of that year had not been enough to make good the economic damage incurred.

The conservation NGO also said that the current rain provided temporary relief to the environment but could not compensate for the negative consequences of the long dry period.

It said species of animals, such as amphibians and water insects, that lived in small bodies of water and moist habitats had been particularly impacted.

Justice Minister Stefanie Hubig says that she intends to tighten the law on murders committed with "gender-specific" motives, with a particular focus on femicides perpetrated by men who are unable to accept a separation or the desire of a woman to live a self-determined life.

"A man kills, or attempts to kill, his partner or ex-partner almost every day in Germany," Hubig told the Sunday edition of the mass-circulation Bild newspaper.

"Any man killing a woman because she wants to leave him must be punished as a murderer," she said, noting that in some cases, courts cite a man's deep sadness or fear of losing a partner to someone else as extenuating circumstances. This can lead to a lesser conviction of manslaughter.

She said that in future, courts should look more closely at such motives to determine whether they were based on sentiments of patriarchal possessiveness.

However, Hubig, who belongs to the center-left Social Democrats (SPD), said she was opposed to making femicide a separate category of crime, as some women's rights organizations have demanded.

She said that, in her opinion, doing so would have "no advantages over what we are intending — and much bigger disadvantages."

The proposals are currently being debated within the coalition government and are expected to be presented to the states and associations for consideration before the end of the year.

They will then have to go before the Cabinet before being put before the parliament.

The powerful IG Metall union said on Sunday it would call another 24-hour strike on Monday as a pay dispute continues.

Altogether, some 23,000 workers at 19 companies in northern Germany have been called upon to participate, including employees at automaker Mercedes-Benz, shipbuilding company Meyer Werft and wind ​energy company Siemens Gamesa.

The union said that a strike on Friday saw some 12,300 workers walk out at 17 facilities.

It says that the strikes are meant to increase the pressure on employers to yield to its demands, which include a 5% ‌wage increase.

So far, employers have ⁠offered a one-off payment next year and a 1.3% wage increase from July 2028.

A new goal celebration was devised by Borussia Dortmund midfielder Felix Nmecha after his previous one caused controversy due to its religious aspect.

After scoring the goal that secured Dortmund's 1-0 victory over Borussia Mönchengladbach (see entry below), Nmecha knelt on one knee and crossed his arms in front of his chest. He then spread his arms, appearing to pray with his eyes closed.

Nmecha told broadcaster Sky that he intends to continue expressing his faith during goal celebrations, adding that he had once again given glory to Jesus.

However, he did not perform the controversial part of his usual goal celebration. In it, he removes an imaginary crown from his head and places it on the pitch in front of him while kneeling.

This celebration has been heavily criticized, even by Dortmund's fan department.

Nmecha explained that the celebrations is about "putting down my 'crown' in front of the soon-to-come king and point out to Jesus, my hope and my strength."

The gesture is used by Ballers in God, a network of Christian football players that has been criticized for the homophobic and queerphobic statements made by its members.

Dortmund president Hans-Joachim Watzke said he asked the player not to do the celebration again.

Bundesliga powerhouse Bayern Munich concluded the 2025-26 financial year with record figures.

According to the financial report released Saturday at the club's annual general meeting, the club's total turnover was €988.2 million ($1.08 billion) — that is around €10 million more than the previous year.

Operating profit (EBITDA) increased from €187.8 million to €190.8 million. Pre-tax profit was €35.3 million, and net profit for the year was €22.8 million.

Bayern had a particularly strong year in terms of sponsorships. Turnover increased by nearly €30 million, reaching €268.4 million. The club also set a record in merchandise sales, earning €152.8 million.

"We have achieved another record year," CEO Jan-Christian Dreesen said. "This shows that our club has a strong foundation. And we continue to invest to ensure the best possible conditions for the sporting success of our teams."

Moreover, Bayern achieved another milestone. They reached 500,000 members — more than any other club in the world.

Over the past 12 years, the German giants have doubled their membership, growing from 250,000 in 2014 to 500,000 this year.

"Record champions, record members, record member champions," president Herbert Hainer said.

Members hold a 70% stake in the club, which is well above the simple majority required by German rules.

The remaining 30% is owned by four German companies with roots in Bavaria: Adidas, Audi, Allianz, and Viessmann.

Bayern won the league and cup double in the 2025-26 season, losing only three of 53 games across all competitions. In Europe, the club made it to the Champions League semifinals.

Two and a half weeks after his sudden death, Free Voters leader Hubert Aiwanger, who was Bavarian Minister of Economic Affairs and Deputy Minister-President, was laid to rest in his hometown of Inkofen in Lower Bavaria.

A memorial service was held at the Parish Church of the Presentation of the Lord and broadcast on a screen outside.  According to the organizers, around 2,000 people attended the solemn service.

Aiwanger's funeral was open to the public. His longtime partner, Tanja Schweiger, the Regensburg district administrator (Free Voters), stated that Aiwanger had been "a man of the people."

For that reason, she said, everyone "who liked and appreciated him" was warmly welcome. The politician had already been honored with a state funeral in Munich a week earlier.

Aiwanger was found dead in a Berlin hotel on November 4 after canceling an appointment for health reasons. The 55-year-old died of natural causes.

Harry Kane, Bayern Munich's star striker, has pledged to return to the field for the important matches at the end of the current campaign after undergoing surgery for a torn ankle ligament.

"I'll be back fresh and ready to go for the business end of the season," he wrote on social media above a photo of himself on his hospital bed, also thanking fans for their messages of support.

Kane, 33, was injured around 20 minutes into Bayern's 1-1 home draw with Cologne in their Bundesliga match on Friday.

Bayern issued a statement on Saturday saying that Kane had "successfully undergone surgery" on his left ankle but was expected to be out for three to four months.

England captain Kane, who finished the 2025–26 season with over 70 goals for club and country, was awarded the Ballon d'Or for his efforts.
