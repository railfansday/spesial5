My earliest memory of gaming is the first time I ever encountered it, @Droopy. My Father died when I was 6 weeks old in January of 1964, and my Mother raised me alone. However, as my Mother worked, each Summer holiday, I would be dispatched to my Aunts and Uncles who lived about 200 miles North of where I lived, and I would spend 6 weeks in the company of my Cousins.

In the Summer of 1973, at the age of 10, I went to my Cousins as was customary, to find that they had a games machine that played Pong (to my recollection, that is all it played although that might be the only game they had) and I played the game as much as my Cousins would tolerate for the entire 6 weeks. Obviously, they were far better at it than me, but that did not stop hours and hours of fun. It's probably difficult to imagine now, but think what it must have been like to go from never having heard of gaming, to encountering it for the first time. It was mind blowing to 10 year old me! By the end of the holiday, I was absolutely hooked.

The first console that I owned myself was an Intellivision console that I bought in 1982 with money that I earned working for British Sugar stacking huge sacks of er, well... sugar! The sacks weighed almost as much as I did, which as I weighed less than 10 stone at the time probably isn't that much, but the work paid really well. Whilst I do not remember how many games I had for it, one does stick out, and that was Tron Deadly Discs. It was very basic, but you simply had to throw discs to destroy the robots within an arena, and you could enter doors that would sort of teleport you around the arena. I always remember the game because of one night where I just didn't seem capable of dying, and because you could not save the game (and I'm not sure you could pause it either) I just kept playing until something like 4 a.m. even though I had to be up at 6 to go to work! That night I got my highest ever score in the game, and also formed probably one of my fondest memories in gaming too. It just felt epic! I imagine these days, faced with the same game, I'd be bored inside an hour, but back then, it felt like the hight of entertainment.

Effectively, I have been gaming for about 53 years now, and owned at least one console since the age of 18. I've owned an Intellivision console, a Sinclair ZX Spectrum, various Amstrad computers, various Commodore consoles, various Nintendo's, various Sega's, each of the PlayStation and Xbox consoles, and a gaming PC.

I injured my back in 1990/1, and it became increasingly bad as time progressed to the point that I had to medically retire from the Police in 2010. Fortunately, my time in Bomb Disposal and the Police meant that I was able to retire with a reasonable pension, and whilst I have not been able to work for the past 16 years, I have been able to game every day since, so injuring my back has had it's upsides even if I cannot walk particularly well or far! So, every cloud and what have you... 😉
