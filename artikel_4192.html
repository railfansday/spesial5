Xenogears Is Finally Getting Remastered, But Not in the Way You Think

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Murillo Zerbinatto is a JRPG enthusiast, writer, and editor. He has played over 300 RPGs during his gaming career, with 90% of them being JRPGs.

There are two perspectives on remasters and remakes. On one hand, we have those who complain whenever a developer announces a new one, wishing they would dedicate development efforts to bringing new IPs to life. On the other hand, we have those who rejoice and feel the nostalgia tingling whenever a game that shaped their tastes returns to the modern era. I fall into the latter camp, which is why I am extremely excited for the upcoming fanmade remaster of Xenogears.

The Elder Scrolls Travels: Shadowkey, the long-forgotten Nokia N-Gage RPG, is returning!

To be fair, three Xenogears remasters are currently being developed by fans, each with its own codename: Weltall, Exodus, and Atlas. Today I'll discuss the Atlas remaster because it seems to be the one the fanbase is most excited about, and it already has an imminent release date of 15 December 2026. However, keep in mind that since this is not an official remaster from Square Enix, you can only legally play it if you own an original copy of Xenogears at home.

The project could hardly be called a Xenogears remaster if it didn't include notable upgrades over the original. According to the creator, Reddit user Ok-Bed3203, the entire game has been rebuilt in the Godot engine and will run directly inside it, provided you supply your own copy of the game. Fans can experience Xenogears Atlas in two distinct visual styles, dubbed Canon and HD.

In Canon mode, the visuals remain faithful to the 1998 release, while incorporating technical fixes such as eliminating flickering triangles or textures, stabilizing sprites and images, and smoothing out screen fades. Meanwhile, High Definition mode introduces enhanced textures and sprites, 16:9 widescreen support (with menus, text, and FMVs preserved in their original 4:3 framing so nothing gets stretched), updated lighting and visual effects, and 3D Gears.

Both modes will support 60 FPS, 120 FPS, or your monitor's native refresh rate. They also include 2K CG cutscenes upscaled by Laichzeit (likely exclusive to HD mode, though not explicitly specified), subtitles in every language, fan translations, and quality-of-life options like text speed adjustments, quick loads, full-screen or windowed modes, and automatic pausing when running in the background.

Independent developer LoreanXavier, also known as Lorean The Gamer, has been working on an unofficial native PC port of P.T. for quite some time.

After the initial release, the developers plan to add a Mods menu that lets you load custom text patches and swap images or audio files by name, along with remastered music from Xenogears' iconic soundtrack and further touch-ups to the 2K movies. The community has also suggested several additions currently under consideration, including random encounter rate toggles, a reduced-flash mode, auto-advancing dialogue, optional voice-over via the Spoken Mirror fan dub (slated to arrive alongside the Disc 2 remake), and a dedicated Android port.

As you probably noticed, the developer mentioned a Disc 2 remake. That sounds like a tall order, because as anyone who played the game remembers, Xenogears features a pristine Disc 1, whereas the second disc was famously rushed after Square cut the budget before the project could be finished, which severely undermined the experience. Atlas aims to give Disc 2 the breathing room it never received. While it is not yet completely clear what that will entail or when it will arrive, it is easily the most intriguing prospect of this entire fanmade remaster.

While the project looks incredible and has me thoroughly hyped, one looming question always hangs in the air these days: was this project made using AI? The developer preemptively addressed that very concern:

“About AI, since it always comes up: yes, I used AI as a tool along the way, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But this is not a vibe-coded project. I checked every cutscene, every movement and every 16:9 margin by hand, then fixed it and compared it with the original. A huge part of the work was the boring part: tracking down the smallest glitches left over from the PS1 renderer, one by one, until they were gone. Monk work, honestly. I hope you'll feel it when you play.”

So there you have it. If you want to experience or revisit Xenogears with revamped visuals, quality-of-life additions, tweaks, and technical fixes, Xenogears Atlas might just be the way to go. This Xenogears remaster launches on 15 December 2026, via Godot for PC, though you will still need your original copy to get it running. Who knows, maybe this will finally wake Square Enix up to greenlight a full-fledged official remaster, or at least sell the IP back to Tetsuya Takahashi.
