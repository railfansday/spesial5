Te Araroa day 13: The long and rainy road

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

It was dry when I woke up, but by the time I was packing up my tent it had started to drizzle. I figured it wouldn’t be too much, so I shoved my waterproof jacket in my bag and headed off. The track wound its way along the coastline, and while the misty rain meant that the views weren’t great, it was certainly atmospheric and I enjoyed the walk in spite of its dampness.

A couple of times, the rain picked up a bit but I managed to shelter under a tree or two and resisted putting my jacket on. I was warm enough and would only get hot with an extra layer. But then it began to really hammer down and I realised I had to put the jacket on or get soaked. I hustled along the exposed section of boardwalk I was on until I reached a tree, leaned my poles against the railing and grabbed my jacket out of my bag. As I was putting my bag down, one of my poles fell down to the beach below. And then as I finished zipping my jacket, the other did too! It was a good job the tide was out, and I was able to climb down the tree trunk and retrieve them. Losing a pole would be a nightmare, as my tent needs both of them to pitch.

A cool bench under a tree on the coastal path

The rain continued pouring as I walked into the small settlement of Opua, where I would get a ferry across the Waikare inlet. I decided to get a snack from the general store as I still wasn’t convinced I had enough to get me through to the next shop. I noticed they had hot hash browns on sale and got two – the perfect thing for a miserable day like this, I thought! I complained to the checkout lady that it was a miserable day for walking, to which she replied that they really needed the rain. Which is true, it had been a very dry winter and spring, and the reservoirs we passed on the way up north were worryingly low. Still, I would selfishly prefer that the rain would come after I’d left the area!

The vehicle ferry goes every 10 minutes or so, and I found an awning to stand under while I waited for it. When it arrived, it wasn’t really clear where pedestrians should go, but I waited for the vehicles to drive on first. The crew member on the deck pointed me to a sheltered area under some stairs, and I went to sit there. It was a short hop over to Okiato, and when I went to pay for my trip (the cost was a mere $2.70) she waved me away. I guess she felt sorry for me in the rain!

Hash browns! Perfect to keep me going on a rainy day

Waiting for the ferry to depart from my perch under the stairs

There was a short walk along the road until I could join the bush track that eventually leads to Russell, at the far end of the peninsula. On a nice day, the bush track would have been very pleasant. But on this rainy day, the vegetation was all wet and I got totally soaked. The track was also very up-and-down and a bit muddy, and I was almost glad to eventually reach the road. The rain had mostly stopped too. The gladness only lasted a few minutes though, as I was now staring down the next 26 kilometres of road walking.

It looks like there’s some kind of aquaculture going on out in the bay

Shortly after starting the road, there was a pullover with a sign indicating that this would be a good place to get a hitch. I probably should have tried to get a ride there, but I wasn’t super comfortable hitching by myself – the only time I have before was on a dead-end road, which feels a bit safer. Also, I’m pretty stubborn and to be honest I wanted to challenge myself a bit.

The road walk went on and on. And on. There was usually a bit of a shoulder, but not always, so I did find myself jumping into a ditch occasionally. There were road works too, which I had to navigate around. I was happy with my decision to bring a hi-viz vest, which I had altered to fit over my pack. Sure, it weighs a bit extra, but getting squashed by a car would be such a stupid way to die. After walking up a hill for a while I decided to take my waterproof jacket off. It hadn’t rained for a while and I figured it would be safe. Naturally, not two minutes later it started to rain again. I wasn’t stopping to put it back on though!

Remembering how a podcast helped me zone out the other day, I popped my headphone in (one at a time, to listen for cars) and kept plugging on. At around lunchtime a little patch of blue sky appeared. I took this as a sign that it was safe to sit down in a lay-by and pull out my food. Don’t get cocky, said the sky, and started spitting at me. I got halfway through my lunch (cold soaked couscous with fresh tomato, cucumber and chilli peanuts, in case you were wondering) before I gave in and packed up.

The road went past lots of mangroves, which seem to be fruiting at the moment

Not too much further on, I reached the turning for the gravel road I would be heading down next. Some hero had built a bench at the junction, presumably for TA hikers since I can’t imagine there are too many other people walking around there. I sat there and was able to finish my lunch and air out my feet a bit. The weather had dried up a bit, but now it was getting very muggy.

It was nice to be off the tarmac. The gravel was a much more forgiving surface to walk on, although I did have to go up and over a fairly substantial hill. It was also a very quiet road, and I didn’t see a single vehicle until I was at the junction where it joined back to the main road. I was having fun listening to my podcasts, but by the time I was heading down the hill, my hip was getting achy from walking on a camber all day and my ankle was feeling a bit sore from rolling it a week ago in Raetea forest. Thankfully there wasn’t too far to go on the tarmac road before I reached The Farm, where I would be staying the night.

As I approached, a load of children came out of the gate. I was feeling pretty dead on my feet and the sudden appearance of about thirty 11-year-olds was a bit overwhelming. Some of the adults pointed me to the office, and finally I was able to pitch my tent and get set up. I was super sticky after the muggy weather and was delighted to have a shower, although it took me a while to figure out which way was hot.

The Farm had… interesting vibes. I felt like it was a bit of a Marmite place – you would either love it or hate it. I didn’t hate it, but I certainly didn’t love it. It was pretty chaotic, with children everywhere (there was a school camp on, but also kids that lived there, kids whose families were visiting) and the communal areas were definitely someone’s house. Still, I was grateful for a flat place to pitch my tent, a hot shower, and somewhere to plug my phone in. It’s amazing how basic your needs can be!
