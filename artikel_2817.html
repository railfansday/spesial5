Trump, NATO chief caution Russia as attacks on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure intensify

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

U.S. President Donald Trump and NATO chief Mark Rutte issued a joint statement on Thursday urging Russia not to take any actions, intentionally or otherwise, that would further expand the Ukraine war.

The statement comes as Ukraine's allies sound the alarm over Russia's growing threat to European security and just one week after Trump's historic trilateral meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Asia.

Trump and Rutte described their meeting at the Mar-a-Lago club in Palm Beach, Florida on Wednesday as "very impactful," with Russia's nearly five-year-long war in Ukraine and the Iran war among the key topics of conversation.

"We also discussed the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the war, and the importance of obtaining a peaceful resolution to the war as rapidly as possible," Trump and Rutte said in their statement.

Moreover, the two discussed the importance of further increasing defense spending as well as investment in the defense industrial base in both the U.S. and NATO countries, according to the statement.

Trump also said in a post on Truth Social Thursday that he "briefed NATO on the tremendous progress" being made in growing defense production, noting that the focus will be on building Patriot air defense systems, THAAD systems and Tomahawk cruise missiles, as well as "many new Weapons, both Offensive and Defensive, that have not yet been viewed by the Public, or other Countries."

"There has never been a time when the United States has produced more Military Equipment or Munitions than now. Our 'cupboard' will soon be full and, when it is, our Allies and Partners throughout the World will be benefitting greatly," he said in the post.

Trump's meeting with Xi and Putin, in which he described the Russian president as his "friend," sparked alarm among European lawmakers about Washington's commitment to transatlantic security.

NATO's Rutte, a seasoned diplomat known as a consensus builder during his nearly 14 years as Dutch prime minister, has repeatedly sought to de-escalate tensions in the alliance and frequently used flattery to win over the U.S. president.

While some have questioned whether this approach has delivered any tangible benefits, Trump has previously described Rutte as a "great leader" and NATO's "biggest asset."

Russia has stepped up its attacks on Ukraine's energy infrastructure in recent weeks, with further attacks on the central Ukrainian city of Kryvyi Rih overnight disrupting power supplies and bringing municipal electric public transport to a standstill, according to Oleksandr Vilkul, head of Kryvyi ​Rih's defense council.

Ukrainian officials on Wednesday said Russian attacks on the country's power sector had damaged energy infrastructure and caused power outages in five regions as cold winter weather sets in.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Wednesday accused the Kremlin of "escalating" threats toward European security, citing actions in the Baltic region, Poland and on the Black Sea, and adding that "Russia knows exactly what it wants. And Russia wants more than Ukraine."

A spokesperson for Russia's Foreign Ministry did not immediately respond to a CNBC request for comment on Thursday morning. The Kremlin has repeatedly denied seeking military confrontation with NATO countries.
