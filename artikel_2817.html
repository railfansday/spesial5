J.J. McCarthy has won at every level; will that continue in Giants’ playoff run?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

When the New York Giants traded for quarterback J.J. McCarthy following Jaxson Dart's season-ending injury, nobody really knew what to expect. McCarthy's 2+ years with the Minnesota Vikings did not go well, and the quarterback-needy Vikings shipped him out of town.

Then, a couple of weeks into his time with the Giants, a Week 6 cameo in what looked like a blowout loss to the New Orleans Saints turned into nine minutes of mayhem that changed the Giants' season. McCarthy went 11 of 21 for 243 yards in slightly more than 11 minutes as the Giants nearly climbed out of a 34-10 hole in a 34-27 loss.

That convinced head coach John Harbaugh to name him the starting quarterback, and McCarthy went 20 of 28 for 252 yards in an overtime victory over the Houston Texans.

Now, three weeks later, the quarterback scorned by a Minnesota team that was forced to make a desperation trade for Kirk Cousins to fix its own issue at the position is being asked to guide the Giants on an eight-week run that will — hopefully — end with the Giants winning their first NFC East title since 2011.

The 23-year-old has been impressive to date, and the Giants are 2-1 in his three starts. With the pressure and quality of competition about to ratchet up, will that continue.

Questions have been asked about how much head coach John Harbaugh trusts McCarthy after the Washington loss, in which the Giants called a quarterback run that failed on a key third-and-3 at the two-minute mark, forcing a punt and leading to the Commanders' game-winning drive. McCarthy has been questioned for, with receivers open underneath and only a field goal being needed to tie the game, throwing a deep shot to Malik Nabers that was intercepted near the end of the Washington game when he had receivers open underneath.

"I think he's proven that he's capable of winning games and making the plays, and I probably feel more strongly about that now than ever," Harbaugh said. "We're definitely in the process of growing that confidence for sure. I mean, I understand what you're saying, for sure. But he's earned that right. I'm excited about where he's going."

Harbaugh said McCarthy has been "playing winning football."

"I want him to play as good as he can possibly play. I want him to be confident. I want him to be aggressive. I want him to be smart. I want him to complete passes. I want him to handle all the blitz checks. I just want him to continue to play the best football he can play," Harbaugh said. "I'm not sitting there like a scout saying he can work on his plant foot or his follow-through. We look at that stuff, the coaches will. All you really focus on is, let's go play the best game we can possibly play. Let's play a winning football game.

"He's been playing winning football. I'm really happy with the way he's playing. Okay, let's play winning football and go win the game. There's going to be some plays that he'll have a chance to make. He'll make most of them. He won't make them all. But I hope he makes the ones that make a difference in the game. That's probably the biggest thing for any quarterback."

McCarthy explained this week what "winning football" means to him.

"Winning the game is the number one thing, and there's a bunch of stats, traits, how a quarterback would play that would contribute to winning the game, and that's all about decision making, timing, accuracy, and just being smart, and knowing that you have the program in your hands every single play when you touch that ball and making the best decisions possible," he said. "That's the number one thing I think makes winning football, and I try to do that to the best of my ability every single week."

The numbers show a McCarthy who has clearly been better in three starts and one real relief appearance as a Giant than he was in 10 starts with Minnesota last season.

Offensive coordinator Matt Nagy said McCarthy's growth since coming to the Giants is "real."

"We need to make sure that we continue to just keep supporting him like we do every week with the play calls, the play designs, the players in the right spot. Let him play fast. He's done that," Nagy said. "Winning helps, and learning through experiences, too. The more we can get wins, the more experiences we get. He's a positive kid. He likes to stay positive. When he's out here at practice, he's juiced up all the time, which I love. He's got great energy. And then on game day, he turns that energy into a calm. So, just continue to get stuff on tape so that he can kind of see where we all grow as coaches with him and him with us."

McCarthy certainly shouldn't be afraid of the big moments to come. He is no stranger to big games. McCarthy won state and national championships in high school. He won a national championship in college at Michigan.

"When it comes to games in late November, when you're getting closer and closer to playoffs, the attention to detail, the urgency to really make sure you're doing your one-eleventh on every single play means the most, and especially against great teams like Jacksonville," McCarthy said.

Backup quarterback Jameis Winston, in his 12th NFL season, said it will be important for McCarthy to not try and do too much on his own.

"The most important thing about big moments is keeping it simple and doing what's required," Winston said. "It's not having an unreal game, but it's being consistent and giving the best chance to win.

"We're going to make sure we do everything possible to support him. That's a collective team thing … we view it as togetherness and how we're going to rally together to win football games."

NFL teams using their third starting quarterback by midseason — earlier in the Giants' case — don't typically make playoff runs. Just three years removed from being the 10th overall pick in the 2025 NFL Draft, and with those high school and collegiate national titles on his resume, McCarthy doesn't come with the typical "who the heck is that guy?" third quarterback pedigree.

"Getting to know him better, and his personality, his attitude, you can see why he's done that, why he's won at every level that he's been on and everywhere he's been," center Bryan Hudson said. "Knowing who he is, he's the same guy every day, which is awesome and what you want to see. That's what gives us a chance every time he steps on the field."

As the competition ramps up and the games get more important, we are about to find out if McCarthy and the Giants will be up to the challenge.
