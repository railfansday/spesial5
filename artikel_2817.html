No. 5 Texas vs. Arkansas final score: Horns blow out Hogs in 45-3 win

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It's Senior Day on the Forty Acres as quarterback Arch Manning plans to participate in what could be his final regular-season home game at Darrell K Royal-Texas Memorial Stadium as the No. 5 Texas Longhorns host the Arkansas Razorbacks.

An SEC championship game and College Football Playoff berth are on the line for Steve Sarkisian's team, which lost its margin for error with back-to-back losses to unranked opponents in October before surviving a home test against then-No. 7 Ole Miss and road challenges at then-No. 18 Missouri and then-No. 21 LSU.

Now Texas is hoping for the type of comfortable victory that has eluded the team in SEC play. At 2-5 in the conference, Arkansas represents a prime opportunity for one, although the Hogs proved how dangerous they can be in taking advantage of an injury to Gamecock quarterback LaNorris Seller to beat then-No. 17 South Carolina 34-6 last week.

Listed as questionable on the first three SEC-mandated player availability reports, Texas junior edge Colin Simmons was ruled out on Saturday in the report released 90 minutes before kickoff.

Senior cornerback Warren Roberson was upgraded from probable to available, while sixth-year offensive guard Laurence Seymore is a game-time decision.

Arkansas won the toss and elected to receive the start the game. After a touchback, a holding penalty drawn by Texas sophomore defensive end Lance Jackson put Arkansas behind the chains, but a positive run and completion put the Razorbacks in 3rd and 4. Initially ruled a completion at the sticks as KJ Jackson got the throw off under pressure, but review overturned the call to force a punt by Arkansas.

A short punt led to Ryan Niblett getting hit trying to field the ball, a 15-yard penalty that allowed Texas to start its first possession just short of midfield. Manning opened the game throwing it, completing a 16-yard pass to junior wide receiver Cam Coleman, a shorter pass to junior wide receiver Emmett Mosley V that went for five yards, and a screen to junior Ryan Wingo that was going to force Texas to convert a third down, but instead resulted a hands to the face penalty on the perimeter that produced another 15 yards in penalty yardage for the Longhorns.

Manning kept it on a zone read, but lost a yard as the play that stretched. A push pass to Wingo worked better, as the speedy wide receiver was able to take the edge, cut upfield, and pick up 12 yards to the 3-yard line. Trying to throw a play-action pass with Manning rolling to his right was unsuccessful, as the Texas quarterback had to throw the ball away. In a two-back formation, Manning kept a zone read and followed blocks by Mosley and fifth-year tight end Michael Masunas for a three-yard touchdown run and a 7-0 lead.

A tough run on first down by Sutton Smith gained four yards, but his second-down effort was stopped from the back side by senior defensive end Colton Vasek. On 3rd and 6, Jackson's intended receiver bobbled the catch and senior safety Jelani McDonald was in the right place at the right time, coming up with the interception and seeing nothing but open field in front of him on a 31-yard return and a 14-0 lead, bolstering McDonald's late-season Jim Thorpe Award campaign.

The interception was McDonald's fourth in the last two games.

After a first-down incompletion, KJ Jackson rumbled up the middle for a 12-yard gain. Smith ran for five yards on the next play, then Jackson connected with Chris Marshall up the sideline, and Marshall was able to make Roberson miss to gain 28 yards. Three runs by Cam Settles picked up another first down, including a big hole created on 3rd and 7 for seven yards. On first down, however, Sutton was stuffed, and Jackson had to settle for an underneath completion on second down for four yards. Facing 3rd and 6, Jackson tried to target Marshall on a slant, but cornerback Kade Phillips had sticky coverage, and Arkansas settled for a 30-yard field goal.

The next Texas possession fizzled out quickly after a run and catch by Brown — his third-down effort was blown up behind the line of scrimmage, and the punt unit committed a delay of game penalty.

A good play by sophomore defensive lineman Justus Terry stopped Arkansas for a two-yard run on first down, but a big hole in the Texas zone coverage afforded Jackson an easy completion over the middle for 16 yards. Jackson opted to snap it before the first quarter ended and it almost resulted in a disaster as Jackson won with an inside move and forced a fumble recovered by the Razorbacks.

On 3rd and 11, the Longhorns only rushed four, and while Jackson was able to get out of the pocket to his right without any pressure, the coverage held long enough to force Jackson to take a five-yard gain and the Arkansas punt unit to take the field, pinning Texas at its 5-yard line on a fair catch by Niblett.

A sixth-yard run by Terry create some space before Mosley found a seam on a screen pass to the boundary behind good blocking, including from senior right tackle Adnre Cojoe, for a 41-yard gain. Another explosive play was produced by the individual effort of Bishop, who spun out of a tackle attempt and ran through another 18 yards.

Wingo ran for five yards on a reverse with a nice upfield cut and Terry took three carries for 14 yards, but Manning couldn't hit Coleman in the end zone and then took a sack on third down when Cojoe got knocked backwards by a defensive tackle who was mostly just trying to set up a twist for his defensive end. A 37-yard field goal by senior Gianni Spetic made it 17-3.

Texas gave up a chunk play on a wheel route by Breylon Russell that either wasn't carried up the sideline or didn't have a safety get over the top of it, gaining 23 yards. A catch and run by Marshall went for 13 yards after Russell was stuffed. Sophomore nickel back Graceson Littleton delivered a big hit on Settles on the next play, but it came at the end of a seven-yard gain. Hoping for undisciplined play by Texas, Arkansas called an end around, but the Longhorns played it well for no gain, Jackson was stopped for a one-yard gain on third down, and then threw incomplete on fourth down, a drop by Russell after Jackson rolled to his left.

After taking over on its own 30-yard line, Manning hit Mosley on an arrow route for four yards, Brown showed some patience and leg drive to gain four yards up the middle, and Manning bounced a designed run behind effective blocks by Masunas, Wingo, and Coleman for 10 yards. A screen to Wingo only picked up two yards, but Manning hung in the pocket long enough to throw a dime to Mosley on a corner route for 29 yards, but the defender punched at the ball enough to force a review that upheld the call on the field.

Working from a clean pocket, Manning delivered another accurate throw, this time to sophomore tight end Emaree Winston, who ran a post route behind a corner by Mosley that put the safety in conflict — when the safety took Mosley, Winston was wide open for a 21-yard touchdown, the first of his career, as the linebacker .

Winston's touchdown catch put Texas up 24-3.

At the two-minute timeout, Arkansas had a nine-yard reception upheld on review, setting up 2nd and 1 from its 34-yard line. Two chunk completions moved the ball well into Texas territory, where the Longhorns held, sacking Jefferson on 3rd and 6 to force a 44-yard field goal attempt that missed wide left.

Trying to score before halftime, Wingo got open for a 29-yard reception and a 22-yard catch that showed off his ability to extend outside the framework of his body. When Bishop got out of bounds on a short reception to stop the clock, Sarkisian dialed up a fake swing pass to Brown that turned into a tunnel screen, gashing Arkansas up the middle as three offensive linemen struggled to find work, allowing Mosley to score from 17 yards out and extending the lead to 31-3.

Unfortunately for Texas, a late-half throw by Jackson resulted in a knee injury sustained by Longhorns junior safety Kobe Black, who had to be helped off the field before the final two plays of the half.

To open the second half, a screen pass to Brown picked up 18 yards, but to extend the drive, Manning had to vacate the pocket on 3rd and 11 when Terry missed his assignment in blitz pickup, but was able to find Mosley over the middle. Two short runs to freshman running back Derrek Cooper forced the Horns to convert a 3rd and 6, another completion to Mosley, who went out of bounds just short of the line to gain. Facing 4th and 1, Terry took a carry up the middle and drove his legs through contact, picking up eight yards.

Dialing up a jet reverse flea flicker throwback screen, Brown worked behind some effective blocks and navigated a lot of traffic down to the goal line, but didn't quite have enough juice to finish it.

After the play, Cojoe needed medical attention on the field for a possible cramp. Cojoe was able to walk off the field under his own power.

In the team's jumbo package, Terry had an easy walk into the end zone behind freshman tight end Charlie Jilek, taking over for Winston as the short-yardage fullback. It was Jilek's first collegiate snap.

The first-team Texas defense remained on the field as Arkansas moved the ball steadily until the Longhorns were able to force a 3rd and 9, an incompletion forced by senior cornerback Bo Mascoe with physical coverage. Staying on the field, the Razorbacks nearly pulled off a big passing play down near the goal line that was ruled incomplete.

Surprisingly, the play wasn't reviewed, and Texas took over with Manning remaining in the game, handing it off to Brown, who had a nice crease to exploit for eight yards on first down. Hurried on second down after an injury timeout, Manning threw a rare incompletion, but Terry was able to convert in short yardage again.

On 3rd and 16, Manning scrambled for 13 yards, allowing the offense to stay on the field to try to move the chains. Working from a bunch formation, Winston made a good catch outside his framework for a 24-yard gain, but the sophomore tight end was called for offensive pass interference on a questionable call that resulted in a punt that bounced into the end zone.

The defense started to work some backups in on the Arkansas drive, including junior defensive back Xavier Filsaime making his season debut. Seventh-year linebacker Darius Snow also saw playing time on defense for the first time since the season opener. The group played well enough to hold Arkansas on 4th and 6, allowing the Longhorns to take over at their own 33-yard line.

Manning departed with about 12 minutes left in the fourth quarter as sophomore KJ Lacey completed first first attempt to Bishop and Cooper gained four yards. Needing to convert 3rd and 2, Lacey checked down to Cooper, who picked up the first down behind a block from Winston. Looking for a shot play, Lacey took a hit while delivering a strike to fifth-year wide receiver Sterling Berkhalter, who made the catch at full stretch, gaining 35 yards.

A good run off the edge by Cooper gained five yards; another went for four. On 3rd and 1, it took a second effort by Cooper to secure the yard to gain. Still running well, Cooper had a clean hole, running through a defender to stretch for the goal line on the eight-yard touchdown run to make it 45-3.

On Senior Day, senior linebacker Brady Sarkisian got in on kickoff coverage, his second snap of the season.

With the Arkansas backup quarterback in the game, Marshall made a 14-yard reception on 3rd and 10, but a holding call eventually put them behind the chains, a situation that sophomore safety Jonah Williams took advantage of with a tackle in the flat. Trying to throw it on 3rd and 8, Hank Hendrix was sacked by the turf monster. The Razorbacks went for it on 4th and 18 and freshman cornerback John Meredith came up with his first career interception near the goal line, producing a gift for Bevo on the other side of the field.

Emptying the bench of Jabbar Juluke's running back room, sophomore James Simon received five carries to end the game at 45-3.
