A Comprehensive Guide to the Madness of <i><em>The Hunting Wives </em></i>Season 1

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Here’s everything you need to know ahead of the second-season drop on Netflix this Thanksgiving

“Melodramatic” barely grasps the intense emotional roller coaster of The Hunting Wives. The Netflix soap opera/erotic thriller introduced the world to the small town of Maple Brook, Texas, where the world revolves around a small group of eccentric, gun-toting divas who disproportionately find themselves at the center of sinful controversies that range from infidelity to murder.

Inspired by May Cobb’s novel by the same name, The Hunting Wives follows Sophie O’Neil (Brittany Snow), a bright-eyed transplant from Massachusetts who moves to Maple Brook with her husband, Graham (Evan Jonigkeit), and young son. This is partly because Sophie accidentally killed someone during a night of drunk driving. But now, Graham works for Jed (Dermot Mulroney), the richest man in town, who is gearing up for a gubernatorial run. His wife, Margo Banks (Malin Akerman), is the hypnotizing beating heart of this small town, along with her crew of strong-willed “hunting wives.” Upon meeting her, Sophie is immediately bewitched by her stoic demeanor and magnetic charm.

Margo’s inner circle includes several shit-talking, heavy-drinking wives who spend their days gossiping or shooting boars. Two of Margo’s small army rank above the rest: Jill Thompson (Katie Lowes), the pastor’s wife who loves her 18-year-old basketball prodigy son, Brad (George Ferrier), a bit too much, and Callie, the sheriff’s wife, who is fiercely (delusionally?) loyal to Margo. Margo and Callie’s love is very much not platonic, but it’s repeatedly jeopardized, especially because Margo is enamored with Sophie. In short, Callie hates (and I mean hates) Sophie.

To understand the show, one thing needs to be made abundantly clear: Everyone is horny, and anything is fair game. Or almost anything. At the center of Maple Brook’s salacious tangle of relationships is Jed and Margo’s (mostly) open marriage. The two of them can sleep with as many women as they want, and I mean only women (a misogynistic arrangement, yes, but this is Deep South Texas, after all). This allows Jed as many trysts as he wants and permits Margo’s secret romance with Callie. This rule, however, doesn’t really stop her from doing whatever she wants when she wants. This is deadly apparent because she’s full-blown romantically involved with (checks notes) her friend Jill’s teenage son, Brad.

The first part of The Hunting Wives finds thrills in socialites at each other’s throats. They toss dagger eyes, pair them with biting insults, and let lust dictate most (if not all) of their decisions. Then, a real tragedy rattles the town. Brad’s real (age-appropriate) girlfriend, the sweet Abby Jackson (Madison Wolfe), is found shot in the head in the forest near the town lake. Many viable suspects live in this crime-ridden town: Pete (Paul Teal), the youth pastor who cares too much about his teens; Jill, viciously protective of her son; and Margo, who, as I mentioned, is sleeping with teenage Brad. The cops, however, only find signs pointing to the unassuming Sophie O’Neil, who spent the night at Margo’s nearby the night before and whose gun was confirmed as the murder weapon. (Uh-oh!)

The night of Abby Jackson’s murder, she leaves the lake party mad at Brad, who failed to show up because, well, he’s having sex with Margo. As the audience, we also learn that two prime suspects roam nearby: An angry Jill left the house to confront Abby, and creepy Pastor Pete parked his car nearby where he was … masturbating to a photo of Abby. But Margo invites Sophie over to her lake house for a night of fun. Without telling Sophie, Margot also invites Brad and his friend, Jamie (Chosen Jacobs), another 18-year-old who has the hots for Sophie—what a disaster! That night, Margo and Sophie share a kiss during Spin the Bottle, sparking one of the most co-manipulative relationships in TV history. On top of it all, they mix Xanax and alcohol, so no one remembers anything. This is troubling when Sophie wakes up alone on the dock with no memory and no gun.

We know Sophie’s gun was used to kill Abby—and she certainly had opportunity. But she had no motive whatsoever. Margo, on the other hand, has all three; Abby discovered Brad and Margo’s affair and threatened to expose it (an affair that, of course, had yielded an unplanned pregnancy and a subsequent abortion). And, in a frantic attempt to keep her wealthy lifestyle intact, she shoots Abby. But that was only the beginning. Margo first attempts to frame Sophie for the murder, but because she holds a soft spot for her, she instead decides to frame Jill, planting Abby’s phone in her house and twisting the story to her liking.

At this point, it’s essential to talk about what happened to Abby’s mother, Starr, who is often bullied by the popular clique of high-powered women. After Abby dies, Starr thinks Brad is the culprit, having seen the two of them engaged in a fight, but she changes her mind after she’s incorrectly told Abby’s had an abortion (it was Margo), and she thinks that Jill killed Abby to protest Brad. That’s when Starr, in a fury, drives to Jill’s with a shotgun in tow, only to get mowed down by the gun-toting pastor’s wife in an act of self-defense.

Jill emerges victorious from her spar with Starr, but it doesn’t last long. The cops start looking at her as the prime suspect because she was the last incoming call on Abby’s phone (and she had a motive). Margo, obviously knowing that’s not the case, accompanies Callie to confront Jill anyway, only to find Starr dead in the kitchen. That’s when Jill pulls a gun on them (this is crazy, I know), and Callie shoots first, killing her. This is also, in some way, a twisted act of love.

As all the shooting is going on, an off-duty cop follows a red herring in the form of Pastor Pete. (Yes, everyone is a little evil in Maple Brook, but he might be the most sinister resident of them all.) Deputy Wanda Salazar (Karen Rodriguez) has a feeling that the pastor isn’t who he says he is and is hiding a dark past. Thinking he is Abby’s murderer, she chases him down, only to find that he’s kidnapped two other girls, Kaycee and Nina, hauling them in a trailer behind his car. What the hell is in the water in this town!?

By the eighth episode, it seems Sophie has perhaps drunk too much of this Maple Brook water. She pieces together that Margo is the killer—and that she manipulated the situation to frame her for murder. Instead of going straight to the authorities (like any normal person might), she confronts Margo. This, of course, gives Margo enough time to alert her crazy, crack-smoking brother Kyle (Michael Aaron Milligan), who pops up as a nuisance throughout the series. Taking on the role of a protective older brother, Kyle tries to run Sophie off the road and intimidate her. What does she do? Run him over and kill him … so, no use going to the authorities anymore. In true Maple Brook fashion, she hides the body instead. And, yes, after all that, we’re ready for season 2, because now everyone’s a killer.

We will see what’s next for this sinister Texan town on November 26.
