Liverpool’s £400m front line clicks but win at Brentford turns on red card

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Only time will tell if Liverpool’s £400m front line can ever justify the extraordinary amount of money that was spent on them, but this was at least a significant step in the right direction. In a gritty victory away to Brentford, all four of Alexander Isak, Florian Wirtz, Bradley Barcola and Victor Muñoz either scored or assisted goals.

Admittedly, the task was made significantly easier by the major turning point of this game: a red card for Brentford’s Yehor Yarmoliuk. At the time of Yarmoliuk’s “headbutting action” towards Liverpool defender Jeremy Jacquet, the visitors were trailing 1-0 and looking worryingly short of ideas in attack.

But as the context of the match changed, so did the level of threat from the visitors. Against 10 men, with more space in which to operate, Liverpool’s forwards looked increasingly dangerous. Wirtz began to pull the strings, Barcola started to cause problems, Muñoz stretched the Brentford defence and Isak became a lurking menace.

For Andoni Iraola, who had failed to beat Brentford in six attempts as manager of Bournemouth, it would have made for an immensely pleasing sight. After last weekend’s thrashing by Manchester City, Liverpool have responded with a 6-3 Champions League win over LASK and this victory against a previously unbeaten Brentford. Iraola will know, however, that his team’s numerical advantage was the fundamental reason for the dramatic shift in the flow of this game.

The Gtech Community Stadium simmered with outrage following Yarmoliuk’s red card, which came in the eighth minute of first-half stoppage time, and the home fans probably had a point. Yes, there was a movement of the midfielder’s head towards Jacquet, but it was hardly an act of violence. Jacquet’s reaction, collapsing to the turf in mock agony, was embarrassing but predictably effective. Cynics would say the Frenchman changed the game for his team. Purists would argue this is no way in which to influence a match.

Keith Andrews, the Brentford head coach, chose against complaining too aggressively. But he did make it clear what he thought of Jacquet’s behaviour. “I do think they were cute and clever,” Andrews said. “He is walking into Yarmo. But we need to look at ourselves and I understand why it was a red. Yarmo is an amazing young man and he will learn from this.”

Brentford had been the better team before this decisive incident and they deserved their lead, given to them by a majestic header by Keane Lewis-Potter, from Igor Thiago’s cross. It was a moment of attacking quality that Iraola’s glitzy attackers had looked unlikely to replicate, especially when Barcola fluffed a one-versus-one showdown with Brentford goalkeeper Caoimhin Kelleher.

To play an entire half of Premier League football with only 10 men is a brutal examination, though, and Brentford were unable to pass the test. Wirtz unleashed Muñoz after an hour, allowing the Spaniard to fire home with his left foot, before Barcola’s header found Isak in the box with 20 minutes remaining.

It was Isak’s fifth goal of the campaign, which means he has now beaten last season’s tally of four. He has scored in all five of his Premier League appearances against Brentford. “I didn’t have too many chances,” Isak said. “I was waiting for my moment.”

Any hopes of a Brentford comeback evaporated in stoppage time, when the home side lost another man to a red card. This time it was Vitaly Janelt, who received his second yellow for fouling Rio Ngumoha, Liverpool’s sparkling teenage winger. From there, Liverpool’s attempts to score a third were comically poor. “We lacked some composure,” admitted Iraola. “But it was a good battle today, and very important points for us. To take three points here is very valuable.”

An encouraging result for Liverpool, then, but this was not an afternoon without concerns. The first-half showing was worrying, and there were also injuries to Dominik Szoboszlai and Jeremie Frimpong. Both players limped off, and Iraola said they will now be out “for some time”.

The loss of Szoboszlai will be a particular concern, as he was rushed back from a hamstring injury for this game but then felt the same muscle again after only 32 minutes of action. “It’s a very delicate situation,” said Iraola.

The injury to Frimpong meant that Muñoz, a winger, played most of the second half as an attack-minded right-back. He did so excellently, and took his goal with impressive composure after Wirtz had pushed the ball into his path. Liverpool’s second was similarly clinical, as Isak pounced on Barcola’s knock-down in a congested penalty area. These, clearly, are the moments of game-changing quality that Liverpool expect from their big-money forwards. Iraola will hope it was a sign of things to come.

Speaking to Sky Sports after his first Premier League win as Fulham head coach, Arbeloa said: “I’m so happy, happy for my players, happy for my supporters, happy for everyone at the club that they deserve this victory. We [waited] a lot of time and some many games that I think we deserved this victory before, but today we did a very good game and yes, I can’t believe that the game finished just 1-0.”
