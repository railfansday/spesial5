Alexander Isak starts to justify £125m price tag with double

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

There was a centre-forward who played for Newcastle a couple of years ago. Do you remember him? He was as quick as greased lightning, played on his instincts, had a shot like cannon and could come up with a finish for every situation.

Everyone wanted him, but Liverpool signed him for £125m in August 2025. His name was Alexander Isak but shortly after arriving on Merseyside, it was like he went missing, as the guy who played in red last season bore only a physical resemblance to the guy who had played in black-and-white.

If you had wondered what had happened to him, he reappeared at Portman Road. They say good things come to those who wait. Although nobody at Liverpool would have expected such a delay to see the real Isak, there were no complaints that his career at the club has finally taken off.

Taking the headlines from Bradley Barcola on a big night at Portman Road was going to take some doing, but Isak did just that with a double salvo in the opening nine minutes, a flurry so potent it was like a boxer charging out of their corner and seeing the first blows land flush on their opponent’s jaw.

Poor Ipswich. There is universal recognition that Liverpool are a team – and a club – in transition but they had four players in Isak, Florian Wirtz, Cody Gakpo and Victor Muñoz who had the potential to dismantle the hosts if they clicked and that is precisely what happened.

“With Alex, it is clear,” said Andoni Iraola. “If he is fit, if he is available, if he is in a good shape, he will produce the numbers. My demand with Alex will not be about the goals. It will be about other things so we can play well. And if we play well, we are in good positions. Then goals will come.”

So for all the noisy backing they received from the stands and for all the effort Gary O’Neil’s side put into a contest that TV companies had chosen to broadcast because there was a potential for drama, Isak blew a hole in Ipswich’s hull in the sixth minute and they were submerging from there on in.

How Isak, who had scored against Nottingham Forest last weekend, needed a night like this when he made a genuine difference. But the same was true, too, for Iraola. We live in an era where someone has to be in the eye of a storm and the Basque will know what comes with his new territory.

It had clearly irritated Iraola that Liverpool had gone behind four times in their opening two fixtures and during his pre-match media briefing, he effectively gave a sermon at one point on how slow starts, or giving the opposition a helping hand, would no longer be tolerated.

Quietly, Iraola is making his presence felt. The task he faces is significantly different than the one O’Neil is accomplishing – keeping Ipswich in the division against the odds – but Liverpool’s head coach is a man who knows his own mind and is not afraid to make decisions.

He had responded to criticism of his midfield at Newcastle on the opening weekend by dropping Ryan Gravenberch for the fixture with Forest and his response to scrutiny about his right-back seven days ago was to drop Jeremie Frimpong and bring in Ronald Araújo.

Iraola certainly picked the right team: Araújo, the loan capture from Barcelona, added stature to the back four and there was harmony in how they moved. The Uruguayan’s first instinct was to stay back and Liverpool looked better for it, even if O’Neil, incredibly, felt Ipswich had the better of things.

Emersonn, Ipswich’s Brazilian striker, did not see the ball in the opening exchanges and, by the time he did, Liverpool had zoomed into the distance to such an extent that you felt it was going to be impossible for them to peg their visitors back.

“I hate having to talk about good performances when you do not get points,” O’Neil declared to a room full of raised eyebrows and you can only assume his message was to ensure that morale in the dressing room was not drooping.

What damage was inflicted with Liverpool’s first significant attack. Gakpo, the subject of an £80m bid from Manchester City, was never going to allow transfer speculation to disrupt his focus and he nudged a perfect little ball into Isak’s path in the sixth minute to show his wellbeing.

The next move announced the return of Isak. One touch gave him an avenue to shoot, the next was a shot with his right foot that was so hard from 16 yards, on an angle, it bent the wrists of Kjell Scherpen as it howled into the net. Critics will feel Ipswich’s keeper should have done better.

Your first thought was to think of the first goal Isak scored in English football in September 2022, a similarly venomous drive, against Liverpool at Anfield. He oozed quality that evening, his pace and skill had Jürgen Klopp’s defenders persistently fretting and this is what he was doing again.

It was no coincidence that three minutes later, he came up with another wonderful goal. This one owed much to the beautiful pass Gakpo presented to him, an inch-perfect through ball with the outside of his right foot, but Isak still showed great poise to bamboozle Scherpen.

“He owes me a few assists now!” chirped the selfless Gakpo.

A hat-trick could have arrived in the 32nd minute but Isak was a kneecap offside as he dispatched a pass from Wirtz and, perhaps, he will have been disappointed to have made way on the hour having not secured the match ball for his efforts.

Liverpool, too, will be frustrated that they did not made sure of three points before the time Isak made way for Barcola – what Alexis Mac Alliser was thinking when he fluffed an unmarked header from six yards, only he will know – but it was not a night to nitpick.

There will be easier assignments on the horizon for Ipswich, even though O’Neil insisted they more than held their own against world-class opponents. And you can say for sure there will be more goals and wins for Liverpool and Isak if they continue in this encouraging manner.

“I witnessed what a great coach he was at Bournemouth. He is not the sort of manager you are going to see making clever patterns in his box. He just does not see the risk. He will want to get the ball to the players who want to make a difference. Coaches do not show how good they are by how they play out from the back. The important thing is when you get it in the final third, you make things happen. That is what he will want to improve. He will want Alexander Isak when he has not got the ball to press. He will want the same of Florian Wirtz. The difficulty will come with Champions League football and not putting too many demands on his players. If he gets it right, he has got a wonderful squad to work with.”

He had not played any competitive minutes since the World Cup so we had to be a little bit more careful.”

The third matchday of the new Premier League season kicks off at Portman Road as newly-promoted Ipswich host Liverpool.

Andoni Iraola has drawn his first two games in charge of Liverpool, both 2-2 stalemates against Newcastle and Nottingham Forest respectively. Liverpool secured a move for forward Bradley Barcola from PSG in the final few days on the window for a fee that could rise to £123m and the Frenchman is set to make his Liverpool debut tonight.

“We are very happy with Bradley,” Iraola said. “He was our priority; to sign a very good player in that position. He has come with the right mentality. I like that he wanted Liverpool a lot, he just wanted to come here. That is a good first sign. Being the player he is and the things he has won recently, he has come with the willingness to help us. I am very happy with him.

“He trained well with us, and if everything goes well in today’s [Thursday] training, he will definitely be available for tomorrow [tonight]. He has trained with us three times. He has not had minutes in pre-season but he has been training normally with the group. It is a decision we have to make. We have to consider that he has not had minutes in pre-season.”

Liverpool won both fixtures when Ipswich were last in the Premier League two seasons ago, including a 2-0 win on the opening weekend in Arne Slot’s first game in charge of Liverpool as they went on to win the Premier League that season.

Ipswich won their first home game on their return to the Premier League, beating Sunderland 2-1 on the opening weekend. However, they fell to a 5-2 defeat in their first away game at Manchester United. Over the course of the summer transfer window, Ipswich made a total of 14 signings and spent over £200m.

“What we can do is perform in a way that always gives us a real chance, which we have done so far,” Ipswich boss Gary O’Neil said. “We were in that game at Manchester United until a couple of moments took it away from us. We were in it, so that is what we need to do, we need to make sure that no matter who it is, whether it is Liverpool, Manchester United, Manchester City, when they come to Portman Road, we need to make sure we are right in the game to give ourselves opportunities. We will always be a threat; we have scored four goals in two games. That is really good for a promoted side. We look a goal threat, and we need to keep that going and that will be tested.”
