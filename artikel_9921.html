What gets lost in these stories is that data centers don’t burn electricity for its own sake. This isn’t just Instagram and TikTok; it’s payroll, inventory, banking, and healthcare records.

If that work weren’t done in hyperscale facilities, it would be done somewhere else: in smaller colos or on-prem server rooms, which are typically far less efficient per unit of work. Hyperscalers run at a PUE close to 1.1, while the average corporate server closet is much worse.

So the right question isn’t “how much power do data centers use?” It’s “how much power does this economic activity use, and is this the most efficient place to do it?” The study’s real findings are about the grid mix (mostly fossil) and the lack of disclosure. Those are fair criticisms, and both are fixable. But a Michigan-sized power bill for a large chunk of the digital economy isn’t obviously a scandal.
