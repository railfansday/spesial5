After James Gunn’s Shocking Departure, It Looks Like Clayface Will Be DC’s Last R-Rated Movie

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Shockingly, yet another major shake-up for DC films is coming with James Gunn and Peter Safran being ousted from their roles as the heads of DC Studios, and it seems likely that Clayface could very well be the last DCU movie of its kind.

After four years overseeing the new DCU, James Gunn and Peter Safran will be stepping away from their leadership positions. While they'll still be working on projects like Man of Tomorrow and The People vs. Gorilla Grodd, it's been announced that Marvel Studios veteran Jeremy Latcham will be taking over as the new, singular head of DC Studios. Naturally, this raises some pretty big questions about the future of the DCU and its overall vision, especially given what is known about Latcham.

Considering that Latcham has publicly discussed his preference for making movies accessible to broad, four-quadrant audiences, it feels very likely that Clayface may very well be the first (and last) R-rated DCU movie.

Announced on November 9, Latcham's appointment marks a pretty significant change for DC Studios. Having previously worked at Marvel Studios on films like Iron Man, The Avengers, Guardians of the Galaxy, and Spider-Man: Homecoming, Latcham's prior superhero history suggests that Skydance is looking for a major pivot, one that suggests a desire to see the DCU becoming more like the MCU. Meanwhile, Gunn and Safran are returning to full-time creative work, though it remains to be seen just how long they'll be involved with the DCU once their remaining projects currently in development are released.

However, the concerns with Latcham go beyond his Marvel background, as he recently voiced an ongoing focus on making sure films are appealing to as general an audience as possible. He's even cited a personal test in an interview with CBN, one he'd conduct during his early days with Marvel Studios. Apparently, one of his key guiding lights for creative decisions was asking himself whether his grandmother would enjoy certain scenes or narratives.

There are also concerns regarding Latcham's faith and more conservative values having a possible effect on future DCU projects, though that remains to be seen (and has arguably been blown out of proportion). What feels more concerning is that focus on wanting to appeal to all ages, suggesting that R-rated films like Clayface will likely face a major uphill battle to get made in the future that they would have under Gunn, not to mention shows with more mature ratings like Creature Commandos, Peacemaker, and Lanterns.

While nothing has been officially said to suggest that DC Studios will stop making R-rated content altogether, it feels entirely plausible based on Latcham's previous comments that Clayface could very well be the last theatrical DCU movie to receive an R-rating.

Ultimately, DC Studios shouldn't simply chase the exact same audience as Marvel Studios all the time. There's an advantage in offering something different, while also taking advantage of the major differences in its characters. Superman should offer hope and optimism and be appealing to everyone, but that doesn't mean DC Studios should shy away from the DC Universe's darkest corners, if only to benefit one of its biggest characters: Batman.

After all, Gotham is supposed to be one of the most dangerous cities in the entire DC Universe, full of some of the most brutal and horrifying villains in all fiction. The less those foes are sanitized, the better, which is why Clayface felt so encouraging for the DCU's future. The fact that an R-rated movie like Clayface exists in the same universe as Superman is a huge benefit.

DC Studios doesn't need every movie to be R-rated, nor should it sacrifice accessibility just for the sake of distinguishing itself from Marvel. That said, it does need room for all kinds of stories, with some that might not appeal to every single moviegoer. If Latcham's leadership does end up pushing DC Studios toward safer, four-quadrant entertainment across the board, Clayface will undoubtedly become a reminder of the creative possibilities left behind.
