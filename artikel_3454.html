I gave up on thermal brushes, then Wavytalk’s Steamlift Pro gave me salon volume in just 15 minutes

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

I had officially closed the book on volumizing hot brushes. Like half the internet, I fell victim to the Revlon One-Step craze a few years back. While it certainly had the smoothing factor down, it felt like it was actively ripping chunks of hair from my scalp with every pass.

My Dyson Airwrap attachment was undeniably gentler, sure, but it never quite delivered the rich, bouncy volume I was actually chasing. Eventually, I resigned myself to heatless curl rods to fake the blowout aesthetic — only to spend the rest of the day mourning the catastrophic amount of curl shrinkage.

Recently, I tested Wavytalk's Steam Sesh. After watching that flat iron tame my hair in 10 minutes flat, I haven’t shut up about steam tech to anyone within earshot. Naturally, my expectations for its sister tool, the Steamlift Pro, were high.

I’m relieved to report it didn’t just meet the bar, it completely cleared it.

The first sign of progress arrived before I even plugged it in. One minor gripe I had with the Steam Sesh was the trial-and-error setup, which practically forced you to scroll TikTok just to figure out how to seat the water tank properly.

Wavytalk clearly took notes. Right inside the box of the Steamlift Pro was a printed visual guide, removing any guesswork.

The mechanics will feel instantly familiar if you’ve used the straightener: pop out the removable tank, add water (distilled remains the smart play if you want to avoid mineral buildup), power it on, and turn to your target temperature. If you want that hit of hydration, hit the dedicated steam switch.

Down from $129 to $99, this 23% saving makes the Steamlift Pro a solid value. If you want bouncy, snag-free 90s volume with gentle steam tech instead of frying your curls on dry heat, grab it while it’s under $100.

Where hot air brushes usually fail curls is tension. Round blow-dry barrels rely on dense, stiff nylon bristles that grab too aggressively, catching on textured coils and snapping fragile strands mid-pull.

The Steamlift Pro swaps out that brutal bristle setup for an integrated 3D comb system built right into the body of the brush. Instead of violently tugging, the teeth firmly grip and separate each individual hair strand as the heated surface glides through.

For someone prone to breakage, the difference in hand-feel was immediate. There was zero scalp-tugging resistance, no terrifying crunch of snapping ends, and no tangled bird’s nest trapped around a rotating barrel. The steam continuously releases through the comb, softening coarse cuticle layers so the hair yields without brute physical force.

My routine was simple. I detangled thoroughly, split my hair into two manageable tiers (top and bottom), and worked in generous sections.

Rather than jumping straight into styling curls, I started by using the Steamlift Pro as a standard smoothing tool, gliding it straight down from roots to mid-lengths to stretch out my natural curl pattern.

Once the baseline was uniform, I went back through the ends to roll and bevel them under for that bouncy, layered finish.

The entire process took 15 minutes. My hair came out silky, glossy, and entirely frizz-free. Most importantly, I retained my actual length instead of watching several inches vanish.

Finding tools that respect coarse, curly, and fragile hair without requiring professional-level arm acrobatics is rare. At this stage in my hair routine, the Steamlift Pro is easily the most capable blowout styler I’ve tested. If you have thick, frizz-prone curls and have spent years burned by viral hot brushes, do not sleep on this steam lineup.
