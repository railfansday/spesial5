I Dug Through the Early Black Friday Cycling Sales—Here’s the Gear Actually Worth Buying Before Winter Hits

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Upgrade your winter rides with deals on cycling gloves, trainers, helmets, tires, lights, and more.

Gear-obsessed editors choose every product we review. We may earn commission if you buy from a link. How we test gear.

Fall riding season is coming to a close, and winter is right around the corner. While late fall and winter mean shorter daylight hours for riding, it’s a great time to save on cycling gear—for yourself or if shopping early for holiday gifts. Many brands are discounting old inventory at great prices, and others are clearing room for new gear hitting shelves after the New Year.

Although many of the best holiday sales aren’t until Black Friday and Cyber Monday, some retailers are running Early Black Friday promotions with big deals and discounts on top-rated gear. In addition to Pre-Black Friday sales, Amazon, REI, bike brands, and other retailers are offering deep discounts on cycling equipment, components, apparel, and accessories you need for road, gravel, and mountain bike rides.

Pre-Black Friday sales are a great chance to stock up on frequently used consumables—such as tubes, tire sealant, chain lube, and nutrition products—and to grab the gear you need for late-fall and winter rides. They’re also a great way to save money on usually pricey riding apparel: If you’ve been holding out on buying a new vest, arm warmers, jerseys, a jacket, or even shoes, some brands are offering deep discounts on cycling clothing and accessories.

Below are sales and deals hubs for several top online cycling gear and bike retailers, plus links to sales from leading clothing brands and bike companies. These are some of the best deals on editor-tested gear we’ve spotted so far. Bookmark this page and return for updates as even more deals come online.

Bike lights are a must-have item for anyone commuting or riding road or gravel in the winter months. Even if you already have a light, I’ve found it super useful to own an extra set to have at the office, on my other bikes, or as spares for friends.

Lezyne consistently makes great front and rear lights. While slightly pricier than budget picks found on Amazon or AliExpress, they’re durable, work well, and last several seasons. Plus, we’ve found Lezyne’s advertised runtimes and lumens are much closer to reality than many no-name lights.

Showers Pass makes our best-tested winter gloves, and they’re currently on sale at 19% off. In their review, our test rider said, “This knit, waterproof glove has a midweight feel and sufficient insulation when temperatures hover around freezing. Most riders will find the Crosspoints reach its limit at about 30-35 degrees, depending on how hard you’re riding.”

The glove traps some body heat, so your fingers may be slightly chilly at first but will warm up within 15 minutes. Better yet, when the mercury climbs, your hands won’t feel hot and sweaty.

Electric mini-inflators burst onto the scene a couple of years ago and have quickly become a rider favorite for pumping up your tires at home, at the trailhead, or on rides. Of the inflators we’ve tested, the RockBros model stands out for its consistency and is a favorite of Bicycling editors. It’s compact, lightweight, and has an accurate gauge. In our testing of 15 electric pumps, it inflated a road tire to 65 psi in 45 seconds, and filled five tires before running out of power.

Shorter daylight hours and lower temps often mean fewer hours spent riding outside on the road or trail. An indoor trainer helps you maintain your fitness through the winter months while getting your riding fix. Garmin’s Tacx Flux2 is the brand’s mid-range smart trainer; with 2,000 watts of resistance, it simulates gradients up to 16%. The Flux also connects to virtual platforms like Zwift and Rouvy for immersive indoor riding experiences.

Stainless bottles have become popular among cyclists over the past several seasons. They keep water chilled on hot rides and don’t give your water a funny plastic taste. Many gravel and off-road riders love CamelBak’s Stainless Podium, particularly for its open/closed twist-lock cap. The Podium Stainless also ranked best in our heat testing last summer and performed well in our winter bottle testing.

Elite road and gravel helmets offer low weight, excellent ventilation, and great looks. However, those benefits usually come at a high cost. Giro’s Agilis Mips helmet is a great option if you want a pro-level look and performance without the associated price

Weighing 299 grams (size medium), my Agilis is only 15 grams heavier than Giro’s premium Aries Spherical model—and the Agilis costs significantly less. The Agilis is very well-ventilated, comparable to the priciest lids on all but the hottest of rides.

This helmet features Mips technology to protect your brain from rotational forces in a crash, and Giro’s Hardbody co-molded shell to better protect it from small bumps and dings when not on your head. Plus, the Agilis uses the Roc-Loc dial system to ensure a proper fit.

MET’s Trenta 3K Carbon is one of the top five road helmets by Virginia Tech’s helmet lab. It’s also one of the best for racing and high-performance riding, as proven by UAE Team Emirates’s results—Tour de France champion Tadej Pogačar and World Champion Brandon McNulty. While UAE riders often race the Italian brand’s Manta aero helmet, they pick the lightweight, well-ventilated Trenta 3K Carbon for climbing stages and training.

Unlike some modern helmets with similarly strong protection, the Trenta feels light and racey on your head, and almost disappears when you’re riding. The straps offer good adjustability, and the Mips Air Node liner protects without feeling bulky. However, the Trenta’s biggest drawback is its price tag. Typically priced over $500, this is an expensive piece of kit, but this deal cuts the cost by almost a third.

Arm warmers are among the most versatile pieces of cycling clothing you can own. This magical piece of kit turns your short-sleeve jersey into a long-sleeve in seconds—and you can change back to short sleeves again mid-ride if the temps rise.

I’ve owned many pairs of arm warmers over the last three decades, and I rarely ride without them on my arms or in my jersey pocket if the temperatures are between 45 and 60 degrees. Castelli makes some of the best clothes out there, and these arm warmers are a steal for the price. They’re easy to pull on or off while riding, and they don’t sag.

A quality rain jacket is a must-have item for riders who live anywhere it rains or snows. Rapha’s Core Rain Jacket is great option for layering over your winter jersey without adding too much bulk. It features a two-way zip for regulating body temperature, reflective hits for riding after dusk or in the early morning, and packs down small if weather clears mid-ride.

If you ride in temps below 40 degrees, a good cycling jacket is a great asset. As a Scottish brand, Endura knows a few things about riding in the cold, dark days of winter, and its MT500 Freezing Point jacket is a great option for trail rides and commuting in low temps. It features PrimaLoft Gold insulation and a DWR finish to shed rain drops. Another great feature is the MT500’s underarm zippers; they allow you to cool off and ventilate without unzipping your entire jacket.

When cold weather hits, bib tights ensure you stay warm on rides. Of the many cycling tights available, Velocio make some of our test editors’ favorites. The One Thermal bibs feature a soft, fleece lining and use a more relaxed, less compressive fit than some of the brands other options. This makes them perfect for road, gravel, and trail rides, or even layering under commuter pants.

Instead of shredding your nice, lightweight road tires on weather-worn winter roads, swap them out for more durable rubber. Vittoria’s Rubino tires are a proven all-season road tire with a smooth, fast-rolling center and siped edges for cornering traction. They’re tubeless-compatible, so you’ll catch fewer flats in the cold, and come in widths from 26mm to 34mm with black or tan sidewalls.

Modern bike computers do much more than display speed and distance. These devices are a critical information hub for many cyclists. Cycling computers can record ride metrics, display real-time performance data, and help you navigate—but that’s only scratching the surface of their capabilities.

The Elemnt Bolt 3 is Wahoo’s tiny-but-mighty cycling-specific computer. Wahoo equips it with a 2.3-inch high-resolution screen, up to 20 hours of battery life, and integration with Strava, Komoot, and other popular cycling platforms.

Another great option is the Hammerhead Karoo. This head unit features a 3.2-inch color touchscreen Gorilla Glass display and up to 35 hours of run time. The Karoo stands out for its mapping and navigation. The navigation, combined with its pop-up hill climb feature and ability to sideload apps, makes the Karoo my overall favorite.

POC makes comfortable, well-ventilated helmets for road, gravel, and mountain biking. The Kortal is a trail-style helmet, featuring an adjustable visor, room to stow your glasses or goggles, and plenty of airflow for hot summer trail rides. The straps are also comfortable, something that often bugs me on other lids.

Fresh bar tape is the quickest and most cost-effective way to improve the look, feel, and comfort of any road or gravel bike. I’ve used Fizik tape on several bikes that have come in for testing, and have also bought it for my personal bikes. It has enough cushion, reliable grip, and is easy to wrap. You can’t ask for much more from bar tape.

While many riders are adding electric mini-inflators to their tool kits, a quality floor pump remains an essential part of every cyclist’s arsenal. They never run out of batteries, pump as many tires as your arms can handle, and are easy to use.

Riders and mechanics have trusted Topeak’s JoeBlow pumps for decades—because they work. Although many JoeBlow models are low-cost and utilitarian, the JoeBlow Ace DX is a Cadillac-level floor pump. It features alloy construction with a metal chuck and can inflate tires up to 260 psi, with three different inflation modes: high-volume, standard, or high-pressure.
