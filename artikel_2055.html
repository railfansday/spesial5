Welcome to the Newcastle United v Arsenal preview

Saturday 21st November and a 5.30pm kick-off.

Newcastle United host Arsenal on Saturday  looking to get a stop-start season back on track against the table-topping champions.

The Toon have picked up a disappointing five points from five home league matches so far this season having lost the last two without scoring a goal.

Saturday represents a huge chance to put that right against an Arsenal side who haven’t got the best of recent records on Tyneside.

A late kick-off allows some extra time in various Tyneside hostelries which usually contributes to a cracking atmosphere. Lets hope Toon fans will be loud, proud and celebrating come full-time .

Newcastle have won two, drawn one and lost three of their past six league matches. Wins have come over Hull City and Crystal Palace whilst we’ve drawn with Fulham. Frustratingly, we’ve also suffered deserved defeats against Coventry, Villa and Everton. We’ve scored six goals in these games and conceded eight. The seven points gained has us languishing in a disappointing twelfth league position but a win could fire us up the table.

Arsenal are the form team in the league right now having won five in a row. Since losing to Brighton in September, they’ve gone on to beat Leeds, Everton, Forest, Liverpool and Hull. They’ve conceded four goals in these matches and scored seven, they’ve kept four clean sheets in the row. They are currently five points clear at the top of the table and will be hoping to have extended that lead come Saturday night.

Newcastle travelled to Fulham before the international break hoping to achieve their fourth straight success in the capital after wins over Spurs, Millwall and Crystal Palace. United started brightly with Harvey Barnes forcing two good saves before Dan Burn thought he’d given us the lead only for his effort to be ruled out for a foul in the build-up from Wissa. At the other end, Hornicek was in fine form making a string of good saves as the sides went into the half-time break goalless. The second half continued in a similar vein with both sides sensing they could win and when the breakthrough was finally made, it was the Toon fans celebrating. Jacob Murphy picked up the ball and beat two men before unleashing an incredible strike into the top corner with just ten minutes to play. Unfortunately the party in the away end was to be very short-lived indeed as just a few minutes later, Fulham equalised to set up a grandstand finish where both teams had chances to grab a late winner. Lewis Hall was played in on goal on the stroke of ninety minutes only to see his weak effort easily stopped before Hornicek pulled off an incredible save to salvage a point for the Toon who had to be content with a 1-1 draw.

Arsenal hosted Hull City in their last match and were frustrated by the Tigers for much of the ninety minutes. They scored late on to seal a 1-0 victory and had cause for further celebration when Man City lost a few hours later. The champions are determined to maintain their grip on the trophy.

-Newcastle have won four of their last six home games against the Gunners in all competitions.

-NUFC have found the net in their last four home matches against Arsenal.

-The Toon have beaten Arsenal at home on nine Premier League occasions.

Graeme Souness was enduring a rotten start to the 05/06 season when Arsenal visited St James’ Park in December. The Toon had failed to score in their opening five matches of the campaign but the arrival of Michael Owen seemed to have brought about a change in fortunes with five wins and two draws in our next ten games. Despite this, Souness was on borrowed time and this match against the Gunners would end up being one of the highlights of his ill-fated reign as manager. Newcastle spent most of the first half on the back foot and were grateful to Shay Given for a number of saves (one in particular even being applauded from Arsenal striker Thierry Henry!) but the Toon were getting stuck in and this physical approach was rattling the Reds who weren’t used to not having their own way.

At the start of the second half, the match descended into a real blood and thunder encounter which proved too much for Arsenal midfielder Gilberto Silva who was sent off for two bookable offences. Arsenal were then furious that an Alan Shearer foul on Sol Campbell wasn’t punished with a card and decided to fight fire with fire leading to a disgraceful assault on Scott Parker from the Arsenal ‘keeper that left our man short of a couple of teeth! Incredibly Parker played on with a mouth full of cotton wool as he bravely battled for every ball until another Arsenal foul left him sprawled out on the turf signalling to be subbed off. By now, Newcastle really had the bit between their teeth (if Scott Parker can pardon the expression!) and the crowd could sense a goal was coming.

With just nine minutes left, Shearer’s physicality allowed him to hold the ball up on the edge of the area and lay it off to Solano who fired home a spectacular effort at the Leazes End to spark wild and ecstatic celebrations. Not for the first (or last) time, Arsenal proved they don’t like it up ‘em at St James’ and Febregas threw a strop when unwisely attempting to start a fight with Alan Shearer before the match had even restarted. As the away side huffed and puffed looking for an equaliser, it became apparent that this would be Newcastle’s day and Micheal Owen even had a chance to double the lead near the end that unfortunately he couldn’t take. The final whistle brought an outpouring of joy and relief as the Toon Army celebrated a Merry Christmas victory. A week later, we’d go to West Ham and a Micheal Owen hat-trick was the cornerstone of a brilliant 4-2 win. If Graeme Souness felt his luck was turning however then he had another thing coming. Owen would break his foot a week later and Souness was given the sack having achieved just one point from the next six matches. Glenn Roeder would come in and steady the ship with a tremendous end to a tumultuous season but that’s a story for another day. Newcastle 1-0 Arsenal.

Malcolm Macdonald – Some of our older readers will have fond memories of this legendary number nine. ‘Supermac’, as he was affectionately known by fans, kick-started his Toon career with a hat-trick in his first home match against Liverpool. What was to follow has gone down in Toon Army folklore! Scoring one hundred and twenty-one goals in just two hundred and twenty-seven appearances has him up there with the best of the best in NUFC history. Perhaps his finest hour came in the 1974 FA Cup Semi-Final when he scored two second half goals to defeat Burnley 2-0 at Hillsborough and send United to their first FA Cup final for nineteen years. Unfortunately, he couldn’t repeat this feat at Wembley as a tame performance was punished by a 3-0 defeat to a Kevin Keegan inspired Liverpool. There was uproar when he was sold to Arsenal for a third of a million pounds in 1976. He went on to score forty-two goals in eighty-four league games for the Gunners.

Andy Cole began his career by playing one league game for Arsenal before being loaned to Fulham and then sold to Bristol City. Kevin Keegan broke the club’s transfer record by splashing out £1.75 million for the striker in 1993. This is a number nine who’s record stands up to anyone else’s – a whopping sixty-eight goals in eighty-four matches before being controversially sold to Man United in January 1995 for a British record transfer fee. Some fans were so angry at the sale that they turned up at the stadium demanding answers which Keegan provided, famously standing on the steps to explain his decision. King Kev would go on to sign both Sir Les and Alan Shearer as the entertainers came ‘so close’ to being champions of England for the first time since 1927 and the debate still rages on about which of the three was the best number nine of NUFC’s Premier League era.

Sol Campbell was something of a legend at Arsenal having run down his contract at arch-rivals Tottenham to join the Gunners on a free transfer. He’d go on to win a number of trophies and even grabbed a goal in the 2006 Champions League final. He played a handful of games for NUFC in 2010 but it’s safe to say he was well past his best when donning the famous black and white.

Matthias Jaissle after a 1-1 draw away at Fulham: “A step in the right direction after the last performance. That’s something we can really build on.I think there were occasions also to win that game. Of course, the disallowed goal, a soft one, I would say. I see it differently then also a big chance at the end. Then you should go home with three points but one point and a performance is something we can work on. It’s now time to reflect and recharge also with a long season ahead of us, then pull out the things we need to improve and there are a couple of aspects, but today showed a clear direction in the right way so we can build on that. We did enough to win. We spoke clearly through the week and we saw that today, there was complete commitment and togetherness in all phases. The first 20 minutes there was a lack of confidence from last week but we grew through the game and that was excellent, something to build on.”

Mikel Arteta after a 1-0 win at home to Hull City:  “Not beautiful but effective. I’m delighted with the win because to go to the international break after winning is one of the best things for a coach or for a player. I think, regarding the performance, we were the better team. I think we had enough chances and situations to make the scoreline much bigger. We weren’t efficient enough to do that, and then we have to rely on generating volume, generating a lot of situations in and around the box until we are able to break them down.This is exactly how we won the game, and Martin, the way he scores, is not the most beautiful one but the most effective one, which is a simple shot and then following the next action, and that was enough today to win again.”

There can be no doubt that Newcastle have been poor recently despite showing some promising signs of improvement against Fulham last match. Games after an international break are notoriously difficult to predict. The bookies may be looking at this as something of a formality but we seem to do a lot better against the bigger sides and have a great recent record at home against the Gunners. I was thinking this may be a score draw but my confidence has been growing throughout the week so I’m going for a wildly-celebrated win followed by a few sore heads on Sunday morning. Newcastle 2-1 Arsenal.
