Cathie Wood and Philippe Laffont Are on Opposite Sides of AMD Stock. Here's Who I Think Is Right.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

One is buying the chip stock after a 42% tumble. The other's fund had sold every share by Sept. 30.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 26, 2026 at 5:53AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Advanced Micro Devices ›

Two big-name tech investors have landed in different places on Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -1.75%). Cathie Wood's Ark Invest added almost 50,000 shares of the chipmaker on Friday, Nov. 20, when the stock dropped 13%. Philippe Laffont's Coatue Management, though, owned no AMD shares at all at the end of September, according to its newest quarterly filing.

The split follows a steep slide. AMD's record close was on Oct. 6, at $649.42. As of this writing, the stock trades near $376, off about 42% over seven weeks.

And in the third quarter, both investors sold. Only Wood has been reported buying back in since.

Coatue's 13F filings (the quarterly forms big money managers use to disclose their U.S.-listed stock holdings) show a fund that has exited AMD twice in 2026. It ended 2025 with around 1.24 million AMD shares, a stake then worth $265 million, and it ended March with none.

That first exit looks badly timed now. The growth stock closed the first quarter at around $203 and almost tripled by the end of June. Even after this fall's sell-off, the stock trades well above its highest first-quarter close of about $260.

Coatue did buy back in during that climb, ending June with about 96,000 shares worth about $56 million. But that was a small stake, roughly 0.1% of the $48.6 billion in holdings the filing showed.

And by Sept. 30, the position was gone again. AMD closed the third quarter at about $612, so the second exit has aged well, with shares off about 39% since.

A 13F shows holdings, not reasons. But Coatue's filing doesn't look like a pullback from chips. As of Sept. 30, the fund had over $3 billion each in Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing, Micron Technology, and Lam Research. Laffont still seems to be betting big on the artificial intelligence (AI) build-out. AMD just wasn't part of it when the quarter ended.

Ark has traded AMD in both directions this year. After buying shares in January and February, its funds sold AMD on dozens of days from late March to late October while the stock rose. The largest sale was on Sept. 29, around a week before the record close, when Ark sold 181,767 shares worth about $110 million.

Then the buying began again. Ark picked up almost 49,000 shares on Nov. 5, a day AMD dropped 17%, and 49,745 more on Nov. 20.

Valued at those days' closes, the two purchases came to about $41 million. Together, they equal a bit over half the shares Ark sold on Sept. 29 alone, bought back at closing prices 30% to 33% lower.

AMD is now around 2% of the flagship ARK Innovation ETF, a far bigger part of the fund than the stake Coatue reported in June.

The case for Wood's buy depends on how fast AMD's business is growing. Its data center segment, home to the company's Instinct AI chips and EPYC server processors, had record sales of $8.5 billion in the third quarter. Growth from the previous quarter has picked up all year, from around 7% in Q1 to 16% in Q2 and then 27% in Q3. Management expects about 50% sequential growth for the segment in the fourth quarter. And for 2027, it expects total revenue to climb over 80%, with data center making up more than 80% of sales.

"It is true that demand is higher than supply," CEO Lisa Su said during AMD's third-quarter earnings call.

Demand that tops supply is promising, but it also means AMD's outlook relies on getting enough parts to make all those chips. Preparing for that growth is a drag on cash flow. Inventory climbed around $3 billion from the second quarter to $11.5 billion, and free cash flow dropped 64% year over year to $554 million.

The price, meanwhile, has come down a long way. Measured against analysts' average 2027 estimate of around $17.60 per share, AMD's third-quarter close near $612 was almost 35 times earnings. The current price of $376 works out to about 21 times earnings on the same estimate.

Ark's September sale and Laffont's second exit both look well timed so far. But for someone buying now, I think Wood is right. I'd consider buying AMD shares at this price, and I'd only rethink that if supply issues put its 2027 outlook out of reach.
