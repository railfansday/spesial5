United Parks: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Advance pass sales for 2027 are up over 15%, a signal of visitor commitments that sits apart from the quarter's weather-driven attendance drop.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, United Parks (PRKS -0.15%) reported a 1.1% revenue decline to $506.5 million as attendance fell by 320,000 guests. Unfavorable weather eliminated 325,000 visits during the quarter, but pricing gains and per-visitor spending kept revenue resilient. Understanding how management manages costs and capital allocation despite operational disruptions reveals the strength of the long-term investment thesis. Three takeaways from the call stand out for long-term investors.

Management bought $218 million of common stock in 2026, shrinking the total share count by approximately 12%. Executives paused repurchases in the third quarter to rebuild cash balances, but plan to resume buying shares once liquidity improves because they view the market price as well below intrinsic asset value.

The total paid pass base turned positive by late October. The base of 2027-specific pass products grew over 15%, providing predictable revenue and locking in visitor commitments before the main operating season begins.

United Parks remains on track to deliver $50 million in gross cost savings across 2026 and 2027. Management launched a secondary cost-reduction phase targeting an additional $40 million to $50 million in savings for 2027 through artificial intelligence tools, operational automation, and labor scheduling improvements.

Forward group bookings and international visitors are turning into growth tailwinds, lifting adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization). Balance sheet flexibility allows steady reinvestment in park attractions while management works to protect operating margins.

Strategic priorities center on maintaining pricing discipline and automating operations to lower expenses. Long-term shareholder returns will depend on converting these structural cost cuts into sustainable cash flow growth.
