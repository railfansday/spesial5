Hackers tried to inject malware for people going to Ikea, CNN, Zoom and other popular sites.

If you visited Ikea, CNN, Zoom or several other popular websites on Monday, it might be wise to update your iPhone or iPad immediately. Cybercriminals tried to infect iPhones and steal cryptocurrency from people who went to those sites, Reuters reports.

The compromised websites include Zoom, BBC, CNN, Salesforce, GitLab, Ikea, HSBC, Equifax, Air France-KLM, Ledger and Peacock, according to cybersecurity researcher Scott Helme and his platform Report URI. Helme said bad actors used Optimizely, a website optimization company, in an attempt to serve up JavaScript malware to people visiting sites from 178 organizations.

Ikea said it was investigating to see if any customers or digital services had been impacted. Zoom said it disabled Optimizely across its production environments and had not identified any effect on customer data. Equifax also has stopped using Optimizely and said it has not identified any compromise to systems or data.

Helme told CNET that “most people who simply read a page are at low risk,” but there are other actions you should take if you did more than that. He said if you entered debit or credit card details on any of the affected sites (the full list is here), check transactions and statements and maybe even get a replacement card.

If you copied and pasted a crypto wallet address on a site, make sure the funds went to the intended recipient, Helme suggested. Also, log out of all sessions on the affected websites and change your password. Finally, update to the latest iOS and restart the device if you use an iPhone or iPad running iOS 18.6 or earlier.

Optimizely said that the criminal “exploited a vulnerability in Experimentation” — the website testing system that clients use — to add the malicious JavaScript. The company said it had fixed the vulnerability, removed the bad code and “taken steps to secure affected user accounts.”

Reuters reported that Salesforce, Equifax and HSBC said their sites had not been compromised by the incident. Reuters also said it could not determine how much theft had occurred in the attack.

Report URI said that more than $7,000 in crypto was stolen within several hours of the attack’s start.

Optimizely is a platform used by many websites to manage content, test new website designs and tailor product recommendations to various target audiences. Customers load JavaScript snippets, which include Optimizely code and customer experiments, onto their websites to optimize site performance.

The snippets also contain a Project JavaScript field, which consists of code written by customers. But on Monday, the cybercriminal injected malicious code into that Project JavaScript field. Because the rest of the snippet was untouched, the files were still served from a host that every affected site already trusted.

The cost of cybercrime is forecast to reach $12.2 trillion by 2031, according to one estimate, an annual increase of 2.5% from 2026. That amount includes “stolen funds, fraud, productivity loss, intellectual property theft, post-attack remediation, regulatory fines and reputational harm.”

The Optimizely incident, dubbed the OptimJack attack, was the latest in a series of internet incidents around the globe. On Thursday, Microsoft’s Outlook email system went down, and the FAA grounded all planes on Friday. Microsoft blamed a software bug from July, and the FAA blamed a router.

Then, on Monday, Chase reported service disruptions and transaction delays, as did some Apple services on Tuesday.

None of these incidents were connected, Helme told CNET.
