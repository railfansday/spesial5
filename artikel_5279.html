Hyperliquid Pushes for 7 Key Tweaks Ahead of UK’s 2027 Crypto Overhaul

The Hyperliquid Policy Center (HPC) sent a letter to the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) on Nov. 18 with seven recommendations on the draft documents CP26/36 and GC26/7. Three of them concern CP26/36, and four concern GC26/7. The letter was signed by HPC chief executive Jake Chervinsky.

The UK's new crypto regime will start to apply on Oct. 25, 2027. The FCA opened its window for crypto license applications on Sept. 30, 2026, and the window will close on Feb. 28, 2027.

According to the letter, "Regulatory obligations belong with persons who have unilateral control over a function that is a regulated activity." HPC argued that attaching obligations to a person who could not discharge them "would offer consumers no additional protection."

In the letter, HPC also compared public blockchains with the internet. "Like a public blockchain, the internet depends on protocols that identifiable persons maintain and upgrade over time," the group wrote.

HPC asked the FCA to assess each participant in a blockchain network separately. Proprietary traders and market makers "supply much of the liquidity on which order books depend," the group wrote. HPC also asked the regulator to describe compliance requirements as outcomes and to hold regulated firms accountable for them.

On Aug. 24, HPC urged the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) to harmonize their rules for perpetual contracts. The group argued that such products should be classified by their economic structure. According to HPC, a harmonized framework would let exchanges compete on "execution quality and liquidity."

On Sept. 30, HPC sent its response to the European Union's review of the MiCA rules. In it, the group asked the EU to treat crypto perpetual futures as derivatives under MiFID II. "Clarity in application, rather than new legislation, is what would assist in regulating perpetual futures," HPC wrote at the time.

Chervinsky previously served as chief policy officer at the Blockchain Association and as chief legal officer at the crypto venture firm Variant.
