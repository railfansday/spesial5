Huddersfield Town & Notts County are cringing at the same thing right now

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Tom Iorpenda was one of the EFL's brightest talents last season while at Notts County, but the 21-year-old has yet to see the pitch in any competition so far this year.

The former Brighton and Huddersfield Town academy product ended a five-year association with the Terriers this past summer, signing for Swansea City for around £1 million towards the end of the transfer window.

Iorpenda had played every game in league and play-off action for Notts in their League Two promotion campaign while on loan from Huddersfield last term, and returned to the Accu Stadium with plenty of momentum and a possible route into first-team action.

In the end, with his contract up at the end of the season, Huddersfield opted to cash in on the versatile midfielder and insert a sell-on clause so they could profit further down the line, instead of possibly losing him for free.

It's safe to say it hasn't worked out yet at all, which must be a real frustration for the League One pair.

It's always difficult when a young star arrives at a club in the latter stages of the summer transfer window. Iorpenda spent the whole summer and pre-season with Huddersfield, signing for the Swans on August 30.

Swansea adopted a much more intense play style ahead of Vitor Matos' second season in charge in South Wales. They rank first in pressing sequences, high turnovers and have the lowest PPDA (passes per defensive action) in the Championship.

That's difficult to get up to speed with when you have a full pre-season, let alone when you don't.

Additionally, Iorpenda joined a Swansea side that, at the time, had won two and drawn one of their first three games and were about to make it three wins from four against Watford on September 1.

So, the 21-year-old arrived at the club with just two Championship appearances under his belt, totalling 50 minutes, from his time at Huddersfield, has had to get up to speed with their intensity and has been trying to dislodge what has been a winning lineup.

It's been difficult, and that's the reason why he still has no appearances so far in a Swansea shirt.

Iorpenda was everywhere for Notts last year. He predominantly played as an attacking midfielder, but Martin Paterson trusted him to man the engine room a bit further back, too, and as loan spells go, it was one of the best in the EFL last year.

Not only did Iorpenda start 46 of the 49 league and play-off games at Meadow Lane last season, but he also scored three and assisted eight, including setting up Jayden Luker (another loanee set for bigger things) for the only goal over two legs in the play-off semi-final against Chesterfield.

Per FotMob, he ranked in the top 3% of players in his position for defensive contributions, duels won and tackles, but also in the top 5% for touches, passes and fouls won. He operated mainly in the left channel, but he covered ground better than most could in League Two that year.

Notts County would have likely had Iorpenda right at the top of their summer wishlist after they secured promotion from League Two last season, no matter how unrealistic the signing appeared to be. He was ready to propel himself up the leagues, and perhaps the Magpies could have been entrusted to continue his development in the tier above.

Unfortunately, the 21-year-old's contract situation worked against them, and now they're being forced to see him ride the bench in the Championship.

Iorpenda's stock has fallen quite significantly since he joined Swansea, which doesn't bode well for a Huddersfield side who felt they could have been earning a little more than the £1m they pocketed in the original sale.

It's unknown what contract talks were held between the midfielder and Huddersfield over the summer, but from his lack of inclusion in any side under Martin Drury to start the season, it was clear he wasn't favoured.

Even the Terriers couldn't deny that he has potential, though, and a £1m + sell-on clause actually went down as a good deal for both sides. Swansea got a high-potential player for cheap, and Huddersfield banked a seven-figure fee for a guy they could have lost for nothing at the end of the season.

At the moment, though, they aren't getting any more than that base amount, as Iorpenda is yet to showcase any Championship quality just yet, mainly because he's had no chance to showcase anything.

Notts will be looking on, annoyed that their ex-loan star isn't enjoying life elsewhere, while the dreams of making some more money immediately from Huddersfield's side aren't coming to fruition, either.

However, it's not as if Iorpenda has been left out of squads altogether. He's making the bench, just not getting on the pitch. In the 12 games he's been available for, he's made the 20-man squad in half of those.

He'll be one of the players looking to impress whoever the newhead coach turns out to be, after Vitor Matos left Swansea on Wednesday to take charge at Ligue 1 outfit Strasbourg.

A potential new playstyle could be implemented that benefits Iorpenda, or the new boss could simply come into the job with a fresh slate, and the ex-Huddersfield man won't already be battling with settled starters.

Either way, it may only be a matter of time before Iorpenda gets to see the pitch in a Swansea shirt for the first time, and those at Huddersfield and Notts County who have cringed at his involvement so far will hope he can make a name for himself when he does.
