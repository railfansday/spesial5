Tracking your heart rate with a heart rate monitor (HRM) during your runs is one of the most powerful ways to optimize your training, whether you’re a dedicated marathoner, power triathlete, or just aiming to move more intentionally. As a runner, I understand that my heart rate is important, but I don’t necessarily understand using it as a training device. That’s why I reached out to an expert to find out why it’s important and how to use that data.

As exercise physiologist and run coach Alyssa Lombardi tells Runner’s World, “Monitoring your heart rate while training can be a great tool to help you understand how hard your body is working. It’s important for a balanced training program to have lots of easy recovery days where your heart rate is low, balanced by some harder intensity workouts where your heart rate is high.” Understanding and utilizing heart-rate zones—ranging from zone 1 (very light) to zone 5 (maximum effort)—can help you tailor your training for performance, endurance, and recovery.

While zone 2 running gets loads of buzz as the golden ticket for endurance building and recovery, Lombardi emphasizes that “all zones have their place in a training program.” She also notes an important caveat: “As an exercise physiologist I can understand the benefits of heart rate training but when I am coaching my athletes I do not have them follow heart rate based training to a T because your heart rate can vary so much day to day.” Lombardi lists how factors like heat, hydration, location, and medications can change your heart rate. “It is a great tool to have but I never have my athletes follow that 100 percent.”

This nuanced approach is critical—HRMs are invaluable tools, but their data should be viewed in context. Each athlete responds differently, and some exceptionally fit runners may see higher heart rates at lower intensity simply because their bodies have adapted to efficiently sustain greater workloads.

Accuracy is paramount for heart rate monitors. A reliable HRM provides consistent, precise heart rate readings during various activities, from cardio to strength training. Inaccurate readings can undermine workout effectiveness and hinder fitness progress, making you wonder if your heart is even trying.

When it comes to heart rate tracking, it’s all about what feels right for you, especially when you’re really pushing it. Chest straps give you accurate readings but can be a bit annoying, while wrist monitors and rings are convenient but might glitch out during intense workouts. So, the real MVP here is whatever you’ll actually wear and trust for your grind—try ’em both if you can.

Say goodbye to connection woes. Thanks to Bluetooth and ANT+, gadgets play nice with all your beloved external devices and go-to fitness apps. Some even have the ability to send the readings right to your doctor.

No one likes a dead heart rate monitor in the middle of a workout, so think about how often you’ll need to swap out those batteries. Make sure you have a good stash of fresh ones on hand, or consider rechargeable options to keep the fun going. That way, your entertainment won’t be interrupted by a sudden power outage.

A durable HRM needs to keep up with your lifestyle, whether you’re breaking a sweat or just enjoying a brisk walk. Chest bands are generally very durable, while smartwatches and wristbands offer good longevity but may be a bit more precious, and smart health tracking rings are designed for continuous wear and can withstand daily activities but may scratch.

Running dynamics, on-device memory, and water resistance can enhance utility for diverse athletes. Additionally, some advanced watches and rings offer features like period and ovulation prediction, providing valuable health insights for users. These capabilities make them more than just fitness trackers, transforming them into comprehensive personal health companions.

These can get pricey. Select a monitor that fits your budget and needs, and don’t forget that some, like the Whoop and Oura ring, have additional membership fees.

Many standard heart rate monitors—especially chest straps and armbands—pose real and annoying challenges for size inclusivity. Limited strap size ranges often make them too small for bigger runners and too large or bulky for petite athletes, affecting comfort, fit, and even accuracy.

For women with large breasts who require more supportive bras, chest straps can be particularly tricky. Supportive or high-compression bras may push the strap down, create pressure points, or cause shifting, which can lead to discomfort, chafing, or inconsistent readings. When shopping for a heart rate monitor, it’s important to consider not only strap length and adjustability but also how well the design works with your body type and workout apparel to ensure both comfort and reliable data.

How accurate are wrist-based HRMs compared to chest straps?Wrist-based optical sensors are good these days—accurate enough for most people—but chest straps still win during activities with sudden heart rate spikes or lots of wrist movement.

Do I need a subscription for my HRM?Most HRMs don’t require one, but some, like Whoop or Oura, do charge a monthly membership for access to their full analytics and insights. That subscription cost is worth factoring into your budget.

Can heart rate monitors detect health issues?HRMs aren’t medical devices, but trends in your heart rate, recovery, or heart rhythm can signal changes worth discussing with your healthcare provider. Some devices, like the Apple Watch, even have FDA-cleared ECG apps to record heart rhythm data.
