JWT Time Claims Explained: exp, iat and nbf in Plain Dates (Plus Clock Skew)

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Disclosure: I'm Nagesh, and I built the free JWT Decoder at base64decodetool.com used in this post. The token below is synthetic and illustrative. It uses made-up claims and a made-up demo secret, so nothing in it is real.

Most JWT tutorials stop at "it's three Base64URL parts". The part that bites in production is the time claims. A token that is "expired" on one server and "fine" on another is almost always a timestamp or clock problem.

This post covers three registered claims from RFC 7519:

All three are NumericDate values: seconds since 1970-01-01 00:00:00 UTC. They are seconds, not milliseconds. JavaScript's Date wants milliseconds, so you multiply by 1000.

Two machines rarely agree to the second. If the issuer's clock runs 20 seconds ahead of the API's clock, a fresh token has an nbf that is still "in the future" for the API, and you get random 401s right after login. A small leeway, commonly tens of seconds to a couple of minutes, absorbs that. Many JWT libraries expose it as an option, such as a clock tolerance. Check your library's docs for the name and its default.

Keep leeway small. It widens the window in which an expired token is accepted. Fix the root cause by syncing clocks with NTP, and use leeway only as a safety margin.

Anyone can read this payload. Base64URL is an encoding, not encryption, and the first two parts need no key. Reading exp tells you what the token claims. It does not tell you the token is genuine. A forged token can say whatever it likes about exp.

Only after the signature checks out against the right key (and the expected algorithm) should you trust any claim, time claims included. In the HS256 example, I recomputed the HMAC-SHA256 over header.payload with the demo secret in Node and got the same signature as Python, so that check passed. Change one character of the payload and it fails.

The page decodes in your browser and says nothing is uploaded. Still, don't paste live production tokens into any website, mine included. Use test tokens.

Checking against its script, the status line only looks at exp, compares it to your device's clock with no leeway, and prints the time in your browser's local time zone. It doesn't convert iat or nbf to dates and doesn't flag a not-yet-valid nbf. For those, use the conversions above. If you paste my token today, it should say Expired, since its 15-minute window ended at 10:15 IST on 13 Nov 2026.
