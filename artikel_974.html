Mauricio Pochettino takes charge of his first Manchester United game and is thrown straight into the deep end as he faces fierce Premier League rivals Liverpool at Anfield.

It’s been a whirlwind week for Pochettino since arriving back in England but the former Tottenham and Chelsea manager has outlined his plan to bring success back to Old Trafford after a turbulent spell and alarming dip of results under Michael Carrick.

United enter the weekend in the relegation zone, with Liverpool having the opportunity to pile more misery on their rivals and continue their progress under Andoni Iraola. The Reds will also be out for revenge after losing both games against United last season, including a 2-1 defeat at Anfield.

No game against Liverpool is a considered a free-hit for a United manager but for Pochettino, ending the club’s nine-match winless run with a first victory away from home this season would be some start.

And finally a look at another disappointing day for Chelsea, who were thumped 4-1 at home by Leeds...

By the end, amid what Xabi Alonso described as a “fragile moment”, even his own cultured touch had deserted him. As his Chelsea team tried to offer something in the final minutes of an utterly nothing performance against a brilliant Leeds United, the ball was easily cleared for a throw. Alonso, famed for his technique as a player, miscontrolled it towards his underwhelming bench.

He’s now seeing his reputation as a coach plummet, as Chelsea fall to eighth. This 4-1 evisceration was not just Leeds’ first win at the home of their old Seventies rivals since 1999 – to add to how Tottenham Hotspur also won here a few weeks ago – but a second defeat in a row, and a fifth in 11 this season.

And yet the very nature of it was still worse than any numbers can tell.

And the report from Arsenal’s draw to Newcastle...

Noni Madueke snatched a 2-2 draw for Arsenal after Newcastle produced a stunning second-half turnaround to claim a deserved point against the Premier League leaders.

Eberechi Eze put the champions in front and Madueke wasted a couple of chances to increase the lead before half-time, with Matthias Jaissle’s side jeered off following a listless first 45 minutes.

They rallied in stunning fashion on the resumption, with Harvey Barnes scoring Newcastle’s first goal at St James’ Park since September 19 before Malick Thiaw put them ahead after the hour from close range.

Here’s a brief look back at some of the main action from yesterday, starting with City’s draw to Fulham in the weekend’s early kick-off...

The best part of five hours after the final whistle blew at the Etihad Stadium and about 150 miles north east, Noni Madueke scored an equaliser to ensure the day ended with the equation ultimately unchanged. Manchester City will still go to Arsenal on Sunday six points behind the reigning champions. They could reduce the gap to three, though the evidence of recent weeks is that it is likelier to stretch to nine.

That would surely end City’s title challenge. The theory had been that it would take a potential punishment for their 114 guilty verdicts to do that and, while City are appealing, a sizeable sanction may be still to come. Perhaps that is fitting: the title could be decided on the pitch, rather than in the courts.

Back to Fernandes and Pochettino, who has committed to the Portuguese as United’s ‘leader’ as he bids to inspire a high-octane style of football to guide the club out and away from the relegation zone. Jack Rathborn reports:

From one skipper to another: Liverpool captain Virgil van Dijk remains uncertain of whether he will remain at the club beyond the end of the season.

The 35-year-old centre-back is approaching the final six months of his contract, which expires next summer.

He believes that he can keep playing at the top level for “many more years” but says he has “no idea” whether that will be at Anfield as Liverpool’s decision-makers continue to ponder over a fresh offer.

One major thing in Pochettino’s in-tray will be the future of his captain, Bruno Fernandes, who has had an indifferent start to this season - much like his whole team - but has been instrumental in the past.

Fernandes has revealed that discussions over a new contract have stalled because it isn’t “the right moment” to be negotiating.

The Portuguese said an offer had been made in the summer, with his contract due to expire at the end of this season, but negotiations were not ongoing as he insisted on prioritising the club’s return to form.
