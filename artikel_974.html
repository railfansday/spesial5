Thitikul completes three-peat at LPGA Tour finale and breaks money record

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Jeeno Thitikul claimed a two-shot victory over Aki Iwai, with Nelly Korda finishing in third spot; Thitikul becomes the first player to win three consecutive CME Group Tour Championship titles and passes Annika Sorenstam in the LPGA Tour career money list

Jeeno Thitikul has won the LPGA Tour's season-ending CME Group Tour Championship for a third consecutive year after storming to a two-shot victory in Florida.

Thitikul carded an eagle and five birdies during an impressive seven-under 65, pulling her clear of a congested leaderboard at Tiburon Golf Club and securing a 12th LPGA Tour title.

She finished on 24 under and two clear of Aki Iwai, who raced into contention with six birdies in the first 10 holes of her final-round 65, while world No 1 Nelly Korda finished in third spot.

Thitikul becomes the first player to win an LPGA Tour event three years running since Inbee Park at the KPMG Women's PGA Championship from 2013-2015, with her latest a fifth of another memorable season.

A $4m (£3.06m) first prize also sees her end Annika Sorenstam's 25-year stint at the top of the LPGA Tour's career money list, moving ahead of the Swede with a total of $24,292,858 (£20.3m).

Thitikul started the final day a shot behind A Lim Kim but made the dream start with a 12-foot eagle at the par-five first, then added a long-range birdie at the fourth and five-foot gain at the sixth to reach the turn in 32.

She started her back nine with a 10-foot birdie to edge back ahead of Iwai, who birdied six of her opening 10 holes in the group ahead and briefly levelled with another at the 13th, before Thitikul took advantage of the par-five 14th to reclaim top spot.

Iwai failed to capitalise on birdie opportunities over her closing holes as Thitikul doubled her lead with a two-putt birdie at the par-five 17th and closed out victory with a stress-free par at the last.

Korda also moved above Sorenstam's career-money record with her third-place finish, although extended her wait for the victory required to enter the LPGA Tour Hall of Fame.

"Still being able to compete when I didn't even really feel like I really had my trust in like a B or C game," Korda admitted. "So just proud of the way that I fought this week to kind of give myself a chance.

"It's really hard to go up against Jeeno [Thitikul] out here. I feel like she just has this place figured out. Maybe one day!"

Pajaree Anannarukarn, Yan Liu and Alison Lee shared fourth spot on 19 under, with overnight leader Kim in tied-seventh alongside Yuri Yoshida and Sei Young Kim after a final-round 72.

England's Lottie Woad jumped inside the top 20 with a final-round 67, while Charley Hull faded to a share of 23rd place after a one-over 73.
