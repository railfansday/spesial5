Airbnb vs. MercadoLibre: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Airbnb's lean marketplace model and MercadoLibre's sprawling Latin American ecosystem pull in very different directions, and the margin gap between them tells a story worth weighing.

Choosing between a global travel leader and a Latin American commerce giant requires balancing high growth against established profitability. Which path is right for your portfolio between Airbnb Inc. (ABNB -3.09%) and MercadoLibre Inc. (MELI +1.46%)?

Airbnb provides a unique global platform for alternative accommodations, while MercadoLibre operates an expansive ecosystem of e-commerce, fintech, and logistics services across Latin America. Both companies benefit from the digital shift in consumer spending, but they navigate very different competitive landscapes and regional economic conditions as they head toward 2027.

Airbnb connects over 5 million hosts with guests seeking unique stays and experiences in over 220 countries, as detailed in its latest annual report for FY 2025. Its two-sided platform offers hosts tools like integrated payments and protection through AirCover, while providing guests with AI-powered search and personalization features. By maintaining a marketplace rather than owning properties, the company stays lean while expanding its presence among travel and tourism stocks.

In FY 2025, revenue reached about $12.3 billion, representing growth of approximately 10.8% compared to the previous year. The company reported net income of about $2.5 billion for the period. While revenue continued to climb, the net margin of roughly 20.4% was lower than in previous years as the company invested in platform updates and marketing.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.3x, indicating a conservative amount of total debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio, measuring the ability to cover short-term liabilities, is roughly 1.4x. In FY 2025, free cash flow reached around $4.6 billion, though stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 34.3% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation.

MercadoLibre operates a massive ecosystem in Latin America, which the company detailed in its latest annual report for FY 2025. It integrates a marketplace for buyers and sellers with its Mercado Pago fintech solution and a proprietary logistics network, Mercado Envios, to ensure fast delivery. This integrated approach creates a powerful flywheel where commerce, payments, and logistics reinforce one another for its 136 million unique buyers.

In FY 2025, revenue reached around $28.9 billion, a significant increase of approximately 39.1% compared to the prior year. The company reported net income of roughly $2.0 billion for the same period. While the top line grew rapidly, the net margin was approximately 6.9% as the company focused on market share expansion and its credit business.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 1.7x, showing how the company uses debt to fund its extensive logistics and credit operations. The current ratio is approximately 1.2x, suggesting a healthy level of liquidity for its day-to-day business. In FY 2025, MercadoLibre generated free cash flow of about $10.8 billion, which is the cash remaining after covering operating costs and capital investments.

Airbnb faces stiff competition from established online travel agencies such as Booking Holdings (BKNG +0.60%) and Expedia Group (EXPE +2.37%), as well as search engines like Alphabet Inc. (GOOG +2.00%). Regulatory challenges pose a major threat, as cities like New York and Barcelona implement strict short-term rental bans that could limit listing growth. Additionally, the company relies heavily on third-party infrastructure from Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -1.74%) for its cloud services and faces potential reputational risks from third-party misconduct.

MercadoLibre must navigate intense competition from both local players and global rivals like Amazon across its diverse markets. The company is particularly exposed to macroeconomic instability and currency volatility in countries like Argentina and Brazil, which can impact its reported financial results. Furthermore, its expanding credit business introduces significant credit risk, while reliance on Apple Inc. (AAPL +2.04%) for app distribution adds a layer of platform dependency.

Airbnb carries a lower Forward P/E but a much higher P/S ratio, which measures market value against sales over the past twelve months.

MercadoLibre evolved from the 'eBay of Latin America' to something more akin to a combination of Amazon and PayPal Holdings Inc. (PYPL +1.19%). In 2025, the company made large investments in its Brazilian logistics network, cut down the free-shipping threshold in Brazil in June, and nearly doubled its consumer loan book. This drove impressive growth for the company and investors, with an increasing focus on financial services over delivering goods.

Yet the immediate concern with left-of-center Luis Lula winning the Brazilian presidential election last month is that the new administration will start addressing Brazil's high government and consumer debt levels, which will keep interest rates there around their current very high levels. That's bad for a consumer-loan-focused business like MercadoLibre, and shares have been hammered this week as a result.

Long-term, however, such fears are probably overblown. In fact, in the current fiscal year, revenue is expected to jump 45% to $41.9 billion, with slightly higher free cash flow and slightly lower net income. Overall, it's a good picture for MercadoLibre.

Airbnb reported an excellent third quarter of fiscal 2026. The business beat consensus revenue estimates by more than $100 million, reporting $4.87 billion in sales, up nearly 19% from the same period in 2025, driven by a strong increase in booked nights. Foreign markets like India and Latin America, two markets in which Airbnb is seeking growth, have been strong this year. The seasonally weaker fourth quarter should see sales close to $3.3 billion and net income around $488 million.

The business is finding ways to expand its popular travel business, embracing features like buy now, pay later (BNPL) on reservations, a form of short-term lending. The network advantage of Airbnb's history, over 2 billion guest arrivals since 2008, and the lack of host presence on AI (meaning a competitor using AI has little data to scrape to form a competing product) give it a competitive moat.

For the full year, management expects sales of $14.1 billion with net income of $3.2 billion.

MercadoLibre comes at a premium, a level we feel is overextended for a Latin America-dependent stock. Airbnb also isn't cheap, but its North America-based travel customer has historically proven to be the more resilient spender, making that P/E ratio more palatable.
