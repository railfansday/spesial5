All 3 Versions Of Coriolanus Snow In The Hunger Games Movies, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Warning! This post contains SPOILERS for The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping

President Coriolanus Snow is easily one of the greatest villains in any YA movie franchise. Across the entirety of The Hunger Games franchise, which first began in 2012, Snow has been depicted in three distinct eras. As a result, the sinister leader of Panem has also been portrayed by three different actors.

President Snow was originally played by actor Donald Sutherland in the first four Hunger Games movies, which were released from 2012 to 2015. Then, Tom Blyth brought a much younger Snow to life in 2023's The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, showcasing Coriolanus' early ambitions during the 10th annual Hunger Games. Most recently, Ralph Fiennes took over the role in 2026's Sunrise on the Reaping, with Snow now serving as President during the second Quarter Quell (over two decades before Katniss Everdeen and the rebellion against the Capitol).

Keeping all three actors in mind, here's our ranking of all three versions of The Hunger Games' primary antagonist to determine "which Snow lands on top."

Without question, Tom Blyth delivers a fascinating performance as a young Coriolanus in 2023's Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes. Having great ambition to restore his family name and status, Snow is eager to please and is determined to prove himself to his teachers at the Capitol's Academy as well as Dr. Gaul, the Capitol's Head Gamemaker. As such, it was during the 10th Hunger Games that Snow's efforts as a Mentor get the Capitol interested in the games as genuine entertainment for the very first time, largely due to his getting the public invested in his Tribute from District 12, Lucy Gray Baird.

By all accounts, Snow's ill-fated romance with Lucy Gray and his growing paranoia make for a fascinating character study. That being said, there's something inherently challenging about a movie that effectively places one of cinema's darkest dictators in any kind of sympathetic light. Snow's future atrocities are well established, making moments of vulnerability in his earlier years intriguing yet difficult to fully embrace without remembering the countless people he'll eventually destroy.

On the whole, Blyth does an excellent job revealing new sides to Coriolanus Snow before he becomes the fully fleshed-out President we know him to be years later in The Hunger Games franchise. It's a strong performance that makes the character's transformation compelling, even if it is difficult to rank him as the very best version of Snow on-screen.

In the newly released Sunrise on the Reaping, Ralph Fiennes' President Snow is a much more direct and aggressive interpretation of Coriolanus Snow (with Fiennes' himself of course being no stranger to playing major villains). Having recently consolidated his power by eliminating allies and enemies alike, this version of Snow is exceptionally cruel, refusing to tolerate any and all threats to his leadership and the established order (even potential ones).

Throughout Sunrise on the Reaping and the 50th Hunger Games, Snow is revealed to have done so many terrible things to so many different Tributes, Victors, and even members of the Capitol itself. This includes having Beetee's 12-year-old son reaped, forcing him to mentor his own son before his death as punishment for Beetee's plot to sabotage the Capitol's communications systems. However, Snow's treatment of Haymitch during the second Quarter Quell is particularly heinous. Even Haymitch's victory at the end of Sunrise of the Reaping doesn't get him away from Snow's control and cruelty, having painted Haymitch as an absolute pariah before taking away everyone he's ever loved.

On the whole, Ralph Fiennes did an exceptional job playing an earlier, perhaps less refined, President Snow. However, nothing quite beats the original performance.

At the end of the day, you simply can't beat Donald Sutherland's original portrayal of President Snow. Having the most screen time by far with roles in all four of the original Hunger Games films, Sutherland's Snow was immediately established as this very formal villain presenting himself to the public as a benevolent leader masking so much sinister intent just under the surface.

Sutherland's President Snow also gets some of the franchise's greatest and darkest dialogue with his takes on power and how to wield both hope and fear, having an unmatched gravitas every time he's on-screen. The darkest thing about him is how reasonable he seems 90% of the time, talking about the necessities of maintaining order even when the primary means is having children kill other children as a public spectacle.

Sutherland remains the definitive President Snow. In with his depiction that you can best see Snow at his fullest. He believes in the systems he's created, recognizes the importance of perceptions and control, and was able to easily identify the threats to the Capitol long before anyone else, and only failed when his rivals sunk to his level with horrifying atrocities of their own.
