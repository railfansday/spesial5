The Asterisk on Robinhood Chain’s $51M Win Over Ethereum

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

Take 12 days out of Robinhood Chain's first quarter, and the "3-month-old L2 beat Ethereum" headline turns around.

For the other 80 days, Robinhood Chain earned about $166,000 a day in network fees. Ethereum averaged about $446,000.

ARK Invest's numbers are accurate. Robinhood Chain did generate about $51 million in Q3 against Ethereum's roughly $41 million. But the coverage repeated the comparison without checking how the $51 million was made or who ends up with it. Going back to the underlying data, and the comparison holds up much less well than the headline suggests.

ARK's DeFi Quarterly for Q3 2026 charts "Network Real Economic Value by Blockchain". In it, Robinhood Chain logged about $51 million in its first quarter, while Ethereum logged about $41 million, down roughly 35% from the previous quarter.

The comparison spread quickly. A chain that launched on July 1 beating the network that secures most of DeFi made an easy headline. ARK's wider data has more context: total network value across the chains it tracks rose about 24% to roughly $349 million, the first quarterly increase in five quarters, though still about 47% below a year earlier. Robinhood Chain processed about $65 billion in DEX volume, fourth in ARK's dataset, and hosted about $13.7 billion in tokenized equities.

ARK's DeFi Quarterly glossary defines Real Economic Value (REV) as "the total value a network generates consisting of both in-protocol transaction fees and out-of-protocol tips," using data from Blockworks Research.

In other words, both figures measure the same thing: gross fees users paid to use the chain. Robinhood Chain's $51 million matches the $51.0 million in user network fees reported in growthepie's Q3 review of the chain. It's not app revenue. growthepie puts the revenue of apps on Robinhood Chain separately, at $591.4 million.

So ARK didn't mix up its metrics. The trouble is that the same metric means very different things on these two chains. The Q3 number doesn't show that.

growthepie's report has the detail the headline leaves out. A 12-day surge, from August 30 to September 10, pushed median transaction costs to 31 times their pre-surge average. That surge produced 74% of the quarter's fees. growthepie names Relay as the largest tracked fee payer.

Outside the surge, Robinhood Chain earned about 37% of Ethereum's daily rate. At that pace, a full quarter would come to about $15 million, well under Ethereum's $41 million. The "out-earned Ethereum" result depends almost entirely on 12 days when fees jumped to 31 times normal.

That doesn't make the surge fake. Users paid those fees. But the surge came from congestion and higher prices, not steady growth. Across the quarter's 793.7 million transactions, users paid about 6.4 cents per transaction on average. Median costs that briefly rose to 31 times normal aren't a pricing model an exchange-backed chain will want to repeat.

Even with the same metric, the two figures go to very different places.

On Ethereum, most of the $41 million is the base fee, which is burned. That reduces ETH supply and benefits every holder. The rest goes as priority fees and tips to thousands of independent validators. Ethereum's REV is close to a public measure of what the network's users pay.

On Robinhood Chain, an Arbitrum Orbit rollup, fees go to the chain's sequencer, which Robinhood operates. In practice, the $51 million is a company's gross receipts. Some of it goes back to Ethereum to pay for posting data and settling transactions there, and the rest is Robinhood's margin. As coverage of the report noted, how much reaches Robinhood's income statement is still unclear. The company's earnings report on October 27 may answer that.

So on one side, you have fees paid to a decentralized base layer, mostly destroyed. On the other, you have gross receipts for one company, before costs. Both count as "network value," but they aren't the same kind of income.

Three more things make the head-to-head weaker:

The accurate version of the headline is less dramatic: in its first quarter, Robinhood Chain charged its users more in total than Ethereum's base layer did. About three-quarters of that came from a 12-day congestion spike, and the money went to Robinhood's sequencer, not to a decentralized validator set.

That's still a strong debut. Robinhood Chain's stablecoin growth, $13.7 billion in tokenized equities and nearly 800 million transactions are all real. But "beat Ethereum" tells you more about one busy fortnight in early September than about which chain has the stronger business. Q4 will be the real test: without a surge, can Robinhood Chain's fees come close to Ethereum's on an ordinary day?
