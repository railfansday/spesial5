Fed officials are split on how aggressively to raise rates after December

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Governor Christopher Waller calls a half-point December hike probable, while St. Louis Fed's Alberto Musalem sees several increases by spring

Christopher Waller, governor of the US Federal Reserve, speaks during the C. Peter McColough Series on International Economics at the Council on Foreign Relations in New York, US, on Thursday, Oct. 16, 2025. (Michael Nagle/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller pushed Wednesday for forceful measures to combat inflation as Fed officials voiced differing views on how fast to raise interest rates.

Waller told CNBC that the Fed will probably have to lift its benchmark rate by 50 basis points when the Federal Open Market Committee meets Dec. 8-9. He said the central bank has to unwind the trio of quarter-point cuts it made last year, and possibly go beyond that.

"You might need to, like, shock people," Waller told CNBC's Steve Liesman. "I'm very, very concerned about inflation. It's kind of headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out."

St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem outlined his views on Tuesday in a speech at the Central Bank of Argentina's Money and Banking Conference in Buenos Aires. He said the economy "will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027 to achieve price stability in a timely manner."

Musalem said he expects the economy to need a real policy rate between 2% and 2.5% before spring 2027, and he estimated the current real rate at the lower end of a 1% to 1.5% range. He said the nominal policy rate "may need to do most of the work." He described a scenario in which no further increases are needed as unlikely but plausible.

That path points to increases at the December, January, March and April meetings, according to CNBC.

Other officials have urged a slower approach. “I don’t think the situation calls for dramatic action,” New York Fed President John Williams said Tuesday, according to CNBC. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack has endorsed more gradual tightening.

Waller said last week that a half-point increase should be considered at the December meeting if October consumer price data resembled the two prior months. He said then that he was leaning toward a quarter-point increase if monthly inflation improved. Musalem said October headline and core consumer price inflation rose more than expected.

The Fed raised rates by a quarter point in September, its first increase since July 2023, and held the target range at 3.75% to 4% in October. Governor Lisa Cook and Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase at that meeting. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan, who votes on the December decision, has said the committee needs at least two more quarter-point increases.

Futures prices reflect a quarter-point increase at the December meeting and another in January, according to CNBC.
