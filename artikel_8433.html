How about all the heat for drying the clothes? With ventless, that stays in the house. And is a lot more than running the condenser. If the air pushed out by a dryer was a problem, they would be equipped with a duct to bring outside air in.
Ventless dryers don't dry clothes by generating heat. They work like dehumidifiers to extract moisture.

As for vented dryers, there is absolutely no problem with using indoor air to dry clothes and then pump that moist air outdoors - it works very well, which is why dryers do not have intake ducts to use outdoor air. But it's also energy inefficient because any air you pump outside has to be replaced, which means unconditioned air from the outside will come into your house via all the leaks in your doors, windows, etc.
To turn liquid water into water vapor, you need heat, ie the enthalpy of vaporization.

If the temperature of the dryer is higher than that of outside, the incoming heat will be less than is exhausted.
