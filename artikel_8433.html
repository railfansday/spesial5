900-Acre Wildfire in White Mountains Likely Caused by Humans, Authorities Say

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

An unusually large wildfire in the White Mountain National Forest near Gorham, New Hampshire, has spread to nearly 856 acres and is 11% contained as of the most recent update. The Wild River Fire was first reported on November 1 in the steep, rugged terrain south of Gorham and spread rapidly, fueled by drought conditions. No residential structures are currently threatened.

While the fire’s cause is still under investigation, a Forest Service spokesperson told New Hampshire Public Radio that experts believe it was caused by human activity.

A closure order is in effect in the area around the Wild River Fire, as shown on the map below. The Appalachian Trail passes northwest of the fire and remains open, although the Appalachian Trail Conservancy urged hikers in the area to use caution, follow all instructions from fire crews and posted signs, and be aware they may see and smell smoke. The organization also cautioned hikers that the Kenduskeag Trail, which connects to the AT at northbound mile 1,894.8, is closed.

As recently as yesterday, the fire was estimated at nearly 1,000 acres, but that estimate shrank today as a result of updated mapping. However, 856 acres is still an unprecedented size for a wildfire in this region. “That’s very uncommon in our area,” Gorham Fire Chief Philip Cloutier told NH Public Radio on Wednesday. “The largest wildfire that we can remember was just under 100 acres, and that was over 20 years ago.”

Despite dry conditions on the ground and challenging terrain, the Forest Service said in a press release that firefighters are making steady progress on suppression efforts. “I am proud of the firefighters who responded quickly and continue to work tirelessly to contain the Wild River Fire,” noted Josh Sjostrom, District Ranger for the White Mountain National Forest’s Androscoggin Ranger District. “We will continue to work with our neighbors and local and state partners to contain this fire.”

Check the White Mountain National Forest Facebook page and the Watch Duty app for updates on the status of the Wild River Fire.
