Kitchen or Bath Remodel – The Cheapest Layout Is the One You Already Have

Moving a sink, toilet, or shower is one of the priciest changes in a kitchen or bath remodel, since new drain, vent, and supply lines mean opening floors and walls, paying a licensed plumber, and pulling permits. Leave each fixture on the drain it uses now, and spend your budget on what you see instead.

Fixtures cost the same wherever they sit, so the money a new layout adds goes into pipes you’ll never see.

Drains run on gravity. The International Residential Code, the model code for houses, requires a sink’s or shower’s drain to drop at least a quarter inch for every foot it runs. Over 12 feet, that’s at least 3 inches of drop the floor has to make room for. Each fixture also needs a vent pipe nearby, so a long move can mean running a new one up through a wall.

The toilet is the hardest to move, since its drain must be at least 3 inches wide. Under a wood floor, rerouting that pipe can mean cutting and reinforcing joists. On a concrete slab, the floor has to be cut open and patched for any move.

So change what you see and leave the pipes where they are. A new toilet fits the old drain when it matches the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the drain’s center. A vanity, counters, tile, lighting, and paint all go in around the pipes you have. The same code’s list of work exempt from permits includes painting, tile, cabinets, and countertops. A relocated drain or supply pipe needs a permit and an inspection before the walls close.

Say a bid for a slab-floor bathroom adds $9,500 to put the toilet and a new shower on the far wall. Leaving the toilet alone and setting the shower in the tub’s spot, on a base made to drain where the tub did, keeps that $9,500 for the parts you see.

Move a fixture only when the layout fails you every day, like a kitchen sink with no counter beside it. Even then, keep the move to a few feet, toward the existing drain and vent.

Before you sign, have every bidder price the job twice, with each fixture where it is and with your new layout, so you see what the move alone costs.
