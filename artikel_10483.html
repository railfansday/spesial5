Friday the 13th is here. Its impact on your work day may not be so scary

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Business can be risky, so it’s natural that superstitions can sometimes creep in when money and careers are at stake.

This workweek ends on Friday the 13th, so in the Western world, the stage is set for fear to influence commerce.

Let’s take a look at the so-called bad luck day’s origins and how it impacts the business world.

What is a fear of Friday the 13th called?

A fear of Friday the 13th is called “paraskevidekatriaphobia,” a term created by psychotherapist Donald Dossey. It combines the Greek words for Friday, 13, and fear.

How many Friday the 13ths are there in a year?

Around every 212.35 days, a Friday the 13th occurs. This figure is based on a complete 400-year Gregorian calendar cycle.

Another way to look at it: there is at least one Friday the 13th a year, but there can be up to three. This year, 2026, Friday the 13th will be in triplicate.

When did Friday the 13th become unlucky?

The truth is, no one really knows how exactly Friday the 13th became synonymous with misfortune.

There are historical and religious references that might shed some light. One of the theories is that the number 12 is considered perfect and complete, so by comparison, 13 is less than.

A popular retelling of Norse mythology states that 12 gods were having a banquet in Valhalla until Loki showed up uninvited, bringing their numbers to 13. The unwelcome guest was able to trick the blind god Höd into killing his brother Balder.

The number 13 and the day Friday also show up in Christianity. According to Christian teachings, there were 13 people present at the Last Supper, which occurred on a Thursday.

The following day, Jesus was crucified. Many also believe Eve gave Adam the forbidden fruit from the tree of knowledge and Cain killed Abel on a Friday.

Where does Friday the 13th show up in pop culture?

History does have examples of traumatic events happening on Friday the 13th. One of these occurred in 1307 when King Philip IV of France detained numerous members of the Knights Templar. Some of these men were eventually executed.

Friday the 13th also shows up in literature. In 1834, an article in Revue de Paris by Marquis de Salvo, entitled “Le Chateau de Carini,” told the story of a filicide committed by a Sicilian count on Friday the 13th.

In 1907, American author Thomas William Lawson published the novel Friday, the Thirteenth, about a stockbroker who crashes the market on the title date.

Perhaps the best modern example of Friday the 13th mania is Sean S. Cunningham’s slasher film franchise, which began in 1980. Jason Voorhees’s hockey goalie mask has become iconic.

Crystal Lake, a prequel series produced by A24, is the latest from the franchise and currently streaming on Peacock.

How does superstition impact business?

In 2006, the same Dr. Dossey, who named the fear of Friday the 13th, also claimed Americans lost $800 to $900 million due to lost productivity driven by fear of the day. However, his nontransparent methodology to get to those figures has drawn criticism. The Wall Street Journal is just one of the publications that questioned its validity.

Although it’s hard to quantify an exact figure, the impact of the fear of Friday the 13th can be found.

In Manhattan and Brooklyn, less than 5% of mid-rise and high-rise residential condo buildings have a floor labeled 13, WSJ reports.

According to Realting.com, 13th-floor apartments are purchased 18% less often on average than their counterparts located on other floors. Apartment sales are also down on the day itself, 19% on any given 13th.

Ironically, a 2007 USA Today/Gallup poll found that only 13% of Americans would be bothered if required to stay on the 13th floor of a hotel. A spooky statistic indeed.
