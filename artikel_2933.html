Jensen Huang's Nvidia exit casts shadow over Wall Street’s $500 billion AI financing bet

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Leadership change at Nvidia has the potential to threaten financing of the artificial intelligence boom and test Jensen Huang's $500 billion vision to turn AI chips into tradable debt securities.

The Nvidia CEO's resignation was announced Thursday following revelations that he had two extramarital affairs — one with an employee and the other with a woman believed by U.S. intelligence to be a Chinese spy.

Shares in the tech giant — which has become central to the AI build-out in recent years — initially plunged more than 13% following Huang's resignation, before paring losses.

Nvidia has never identified a successor to Huang, who has led the chipmaker since its founding in 1993. Colette Kress, chief financial officer, has been named interim CEO.

The company has been dubbed the "central bank" of the AI build-out, with Huang seen as the "hypeman" for the sector and its chips considered the industry's gold standard.

Huang's departure raises a central question: whether his successor can carry forward a financing strategy designed to transform GPUs from rapidly depreciating hardware into bankable infrastructure assets

The $4.2 trillion firm's industry dominance, with Huang front and center, allowed it to sign memorandums of understanding with some of Wall Street's biggest asset managers, intended to secure third-party capital for a landmark $500 billion AI financing drive.

The preliminary agreements, involving Apollo Global Management, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR, sought to tap institutional investors and private capital to finance a range of computing infrastructure, including data centers and chip factories. The funding would involve specialized financing vehicles, with Nvidia's AI chips used as underlying collateral, in arrangements comparable to real estate or toll roads securitizations.

The financing arrangements are still preliminary and subject to final agreements.

BlackRock and Goldman Sachs declined to comment on today's resignation. The other firms weren't immediately available for comment.

The arrangements could now face heightened scrutiny in the aftermath of Huang's departure.

Sunaina Sinha Haldea, global head of private capital advisory at Raymond James, said a leadership change gives every firm involved a "natural moment to pause and look at the terms again."

"The best thing a new CEO can do is leave these partnerships alone in the first few months and focus on getting the final agreements signed," Haldea told CNBC via email. "Jensen brought these firms to the table with his vision, and any potential successor will keep them there by showing that the business behind that vision hasn't changed."

She added: "I don't think the capital walks away, because these lenders are underwriting Nvidia's chips and the customer contracts behind them, but they could want to be paid more for the uncertainty."

Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo, said Huang's successor would need to reassure lenders that Nvidia's technology roadmap, customer relationships and strategic vision remain intact.

"I wouldn't expect Nvidia's financing partnerships to unravel simply because Huang leaves, but a leadership shake-up could make Wall Street more cautious, potentially slowing commitments or tightening financing terms," Chanana told CNBC via email.

"The bigger risk is a ripple effect across AI financing, particularly when rising debt, circular financing and uncertain investment returns were already under scrutiny," Chanana added. "This is not a financing crisis yet, but it could become a catalyst for a broader repricing of risk across the AI ecosystem."

Haldea added that she expects to see more discipline among investors in the aftermath of the scandal. "Lenders will ask harder questions about who is really carrying the risk in these structures, and that scrutiny will spread throughout the AI infrastructure deal market."
