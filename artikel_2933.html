Under CEO Satya Nadella, Microsoft Stock Has Soared at a 23% Annualized Rate for 12 Years. Can the Tech Titan Keep Up the Pace?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Microsoft's AI spending is generating solid results, but sustaining rapid growth gets progressively harder for multitrillion-dollar companies.

By Marc Guberti – Nov 18, 2026 at 9:57AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Microsoft ›

Microsoft (MSFT -0.69%) has enjoyed a roughly 14-fold share price increase since CEO Satya Nadella took over in 2014. That amounts to an annualized growth rate of 23%, but some investors worry that the company’s heavy spending on AI could disrupt future gains.

Such worries are justified, since experienced investors may remember that the tech stock went through a “lost decade” of long-term negative returns before Nadella took over. No Microsoft shareholder wants a repeat of that, and while those concerns appear overblown, they shouldn’t be ignored.

In its fiscal 2027 Q1, Microsoft's results featured 19% year-over-year revenue growth. That was a little lower than prior quarters, but was still a respectable growth rate. One thing that stood out is the relative lack of growth in non-AI segments.

Microsoft groups all of its revenue under two categories: "agents and infrastructure" and "devices and consumer." The latter segment made up $15.5 billion of Microsoft's $92.3 billion in quarterly revenue. That part of the business was down by 3% year over year, with Xbox sales growth remaining sluggish for multiple years.

Unsurprisingly, the agents and infrastructure segment performed well, posting 25% year-over-year growth. Microsoft's AI spending goes toward this category, and its 73% year-over-year increase in commercial remaining performance obligations is a meaningful result.

Azure has been the company's bread and butter from a growth perspective, and it certainly didn't disappoint at the start of the tech giant's fiscal 2027. This part of the business was up 48% year over year, and Microsoft Cloud revenue was up 30% year over year.

It's important to keep this context in mind when assessing its rising AI infrastructure costs. Microsoft is the second-largest cloud infrastructure provider in the world, and is investing hundreds of millions of dollars a year into expanding and enhancing its cloud platform. If it reduces its capital expenditure, then No. 1 Amazon Web Services and Alphabet-owned No. 3 Google Cloud will be more than happy to take big bites out of Microsoft's market share.

Maintaining high growth rates for Azure will be critical to the stock's performance. If Microsoft’s cloud computing growth decelerates, it could trigger a prolonged correction for the stock. The company is sitting on enough cash to support higher AI spending, and each additional investment in infrastructure makes it that much harder for competitors to take any of Microsoft's AI data center market share.

Right now, its AI investments are bearing positive results. Elevated revenue growth rates and net operating income are some of the highlights. It's powering the remaining part of Microsoft's business that is still gaining market share, at a meaningful price that justifies the current price.

Although AI spending is necessary for Microsoft to keep up with the competition, that doesn't mean it will be able to maintain its long-term 23% annualized growth rate.

The company produced only a 16.1% annualized revenue growth rate over the past five years. It's hard for a company to deliver annualized stock returns that exceed its annualized revenue growth rate. That partially explains why Microsoft stock has only an annualized return of 9.9% over the past five years.

Microsoft is also approaching a $4 trillion market cap. It's harder for trillion-dollar tech giants to deliver the type of growth required to justify significant share price gains compared to smaller tech companies.

Microsoft's net income has been trending higher as its margins expand, but the company would likely have to deliver at least 23% year-over-year revenue growth over an extended period to achieve a steady 23% CAGR in its stock price again. That prior period of rapid stock price growth also included a time when Microsoft's cloud segment was much smaller, and a smaller amount of revenue and earnings gains were required to move the needle for the hyperscaler.
