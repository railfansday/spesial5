European Film Awards: ‘Fatherland’ & ‘La Bola Negra’ Top Nominations – Full List Of Films & Credits

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The European Film Academy has unveiled the nominations in the 16 key categories of its 39th European Film Awards taking place in Greece in January 2027.

Paweł Pawlikowski’s Fatherland leads the nominations, making it into 11 categories, followed by Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi’s La Bola Negra with ten nominations and Cristian Mungiu’s Cannes Palme d’Or winner Fjord with five.

The nominations were announced in front of a live audience on Saturday at the Pavlos Zannas Theatre in the iconic Olympian building in Thessaloniki, as part of the Thessaloniki International Film Festival. The winners will be unveiled at an awards ceremony on January 16 at the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Cultural Center in Athens, Greece, co-organised by the European Film Academy and the Ηellenic Film and Audiovisual Center. Watch on Deadline

The nominations are based on the votes of 5,700 members of the European Film Academy who have been watching the films included in the annual European Film Awards Shortlist, and will now vote for the winners.

Pawlikowski’s first film in eight years, Cold War drama Fatherland stars Hanns Zischler and Sandra Hüller as writer Thomas Mann and his daughter Erika as they travel across Germany in 1949 from Frankfurt in West Germany to Weimar, East Germany.

The Polish filmmaker shared the Cannes Best Director prize in May with Calvo and Ambrossi, whose La Bola Negra is one of the frontrunners across the award season this year.

Revolving around the interconnected stories of three gay men in 1932, 1937 and 2017, La Bola Negra has been raking in audience awards and storming the box office at home and internationally since its Cannes world premiere and Best Director win.

Mungiu’s Fjord, starring Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve as a Romanian and Norwegian couple whose plans for a new life in a fjord village come up against neighbors with a very different outlook on life, won the director his second Cannes Palme d’Or this year, after 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days in 2007.

Fifteen films have garnered a nomination in the European Film category spanning fiction, documentary and animation.

Previous recent winners in the category have included Best International Feature Film Oscar winners Another Round and Sentimental Value as well as Quo Vadis, Aida?, Triangle of Sadness and Anatomy of a Fall, which won the Best Original Screenplay Academy Award.

A number of documentaries have been nominated for Best Documentary and Best film including bombshell exposé NAZA, French box office break out Whispers in the Woods and Letters from A Silenced Country. Best Animation nominees also making it into the Best Film category include festival breakouts In Waves, Iron Boy and Jim Queen.

The upcoming ceremony in Greece will be the second time the European Film Awards take place in a new January slot as part of a strategy to place them at the heart of the wider awards season, having taken place in December for many years.

The January 16, 2027 slot once again falls the day after the end of nomination voting in the Academy Awards on January 15, and before the January 21 nominations announcement. This gives European talents in the running for the Oscars time to return home to Europe for the awards. The Golden Globes will also be done and dusted with its 2027 ceremony set for January 10.
