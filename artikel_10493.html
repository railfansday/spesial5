Nvidia stock soars on stunning news of Jensen Huang’s return, leading a market rebound for AI giants

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Update Monday, 9:40 a.m.:

Shares of Nvidia Corporation soared more than 30% on Monday after the company’s board of directors announced that founder Jensen Huang would return as CEO.

The surprise announcement comes less than two weeks after Huang was ousted over his relationship with an alleged Chinese spy, a scandal that rattled markets as investors pondered the uncertain future of a semiconductor giant at the center of the AI boom.

Jensen’s reinstatement is “effective immediately,” Nvidia said.

“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” Harvey C. Jones, Nvidia’s chairman of the board, said in a statement.

The company said its investigation into reports that Huang was “the target of a foreign intelligence” effort would continue.

In a remarkable disclosure, Nvidia’s announcement also said the board’s decision was made after receiving input from “senior levels of the United States government” that Huang’s return would not be a threat to national security.

News of Jensen’s return added fuel to a market bounce-back on Monday morning, not just for Nvidia but for other so-called Magnificent 7 stocks—most of which were already up in premarket trading after a punishing losing streak the week before.

Original story:

Last week was one of the worst weeks for stock markets in years.

America’s three major indices saw sharp declines as investors turned sour on AI-related stocks. The Dow ended the week down 8%, the S&P 500 down 8.6%, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq, where most AI-adjacent stocks trade, down 10%, placing it in correction territory.

But this week, the narrative is looking a little different. In premarket trading today, Dow, S&P, and Nasdaq futures are all bouncing back. Here’s which stocks are moving, why, and what to expect in the holiday week to come.

Tech stocks poised for a comeback?

Some of the hardest-hit stocks last week were tech stocks, especially companies operating in the AI space. 

Over the previous five trading days, Nvidia shares plummeted more than 20%, Meta Platforms fell nearly 12%, Microsoft and SpaceX declined over 7.6% each, Google lost 7.8%, and Amazon fell 5.6%.

Even Anthropic, which saw its shares jump 17% in its IPO on Thursday, ended the week up just 7% after falling 8.5% on Friday.

So far, today is a very different story. In premarket trading, as of the time of this writing, America’s three major stock markets are posting a comeback. Currently, S&P Futures are up 2.8%, Dow futures are up 2.1%, and Nasdaq futures are up 3.4%.

Many of the tech stocks that got hammered so hard last week are leading the charge today, including:

  • Nvidia Corporation (Nasdaq: NVDA): up 7.3%
  • Microsoft Corporation (Nasdaq: MSFT): up 3.3%
  • Tesla, Inc. (Nasdaq: TSLA): up 4.4%
  • Amazon.com, Inc. (Nasdaq: AMZN): up 3.6%
  • Alphabet Inc. (Nasdaq: GOOG): up 5.4%
  • Meta Platforms, Inc. (Nasdaq: META): up 3.6%
  • Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (Nasdaq: SPCX): up 2.8%
  • Micron Technology, Inc. (Nasdaq: MU): up 4.6%

Outside of the biggest of big tech, other AI-adjacent companies are also rebounding, including neoclouds:

  • CoreWeave, Inc. (Nasdaq: CRWV): up 6.1%
  • Nebius Group N.V. (Nasdaq: NBIS): up 7.1%
  • IREN Limited (Nasdaq: IREN): up 6.3%

All three companies fell by double-digit percentages last week.

What’s leading the turnaround?

Simply put: This appears to be a case of buying the bounce.

Stocks had a horrible week, with some of the biggest tech players seeing exceptionally steep declines; investors are now buying back into those same companies at a steep discount compared to their October highs.

That’s good for investors who want to scoop up what they see as heavily discounted shares, but the rebound in these tech stocks—and the broader markets—does nothing to address the fundamental concerns that caused last week’s steep sell-off. 

Investors have grown fearful that we are in an AI bubble, one that looks dangerously close to popping as Big Tech companies continue their AI-driven capex spending sprees. Those concerns will not be so easy to wave away.

Challenges still ahead

While today’s early-morning recovery (if it holds) is no doubt music to investors’ ears, major challenges still lie ahead that could determine how long a recovery lasts, or whether markets head further into correction territory.

On the tech side, one of the most pressing issues is who will lead Nvidia after its cofounder and CEO, Jensen Huang, resigned earlier this month in the wake of scandal over his extramarital relationship with an alleged spy.

Nvidia is perhaps the single most important company in the AI space, and its fate will heavily impact the broader industry. Right now, there is much uncertainty about who will be the company’s next CEO, even as supporters of Huang clamor to get him reinstated.

Beyond Nvidia, investors will be closely monitoring hyperscalers’ capex for signs of spending slowdowns that could ripple across smaller players in the AI space.

Industry watchers will also be closely tracking how Anthropic’s shares trade in the next several weeks given its much-hyped market debut last week.

A healthy appetite for the stock could signal faith in the AI markets, whereas a pullback could have the opposite effect.

Of course, less market activity than normal is already expected this week as markets will be closed on Thursday for the Thanksgiving holiday. And on Friday, markets have a shortened trading session that ends at 1 pm ET.
