I've Been Wrong About Paypal Stock for 2 Years. Here's Why I'm Finally Changing My Mind.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

PayPal raised its 2026 guidance as Venmo and AI-powered commerce gain traction. Here's what could keep the turnaround going, and what could stall it.

By Rick Orford – Nov 18, 2026 at 7:15AM EST

Investors have been skeptical of PayPal (PYPL -0.33%) for the past two years, and for good reason. Specifically, the company has struggled with slowing growth and intensifying competition.

That said, in its third-quarter 2026 results, reported on Oct. 27, PayPal raised its full-year adjusted earnings guidance following its stronger-than-expected performance. On top of that, between Venmo's growth potential and PayPal's push into artificial intelligence (AI)-powered commerce, the company may finally be turning a corner.

Under new CEO Enrique Lores, who took over in March 2026, PayPal is undergoing a broader transformation to simplify operations, improve accountability, and identify new sources of growth.

Instead of relying heavily on its traditional checkout business, management is seeking to derive greater value from its ecosystem, including Venmo, merchant processing, and consumer financial services.

And there are already some encouraging signs. Management highlighted improving momentum across several of its key growth businesses, suggesting that its turnaround efforts are starting to pay off.

Of course, PayPal faces several risks. For example, its branded checkout business remains a concern, with growth of just 1% in the third quarter, highlighting competition from other payment providers. On top of that, the company faces execution risks as it restructures operations and expands consumer financial services.

Overall, although it's still in the early stages, the combination of a more focused strategy and improving execution gives investors a reason to reconsider the company's long-term prospects, especially as it finds new ways to drive growth, starting with Venmo.

Specifically, one of the more interesting parts of PayPal's business is Venmo, which has grown into a broader consumer financial platform. Lately, the company has been expanding Venmo through debit cards, merchant payments, and other financial services. This matters because every additional use case creates another opportunity to generate revenue from an existing customer relationship.

In fact, during the third quarter of 2026, Venmo's payment volume grew 14% year over year, marking its eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth and making it one of the company's strongest growth drivers. Further, its Pay with Venmo offering grew by 43%, suggesting consumers are using the platform more for everyday purchases rather than just for money transfers.

Looking ahead, if management can successfully monetize more customer activity on Venmo, the platform could become a meaningful contributor to the company's earnings recovery.

But Venmo isn't the only part of PayPal's ecosystem showing promise.

Meanwhile, PayPal is looking at what could become its next major growth opportunity: AI-powered commerce. After all, even if consumers increasingly discover products through AI platforms, they still need reliable ways to complete purchases.

Plus, payments, identity verification, and security remain important to the shopping experience.

In January 2026, PayPal announced collaborations with Microsoft to power Copilot Checkout and with Alphabet to support Google's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP).

Beyond that, PayPal has been expanding its capabilities through its agreement to acquire Cymbio, a commerce orchestration platform that helps merchants make their products available across AI-driven shopping channels.

Looking ahead, if this new shopping model gains widespread adoption, PayPal could benefit from a new source of payment activity while helping merchants reach customers across multiple digital platforms.

With that, PayPal is pursuing these newer opportunities alongside its broader push to make its existing consumer and merchant businesses more profitable.

Venmo, AI-powered commerce, and a stronger focus on execution give PayPal several opportunities to reignite growth. These days, at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of just 9.9, PayPal looks discounted, suggesting that investors are pricing in a lot of skepticism around its recovery.

If management can sustain the turnaround and grow earnings consistently, there could be room for a rerating as investor confidence improves, which means the market could pay a higher multiple for each dollar of earnings. That said, the turnaround is far from guaranteed, but for the first time in a while, investors have reasons to be optimistic.
