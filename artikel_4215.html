Xbox Nano Controller Gets Its First Reviews, And A Secret Hidden Feature Is Revealed

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Update [Wed 4th Nov 2026, 7:33pm]: That hidden feature is now public, with Xbox announcing it today:

News Xbox Announces Two New Methods To Play Games Through The Cloud

"Starting today, we’re rolling out two previews for XBOX Cloud Gaming."

Original Story: Today's the day for the Xbox Nano Controller! This mini design with a portable nature goes on sale for £44.99 / $49.99 with a release date of November 4th, and therefore the first reviews have started going live for it.

Before we get to those, we also want to bring your attention to a secret hidden feature of the Xbox Nano Controller that's been uncovered by The Verge, which is that it can connect directly to Xbox Cloud Gaming via Wi-Fi in order to reduce latency when playing over the cloud.

The outlet expects this to be announced soon by Microsoft (it's seemingly still in testing internally), and that it'll also likely be part of the Xbox Elite Series 3 Controller whenever it releases as well. Windows Central believes that the tech will be part of "all future Xbox controllers".

"I’m expecting Microsoft to announce this as a preview for the new Xbox Nano controller soon, before it’s fully supported across Xbox Cloud Gaming on the web and on smart TVs."

So, let's get to those reviews! Here's what the critics are saying about the Nano controller so far:

"The smallest Xbox-branded controller to date, the nano is designed for gamers with smaller hands. Its compact nature also means it’s easier to take it out and about with you. It’s basically a standard Xbox controller with all the controls you’d expect, but in a much smaller package. And for those reasons, it’s undoubtedly excellent. It’s easy to set up, doesn’t take up much room when you have it on you, and does exactly what it sets out to do. While this is not necessarily a bad thing, it doesn’t go hugely beyond that, though."

"I’m not young, but I do have small hands, and I’ve been surprised at how comfortable the Nano is during gaming sessions. At just over 5 inches long, almost everything on this controller has been shrunk down somehow. The ABXY buttons are ever so slightly smaller than a regular Xbox controller, and the thumbsticks feel like miniature versions. Microsoft hasn’t changed the D-pad, though, or the size of the glowing Xbox button at the center of the controller."

"I recommend the Nano for someone who likes to game on the go, travels frequently or has smaller hands. It’s not as diminutive as other similarly sized controllers, and that extra size makes the Nano more comfortable than those other mobile controllers — but when I’m home, I’ll stick with my full-size gamepads, thanks."

"In any case, this is a really nice addition to the Xbox controller lineup. It's going to be ideal for travel due to its weight and size, and is easily pocketable. It might also be a better option for younger gamers with smaller hands. Microsoft has been marketing it against Minecraft Dungeons 2, which offers a glimpse at how they're thinking about positioning it."

As you can see, the Nano Controller seems to be a nice addition for anyone who wants a really compact design built for portability and small hands, and it certainly won't break the bank at its price point.

We'll hopefully have our own review of the Nano here at Pure Xbox very soon, but we haven't got our hands on it just yet. Look out for our impressions later in November!
