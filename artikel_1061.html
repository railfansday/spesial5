Erasmus could face World Rugby sanction after extraordinary officiating claims

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Rassie Erasmus claimed officials missed as many as 17 illegal tackles against his side in their defeat by France, and asked if "manipulation" of the result could be behind the reason not to look at the incidents; Springboks boss also thinks Ireland's Sam Prendergast will play on Saturday

World Rugby could review Rassie Erasmus' comments after the Springboks head coach made a string of extraordinary claims regarding officiating and decisions going against his side.

Not for the first time, Erasmus delivered a strikingly outspoken press conference on Monday, suggesting officials missed 17 illegal tackles against his side in their narrow loss to France, going as far as to question whether "manipulation" of the result is behind why incidents such as these are not picked up by officials during matches.

He also predicted that a "technicality" will emerge that sees Ireland's Sam Prendergast free to play Saturday's game against South Africa, after the fly-half was cited for a hip-drop tackle in his side's win over Fiji last weekend.

World Rugby could review Erasmus' comments in the context of the match official abuse sanction framework that was introduced earlier this year.

That process was brought in to reduce comments that lead to "the unacceptable increase in abuse and threats towards match officials".

Under this framework, an independent three-person panel review the comments to determine if they were made with a view to undermining confidence in officials, with potential punishments including multiple match bans.

Erasmus has found himself in hot water for publicly admonishing the performances of officials in the past. In 2021, he was banned from all rugby activity for two months for his behaviour towards match officials during his side's Test series against the British and Irish Lions, during which he released an hour-long video critiquing the referee's display in the first Test.

On Monday, Erasmus questioned why many of France's knee-drop tackles he believed to be illegal were not reviewed by the video referee.

"Let's say 11 of those were at least a penalty and three I thought was at least three yellow cards. And not one was sanctioned and not one was showed out or cited," Erasmus said in widely-reported quotes.

"I'll send you the playlist but I'll get into trouble… So I can't send the playlist to anybody and we can only send five clips to World Rugby. So we'll probably query it but the answer will probably be: 'Yes, it was a mistake, it should have been penalised'.

"On numerous times we mentioned to the referee 'we think there's a knee-drop tackle'. I'm saying to you my feeling is that knee-drop tackles is out. They happened 17 times in our game. Not one was penalised."

"And if the TMO didn't see it, then maybe technology must be better. If he saw it and ignored it, what does that boil down to? Manipulation of the game or the result? Or players getting injured?

"So, all I'm asking is don't take the TMOs out and discourage them to get involved. Then the wrong team will win and players will get injured. That's all I'm saying."

It is not yet known if Ireland fly-half Prendergast will be available for Saturday's crunch Nations Championship meeting at Dublin's Aviva Stadium, after he was cited for an alleged illegal hip-drop tackle against Fiji.

Despite the lack of ruling, Erasmus made it clear that he does not expect Prendergast to be penalised.

"We are in the northern hemisphere," Erasmus said. "I think there is going to be a technicality and he is going to play.

"We all thought it's an immediate red card. Everybody watched the game live because that's exactly what they said the hip-drop tackle is," he said.

"I swear I'm not moaning. I'm just trying to give you a perspective of somebody who comes and plays, for example, in France or in Ireland."

Ireland and South Africa have enjoyed an intense and at times fraught rivalry in recent years, with Ireland head coach Andy Farrell winning three of his five meetings against the Springboks.

However, South Africa were victorious in the most recent chapter of the rivalry, winning in Dublin a year ago in a game which saw five Irish players carded.

Should he be available, Prendergast would be expected to start at No 10 on Saturday, something Erasmus believes would not be fair given his tackle last weekend.

"It's difficult to understand when the TMO sits there and he sees that," he said.

"And it was something we discussed at the World Rugby meeting, and the examples of how it is was shown over and over, and we all had input.

"Somebody sees that and he decides there's nothing wrong with that."
