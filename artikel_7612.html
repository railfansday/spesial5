Peruvian National Extradited for Overseeing a Transnational Scheme to Impersonate Government Officials and Defraud U.S. Consumers

Karount Jannet Toledo Vazquez, 49, of Lima, Peru, was extradited to the United States and made her initial appearance in Miami federal court to face charges for her role in operating a large fraud and extortion scheme. Toledo was arrested on Oct. 21 by Peruvian authorities pursuant to a U.S. extradition request.

“The Justice Department will pursue and prosecute predatory transnational schemes responsible for defrauding U.S. consumers,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “We thank the Republic of Peru, including the Peruvian National Police, for its assistance extraditing this individual to face charges here in the United States. The Justice Department’s Cyber-Enabled Scam Initiative team will continue to work closely with law enforcement partners across the globe to bring to justice criminals who attempt to defraud U.S. victims from outside the United States.”

“No one should have to live in fear of arrest or legal consequences because of criminals masquerading as government officials,” said Acting U.S. Attorney Yara Lorenzo Klukas for the Southern District of Florida. “As alleged, this scheme exploited the trust of hardworking individuals, using deception, fear, and intimidation to rob them of their hard-earned money. This extradition marks an important step toward securing justice for the victims. Our office remains steadfast in its commitment to protecting the American public and holding accountable those who seek to profit from the fear and suffering of others.”

“This marks the fifteenth arrest and eleventh extradition in this investigation, which was made possible by the outstanding collaboration between Miami Division Postal Inspectors, the Justice Department, and our international partners,” said Inspector in Charge Bladismir Rojo of the U.S. Postal Inspection Service (USPIS) Miami Division. “Transnational criminal organizations who victimize our citizens are not beyond our reach.”

According to the indictment, Toledo owned and operated a Peruvian call center from at least 2013 through 2018 that defrauded and extorted U.S. residents by falsely threatening them with arrest, court proceedings, and other legal consequences. Toledo and her co-conspirators placed unsolicited calls to Spanish-speaking consumers in the United States and falsely claimed that they were qualified or selected to receive financial assistance for programs to improve their English language skills. On later calls, Toledo and her co-conspirators falsely told victims that because these victims had previously expressed interest in an English language program, they were contractually obligated to pay large sums of money for the program and products associated with the program. Toledo and her co-conspirators falsely impersonated police officers, U.S. federal officials, and representatives from universities, and threatened victims with court proceedings, arrest, and other legal consequences if they did not pay.

Toledo is charged with conspiracy, wire fraud, extortion, money laundering, and false impersonation of an officer of the United States. If convicted, Toledo faces a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison per count of conspiracy, wire fraud, extortion, and money laundering, and three years for each count of false impersonation of an officer of the United States.

Criminal Division Senior Trial Attorney and Transnational Criminal Litigation Coordinator Phil Toomajian and Trial Attorney Daniel Lipkowitz are prosecuting the case. Assistant U.S. Attorney Annika Miranda for the Southern District of Florida is handling asset forfeiture. The Justice Department’s Office of International Affairs, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of Florida, the State Department’s Diplomatic Security Service, the U.S. Marshals Service, the Peruvian National Prosecutor General’s Office, and the Peruvian National Police provided critical assistance.

This case is part of efforts being undertaken by the Department of Justice Criminal Division’s Cyber-Enabled Scam Initiative (CSI). The CSI is a dedicated team of prosecutors built to find scammers who prey on Americans, disrupt their networks, and hold them accountable. CSI brings together expert prosecutors from across the Criminal Division to accelerate the prosecution of cyber-enabled financial crimes, including complex frauds, government imposter schemes, romance and inheritance scams, financially motivated sextortion, lottery and sweepstakes scams, and other predatory schemes. With nationwide jurisdiction and global reach, CSI leverages every available tool to dismantle criminal operations, pursue bad actors, recover criminal proceeds, and obtain justice for victims. The Criminal Division partners with U.S. Attorneys’ Offices across the country and is a founding member of the Scam Center Strike Force.
