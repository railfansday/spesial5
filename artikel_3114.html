HomeKit Weekly: Meross’s new presence sensor splits a room into three zones for Apple Home

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Presence sensors tell Apple Home when someone is in a particular room, which is awesome for running automations based on that. They don’t tell it where in the room you are, though. That’s why these types of automations end up being all-or-nothing type things: you walk into a room, and every light comes on at full brightness, whether you came in to read at your desk or to grab something off the counter. The Meross MS605 splits its detection range into three distance zones, and each of them shows up in Apple Home as its own presence sensor so you can build automations around them.

You’ll set the zones in the Meross iPhone app during setup, choosing a distance for each. Once the Meross MS605 is in Apple Home (via Matter), all three appear as separate presence sensors, and you can build automations for each individually. If you only want two, disable the third in the Meross app, and it disappears from the Home app as well.

A bathroom is the obvious use case here for early mornings. Set the far zone to turn a lamp on at 5% and the near zone to bring up an overhead light, etc.

The same idea works anywhere the room has more than one job. A hallway can light the path dim from the far zone, then go to 100% when you get close. An office can keep the overheads on while you’re anywhere in the room and turn on the desk lamp when you actually sit down as well. I find it incredibly useful for overall dimming, though.

The Meross MS605 uses mmWave radar and PIR together, which is the current best practice for presence detection. Meross says the radar picks up micro-movement, so it knows you’re there when you’re sitting still, and the PIR filters out things that move but aren’t warm, like curtains and a robot vacuum. There’s also a radar-only setting if you want it as well.

Range is up to about 20 feet for motion and 13 feet for presence, with each zone configurable within those limits. There’s a built-in light sensor, too, so you can gate your lighting automations based on actual room brightness instead of guessing when you’ll want it with a sunset/sunrise trigger.

The Meross MS605 is IP67-rated and operates from -4 to 140 degrees Fahrenheit, so a covered porch, a garage, or a basement is all fair game for placing the sensor.

The initial setup runs via the Meross app over Bluetooth, and the app is required afterward. Zones, sensitivity, presence-and-absence timing, recalibration, and firmware updates all live in it. Day-to-day control and automation happen in Apple Home, but anything you want to tune requires going back.

One minor thing to know before you mount somewhere hard to reach: changing settings over Bluetooth requires pressing a physical button on top of the sensor, so keep that in mind when configuring your settings and initially placing it.

The Meross MS605 unlocks some unique use cases with its three zones. Three zones from one sensor is a new automation tool, and the IP67 rating means you can put it in places other presence sensors can’t go. It’s powered by a CR123A battery, which is included in the box.
