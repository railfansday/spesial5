Anthropic’s Alpöge says the 250,000-page Riemann proof figure was a joke

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The pages were real, he says, but never meant as a write-up. His Lean code has already been certified, and Clay now lists the problem as active rather than solved.

Levent Alpöge says he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis. Now the Anthropic mathematician says the 250,000-page figure tied to his proof was a joke. He made the point late on Monday in a post on X headed “Stupid things I did”.

“250,000+ pages was a joke guys!, I find big number extremely hilarious,” Alpöge wrote.

The number came from a footnote in the four-page note he released with his Lean code on 20 November. In it, he wrote that he had “amused” himself and “hit 250,000+ pages”. The note also said that posting such a text would be inappropriate, so he published only the code, as TNW reported on Saturday.

The figure spread fast. The Register put it in its headline on Tuesday, calling the written proof “250,000 pages of slop”.

Alpöge said he had tried to signal the joke through the pedantry of the main theorem. His note described a Claude draft whose Theorem 1 opened by pointing out that the real numbers are totally ordered. But “people ran with it unfortunately”, he wrote.

When one X user joked that he had made the number up, he said the pages were real:

“No it wasn’t made up! But it would have been ridiculous to take that seriously as a real writeup is all,” he replied on X.

In the same thread, Alpöge said his Lean submission holds unnecessary material. Cleaning it up was harder than he expected, Aamir Khollam reported for Interesting Engineering. He also answered criticism of his write-up from mathematician Scott Armstrong, saying clear exposition has long been hard for him.

Some readers took the thread to mean the proof was in doubt. A few hours later, he pushed back:

“People seem to be reading this as me saying there’s something wrong with the proof. I’m pretty confused. No? It literally checked out days ago?” Alpöge wrote on X.

He was pointing to the checks of the code. On 21 November, the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative certified the proof as correct.

The Clay Mathematics Institute, which offers $1m for a proof, has moved the Riemann hypothesis from “unsolved” to “active”, The Register reported. It does not yet count it as solved.

Under Clay’s rules, a solution must appear in a qualifying outlet and stay open to scrutiny for at least two years. It also needs broad acceptance from mathematicians. Kevin Buzzard, of Imperial College London, has said that nobody yet understands what the proof shows.

It is not the first time Alpöge has used X for a major result. In July, he posted there that Claude had found a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture.
