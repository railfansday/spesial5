Sean Neave won Wolves the game against Bristol City, and he can do the same vs Wrexham: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Wolves staged an impressive fightback to defeat Bristol City on Wednesday night. Scott Twine put the Robins ahead, and it appeared they would be securing a valuable three-points against an in-form Wanderers side.

However, Cesar Peixoto would introduce Sean Neave in place of Fer Lopez on the 73rd minute, and the move changed the game. First, the Newcastle United loanee pounced on the rebound to level the scores, before meeting Ladislav Krejci’s header back across the box to slide home and win it for the Old Gold with a minute to spare.

The goals were Neave’s first and second for Wolves, and in senior football. The 19-year-old has offered some bright performances off the bench to date, and the traits that helped him win the Bristol City game could see him play a key role against Wrexham this Saturday too.

Neave is a raw and developing young striker. He had only played two times for Newcastle United’s first-team before joining Wolves, but a prolific record at U21 (19 goals in 36 games) and U18 (21 goals in 37 games) means he comes highly regarded from his parent club.

For the most part, Neave has been used as an impact player from the bench. He started and assisted in the win over Burnley and played the first 45 minutes against Portsmouth in the following game.

However, at the moment, it feels he is most effective as an impact sub, and the Bristol City game showed that. Having watched Neave closely at both Wolves and in his Newcastle United development, he has a great burst of pace to pounce on loose balls and run beyond the defensive line.

This was on show in his goals against Bristol City – first pouncing on a rebound before meeting a header at the back-post to tap in his second. In between those two goals, he got the jump on the Robins defence to run in behind before guiding an effort wide off the inside of his foot.

Neave’s sharpness, speed and quick movement will be a worry for Wrexham. A lot of their centre-backs are strong, but they do not have the speed to deal with Neave.

By either playing in balls for Neave to run onto or subbing him on late against tired legs, he can expose the lack of pace among the likes of Joe Worrall, Max Cleworth or Dom Hyam.

Game time is key for the development of all young players, and Neave arrived at Wolves knowing he would have to earn his opportunities. Raul Jimenez joined to lead the line, while Adam Armstrong has a fantastic record at Championship level.

However, Jimenez’s injury means Neave has found opportunities, and he’s taking them. The brace against Bristol City will hopefully be an exciting sign of what’s to come from the teenage striker – both over the course of this season and in his career.

Neave will be taking it one step at a time, but just as he did against the Robins, he can be a thorn in the side of Wrexham’s defence with his pace and movement on Saturday.
