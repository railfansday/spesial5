2026 BCDA Grand: Sag Harbor Bay

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

When sustainability is at the core of a project’s design, rather than a collection of added-on features, its relationship with the landscape is symbiotic.

That’s the case with BMA Architects’ Sag Harbor Bay, perched atop a wooded bluff overlooking the water. Its low, horizontal form stays within the tree line and was oriented to minimize grading and tree removal, nestling into its surroundings, rather than competing with them.

“For us, sustainable design is about making thoughtful decisions that benefit both the environment and the way people live and enjoy their homes,” says Blaze Mazoid, founding partner with BMA Architects.

A central pavilion houses the main living spaces, with features like a vaulted ceiling and an eye-catching sculptural fireplace, while flanking wings lead to the primary suite and children’s areas. Outdoors, a sunken winter courtyard is carved into the hillside.

This home leverages indoor-outdoor connection refreshingly, with water views seen through the tree canopy, for an authentic experience.

Materials inside mirror those seen out the massive windows, such as mosaic stone, wide-plank, European heart oak floors, and warm cedar shingles that resemble tree bark. These all help to blur the boundaries between inside and out, making this home feel naturally rooted in the landscape.

Award: GrandCategory: Custom Home More Than 3,000 Square FeetArchitect: BMA ArchitectsBuilder: Seascape PartnersSize: 7,640 square feetLocation: Sag Harbor, New York

“At Sag Harbor Bay, sustainability was integral to the design from the outset. By working with the natural topography, preserving mature trees, maximizing natural light and ventilation, and selecting durable, regionally appropriate materials, we were able to create a home that respects its sensitive waterfront setting while enhancing long-term performance,” says Mazoid.

Additionally, a whole-home sustainability mindset drove many of the behind-the-walls features, such as high-efficiency HVAC, LED lighting, and a well-insulated envelope that improves this home’s ecological performance.
