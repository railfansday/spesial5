From USS Oriskany to HMCS Annapolis: 5 ships sunk on purpose between 1921 and 2015

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

These ships sunk on purpose include an aircraft carrier turned artificial reef, a nuclear test target and a German battleship used for gunnery practice

A retired warship does not always end its career in a scrapyard. Some are taken out to sea and sent to the bottom on purpose. The reason behind each sinking shows what a navy or government still wanted from the hull.

Three motives run through the history of deliberate sinkings. The first is weapons testing. Firing on a real hull lets a navy see how its shells and bombs perform against actual armor and structure. The second is nuclear research. In 1946, the U.S. gathered a fleet of target vessels at Bikini Atoll in the Marshall Islands to measure what atomic bombs would do to ships. The third is the artificial reef. After hazardous materials are stripped out, a cleaned hull can be placed on the seabed. There it offers marine life a hard surface to settle on. It also gives recreational divers a site to explore.

Scuttling is the term for deliberately sinking a vessel, usually by its own crew or owner. Not every ship here fits that definition neatly. SMS Baden, for one, was built for Germany and sunk by Britain.

This slideshow covers five ships sunk on purpose between 1921 and 2015. It is a selection, not a complete or ranked list. The group includes two American aircraft carriers and a World War II-era transport that became a missile-tracking ship. It also includes a German battleship taken by the British after World War I and a Canadian destroyer escort. One of them, USS Oriskany, is widely described as the largest vessel ever intentionally sunk to form an artificial reef.

The five ships also trace how the purpose behind deliberate sinkings has shifted. The earliest, SMS Baden, served as a target for British naval gunnery in 1921. USS Saratoga went down in 1946 during nuclear weapons testing at Bikini Atoll. The three most recent were sunk in 2006, 2009 and 2015. They went down off Pensacola and Key West in Florida and in Howe Sound, British Columbia. USS Oriskany and HMCS Annapolis were both sunk to form artificial reefs.

Each slide below covers what the ship was, where it went down and why it was sunk.

USS Oriskany was sent to the bottom off Pensacola, Florida, on May 17, 2006, to become an artificial reef. The retired carrier is widely described as the largest vessel ever intentionally sunk to create one.

The Oriskany was an Essex-class aircraft carrier. The U.S. Navy commissioned it in 1950. Its deliberate sinking came 56 years later.

The carrier now rests in about 212 feet of water off the Florida Panhandle.

USS Vandenberg was sunk off Key West, Florida, on May 27, 2009. The former missile-tracking ship now rests in about 140 feet of water.

The vessel did not start out under that name or in that role. It was launched in 1943 as USS General Harry Taylor, a transport.

It was later converted into a missile-tracking ship and renamed USS Vandenberg.

The hull measures about 523 feet long. That length far exceeds the depth of the water where the ship now lies.

The ship went to the bottom 66 years after its launch. Over that span, one hull carried two names and filled two distinct roles before it was sent to the seafloor.

USS Saratoga, a Lexington-class aircraft carrier, sank at Bikini Atoll after the Baker test in July 1946. The carrier was one of the target ships in Operation Crossroads, the series of U.S. nuclear weapons tests held at the atoll that month.

Saratoga was not sent down to build a reef or to give gunners a practice target. Its job in Crossroads was to sit in the target area and take the effects of a nuclear detonation.

Baker was the second test of the operation. Saratoga went under only after that blast.

That sets Saratoga apart from ships scuttled with charges or placed on the seabed at a chosen depth. Its sinking was a result of the weapons test, not the goal of the operation.

The carrier's wreck remains at Bikini Atoll, where Operation Crossroads took place.

SMS Baden was a German battleship that the British took over and then sank on purpose as a gunnery target in 1921. British guns sent it down, not its own crew.

Baden was not among the German warships scuttled at Scapa Flow. Its sinking was a separate event with a separate purpose.

After the battleship passed into British hands, it was not returned to service or turned into a reef. The British used it as a target and fired on it until it sank.

HMCS Annapolis, a Canadian destroyer escort, was sunk deliberately in Howe Sound, British Columbia, in 2015 to create an artificial reef.

The HMCS prefix is used for commissioned ships of the Royal Canadian Navy. Annapolis is the only Canadian vessel in this selection. Its wreck is also the only one here that rests in Canadian waters.

The 2015 sinking makes Annapolis the most recent ship on this list.
