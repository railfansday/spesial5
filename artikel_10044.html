The report he co-authored found that in the short-term, mandating AI tools may ramp up productivity.The Hawthorne Effect [wikipedia.org] is based on a series of experiments in the 1920s and 30s. They tried varying lots of aspects of a work environment to see how it affected productivity, like lighting levels, break schedules, and whether workers were given snacks. They found that just about any change led to an increase in productivity, but only for a short time. Then it went back to where it started. The particular change didn't really matter, only that you changed something, and that people knew they were being studied.This report is likely something similar. If you make people work differently, for a short time they become more engaged. They may also be focused on proving to their manager they can use the new technology. But it doesn't last. Productivity drops again. It's a mistake to assume the effect had anything to do with the particular change you made.

The only thing worse than not having metrics... ... is having metrics.

Organizations measure what is easy for them to measure, not what actually matters. The drift between what matters and what gets measured always produces dumb behaviors.

Measuring token usage is perhaps a proxy for "is this person trying to use AI?". That question is only interesting if you are quite confident that at some point, your staff will be able to grow the business value they produce as a result of using AI.

What you actually should measure is business value delivered. Companies are bad at that (Because its difficult), So instead they Measure what is easy to measure, and they don't test the assumption that AI helps staff deliver more BV.

At my small software company, having developers use Claude is delivering tremendous BV. We are getting higher quality PRs, code reviews, and built software, and we are getting it faster. We are getting MUCH faster bug investigations. Overall time to fix is down. We are shipping faster and we are paying down technical debt.

(we may be accumulating new technical debt we don't see yet. time will tell)
