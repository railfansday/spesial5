Tanggal: 2026-11-19

I agree. But I wonder if people suggesting a smaller conference are really concerned about total numbers versus making the experience a bit more comfortable and social. Upthread someone mentioned long lines for the buffet; I remember the same thing last year, as well as the ballroom being so packed with tables that it was difficult to navigate to empty seats if you didn't arrive very early. I left last year's conference with the same contradictory feeling mentioned by someone else: I thought the content was great and wanted more people to be able to experience it, but at the same time, it often felt that there were too many people crammed into too small of a space.

Maybe crowded physical space is something we just have to put up with, as a tradeoff for keeping the conference fee relatively affordable. But maybe there are also ways within that framework to facilitate more small group interaction, as others have suggested here. It sounds like a number of steps were already taken in that regard this year (sorry I wasn't there to experience those), but judging by this thread it seems there is hunger for more.
Some people don't seem to quite understand that the organizers are all volunteers. Seriously. All volunteers. If you want to be an organizer, you can be an organizer. It's a coalition of the willing. We had T-shirts this year for the first time. Guess how they got there? They sat in our garage for a couple of weeks, then were loaded into our car, and then driven 6 hours to the conference. Meanwhile people are like "I had to pass within 2 feet of someone and say excuse me twice on my way to my seat."

But as someone who has run conferences NOT as a volunteer, it might be helpful for people to understand the business model of a conference.

A 700-1000 person conference hotel must be booked 2-3 years in advance, signing not only a food and beverage minimum that is typically low to mid six figures but guaranteeing something like 1000-1500 room nights at the hotel will be sold (the actual event space is free). That is to say, if nobody comes to the conference and buys those rooms, the person who signed the contract will be buying them. The Bogle Center is probably risking 7 figures (certainly mid to high six figures) on each of the conferences it contracts to put on. A typical conference meal costs $75-120 a head. A typical soda sitting there in the hallway for you to pick up costs $7-10. It's hard not to shake your head when you see someone pick up 3 or 4 and put them in their bag for later. The AV team might cost $50K for a fairly bare bones production. Gouri and Christine said they visited 14 hotels to pick that one out. That doesn't happen in a single day. They must have dedicated a long weekend JUST to pick out the hotel. While holding down their regular jobs and doing their regular life things.

The first time I ran a conference, we sold 300 tickets, one for every chair we could fit in the room. Not the most awesome experience climbing over people to get to the only empty seats vs standing in the back. Rookie mistake. Haven't done that since. I mean, feedback is super helpful when organizing serial conferences, but please try to have a little understanding of the magnitude of the undertaking. After running conferences for the last 8 years, our daughter's 175 person wedding last Spring was child's play by comparison.

Bottom line: It's fine to run a conference for 200 people. As long as you plan for 200 people. What you cannot do, however, is plan for 700 people and then have 200 people show up. You'll go very broke very quickly. When we run a conference, we have two full-time employees focused solely on that conference for the entire year and 15 others who work on it occasionally and then dedicate a full week to it. Meanwhile, the Bogle Center is doing a similarly sized conference almost entirely with volunteers.

It's not about providing everyone the perfect, customized experience they wanted. It's about doing the best you can without hemorrhaging hundreds of thousands of dollars and hoping everyone is reasonably happy.

Want to get involved? They'd love to have your help. Not interested? Give feedback anyway, just don't be shocked if it isn't all incorporated. Sometimes there is a reason things are the way they are. Like the reception. It was supposed to be outdoors in an awesome venue by the pool. Then a random thunderstorm stopped by at just the wrong time. So in 15 or 20 minutes the entire plan laid out months ago had to be changed.
