Can you share more on your motivation for the marital trust after the credit shelter trusts. I am guessing there are additional assets that exceed the estate tax limits in your state or beyond.

From the little I've researched, and assuming the above, additional trust levers seem to get more restrictive and complicated (and then costly from legal support standpoint). After the first spouse dies, you are also getting into irrevocable. So I am assuming you would give up step up basis on the assets going to the marital trust. Did your attorney share any other alternatives you are evaluating?
If all goes according to plan, we expect our estate to be at or over the federal estate tax exemption once we divest from businesses. We are finding it difficult to plan, since we don't know what the companies will eventually sell for. We intend to use a portion of the federal estate exemption to transfer assets out of our estate and into irrevocable grantor trusts for our children.

No alternative options were presented regarding the basic estate plan with the credit shelter/marital trust setup. It is my understanding that the assets funding the credit shelter would receive a step up in basis at the first spouse's death and would then be excluded from the surviving spouse's taxable estate. Assets funding the marital trust would be included in the surviving spouse's estate and would receive a step up at their death.

We are comfortable with the general structure but have concerns with the mandatory funding formula and restrictions/loss of control for surviving spouse. We're discussing if it might make more sense to leave assets outright to surviving spouse rather than funding the marital trust. We don't want to handcuff each other and would like to avoid a situation where the surviving spouse musk seek approval from a trustee to access or distribute funds. Trying to balance possible estate taxes while maintaining flexibility and trying to understand which restrictions are truly necessary and which create more complications than benefits. It is currently written that the surviving spouse is trustee but will be limited to HEMS.
