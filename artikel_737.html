John Oliver Fills in for Tom Papa at Michael J. Fox Foundation Gala After FAA Ground Stop

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Friday's four-hour halt to flights taking off across the U.S. also served as fodder for Josh Johnson's stand-up set, where he poked fun at last week's Microsoft outage as well.

By Saturday, air travel across the U.S. had resumed after the Federal Aviation Administration’s four-hour nationwide ground stop on Friday.

But the effects of the stoppage were still unfolding, including in the entertainment world. At Saturday night’s Michael J. Fox Foundation for Parkinson’s Research gala in New York, host Denis Leary told the crowd that while they were fortunate that not many of the stars set to appear were affected by the ground stop, stand-up comedian Tom Papa, who was scheduled to perform at the benefit, was stuck in Los Angeles due to the grounding. In fact, he’d boarded his flight and was about to take off before the FAA ground stop on Friday.

But luckily the Michael J. Fox Foundation found another stand-up comedian in New York who could fill in: John Oliver. The Last Week Tonight host was a surprise performer at the star-studded annual benefit, where he initially joked that he was disappointing the audience after they’d already been told the scheduled comedian wasn’t coming and Leary got the crowd thinking maybe Jon Stewart would be filling in since he mentioned that Oliver knows Stewart.

The FAA flight stoppage also served as fodder for fellow stand-up and Jon Stewart associate, Josh Johnson, as the Daily Show host used the beginning of his stand-up set to poke fun at Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy’s reality TV past on The Real World: Boston and how the way he’d said a cyberattack wasn’t responsible for Friday’s ground stop (the FAA insisted the pause was due to air traffic control communication issues) made Johnson think it was definitely a cyberattack.

Johnson also got some mileage out of the Microsoft outage that lasted from midday Thursday into early Friday, joking that it was great for people who were bad at their jobs, because they could simply claim they sent an email that no one could check.

“This is a dream come true for a low-tier employee,” he said.

Elsewhere at the event, which raised a record-breaking $6 million, former Late Show hosts Stephen Colbert and David Letterman made separate appearances where they jokingly stressed their ongoing relevance despite their CBS late night shows coming to an end.

Colbert joked, as he introduced a video of scientists working on medical treatments for Parkinson’s as the Fox Foundation aims to find a cure for the neurodegenerative disorder, that being at the glitzy event at the Waldorf-Astoria in Manhattan made him “feel like [he has] a job.” And Letterman complimented the well-heeled audience in the hotel ballroom as he took a video on his phone to “prove to people that he’s still in show business.”

The event ended with what’s become a tradition at the gala of Fox joining the headlining musician onstage for a Back to the Future-inspired rendition of “Johnny B. Goode.” This year, Fox joined John Mayer and Brad Paisley along with No Doubt’s Tom Dumont and Shoshana Bean. And Mayer, like Chris Martin, shared that the “new sound” Fox showed off as Marty McFly had made him want to pick up a guitar when he was a kid.

At the end of the benefit, Fox grabbed the microphone and joked that being able to perform with Mayer, Paisley and others was “definitely worth having Parkinson’s for” and expressed a renewed commitment to finding a cure.

Earlier on the red carpet, Fox’s friends and former colleagues spoke about how happy they were that he’d been able to return to acting this year in Shrinking.

“When I saw the footage I said, ‘This is such a perfect pairing [of Fox and Harrison Ford’s character who has Parkinson’s], under a perfect premise,'” Leary told The Hollywood Reporter.

Fox’s former Spin City co-star Alan Ruck suggested that the work done through Fox’s foundation and improvements in Parkinson’s treatments may have contributed to him being able to act again.

“I know that the medicine has improved a lot since the year 2000, because that’s when he stepped away from Spin,” Ruck offered. “Because it wasn’t enough for him to go to work. It was debilitating. But the medicine has improved so much, now he’s on Shrinking. He’s done a number of things since then so that’s encouraging that all of this research is paying off. And through this foundation, he is helping people’s lives improve.”

And Leary, who’d worked with Fox on FX’s Rescue Me, teased that he might have another acting gig for his friend.

“I think he’s a great actor. So I jealously always want to create a character for him in whatever I’m doing. So I’m actually thinking about creating a character for him in this next thing I’m going to do,” Leary told THR.

Other stars who attended the event included Christopher Lloyd, Clark Gregg, Ted McGinley, Susie Essman, Ali Wentworth, George Stephanopoulos, Willie Geist and Terry Crews, whose wife Rebecca King Crews has been living with Parkinson’s, going public with her diagnosis earlier this year.
