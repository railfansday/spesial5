Sheffield Wednesday match v Plymouth Argyle hinges on a little-known EFL rule

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

A new era is underway at Sheffield Wednesday following the appointment of Dave Challinor as the club's new manager.

After parting company with Henrik Pedersen last month, Wednesday named Challinor as his replacement this week, but it remains unclear what the 51-year-old's first few weeks in charge at Hillsborough will look like.

Challinor had been out of work since leaving Stockport County in June following a near five-year spell at Edgeley Park, during which he led the Hatters from the National League to League One, and he missed out on promotion to the Championship in what proved to be his final game as his side were beaten 4-1 by Bolton Wanderers in last season's play-off final.

As he prepares to return to the touchline for the first time since Stockport's Wembley heartbreak, Challinor will take charge of Wednesday for the first time when they face League Two side Oldham Athletic at Boundary Park in the FA Cup first round on Sunday.

Challinor's second game at the helm will also be a cup fixture as the Owls welcome another League Two outfit, Accrington Stanley, to Hillsborough in the EFL Trophy, but he is yet to discover when his first league game will be taking place.

Wednesday, who currently sit ninth in the League One table, are due to host fifth-placed Plymouth Argyle on Saturday 14th November, but after the South Yorkshire outfit had a number of players called up for international duty, there are doubts over whether the match will go ahead as planned.

With the Premier League and Championship out of action, international breaks used to be an opportunity for League One to take centre stage, but postponements have become more common in recent times.

During the extended three-week international break earlier in the season, just four League One fixtures took place on each weekend, but Plymouth were one of the few teams to play twice, losing 1-0 at home to Burton Albion before winning 2-0 at Leyton Orient.

In contrast, Wednesday had both of their games against Stevenage and Cambridge United called off, which led many to assume that it was a mere formality that their clash with Argyle next Saturday would also fall victim to the international window.

However, speaking in his pre-match press conference ahead of Sunday's FA Cup trip to Oldham, Challinor claimed that he expects the game against Plymouth to go ahead as things stand, although he admitted that the situation could change over the coming days.

"There's still, I suppose, a little bit of uncertainty around the Plymouth fixture, but at the minute that looks like going ahead," Challinor told The Star.

"At the moment, that's where we're at. We'll learn more today, and we'll learn more over the weekend. Because even within the squads that have been picked, if there are players that are on standby lists, if someone picks up an injury and drops out, then they might well be called up. So, at the minute, we're preparing and we're prepared in terms of our schedule for next week, that the game will go ahead on Saturday."

League One clubs can usually apply for a postponement of a fixture if three of their players are selected for international duty, and Wednesday have already surpassed that number, with Jordi Liongola, Sean Fusire and Kieran Morrison being included in the Burundi, Zimbabwe and Northern Ireland squads respectively.

However, as per EFL rules, in order for a postponement request to be made, the players who have been called up must meet certain criteria, including starting or making a substitute appearance before the 75th minute in at least half of the club's matches or four of its last five.

As Liongola made just two appearances before sustaining a hamstring injury that has kept him out since mid-August and Fusire has featured three times in all competitions this season, the duo do not satisfy the requirements, meaning that Morrison's call-up is the only one that counts towards the eligible total so far.

After suffering another injury setback, Ali Al-Hamadi has been left out of the Iraq squad for the upcoming international break, but the Owls could yet have more players summoned by their national team, including Yan Valery, who is waiting to discover whether he will be included in the Tunisia squad, which is expected to be named on Saturday.
