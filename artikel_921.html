Underperforming Manchester City are an accident waiting to happen

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Enzo Maresca had two home games in four days to get Manchester City into decent shape for Sunday’s clash against Arsenal, which now feels like a must-win. On Tuesday, however, they ended up being taunted by Rasmus Hojlund on another evening that raised more questions than answers.

It is now one win in seven for Maresca, who cannot keep a clean sheet and has a £327m midfield that have played well individually, but is yet to form a unit. Worryingly, their reliable source of goals, Erling Haaland, had his quietest game of the season. It required Rayan Cherki to rescue a point against Napoli.

Maresca needs to find a solution in the next five days before heading to the Emirates to face the champions in what could be a defining weekend for the season. Defeat would put them nine points off the pace, with Maresca still trying to find his best combination of players to pick.

Club captain Rúben Dias was on the bench here and Josko Gvardiol was left out of the draw against Fulham at the weekend, which also ended as a draw rather than a morale-boosting win to get City out of their slump.

There is enough quality in City’s midfield to trouble Arsenal, but the conundrum for Maresca is how to fit them all into his system. Elliot Anderson was pushed towards the right against Napoli rather than in a central position to control the game, while Ayyoub Bouaddi was often alongside “inverted” full-back Nico O’Reilly.

Bouaddi showed his attacking instincts with City’s first equaliser; incredibly his first goal as a professional. There cannot be many £86m players who have yet to score in senior football. Enzo Fernandez also started against Napoli but was quiet before being taken off for the last half an hour.

It is matchday five of the Champions League as Manchester City host Napoli at the Etihad. These sides last met back in September 2025, with Manchester City winning 2-0 at home courtesy of goals from Erling Haaland and Jeremy Doku. Manchester City’s cause was helped by an early red card for Napoli’s Giovanni Di Lorenzo. Napoli’s only win over Manchester City came all the way back in November 2011 when they won 2-1 after a double from Edinson Cavani.

Manchester City have won three of their opening four Champions League matches this season, with their only defeat coming at home against back-to-back champions PSG. They drew 1-1 at home against Fulham in the league on Saturday, which means they are now without a league win in four games.

“We are always looking to win games, no matter the competition,” Manchester City boss Enzo Maresca said. “For sure, the four games in a row in the Premier League that we did not win has been a shame. Tomorrow [tonight] we have a chance to win the game and continue our path in the Champions League that at the moment has been very good. First of all, it is important to qualify and then probably, if you are not able to finish in the top eight, you have two more games that with the amount of games that we have, it is always important to reduce games. So the target is to try and finish in the top eight.”

Napoli, managed by Massimiliano Allegri, have struggled thus far in this season’s Champions League, winning just one of their opening four games and are all the way down in 27th. They come into tonight’s match off the back of a 1-0 home win over Torino in Serie A on the weekend courtesy of former Manchester United midfielder Scott McTominay’s winning goal. However, they still sit down in eighth in the Italian top flight, a massive 14 points off the top through just 12 games.

“This match will not be decisive,” Allegri said. “It is a simple mathematical equation, but obviously we will try to do well, even against a great team like Manchester City. It will be a beautiful and fascinating challenge and we want to play to the best of our ability. Ultimately, our goal is to qualify. We must not repeat the mistakes we made in Porto, where we played a great game for 70 minutes and then let ourselves go, conceding chances too easily. We will have to be able to play both with and without possession.”
