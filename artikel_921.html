Gianni Infantino’s controversial World Cup sell-off plan was ‘set to run for 96 years’

Infantino scrapped his plan in August following significant backlash

A controversial plan proposed by Gianni Infantino to establish Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE) would have transferred commercial rights for the World Cup for up to 96 years, according to leaked documents.

First revealed in a report by The Times on 28 July, the project – which involved private investors taking a minority stake – was dropped by Infantino on 1 August following opposition from three continental confederations.

Fifa had previously confirmed in July that the private investment group was to be led by Thrive Capital, a firm established by Joshua Kushner, the brother of United States President Donald Trump’s son-in-law.

Reports published on Monday by The Guardian and the New York Post cited leaked files showing that all commercial rights for Fifa competitions would have been handed to FFE for commercial exploitation over a 96-year period.

The Guardian reported that the contract was structured on a rolling basis up to a maximum of 96 years, with the New York Post adding that only a severe contract breach by FFE could terminate the agreement.

Both outlets reported details of an FFE management bonus pool, which could have awarded $50 million (£38.3m) for every $1 billion added to the initial $20bn valuation of the business.

The files also indicated that the board of FFE was to be chaired by the sitting Fifa president, a position currently held by Infantino.

A Fifa spokesperson said: "Fifa has consistently stated that the Fifa Forward Enterprise proposal would have been subject to the approval of the Fifa Member Associations and the Fifa Council.

"For the avoidance of doubt, any potential Management Incentive Plan was exclusively for members of FFE management, of which Fifa officials could not have been part. This is customary for proposed structures of this nature.

"Fifa’s leadership would only have had leading roles in the board, which would not have led to any additional remuneration. The remuneration of the Fifa President is set by the independent compensation sub-committee.

"Fifa intends to reply to questions submitted by its Council members and has no intention of breaching confidentiality by commenting first to media."

Fifa has invited Council members to review files relating to FFE at its Zurich headquarters.

Football Association chair and Fifa vice-president Debbie Hewitt intends to accept, though she and other European representatives on the Council are seeking clarity regarding which documents will be available, the review protocols, and non-disclosure terms.

Meanwhile, Uefa has lodged applications in the US legal system seeking document disclosure from Fifa, Thrive, and advising bank JP Morgan, with a view to possible Swiss criminal proceedings.

Fifa has described Uefa’s court filings as a "smear campaign".

Uefa stated in August that it had "lost confidence" in Infantino over the FFE proposal and has backed French Football Federation president Philippe Diallo to challenge the Swiss official in next year’s election.

However, Infantino remains the firm favourite to win a fourth term at the Fifa Congress in Rabat, Morocco next March.
