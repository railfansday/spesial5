Rice: More to come from Arsenal's £105m 'bargain'

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Declan Rice has agreed a new long term-contract at Arsenal so can he continue to go from strength to strength? Jack Wilshere, his former team-mate at West Ham, tells Sky Sports Rice is "definitely in the conversation as one of the best midfielders in the world"

Football Features Writer @nicholaspwright

Arsenal's Kroenke family owners tend not to get involved in transfer dealings but when it came to Declan Rice, and the prospect of spending £100m on a player for the first time, they needed to be sure that it was a sensible investment.

So, in the conference room at Arsenal's training ground, co-chair Josh Kroenke asked to be walked through the deal by Mikel Arteta, former sporting director Edu, then executive vice-chair Tim Lewis and now chief executive officer Richard Garlick.

"My eyebrows raised because I didn't know if we were in that phase just yet to go after a player like that," Kroenke recalled. He wanted to know what he would bring and how Arteta would use him. He wanted reassurance that he could hit the ground running.

Kroenke left the meeting in no doubt about the deal, either financially or in footballing terms. A club-record approach to West Ham was greenlit and Rice has of course gone on to prove an inspired addition, his new deal reflecting his importance.

"Declan Rice, we got him half price," is the refrain among Arsenal supporters. A player instrumental in their Premier League title success, who insisted "it's not done" then did more than anyone to drive them over the line, is now tied down for his peak years.

He has brought Arsenal even more than the club anticipated, arriving as a No 6 before being unshackled as a rampaging No 8, then reverting to a deeper role this season.

He scores, creates and dominates physically. His deadly set-piece delivery is another string to his bow.

"He is becoming a total player," said Arteta, who has even called on him at right-back. Of course, Rice filled in admirably in that role. "A total player has to be able to do as many things as possible and he has the capacity to do that," added Arteta.

Rice's adaptability and willingness to evolve his game have been themes of his time at Arsenal and the same traits were apparent to those who worked with him at West Ham, where he made an instant impression on his former team-mate Jack Wilshere.

"I love Declan, first of all a person," Wilshere tells Sky Sports. "I could see that straight away when I went in. He was very humble, his eyes were open, he wanted to learn, he would ask the right questions, and he just went from strength to strength.

"His footballing ability speaks for itself, but I wouldn't say he's someone who it came naturally to. He had to work on his right foot, on his left foot. But physically, he was always a beast and that would help him get out of certain situations."

Wilshere also noted the leadership qualities that have come to the fore at Arsenal. "Even when I went there and he was 19 or 20 and finding his way in the team, he had natural leadership and a natural aura about him that you could see," he says.

"He has a good understanding as well. When you look at players making big moves now, for me, one of the most important things around it is timing. I think Dec got his timing right.

"He spent a good few years at West Ham becoming a hero, becoming an important member of that team, trying to get that team up the table to finish high as possible. He won a trophy with them, and then he decided to go and compete for bigger things."

Wilshere and Rice crossed paths again following his move to Arsenal, where Wilshere, now head coach of Luton Town, was working as an academy coach and shadowing his old team-mate Arteta.

"I know how important he was to Mikel at that time when Mikel brought him in, and how he was going to be that shining light of the team who would always be present," he says.

"That's another thing that's really impressive about him: his robustness. He plays every single game. He dominates every single game physically."

Rice's record bears it out. Last season was his most demanding yet as he made 55 appearances in all competitions for Arsenal, many of them while managing hamstring nerve pain, before making a further eight for England at the World Cup during the summer.

He is an incredible athlete who continues to show remarkable resistance to injury. He has only missed five out of 119 Premier League games since his arrival at Arsenal. Only four outfield players have played more minutes in the competition in that timeframe.

His reliability is invaluable to Arteta and Rice continues to find ways to improve. Having moved back to the base of midfield this term, switching to the right side of the pivot to accommodate Myles Lewis-Skelly and Bruno Guimaraes, he has become notably more incisive with his passes.

According to Opta, Rice ranks second among Premier League players - and top among midfielders - for line-breaking passes this season, behind only Jan Paul van Hecke. He also ranks second only to Martin Odegaard for line-breaking passes in the final third.

He is sometimes criticised for sideways passing but he is averaging nearly twice as many line-breaking passes than last season, showing clear development in yet another area of his game.

The hope for Arsenal is that Rice will continue to unlock more levels after committing his future to the club. And certainly, that is the expectation from those who know him.

"I actually think there's more to come from Dec," adds Wilshere. "I've said it for a few years now. To be fair, he has started to add goals, but I still think he can add more with his physical ability, with his ability to strike the ball off his left foot, right foot.

"But he's already a top player and definitely in the conversation as one of the best midfielders in the world."
