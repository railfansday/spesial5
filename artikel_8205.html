Are Apex Ski Boots Any Good? A Bootfitter’s Honest Review.

Are Apex ski boots any good? A bootfitter skis the Antero VS Carbon at Kirkwood and explains exactly who they suit, and what you give up in feel on snow. (Photo: Jake Stern)

Overview: Apex ski boots pair a snowboard-style boot with a plastic-and-carbon exoskeleton that snaps into ski bindings. They suit skiers who want comfort and a few easy laps, including older skiers and those with mobility issues. The tradeoff is feel: a bootfitter who tested the Antero VS Carbon at Kirkwood found it carves when you angulate, but left him disconnected from the ski, especially in bumps.

Unloading Chair 6 at Kirkwood, I hesitated before dropping in. Sure, I’d skied the liftline bumps a dozen times without a second thought, but then I was wearing my own boots. I looked down at the contraptions buckled to my feet, a maze of tubular webbing, carbon cages and… snowboard boots, and sighed. I had promised my editor I’d test these Apex ski boots, and you can’t do that in your living room.

Founded in Colorado in 2006, Apex Ski Boots reimagined what a ski boot could be. They paired a snowboard boot with a plastic-and-carbon exoskeleton that snaps into ski bindings, purporting to offer comfort, warmth, and exceptional power transfer. Apex has three models: the Crestone VS, the Blanca VS, and the Antero VS Carbon, which I tested. After most of a decade of rabid reader requests to test Apex boots for SKI’s boot guides, we finally relented. I went to Kirkwood to see what all the fuss was about.

Pros and Cons + Soft boot is more comfortable than a rigid, traditional ski boot + Easy entry + You can shed the exoskeleton and walk around in the soft boot after skiing – Complicated to buckle up – Lacks precision and support on snow

I fit boots for a handful of years and you’re bound to pick up some biases. The crusty old sages who gift you their knowledge will also make sure their prejudices become yours. Van Osgood, a mentor of mine and an artist with a dremel, is a dyed-in-the-wool Apex Ski Boots hater. I lost count of the times I heard him describe making a turn in the hybrid boots: “First, you think about turning, then, a second later, the ski starts to tip, then, a second later, the ski starts to come around,” he’d say. “Think about that in tight trees.”

But I slowly overcame that bias, simply by watching skiers come into the shop carrying those boots. Older skiers, folks with mobility issues, and people who just wanted to take a few laps in comfort loved the things. I began to associate them with people who didn’t necessarily charge, but who loved the sport just as much as I did. The strong caveat here is an old friend, Tommy Ellingson, who can throw a cork 7 in Apexes, but I firmly believe he can do that in literally anything.

I wanted to see how the slander, hearsay, and praise actually shook out on snow—especially after the Apex rep talked up the Antero VS Carbon as made for “the double blacks and beyond.” I sized down aggressively to match my alpine boots—I measure just shy of a 28.5 but comfortably wear a 26.5—so the rep set me up with a heat mold and then told me to go skiing. Where better to test the double-black diamond performance claim than Kirkwood?

I didn’t head straight for Y-Spine. Instead, I dialed in the snowboard boots at the car and walked the chassis over to Chair 11 to get them on a groomer. The first thing I noticed is that to get the boot to feel even remotely snug, I had to dial in the buckles so tightly that the boots were much less comfortable than my daily drivers, the Armada AR ONE 130. I felt pressure points on my instep that made me loosen the dials for lack of circulation, which left some lateral play in my forefoot.

On snow, I decided I had no choice but to trust the boot. I remember thinking before tipping my skis: either this works, or I crash, but I have to try. It turns out I was being melodramatic. If you angulate, the boot tips the ski and you will carve a turn. That said, I’ve never felt so disconnected from my skis. I felt like I was floating far above the ski and couldn’t perceive any rebound out of the turn. I knew I was making an arc by looking down and seeing my progress, but there was zero proprioception to speak of—I didn’t have a clue where my body was in space.

Skiing bumps was an even stranger experience. The skis turned, and I could pivot against the bumps and make it down just fine, but turns felt incredibly vague, like I was skiing bumps more by memory than by feel—the boots took away the sensation of the snow underfoot.

I went back to my car after six laps to think. How did I want to relate this story to the world? To be clear, I had no interest in dunking on Apex. For a long time now, Apex boots have filled a niche for skiers who love the sport but have no interest or ability to buckle themselves into a plastic shell. And judging by online reviews, there are plenty of skiers who genuinely love these ski boots.

But with the advent of boots like the Rossignol Vizion and Atomic Nexus, I’m at a bit of a loss for where Apex now fits in the market. Because there are now high-performance options for folks with mobility issues, and those boots are far easier to get in and out of than a snowboard boot with multiple BOA dials inside a chassis with multiple buckles. And those boots are far more comfortable, as I find a squishy ski boot liner beats the stout snowboard liner—though I imagine the less “high-performance” models of the Apex offer better comfort.

What I’m left with is that somewhere out there exists a market for Apex ski boots, but I think that market is shrinking as new boot tech arrives each year. I’m happy to say that I gave them an honest shot, and they’re not for me. Apex boots make sense for skiers who want a few comfortable laps and don’t care about precision or feeling the snow underfoot; if you ski for feel, skip them. But I have the utmost respect for anyone who wants to get on snow and thinks this is the best option for them. I’ll leave you with proof that they carve, though I won’t be winning any beer-league GS events in Apex boots.
