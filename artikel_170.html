Mahmood ‘smells a rat’ over housing provision for vulnerable people in Birmingham

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Warnings over exploitation risks come amid push for investigation into supported exempt accommodation sector

The home secretary has launched an attack on providers of housing for vulnerable residents in her Birmingham constituency, saying she “smells a rat” after warnings over spiralling costs and risks of exploitation.

A push for an independent investigation into the supported exempt accommodation (SEA) sector in Birmingham, one of the largest in the country, is under way.

The SEA system is designed to support vulnerable people with additional needs, such as people with addiction, women fleeing domestic abuse and former prisoners, by providing them with housing as well as care, support and supervision.

However, politicians, charities and residents have raised concerns about the sector, including the existence of rogue providers who fail to provide the required support, as well as the risks to vulnerable residents of exploitation and antisocial behaviour. This week, an internal examination at Birmingham city council found more than £615m in SEA housing benefit had been paid in 15 months without “timely evidence” that relevant support had been given to residents.

Shabana Mahmood, the MP for Birmingham Ladywood, told the Birmingham Mail: “I see the impact of supported exempt accommodation all over the patch, and the use of it and the abuse I know happens in this sector is causing many of the issues in my communities.

“When you look at what is going on in the rest of the country we are such an outlier … I smell a rat.”

Mahmood said she was talking to Pat Mc Fadden, the work and pensions secretary,about the enhanced housing benefits that fund the sector.

Birmingham has more than 34,000 people living in SEA across approximately 12,200 properties, far outstripping the 22,000 SEA residents in London, despite the capital having a population seven times larger than Birmingham.

Some have argued increased demand and need in the city has made it a hotspot for SEA. However, others have criticised the local council for a lack of oversight and compliance, as well as national government for poor regulation.

Mahmood said she had regularly shared her concerns with the city council, including to leaders during the council’s 14-year Labour administration. “I can’t tell you how much I fought with my former Labour colleagues over this issue,” she said.

An independent review into the SEA sector, commissioned by the council’s cross-party audit committee and published this week, found in the year commencing April 2024 the council had paid £404.4m in SEA housing benefit. In a four-month period from April this year, the council had paid a further £211.8m – totalling more than £615m across 15 months.

The review assessed the local authority’s approach to the SEA sector and concluded it was not fully effective. “Risks are not always effectively managed, which may impact the achievement of objectives in some areas,” it said.

In particular, the report found 12,700 live cases were awaiting a review to prove care, support and supervision were being given to residents in SEA after the tenancy had commenced. This meant the council continued to provide enhanced housing benefit payments “without timely evidence” that relevant support was being given to residents.

The review also found, in some cases, SEA housing benefit claims were active for more than a decade, some more than 20 years, without being subject to eligibility checks.

Marcus Bernasconi, a Labour councillor, backed the need for an independent investigation and said the review showed the council had “totally lost control of supported accommodation with the bill for the taxpayer running into the hundreds of millions of pounds with no idea if genuine support is being provided”.

Government legislation to strengthen regulation in the sector, including a new licensing scheme, was passed in 2023 but remains under consultation.

A Birmingham city council spokesperson said the independent review “provides assurance that the council’s process for assessing housing benefit claims is robust and includes additional controls beyond what other councils have. The report also sets out measures the council will implement to improve monitoring and consistency across its processes relating to supported exempt accommodation.

“However, it remains the case that there is no adequate regulation for the council to effectively regulate the supported exempt accommodation sector in Birmingham. Even if the council had the additional measures mentioned in the audit report in place before, there would be no guarantee they would have impacted the supported exempt sector in Birmingham.”

A government spokesperson said: “We’ve been monitoring issues around supported housing in Birmingham closely and we’re actively considering next steps to ensure that all supported housing in the city is high quality, and that poor providers cannot take advantage of the system designed to help the most vulnerable.

“More broadly, we are taking action to raise standards in the sector – including ensuring councils have stronger powers to fine non-compliant landlords.”
