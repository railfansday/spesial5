Colombia: Car bomb attack injures at least 23

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The explosion in Villavicencio coincides with the tenth anniversary of a peace accord with FARC guerrillas, casting doubt on Colombia's peace process.

At least 23 people were injured in Colombia on Monday night when a car bomb tore through the central city of Villavicencio, local authorities confirmed on Tuesday.

The explosion occurred at approximately 9:30 p.m. (3:30 a.m. CET Tuesday) in front of the headquarters of the regional government of Meta province and caused severe structural damage to nearby buildings.

Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella confirmed the incident and said no deaths had been reported "so far."

Reporters for the AFP news agency said the blast knocked out power across parts of the city center, leaving damaged cars lining nearby streets and ripping open shopfronts.

"The doors were bent in, the windows shattered, some display cases inside broken," said local resident Johan Perez, who owns a hat shop. "We are alive by a miracle."

The Spanish EFE news agency reported that a well-known local restaurant was completely destroyed and suggested that the structural integrity of the government building itself may have been compromised, raising concerns that it could collapse.

The attack has not been publicly claimed by any group but it came on the eve of the 10th anniversary of Colombia's landmark peace accord with FARC guerrillas.

The agreement persuaded the FARC, once Latin America's largest guerrilla army, to lay down its weapons after more than half a century of conflict.

Shortly before Monday's blast, President De la Espriella had denounced the peace accord as a "false peace."

On Tuesday, he visited the explosion site and blamed the attack on Antonio Medina, a rebel commander and member of the dissident group FARC-EMC.

President De La Espriella, who took office in August and has launched a United States-backed military offensive against armed groups and drug traffickers, blamed "narco-terrorists" for the attack.

"I warn the narco-terrorists responsible: you will answer for every drop of blood you have spilled and every business and property you have destroyed," he wrote on social media.

"We will deploy the full power of the state to hunt you down, dismantle your organizations and take you out." De La Espriella added.

The incident marks the latest violent resurgence of a conflict that has shaken Colombia, the world's largest cocaine-producing nation, over the past two years.

Between January and November, authorities recorded 358 drone strikes, more than one a day, according to a Defense Ministry document obtained by AFP.

Last year, a presidential candidate was assassinated on the campaign trail.
