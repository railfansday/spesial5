Democrat writes off Trump’s attempt to blame party for flight grounding as ‘absurd’

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Dan Koh, an incoming House representative and ex-Biden staffer, blasts Trump for blaming previous administration

Donald Trump’s efforts to blame Friday’s four-hour nationwide flight stoppage – and the resulting travel chaos – on Democrats are “absurd”, an incoming Democratic member of Congress said on Sunday.

The president remained largely silent and out of public view as air travel shut down on Friday, but on Sunday he emerged to issue a series of posts blaming the incident on the Biden administration.

“The Biden Administration was an FAA disaster,” he said in one post on Truth Social. “Everything they touched turned bad. Big money spent unwisely, making an old and broken system worse than when they began. We have the knowledge and plan for the Flight Control Systems, we need Congress to give the money, FAST! Our FAA is doing a great job, and is ready to go.”

In another post, Trump said: “If Congress approves the desperately needed money for Air Traffic Control, we can get it built quickly and efficiently. It will be the BEST SYSTEM anywhere in the World. Much of our current air control system is 50 years old – RIDICULOUS! We have already started the work, but it should be done all at once, not in drips and drabs. Congress must approve. The Biden Administration set it back years!”

Dan Koh, a US House representative-elect from Massachusetts and former Biden administration official, blasted the effort to cast aside blame on Sunday.

“It’s kind of revisionist history – you might as well start blaming Ronald Reagan for the FAA strike in the 80s as part of this, too,” he said on Sunday during an appearance on MS NOW’s The Weekend. “It’s absurd.”

Koh said Joe Biden secured “historic funding not only to the FAA but over a trillion dollars of infrastructure money” between Trump’s two presidencies. But, Koh added, “a lot of it … Donald Trump has reversed on the grounds of it being too woke”, invoking a term that those on the political right use to refer to things they perceive to be overly leftist.

The congressman-elect said he talks frequently with mayors and city council members in his district about “all this money that would help them with upgrades to transportation including flights” – but “they don’t even know if they’re going to get any more because the [Trump] administration has not even gotten back to them on the status.”

“This is the reality: Donald Trump likes to say it’s Biden’s fault, but it’s actually his policies that are stopping the funding from hitting the towns and cities,” Koh said.

Biden’s signature legislative achievement, the bipartisan infrastructure law, allocated $25bn to the FAA to improve air traffic control. The Republican controlled-Congress also approved $12.5bn to modernize air traffic control in July 2025, months into Trump’s second presidency.

Trump’s transportation secretary, Sean Duffy, has said the FAA needs $50bn for improvements and has blamed Democrats for the stoppage – which evidently resulted from botched maintenance work upgrading telecommunication lines from old copper wire to fiber optic cables in at least one remote location, leading to the delay or cancellation of thousands of US flights mostly on Friday and Saturday.

“The Congress was very specific in how I spend that money,” Duffy said during an interview on CNN’s State of the Union on Sunday. “I have a certain allotment for radar, a certain allotment for radios, a certain allotment for building new infrastructure for centers for air traffic controllers. They didn’t give me money for telecom. They didn’t give me money for new software.

“We’re going to spend that $12.5[bn] by the end of 2028. But I need to rest of the money for software and a new telecom architecture that will be way more resilient.”

US senator Chris Van Hollen, a Maryland Democrat, noted in his own interview on Sunday that Republicans had spent only a third of the $12.5bn allocated.

“It’s really rich to hear the secretary pointing the finger at Congress when Congress has given the administration what they asked for,” he said on CNN’s State of the Union. “If they need more, they should come up and justify it.”
