Inside Diego Gomez's remarkable rise to Premier League's king of the wondergoal: Plucked from a rural Paraguayan village at 13, doing Lionel Messi's running for him at Inter Miami and starring as Brighton's aggressive machine

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 02:00 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 08:07 EST, 21 November 2026

Scattered across the flatlands of Paraguay’s deeply religious Misiones District, where families rely on the land to feed themselves through cattle ranching and cotton production, the people are idolising one of their own.

Diego Gomez’s ascent from the humble beginnings of a farmer’s son to the heart of a Premier League title race is a Hollywood director’s dream. While it is a script that has been read many times before, like an old movie or a classic song, it is also one that never gets old.

In this particular iteration, it begins on the dirt tracks of Mburika Reta, which literally translates to the ‘land of donkeys’ in Paraguay’s native Guarani, before winding through the country's capital, Asuncion, and briefly stopping among the palm-lined suburbs of Miami alongside Sergio Busquets and Lionel Messi. It is now gathering pace on England’s south coast, where Brighton find themselves in the unfamiliar territory of hunting down the Premier League champions.

Gomez is the epitome of Fabian Hurzeler’s machine: energetic, aggressive and relentlessly forward-thinking. It is through this collective sense of selflessness that the Seagulls are second in the table, having already defeated both league leaders Arsenal and Manchester City this season. And for Hurzeler’s versatile midfielder, every performance and goal is a reminder of the moments that forged him.

‘He always told me to play forward and, when I have the ball, to have no fear,’ the 23-year-old said last year of his former team-mate Messi.

Cue a spectacular goal from the halfway line against Crystal Palace last month, the Amex crowd growing feverish as the ball sailed over Walter Benitez before crashing into the back of the net.

Diego Gomez pictured as a young teen playing for Libertad (left), is now thriving for Paraguay (right) and one of Brighton's most important players

Gomez is congratulated by Pascal Gross after scoring a brilliant goal against Brentford

Another quickly followed against Brentford last weekend, with Gomez barely bringing the ball under control before hooking it into the net from 25 yards out with his supposedly weaker left foot, leaving half of his team-mates clutching their heads in disbelief.

Inevitably Gomez’s rise has not been without its setbacks. It was his mistimed tackle on Desire Doue, stretching out a leg to topple the French winger in the penalty area, that led to the heartache of Paraguay’s World Cup elimination this summer, after Kylian Mbappe slotted home the resulting spot-kick.

Yet rather than be defined by this mistake, Gomez returned home to a hero’s welcome, mobbed by locals as he was paraded through the streets of the usually sleepy San Juan Bautista in an open-top car.

‘He has the right attitude towards football,’ Hurzeler said shortly after his arrival. ‘He loves to play. You can feel his passion in every talk with him.’

Across his 10 appearances this season, Gomez has won 66 duels, more than any Brighton player. His 35 aerial duels won, second behind only Croatian centre back Luka Vuskovic, point to his impressive spring, and he has won 10 tackles, again more than any other Brighton player.

At the other end of the pitch, meanwhile, he has taken the most touches in the opposition box and has scored two goals and recorded two assists this season.

While Gomez’s physique gives the impression of a battering ram, he is surprisingly speedy, recording Brighton’s fastest top speed of the season against Chelsea at 21.01mph.

But who is this kid who was plucked out of nowhere? Who, at the age of 13, moved four hours away from his rural village to the capital to play football, navigating the bus networks as a child to get to training at FC Libertad before a move to join David Beckham’s glitzy side arrived seven years later?

Gomez, seen with his partner Geraldine Villagra, has taken an unusual route to the top

In this case, it was Inter Miami who did the plucking, with Gomez identified by Brighton’s scouting network while he was playing in the MLS in 2024. Gomez formally joined the club as a 21-year-old in a deal worth £11million in January 2025, having agreed the move six months earlier.

Former Brighton centre back Gordon Greer is now the club’s pathway development manager and spoke regularly to Gomez during this time, travelling to Florida to see him during the latter stages of the MLS season. It’s fair to say he liked what he found.

‘We knew that he would do well because of his application,’ Greer says. ‘He was just a really talented, young, hungry footballer. You could see that he had raw potential, and I think Brighton do that really well. They sign players who have got room to improve.

‘The one thing about Diego that is really impressive is his attitude, his work rate. He’s a really driven character and he’s such a humble kid. You can ask Diego to do certain things and he’ll give it his all, no matter what you ask him to do, whether he’s good at it or not. As a coach, it must be amazing to have someone like that in your team.’

During his six-month wait for the move, Gomez undertook regular English lessons, tuned in to Brighton’s games on television and spoke to Hurzeler about the areas he could improve before making the switch. All of that left him with a couple of weeks’ head start, which in Premier League terms is massive.

Meanwhile, Brighton’s plethora of South American footballers at the time, most notably Julio Enciso, who played alongside him at Libertad, helped him settle quickly. So too did Gomez’s girlfriend, Geraldine Villagra, who moved to Brighton to live with him, while his family regularly visit from Paraguay.

Gomez is the epitome of Fabian Hurzeler’s energetic machine for the Seagulls

Spending 18 months alongside Messi, Busquets and Luis Suarez was also another important step in Gomez’s development, despite his understandable initial nerves. He forged a strong understanding with Messi on the pitch and was the engine that enabled the Argentine genius to thrive. Much like at Brighton, though, that did not stop Gomez from producing moments of magic of his own.

‘The fact that he had these superstars in the team probably helped him,’ Greer says. ‘We don’t have any superstars in our team like Messi, but everyone gives their all.

‘At Inter Miami, Messi was by far the best player, but Diego did a lot of the running for him and had a fantastic season in which he contributed a number of goals and assists. He just worked away at it. Regardless of the kind of environment you put him in, he finds a way through it.’

The audacity of Gomez’s two recent goals, the strike against Palace that inevitably won the Premier League Goal of the Month award for October and the Brentford one which is firmly in contention for November, almost contradicts that industrious nature. Has Greer been surprised by them?

‘He scored some really good goals for Inter Miami, but not as good as the ones he’s scored of late for us,’ he says. ‘He wants to achieve big things. It’s no surprise... well, it is a surprise when you see him stick one in the top corner with his left foot!’

Gomez endeared himself to many when he broke down during a pre-World Cup press conference while speaking about the privilege of representing his country, and he remains deeply aware of where he has come from.

Earlier this year, Brighton chose to extend their UNICEF partnership to Paraguay, with Gomez becoming an ambassador for a programme that aims to develop young people's skills and confidence through sport and will reach thousands of children across the nation, a role he regards as a great honour.

After Paraguay’s elimination from the World Cup, Gomez attended one of the events in Asuncion, where he first plied his trade and where UNICEF will soon roll out the programme.

‘During his personal time, after coming back from the World Cup, he could have done lots of other things and chose instead to do that,’ Anna Easthope, Brighton’s head of marketing, adds. ‘He realises the impact of meeting kids, giving them inspiration and letting them know that sport is for everyone.’

If a hero’s welcome followed World Cup elimination, imagine the reception a Premier League title would bring to Misiones.
