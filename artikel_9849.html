A missing version broke verification of signed ARD search results

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Posted on Nov 10 • Originally published at modelmarket.dev

The weather result on modelmarket.dev looked complete until the verifier compared it with its signed subject. The subject named a version; the search result did not.

The full catalog entry had version. A serializer had dropped it from the search representation while keeping the trustManifest that signed it. We caught that on November 4, during an ARD rollout to six AIMarket hubs, and deployed a second build.

The useful part of this bug is where it failed. Checking a signature and checking that the signature belongs to the result in front of you are separate operations. You can reproduce the second failure with a public search result and a few lines of Python.

ARD is Agent Resource Discovery. This implementation follows parts of Initial Contact revision 00 and Manifest and Lookup revision 00, individual Internet-Drafts dated October 30. They remain work in progress, with no IETF endorsement or approved-standard status.

In this implementation, trustManifest.signature is a detached JWS. Its signing input is the protected header followed by the JCS-canonicalized trustManifest with signature removed, both base64url-encoded and joined by .. The signed subject contains the resource's identifier, type, version and digest.

The missing version breaks step 2. The JWS signs the inner manifest, so deleting a field from the outer result need not alter the signature at all.

Controlled reproduction: only the outer result's version is removed. The signed manifest and descriptor bytes stay unchanged.

A November 8 check also verified the live result's DID binding, Ed25519 signature and descriptor digest. After that same in-memory deletion, the signature and digest still passed while the subject comparison failed. The regression test now compares the whole subject, including field presence, rather than selected coordinates.

The original rollout checks had a coverage gap: weather searches on the other hubs returned unsigned peer listings. They exercised lookup and descriptor retrieval without ever entering the signed local-result path. The main hub's own weather result reached it.

Five of the six deployments sit at a domain root. Each signs its own entries with its existing Ed25519 key under a did:web identity.

An indexed peer listing keeps the peer's publisher identity in its identifier and names the peer as source. The indexing hub leaves that entry unsigned: it is a discovery hint, with publisher authority still to establish. The sixth deployment, at independentai.network/hub, also serves unsigned entries because that installation has no did:web identity configured in this implementation. Its origin-level well-known address redirects to the manifest under /hub.

This is why the walkthrough prints both source and score. Source tells you whose listing you are examining; score only ranks search relevance. Establishing who signed a descriptor says nothing by itself about the correctness of a weather reading or whether the price is good.

The published CDDL grammar did not compile unchanged with cddl 0.12.14. It redefined prelude names, and the tool stopped on the content-delivery group choice inside an entry map.

The conformance folder retains that original and a separate, documented checkable copy. The latter removes the conflicting definitions and uses two map alternatives for delivery. Manifest, listing-page, search-request and search-response samples then validated; an entry missing its URL and a search score of 101 were rejected.

The open extension map still let the grammar accept both url and data on one entry. That exclusion needed an application test. These are checks of sample shapes and specific rules, not evidence of complete conformance or cross-vendor client interoperability.

There is a concrete client compatibility issue too: these hubs sign with EdDSA, while the discovery draft makes ES256 mandatory to implement. An ES256-only verifier cannot check these signatures. Check the advertised algorithm before choosing a verifier for this catalog.
