Anthropic’s Claude builds custom gadget blueprints, but consistency stays hidden

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Anthropic's Claude designed circuit boards, enclosures and firmware for three working gadgets assembled and tested by engineers.

Anthropic says Claude turned short gadget briefs into designs for three working prototypes, coordinating circuit boards, enclosures, and firmware that human engineers then assembled and tested.

For the speakerphone, Anthropic said Claude produced specifications and schematics, placed components on a printed circuit board, routed the copper connections, designed a 3D-printable case, wrote the device's software, and prepared a parts list. The other two devices followed a similar process.

According to Anthropic, Claude checked whether the speakerphone's board would fit inside its case and found that the mute switch would collide with a wall during insertion. It added an assembly step that tilted the board into place.

For the voice-assistant hub, Claude changed the enclosure's shape to shorten microphone ports. It also wrote speakerphone firmware that dimmed the LEDs as audio became louder, keeping the device within the board's designed electrical-current limit, Anthropic said.

Anthropic said Diode manufactured all three boards directly from Claude's design files, without exchanging manufacturing adjustments, and that the prototypes worked without circuitry fixes.

Anthropic said they had not undergone electrical validation, antenna tuning, thermal testing or certification and were not designed for mass production. A functioning prototype still needs substantial engineering work before it becomes a commercially ready product.

The disclosure also provides no overall success rate across repeated end-to-end design attempts. Its three working examples show what the collaborators say they achieved, while leaving unanswered how consistently the process could deliver another usable device.

Anthropic's planned next steps include denser, faster, and more complex boards, simulation checks, and learning from measurements of finished devices. For engineers and hardware builders, that would extend the work from producing a design toward checking how it behaves once built.
