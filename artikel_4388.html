Though the brand remains a bit of an apparition in the U.S., Vietnamese-based EV automaker Vinfast continues to forge ahead on a global scale. Key to that mission is the VF9, a three-row, dual-motor electric SUV intended to compete with the Kia EV9 and the Rivian R1S. The VF9 is the brand’s second model to hit the market in the U.S. following the two-row VF8. Eager to make a good impression out of the gate, VinFast enlisted vaunted Italian design firm Pininfarina to style the VF9. Available in two trims, Eco and Plus, the interior design is contemporary but favors function over flash. Standard features include a heated driver’s seat, a 15.6-inch infotainment display, and head-up display. Power output from the dual-motor powertrain is 402 horsepower, and the EPA has certified the VF9 Eco for a maximum of 330 miles of range and the Plus for 290 miles. Currently, the VF9 is assembled in Vietnam, and is only on sale in 16 states. However, the company's plans to build a production facility in North Carolina are still in play, and its sales footprint could grow if and when the plant comes online at a still undetermined date.

Following a short run of 2024 VF9 Plus models, the 2025 model year is slated to include the Eco base model, which offers more range and a lower price. It’s unclear exactly when the Eco trim will become available, but when it does it will be the VF9’s range leader with an EPA-estimated 330 miles of driving between charges.

Both models come with the same all-wheel-drive electric powertrain, which consists of two electric motors totaling 402 horsepower and 457 pound-feet of torque. VinFast estimates that the VF9 should reach 60 mph in as little as 6.6 seconds. When we get a chance to test-drive the VF9 for ourselves, we’ll update this story with driving impressions and test results.

The VF9 will be offered exclusively with a 123.0-kWh battery pack. The EPA estimates the VF9 Eco can go as far as 330 miles, but that number drops to 291 miles for the Plus model. VinFast says the VF9 can charge from 10 to 70 percent in 35 minutes.

The EPA estimates that the Eco model will return 79 MPGe city and 71 MPGe highway while the more luxurious Plus trim comes in slightly lower at 71 MPGe city and 66 MPGe highway. When we get a chance, we'll take the VF9 on our 75-mph highway fuel economy route to test its real-world efficiency. For more information about the VF9’s fuel economy, visit the EPA's website.

The VF9’s cabin looks premium in photos, sporting a three-row layout and a design that wouldn’t look out of place in an Audi. Faux-leather power-adjustable seats with heat are standard, but the upscale Plus trim adds leather upholstery, a massage feature, and second-row captain’s chairs. Dual-zone automatic climate control is standard and features an air ionizer to help remove smells from the cabin; going with the Plus trim adds a third climate control zone for the rear passenger compartment. A power-operated rear liftgate will open hands-free if you kick your foot under the rear bumper, but we’re not yet sure how much cargo space we’ll find back there once the hatch is open.

A large centrally located 15.6-inch dashboard-mounted display serves as the infotainment touchscreen as well as the digital gauge display, a design similar to that of the Tesla Model Y. A head-up display also projects critical information onto the windshield in front of the driver, so those who don’t like the idea of glancing to the right to keep an eye on speed will be able to have that info available straight ahead. A voice assistant is included to allow users to interact with the system without touching the display. There are other features to help pass time while charging, such as onboard video games and an e-commerce service embedded into the software.

All VF9 models come with a comprehensive package of driver-assistance features, including some convenient ones such as automatic high-beam headlamps and traffic sign recognition.
