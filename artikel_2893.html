Knicks Bulletin: ‘I like the city of Portland’

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

If you are like me, you thought you could go to bed early yesterday.

If you are like me, then you realize you were not doing so, because at the end of the day it's the Knicks and they do (yes, after the championship too) Knicks things.

Oh, well, good for all, we stayed awake, and tears rained at the end of the day.

On Mikal Bridges maintaining his composure through a tough start to the season:

"He's the same. Whether he's quote-unquote struggling, he just keeps playing and he doesn't let anything impact him. He's gonna let it fly, and if he can push it, he's gonna push it. He's gonna try to defend, but he's not gonna get too high. He's not gonna get too low, and he's been there and done that. So for him to have the ball in his hands at that point in the game, there's a lot of confidence not only coming from him, but from his teammates and the staff."

On managing the rotation without Karl-Anthony Towns and OG Anunoby:

"I have an idea. I always try to go into the game with a game plan with my minutes sheet. Very seldom do I follow it. We'll have a minutes sheet going into the game."

On Landry Shamet's effort, shooting and defensive versatility:

"I felt he was a really good basketball player coming in. The biggest thing is he plays hard. So even when he's not shooting — because he hasn't shot the ball great every single game — but when he's not shooting the ball, you still feel his impact.

"He plays so hard. He's probably asked to come out of games more than anybody else because he really gasses himself being out there. Every play is that meaningful to him.

"Obviously, you can't leave him open. He's been knocking down shots for the most part. We put him on the ball in pick-and-roll situations, guarding pick-and-roll guys; we put him off the ball so he's had to chase guys. We've even put him on bigger wings and he's done a heck of a job fighting those wings. He's been phenomenal for us. We hope he continues to be that way."

On Shamet exceeding his expectations and being capable of starting for winning teams:

"It's a good question. I didn't know if he'd be this], because I think he can start for a lot of teams in this league, and I'm talking winning teams, not just any old team, but for winning teams.

"I didn't necessarily look at him that way, but I did feel he was definitely a rotational player for this team, because again, you can't teach some of the things that he does out on the floor. They're special. And it's hard to teach that, but in the same breath, you need it in order to have some success, in my opinion."

On James Wiseman's performance against Portland:

"I thought James was great. He ran the floor in both directions, he talked defensively, he impacted the ball at the rim in a phenomenal way. I thought he was really good in his minutes."

On the importance of quick decisions and purposeful movement in the Knicks' offense:

"We have to make sure that every time we cut, it's a scoring cut and our decisions are quick."

On Shamet's off-ball movement creating opportunities for teammates:

"You can tell when a guy goes from Point A to Point B with a little bit of velocity. A guy like Landry Shamet, he is doing it every single time. With him shooting the ball the way he's shooting it, that gravity that he carries when he moves, you can't really see it or feel it if you don't know the game well. But when he moves that quickly from Point A to Point B, everybody is just leaning just a little bit in that direction, which may create an opening or an advantage for your teammate that's away from him."

On getting every Knicks player to move aggressively without the ball:

"Trying to get everybody to do that all the time is huge. Because the defense doesn't have a chance to catch up, they don't have a chance to get set, and they just gotta react. And sometimes they'll overreact because you're going so hard. And you can see it as the game goes along. If guys aren't doing it, we try to mention it to him."

On Deni Avdija's ability to draw fouls and influence officials:

"For a young guy, he's one of the best in the NBA when it comes to gamesmanship with the officials. He knows how to drive, and as soon as he feels the slightest hit, he can react good enough, like, I'd call a foul for him. He can react good enough to get the officials' attention and get him to the free-throw line."

On Avdija's development as a shooter and offensive threat:

"He's figured it out at a really young age. He's also worked extremely hard on his 3-point game, so you can't give him space. You gotta play up on him, and now he's strong enough and quick enough to go through you and get by you and finish and/or get fouled. He's a handful."

On intentionally pairing Jose Alvarado with Jalen Brunson:

"A lot of times we'll pair Jose and Jalen together and we went into that consciously thinking about doing that against Minnesota, and tonight we'll go into the game consciously thinking about it so all three of those guys will play."

On discovering the Alvarado-Brunson combination against Toronto:

"Against Toronto in the second game, we threw Jose out there with Jalen and we liked it but that was not on my minutes sheet. Going into our last game … he was actually on the minutes sheet to play with Jalen some, and I kinda liked that combination."

On Tyler Kolek remaining part of the guard rotation:

"Tyler's been playing well so Tyler will play some without Jose and/or Jalen on the floor."

On rarely following any predetermined rotation plan:

"I go in with a minutes sheet. I don't know if we've followed the minutes sheet once so far this year or let alone even last year."

On allowing players' families to travel with the team to build trust:

"In order to get players' trust and get true connectivity, it's anybody associated with them. That's why it's important to have families around."

On Mikal Bridges overcoming his shooting struggles through consistent work:

"Yeah, that's just who he is. I don't know how many times I've said this before, but when you continue to put work in your game every single day, and things may not be falling in your favor, but you keep doing the right thing every single day, you keep chipping away, chipping away. Eventually, things are gonna turn for you. And over the past week or two, things have been turning for him, and now he's on a roll."

On repeatedly hitting game-winning shots against Portland:

"I like the city of Portland. [Damian Lillard] is one of my best friends. I like the city of Portland."

On maintaining his effort and making plays in crunch time:

"I try to play hard throughout the whole game, and obviously when the time gets short and it's time to win a game, I think everybody's senses are heightened. Just try to win a game and make a play."

"Yeah, just trying to get Jalen open and create space. How Josh threw it kind of indicated I was going to be open, so just go make a play. It's great. First, you win the game, but second, seeing the joy in those guys. Obviously, tonight with the alumni stuff, it's a pretty cool moment to be in that situation, especially after a tough fourth quarter. So it felt good."

On sharing Bridges' struggles while adjusting to the season:

"Me and him are in the same boat… we're trying to figure it out."

On Bridges remaining prepared despite his early-season struggles:

"Knowing that any good and bad stretch in the NBA can change very quickly, he's always in a position to put himself in a good situation."

On Bridges' game-winner rescuing the Knicks after their fourth-quarter collapse and his dumb late foul:

"Shoutout to Mikal for making the big-time 3, to kind of mask the nonsense that happened."

On the Karl-Anthony Towns unfair criticism during his years in Minnesota:

"When we first got him in Minnesota, the playoffs, they were saying how he's a very good player that couldn't win, couldn't get the team to the playoffs. Then, when we got to the playoffs, any time we struggled, it was him that took the fall, unfairly. And I think it was great to see not only the impact that he had on this championship team, but to see the way that he's playing now. … I love KAT; I was happy for him to get to the mountaintop; I was happy for him to play a major role.

"Then, when we made a deep run, they said you can't win a title with him, and he got the blame."
