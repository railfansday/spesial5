Broadcom vs. Qualcomm: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Broadcom's AI and software push and Qualcomm's mobile dominance pull in very different directions, and their valuations tell a surprisingly close story.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 25, 2026 at 11:32AM EST

As the race for dominance in infrastructure and wireless connectivity heats up, two semiconductor titans are vying for capital. Investors must decide between Broadcom (AVGO -6.25%) and Qualcomm (QCOM -4.04%) for the long term.

Broadcom has pivoted heavily toward artificial intelligence and enterprise software, while Qualcomm remains the heavyweight champion of mobile handset technology. They represent two different paths through the technology landscape, one focused on the data center and the other on the palm of your hand.

Broadcom operates as a leader in semiconductor stocks, providing essential components for data centers and networking. It maintains long-standing relationships with hyperscalers and companies with AI frontier models. Notable recent deals include a $200 billion memorandum of understanding with Samsung and a $30 billion chip commitment with Apple Inc. (AAPL -1.74%). Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as aggregate sales to the top five end customers account for approximately 40% of its total net revenue.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 2, 2026, revenue reached about $89.1 billion, representing an increase of approximately 48.7% compared to the previous year. This substantial growth follows the company's strategic move to integrate high-value software assets alongside its hardware offerings. Net income for the same period was approximately $38.3 billion, reflecting a strong bottom line as the company capitalizes on the rising demand for AI-optimized infrastructure.

As of its November 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.8x. This metric, which measures total debt against shareholder equity, suggests a moderate level of leverage. The current ratio, which compares current assets to current liabilities, is roughly 1.7x. In the fiscal year ended Nov. 2, 2025, free cash flow reached about $26.9 billion. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 27.5% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Qualcomm focuses on foundational wireless and computing technologies, primarily for handsets, automotive, and Internet of Things (IoT) markets. It derives a significant portion of its revenues from major mobile customers, including Apple and Xiaomi (XIACF -0.48%). While it is a leader in mobile modems, the company is actively expanding into automotive and data center solutions to diversify its reach. It also maintains a robust licensing business, working with hundreds of companies globally through its cellular patent portfolio.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue was roughly $43.1 billion, which reflects a decrease of nearly 2.6% over the prior twelve-month period. This slight decline occurred as the company navigated a shifting mobile market and transitioned its focus toward new growth areas. Despite the revenue dip, the company reported net income of approximately $13.6 billion for the same period, showing its ability to maintain significant profitability in its core licensing and chip segments.

As of its September 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.5x. This figure indicates the relationship between total debt and shareholder equity. The current ratio is approximately 1.9x, indicating the company's ability to cover short-term obligations with current assets. In the fiscal year ended Sept. 27, 2026, free cash flow was close to $8.7 billion. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 31% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Broadcom faces risks from high customer concentration, as a small number of end customers and distributors account for a significant portion of its revenue. The company is also navigating intense competition and rapid technological change in the AI sector, which creates pricing pressure as standards evolve. Operations depend heavily on a limited number of contract manufacturers, specifically Taiwan Semiconductor, for wafer production. Furthermore, Broadcom manages legal and operational risks tied to its acquisition of VMware, including complex integration and tax liabilities.

Qualcomm deals with significant revenue concentration from a few mobile handset customers, making the loss of a single partner like Apple particularly impactful. Geopolitical tensions between the U.S. and China pose a threat to sales and supply chain stability in that region. Strategic expansion into automotive and data center markets brings Qualcomm into competition with established players like Marvell Technology (MRVL -9.32%). Additionally, the company faces ongoing legal and regulatory scrutiny regarding its patent licensing practices and antitrust investigations.

Qualcomm appears cheaper based on its significantly lower P/S ratio, although both companies trade at very similar Forward P/E levels based on future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Broadcom is seeing tremendous top-line momentum, with management projecting that fiscal 2026 will bring in about $115 billion in sales, almost double 2025's, and that the following year, fiscal 2027, will see revenue double 2026's to $230 billion. Net income in 2026 will be just about double 2025, at $45 billion, with profit of about $79 billion in 2027.

The strength is coming from AI, with its six main hyperscale customers all increasing their long-term orders for chips and other equipment from Broadcom. These six hyperscalers include Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -4.69%), Meta Platforms (META -1.86%), Anthropic (ANT -1.74%), OpenAI and two firms whose names are not disclosed.

There is some risk to all this growth: one is that the AI boom could go bust, but probably more significant is that Broadcom and private equity giant Blackstone (BX -3.31%) have a joint financing venture that finances frontier AI labs to buy Broadcom equipment. The failure of a client to repay the loan or shifts in interest rates could cause problems with the finance arm and the business at large.

Meanwhile, Qualcomm's mobile phone business has been hammered this year, as weak Chinese demand and AI-driven memory shortages have caused handset-derived sales to fall below prior-year levels. Yet the AI boom has an upside for Qualcomm.

At the company's June 2026 investor day, Qualcomm raised its non-handset revenue target to more than $40 billion by 2029, nearly double the prior target of $22 billion. Qualcomm is reportedly partnering with OpenAI to develop smartphone processors with advanced AI capabilities. Analysts see this partnership as a positive for Qualcomm since it would enable advanced agentic AI on smartphone platforms. It would also diminish the business risk of Apple developing its own chips and dropping Qualcomm as a supplier.

Qualcomm may seem more attractive with its lower P/S ratio compared to Broadcom, but it seems too speculative right now on AI phone prospects, especially as its core mobile phone business is facing strong headwinds. Broadcom's growth and AI exposure are too good to ignore, and its forward P/E ratio of 15.8x is just slightly more than Qualcomm's and below the average forward P/E of the S&P 500. For 2027, Broadcom is the better buy.
