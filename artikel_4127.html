An Elder Scrolls Classic Is Finally Getting the Remaster It Deserves After 22 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

As a nostalgic gamer, I have a soft spot for forgotten games that were left behind by time, especially when they're part of a franchise as beloved as The Elder Scrolls.

While most players have spent the past two decades exploring Morrowind, Oblivion, and Skyrim, one rather unusual entry in the series has been sitting on a much less familiar platform: the Nokia N-Gage. Thankfully, that's about to change!

The Elder Scrolls Travels: Shadowkey, a first-person RPG originally released in 2004, is getting a second chance thanks to an independent project called Shadowkey Reforged.

Developed by Dan Whelan, the project brings the forgotten spin-off to Windows, Mac, and Android with a new engine, widescreen support, controller compatibility, and plenty of visual and gameplay improvements. Even better, it's launching today, November 11.

Morrowind is rightfully regarded as one of the greatest open-world RPGs of all time, but it's aged badly and would benefit from a full remake.

For anyone unfamiliar with it, Shadowkey was the third entry in The Elder Scrolls Travels, a series of portable spin-offs released between Morrowind and Oblivion. Unlike the mainline games, however, it was exclusive to Nokia's N-Gage, a rather ambitious handheld-phone hybrid that didn't exactly have the longest life on the market.

That exclusivity made Shadowkey one of the more inaccessible chapters in the franchise's history. And while you could technically track down the original hardware and a copy of the game, that's hardly the most convenient way to revisit a 22-year-old RPG.

That's where Shadowkey Reforged comes in! Rather than simply emulating the N-Gage, Whelan built a new engine capable of running the original game's data and scripts on modern devices. Players can choose between a Classic mode that preserves the original presentation and a Modern mode featuring upgraded artwork, improved lighting, and several gameplay additions. So yes, it is literally the best of both worlds.

The latter isn't just a fresh coat of paint, by the way. Modern mode introduces features inspired by newer Elder Scrolls games, including stamina-based sprinting, stealth, and ragdoll physics. You can also toggle individual features to customize the experience instead of committing to one preset. And if you happen to have The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind installed, there's another neat little bonus: Shadowkey Reforged can use its soundtrack and sound effects, preserving a connection between two games released during the same era, in case you want an extra touch!

Interestingly, Whelan has also been transparent about using AI during development. According to the developer, AI tools helped with coding, building the engine, and debugging, while the updated artwork combines AI-generated images with Photoshop and manual sprite editing.

Shadowkey Reforged is available as a free, unofficial project, with versions for Windows, Mac, and Android. However, there's one important detail players should know before getting too excited: the download doesn't include the original game's files. This means you'll need to provide your own copy of Shadowkey to play it – you can check more info about that on the port’s website.

Still, it's great to see such an obscure piece of The Elder Scrolls history getting another shot at life. Bethesda hasn't announced an official remaster, but Whelan's project might be the closest many players will get to experiencing this forgotten adventure without digging up a Nokia N-Gage.
