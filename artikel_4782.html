Fed officials saw October rate hold as a ‘close call,’ minutes show

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The latest FOMC minutes suggest greater appetite for higher rates than the Fed’s 10–2 vote indicated, with markets now pricing in a December hike.

While the Federal Open Market Committee decided not to raise interest rates during its October meeting, some participants who supported holding rates steady considered the decision a “close call,” according to newly released minutes Wednesday.

The Federal Open Market Committee voted 10–2 in October to keep its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4%. Fed Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack favored a quarter-point increase.

“The minutes suggest broader support for an October hike than the 10–2 vote alone implied — and that was before payrolls and CPI both came in hot,” Lewis Huang, an analyst at crypto exchange Bitget, told Cointelegraph.

Markets are now overwhelmingly expecting a December rate hike. CME FedWatch has put the odds of a December quarter-point hike at about 90% and a half-point increase at about 10% at the time of writing.

“With a December hike already fully priced in, the focus shifts to the size of the move and the policy path beyond December,” said Huang.

“Governor Waller has said a half-point increase should be considered; evidence that other officials were already open to that scale of tightening in October could push the odds of a 50-basis-point move higher,” he added.

Fed officials also raised concerns about ongoing inflationary pressures due to the AI build-out, with participants noting the demand for AI technology was pushing up the price of some consumer goods.

Officials separately discussed the financial risks if the returns on AI investments failed to live up to investor expectations.

“Most participants observed that AI-related debt had grown rapidly and that much of this growth had taken place through complex and opaque financing structures,” the minutes show. “A few participants remarked that AI-related exposures were becoming increasingly concentrated among a small number of firms.”

Fabian Dori, chief investment officer at Sygnum Bank, said markets are already pricing in a series of hikes into next year.

“That likely keeps long-term yields and the dollar firm, and the question for Bitcoin is whether debasement concerns and advancing institutional adoption can outweigh it,” Dori said.
