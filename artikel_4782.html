Tom Lee Says Ethereum Is as Scarce as Bitcoin—And Bitmine Just Bought More

Bitmine Immersion Technologies kept its weekly Ethereum buying streak alive, adding 7,819 ETH worth roughly $13.8 million, as chairman Tom Lee leaned on a new argument for the battered token: its supply grows no faster than Bitcoin’s, making it just as much “hard money.”

The NYSE-listed company said Monday its holdings reached 6,085,564 ETH, worth roughly $10.4 billion at a reference price of $1,713 per coin, as of Nov. 22.

That’s 4.98% of Ethereum’s 122.2 million token supply, leaving Bitmine 99% of the way to its goal of owning 5% of the network, the “Alchemy of 5%” target it has chased for 17 months. The firm has bought Ethereum every week since launching its treasury strategy on June 30, 2025.

Lee’s pitch this week centered on scarcity. Since Bitmine pivoted to Ethereum in mid-2025, he said, ETH’s supply has grown just 0.90% on an annualized basis, roughly in line with Bitcoin’s 0.82%, meaning “both are ‘hard money’ measured in supply growth.” (Disclosure: Tom Lee is one of several investors in Dastan, Decrypt’s parent company.)

He contrasted that with more inflationary rivals, citing annualized supply growth of 7.6% for Solana, 5.4% for XRP, and 2.9% for Cardano. The framing builds on Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin’s recent declaration that ETH is a store of value, which Lee has repeatedly invoked.

Counting cash, other tokens and “moonshot” investments, Bitmine’s total holdings stood at $11.1 billion, including 216 Bitcoin, $384 million in cash and marketable securities, a $180 million stake in Beast Industries and a $54 million position in Eightco Holdings.

Bitmine remains the world’s largest Ethereum treasury and the second-largest crypto treasury overall, behind Michael Saylor’s Strategy, which the release said holds 849,327 BTC.

Strategy also kept buying through the downturn, recently adding $30 million in Bitcoin while shifting $1 billion from its dollar reserve to a cash pot.

The purchase lands as crypto claws back some ground after a bruising November. Bitcoin traded around $62,700 on Monday, up about 3.2% on the day, with Ethereum near $1,769, up 3.5%, as risk appetite steadied.

The rebound coincided with a dramatic reversal at Nvidia, which reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO just days after its board had ruled out his return, lifting sentiment across the AI-linked assets that crypto increasingly tracks.

Bitmine has 5,227,037 ETH, about 86% of its holdings, staked through its MAVAN platform, with projected annualized staking revenue of roughly $245 million.
