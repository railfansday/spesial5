LIVE – Why Is Crypto Down? Jensen Huang Chinese Spy Scandal Sends AI Stocks Into Chaos

We’re back to the same question again: Why is crypto down? Bitcoin and altcoins are taking another beating as Wall Street’s AI trade starts unraveling. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is now at the center of a growing Chinese espionage scandal. His alleged relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset has put the world’s most valuable chipmaker under fresh scrutiny.

The timing could hardly be worse for investors. Technology stocks have already suffered a brutal selloff, with the Nasdaq entering correction territory. Nvidia shares plunged 9.3% during Thursday’s rout, while semiconductor stocks suffered their worst session in years.

Now, an investigation into Huang’s personal and professional connections could introduce another shock. If confidence in Nvidia starts cracking, the consequences could extend well beyond the AI industry. Bitcoin and the wider crypto market are already feeling the pressure.

The latest crypto selloff follows a dramatic reversal across US technology markets. Bitcoin erased its post-election rally, falling below $75,000. Ethereum dropped more than 7%, while XRP and Solana also suffered heavy losses.

The Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.5% during the same session. The Nasdaq 100 lost 6.4%, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index crashed 12%. Nvidia and AMD were among the biggest casualties.

More than $2.5 trillion in Nasdaq market cap disappeared during October. Investors are increasingly questioning if massive AI infrastructure investments will deliver sufficient returns. Major technology companies continue pouring billions into chips, data centers, and computing infrastructure.

Then add political uncertainty, which is adding another headache. Democrats secured control of both congressional chambers during the US midterm elections. Investors now fear greater opposition to data center expansion and potentially tougher regulatory scrutiny.

The resulting selloff has exposed how closely crypto trades with speculative technology investments. Bitcoin increasingly moves alongside high-growth equities when institutional investors adjust their risk exposure.

The damage has also spread into derivatives markets. More than $1 billion in leveraged crypto positions disappeared during Thursday’s selloff. Most liquidations involved traders betting on higher prices.

That forced selling can quickly turn an ordinary correction into something considerably uglier. As Bitcoin falls, liquidations push prices lower and expose leveraged altcoin positions to further losses.

Nvidia’s problems have suddenly become considerably more personal. The company’s board announced an independent investigation yesterday. It wants to examine reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation.

The controversy involves Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed. US authorities reportedly suspected her of being a Chinese intelligence asset.

Megaspeed has also faced scrutiny over allegations involving access to advanced Nvidia chips restricted from export to China. Republican lawmakers have requested a briefing from Huang regarding the allegations.

Separately, Huang admitted to being unfaithful to his wife. He apologized to his family and colleagues, accepting responsibility for his actions.

The combination creates an uncomfortable situation for Nvidia shareholders. Huang is not simply another corporate executive. He remains the public face of the AI revolution and one of its most influential decision-makers.

A prolonged investigation could introduce uncertainty around Nvidia’s leadership, relationships with international customers, and exposure to geopolitical tensions.

Nvidia’s board says it has no reason to believe company secrets or national security were compromised. However, the investigation could keep the controversy alive while investors are already questioning AI valuations.

The biggest concern for crypto is what happens if the AI selloff spreads. Nvidia has become a central pillar of the global technology investment cycle. Its chips power the infrastructure behind many of the world’s largest AI projects.

If investors begin questioning the sustainability of that investment cycle, semiconductor stocks, data center operators, and technology companies could face another round of selling.

Bitcoin is particularly exposed because institutional investors increasingly treat it as part of their risk portfolios. When confidence disappears from technology markets, crypto often loses buyers alongside speculative equities.

The Binance security incident has added another source of pressure. The exchange’s clients withdrew approximately $6.1 billion following the exchange’s recent security breach.

That creates an uncomfortable combination of falling institutional confidence, weaker market liquidity, and leveraged positions being forced out. Bitcoin now needs a recovery in risk appetite to regain momentum. Otherwise, another wave of technology stock selling could drag crypto prices lower.

So why is crypto down? The AI market has a new problem to deal with, and Bitcoin is discovering that Nvidia’s troubles can become its troubles too.
