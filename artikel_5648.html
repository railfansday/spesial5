NRMLA asks Congress to restore HUD housing counseling funds

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

NRMLA asked congressional appropriators to restore rescinded HUD housing counseling funds tied to the HECM process. The group said mandatory third-party counseling is required to legally close federally insured reverse mortgages.  AI Summary

The National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association (NRMLA) is urging Congress to restore funding for the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)’s housing counseling program, warning that cuts could disrupt the origination of federally insured reverse mortgages.

In a Nov. 2 letter to congressional appropriations leaders, NRMLA President Steve Irwin raised concerns about the presidential pocket rescission of appropriated funds for HUD’s housing counseling program, specifically the funding that supports the Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) program.

NRMLA said independent, third-party counseling is a statutory requirement for every prospective HECM borrower. Under Section 255 of the National Housing Act, borrowers must receive counseling from a HUD-approved agency before a loan can close.

A counseling certificate issued by a certified counselor is required for a HECM loan to be legally closed, the trade group said.

The association also emphasized that lenders cannot pay for a borrower’s counseling session, direct borrowers to a particular counseling agency or use an agency affiliated with the lender. These restrictions are intended to ensure seniors receive objective information about reverse mortgage costs, obligations and alternatives.

Because lenders are prohibited from funding or steering the counseling process, NRMLA said federal appropriations are essential to maintaining the independent counseling infrastructure required for HECM originations.

Without the funding, the association warned, mandatory counseling could become less accessible and affordable for seniors, potentially creating obstacles to loan closings and limiting access to the federally insured reverse mortgage program.

The HECM program allows eligible older homeowners to access their home equity to support aging in place and improve their financial security. Irwin asked congressional leaders to take action to restore the funds, arguing that the funding is necessary to preserve the program as a housing finance option for seniors.

“We respectfully urge you to take the necessary action to restore the appropriated HUD housing counseling funds so that eligible seniors may continue to benefit from the HECM program,” he wrote.

The association represents more than 300 member companies and more than 2,000 member delegates. Its members originate more than 90% of reverse mortgages, according to the letter.
