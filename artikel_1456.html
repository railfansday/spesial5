Raul Jimenez’s late penalty seals share of spoils for Wolves against Blackburn

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

An injury-time penalty from returning hero Raul Jimenez ensured Wolves came away with a point in their Championship opener after Blackburn had produced a comeback.

Wolves led through Marshall Munetsi’s cool finish on 18 minutes only for Sean McLoughlin’s first Blackburn goal to draw the visitors level on 27 minutes with a well-timed run and header.

The visitors turned the game around on 48 minutes through Andri Gudjohnsen’s close-range strike only for substitute Jimenez to level the game three minutes into injury time from the spot after Ryan Alebiosu handled the ball in the box. Jimenez and his wife marked his return to Molineux by giving 5,000 fans black scarves emblazoned with the words ‘Si Senor’ - a nod to his song - and Jimenez did not disappoint as he rifled in his penalty.

Cesar Peixoto wants Wolves to play with their heads rather than their hearts after the home side rescued a late point in the first game of the Championship season following relegation. Wolves replaced Rob Edwards with Portuguese coach Peixoto during the summer and he felt his side’s loss of organisation allowed Blackburn to take control of the game.

Peixoto said: “One point is not what we want, we want three points. We lost some organisation in the second half and played more with our hearts and not our heads and brains.

“We have to keep our mental stability during the 90 minutes because we’re better when we’re organised. We need to play our way with our identity. It’s a lesson for us to understand when we lose organisation it’s more difficult for us and more easy for the opposition.

“We’re working hard to bring more solutions [into the club] but we need to work hard with the players we have. It’s the first game, there’s a lot to work on and a lot to improve.”

Tony Mowbray took charge of Blackburn in the league for the first time since returning to the club for a second stint in the dugout. Rovers recorded their lowest league finish since Mowbray’s first stint in charge of the club last term but the Blackburn boss is confident his side have enough to beat the drop and climb up the table this campaign.

Mowbray said: “Ultimately, it feels like a defeat, it’s a bit disappointing and a bit frustrating. I just felt a lot of decisions were starting to swing their way. It’s harsh to take, I don’t think they deserved a second goal. We asked the players to be brave and we were collectively very strong.

“Everybody has us relegated, probably on the back of finishing 20th last season and losing a couple of our better players and not spending much money, which is a good motivation for the team. We deserved to win the game, we showed there’s enough quality to give teams problems and I hope there’s help on the way [in the transfer window].”

The first chance of the season came the way of Blackburn as a cross-field pass found Alebiosu down the right, who floated a ball into the box only for an unmarked Oladipo Afolayan to head wide from eight-yards out.

However, Wolves scored the first goal of the new campaign when Fer Lopez played a defence-splitting through ball into the path of Munetsi, who coolly found the net with a first time effort from the edge of the penalty area.

The 2026/27 Championship season kicks off at Molineux as Wolves host Blackburn, with the home side hoping to put the bad memories of last season firmly behind them. There are new playoff rules in place for this season, with six places now up for grabs in the play-offs.

Wolves are aiming to return to the Premier League at the first attempt, having endured a miserable season in the top flight last year as they finished bottom with just three wins to their name. It has been a summer of change at the club as they chose to change managers. Rob Edwards lost his job in what was a shock move and was replaced by 46-year-old Cesar Peixoto, with this being his first head coaching position outside of his home country Portugal. Back in 2017/18, Wolves won the Championship title led by Peixoto’s compatriot Nuno Espirito Santo.

“I am ready,” Peixoto told Wolves’ club website. “We have worked very hard during pre-season, and I think we have had a great pre-season. All the players have worked very, very hard and they understand what I want from them. I am ready and confident going into the first game. Football is football, whether it is in Portugal or England, but there are different characteristics to the game here. We have watched, observed and analysed the Championship. We did that work before I came to the club, so we know what to expect.

“It is going to be a tough season with good teams, but we are prepared to fight for the three points game after game and week after week. We know what we want from the team and we know what our big goal is. We’re going to work very hard and fight to achieve that goal.”

They have brought in vast experience on the player side in the shape of Kieran Trippier and Raul Jimenez, who is back at Molineux three years after leaving for Fulham. Although they have lost Brazilian midfielder Joao Gomes to Aston Villa, most of their squad has stayed intact as they pursue promotion back to the top flight. It is a special year for the club as they celebrate their 150th anniversary.

Blackburn, led by experienced manager Tony Mowbray, did not enjoy a great season in the Championship last year, finishing down in 20th place and just five points above the relegation zone. 14 years have now passed since Blackburn last played in the top flight.

“I think it is a tough start,” Mowbray said. “On paper, I would suggest the first couple of games, particularly, jumped out as a tough start. One of the Premier League sides from last season, away from home. They have got a very talented squad, a pretty expensive squad. They have got some very good individual talent. Yet, there is an argument to say they have not won many football matches in the last calendar year. So, what we get, we will have to wait and see.”

These sides last met in the FA Cup during the 2024/25 season, with Wolves winning that fourth-round tie 2-0 thanks to goals from Matheus Cunha and Gomes, both of whom are now plying their trade elsewhere. The omens would appear to be on Wolves’ side as they are unbeaten in their last eight league meetings with Blackburn. Both sides progressed to the second round of the League Cup last weekend, with Wolves beating Port Vale 3-0 and Blackburn overcoming Burton Albion 2-1.
