‘Doctor Who’ Is Officially Returning, But There’s a Catch

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

What does the future of Doctor Who look like? It wasn't long ago that a partnership with Disney and the return of showrunner Russell T. Davies left fans feeling as if the show's heyday was on its way back. However, this couldn't be further from the truth, as Davies' return faced poor ratings, mixed fan reception, and the controversial exit of the new titular Time Lord, Ncuti Gatwa. This all culminated in the inevitable shelving of the series, as the BBC put it out to competitive tender earlier this year.

Line of Duty creator Jed Mercurio, through his production company, Hat Trick Mercurio, looks like the favorite to take over the show, with a 2028 or 2029 return rumored. The aforementioned former showrunner, Davies, claimed that a decision on the show would arrive sooner rather than later, saying, "You’ll start to see production with a brand-new Doctor, a brand-new companion, summer next year," in a previous interview. Ahead of that confirmation, Whovians are to be treated to the big-screen return of a fan-favorite episode.

On November 23 and November 28, as part of Doctor Who Day celebrations, The Satan Pit Extended Cut hits theaters in the UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and the U.S. It was already confirmed that the new feature-length edit of the 2006 episodes "The Impossible Planet" and "The Satan Pit" will feature deleted scenes and alternate shots, but now we know that a "specially shot" sequence awaits fans.

"Before the adventure gets underway, audiences will be treated to something specially shot; a lighter, more playful moment featuring a familiar face," reads the latest publication of Doctor Who Magazine, although it is unclear who this "familiar face" is. The screening will also feature an exclusive interview with director James Strong.

The future of Doctor Who might finally be looking up, but former Time Lord Peter Capaldi isn't so positive. The Thick of It star warned in a previous interview that there is only "a small group of professionals" who could take charge of Doctor Who, and that BBC executives who "don't really understand it" threaten to lose what was once so beloved about this sixty-year-old institution. Whatever happens, Whovians will just be glad that their favorite timey-wimey series looks likely to return.
