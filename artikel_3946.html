Jennifer Svedberg-Yen loves Mass Effect's "attention to detail in the narrative and the storylines"

The writer behind Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 says the recent closure of Mass Effect and Dragon Age studio BioWare "broke my heart," and she's currently playing the first two sci-fi games in the series "in honor."

Speaking in a recent interview with GamesBeat, Jennifer Svedberg-Yen talks about the importance of narrative, noting that "anybody who's interested in franchises or IP as a broader potential can’t discount the power of story." She adds that "I love a lot of games that have no narrative at all," so it's not to say that one is better than the other, but she points to the Mass Effect games as excellent examples of games that nail their storylines. Needless to say, last month's studio closure was a huge blow for her.

"I'm playing Mass Effect and Mass Effect 2 right now, in honor of BioWare. That broke my heart," she says.

Svedberg-Yen points to "the attention to detail in the narrative and the storylines" as something she particularly enjoys. "The way that they play out through the three games when you import your storylines over is incredible," she adds. "I love that. I feel like that's a remarkable achievement. I'd love to see more games like that."

Good thing EA didn't just cancel a new Mass Effect game then, huh? Oh, wait.

Mass Effect 5's cancellation was, sadly, announced last month along with BioWare's closure. It was apparently set to be called Mass Effect: Universe – a name one former dev says was partially inspired by the fact that "you had so many choices that you could plug your saves together to create parallel universes."

Whether Mass Effect could be saved by being sold to another studio is another matter that former BioWare devs can't seem to agree on. One previously shared his belief that "EA never actually sells anything," while one of the studio's former executive producers, Mark Darrah, said that while no one working on the series at EA "might make them more likely to sell off those IPs," it equally "might also make them more likely to try to forget that they ever existed and bury them in the vault."
