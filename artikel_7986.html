Eight Sleep’s Alexandra Zatarain on women-centered tech, the science of better sleep, and planning a career around motherhood

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

By Sara Goldstein Updated Nov. 12, 2026

This article is sponsored by Eight Sleep. Thank you for supporting the brands that support Motherly and mamas.

Alexandra Zatarain helped build Eight Sleep into a tech unicorn, and she’s expecting her first child in January. Going through pregnancy while running a global company has reshaped how she sees her own body, her industry and the product she’s building. On this episode of The Motherly Podcast, the co-founder of the sleep-technology company talks about the research that showed Eight Sleep’s technology was improving men’s sleep more than women’s and what the company did about it, why sleep is the most important thing women can protect, and the career advice she wishes more women heard before becoming mothers.

Meet the expert: Alexandra Zatarain is the co-founder and VP of brand and marketing at Eight Sleep, the sleep-technology company behind the Pod. The Pod is an intelligent mattress cover that cools or heats each side to your ideal temperature automatically, maximizing deep sleep and minimizing wake-ups. A marketer and brand builder, she grew Eight Sleep from launch into a tech unicorn and helped create what the company calls the sleep fitness movement. Eight Sleep now sells in 37 countries. She’s expecting her first child in January with her husband, Matteo, the company’s co-founder and CEO.

Alexandra Zatarain: So many things. I just turned 37, and I’ve seen so many friends go through pregnancy before me. After my first trimester, I felt so bad, thinking, what a terrible friend I was. When you haven’t experienced it, you have such little empathy for what a woman’s body is going through, especially when life keeps demanding so much of you and it doesn’t stop. I really didn’t understand that you could feel so different in your body for so long. It’s built so much more admiration for women and what we go through, and it’s let me embrace a feminine side of myself that in tech you usually don’t get to bring out.

Alexandra Zatarain: It’s made me realize that the only way the world out there is going to change for our benefit as women and mothers is if more of us are the ones making the decisions. I just don’t see any way around it. We have a lot of parents at Eight Sleep, every executive on our team is a parent, but as a woman the experience is completely different. Unless we can share that first-hand knowledge, it’s going to be hard to make progress as fast as we need to. It’s unfortunate that it takes the first-person experience to become that advocate, because everyone should appreciate what mothers go through, given that we all come from a mother.

Alexandra Zatarain: A couple of years ago, our clinical team, which is more than 90% women, was analyzing data from our user base and noticed our technology had been improving men’s sleep more than women’s. They asked, what are we missing in how we design this? It turned out the algorithms that manage temperature while you sleep had been fed more data representing men than women. That’s a classic problem in science, where women are underrepresented because our bodies are more complex. You have to account for where someone is in their hormonal journey, pregnancy, postpartum, perimenopause. So we invested a couple million dollars into research and developed features from it. That changed how we think about ourselves, less about helping everyone in a broad way and more about solving specific problems women have had for ages.

Alexandra Zatarain: Sleep is the most important pillar of health. You would die sooner from sleep deprivation than from food deprivation, which tells you how fundamental it is. For women it matters even more because our hormones rule our lives, and sleep is the one tool we have to regulate them. If you sleep well, your chances of better hormonal balance go up significantly. It’s the best medicine we have, more than working out, more than any meal. The challenge is that those same hormones are often playing against us, making it harder to sleep well in different seasons of life, so keeping our routines as stable as possible helps counter what our bodies are going through.

Alexandra Zatarain: The mindset piece is really important. There’s so much going on, the anxiety, the intrusive thoughts, so not being too hard on yourself is the first step. Understanding that the season will pass should put you a little more at ease, because it’s not going to be your life forever. The other piece is setting yourself up with the right systems, and the most important one is available to everyone for free, which is routine. It helps your body a lot. When you do need to sleep, it’ll be easier for your body to fall asleep because it’s used to the schedule.

Alexandra Zatarain: I’m very rigid about my sleep schedule. I travel a lot, so sleep is number one for me. I make space for eight to nine hours every night, and I’m pretty good at hitting it. I go to bed by 9:30 or 10 every single night no matter where I am in the world, and I don’t really use an alarm unless I have to wake up before 6, because my body just wakes up on its own. That schedule is the thing that’s changed my life the most. Second to that is the environment, the temperature in my room and my bed, the lights going dark, no noise.

Alexandra Zatarain: I think of myself as an athlete, and we’re all sort of athletes in a way. My sport is making decisions about how to build this business, and that means I have to train, stay healthy and think long term. It’s not a sprint. We’ve been doing this for 12 years, so the question is how do I not burn out and stay here 20 or 30 years more. I’m also lucky I built this with my husband, so there’s no real line between business and personal life for us. It’s all mixed in, but it’s so purpose-driven that achieving that purpose every day is what feeds the energy.

Alexandra Zatarain: I’m very open with them about how intentional I was. I did my embryo freezing in my early 30s because of where I was in life. And I tell them that if they want a career after they have a family, the best time to build equity in that career is to push as hard as you can before. Know what you want, because time isn’t always on our side as women. Move up the ranks, so that by the time you have kids you have more flexibility and maybe a higher income. It is possible, but it takes planning, it will require compromises, and very importantly it will require the right partner.

Alexandra Zatarain: It starts with the people around you understanding what you truly need. Sometimes it’s as simple as the encouragement of, yes, go take a nap, yes, your body’s going through a lot. It gets harder once the baby’s home and the mom is maybe exclusively breastfeeding, because it really all falls on you. So have a plan in place ahead of time for how the load is going to be shared. Our partners are much more involved than previous generations, so have those conversations early. It doesn’t end with the birth plan. That’s where everything starts.

Alexandra Zatarain: The first trimester was difficult, a lot of nausea, and it was very hard to function at the level I’m used to. I almost never get sick, so feeling sick every day was a huge shift. It put me in the trajectory to build the team that would make it possible for me to become a mother, to figure out what to delegate and who to elevate, and I’ve built a stronger team as a result. But the biggest thing is that it’s taught me to let go. I can’t control how I feel every day. As a Type A person I always want to be in control, and this has been like therapy, learning that I’m not in control of my body. I’m building a human, and it’s fine if for this season things just work differently.
