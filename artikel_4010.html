Nauctis’ First Commander Reveal Has MTG Fans Divided On Its Playability

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

A high mana value and pseudo-Eminence have left players unsure if a spoiled Nauctis: The Sunken Realm Commander is worth playing around.

It's clear that the Magic: The Gathering fan base has already turned its attention firmly to the upcoming launch of Nauctis: The Sunken Realm, following the disappointing reaction to Star Trek.

With the confirmation that four Commander precon decks are launching with the set, alongside some very unpopular changes to the Commander precon products, we also had one of the lead Commanders spoiled in the form of a piece of Japanese promo material, and it has led to a rather lengthy debate about whether the assumed lead Commander of the Terrors of the Abyss deck is actually any good.

Using machine translation, players were able to get a better look at the Japanese cards leaked via a piece of marketing material that, in all likelihood, wasn't supposed to go live yet. In it, we get a look at both Commander options for the Terrors of the Abyss precon, and the presumed lead Commander, The Noble Serpent, is the card at the center of the ongoing debate.

It is an eight-mana (5C, 1B, 1G, 1U) 7/9 and comes with the following rules text: At the beginning of your first upkeep of the game, if The Noble Serpent is in your Command Zone, you mill four cards. At the beginning of your end step, return target creature card that is a Sea Monster from your graveyard to the battlefield.

Sea Monsters are defined as Krakens, Leviathans, Octopuses, Serpents, and Whales, which suggests we could see a lot more of these creature types in the main set for Nauctis, too.

However, players have a problem with the balance of The Noble Serpent and how the card is structured in general. The one-time Eminence ability that triggers at the beginning of your first upkeep is very strange, and it might make more sense to trigger every turn, but for fewer cards. That would then incentivize getting The Noble Serpent onto the battlefield as often as possible, so you can keep digging in your graveyard for creatures to put on the battlefield for free, but there's the risk/reward aspect of not wanting to let them sit in your Command Zone for too long.

The other issue is the creature's cost. At eight mana, it's an expensive Commander, and it feels like its power and toughness could have been lowered to justify knocking a few points off the cost, because the ability to dig through your graveyard once per turn for a free creature is very exciting. While there are easy ways to generate more mana sources early in a game, be it through artifacts or cards in the blue/green card pool, you don't want to compromise the integrity of this deck and restructure its ramp capability just to pay the Commander's cost.

We can see why some players are sold on this card, because it has a really exciting reanimation interaction with your own graveyard and what is sure to be some huge, game-changing creatures, but the cost is a concern, as is the fact that it's the kind of card that just sees you instantly targeted the second it lands on the battlefield because no one wants to deal with what it's capable of.

A quick note on the backup Commander for this deck: it's a four-mana Squid Warlock that gives all creatures Emerge (a mechanic that lets you sacrifice a permanent and use its mana cost towards the casting cost of the spell with Emerge).
