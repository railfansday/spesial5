Video Assistant Referee (VAR) has its fair share of critics. Introduced to the Premier League at the start of the 2019/20 season, the technology was designed to help the on-pitch officials and ensure matches were officiated seamlessly and without controversy.

However, it has not had the impact that it was intended to have. If anything, the number of refereeing controversies has gone up, and many high-profile names, including Roy Keane and Wayne Rooney, have lost patience and want to see VAR scrapped.

But what would the 2026/27 Premier League table look like if every refereeing decision was correct? After every matchday in England's top tier, Squawka asks their followers to vote on controversial VAR decisions and whether the right decision was made or not.

Using that data, they have created a Premier League table without VAR errors following matchday 11, and it makes for interesting reading.

The table has been compiled on the basis that penalties, should they have been awarded, would have been converted.

Sunderland have had a dismal start to the campaign. The Black Cats, who are competing in the Conference League after finishing seventh in 2025/26, are currently 18th in the Premier League, having picked up nine points from their opening 11 games. They can be grateful for VAR that their troubles aren't any worse, though, as they would be rock bottom without its errors.

Crystal Palace would be second bottom, with Manchester United rounding out the relegation places, even after Leny Yoro had his red card overturned after he was wrongly sent off in a 4-0 defeat at Anfield in Mauricio Pochettino's first game. Nottingham Forest, who are currently 17th in the Premier League, would be two points better off, though that wouldn't change their position.

Back in September, Forest and Tottenham Hotspur drew 0-0 at the City Ground. VAR is adjudged to have wrongly intervened to disallow Neco Williams' goal for the home side. The Premier League released a statement explaining the goal had been ruled out for handball, but many felt the footage was inconclusive and not clear and obvious. Had VAR made the right decision, Forest would have won 1-0.

Coventry City currently sit 14th in the table, which is one place higher than they would be without technology. Fulham also find themselves one place higher than they should be, which should mean newly promoted Ipswich Town should be two places higher and in 14th instead

Hull City have had a tremendous start and currently sit 11th in the table, but they would be a point and one place worse off without VAR errors.

Tottenham Hotspur would find themselves one place worse off down in 11th if VAR decisions were correct, though their recent uptick in form should see them consolidate on their top half place soon enough. Newcastle United currently find themselves in 13th in the table, which is also the case in this table.

Premier League Table Without VAR Errors (20-11)

Everton are currently sitting in ninth and propping up the top half of the table with 16 points and a -2 goal difference from their opening 11 games. But while their points total wouldn't have changed, the VAR decisions being correct would have them a place lower thanks to the fact Aston Villa should move up three places - the most of any side.

Leeds have had a strong start to the season, but they would be one place worse off in eighth in the table without any VAR errors, while Bournemouth would be dropping down to 7th in the table, two places lower than their place in the Europa League qualification spots right now.

Second only to Villa, Chelsea would be another big winner in the top-half without VAR. While they currently sit eighth, with questions being rasied about their defence - as well as Xabi Alonso's management - technology has denied them of three points, meaning they could be up to sixth and level on points with Brentford, who are above them.

Manchester City would be two points worse off in fourth place, on 19 points, if VAR errors were fixed. They won 1-0 at Old Trafford thanks to Erling Haaland's winner. ProRef released a statement revealing that the goal should have been ruled out due to Enzo Fernandez being in an offside position. Without that goal, the match would've ended in a goalless draw.

Despite still being a relatively new feature of the game, VAR has already led to numerous errors in football, but which ones have been the worst?

Brighton would still be second place on 22 points, but they would be sharing that points tally with Liverpool, who wouldn't have lost against Arsenal without VAR errors.

The Reds felt aggrieved that they weren't given a penalty when Alexander Isak's shirt was tugged by Jurrien Timber in the box, preventing him from having a header at goal. If VAR had made the right decision, Liverpool would've been given a spot-kick, and it is assumed they would have scored and that the spoils would have been shared.

Despite that, Mikel Arteta's side would still be well clear at the top of the table without VAR errors.
