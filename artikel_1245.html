Sensational Spurs storm Stamford Bridge as Palmer loses his head

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

So much for Xabi Alonso’s call for cool heads from his Chelsea players. Cole Palmer lost his and was sent off, while goalkeeper Mike Penders punched the ball into his own net to give Tottenham Hotspur a helping hand on their way to a second win at Stamford Bridge in 42 attempts.

This was hardly “the Battle of the Bridge”, but Chelsea blew up during a ridiculous 10-minute spell towards the end of a game in which Tottenham had bossed possession but not looked like scoring.

Palmer was lucky to stay on the pitch in the 70th minute, when he shoved his pelvis into the face of Mateus Fernandes after the Tottenham midfielder had stopped him racing through with a foul and a handball.

Christopher Kavanagh, the referee, let him off with a yellow card, but 10 minutes later Palmer inexplicably gave Kavanagh no choice but to book him again and send him off, following a late challenge on substitute Conor Gallagher.

Palmer was just about the only Chelsea player not to have been sent off last season, but it seems that Alonso has not yet managed to deal with the club’s disciplinary problem.

Jordan Henderson had given Palmer a talking to at the end of the win at Everton in which the forward was booked. But he evidently did not heed the advice or the words of his manager.

Alonso had said he was confident his players could keep calm ahead of the game, but he got that wrong – along with his team selection and substitutions.

In between Palmer’s two bookings, Penders suffered his own moment of madness with a bizarre own goal on his league debut for Chelsea. The Belgian was standing in for Emi Martinez, who watched in disbelief from the home dug-out after undergoing surgery on his jaw.

Fernandes sent in a corner that Jan Paul van Hecke challenged for. Penders looked like the easy winner in that contest, but he got his punch horribly wrong and the ball spun backwards and over the goalline.

A man and a goal down, Chelsea then saw hundreds of their own fans walk out when Mathys Tel scored a brilliant second for Spurs with three minutes remaining. The visiting fans, who had seen one win at the Bridge since 1990 prior to Saturday, could hardly believe what they had seen.

Tottenham head coach Roberto De Zerbi celebrated wildly with his staff. The two benches had clashed and De Zerbi had been booked after Palmer’s first yellow card that the Italian felt should have been a red. But De Zerbi will not care about that now, with Tottenham’s season finally up and running thanks to successive league wins against Coventry City and Chelsea.

Tottenham should really have been ahead in the first half. De Zerbi revealed before the game that Dominic Solanke had dropped down to the substitutes’ bench because the striker “felt fatigue” after the victory over Coventry.

With Solanke resting up on the bench until the 65th minute, De Zerbi moved Omar Marmoush, who had scored his first Spurs goal against Coventry, into the middle and brought in Tel on the left.

Tel was involved in the 29th-minute move that should have resulted in the opening goal for Marmoush. The Frenchman played a one-two with Andy Robertson and the Scotsman picked out Marmoush with a low cross. But, six yards out and with time to pick his spot, the former Manchester City man allowed Penders to make a save.

The missed Marmoush chance was the first time Penders had been called into action. At the other end, Tottenham goalkeeper Antonin Kinsky made unnecessary work for himself.

Kinsky played a bad pass straight to Pedro Neto. The ball was worked to Henderson, but Kinsky recovered to make a save and then stopped the follow-up from Neto. The offside flag then went up, but it was unclear whether it would have spared Kinsky on both occasions had Chelsea scored.

It was not the first time Kinsky had handed possession back to Chelsea in a dangerous area, although the 23-year-old had also made a good save to palm away a curling effort from Palmer. That came after Fernandes had the ball pinched from him far too easily.

Neto was Chelsea’s most dangerous player, but it was the wrong call by Alonso to leave Danny Welbeck on the bench, with João Pedro out injured, and he was too slow to change things. Leaving it until the 88th minute, when the game had already been killed by Tel, to send on the striker was a clear error.

Storm Boelo created wet-and-wild conditions on the Stamford Bridge pitch, while there were still remnants of Storm Austen, or should that be Storm Austin, on the home bench.

Chelsea set-piece coach Austin MacPhee let out an incredibly loud “for f---’s sake” when one corner routine went wrong. Nobody could hear what he was shouting over the Tottenham celebrations when Penders scored his own goal, but it would almost certainly have started with “f” again.

James Maddison had been a doubt before the game after missing training during the week and it only took four minutes of the second half for him to signal to De Zerbi that he needed to come off.

Maddison had scored a brilliant goal on his first league start in 18 months against Coventry, but he had to be replaced by former Chelsea midfielder Gallagher at Stamford Bridge.

The Chelsea fans predictably booed Gallagher, a graduate of the club’s academy who at times even stood in as captain. The foul on Gallagher that earned Palmer his marching orders silenced the jeers and the Chelsea fans’ boos only returned at the final whistle for their own players.

Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso has told Cole Palmer he must learn from his red card that contributed to his team’s London derby defeat by Tottenham Hotspur.

Tottenham head coach Roberto De Zerbi insisted Palmer should have seen red for shoving his pelvis into the face of Mateus Fernandes. But, having got off with a yellow card, the Chelsea midfielder then fouled substitute Conor Gallagher.

“I haven’t seen them [the yellow cards] on TV, but as far as I have been told, he [the referee] probably got it right,” Alonso said. “So, for sure for Cole, but for everyone. We think [up until] now we haven’t received any red cards [this season] and we need to continue that way because we need to have this maturity to deal with difficult moments, when you have conceded 1-0, there was still 15-20 minutes to go, so we have to be calm. I need to check the action to talk with Cole, but it is one to learn for the future for sure.”

Asked if he thought Palmer should have been sent off for the first yellow card, De Zerbi, who was booked for his protests, replied: “Yep.”

Jordan Henderson had spoken to Palmer at the end of the previous week’s victory over Everton in which the forward could also have been booked for a second time and sent off.

But Alonso claimed he does not have any wider concerns about Palmer’s character. He said: “Today, for sure, yes, but it is not a [wider] concern. I think that Cole when he is focused on the game, when he is enjoying and today after the 1-0, it is a moment to keep calm, a moment to keep mature and for everyone.”

De Zerbi hailed his side’s character and claimed that Tottenham have “suffered enough” after two 17th-placed finishes and a slow start to the season until successive victories over Coventry City and Chelsea.

“We want to improve, improve and improve, to be better game by game, week by week. I think we have suffered enough,” De Zerbi said. “Changing the spirit of the group, changing the performance of the players, especially the more fragile players, maybe the younger players. We played very well. Great individual performances, great collective performance because we played very well. And then we put something different than Tottenham, I think, in the last period. Different attitude, different relationships with the players, different group.”

Chelsea were without João Pedro because of an injury and Alonso is waiting to find out how long the striker will be out.

“We don’t know yet because he has a lot of fluid,” Alonso said. “So he felt it yesterday [Friday] and we need to wait probably until tomorrow [Sunday] to do the test and to know the whole situation and the whole seriousness of the injury. We will know soon enough.”
