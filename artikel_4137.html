8 Hard to Beat Game Boy JRPGs

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Back in the day we didn’t have a ton of ways to check how to beat something or find any guides that would be readily available. A lot of the time you just sort of muddle through until you figure out exactly what you’re supposed to do.

The Best Game Boy Advance JRPGs That You Need to Play in Fall 2026 are perfect for enjoying while surrounded by the colors of autumn.

Even for some of the best JRPGs available you could spend a ton of hours just getting through one part. It was a glorious time where conquering that challenge felt rewarding because of how difficult that particular JRPG section may have been.

A lot of the games on this list are from series and part of that is because most games that released on the original Game Boy were not JRPGs as they weren’t quite as big in the states. Regardless, any game on this list is sure to give you a rough go of it if you aren’t prepared.

While Rolan’s Curse II is a bit more of an action-RPG, the game still takes several notes from JRPGs at the time and typically is counted amongst them. Instead of traditional turn-based mechanics, the game uses real-time combat making it more difficult than other JRPGs.

Many would go on to call it a Zelda-like game, but thanks to the inclusion of a party system with several characters it does fit the JRPG genre. The real-time aspect of the game made it more difficult to play, but also could force players into continuous encounters making things escalate in difficulty rather quickly.

The original Rolan’s Curse is a bit different from the second entry since it doesn’t include a cast of characters to play as. Much as you might imagine the game centered around the same core principals although the upgrades and such are for your character.

Two things specifically make this one a bit tougher, one being that there’s limited ways to recover yourself and second the combat is fairly punishing. It’s a game that definitely requires you to play with focus and without that focus you’ll find yourself dying a lot.

The very first game to ever be released in the SaGa series, The Final Fantasy Legend has some interesting history behind it. The game featured fantasy and sci-fi elements alongside each other as players built a party to take on the challenge ahead of them.

The difficulty of the game stemmed from how unusual the character development systems were combined with some harder combat as the game progressed. Players who didn’t take advantage of the systems for leveling would find themselves at an extreme disadvantage.

Despite the title of this one, it’s actually the first game in what would eventually become the Mana series. A much closer game to Zelda than many would imagine, but mostly thanks to weapons and such doubling as exploration tools.

Outside of that the game featured a party system in which an NPC could tag along on an adventure to help out. The real difficulty of this one comes from keeping tabs on your timing and positioning where even a small mistake could be costly.

A strange game for the time period that used a first-person view instead of top down for a fantasy JRPG. It took notes from things like Dragon Quest in terms of giving players the ability to investigate and engage with the world on a deeper level.

Since everything was fairly cryptic and there wasn’t much information given, players could often find themselves stuck on certain parts of the game. It took a lot more effort to get from one place to another if you didn’t want to do the investigating you had to.

This game changed some of the formula from the first one and introduced a tried and true JRPG mechanic, a party system. Rather than controlling one character the player now had a party to manage, but still used a first-person turn-based mechanic for gameplay.

Arguably a bit harder than the first game since now you dolled out resources amongst a party over just an individual. You could, of course, still miss plenty of things without investigating which led to players being at an extreme disadvantage some of the time.

Probably one of the crazier games in the series with a lot of going on in just about every part of the game. The odd fantasy and sci-fi bits of the game mixed gave players a lot of choice in how they handled leveling their character and gaining equipment.

In true JRPG fashion, what makes the game unique also makes it incredibly difficult. By transforming your party members into various races based on items you gave them could really screw up your journey if you weren’t careful.

Many JRPGs of the time period were doing some experimental work with various mechanics to spice up how their games played. Final Fantasy Legend II was no exception to this rule and even took it a step farther making it arguably one of the hardest JRPGs for the time.

Even the best JRPGs have some form of resource management, but those usually don’t apply to the story. MAGI, a collectible resource in the game, could be used to both enhance your character or progress the story forcing a difficult choice between power and story.

Fans of handhelds that like to challenge themselves should definitely check out this batch of Game Boy Advance JRPG adventures!
