Doing Crime Barely Covers My Menswear Habit in GTA VI

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

I am still slightly delirious. When GTA 6 came out at midnight last week, I played it for eight hours straight, all in the name of real, boots-on-the-ground journalism. Well, there was a quick power nap somewhere around 5 a.m., but otherwise I've been working very hard. I have a new wardrobe and some concerns about the price of digital jorts.

Most people will have spent their first-ever session getting through the missions, exploring the map, and finding out what Jason and Lucia have got themselves into. Not me. My main goal was to visit as many shops as possible. It's been 13 years since GTA 5; I wanted to see what had happened to the menswear.

Before we get into it, you should know: Your in-game clothes matter more than ever compared to other entries in the Grand Theft Auto universe. As I wrote last week, the new “heat” system lets cops remember what you were wearing, so losing them doesn't necessarily mean you're in the clear. Changing your hat or sunglasses won't clear the clothing clue, either. You need a full fit change. So technically, all this shopping could help me get away with something. That's my justification, anyway.

Unfortunately, even virtual shopping requires cold, hard cash—and early on, that takes considerably more effort than I'd hoped. You start with almost nothing, spending much of the opening stretch around Key Lento in swampy Mariana County. I'd gone in expecting to buy loads of clothes. Instead, I was checking what I could afford.

My first opportunity came in a random gun shop that also sold some workwear. I had a quick browse then went straight for the camo shorts. Back in August, British GQ's senior style editor Mahalia Chang declared that “camo shorts are fucking back, baby,” having watched Jason spend much of the trailer wearing them. Who was I to argue?

Nearby, in Hamlet, I found a few thrift shops and picked up a battered chore jacket with more than a little Carhartt Detroit about it. It looked properly used, which suited both my budget and the general condition of my pickup. I bought a watch, too, but more on that another time.

I was already getting quite into it. I was comparing pieces, checking prices, and deciding what could wait. There were clothes I wanted and clothes I could afford. Annoyingly, the game understood the difference.

I bought a trucker cap at one point and was very happy with it. Over the summer, I wrote about wanting to give them another go despite having a head my mates call “a sniper's dream.” Jason doesn't have that problem (in a metaphorical sense, anyway), so I thought I'd let him enjoy one. Shortly afterwards, a gang of lads picked a fight with me, punched me, and knocked it off his head. I fled, only realizing once I was safely away that I hadn't picked it back up. I'd barely had a chance to enjoy it. I hope one of them's wearing it at least.

After a few missions, I finally had enough money to justify another shopping trip. I got into my beaten-up pickup and headed downtown, where the prices started getting a little more serious. Denim jackets were around $120. A pair of dark wash jorts I really liked cost $45, which was slightly more than I wanted to spend.

I appreciate that this is a game in which you can steal a car, and I was still having a small financial discussion with myself about the price of shorts. For a game called Grand Theft Auto, I'd done surprisingly little car stealing in the time I'd been playing.

The map is absolutely massive, and there almost 70 clothing stores to work through, so visiting everything was never gonna happen in one sitting. Cars now use fuel, too. Driving miles out of your way to look at a jacket comes with an additional expense, and I had to leave enough money to get home.

There was a reason to keep exploring, though. I went into another shop expecting more of the same and realized the stock was different, so now I wanted to check the other shops before buying anything. Apparently, I'd waited 13 years to recreate how I spend my Saturdays.

Naturally, I decided to drive to Ocean Beach, a more expensive part of the city where I had very little business shopping. Inside Nola, the person at the till looked me up and down in my shitty workwear and asked, “Eh, are you looking for somebody?”

It was an impressively efficient assessment of my finances. Nola felt a little like Casablanca, the French-Moroccan label known for colorful prints, sportswear, and clothes that suggest a considerably nicer holiday than the one you're on. I'd previously called Hawaiian shirts “the unofficial uniform of Vice City.” Here, joining in cost about $330. Blazers were about $430. I could look, provided I didn't get any dirt on anything.

Then I accidentally punched the shop assistant; I'm still getting used to the controls. At least, I think it was an accident. Either way, I had to flee before I could give the blazers much consideration. When I came back later, the store was temporarily closed. I hope it wasn't due to staff injuries.

Other boutiques were even less accommodating to my balance. Italian house Ferruccio Tucci (probably a GTA-ified Emilio Pucci) had $3,000 suits and $1,200 horsebit loafers. I went in to browse and accidentally bought a pair of $350 shades, which was a very expensive way to learn which button does what. The annoying thing is that they looked genuinely sick—exactly the sort of thing I'd wear IRL, which made it much easier to justify keeping them. Well, that was fuel and dinner gone for the day. At least I could look good while walking home.

Later, driving past Waxman Island, I came across Vice City's Eris flagship. Obviously, I had to go in. For anyone who hasn't spent the past few months staring at fictional trainers, Eris is basically GTA's answer to Nike. Nike takes its name from the Greek goddess of victory; Eris is the goddess of strife and discord. The shoes are often just as recognizable as the joke.

The store kinda reminded me a little of Niketown in London—currently closed for refurbishment—with neon lights and big statues and enough footwear to keep me occupied considerably longer than was sensible. There was the Rise Low, an Air Force 1 lookalike that I ended up getting in two colourways; the Strife 360, with its very Air Max 97-ish shape; and the Rook 1 High, which follows the Air Jordan 1 High. I own more than 400 pairs of sneakers IRL—I probably should've known that going in here would be a problem.

And Eris is only one option. There are alternative sneaker labels, beach shops, souvenir shops, and smaller, DIY-looking streetwear brands all dotted around the city. I'd barely begun working through them. Every time I thought I'd seen enough to stop, there was another shop worth going into.

The most expensive-looking thing I acquired didn't come from a boutique at all. Slight spoiler: A naked man offered to give me his “Crowex” watch after pulling a plastic egg out of his own ass. (Yes, you read that right.) You'll know him when you see him. I'm not gonna go into any more detail, partly because I'm tired and partly because I'd like you to experience that transaction yourself.

Eight hours in, I'd hardly scratched the surface. What surprised me most was how long the expensive stuff felt like it would take to earn. Those suits and loafers were sitting there, available to try, while my balance suggested another few missions first. There's a lot of city left to explore, quite a few shops I want to revisit, and a pair of jorts I'm still thinking about.

For now, though, I need some sleep. And Jason needs to earn back that $350.
