10 Most Iconic Archie Comics Stories of All Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

For more than eight decades, the fictional town of Riverdale has been America’s quintessential backdrop for teen and pop culture, outlasting even the most iconic comic book franchises. What began in 1941 as a lighthearted showcase of malt-shop mishaps and high school rivalries evolved into a sandbox of stories that has stayed endlessly recognisable ever since.

Much of that longevity comes from Archie Comics’ willingness to take risks. Rather than relying solely on slapstick comedy, the publisher used its characters to address real-world social issues, lead bizarre genre crossovers, and eventually dismantle its own squeaky-clean image. Moving from zombie apocalypses to more grounded stories, few comic book universes have a catalogue as varied or as culturally resilient.

Over its more than eighty-year publication history, Archie Comics has produced iconic stories that have not only stood the test of time but also rank among the best the medium has to offer. This article highlights 10 of the most iconic Archie Comics stories of all time, representing the absolute highlights of its legacy.

Archie vs. Predator delivered one of pop culture’s wildest tonal collisions by pitting Riverdale’s cheerful teens against cinema’s deadliest extraterrestrial hunter. Released in April 2015 and written by Alex de Campi, the four-issue miniseries begins with an idyllic spring break trip where Betty and Veronica accidentally attract a ruthless Yautja warrior back home.

The story doesn’t shy away from the predator’s lethal brutality as he kills several armed people while hunting Veronica. The series gleefully unleashes full-blown R-rated slasher carnage across Riverdale’s bright, nostalgic aesthetic, systematically slaughtering beloved supporting characters in hilariously gruesome fashion. Its willingness to embrace its premise made it an instant cult phenomenon,

Archie #1 sees Mark Waid and Fiona Staples reimagine one of comics’ most enduring characters, ushering Riverdale into the modern era after seventy-five years of continuity. The relaunch traded classic house-style gag strips for a grounded, serialised teen dramedy, opening with a mysterious fallout known simply as "the Lipstick Incident" that fractures Archie and Betty’s lifelong romance.

Waid and Staples turned Riverdale’s teens into complex, relatable modern adolescents, marking a milestone for the company by reintroducing the characters in a way that felt fresh yet familiar. The new look and design suited a new generation of readers and offered a welcome change of pace for lifelong fans.

Chilling Adventures of Sabrina was published by Archie Horror, an imprint of Archie Comics, and offers a darker take on Sabrina the Teenage Witch. Written by Roberto Aguirre-Sacasa with artwork by Robert Hack, the series follows sixteen-year-old Sabrina Spellman as her upcoming Dark Baptism forces her to make an agonising choice between her mortal boyfriend and her sinister coven.

Steeped in gothic dread, satanic rituals, and genuine psychological terror, the comic stripped away every trace of sitcom whimsy to create an unsettling folk-horror masterpiece. Chilling Adventures of Sabrina became a major critical sensation, inspiring a loose television adaptation of the same name that ran for four parts and 36 episodes.

Published in July 2014, Life with Archie #36 delivered one of the most shocking moments in modern comic history by killing off Riverdale’s eternal everyman, Archie Andrews. This was the second-to-last issue in the series, bringing a melancholic close to the adult timelines where Archie married Betty and Veronica.

Archie dies after foiling an assassination attempt at Pop’s Chock’lit Shoppe that targeted Senator Kevin Keller’s life. Archie heroically dove into the line of fire and took a fatal bullet to the abdomen. His dying words to Betty and Veronica were “I’ve always loved you”. Archie dies protecting a friend, providing a fitting end and farewell to the selfless character.

Afterlife with Archie shattered every wholesome expectation surrounding Riverdale by plunging its iconic teenagers into a visceral zombie apocalypse. The series begins when Sabrina Spellman uses forbidden dark magic to bring Jughead’s beloved dog, Hot Dog, back to life. This act inadvertently unleashes an unstoppable undead plague across town.

The book earned widespread praise for embracing horror elements rather than the bright coloured panels the franchise was familiar with, and for treating Riverdale’s cast with brutal emotional stakes. Afterlife with Archie proved so wildly successful that it launched the Archie Horror imprint and directly paved the way for the dark, genre-bending reinvention of television’s Riverdale.

The Archieverse was forever changed with the debut of Kevin Keller, who is the first LGBT+ character in Archie Comics history. The fan-favourite character made his debut in Veronica #202, inadvertently making it one of the most sought-after comics in the franchise's history, selling out upon its initial release.

The issue opens with Veronica immediately falling head over heels for the new handsome transfer student, unaware that Kevin has no romantic interest in her or any girl at Riverdale High, completely subverting expectations. The story handles Kevin's sexuality tactfully, integrating him into Riverdale’s lighthearted rhythm through effortless comedy as Jughead helps Kevin navigate Veronica’s relentless, misguided romantic schemes.

Archie Marries Veronica / Archie Marries Betty finally answered the franchise’s oldest question through two distinct alternate futures. Written by Michael Uslan, the ambitious six-part arc was originally presented in Archie #600-605. Issues #600-602 present a story set in an alternate future where Archie, after his college graduation decides to marry Veronica Lodge.

In Issues #603-605, he chooses to marry Betty Cooper instead of Veronica. It treats both possible marriages with deep emotional maturity, elevating Riverdale’s familiar characters into complex adults with realistic struggles and lingering regrets. The event was an immediate hit, selling out shops nationwide and inspiring the acclaimed Life with Archie series that followed.

Love Showdown is a four-part comic book story arc released in 1994, featuring Archie Comics characters and finally tackling Riverdale’s most famous romantic stalemate head-on. The famous love triangle between Archie, Betty, and Veronica was supposedly put to an end in this comic, making it one of the most iconic moments in Archie Comics history.

The story kicks off with Archie receiving a mysterious love letter. Both Betty and Veronica inevitably think the other sent it due to Reggie’s meddling. This caused the girls to compete for Archie’s affection, urging him to choose between them once and for all. This leads to a moment where Betty upstages Veronica at a popular dance.

Nonetheless, it was ultimately revealed that the one who sent the letter was neither of them and none other than Cheryl Blossom, a girl from Archie’s past. At the end of the story, Archie unexpectedly chose Cheryl instead of the two. Archie’s Love Showdown Special would later bring the love triangle back to its former status quo.

Published in 1994, Archie Meets the Punisher is one of the most improbable crossovers in comic book history. Written by Batton Lash, with artwork by John Buscema and Stan Goldberg, the legendary one-shot brings together Marvel’s ruthless, gun-toting vigilante Frank Castle and Riverdale’s wholesome, milkshake-sipping teenagers, creating an unforgettable pop-culture fever dream that somehow works brilliantly.

The absurd premise centres on mistaken identity: a drug dealer named Red, who looks like Archie Andrews, flees to Riverdale to evade Castle’s relentless pursuit. As the Punisher stalks the quiet town’s high school hallways and sock hops, Archie and his friends are caught in the middle of lethal organised crime when thugs from New York arrive searching for Red.

The story plays the wildly contrasting tones of the two franchises completely straight, making it instantly iconic and entertaining. It also allowed Buscema’s gritty realism and Goldberg’s classic house style to share panels effortlessly. Archie Meets the Punisher shattered boundaries for inter-company crossovers, paving the way for other wild encounters in Riverdale, like those with KISS and the Predator.

Released in 1941, Pep Comics #22 quietly altered comic book history by introducing a clumsy, red-headed teenager named Archie Andrews, as well as Jughead and Betty, who would become fan favourite characters. This comic went against the grain and proved that everyday adolescent misadventures, high school crushes, and relatable neighbourhood humour could captivate young readers nationwide.

In this story, Archie “Chick” Andrews, desperate to impress his new neighbour, Betty Cooper, chose to walk on a fence blindfolded, but this death-defying stunt ends in disaster. He inevitably falls and destroys Betty’s father’s painting and her mother’s vase in the process. As recompense, he becomes Betty’s assistant at the taffy stand at a carnival.

Unfortunately, Betty gets him as a replacement for the tightrope walker, and it all ends with him falling into the taffy machine, spilling its contents all over Mr Cooper. The cultural impact of this comic cannot be overstated, leading to Archie getting his own solo title and the entire publisher being rebranded as Archie Comics.
