Pokemon collectors fear fake PSA slabs after AliExpress sells custom labels and cases for any card

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Pokemon TCG collectors are raising concerns after an AliExpress listing appeared to offer custom PSA-style labels for almost any card, complete with front and back designs, barcodes, QR codes and a holographic patch.

The AliExpress listing was shown in a video shared by collectibles commentator Max Long. It appeared to be priced at $4.05, with more than 5,000 sales displayed on the page. The product is advertised as a customizable label rather than a PSA-graded collectible, but collectors fear the designs could be placed on raw cards and resold as genuine slabs.

The concern grew after Japanese Pokemon card collector Pokemonlana posted screenshots of the same style of label using two different PSA certification numbers.

One was printed on a Dark Persian front label, while the reverse used a separate number. The collector questioned how buyers could distinguish the copies from real PSA labels and pointed out that buyers had left positive reviews on the listing.

Roman Pioneer, founder of the authentication database CheckCOA, examined the listing and similar products after the posts began circulating. His investigation found AliExpress stores offering custom front and back labels alongside unsealed card cases.

One store, called BEST REPLICA LABELS YOU’LL FIND, advertised a “DIY custom rating card label” and had reportedly sold 170 units with a 4.7-star rating.

Buyer photos showed labels using PSA certification numbers tied to a Paldean Fates Mew ex and a Rocket Dark Gyarados. Both numbers corresponded to real PSA records, meaning a buyer who only checks the certification number could see a genuine card description and grade.

A second store, High Quality Cards Store, advertised custom labels made from details supplied by the buyer. Its product video showed a copied PSA label being tilted under a light so the silver patch reflected in a similar way to the security feature on a genuine label.

One label used a deactivated PSA number, while another used an active certification number for a GEM MT 10 One Piece Zoro card.

The danger is not limited to completely fake labels. Another Japanese listing showed genuine PSA, BGS and ARS labels removed from their original cases and sold together for 6,800 yen. The labels themselves were authentic, but collectors warned they could potentially be placed into new cases with different cards.

CheckCOA marked those loose labels as suspected rather than confirmed counterfeits. However, the listing highlighted how a genuine certification label can become detached from the card it was originally issued for.

PSA itself warns that checking a certification number does not eliminate the risk of buying a counterfeit collectible. The company says counterfeiters can copy real numbers from public records and encourages buyers to compare cards with PSA’s own images, inspect the case for signs of tampering and purchase from trusted sellers.

PSA’s 2025 fraud report also identified Pokemon as its most frequently counterfeited category and said counterfeit Pokemon submissions increased by 125% year over year.

Some collectors have pushed back against claims that the copies are impossible to detect, pointing to details such as fugitive ink, print quality and missing security patterns. Others have called for future slabs to use NFC or RFID authentication.
