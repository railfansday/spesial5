Frank appointed Crystal Palace manager

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Thomas Frank returns to the Premier League with Crystal Palace after being sacked by Tottenham in February; he will replace Pierre Sage at Selhurst Park; Palace are bottom of the table with just seven points from their first 10 games

Crystal Palace have appointed Thomas Frank as their new manager.

The Dane replaces Pierre Sage, who was dismissed on November 12 following a seventh league defeat of the season to Liverpool.

Frank has signed a deal until the summer of 2029 - and took charge of his first training session as Palace boss on Monday.

Palace chairman Steve Parish said of Frank: "He has a proven track record of both creating winning teams and improving players making him the perfect fit for our club.

"I'm confident Thomas' extensive experience in the Premier League will help us achieve our goals for the rest of the season."

Frank was among a list of names that Palace considered in the summer before Sage was appointed to the role.

Other candidates that Palace were interested in included Frank Lampard, Sean Dyche, Vitor Pereira, Kim Hellberg, Vitor Matos, Tony Popovic, Jens Berthel Askou and Ole Werner.

Frank added: "It's an honour to sit here and be in charge of this fantastic Football Club. I'm really, really looking forward to it.

"Crystal Palace is an iconic club in England and in the Premier League. I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players and everyone around the place, and I'm really looking forward to meeting them."

Frank had great success at Brentford during his seven-year spell between 2018 and 2025, getting the Bees into the Premier League and then establishing them as a top-flight club, reaching a ninth-place finish in 2023.

He then moved to Tottenham last season to replace Ange Postecoglou, but was sacked after less than eight months in charge with the club in 16th place, five points above the relegation zone.

Spurs only sealed survival from relegation on the final day of last season, by which stage Roberto De Zerbi was head coach.

Frank failed to win over the Tottenham faithful, with his short tenure littered with setbacks, including drinking from a coffee cup which carried the badge of rivals Arsenal.

Palace are bottom of the Premier League with seven points, two from safety. They face the team in 17th place - Coventry - when the league resumes on Saturday.

They are also 21st in the Europa League table - which would currently see them through to the knockout play-off phase - with one win, two draws and one loss so far. The Eagles face Real Sociedad - a place ahead of them on goal difference - at Selhurst Park in their next European fixture on November 26.

Frank was Crystal Palace's No 1 choice to take over from Sage.

Palace believe their squad is much better than their current position in the Premier League table.

Frank is believed to be rejuvenated and refreshed after taking some time away from the game, having recently returned from Australia.

His first task is to get Palace out of the relegation zone, with the club two points from safety.

The Dane's willingness to use a variety of formations could be a key factor in bringing the best out of Palace's current squad.

Frank was also interviewed for the Manchester United and Chelsea jobs in the summer of 2024.

Two members of Frank's backroom team have already been announced.

Current assistant manager Paddy McCarthy is staying on, alongside goalkeeper coach Andy Quy, who coincidentally worked with Frank at Brentford.

However, Palace are working on putting together Frank's coaching team.

They are approaching Chelsea to get Bernardo Cueva. He was a set-piece coach under Frank at Brentford before moving to Chelsea - but Cueva would work as a coach at Palace.

Palace also want to add Justin Cochrane to Frank's coaching team. The two worked together at Brentford and Spurs and Cochrane is currently part of Thomas Tuchel's England coaching team.

Frank also wants Chris Haslam to join Palace with him. Haslam was a fitness and performance coach for 14 years at Brentford, and then performance and assistant coach under Frank at Tottenham.
