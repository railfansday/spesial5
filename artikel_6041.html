Tony Fernandes Pushes AirAsia’s Comeback to 2028

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Tony Fernandes now says 2027 will be a year of consolidation for AirAsia, two months after the airline group promised a fourth-quarter capacity rebound. Engines, not just fuel, are keeping aircraft on the ground.

AirAsia co-founder Tony Fernandes has pushed back his projections for the low-cost airline group's return to growth. He said 2027 would be "a period of consolidation" and that strong growth would resume only by 2028.

Fernandes set out the timeline in a post on LinkedIn on Wednesday. He also said engine problems were still keeping part of the fleet out of service.

"We are still struggling to get all our fleet back due to engine issues, but hopefully January," he wrote. "If we had got all our engines then we would have been in a far stronger position.”

He also warned that cost-cutting was not finished. "More work to be done there and there will be more painful decisions," he added.

AirAsia co-founder Tony Fernandes has pushed back the low-cost group's return to growth, saying in a LinkedIn post that 2027 will be "a period of consolidation" and that strong growth will resume only in 2028 — a clear step back from his July comment that the airline was "ready for a massive comeback story." He said engine issues are still keeping part of the fleet out of service, with a hoped-for return in January, and that cost-cutting isn't finished: "there will be more painful decisions." The 2028 date matches the group's fleet plan, with first A220-300 and A321XLR deliveries due that year, against a long-range target of more than 500 aircraft by 2039. The group has already suspended 81 routes and said it would return 25 aircraft to lessors by the end of 2026, and it is trying to raise up to $1 billion on international debt markets, mostly to refinance COVID-era debt, with Fernandes expecting the money by December or January. It had RM954 million ($234 million) in cash at the end of June against RM18.4 billion ($4.5 billion) in current liabilities. Malaysia's transport and finance ministries are still in talks with AirAsia, and former transport minister Anthony Loke told Reuters the government would not "simply pump money" into the airline. Malaysia Airlines has already overtaken the AirAsia group as the largest operator between Malaysia and India for the first time since 2021.
