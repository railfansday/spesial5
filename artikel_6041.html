$800 Hampton Inns In New York: How City Hall Rewarded Rent-Seeking Contributors

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Ashley Schapitl complains that a New York Hampton Inn can run $700 to $800 a night. She’s elected neighborhood commissioner in Washington, D.C. and works for a lobby shop. She was deputy assistant secretary for public affairs at Treasury during the Biden administration. What she seems not to realize is that the cost of New York City hotel rooms was a political choice engineered to protect incumbents from competition, drive up rates, and make it possible to redistribute excess profits to hotel unions. New York would be expensive anyway. Land and construction are costly (the latter is not unrelated) and there remains strong demand. But higher costs were intentional policy. The City Limited New Hotels In December 2021, New York extended its hotel permit requirement citywide. Even on land already zoned for hotels, most projects need the City Planning Commission’s approval, subject to City Council review. This was a pet policy initiative of the Hotel and Gaming Trades Council. Even projects expected to pass can take two to three years to get a permit No one expects that a nonunion hotel could ever get approved. The union gets an effective veto over new projects. City planners outlined that the new rules could be expected to reduce the number of new rooms in the city by over 25% – down to 127,660 by 2035 versus 174,730 without the policy. They also projected 18,970 fewer hotel jobs. This wasn’t a byproduct of the policy, it was the point – less supply, higher rates, redistribute income to incumbent workers. As I wrote when this was proposed, existing hotel owners and the union both have something to gain. Less competition makes an existing room more profitable. A strike would leave those expensive rooms empty, so the union can bargain for the difference. The Airbnb Crackdown Was Part Of This, Too In 2023 New York began barring Airbnb and other platforms from processing unregistered bookings. For stays under 30 days, the host generally has to stay in the apartment and can take only two paying guests. That limits supply but makes the argument for new hotels even stronger, but those are effectively barred too. A study in the European Journal of Political Economy estimated that the short-term rental crackdown added $14 to $19 to the average hotel night. Hotels took in an estimated $2.1 billion to $2.9 billion more over the first 18 months while selling roughly the same number of nights. Local Businesses Are Hurt, Too A family that cuts a three-night New York trip to two buys one fewer dinner. Another goes somewhere else instead. Or they do the full New York trip but spend less while they’re there. Restaurants see less money. There are fewer restaurant jobs, and tip income for waiters goes down. A working paper tracking card transactions across 19 metro areas estimated that after the Airbnb crackdown, New York restaurant spending fell 7.5%. Restaurants that relied on out-of-town diners took a disproportionate hit. In neighboring New Jersey markets, restaurant spending rose 10.1%. And the hotel effect magnifies this further.
