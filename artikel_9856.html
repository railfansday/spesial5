Professional Success Starts With Inner Work

Smarts and talent are not the strongest predictors of success. The skills you build inside yourself are. But how do these inner skills lead to gaining professional success?

Think about a work setting where someone is given a task that is completely new to them. If they are growth orientated and have grit, they would embrace the opportunity to learn from the experience and develop new skills. Even if they encounter issues, they will work towards the solution instead of viewing them as setbacks. Being flexible allows the person to pivot their approach and thinking to find the right fix. Having self-efficacy also gives them the confidence to believe in their own skills to finish the task and persevere through the challenges.

Inner skills such as grit, flexibility, self-efficacy, and a growth mindset push people to develop themselves professionally without feeling too overwhelmed and giving up. But building inner skills takes more work than you may think. They can degrade over time if not used often, leading to you falling back to unproductive habits and feeling stressed.

Businesses are losing a lot of money from weak leadership, including $550 billion due to inefficiency and $1 trillion because of high employee turnover rates. Employees who quit in 2025 have stated that the main reason for them leaving their jobs was because they did not get along with their bosses and managers.

A little over 40% of managers in the U.S. have received leadership training, which is affecting the confidence U.S. organizations have in their leaders’ ability to develop growth in others. Companies that are putting importance in having training are seeing higher levels of employee engagement, emotional intelligence, and team collaboration.

With greater emotional understanding and self-awareness, leaders can reduce turnover rates by 21% in high turnover sectors and 51% in low turnover sectors. Almost 80% of workers are coming to work more than before as well.

With better leadership, employees feel more productive at work, leading to 63% less safety issues, 70% greater physical and mental health, and 18% better sales productivity. Feeling better at work can even translate to stronger family relationships at home using the inner work. People feel greater marital satisfaction and have less conflicts with their family when they gain emotional understanding and more self-awareness. Parents in particular can raise emotionally healthy kids who know how to regulate their emotions when they do it themselves.

Decision-making and financial performance are strongly connected, which is why a growing number of companies are emphasizing decisive leadership. Those that use this leadership style are more likely to last longer than the competition while also providing three times more on shareholder returns.

Almost 80% of B Corporations brought in more money for their business versus their non-B Corporations counterpart, which brought in 60%. B Corporations inherently balance profit with a purpose, but a purpose can be best understood if the leaders have the capacity to make decisions that consider others’ needs and align with the company’s values. This can only be done when the leaders work on themselves to develop the personal qualities that can really make a difference.
