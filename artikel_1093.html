Michael Carrick saw his job saved in the most remarkable way as Manchester United fought back from 2-0 down against Roma in the Champions League to snatch the unlikeliest of 3-2 wins at Old Trafford.

Carrick, who had seen United go winless in nine games for the first time in 40 years, was desperate for a change of fortunes to save his skin but his time at Old Trafford appeared to be coming to an end as the hopeless Red Devils were 2-0 down inside 20 minutes as part of a diabolical first-half display.

But they were a different team in the second half and were given a lifeline when Bruno Fernandes scored a re-taken penalty – with a Roma player encroaching into the area during the initial miss – after Matheus Cunha was fouled.

Cunha then became the hero when he capitalised on a Roma error seven minutes from time to equalise before firing a winning goal in style shortly after, although he was booked for his celebration and then sent off injury time after an clumsy lunging tackle earned a deserved second yellow.

Bruno Fernandes denied that Manchester United’s remarkable comeback win against Roma saved Michael Carrick from the sack but admitted the manager was right to drop him for criticising his tactics last month.

He said: “The manager took a decision of making myself be on the bench because I wasn’t performing, and that’s fair.

“Everyone felt that was a punishment for the words that I had said. And if it was, it was right. The manager is the one that makes the decisions. I have to accept them. I have to go forward with that.

“It doesn’t change the belief I have on the manager and on the staff and and every player that is in this club, and it doesn’t change my aim to win every f***ing game that I have ahead of me.”

But after last night’s turnaround, United are back in the win column.

Michael Carrick may still need a greater comeback than this if he is to keep his job. But even if it does prove his last Champions League night at Old Trafford, at least he was given a glimpse of the Manchester United he remembers and which, increasingly, had seemed consigned to the past.

There was an extraordinary fightback, a victory plucked from the jaws of defeat. The crowd who had reacted to the award of a penalty by calling for the Glazer family to go ended up galvanised and roused, cheering a team who had been an absolute embarrassment for 45 minutes to a victory few saw coming,

Carrick had said he knew it sounded crazy when he said he was looking forward to this game. By half-time, his optimism appeared delusional. But now and again, even when they are at their lowest ebb for decades, United can justify the faith.

"We needed a bit of a kick,” he said on Amazon Prime Video. “We were a bit reactionary. When we lost the ball, we weren't reacting as quickly as we could have and the boss really drilled it into us that we need to be more active and take the game ourselves.

"We know what we're capable of. We know we're a dangerous team and we know we can change a game whenever, it's just it all coming together at the right time. It has tonight and hopefully we carry it on for the rest of the season."

The producers on Amazon Prime Video might have had a bit of a scare to see Bruno Fernandes drop an F-bomb live on air, but Wayne Rooney was delighted to see that passion overflowing from the Man United captain.

“Bruno Fernandes has shown a bit of passion in defending himself on what he thought was right, but more importantly we have seen that passion and fight on the pitch because the first half was terrible,” he said. “The first half had none of what we saw in the second half.

“No passion, no tackling, no getting to the ball, no aggression, and the complete opposite of the second half.

“And when that happens in the second half, you see the crowd get behind the team and you felt like they were going to win the game.”

Manchester United are back in the win column - but Michael Carrick isn’t out of hot water yet.

Tonight’s win should give him until at least the international break. Now focus turns to the Premier League, where his side are hovering above the relegation zone on goal difference.
