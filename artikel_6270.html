These 9 Hotels for Broadway Lovers Put You in the Heart of NYC’s Theater District

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

As a Californian who discovered the wonderful world of Broadway by borrowing CDs from my local library, it was only fitting that the first trip I ever planned by myself was to New York City—and the entire itinerary revolved around collecting Playbills. Now, after living in the area for more than two decades, I’m that unlikely New Yorker who still claims Times Square as her favorite and most-frequented neighborhood. I just love the Theater District—where else can you find 41 venues featuring the world's best live theater all within a single mile?

In this age of event tourism, Broadway has remained a constant. And seeing a show is about more than just the storyline of a play or the numbers in a musical—it’s an experience that you’re a part of. Over my 20 years as an entertainment editor and journalist, I’ve interviewed numerous Broadway stars, and they always say they feed off of, and are fueled by, audience reactions. You’re not just a passive viewer; your presence drives the show. It's something special to take part in.

If you've got a Broadway trip in your future, chances are you're going to need a hotel that will help you maximize your experience. The list below features properties with themed suites, theater lore, and locations just steps from the Great White Way. Read on for the best places to stay in NYC for a show-stopping Broadway getaway.

Closest theaters: 1-minute walk to Palace Theatre, 3-minute walk to James Earl Jones Theatre and Lunt-Fontanne TheatreWhy we love it: Right across from Palace Theatre, pre- and post-show prix-fixe menus, Studio 54 history

These days, Studio 54 is one of Broadway’s most popular stages (currently home to The Rocky Horror Show ), but back in 1977, Ian Schrager opened it as a nightclub, and it has since come to epitomize celebrity culture for a generation. Schrager brought that same visionary spirit when he opened the 452-room Times Square Edition in 2019, just across the street from the Palace Theatre. One of my favorite spots in Times Square is the hotel's Terrace and Outdoor Garden, a lush dining oasis that offers three-course Broadway Express prix-fixe menus both pre- and post-show. For those looking for an upgraded theater experience, the Edition's Showstopping Stay package—which can be booked up to 10 days in advance—includes two premium tickets to select shows, dinner with Champagne, theater-themed amenities, and house-made snacks.

Closest theaters: 9-minute walk to Studio 54, 10-minute walk to Vivian Beaumont Theater, 11-minute walk to Broadway TheatreWhy we love it: Central Park views, Broadway VIP Experience

Though a few blocks north of the Theater District thrills, the Mandarin Oriental is still less than a 15-minute walk to most theaters—yet it feels a world apart. It overlooks Central Park in one direction, and its 75-foot lap pool has views of the Hudson River. Guests are pampered with a true five-star experience every step of the way, whether it’s raising a glass at The Bar speakeasy or lounging around at celeb-favorite MO Lounge. You can also rub shoulders with the stars via the hotel's Broadway VIP Experience package, which includes an hour-long meet-and-greet with a Broadway cast member over bites and Champagne at the lounge. Also included are orchestra tickets (choose from 13 shows, including Hamilton and Harry Potter and the Cursed Child) and show merch, as well as add-ons like a theater transfer in a Mercedes, a 90-minute guided tour of the Museum of Broadway, or even acting lessons or a private concert with a stage star.

Closest theaters: 3-minute walk to Belasco Theatre, 4-minute walk to Hudson Theatre, 5-minute walk to Stephen Sondheim TheatreWhy we love it: Official partner of the Tony Awards, recently refreshed, early check-in option

If hotels could earn Tonys, the Sofitel New York would be a clear winner. As a longtime partner of Broadway’s iconic awards show, many a theater star has walked through its doors. That includes George Clooney during his Broadway debut in Good Night, and Good Luck, Jonathan Groff during his turn as Bobby Darin in Just in Time, and Mia Farrow while she was in The Roommate. Just last year, the property underwent a full makeover, reimagining its 398 rooms and suites (some with Empire State Building views), as well as its lobby, fitness center, and dining options (including Social 45 Bar & Bistro), drawing on the Art Deco glamour of Paris and New York. While an exclusive Broadway package is currently in the works, the Sofitel does currently offer The Ultimate Manhattan Luxury Staycation experience, which includes a welcome drink, dining credit, signature pastries, and early check-in at noon, perfect for squeezing in an extra Wednesday or weekend matinee.

Closest theaters: 2-minute walk to Eugene O’Neill Theatre, Walter Kerr, and Longacre TheatreWhy we love it: Quiet, designed by a Tony-winning scenic designer, curated theater mementos

Despite being footsteps away from several Broadway theaters, Civilian Hotel manages to create a cozy boutique experience, starting with an entrance just around the corner from Eighth Avenue. One step inside, and you feel like you’ve earned a backstage pass— hotelier Jason Pomeranc teamed up with scenic designer David Rockwell (Hairspray, Legally Blonde, Elf) on a classic theater theme featuring everything from marquee-inspired lighting to dramatic curtains. Even its 203 guest rooms channel vintage dressing room vibes. Rockwell's goal was to create a space for NYC’s creatives—performers, designers, directors, producers, and writers—that paid homage to classic Broadway. Stop by the Blue Vault tasting room and lounge to spot some theater relics, and the Olio Collection rooftop bar for more than 250 pieces of theater-inspired art.

Closest theaters: 1-minute walk to Palace Theatre, 3-minute walk to Marquis TheatreWhy we love it: Location across from TKTS booth, buzzy Sky Lobby

When it comes to Theater District locations, you can't find much better than the 661-room Tempo by Hilton New York Times Square. Making its debut in the dense area in August 2023, it snagged a prime spot right across from Duffy Square’s TKTS booth, the bright red discount ticket outlet that sells last-minute show tickets for up to 50% off (psst, check here for today’s offers). Even closer is the Palace Theatre, currently home to the musical The Lost Boys, which the property has partnered with on a themed suite, bookable through September 14, 2027. Inside the suite, you'll find an ’80s seaside feel mimicking the musical’s world of Santa Carla.

Closest theaters: 2-minute walk to Eugene O’Neill Theatre, Walter Kerr, and Ambassador Theatre; half a dozen more a 3-minute walk awayWhy we love it: Vacation vibes at its Bar Sprezzatura, daily complimentary wine in the Living Room Lobby during Evening Social Hour, Roundabout Theatre Company partnership

The first time I stepped off the elevator into Kimpton Theta New York’s Bar Sprezzatura, I immediately craved an Aperol spritz. That’s how much I was transported to the Italian Riviera. The contrast between the lively Midtown streets below (the bar sits on the 15th floor) and the laid-back rooftop bar made me fall in love with the 364-key property instantly, and with multiple two- and three-bedroom suites, it's perfect for families and groups looking to book a foodie-friendly Broadway bonding trip. This November, the hotel launched the Roundabout Theatre Co. x Kimpton Theta package, giving guests 20% off tickets to Roundabout Theatre Co. shows (currently The Rocky Horror Show and Imaginary Invalid) plus pre- or post-show drinks at the bar.

Closest theaters: Attached to Marquis Theatre, 1-minute walk to Minskoff Theatre, 2-minute walk to Richard Rodgers TheatreWhy we love it: Ideal for Marquis Theatre shows, home to NYC’s only rotating restaurant, Broadway Bound Dining Package

For a mega-sized hotel smack dab in the middle of Times Square, the Marriott Marquis still has a sense of character. When I first moved to the area 23 years ago, I used to frequent its eight-floor Broadway Lounge, looking out at Times Square and marveling that I had finally “made it." Nowadays, I still pop in for a dose of nostalgia, gazing up at the 36-floor atrium’s elevators leading to the hotel's 1,900 rooms—a constant in a world that’s changed so much since it opened in 1985. But that throwback feel comes with a new spring in its step, most notably at its 48th-floor spinning restaurant, The View, reimagined by Danny Meyer in 2024. (Note: TodayTix members get free bubbly and bar snacks here, depending on their tier.) But what makes the property so unique for theater fans is that it’s the only hotel ever built with a Broadway theater inside—yes, the Marquis Theatre is literally on the third floor. Also special: The Broadway Bound Dining Package includes the chance to mingle with a theater star!

Closest theaters: 2-minute walk to the Nederlander Theatre, New Amsterdam Theatre, and Lyric TheatreWhy we love it: Themed rooms, located on 42nd Street, close to other attractions

Now here’s a hotel that reflects both “A Whole New World” and “The Circle of Life.” Through its partnership with Disney’s two long-running musicals, Hilton New York Times Square guests can go all in with themed rooms curated by Broadway designer Devin Sparkles Design. Aladdin's Times Square Palace Suite is decked out just like Agrabah, down to a genie lamp, Moroccan magic carpets, and Jasmine-inspired silks. Over in The Lion King’s Royal Suite, it’s all about the wonders of the Serengeti Pridelands, with hand-painted Rafiki-like patterns and bird puppets flying from the ceiling. Both include wake-up calls from each musical’s most iconic characters—the Genie and Rafiki—as well as themed drinks. But perhaps the most valuable part of the package, especially for those driving in? Parking in Times Square.

Closest theaters: 1-minute walk to Hudson Theatre, Belasco Theatre, and Lyceum Theatre

Why we love it: Passageway running between Hudson Theatre’s entrance and stage door, June 2026 refresh

Ever since my first Broadway trip to NYC in 2002, I’ve been stage-dooring shows. The Hudson Theatre shows can be tricky for this, as the theater’s entrance is on 44th Street, but its stage door is on 45th. It wasn’t until I discovered that the Millennium Hotel had a secret passageway—which is also the entrance to the hotel—that I started snagging spots close enough to get autographs (including from Daniel Radcliffe during Merrily We Roll Along). That said, I've always been jealous of those actually staying at the 751-room property, with a trio of theaters literally at their doorstep. Those eager to dive even deeper into the Theater District's history will love that the property is just across the street from the Museum of Broadway.

Is it expensive to see a show on Broadway?

Yes, price tags can be high—the year-to-date average per ticket for 2026 is $122.67. But there are also plenty of ways to find discounts through lotteries, TodayTix, Theatr, and the TKTS Booth. I’ve only paid more than $100 five times in my life—and never more than $200. (That’s why I haven’t been to a movie in nearly a decade, but have seen nearly 40 shows in the last year!)

What show should I see on Broadway this winter?

For last-minute trips, hot tickets right now include Bowen Yang’s record-breaking run in Cole Escola’s Oh, Mary! (Lyceum Theatre), plus A Few Good Men (Vivian Beaumont Theatre), Much Ado About Nothing (Winter Garden), Other Desert Cities (Hudson Theatre), and School Girls; Or, The African Mean Girls Play (Samuel J. Friedman). And of course there are long-running hits like Wicked (Gershwin Theatre), Hamilton (Richard Rodgers Theatre), Harry Potter and the Cursed Child (Lyric Theatre), The Great Gatsby (Broadway Theatre), and Disney’s The Lion King (Minskoff Theatre).

Are there any shows I should plan a 2027 trip around?

As we’re standing on the precipice of one of the most anticipated Broadway seasons, there’s no better time to plan a theater getaway. Coming soon are Dolly: A True Original Musical (opening December 7 at St. James Theatre), the Julia Roberts and Paul Rudd–led Small Mouth Sounds (February 10 at Ethel Barrymore Theatre), Jonathan Groff’s The Amish Project (February 10 at Broadhurst Theatre), Dirty Dancing (February 12 at Lena Thorne Theatre), Evita on Broadway (February 27 at Winter Garden Theatre), Lin-Manuel Miranda’s Warriors (March 9 at Lunt-Fontanne Theatre), Purple Rain (March 12 at Majestic Theatre), The Sound of Music (March 23 at Vivian Beaumont Theatre), Damn Yankees (March 24 at Marquis Theatre), and the one I’m most thrilled about, the West End transfer Paddington: The Musical (March 30 at Al Hirschfield Theatre)—I already have my ticket for the first preview!
