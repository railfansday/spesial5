Ostensibly a mid-size four-door pickup truck, the Ineos Grenadier Quartermaster stretches the definition. Based on the stately Grenadier Wagon, the Quartermaster has a slightly stretched wheelbase and, crucially, a five-foot bed (61.5 inches to be precise) where the wagon—and SUV by any other name—has an enclosed cargo area. The mechanicals are nearly identical, the Quartermaster using the same BMW-sourced 281-hp turbocharged inline-six, eight-speed automatic transmission, solid axles, and four-wheel drive system as the wagon. While its retro-Rover styling could be construed as pretentious, its 1840-pound payload capacity and 7716-pound tow rating tell a different story. Yes, the fuel economy is daunting, and the steering remains vague despite some recent updates, but the Quartermaster knows exactly what it is and who it appeals to, and in that sense it’s without fault.

You won't find another off-roader on the trail with a central nervous system like the Quartermaster's. A turbocharged 3.0-liter inline-six from BMW, good for 281 horsepower and 331 pound-feet of torque, mates with an eight-speed automatic transmission and a permanent four-wheel-drive system with a two-speed mechanical transfer case. A locking center differential is standard, but front and rear lockers are also available. Under the body lie two solid axles that are suspended with coil springs at all four corners.

Like the Jeep Gladiator pickup, the Ineos uses recirculating-ball steering rather than a modern rack-and-pinion setup. It takes some getting used to—it feels less precise and requires more corrections—especially if you are familiar with modern electric power steering setups. Think of it as underscoring the vintage feel of this truck.

Second Row: Though the Quartermaster has four doors like its SUV sibling, rear seat room is limited.
