10-Minute Yoga to Beat Holiday Burnout

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Isa Brand is a yoga educator, teacher trainer, and brand director at YogaRenew. Isa’s sequences, including this yoga practice for holiday stress, encourage students to engage in self-inquiry and exploration as they unwind and recharge.

‘Tis the holidays, a time when seemingly *all the feels* are swirling in the air. Whether you’re experiencing mixed emotions about reuniting with family, navigating how to meaningfully spend the holidays by yourself, or simply trying to hold it together until the new year, the season tends to bring about mixed emotions for a lot of people. And you might not realize just how much of that mental stress your body carries—that is, until you pull a muscle while wrapping a gift.

So whenever you start to feel fatigued, tense, or in need of a respite from your uncle’s annual political speech at the table, it can be a game changer to sneak away for several minutes and do some soothing yoga for holiday stress. Consider the following poses a much-needed break from the collective chaos, as if you’re recharging your body and putting your mind on airplane mode. Made entirely of reclined and seated postures, the sequence encourages deep breaths, slow movements, and a restful vibe you can carry with you to the end of the year and into the next one!

This quick practice can be done any time of day or night to release tension throughout your body and mind. And you deserve self-care all times of year, so consider continuing to use it throughout the year.

You don’t need any props, although you might want a couple pillows or a bolster.

Lie on your back with your arms overhead and your legs straight. Reach your hands and feet away from you in a full-body stretch, then release. Gently circle your wrists and ankles in both directions a few times.

Still lying on your back with your legs straight, draw your right knee toward your chest. Keep your left leg straight or bend it and plant your foot on the mat. Grasp your outer right foot or behind your thigh. Use your hand to gently guide your knee toward your right underarm in Half Happy Baby. Take a few breaths here, then release. Switch sides.

From lying on your back with your legs straight, draw your right knee toward your chest. Keep your left leg straight. Draw your right knee across your body toward the left side. It might reach the floor, or if it doesn’t, you can slide a couple pillows or a bolster underneath it for more support. Reach your right arm straight out to the side in line with your shoulder in Supine Spinal Twist. If it’s comfortable, allow your gaze to turn toward the right. Take a few breaths here, then switch sides.

Still lying on the mat on your back, roll onto your left side with your legs bent and your knees stacked on top of each other in a fetal position. Press your hands into the mat so you’re sitting up with your knees still bent at your right side. If this is uncomfortable for your knees, try straightening your legs slightly. Plant your right hand on the mat outside your right hip and reach your left arm overhead and overhead toward the right. Imagine creating a long arc with the left side of your body. Take a few breaths here.

From Mermaid Stretch, reach your right arm behind your back, bending your elbow and reaching your hand toward your left hip, as you lower your left ear toward your left shoulder. You can gently place your left hand on top of your head and let the weight of your hand assist the stretch, if you like, making sure you’re not forcing it. Take a few breaths here, then release.

Repeat Mermaid Stretch and Mermaid Neck Stretch on the opposite side.

Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet planted on the mat about hip-width apart. Reach your fingertips toward your heels. Press into your feet and lift your hips in Bridge Pose. Keep your arms alongside you or reach them overhead if that feels comfortable. Take a few breaths here, then slowly lower back down to the mat.

From lying on your back, straighten your legs toward the ceiling or, if it’s more comfortable, keep them bent. Gently shake your feet and legs for as long as feels good. Then shake out your arms.

From lying on your back, straighten your legs and reach your arms overhead. Reach your feet and hands toward the right, creating a long arc with the left side of your body. For more sensation, cross your left foot over your right ankle. Take a few breaths here, then switch sides.

Lie on your back with your arms by your sides or extended straight in a T shape and your legs straight. Let your entire body relax in Savasana. If you like, close your eyes. Stay for several slow breaths.
