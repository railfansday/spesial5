How to Master the Run/Walk Method: Train for a Half Marathon

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Run your first or fastest half marathon with the support of walk intervals.

Runners can’t get enough of the half marathon—and for good reason. Not only is it a practical and challenging distance for all levels of runners, but it’s also a race widely offered at running events across the country. Dig through race calendars, and you’ll likely find plenty of half marathons in bucket-list locations and even your own backyard.

And whether you’re looking to run your first or best half marathon, the run/walk method could be the smart, sustainable strategy you need to strongly cross the finish line.

Runner’s World’s program, How to Master the Run/Walk Method, give you the training tools and plans necessary to conquer the 13.1-mile distance with walk intervals. Beginners and experienced run/walkers can find one of several training plans that fit their goals and give them confidence in their physical and mental fitness heading into race day.

Training Principles for Consistent Success

The half marathon consistently ranks as one of the most popular race distances among runners. According to the 2026 RunningUSA Global Runner Survey, 38 percent of nearly 12,000 surveyed runners said the half was their favorite event—20 percentage points higher than the next most preferred distance (the marathon). The run/walk method can make the distance manageable for all levels of runners and even help you get a new personal record.

“I ran my best half marathon using run/walk during the Savannah Rock ’n’ Roll Half Marathon,” says run/walker Cheryl Munn. “It was really hot. People were dropping like flies. I was able to maintain and be consistent by just keeping up the intervals.”

To find your goal half marathon pace, use your data from your Magic Mile time trial. Multiply your timed mile by 1.2 to get your half marathon race pace. You’ll also use that pace to set your training paces and run/walk intervals you’ll use during your buildup to race day.

Runner’s World has three run/walk training plans to choose from to prepare for your half marathon: a beginner, intermediate, and advanced plan. Over anywhere from six to 16 weeks, you’ll incorporate run/walk workouts, easy walks, long runs, cross-training sessions, and rest days into your training block to build your endurance, speed, and confidence ahead of your half marathon.

Ideal for: Runners who have never completed a half marathon before.

Using this plan, you’ll have two midweek run/walk workouts lasting 30 to 45 minutes each week, along with one easy walk for 20 to 45 minutes, two days with a cross-training option, and at least one rest day. You’ll also have weekend long runs, which start at two miles and work up to 14 miles—longer than the 13.1-mile race distance. Pro tip: If you’re new to the run/walk method, start your workouts with five to 10 seconds of running, and then walk the rest of the minute. Or, if you’ve been using run/walk for at least three months, you can follow our run/walk ratio suggestion chart to determine your ideal ratio.

Ideal for: Athletes who have been running at least three times a week for three to six months and want to work up from a 5K to a half marathon.

Each week with this plan, you’ll complete two midweek run/walk workouts lasting 30 to 45 minutes, an easy walk, two optional cross-training days, and one rest day. You’ll start with five miles for your first long run and progress to 14 miles by week six. Compared to the beginner plan, the intermediate plan features an increase in mileage.

Ideal for: Runners who have already raced a half marathon before and want to improve their time. You’ll also want to have a solid fitness base before starting the plan, making sure you can complete double-digit miles.

This plan features speedwork once a week, as well as race pace miles one to two times per week based on your Magic Mile time trial. You’ll also have two optional cross-training days and a rest day each week. Your long run begins at five miles and progresses to 14 to 17 miles toward the end of the training block.

A staple speed workout you complete following the advanced plan is 800-meter repeats. You’ll run at 30 seconds per mile faster than your half race pace for 800 meters, followed by 400 meters of walking—which you’ll repeat until you hit your target amount of reps. For example, if your half marathon pace is 12 minutes per mile, you’ll run at 11:30 pace for 800 meters for each rep. For your half marathon pace work, you’ll incorporate race pace miles into both your midweek and long run mileage, allowing you to practice your goal speed and run/walk interval ratios.

Remember all of that patient, consistent work you put in during training? Trust that preparation on race day. It’s tempting to get caught up in the race day atmosphere and complete your first adrenaline-filled miles way too fast. As you begin your race, keep your run/walk ratio and goal pace in mind, and pace yourself strategically in the early miles so you don’t burn out.

The half marathon is such a popular distance that the race course may be flooded with other athletes. As a run/walk athlete, keep in mind that not every runner around you will be taking walk breaks or taking them at the same time as you.
