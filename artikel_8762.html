When shopping for the best under-desk walking pad, keep these factors in mind:

Size and Portability: Ensure the walking pad’s dimensions fit under your desk and in your workspace. Consider weight and whether the machine has wheels for easy storage or movement.

Belt Size: Wider belts provide more stability and comfort, while narrower pads are better for tight spaces. Longer deck lengths work best for those with a longer stride.

Speed Range: Most walking pads offer speeds from 0.5 to 4 mph—enough for gentle walking to brisk paces. Some 2-in-1 pads allow faster speeds when used away from a desk.

Incline Options: A few models feature adjustable inclines for varied intensity, though many are flat for multitasking. If you want more of a workout, look for pads with at least a minor incline capability.

Weight Capacity: Check each machine’s weight limit, especially if you need extra support or want a more robust frame.

Noise Level: Choose a quiet pad for minimal distraction, particularly if you share your space with others or take calls frequently.

Ease of Use: Look for simple controls (often via remote) and clear displays. Consider features like app compatibility, folding capabilities, and maintenance requirements.

Price: Budget models are available for under $200, while high-end or commercial-grade options cost significantly more. Decide which features are worth extra investment for your specific needs.

So, what’s the difference between a treadmill and a walking pad? There are a lot of parallels for sure, and functionally they’re very similar. However, the real difference between a treadmill and a walking pad happens under the hood. Walking pads have smaller, quieter motors that don’t allow you to pick up the pace dramatically, but they keep them quiet enough to use while you work or read. They’re also a lot lower to the ground, and that low step height makes them a great choice for folks who have joint and mobility issues. They’re shorter and narrower than traditional treadmills so that they fit easily under desks. Luckily, they’re also easier to store, and many of them fold into little packages or can be stored on their side. And the point many people love the most? They’re usually a lot cheaper.
