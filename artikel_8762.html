5 Yoga Poses to Get You Through Any Holiday Gathering

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Certain seasons come with a lot, and we mean a lot, of socializing. On the one hand, this means a chance to spend quality time with family and friends, which is always a win. But if you deal with any amount of social anxiety, gatherings can also come with stress and general overwhelm.

If leaving isn’t an option—just kidding, we know you probably want to be there—there are ways to find grounding and calm within your own being. You can practice breathwork, take a walk, even excuse yourself and enjoy a yoga pose or two for a minute. Or, if you resonate with the asanas on this list, perhaps five!

These yoga poses are meant to be easily accessible. Escape for a few moments to try one of the more involved poses or enjoy a super subtle modification mid-party!

Holiday gatherings tend to involve a fair amount of standing around—which is where your casual Tree Pose (Vrksasana) comes in. Popping one foot up—whether against your upper or lower leg or, more stealthily, your ankle—can be done at literally any time: while you’re doing the dishes, chatting with loved ones, or experiencing a lull in activity. The reminder that you’re in control of your sense of balance can help you feel steadier on your own two feet in all ways.

Folding forward can provide a sense of tremendous release and relief. Hinging at your hips in Standing Forward Bend (Uttanasana) and allowing your head and upper body to hang heavy—sometimes referred to as Rag Doll—serves as a subtle inversion, activating your parasympathetic nervous system and encouraging calm. Plus, this pose is normal enough to fold into at any point. Just tell your people that you need a quick stretch.

Finding strength within yourself can make chilling out a little easier. Pressing up—or jumping back—into Plank Pose activates your core, arms, and legs, then asks you to pause. Turning your focus toward this physical task, whether for a few breaths or a full minute, can help snap you out of an anxious spin and get you back into a celebratory state.

Sometimes, the best thing you can do is retreat to a spare bedroom (or the couch or the floor) and lie down for a while. Savasana can certainly provide some calming release, but for something a little more likely to get you out of your head and into your body, try Reclining Bound Angle Pose. It’s a subtle stretch that rejuvenates without being daunting—sort of like a best-case-scenario social engagement.
