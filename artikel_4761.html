Finding the Tailwinds That Help Us Move Forward

So often, our attention gravitates toward what is wrong, challenging, or missing. Yet when we focus solely on what is not working, we lose sight of the things that are helping us along.

I find it helpful to think about the headwinds and tailwinds moving through our lives.

Headwinds are the pressures, stressors, and challenges that push against us. They are the things coming at us, demanding our attention, threatening to knock us off course, or making it harder to move in the direction we want to go. Some are temporary, while others are part of circumstances we cannot quickly change. Tailwinds are the forces supporting us from behind and within. They include our strengths, skills, relationships, routines, experiences, and values that help us keep going. They may not remove the headwinds, but they can help us meet them with greater support and perspective.

In Stress Wisely, I explore wellness through eight interconnected dimensions: physical, emotional, intellectual, social, environmental, occupational, financial, and spiritual. Each offers a different lens through which we're able to notice what is hindering us, what is helping us, and where we might find room for growth.

Wellness is an ongoing process of understanding what we need, noticing what supports us, and making adjustments as our circumstances change. Perhaps we are not sleeping as well as we would like, feel disconnected from the people we care about, are carrying financial stress, or have lost touch with the activities that give our lives meaning. These concerns deserve our attention, and sometimes they call for change, additional support, or a closer look at what is possible within our circumstances. At the same time, my gentle invitation is to ask ourselves: What is already working for me here?

It is easy to focus on what our bodies are not able to do or what we wish we could change, but we can also appreciate what they help us experience and what we are doing to care for them. Perhaps a 30-minute walk every day helps you clear your mind, a consistent bedtime is allowing you to show up with better energy, or you've learned to recognize when you are running on empty and need to slow down. These everyday choices matter.

If you thought of your body as a vehicle carrying you through life, what is helping you take good care of it and keep it going?

Emotional wellness is about learning to recognize our feelings and respond to them with greater understanding instead of ignoring them or expecting ourselves to push through them. One tailwind may be your growing ability to name what you are feeling. Maybe you give yourself 90 seconds to sit with an emotion and let it move through you, find comfort in a creative outlet, or reach out to a trusted person rather than carrying everything alone. These are ways of making room for our emotions as we navigate the hard parts.

What helps you process your feelings and meet yourself with a little more compassion?

We learn through curiosity, reflection, conversation, and experience. Each and every one of us has skills we've developed over time, mistakes we've learned from, and different perspectives we've opened ourselves up to that have broadened our thinking.

Thinking of a particular area in your life, what have you learned, figured out, or become more capable of doing?

We are not meant to navigate life alone. A friend who checks in, a colleague who understands, or a community where we feel we belong can remind us we are supported and valued. We can also notice the strengths we bring to our relationships, such as being a good listener, a thoughtful encourager, or a practical problem-solver. Connection is about the support we receive, but it's also about the ways we contribute to one another.

Who helps you feel safe, seen, and supported? What do you bring to the relationships that matter to you?

Our surroundings can influence how we feel and move through our days. We can't always change our environment, but we can notice the places that bring a little ease. Maybe it's a favourite park bench, a sunny spot by your living room window, or a calm corner where you can collect your thoughts. Creating and spending time in hope-filled havens that offer comfort and inspiration can make a difference.

Which places help you feel more settled, connected, or at ease?

Our contributions extend beyond paid work to include caregiving, parenting, volunteering, and the many responsibilities that make up our days. When the demands feel relentless, we may overlook the care we bring to our work, the problems we have solved, or the difference we make in the lives of others. A tailwind might be recognizing a task you carry out that makes someone else's day easier, setting a boundary around email, asking for support, keeping a commitment that reflects your values, or celebrating the small wins.

Where do you find meaning in the work you get to do, and what helps you do it in a sustainable way?

When financial pressures are weighing on us, it can be hard to see what we're doing well and what is working for us. Recognizing what's helping us doesn't mean pretending those worries aren't real. Perhaps you have become more aware of your spending, started a conversation you've been avoiding, learned something about budgeting, or reached out to understand your options.

What is helping you feel more informed, supported, or prepared when it comes to your resources?

Spiritual wellness can be rooted in faith, and it can also be found in our values, sense of purpose, and connection to something larger than ourselves. Noticing the moments that remind us of what matters most can help us make choices that reflect it. Maybe it's spending time in nature, being of service, a prayer practice or familiar ritual, or making time for the people and causes you care about.

When do you feel most connected to what matters most to you?

We cannot always choose what life brings our way. Yet even when things feel difficult, we can widen our view so that what is hard is not the only thing we see.

This is where I believe gratitude is one of the mightiest forces we have for living well. It draws our attention to what might otherwise go unnoticed, helping us recognize what is sustaining us today and what we can carry forward into tomorrow.
