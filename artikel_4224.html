With Sony recently confirming that it will no longer port its PlayStation games to PC, there has been a rush to perfect emulation of its titles. Over the summer, 2025's Ghost of Yotei was booted on PC via an emulator for the first time, and the same happened with the PS5 launch title, Demon's Souls.

However, while SharpEmu is certainly the choice for running emulated versions of PS5 games, one player is trying to get Astro Bot to run natively on Windows, without the need for an emulator, and so far, they've had some success. After jumping through a few hurdles, the game is running on Windows, with up to 120 FPS via frame generation.

The native Astro Bot PC port was shared on Reddit by Equal_Classroom_872. It runs through a "Windows executable using AnyPS5, with a Kyty-based Vulkan graphics layer."

Across four of Astro Bot's different areas, Equal_Classroom received varying frame rates. In Sky Garden, they received 32-34 fps; in Hoover Beach, 51–53 fps; in Snowy Canyon, 43–44 fps; and in Cymbal Party Land, 37–39 fps. This was, however, boosted drastically via frame generation.

With 2x frame gen, the game reached around 63 FPS; with 4x, it reached 124-128 FPS. Naturally, the frame generation has caused some controversy, as they aren't native frames, but it is a way to boost performance.

The port also includes DLSS Super Resolution, NVIDIA Reflex integration, and controller support, including DualSense, DualShock 4, Xbox, Switch Pro, plus motion controls.

Unfortunately, like many other PC ports recently, this is almost certainly vibecoded. We've seen it with the likes of Super Mario Galaxy and Need for Speed: Underground 2, and Equal_Classroom actively cites AI in their post.

It comes at a time when public perception of Sony and PlayStation is at an all-time low. The PlayStation fanbase has been rallying against the company's decision to stop producing discs starting next year. The backlash has included protests, petitions, new bill proposals, and class-action lawsuits against the move, and Sony even won a Hall of Shame award.
