Why Micron Stock Is a Screaming Buy Before Year-End

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Micron recently announced an expansion to its share buyback program.

Surging demand for high bandwidth memory (HBM) and DRAM has fueled a historic memory supercycle, helping Micron Technology (MU -2.52%) generate record revenue, profits, and cash flow.

Even so, investors have recently experienced a meaningful reversal. Micron reported fiscal fourth-quarter earnings on Sept. 30. Since then, Micron stock has fallen from $1,065 to $762 as of this writing (Nov. 13), representing a 28% decline.

The sell-off is surprising given the company's robust financial performance. However, I think investors are overlooking a powerful catalyst approaching on Dec. 9. Let's dig into why I think Micron stock is an obvious buy before that date.

Micron's recent decline is not about disappointing financial results. During its fiscal fourth quarter, revenue surged 379% year over year to $54.2 billion. Adjusted earnings reached $33.42 per share. Management also guided for $61.5 billion in revenue for the first quarter of fiscal 2027, underscoring the strong demand for memory chips.

Nevertheless, semiconductor stocks have faced heavy selling pressure since November's midterm elections. Democratic victories in Congress have introduced uncertainty surrounding future policies governing data center construction, electricity costs, and environmental permitting.

Many Democratic candidates campaigned on addressing the impact of artificial intelligence infrastructure development on local communities. Investors are now questioning whether new regulations could slow the pace of hyperscaler capital expenditures (capex).

This is important for Micron because companies building data centers require advanced memory solutions as part of their chip stack. A deceleration in infrastructure investment could affect memory demand, pricing, and profitability.

There is also company-specific uncertainty regarding Micron's reported pause in constructing its Fab 11E manufacturing facility in Taiwan amid ongoing labor disputes.

With that said, uncertainty about future spending or manufacturing capacity is not evidence that the memory boom is fading. Micron's latest financial guidance suggests the company's underlying business remains in a good position.

On Oct. 8, Micron's board of directors increased its authorized share repurchase program to $35.16 billion. Micron's CHIPS Act funding agreements restrict its ability to conduct share buybacks under the expanded authorization until Dec. 9. This date does not mean Micron will immediately begin buying back shares. Rather, it represents when the company can start executing repurchases.

The recent pullback makes Micron's buyback program more powerful. Consider a hypothetical example: At $1,065 per share, $35.16 billion could repurchase about 33 million shares. At $762 per share, the same amount could repurchase roughly 46 million shares. That's nearly 13 million additional shares that Micron could retire simply because its stock price has declined.

Of course, actual repurchases will occur at current market prices, and Micron isn't obligated to exhaust its authorization. Nevertheless, the math above shows why lower stock prices can benefit companies executing buyback programs.

When a company retires outstanding shares, its earnings are distributed across a smaller ownership base. Assuming net income remains unchanged, earnings per share (EPS) increases.

Since stock prices are frequently measured using earnings multiples, a rising EPS can support a higher share price even without a commensurate increase in the company's total profits. In turn, this could fuel valuation expansion if investors regain confidence in the durability of the AI infrastructure build-out and memory's role in this cycle.

For Micron investors, this creates a lucrative opportunity. The company is already generating heaps of cash flow from AI-driven memory demand. If the momentum continues throughout fiscal 2027, earnings growth combined with a declining share count could be a powerful tailwind.

I see Micron's sell-off as an attractive entry point right now. Rather than waiting for the company to announce its first buyback, I'd consider building a position in Micron stock now. The opportunity to buy a rapidly growing business at a lower valuation will not last once market sentiment improves.
