Nvidia’s 103% Revenue Growth Took a Backseat to Its Internal Drama

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The AI chipmaker is growing like wildfire, but its future is still in flux.

There's no denying that Nvidia (NVDA -0.44%) is the bellwether for the state of artificial intelligence (AI). That said, the drama surrounding its former CEO Jensen Huang continues to consume all the oxygen in the room. Reports alleging that a Chinese spy targeted Huang, that Nvidia's AI-centric chips were smuggled through other countries into China to circumvent export restrictions, and the resulting crackdown by the U.S. government are all front of mind for investors, who rightly wonder what the future holds.

As such, Wall Street and Main Street alike were sitting on the edge of their seats ahead of the company's quarterly financial report after the market close on Tuesday. Given the nature of recent events, the numbers took a back seat to the internal drama and to what the future holds for the chipmaker.

The fact that Nvidia's growth continued to accelerate may have served to remind shareholders why they invested in the company in the first place. For its fiscal 2027 third quarter (ended Oct. 25), the company generated record revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year and 20% quarter over quarter. This drove adjusted earnings per share (EPS) up 111% to $2.68. The highlight was Nvidia's sustained gross margin, which clocked in at 74.9%.

For context, analysts' consensus estimates were calling for revenue of $108.7 billion and adjusted EPS of $2.47, so Nvidia sailed past expectations with room to spare.

The data center segment, which includes processors used for AI, cloud computing and high-performance computing -- and, of course, data centers -- continued to accelerate, delivering another record-setting performance. Segment revenue of $107.6 billion soared 110% year over year and 21% sequentially.

The edge computing segment (previously the "gaming" segment) -- which includes PCs, workstations, game consoles, robotics, and automotive -- reported respectable growth, up 42% year over year and 14% quarter over quarter.

In a move designed to restore investor confidence, the board of directors increased Nvidia's buyback authorization by an additional $150 billion (with no expiration).

Management's forecast suggests the company's impressive growth will continue in Q4. Nvidia's outlook calls for revenue of $130 billion, representing year-over-year growth of 90%. The company also reminded investors that its gross margin is poised to moderate, falling to 72% at the midpoint of its guidance -- in keeping with the company’s previous forecast.

Nvidia also increased its fiscal 2028 guidance, forecasting 75% growth for the year, up from its previous outlook of 70% -- on top of its beat-and-raise quarter.

Board chair Harvey Jones sought to reassure investors, saying of interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress, "We have an incredibly talented leadership team, and we have every confidence in Colette's leadership, integrity, and ability to deliver as interim CEO." He went on to say that the board "has no intention" of taking Huang back.

In her own opening remarks, Kress chimed in, saying, "Our ambition will not change. Our passion to innovate will not change. And our commitment to customers, partners, and shareholders will not change." Of the company's results and future potential, Kress had this to say: "Our momentum is real. Nvidia is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people." She closed the earnings call by saying, "We've got this."

These comments, the robust guidance, and the increased buyback authorization were all a coordinated effort to ease investor concerns about Nvidia's future in a post-Jensen Huang world. The efforts seemed well received, with Nvidia stock up more than 5% in after-hours trading (as of this writing).

There are still many unknowns, with the biggest being who will ultimately fill Nvidia's CEO position. There are also recent reports that the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has launched an investigation into Nvidia, though the exact nature of that probe is unclear, according to a report that first appeared in The Wall Street Journal.

Nvidia stock is selling for less than 20 times earnings, so the valuation may seem intriguing. At this juncture, however, remember the old adage: "Caveat emptor" -- Let the buyer beware.
