3 Gilded Age Estates You Can Buy Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Envious of HBO’s The Gilded Age? Live out your Russell family dreams in one of these real-life period estates.

Above: Listed for $75 million, the Hillandale estate sits on 268 acres in Stamford, Connecticut.

HBO’s The Gilded Age is back for a fourth season, once again bringing viewers into the lavish homes where the Russell and van Rhijn families’ dramas unfold. Filmed at some of the era’s most impressive estates, the series serves up grand ballrooms, château-style manors, and sumptuous drapery in abundance. It’s enough to stir up a little real estate envy. Fortunately, three extraordinary Gilded Age homes are currently on the market, offering the chance to live out that fantasy in real life. Take a peek inside below.

Dating to the turn of the century, this lavish European-style estate—known as Hillandale—once belonged to the Sulzberger family, owners of The New York Times. Located along the Connecticut border with New York, the star of the 268-acre compound is an eight-bedroom mansion designed by architecture firm Hunt & Hunt that spans more than 15,000 square feet. Featuring elaborate millwork, moldings, plasterwork, and stained glass, the manor features plenty of amenities. Olympic-sized indoor pool? Check. Elevator? There are two. Grounds? They include formal gardens, four guest houses, a private lake, and even a hedge maze. The opulent spread is listed with Leslie McElwreath of Sotheby’s International Realty.

An Anglo-Italianate brownstone in New York’s Chelsea Historic District, the Van Ness House was built in the mid-19th century and was later enlarged to its present 7,800-square-foot size. Revamped by ELLE Decor A-List designer Miles Redd, the seven-level landmark pays homage to its elegant roots, with preserved plaster moldings, custom millwork, elegant wallcoverings, and heated oak floors in a retro chevron pattern. In the back, a landscaped private garden is paved in limestone and dotted with linden trees that turn golden in the fall. Atop the five-bedroom townhouse, which is listed with Nick Gavin and Amber Gregory of Compass, is a rooftop garden with views of Saint Peter’s Church.

Spanning 14,100 square feet, this Beaux-Arts mansion right off Fifth Avenue was constructed in 1902 by architect James R. Turner and William G. Killian. Its interiors, meanwhile, are by John Russell Pope, the genius behind the Jefferson Memorial and the National Gallery of Art. Clad in a medley of brick, limestone, and marble, the eight-bedroom house has retained its stately proportions throughout the years, including a formal dining room lined in carved wood panels. Also inside are a trio of staircases, an elevator servicing six of the seven floors, and two rooftop terraces. It’s listed for $35 million with Brian Meier and Amanda Barba of Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices.
