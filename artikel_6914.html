Iowa district to settle with recruiting firm after ex-superintendent’s ICE arrest

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Let K-12 Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The former Des Moines school leader overstayed his visa, according to ICE. In response, the district sued a firm it claimed should have prevented his hiring.

Des Moines Independent Community School District — whose sitting superintendent, Ian Roberts, was apprehended by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement in 2025 — has reached a settlement with the recruitment firm that the Iowa district tapped to help with the superintendent search that resulted in Roberts’ hiring.

The settlement's notification, filed with the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Iowa on Nov. 9, ends a more than yearlong legal dispute that was triggered by Roberts' ICE arrest for allegedly staying and working in the country illegally.

The school district announced in October 2025, a month after Roberts' arrest, that it was suing JG Consulting, which the Des Moines School Board contracted to help with its superintendent search in the 2022-23 school year. The firm facilitated the hiring of Roberts in 2023.

The district said it was a "victim of deception" by Roberts following his September 2025 arrest and claimed in its lawsuit against JG Consulting that the firm failed to properly vet Roberts in violation of its contract and was negligent when it presented Roberts as a suitable candidate for the job.

In response, JG Consulting sued the district this year for defamation, saying that the hiring of Roberts was the district's own mistake and that the firm's agreement with the district did not require confirmation of immigration or work status for superintendent candidates.

The firm also said that it could not do so, since “only an employer can conduct an I-9 or E-Verify confirmation of an employee,” according to court documents.

However, the firm said, it flagged to the district that Roberts’ “resumes contained inconsistencies.”

“Ultimately, despite these red flags, the District unanimously selected Dr. Ian Roberts as the superintendent in May 2023 over a host of other potential candidates presented by JG Consulting,” the firm said in its lawsuit. “The District’s decision turned out to be ruinous.”

In a Nov. 9 court document, both parties told the court a settlement agreement had been reached. Details of the settlement have not been made public but may be filed with the court by Dec. 9.

The district told K-12 Dive in an email that the parties have reached a settlement on the material terms and are still finalizing the written agreement.

James Guerra, CEO of JG Consulting, told news outlet Iowa Public Radio that, "All parties have agreed that the criminal actions of Mr. Roberts are for him to own" rather than the fault of the firm, school district or another party.

"So all blame is being shifted to Mr. Roberts."

Roberts' arrest has been one of the most high-profile apprehensions in the K-12 sector resulting from the Trump administration's crackdown on immigration, which school leaders say has taken a toll on school communities, attendance, climate and newcomer student enrollment across the nation.

Roberts' employment put the Des Moines Independent Community School District’s hiring and vetting processes under the microscope at the state and district levels, with the Iowa State Department of Education launching an audit after a request by state Sen. Tony Bisignano from Des Moines. The state's Board of Educational Examiners also faced a state audit as part of the fallout.

The Iowa state auditor's office shared with K-12 Dive last year that the audit would include a review of some licensing and background check procedures.

In April, the state auditor’s office released its report of the re-audit, which found some "unsupported" and "questionable public purpose disbursement," including instances of the former superintendent using the district’s procurement card, such as payments to nonprofit organizations and donations. However, these expenses were reimbursed by Roberts prior to the end of his employment.

The Des Moines School Board unanimously voted in September to appoint Matt Smith, who took over as interim superintendent in 2025 following Roberts' arrest, as the district’s new leader. Smith also previously served as the district's interim superintendent in the 2022-23 school year, leading the school system through a major cybersecurity attack in January 2023.
