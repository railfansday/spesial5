United Airlines Suffers “Widespread System Outage,” Grounds Flights Nationwide 3 Days After ATC Meltdown

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

United Airlines, the world's largest airline by fleet size, experienced a system outage on Monday evening, grounding all departing mainline flights in its fleet. This did not affect services that were already airborne or those operated by United Express.

The system outage, which affected the ability for the airline to calculate the aircraft's weight and balance, forced planes to stay at the gate, delaying thousands of passengers throughout the airline's network. The system has since been resolved, and flights will now be able to depart, despite being delayed.

United confirmed that the outage was no indication of a cybersecurity issue. The system outage forced flights to remain grounded until the system was back up and running. Several reports by NBC Chicago and CBS News outlined that pilots had requested with air traffic control to remain at the gate until the system outage was resolved.

This outage comes just days after the US was rocked by the rare grounding of all departing flights to, from, and within the US due to discrepancies in radar data following an upgrade from copper to fiber optic cabling by a contractor. L3Harris was performing maintenance at a route site in El Dorado, Arkansas, which caused the malfunction and resulted in the longest nationwide ground-stop since the September 11 terrorist attacks.

Simple Flying reached out to United for comment on today's outage, and received the following statement from an airline spokesperson:

We have resolved a technology issue that delayed flight departures. While some flights were delayed, there was no impact on flights in progress, or on United Express flights. There is no indication that this was a cybersecurity-related issue.

Due to the outage, all departing flights for United were grounded until the issue was resolved. This did not affect flights that were already airborne, as calculations for these flights' weights and balance had already been ascertained. United Express, the carrier's regional partner, was not affected by the system going down, and all flights were able to run without disruption.

United, a Star Alliance partner, is a major airline in the US, and carries more than 175 million passengers a year across its global network. It operates to more than 350 destinations in all six inhabited continents. The airline operates the largest fleet of commercial aircraft in the world and is the third-largest operator in the US after American by passengers carried.

Following the weight and balance system going down, the airline was quick to post to its social media channel on X, warning passengers of the technology-related issue that affected its mainline services. Flights are now safe to depart, and across its network, services can now resume their departure formalities. It is expected that there could be some delays across the rest of the evening.

While there are still some delays and cancelations due to aircraft and crews being out of place, the figures are far better than over the weekend.

The outage lasted around an hour, with the problem being fully resolved by 6:00 PM EST. Having only affected departing flights, the airline has since taken to its socials to put travelers' minds at ease that operations can now resume. The airline apologised for the outage and any inconvenience caused:

"We sincerely apologize for the inconvenience. We're aware of the widespread system outage, and our teams are working hard to get things back up and running as soon as possible. We appreciate your patience while we work through this."

There is no correlation between the outage at major US banks and the outage at United this evening. This came as major banks, including Chase, Bank of America, US Bank and Wells Fargo, experienced issues with customers accessing their personal information.

United's system outage is also unrelated to the FAA ground stop that affected all US-bound flights on Friday.
