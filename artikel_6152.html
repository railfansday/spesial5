Thailand’s $118 billion goal targets spending rather than arrivals, which have stalled at about 33 million. Rising prices will surely lift revenue regardless, but higher rates aren’t the same as higher value, and the plan has no budget yet for the terminals, marinas and guides needed to attract bigger spenders.

Thailand is targeting at least 4 trillion baht ($118 billion) in annual tourism revenue by 2032 under a draft five-year national plan. That’s about 48% more than the 2.7 trillion baht it earned in 2025, including domestic tourism. The plan also aims to lift Thailand into the top 30 of the World Economic Forum's Travel & Tourism Development Index.

The targets come as Thailand's foreign arrivals are poised to fall for a second straight year.

The Fourth National Tourism Development Plan focuses on raising the economic value of tourism, lifting service standards, winning higher-spending visitors, and spreading income to small businesses and communities.

Thailand has set a target of at least 4 trillion baht ($118 billion) in annual tourism revenue by 2032 under a draft Fourth National Tourism Development Plan, about 48% above the 2.7 trillion baht earned in 2025. The target requires roughly 5.8% annual growth at a time when arrivals are heading for a second consecutive down year: foreign arrivals fell 7.2% to 32.9 million in 2025, were down 4.1% through October 17, and the government has cut its 2026 forecast to 33 million from 36.7 million. With volume stalled, the plan leans on spending per visitor through cruise and yacht tourism, higher-spending source markets, events, and dispersal beyond Bangkok and the beaches, plus entry-rule changes including a 450-baht ($13) tourist fee targeted for the first quarter of 2027, faster e-visa processing, and shorter immigration waits at Suvarnabhumi. The government named Taiwan, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, and Singapore as priority short-haul markets, leaving out China, still Thailand's largest source market this year but down to 4.4 million visitors in 2025 from 11 million in 2019. Analysts quoted said the revenue target is easier to hit than an arrivals target in an inflationary period, but the plan names no budget, no arrivals assumption, and no spending-per-visitor goal, and the cruise terminals and marinas it calls for have no funding or timeline.
