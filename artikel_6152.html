JetBlue Launches 16 New Nonstop Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

JetBlue has been growing rapidly at Fort Lauderdale, driven by its refocused priorities, the end of Spirit, and more gates and other space. Between December 2026 and March 2027, Cirium Diio shows that the airline has an average of 143 daily departures from the South Florida city. It has overtaken the 138 daily services from New York JFK to become the carrier's most-served market.

JetBlue plans a maximum of 157 daily departures from Fort Lauderdale, which will be available on ten dates between December 18 and January 3 for the Christmas traffic. Between the airline's debut there in February 2000 and October 2026, the previous high was 126.

From July 2026 onwards, and building on earlier launches, the carrier commenced the following domestic routes from Fort Lauderdale. Most took off in early July, and with usually hefty frequencies from the get-go. Its offering does not only target the large local market, but also connectivity to the Caribbean and Latin America. However, some did extraordinarily poorly, as shown in the final section of this article, with far too much capacity.

The most recent launch was on November 19, when the airline reintroduced transcontinental flights to San Diego. This became JetBlue's third route from Fort Lauderdale to California, joining Los Angeles and San Francisco. The return to the Southern Californian destination is notable, as Southwest introduced service two days later. Three airlines now operate for the first time, although Southwest's offering is limited.

The carrier has not previously served most of the following five routes, which is especially good to see. They contribute to JetBlue having an average of 32 daily departures beyond the US border between December and next March. The maximum offering will be 35 daily, down from the record of 39, held in 2019.

Between December and March, JetBlue will now have 38% of the international flights from the South Florida airport. The next-largest operator will be Air Canada, with 12%. When the US carrier's higher-capacity equipment is considered, it'll now have nearly one in two seats for sale (46%), with 21 destinations served.

Recall that eight domestic routes began or resumed on July 8 or July 9. It is worth seeing how they performed by load factor, and the usual caveats apply. For this, the US Department of Transportation data was used, but this currently only exists until August 2026. As such, the analysis is based on less than two months' information, which is, of course, a very obvious limitation.

This source shows that the eight markets only had an average load factor of 59.4%. Cleveland was just 34.4% (!), which was astonishingly low, even so quickly after commencement. Nashville was second (45.3%), followed by Ponce (51.1%), Houston Intercontinental (57.7%), Charlotte (59.7%), Detroit (63.1%), Baltimore (66.2%), and Charlotte O'Hare (79.2%). It'll be good to see how things change when more data is available.
