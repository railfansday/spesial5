PACE Organization InnovAge Agrees to Pay $36M to Resolve False Claims Act Allegations of Failing to Provide Comprehensive Medical Services and Timely Specialist Referrals to Seniors

InnovAge Holding Corporation (InnovAge), headquartered in Denver, Colorado, has agreed to pay a total of $36 million to resolve allegations that it violated the federal False Claims Act and state laws by failing to provide required comprehensive medical services and timely referrals to medical specialists as expeditiously as its patients’ health conditions required.

“Failing to provide comprehensive medical services and timely specialist referrals threatens the health of our most vulnerable seniors,” said Acting Assistant Attorney General Jonathan D. Guynn of the Justice Department’s Civil Division. “This historic, first-of-its-kind settlement demonstrates the Civil Division’s commitment to combating healthcare fraud by PACE organizations.”

“Elderly patients suffer when PACE providers like InnovAge do not fulfill their obligations to schedule patient visits with medical specialists in a timely manner,” said U.S. Attorney Peter McNeilly for the District of Colorado. “The U.S. Attorney’s Office in Colorado is committed to holding PACE providers accountable to ensure they provide comprehensive and timely care.”

“PACE organizations that receive Medicare and Medicaid funding must meet their obligations to provide timely and comprehensive care to the vulnerable seniors they serve as delays can jeopardize the health of patients,” said Acting Deputy Inspector General for Investigations Miranda L. Bennett of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Office of Inspector General (HHS-OIG). “This settlement demonstrates that organizations that fail to comply with those requirements will be held accountable. HHS-OIG remains committed to working with our law enforcement partners and leveraging the False Claims Act to help ensure providers comply with program requirements and deliver the care their patients need.”

InnovAge provides services to seniors under a dual Medicare-Medicaid program called the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE). InnovAge has PACE centers in several states, including California, Colorado, New Mexico, and the Commonwealth of Virginia. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) pays PACE organizations a fixed monthly amount for each Medicare beneficiary enrolled in their programs. In return, PACE organizations must provide comprehensive health care services to their participants, and all services must be provided as expeditiously as the participant’s health condition requires.

This settlement resolves allegations that, from May 2016 to October 2022, InnovAge failed to provide comprehensive health care services as expeditiously as required by its patients. Specifically, InnovAge allegedly failed to maintain sufficient specialist provider networks and, in thousands of instances, InnovAge failed to timely schedule appointments or consultations for specialist medical care, such as for oncology, cardiology, surgery, and neurology, for its patients.

The United States and various states further alleged that InnovAge’s internal reports of providers’ medical orders regularly showed numerous and recurrent delinquent specialist referrals. Several senior InnovAge physicians also complained to InnovAge about the company’s scheduling practices and delays for specialist referrals. These complaints included that InnovAge’s scheduling practices and delays had negative health care consequences for participants, placed legal risk on InnovAge’s healthcare providers, and put InnovAge out of compliance with CMS requirements. In addition, InnovAge’s internal audits found significant delays in specialist care, including numerous delayed orders, of which many were over a year outstanding.

As a result of this settlement, InnovAge will pay approximately $25.7 million to the United States and a combined $10.3 million to the States of Colorado, California, and New Mexico, and the Commonwealth of Virginia.

The civil settlement includes the resolution of federal and states’ claims brought under the qui tam or whistleblower provisions of the False Claims Act and analogous state statutes by two former employees, Monita Poudyal and Karen Lapcewich. Under those provisions, a private party can file an action on behalf of the United States and receive a portion of any recovery. The qui tam cases are captioned United States ex rel. Poudyal v. InnovAge Holding Corp., et al., No. 1:21-cv-02464 (D. Colo.) and United States ex rel. Lapcewich v. Total Community Options Inc., et al., No. 1:22-cv-01686 (D. Colo.). The relator share of the federal settlement will be $4.5 million.

This year the Administration launched the Task Force to Eliminate Fraud and the National Fraud Enforcement Division to enhance the Administration’s war on fraud, waste, and abuse in federal programs. When unscrupulous actors exploit these programs for their own financial gain, they defraud the government, harm the people these programs are designed to aid and protect, and undermine American businesses that play by the rules. The Civil Division’s FCA enforcement plays a critical role in combatting such fraudulent schemes, recovering billions of dollars for the American taxpayers, and holding wrongdoers accountable. FCA matters will continue to be on the forefront of the battle against fraud, and the Civil Division’s FCA work will support and advance the mission of the Task Force to Eliminate Fraud and the National Fraud Enforcement Division.

The resolution obtained in this matter was the result of a coordinated effort between the Justice Department’s Civil Division, Commercial Litigation Branch, Fraud Section, and the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Colorado, with assistance from the Department of Health and Human Services, Office of Inspector General.

The investigation and resolution of this matter illustrates the government’s emphasis on combating healthcare fraud. One of the most powerful tools in this effort is the False Claims Act. Tips and complaints from all sources about potential fraud, waste, abuse, and mismanagement, can be reported to the Department of Health and Human Services at 800-HHS-TIPS (800-447-8477).

Trial Attorneys Yifan Wang and David G. Miller of the Civil Division and Assistant U.S. Attorney Lila Bateman for the District of Colorado handled the matter.

The claims resolved by the settlement are allegations only and there has been no determination of liability.
