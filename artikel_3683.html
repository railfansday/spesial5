Viola Davis and More Condemn 'George Floyd the Musical' Amid Backlash

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Producers of the as-yet-unproduced cast recording of George Floyd the Musical are defending the project amid criticism from Viola Davis, Ahmir “Questlove” Thompson and numerous prominent Broadway stars.

“Busted Broadway seeks to push the limits of what is possible in musical theatre by producing provocative and satirical musical cast albums,” read a Thursday, November 19, statement from New Jersey-based independent music label Busted Broadway. “Our George Floyd the Musical is NOT making light of, condoning, or mocking the horrific 2020 murder of George Floyd. It’s a satire on the CULTURE, rather than the person. Never judge a show by its title, as many who have never seen the script or score appear to be doing now.”

The show drew a harsh rebuke via social media when activist and actress Malynda Hale brought to light that Broadway publication Playbill recently ran a job listing for a cast recording session.

“I’m usually not speechless but … yeah,” she said in a Wednesday, November 19, Instagram Reel that flashed the job listing on screen.

Busted Broadway actors sought “comedic singers and musical theater vocal talent” to record and improvise content for George Floyd the Musical. A disclaimer on the job advertisement warned that Busted Broadway’s projects are “too ridiculous, too raunchy and too absurd for the stage.”

“If you are uncomfortable performing offensive material, we ask that you do not submit for this project,” the company requested.

A statement on the Busted Broadway website claimed that the troupe was formed in 2025 to address “a necessity to reinvigorate the barren wasteland of mundane, mediocre musical theatre shows foisted upon audiences today.” Other projects promoted by the theater company include Human Centipede the Musical and Genghis Khan the Musical.

“In an age where entertainment feels increasingly safe, sterile, and insufferably self-serious, Busted Broadway exists to shake the snow globe and remind audiences that laughing at dark humor is not only allowed but necessary,” the site’s About Me page read.

A listing for George the Musical promotes “The $20 Bill that Changed the World” as its tagline, along with a cartoon rendering of three police officers standing over an unconscious Floyd.

Former Minneapolis police officer Derek Chauvin was convicted of unintentional second-degree murder, third-degree murder and second-degree manslaughter in April 2021 over Floyd’s May 2020 murder. Four police officers confronted an unarmed Floyd for allegedly using a counterfeit $20 bill at a local grocery, before Chauvin physically restrained Floyd and knelt on his neck for nearly 10 minutes, causing Floyd to asphyxiate.

In the wake of George the Musical‘s job listing going viral, many notable figures have condemned the project.

“I mean……WHAAT??!!! I would’ve lived to have been in the room when this one was pitched,” Davis, 61, responded to Hyde’s Instagram video.

Questlove, 55, also expressed disbelief over the project, replying, “OMG!!!!!!!! it took 17 seconds to register. I saw the headline for 4 secs and was like ‘wait they made a broadway play on how he developed those sandwich machin————OH GOD WAIT READ THAT AGAIN!!!!!!!!!!????????'”

Playbill has publicly apologized for running the listing, insisting that it slipped through an internal procedure designed to weed out offensive advertisements.

“Late yesterday afternoon, Playbill Jobs incorrectly approved a job listing that had no place on our site,” a statement read. “Once leadership was alerted, the listing was immediately removed, and we began reviewing and revising our approval processes. We apologize unequivocally for giving this hateful listing any further air, and we promise to do better going forward. We at Playbill aim to lift up this community with empathy, consideration, and respect. We hope those who read and follow us do the same.”

Playbill acknowledged the offense caused by its apparent unintentional promotion of George Floyd the Musical “has disappointed those who trust us as an outlet.”

“For that, we are sorry, and are committed to regaining your trust,” the statement concluded.

The ad has been removed from Playbill’s site.
