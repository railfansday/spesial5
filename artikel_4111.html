Gallery: I Spent £40 On Japanese Chocolate And All I Got Was This Zelda Merch

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Japan is living in the future - quite literally.

As I wander the neon-soaked streets of Akihabara this evening, it strikes me that I'm nine hours closer to The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time's release than any of you poor sods back in Blighty. In fact, by the time you read this, I'll probably already be playing Link's latest. Sorry not sorry.

I honestly hadn't planned to be in Japan for the Ocarina launch - it sort of just happened that way. I'm glad, though; I've always dreamed of being in Tokyo's Electric Town for a moment like this.

Like, imagine being here when the clock struck midnight for the PS2's launch? I would have been sooooo happy!!! OR I would have failed to appreciate the present moment for what it was and only realised how special it was years later through a lens of nostalgia and with a tinge of sadness, while hopelessly fantasising about another time and/or a different life? Anyone else??? No, okay, sweet.

ANYWAY, while the advent of internet shopping seems to have robbed us of the very concept of the midnight launch (I can't find one for Ocarina anywhere in Tokyo), there's still an excitement in the air befitting a release of this magnitude.

Zelda is everywhere. From neighbourhood game stores to subway screens and bus stops, it's genuinely hard to miss.

Like when the trailer plays out to a captive audience on the heaving Yamanote Line. Or when audio from the Zelda Direct gets pumped into the streets outside electrical giant EDION. Or when I stumble across the biggest gaming section I've ever seen in my life (in a place called Yodobashi Akiba) and Ocarina has about five sections dedicated to it.

It's wild stuff. And it's not just the enthusiast stores getting involved, either; there's Zelda stuff in every Lawson I enter.

For the uninitiated, Lawson is a konbini (convenience store) that sells the best egg sandwich you'll ever have in your life. And some other things, I'm told. But mainly the egg sandwich.

With over 1,600 stores in Tokyo alone, it’s part of everyday life for pretty much every single city-dwelling Japanese person. So when Nintendo suggested taking over one of Lawson's Akihabara stores and plastering it with Ocarina of Time art and 40th Anniversary merch, a money expert man probably said, “Yes, that is a very good idea for making lots of money.”

Just like it was a good idea for Japanese chocolatier Lotte to partner up with Ninty on a promo campaign that's made this writer's wallet £40 lighter and waistline two inches thicker overnight.

It goes thusly: for a limited time, anyone who buys three eligible Lotte products can receive one of eight clear sleeves featuring artwork from the series’ most beloved entries.

Now, with there being eight different designs, that would mean purchasing 24 boxes of chocolate, which only a complete idiot would do hi-I'm-Rory-nice-to-meet-ya.

Honestly though, these slender sleeves prove the perfect souvenir; my bag is already bursting with merch from Nintendo Kyoto, plushes from three different Pokémon Centres, and a mint-condition Lime Green DSi I picked up for £38. God bless the struggling yen.

But as I head to Narita Airport (I leave tomorrow and I don't want to talk about it) and see the reviews come rolling in, I can't help but feel Nintendo has another mega hit on their hands with this game.
