Is There a Right Way to Respond to a Grammy Snub?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Artists like John Summit and MUNA have reacted to being shut out by the Recording Academy. But is there a correct way to approach it?

Not to go all “question for the culture” on everyone, but this year’s Grammy nominations have been on my mind for too long for me not to ask: Is there really a “right” way to acknowledge being snubbed by the Academy?

While the Recording Academy granted cultural aficionados the pleasant surprise of recognizing longtime underdogs like Raye, Remi Wolf, Tinashe, and Underscores, some names were noticeably absent from this year’s nominations list—and they had something to say about it.

For starters, EDM giant John Summit made headlines for his unfiltered reaction to being left out of the Dance and Electronic categories.

“never submitting to the grammys again unless shit changes, it’s all a fucking joke,” he wrote, criticizing the Academy for only recognizing “music industry fueled pop stars.”

After receiving backlash from online bystanders—including Charli xcx, who he previously requested to hop on a track in 2024—he walked back his claims, writing, “i realized i didn’t do a good job of making my point earlier. my intent was not to drag other artists down and i seriously congratulate everyone who was nominated. they fully deserve the recognition. i just think there should be an extra category for more dance music djs & producers.”

John isn’t wrong to point out the Academy’s standards in certain categories, but his delivery was a bit aggressive. Especially after he seemingly diminished fans’ opinions and deemed them “lil teeny bopper pop stan accounts with zero real world life experience.” It’s…a choice to say that about the people who move culture forward.

Naturally, his reaction got me thinking about the unspoken etiquette of addressing a snub. It’s valid to be upset over not being recognized by your peers, as well as seasoned vets in the biz. But you don’t want to ruin your chances for redemption, either.

Take Noah Kahan, for example. He reassured his fans that he’s “done everything I’ve ever needed to do in my career because of YOUR support” ahead of the nominations, only to be shut out in the core categories: Record of the Year, Song of the Year, and Album of the Year. Though his album The Great Divide still earned a nod in the Best Contemporary Folk Album category, he acknowledged his snub with three words: “Life goes on.” Simple, yet effective!

Another artist who didn’t land a nod was pop princess and Cosmo cover girl Kim Petras, who made history as the first openly transgender woman to win a Grammy back in 2023. Her career took a tumultuous turn when she signed a major-label deal after riding the success of her award-winning track with Sam Smith, “Unholy.”

Years into her deal, she publicly accused her label of gatekeeping funds and resources, and pleaded to be released from her contract. She ultimately returned to her independent roots for her latest record, Detour, funding most of the studio sessions, visuals, and live performances herself. When the album didn’t earn a nod, fans claimed she was “snubbed,” since her hard work deserved recognition.

“I wouldn’t say so … I just left the industry tbh and I made the music to make the music cause I love it so much,” she wrote in response, claiming that her “nomination days [are] over” as she roots for Tinashe and Underscores.

The self-proclaimed “Greatest Band in the World,” MUNA, also started gaining momentum since trading a larger label for Phoebe Bridgers’ independent Saddest Factory Records in 2022. Their latest album, Dancing on the Wall, drew massive crowds at festivals like Lollapalooza and All Things Go last summer. The Academy has yet to acknowledge them, despite continued success.

The band’s lead singer, Katie Gavin, released a short video addressing fans after nominations dropped, saying, “I wasn’t expecting it, and I understand it. And it’s tough for a band like us because we make pop music, but we’re not as big as other pop artists.”

She added that they attempted to submit for alternative categories, but they ultimately didn’t make the cut because of their inherent pop sound. In the end, she kept things candid.

To put it plainly, there isn’t a definitive right or wrong way to go about being slept on. If artists decide to publicly address their snubs in the future, they should grab their pens and papers and take notes from Katie’s final sentiment.

“I’m really proud of all the people that did get nominated. There was so much amazing music this year,” she shared. “I hate being disappointed, but I’m not giving up hope.”
