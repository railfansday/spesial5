Hugh Jackman’s R-Rated Fantasy Reboot Officially Hits a Bullseye on Streaming

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Almost a decade ago, Hugh Jackman starred in Logan to bid farewell to the character who launched him to global stardom. The feature had a lot of emotions riding on it; it was supposed to be Jackman’s swan song as an aging Wolverine, who can’t heal as he did in his prime. The story saw him taking a young Laura (Dafne Keen) under his wing (or claw) to take her across the border to a safer place for young mutants. His gut-wrenching performance broke millions of hearts worldwide who grew up loving Wolverine, and James Mangold’s direction gave the movie a gritty, raw aesthetic that won’t be seen in a superhero movie anytime soon. Jackman did end up returning as the character in Deadpool & Wolverine, but the gritty Logan still showcased his ability to carry a dark, R-rated take on a typically more family-friendly character.

Jackman’s talents know no bounds; he can effortlessly shapeshift into any character, whether it the iconic Wolverine or PT Barnum in the period musical The Greatest Showman. This year, his performance in The Sheep Detectives became the emotional core of the feature, proving his presence can uplift any movie. However, one of his roles took him back to a Logan-esque character but failed to bring audiences to theatres. The movie in question is The Death of Robin Hood. Michael Sarnoski wrote and directed the adaptation of the 17th-century ballad Robin Hood's Death, brilliantly deconstructing the cheerful, romanticized folklore to deliver a brutal, plaintive Robin Hood. Jackman brought an earnest sincerity to the role of a tortured soul who wants to end the cycle of violence he ignited.

The slow burner examines the themes of the cycle of revenge, morality, and redemption. Set in 1247 A.D., we follow an aged, grizzled Robin Hood (Jackman), who lives in self-imposed exile. A lifetime of theft, murder, and endless conflict has left him broken. Unlike the heroic champion of the poor, he is now a hardened, brutal thief whose violent past has spawned countless vengeful enemies. When his old companion Little John (Bill Skarsgård) tracks him down to help settle a personal score, the ensuing battle goes horribly wrong. Gravely wounded, Robin wakes up in a remote, tranquil island where he is cared for by Sister Brigid (Jodie Comer). When his past inevitably catches up with him, Robin finally confronts the cycle of violence he helped create.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

As it happened with many films this year, fans were more eager to watch them at home than in theaters, becoming a PVOD or streaming hit. The Death of Robin Hood is spelling its success in a new way by spending over 120 days on Apple TV Store and over 80 days on Prime Video. Its continued hold on the Top 10 charts is a testament to the maker’s vision and fans’ love for Jackman’s performance.
