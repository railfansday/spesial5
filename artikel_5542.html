Canada's ambassador warned Trump's tariffs are raising the cost of investment

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Mark Wiseman called for removal of Section 232 and 338 duties as U.S.-Canada talks resume after collapsing in August

Mark Wiseman, Canada's ambassador to the U.S., cautioned on Wednesday that the tariffs President Donald Trump has imposed make investing more expensive and give companies reason to hold back on growth, according to CNBC.

"Uncertainty increases your cost of capital," Wiseman told attendees of the B20 summit, an event the U.S. Chamber of Commerce organized in Washington for global business leaders, according to CNBC. "If you're going to build a new plant, add a new ship, spend money on R&D, the more certainty you have, the more likely you're going to do that."

Wiseman, a former BlackRock $BLK executive, became ambassador in February. He urged that the duties levied under Sections 232 and 338 of American trade law be lifted, arguing that settling the dispute would let businesses plan their investments over the long haul. "The sooner, the better," he said, according to CNBC. He said Canada wants to get back to "as close as we can to free trade."

Washington and Ottawa returned to the bargaining table after discussions broke down in August, according to CNBC. The talks center on lowering duties on steel and cars from Canada. Trump has levied duties as high as 50% on some Canadian products, and Canada has hit back with countermeasures aimed at U.S. exports worth billions of dollars. Trump has justified the tariffs as a way to shield American manufacturers and workers, and he has charged Canada with discriminatory trade practices.

According to CNBC, Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday that Canada still treats the Trump administration as its main counterpart in trade talks.

U.S. exports to Canada totaled $265.9 billion in the first nine months of 2026, and imports from Canada totaled $304.6 billion, according to Census Bureau data. That left a goods trade deficit of $38.7 billion.

Trump signed a proclamation on Oct. 23 changing the list of Canadian products covered by Section 338 tariffs, effective Oct. 30. The 50% duties apply whether or not a good originates under the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement and stack on top of Section 232 tariffs. Gellert Global Group, a U.S. food importer, has filed a lawsuit in the U.S. Court of International Trade arguing the administration exceeded its authority under Section 338.

New York Fed economists found in a study of tariff pass-through into consumer prices that nearly 90% of a tariff is reflected in higher import prices. About 26% of any given tariff increase reaches consumer goods prices.

Germany's ambassador to the U.S., Jens Hanefeld, said at the same summit that tariffs were something firms could adjust to, but that an "ever-changing regulatory environment" proved harder to handle, since it pushed companies to devote more money to legal help in making sense of evolving trade rules, according to CNBC.
