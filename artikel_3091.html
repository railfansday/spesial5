Grupo Aval: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Persistent inflation and high local rates will test whether Grupo Aval's lending gains hold, a tension management openly acknowledges.

On its third quarter of 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 11, 2026, Grupo Aval (AVAL -6.18%) announced attributable net income of 368 billion Colombian pesos, down 29% year over year as lower investment returns weighed on overall earnings. The bank's core lending business and asset management divisions expanded their market footprint during the period. Three key takeaways from management's presentation clarify the company's long-term trajectory.

The acquisition of Itau's Colombian retail assets immediately expanded the group's lending footprint, generating more than two-thirds of total loan growth for the quarter. The deal expanded the bank's net interest margin on loans, which measures loan profitability after funding costs, demonstrating how added scale in consumer lending directly enhances core earnings power.

Following the merger of four trust subsidiaries into Aval Fiduciaria on Jan. 1, 2026, the unit delivered historical efficiency gains. Operating expenses plummeted relative to revenue, driving the asset management division's return on equity -- a key measure of how effectively management generates profits from shareholder capital -- to 42.3%.

A major legal risk cleared on Oct. 26, 2026, when a court dismissed the case against infrastructure subsidiary Corficolombiana regarding the Ruta del Sol 2 project. Corficolombiana satisfied all requirements under its deferred prosecution agreement -- an arrangement where legal charges are dropped after a company meets specific compliance conditions -- removing a persistent structural drag on the group's valuation.

Persistent inflation and high local interest rates present near-term macroeconomic headwinds across Colombia. Management is relying on disciplined loan repricing and ongoing expense rationalization to navigate this slower environment while preserving core lending returns.

Looking further out, the group expects broader economic stabilization and lower borrowing costs to reactivate commercial lending across energy and infrastructure projects.
