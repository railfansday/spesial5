All 92 English Clubs Ranked By Average Attendance For the 2026/27 Season

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

All 92 English football clubs, from League Two to the Championship and Premier League, have been ranked by their average attendances for the 2026/27 season so far.

The results make for a fascinating read. While some of England's biggest clubs, including Manchester United, Liverpool and Arsenal, unsurprisingly find themselves near the top, there are certainly a few curveballs along the way.

Some clubs boasting the biggest stadiums haven't necessarily managed to pack out their impressive arenas, while a number of typically 'smaller' sides (for want of a better word) have punched above their weight and ranked higher than expected, thanks in part to their flying starts to the campaign.

Ranking at the very bottom, with an average attendance of just 1,853, are Accrington Stanley. The club's main claim to fame remains their 1989 milk advert, in which a child famously asks, “Accrington Stanley, who are they?” They rank as one of the smallest clubs in the country, but have to be given some sort of let-off for their close proximity to Manchester and Liverpool.

Barnet, Fleetwood Town - who recently knocked Arsenal out of the League Cup - and Salford City, rank just above them, with the latter being owned by some of Man United's Class of 92 but still struggling to find their own identity. This comes despite sitting top of League Two and among the favourites for another promotion.

Other notable clubs who feature in this section are Grimsby Town in 72nd, who own one of the ugliest stadiums in British football, while Swindon Town - who found themselves in the topflight in the 1993/94 season - rank two places ahead, with the Robins managing just 7,608 fans at their games this season.

English football's top-flight is one of the most closely-followed leagues in the world.

Oldham Athletic, York City and Blackpool are among several clubs in this section that have struggled to stay afloat in recent years, with all three coming perilously close to extinction because of financial troubles. Part of the problem may stem from the fact that none regularly attract more than 10,000 fans to home matches, although the wider state of the English game is a far bigger factor.

Despite enjoying three successive promotions over the last three seasons as they set their sights on reaching the Premier League sooner rather than later, Wrexham still have around 200 empty seats per match at present. They rank 54th on this list, behind the likes of Barnsley, Lincoln City and MK Dons.

Bournemouth's rise to prominence followed a similar path to Wrexham's, but they have yet to move away from their old, much smaller stadium. As a result, they boast the lowest average attendance of any Premier League side, ranking 51st overall, just one place above Luton Town, who have endured successive relegations in the opposite direction.

Brentford, who have to contend with the fact that several of their London neighbours boast much bigger fanbases, sit 42nd, behind Watford, Swansea City and Huddersfield Town.

Millwall, meanwhile, are renowned as one of the toughest clubs in English football and, alongside south London rivals Charlton Athletic, find themselves averaging just under 20,000 fans at home matches. It's a similar story for two-time FA Cup winners Portsmouth. Another side with a reputation for making plenty of noise are Stoke City, who average 22,288 in the Championship. That figure just about keeps the age-old question of 'can he do it on a cold, wet Tuesday night in Stoke?' alive and kicking.

Other top-flight sides in this section include Hull City in 29th, Crystal Palace in 27th and Fulham in 22nd. None of them quite manage to attract more supporters than Leicester City, however, despite the Foxes sitting 10th in the division below.

The three biggest clubs missing out on a place in the top 10 are Nottingham Forest (16th), Leeds United (13th), and Aston Villa (11th). But there is quite a jump between them and Chelsea, who just about make it in 10th with an average attendance at Stamford Bridge of 39,507.

Arsenal, top of the league as they look to retain their first Premier League title in 22 years, are surprisingly only sixth, behind both Manchester City and Liverpool. This means they also rank third in London, with Tottenham in third and West Ham in second.

But there's no surprise that Manchester United's Old Trafford, the biggest club stadium in the UK, ranks top. Fanbanter's findings suggest their attendance has increased from last term, despite the Red Devils having recently sacked Michael Carrick for Mauricio Pochettino as they sit 18th and in the relegation zone.
