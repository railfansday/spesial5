Man United's appointment of Mauricio Pochettino feels like the Glazers are back calling the shots at Old Trafford - with Jim Ratcliffe's henchmen Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox looking like irrelevant also-rans, writes IAN HERBERT

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Published: 12:00 EST, 12 November 2026 | Updated: 15:14 EST, 12 November 2026

Manchester United chief executive Omar Berrada once cited a line from the John Lennon song Beautiful Boy, when relating how an intended 12-month attachment at Barcelona had ended up with him staying there for 13 years. ‘Life is what happens to you while you’re busy making other plans.’

Lennon’s lyric seems far more appropriate to what Mauricio Pochettino’s appointment tells us about Berrada and his dubious grip on United, today.

While Berrada and his director of football Jason Wilcox have been busy making plans for United’s world domination – ‘to create the new history,’ as he described it to the journalist and broadcaster Andy Mitten just five months ago – it seems clear a Glazer family petrified by the shocking descent of their Premier League asset have stepped in to get Pochettino installed.

If it had been left to Berrada and Wilcox, we may have been treated to the sight of Eddie Howe wandering into the Old Trafford madhouse in the next few days. It’s been widely known and reported that Howe was Wilcox’s preference, while Christopher Vivell, director of recruitment, wanted Julian Nagelsmann.

Sources have told us that the Glazers wanted Pochettino and ordered Sir Jim Ratcliffe and his men to fall in line with that. They point to a connection between the Glazers, Pochettino and US billionaire hedge fund owner Ken Griffin.

Griffin was the main financial contributor to the salary which saw Pochettino hired by US Soccer in 2024 and again to that contract extension he signed in August. It was also Griffin, the then youthful founder of Citadel, who provided substantial funds for the Glazers’ takeover of United in 2005. Manchester United deny this, insisting that Pochettino is not a Glazer appointee.

Should Berrada and Wilcox both survive the latest embarrassment – and it would be a travesty if they do, as United pay US Soccer £7.5million in compensation for Pochettino, who would have been out of contract if they’d moved for him in August – then both will be a virtual irrelevance now. Also-rans, bypassed by the latest executive dice roll for United.

Perhaps the TV cameras will pan to them celebrating in the executive seats during some United upturn under Pochettino, weeks or months from now. If so, let it be remembered that it took the influence of others to bend things United’s way.

If Jason Wilcox (left) and Omar Berrada (right) had got their way, we would seemingly have been treated to the sight of Eddie Howe wandering into the Old Trafford madhouse

Instead the Glazers insisted that Mauricio Pochettino (pictured) would have the keys and Sir Jim Ratcliffe and his men fell in line with that

Let us not pretend there’s been any visionary executive oversight from the Glazers. They’ve already rejected Pochettino twice, in favour of Ole Gunnar Solskjaer in 2018 and Erik ten Hag four years later.

But sources close to Joel and Avram Glazer, the members of the dynasty who take some interest in their British sports asset, have indicated the brothers have been pushing to get him installed for some weeks.

The Glazers’ connection with US billionaire hedge fund owner Ken Griffin seems significant. Griffin was the main financial contributor to the salary which saw Pochettino hired by US Soccer in 2024 and again to that contract extension he signed in August.

It was also Griffin, the then youthful founder of Citadel, who provided substantial funds for the Glazers’ takeover of United in 2005.

It seems that the Old Trafford hierarchy, like so much about the club, will be skewed from now on. Pochettino does not answer to Berrada and Wilcox in the same way that Ten Hag, Ruben Amorim and Michael Carrick have.

He transcends the structure imposed by Ratcliffe, the so-called controller of football operations, and becomes the most powerful man at Old Trafford. The individual on whose vision the futures of Berrada and Wilcox now rest. Should they stay, this is the last chance saloon for them.

United's slump in form this season has been so severe, the club have been forced to dispense with Michael Carrick just six months after giving him a two-year deal

Sources close to Avram (left) and Joel Glazer, the members of the dynasty who take some interest in United, indicate they have been pushing for Pochettino for some weeks

There should be optimism. Pochettino, more than Howe, Julian Nagelsmann, Unai Emery, Antonio Conte and all other available candidates, has the faculty, charisma and fundamental toughness to instil some mettle into United and drag the club from what Ratcliffe and Co have reduced it to. To instil the identity and culture missing for so long.

Time will only tell if he can actually make a go of the most complicated and challenging job in world football, which Ratcliffe has rendered so much harder, by stripping away so many good, essential people from the Old Trafford payroll. That really is a monumental ‘If’.

One United insider spoke for many today when observing: ‘I haven’t got a clue who can make a go of it with the people above them.’

Pochettino will find United no longer command the same spending power, for transfers and wages, as other top clubs, though their name still carries a cachet, if only they can be nimbler and smarter.

He will discover that one of the most godforsaken parts of the operation is the interminable decision-making process: multiple individuals sitting around the table whose opinions must be sought in rule by committee. Some of those who have been shown the door by Ratcliffe consider themselves well out of all that.

The individual United most need now is actually David Gill, the stellar chief executive who left in the same summer as Sir Alex Ferguson, 13 years ago.

Gill would bring a command and intellect way beyond the remotest comprehension of Berrada. He probably wouldn’t want the association with Ineos but nevertheless, United do need to find a proven, world-class chief executive officer. And a sporting director befitting the biggest club in the world.

In that interview with Mitten back in June, Berrada seemed very pleased with himself – sitting on a large sofa in a United meeting room with immaculately bright white adidas trainers and declaring that United had travelled a long way in 12 months.

‘I’m naturally optimistic by nature, although my wife says I should smile more,’ he said. ‘We’ve made some very difficult decisions, but they have put us where we are today.’

It was his 'aspiration’ to ‘make United great again’, he declared. He is a footnote to another unhappy chapter in the modern history of Manchester United, now. The monumental task lies at Pochettino’s door.
