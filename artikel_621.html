Amazon's early Black Friday sale just dropped the Sony PS-LX3BT wireless Bluetooth turntable to $238, down from its $398 list price. That saves you $160 and marks the lowest price ever for a record player Sony only released this spring at $399.99.

Setup is straightforward right out of the box: pop on the silver aluminum die-cast platter, hook the rubber belt around the motor pulley, drop on the slip mat, and snap on the clear plastic dust cover. Designed for one-step automatic playback, you just press the Start button to spin 12-inch albums or 7-inch singles at 33 1/3 or 45 rpm, and the aluminum tonearm automatically drops down and returns to its cradle when the side ends. Even longtime manual-turntable fans are finding plenty to love about how effortless it is to use. In Mashable's hands-on review, Haley Henschel called the PS-LX3BT a "stylish turntable with idiotproof plug-and-play functionality", adding that she was "genuinely bummed to ship it back" once her testing wrapped up. Coming from a manual wired record player, she admitted, "I've been converted to the dark (automatic) side," praising how seamless the one-button playback and quick Bluetooth pairing felt for anyone new to vinyl.

Beyond its simple controls, the turntable offers plenty of perks and listening options. You can stream records wirelessly to Bluetooth speakers, soundbars, or headphones with Qualcomm aptX Adaptive support, or plug straight into wired speakers using the built-in switchable phono EQ and attached audio cable. It also features a rear USB-B port for ripping vinyl tracks to a laptop (cable sold separately), plus an aluminum tonearm and a moving-magnet cartridge that keep groove tracking smooth.

As Henschel summed it up, the automatic controls and wireless streaming are "boons for newbies" looking for a reliable, fuss-free setup without messing with counterweights or external preamps. She also noted that the full retail price feels a little steep for what you get and recommended buying it on sale, so this $238 early Black Friday drop is right on cue.
