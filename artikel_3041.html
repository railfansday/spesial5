If Inflation Stays Hot, These 5 ETFs Could Be Smart Buys

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

No ETF is a guaranteed hedge against inflation, but some are built to be more effective.

By Dana George – Nov 23, 2026 at 3:45PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Schwab Strategic Trust - Schwab U.s. Tips ETF ›

If you fill your gas tank or buy groceries, you undoubtedly understand how inflation erodes the purchasing power of cash. And if you invest, you may be on the lookout for assets that can help cushion your portfolio.

While no exchange-traded fund (ETF) is a guaranteed inflation shield, some do a better job than others at protecting your portfolio. Here are five of them.

SCHP invests in Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS). The principal adjusts with inflation, helping preserve purchasing power, and pays interest on that adjusted principal.

TIPS can help preserve purchasing power over time. However, a TIPS value may fluctuate if market prices fall.

VTIP is a shorter-duration alternative. During inflationary times, VTIP may be less sensitive to interest rate changes than funds holding longer-term bonds because it focuses on shorter-maturity TIPS.

If you seek inflation-linked bonds with less duration risk, VTIP is worth considering. Keep in mind, though, the fund still carries risk, and returns depend on both inflation adjustments and changes in bond prices.

Switching to yet another kind of exposure, PDBC invests in commodity futures across markets, such as agriculture, metals, and energy. Commodities are closely tied to costs that often drive inflation, so they may perform well when raw material prices rise.

However, commodity prices can swing sharply, and futures-based funds naturally face complexities that you may not be accustomed to.

Gold is viewed as a way to preserve wealth over time. Unlike cash, it can't be created at will, and gold may rise in value as cash loses purchasing power. Gold can also help balance a portfolio loaded with stocks and bonds.

On the downside, gold is not directly tied to the CPI and can fall even while inflation remains elevated. In addition, its performance may lag when interest-bearing assets become more attractive.

Still, investing in GLDM is one way to diversify risk.

XLE holds energy sector companies, so if oil and gas prices rise, the fund's value may rise, making it a hedge against inflation.Yet energy stocks are still stocks, and regulation, company costs, demand, and even market sentiment can outweigh the effect of higher fuel prices.

While each of these funds can be a great addition to your portfolio as inflation heats up, they're not interchangeable. SCHP and VTIP focus on inflation-linked government bonds, while PDBC tracks a collection of commodity futures. GLDM provides gold exposure, while XLE invests in energy companies.

Each ETF responds to inflation through a different mechanism, and while each can help, they can all disappoint if the market moves the other way.

The takeaway is to treat inflation-focused ETFs as possible portfolio pieces rather than all-in solutions. Before investing, consider fees, diversification, tax treatment, and how much volatility you can tolerate.
