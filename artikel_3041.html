Dutch Bros vs. Celsius: Which Stock Is the Better Buy as the Market Keeps Falling?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Both companies are growth stocks, but one stands out more than the other.

By Jack Delaney – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 5:16PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Dutch Bros ›

Long-term investors view broad market sell-offs as offering discounts on growth stocks. That's because, over the long run, stock prices tend to go up. So even amid some general uncertainty now, there could be good deals. Two such stocks we'll look at today are Dutch Bros (BROS -4.01%) and Celsius (CELH -7.36%).

Dutch Bros operates a rapidly expanding network of drive-thru coffee shops, while Celsius markets functional energy drinks through global retail channels. Both companies are disruptors in the competitive beverage stocks landscape. This comparison looks at their growth trajectories and financial health to help you decide which stock fits your strategy.

Dutch Bros operates a high-growth model focusing on drive-thru shops that serve a variety of hand-crafted beverages. The menu includes coffee, tea, lemonade, and the popular Dutch Bros Rebel energy drink. As of June 2026, the company managed over 1,200 locations in nearly 30 states. Customer concentration is a factor to watch, as the company's brand recognition is somewhat limited.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $2 billion. This represented a revenue growth rate of roughly 30.7% compared to the prior year period. The company reported a net income of approximately $102 million during this same timeframe. This growth is driven by both new shop openings and steady traffic at existing locations.

Celsius creates and markets functional beverages that have gained significant popularity in the U.S. and international markets. Its portfolio includes brands like CELSIUS and Alani Nu, sold through convenience stores, fitness centers, and large retailers. The company is substantially reliant on PepsiCo (PEP +1.11%) as its primary distributor in the U.S. and Canada. This single customer accounted for over 43% of revenue in 2025, and such high customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue for the company reached roughly $3.2 billion. This reflects significant momentum with revenue growth of approximately 49.1% over the preceding year. During this timeframe, the company generated net income of nearly $230 million.

Dutch Bros faces intense competition from established coffee chains and other quick-service restaurants. Its high geographic concentration in the Western U.S. remains a concern, as most of its shops are located in that region. Rapid expansion also carries risks of identifying suitable real estate and maintaining its unique corporate culture. Regulatory changes regarding labor laws or potential drive-thru bans in specific municipalities could impact its operational model.

Celsius is heavily dependent on its distribution agreement with Pepsi, which provides the backbone of its retail reach. This relationship creates potential conflicts of interest since Pepsi holds a significant ownership stake and board representation. The company also faces fierce competition from global players such as Monster Beverage (MNST -0.57%) and Red Bull. Additionally, integrating recent acquisitions like Alani Nu and Rockstar presents ongoing execution risks for the management team.

Celsius appears more attractively valued than Dutch Bros, as it offers a lower earnings multiple despite maintaining a significantly faster revenue growth rate.

For investors taking the long view, broad market dips offer buying opportunities. After all, Dutch Bros opened at $35.50 on Nov. 16 and closed at $32.78 on Nov. 20. Celsius also saw its stock price dip, opening at $32.47 on Nov. 16 and closing at $28.46 by Nov. 20.

Choosing between the two growth stocks for a portfolio, however, I would rather own Dutch Bros.

One of the key reasons is that, as a growth stock, Celsius appears to be increasingly relying on acquisitions. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue for its CELSIUS brand fell by approximately 6%, and U.S. retail sales fell by 7%. It saw strong growth through its acquired Alani Nu brand, with revenue climbing 24% and retail sales growing 57.9%.

In comparison, in its third quarter, Dutch Bros grew revenue by nearly 30%, same-shop sales increased by 5.4%, and customer traffic grew by 1%. Granted, it's not an apples-to-apples comparison, given the differences in how the businesses operate. Still, it shows that the growth opportunities for Dutch Bros come through its core offerings, as its stores are attracting more customers, selling more, and have future sales growth opportunities through new store openings.

While Celsius is buying growth, Dutch Bros is creating it. That makes Dutch Bros the stock I would want to buy for the long term, using sell-offs to pick up shares at a discount to where they could be trading in the future.
