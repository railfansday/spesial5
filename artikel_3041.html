History Says the Nasdaq Gained an Average of 17% in the 6 Months After a Correction. Here’s the Simplest Way to Invest.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Times of market stress are usually good opportunities to put capital to work.

The Nasdaq Composite Index (^IXIC -0.91%) officially entered correction territory on Nov. 5, when it dropped more than 5% just that day. This move marked an 11% fall from the previous all-time high on Oct. 6. In focus is the uncertainty surrounding what changes a Democrat-led U.S. Congress will make to the artificial intelligence (AI) industry.

As of the morning of Nov. 12, the Nasdaq has recovered somewhat. It's up 5% in the past week.

Investors might initially react with a sense of panic. They might think it's a good idea to wait on the sidelines until the situation stabilizes. If you choose to adopt this line of thinking, it seems the cautious approach is to be patient and sit in cash.

History says this would be a huge mistake, however. During the 10 previous occurrences, the Nasdaq produced an average return of 17% in the six months after the start of the correction, with 2022 being the only time there was a negative return. That kind of track record is hard to dismiss.

Here's the simplest way you can invest in the Nasdaq to capture what could be a sizable gain over the coming half year.

Investors can decide to buy shares of the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ -1.32%) to gain exposure to the Nasdaq Composite index. There are thousands of stocks in the overall benchmark. The QQQ is an exchange-traded fund (ETF) that contains only the 100 largest non-financial stocks that trade on the Nasdaq exchange. At a high level, it can be viewed as a more concentrated approach.

Its expense ratio of 0.18% is compelling. If you invest $10,000 in this ETF, the cost amounts to $18. Compared to expensive active fund managers, this is a steal.

This fee structure is impressive when you look at the performance. The Invesco QQQ Trust generated a total return of 539% in the past decade (as of Nov. 12). That works out to an annualized return of 20%.

The top 10 stocks make up a notable 46.3% of the entire portfolio, and there is strong exposure to leading technology businesses in the QQQ. At 8.4%, for example, Apple (AAPL +0.09%) is the biggest holding. Go down the list, and you'll quickly see how prominently featured leading AI enterprises are. By owning this ETF, you're placing a bet on the belief that these companies will benefit financially from this emerging technology.

Investors who are aiming to play offense right now, during what appears to be a period of elevated uncertainty, deserve some credit. Past trends prove that corrections present excellent buying opportunities. You just have to be greedy when others are fearful, as Warren Buffett once said.

The Invesco QQQ Trust provides tremendous growth potential, as noted. This is a high-potential ETF that can add upside to any portfolio. Of course, excess volatility from owning technology stocks can also be hard to stomach. That's the trade-off you must navigate.

It's all easier said than done. The data is clear on what returns you can expect. However, dealing with your emotions is a completely different challenge. Maintaining a long-term perspective is the best way to manage any trepidation you might have right now.

The post-correction six-month track record is extremely encouraging. Who doesn't want an outsize return in such a short time frame? But there is no guarantee that this will happen this time. The market's success depends so much on how the AI trade will evolve. Heavy concentration can also increase downside risk if these businesses report financial slowdowns that harm sentiment.

So, investors should only buy the Invesco QQQ Trust if they have the intention of owning it for five years or more. That's enough time to let the fundamentals drive performance.
