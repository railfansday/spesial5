If you’ve got a dead car battery, you’ll want the option to revive it without waiting for help. According to AAA’s most recent report, of the 27 million calls received across the country for roadside assistance, 7 million of those vehicles had battery trouble. The causes may include anything from neglecting to turn off your car’s ignition or headlights, residing in an extremely cold climate, or even a slowly deteriorating alternator.

A portable jump starter, which is a compact and powerful battery consisting of lightweight lithium-ion, is a handy device that’s faster, safer, and more convenient than jumper cables because you don’t need to find another car with a willing driver.

Like all batteries, lithium-ion cells have different power ratings and can become depleted from repeated use or by sitting idle over time. That’s why before purchasing a portable jump starter, consider these two specs: peak amperage (amps) which conveys how much power a device can deliver, and capacity (rated in milliampere-hours, mAh), indicating how long a device can hold a charge when stored and still have enough power to provide a jump. It’s also important to monitor the charge level on your portable jump starter from time to time. I do this regularly while waiting to fill my gas tank.

There are other convenience features these devices offer, like USB ports to recharge portable electronics and lights for illumination when it’s dark. Many portable jump starters have what’s called a boost button, which I recommend having, as it supplies extra momentary power for severely drained batteries. After several rounds of testing, research, and evaluation, these are the top portable jump starters to buy now.

Portable jump starters are essentially heavy-duty batteries that come with jumper cables, controls, and accessories like lights, charging ports, and air pumps. Here’s what to look for when shopping for a portable jump starter that fits your vehicle, needs, and budget.

The two most important specs to look for when comparing portable jump starters are peak amperes (amps) and milliampere-hours (mAh). Peak amps indicate the maximum short-term current a device can deliver, while mAh measures how much electrical charge a battery can store.

Bigger is better for both specs, although mAh isn’t always as prominent or even stated with portable jump starters compared to peak amps. About 400 to 600 peak amps is sufficient to jump-start vehicles like cars and crossovers, although large SUVs, trucks, RVs, and commercial vehicles may require up to 1,500 or 2,000 amps. Some portable jump starters come with a boost button that provides roughly 10 additional amps and is helpful when reviving a very dead battery that needs an extra jolt of juice. At least 10,000 mAh of capacity means the battery in a portable jump starter will hold enough charge for 3 to 6 months to deliver 5 to 10 jump-starts.

Portable jump starters are a much safer alternative to using jumper cables to revive a dead battery because connecting jumper cables to a battery’s terminals can cause sparks that can sometimes ignite gas produced by the battery and cause an explosion. Also, if you connect the cables incorrectly, called reverse polarity, it can severely damage a car’s electrical system. Unlike jumper cables, portable jump starters have built-in protection against issues like sparking and reverse polarity in addition to other safety features and are much less risky and easier to use.

Most portable jump starters also come in handy for recharging phones, tablets, and other portable devices. USB output ports are the most common, but a few devices also provide a 12-volt outlet. While USB ports typically charge at 2.5 to 5 volts, a 12-volt outlet can not only charge a device faster but can also be used to power devices such as portable air pumps and coolers.

If you need to jump-start a vehicle at night, you’ll be thankful your portable jump starter has an LED flashlight. Typically a portable jump starter’s lighting feature also provides strobe or SOS patterns to warn other motorists on the road of your presence.

Look for a portable jump starter with a boost button to give an extra jolt of juice to a very depleted battery when the device’s standard output can’t crank the engine. This surge of power gives you about 15 to 30 seconds to start the car once the boost button is pressed.

A portable jump starter rated for 400 to 600 amps is sufficient to jump-start most passenger vehicles such as cars, crossovers, and smaller SUVs. But for larger SUVs, trucks, RVs, and commercial vehicles you want to look for a device with at least 1,500 to 2,000 amps. Weather also affects a car battery and engine, so if you live in a very cold climate more power may be needed to jump-start a low or dead battery.
