Type 2 Diabetes Linked to a 53% Higher Risk of Alzheimer’s, Massive Study Finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A 53% higher Alzheimer’s risk linked to type 2 diabetes tells only part of the story, and researchers have now estimated how frequently new cases occur.

People with type 2 diabetes face a 53% higher risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease, according to a major research review involving data from more than 27 million study participants. The findings strengthen evidence linking diabetes to Alzheimer’s and provide new estimates of how frequently the disease develops among people with the condition.

The Curtin University-led systematic review and meta-analysis examined 40 studies and found that approximately five new cases of Alzheimer’s occur annually for every 1,000 people with type 2 diabetes. Unlike earlier reviews that often grouped different forms of diabetes and dementia together, the researchers focused specifically on type 2 diabetes and Alzheimer’s disease.

The researchers screened 9,431 scientific articles before selecting 40 studies with a combined total of more than 27 million data points, calculated from unadjusted sample sizes. Of these, 23 studies provided information on Alzheimer’s incidence, while 26 examined the association between type 2 diabetes and Alzheimer’s risk.

The pooled incidence was 4.71 new Alzheimer’s cases per 1,000 person-years. After accounting for other factors included in the original studies’ statistical analyses, people with type 2 diabetes had a 53% higher relative risk of developing Alzheimer’s than those without diabetes. The findings demonstrate an association, not a direct causal relationship.

Studies using administrative health records reported an average of 4.00 new Alzheimer’s cases per 1,000 person-years, compared with 10.24 cases in studies using other methods to identify diagnoses. The substantial difference suggests that how Alzheimer’s is identified can influence reported incidence rates.

Among people with type 2 diabetes, Alzheimer’s incidence was numerically higher in women than men, at 3.62 versus 2.76 cases per 1,000 person-years. However, the relative risk associated with diabetes was similar between sexes, with increases of 73% in women and 76% in men. Additional analyses generally supported the overall findings, although incidence estimates varied considerably across studies.

Study author Dr. Emmanuel Adewuyi, an NHMRC Investigator Fellow at the Curtin School of Population Health, said the research provides a more precise assessment of Alzheimer’s risk among people with type 2 diabetes.

“While many earlier reviews combined different types of diabetes or dementia, this review focused specifically on type 2 diabetes and Alzheimer’s disease and estimated how often new cases occurred,” Dr. Adewuyi said.

“Relative risk tells us how two groups compare, while incidence tells us how often new cases occur: putting those numbers together gives us a clearer picture of the burden of Alzheimer’s disease among people living with type 2 diabetes.”

Reference: “Incidence and risk of Alzheimer’s disease in individuals with type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis” by Thi Thu Huong Nguyen, Asa Auta, Emmanuel Agada David, Chinedu I. Ossai, Victory Olutuase, Moulinath Banerjee, Yun Zhao, Davies Adeloye, Gavin Pereira and Emmanuel O. Adewuyi, 9 November 2026, Alzheimer’s Research & Therapy. DOI: 10.1186/s13195-026-02254-7
