10 Biggest DCU Questions We Have After James Gunn’s Surprise Exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Remarkably, the DC Universe is headed for another major upheaval following the surprise ousting of James Gunn and Peter Safran from their roles as the heads of DC Studios. Just as the largely rebooted cinematic universe was finding its footing, the decision was made by Skydance to find new leadership, despite Gunn and Safran believing their roles were safe following the recent Warner Bros. acquisition.

Despite having just begun, the DCU under Gunn and Safran had been growing quickly. While 2025's Superman established a new foundation for the DCU, shows like Peacemaker, Creature Commandos, and Lanterns expanded the universe alongside the Supergirl and Clayface films, which both hit theaters this year. Looking ahead, James Gunn's Man of Tomorrow is set to release next year, and the same goes for The Batman - Part II (along with many other shows and movies in development). However, this new change in leadership throws much of this into question, with what will no doubt be a pretty big shift in priorities.

Of course, fans naturally have more than a few questions about what led to Gunn and Safran's firing, and what's next for the DC Universe. Keeping that in mind, here are 10 of the biggest questions we have about the DCU right now in light of what's been revealed, what's rumored, and what we still don't know following Gunn and Safran's exit.

When the news was first announced on November 11th, official statements from Skydance and Gunn were fairly professional and complimentary, seeming to indicate a mutual transition with Gunn and Safran returning to full creative work. Gunn himself expressed some relief at having more personal time, and an eagerness to focus on projects he's most passionate about, admitting that the corporate responsibilities were taxing (aligning with past comments from Gunn).

That said, reports (substantiated and otherwise) have suggested a far more complicated scenario. According to news outlets like TheWrap, Skydance CEO David Ellison had been quietly discussing a transition with Gunn and Safran's replacement over a month before the announcement, and that Gunn and Safran were only informed as recently as November 6th, just five days before the news went public.

There have also been reports about corporate tensions with other executives, seeing as how James and Safran were meant to report directly to Ellison under their existing contracts, just as they had been with Warner Bros. CEO David Zaslav. This was instead of operating under executives in charge of the rest of Paramount/WB's theatrical and television output.

Whatever the full story is, it seems quite likely that Gunn and Safran leaving wasn't their choice, and that this was apparently a long time coming. After all, James and Safran had indicated that their roles were safe following the merger (clearly...they weren't).

The timing of it all is arguably the biggest surprise. While Supergirl did struggle at the box office both critically and commercially, Superman was a large success, and the DCU has delivered several well-received television projects, particularly Lanterns with record viewership numbers for HBO. Even Clayface broke $100 million, a remarkable feat for an R-rated horror movie. So...why make such a significant leadership change? Additionally, why not wait until after Man of Tomorrow releases next year to gauge its performance and make a decision about Gunn then?

Ultimately, the decision indicates a significant lack of patience on Skydance's part for the way Gunn was building the DCU, and more than likely a level of greed that the returns weren't as high as expected right off the bat from their newly acquired superhero cinematic universe. It's been heavily suggested that Skydance wants more high-profile heroes and major films like a Justice League movie. However, it took 4–5 years for the MCU to build toward 2012's Avengers, and they did it with, at the time, lower-tier Marvel heroes (AND not every MCU movie before then was a winner).

The DCU is really only in its second year, making the entire decision feel incredibly premature. It's not like it's in a worse-off position than the MCU was at the same point in its evolution.

Skydance has selected Jeremy Latcham to serve as DC Studios new, singular head, who will notably be reporting to Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, Co-Chairs oSkydance Motion Picture Group.

Jeremy Latcham was a senior executive at Marvel Studios who helped develop key early MCU projects including Iron Man, The Avengers, Guardians of the Galaxy (working with James Gunn), Avengers: Age of Ultron, and Spider-Man: Homecoming. He then left Marvel in 2017, and has since produced more independent projects, as well as Bad Times at the El Royale and Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves. The most recent film Latcham produced was Nate Bargatze's The Breadwinner.

While his resume does communicate experience with superhero franchises and blockbusters, there are concerns about his creative philosophy and desire to make four-quadrant films with mass appeal and accessibility. Combined with his more conservative values and Christian faith, there are concerns that the DCU might become more sanitized (though that remains to be fully seen).

Fortunately, the immediate slate appears relatively secure. Man of Tomorrow is in post-production, The Batman Part II is already filming, and The People v. Gorilla Grodd has completed production as well. Gunn and Safran are also expected to remain involved in projects they've already helped develop.

That doesn't guarantee every announced project will survive indefinitely, though. Development can change quickly when leadership changes, especially for projects that haven't entered production. For now, the safest assumption is that anything already underway will continue, while the longer-term slate could face a fresh evaluation.

Another complete reboot seems unlikely. Gunn and Safran have already established characters, relationships, and storylines that would be difficult to discard without confusing audiences. Starting over again would also undermine the studio's efforts to build a recognizable shared universe.

However, a creative pivot is much more plausible. Gunn reportedly had plans to introduce DCU Batman and Wonder Woman while moving Superman's story toward larger Justice League adventures. Latcham could accelerate those introductions or change how the franchise connects its heroes. The universe may remain intact, even if its overall direction changes considerably.

Gunn has confirmed that he's writing another DC project beyond Man of Tomorrow, though he hasn't revealed what it is. One possibility is Superman 3, which would make sense given his ongoing interest in Clark Kent and the larger Superman mythology.

It's certainly not confirmed, but Gunn returning to the character after Man of Tomorrow would be a natural next step. He could also be developing something entirely different, especially if he wants to explore a corner of the DCU that hasn't received much attention yet.

The official statements suggest Gunn and Safran will continue helping with a smooth transition, and Gunn's comments indicate that he isn't immediately abandoning DC altogether. However, it's unclear how much creative influence he'll retain once Latcham officially takes over.

Gunn may remain attached as a filmmaker and producer, but that isn't the same as having final authority over the universe's direction. His relationship with Latcham could help preserve some continuity, particularly given their history working together at Marvel. Still, the extent of that involvement remains one of the biggest unknowns.​​​​​​​

Probably. Batman and Wonder Woman are two of DC's most recognizable heroes, and it's difficult to imagine a long-term Justice League strategy that keeps them on the sidelines. Gunn had reportedly been preparing to introduce both into the DCU, but Skydance may want to accelerate those plans rather than wait for the franchise to develop gradually.

Latcham's Marvel experience could also make him particularly interested in building toward a recognizable superhero team. That doesn't mean a Justice League movie is immediately around the corner, but expect Batman and Wonder Woman to become major priorities.​​​​​​​

Gunn's DC Studios made room for stories outside the shared universe, allowing Matt Reeves' The Batman franchise to continue independently. Dynamic Duo similarly represents an opportunity to tell a different kind of DC story without worrying about the main continuity.

Whether Latcham maintains that flexibility is another question. A more franchise-focused leadership approach could favor projects that directly support the central DCU, potentially making Elseworlds productions harder to get approved. Still, The Batman - Part II is already filming, so its immediate future looks secure.​​​​​​​

This may be one of the biggest concerns surrounding the transition. Clayface demonstrated that DC could embrace full-on body horror, while Peacemaker, Creature Commandos, and Lanterns have helped establish a willingness to explore more mature storytelling. Latcham's emphasis on accessibility and four-quadrant appeal could make that approach less common.

However, his grandmother test doesn't automatically mean R-rated projects are finished. Latcham has worked on films with mature ratings, and accessible storytelling doesn't necessarily require avoiding darker material. Still, if Skydance prioritizes broad theatrical appeal, Clayface could become an exception rather than the beginning of a new trend. The future of DC's more daring projects may depend on whether Latcham sees their variety as an advantage or a risk.
