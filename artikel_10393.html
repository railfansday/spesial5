Kevin O’Leary tells Gen Z: ‘Every generation has had whiners like you’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Shark Tank investor and businessman Kevin O’Leary is at odds with Gen Z, who say building wealth and success is harder for their generation than it was for his.

In Jubilee Media’s latest Surrounded video—an online series where a single public figure faces 25 individuals with opposing viewpoints—O’Leary, known as “Mr. Wonderful,” takes on a slate of questions regarding success and wealth.

But it’s one particular conversation in the nearly two-hour-long video that has gained traction on social media.

When the discussion turned to O’Leary’s assertion that “if you want to be successful, work-life balance comes second,” one participant pushed back on how realistic that advice is today.

“We used to have work-life balance—we don’t have that anymore,” one of the participants told O’Leary. “My question to you is, how do you believe that our generation is supposed to be successful and have a work-life balance if we can’t even afford what success would look like, which is owning a home and not being straddled by student loans and medical debt.”

O’Leary quickly replied, “Every single generation has had whiners like you in it. Each generation is surprised to find out things change in the economy all the time.”

Twenty years ago, O’Leary continued, a liberal arts or humanities degree could be considered an “economic disaster,” whereas today, videographers and storytellers are “making anywhere from $200,000 to $400,000 working for corporations.”

‘The kids . . . have totally given up’

As the clip made the rounds on social media, some agreed with O’Leary’s stance on the matter, echoing the age-old claim that younger generations are less willing to work than their older counterparts.

“Not a huge Kevin O’Leary guy, but the kids he’s speaking with have totally given up. They need to keep pushing, they break through or change careers,” one user said on X.

But for most, Mr. Wonderful’s comments came across as disconnected from reality.

“With a straight face, Kevin said “photographers, videographers are making $200-$400k a year.” Out of touch doesn’t even begin to describe this guy’s ignorance,” one user replied on YouTube.

“Kevin O’Leary needs to go walk in the shoes of a middle-class American for a week. He has no idea what he’s talking about,” another added on X.

For others, the conversation further highlighted the tensions between generations.

A user on X added: “Kevin’s generation got theirs, bought houses cheap, got money to build businesses, got cheap college, then changed the rules to fuck every other generation after them.”

The video and the discourse surrounding it also pointed to a larger problem: Neither side can reach consensus.

A user added: “Problem is, the truth is somewhere in the middle of both tales being told, and nobody wants to acknowledge that their respective sides are both overstating the opposing ends of the equation.”
