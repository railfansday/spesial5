Jensen Huang returns, but controversies keep growing around Nvidia

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

THIS WEEK IN ENTERPRISE by Robert Hof

Jensen Huang’s back, and not everyone is happy with that.

Nvidia’s board reversed the resignation of its former and current-again CEO Monday, apparently under pressure from the well-known business genius and lame-duck President Donald Trump, who worried that the stock market and the AI trade would suffer without him at the helm.

Huang (pictured) clearly has management talent, having built one of the most valuable companies in the world. But his return, amid continuing revelations and questions about Nvidia’s business, sets an awful precedent in business. No one person should be too important to fail, and Huang set up a corporate structure that courts disaster if he’s forced to leave again or gets run over by a bus. That’s a world-class failure of a CEO and his board at one of their most basic jobs, and his tech-bro enablers should know better than to clamor for his return.

After all, internal and government investigations continue, as more details come to light about probably the worst of the whole affair(s). It’s clear that at a time when there’s existential worry about China closing the U.S. lead in AI — a potential defense and cybersecurity disaster — the chief supplier of AI chips was allowing cutting-edge chips to be sent to China. Oh, and this week’s stock market pretty much discounted much of the supposed benefit Huang’s reinstatement was supposed to provide.

So what are we left with? The CEO of the pivotal company in AI is back with not even a slap on the wrist, let alone any consequences for multiple affairs — one with a woman who shuttled both barred GPUs and apparently inside information to China, another with an Nvidia VP, plus at least a couple others he fessed up to. Oh, and he lied about them until he couldn’t anymore. Any semblance of corporate governance and ethics — at Nvidia and everywhere else by example — is utterly torched.

Hey, if you think that’s a buying opportunity, I’ve got some high-end GPUs in Singapore to sell you. Anyway, let’s move on before I tell you what I really think.

Paul Gillin dove into why open-weight AI models that enterprises can tailor to their specific needs are taking off this year. “The shift toward open-weight models represents a permanent maturation of enterprise IT from experimentation to disciplined financial and architectural management,” he notes. “Success is no longer about raw model parameter size or benchmark ranking, but matching the model to the task.”

Last week Palo Alto Networks said it will acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 billion. This week was CrowdStrike’s turn, as it pulled the trigger on buying Zscaler for a pretty stunning $44 billion. As AI makes cyberattacks far easier and more numerous, it seems a sure bet that today’s rickety cybersecurity industry with hundred of point products won’t stand.

AI continues to be a big driver of upside earnings surprises, but investors increasingly wary of AI spending are all over the place. Dell in particular more than doubled its revenue, only to see its shares go nowhere Wednesday, while HP got hit by the PC slowdown but saw its shares rise more than 12%. Likewise, HPE’s stock slid despite an upbeat forecast.
