Fed Governor Lisa Cook's lawyer signals she won't attend Dec. 14 White House hearing

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Lawyer Abbe Lowell says Cook would appear before an unbiased panel with fair procedures, but calls the current process unfair

Abbe Lowell, the lawyer for Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, wrote to the White House that she is under no legal duty to attend a Dec. 14 hearing on unproven mortgage fraud allegations, according to Bloomberg.

The letter, which carries a Nov. 16 date and became public Tuesday, suggests more plainly than anything before it that Cook intends to stay away from the hearing, according to CNBC. Lowell addressed it to Will Scharf, presidential counsel.

Lowell argued that the committee President Donald Trump created last month serves as a pretext and endangers the central bank, according to CNBC. "Governor Cook has no legal obligation to participate in an unfair hearing — and indeed, has a duty to the Federal Reserve to decline to participate in any proceeding that would undermine the institution's unique status and independence," he wrote.

Lowell also called the process "a façade designed to obscure a predetermined decision to fire Governor Cook," according to Bloomberg. He said recent White House correspondence did not address her concerns.

Lowell left open the possibility of an appearance, according to Bloomberg. "If the White House establishes an unbiased panel and implements fair procedures that do not prejudge the outcome — such as providing the evidence it claims to have gathered with sufficient time for review and response — Governor Cook would be prepared to appear," he wrote. He added that the White House's Nov. 2 letter, with its ultimatums, fails to offer a workable way to move ahead.

CNBC's request for comment on the letter did not get an immediate reply from the White House.

Trump established a three-member committee in October to investigate allegations that Cook made false statements on mortgage documents. A White House memo said the hearing would be closed to the public but transcribed, and that the committee would then recommend whether cause exists to remove her from the Fed's Board of Governors. The hearing was first set for Nov. 5.

Lowell later asked the White House to postpone that hearing, citing what he called "demonstrably biased members" on the committee. He said Cook had official obligations in Europe and could attend no earlier than after the Fed's Dec. 8-9 policy meeting.

Cook denies wrongdoing, has not been charged with a crime and has sued to keep her job. Trump's effort to remove her began in August 2025. In June, the Supreme Court ruled 5-4 that she was entitled to notice and a chance to respond before any removal, and it left open what legally qualifies as cause.
