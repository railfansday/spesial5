Something potentially enormous could be approaching for Tesla (TSLA) by January 1, 2027. Mark Zuckerberg’s Meta AI predicts that TSLA stock could be a standout performer through Q4 and into 2027, based on a hypothetical landmark catalyst that isn’t impossible.

The concept focuses on Tesla obtaining widespread regulatory approval for unsupervised full self-driving robotaxi operations in several major markets, while also scaling up the production and deployment of Optimus humanoid robots beyond current expectations.

In this scenario, Tesla would quickly shift from primarily an electric vehicle manufacturer to a high-margin platform company centered on autonomy and robotics. This transformation could open up multi-trillion-dollar markets and significantly increase the company’s stock valuation.

On the higher timeframes, TSLA has been consolidating in the $390–$415 region after a recovery from lower levels earlier in the year. Price continues to hold above key intermediate supports while facing resistance around the $420–$450 zone.

In a standard bull market for growth and technology stocks, a sustained breakout above $480–$520 would confirm intermediate strength and open the path to higher-cycle targets.

Under the extreme robotaxi approval and Optimus scaling scenario outlined above, clearing significant resistance levels would likely trigger powerful measured-move extensions and multi-cycle projections from the longer-term base.

Momentum indicators currently sit in neutral-to-constructive territory with substantial room to run into the strong overbought readings typical of high-beta technology rallies.

Key nearer-term supports sit near $380–$390 and $350–$360; holding those levels would keep the broader recovery structure intact.

In recent weeks, Tesla has delivered several updates that support longer-term optimism around autonomy and robotics. In its Q3 2026 results, the company beat adjusted EPS estimates and generated positive free cash flow, even as it ramped spending on AI and future products.

More importantly, Tesla confirmed that production of Optimus humanoid robots has begun on high-volume lines in Fremont, marking a shift from prototypes to early manufacturing.

On the autonomy side, the Cybercab robotaxi service continues to expand, with the Austin fleet growing rapidly, plans to launch in Dallas, and an ambition to deliver the first Cybercabs to customers by the end of 2026.

Unsupervised robotaxi miles have increased sharply, while Full Self-Driving subscriptions remain on a strong upward trajectory. Tesla has also entered volume production of the Semi.

While near-term profits remain under pressure from heavy investment, these operational milestones reinforce the narrative of Tesla evolving beyond vehicles into high-margin autonomy and robotics platforms.

Under this highly speculative scenario, and assuming supportive market conditions return, Tesla (TSLA) could reach between $1,800 and $3,000, with a stretch target toward $4,500+ by January 1, 2027.

This remains pure speculation and entertainment, not a realistic base-case outlook. The catalyst would require extraordinary regulatory progress and execution on robotaxi and Optimus at a scale that is currently highly unlikely.
