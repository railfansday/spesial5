In the end, Nvidia’s board backed down and reinstated Jensen Huang as the “new” Nvidia CEO. The announcement triggered a broad market rebound, sending stocks higher and adding trillions in market value. But skepticism remains high, particularly across the crypto market, where investors are still waiting for signs of a sustained recovery.

Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday, November 23, 11 days after he resigned, and the stock exploded. The new Nvidia CEO is the old one, and investors cheered. Shares jumped from about $136 to as high as $169 in pre-market trading after the 8:55 a.m. ET announcement.

That peak was 35% above Friday’s close of $125.53. The stock opened near $163 and traded around $160 shortly after, still up 27%. Crypto went along for the ride. Bitcoin (BTC) rose as much as 5% to about $64,100, and Ethereum (ETH) as much as 7%.

The reversal came a day after President Donald Trump urged the board to bring Huang back. “I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang,” Trump wrote on Truth Social on Sunday.

So how much of the scandal sell-off has Nvidia won back, and what comes next?

Nvidia’s board “voted unanimously to reinstate Mr. Huang” after meetings with him, it said in its announcement. It said it was satisfied with his “candor and ability to lead.”

It is a sharp turn. On November 17, chair Harvey Jones said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO” and had hired a search firm for a new Nvidia CEO.

The government helped change that. The board said it heard from “senior levels of the United States government” before deciding. Based on that input, it is confident Huang’s return “poses no national security risk.” Huang resigned on November 12 after admitting an affair with a woman US intelligence suspects of spying for China.

“I am honored to return to NVIDIA,” Huang said. “I can’t wait to get back to work.”

Colette Kress, who ran the company as interim CEO, stays on as finance chief. The board’s independent investigation into the foreign intelligence effort will continue, and Nvidia said it will keep cooperating with government inquiries.

To measure how much the new Nvidia CEO news repaired, we compared Monday’s price with Nvidia’s closes before and during the scandal.

Nvidia closed at $186.64 on November 6, the last session before Huang’s affair came out. By Friday it was down to $125.53, a loss of $61.11 a share. At $160, the stock has recovered 56% of that drop. At the $169 peak, it had won back 71%.

The peak took it above its close on November 12, the day Huang resigned ($166.19). Even at $160, the jump adds about $830 billion to Nvidia’s market value in a single morning.

The stock is still far from its highs. It sits 33% below its October 6 record close of $239.24, because it had already fallen 22% on AI spending worries before the scandal began.

Bitcoin’s move shows how closely crypto now trades with Nvidia. BTC jumped from about $61,100 to above $63,000 in the same minutes as NVDA, and crypto stocks such as Strategy (MSTR) also rose.

Nvidia stock trades around $160, below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $186, and its 200-day EMA at $202.

What comes next depends on Washington more than the boardroom. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants answers from Nvidia by November 25 on whether its chips reached China through shell companies. A reported SEC look at its accounting is still open. Huang is also due to testify to Congress in December.

With the new Nvidia CEO back in charge, those answers will decide whether the stock can hold above $150.
