Xiaomi's bonkers dual-screen phone is here and I've tested its camera.

Editor at Large; Lead Photographer, Europe

Editor at Large; Lead Photographer, Europe

Xiaomi has taken the wraps off its latest 18 Pro Max Android phone in Paris. I’ve had the phone for the past few weeks and have been putting the camera to the test in my beautiful home of Edinburgh, Scotland, as well as on the beautiful (and rainy) streets of Paris. You can check out my review of the phone if you’re keen to hear more about that wild rear screen on the back, but here I wanted to share some of my favorite photos I’ve taken with the phone so far.

The images seen here are taken with the phone’s various built-in camera modes, largely the black and white mode, as I love the dramatic, filmic look it produces. They’re presented either as-shot or with minimal adjustments using the phone’s built-in editing tools.

Taken using one of the phone’s built-in color styles, I love the vibrancy in this shot. The reds really pop, while the wider angle of the main camera emphasizes the tunnel effect of the alleyway, drawing the eye to the person and their dog.

I may be spoiling the rest of the images here by saying that this is my absolute favorite shot I’ve taken, but there we go. I took this using one of the dedicated black-and-white modes, and I think the tones here are perfect. Excellent dynamic range and contrast, and just the right amount of film grain. I positioned the figure between the buildings and had the phone almost on the floor to turn the rain-soaked tiles into a reflective surface to add some drama to this scene. I then applied a wide cinematic crop in post to give it that extra edge. Lovely stuff.

Most of my favorite photos with the phone were taken using the black-and-white mode. It’s no surprise; I shoot in black and white very often on my Leica Q3 43, and I used the monochrome mode for my favorite shots with Xiaomi’s Leitzphone earlier in the year.

The phone captures excellent black-and-white photos at night, too.

And by exposing for the bright highlights in this scene, I was able to create this stark, moody image of my handsome orange cat, Toulouse. Is he the best cat you’ve ever seen? Of course he is.

Back in color, this UFO that had presumably crash-landed in this Parisian market looks great in this filmic color mode. The oranges and reds pop against the other tones in this scene.

While this quick street portrait is similarly punchy.

I used manual mode here, taking full control over the white balance and exposure settings to achieve the tones I wanted. It’s a great-looking shot with excellent dynamic range. Xiaomi’s phone doesn’t appear to go too hard with its software processing either, meaning that even fine details look quite natural. Other Android phone-makers could take note.

As with the photo of Toulouse the cat, I exposed for the highlights here, forcing the rest of the scene under this bridge into shadow. As a result, it looks moody and dramatic but crucially it also looks exactly as I hoped it would. Many phones would try to artificially lift the shadows here, which would ruin the vibe I was going for.

I took this with the phone’s telephoto zoom lens, aiming to focus more on the digger as the subject and use the water for reflection. I love how this shot looks, especially in black and white, as the cloud detail behind it is especially noticeable.

While here I used the full wide angle of the main camera in order to create a lot of negative space around the figures, emphasizing the size of the building around them.

That building was Le Grande Arche in Paris and despite it being a miserably cold, wet and cloudy day I think the phone has captured a satisfyingly stark scene here.

I don’t like flying, so I distracted myself on the flight out to Paris by taking photos of things with the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max, including this man having a look out of the window.

And this shot of my plane’s wing somewhere above the UK. Again, in black and white. When it looks this good, why would I choose anything else?

And while we’re still in black and white, a particularly friendly pigeon. Hi, friend!

And to round us off, this dude in shades looking at a menu.

I’m genuinely really thrilled with so many of the shots I’ve taken with this phone. The black-and-white mode in particular is spectacular, and I’m looking forward to taking more shots with it over the coming weeks and months.
