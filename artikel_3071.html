3 Energy Stocks With Dividends Built to Last the Next 10 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Chevron, Brookfield Renewable, and Williams are all reliable income investments.

Energy stocks can be volatile short-term investments, since commodity prices are cyclical. However, the biggest blue chip energy stocks can still be reliable long-term investments, since their scale insulates them from lower commodity prices.

Many of those top energy stocks also return a large share of their excess cash to investors through large dividends. Let's take a look at three of those high-yield energy stocks -- Chevron (CVX -3.95%), Brookfield Renewable (BEPC +1.57%), and The Williams Companies (WMB -0.34%) -- and see why they're worth buying and holding for at least the next ten years.

Chevron, one of the world's largest integrated energy companies, pays a forward yield of 3.3%. It's raised its dividend annually for 39 consecutive years, even as the U.S. economy endured three official recessions and five bear markets.

Chevron is resilient because it operates in 180 countries and owns a diverse mix of upstream, midstream, and downstream assets. That scale dilutes its operating expenses and allows it to easily cover its capex and dividends as long as the price of Brent crude oil -- currently at $92 -- stays above its corporate breakeven price of $50 per barrel. The last time Brent traded below $50 per barrel was during the darkest days of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Chevron expects to increase its oil and gas production by 2%-3% annually through 2030. It plans to hit that target by expanding its Tengiz Field in Kazakhstan, drilling for more oil in the Permian Basin, launching more deepwater projects in the Gulf of Mexico, and increasing its presence in Guyana, one of the world's fastest-growing oil regions. Therefore, Chevron is a great income-generating energy stock you can buy and forget for at least the next 10 years.

Brookfield Renewable Corporation, which was spun off from Brookfield Renewable Partners (BEP +1.43%) in 2020, builds hydroelectric dams, wind farms, solar power plants, and other green energy projects across 25 countries. It's raised its dividend every year since that spin-off, and it currently pays a forward dividend yield of 5.1%.

With an operational capacity of 51.4 GW and a pipeline of over 200 GW of renewable projects (including 85.7 GW of advanced-stage projects in active development), Brookfield is a great all-around investment in cleaner energy. Roughly 90% of its power generation is locked into long-term contracts with a weighted-average remaining contract duration of 12 years. About 70% of its revenue is also indexed to inflation, which insulates it from rising costs.

Brookfield easily covered its dividends with its funds from operations (FFO) last year, and it plans to keep raising its payout by 5%-9% annually. Those strengths make Brookfield a safe green energy play that will continue to generate stable income over the next decade.

The Williams Companies operates over 32,000 miles of pipeline in 24 states. It owns Transco, the largest natural gas pipeline in the U.S., which links the Gulf Coast to the Atlantic seaboard. It handles roughly a third of all natural gas produced in the country.

Williams is benefiting from the rapid growth of the data center market, which gets more than 40% of its electricity from natural gas, as well as other industrial markets. As a midstream pipeline company, it merely charges upstream and downstream companies "tolls" to use its infrastructure. That business model is well insulated from volatile commodity prices, since it merely needs those resources to keep flowing through its pipes to generate stable profits.

Williams has paid continuous dividends for the past 52 years, and it's raised its payout annually over the past nine years. It currently pays a forward yield of 3.1% and offers a good blend of growth and income, with some passive exposure to the booming AI market.
