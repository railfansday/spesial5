U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent Just Delivered Great News to Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. Should You Buy Nvidia Stock Before Nov. 25?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

On CBS's Face the Nation, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent seemed to change his tone regarding a potential return of Jensen Huang to Nvidia.

By Bram Berkowitz – Nov 22, 2026 at 3:48PM EST

Things certainly change fast in today's news cycle.

Last week, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent appeared on Fox News and delivered what seemed to be a blistering rebuke of Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), which is facing allegations of potentially violating export controls.

Furthermore, Bessent called for a "dramatic shake-up" at Nvidia, suggesting that the artificial intelligence chip giant's next leader should be from "outside the company."

But now Bessent seems to have left the door open for former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang to return as CEO. It's certainly good news for Huang. Should you buy Nvidia stock before Nov. 25?

Looking back at the Fox interview, I interpreted that to mean Bessent and his colleagues at the Treasury Department were not interested in seeing Huang return as Nvidia's CEO. But Bessent appears to have changed his tune, or was simply misunderstood.

On CBS' Face the Nation on Nov. 22, when pressed whether Nvidia should look for an outside candidate to lead Nvidia, Bessent replied, "I said they should go to the outside, but he's on the outside now," seemingly leaving the door open for Huang to return as Nvidia's CEO.

As of this writing, the odds of Huang returning as Nvidia's CEO on the prediction-markets platform Kalshi have increased from 7% on Nov. 20 to 12%.

The new sentiment may be tied to recent comments from President Donald Trump, who has come out in support of Huang and called on Nvidia's board of directors to reinstate the man who co-founded the company over three decades ago.

Huang resigned from Nvidia on Nov. 12, following allegations of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with a woman who may have been a Chinese spy. The alleged woman, Alice Huang (no relation), has also been accused of setting up a network to illegally divert Nvidia chips to Chinese companies, despite export restrictions.

Nvidia's board has launched an independent investigation into the matter, and regulators have probed the company at the center of the allegations, a Singaporean data center company called MegaSpeed. Huang is also set to testify before Congress.

Last week, Bessent also said that Nvidia must inform the Trump administration by Nov. 25 whether it set up shell companies to evade export controls and sell its most advanced chips to Chinese firms. In his interview on Nov. 22, Bessent said the Treasury Department had not yet received those answers.

Furthermore, Bessent said he's interested to see "where the buck stops."

"... I said was we will see which level of the business knew about this. Was it at the lower levels? Was it the middle levels? Did it go all the way to the top?" questioned Bessent, while on Face the Nation. "And I have confidence that the Nvidia committees are moving very quickly on this and will come up with some very wholesome and robust answers."

Nvidia's board has already said it has no intention of bringing Huang back, although there is certainly a cohort who want to see Huang return.

Bessent's recent remarks, coupled with Trump's support, seem to open the door for Huang's return, at least from the administration's perspective. That could theoretically allow Nvidia's board to answer questions from the Treasury Department that the administration is more willing to accept, and potentially reduce certain regulatory pressure.

But Nvidia's board does not appear to want Huang to return, potentially indicating they may be willing to place more blame on the former CEO in responses they submit to the administration.

The situation is quite fluid, and new information about the Huang scandal seems to emerge every day. I certainly wouldn't make an investment decision based on the answers the administration might receive from Nvidia on or before Nov. 25. Timing the market is nearly impossible, too.

That said, I do think investors can, at the very least, start dipping their toes in the water and accumulate a smaller position in Nvidia, especially given management's recent upbeat outlook.

Nvidia trades at roughly 7.5x its projected adjusted earnings per share for its fiscal year 2028. In my mind, this valuation is pricing in a significant amount of the risk the company faces, though not necessarily all of it.
