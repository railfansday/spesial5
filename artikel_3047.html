Stock Market Today, Nov. 19: Nvidia Slides as Investors Weigh CEO Transition Fallout Despite Strong Q3 Results

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Today, Nov. 19, 2026, Nvidia shares fell despite a strong quarter and an early boost from Anthropic's IPO, as traders continue to weigh the leadership change and what comes next for AI spending.

By Robert Izquierdo – Nov 19, 2026 at 5:00PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -3.30%), a leader in AI accelerators, chips, and data-center networking hardware, closed at $145.98, down 3.30%. Strong third-quarter results, a raised outlook, and investor excitement over Anthropic's IPO supported shares early, but leadership and AI-spending concerns ultimately outweighed these factors. Wall Street is watching the next earnings cycle for AI-demand and supply insights.

Trading volume reached 311.8 million shares, coming in about 95% above its three-month average of 160.0 million shares. Nvidia IPO'd in 1999 and has grown 355,802% since going public.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.02%) closed at 7,176, down 2.02%, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -2.25%) closed at 23,879, down 2.25%. Among semiconductor rivals, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -7.72%) closed at $489.19, down 7.72%, and Broadcom (AVGO -2.09%) closed at $346.65, down 2.09%, underscoring pressure across semiconductors stocks.

On Thursday, Nov. 19, Nvidia continued to face pressure even after strong Q3 results, as investors weighed artificial intelligence spending and leadership uncertainty. Nvidia's move shows Wall Street is still asking whether demand can justify the sector's pace of buildout.

Earlier this week, Morgan Stanley trimmed its price target to $260 while keeping an Overweight rating after the earnings release. That kept analyst attention on Nvidia's valuation and growth outlook.

On Nov. 17, Nvidia reported record fiscal Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, representing 103% year-over-year growth, and said its new Vera Rubin NVL72 has shipped. Management also forecasted fiscal Q4 revenue of approximately $130 billion, ahead of Wall Street expectations. The Q3 update reinforced demand momentum in the AI sector.

Despite the spectacular results, the Nov. 12 resignation of Nvidia CEO and co-founder Jensen Huang continued to weigh on shares, as investors are still recalibrating that risk alongside the earnings beat. Fallout from Huang's departure included the U.S. government tightening rules on Monday to prevent Chinese businesses from remotely accessing advanced Nvidia AI chips via overseas data centers.

This added to fears that incoming Democratic Congressional leadership will continue to add new regulations to the AI sector. As a result, AI stocks have been hit lately, despite the long-anticipated debut of Anthropic's IPO.
