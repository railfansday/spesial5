Xbox Will Make You Watch Three Minutes of Ads for Just 30 Minutes of Gaming

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Microsoft has found another way to make money from Xbox, and this time, you'll be paying with your time rather than your wallet. The company has detailed its ad-supported cloud gaming service, which offers 30 minutes of gameplay in exchange for watching three minutes of commercials.

The service is being marketed to advertisers as Xbox Video for Brands. For gamers, it's an alternative to the subscription-based Xbox Cloud Gaming service, allowing eligible users to stream games without an active Game Pass subscription.

A rising number of players are citing prohibitive monthly costs as their reason for canceling gaming subscriptions.

Free access to cloud gaming certainly sounds appealing, but half an hour isn't much time, especially when you have to sit through three minutes of ads before you can even start playing.

In a November 12 announcement, Microsoft confirmed that its ad-supported cloud gaming service will feature a three-minute pre-roll advertising block before each 30-minute gaming session.

These advertisements will be full-screen and non-skippable, although they won't interrupt active gameplay. Instead, they'll appear before the session begins, during the cloud-loading process.

Microsoft describes this as an opportunity for advertisers to reach gamers just as they're preparing to play. The company believes that this makes Xbox an attractive advertising platform, since the audience is already engaged and ready to start gaming.

But from a gamer's perspective, three minutes of advertisements for just half an hour of gameplay doesn't sound particularly generous. The ads won't interrupt your gameplay, but the 30-minute session limit certainly will.

Consider how much time you might spend doing something relatively mundane in an RPG or an open-world game, whether that's exploring a new area, completing a quest, or simply getting sidetracked. You could easily spend most of your allotted time without making much progress.

The ads won't interrupt your gameplay, but the 30-minute session limit certainly will.

To be clear, Microsoft isn't adding commercial breaks to games you've already purchased. The advertisements are limited to this optional cloud gaming tier, and they'll play before you start gaming rather than interrupting an ongoing session.

The announcement also leaves some details unclear, including whether every region and session will have precisely the same advertising requirements.

What's rather disappointing about the newly announced arrangement is that Microsoft's earlier ad-supported cloud gaming tests were considerably more generous.

When the company began testing the service with Xbox Insiders in July, users could access one-hour gaming sessions after watching advertisements. Earlier reporting indicated that these introductory ads lasted approximately two minutes. That's twice the gameplay time, with fewer advertisements to sit through.

The new arrangement looks considerably less appealing by comparison, although free access still has its uses. If you occasionally want to try a game without committing to a Game Pass subscription, even 30 minutes might be enough. For anything more substantial, however, the time limit could become frustrating rather quickly.

There's also the question of what happens when those 30 minutes run out. Can you immediately start another session after watching more ads, or will you have to wait in another queue? And how smoothly will the service handle resuming a game after the previous session expires?

Microsoft hasn't explained these details in its latest announcement, so we'll have to see how the system works in practice.

The introduction of advertisements is part of a much larger restructuring of Xbox Cloud Gaming. As DualShockers previously reported, Microsoft has also introduced pay-as-you-go cloud gaming packages, allowing you to purchase streaming time without subscribing to Game Pass.

The introductory prices include $3.99 for 10 hours, $7.99 for 25 hours, and $11.99 for 50 hours of gameplay. These packages offer ad-free streaming, and the purchased hours remain available until they're used.

Meanwhile, Game Pass subscriptions now have monthly cloud gaming allowances, with Ultimate receiving 15 hours, Premium receiving 10 hours, and Essential receiving five hours. If you exhaust your included hours, you can purchase additional time or use the ad-supported service where available.

Microsoft has justified these changes by pointing to the growing costs of cloud gaming as more people use the service. Streaming certainly has its advantages, particularly if you don't own a powerful gaming PC or a modern console. And with the next Xbox reportedly carrying an eye-watering price tag, being able to play without buying new hardware might sound even more appealing.

But Microsoft's approach to the service is changing. Instead of simply treating cloud gaming as another benefit of the Xbox ecosystem, the company is increasingly dividing it into subscriptions, purchased hours, and advertising-supported sessions.

And while the latter doesn't cost anything, you're still paying with your time. Watching a few advertisements to try a game might be reasonable enough, but sitting through three minutes of commercials every time you want another half hour of gameplay sounds considerably less appealing.

Xbox CEO Asha Sharma has hinted that Xbox Game Pass prices could go down in the future, though this could be part of a shift to digital ownership.
