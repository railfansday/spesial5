As he did with the Giants, Colts QB Daniel Jones is struggling, right after getting paid

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Daniel Jones’ career has had some ups, but also plenty of downs, both with the Giants and with the Colts. He has a knack for having his “ups” in contract years, and his “downs” after getting paid.

Jones was awful in Thursday night’s loss to the Texans, continuing the worst season of his career — which comes after the Colts signed him to a two-year, $88 million contract in the offseason.

It’s not the first time Jones has performed poorly after getting a big deal. In fact, that’s all Jones has done throughout his NFL career.

Jones’ career started when the Giants made the surprising decision to select him with the sixth overall pick in the 2019 NFL draft. That earned him a four-year, $25.7 million contract, with the Giants having a fifth-year option. Jones didn’t live up to expectations on that rookie contract, and was playing some of the worst football of his career when his third season ended early with a neck injury, so the Giants declined his fifth-year option.

But Jones then played very well in his fourth year, leading the Giants to a surprise playoff berth and a win in the wild card round. That led the Giants to sign him to a four-year, $160 million contract in 2023, committing more money to him than his fifth-year option would have cost. Jones responded to that with a fifth season in which he played poorly, suffered another neck injury and then suffered a season-ending torn ACL.

Jones returned in 2024 but was so bad the Giants first benched him, then cut him. Jones spent the rest of that season on the Vikings’ practice squad.

In 2025 the Colts signed him to a one-year contract, and he responded by winning a quarterback competition with Anthony Richardson and then playing the best football of his career early in 2025 as the Colts jumped out to an 8-2 start. He started to struggle down the stretch in that season, and then his season was cut short by a torn Achilles tendon, but the Colts were so impressed by his start that they signed him to a two-year, $88 million contract in March.

And now, in the first year of that contract, Jones is worse than ever. His 13 interceptions are the most of any quarterback in the NFL this season and already a career-high, with six more games to play. He’s paired that with just seven touchdown passes. He still hasn’t had a single game this season with more touchdowns than interceptions. His 65.4 passer rating is a career low. His 30.3 QBR is a career low. His 58.4 percent completion rate is a career low. His 5.6 yards per pass average is a career low. Jones was once an effective runner, but his 2.9 yards per carry average is also a career low. Any way you slice it, Jones is worse than ever.

He’s also more expensive than ever. Whether the Colts keep him or cut him, he’s going to have a massive salary cap hit in 2027, the kind of cap hit that will make it that much harder for the Colts to rebuild their roster.

If the Colts cut Jones, he may sign another one-year contract with another team. No matter how well he plays, that team would be wise not to give him a lucrative extension.
