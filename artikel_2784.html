9 NFL rookies who are off to surprising starts in 2026 season

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Through 10 weeks, the 2026 NFL Draft class only has a select few players taking on starring roles right away.

The latest crop of rookies features plenty of success stories, as well as some early letdowns. For all the promise shown thus far, however, only a handful of top picks – such as New York Giants linebacker Arvell Reese, New York Jets edge rusher David Bailey, Kansas City Chiefs cornerback Mansoor Delane and Washington Commanders linebacker Sonny Styles – have risen to the level of top performers regardless of experience level.

Still, several players taken on Day 2 or 3 deserve the midseason spotlight after turning heads with their initial contributions.

Here are nine rookies who are off to surprising starts so far this fall:

The fifth-round pick from Nebraska has served as a fascinating case study in weighing instincts against athleticism at running back. An All-American at Nebraska, Johnson saw his star fade a bit during the pre-draft process as more physically gifted ball carriers stepped into the spotlight.

As Kenneth Walker III's backup, however, he's quickly reinforced his value. But with the highly priced free agent signing sidelined by a groin injury, Johnson delivered his most meaningful outing to date by recording 171 yards from scrimmage on 28 touches in a 35-13 win over the Atlanta Falcons. That kind of performance might give Kansas City the comfort to continue resting Walker ahead of a marquee Thanksgiving matchup against the Buffalo Bills.

Johnson hasn't merely benefitted from what's been blocked for him, as his 45.6% rate of forcing at least one missed tackle is the best in the NFL among players with at least 50 touches, according to Next Gen Stats. That slipperiness and reliability as a receiver – he has caught 20 of 23 targets for 307 yards – have made him indispensable to a Chiefs offense that hasn't been able to count on igniting big plays downfield after its hot start. Fold in his solid contributions as a kick returner (318 yards on 11 returns), and Kansas City should be thrilled with its budget backup.

Selecting Black, a non-combine invitee, in the third round ahead of some more highly regarded options earned San Francisco a good bit of scrutiny on draft day. So far, though, a franchise that's had a spotty record of identifying mid-round running backs is faring well with its latest complement for Christian McCaffrey.

Black is already up to 356 yards and two rushing touchdowns on 79 carries. Known for his hard-charging style, the former Indiana standout has seen more than 82% of his production (295 yards) come after contact. With three consecutive games of at least 11 carries, his workload should remain steady for a team that surely would like to avoid overexerting McCaffrey down the stretch.

When Sean Payton praises a rookie, it's worth paying attention to the Broncos coach, who is famously stingy in handing out reps to first-year players. In late October, however, Payton declared his interest in finding more touches for the fourth-rounder. It has since become even more apparent why he was seeking a change of pace.

Payton's statement came after Coleman, who missed the previous four games due to a high ankle sprain, had 69 yards on 11 touches against the Arizona Cardinals. In the two games since, the Washington product has notched 192 yards from scrimmage in two games. Having that kind of consistency on early downs – and some semblance of a big-play threat – has made a substantial difference for an offense that ranks 31st in third-down conversion percentage. With Coleman keeping the attack on schedule more easily than J.K. Dobbins has, Denver has more frequently avoided the obvious passing situations that have proved so daunting.

Utilizing a bell-cow back has never been Payton's way. For a team that has grown accustomed to persevering in close contests and has had to count on efficiency, though, Coleman seems like the clear lead option the rest of the way.

Thrust into a starting role for a team with no clear-cut weapons, the third-round pick had every excuse this season to post limited returns. Yet for all the roadblocks the Dolphins' offense has put up, Douglas has delivered as an efficient downfield threat.

Though he missed three early games with an ankle injury, his 61.2 yards per game is tops among all rookies. He's still been responsible for a good number of miscues, but Miami's season has largely been defined by a trial-and-error approach anyway. With the Dolphins' future at quarterback unsettled, the emergence of a 6-4 target who averages 16.7 yards per catch should help first-year coach Jeff Hafley feel more at ease about where the offense is headed.

As the No. 39 overall pick on a team with a dearth of competition at receiver, Boston looked poised to have a big role out of the gates for the Browns. Still, in propping up Cleveland's passing attack, the well-built wideout might have pushed his way to the front of the race for Offensive Rookie of the Year.

Boston leads all 2026 draft picks with 523 receiving yards. Establishing a downfield connection with Deshaun Watson hasn't been easy, as his 33 catches on 62 targets have reflected. But in repeatedly beating man coverage and skying for contested catches, the 6-4, 215-pound target has erased all pre-draft concerns about his viability as a vertical threat. Outside of a 60-yard catch-and-run in an overtime loss to the New Orleans Saints, Boston has been relatively quiet in the last five games. Yet that clearly seems to be more a reflection of Watson's limitations rather than his own.

Todd Bowles' defense repeatedly came apart at the second level last season. Between that glaring vulnerability and the retirement of franchise fixture Lavonte David, Tampa Bay seemed due for a new day at linebacker. Through the first half of the season, Trotter has outpaced even the most optimistic projections of his play.

Operating downhill has never been a problem for the son of three-time Pro Bowl selection Jeremiah Trotter. With the Buccaneers, the younger Trotter has continued to throttle opposing rushing attacks. His greatest strides, however, have come in coverage, with his Week 1 pick-six of Joe Burrow foreshadowing his growth. That might never be his strong suit, but so long as he can at least hold his own against backs and tight ends, he could end up being a far more versatile defensive piece than many envisioned him as after his career at Missouri.

It's not easy to find bright spots on a Patriots defense that's conceded 80 points over the past two games. But Jacas, the second-round pick from Illinois, surely qualifies as one.

At 6-4 and 260 pounds, the former high school wrestling champion has been extremely difficult for opposing blockers to pin down. His ultraphysical and unrelenting approach to pass rushing has yielded 3 1/2 sacks and 25 pressures on the season. He's also been a steady presence against the run. Jacas' efforts haven't been enough to vault a unit that continues to settle in the middle of the pack in creating havoc for opposing quarterbacks, but he's entrenched himself as an asset on the edge for years to come.

Injuries at cornerback ravaged Carolina's secondary, thereby shutting down any shot the unit had of achieving a long-awaited breakthrough. But don't blame Wheatley, the fifth-round safety out of Penn State who's done more than anyone could have expected in the early going.

After being a healthy scratch in the season opener, Wheatley acquitted himself nicely while steadily becoming a key piece on the back end of the Panthers' defense. With Todd Wash taking over for the ousted Ejiro Evero as interim defensive coordinator, the rookie now figures to be entrusted with even more after he got his first start and played in 100% of the defensive snaps against the New Orleans Saints. Limited ball production (one interception and four passes defensed) might deprive him of wider acclaim. But Carolina needs someone it can count on to make the right decisions in coverage and as a run stopper, and Wheatley's acumen on both fronts is serving him and the Panthers well.

Fourth-round edge rusher Dani Dennis-Sutton warranted mention on this list, thanks in large part to an astronomical 18.2% pressure rate that stacks up with many of the league's best. Yet the Penn State product has stood out as a sub-package sparkplug, whereas Cisse has held his own as a Day 1 starter.

The second-round pick from South Carolina was peppered with questions throughout the pre-draft process of whether he was properly prepared to be a consistent coverage presence. In Green Bay, however, he's shown himself to be a quick study after bouncing back from giving up a touchdown in each of his first two games. He earned Defensive Rookie of the Month for October after recording a sack, an interception and seven passes defensed in five games during that span. A rib injury knocked him out early in the loss to the Minnesota Vikings, but Cisse should be back in the fold this week as part of one of the league's best playmaking secondaries.
