Anthropic shares fall below IPO price. Traders see even more pain

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Anthropic shares are back trading below the IPO price after a 13% sell-off Tuesday and a 1.5% drop Monday, despite a broad rally in most big tech artificial intelligence names. Trading under $76 on Tuesday, the stock's now 8.5% below its initial public offering level and 34% beneath its intraday high of $115.

It took just four days for ANT to fall under its offering price, compared with 21 trading days for SpaceX, which rallied out of the gate.

Options volume on the stock is picking up as the shares drop and implied volatility jumps to 96, according to ThinkOrSwim data. Almost 500,000 options traded by noon ET on Tuesday for a total premium of about $250 million, the sixth-most active single-stock options in the market, SpotGamma data shows.

Almost 300,000 puts traded versus about 200,000 calls, but the sentiment implied by the trades was not overtly bearish, and judging by the biggest single trades of the day, traders are divided on what happens next.

The biggest single option trade of the day was a $7.5 million purchase of almost 11,000 75-strike puts expiring in mid-December. The trader paid above the ask, suggesting an eagerness to get the trade done at a cost of $6.85, according to SpotGamma. With Anthropic trading at $77, the position needs Anthropic to drop 11% by expiry to pay off.

"Anthropic remains bumpy as investors add risk to other names," said noted tech analyst Dan Ives.

Only one obvious call purchase appeared among the top 10 trades: a 3,000-lot purchase of 85-strike calls expiring Jan. 15 that cost $7.10 each, for a total of $2.2 million. That trade needs Anthropic to rally 19% within the next two months to break even.

Arguably the most interesting action was a string of almost a dozen put sales, ranging from $400,000 to $1.6 million each, all within moments of each other, selling the 120-strike puts expiring Dec. 18. The puts bring in $40 each, meaning the breakeven for the trade is at $80. Selling in-the-money puts in high-volatility stocks is often a way for traders to get long stock without the capital requirements of buying the underlying equity.
