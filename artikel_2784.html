2026 NFL Trade Deadline Fantasy Fallout: Colts keep Jonathan Taylor, Chiefs deal for Josh Downs, 49ers land Keenan Allen

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The NFL trade deadline is complete and fantasy football is in a whirlwind! Fantasy analyst Joel Smyth breaks down some key deals. And go here for Hayden Winks' breakdown of Kirk Cousins' return to Minnesota.

This trade is not only a win but a lifesaver for Josh Downs' fantasy outlook (you can read the full trade terms here). He's the dynasty fantasy winner of the day if he re-signs with the Chiefs, and he can finally be usable in redraft leagues!

The Colts lost Alec Pierce to injury and traded Michael Pittman Jr., yet Downs was still limited to a sub-20% target share. Kansas City has a similar lack of pass-catchers as Indianapolis, but gives Downs more of a chance as one of the most talented receivers in a new offense. He should fit well on a team that has the lowest average depth of target in the NFL.

I'm worried for Rice once Downs is playing full snaps, but researching helped ease concerns a little bit. The negatives: Rice is a volume-dependent WR. He had been recently looking much improved compared to the first few weeks of the season. However, his style is very similar to Downs', running over 50% of routes from the slot, and having over 70% of receiving fantasy points coming on throws under 10 air yards. The big positive here is the schedule, not in terms of fantasy points allowed to WRs, but the game scripts ahead. In a five-game stretch, the Chiefs play the Bills, Rams, Bengals, Patriots and 49ers, all teams that can bump up the passing volume and scoring. Surprisingly, the Chiefs rank third in pass rate over expected, behind only the Packers and Bengals; they have just been consistently in the lead.

The Chiefs' upcoming schedule might be more impactful for Mahomes than Downs, although the deal is a positive. The passing production for Mahomes over the past few years has been modest in fantasy, often relying on his rushing for his ceiling, and I'm not sure this massively changes anything. It's a plus, but maybe a 1-PPG bump compared to having a different wideout in the game.

Jonathan Taylor is the lone survivor in Indianapolis. After dealing Quenton Nelson, DeForest Buckner, Keenan Allen (more on him below) and Josh Downs, the Colts decided to hold on to Taylor in a failing season without their first-round draft pick available. The share of touches that has dipped down to 63% in the last few weeks should rise back up for Taylor, as there is less reason to save his legs. The only other positives are the schedule should keep some games close, and the receiving, which has often been underrated for Taylor, should be solid overall. However, the negatives win outright, with Taylor's ceiling coming when the touchdowns pile up. Since Week 5, the Colts offense ranks 31st in touchdowns per game with 1.4. He's still startable in fantasy, but the long-term risk is elevated.

Allen has quietly put together a solid fantasy season, outscoring Downs with 10.1 PPR PPG while inside the top 20 WRs in targets. The targets should still be available in San Francisco (go here for the full trade details), with no WR having over a 10% target share outside of Mike Evans. The 49ers have been fantasy gold for receivers this decade, often overlooked because of the lack of one sticking long term. Since 2020, they rank first in fantasy points per target and still second in 2026 to the Cincinnati Bengals.

Especially when other wideouts are not available, Allen can continue to be played as a flex option, now more confidently.
