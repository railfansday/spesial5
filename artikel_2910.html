Giants-Jaguars: Instant grades after New York’s victory over Jacksonville

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The New York Giants defeated the now 7-3 Jacksonville Jaguars on Sunday to advance to 6-3-1. New York controlled the game, and Jacksonville was sloppy and undisciplined for much of the game. Still, the Giants' inability to put down an opponent after establishing a lead caused some fear in the second half. New York was up 20-6 at halftime, and was receiving the ball, but a missed Dominic Zvada field goal and a fumble by Cam Skattebo set an unfortunate tone for the offense throughout the second half.

New York went three-and-out three times in the second half, and four-and-out once. The offense continues to struggle, but the defense responded well. New York went 4 of 15 on third down and finished with just 238 yards of offense, but 13 Jacksonville penalties, two turnovers, and constant pressure on Trevor Lawrence were enough for New York to earn the victory. Here are the immediate grades from the Giants' sixth win of the season

The Giants offense struggled again. They were particularly bad in the second half. J.J. McCarthy finished 18 of 38 for 173 yards with a touchdown — a bad 4.6 yards per completion. McCarthy also missed some big-time throws to Darnell Mooney. He did have 33 yards on the ground and came up with a critical 19-yard pass to Malachi Fields to seal the victory after struggling throughout the second half; he went seven straight throws with an incompletion in the second half. It wasn't pretty, but the offense did enough to get the win.

Jameis Winston handled the red zone duties on the Giants' first touchdown drive. He checked out of a run and threw a bad ball to Isaiah Likely that could have been caught. The Giants went for it on fourth down, and Winston scored on the tush push.

New York struggled to consistently run the football. They averaged just 3.1 yards per carry, and that's beefed up by McCarthy's 4.7 YPC. Cam Skattebo — who had a costly fumble in the second half — had 14 carries for just 34 yards, but did catch three passes for 14 yards. Devin Singletary received snaps to spell Skattebo, but neither running back did much on the ground. The pass protection from Skattebo, though, was good, from what I could tell, once again.

The Giants' receivers only had 173 collective yards; 55 were to Malik Nabers, who secured seven of 12 targets with a touchdown. Malachi Fields caught a key 19-yard dig route that helped the Giants secure the victory, but the offense — in general — was quiet for much of the game. We did see Jalin Hyatt catch one pass for 4 yards and Xavier Gipson had a catch for 6 yards. Darnell Mooney created a lot of separation, but could not secure any of his five targets — McCarthy missed him badly on a deep post route.

The Giants tight ends were not heavily involved. Isaiah Likely caught one pass for 35 yards, and Chris Manhertz caught one ball for 14 yards. Theo Johnson had a clutch 15-yard grab over the middle of the field on third down, but Skattebo fumbled on the next play.

The Giants offensive line struggled early against Jacksonville; J.J. McCarthy faced two free rushers on the first two plays of the game. The pass blocking stabilized, and McCarthy was only sacked once, albeit there were too many free rushers. The offensive line struggled to create space at the point of attack, which is an annoying reality that has persisted for much of the season. Overall, the offensive line was less than stellar.

D.J. Reader picked up a first-quarter sack of Trevor Lawrence after a game shared with Abdul Carter, who spun back inside to help Reader finish the sack. New York's defense was STOUT and shut down Jacksonville's rushing attack. The defensive performance was an A+ overall, with Reader highlighted for four tackles and a sack, and C.J. Okoye with one STOP on early downs. Shelby Harris contributed three tackles.

Abdul Carter told the world to WATCH and he SHOWED UP with a big-time first quarter sack and a second down sack in the second quarter, as well as some valuable pressures. He timed the snap magnificently and strung moves together all game. Carter was sensational. However, Jacksonville figured out his timing in the fourth quarter, and drew an encroachment penalty.

Kayvon Thibodeaux was flying around the field and making important assisted tackles in short-yardage situations. He had a free run — as Dennard Wilson is one to dial up — at Trevor Lawrence, but could not get him on the ground. Dawuane Smoot had a sack negated, but continues to be a solid practice squad call-up.

The Giants linebackers were flying all over the field. Arvell Reese remains a heavy hammer that punishes offensive players. Micah McFadden missed a key fourth-down tackle on the Jaguars' first offensive drive, but he made several tackles throughout the game, including a few short-yardage stops. McFadden had Lawrence tee'd up multiple times throughout the game but struggled to get him down to the deck. McFadden was dinged up at the end of the game after missing a tackle in space against Chris Rodriguez Jr. Both McFadden and Arvell Reese had seven tackles; Zaire Barnes saw snaps and had four tackles.

The Giants used multiple cornerbacks in the game, as Greg Newsome II and Tae Banks missed time during the contest. Tae Banks made an early impact by intercepting Trevor Lawrence on the Jaguars' second offensive drive. Dru Phillips had a pair of HUGE pass deflections: one on a fourth-and-one route in the red zone and the other on third-and-goal with 3:43 left in the game, which prompted Cam Little's fourth field goal. He did, though, miss a few tackles. Phillips did surrender a third-and-twenty on a deep corner versus Washington.

Paulson Adebo received snaps after Newsome left and did not return; he made a great play on the football in Cover-4 on a deep post to Parker Washington on a first-down shot that went INC. And then, on the following play, Paulson Adebo intercepted Trevor Lawrence on a possible miscommunication with a Jaguars receiver.

Jevon Holland set a tone early by dislodging the football from Parker Washington. Holland was around the line of scrimmage making tackles, and he almost came away with an interception in the end zone on Jacksonville's second drive. Holland led the team with nine tackles, and Ar'Darious Washington had three.

Dominic Zvada is frustrating. He made a kick from the right hash! It started the Giants scoring, and then the rookie booted a 54-yard field goal at the end of the first half – AGAIN – from the right hash. It's a positive development for the rookie kicker. However, Zvada then missed a 32-yard field goal from the right hash to start the third quarter, and made his fourth kick. He remains inconsistent and borderline unreliable.

Jordan Stout averaged 60 yards per punt. He is very good at what he does. Xavier Gipson handled one punt and returned it for 27 yards, while also looking capable on his kickoff returns. The kick coverage failed on the first kickoff of the game, but Tyler Nubin made his presence known multiple times on coverage plays — he finished the game with three tackles and is becoming one of the best special teams players on the team.
