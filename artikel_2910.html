Should You Buy Take-Two Interactive After the GTA VI Release?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The long-awaited GTA VI video game is expected to deliver a "historic year" for Take-Two Interactive.

By Dave Kovaleski – Nov 19, 2026 at 12:01AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Take-Two Interactive Software ›

Gamers have been waiting more than a decade -- 13 years, to be exact -- for the latest edition of the popular Grand Theft Auto (GTA) video game franchise, and it's finally here. It's not just Christmas morning for GTA fans; it is like Christmas after getting coal in your stocking 13 years in a row.

GTA VI, produced by Take-Two Interactive (TTWO +6.22%), comes out on Nov. 19 at midnight in the Pacific time zone, and Take-Two's stock surged in advance of the release. That’s late Wednesday evening or early Thursday morning, depending on how you look at it. On Wednesday, Nov. 18, Take-Two stock jumped nearly 7% to around $220 per share, but it is still down about 15% year-to-date.

Is the release of GTA VI the catalyst that Take-Two stock needs to take off?

According to industry reports, GTA VI has received some 17.1 million pre-orders, with net revenue from the sales at $1.65 billion. This is reportedly the largest pre-order total for a video game ever.

On Take-Two's recent earnings call, CEO Strauss Zelnick said pre-orders "are tracking far in excess of our initial expectations."

For fiscal 2027, Take-Two anticipates net bookings of $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion -- that is revenue from games sold and in-game purchases. This would be up some 21% at the midpoint from the $6.7 billion in net bookings in fiscal 2026. The difference is largely due to the boost from GTA VI.

For perspective, GTA V, the last version (which came out in 2013), has sold 235 million copies, making it the second-best-selling video game of all time, behind Minecraft. The entire Grand Theft Auto franchise has sold about 480 million copies.

GTA is not Take-Two's only game franchise. It also owns the NBA 2K and Red Dead Redemption franchises, along with mobile games through Zynga, including Words with Friends, but GTA is its primary revenue driver.

For fiscal 2027, Take-Two raised its revenue guidance to $7.98 billion to $8.18 billion, up from $7.9 billion to $8.1 billion, and boosted its earnings per share range to $0.57 to $0.75, up from $0.55 to $0.75. It also expects to generate more than $1 billion in net cash, which will help it grow.

Zelnick said it will be a "historic year for our company," as it looks to "explore emerging business opportunities, expand our geographic footprint, and pursue accretive M&A."

Take-Two stock has dropped about 15% year-to-date as net bookings fell 18% to $1.6 billion in its Q2 while revenue plunged 15% year over year to $1.50 billion. The firm had a net loss of $54 million, or $0.29 per share last quarter.

But for Q3, it expects to double its net bookings to $3.35 billion to $3.5 billion, due mostly to GTA VI sales, while net income is anticipated to soar to $250 million to $276 million, or $1.30 to $1.44 per share.

Some 94% of Wall Street analysts rate Take-Two stock as a buy, with a median price target of $290 per share. That would suggest 32% upside.

With a forward price-to-earnings ratio of 28, Take-Two stock is not cheap, but it is not overvalued either, relative to its growth expectations. The GTA VI effect will have a multi-year impact on sales, as GTA V has shown, with analysts anticipating $9 billion in revenue in fiscal 2028, as a consensus.

There may be a sell-the-news dip, as some of the recent gains have already been priced in, given strong pre-order results and widespread anticipation. Since it dipped to $183 per share on Oct. 30, Take-Two stock has jumped about 20% to $220 per share. So perhaps it's best to allow the price to settle a bit. But in the longer term, Take-Two stock looks like a solid buy.
