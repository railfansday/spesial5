Media Briefing: Publishers’ revenue growth defies traffic declines, but tougher comparisons loom

This week’s Media Briefing looks at how The New York Times, People Inc., News Corp and Reuters are navigating tougher year-over-year revenue comparisons after reporting strong growth despite declining referral traffic and AI disruption.

Declining referral traffic and AI disruption aren’t stopping some of the largest publishers from growing revenue, according to recent earnings. The New York Times, News Corp, People Inc., Reuters all reported gains in the third quarter, fueled by a mix of subscriptions, advertising and content licensing.

But their latest earnings also point to a common challenge heading into the final part of the year: sustaining that momentum as last year’s revenue gains make for tougher comparisons.

Notably, AI licensing has delivered real revenue growth for People Inc. and Reuters, especially as they look to diversify revenue beyond advertising. But the initial boost from those deals is becoming harder to replicate as those agreements mature.

“The short-term revenue win that publishers got by striking LLM deals helps for a quarter or four to eight, but it’s often at the expense of lost audience and inventory,” said Matt Prohaska, CEO and principal of Prohaska Consulting.

The New York Times is also bracing for tougher year-over-year comparisons in the fourth quarter, although in its case, the challenge stems from an advertising supply increase in 2025.

Meanwhile, News Corp’s latest earnings painted a more mixed picture, revealing publishers face different levels of exposure to declining audiences and traditional advertising pressures, even within the same company. The company posted strong revenue growth in its Dow Jones business, amid declines in its News Media division.

Digiday broke down what media executives said in their earnings call this week so far (still a few more to go, with USA Today Co., BuzzFeed and Ziff Davis later this week), and what it reveals about where publishers see their businesses heading next:

What was said: “The comps get tougher as the year goes on. Q4 is our biggest quarter in ads this year and last year. I’ll remind you we grew 25%. And last year we benefited from these sort of structural supply increases in certain parts of the portfolio.” – Meredith Kopit Levien, CEO, The New York Times Company.

What it really means: The Times benefited from a significant expansion of ad inventory last year, helping drive 25% YOY growth in digital advertising in Q4 2025. That sets a high baseline for the same quarter this year, making it more difficult to sustain that pace of growth, Kopit Levien said in the earnings call on Nov. 4. Digital advertising revenue growth has already slowed, from 20.7% YOY in Q2 2026 to 12% in Q3.

Because of this, the Times expects more modest advertising growth this quarter – though Kopit Levien said in the earnings call that the company will continue to add supply going forward, though it will be “more incremental and more across the portfolio.”

What was said: “The licensing revenue, as I said and as we saw, was really impressive. We continue to have real success across Apple News, AI, and other content partnerships. In Q4, those growth rates will slow some as we lap the Meta AI deal that we announced last Q4. We still expect full-year growth to be about what we delivered in licensing in the first half which translates to something roughly about 20% Q4 growth. Again, our focus is to get back to the growth we want and expect.” – Tim Quinn, CFO, People Inc.

What it really means: People Inc. has three publicly disclosed AI licensing deals, with OpenAI, Meta and Microsoft. The Meta deal Quinn referenced during the Nov. 3 earnings call was signed last December. While People Inc.’s licensing revenue grew 49% year over year in Q3 2026, Quinn cautioned investors against expecting a similar growth rate in the fourth quarter, as the company begins to lap the initial revenue boost from its Meta deal.

But People Inc. execs remained optimistic about the AI licensing market. “They will continue to evolve and there’s a big place for our content. There’s increasingly a place for recency — meaning new content — in that marketplace,” Quinn said in the earnings call.

However, the majority of People Inc.’s licensing revenue growth came from non-AI content licensing deals. Apple News has been a “great success,” Quinn said, alongside distribution deals with platforms such as Yahoo and MSN. In total, about 15 of People Inc.’s brands are successfully generating content licensing revenue, Quinn added. Overall, People Inc.’s digital revenue grew 2% YOY in Q3 2026.

What was said: “We expect Reuters growth to be flattish year-over-year in the fourth quarter due to lapping $5 million of transactional AI licensing revenue from the prior year period, along with tougher comps at agency and in portions of the professional business.”  – Gary Bischoping, CFO, Thomson Reuters.

What it really means: Reuters also has three publicly disclosed AI content licensing deals: with Meta and Microsoft in October 2024, and a deal with Amazon in May 2026. However, a Reuters executive previously told Digiday that the company has other AI content licensing deals that it has not spoken about publicly, and cited the company’s earnings as a sign of this. The full list of Reuters’ AI licensing partners and the number of deals is not clear. But what is clear is that revenue from these deals has helped boost Reuters’ overall business.

Reuters organic revenue grew 12% in Q3 2026, thanks to about $19 million of transactional generative AI content licensing in the quarter, per Bischoping. When excluding transactional AI licensing revenue in the third quarter of both 2026 and 2025, Reuters’ revenue grew 7% YOY. While AI licensing was a meaningful contributor to Reuters’ growth this past quarter, it can create volatility in year-over-year comparisons, as its not recurring revenue for the company.

Also, stronger prior-year performance in Reuters’ agency and professional businesses will mean tougher comps in Q4 2026, Bischoping said.

What was said: “News Media continues to navigate complex market pressures, including a challenging advertising environment and the ongoing evolution of the search and social algorithm landscape. As mentioned earlier, that search landscape will change dramatically with the advent of agentic AI. Revenues for the segment declined slightly versus prior year to $540 million.” – Robert Thomson, CEO, News Corp

What it really means: Revenue at News Corp’s Dow Jones business — which includes titles like The Wall Street Journal, Barron’s and MarketWatch — grew 8% year over year, fueled by subscriptions, licensing and its professional information businesses, according to its earnings released on Nov. 4. Digital advertising revenue increased 12%, matching the growth rate reported by The New York Times in Q3. These results are an example of how large publishers are finding ways to grow revenue beyond traditional traffic-dependent business models, even as some face tougher year-over-year comparisons ahead.

However, revenue at the company’s News Media division — which includes The Sun, The Times of London and the New York Post — declined 1% YOY. The division’s ad revenue fell 2% YOY, despite gains in subscription pricing and content licensing revenue. Audience declines were also evident: The Sun’s global monthly unique users fell 26% YOY in September, while the New York Post’s digital audience declined 11%.

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic have agreed to support a new robots.txt “policy token” standard that would allow publishers to opt out of their content being scraped and used for AI training, without needing to configure restrictions for each one individually, The Verge reported.

Condé Nast faces $35 million ad revenue shortfall

Condé Nast is reportedly on track to miss its Q4 U.S. advertising target by more than $35 million, amid senior sales departures and uncertainty over its commercial strategy, Adweek reported.

People Inc. chief executive Neil Vogel told staff in a memo that the company will cut 195 people, as a result of the “rapid changes in media,” The New York Times reported.

New York Times and other publishers sue Brave over AI scraping

The New York Times and several other newspapers (including the Daily News, the Chicago Tribune, and the Orlando Sentinel) have sued Brave, accusing the web browser company of illegally scraping their content for AI platforms and allowing users to bypass their paywalls, Reuters reported.
