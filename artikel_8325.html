When someone already has everything, skip one more thing to store and give them an experience, time back, a treat, or something personal. Tickets, a class, a house cleaning, a book of their own stories, or a donation in their name all fit. Pick by how they like to spend their time, then by your budget.

Most gift givers reach for an object anyway. In a survey of 219 of them for a study in the Journal of Consumer Research, researchers Cindy Chan and Cassie Mogilner found that 78% reported their most recent gift was an object rather than an experience.

In the paper’s four studies, published in December 2016, experiences strengthened a relationship more than objects did, by the recipient’s own account, whether or not the giver came along. The difference came from emotion, which recipients felt while using the gift, not when they opened it. An object chosen to stir emotion can strengthen the relationship too.

The 20 ideas below come in five groups, each with a typical price. They suit a parent, a boss, a partner, or a relative with no wish list, at a birthday, a retirement, or the holidays. Start with how they like to spend a free afternoon, then pick within your budget.

On a tight budget, give your own time as a gift instead, such as a night of babysitting or a home-cooked dinner.

These are objects, but ones chosen to stir emotion, the kind the study found can strengthen a relationship too.

If you’d rather make something, homemade gifts made in a batch keep the cost down.

If the gift needs a date, set it before you hand it over. Book the class, schedule the cleaning, or line up the sitter for a specific night, so they don’t have to arrange anything themselves.
