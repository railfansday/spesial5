Introducing: Nomadic Nurse Goes Hiking

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Hey hikers!! My name is Emilie and I am originally from flat land Ontario in Canada, but now I work as a nurse in beautiful British Columbia. I am hiking the Pacific Crest Trail in 2027 which is why I am here, because my friends and family will all be wondering where I am, so it is easier to give updates this way.

“Flat land” Ontario at 1000ft – top of Sleeping Giant

Though I love day hiking, I have never done a long distance thru-hike. I grew up car camping in Ontario and I have recently fallen in love with backpacking through mountains. I have hiked the West Coast Trail in BC and I am going to hike the W-trek in Patagonia in early 2027. I have always loved the outdoors and being active, which is why I have chosen to thru-hike in 2027.

Other than my outdoor hobbies, I am getting more into crafts such as crocheting and painting. One of my goals one day is to get a small watercolour book and paint the scenery of places I have hiked to! I don’t think that is very ultralight though, so perhaps I will save that for my post-PCT trail life.

I am a travel nurse, which means that I constantly move from town to town to work in under-serviced hospitals, so I am never in the same place for longer than a month. I regularly live out of a suitcase, so down-sizing to a backpack shouldn’t be too hard for me! I already know what my essentials are and have learned to not pack my fears into my suitcases.

I choose to work in rural British Columbia, which means I am lucky to see the rugged and less tourist-heavy regions of BC. I am writing this post from North Vancouver Island, which has one of the highest cougar concentrations in the world, though I have yet to see one. I am hoping that won’t be one of the “firsts” I get to experience on the PCT.

Views from rural British Columbia – not a bad place to work

Other than the obvious which is that hiking is better than working, the short version is that I am hiking because I can. Unfortunately, I do see a lot of sick patients in my job, a lot are older however some are young. I have learned from my patients that life is worth living when you are healthy and capable of experiencing it. “Do it while your body allows you” as they always say.

Society tells you that you need to work hard while you are young to enjoy your retirement. I see so many recent retirees dying, and wonder if they truly got to do the things that make them happy in life. On hard trail days, I will think of patients who aren’t able to be out there.  Stay tuned for a future post going more in depth regarding my reasons to hike.
