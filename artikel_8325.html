The Stairway To Heaven

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

After a crummy day yesterday I hope to enjoy today a bit more. I attempt to catch another sunset, have I used up all my sunset luck?

Gosh, yesterday’s post was tough. I need a solid bounce-back day. I am not sure how to force that, but polishing off the last of my cold brew coffee can’t be a bad start. I did at least have the excitement of entering a new state, New Jersey, today. I hoped that it would be less rocky than New York. The rocks covered in leafs had proven an unexpectedly formidable opponent. Here I thought I was in the clear once I finished the Whites…not so much it seems.

I headed southbound down the trail from Route 17A moments after sunrise, which is about 11:30AM this time of year. Well, not  really, but it feels crummy being on trail with the sun barely above the horizon only to find that it is already almost 8:00AM. The sun is increasingly not on my side, especially as I planned to take advantage of the flatter terrain and hike over 25 miles.

Shortly after starting out I passed a sign showing the distance to Georgia and Maine. It also advised hikers to “choose happiness”. I don’t know exactly how to do that, but I put on a smile anyway. Anything to avoid having another day like yesterday. In truth, I was over yesterday and feeling good, the extra caffeine had perked me up. After about 2 miles of calm trail, the trail climbed up to a rocky ridge which it followed for the next 4 miles. Looking East from the rocks was Greenwood Lake and the town on the shore of the same name. Planes flew over head every few minutes, a consequence of being under a flight path to the NYC airports, no doubt.

The rocky ridge got tougher as I ventured further south. It undulated up and down constantly and the rock was often smooth, which had the effect of turning my trail running shoes into trail skis. At this point I could feel my left ankle hadn’t bounced back from being twisted yesterday as well as I had hoped. It isn’t a serious problem, a bit like a bruise, but it makes me quite apprehensive when descending a section that doesn’t offer a solid landing spot for my left foot. I took it easy on the descents, but not easy enough. Yep, I ended up turning my ankle again, triggering a pang of pain in the same spot. Again, it wasn’t serious and didn’t slow me down, but these experience sure are getting old at this point.

I believe the light up there is known as “the sun”.

View from the trail above Greenwood Lake.

I had several nice views from the ridge, which eventually reaches the New Jersey border. The trail turns up the state line and, after a few last minute rock scrambles, mercifully smooths out. The next few miles passed through a relatively remote area without much to see but trees. Eventually I came to a trailhead with a clear view north to the town of New Milford.

Another few miles later I came to an enormous pile of rocks with a sign at the top pointing to a blue blaze trail to the Pinwheels vista. It turned out to be a viewpoint popular with day hikers. Far in the distance was a stone tower than I learned marked the high point in New Jersey. The AT passes just past the high point, so I’ll need to make a note to stop by. I took a few photos from the rocky outcrop and moved on to a seriously steep and rocky section of the trail known as the “Stairway to Heaven”. I found it to be as beautiful as it was treacherous. A valiant effort was made to build steps in the hillside, but the leaves had made them slippery. It was fortunate that the section of trail was popular, which did mean many of the leaves were kicked off the rocks. I was taking it slow, but, understandably, I was still quicker than most of the day hikers. I reassured several hikers that, having hiked most of the Appalachian Trail, they were tackling some very difficult terrain. A pair of hikers spoke to me on their way up. When I told them I was hiking the trail twice this year, one could only respond “Wowza!” I haven’t heard that in quite a while!

Rocks, or “ankle testers”, as I have come to refer to them.

The NYC skyline is just visible from the trail above Greenwood Lake.

One of many dirt roads the AT crosses in this remote stretch.

After descending the Stairway to Heaven the trail spends several miles traversing a marshy area, quite a bit of it on a boardwalk. The reeds stretched out a short distance from the trail and offered a different scene from recent days. At the end of the boardwalk the trail climbs up, eventually crossing over Pochuck Mountain. A rocky outcrop provided a great view to the west. My original plan for the day was to hike a few more miles south before turning around and camping at the Pochuck Mountain Shelter. This viewpoint was only another 0.8 miles north, so I could camp here since there was plenty of flat space. The only question was whether I could make it back in time for sunset.

Looking back up the rocks and Pinwheels Vista.

Crossing through the marsh on a boardwalk.

I balanced a cautious but expedient descent from Pochuck Mountain, crossing back into another wetland section. I hiked double time on the flat trail, reaching Carnegie Road after 5:00PM. A resident allows hikers to fill up their water bottles at their house, which was clutch as I tried to make time back to the rocky outcrop. I continued retracing my steps, increasing my pace until I looked like I’d lost my mind. I took big steps up Pochuck Mountain with time running out. I could see that clouds were moving in far in the distance. Sunset would either be epic or a washout.

Well, it was the latter. It turns out the sun was a little too far south. If it had been more to the west it would have been setting between clouds, and much more visible from the viewpoint. I had been on a hot streak with sunsets lately so I guess I was due for a dud. It happens. At least I had had a good day, and had a much shorter trip back to the van tomorrow.

Passing the viewpoint for the first time. The tower you can see on the highest hill marks the New Jersey high point.
