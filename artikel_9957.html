US allies deepen deal to guard AI supply chains against 'hostile actors' (cough, China, cough)

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Securing supply chains or coercing other nations to help Washington sideline Beijing and its AI ambitions?

Nine countries including the UK have signed a US declaration pledging to secure supply chains for AI tech, as pressure grows for tighter export controls following the high-profile resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.

The US is firming up its Pax Silica initiative on AI and supply chain security. Under the San Francisco declaration, signed on November 15, the coalition says it is entering a new phase that turns shared principles into a working partnership with shared responsibilities.

Pax Silica was announced in December last year, ostensibly for the participants to work together on vital parts of the global technology supply chain, including frontier foundation models, compute and semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, minerals refining and processing, and energy.

But the list of participants was notable for not including one country – China – leading many to suspect that Pax Silica is yet another effort by Washington to squeeze out its main rival in the global technology and economic stakes. Hong-Kong based South China Morning Post refers to it as Trump's "campaign to China-proof AI and chip supply chains."

The latest declaration states that "the security of our peoples cannot be separated from the security of our economies and those of our closest partners," and sets out what must be protected, as well as committing to "protect it together."

It asserts that protection should cover key foundational technologies essential to economic security, listing "software, materials, knowledge, components and inputs…essential to AI supply chains."

This includes anything that "theft, illicit transfer, or acquisition by hostile actors would confer a meaningful military or intelligence advantage, or undue economic leverage over the participants, or whose unauthorized transfer or misuse could pose significant risks to the economic and national security of the participants."

Also listed is the protection of critical infrastructure such as ports, terminals, transport, communications, and logistics networks, and the ICT and energy infrastructure these depend upon.

The declaration was jointly released by the US administration, and the governments of Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, United Arab Emirates, and the United Kingdom.

"The know-how to design a chip, the machine that makes it, and the port that ships it can all be in different countries," the US under-secretary of state for economic affairs Jacob Helberg told the Financial Times.

"What makes the San Francisco Declaration historic is that Pax Silica partners have agreed to treat the security of those technologies and the infrastructure behind them as a shared responsibility."

This move comes amid reports that both Republican and Democrat lawmakers want to push through further measures to prevent China from accessing advanced chips for AI processing.

It follows the recent news that Jensen Huang, the now former CEO of top GPU maker Nvidia, admitted to an affair with a woman suspected by the US of being a spy for Beijing. She worked for a Singapore-based company called Megaspeed that bought Nvidia hardware and was also suspected of making it available to Chinese users. US, Singaporean, and Malaysian police investigations are still probing whether chips were diverted or accessed remotely by Chinese firms.

American efforts to control Chinese access to its technology started under the first Trump administration, and continued under President Joe Biden’s leadership.

A report out last week claimed US export controls are not stopping AI labs in China from using restricted Nvidia chips, and many of their cutting-edge models were trained using Blackwell GPUs.

It is understood that President Trump is to meet with China's premier Xi Jinping this week for bilateral talks on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Co-operation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen. ®
