US blacklists Megaspeed hours after Jensen Huang returns to Nvidia

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Chip boss gets his old job back, but Washington isn't done investigating one of his former flame's businesses

Jensen Huang was barely back in charge of Nvidia when Uncle Sam slapped Megaspeed –­ the Singaporean cloud provider at the center of the scandal that cost him his job ­­– on its trade blacklist.

The US Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) has added Megaspeed and a number of affiliated businesses to its Entity List, restricting their access to American technology amid an ongoing investigation into the suspected diversion of advanced GPUs to China.

The final rule [PDF], filed Monday afternoon and scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on Wednesday, names seven businesses across Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand, including Megaspeed's owner, Swiftdata, and Malaysian subsidiary Speedmatrix.

Also in Washington's crosshairs is Apex Enterprise Solutions, a Singapore-based subsidiary of Chinese cloud computing giant Alibaba.

BIS said the businesses posed a significant risk of diverting advanced computing hardware to recipients or applications not authorized by the US government. Anyone looking to supply the seven companies with goods subject to US export controls will need a license, with applications subject to a "presumption of denial."

Despite the timing of Monday's announcement, the US government's interest in Megaspeed predates the revelations about Jensen Huang's private life by some considerable margin.

In a joint statement issued last week, the Departments of Justice and Commerce confirmed that the latter had opened an investigation into Megaspeed in 2024 after spotting suspicious activity while monitoring potential violations of semiconductor export controls.

Investigators requested records from Nvidia relating to both direct and indirect sales to Megaspeed in May 2025, and the GPU giant put the Singaporean outfit on its internal "no sell" list the following month. US investigators, accompanied by Singaporean law enforcement officials, subsequently visited Megaspeed's premises and interviewed its then-CEO, Alice Huang.

The Justice Department has since taken the lead on the criminal investigation.

Alice Huang, who is not related to the Nvidia boss, is a businesswoman with whom Jensen was said to be having an affair – he admitted to extramarital infidelity earlier this month, although he never name-checked the Megaspeed exec. According to reports, US intelligence officials suspected she was working on behalf of Chinese intelligence, though Beijing has rejected the espionage allegations.

Megaspeed maintains that its business is lawful and says Alice Huang formally left the company in August last year. It has also enlisted outside lawyers to conduct an internal investigation.

Nvidia's own Huang lasted all of 11 days in corporate exile. The chipmaker's board unanimously voted to reinstate its co-founder as president and CEO on Monday, less than a week after board chairman Harvey Jones indicated that bringing him back wasn't on the cards.

The U-turn followed a lobbying campaign by some of Silicon Valley's biggest names, including Elon Musk, and some rather more consequential intervention from the White House. President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he had personally persuaded Nvidia's directors to give Huang his job back.

Nvidia's board said it had received assurances from senior US government officials regarding the national security implications of Huang's return.

There are still plenty of people in Washington who have questions for Huang, however. The Nvidia boss is due to sit down with three congressional committees on December 1, with the chipmaker's general counsel, Tim Teter, scheduled to face lawmakers the following day.

Nvidia has also established a board-level committee to oversee national security and export control compliance, putting its trade compliance personnel under the supervision of three directors.

Nvidia shareholders may be delighted to have Jensen back, but the returning CEO has barely a week to prepare for his congressional grilling over the affair that helped bring about his departure.  ®
