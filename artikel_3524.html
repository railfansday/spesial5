Kim Zolciak Facing 6-Figure Default Judgment Over Defunct Business

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Kim Zolciak could be on the hook for six figures over her now-defunct business amid her son Kroy “KJ” Biermann Jr.’s sexual battery case.

Court documents obtained exclusively by Us show that Headway Capital requested a default judgment against Zolciak, 48, on Tuesday, November 17 — and it could cost her a pretty penny.

The financial institution sued the Real Housewives of Atlanta alum in April, claiming she entered into a business-use line of credit and security agreement for 8 Busy B’s LLC and allegedly failed to repay $75,000.

Now, Headway Capital is asking for that amount plus thousands more after Zolciak “failed to file any responsive pleadings” to the lawsuit, according to the docs obtained by Us.

The company claims it’s entitled to the principal amount of $75,000, $55,650 in accrued interest at the contract rate through August 13, 2024, and $11,778 in accrued pre-judgment interest at 7 percent from August 14, 2024, to November 11, 2026. It’s also asking for Zolciak to pay $14,266 in attorneys’ fees, bringing the total to nearly $167,000, if the judge signs off.

Us reached out to Zolciak’s rep for comment.

In July, the Bravolebrity was sued by Portfolio Recovery Associates, LLC, a debt collection company, over an alleged unpaid credit card bill of nearly $17,000.

As Us exclusively reported, Portfolio Recovery Associates, LLC, dismissed its lawsuit against Zolciak last month, marking a small legal win amid her son’s legal issues.

Biermann, 15, was arrested in August for aggravated sexual assault and aggravated sodomy. He was indicted on September 3 for aggravated child molestation, aggravated sexual battery, aggravated sodomy, false imprisonment and three counts of sexual battery against a child under the age of 16 stemming from an alleged April 2026 incident at a Lifetime Fitness facility in Alpharetta, Georgia.

Following his arrest, Biermann denied the allegations. “First and foremost, this is not the kind of information that should be for public dissemination. These are juveniles,” his lawyer at the time, Jason Sheffield, told Us in a statement. “Secondly, my client is 100 percent innocent and tried to present evidence to the arresting officer, including a passed polygraph by a notable and respected sex offender polygrapher.”

He continued, “Additionally, the officer’s report notes the [Georgia Bureau of Investigation] found no male DNA on or inside the areas the alleged victim claimed she was assaulted. Had the officer sat down with me, I feel certain she would’ve never made an arrest.”

Amid Biermann’s sexual assault case, prosecutors uncovered videos on the teen’s phone involving alleged sexual encounters with a family member under the age of 12. Investigators claim they discovered he used “predatory [and] derogatory” language toward the alleged victim.

Neither Biermann nor his attorney have addressed those accusations.

Biermann’s next scheduled court date is a status hearing on January 20, 2027.
