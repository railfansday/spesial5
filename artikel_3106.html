Got $500? Buy This Top Artificial Intelligence (AI) Chip Stock Following the Latest Sell-Off

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Applied Materials stock is too cheap to ignore given the incredible growth it has been clocking.

By Harsh Chauhan – Nov 18, 2026 at 6:36AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Applied Materials ›

The semiconductor sector has witnessed turbulence in the second half of 2026, as evidenced by a 28% drop in the PHLX Semiconductor Sector since the beginning of July.

The pullback has been triggered by various factors, including a September rate hike by the Federal Reserve, the possibility of further hikes to keep inflation under control, and the viability of robust artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure spending amid a high-interest rate environment. These headwinds have crushed some top semiconductor stocks lately, including Applied Materials (AMAT -1.49%).

Shares of Applied Materials have retreated 46% since the beginning of July. However, a closer look at the company's latest quarterly results clearly suggests that the stock's sell-off is overdone. Let's look at the reasons why Applied Materials stock is a top buy right now.

Applied Materials released fiscal 2026 fourth-quarter results (for the year ended Oct. 25) on Nov. 12. The company's revenue jumped by an impressive 53% from the year-ago period to a record $10.41 billion. In fact, its quarterly revenue growth was significantly higher than the annual revenue increase of 21% to $34.45 billion.

Even better, Applied Materials' non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) increased by 92% year over year to $4.16 in the previous quarter. That was well above the company's fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS growth of 37% to $12.89.

It is quite easy to see why Applied Materials' growth accelerated remarkably in the previous quarter. The company sells wafer and fabrication (WFE) equipment that's used by semiconductor foundries, integrated device manufacturers (IDMs), and memory manufacturers to make chips. The strong demand from the foundry/logic and memory markets for advanced AI chips has been a massive tailwind for Applied Materials.

Importantly, Applied Materials expects the momentum to continue in 2027, predicting a 30% increase in WFE demand next year. What's more, the company is confident it can grow faster than the WFE market by gaining more share in this space. Industry association SEMI predicts that the WFE market could clock 23% growth in 2026, and Applied Materials' growth last quarter clearly suggests that it is growing significantly faster than the broader market.

SEMI expects a 21% increase in WFE sales next year. However, Applied Materials is more confident in this market's growth due to the construction of new semiconductor fabs worldwide. Management noted on the latest earnings call that the "demand for advanced semiconductors remains much higher than supply, and multi-year investments [in] new fab capacity are needed for the industry to close this supply-demand gap."

Consulting firm McKinsey also recently raised its 2030 global semiconductor industry revenue forecast to $2.3 trillion, up from $1.6 trillion, noting that demand for advanced chips to support the AI build-out will be a tailwind for the sector. So, Applied Materials' impressive growth momentum isn't going away any time soon.

This explains why the company's guidance for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 points to an improvement in its financial performance. Applied Materials expects $11.1 billion in revenue this quarter, implying a 58% increase over the prior-year period. The non-GAAP EPS forecast of $4.35 points to an 83% increase over last year.

What's worth noting is that Applied Materials' revenue fell slightly in the same quarter last year, while its EPS was flat on a year-over-year basis. So, healthy investments in AI data center infrastructure are now driving stronger growth, and that trend seems sustainable given the semiconductor industry's long-term growth potential.

Each share of Applied Materials trades at just under $400, as of this writing. So, if you've $500 in investable cash right now after meeting your expenses, saving for difficult times, and clearing high-interest debt, you can consider putting that money into Applied Materials stock.

That's because the company is not just clocking terrific growth; its recent slide has made it extremely attractive from a valuation perspective. Applied Materials trades at just 21 times forward earnings, a discount to the iShares Semiconductor ETF's price-to-earnings ratio of 39. That's quite attractive for a company whose EPS is poised to grow at a healthy rate over the next three years.
