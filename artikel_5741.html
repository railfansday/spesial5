Cool Temps and HF; Death After On-Field Collision; Shave and Shake in Stenting

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

— Recent developments of interest in cardiovascular medicine

A large nationwide study found an association between cooler ambient temperature and heart failure (HF) hospitalizations. (JAMA Network Open)

Energy drinks and stimulant supplements were frequently involved in cases of cardiac arrest and heart attack in young people, according to an FDA adverse event report analysis highlighted by the American Heart Association.

Abbott announced FDA approval for the Amplatzer Paraclose PVL Occluder, a transcatheter device for paravalvular leakage around a surgical heart valve.

The company also shared that the next-generation Amulet 360 Left Atrial Appendage Occluder had strong 1-year closure results.

A Spanish soccer player died in the hospital after having gone into cardiac arrest following an on-field collision. (AP)

An intervention to strengthen emergency dispatch and activate trained community responders was linked to increased bystander-initiated CPR before the arrival of emergency medical services for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, a cluster-randomized trial in France showed. (Lancet Public Health)

Heart muscle has the unique ability to burn glucose in the cytosol while simultaneously burning lactate in the mitochondria. (Nature)

In a randomized trial of people with severe pure aortic regurgitation, transfemoral transcatheter aortic valve replacement reduced 1-year clinical events at the cost of more 30-day bleeding and new permanent pacemaker implantation. (Circulation)

The SIRONA trialists compared sirolimus- versus paclitaxel-coated balloons for femoropopliteal artery disease and found no major differences in walking function and quality of life, though clinically driven target lesion revascularization rates did favor the sirolimus group. (JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions)

Ten years into the DECISION-CTO trial, an initial percutaneous coronary intervention strategy for chronic total occlusion was not associated with a survival benefit. (Journal of the American College of Cardiology)

Mount Sinai operators achieved better stent placement among calcified coronary nodules if they employed both rotational atherectomy and lithotripsy to aid stent expansion, a so-called "Shave and Shake" strategy, the NODULE-SHOCK trial showed.

Among stroke patients undergoing mechanical thrombectomy, lower cervical curvature was associated with greater recanalization success, researchers found from a retrospective analysis. (Stroke)

In Dutch patients with angina and nonobstructive coronary arteries, coronary microvascular dysfunction apparently had no effect on clinical outcomes, registry data showed. (JAMA Cardiology)

Preterm infants tended to have worse survival with pharmacological therapy for patent ductus arteriosus, without any reduction in bronchopulmonary dysplasia, according to a meta-analysis. (Lancet Child & Adolescent Health)
