Van Gogh painting sells for record $190.4m after row over its Nazi history

Auction house Sotheby's has sold a painting by Vincent van Gogh for a record $190.4m (Â£144.8m).

The sale of ChÃ¢taigniers en fleurs ("Chestnut Trees in Blossom") shatters the renowned artist's previous auction record of $117.2 million set in 2022.

The historic sale in New York on Wednesday comes after an eleventh-hour agreement was reached to resolve a dispute regarding claims the art piece was previously sold under duress during the rise of the Nazis.

ChÃ¢taigniers en fleurs was painted in 1890 in the final two months of Van Gogh's life. It is considered one of the most important works of art from that period to remain in private hands, according to Sotheby's.

It was not immediately clear who purchased the painting. The BBC has contacted Sotheby's for comment.

The oil painting, which was estimated to carry a value of $180m (Â£136m) prior to the sale, was put up for auction as part of a set called the Blaquier-Arrieta Collection.

Sotheby's announced in the hours before the auction that it had resolved a dispute between the Blaquier-Arrieta family and the heirs of the Fischer family - the prior German owners of the work - regarding the sale.

Representatives acting for heirs of the Fischer family had claimed the painting may have been sold under duress during the rise of the Nazis.

A lawyer for the family's heirs reportedly wrote to Sotheby's on their behalf, telling the auction house that the painting was sold in Amsterdam before World War Two because of financial losses the Fischer family's publishing company suffered under the Nazis. The BBC has contacted the lawyer for comment.

"In recognition of the painting's complex Nazi-era history and the suffering endured by the Fischer family during the Holocaust, the two families have reached a mutually satisfactory resolution and the work will be sold pursuant to their agreement," Sotheby's said on Wednesday.

Sotheby's website details the provenance of the oil painting and how it has researched the artwork changing hands since 1890.

It says Samuel Fischer - a famous German publisher - acquired it 1909 before it was passed down in his family after his death in 1934.

The painting was deposited with Paul Cassirer in Amsterdam in 1932 or 1933 before it went to Henriette and Marcel Kapferer in Paris in 1936, Sotheby's said.

The Blaquier family acquired it in 1975 and owned it for more than half a century before the latest sale, the auction house said.
