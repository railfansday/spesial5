After Xi Purges China’s Generals, a Military Inquisitor Rises

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The rise of Zhang Shengmin suggests that China’s top brass remains embroiled in investigations, potentially complicating preparations for war over Taiwan.

When China’s leader, Xi Jinping, endorsed the expulsion of dozens of disgraced officials at a recent Communist Party meeting in Beijing, one man stood out as a clear winner.

Taking his new place at the front of the meeting was Gen. Zhang Shengmin, the somber-faced enforcer of Mr. Xi’s purges inside the military. He had been promoted to the Politburo, the party’s top leadership council, the latest step in his improbable rise from low-profile inspector to a position second only to Mr. Xi in China’s military hierarchy.

General Zhang has been the principal overseer of Mr. Xi’s campaign to purify the People’s Liberation Army. His latest elevation signaled that the investigations, indoctrination drives and loyalty tests could continue for years, as Mr. Xi searches for officers he believes are irreproachably loyal, experts and officials said.

“Twist the blade inward, with strict measures to cure disorder,” China’s main military newspaper said after the party meeting that was held late last month, referring to the continued discipline campaign.

Three officials in Taiwan said they believed Mr. Xi’s loyalty campaign would keep the Chinese military focused on its own problems. That could hinder preparations for possible war over Taiwan, the self-governed island democracy that Beijing claims. The officials spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss internal assessments.

The leadership uncertainty comes as China’s military has continued to pressure Taiwan. Dozens of P.L.A. ships held unannounced drills in seas off the Chinese coast in recent weeks, with some, including a carrier strike group, still remaining, Joseph Wu, the secretary-general of Taiwan’s National Security Council, posted on X on Thursday.
