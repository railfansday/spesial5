The virtues of patience

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

It’s 13 December 2020. Arsenal versus Burnley.

It was freezing and wet at the Emirates. Only 2,000 fans had been allowed in because of Covid, and my mum, my dad, my mate Ben and I made up four of them. We were socially distanced and wearing masks. My glasses kept steaming up.

This was supposed to be fun. Watching my team.

Willian was grotesquely slow. Alex Lacazette was profligate. Rob Holding was bald. Very bald. And Granit Xhaka was angry. So angry, in fact, that in the second half he put his hands around the throat of some opposition goon and, after a VAR review, got himself sent off. There were boos. As many as 2,000 people could muster, anyway.

Then our captain, Pierre Emerick-Aubameyang, headed a corner into his own net. You couldn’t make it up. Except you could. It was the only goal of the game.

By then the steam fogging up my specs might as well have been coming out of my ears. In his player ratings, Andrew M called Xhaka the ‘King of the Idiots’ and described the match and the result as ‘utterly shit’. Which was fair.

It was our seventh defeat in 10 league games and our fourth in a row at home, something that hadn’t happened since 1953. We were 15th, five points above the relegation zone.

And who was top? Sp*rs. Managed by everyone’s favourite sociopath, Jose Mourinho. Having beaten us in the North London derby the week before, they couldn’t stop gloating.

I’d bought into the Arteta project hook, line and sinker. I still wanted him to be the right man for the job. I was just finding it harder to explain why I thought he was. The FA Cup win had been four months earlier. It felt like years.

The tree, to paraphrase our manager, was shaking.

It had been shaking for a good two and a half years by then. The soggy bottom of the Arsene Wenger era had left its scars. Those two 3-0 defeats to City in the space of a week still make me wince. Then came the hope that Unai Emery might get us back on track. By the time we’d seen how that played out, I’d got into the habit of talking myself down whenever things looked up. Maybe we’ll get better. Give it time.

The trouble with rock bottom is you never know if there’s another floor below it. Was Burnley ours? I’m still writing about it six years later, so it was probably mine. There were games against Southampton and Everton before Christmas, and I can barely remember either of them.

Then came Boxing Day and a visit from Frank Lampard’s Chelsea. Is it reductive to say that was the day everything changed for Mikel Arteta’s Arsenal? Probably. But my word, watching Bukayo Saka, Emile Smith Rowe and Gabriel Martinelli tear about the place was the fix this addict needed. We went on an unbeaten run after that. Seven whole games! And for the first time in weeks, I was looking forward to what came next.

And here we are in November 2026. Reigning champions of England. Top of the league again. Five points clear. Odds-on favourites to retain the title after 10 games. Sure, throwing hundreds of millions of American dollars at the problem helped. But if there’s a lesson in all this, it’s that hard work and patience pay off.

Which is why I’m using today’s blog to appeal directly to Sir Jim Ratcliffe to give Michael Carrick a stay of execution.

Come on, man, you’re probably still paying off Erik ten Hag and Ruben Amorim, maybe even Ralf Rangnick. Do you really want to go back to the drawing board all over again? Carrick is the man for the Manchester United hot seat. Just look at the way he wore a poppy-embroidered armband as well as a poppy badge at the weekend. The man doesn’t do half measures. He’s a two-scoops guy. Surely that’s something you, a multibillionaire British-born petrochemicals tycoon who lives in Monaco, can get on board with?

I know Mauricio Pochettino might be tempting. He’s got floppy hair, a tan and, despite the Sp*rsy stench, has come to resemble a rather huggable teddy bear. But think it through, Jim. What if he comes in and everybody perks up? It’s much harder to make job cuts and to force out season ticket holders when people are enjoying themselves. Best get through that process first while Michael turns the oil tanker round. It’s not far off, promise, you’re on a two-game unbeaten streak!

Anyway, what would sacking Carrick say about patience? Look at Arteta. There were people who wanted him gone after Burnley and, as you’ve just read, I was close to joining them. Imagine if the club had listened to me. All those years of work, all that progress, all the joy I’ve had watching Arsenal lately. We could have thrown it all away because a man in a damp mask couldn’t see out of his glasses.

So give Carrick time. Give him January. Give him a full pre-season. And if things still haven’t picked up by this time next year, ask yourself whether one full pre-season, and £100 million on Youri Tielemans and Andrey Santos, was ever really enough.

I can only speak for myself, Jim, but I haven’t looked forward to watching Manchester United this much in years.

The Interlull kicks into gear tonight. David Raya, Mikel Merino and Martin Zubimendi are in action for Spain against Czechia, Riccardo Calafiori’s Italy take on France, and England face Croatia at Wembley.

Ezri Konsa and Myles Lewis-Skelly will both miss out at Wembley. Konsa is working on his fitness but will be available to face Spain on Sunday, while our Hale Ender is suspended after picking up two bookings in the Nations League.

Elsewhere, ahead of Sweden’s game against Bosnia and Herzegovina, Viktor Gyokeres has been speaking to the press. Like Martin Zubimendi, he says he’s happy at Arsenal.

“I want to,” he said, when asked if he’s keen to stay at the Emirates.

“It feels good when I’ve played. We’ve won most of the games. It was a tough loss in the cup (against Fleetwood). Otherwise it’s felt good for myself and the team, says the forward. I’ve been doing well lately when I’ve been playing. Right now it feels good.

“The communication with (Mikel) Arteta is good. We talk about football and how we should play. Before a match, if I’m going to start, we talk about the opponents and what I should think about.”

He was also asked whether the criticism he gets bothers him.

“Sometimes it might get to me, but overall not so much. If I score goals and assists in the Champions League, I couldn’t care less what they say. It’s nice to do those kinds of performances. To deliver for myself and for the team. That’s where the focus lies.”

To be fair, I’ve not seen any serious chatter about selling Viktor in January. Whatever you think of him as a player, it would be a strange move unless we’re planning to spend serious money on the attack. And even then it would feel like an unnecessary risk given Kai isn’t exactly pulling up trees and new faces will need an adaptation period.

If he’s happy keeping his head down and doing his bit for the team, I’d keep him around until the summer and reassess then.

Ok. I’m going to leave it there for today. All eyes on Old Trafford, I guess.
