How Should We Memorialize the Trump Era?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Anti-monumentalism responds not with grand equestrian statues or neoclassical columns, but with absence and anxiety, minimalism and melancholy.

At the intersection of the Harburger Ring and Sand Street in Hamburg, Germany, there is a most unusual public memorial, visible only to those who already know what they’re looking for.

When the “Monument Against Fascism” was unveiled in 1986, it was a 40-foot-tall (~12.2 m) rectangular aluminum pillar covered in lead sheeting on which Hamburg residents were invited to inscribe their own pledges to fight authoritarianism. Over the next seven years, the column was lowered into the ground in stages, so that today the monument is entirely subterranean, visible only through a small glass window next to the plaque.

Designed by married conceptual artists Esther Shalev-Gerz and Jochen Gerz and commissioned by the Municipal Council of Hamburg-Harburg, the monument was intended to confront both Germany’s Nazi past and the reemergence of far-right politics in a provocative, arresting manner that purposefully eschewed the monumental architecture that fascists themselves exalted. The result was Shalev-Gerz and Gerz’s consummate anti-monument, a reminder that the war against fascism is as personal as someone’s handwriting and as continual as the ground beneath our feet.
