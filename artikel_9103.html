Student Photographer Who Lost Right Eye While Covering LA Protest Sues Federal Government

A student photographer who lost his right eye after he was allegedly struck by a projectile fired by a Department of Homeland Security (DHS) agent while covering a “No Kings” protest is suing the federal government.

Warning: The following photos contain content that some may find upsetting.

Tucker Collins, an 18-year-old freshman at the University of Southern California (USC), was left partially blind after photographing demonstrators in downtown Los Angeles on the afternoon of March 28. Collins was allegedly photographing a confrontation between protesters and federal agents when he was allegedly hit by a less-lethal projectile.

Footage shows Collins taking pictures of demonstrators outside a federal facility before suddenly falling to his knees as a crowd-control projectile reportedly struck him in the right eye, fracturing bones in his eye socket. Collins was taken to a hospital, where he underwent two and a half hours of surgery in an attempt to save the eye. The injury was too severe, and the eye was surgically removed.

Collins sued the federal government on Monday, alleging that law enforcement officers used excessive force and violated his civil rights. According to a report by The New York Times, his attorneys allege that Department of Homeland Security (DHS) officers “assaulted” and “used unjustified force against him” as he photographed the protest.

The lawsuit seeks various damages from the federal government, “including past and future medical expenses, prosthetic and ophthalmologic care, educational expenses, and loss of earning capacity.”

Collins’s attorney, V. James DeSimone, and his family are also demanding criminal charges against the officer responsible for shooting him. They argue that the officer’s actions violated California law governing the use of projectile launchers by law enforcement.

The lawsuit also details how Collins’s injuries have limited his ability to pursue activities he once enjoyed, including photography. Collins says he is no longer able to safely photograph public events similar to the protest where he was injured. DeSimone also condemned the lack of criminal accountability in the wake of the shooting.

“It is deeply disturbing that the maiming of Tucker Collins, resulting in the loss of his eye, was widely reported and captured on video and, yet, there is no law enforcement response, to date, on the Federal, State or Local level to prosecute the offending officer for his crime,” DeSimone says in a statement. “Once again, it is left to civil rights attorneys to attempt to achieve some semblance of justice for the permanent and irreversible harm caused to this courageous young man.”
