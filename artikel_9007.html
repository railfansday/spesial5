Sid Lee Architecture references "rhythm of the vine" for curved Ontario hotel

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Photography by James Brittain (unless stated otherwise)

Canadian studio Sid Lee Architecture has created a curved-glass hotel that takes cues from the burgeoning wine region of Ontario.

The Montreal-based studio recently unveiled The Clayfield Hotel, a 60-room hotel with a restaurant, ballroom and rooftop terrace in Niagara-on-the-Lake.

The rounded 94,000-square-foot (8,732-square metre) hotel sits on a property in the historic village that is being transformed by winemakers.

“The Clayfield was conceived as a new hub for Niagara-on-the-Lake, a place where wine culture and hospitality find a home,” Martin Leblanc, architect and co-founding partner of Sid Lee Architecture, told Dezeen.

“Its layered contours, rounded geometry and rhythmic wood elements translate the surrounding vineyard landscape into a building that celebrates the region’s contribution to Canadian winemaking.”

The hotel was conceived as a pavilion set in the emerging townscape between vineyards and the Clayfield Commons housing development on an agricultural road.

The curved form allows access and connection to its surroundings in multiple directions.

“The hotel acts as a threshold between these typologies, connecting the vineyard and the denser village-like development around it,” Leblanc said.

“Its exterior wood elements recall the rhythm of the vine rows, the layered concrete refers to the clay soil and the reflective glass registers the changing light, sky and seasons.”

The studio said that the organic, natural flow of a hotel is essential to the lasting success of the design.

Working back and forth between the architecture and the interior design, the studio laid out spaces to progress from public to private and back again, integrating moments for visitors to discover the landscape.

“As visitors approach and circulate around the building, it frames changing views of the vines, the plaza and the emerging neighbourhood,” Leblanc said. “The lobby acts as a gateway to the vineyard, while the rooftop terrace reconnects the hotel experience with its broader surroundings.”

Meanwhile, the staircase acts as a connector, transition and centrepiece between the plaza, lobby and upper levels.

“Inside, we chose a palette that speaks to the natural beauty of Niagara’s wine country: walnut panelling, concrete, dark amaranth-coloured leather, greige limestone and woven fabrics,” Leblanc said, calling out the limestone registration desk chiselled from the Niagara Escarpment and the restaurant’s curved bar that resembles the shape of the building.

With mostly Teknion furniture, each space represents a season of the vineyard, from spring in the lobby to winter in the guest rooms with more quiet, reserved furnishings that complete the rooms that fan out from the rounded concrete strata.

Founded in 2009, Sid Lee Architecture has six offices across North America and Europe. The studio previously remodelled its own offices in an I.M. Pei office building, overhauled the interiors of the iconic Fairmont Queen Elizabeth Hotel and created an artisan-inspired workplace for video game designers – all in Montreal.
