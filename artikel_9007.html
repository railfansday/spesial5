inside GTA VI’s vice city, from florida field trips to the design of its streets

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

the fictional miami in GTA VI features art deco architecture and detailed urban environments informed by over a decade of research.

Grand Theft Auto VI is finally arriving on November 19th, 2026, and it will return to the Miami-inspired architecture and streets of Vice City. Ahead of the release, the team at Rockstar Games has shared thirty-nine new images to give a closer look at the game‘s sprawling urban environment.

One captures a resort swimming pool from directly above, its turquoise water winding between palm trees and shaded terraces. Others look out across crowded marinas, waterfront neighborhoods and a skyline that stretches for miles.

The game takes place in Leonida, a fictional version of Florida that extends well beyond the familiar streets of Vice City, which was first introduced in the series in 2002. Its story follows two small-time criminals, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, whose lives become entangled in a larger conspiracy. While the pair are central to the game, Rockstar’s development process actually began with the setting.

Back in 2015, a small group of Rockstar developers traveled to South Florida to get a better sense of the places they would eventually recreate. They spent time in Miami, traveled through the Florida Keys and explored the Everglades, looking at the landscapes and neighborhoods that give the region its particular character.

As GTA VI producer and Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson told The Verge in an interview published on November 18th: ‘We just went around to feel it out.‘ The developers had decided on Florida before they knew exactly who would inhabit their version of the state.

The team used those early trips to decide which parts of Florida would make their way into the game. From there, Rockstar’s map designers began assembling their own interpretation of the region, with conversations about its inhabitants and the stories that might take place there. Nelson explained that the geography helped shape the narrative, following Jason and Lucia from smaller criminal operations toward the larger city.

Miami’s architecture became an important part of the research. Rockstar developer Aaron Garbut explained to the Associated Press that the team studied the city’s history and buildings, alongside its visits to local neighborhoods. Developers also flew over the Everglades to understand the landscape from above.

The game’s version of South Florida brings together the familiar Art Deco hotels of Miami Beach and the newer glass towers overlooking Biscayne Bay. Inland, commercial strips and lower-rise neighborhoods give the city a different character.

Some of the architectural references are surprisingly specific. Julie Fornary, a tour guide with the Miami Design Preservation League, pointed out to the Associated Press that the historic Hotel Victor on Ocean Drive appears in the game as Hotel Dixon. She interprets the new name as a reference to architect Lawrence Murray Dixon, who designed the original hotel in the 1930s. Elsewhere, familiar buildings and streets reappear under fictional names, sometimes with their positions rearranged to suit the game’s geography.

Rockstar’s newly released images offer a particularly interesting look at how the city has come together. From above, a large resort pool forms a striking pattern of curved edges and planted islands, surrounded by closely arranged lounge chairs. Another image looks toward a dense cluster of high-rises across a marina filled with boats.

The city’s waterfronts are crossed by bridges and lined with homes, while taller developments rise further inland. These are the kinds of views that make Vice City recognizable as Miami, even when the individual buildings have been changed.

At street level, the environment has a different texture. One screenshot captures a gas station beneath a large illuminated tire sign, with the distant towers of downtown visible beyond the power lines. Another follows a canal through a residential district, where a bridge connects low-rise streets on either side of the water. Rockstar uses changes in scale and building type to distinguish one part of the city from another, giving its neighborhoods their own visual identities.

Much of this attention extends beyond what players can see. In development notes shared with GamesBeat, Rockstar’s vice president of audio, Alastair MacGregor, explained how the game’s sound changes with its surroundings. Refrigerators in aging convenience stores make louder, rougher sounds than the appliances found in more expensive shops.

Conversations drift through busy streets, with fragments of speech traveling different distances depending on the wind. The way people speak and dress also changes between neighborhoods, while Rockstar’s designers have developed interconnected systems that allow events in one part of the game to affect others.

More than a decade after those first trips to Florida, the studio has assembled an enormous fictional landscape from the places it studied. Its streets draw on real architecture and familiar urban conditions, but the city has been rearranged for people to explore, with a new story waiting around each corner.
