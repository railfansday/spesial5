The Best Galapagos Cruises in 2027 That Combine Luxe Amenities With Rugged Adventure

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Visiting the Galapagos can feel like stepping onto another planet. This chain of volcanic islands is a mystical realm of otherworldly lava formations, sweeping oceanic vistas, ancient flora, and endemic animal species not found anywhere else on earth.

This unique archipelago of 20 scattered islands—plus dozens more islets and notable rocks in between—is best explored by a specialty cruise. Sailing through the Galapagos Islands allows travelers the ease of navigating to the destination’s smaller locales, getting up close to rare wildlife, and the constant presence of naturalists who can maximize every moment, from pointing out passing marine life to guided stargazing on deck.

The best Galapagos cruises combine tourism with conservation.

The best Galapagos cruises are intimate voyages aboard specialized ships designed to help preserve the islands’ delicate ecosystems. When the day of intrepid exploring is finished, the ship becomes your floating home, offering travelers a serene space to relax and debrief about the wonders you've just experienced. Here’s a look at the best Galapagos cruises to take in 2027, spanning everything from boutique yachts and all-inclusive cruises to top-of-the-line expedition ships.

Ecoventura's Theory is not only luxurious, but it welcomes families (with children ages six and up) with open arms.

For an intimate, curated voyage through the Galapagos, you can’t go wrong with an Ecoventura cruise. The line operates three Relais & Chateaux yachts, Origin, Theory, and Evolve, each of which has just 10 staterooms for a total of 20 guests. Each stateroom offers oversized windows and a sleek, modern design.

Travelers can choose between Ecoventura’s two seven-night itineraries: The “Beaches and Bays” voyage visits San Cristobal, Espanola, Floreana, Santa Cruz, Bartolome, and North Seymour Islands, and the “Volcanic Wonders” sailing calls upon more remote locations like Isabela and Fernandina Islands. (There’s also the option to combine both into a 14-night sailing.)

The line’s expedition guides, who are trained in birdwatching, stargazing, and nature photography, escort travelers to notable locations, like the red sand beach on Rabida Island with its flocks of American Flamingos, and the iconic pinnacle rock of Bartolome Island. There’s also kayaking, zodiac rides, and snorkeling among sea turtles, sea lions, reef fish, and more. Scuba divers can board the line’s 16-passenger luxury vessel, Galapagos Sky, for multi-day dive excursions to seek out whale sharks, hammerheads, and manta rays.

Galápagos sea lions, as seen on a cruise

One of the most luxurious options in the Galapagos, Silversea has brought its lavish, all-inclusive concept to the rugged archipelago aboard its destination-specific ship Silver Origin. The all-suite, all-balcony vessel holds 100 passengers and offers butler service in every suite, plus an in-room bar stocked to your preferences. Each suite is ultra-spacious, but the villa category offers sweeping terraces, vast living spaces and dining areas, and a separate bedroom. Elsewhere on the ship are two restaurants, a lounge, spa, fitness center, and stargazing platform— collectively, a promise that passengers will never be bored.

Silversea’s seven-day Galapagos itineraries all sail round-trip from the island of San Cristobal. Depending on the exact sailing, voyages call upon a wide variety of islands, including Isabela, Fernandina, North Seymour, Santa Cruz, Espanola, and more. With the included excursions, guests can explore the lunar landscapes of Isabela’s ancient lava flows, witness the seabird sanctuaries of Prince Philip's Steps, participate in guided nature hikes, deep-water snorkeling, zodiac cruises, and kayaking. Luxe touches, like free expedition gear and unlimited pours of Champagne on board, push the voyages over the top. Plus, Silver Origin’s specialized features—like a dynamic positioning system and advanced propulsion—are easier on the local ecosystem's fragile marine life.

The National Geographic Gemini hosts some of the best Galapagos cruises for bringing kids along.

Inspire your kids to learn about science and exploration by hopping on board one of Lindblad Expedition's four Galapagos ships: National Geographic Endeavour II, National Geographic Islander II, National Geographic Gemini, and National Geographic Delfina. These journeys, led by National Geographic experts, give kids the chance to become National Geographic Explorers-In-Training. The exclusive, hands-on program is designed to teach kids about geographical exploration and scientific research. The National Geographic Gemini, which launched in 2025, can accommodate up to 48 passengers in 27 suites. More than a dozen suites come with private balconies, and one of those balconies is large enough to accommodate a private hot tub. But don’t fret if that suite is already booked; there’s a whirlpool hot tub on the sundeck that’s open to all guests. There’s also a library, well-stocked bar, and meals made with locally sourced ingredients on board.

andBeyond's 124-foot expedition yacht Galapagos Explorer

With its 124-foot expedition yacht, Galapagos Explorer, andBeyond offers a truly exclusive experience. The yacht has only six rooms to accommodate 12 guests, and it’s also available for a fully private charter. Across the yacht’s four decks are a hot tub, sun deck, swim deck, as well as indoor and outdoor dining rooms and bars. In the five cabins and one larger suite, passengers will find a warm design accented with an oceanic color palette and natural materials like carved wood accents and indigenous textiles—making the ship feel more like a boutique hotel than a cruise. &Beyond’s seven-day itineraries call upon multiple islands either to the east or west, with a mix of popular and more remote locales, like Genovesa, Bartolome, Santa Cruz, Floreana, Espanola, Isabela, and Santa Fe. Snorkeling, kayaking, guided hikes, and island tours are all part of the daily activities with the expedition team, who can personalize each excursion thanks to the small number of guests on board.

The 164-foot superyacht Aqua Mare carries 16 passengers and 16 crew members.

The 164-foot superyacht Aqua Mare is all about bespoke attention. The small vessel carries 16 passengers, with 16 crew members to look after guests’ every need. Within the ship’s elegant interiors are seven cabins with gleaming woodwork, marble-accented bathrooms, and an owner’s suite with multiple bedrooms and living spaces.

Spanning the rest of the yacht’s four decks are a panoramic sundeck, hot tub, and indoor and outdoor dining rooms, where chef Pedro Miguel Schiaffino serves up Peruvian Nikkei-style cuisine. Expect selections like potato llapingachos, tuna crudo, barbecued plantains, and shrimp ceviche created with ingredients sourced from local farmers, fishermen, and other purveyors. The lower-deck swim platform and beach club is the launch point for kayaks, paddle boards, and snorkeling, or lazy days by the water.

Itineraries cover a wide breadth of islands, from the northern reaches of Genovesa to the southernmost Espanola, and Santa Fe, Santa Cruz, and Santiago in between. Western-focused itineraries arc around the far West side of Isabela Island, which allows for rarefied activities. Guests can hike among the penguin colonies and lava rocks of Punta Moreno, as well as the saltwater Darwin Lake located inside of a volcanic crater at Caleta Tagus.

Ecuadorian-owned, family-run expedition line Quasar operates 8-day and 15-day safari-style itineraries that focus on facilitating meaningful wildlife encounters for the small group of guests on board. With these immersive sailings, you might be swimming with sea turtles one day and cruising on a Zodiac boat amid a pod of dolphins the next. The line’s two yachts are designed for intimate trips, carrying a maximum of 32 guests. The 16-passenger Grace yacht has an especially intriguing history: Grace Kelly, who used it for her honeymoon in 1956, and has since been carefully restored to its original opulent glory. Guests can even stay in the revamped Grace Kelly suite, where the iconic actress spent her two-week honeymoon. Both yachts offer modern amenities like oversized hot tubs, al-fresco bars and lounges, sun decks, and more. Everything from meals to shore excursions to the open bar is included in the cruise fare.

Guests also have onboard access to kayaks, paddle boards, and snorkeling equipment for interacting directly with marine life like reef sharks, sea lions, and marine iguanas whenever the mood strikes. With such a small group, tours are immersive and personalized. Travelers can expect daily Zodiac landings across remote sites on islands like Espanola, Genovesa, Fernandina, Isabela, and Bartolome with expert naturalist guides.

While Flora can fit up to 100 passengers on board, excursions are limited to groups of 12 guests or fewer.

Sailing aboard the purpose-built ship Celebrity Flora strikes a perfect balance between relaxing on a luxurious floating resort and chasing the thrill of an adventurous expedition. The all-suite, all-veranda ship offers each guest a personal suite attendant, menus curated by a Michelin-starred chef, and an all-inclusive policy that covers drinks, meals, shore excursions, and even flights. Travelers can also expect a personal touch from crew, with 100 passengers on board and excursions that are limited to groups of 12 guests or fewer. Tours included memorable experiences like walks through lava tunnels, panga boat rides along rocky shorelines teeming with Galapagos penguins and sea lions, guided hikes to see blue-footed boobies, frigate birds, giant tortoises, rare flora, and ancient cacti.

Opportunities to spot diverse wildlife don’t end once travelers return to the ship. Celebrity Flora is outfitted with Sky Suites featuring the line’s signature Infinite Veranda that brings the extraordinary outdoor surroundings directly into your room. The window transforms into an open-air veranda at the touch of a button, allowing travelers to fully immerse themselves in the wondrous landscapes without even leaving their room. Plus, features like solar panels, reverse-osmosis water filtration, and dynamic positioning are gentle on the islands’ precious resources.

How early should I book a Galapagos cruise?

For the widest selection of vessels and sailing dates, aim to book your cruise four to nine months ahead of your intended sailing. More spontaneous travelers may find last-minute deals booking two to eight weeks ahead of their intended departure, though some itineraries will be sold out by then.

Getting to the Galapagos requires some logistical planning. After flying into Quito or Guayaquil, passengers typically spend a night in one of these mainland destinations before flying to the islands to board their ships. Your cruise line will tell you where to be on the islands for your departure, and in some cases, flights from the mainland will be included (or booked for an additional fee).

When is the best time to cruise the Galapagos Islands?

With a consistently mild climate year-round, the Galapagos can be visited at any time, although certain seasons offer exceptional wildlife viewing. December is when the giant tortoises hatch, and July marks the mating season for blue-footed boobies and short-eared owls. In April and May, marine iguanas typically hatch, while sea turtles begin laying their eggs at the start of the year. Given the diverse wildlife and year-round natural phenomena, there's never a "wrong" time to visit the islands.

The Galapagos Islands aren’t a bargain destination. Expect to pay somewhere between $5,000 and $12,000 per traveler, depending on the ship, itinerary, and length of the voyage, though there are options starting in the $3,000s.

What kind of wildlife can I expect to see on a Galapagos cruise?

The Galapagos Islands are renowned for their unique wildlife, including giant tortoises, marine iguanas, blue-footed boobies, and sea lions. The islands’ ecosystems are so diverse that you can see a variety of species in different environments, from volcanic shores to lush highlands.
