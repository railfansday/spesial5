A24 didn't dilly-dally in putting the Oscar-contender Primetime out on digital even though the movie hasn't yet opened in most of the world. It remains to be seen if the studio follows a similar strategy with You Can See Everything, the three-hour documentary about disgraced tech founder Elizabeth Holmes. Coincidentally, both films have been helmed by Lance Oppenheim, who shared directing duties with Nathan Fielder on the documentary. The critically acclaimed epic has emerged as something of a phenomenon in its domestic run, having broken records for documentary features and cementing itself as a contender for a Best Picture nomination at the Oscars.

That said, the film's theater count has been declining in recent weeks, even as it makes a bid for the coveted $100 million mark at the domestic box office. Primetime, starring Robert Pattinson, tapped out with just under $50 million domestically. The film has yet to be rolled out in most overseas markets, and it would appear that distributors are waiting for it to attract awards season attention before they release it.

You Can See Everything has earned even higher praise, with A24 hounded by global audiences for delaying its international rollout. The film follows Holmes in the month before her incarceration for fraud, as she invites a documentary crew led by Fielder to live with her and her husband. The movie is now sitting at a "Certified Fresh" 96% critics' score and a "Verified Hot" 95% audience score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "Nathan Fielder's ironclad commitment to the bit is pushed to its limit in You Can See Everything, an inscrutable Rorschach Test of Elizabeth Holmes' psyche that's as absorbing as it is haunting."

The film opened in eight theaters domestically, delivering one of the best per-theater averages of the year. It was subsequently expanded to 2,655 theaters the following week, and then 3,243 theaters the week after that. Having completed a month of release, the film is now playing in 1,845 theaters nationwide. This represents a nearly 1,400-theater decline from its widest footprint.

The movie has so far grossed $80 million at the domestic box office, and will make an earnest bid for the $100 million mark in the coming weeks. Only one (non-nature) documentary feature has ever passed this milestone — Michael Moore's controversial Fahrenheit 9/11, which grossed $119 million in 2004. You Can See Everything has already surpassed the lifetime haul of March of the Penguins.
