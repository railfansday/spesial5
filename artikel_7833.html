‘Judge Stone’ by Viola Davis and James Patterson wins inaugural Scott Turow Prize for Legal Fiction

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Viola Davis (left) and James Patterson appeared in New York City in March for a discussion of their book, “Judge Stone,” which was chosen as the winner of the inaugural Scott Turow Prize for Excellence in Legal Fiction. (Photo by Dia Dipasupil/Getty Images)

Judge Stone, by acclaimed actor Viola Davis and prolific author James Patterson, has been chosen as the winner of the inaugural Scott Turow Prize for Excellence in Legal Fiction.

The prize, which is being awarded by the ABA Journal, the University of Miami School of Law and the University of Miami College of Arts & Sciences, was determined by a panel of judges that featured former ABA Presidents Deborah Enix-Ross and Carolyn Lamm, California Court of Appeal Justice Lee Smalley Edmon and Turow himself.

A public poll hosted by the ABA Journal also chose Judge Stone as the favorite for the prize from among the field of three finalists, which also included A Pair of Aces by Marie Benedict and Victoria Christopher Murray, and Grave Danger by James Grippando.

The Turow Prize aims to honor the work that best illuminates the role of lawyers in society and their power to effect meaningful change. It is a successor to the prestigious Harper Lee Prize, which the Journal and the University of Alabama previously awarded to outstanding works of legal fiction, including novels by John Grisham, Michael Connelly and Grippando.

Judge Stone is a legal thriller about a Black woman judge from a small town in Alabama who presides over a controversial case. It is the first collaboration between Davis and Patterson.

Davis is one of a select group of celebrities who are “EGOT” winners for taking home Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony awards. In 2025, she was honored by the Golden Globes with the Cecil B. DeMille Award for “outstanding contributions to the world of entertainment.”

She won an Emmy for TV’s How to Get Away with Murder and has appeared in numerous films, including Academy Award–nominated Doubt, The Help and Fences (for which she received the Oscar). She won two Tonys (for Fences and King Hedley II) and a Grammy for best audiobook, narration and storytelling recording for her memoir, Finding Me. She also took home a Screen Actors Guild Award for her role in Ma Rainey’s Black Bottom.

In his long career, Patterson has created notable characters and series including Alex Cross, the Women’s Murder Club, Jane Smith and Maximum Ride. He’s written true stories about the Kennedys, John Lennon and Tiger Woods, and has coauthored No. 1 bestselling novels with Bill Clinton, Dolly Parton and Michael Crichton. He’s received an Edgar Award, ten Emmy Awards, the Literarian Award from the National Book Foundation and the National Humanities Medal, according to jamespatterson.com.

Named in honor of pioneering attorney and international bestselling author Scott Turow—the “father of the modern legal thriller”—the Turow Prize seeks to unearth compelling works of fiction that illuminate the dynamics of law, the justice system and the human experience.

Turow’s 13 fiction and two nonfiction books, including Presumed Innocent and the current Presumed Guilty, have sold over 30 million copies worldwide and been adapted for film and television.

Davis and Patterson will receive a cash prize and a signed copy of Presumed Innocent at the award ceremony and reception on Nov. 19 hosted by the University of Miami, along with the exclusive right to feature the prestigious Turow Prize logo on their winning book’s cover.
