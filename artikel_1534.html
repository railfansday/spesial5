Man Utd, Celtic & Palace considering options? The best managers currently out of work

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Former managers of Liverpool, Manchester City and Tottenham are among the biggest names currently available.

With clubs like Manchester United, Celtic and Crystal Palace all rumoured to be considering a change in the dug-out, there are some proven managers and promising coaches who are ready to work.

We’ve ranked the nine best coaches who are currently available.

Does Bielsa have one more job in him at the age of 71?

One of the most respected and influential coaches of his generation, the Argentinian remains a unique figure in football. When it clicks, it clicks spectacularly. Just ask Leeds United fans.

It would be a real shame if such a brilliant, idiosyncratic manager bowed out after Uruguay’s underwhelming World Cup campaign and all the bad blood that followed.

Surely there’s one last club out there willing to take a punt on El Loco?

We’ve forgotten a bit about Motta, given it’s over a year since he was last in work.

He didn’t pull up any trees at Juventus, but there was a reason he landed the job in the first place, having worked wonders at Bologna

And you look at the way that things have gone at Juventus since his sacking, and the problems were surely bigger than him.

We could easily see him back in Serie A, continuing the league’s fine managerial merry-go-round tradition of the same names at the same clubs (Max Allegri at Napoli, you say? Sure) but we’d love to see him test himself in another country.

It only took a matter of weeks for Frank’s successor at Tottenham to rant about the team “lacking in attack, midfield, defence and brain”.

Superb copy, Igor Tudor, we can only thank you.

The Dane didn’t rise to the occasion at Tottenham, that much is clear, but his steady, proven record over multiple seasons at Brentford should keep him in contention for solid, midtable jobs. Crystal Palace look most likely to hire Frank.

“Available” with an admittedly pretty big asterisk.

As with Jurgen Klopp when he left Liverpool, we’re completely inclined to take Howe at his word when he says he needs a rest. You could see the toll that the Newcastle job took on him.

We’d be amazed if Howe is back in work before Christmas, at the earliest, but it’s surely only a matter of time before he starts getting linked to vacancies again.

Our early money’s on Everton when David Moyes leaves. Howe is famously a boyhood Evertonian, and it feels like the right fit. If that goes well, maybe we’ll finally see him managing England down the line.

But Celtic, with whom Howe already held talks, might be on. Watch this space.

Two underwhelming major tournaments at the helm of Germany have seen Nagelsmann’s stock drop a bit. His next job in the club game will be something of a salvage job when it comes to his reputation.

It’ll be interesting to see what kind of job he gets next, with bags of experience at the age of 38. Taking RB Leipzig to the Champions League semi-finals and performing broadly to par with Bayern Munich, delivering an obligatory Bundesliga title, should see him land a decent European-level gig.

Back to the Bundesliga when the Borussia Dortmund job next comes up, maybe? We could certainly see him take on a new challenge in the Premier League, too.

We predicted six clubs where Slot could find himself next after his Liverpool sacking.

The Netherlands job was inevitably mentioned after Ronald Koeman’s departure, but that one ended up going to Xavi.

Deschamps’ time with France is over but he is still only 57, suggesting his managerial career is not at an end.

He has club coaching experience too having led Monaco, Juventus and Marseille, winning trophies with all of them, and so should be a strong contender for any top job if he fancies it.

If a club is ambitious and doesn’t have the distraction of European football, Conte’s track record speaks for itself – an instant impact and titles delivered at Juventus, Chelsea, Inter and Napoli.

His name has always been mentioned with regard to Manchester United’s trevails in the post-Ferguson era.

He probably isn’t the man to build a successful, sustainable long-term ‘project’ but a rocket up the arse might just give them a temporary reprieve in their ongoing psychodrama.

Your reminder that Amazon Prime are currently shooting their All or Nothing: Manchester United documentary series. Conte working under Sir Jim Ratcliffe? Pure box office.

The Catalans’ decade of unprecedented success at the Etihad means he’s earned himself a rest – but could a national team job tempt him out of any sabbatical?

It doesn’t look as though he’ll be throwing himself into another job any time soon, and managing another Premier League club feels borderline impossible, but that won’t stop the tabloids from inevitably putting his name into the hat for every big job that emerges.

We’re fascinated to see what happens next. As long as it isn’t an easy cash grab out in the Middle East.
