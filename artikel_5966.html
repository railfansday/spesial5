Video shows two women walking toward the front with their belongings, followed by a uniformed officer. Swanson says three were removed. He says several passengers missed connections in Charlotte.

Yelling at a flight attendant because your flight is late is a lousy strategy for getting home sooner. Last year I wrote about an American Airlines passenger who told a flight attendant to shut up during the safety demonstration and wound up getting the plane sent back to the gate.

It appears this was American Eagle flight 5675 on November 21, operated by PSA Airlines using a Bombardier CRJ900, registration N557NN. That flight was scheduled to leave Knoxville at 8:04 p.m. and arrive in Charlotte at 9:28 p.m. It took off at 9:57 p.m. and landed at 10:34 p.m.

Charlotte is a particularly bad place to lose your connection buffer. I’ve written about how arriving on a regional jet can mean waiting for a valet checked bag and then making a long walk from Concourse E. A late evening arrival also leaves fewer chances to recover with another flight that night.

However, that aircraft’s inbound flight from Charlotte didn’t land in Knoxville until 8:35 p.m. It was supposed to have arrived at 7:34 p.m. The plane was still arriving 31 minutes after it should have departed on its next trip.

The women weren’t responsible for the original delay. Removing them may have used up whatever chance remained to make his connection, but we don’t whether he’d have made it without the argument.
