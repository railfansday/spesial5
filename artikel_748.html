Boston Museum of Fine Arts Announces Expansive ‘Taylor Swift: The Eras Collection’ Exhibition

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The show will include more than 50 instruments, outfits and objects from throughout the singer's career.

Get ready to spend a night at the museum with Taylor Swift. Boston’s Museum of Fine Arts announced on Tuesday (Nov. 17) that it will mount the “Taylor Swift: the Eras Collection” exhibition in the spring. The career-spanning show promises to display more than 50 iconic costumes, instruments and objects from Swift’s archive, celebrating two decades of her art.

According to the announcement, the exhibition will feature a vignette dedicated to each of Swift’s 12 eras to date, bookended by the videos for the “Tim McGraw” single from her 2006 self-titled debut album through “The Fate of Ophelia” from last year’s The Life of a Showgirl. Also included will be objects from Swift’s record-setting Eras Tour (2023-2024), including a custom red Alberta Ferretti gown worn during the folklore and evermore sets on the global stadium outing.

Among the other objects on display will be the bejeweled Midnights bodysuit designed by Zuhair Murad, as well as the tinsel-fringed jacket from the show’s “Karma” finale and a recreation of the “Fortnight” video set from The Tortured Poets Department era featuring Swift’s Victorian-inspired dress and typewriter.

The Boston museum will be the only venue for the exhibit, which will also include a number of Swift’s signature instruments, including a Deering banjo from the Speak Now tour, a mandolin from the “Willow” video and the shell of a pink-and-gold piano from Swift’s “Lover” performance at the 2019 American Music Awards.

“This is the first exhibition in the MFA’s history devoted to a musician, and we’re thrilled to invite visitors for an up-close look at the fashion and instruments of one of the most influential cultural figures of our time,” MFA director of public programs Kristen Hoskins said in a statement about the show that opens on April 10 and is slated to run through Aug. 15. “I’m excited to welcome audiences across generations to this unique experience at the Museum.”

Tickets for the exhibition ($40) will go on sale to the general public on Swift’s birthday, Dec. 13, at 6 p.m. ET; click here for ticketing details.

On Monday, Swift made Grammy history when the nominations for next year’s ceremony were announced and she once again got nods for album, record and song of the year, marking the fifth time she’s hit that rare trifecta and setting a record for the most times any artist has ever had nods in the big three all-genre categories in the same year.
