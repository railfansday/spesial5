Leclerc denies having 'No 1 clause' in Ferrari contract

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Lewis Hamilton, meanwhile, confirms they remain "free to race" after talks following their collision in Las Vegas, for which Leclerc was punished by stewards; watch the Qatar GP on Sky Sports F1 this weekend, with qualifying at 6pm on Saturday and the race at 4pm on Sunday

Charles Leclerc has strongly denied suggestions he has a No 1 driver clause in his Ferrari contract.

The Monegasque, who has been with the team since 2019 and signed an extended deal earlier this year, was speaking ahead of the Qatar Grand Prix as both he and Lewis Hamilton faced the media again four days on from their latest clash at the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

Leclerc was given a 10-second penalty by the stewards for causing a collision as he attempted to overtake Hamilton for sixth place in Las Vegas, with the incident also earning him severe criticism from Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur.

The Las Vegas collision came just three weeks after a fierce battle between the team-mates - with Hamilton running first and Leclerc second - on the opening lap in Mexico City cost Ferrari a likely one-two, with clear-the-air discussions required after Red Bull's Max Verstappen won that race.

Vasseur has hitherto played down the need to impose strict, pre-determined rules of engagement on his drivers to avoid future repeats. There had subsequently been speculation that, perhaps, there were contractual reasons on Leclerc's side preventing the team from formally intervening in this way.

But offered the chance on Thursday to address claims about the presence of a so-called 'No 1 driver' clause in his deal, Leclerc said: "I normally never disclose what I have in my contract, but I think this one is an important one to speak about.

"And I absolutely have zero clause that says that in my contract.

Leclerc bore the brunt of Vasseur's post-race criticism last Sunday and, asked if he agreed with the Frenchman that the crash with Hamilton had indeed been "unacceptable", replied: "That it is unacceptable? Of course.

"As a team, we cannot afford to clash between team-mates and I think that is very clear for everyone. For the clash in particular of Las Vegas, as I said, I took responsibility.

"I think there's just tension around, that we just need to dissipate and make sure that we look at more positive days in the future.

"That's all I'm looking for. But yeah, I do agree. It's unacceptable that both Ferrari clash together, of course."

Vasseur had promised further talks with his drivers following the race in Vegas, having insisted the clashes between the pair "cannot continue".

The drivers confirmed on Thursday that talks had taken place, with both the team principal and among themselves, with Hamilton saying they had been "good discussions" and "positive".

Neither driver said they would elaborate on the specifics of what was discussed, with Leclerc stating in the official FIA news conference that he "cannot disclose" what possible "solutions" were agreed to avoid future repeats or whether any restrictions were in place for the season's final two rounds.

Hamilton later confirmed they remained free to race each other this weekend in Qatar

"We're still free to race, and I think that's the ultimate goal of being here. We want to race, right?" said Hamilton to Sky Sports F1.

"Of course, we don't want what happened last week to ever happen, so we communicate. Charles and I had a good sit down earlier on and had our chat, and I I think moving forwards we can be fine.

"We literally focus on racing hard and fair, and continue to try and enjoy it. We do enjoy racing together, and we do have a really good relationship.

"It's obviously intense in the background, well on the outside, it seems a lot more intense than it really is inside."

Hamilton has previously called for the team to adopt clearer guidelines on how he and Leclerc are able to race each other but, speaking on Thursday, said it was Vasseur's final call to make.

"That's for Fred to decide ultimately," he said. "I made my point clear already about them.

"So yeah, there's not really much more to say on it really. I said my piece before and and my point hasn't really changed."

Leclerc and Hamilton are in their second season as Ferrari team-mates and will at least spend one more year in tandem in 2027, with the potential for more.

Asked if he thought they could move forward in harmony, Hamilton said: "I do. We're going to figure it out.

"We're team-mates at the end of the day and we do always learn from these experiences whilst maybe it doesn't necessarily look like it.

"There will be learnings that we take from this difficult period, and I still believe in everything we're building and working towards in the background.

"I came here with the goal of taking Ferrari back to the top and it has been a massively challenging season but it's so easy to look at the negatives but I like to look at the positives this year."

Ferrari can clinch the runner-up position to world champions Mercedes this weekend in Qatar and Hamilton added: "We are fighting for second in the Constructors' Championship, we have had wins, I think we've had the best car chassis-wise particularly this year and that's down to all the amazing people back at the factory who have worked and brought upgrades. I am so proud of them.

"Did we have the straight-line speed? No. That's something we are working towards for next year. If we are able to galvanise and improve on that section, we're going to be mighty next year in my view.

"I'm just trying to look at the positives, I think everyone seems to be focusing always on the negatives, which is obviously the easiest thing to do in life. But I try to remember the positives, and we've really done some amazing things this year together."

Hamilton added: "I like to think that we are stronger. I think that if everything was just great all the time I don't think you learn anything.

"When you're winning all the time as well, you don't learn a lot through that. You learn really through the hardest times, and these are the definitely very, very challenging times for Fred, for Charles and I, but these are the moments that you really learn and grow closer together, through conversation, communicating and having those sit-downs.

Leclerc believes his off-track relationship with Hamilton is "really strong" and they now needed to find a workable way forward when racing each other on track.

"We're just really very much focused on trying to find a solution that works," said Leclerc.

"Particularly because I really believe we are in a good momentum and if next year we are to be in a position of fighting for the championship, it's even more so important to find the right solution for both of us to bring Ferrari back to the top.

"At the end of the day, we are two, one in each car, and we need to make sure that things just work out. At the moment, it's not working.

"Then, of course, I can take the blame from one episode and whatever. That doesn't really matter. It's just important that we find a compromise and a solution that works for both."
