Oman’s arrivals have held up this year, but its hotels lost more than a tenth of their guests and revenue as Europeans stayed away. Regional travelers are still arriving but make shorter visits at lower rates.

Oman’s hotels are taking a far bigger hit from the U.S.-Iran war than the country’s headline arrival numbers suggest, Skift’s analysis of tourism data shows.

The country received 2.5 million visitors from January to August, down 0.8% year over year, according to the National Centre for Statistics and Information’s (NCSI) latest tourism report. The figures are the most recent available official arrivals data. In 2025, the country drew nearly 4 million visitors, against a Vision 2040 long-term target of 11 million a year.

Over the first eight months of this year, guest numbers at Oman’s three- to five-star hotels fell 10.7% to 1.35 million, shrinking hotel revenue by 11% to 156.5 million Omani rials ($407 million).

Oman's headline visitor numbers look almost flat, but its hotels are absorbing a much deeper hit from the U.S.-Iran war and wider regional conflict. Total arrivals fell 0.8% to 2.5 million between January and August, while guests at three- to five-star hotels dropped 10.7% to 1.35 million and hotel revenue fell 11% to $407 million, according to the National Centre for Statistics and Information. International air passengers fell 9.7% on 9.1% fewer flights, while domestic passengers rose 5.2%, suggesting land crossings from the UAE and Yemen are propping up the headline figure. September data from CoStar shows no recovery: occupancy fell 6.4 points to 47.3%, average daily rate dropped 10.5% to about $100, and RevPAR fell 21% to about $47. Hotel guest mix data points to European losses as a driver, and Gulf Hotels warned in a Muscat Stock Exchange filing that 2026 revenue and profit could fall short of budget, with its chairman citing the continued conflict in the Gulf region and connectivity problems to key European source markets. Operators describe shorter stays, later bookings, and heavy reliance on regional and local demand. Supply is slipping too, with Oman now set to add 700 rooms by year-end rather than the previously forecast total, and 3,100 more rooms due across 2027 and 2028.
