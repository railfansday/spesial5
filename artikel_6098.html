How Much Do Captains & First Officers Earn At US Carriers In 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

YAKOBCHUK VIACHESLAV | Shutterstock, Simple Flying

Becoming a pilot remains one of the world's most lucrative professions. Salaries vary depending on many factors despite union standardization. These factors include whether the pilot is a first officer or a captain, the aircraft they fly (more seats usually means more money), years of service, and hours flown each month.

A United Airlines widebody captain makes $483.74 per hour after 12 years of service, Air Gazette reports. This is an end-of-career level of compensation. Early-career pilots are still well paid, with legacy airline captains making over $100 per hour.

There is a lot of similarity in the pay pilots can expect at legacy carriers at comparable points in their careers. Starting pay is highest at United, with first officers making $125.52 per hour on their first day, compared with Delta Air Lines' $118 and American Airlines' $113. This puts the annual salary at United for first-year first officers at $120,499 (assuming 80 flying hours a month), while the same pilot at Delta would make $113,280 and at American Airlines $108,480.

At each of these airlines, mid-career first officers (in their fifth year of service) will earn substantially more. The annual wage for a pilot flying 80 hours per month at United is approximately $227,405, higher than the $182,400 paid by Delta and $187,200 paid by American Airlines.

Widebody captains with 12 years of service are the gold standard for these legacy airlines, earning enormous compensation. The hourly wage for such captains is $483.74 at United, $465.13 at Delta, and the same at American Airlines. This translates to about $464,390 for a United widebody captain who flies 80 hours a month, while similar Delta and American Airlines captains earn around $446,525.

Across each stage of their careers, as both a captain and a first officer, pilots earn roughly comparable amounts by working for the three legacy carriers. Other differentials then factor into which airline a pilot tries to work for. This includes 401(k) contributions, per diem allowances, and more. Delta offers profit sharing as an additional perk. Career progression opportunities matter more to many pilots early in their careers than maximizing earnings. For pilots, the main progression is from first officer to captain. Historically, a pilot had to wait 10 to 15 years for the upgrade, usually waiting for captains to retire ahead of them. Industry-wide retirements are running at over 5,000 a year, but Delta could have the best opportunities for first officers on the cusp of promotion. That is because the carrier has projected 506 mandatory retirements in 2026 alone.

Historically, regional airlines offered exceptionally low salaries. Pilots took a hit to career progression while they waited for an opportunity with one of the US' legacy carriers. The situation has now turned around, with regional carriers offering strong salaries from the very start of a pilot's career.

The wage increase was caused by a post-pandemic surge in demand that regional carriers couldn't keep up with. They had to raise their salaries to attract enough pilots. Pilots' union negotiations with legacy airlines in 2023 and 2024 also forced regional airlines to respond.

In 2026, first officers at regional airlines make $50 to $90 per hour, while captains make $100 to $150 per hour. This is still far lower than Delta, American Airlines, or United, but is enough to make a great living. As with the big three airlines, experienced captains earn substantially more than early-career first officers. American Flyers estimates regional captain salaries sit between $140,000 and $150,000. Experienced pilots at the best regional carriers can make substantially more. The salary for a captain at Endeavor Air in their 12th year of service is over $200,000. As with any job, pay is just one of multiple factors influencing where a pilot might decide to work. In 2026, there are plenty of reasons to take the relative pay hit and work at a regional airline. The most obvious deciding factor beyond pilot salaries is the career progression that these pilots can access. This isn't only about transitioning to a major airline, as regional airlines offer experience that isn't readily accessible at United, Delta, or American Airlines. For example, these airlines often fly to remote locations where pilots learn to operate in adverse weather conditions and on challenging runways.

Career progression at regional carriers can also be faster even for those who don't want to work at Delta, United, or American Airlines. This is because first officers can progress much faster to the role of captain.

The newly found job security for regional pilots can also attract many professionals. The sector's enormous growth means unexpected redundancies are far less of a concern. This security means pilots can be more flexible with their career choices.

Salaries in the industry have continued to rise.

Commercial aviation is not the only career path available to aspiring pilots; the military is also a compelling option. The US Air Force is the largest military aviation employer, so it offers salaries that can compete with the civilian sector for long-term retention. A newly commissioned Air Force pilot will make a base salary of $50,000 to $60,000 each year. Junior Air Force pilots make $75,000 to $90,000 annually while on their first operational assignments. This quickly rises above six figures when hazardous duty and combat-related pay is factored in. Mid-career pilots, around the O-3 and O-4 levels, can expect between $120,000 and $160,000 annually. Senior officers will make around $180,000 annually.

The US Air Force also says that pilots access six forms of benefits from their service that go beyond their base pay. Many of these benefits have no equivalent in civilian life. They include low- or no-cost health insurance, covered food and housing costs, a savings and retirement plan, funding for family and personal recreation, paid and reduced-cost vacations, and funded pilot training and college education. These excellent benefits go some way towards making up for the lower pay compared with commercial flying. However, some pilots feel these benefits are canceled out by the lifestyle restrictions that exist for all military service members.

Pilots can also pursue a military aviation role through the US Navy, often receiving additional pay due to the inconvenience of being at sea for extended periods. The entry-level salary is around $80,000 to $100,000 each year. The mid-career salary is between $130,000 and $170,000 per year. Senior commanders with flying roles can make more than $190,000. Such senior roles in both the US Navy and Air Force come with command responsibilities in addition to flying.
