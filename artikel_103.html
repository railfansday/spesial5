Trump Pushes Venezuela for Agreement Allowing U.S. Troops to Stay

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The two countries have long been adversaries, and the United States attacked Venezuela this year, but Secretary of State Marco Rubio is starting talks on a legal agreement.

Edward Wong reported from Caracas, Venezuela, and Washington. He has covered global affairs for more than two decades, including the American war in Iraq.

The Trump administration is negotiating an agreement with Venezuela that would lay the foundation for a potential long-term U.S. military presence in the country, U.S. officials said.

The talks could open the door to a remarkable and perhaps risky new stage in the U.S. relationship with Venezuela, as the two former adversaries attempt to move toward military cooperation.

Venezuela’s top officials have become subservient to President Trump since U.S. troops killed scores of people in a nighttime assault on Venezuela in January and took Nicolás Maduro, the country’s leader, to a prison in New York.

In a speech in Washington on Monday, Mr. Trump referred to “our conquest of Venezuela.”

The negotiations are the latest manifestation of the U.S. government’s increasingly militarized policies in Latin America. Mr. Trump and his top aides have ordered troops to carry out operations across the region, including joint actions with forces in Colombia, Ecuador and Venezuela.

The U.S. military has also carried out scores of lethal strikes on civilian boats in the Caribbean and the eastern Pacific that many legal experts say are extrajudicial killings.

The Trump administration has forged closer security ties with many of the region’s right-wing governments and has troops and military assets at bases across Central and South America.
