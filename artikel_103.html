Trafalgar Square Christmas tree felled in Norway

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Karen Scarborough (left) and Anne Lindboe got to work with a two-handled saw

This year's Trafalgar Square Christmas tree has been cut down in a forest outside Oslo, ready to begin its journey to London.

The 21m (69ft) tree, nicknamed Jolly Cheery, is about 75 years old. It was felled on Friday by the Lord Mayor of Westminster, Karen Scarborough, and the Mayor of Oslo, Anne Lindboe.

Norway has sent a tree to the capital every year since 1947 as a thank you for Britain's support during World War Two, when King Haakon VII and the Norwegian government lived in exile in London.

Its lights will be switched on by the two mayors at a ceremony in the square on Thursday, 3 December.

Scarborough said: "It's my greatest honour to take part in this wonderful tradition for both the cities of Oslo and London.

"This tree is a powerful symbol of friendship and peace, reminding us to be there for one another during the festive period."

The 21m tree has been nicknamed Jolly Cheery

Scarborough thanked "the people of Oslo and Norway for this truly fabulous gift" and added: "With the care and dedication of the team, I'm sure it will look magnificent on Trafalgar Square."

Lindboe said: "Oslo has been sending a Christmas tree to London for almost eight decades, and every year we send a tree across the North Sea with the same message: we have not forgotten.

"The tradition has a powerful history, and its message remains just as relevant today.

"I hope Jolly Cheery spreads warmth and Christmas cheer to everyone who passes by, and reminds us how much it means to be there for one another, especially in difficult times."

Count the rings: Anne Lindboe (left) and Karen Scarborough (right) with a slice of the 75-year-old trunk

For the second year running, the tree was chosen with the help of a social media poll.

Organisers said it would travel by road in Norway and England using only electric and biodiesel vehicles, cutting the journey's carbon footprint by 80%. It will cross the North Sea on a regular cargo ship from the port of Brevik.

Once in London, it will be decorated in the traditional Norwegian style, with 25m strings of festoon lighting fitted with energy-efficient bulbs.
