USWNT squad: Emma Hayes names veteran group for Concacaf championship and World Cup qualifier

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Win over El Salvador would qualify US for Brazil 2027

In the two years since the Paris Olympics, Emma Hayes has thoroughly tested fresh faces and new combinations, gathering a considerable amount of data about the US women’s national team. It’s been a thorough process, with 33 players earning their first caps since Hayes assumed the post, to bring her roster of options to 61 players.

At last, the main event is nearing. On 27 November, the United States will enter the Concacaf W Championship with a clear objective: beat El Salvador. Winning the whole thing is nice, of course, and the USWNT have done so in nine of the tournament’s 11 installments to date. But by far the most important game on that journey is the first one, that quarter-final date with clear stakes: overcome El Salvador, or rely on a playoff to reach next summer’s World Cup.

On Wednesday, Hayes acknowledged that the way El Salvador are expected to set up – in a low-defensive block, looking to disrupt the US’s ability to control the game – will pose a different challenge than recent friendlies against fellow World Cup hopefuls Spain and Brazil.

“It’s a much different game to the previous games you’ve just played in, and transitioning your thinking process, especially tactically,” Hayes said after announcing her squad. “We’ll still have to build up against an aggressive press, but we’re going to have to break down a team that will sit and defend the box with numbers. I do think we have been working on that as a team, but we might not have experienced that for a number of camps. So, switching into that and reminding our team of the key things in which that has to come through, in the way we train and the types of training we do.”

Hayes’s 23 selections for the tournament suggests what her preferred core may look like for the remainder of the cycle. Only four outfield players on her roster arrive with fewer than a dozen caps: midfielder Croix Bethune (seven), who sent the Kansas City Current to the NWSL championship last weekend with a brilliant curling free-kick, plus defenders Kennedy Wesley (nine), Kate Del Fava (one) and Evelyn Shores, who becomes only the second uncapped USWNT player selected for a World Cup qualifier (following Taylor Flint, née Kornieck, 2022).

Eight players from Hayes’s Olympic gold medal squad remain in the fold, including the star attacking trio of Trinity Rodman, Mallory Swanson and Sophia Wilson. Sam Coffey returns after an injury prevented her from joining the team in the previous window, while Naomi Girma misses out due to persistent muscle issues. Claudia Dickey, Mandy McGlynn and Phallon Tullis-Joyce will continue to vie to start in goal, continuing their open competition following Alyssa Naeher’s retirement.

The average age of this roster is 26.1, with only two players (Lindsey Heaps and Rose Lavelle) at least 30. With an average caps tally of 40.7 per player, Hayes will be looking to her veteran options for the crucial one-off qualifier – especially with the team convening just two days before the quarter-final.

“I think this, for us, qualifying for a major tournament first and foremost,” Hayes said. “I’ll be leaning on our experienced players. They know how to do this, and they’ve been with us the longest to be able to deliver that, and I think for the less experienced players, have had a good body of work in the last couple of years.”

The Concacaf W Championship doubles as qualifying for both the 2027 Women’s World Cup and the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. All four semi-finalists will be guaranteed a spot in the World Cup, and the top three finishers will also qualify for the Olympics field. (The US have a place at LA 2028 automatically as hosts). The losing teams in the quarter-finals will compete against each other for Concacaf’s two spots in the inter-confederation playoffs, which are scheduled for February in Morocco.

As the first competitive games for the US since that gold-medal summer of 2024, the business end of the bracket will provide vital tests against regional rivals. Jamaica project as the favorite to face off in the semi-final should both teams advance, a stern test for a center-back corps operating without Girma and Emily Sonnett. A potential final could pit Hayes’s side against long-time rival Canada or Mexico, a program that has rapidly risen in stature this decade and hopes to make a first Women’s World Cup since 2015.

All of that is a matter for another day. Until then, the US will do what’s necessary to avoid what would be an unprecedented catastrophe: missing a World Cup.

“We know what we need to do,” Hayes said. “We need to just make sure we come into camp with really clear mind about those things. Focus on the game in front of us. That’s the next one. always say this: our identity is not defined by results. Being able to lock into what that identity is really quickly after a gap between lots of NWSL club games is really important when you only have a two-day build before the game.”
