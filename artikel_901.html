'I didn't know' - Price's controversial 145 checkout at Grand Slam explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Gerwyn Price busts his score after hitting D20 - 10 over his required tally, but is given leg on account of having been given incorrect information by referee; you can watch the Grand Slam of Darts in Wolverhampton live on Sky Sports up until the final on November 22

Gerwyn Price produced a controversial 145 checkout en route to a routine Grand Slam of Darts victory over Sebastian Bialecki on Monday night in Wolverhampton.

Price, the three-time champion in Wolverhampton, came through 5-2 against Poland's Bialecki to book his place in the knockout stage.

However, there was controversy during the final Group E match as Price hit a 145 finish when he had 135 remaining in the fourth leg of the match.

'The Iceman' needed 135 to hold his throw, and he thought he had taken it out.

After hitting T19 he asked referee Charlie Corstophine what he had left and was told 88.

With 88 in mind, Price incorrectly hit T16 and D20 to finish off the leg and was given the hold.

Of course, Price believed he had gone the correct route for what he needed and went on to set up a last 16 date with Ryan Searle.

"I didn't know until I just walked in [to the press conference]," said Price. "I hit the first treble and I asked what I'd got left and he said 88.

"So I went treble 16 double top and he said game shot."

The Welshman admitted he was glad he didn't know or it would've weighed on his mind.

He added: "I know. I swear to God, I didn't even know until I came back here. I'm glad I didn't know because it probably would have played on my mind."

6.4.7 If the incorrect score resulted in a leg win the result will stand unless corrected prior to the start of the next leg.

Both players went into the contest with one win each after beating Joe Hunt, which made it a straight shoot-out with only the group winners reaching the knockout stage this year.

Price had opened his week with a 5-3 win over Hunt on Saturday, racing into a 4-0 lead before Hunt won three legs in a row. Bialecki needed a deciding leg to beat Hunt 5-4 on Sunday.

The new format has already seen Ross Smith, Jonny Clayton and Luke Woodhouse suffer shock exits and Price said: "I prefer the old one [format], obviously. I've had some good memories here. You can slip up once, but in this one you can't.

"As you can see, a couple of good boys have gone, and they probably thought they'd have a second chance. But once you lose a game, that's it; you're gone."
