Quentin Grimes' late three-pointer lifts Lakers to narrow win over Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Lakers just finished a stretch during which they played four games in six days, a stretch that included back-to-back games this week, a stretch in which their mental and physical fortitude was tested.

They were not able to hold up during Wednesday night's defeat to the Utah Jazz, but the Lakers were able to bounce back with a 106-104 win over the hapless Sacramento Kings on Friday night at Crypto.com Arena behind another winning shot from Quentin Grimes.

Even though the Lakers were facing one-win Sacramento, they had to fight to the final few precious seconds to improve to 14-3 and 2-1 in NBA Cup play. They only secured the win after guard Zach LaVine missed a three-pointer just before the buzzer.

Lakers coach JJ Redick said he had some choice words for his players at halftime.

"I feel I did a decent job of regulating at halftime. They may have a differing opinion of that," Redick said. "Can't repeat every word I used. The main message was, 'We've got to be tougher. We've got to bring a little bit of energy. Certain nights, whether it's the team, how we're playing, the atmosphere in the arena, you can just be a little flat.' And I thought we just had to be tougher and pick up our energy. They accepted the challenge."

The score was tied 103-103 when the Lakers forced Kings rookie guard Darius Acuff Jr. into a turnover.

Grimes then hit a step-back three with 56.3 seconds left to give the Lakers a 106-103 lead.

It marked the second time this week Grimes made a winning three, the first coming off a banked-in trey Sunday at Phoenix with 32.7 seconds left — the day he said, "I called game."

Lakers center Walker Kessler scores off an offensive rebound against the Sacramento Kings on Friday. (Gina Ferazzi / Los Angeles Times)

"I think so," Grimes said, smiling. "Luckily, didn't have to bank it in, it just kind of went in pretty good. But yeah, it feels good to get another one of those games in, for sure."

Grimes didn't have the best shooting night. He was two for seven from the field, one for five from three-point range. Grimes missed his first three-pointer in the fourth quarter.

But he was unfazed with the game on the line, hitting a 26-foot jumper.

"Yeah, it don't matter. [You] shoot the next one. I mean, [it's a] make-or-miss league," said Grimes, who scored a career-high 47 points against Charlotte on Tuesday. "You gonna make it, you gonna miss it. Don't matter if you go 0 for 10 or you can go 10 for 10. Next one, get it up, and then luckily that was the biggest shot in the game and the shot of the night."

After Acuff missed a three-point attempt, Luka Doncic turned the ball over, giving the basketball back to the Kings (1-14) with 23 seconds left.

Lakers star Luka Doncic drives to the basket against Sacramento Kings guard Zach LaVine (8) and forward Keegan Murray in the first half Friday. (Gina Ferazzi / Los Angeles Times)

LaVine made one of two free throws to pull the Kings to within 106-104.

Then Walker Kessler, who had 15 points and seven rebounds, missed two free throws with 5.3 seconds left, leaving the door open for the Kings.

But LaVine missed his three-point try as time expired, allowing the Lakers to escape on a night when Doncic had 25 points and Matisse Thybulle had 14.

So, it appeared that whatever Redick spewed at his team at halftime resonated.

"Yeah, I mean he yells," Doncic said. "But I'm used to it. So, got us all fired up. So, sometimes you get to yell."

Austin Reaves missed his fourth consecutive game because of a left heel bruise, but Redick said his star guard is making progress. The Lakers next play Monday night at home against the Utah Jazz and then again Friday at San Antonio. The hope, Redick said, is to get Reaves back for one of those games. Redick said Reaves had a "great day" on the court Friday and "made a lot of progress."

"We're trying to manage his pain," Redick said. "So, hopefully he'll be back in one of the next two games."
