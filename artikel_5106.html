Another XRP Record Ahead? Ripple CEO Predicts XRP Demand Surge as Inflation-Stricken Markets Seek Alternatives to Fiat

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

XRP could be on the verge of another major adoption milestone as inflation-hit economies increasingly look for alternatives to traditional currencies. At least that’s according to Brad Garlinghouse.

The Ripple frontman sees growing potential for the cryptocurrency, arguing that demand could rise over time as people in struggling markets seek ways to protect their wealth from weakening fiat currencies. His comments spotlight a potential new driver of XRP adoption and raise fresh questions about how far demand could grow.

Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse believes inflation-hit economies could become an increasingly important source of demand for XRP as people seek alternatives to traditional currencies. Speaking during a recent interview with Bloomberg Television at the Singapore FinTech Festival, he explained how persistent inflation and declining purchasing power could encourage more people to explore digital assets.

“XRP is going to draw people’s interest over time, particularly in markets where you have more inflationary dynamics,” he opined.

Garlinghouse argued that the declining value of fiat currencies could make assets with predictable or limited supplies more attractive to investors. To illustrate his point, he cited the U.S. dollar, describing it as one of the world’s best-managed currencies while noting that it had lost approximately half its value over the past decade.

He added that the situation is considerably worse in countries with weaker monetary management, where some national currencies have reportedly lost between 80% and 90% of their value. Such conditions, in his view, could strengthen the appeal of digital assets whose supplies cannot be expanded at the discretion of central banks or governments.

However, scarcity alone is not enough to attract investors. Garlinghouse also highlighted liquidity as an important consideration, noting that people want assets they can easily hold, transfer, and exchange. This combination could work in XRP’s favor as interest in alternative financial assets grows across inflation-hit markets.

His remarks point to a potential source of XRP demand beyond speculative trading and institutional investment. While increased interest does not guarantee sustained buying pressure or higher prices, broader adoption in economies struggling with currency depreciation could ultimately boost XRP’s long-term appeal.

During the interview, Amin also asked Garlinghouse whether he could see Bitcoin eventually rocketing to the $1 million mark, a target previously floated by several prominent figures in the crypto industry.

The Ripple chief was receptive to the possibility, although he admitted that predicting when such a milestone might happen was difficult. His optimism was largely rooted in the declining purchasing power of traditional currencies rather than any specific Bitcoin price model.

Bitcoin has a hard supply cap of 21 million coins, while XRP’s total supply was established at 100 billion tokens. Their fixed supply structures distinguish them from government-issued currencies, which can be expanded through monetary policy.

Nevertheless, scarcity does not automatically translate into higher valuations or make either cryptocurrency immune to inflation. Both Bitcoin and XRP remain highly volatile assets whose prices are influenced by market demand and broader economic conditions.

Bitcoin surged above $64,400 as it extended its weekend recovery following Friday’s sharp drop below $56,000, while Ripple’s XRP also staged a rebound, briefly reaching $1.02, per CoinGecko. However, both cryptocurrencies have since surrendered part of those gains. At the time of writing, BTC had pulled back to around $61,271, while XRP had also retreated to $0.9435, reflecting renewed selling pressure after the brief recovery.
