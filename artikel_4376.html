The Suzuki Ignis is back, and this time it will be a low-cost EV sold globally [Images]

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Suzuki offered a first look at the all-new Ignis, its first compact EV designed specifically for global markets.

The first-generation Ignis went on sale back in 2000 as a three- and five-door hatchback, and in 2016, Suzuki reintroduced it as a compact crossover.

After discontinuing the gas-powered version just last year, Suzuki is now bringing the Ignis back. This time, as a fully electric compact EV.

Suzuki revealed the all-new Ignis EV on Wednesday, deeming it the brand’s “first compact EV for global markets.”

The electric city car is based on the original Ignis and clearly draws inspiration from the e Sky, Suzuki’s first kei EV that debuted last month in Japan.

Unlike the e Sky, the new Ignis EV is designed for global markets, including Europe. Suzuki said the Ignis was developed under the concept of “Easy to Switch,” which, as you can guess, means it’s designed to make it easy for drivers to switch from a gas-powered car to electric.

To ensure it can handle urban areas and winding roads in Europe and other regions, Suzuki put the electric city car through “extensive testing” on public roads.

Suzuki fine-tuned the chassis and power delivery specifically for the different road conditions and needs of drivers in major global markets.

The global Ignis EV is also slightly larger than the e Sky, measuring 3,570 mm long, 1,565 mm wide, and 1,635 mm tall, with a 2,460 mm wheelbase.

It’s built on the same dedicated “HEARTECT-e” EV platform as the e Sky, with a single electric motor that delivers 90 hp (67 kW) and 98 lb-ft (133 Nm) of torque.

The Ignis also uses the same 26 kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery as the e SKY. However, Suzuki hasn’t revealed driving range figures yet. Suzuki didn’t specify, but the LFP battery pack used in the e SKY is supplied by BYD’s battery unit to keep costs down.

According to Suzuki, the e SKY for Japan offers a WLTC driving range of up to 310 km (192 miles) and a “class-leading” AC energy consumption rating of 97 Wh/km.

Inside, the new Suzuki Ignis EV adopts a similar setup to the e Sky with a space-maximizing four-seat layout and a wide infotainment display.

Unfortunately the Seagull is growing in size so it would be good to have the option of this small Suzuki.

It seems likely it will be much more expensive in Europe as all EVs are.

Suzuki will launch the Ignis in Europe and other markets starting in summer 2027. Prices have yet to be announced, but the Ignis will enter an increasingly competitive segment of entry-level compact EVs, including the Renault Twingo, BYD Dolphin Surf, and Honda Super-N.

In Japan, Suzuki’s e SKY undercuts much of the competition with prices starting at just ¥2,120,000 ($13,500).

For context, the Renault Twingo E-Tech starts at under €19,490 ($21,500) in Europe and £14,745 in the UK. BYD’s Dolphin Surf starts at around €22,990 ($25,500) and £18,675 ($24,500) in the UK, while the Honda Super-N starts at £18,995 ($25,000) in the UK.
