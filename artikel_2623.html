The Sixers’ NBA Cup dreams come down to Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

For the first three years of the NBA Cup, the Sixers failed to advance past the group-play stage. This season, they have a chance to make it to the knockout round for the first time ever.

Wednesday unfolded exactly how the Sixers needed it to. Despite being without Joel Embiid and losing Jaylen Brown to a hamstring injury in the first half, the Sixers held on to beat the Miami Heat, 111-104. A few hours later, the New York Knicks pasted the Cleveland Cavaliers, 121-102. (Can someone please stick a fork in the Cavs? For their own sake?)

Here's an updated look at the East Group B standings heading into Friday:

For the Sixers, it's simple: win and they're in. If they beat the Indiana Pacers, they'll clinch East Group B no matter what happens in Knicks-Heat. The Knicks already have two losses, and the Sixers now have the head-to-head tiebreaker over the Heat courtesy of Wednesday's victory. The Cavs have the head-to-head tiebreaker over the Sixers, but that's no longer an issue since they're locked in at 2-2.

If the Sixers lose to the Pacers, their minus-12 point differential will keep them out of the wild-card mix. You might not think that's a big deal, but as Joe Flynn noted on Bluesky, the stakes are sneakily high.

Given the three-year sample size, you might be inclined to discount that as a meaningful correlation. Fair enough. With that said, there's also a financial prize for advancing to the knockout round and beyond, which actually is a big deal for a number of players on the Sixers' roster.

This was the financial breakdown last year, according to Colin Salao of Front Office Sports:

Advance to the knockout round: $53,093 per player

Advance to the semifinals: $106,187 per player

Advance to the NBA Cup final: $212,373 per player

Sure, LeBron James and Joel Embiid might not bat an eye at an extra half-million, but most of their teammates are earning $6 million or less this year. Hell, even LeBron is only earning $3.9 million on his contract. Winning the NBA Cup would be more than a 10 percent bonus!

In years past, stars like LeBron, Giannis Antetokounmpo and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander have spoken about how their lesser-paid teammates give them extra motivation to advance deep into the Cup's knockout round. For young players such as Labaron Philon Jr. ($3.6 million), Dominick Barlow ($3.4 million), Ariel Hukporti ($3.4 million), Jabari Walker ($2.6 million) and Adem Bona ($2.3 million), the Cup prize is a hefty portion of their salary.

For a basketball history buff like LeBron, there's extra incentive to reach the Cup final this year. The Cup semifinals and finals were held in Las Vegas in each of the first three years, but the NBA changed it up this year by moving the semifinals to normal NBA team markets and hosting the final at Hinkle Fieldhouse in Indianapolis. LeBron gushed about the Palestra earlier this year, and it's easy to imagine him getting fired up to play at another iconic non-NBA venue.

The Sixers very well might have to beat the Pacers without Brown, who aggravated his hamstring injury and is set to undergo imaging Thursday. If Embiid can return, they're likely headed for an all-time schedule loss against the Houston Rockets on the second night of a back-to-back Saturday, as both LeBron and Embiid will be more incentivized to suit up against Indiana to clinch a spot in the Cup's knockout round.

If the Sixers take care of business Friday, they'll earn at least $50,000 per player. Not bad for a day's worth of work.
