What Are the Different Types of Storage Beds?

Lift-up beds: Also called an "ottoman bed," this is a model where the mattress base lifts up with the aid of gas struts (pressurized cylinders that do most of the lifting for you), which retailers often call hydraulic lifts. This typically offers the most room out of the storage bed options.

Drawer beds: Fairly self-explanatory: drawers (either under, alongside, or at the foot of the bed) are this frame’s storage approach.

Headboard storage beds: A headboard storage bed draws from mid-century styles, where built-in shelves or nightstands create storage opportunities. This is a good option if what you need to store is also aesthetically pleasing.

Cubby beds: Instead of drawers, these cubbies are open shelving solutions underneath the bed frame, particularly suitable for holding books if you’re looking for a stylish way to create a bedroom library.

What’s a “Hydraulic Lift” in a Storage Bed?

When your storage bed has interior storage (in other words, when you lift the mattress base upward to access its storage), it needs a lift mechanism to make it possible for you to lift the bed without breaking your back, and to keep the slatted frame upright while you lift your items in and out. Most use gas struts, though you'll often see them sold as hydraulic. If your storage bed has interior storage and no lift, you will collapse yourself and ultimately be pancaked by your own sleep-and-store investment.

What Features Should I Look for in the Best Storage Beds?

A good storage bed, in our opinion, doesn't look like a storage bed. It looks like a regular bed. It's crafted from, ideally, kiln-dried hardwood, it has a slatted base so you don't need to bother with a box spring, and it has non-sticky drawers (thank you, Thuma) or a lift mechanism to help with the lifting of interior storage (thank you, Saatva). Look for a solid wood base, linen upholstery, and invest in some vacuum-sealed bags. I live by these.
