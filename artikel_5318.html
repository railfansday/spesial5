Ethereum Dancing on Two Fronts as Quantum Upgrade Advances and Treasury Leverage Bites

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Ethereum developers want to upgrade the network to be quantum-ready by 2027, potentially accelerating one of its biggest security overhauls. Nasdaq-listed BTCS has been selling ETH below its purchase price to manage $64.1 million in outstanding Aave debt.

Both stories carry significant implications for Ethereum’s next growth phase. One could reshape its technical roadmap, while the other underscores the financial risks of treating ETH as collateral for aggressive DeFi strategies.

Ethereum research group Ethlabs wants developers to bring forward the network’s post-quantum security timeline to 2027. Its proposal, called pqai27, argues that advances in artificial intelligence are accelerating the risks surrounding quantum computing and existing cryptographic systems.

The Ethereum quantum upgrade would aim to protect accounts, transactions, and network consensus against future quantum attacks. Ethlabs also wants Ethereum to become more resistant to AI-powered attacks that could expose vulnerabilities in its software.

The proposal comes two years ahead of the Ethereum Foundation’s existing December 2029 target for full post-quantum readiness. Google has separately targeted 2029 for its own post-quantum transition, while the U.S. government has moved its quantum security deadline from 2035 to 2030. Ethlabs wants two milestones achieved during 2027.

The first, called pqai-ready, would establish post-quantum consensus and give users a pathway toward quantum-resistant accounts. The second, pqai-scaled, would bring quantum-resistant transactions and data availability to full network capacity.

Getting there requires developers to rethink upcoming network upgrades. Ethlabs has already offered to withdraw its Quick Slots proposal from the planned Hegotá hard fork to redirect resources toward quantum security.

Quick Slots would have reduced Ethereum’s block production interval from 12 seconds to 10 seconds. The group now wants to prioritize security and revisit faster block production in 2028.

Frame Transactions, meanwhile, remain central to the proposed transition. They would allow accounts to use more flexible authentication methods, including signatures designed to resist quantum attacks.

The Ethereum quantum upgrade could also change transaction economics, as Quantum-resistant signatures generally require more data, potentially increasing transaction costs and creating additional demands on wallet providers.

Ethlabs is effectively asking Ethereum developers to prioritize long-term security over certain performance improvements. This is a shift in priorities as the network competes for institutional adoption and real-world financial activity.

While Ethereum developers focus on future security, corporate ETH holders face a more immediate problem: leverage. BTCS disclosed in its latest quarterly filing that it sold ETH below its original purchase price to manage collateral levels and repay DeFi borrowings.

As of today, the Nasdaq-listed company had $64.1 million in outstanding Aave debt, including accrued interest. It backed that borrowing with 46,676 ETH valued at $99.3 million. That left its collateral worth 155% of outstanding debt. Based on those figures, the position carried an estimated liquidation threshold near $1,717 per ETH.

BTCS also reported $30.6 million in realized losses on digital asset transactions during the first nine months of 2026. The company attributed the increase primarily to ETH sales used to manage collateral and repay DeFi borrowings, alongside accounting losses from depositing ETH into liquidity pools.

The company did not disclose how much ETH it sold below cost or how much of its realized losses came directly from those sales. But its borrowing history reveals the scale of the strategy. Between January 2025 and November 11, 2026, BTCS borrowed $152.4 million in stablecoins through Aave, primarily USDT and GHO. It repaid $89.4 million over the same period.

The company said no full or partial liquidation had occurred as of November 11.

Nevertheless, the position illustrates the pressure that leveraged corporate treasury strategies can face when ETH prices fall. Borrowing against ETH creates additional capital opportunities, but declining collateral values can force companies to sell holdings or repay debt.
