Fire In The Sky

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

A take in a fiery sunrise and discover that New Jersey holds its own when it comes to Appalachian Trail scenery.

White-tailed deer seem to like hiking up on this ridge. I nearly walked straight into one yesterday evening on my way into camp. I had a feeling I might get a visit from it during the night. Sure enough a deer was kind enough to give me a 4:00AM wake-up with its signature snort. By the time sunrise came around I was mostly alert. It helped that the overnight low was around 42 degrees, which felt balmy compared to recent nights. As light broke, the same rooster from last evening beckoned the sun. A layer of cotton clouds filled the sky, turning into a flame-like orange when the sun finally appeared. It was a true feast for the eyes and I hardly needed to step out of my tent to see it.

Having already emerged from my tent, I needed only to have breakfast before I felt it was time to start hiking. It was going to be a long day.

Pre-sunrise. I can still hear the rooster below.

The trail south from my camping spot continued the trend of flat and mild miles, much to my relief. The views continued as I passed down the trail. I felt almost like I was back in a highland trail area. The trail was smooth and easy, and I could look in any direction and have a beautiful view. I was content with the views of the Yards Creek Reservoir and the east end of Mount Tammany, but the trail was about to surprise me in a major way.

After entering the Worthington State Forest the trail emerged onto an open peak with 360 degree views. The peak, called Raccoon Ridge and/or Kittatinny Mountain, looks out on the Hudson Valley to the east and the Delaware River and Pennsylvania to the west. The trees below the peak were a mix of deep brick red and mustard yellow. The sun has suddenly emerged from the clouds, heating the ground below and causing a layer of fog to emerge from the valley. I didn’t have spectacular views in New Jersey on my Appalachian Trail bingo card, I’ll have to settle for egg on my face.

Not far down down the trail I saw something rather confounding. An Appalachian Trail white blaze was running away from me, bounding down the path ahead. Upon closer inspection it was yet another white-tailed deer. I probably should have known. This one chose to stop and watch me pass, allowing me to catch a picture of it staring back at me with curiosity.

The Delaware River and Pennsylvania viewed from Raccoon Ridge.

I continued my mission south, eventually reaching the 1,300 mile marker (from Springer Mountain). I didn’t see a marker on the trail so I took it upon myself to use my precious time today to make one from pebbles on the trail. About half a mile down the trail Dunnfield Creek appeared and guided me down to the trailhead. A flurry of day hikers were scoping out the creek. I thought they might have been given the day off since it is election day. I turned around at the trailhead and made my way back up along the creek.

Shortly after noon I was back at Sunfish Pond and opted for the trail that follows the southern shore to try something new and pass by a viewpoint that looks across the pond. The return to Raccoon Ridge was next, somehow the lighting and scenery was even better than it had been earlier in the day. I am proud of the pictures I was able to take. If anyone asks me what state was the most surprising, relative to expectations, I think the answer is going to be New Jersey. I already want to come back and see this section again.

The Delaware River and Pennsylvania viewed from Raccoon Ridge.

A few miles north of Raccoon Ridge I came upon a group of hikers taking a break by the trail. They were intrigued by my attempt to hike the AT twice this year and fired off plenty of questions. The nearby Mohican Outdoor Center managed by the Appalachian Mountain Club likely attracts visitors to the area. I took one of my trademark diversions from the AT to get a look at the facility, but didn’t go inside to save time. Handily, there was potable water on the trail, allowing me to top off my water bottle, which I definitely needed having hiked almost 20 miles to this point. I continued onto the Rattlesnake Swamp trail which runs parallel to the AT all the way back to Millbrook Road. I didn’t see any rattlesnakes, but I did see a group of young hikers on an expedition from Philadelphia. It looked like some of them were backpacking for the first time. Comically, their packs were easily twice the size of mine.

Turning off the AT to visit the AMC Mohican.

Exiting the Rattlesnake Swamp Trail and rejoining the AT.

Although I was now back at my van on Millbrook Road, I still had another 7 miles to work through in order to connect to the spot where I’d turned around yesterday. I said it was going to be a full day. Thankfully the trail was nice to me the entire time, making the miles feel easy. Apart from a beaver pond and a few open views near some power lines there wasn’t much to report from the last miles. I was certainly beat by the time I got back to my van. Having to finish a long day by 5:00PM seems completely foreign to me, but that is what the daylight dictates this time of year.

I was overdue for some calories so I crossed the Delaware River into Pennsylvania for the first time since July and got myself some town food. I worked on my blog posts from the past two days in the hopes that I could repeat the feat of last week of giving myself a mostly free Sunday. Tomorrow I’d begin another 2-day backpacking stint, thankfully with far fewer miles than today.

View from the trail north of Millbrook Road.
