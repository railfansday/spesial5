Hike of the Week: Explore a Wilderness With Wolves and Endless Flat-Topped Peaks

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Flat Tops Wilderness Area in northwestern Colorado is one of my favorite places to drop off-grid. Among its basalt peaks and sweeping valleys are some of the state’s best trails, in my opinion. Its “hidden-gem” days are long gone—lots of people, especially those from Colorado’s Western Slope, recreate here. Yet, you can still find areas where you’re the only human for miles.

That solitude can be intoxicating. The 10.9-mile out-and-back to Marvine Lakes via the Marvine Trail could easily be a dayhike. But when I visited with a group of friends this summer, we wanted to spend time marinating in the scenery, lounging on the lakes’ shoreline with some beers, and enjoying a nice bagged meal with friends. I saw more wildlife on that trip than I have on any other in a long time: We crossed paths with elk, mule deer, beavers, and a silver-colored canid that looked much bigger than any coyote I’ve ever seen. Was it a wolf? I can’t say with certainty, but the Flat Tops is one of the areas where Colorado’s wolf reintroduction program has been most successful.

The trail starts at East Marvine Campground, about 35 miles east of Meeker. When we arrived, several outfitters were saddling horses and preparing to ride out with their groups. The trail is multi-use, though mountain bikes and motorized vehicles are not permitted in wilderness areas. Dogs are allowed, and leashes are not required. But hikers should stay both stay horse- and bear-aware.

The hike itself isn’t too strenuous, climbing just 1,045 feet over the 5.4 miles to the lakes. It passes small beaver ponds, pops out into meadows where you might catch a glimpse of browsing elk or deer, and winds through thick old-growth pine and spruce forest. The hike follows pristine Marvine Creek the entire way. Water is never an issue as long as you’ve got a means of purifying it.

When you arrive at lower Marvine Lake, you’ll find turquoise water reflecting the stratigraphy of the towering cliffs that hem the valley. The trail splits and goes around both sides of the lake, passing dispersed camping spots. We picked one on the very far end of lower Marvine Lake, between it and upper Marvine Lake. Our group hung out well past dark, expecting the kind of stars that only the remote corners of the Rockies can deliver. We were not disappointed.

We hiked out the next day, retracing our footsteps to the trailhead. If you wanted a longer hike, the Marvine Trail extends deeper east into the Flat Tops Wilderness Area and connects to an entire trail network. Following it all the way out to Twin Lakes and returning via the East Marvine Lakes Trail would make a 22.5-mile loop and end at the same trailhead you started at, with more alpine ponds and lakes along the way.
