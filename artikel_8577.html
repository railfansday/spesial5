Use TurboTax Experts for Business if you’re a sole proprietor or run an S corp or partnership and want a tax expert to check your return or prepare it. For an S corp, doing the entry yourself in Expert Assist Business costs less than half what Full Service charges. A C corporation can’t use it, so hire a CPA.

For a business owner, the business return is the hardest part of tax season to do alone. A sole proprietor adds Schedule C, the form that reports business profit, to a personal return. An S corporation’s Form 1120-S and a partnership’s Form 1065 are due a month before personal returns, each with a Schedule K-1 that reports every owner’s share of the profit. TurboTax Experts for Business lets you prepare that return with an expert on call, or hand it to an expert who prepares, signs, and files it.

TurboTax belongs to Intuit, which began operating in 1983 and is based in Mountain View, California. TurboTax brought Intuit $5.3 billion in revenue in the fiscal year that ended July 31, 2026. TurboTax says it has more than 10,000 business tax experts and matches each owner with one who knows the owner’s industry. The experts report an average of 13 years of tax experience. The prices below are the ones it still listed in mid-November 2026, for 2025 returns, since it hadn’t yet posted prices for 2026 returns.

TurboTax prices each tier by the return your business files. Expert Assist and Full Service both include unlimited expert help by phone, chat, or one-way video, from 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. Pacific time every day of tax season.

Those prices held for sole proprietors who filed by March 31 and for S corps and partnerships that filed by April 30, with late-season prices after that. If your return needs so much help that the expert ends up preparing it, IRS rules can require the expert to sign as preparer, and TurboTax’s terms say you may then have to switch to Full Service.

A dedicated expert matched to your industry starts with a video call, prepares the return, and walks you through it before signing and filing. Last season, an S corp or partnership return cost $1,549, plus $69 for each state. A sole proprietor’s price started at $249, which TurboTax says covers a W-2, Form 1040, Schedule C, and quarterly tax estimates. More forms cost more, and you get an estimate before work begins. Sole proprietors can also meet their expert in person at one of TurboTax’s Expert offices.

For a sole proprietor with just a W-2 and a Schedule C, Full Service started only $20 above Expert Assist, $249 against $229, though its price rises as forms are added. For an S corp or partnership, handing off the return cost $910 more, $1,549 against $639. Since Expert Assist already includes an expert to ask and a final review, that $910 buys the expert’s time doing the preparation.

Full Service won’t do your bookkeeping, though. TurboTax’s terms require fully reconciled books, meaning records that match your bank and card statements, and let the expert refuse to start until they do. They also limit Full Service to businesses with a December 31 year-end, and new customers can’t use it for a business’s final return. If you can’t finish by the deadline, your expert files Form 7004, the IRS’s extension request for business returns, which adds six months.

This tier is a subscription for sole proprietors and single-member LLCs (limited liability companies) who want one expert all year. Your expert reconciles your books in QuickBooks each quarter and works out your quarterly estimated tax payments. You also get quarterly planning sessions, a check on whether another business structure would cut your taxes, and your return filed through Full Service at no extra cost. TurboTax’s terms leave tax planning out of its other tiers.

It costs $99 a month, including a QuickBooks Simple Start subscription billed alongside it, or $1,069 billed yearly, which TurboTax says saves $119. You can try it free for 30 days. It’s open only to owners new to QuickBooks, and an S corp and partnership version at $199 a month is listed as coming soon. The estimates leave out sales and payroll taxes, and canceling brings no refund for the rest of the billing period.

Experts for Business prepares Schedule C for sole proprietors and single-member LLCs, Form 1120-S for S corps, and Form 1065 for partnerships and multi-member LLCs. It won’t prepare Form 1120, the return a C corporation files to pay its own income tax, or one for a business taxed as a C corporation. Trusts and estates, nonprofits, and returns needing more than five state filings are out too.

A C corporation that wants a professional needs a CPA or another paid preparer. One doing its own return can use TurboTax Business, TurboTax’s Windows desktop software, which prepares C corporation, trust, and estate returns as well as S corp and partnership returns. It has no expert to ask, and TurboTax’s audit support guarantee doesn’t cover it.

S corp and partnership returns come due early. The IRS’s tax calendar puts their deadline on the 15th day of the third month after the tax year ends. For a business whose tax year ends December 31, the 2026 return is due Monday, March 15, 2027, and Form 7004 extends that six months. TurboTax’s pages still showed March 16, the 2026 deadline, which fell a day late because March 15, 2026, was a Sunday. A sole proprietor’s Schedule C is due with the personal return on April 15.

The owner’s personal return belongs to TurboTax’s personal software. A sole proprietor can also get expert help on Schedule C there, in Expert Assist Premium, which listed at $209 last season against $229 for Expert Assist Business. S corp and partnership owners who file the business return first get 20% off one 2026 personal federal return in TurboTax Online, state fees excluded.

Audit Defense has limits of its own. TaxAudit’s membership terms rule out businesses with more than $5 million in gross receipts, meaning total sales before costs. They also cover income tax audits only, not payroll or sales tax audits, and they don’t include representation in court.

TurboTax Experts for Business is a good fit for a sole proprietor or a small S corp or partnership that wants an expert to check or prepare its return. A sole proprietor with a simple Schedule C should check the Full Service price first, since it started just $20 above Expert Assist’s last season. An S corp or partnership gets the better value from Expert Assist Business, with an industry-matched expert, a final review, and Audit Defense for $639, against $1,549 for Full Service.

It’s the wrong fit for a C corporation, which needs a CPA or desktop software, and for owners whose books aren’t reconciled. S corp and partnership owners who keep their own books should open an Expert Assist Business return well before the March 15, 2027 deadline.
