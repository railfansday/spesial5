5 things we learned from the Chiefs feasting on Dirty Bird

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

For most of the first half, it looked like the Kansas City Chiefs were the better team, even if the offense on the field didn't reflect that. Then, just before halftime, the Chiefs trotted Harrison Butker out to attempt a field goal — and he made it!

Harrison Butker actually made a field goal.

An Atlanta Falcons player jumped offside.

The extra five yards gave the Chiefs a first down. The very next play, quarterback Patrick Mahomes threw a dime to his brand-new target, Josh Downs, who brought in the pass for a touchdown.

Here are five things we learned from the Chiefs' 35-13 victory over the Falcons.

I say this a little tongue-in-cheek. We've known this team was better than their 2025 iteration pretty much from the jump. But it's like they say, you are only as good as your record. And up until now, the Chiefs had not surpassed their disappointing 6-11 record from a year ago.

With this week's drubbing of the hapless Falcons, the Chiefs have amassed their seventh win of the season, ensuring that no matter what happens the rest of the way out, they've locked up a better record than last year.

Now, the only question that remains is: how much better are they?

A lot has been said about the decline in the Chiefs' air yards per attempt in recent years, including their modest 4.15 air yards per attempt over the last four games heading into Atlanta.

Over the years, we've heard every speculated reason for why the Chiefs couldn't seem to find success throwing the deep ball.

Some said it was because we traded away Tyreek Hill.

Some said it was because Mahomes had lost his ability to throw the ball 20+ yards accurately.

Some blamed it on coaching and playcalling.

Some said it was a deficit of talent at the receiving position.

Some said teams adapted to take away the deep ball.

Most said it was a combination of all four — but we now have definitive empirical evidence that rules Mahomes out of the equation. On Sunday, Mahomes completed six out of seven passes downfield.

Mahomes never lost the ability to throw the ball deep — he just needed the pieces to fall back into place for it to make sense to air it out.

We knew the Chiefs needed help at wide receiver.

We knew that the Chiefs had their eyes set on trading for wide receiver Josh Downs from the Indianapolis Colts.

And when they pulled off the trade just before the deadline, we all crossed our fingers and prayed he was the missing piece to this passing attack. And while the jury is still out on whether the issues around this passing attack are fully solved, I think it's fair to say Downs looks like everything we were hoping for.

Sure, it's a small sample size, and he's not a superstar.

But he's a good route runner, with great hands, and a smart head on his shoulders.

Something the Chiefs have been missing at wide receiver for the last couple of seasons.

The only question now is, just how good is Josh Downs?

It's hard to say, especially when he's had the likes of Anthony Richardson, Gardner Minshew, and Daniel Jones throwing him the football over the last four seasons.

Another thing that the jury is still out on is how much better this team is with Downs on it?

Maybe we already knew this one before this game.

But there is a difference between being the change-of-pace back and being the workhorse expected to put the offense on your shoulders and carry it downfield to the promised land.

With starting running back Kenneth Walker sidelined with injury, the Chiefs rookie running back got his first chance to be the first man up in the backfield, and boy howdy, he did not disappoint.

I don't think there is any question that Johnson has been the best rookie running back in the NFL this season. It's not surprising, considering he was fourth in the nation in rushing last season while playing at Nebraska.

A lot will be made this week about the Chiefs rediscovering their deep ball.

But none of the above happens without Johnson keeping the defense honest.

We obviously want Kenneth Walker III back as soon as possible, but until then, we've got E-Money.

With the turmoil at the Chiefs' offensive tackle position this season, Kansas City has at least been able to rest on the fact that when left guard Kingsley Suamataia is not in head coach Andy Reid's doghouse, the Chiefs have one of the better interior offensive lines in the NFL.

So when right guard Trey Smith left the game with a concussion, the Chiefs had no choice but to put Mike Caliendo back in.
