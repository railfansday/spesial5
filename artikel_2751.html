NFL news, live updates: Rams' Myles Garrett in line to return Sunday; Shedeur Sanders to start for Browns with Deshaun Watson in concussion protocol

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

We're officially into Week 9 of the NFL season (and the looming NFL trade deadline), where teams will have to make a decision on the intended course of the remainder of their season — are they going all in? Or is it time to tear it all down and look toward the future?

NFL fans also await news on the fate of star running back Jonathan Taylor, who has been touted as a potential trade-deadline mover as the Indianapolis Colts kicked off their franchise reset with a fire sale. They've already traded away two foundational players, Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner, for several draft picks as they look to the future, leaving us to wonder whether Taylor could be next.

The Los Angeles Rams could be a deadline buyer, as they've won back-to-back games and are already getting a long-awaited midseason reinforcement in the form of edge rusher Myles Garrett, a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year and the league's single-season sack record holder.

Garrett's in line for a return to game action on the road Sunday against the Washington Commanders. Coming back from early-season knee surgery that landed him on injured reserve, Garrett has practiced all week.

Shedeur Sanders will start at quarterback for the Cleveland Browns in Week 9 against the New Orleans Saints, head coach Todd Monken announced.

Deshaun Watson returned to practice in a limited capacity on Friday, but remains in concussion protocol. Monken said it's unclear if Watson will serve as the backup this week, though he will be listed as questionable

The Browns' No. 1 quarterback took a helmet-to-helmet hit during the first quarter of Sunday's loss to the Pittsburgh Steelers. Watson missed only four snaps, but later reported experiencing concussion symptoms on Monday night.

Follow along with Yahoo Sports for the latest NFL news, injury updates and more ahead of Week 9.

The Lions will be without linebacker Jack Campbell for at least the team's next four games, as they are adding him to injured reserve.

Campbell was involved in a multi-player tackle in Detroit's game against the Vikings that resulted in him leaving the game on a backboard and being taken to a local hospital.

The Lions haven't given many updates, and the most recent was from Campbell himself, who on Wednesday said that he was still in the hospital recovering. The linebacker said he has "full movement" in his lower body and is making progress on movement in his upper body.

Neither Detroit nor Campbell have given any sort of timeline for when he could return to the field. By being added to IR, Campbell would be first eligible to play again in Week 13, depending on his recovery.

Not only will the New Orleans Saints reportedly welcome rookie wide receiver Jordyn Tyson into the fold Sunday against the Cleveland Browns, but they also plan on activating left tackle Kelvin Banks Jr., per ESPN's Jeremy Fowler.

Banks, last year's No. 9 overall pick, suffered a high ankle sprain in September that required surgery. He hasn't played since Week 2.

The delayed debut of New Orleans Saints rookie wide receiver Jordyn Tyson is reportedly right around the corner.

According to Jordan Schultz, Tyson will be activated off IR to face the Browns in Week 9. The No. 8 overall pick in the 2026 NFL draft has yet to play due to a hamstring injury.

Shedeur Sanders has already been named as the starter for Week 9. The question now is if Watson, who officially cleared concussion protocol on Saturday, will serve as the backup. If he is unable to, Dillon Gabriel will be QB2.

The Denver Broncos are looking to add a tight end before Tuesday's trade deadline.

Evan Engram has 13 receptions for 136 yards and 1 touchdown, and the team reportedly wants to upgrade.

The Broncos aimed high, checking in on Arizona Cardinals All-Pro Trey McBride, who currently leads the NFL with 66 catches. Understandably, the Cardinals said no, according to ESPN. McBride was signed to a four-year extension last year that made him the highest-paid tight end in the league at the time.

Other tight ends that could be available may include the Philadelphia Eagles' Dallas Goedert, Noah Fant from the New Orleans Saints, the Atlanta Falcons' Austin Hooper and the Dallas Cowboys' Luke Schoonmaker.

According to Adam Schefter, Henry is expected to remain with the Ravens through Tuesday's NFL trade deadline.

Following Baltimore's Thursday night loss to Jacksonville, Henry reiterated that he wants to stay with the Ravens.

"I have not mentioned me going anywhere else," he said. "I'm saying, 'Let's go back to work. Let's try to get better and turn this thing around.'"

The Colts remain interested in moving pieces out as they rebuild. According to Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter, along with discussions involving running back Jonathan Taylor, the team is listening in on suitors for quarterback Daniel Jones.

The Ravens were reportedly a team that call, but that was before they went and acquired Jacoby Brissett this week from the Cardinals.

Jones has a $10.51 million injury guarantee for this season that is fully guaranteed in March 15 if he's still on the Colts' roster. Jones could waive the injury guarantee if he wanted to move on.

The Seattle Seahawks sent a pair of draft picks to the Indianapolis Colts in exchange for six-time All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson.

After just one game in a Seahawks uniform, Nelson is now out.

He's "week-to-week," according to Seattle head coach Mike Macdonald, who told reporters Friday that the 30-year-old offensive lineman needs a minor knee procedure.

"We're not going to put him on IR," Macdonald said, via The Seattle Times' Bob Condotta.

When asked what happened to Nelson, Macdonald said the interior o-lineman had something he was dealing with when he arrived.

"We re-imaged it, and it was aggravated to some degree, so we've kind of done the due diligence, and everybody agrees it's best to take care of it now and play the long game."

Notably, the Seahawks will also be down quarterback Sam Darnold (quad) this week against the Arizona Cardinals. Backup Drew Lock will start in his place.

Detroit Lions safety Kerby Joseph is out for Week 9's matchup with the Miami Dolphins. However, Joseph took first-team reps as a limited participant in practice all week and "looked awesome," according to Lions safeties coach Jim O'Neil.

"The focus right now is let's see if we can stack some days," O'Neil told the Detroit Free Press' Dave Birkett. "Let's see if he can look like he looked yesterday for three or four days in a row."

Joseph, who turns 26 on Nov. 14, hasn't played for the Lions since Week 6 of last season due to a cartilage condition in his knee. He was an All-Pro selection in 2024, his third NFL season, compiling 9 interceptions and 83 tackles.

The Lions signed Joseph to a four-year extension in April 2025 that made him the highest-paid safety in the NFL at the time.

Injuries continue to plague the Eagles, who will again be without top receivers DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks this week against the Giants. The team has officially ruled out both wideouts for Sunday's game, after neither practiced on Friday.

Both Smith, who has been dealing with a hamstring injury, and Wicks, who has an ankle injury, have missed two games with the injury, and are set to now miss a third.

However, there's a bit of good news: Saquon Barkley, who was listed as limited in practice with hamstring and back injuries, should be clear to play on Sunday.

Kansas City Chiefs head coach Andy Reid told reporters Friday that rookies Peter Woods and Mansoor Delane won't play Sunday against the New York Jets.

"They're making progress, so that's a positive thing," Reid said of the defensive tackle and cornerback, per ESPN's Nate Taylor.

Woods is dealing with a neck injury, and Delane is navigating a shoulder issue. Delane, this year's No. 6 overall pick, hasn't played since a Week 7 loss to the Seattle Seahawks. Woods, whom the Chiefs selected 29th overall back in April, suffered his setback last week in a defeat to the Denver Broncos.

The New England Patriots have ruled out both wide receiver A.J. Brown (ankle/knee) and running back TreVeyon Henderson (illness) for Sunday's game against the Green Bay Packers.

They've listed tight end Hunter Henry — the team's receptions leader — as "questionable" with a foot issue.

Brown, whom the Patriots gave up a pair of picks for (including a 2028 first-rounder), has appeared in only three games this season. He suffered a high ankle sprain in the season opener and noted late last month that he wasn't 100 percent upon returning to game action.

As for Henderson, the second-year back revealed on Friday that he was hospitalized after experiencing "unexplained neurological symptoms."

Although the results of Henderson's examination were "positive and reassuring," doctors have yet to determine a cause, Henderson explained in his social media post.
