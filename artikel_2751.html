How Alaska's prior 3A volleyball finalists took different paths back to state this year

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Nov. 13—Less than 20 miles separate the communities of Kenai and Nikiski in Southcentral Alaska.

Their high school varsity volleyball teams have combined to claim the last four 3A state titles and have faced off in the finals in each of the last two years, with the Kenai Central Kardinals topping the Nikiski Bulldogs to win the 2025 title.

Both perennial contenders are back at this year's state tournament, which runs through Saturday at the Alaska Airlines Center in Anchorage. But the paths they took to get back to the sport's biggest stage were polar opposites.

Last year, Kenai was powered to the program's third championship in the last four seasons by All-State senior outside hitter Sophie Tapley, who is now a freshman at the University of Alaska Anchorage.

Instead of looking for one player to step up and fill the star-shaped void Tapley left in their lineup, the Kardinals have collectively elevated their game to make up for the loss.

"It's been a whole team sort of effort," head coach Tracie Beck said. "I have a libero that's a senior and a setter that's a senior, and so those guys are kind of the base, but all of them have stepped up in different ways, especially those young ones."

In their opening-round sweep of Barrow in this year's tournament Thursday morning, Beck specifically praised the efforts of freshman Caitlyn Cramer for stepping up as a server, a trait many of her underclassmen share.

"They're serving consistently and getting points by themselves at the serving line," Beck said. "It's crucial. It's kind of taking the whole team where we had a big gun that we could look to and shut things down the last few years. So the team is definitely well-rounded. Everybody has to play their role for us to find success."

As for top-seeded Nikiski, a major catalyst for the team's success has been the prolific contributions of 6-foot junior middle blocker Blakeley Jorgensen.

"Blakeley is a great player and that's definitely who they rely on when times are tough," Beck said. "In small towns, we don't get big players like 6-footers, 5-9, 5-10, 5-11."

Jorgensen's 529 kills entering the state tournament lead the Bulldogs by nearly 200, and her 109 total blocks reflect dozens more than her next closest teammate.

"I think just having that next-play mentality and letting go of mistakes is one thing that helps me," Jorgensen said.

She was a freshman on the 2024 Bulldogs team that won state and added to her state tournament Player of the Game certificate collection on Thursday by leading the Nikiski to a three-set sweep of Nome-Beltz, marking the fourth of her career.

"It's really nice getting these, but I just play for my teammates, and I just want to support them," Jorgensen said. "Especially my seniors. We have eight this year, so it's a lot."

Behind her for the second-most kills and blocks on the team is senior Mandee Roofe, who was injured in last year's state tournament and is another powerful presence in the middle of the net at 5-foot-11.

Her mindset heading into the rest of the tournament is to "play hard and have fun," with less emphasis on trying to avenge last year's unsuccessful title defense.

"We're just going to try our hardest and go out strong," Jorgensen said. "Right now we've got some injured players, some sick players too, so we're trying to play through that and play as a team and work together."

Kenai and Nikiski faced off four times heading into the state tournament, with the Kardinals holding the edge in the head-to-head record at 3-1.

"I love playing Nikiski," Beck said. "They're our neighbors and (Nikiski head coach) Stacey (Segura) and I are very competitive. I respect what she has done with that program. She was here longer than me, so I tease her about being the old goat around."

The two teams and coaching staffs know each other so well that their matches have the feel of a friendly intersquad scrimmage rather than a fierce rivalry grudge match.

"It's a fun matchup and it's always great to play them," Beck said. "You know them about as well as you do your own players."
