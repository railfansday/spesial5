OHSAA suspends Feister brothers ahead of Massillon football showdown game

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

(This breaking news story will be updated as new information becomes available.)

The Feister brothers are out again from Massillon football.

On Nov. 19, OHSAA Executive Director Doug Ute suspended Massillon's Brayton and Brydon Feister from playing football for 30 days.

Unlike before, it has nothing to do with nuances of Ohio High School Athletic Association bona fide residence exception rules. Or the Feister family's constitutional rights and their freedom to decide which house, community and school is best for them.

This time, it involves questions about purchases worth hundreds of thousands of dollars and possible recruiting.

A source familiar with the investigation who is not authorized to speak publicly said the family has been asked to provide documentation to explain apparent cash purchases of their Massillon house and two vehicles. OHSAA rules prohibit recruiting inducements.

The association has not yet been satisfied with the answers.

"Our bylaws are designed to protect the integrity of interscholastic athletics and ensure a level playing field for all member schools," OHSAA Executive Director Doug Ute said in a statement released by the organization. "We take allegations of recruiting and impermissible benefits seriously, and we have a responsibility to investigate those concerns thoroughly before reaching a final determination. As I've previously stated, it is always difficult to remove students from athletic competition. However, we are charged with enforcing the rules and regulations our membership has voted into place and we are hopeful for a swift and complete resolution to this investigation soon."

According to the OHSAA release, the suspension will remain in effect for a period not to exceed 30 days while the investigation continues. The season ends with the state finals Dec. 3-5, in Canton.

The OHSAA will not comment further while the investigation is pending.

The decision does not affect the team's eligibility.

Massillon plays Columbus Bishop Watterson on Nov. 20 in Zanesville in a Division II, Region 7 final. Many pundits have said the Tigers and Eagles are the two best teams in Ohio, regardless of division.

Public records show in early May the family purchased a $360,000 house in Massillon without a mortgage being recorded in Stark County. Other public records show the family bought a $238,655 residence in Green last year, and took out a mortgage for $226,722.

The house in Green was transferred to a family business three months ago, but public records show the mortgage has not been paid off.

The source said the OHSAA investigation found the family also acquired a Dodge Charger, valued at $107,000, and a Dodge Durango SUV, valued at $35,000, at around the same time as the Massillon home purchase.

At least one of the vehicles — which are driven by the brothers — is visible in some photos the Feister family themselves shared on social media earlier this month.

At the time, they wrote that someone with the OHSAA shot the photos and sent them to Massillon school officials. They complained in a Facebook post that it was evidence the association was stalking and harassing them.

"Our family is devastated," Bo Feister, father of the boys and a daughter, wrote. "Our children are terrified. And frankly, we never imagined we would find ourselves in a position where we would have to publicly beg our own community for help protecting our children."

If either of the Feister brothers had signed a name, image and likeness deal, the details would have been disclosed to the OHSAA. That's part of the NIL rule which association member schools approved in 2025.

The city wears its tradition of gridiron excellence with great pride and it fuels a deep and sprawling fanbase.

The Massillon Washington High football story dates to 1894. Its 978 wins make it the second-winningest program in American high school football history, trailing only Georgia's Valdosta High. Along the way, the Tigers collected 24 Ohio state championships during the pre-playoff era, along with another on the field in 2023.

However, this season was filled with trials and tribulations.

Beginning on Sept. 25, Tigers fans probably found themselves reading court dockets more than box scores.

In the ensuing six weeks, a series of legal back-and-forth decisions on the athletic eligibility of the Feister brothers and four other transfers made it difficult to keep up with who would or would not suit up on a given Friday night.

The tug of war was over these six players:

Senior Brayton Feister, a 6-foot-3, 230-pound linebacker and running back, who transferred from Akron Archbishop Hoban. He's verbally committed to play football at the University of Oregon.

Brydon Feister, a 6-foot-4, 240-pound junior defensive end, and all-state player at Hoban last year.

Gary Yeager, a freshman running back at St. Vincent-St. Mary last season.

Micah Walker, a 6-foot-7, 330-pound junior starting right tackle, who transferred from Nordonia.

Da'Shaun Williams, a 6-foot-6 junior tight end, who transferred from Euclid.

Dey'Veair Adams, also a St. Vincent-St. Mary transfer, now a sophomore safety at Massillon.

OHSAA rules on such transfers would have permitted all six to play only the first half of the season, after their moves into the district. Each, though, had sought a bona fide residence change exception from the association. It's one of a variety of recognized exceptions that can restore full season athletic eligibility.

By midseason, the OHSAA had not yet ruled on the exception requests.

Because the six were stuck in eligibility limbo their families obtained a temporary order on Sept. 25 in Stark County Common Pleas Court to allow them to keep playing football.

On Sept. 28, the OHSAA ruled the Feisters ineligible.

The association found the Feisters moved to Massillon from Green so the brothers could transfer from Akron Archbishop Hoban. A residence change exception is granted only in situations where the move created a need to transfer — not vice versa.

The state later granted Williams an exception, removing him from the fight.

After a daylong hearing on Oct. 6, visiting Stark County Common Pleas Judge Andrew Norman sided with the OHSAA on the Feisters.

During that hearing, Bo Feister testified that other schools had actively recruited his sons, not the other way around. He said the family found their dream home in Massillon, which prompted the move from Green.

The OHSAA opened a recruiting investigation shortly after.

On the witness stand, Bo Feister didn't detail the family's financial situation, other than to say they'd struggled mightily for years. The family, he explained, had long lived in a trailer park in Tuscarawas County prior to the move to Green.

Three days later the protection order for the Feisters was removed by Norman, severing their situation from three remaining players in limbo. With the Feisters on the sidelines, the Tigers lost 35-21 that night to visiting Cornerstone Christian, of San Antonio, Texas.

"Participation in interscholastic athletics is a privilege, not a right," Norman wrote in his 21-page decision.

Lee Plakas, attorney for the families, appealed to the 5th District Court of Appeals. He also asked the court for an emergency order to make the Feisters eligible again until the full appeals complaint was resolved.

"We have the right to make decisions in private for our family without someone second-guessing our reasons," Plakas told the Canton Repository at the time.

So, the Feisters were back for a 48-14 Tigers pounding of Warren Harding, a 31-0 shellacking of archrival Canton Mckinley, then 48-0 and 21-0 playoff wins over Columbus St. Francis DeSales and Sunbury Big Walnut, respectively.

On Oct. 23, the eve of the McKinley game, the OHSAA had filed a complaint in the Ohio Supreme Court. OHSAA attorney Joseph Fraley asked the high court to nullify the 5th District action.

"The OHSAA is a representative organization" Fraley told the Canton Repository at the time. "The response by the membership was significant."

On Nov. 5, the high court dismissed a Ohio High School Athletic Association complaint, effectively guaranteeing eligibility for the Feister brothers through the playoffs. That ruling, though, was limited to cases tied to the bona fide residence exception.
