TTArtisan’s AF 12mm f/2.8 Is a $489 Ultra-Wide Prime for Full-Frame Cameras

TTArtisan has unveiled the AF 12mm f/2.8 ASPH., a new ultra-wide-angle lens for full-frame Sony and Nikon mirrorless cameras. Better still, it’s very affordable.

TTArtisan says its new lens is built for landscapes, architecture, and night-sky photography, all common applications for ultra-wide lenses. With its f/2.8 aperture, it should work well for astrophotography, though real-world performance will depend on how well it controls optical aberrations. For its part, TTArtisan promises “excellent aberration control” alongside “outstanding resolution,” even when shooting wide open.

The AF 12mm f/2.8 ASPH. lens has 16 optical elements arranged in 10 groups, including a pair of aspherical elements and three extra-low dispersion (ED) elements. The aperture diaphragm features just six blades, a relatively low count by modern standards, though it should produce distinct sunstars.

Many ultra-wide lenses are big, heavy, and difficult to use with traditional front filters, but the TTArtisan AF 12mm f/2.8 ASPH. is an exception. It weighs between 336 and 349 grams (11.85 to 12.31 ounces) and measures 72 to 74 millimeters (2.83 to 2.91 inches) long, with the Nikon Z-mount version being slightly heavier and longer than the Sony E-mount variant. The lens accepts common 82mm front filters, which is great news for landscape photographers who want to use circular polarizing and neutral-density filters. Because the front element is bulbous, mounting filters requires an extending filter-thread adapter, though TTArtisan includes one in the box and it adds minimal bulk.

The autofocus-equipped lens focuses as close as 0.18 meters (7.1 inches), making it well suited for emphasizing close-up foreground elements. Given its ultra-wide 122-degree field of view, though, it’s clearly not a macro lens. TTArtisan says the lens uses a high-performance stepping motor and supports eye- and face-detection autofocus modes on compatible cameras.

Speaking of compatibility, TTArtisan says the lens works seamlessly on Nikon Z-mount bodies, while some older Sony E-mount cameras have limited functionality. The Sony a7 III, for example, is fully compatible for photo modes but only partially compatible for video. The original a7 and a7 II also lack full compatibility. TTArtisan notes that autofocus performance may be limited on certain bodies, while firmware updates are in development for others. More compatibility details are available on the company’s website.

The TTArtisan AF 12mm f/2.8 ASPH. lens is available to preorder now for Nikon Z and Sony E-mount cameras at $489.

Although TTArtisan does not list an L-Mount version for sale, one is referenced on the product page. Since the company already makes several lenses for L-Mount, it is reasonable to expect the AF 12mm f/2.8 ASPH. to arrive in that third mount down the road.
