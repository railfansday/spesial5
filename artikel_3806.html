Space station horror-FPS Derelikt is a lively retro throwback to Alien: Resurrection, though it's better at shooting than scaring

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

I'm a reasonable man. I don't expect every PS1-styled, cosmic horror, nakedly Alien: Resurrection-homaging retro FPS to suffocate me under a blanket of misery. Some of the lighter moments in Derelikt, which released last week, even land: I was grinning my cheeks off as I gorged on the Eldtrich knowledge sealed within an alien obelisk, while repeatedly hanging up on the aghast scientist who kept videocalling me to stop.

Still, Derelikt is fortunate that it's a good shooter, as it often gets muddled on whether it wants to be a horror game as well. It certainly threatens the spooks, trapping you in an oppressively dingy asteroid rig in the orbit of Saturn - the scariest of the ringed gas giants! - and warping most of the other staff into vaguely echinodemic monsterpeople. Also, opening the inventory reveals it to be made of rectangles, a classic symptom of a video game character about to get mangled in the nerves and face.

But nah, shotgun go boom boom. It turns out Derelikt's derelict dwellers are both perfectly susceptible to mundane lead, and sparing users of ambush tactics at most; several even stand around with their backs turned, as if waiting for you to surprise them with a lethal pipe swing. And, even on Medium difficulty, the opening spell of fight-or-run scarcity quickly gives way to an abundance of ammo and health pickups that can easily fill the pocket grid to bursting. I don't claim to have been playing perfectly, but I'm about halfway through and the only way I see myself running low on bullets is if I start using them to punch the complete works of Thomas Campion into a wall.

It's indecisive tonally, as well. The dialogue, and there are a fair few survivors to chat with, seems interested in emulating the gravitas-starved, "Woah man" style of New Millennium action game scripts. Which I can sort of see the logic behind, given the visuals and sound design also, and arguably much more painstakingly, mimic that era. But the downside remains that it ends up with a gravitas-starved, "Woah man" style, just with an extra dash of sideways-winking awareness. Again, it only sometimes works, and it's never scary or eerie. But then there are also more earnest attempts to spin uneasiness, with pages of deranged scribbles left behind by the dwindling crew, and at least a couple of genre-classic wall daubings, which tend to leave me wishing developers Visuwyg had just picked a lane.

Again, though, underneath all the smartarsing is a quality little shooter. The retro presentation does little to soften the heavy, industrial kick of its gunfire, and although many of the meatier freaks are more absorbent than they look, their pained thrashing really helps sell the impact of every individual shot. I also don't really mind the lack of Dead Spacey surprise attacks, as the station's narrow confines tend to force you into tenser, close-range encounters in an organic way anyhow.

More impressive still is how Derelikt manages to fabricate a cold, apprehensive atmosphere befitting its fallen space base, in direct defiance of its own quasi-comedic script. This is mainly thanks to the clanging, low-lit station itself, which teases you with just enough flashes of comfort – a stretch of marble floor here, a messy rec room there – to make your quick return to its steel guts feel all the more unpleasant. Some credit must also go to the puzzling, the cardkey hunts and code searches of which initially had me fearful of the more obstinate, opaque, classic horror style preferred by fellow backwards-looker Silver Pines. Instead, it's more like a compacted Deus Ex, hinting at the need to peek at people's computers and scour the environments for the needed codes. Which makes for generally smoother progress, yes, but also gets you more involved and present inside Derelikt's infested walls.

Midway-through impressions, then: it's a little silly, and perhaps a little confused, but Derelikt finds its focus whenever something needs making dead. I shall continue blasting xenos and burning my eyes out with magic rocks until – no, madam, please hold – further notice.
