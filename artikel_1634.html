Liverpool now in bizarre transfer situation, Andoni Iraola task with finding answer

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Liverpool have found themselves in a bizarre situation. Andoni Iraola has been tasked with finding an answer to it.

Liverpool did something strange in the summer. They decided to buy Bradley Barcola and Victor Munoz, essentially to replace Mohamed Salah. Neither, however, played on the right.

This was strange simply for the fact they were replacing a right-winger with two left-wingers. It was even more strange because the squad already boasted Cody Gakpo and Rio Ngumoha - two more players who naturally operate on the left.

And it was strange for a third reason: Liverpool didn’t have any other right-wingers. Salah was the only one, he left, and they didn’t sign anyone to naturally play his role.

Thus the Reds went into this campaign with a wildly imbalanced attack. Four players who wanted to play on the left and none who were natural from the right - essentially, all four want to cut inside on their right foot and attack defences facing the goal.

None could do that from the opposite side. It’s a massive problem. Or at least, we thought it would be. Bizarrely, the opposite has happened.

Liverpool's opposite problemLiverpool do indeed have a problem where all their wingers play better on the same flank. However, that flank turns out to be the right-hand side. In other words, the side that none of them are used to playing on. But it's true. Barcola looks better out on that flank, as does Munoz (who we thought might lock down the role in pre-season). Ngumoha, despite being so young, has given his best displays on the right. Even Gakpo, who you'd think lacks the pace to be effective on that side, has provided match-winning displays from the right. It's all a bit backwards.

So what's actually happening? The truth is, Liverpool's system isn't setup to benefit wingers looking to cut inside. They play with a very static striker in Alexander Isak who remains in the box more often than not, and a no.10 in Florian Wirtz who roams all over the pitch.

Factor in that teams play with a low-block against the Reds more often than not and suddenly there isn't really space to be effective cutting inside. Opposition teams are company defensively, trying to cut off space there anyway. Isak and Wirtz then take away further space, forcing the wingers to stay very wide.

It means the space is on the flanks and pacey wingers take advantage of that space by going on the outside. Right-footed players struggle to do that effectively on the left but on the right? They can shine. Getting beyond their full-back, driving to the byline, forcing centre-backs to step out and stretching defences.

It works very well, even if it definitely wasn't the plan. Now Iraola has to make a massive decision about it, too. If this plan is working, should he double down on it? Go the traditional winger route and feed a striker in the box?

Or is it a sign that he needs to re-think his tactics to get the best out of these players for what they're typically known for? We're not sure there's a right answer, honestly.

What matters is that Iraola moves forward with a plan effectively - no middle ground. He's got to pick a direction and he's got to do it soon.
