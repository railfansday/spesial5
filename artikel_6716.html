GQ’s 13 Breakout Stars of 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Sometimes it’s fun to be early to the party. GQ launched the Breakouts portfolio in 2014 to celebrate the Hollywood upstarts who were poised to do big things. And not to brag, but we nailed it; that first group included Miles Teller, Shailene Woodley, Eddie Redmayne, and the late, great Chadwick Boseman. Over the ensuing years, we featured plenty more soon-to-be-superstars: Rami Malek. Brie Larson. Daniel Kaluuya. Riz Ahmed. Barry Keoghan. The kids from Stranger Things, when they were still kids. The cast of Succession before Succession became everyone’s favorite show.

In 2017, we put a baby-faced Timothée Chalamet on the list for his turn in Call Me by Your Name. (Our global entertainment director, Dana Mathews, fondly recalls him turning to her during the Man of the Year Party and saying, “This is the best party I’ve ever been to in my life.”) Since then, Chalamet has appeared on three GQ covers, earned four Oscar nominations, and become a household name (and maybe been to a few more great parties).

We took a few years off (okay, fine, seven), but 2026 feels like exactly the right time to revive GQ’s Breakouts portfolio—now through a wider lens. Yes, though we’ve got eyes on Hollywood’s next big things—like, say, Sly Stallone doppelgänger Anthony Ippolito; Gen Z class clown Owen Thiele, of Adults; Misty Green’s astounding leading woman, Rosalind Eleazar—not everyone is working the camera. We’re excited to introduce you to the West Coast’s hottest up-and-coming rapper in AZ Chike and put you on to Fcukers, whose debut album, packed with rowdy electro-pop bangers, made them first in our hearts and on our pregaming playlists. We’ve also got podcaster and journalist Pablo Torre, whose relentless reporting upended a $7.8 billion NBA franchise, and Jordan Firstman, an internet-born comedian who wrote and directed a film that commanded a lengthy standing ovation from Cannes audiences (right after they finished crying).

Point is, our Breakouts class of 2026 brings together 13 fully minted stars who did something that feels increasingly impossible: They captured our attention in a time when it feels like attention is more fleeting than ever. Welcome to their party.

Age: 25Breakout moment: Playing the queen bee on Ryan Murphy’s ’80s glam fever dream The Shards.—————

It’s a different kind of attention that I actually prefer. Because for so long I was getting attention, but not for a specific thing. And that was confusing and kind of messed with my head a bit. I was really young and people knew who I was, but I couldn’t really point to the work that they knew. And so it’s really fun for me to have people [now say], “I watched The Shards,” or “I watched Bottoms,” and then we have something to talk about.

Infinite Jest—no, I’m kidding, they’ve already read it, all of them. A Man’s Place by Annie Ernaux is very good and easily digestible. I think Just Kids is wonderful. What We Talk About When We Talk About Love. That one’s really great. Oh, and this is so classic, but Nine Stories by J.D. Salinger is really good. I know guys—male actors especially—love The Catcher in the Rye.

I got really worried the other day because I started getting a lot of followers. It was two days of so many followers, and I was like, “Oh my God, I’ve been doxxed.” The last thing I logged was Hitch. My top four are A Woman Under the Influence, The Dark Knight, Phantom Thread, and Eternal Sunshine. My Odyssey review has 112 likes. I don’t care how many followers I have on Instagram, how many likes I get. Letterboxd is truly a hit of adrenaline every time someone likes my review. —Frazier Tharpe

Age: 30Breakout Moment: Being the infectiously charismatic friend-to-all on FX’s zoomer sitcom Adults.—————

Sadie Sink knew who I was, and that was kind of cool. I just love her. She’s so talented and so adorable. It’s like meeting the queen before she’s the queen.

I do watch the fan edits. They’re fun. Some of them pick the worst photos of me, and I’m like, “This is a hate crime, but thank you for putting it on TikTok.”

I got two very smart pieces of advice. One was, “Run. Don’t do this. Save yourself.” And then the other person said, “Stick with it.” I’m kind of doing both. I am simultaneously sticking with it and trying my hardest, but also I’m like, Once you have a win, you do have to celebrate it and run as far away as you can and hide.

I bought a bag from The Row…which looks like—and I say this with love—it could be $20, but it’s the most expensive thing I own. And I did this because I went shopping with Hailey Bieber, and when you’re shopping with Hailey Bieber you buy things you think you can afford but you probably can’t…and now it’s too late to return. It’s a beautiful bag—it’s quiet luxury, babe. It’s so quiet you don’t know it’s The Row. —F.T

Age: 38Breakout moment: Her riveting performance as the title character in Chris Rock’s Misty Green (and her role in Slow Horses).—————

I mean, the first time I watched it, I just thought, Oh—you’re going to be in every frame. [Laughs]

[In an American accent] “Murderer.” Mur-der-rer. I think every Brit finds that hard. I was like, “Can I say ’killer’ instead?” And “mirror.” It’s when you’ve got a double r. But I had a great dialect coach.

I would’ve loved to have been in a scene with Philip Seymour Hoffman. That would’ve been my dream. And I would love to work with Sandra Hüller.

I was onstage with Ralph Fiennes. We finished Misty Green—I did an overnight shoot on Friday into Saturday morning, got on a plane, arrived in London on Sunday, and went straight into rehearsals with Ralph. It was a pretty mad summer.

I was getting ready before the Misty Green premiere [at the Toronto International Film Festival]. And my mom came down. She sat there and she was just sending pictures and she just had tears in her eyes. Just seeing this whole period at TIFF—for her, it’s her little girl. And every now and again, I would just look at her and she was just happy and in awe in a way. It warmed my heart. —A.P.

Age: 35Breakout moment: The Cannes hit Club Kid, which he wrote, directed, and starred in as a party promoter navigating unexpected parenthood.—————

I still do, yeah, at the phone call scene at the end. But it feels very distant from me. I feel like I’m watching it more as an observer now, which is nice, and I feel different than when I made it. I was such an open wound.

Cannes was such an emotional experience. I’m 35, and I’ve always known that I had something to say and I knew how to say it, but I’ve never been given the opportunity. So, honestly, to feel like I was being seen and understood was like 20 years of validation that I didn’t get. And so something shifted, that week, in me. I’ve noticed myself not having to use humor as a shield anymore, as much. I’m less afraid to be vulnerable—and not just in my public life but in my personal life too.

Of course, you shouldn’t need the validation of anyone to feel like you’re worthy, but let’s be real.…

What gagged me this year? I would say, honestly, as a statement of ambition, the Javis’ movie [La Bola Negra]. You marvel at it because you’re like, “How the fuck did they pull this off?” To even want to tell that complex of a story, and to just do it and go balls to the wall, is very inspiring.

I was like, “This is too long. It’s embarrassing now.” —A.P.

Age: 41Breakout moment: Reporting on how the Steve Ballmer–owned Clippers were allegedly funny with money, on his podcast Pablo Torre Finds Out, leading to one of the most severe team penalties in sports history.—————

Two days ago, I walked into this event that The New York Times was holding…it was like the 175th birthday party [for the paper]. I walk out the elevator and literally the first person I see is [NBA commissioner] Adam Silver. I won’t say exactly what he told me, but the tenor of our conversation was like, This is the HBO series adaptation of what this moment would be.

As a first-generation American—my parents are from the Philippines—there’s always been a dream of mine to be cast as a white person. So…Andrew Garfield, come on. Let’s do this.

The funny part is that I’m not even done reporting this story yet. The federal government is now investigating the Clippers, and so I’m now investigating the government’s investigation in the era of Trump too, which is like its own rabbit hole inside of a rabbit hole, which is fucking wild. But at the same time, it feels like now our number is listed in the phone book…actual other NBA owners are calling me up saying, “Can you look into this?”

It’s hard not to be sappy about this, but the lesson I learned is that if you’re telling the truth, you can walk into any room and feel like that as a suit of armor. Even when the person you’re investigating is one of the 10 richest people in the world. —F.T.

Age: 37Breakout moments: Stealing scenes as a ’70s Provincetown line cook in the Anthony Bourdain biopic Tony, and his stand-up special Uncle Stav.—————

I just did The Drew Barrymore Show. That experience was so magical. I was so nervous to be around Drew Barrymore. I’ve done a lot of cool shit, and that’s the one where I was like, “I am actually nervous to the point where I’m not a professional. I’m just a stammering little boy who’s with the girl he had a crush on from The Wedding Singer when he was a fat 10-year-old, and I’m trying to keep it together.”

Last week I allowed myself an aimlessly wander around New York City day. Got a little stoned, walked around, stumbled into a movie. Walked along the water on the West Side. Sat down to a little random omakase. Rode a Citi Bike around. I call it the hero’s journey, where you leave your home at, whatever, 11 a.m. ,with just the bare provisions, and you’re not allowed to go home. You just have to keep moving and keep doing stuff.

I filmed this Judd Apatow movie The Comeback King, with Glen Powell, Cristin Milioti, Madelyn Cline. That comes out in February. I’m excited to do stand-up—for fun, not to explicitly work on another hour. I’m excited to go back to clubs and just like, “Can I be the funniest person in this club tonight?” And that’s my only goal.

To be honest with you, the last three years, it’s [been] all go. But you got to have these big years, or else GQ doesn’t talk to you at the end of the year. —A.P.

Age: 31Breakout moment: Storming the West Coast rap scene with collabs from Pharrell Williams and Tyler, the Creator on his major-label debut album, No Rest for the Wicked.—————

Not like I made it, but I have arrived. We went to Magic City on a Monday for the first time. They were playing the fuck out my records in that motherfucker. The DJ played “Look Like My Mama” in the strip club. You ain’t never heard Tyler, the Creator get played in a strip club. We crossing worlds that’s never been crossed before.

The day before the album came out, we were in Area 29 in Houston, doing our thing. Regular night, it ain’t too packed, throwing money. They tap me on my shoulder like, “My boy want to holler at you.” They take me upstairs to this private section, pull the curtain back…it’s Travis Scott.

It was epic, bro. N-gga came out from behind the curtain like Batman. The light hid half his face. He said, “I fuck with everything you got going on.” We go back down to that section, 15 minutes pass, then one of the owners came and grabbed me: “Look, look, look.” Travis was up there, playing [Chike’s song] “Packed Up.” —F.T.

Age: 27Breakout moment: Being a dead ringer for Sly Stallone in I Play Rocky, the film about…Sly Stallone playing Rocky.—————

In the thick of prep. I was training at Gleason’s Gym with Stephan James, who plays Carl Weathers. We were beating the crap out of each other and figuring out how to box. I had gained a bunch of weight, and I was trying to actually cut a few pounds, so I was in those sweatsuits, like a garbage-bag type thing. On the way to the gym, I would listen to Stallone and on the way home.… Everything I could find up to 1980, 1985, every piece of Stallone footage that existed.

I [asked] Seth Rogen, “Dude, how do you handle this stuff?” And he said, “Just go with the flow.” It was such a simple answer, but from somebody like him, I was like, He’s got a good grip on this. The simplicity of it landed.

Philip Seymour Hoffman is one. Chadwick Boseman. Christian Bale, Heath Ledger.

[The first Rocky] is the best movie—but when I was younger, the one that caught my eye was four. Rocky IV is nuts. —F.T.

Ages: Ambrossi, 42 | Calvo, 35Breakout moment: Cowriting and directing La Bola Negra, the gay Spanish historical epic loosely based on an unfinished Federico García Lorca play.——————————————

Javier Ambrossi: Very important. We understood that sometimes people expect LGBTQ+ stories to be niche or for a specific audience. We wanted to do a queer story about universal love.

Ambrossi: It’s a way to put in context how different we are. We change a lot, and the story of our grandparents and our story is very different.

Javier Calvo: What do we do with the freedom our ancestors fought for, and died for?

Calvo: Isabelle Huppert. For me, she’s everything.

Ambrossi: I will go with Julia Roberts. My Best Friend’s Wedding is a very important queer story. The relationship between a woman and a gay man—it was the first time I saw that onscreen. And Rupert Everett was so bold, and I could tell in his eyes that he was gay. I was a kid and I was like, “He’s not faking it.”

How did you celebrate when you won best director at Cannes?

Ambrossi: I put the award on the table and went to sleep. And then Javi came over at 8 a.m.…

Ambrossi: “Give me the award!” And he took it! —A.P.
