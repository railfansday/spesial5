Asset Based Lending closes $200M securitization

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Asset Based Lending closed its largest deal, a $200M unrated securitization backed by business-purpose residential loans. ABL 2026 RTL-1 allows up to 65% ground-up infill construction collateral and brings cumulative securitization volume to $723M.  AI Summary

Asset Based Lending (ABL), a private real estate lender, announced on Tuesday that it closed a $200 million securitization, its largest to date, backed by business-purpose residential real estate loans, including a significant concentration of ground-up construction loans.

The transaction, ABL 2026 RTL-1, priced Oct. 15 and closed Oct. 26. It marks the company’s fourth unrated securitization and brings its cumulative securitization volume to $723 million.

Up to 65% of the collateral pool is eligible to consist of ground-up infill construction loans, a segment that has been central to ABL’s lending strategy since 2014.

The transaction came amid volatility in the securitization market and increased investor scrutiny of credit risk, said Daniel Gotay, ABL’s head of capital markets. Despite these conditions, the company secured what he described as some of the tightest pricing levels for an unrated issuer.

“What is most exciting about this one is that we’re not just doing our fourth, but that the market was a tricky market this time around,” Gotay said in an interview with HousingWire. “For us to execute at one of the sharpest levels for an unrated issuer, and to do that with the highest concentration of ground-up construction, really spoke to the presence that we’ve worked hard to build on Wall Street.”

Gotay said investors were particularly focused on how lenders would manage credit and construction risks over the next two years. ABL completed more than 20 virtual investor meetings ahead of the transaction, highlighting its underwriting and servicing practices.

The company services its loans in-house and uses a high-touch approach to managing projects, Gotay said. He added that investors also evaluated ABL’s broader portfolio, which has maintained a weighted average credit score of 740 and a weighted average loan-to-value ratio of 64%.

ABL has kept its weighted average LTV in the low 60% range for much of the past decade, Gotay said, contrasting that with some competing securitizations featuring LTV ratios in the upper-60% to low-70% range.

Investor appetite for the asset class remains strong despite heightened caution, Gotay said. International capital, including funds based in the United Kingdom and Japan, is increasingly looking to invest in U.S. asset-backed securities. “Even though they’re a little more on guard because of what’s happening, they still have to deploy their money for this type of asset,” he said.

Kevin Rodman, CEO of Asset Based Lending, said the transaction reflects the company’s experience in financing residential construction.

“We are proud to lead the industry in financing ground-up construction, and to do it at a cost that keeps our pricing competitive for the builders we serve,” Rodman said in a statement. “This transaction underscores the strength of the platform our team has built and the confidence investors have in our credit. Every one of these loans puts new housing on the ground.”

Gotay said ground-up construction has long been a core part of ABL’s business and will remain a significant share of its lending activity.

“The firm is really built to manufacture and manage infill ground-up construction since, really, 2014,” he said. “That’s been our specialty in the market and where we win the most deals.”

Performance Trust Capital Partners served as structuring agent, while Nomura Securities International and Mizuho Securities USA served as joint bookrunners.

The securitization also reflects ABL’s expanding geographic footprint, with collateral spanning the Northeast, Sun Belt, Rust Belt and South. The company said it plans to grow beyond its established Northeast presence and finance residential development in additional markets across the country.
