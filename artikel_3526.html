10 Best Black Friday Board Game Deals

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Two of the great classics get some great prices this year

One of the great joys of the holidays is finally getting everyone around the same table. Another is watching your cousin realize that the road they’ve been building for the last six turns is about to go nowhere. You don’t get many chances to do that as an adult. Make the most of it.

This year’s Black Friday board game deals make it easy to bring something to the table. Catan and Ticket to Ride are both under $25 at Amazon, and Azul is even cheaper. If you’re shopping for someone who already has those three, there’s a racing game about taking corners faster than you should, a two-player civilization game you can finish in half an hour, and a co-op mystery where the murder victim is still trying to help.

Prices and availability confirmed at time of writing, but could change throughout the day.

There’s a reason people still talk about their worst Catan trades years later. You build roads and settlements with resources that come in on dice rolls, but sooner or later you’ll need something only another player has. That’s when you find out exactly how long your family can hold a grudge.

Collect matching train cards, claim routes across North America, and quietly hope nobody notices how badly you need that last stretch of track. The rules are easy to teach, and a single well-placed train can give everyone something to argue about after dinner.

Take colorful tiles from a shared supply and fit them into a pattern on your own board. The fun is in deciding whether to grab the tiles you need now or leave someone else stuck with more than they can use. It’s an unusually pretty game for something that encourages that much pettiness.

The best moments in Heat come when you know exactly how fast you should take a corner and decide to go faster anyway. You manage your speed with cards, and pushing your car too hard can leave your deck clogged with Heat just when you need a clean run.

This two-player riff on the beloved civilization-building game sees you drafting cards, gathering resources, and constructing Wonders across three ages. Each card you take can uncover one your opponent was waiting for, so even picking up some desperately needed stone can feel like a mistake. You can win outright through military or scientific supremacy, which keeps the pressure on even when you’re ahead on points.

If your family already plays Hearts or Spades, here’s a way to get a Tolkien game to the table without teaching everybody a new rulebook from scratch. You work together to meet character-specific goals across a series of chapters, and the trick is figuring out when winning a hand would actually make things worse for the Fellowship.

Pick a card, pass the rest, and try to remember whether the person across the table is collecting dumplings. The bigger Party! box lets you change up the menu and seats up to eight, so it’s a good one to pull out when more people want in.

This is the one to hand the cousins while you clear the dinner table. You’re trying to avoid drawing an exploding kitten, and most of the fun comes from making the next person deal with a card you know they don’t want.

One player is a ghost who can only communicate through surreal illustrations. Everyone else is a medium trying to solve the ghost’s murder, which means someone will inevitably spend five minutes arguing that a picture of a fish is clearly a clue about a hat.

Everdell remains one of the most beautiful modern board games on the market, pairing gorgeous woodland artwork with a surprisingly deep worker-placement and tableau-building experience. Players gather resources, recruit charming animal citizens, and construct a thriving forest city over the course of four seasons. Despite its cozy appearance, Everdell offers meaningful strategic choices and enough depth to keep experienced board gamers engaged, making it an easy recommendation for anyone looking to graduate beyond gateway games like Catan and Ticket to Ride.
