November: A Dangerous Time of Year in the Mountains

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A glut of early-winter mountain accidents around the world suggests that this transition season may be the most dangerous time of year. It’s not too cold, and many places don’t have enough snow to impede hiking. It’s tempting to let our guard down.

Just north of Los Angeles this week, an experienced hiker and trail runner died when he slipped on an icy slope while trying to help his son.

Guillermo Batres, 44, was hiking on 2,865m Mount Baden-Powell in California’s San Gabriel Mountains with his 14-year-old son, Sebastian. Sebastian slipped on the icy trail but managed to grab a tree. Batres rushed to help him but slipped himself, falling 150m down a ravine.

Batres, in green, and his son seconds before the fatal slip. Photo: Screenshot

Two passing hikers gave the teen microspikes and helped him to safety. A sheriff’s helicopter lowered a paramedic into the ravine, but Batres was already dead.

Also in California, in Angeles National Forest, Nathan Sakaue, 14, died after falling 150m into an icy ravine. A second teenager also fell but survived with injuries. A third member of the party remained above and summoned help.

Search and Rescue services in the Angeles National Forest have responded to six incidents over the last seven days.

Angeles National Forest, California. Photo: Shutterstock

Elsewhere, a 31-year-old man from the Denver area reached the summit of Colorado’s Humboldt Peak (4,288m) on Sunday morning before suffering a fatal fall.

Winter conditions hampered the search, which lasted two days. Colorado Army National Guard personnel located his body yesterday afternoon.

In Banff National Park, Canada, two skaters fell through thin ice at Lake Louise and were able to clamber out, while a day later, a third skater fell through and died of hypothermia. Skating on “wild ice” is a popular sport in the Canadian Rockies at this time of year, but temperatures fluctuate day to day, and ice that is solid enough today may quickly thin within hours if temperatures rise above freezing.

Early-season skating on Lake Louise in Banff National Park can be magical, as long as the ice is thick enough. Photo: Jerry Kobalenko

In France, a 22-year-old solo hiker was found dead beneath snow following a 24-hour search by Chamonix mountain gendarmes.

Following recent heavy snowfall, avalanche danger in the Mont Blanc massif reached Level 4 out of 5 above 2,200m. Authorities had urged people to reconsider mountain outings, but whether an avalanche caused his death has not been established.

Also in France this week, three separate parties needed rescue when wintry weather set in. On Mont Valier in the Pyrenees, three trail runners poorly equipped for snow needed helicopter evacuation. Elsewhere in the Pyrenees, two inexperienced hikers became stranded in snow and needed a helicopter rescue. And in Isère in the French Alps, four young hikers struggled to descend in deteriorating weather and snow. Rescuers accompanied them to safety.

Italy’s Apennine Mountains. Photo: Shutterstock

A 56-year-old man slipped on snow at around 2,500m between Corno Grande and Corno Piccolo in the Apennine Mountains of central Italy. He suffered serious injuries. His wife administered first aid and called rescuers. As darkness descended, an Italian Air Force helicopter capable of night operations was brought in and eventually airlifted the man to a hospital.

Finally, in Japan’s Ōmine Mountains southeast of Osaka, a mountaineer in his 40s became lost and stranded in the snow and needed rescue.

Accidents happen in mountains throughout the year, but late November is particularly perilous. At times it can feel almost summery, and then without warning, winter hits.
