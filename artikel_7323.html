Why Brands Need to Rethink the Business of Creator Commerce

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

This post was created in partnership with Button

Creators are becoming sophisticated commercial partners, with capabilities and financial interests that extend well beyond content delivery. Their relationships with brands are evolving alongside their companies.

During an ADWEEK House Commerce Summit panel co-hosted with Button, industry leaders and creators examined the changing economics of influence, from the investments behind content production to the challenge of measuring sales.

Creator commerce has grown increasingly complex, with creators taking a more active role in how their content generates revenue.

“They are entrepreneurs. They are not influencers. They are buying ads for their own content so that they can make more money,” said Mike Jaconi, co-founder and CEO of Button.

Some creators manage their own advertising operations, with in-house teams overseeing media buying and performance. Others hire agencies for that expertise, adding another layer of business management to their work as content producers.

The creative work has grown more ambitious, too. Sarah Randall, a content creator who produces a lifestyle and home renovation series with her husband, predicts a shift toward episodic storytelling, with brands integrated into ongoing narratives rather than one-off sponsored posts.

Such productions require intense coordination. Randall described shoots involving professional cameras, lighting, childcare arrangements, and months of preparation. When agencies restrict direct access to brand decision-makers, delayed approvals can disrupt an entire production.

“We just want to talk to you, the brands. We want to create a good story together,” Randall stressed.

The commercial value of creator content isn’t always reflected in its popularity. Ali Thomas, co-founder of The Bargain Sisters, recalled joking about posts that attracted only a few thousand views yet generated substantial affiliate earnings.

“We’ll be able to put our kids through college based on what we earn off of that,” she shared.

Other posts could also go viral without producing meaningful sales. But as Randall noted, engagement remains valuable for brand awareness campaigns. Success depends on the partnership’s objectives.

Jaconi warned that faulty commerce infrastructure can further obscure results.

“I wish that they—the marketers, the retailers—would consider how much revenue they’re losing by having bad links, faulty attribution, and really inconsistent shopper experiences,” he pointed out.

For Cassie Thomas, co-founder of The Bargain Sisters, that risk influences which retailers receive the Sisters’ promotion. “We just won’t link unless we have deep linking. We just won’t,” she said.

Randall illustrated the problem: When an affiliate link opens a retailer’s app without preserving tracking information, the creator can lose commission while the retailer loses visibility into the referral.

Jason Fairchild, CEO of tvScientific by Pinterest and general manager of programmatic and affiliate at Pinterest, sees an opportunity for more comprehensive measurement. Pinterest connects consumer intent with visually compelling creator content. Fairchild advocates combining view- and click-based data to assess incremental impact.

“For me, it’s the full impact of a given ad, meaning not just the last click,” he explained.

Looking toward 2027, Jaconi expects creator content to power larger media investments, where “creators will create this organic content, and then that content will be converted into giant paid media campaigns.”
