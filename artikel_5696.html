Star of 'The Pitt' Teams With Scrubs Maker to Wipe Out Medical Debt

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

— Noah Wyle says the initiative is "half philanthropy and half protest"

Noah Wyle, who stars as Dr. Michael "Robby" Robinavitch on "The Pitt," is teaming up with scrubs maker FIGS to relieve $200 million of medical debt.

Wyle and FIGS are each donating $1 million over the next 3 years to the nonprofit Undue Medical Debt, which buys up medical debt for pennies on the dollar. Wyle and FIGS' contributions are expected to relieve the debt of about 150,000 Americans.

The first round of people will be alerted that their medical debt is wiped ahead of this holiday season, the partners announced.

"This is an act of half philanthropy, and half protest, to underscore what the need is, and see if we can't get our arms around some better solutions," Wylie told CNN.

Wyle said he stumbled upon the debt-erasing organization last year and was impressed by "how much you could do with a little bit of money and have it go a long way."

Undue Medical Debt buys portfolios of medical debt, with each dollar spent erasing about $100 in medical debt. Afterwards, patients receive a letter letting them know their debt is no longer owed and they don't have any lingering penalties.

Americans have a whopping $220 billion in medical debt, which is carried by approximately 1 in 3 people, the companies said. Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) has called medical debt crisis "a uniquely American phenomenon."

Wyle told CNN that while the average amount of medical debt carried is not a lot, "it's enough to keep you in poverty. It's enough to make you defer going back to the doctor to get the treatments you need, or to make really difficult choices between heat and healthcare, food and healthcare."

In previous interviews, Wyle revealed that the upcoming season 3 of "The Pitt," which takes place in the winter, will explore some of these hard choices that patients have to make between healthcare and other basic needs.

Last season, the show tackled the looming, life-altering influence that medical debt can have on patients' lives. One patient checked out of the emergency department against medical advice. He was uninsured and already had $100,000 in medical debt that he had been hiding from his family

Even when his doctors made it clear that he was putting his life in danger by leaving, and went above and beyond to secure the patient a discount from the hospital, he couldn't bear to bring on any more medical debt, and left. Spoiler alert: that same patient returned to the emergency department later that day in even worse condition.

This storyline highlighting the real impacts of medical debt and how it shapes patients' decisions was heart-wrenching for viewers, but also for the fictional emergency department physicians on the show who couldn't do anything to get their patient the care he needed.

"Trust is the bedrock of the relationship between patients and clinicians like nurses, pharmacists, and doctors, and that trust is increasingly undermined by the burden, and even fear, of medical debt," Undue Medical Debt's CEO and president Allison Sesso said in a press release.

"On the other side of January, with the 'Big Beautiful Bill,' a lot more people are going to find themselves without health insurance, which means going out of pocket for these kinds of things and incurring more debt," Wyle said on CNN. "It's kind of an unsustainable system, and it's one that really needs to be addressed."
