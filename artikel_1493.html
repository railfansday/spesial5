Tandem repeat variation shapes immune cell type-specific gene expression

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Tandem repeats (TRs)—highly polymorphic, repetitive sequences across the human genome—are important regulators of gene expression but remain underexplored due to challenges in accurate genotyping and analysis1. Here we generate whole-genome and single-cell RNA sequencing from more than 5.4 million blood-derived cells across 1,925 individuals in two cohorts2, and perform meta-analysis to characterize the impact of variation in more than 1.7 million TR loci on immune cell type-specific gene expression. We identify more than 69,000 single-cell expression quantitative trait loci, 30.7% of which are specific to 1 of 28 immune cell types, and reveal dynamic regulatory effects using cell-state inference. Matched single-cell assay for transposase-accessible chromatin with high-throughput sequencing profiles from more than 3.4 million nuclei in 922 individuals3 uncover chromatin accessibility quantitative trait loci for nearly one-third of expression-associated TRs, supporting coordinated effects on cis-regulatory architecture. Fine-mapping implicates 1,490 TRs as candidate causal drivers of gene expression in 6.1% of tested genes, and colocalization analyses highlight more than 200 genes in which TRs likely mediate genetic associations with immune and haematological traits. Together, these results provide a genome-wide, multiomic view of TR-mediated regulation in the human immune system, establishing TRs as key contributors to cell type-specific regulatory variation and complex trait architecture.
