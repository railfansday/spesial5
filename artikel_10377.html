Networking is awkward: Marriott and Second City want to improve your conference experience with improv

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Corporate networking hours can be painfully awkward. Picture it: dozens of professionals clutching lukewarm drinks, trading similar elevator pitches, and desperately scanning badges to find someone worth talking to.

“You have a name tag, a drink in your hand, and no guidance,” Tyler Dean Kempf, creative director of Second City Works, told Fast Company. “It’s up to you to engage people in conversations that are usually focused entirely on work.”

Marriott International thinks it has a fix—and it involves pulling a move straight out of the comedy playbook.

On Tuesday, the hotel giant announced Unscripted Networking: The Second City Takeover, a new program developed exclusively with Chicago’s 65-year-old improv comedy institution, the Second City.

The program is rolling out across more than 125 properties in Marriott Bonvoy’s Convention & Resort Network throughout North America and the Caribbean. It offers a 45- to 60-minute experience that swaps traditional small talk for structured, improv-inspired group play.

A different kind of workplace networking event

Rather than putting individuals on the spot for solo stand-up bits, the program pairs two professional Second City hosts with a live music director who formats the session like an interactive variety show.

The events are designed for groups of 50 to 750 attendees; the hosts lead rapid-fire transitions through low-stakes exercises by asking participants to debate topics like coffee versus tea, or window versus aisle seats, rather than their resume highlights.

In one drill, Kempf noted, attendees act as “corporate wingmen,” learning about a partner before introducing them to another pair in the room to expand their circle.

Kempf emphasized that while pitching improv to corporate professionals might send introverts running for the exit, the structure explicitly removes the fear of performance.

“When we say the word improv, people get scared because our minds go to being vulnerable, having no script, or performing on stage,” Kempf said. “There’s no performance in this. Our hosts demonstrate vulnerability first from the stage, which signals that it’s safe for people to drop their guard down on the floor.”

Let’s get together

Amanda Cox, vice president of Sales, Convention & Resort Hotels, East Region for Marriott International, said as hybrid work environments reduce daily office interactions, live events are bearing an unprecedented burden.

“In recent years, there’s less gathering around the proverbial water cooler,” Cox said. “Live meetings and events are being looked upon to be torchbearers of an organization’s culture—and that’s a lot of pressure on event planners.”

That pressure is compounded by the challenge of bringing together a multi-generational workforce with different communication norms.

Cox noted that in customer insight sessions with event organizers, Marriott found planners struggling to get younger hires and veteran executives on the same page. 

“It is amazing the barriers that break down when you’re immediately in a place of creative collaboration,” Cox said.

Yes, and…

Marriott ran nine internal engagements with Second City over the past year to test the concept before scaling it across its resort portfolio.

Cox said that incorporating the program at the opening reception of a typical three-day conference can result in deeper engagement throughout the rest of the event.

“When you start with that genuine connection, you see the return on investment grow throughout the entire meeting,” Cox said. “You’re getting deeper much quicker because you’ve stepped away from small talk into substantial connection.”

By bringing comedy-backed behavioral science into the ballroom, Marriott and Second City are making a clear bet: The fastest way to get corporate professionals to drop their guard isn’t another open bar—it’s getting them to play along.
