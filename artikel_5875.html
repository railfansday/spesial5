From Hollywood mogul to Biden-Harris insiders, Democrats turn to familiar faces to shape their next chapter

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

National Review columnist Caroline Downey and Democratic strategist Al Mottur join 'The Faulkner Focus' to discuss Abdul El-Sayed's Senate victory in Michigan. The panel discusses Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez's rising influence and the impact on the 2028 Democratic primary.

On the heels of the blue wave in the 2026 U.S. midterms, incoming Democratic leaders are tapping an eclectic mix of political veterans, business executives and entertainment figures to help shape their transitions into power.

From a Hollywood mogul and former Biden administration officials to figures with ties to Republican leaders, the appointments offer an early glimpse into the people Democrats are turning to as they prepare to govern — and the experience and connections they hope to bring into their new administrations.

Several incoming Democrats are turning to familiar faces from the Biden administration. In New Mexico, Gov.-elect Deb Haaland tapped former Biden presidential speechwriter Felicia Salazar and former Interior Department official Joan Mooney for her transition team, while California Gov.-elect Xavier Becerra selected Arsenio Mataka, who previously served as his senior counselor at the Department of Health and Human Services under Biden, to direct policy for his transition team. In Ohio, Democratic Gov.-elect Amy Acton tapped Elisabeth Hire, a former aide to then-Vice President Biden, as a transition co-chair.

Haaland's campaign and Becerra's campaign each did not respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.

The transition teams will help incoming leaders prepare to take office, including identifying potential administration officials, shaping policy priorities and laying the groundwork for their first days in power. The selections also offer a window into the experience, relationships and perspectives newly elected Democrats are turning to as they prepare to govern.

Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed, D-Mich., tapped Egyptian-American lawyer Brenda Abdelall as the director of his transition team, Politico Playbook first reported in early November. Abdelall previously claimed that Zionists were "controlling" much of U.S. politics, The Washington Free Beacon reported Nov. 6, though Harris' 2024 presidential campaign said at the time that the remarks did not reflect Abdelall's current views.

"The Zionists have a strong voice in American politics," Abdelall told the New York Sun during a conference for the American Muslim Council in 2002, the Free Beacon reported. "I would say they're controlling a lot of it."

Abdelall previously served as the assistant secretary of partnership and engagement at the U.S. Department of Homeland Security. She also directed the Kamala Harris 2024 presidential campaign's outreach to Arab American voters, Reuters reported.

El-Sayed campaign spokesperson Roxie Richner told Fox News Digital on Nov. 9 that Abdelall "comes to the role of transition director with decades of experience leading teams at the highest levels of politics, including the Biden-Harris administration."

"While at the Department of Homeland Security, Brenda worked to implement the nation’s first National Strategy to Counter Antisemitism and frequently spearheaded efforts to counter the rise of hate crimes and violence across the country," Richner added. "The transition team is committed to building a team that reflects the great diversity of Michigan and ensuring that the El-Sayed office serves all communities across the state."

El-Sayed is set to assume office on Jan. 3, 2027.

Democratic Georgia Gov.-elect Keisha Lance Bottoms announced Nov. 6 that filmmaker and actor Tyler Perry would serve as one of 10 co-chairs of her transition team, dubbed "Georgia's Promise," WBAL-TV reported. Perry has long been known for his philanthropic efforts, including supporting underserved communities and charitable causes across Georgia. Bottoms lauded Perry as "an entertainment mogul who is also a huge philanthropic part of our Georgia community and owns Tyler Perry Studios in Atlanta," WDSU reported.

The news that Perry will serve on Bottoms’ Georgia transition team has sparked some criticism due to the entertainment mogul facing two active lawsuits accusing him of sexual assault and misconduct, allegations his attorneys have denied, Variety reported Nov. 6.

Perry has denied all sexual assault and harassment allegations made against him, People reported on March 3.

Maxscene, a division of Tyler Perry Studios, did not respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.

Bottoms previously was an executive producer on Netflix’s "Miss Governor," a 2025 political comedy sitcom produced by Tyler Perry Studios, per online film database IMDb.

Other prominent figures that have been tapped for Bottoms' transition committee include Home Depot co-founder Arthur Blank and former CEO of Walgreens Boots Alliance Roz Brewer, WBAL-TV reported.

Bottoms’ campaign did not respond to Fox News Digital’s request for comment.

Bottoms is set to take office on Jan. 11, 2027.

Democratic Attorney General-elect Nathan Johnson's transition team includes Mark Penley, a former deputy attorney general who reported outgoing Republican Attorney General Ken Paxton to the FBI, and Erin Epley, who served as lead attorney in Paxton's impeachment, The Houston Chronicle reported Nov. 16.

Johnson also tapped Amanda Crawford, a former appointee of Republican Gov. Greg Abbott, and Thaddeus Cleveland, a Republican sheriff from Terrell County, as part of his transition team, according to the Chronicle.

Johnson's campaign did not respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.

Democratic Gov.-elect David Crowley is also turning to the world of professional sports, tapping Alex Lasry, a former senior vice president of the NBA's Milwaukee Bucks, to serve on his transition team, Wisconsin Public Radio reported Nov. 11.

Lasry, the son of former Bucks co-owner and billionaire businessman Marc Lasry, helped oversee the franchise's operations during its rise to the 2021 NBA championship and played a key role in developing Milwaukee's Fiserv Forum arena. He also mounted an unsuccessful bid for the Democratic U.S. Senate nomination in 2022.

Crowley's campaign did not respond to Fox News Digital’s request for comment.
