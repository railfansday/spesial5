Do Ferrari need to make Hamilton their No 1 driver?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Listen to the latest edition of The F1 Show podcast as Simon Lazenby, David Croft and Jamie Chadwick debate what's next at Ferrari after another driver clash; watch the Qatar GP on Sky Sports F1 this weekend, with qualifying at 6pm on Saturday and the race at 4pm on Sunday

Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton's latest run-in at the Las Vegas Grand Prix has ramped up scrutiny on Ferrari and what the team will do to try to avoid more costly on-track incidents between their two drivers.

Just two races after a fierce battle when running first and second on the opening lap in Mexico City cost the team victory to Red Bull's Max Verstappen and led to clear-the-air discussions, the team-mates collided when battling over sixth place in Las Vegas in an incident Leclerc was found at fault for by the stewards and then received the post-race ire of team boss Frederic Vasseur.

Vasseur has said such incidents "cannot continue" but how does the Frenchman get on top of the situation heading towards 2027, when the team will be hoping to launch a fresh bid to end what stands as an 18-year world title drought?

The Ferrari fallout proved a central topic on the latest edition of Sky Sports' The F1 Show podcast ahead of this weekend's Qatar Grand Prix, in which host Simon Lazenby was joined by David Croft and Jamie Chadwick.

Asked how big the problems between Hamilton and Leclerc were becoming for Ferrari, Chadwick said: "Significant when you think about what they're going to potentially be going into next year with.

"I think this is the time of the year where you don't want to be having these kinds of conflicts because suddenly it will escalate and it will mean that more serious conversation is going to be had in the background."

Former world champion Damon Hill recently said he believed Ferrari should make Hamilton, the seven-time world champion and F1's most successful driver, their clear No 1 next year if they want to win the world championship.

Speaking on Sky Sports F1 after Sunday's incident in Las Vegas, Jacques Villeneuve, the 1997 title winner, suggested Leclerc - who is in his eighth season with the Scuderia - had "lost" the leadership of the team to Hamilton, who is in his second, and "if there's one of the two drivers knows how to win championships, it's Lewis".

Villeneuve did, however, question whether Ferrari would be able to prioritise Hamilton contractually.

Hamilton said after the race that he has "never asked" for No 1 status at the team, although implied they could have given him more backing when he was second to Kimi Antonelli and around 40 points ahead of Leclerc in the championship earlier this season.

And although Hamilton and Leclerc are both among the sport's biggest names and most-talented drivers, Croft believes events of this season have effectively forced the team's hand and they need to prioritise one over the other in order to achieve better results.

"Whether Lewis Hamilton is asking to be No 1 or not, I don't think matters any more," said Croft.

"I think the way things have panned out this season the matter has been forced.

"Lewis has to be the No 1 because Charles keeps putting himself in a position whereby Ferrari suffer when the two drivers come together.

"He got the penalty on Sunday. Whether you think it's a penalty or not, the move was ambitious and it was ambitious if it was a move on Lando Norris, it was ambitious and a bit foolish if it's a move on your own team-mate.

"So Charles is kind of forcing, albeit not deliberately, Ferrari to make these decisions. He did race Lewis hard in Mexico City, and we saw what happened there, and he raced Lewis hard in Monza, and we saw what happened there."

Long regarded as one of the fastest drivers in the sport, Leclerc, 29, is a graduate of Ferrari's academy and joined their race team in 2019 after a debut season of F1 at Sauber.

He outperformed four-time champion Sebastian Vettel and then Carlos Sainz across their respective seasons as team-mates and was comfortably ahead of Hamilton by over 80 points after the Briton joined from Mercedes last year.

But Leclerc has faced a resurgent force in Hamilton this year, with the 41-year-old holding a 17-point advantage over him in the championship with two races to go.

"Charles is a fine driver, don't get me wrong, but his pole-to-win conversion rate (27 to nine) is not enough," added Croft on Leclerc's Ferrari record so far.

"You can also argue that the team haven't given him the car to make that conversion rate better, but he does make mistakes, we've seen that.

"We've seen in the past when he's been leading a race and he's crashed out all by himself. He's not the finished article and I don't want to be too harsh on Charles because I think he's a truly wonderful human being, but he's no Lewis Hamilton when it comes to his record and his performances and his consistency over the years."

Vasseur said he would hold fresh discussions with his drivers this week.

But do those talks need to now go further in terms of setting out firmly what is and is not acceptable - and establishing consequences for any transgressions?

Chadwick said: "I think the biggest issue [to this point] is the conversations haven't been serious enough.

"I think a clear-the-air talk as in 'okay it's not your fault, it's not your fault' and 'okay this is how things are' versus, actually, what are the rules of engagement? What are the reprimands if you do take your team-mate out and are they severe enough that actually neither driver is going to dare put themselves in that position?

"Clearly not because when you look at the move Charles did. Okay I kind of agree a little bit it wasn't not on, but it's definitely not on on your team-mate. I think you can maybe risk a move like Lando did on Lewis and I think that's also actually where Charles was a bit unlucky [in that he] went for it and then he kind of sees the door closing a bit and then he backs out and then it's kind of worse because he's over the kerb and that's what caused him to hit him.

"If he had actually just really committed to it maybe it would have been a bit better but, either way, you just never do that on your team-mate and clearly he didn't see there being any risk as to making a move like that on his team-mate. In his mind in that moment, Lewis is the same as any other driver on that grid. it's not a car in the same team as you.

"So that definitely needs a lot of discussion behind the scenes."

Chadwick, though, believes it is possible to win world titles with two No 1 drivers.

"I think it can still work with two drivers of top calibre but there has to be clear rules of engagement," she added.

"That's what we were arguably critical of, but it worked for McLaren last year."

But given the repeat nature of the on-track clashes and flashpoints between Hamilton and Leclerc this season, Croft is unsure much will actually change despite the incident and another round of discussions.

"They either say 'Lewis is No 1 going forward' and 'sorry, Charles, you're not going to get involved with this and you're going to drive for your team-mate because we need a championship here'. Or they get a new team principal who can actually maybe have a better sway over the two drivers, because at the moment it looks like whatever Fred says it just gets ignored out on track. Or they can just carry on as they are doing.

"And if I was going to put whatever dollars you had left over from Vegas on this one, I'd probably say Ferrari are just going to carry on and do what they did this year next year as well because they've had every chance so far this year to actually address the situation and they've not addressed it right so far."
