Stella 'liked' Piastri's fiery response to McLaren 'be careful' warnings

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

McLaren team principal Andrea Stella says he liked Oscar Piastri's fiery response to radio messages warning the Australian to "be careful" battling his team-mate Lando Norris during the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

Piastri started from 17th on the grid after taking an engine penalty but drove impressively - during an extraordinary race that featured 352 overtakes - to join the battle at the front in latter stages, closing in on a leading train of cars that featured Norris.

As Piastri chased George Russell for fourth with Norris just in front of the Mercedes, a radio exchange was played out on the television broadcast which featured him twice being told by his race engineer to "be careful" around his team-mate, to which he responded: "Stop telling me to ****ing be careful."

The McLaren pair ultimately benefitted from late errors by Russell and his Mercedes team-mate Antonelli to claim a double podium, with Norris second and Piastri third behind Red Bull's Max Verstappen.

Asked about Piastri's radio exchange after the race, Stella said: "I actually liked it. I like it. That's the character that you expect from a racer.

"It's not like the Michael Schumacher, the Fernando Alonso, the Kimi Raikkonen - that I worked with before - were any easier, and (they) were champions.

"That's the character you want to see. And that's also what you have to do from the pit wall. You need to make sure that the driver has a clear objective in his head.

"And you want to do it up until the point the driver makes you feel, 'I've got it. Don't go beyond.' That's exactly where you want to be from outside the cockpit.

"So, actually, well done to the pit wall and well done to Oscar in relation to this situation. That's exactly where we want to position the interaction between the team and the driver, and what we have learnt through some of the experiences we've gone through."

With Norris clearly outperforming Piastri this season, there have been relatively few challenges for McLaren in managing their drivers, which is in significant contrast to last season when Norris came out on top in a battle for the drivers' title that went down to the final race of the season.

The only major flashpoint of this season came when an aggressive attempt to pass Piastri from Norris on the final lap of the Sprint in Singapore resulted in contact that cost both drivers points, leading to criticism from Stella and an apology from the Brit.

Stella provided more detail on his approach to managing his drivers as he attempted to further explain why he liked Piastri's fierce radio response in Las Vegas.

"I'll have to up a little bit of IP (intellectual property) here, in terms of how you manage drivers, but let me be clear, because we avoid this matter to create any noise," Stella said to gathered reporters.

"If we go and we do a leisure go-karting together, our personalities will change slightly from the personality we have right now when we are enjoying sitting here. We will dig out the racing spirit, we will dig out something that is the reason why these guys do what they do, and all the sacrifices that they have gone through to be where they are.

"And that's where you have to come and help them, come and help them modulate, adapt. We want to use it. That's why I said I really liked Oscar's answer. That's the racer I want to see. But at the same time, it's a team effort in modulating it. We want to use just the right quantity of it that doesn't take us too far.

"And if you see Lando in Singapore, Lando certainly, you don't have to remind him of the team interest and that he's a team player. He's a phenomenal team player, yet his vision, his way of racing got a little too channelled, and in the final lap of the Sprint, he attacked Oscar in a way that led to a collision.

"And he was incredibly apologetic. And, if anything, he said like, 'guys, just don't assume that we do all the processing, because we are so busy just racing, keeping the car on track, and this year's car absorbs our bandwidth. So, just help us. Take it to the point where we can tell you, 'ok, that's enough. I've got it.''

"And that's exactly the point where we want to take it. The same message was given to both drivers, because that's a common learning. This is nothing to do with Oscar, this is just to do with dealing with drivers."

McLaren also benefitted in Las Vegas from Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc colliding when they were also in contention for the leading positions.

Leclerc was given a 10-second penalty for causing the collision which spun Hamilton, and ultimately led to Ferrari failing to maximise the solid race pace they produced.

The incident was the latest in a series of clashes between the Ferrari drivers, with the previous one having cost the team a likely one-two in the Mexico City Grand Prix when Leclerc attempted to pass Hamilton for the lead on the opening lap.

Asked whether he had sympathy for his Ferrari counterpart Frederic Vasseur, Stella said: "When it comes to commenting on other teams, that's something I would not want to do. I can only say that it's a very difficult subject.

"Even last year, in which I would say we've been successful in managing two drivers in that sort of situation, fighting for their dream, which is the drivers' world championship, it's just been an incredible amount of work.

"Work that sometimes, I say, we paid in the fact that 2026 was affected by the effort that we needed to put into 2025.

"So, it's something that every team principal needs to deal with and it's probably one of the most difficult aspects to managing Formula 1, because it's the only one in which you can't simply say, 'let's go as a team.'

"Because the drivers, they compete for themselves. So, definitely sympathy, but for team principals in general, not only for Fred."
