Best Personal Finance Apps – One App Worth Keeping per Job

Use one personal finance app for each money job, such as budgeting, spending, investing, and cutting bills, and choose each by its yearly cost and how it makes money. A free app still earns money from someone, so learn who pays for it before you link your accounts.

Even a free app that millions used can go away. Intuit shut down Mint, its budgeting app, and invited its users to Credit Karma. So for each pick, this list says whether it makes money from your subscription, from businesses that pay it when you buy or trade, or from an upgrade it sells you.

Each one is the app worth keeping for its job, judged by what it costs a year and what it does that your bank’s own app doesn’t.

Quicken Simplifi builds one spending plan from every account you link, not just one bank’s, and projects your cash flow. Quicken sells it only as an annual subscription.

For more information, read our full Quicken Simplifi Review.

Chime® charges no monthly or overdraft fees, since most of its money comes from a cut of the fee stores pay when you use its card. Round Ups save the change from each purchase.

For more information, read our full Chime Review.

Empower’s dashboard tracks your net worth and retirement plan for free, and Empower uses it to offer paid investment advice. You can keep the tracking and turn the advice down.

For more information, read our full Empower Personal Wealth Review.

Robinhood lets you buy stocks and exchange-traded funds in small amounts, with no commission or monthly fee. Trading firms pay it to send them your orders, and it earns income on your idle cash.

For more information, read our full Robinhood Review.

Rocket Money’s free plan lists the subscriptions and recurring bills in your linked accounts. It sells paid plans in which it cancels subscriptions or negotiates bills for you.

For more information, read our full Rocket Money Review.

Brands pay Ibotta a commission only when one of its offers leads to a sale, and they fund the cash back you collect, so the app is free to use.

For more information, read our full Ibotta Review.

Capital One Shopping is free because stores pay it a referral commission when you buy through its links. It’s the shopping tool to keep, since it checks other sellers’ prices as well as coupon codes.

For more information, read our full Capital One Shopping Review.

With Greenlight, kids earn money by finishing chores you set, then spend it with their own debit card, under limits you control. You pay for it with a monthly plan.

For more information, read our full Greenlight Review.

This list gives each money job one app, along with what it costs and who pays for it. Install the one for the job costing you the most, and add another app only once you’re opening that one every week.
