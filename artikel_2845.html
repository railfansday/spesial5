Current silence Rowdy Audi, Spirit fall short in final again: ‘It hurts way worse than the others’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

WASHINGTON, D.C. — How much more can the Washington Spirit's hearts break?

Three championship appearances in the last three years. Three times those games ended with the Spirit left on the shadowy side of a shiny trophy. Every head of the steadfast Spirit supporters was downcast as they bobbed down the staircases exiting Audi Field after their 2-1 defeat to the Kansas City Current. Empty-handed once again.

After the Orlando Pride in 2024 and Gotham FC in 2025, the Spirit were meant to triumph on their third attempt at a championship. It was supposed to happen at Rowdy Audi, before many of the 19,365 people who flocked to the nation's capital to be reminded that fairytale endings still existed in football.

This journey was not meant to end with Trinity Rodman crumpled on the pitch with her head in her hands. The league created the now-defunct High Impact Player rule to keep Rodman, an MVP finalist this year, in the U.S. Instead, she suffered another painful loss.

"Obviously, this one hurts way worse than any of the other ones," Rodman said in the mixed zone after the game. "I don't even think you have words in this moment. It's too fresh."

The match was still fresh when Kansas City landed what may be remembered as the knockout punch. In the fifth minute, 24-year-old Current right-back Laney Rouse challenged Rodman near the touchline. Rouse, formerly of the University of Virginia, lunged with the whole side of her body and caught Rodman in the shoulder.

"As a team, you always want to put the first tackle out," Rodman said. "You're taught to make the first hit. Credit to that, because I wish I was the one that put (Rouse) on the ground, but at the end of the day, I think they did a really good job of setting the tone and controlling a lot of the first half for sure."

Referee Joshua Encarnación did not issue any further caution apart from Rouse's foul. Just like that, a precedent was set, and in the process Rouse accomplished something very few players can do at Rowdy Audi: she silenced it.

The crowd had been booming since the warm-up. Current center-back Kayla Sharples said the pause in the game during Rodman's treatment offered them a moment to regroup.

"It did kind of get quiet after the initial fall, so we were actually able to communicate a little bit better, locking eyes with each other, trying to stay engaged and focused," Sharples said. "Those were key moments."

Asked how her shoulder was feeling after the game, Rodman offered a dry smile. "Phenomonal."

When the crowd resumed, they jeered every teal-colored touch of the ball and unsavory challenge. They made it known every time a Spirit player was called for a foul they felt was unjust. By the 35th minute, 14 fouls had been called, split fairly evenly between the two.

The Current have been saying throughout the postseason, and increasingly as a source of pride, that things have been personal for them this year. They lost just three games in their shield-winning 2025 campaign, and this year they equalled that number of losses in their first four matches. Doubts swirled around the qualifications of head coach Chris Armas and his lack of experience coaching women's professional soccer prior to his appointment in January. The Current parted ways with former U.S. women's national team head coach Vlatko Andonovski in September, a move that invited strong reactions from several active and former Current players.

"My job as the head coach is just to try to assess the situation and, through tough times, help us be steady," Armas said after the game. "The question would be: will we have enough time?"

After a scoreless first half, the fear of another stale 1-0 championship scoreline, the fate of the last two finals, began creeping into view. Kansas City midfielder Vanessa DiBernardo, four months into her return to the pitch from maternity leave, stepped up to take one of the team's coveted set pieces, a corner kick that had resulted from Malawi forward Temwa Chawinga's pacey efforts. DiBernardo's delivery curled beautifully into the far-side netting, behind the leaping attempts of Spirit goalkeeper Sandy MacIver and of Sharples, who jumped directly in front of MacIver and made enough contact to elicit another round of protests from the DC crowd.

This time, VAR intervened to verify that Sharples had not fouled MacIver and upheld Encarnación's initial decision. By then, the Current had already swamped DiBernardo to celebrate her second goal of the season. Her first, on October 25, had also come against the Spirit.

DiBernardo was activated in July. Returning to the pitch after having her daughter Charlie was a way for her to feel like herself again after experiencing the "wild ride" of motherhood.

"I knew it was gonna be difficult to kind of get back to who I was as a player, and I knew I was gonna be different as a person," DiBernardo said, "But I feel like our team gave me so much grace and time to be able to do that. It was really special to go through my pregnancy, but also my return to play with this group of people and kind of got to share the moment altogether."

Armas said his staff and others around the club who were familiar with DiBernardo's game predicted she would "feel like a new signing" when she returned.

Kansas City can empathize with the searing pain of missed opportunities felt by the Spirit. After exorcising their quarter-final memories and then beating the team that caused them in this campaign, why not cap that off with a trophy?

But the Spirit were not to be easily bowed. A late Rodman goal was just enough to revive their hopes, but only one minute remained of the nine minutes of stoppage time. Ultimately, the Spirit took too long to recognize their ability to influence the match — or that they might need to engage with the grotesque in order to win.

Matters of the heart are personal. This season, the Current channeled their past sorrows into competitive resolve, and they will return to Kansas City with another title to adorn the City of Champions.
