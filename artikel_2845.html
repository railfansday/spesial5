WTA apologizes after Jessica Pegula caught on hot mic calling Mirra Andreeva a ‘diva’ on livestream

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The WTA apologized Thursday for failing to clearly tell players and coaches that livestream microphones were recording practice sessions at Indian Wells.

The apology came after a hot mic caught Jessica Pegula talking about Mirra Andreeva and calling her a "diva" during a conversation with one of her coaches, Mark Knowles, which they both believed was private.

However, according to The Athletic, the session was being livestreamed on the WTA Tour's YouTube channel.

During the conversation, Pegula said she had "lost a little respect" for 1994 Wimbledon champion Conchita Martínez, Andreeva's coach, because she pokes (Andreeva) and eggs on her behavior.

"I think she's a nice girl; she's just a diva. She needs to be humbled," Pegula said of the 19-year-old French Open champion.

Pegula also compared Andreeva to Coco Gauff, saying Andreeva could surpass Gauff by adopting her "mentality."

The comments came following a practice session in which Pegula played against Andreeva. The Russian took four straight games from Pegula during the session, per The Athletic, but she also grew annoyed and started hitting balls toward her coach, Martínez. That behavior is apparently what irked Pegula.

In a statement, the WTA apologized for "failing to meet our responsibility to ensure players and coaches were properly informed."

A representative for Pegula told The Athletic that the tennis star "had absolutely no idea that her conversation with Mark was being livestreamed and certainly never expected a private discussion during practice to be made public."

The WTA has since removed all practice streams from this year's event from the platform. However, future practice sessions remain scheduled to be livestreamed on the WTA YouTube page. A Thursday practice session between Taylor Townsend and Katerina Siniakova was livestreamed without audio.

This is the latest in a series of incidents involving professional tennis players and audio or video access to their actions that they would rather keep private.
