Peloton Generated $454 Million in Free Cash Flow Over the Past Year. Here's the 1 Thing the Stock Still Needs.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Cleaning up the finances only goes so far before shareholders demand more.

By Neil Patel – Nov 23, 2026 at 12:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Peloton Interactive ›

Investors loved Peloton Interactive's (PTON +6.02%) jaw-dropping growth before and during the depths of the COVID-19 pandemic. The business over-invested during these good times, which resulted in troubled financial performance when demand started to tank as mobility restrictions eased and gyms opened back up.

Peloton is now in a stronger financial position. It reported $454 million in free cash flow (FCF) during the trailing 12-month period. This is clearly a very encouraging sign for a company that has also posted positive net income based on generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) in three straight quarters. The fitness innovator has gotten in better fiscal shape.

The consumer discretionary stock has climbed 25% in the past eight months as market sentiment starts to reflect the lower financial risk that's present. But the company needs a durable catalyst for investors to be rewarded over the long term.

It's worth highlighting Peloton's improved financial situation under Chief Executive Officer Peter Stern, who took the top job at the start of 2025. As of Sept. 30, the company had a net cash (meaning total cash and cash equivalents exceeding total debt) position of $43 million. After quarter end, the business also made a voluntary $280 million term loan paydown using cash on hand.

"Our strong balance sheet, significant cash generation, and confidence in our innovation pipeline [allow] us to reduce gross leverage while continuing to invest in the business and drive shareholder value," Chief Financial Officer Siddharth Thacker said on the first-quarter 2027 earnings call.

The trend is notable. At the end of fiscal 2022, Peloton registered negative FCF of $2.4 billion. And at that time, it had $1.6 billion between a term loan and convertible senior notes. Stern and his team have focused relentlessly on cost cuts.

Risk-averse investors might have avoided the business in the past because it wasn't operating on stable ground. This is less of a concern today. During the latest quarter, interest expense accounted for 26% of operating income compared to a 78% share in Q1 2026.

Shares have risen 25% in the last eight months. They are still extremely cheap. Investors can buy the stock by paying slightly more than five times trailing FCF. Don't rush to own the interactive fitness enterprise just yet.

Operational efficiencies can only go so far. Eventually, the market wants to see growth, which is the missing catalyst that can sustainably move the stock higher over the long term.

Growth has been a major point of uncertainty for Peloton in recent years. Management expects revenue to decline 4% in fiscal 2027. If this forecast ends up becoming reality, it would be the sixth straight year that the top line shrunk.

It's encouraging to see that Peloton keeps innovating, though. Peloton IQ leverages artificial intelligence (AI) to personalize workout plans. In addition to driving sales to commercial partners, the company is also pushing further into running, recently introducing a revamped treadmill portfolio, with three models reflecting a good-better-best pricing strategy. In the fall of 2027, Peloton plans to "launch hardware in a new category," according to Stern.
