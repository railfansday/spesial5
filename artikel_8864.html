14-Minute Daily Yoga to Help You Build a Consistent Practice

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Practicing stretches for energy can be a simple way to feel more awake and alert throughout the day. This practice was created by yoga instructor Laia Bové, who has taught students of all levels for more than a decade.

Maybe you’ve heard that yoga can help improve your energy level, or you’ve already felt that fresh-out-of-yoga-class and ready-to-take-on-the-world feeling while walking out of a gym or studio. If only you could bottle that feeling and use it anytime you’re slumped at your desk, feeling exhausted from the week and more than a little bleh. The truth is, you sorta can.

This yoga practice is designed to help connect you with how you feel—physically, mentally, and energetically—so you can walk away ready to re-engage with the world as a refreshed version of you. And it only takes 14 minutes.

Perhaps the most energizing benefits of yoga result from a consistent practice. And it doesn’t need to take long. Doing this yoga sequence for a few days or weeks in a row can also help you build a more sustainable habit thanks to its manageable length and simple postures.

Whether you want to practice first thing in the morning, take a break midday, or relax at night, give this yoga class a try.

Sit cross-legged or in another comfortable position. Reach your arms toward the sky. Exhale as you lean toward the left, keeping your arms overhead, and feeling a stretch along your right side. Inhale and return to center with your arms overhead. Exhale as you bend toward the right, feeling a stretch along your left side. Inhale and return to center.

From your seated position, reach your arms out to the sides in a T shape. Inhale as you reach your chest forward, lift your gaze toward the sky, bend your elbows, and draw your arms slightly behind you in seated Cow Pose. Exhale as you draw your navel toward your spine, round your back, and wrap your arms around yourself in a seated Cat Pose variation. Repeat a few times at your own pace.

Reach your arms straight out to the sides. Exhale as you turn your palms toward the sky and your elbows to face the floor. Reach your thumbs toward the wall behind you and your pinkies toward the sky. Take a few breaths here.

Exhale as you rotate your arms forward almost 360 degrees so your elbows and palms face the sky again. Reach your thumbs toward the wall behind you and your pinkies forward. Take a few breaths here.

Exhale and twist your chest toward the right. Place your left hand on your right knee and your right fingertips on the mat behind you. Take a few breaths here.

Inhale and return to center. Reach both arms overhead, then twist your chest toward the left. Place your right hand on your left knee and your left fingertips on the mat behind you. Take a few breaths here, then return to center.

Sit with your legs bent, feet on the mat, and your hands behind your hips with your fingertips pointing toward your body. Exhale and press your hands and feet into the mat as you lift your hips into Reverse Tabletop. Gaze forward or at the sky. Breathe here. As you exhale, lower your hips back to the mat.

Place your hands on the mat a few inches behind your hips with your fingertips facing forward. Bend your knees and place your feet flat on the mat in front of you. Lean back slightly, placing some weight into your hands. Lift your right foot off the mat. Then lift your left leg to meet it as you grasp behind your thighs in a Boat Pose variation. Breathe here. Lower your right toes to the mat, then the left.

Sit on the mat and draw the soles of your feet together, as close or as far from your hips as feels comfortable. Inhale and lift your chest. Exhale and fold forward, gently resting your hands on your ankles or feet in Bound Angle Pose. Stay here for a few breaths or you can instead make it a dynamic stretch if you lean forward, roll to an upright position, and then reach your chest forward over your legs again, repeating that motion a few times. Roll up to seated.

Come to your hands and knees. Spread your fingers wide and make sure your shoulders are stacked over your wrists and your hips over your knees. Make circles with your hips so your shoulders move ahead of your wrists, and then move your hips toward your heels. Repeat in the other direction.

From your hands and knees, tuck your toes, sink your hips toward your heels, and walk your hands back until you’re sitting on your heels in Toe Squat. If the stretch along the bottoms of your feet is too intense, you can keep your hands on the mat and your chest leaning forward to help minimize the stretch. Take a few breaths here.

Lower your knees, chest, and chin to the mat. Press your hands and the tops of your feet into the mat as you lift your chest any amount in Cobra Pose.

Return to your hands and knees. Tuck your toes and shift your hips up and back into Downward-Facing Dog. Take a few deep breaths here.

From Down Dog, lower to your knees. Inhale and straighten your right leg straight behind you. Press the ball of your foot into the mat or lift your foot hip height. Take a few breaths here.

From Bird Dog variation, step your right foot forward between your hands. Lift your chest, draw your navel toward your spine, and place your hands on your hips or reach your arms overhead in Low Lunge. Take a few breaths here.

As you exhale, lower both hands to the mat to frame your right foot and step back into Down Dog. From Down Dog, step your left foot forward between your hands, lower your back knee to the mat, and repeat Low Lunge on this side.

From Low Lunge, step your back foot forward to meet your front foot. Place your hands on your shins or thighs and inhale as you lift your chest slightly and lengthen your spine in Standing Half Forward Bend.

From Standing Half Forward Bend, exhale and lower your chest toward your thighs. Place your hands on the mat or on blocks in Standing Forward Bend. Relax your head and neck.

From Standing Forward Bend, slowly roll up and reach your arms overhead. Lift your gaze toward the sky in Upward Salute. Lower your arms in Mountain Pose.

Reach your arms overhead again, then lower back into Standing Forward Bend.

From Standing Forward Bend, bend your knees as much as you need to plant your hands on the mat and step one foot behind you, then the other, into Plank. Take a couple breaths here.

Lower your knees, chest, and chin to the mat.

Sink your hips toward your heels and reach your arms forward with your palms planted on the mat or you can bend your elbows and stack your forearms. Lower your forehead to the mat, a block, or your stacked arms in Child’s Pose. Breathe here.

Sit cross-legged on the mat in Easy Pose or come into another comfortable position. Take a few deep, slow breaths before returning to your day.
