ukrainian artists reimagine historic tree of life mosaic with 5,000 reclaimed fragments

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

at COP31 in antalya, an interactive installation invites visitors to assemble a colorful artwork celebrating cultural memory, creativity and collective renewal.

A historic Ukrainian mosaic is finding a new life at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, where thousands of reclaimed fragments come together in an interactive installation celebrating art, memory, and collective creativity. Titled Tree of Life, the project reimagines a celebrated 1967 artwork from Mariupol through a contemporary composition made from approximately 5,000 pieces of glass, ceramics, tiles, and recovered construction materials. Initiated by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in Ukraine, the installation was developed by artists from the Monument-ta mosaic studio and the Alla Horska and Viktor Zaretskyi Foundation, with financial support from the Government of Japan.

The project draws inspiration from a monumental mosaic created in Mariupol in 1967 by a group of Ukrainian artists led by Alla Horska. Known for its vibrant colors, organic forms, and symbolic imagery, the original work became an important example of Ukrainian monumental art. Concealed behind a brick wall in the 1970s, the composition was rediscovered in 2008, bringing renewed attention to its artistic and cultural significance. Nearly six decades after its creation, its distinctive visual language has been reinterpreted for an international audience, connecting artistic heritage with contemporary ideas of material reuse and collective participation.

The contemporary Tree of Life combines approximately 5,000 individual fragments, including 55 kilograms of construction materials recovered from buildings in the Kyiv, Mykolaiv, and Kharkiv regions. Rather than using pieces from the original Mariupol mural, the artists have created an entirely new composition inspired by its forms and symbolism. Measuring approximately 1.25 meters high and 2.1 meters wide, the installation consists of 51 individual elements arranged to form the outline of Ukraine, with an additional branch extending toward Crimea. Colorful botanical patterns and decorative details reference the artistic vocabulary of the 1967 mosaic while introducing a new visual identity.

A team of ten artists spent 84 hours assembling the composition, developing a system of 205 magnets that allows its individual elements to be attached and repositioned. Each section weighs between one and two kilograms, making the artwork both modular and interactive. Visitors to the Ukrainian Pavilion are invited to place the pieces themselves, gradually completing the image through a shared process. What might have been a conventional exhibition piece becomes an invitation to participate, with the audience taking an active role in bringing the composition together. The result is an artwork that continues to evolve through the gestures of those who encounter it.

Beyond its artistic references, Tree of Life explores the creative possibilities of material recovery and circular design. By incorporating existing glass, ceramics, and construction materials into a new composition, the project demonstrates how everyday fragments can acquire fresh cultural and aesthetic value. The approach also connects the installation with broader conversations at COP31 surrounding resource efficiency, sustainable construction, and the circular economy. Here, reuse is not presented as a purely technical process, but as an opportunity for experimentation and expression, where materials carry stories from one context into another.

Presented as part of the Ukrainian Pavilion’s programme, Ukraine is the Solution, the installation brings together cultural heritage, contemporary craftsmanship, and environmental awareness. Its interactive format encourages visitors to consider reconstruction as a collective creative process, while celebrating the enduring relevance of Ukrainian artistic traditions. Rather than simply reproducing a historic image, the artists have developed a new work that invites participation and reinterpretation. At a conference dedicated to imagining more sustainable futures, Tree of Life offers a particularly tangible proposition: sometimes renewal begins with what is already there.

artists: Monument-ta mosaic studio and Alla Horska and Viktor Zaretskyi Foundation

artistic concept: Tetiana Tadai and Olena Zaretska

location: Ukrainian Pavilion, COP31, Antalya, Turkey
