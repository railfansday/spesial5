Josh Kerr Says You Probably Don’t Need Water for Runs Under 12 Miles. The Internet Had Thoughts.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

His comments have divided runners online and highlighted that there's much more at play than how many miles you're logging.

I've worn a hydration vest exactly once, and it was less than pleasant. The two bottles bounced loosely against my chest, with water leaking down my shirt throughout the run. It was not a flattering look. Every step came with its own sloshing soundtrack. I spent so much time adjusting the thing (despite an across-the-chest strap) that I barely enjoyed my run.

So when Josh Kerr recently suggested that recreational runners might be going a little overboard with their hydration gear, I understood where he was coming from. During a Reddit AMA hosted by BBC Sport, the Scottish middle-distance star, who casually broke the mile world record in July with a 3:42.66 finish, shared his thoughts on the growing popularity of running vests.

"I'd say that if you're running less than 12 miles, you probably don't need to bring water bottles with you," Kerr said, adding that people running in cities can generally stop at a store if they need something to drink.

For context, we're talking about a world-record holder, Olympic medalist, and world champion in the 1500 meters. He knows a thing or two about logging miles. And while 12 miles might look quite different on his watch than mine, I don't think his suggestion was particularly outrageous.

And because everything is contested on the internet, plenty of people disagree.

Runner @natalie.the.capn shared Kerr's comments on Threads and explained that she wears a hydration belt on every outing so she has somewhere to keep her phone, particularly in case of an emergency. "Funny how men rarely think of that," she wrote.

I do understand her concern. I'm a guy, so I'm definitely not going to pretend I have the same safety considerations that women often face while exercising alone. I also bring my phone everywhere, usually carrying it in my hand because iPhones only get bigger and the pockets on most of my running shorts haven't kept up.

She also pointed out that some of her routes take her six miles or more between places where she can get water, which is an entirely different experience from mine.

I live in New York City, where Central Park has plenty of water fountains (that work ... most of the time) and there's usually a deli within walking distance to snag a Gatorade. I can see why Kerr's approach makes sense here, especially for shorter runs. I've also tried handheld bottles and waist straps, and neither has been particularly enjoyable. Between the bouncing, adjusting, and occasional hand cramps, I'd love to carry as little as possible in my hands.

That changes a lot during marathon training, something I've put myself through four times now, when I'm out for considerably longer and need water to wash down those pasty gels. Aside from the hydration aspect, stopping to buy something, pausing my watch, and interrupting my stride halfway through a long run is a vibe killer.

And I'm certainly not arguing against being prepared. I learned the hard way that sunscreen deserved a permanent spot in my prerun routine after years of neglecting it ended with some casual skin cancer surgery.

The Threads debate, naturally, has brought out some pretty different opinions. One commenter, siding with Kerr, wrote, "Yeah im gonna listen to the guy who runs for a living but thanks." Others pointed out that beginners, slower runners, and people training in hot weather could have very different hydration needs.

They're definitely right about that. Twelve miles is a long way for plenty of runners, no matter what their experience level, and access to water, weather, pace, and individual needs all change the equation. Kerr even acknowledged that people have different reasons for carrying gear, something that seems to have gotten a little lost as his comments made their way around social media.

I still think he has a fair point about how much equipment we sometimes convince ourselves we need on a run. That said, if your vest makes your runs more comfortable or gives you peace of mind, by all means wear it. For me, I'll be keeping my phone close and bringing an appropriate amount of water when I need it.

As for my hydration vest, it's been collecting dust ever since that first outing, and I haven't missed it one bit.
