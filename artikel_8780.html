Electronic devices always make great holiday gifts for cyclists. Whether you’re buying for a rider in your life or upgrading your cycling computer, radar, smart trainer, or power meter on a budget, Black Friday is the best time to score big savings. And Garmin makes some of the best and most popular training and navigation devices for road, gravel, fitness, and mountain bike riders.

Garmin’s GPS computers, smartwatches, radar taillights, power pedals, and Tacx indoor trainers have received great reviews from Bicycling editors and top rankings in our head-to-head tests. The brand’s gear doesn’t often go on deep sale, so these early drops are worth jumping on before they sell out. However, with so many models marked down across both categories, it’s easy to feel overwhelmed. Plus, devices can look similar at first glance, but the details matter when choosing the right tool for riding, training, and everyday fitness.

Garmin GPS head units are one of the smartest upgrades you can make to your road, gravel, mountain, or electric bike, and a much safer alternative than strapping your phone to the bars to see your route. Cycling computers also capture, process, and display data from accessories like heart rate monitors, power meters, and even electronic shifting to help you ride better, train smarter, and stay connected. And because Garmin lets you customize nearly everything—from data fields to navigation screens—you can tailor exactly what shows up.

Buying Tip: Buying previous-generation cycling computers is a great way to save money. Older models usually have almost the same performance at a big discount. Cyclists often even prefer the old models if they’re lighterweight or have better battery life.

Cycling radar is one of the most important pieces of bicycle technology released in the last decade. On-bike radar systems mount to the rear of your bike and feature an integrated taillight. Paired with a cycling computer or smartwatch, they notify you about vehicles approaching from behind so you can move out of the way. They are a must-have item for road and gravel riders.

Buying Tip: Garmin is a pioneer in cycling radar tech and makes the most reliable, consistent systems. The Varia RTL515 is our benchmark and best-value pick, while the RearVue 820 is newer and more powerful; it’s our choice for best performance. The RCT715 is the 515 with an integrated rear-facing camera.

While dedicated cycling computers are the go-to choice for road, gravel, and data-heavy training, many riders prefer Garmin’s smartwatches because they keep everything in one place, especially when they track more than bike rides—anyone who mixes riding with running, hiking, paddling, strength training, or even walking the dog. Garmin watches have powerful GPS, workout metrics, and daily health data in a streamlined device that’s always with you (and they even tell time).

Buying Tip: Mountain bikers often appreciate the simplicity and safety of recording their rides from the wrist rather than risking a pricey head unit in a crash. Triathletes, duathletes, and cyclocross racers love the seamless transitions a watch allows. There’s no need to swap devices or merge files after an event.

Most riders know Garmin from its computers, smartwatches, and cycling radar. But Garmin also owns the indoor trainer brand Tacx. Its smart trainers and indoor bikes are highly regarded for their excellent performance, integration, and useful features. One cool feature we love—especially if you live somewhere prone to winter power outages—is that the Neo 2T generates its own resistance electromagnetically. That means you don’t have to scrap your workout plans just because the lights go out.

Buying Tip: Smart trainers are great for riding in virtual worlds like Zwift. For a deep dive into more great indoor trainers and buying info, check out my story “Ready to Ride Indoors? Here’s How to Pick the Best Trainer.”

Want to step up your training for next season? The best way to measure cycling gains is by training with power—and for that, you need a power meter. Power pedals, like Garmin’s Rally series, are easier to install than crank-based power meter options. Plus, you can swap power pedals easily between bikes. This not only saves cost, but it also gives you consistent data from bike to bike.

Buying Tip: Garmin offers a handy option to buy its power meter in a package including two pedal styles (road and mountain bike/gravel) with swappable spindles. This way you can measure power across riding disciplines.
