Johnny Depp’s ‘Ebenezer’ Unwraps $19M to Win Weekend in Box Office Return

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Fellow newcomers 'Ghost Soldier' and 'How to Rob a Bank' are getting quieter starts over the weekend.

Ebenezer unwrapped $19 million domestically as star Johnny Depp‘s studio comeback vehicle opened in first place at the domestic box office.

Paramount Pictures released director Ti West‘s horror-tinged adaptation of Charles Dickens’ A Christmas Carol in 3,535 North American theaters this weekend and landed an A- CinemaScore from audiences. Of the opening Thursday night audience, 62 percent of the crowd was over age 30, suggesting that the film was especially appealing for those who likely have positive associations with seeing Depp’s work throughout the decades. Ebenezer earned 4.5 out of 5 stars on PostTrak, with 50 percent of guests citing Depp as their reason for seeing the movie.

It took in $23 million from international markets for a global start of $42 million.

Rupert Grint, Andrea Riseborough, Sam Claflin, Daisy Ridley and Ian McKellen round out the cast for the $38 million-budgeted feature that stars Depp as the miserly Ebenezer Scrooge who is visited by three spirits. Ebenezer marks Depp’s first studio project since 2018’s Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald, as he took a hiatus from high-profile work following abuse allegations from ex-wife Amber Heard that Depp has denied, in addition to negative headlines amid his and Heard’s 2022 defamation trial.

Paramount had been pleased with data suggesting that audiences want Depp back and that he still holds wide appeal, with the marketing including the actor appearing in character as Scrooge to remind fans of other transformative roles like Captain Jack Sparrow and Willy Wonka. Ebenezer has a PG-13 rating but is still geared toward the family crowd. Critics have been split on Ebenezer, as it holds a 53 percent approval rating from Rotten Tomatoes reviewers. In his review for The Hollywood Reporter, critic Frank Scheck declares that the film “fulfills its ostensible goal of restoring the actor to A-list status.”

Among other new arrivals, Sony Pictures released Ghost Soldier in 2,558 North American locations on Wednesday, with director William Eubank’s action movie grossing around $5.2 million for the three-day frame and a $8.3 million domestic cume. Jim Caviezel plays a former Green Beret who must defend his Wyoming community against a corporate threat. Hailing from Mandalay Pictures, Ghost Soldier holds a 42 percent score from Rotten Tomatoes critics and a 90 percent from audiences.

Not bagging much of a haul is Amazon MGM Studios’ How to Rob a Bank, director David Leitch‘s heist movie, also grossing $5.2 million for the frame. Nicholas Hoult, Zoë Kravitz and Anna Sawai star in the $40 million-budgeted ensemble film about a group of bank robbers who document their exploits on social media. It has taken $10.3 million globally.

How to Rob a Bank is opening well below Leitch’s recent work, including 2022’s Bullet Train ($30 million domestic debut) and 2024’s The Fall Guy ($27.7 million domestic debut), although those featured more proven box office draws in Brad Pitt and Ryan Gosling, respectively. The new movie, which earned a B- CinemaScore, has come up a bit empty with critics. It holds a 33 percent approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and an 80 percent audience score, with THR chief film critic David Rooney noting in his review that “the fun wears a bit thin.”

Warner Bros.’ The Cat in the Hat is set to pounce on second place in its sophomore frame, grossing roughly $10 million domestically for the weekend to bring its North American sum to $36.7 million for a respectable decline of around 50 percent. Bill Hader voices the titular unpredictable feline in directors Erica Rivinoja and Alessandro Carloni’s animated retelling of Dr. Seuss’ classic children’s book.

Overall, the domestic marketplace collected $90 million for the quiet frame, down 25.9 percent from last weekend, although it was up 18.4 percent from the comparable 2025 weekend. The domestic total for 2026 hits $8.768 billion through Sunday.

After debuting in the top spot last weekend, Godzilla Minus Zero heads to third place with an estimated $7.5 million domestically for its sophomore frame as its North American cume is expected to hit $48.1 million. Filmmaker Takashi Yamazaki’s sequel to 2023’s Godzilla Minus One looks to have a sizable decline of around 75 percent.

Sony Pictures’ Ramayana: Rise of a Legend is likely to notch $5.4 million in its second weekend for a domestic total of roughly $24 million through Sunday and a pleasing 38 percent drop. Its cume would rank director Nitesh Tiwari’s religious epic as the second-highest-grossing Indian feature ever at the U.S. box office. The two-part movie project carries a production budget reported at $420 million and beyond.

Likely to close out the top five when final numbers are reported is A24’s Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything, which continues its impressive run into its fifth weekend with around $5.1 million for the frame and a domestic total of $88.6 million. With an enviable decline of around 37 percent so deep into the run, the buzzy feature from directors Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim continues to build awards momentum.

Among the weekend’s debuts in limited release, Neon launched James Gray’s Paper Tiger in six theaters in Los Angeles and New York, with the crime drama heading to an estimated $203,000 for the frame, averaging $33,884 per screen. Adam Driver, Scarlett Johansson and Miles Teller star in the feature that Rooney’s review praised as “a drama of almost overwhelming power.”

Next weekend sees the arrival of Lionsgate’s The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, expected to become one of the fall’s top-grossing titles.
