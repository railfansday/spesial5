Snubs & Surprises in the 2027 Dance/Electronic Grammy Nominations

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The nominations once again reveal the dance world's fractured factions.

The 2027 Grammy nominations were announced early Monday morning (Nov. 16), with the dance/electronic nominees setting off a dance scene debate currently blazing on social media and also happening off the record via DM, text and phone.

Let’s start with the nominees: As expected, Madonna is the leader, with her July club voyage Confessions II getting nods in all four dance categories: best dance/electronic album, best dance recording, best dance pop recording and best remixed recording. Slayyyter is a close second, with her March project Wor$t Girl In America garnering three nominations across best dance/electronic album, best dance/electronic recording and best dance pop recording.

Projects by Robyn, Skrillex and Tame Impala round out the albums category, while Fred again.., Tame Impala, Tinashe and Underscores make up the rest of the best dance/electronic recording nominees.

One person who isn’t nominated is John Summit, who tweeted his irritation with the nominees this morning, writing that he’s “Never submitting to the grammys again unless s–t changes, it’s all a f–king joke.” He then wrote, “i’m not just talking about myself either there’s zero respect for real electronic dance music and djs. the whole dance category is just music industry fueled pop stars.”

The tweet got a few notable replies, including one from Charli xcx, who quoted-tweeted Summit and wrote “not this guy again!” The producer nightfeelings also responded to the tweet saying that he worked on Tinashe’s Melatonin and that “I actually come from dance music, my first project released on the same labels as Aphex Twin, I’ve performed at Berghain, Andy Weatherall played my tune Cheaters in every set for awhile. I made a song with Romanthony. Just gonna leave that there.”

So, there’s a lot to unpack here, particularly in regard to what “real electronic dance music and djs” even means. In a notoriously gatekeeping genre, this sentiment highlights the reality that the dance world isn’t a cohesive ecosystem of likeminded pals. Instead, it’s a loosely defined global scene made up of a dizzying taxonomy of subgenres that are all (sometimes unhappily) living together under a “dance/electronic” umbrella that covers styles that sound nothing like each other and includes artists who might never want to be on a lineup together.

This scope means that certain – and in fact most – areas of dance music are left out of the Grammys conversation, despite how popular they might be within their respective scenes.

Genres including trance, drum & bass, house, tech house and bass and dubstep that isn’t made by Skrillex have barely been acknowledged by the Academy, and even many of commercial dance music’s biggest and most profitable artists of the moment, like Summit, have been excluded from the conversation. Certainly profitability isn’t always synonymous with being nomination-worthy, but in an Awards system that honors name recognition, it’s noteworthy that some of the biggest stars of the moment – Summit, Prospa, Cloonee, Mau P – have never been nominated. Meanwhile, dance artists that are also pop stars – Madonna, Slayyyter, Tame Impala, Tinashe – lead the nominations.

“Understood the politics and the need to appeal to the largest audience…but still….house music community should have some representation in one of these ‘dance’ categories,” reads a post from venerable dance label Nervous Records. “Yes we should be building our own legit platform award scenario, and yes we should as a community be actively lobbying the Grammys…but still…they can do better…I’m sorry but whoever is involved in presenting these nominations to the voting committee, you’re not paying attention.”

In a comment responding to this post, Afro-house duo Flagrant Drvms sheds light on the reality of voting, writing that “As voting members we understand the gripe and felt the same this morning. Unfortunately, there are 16K voting members that are voting for these songs. So either members whom are uneducated when it comes to our scene and genre are voting at random or there’s some sort of favoritism that we are unaware of but that being said, we need more members whom understand this category.”

As some side-eye the nominees, others are celebrating what is arguably a fantastic collection of artists who’ve all made worthy projects. The fundamental issue Summit and others are pointing out is the heavy amount of overlap between the dance/electronic and dance pop categories, with Slayyyter, Tinashe and Madonna all having songs (from the same projects) in both.

This dance world has been particularly nervy about massive pop stars getting nominated in the dance/electronic categories since 2023, when Beyoncé’s Renaissance won for dance/electronic album, even though many in the dance world didn’t view it as an entirely dance/electronic album. Additionally, when a global superstar drops a one-off dance album, eyebrows tend to raise given that this inclusion means less space for artists who’ve made a career in the space. To wit, Tame Impala never made a dance album before, and might never again.

The fact is that when a pop star makes a dance project, they tend to win. As Flagrant Drvms pointed out, the voting body for these categories is vast and has varying degrees of familiarity with dance music, giving an advantage to artists with more name recognition.

The Grammys acknowledged this issue in 2024 with the addition of the best dance pop recording category, which became a place for songs by dance pop/pop stars working in dance. (This year, the nominees in that category are Madonna, Slayyyter, Zara Larsson, Raye and Tinashe.) The dance world would very likely welcome the addition of a dance pop album category that would create a dedicated space for dance pop and generally provide more space for dance music at the Grammys.

In fact, last year, Billboard even revamped its charts to try and address this same issue, changing the eligibility requirements for Hot Dance/Electronic Songs to only include “songs primarily recorded by DJs or producers with an emphasis on electronic-based production” and also adding Hot Dance/Pop Songs for “dance-centric titles by artists not traditionally rooted in the genre.” This framework could lend itself to a new dance pop album category at the 2028 awards.

In any case, these are the 2027 dance/electronic category snubs and surprises.

The Danish duo had a major hit this year with “Talk To You” ft. Ultra 54. Huge at clubs and festivals, the song is currently at No. 6 on Dance Airplay this week (a new peak), reached No. 4 on Hot Dance/Electronic Songs and spent 17 weeks at No. 1 on the WARM chart. A nod would have given ANOTR – a white-hot name in dance at the moment – its first Grammy nomination.

In a scene where consensus is rare, one thing most people agree on is the greatness of Honey Dijon. The producer released her excellent album The Nightlife this year, with the project putting forth stylish house music that at once honors the roots of the genre while sounding deeply modern. While Honey was nominated in 2023 for her work on Beyoncé’s Renaissance, an artist nomination would have been a deserved and extremely cool look – and just imagine what she’d have worn on the red carpet.

Call it hyperpop, call it progressive pop, call it avant garde dance – in any case, the producer became a music world darling this year following the release of her March album U. The glitchy “Music” from that set got a nomination in best dance/electronic recording, adding a fresh name and sound to an otherwise well-established group of nominees.

While responses to Summit’s tweet calling out the Grammys are mixed, there’s no denying he’s had another massive year. His April album CTRL ESCAPE was one of the key releases that helped drive dance music’s 18.9% year over year growth, and it’s not only a statistical hit, but is full of bops and generally carves the sound of commercial dance music in 2026.

It wasn’t enough to get him nominated, though, with the Grammys once again leaving Summit out despite the fact that he’s one of the biggest dance artists in the world. While size obviously doesn’t always equate to being nomination worthy, Summit’s got some pretty undeniable hits that toe the line between dance and pop. Given the Academy’s previously discussed predilection for dance pop, it’s surprising not to see him here.

One of the defining hits of the year, this thumpy edit (from Pink’s Fancy Some More? album of remixes of her 2025 Fancy That? LP) peaked at No. 1 on the Hot 100, put a rocket launcher on Zara Larsson’s career and gave Alyssa Liu a beat to skate to when it soundtracked her post-gold medal Exhibition Gala program at the Olympics in February. And yet, it wasn’t even submitted in any of the dance categories, a strange turn of events.

Like ANOTR, these two artists had a major dance scene hit with their March track “Free Your Mind,” featuring the singer Sybil. Fresh and stylish, the song was a key release in terms of defining the sound of commercial dance music this year, and its absence demonstrates the gulf that often exists between what makes a hit in the dance scene and what resonates with (or is even known by) voters. Prospa’s excellent Free Your Mind album, from which the song comes, also seemed like an obvious dance/electronic album nominee.

The French producer submitted a pair of hits, “Jamaican (Bam Bam)” and “Movin’ to the Sun.” This latter track caught shade when Hugel acknowledged that the original idea was made by ideas that were put into Suno, which sparked a debate about whether or not it should be considered for a nomination at all. While it was ultimately let it for nomination consideration, it’s possible the conversation around this song – a Dance Airplay No. 1 – hurt its chances.

Beyond the Madonna + Kylie Minogue + Stuart Price “Love Sensation” remix nominated in the best remixed recording category, Madonna released a flurry of other Confessions II edits. “Danceteria Afterhours” featured Charli xcx, while Peggy Gou remixed “I Feel So Free” and Floorplan (the duo made up of Robert and Lyric Hood) put forth an edit of “Love Sensation.” It’s likely that Madonna’s team decided to focus on just one of these remixes in order for them to not drown each other out, and with the pure star power happening with the Minogue edit, it’s ultimately not that much of surprise that that one was submitted.
