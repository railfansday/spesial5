Prophetic unveils SiteAI Drafter for homebuilder land teams

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Prophetic has launched SiteAI Drafter, a new feature for its SiteAI platform that lets land acquisition teams draw high-precision, compliant subdivision site plans and see lot yields update in real time. With added CAD export, both automated and drafted plans can move directly into detailed civil engineering, therefore cutting feasibility time and early design costs.  AI Summary

Prophetic has launched SiteAI Drafter, a tool that lets land teams draw high-precision, compliant subdivision site plans in minutes and export them directly to CAD.

The company, which describes itself as the operating system for land acquisition, introduced SiteAI Drafter on Tuesday as a new feature designed to help residential land teams move from site concept to engineering faster and at lower upfront cost, according to its announcement.

SiteAI Drafter is now available to all SiteAI users and adds manual, CAD-ready drafting to a platform that has focused primarily on automated layouts and early-stage feasibility analysis.

SiteAI generates subdivision site plans with lot and unit yield that account for setbacks, wetlands, flood zones, open spaces and stormwater ponds. Builders and developers use the system to run feasibility studies in seconds instead of weeks, replacing initial engineering reports that can cost from $3,000 to more than $10,000.

The platform’s automated layouts estimate maximum theoretical yield and quickly screen large numbers of parcels. Because SiteAI incorporates site constraints at the outset, it can flag issues that might not surface until much later in a traditional workflow, when engineering work is further along and sunk costs are higher.

For acquisition and development teams working in a competitive land market, this kind of early, standardized analysis is used to compare sites on a level playing field and decide which deals to advance before ordering surveys or full design work.

While automated layouts help screen many parcels at once, land teams often have a preferred concept for a specific site based on local jurisdiction standards, topography and planned product types. Under traditional processes, testing that concept typically requires drafting in CAD or waiting for an engineer, which can slow decisions and tie up billable time.

SiteAI Drafter allows users to draw their own road network and configure lots while the system enforces basic subdivision rules in the background. The tool understands roads and rights of way, lot dimensions and product mix so users can experiment with different layouts while staying within buildable constraints.

The result is a customizable subdivision plan that can reflect the specific community a builder wants to take to a seller, investment committee or municipal review, while still providing a defensible yield number.

Beyond drafting, Prophetic is adding CAD export so that plans created either by SiteAI’s automated engine or by SiteAI Drafter can be used as a starting point for detailed civil engineering design.

This is intended to shift engineers and planners away from redrawing concepts from scratch and toward refining the land team’s preferred layout. Prophetic said the export capability also opens the platform to civil engineers, planners and land developers who want to generate quick concepts before full design or to show builders what a site can hold before a first meeting.

“Every land team plans a site a little differently,” said Oliver Alexander, founder and CEO of Prophetic. “Sometimes you want an answer in seconds across dozens of parcels. Sometimes you already know exactly where the entry and the main road go, and you want to see what your plan yields. SiteAI now works however you do. And with CAD export, the plan doesn’t stop with the land team. It goes to the engineer as a starting point, not a sketch to redraw. That’s what an operating system for land acquisition should do: carry the work forward and help our users win more deals.”

As higher rates and tighter capital make land bets more costly, builders, developers and their capital partners are under pressure to vet more sites with fewer people and less time. Early engineering work is expensive, and using consultants for preliminary concepts can slow down offers in competitive bid situations.

Tools that combine automated yield analysis with guided drafting give acquisition leaders a way to pressure-test their preferred community design before committing to full engineering fees. For homebuilders and land developers, the ability to walk into a seller or investment committee meeting with a concept plan, yield range and CAD-ready file can help shorten decision cycles and reduce the risk of surprises later in entitlement or design.
