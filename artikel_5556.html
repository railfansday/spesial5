Newer COPD Diagnostic Criteria Highlight Challenges Capturing Subclinical Cases

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

— Multidimensional framework reclassifies a meaningful group of patients

A new multidimensional diagnostic schema for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) performed at least on par with GOLD criteria in a population-based cohort study.

While both schemas picked up a similar number of patients overall, one-third of individuals differed between the two, Jonas Eriksson Ström, MD, PhD, of Umeå University in Sweden, and colleagues reported in JAMA Internal Medicine.

Those who were symptomatic and flagged only by the multidimensional criteria were at elevated all-cause mortality risk (HR 2.58, 95% CI 1.66-4.01), whereas those flagged only by GOLD were not (HR 0.94, 95% CI 0.44-2.00).

Notably, 32.5% of the multidimensional criteria diagnoses were in asymptomatic patients, and they too had elevated all-cause mortality after adjustment for age and sex (HR 2.16, 95% CI 1.41-3.31).

"The multidimensional schema identified increased mortality risk among individuals not captured by GOLD, suggesting improved prognostic stratification, while the identification of many asymptomatic cases highlights an existing detection gap in current symptom-driven care pathways," they wrote.

The 2025 multidimensional framework to diagnose COPD added CT imaging features (visually assessed emphysema and bronchial wall thickening) to the GOLD criteria's reliance on respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation. Patients can be diagnosed based on airflow obstruction plus at least one minor criterion from respiratory symptoms or CT changes; without airflow obstruction, COPD can be diagnosed if patients have abnormal chest CT imaging results and meet at least two other minor criteria.

The new framework has been validated in multiple prior research cohorts enriched for COPD, but this is the first report on its performance in a clinical population, noted an accompanying editorial by Matthew Moll, MD, MPH, of Mass General Brigham in Boston, and Emily S. Wan, MD, MPH, of the University of Minnesota in Minneapolis.

While the new multidimensional diagnostic strategy for COPD holds advantages in capturing and acknowledging heterogeneity in COPD, according to the editorialists, they pointed to challenges in translating the algorithm from research to clinical cohorts and medical practice.

"First, the feasibility and additional diagnostic burden of complete profiling (e.g., spirometry, chest CT, and symptom assessment) is not known," they noted.

They agreed with the researchers that current clinical pathways are poorly set up to capture asymptomatic cases other than perhaps incidentally on CT imaging for lung cancer screening.

And importantly, how to manage nonobstructive cases isn't clear, Moll and Wan added. "The majority of pharmacological therapies for COPD were developed and tested exclusively in populations with spirometric obstruction," they wrote. In the RETHINC randomized trial, symptomatic current or former smokers with preserved spirometry showed no benefit on symptoms or respiratory health status from dual long-acting bronchodilator therapy compared with placebo.

"Near-term studies to examine the utility of nonbronchodilator based therapies, such as inhaled corticosteroids and biologics (e.g., dupilumab [Dupixent], mepolizumab [Nucala], and others), are needed," the editorialists argued.

The researchers analyzed the Swedish Cardiopulmonary Bioimage Study of a random sample of adults ages 50 to 64 years seen at six university hospitals with characteristics matching the general population. "This represents an idealized setting in which all individuals -- including those without symptoms -- undergo full diagnostic evaluation," Ström and colleagues wrote. The cohort had median follow-up of 7.8 years.

Of the 29,281 individuals with complete profiling, 4.6% met the multidimensional algorithm's criteria for COPD diagnosis. Among those with respiratory symptoms, overall prevalence of COPD was 14.2% by GOLD and 15.1% by multidimensional criteria.

Framework-reclassified patients without symptoms had the highest mean age, lowest prevalence of never-smoking, and lowest mean body mass index, but their association with increased all-cause mortality was attenuated after adjustment for smoking.

The 16.7% of the newly reclassified patients who had nonobstructive COPD showed enrichment for females and for individuals with overweight or obesity and more chronic bronchitis.

"Taken together, these findings indicate that nonobstructive COPD may not simply represent an earlier stage of typical COPD, but rather a reproducible bronchitic-predominant phenotype. Whether it also reflects a distinct endotype warrants further investigation," the researchers wrote.
