Ranking the 10 best centre-backs in world football: Van Dijk falls, Jacquet in, but who is top?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

World-class defenders from Tottenham, Manchester City, Barcelona, PSG, Arsenal and Liverpool feature in our ranking of the top 10 centre-backs in world football – but who comes out on top?

There’s something of a consensus on who is the best centre-back in the world, but who are his closest challengers? And will we go against the grain?

We’ve ranked the top 10 centre-backs in world football.

We’ve lost patience with Marc Guehi, who’s been rubbish for Manchester City this season. His replacement is Jacquet, one of the standout signings of the Premier League campaign so far.

The Frenchman been comfortably better than his centre-back partner in recent weeks, but the sample size remains small. Sustain this form and he’ll be one of the foundations of Andoni Iraola’s project, destined to climb higher in this ranking.

Now we have a defender addicted to red cards from a team who finished 17th in the last two seasons. Never say we’re not prepared to stick our necks out here at Planet Football.

He was fantastic at the World Cup for Argentina and now has found a match made in heaven with Diego Simeone’s Atletico Madrid.

Romero has made an outstanding start to life in Spain, bringing aggression, intensity and a welcome dose of nastiness to an already combative defence. Simeone’s ideal centre-back.

While unlikely to challenge any serious ‘best in the world’ debates, Tah is quietly a very decent, unassumingly solid centre-back.

He announced himself with a starring role in Bayer Leverkusen’s Invincible Bundesliga title and has continued to show that he belongs at the top level at Vincent Kompany’s Bayern.

“I’m very proud of how I’ve helped redefine the role of the modern defender,” Bastoni told reporters last year.

He’s got a point. Technically brilliant, progressive in possession and comfortable defending on the front foot, he’s got everything in his locker to become one of the outstanding defenders of his generation.

Yet we’re still waiting for him to put it all together. Inter’s Champions League final collapse and Italy’s failure to qualify for the World Cup were major disappointments, even if neither was solely Bastoni’s responsibility.

More recently, the red card against Shakhtar and a disappointing Milan derby display, albeit one in which he scored, have done little to silence the doubters. Inter’s gung-ho approach can leave him exposed, but there’s still a nagging sense that Bastoni should be even better than he is.

Van Dijk dropping to mid-table in a ranking like this would have been unthinkable for much of the past decade. He’s been the outstanding centre-back of his generation, redefining what it means to be a dominant defender in the Premier League.

He’s still very good on his day, with positional intelligence, leadership and natural one-on-one defending that remain hugely valuable. But the errors are becoming more frequent, and there are growing signs that age might finally be catching up with him.

We’re certainly not writing him off. There’s plenty left in the tank, but the days of taking his place among the very best in the world for granted might be behind him.

The Brazilian finally got his flowers for 13 exceptional years of service for PSG, captaining them to successive Champions League titles.

It feels like he’s been around forever, but Marquinhos is still only 32 and might yet have a few years at the very top.

Pacho’s remarkable, trophy-laden rise is worthy of a place in the upper echelons of this list.

After winning the league title with his boyhood club Independiente del Valle, he was snapped up by Royal Antwerp and quickly added a Belgian league and cup double to his honours list.

No silverware was to follow at Eintracht Frankfurt, but he only needed a year in the Bundesliga to effortlessly demonstrate he belongs at the very top European level.

Pacho’s debut season with PSG ended with him capping off a treble with a clean sheet in the Champions League final, as well as a place in the Ligue 1 Team of the Year.

He retained his place in the team as PSG retained the Champions League in 2026. Ecuador were pretty rubbish at the World Cup, but they didn’t concede many goals with Pacho at the back.

When Gabriel first arrived at Arsenal from Lille half a decade ago, he looked solid but unspectacular. A decent meat-and-potatoes defender, but too unrefined to be spoken of among the game’s very best.

Saliba’s ball-playing ability still eclipses Gabriel’s, but for the profile of centre-back that he is – physically imposing, aerially dominant, a leader at the back – Gabriel has effectively perfected his game over the past two seasons.

He’s been superb again this season, even without his usual partner alongside him. Arsenal’s defensive record owes plenty to their brilliant goalkeeper, Arteta’s risk-averse tactical approach and a supremely organised wider structure, but it certainly helps to have a centre-back who doesn’t put a foot wrong. Gabriel was immense in their first win at Anfield since 2012 and has been an ever-present at the heart of their defence.

Cubarsi doesn’t turn 20 until January, yet Cubarsi is already a fixture in Barcelona’s defence and a World Cup winner.

He’s not without his weaknesses. PSG exposed him with their four-goal defeat, and he’ll want to forget his contribution to Real Madrid’s El Clasico winner. He’s hardly going to bully Erling Haaland in a physical contest.

But that doesn’t necessarily have to be a prerequisite for being a brilliant centre-back. Spain showed at the World Cup, and Barcelona have demonstrated repeatedly this season, that Cubarsi’s reading of the game and extraordinary composure can more than compensate for his physical limitations.

There is no questioning that Saliba is a generational ball-playing centre-back.

Gabriel tends to dominate the headlines for his match-winning exploits from set-pieces, but Saliba is arguably even more impressive in his own understated way. A proper Rolls-Royce defender, he’s more Rio Ferdinand than Nemanja Vidic.

Arsenal have coped perfectly well in his absence this season, which is testament to the wider defensive structure and the protection afforded to whoever steps in. But don’t mistake that for Saliba being any less important.

He’s arguably the Gunners’ best player, and an already formidable side will be even more frightening when he returns.
