Ariana Grande Reacts to 'Insane' Backlash Over Her 'Petal' Music Video

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Ariana Grande is addressing the “insane” backlash she received for her “Petal” music video.

“It’s just kind of like, ‘Oh, when will it be about the art? When will it be about the work?’” the pop star, 33, said on the Wednesday, November 25, episode of the “Las Culturistas” podcast. “That was largely what the video was about, is like, ‘Oh, here comes this girl, talented, little,’ and there’s always some distortion around the performance or around the art, whatever it is. And for a while she tries to accommodate and be valuable.”

Grande continued, “My favorite part is that she comes back to herself at the end. She’s wearing the same color. She’s wearing the same hair. She’s wearing the same thing. And then that’s when they say to her, ‘Oh, it’s just so weird. Go back to your old you.’ And she’s like, ‘I am. I found it. I connected with my inner — this is me. Wait, but I did. I did. This is it. This is me trying again. As me.’”

The music video for Grande’s song “Petal,” which premiered in July, portrayed the singer as an aspiring actress named Pepper, who repeatedly receives harsh critiques from male executives at Hollywood auditions. Exhausted by the industry’s expectations, Pepper snaps and massacres the panel of execs with a chainsaw. It’s later revealed that Pepper was acting in a film the whole time.

The video sparked controversy as online users criticized the violent imagery. Additionally, fans expressed concern over Grande’s appearance, spawning a debate about celebrity weight loss culture and body image as a whole.

During her podcast appearance, Grande described the conversations surrounding her body as “its own meta, insane perception” of the video, which she found “really interesting.”

Despite the backlash, Grande said she feels “so proud and grateful” of the project.

“I would put it out again today, exactly as is. I wouldn’t change a thing,” the Wicked star said. “I think my experience of putting art out and having people receive it is so different than how it used to be, even just a few years ago.”

She continued, “People’s reactions have never dictated how I’ve created moving forward. I’ve never changed my musical style or whatever based on what I think people want from me. I’ve always moved authentically in that way, that’s something I’m proud of.”

Still, Grande said the criticism has taken a toll on her mental health in the past.

“It has always, in the past, I think, hurt me a lot,” she said. “And now I really do feel like as long as I’m creating from an authentic place and being honest and sharing what I know needs to come out and making things that I love — let the chips fall where they may. My people will be my people, and it’s OK if they’re not.”

After the intense online scrutiny over the video, Grande announced that she was taking “ a step back” from the public eye following the end of her Eternal Sunshine tour, which wrapped up in September.

“She looks forward to finishing the tour and ending it on a high note, both healthily and happily,” her rep said in a statement at the time.

Addressing the decision on stage in Chicago in August, Grande told fans, “No matter what noises exist out there, nothing will be able to distort my reality or be more real to me or not as real to me than this love that we share and how it feels to make art with people that I love and respect. Absolutely nothing will change that.”
