Giannis’ gravity keeping the Heat’s offense both grounded and in orbit

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

MIAMI — The uniqueness of Giannis Antetokounmpo is that he is playing alongside the NBA's leader in assists and yet he continually stands as the fulcrum of the Miami Heat's attack.

Yes, Davion Mitchell stands atop the NBA's assist leaderboard at 9.0 per game, and, yes, Antetokounmpo stands 30th, at 5.5.

But when the ball starts working its way around the offense, what Antetokounmpo does next often dictates what the Heat do next.

For Antetokounmpo, the approach is rudimentary.

"It's easy," he said. "If I have one guy in front of me, I tend to go. If I have two or three guys in front of me, I pass."

Only it's not that simple nor that basic.

Having Antetokounmpo in the mix, coach Erik Spoelstra said, creates a unique ecosystem.

"Giannis is really allowing and helping our offense to exponentially grow," Spoelstra said, "getting off the ball, getting other guys involved, going to dribble-handoffs, playing on the baseline, and yet he's still able to do what he does at his best."

So yes, getting his, fourth in the league in scoring at 28.2 points per game, but also helping others get theirs.

"Great players always can figure it out," Spoelstra said, with the Heat idle until Sunday night's game against the Oklahoma City Thunder, at the close of this five-game homestand. "But I think he's really helping unlock a lot of other guys and their confidence and allowing other guys to play out of a rhythm and a flow and become serious weapons for our team.

"That's helping our offense get to a higher level."

Early on, it was as if the Heat were attempting to conjure a system around Antetokounmpo.

Then it became clear that Antetokounmpo is the system.

"I mean, it's been night and day," Mitchell said of the Heat offense reaching another level. "I think that even towards the beginning of the game, you can just tell he's getting everyone involved and everyone's just kind of following the rhythm because he can take over a game at any point of the time.

"And I mean, he just kind of just trusts our teammates to get people in the rhythm, get them flowing. That's why you see so many guys just making shots."

Over their last 11 games, a 9-2 run in the standings, the Heat rank No. 2 in offensive rating during that period, and third in pace.

It has come with Antetokounmpo opening with the starters but then cycling back in the second period to typically play with four reserves.

The approach has allowed Bam Adebayo to both work in concert with Antetokounmpo but also at times as featured scorer.

But Adebayo said it's no longer a case of feeling stunted with the two on the court together

"Just his willingness to get off the ball, his willingness to participate in other ways offensively," Adebayo said, "to me, it just shows how easy we can make the game for him.

"Obviously, you get him in different spots where he's not just at the top of the floor, then teams can just build a wall."

Now a full month into the season, it is easier to see the pieces coming together, including the Antetokounmpo collaboration with 3-point ace Klay Thompson.

"When you have somebody who's such an aggressive force of nature, that can get downhill, by the very nature of how he plays, there's some tension to that," Spoelstra said of the stresses Antetokounmpo can put on defenses. "But I think what's beautiful about his mind is he's able to see the possibilities when he can just go to a dribble-handoff or just swing it and kick it and let somebody else play.

"Those are tough decisions for a defense."

As Spoelstra knows well from when he was the opposing coach.

"We used to be in that situation with him at times, when he's trying to go downhill and you're loading up and then all of a sudden he goes to a handoff, you're asking the help defender to do two things.

"That's contradictory when it's Klay Thompson. Because you're always told over your whole career, never take a body off of Klay Thompson. You're told your whole career, always have three defenders against Giannis."

"How many times can you put teams where they're making those kinds of contradictory decisions, and then doing it without a play call, and doing it with a motor, doing it before the defense can get set?" Spoelstra said. "That's something we're getting better at and I still think we can get to a higher level."
