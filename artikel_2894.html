CFO Colette Kress Just Made This Subtle Shift That Could Save Nvidia Shareholders

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Nvidia may now take a more deliberate approach to customer financing.

By Will Healy – Nov 23, 2026 at 8:06PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%) continues to impress shareholders. Even though large companies tend to grow slowly, Nvidia has challenged that assertion by delivering triple-digit revenue growth, much as it did in the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended Oct. 25).

Unfortunately for shareholders, CEO Jensen Huang financed much of this growth through "circular financing," in which the company finances customers to sell products.

CFO Colette Kress, who was briefly interim CEO, questioned this strategy. She reined in much of this financial support for other companies' artificial intelligence (AI) projects. Assuming Huang follows her lead, here's how this approach could save Nvidia shareholders.

In recent months, investors have become more concerned about how Nvidia generated its revenue growth.

Nvidia was to provide financial backing for an OpenAI data center in Ohio. Huang wanted to backstop $250 billion of OpenAI purchases, but Kress stepped in to cut that commitment to $105 billion.

Although Kress has rejected the "circular financing" label, as interim CEO, she stated on the third-quarter 2027 earnings call that Nvidia would be "disciplined and selective" in its support for the neoclouds, which will likely reassure those investors who questioned Huang's prior approach.

This is critical for investors because analysts often view such financing as consistent with the end stage of a boom.

They have good reason to worry. Cisco Systems (CSCO +6.80%) was at the center of the dot-com boom when it backstopped several of its customers. In 2001, Cisco famously had to write down $2.25 billion in one quarter when several of the customers it backed declared bankruptcy. That write-down contributed to a stock price decline so profound that it took 26 years for its stock to return to its 2000 high.

With "everything Kress told us," the company carries around $118 billion in guarantees, several orders of magnitude higher than Cisco's write-down. Still, if projects fall behind schedule, the company will now revoke guarantees, a move that could shield it from painful write-offs later.

Moreover, Nvidia may not be vulnerable to a slump as severe as Cisco's. Nvidia's 18 P/E ratio as of Nov. 23 is a tiny fraction of the 222 P/E ratio that Cisco reached in early 2000, suggesting less downside if an AI bust occurs.

Furthermore, Kress's approach did not appear to meaningfully slow Nvidia, even in the short term. For fiscal Q4, it guided for $130 billion in revenue at the midpoint, a 91% year-over-year increase if that forecast holds. While that is slightly less than fiscal Q3's 103% revenue increase, it may be a small price to pay to mitigate the specter of massive write-downs later.

Kress's move to rein in customer financing could alleviate a significant worry about the stock, assuming Huang follows his advice now that he has returned as CEO.

Indeed, the possibility of bankruptcies among major customers leaves Nvidia financially exposed, and that vulnerability could hurt the stock.

Nonetheless, Kress's decision to be "disciplined and selective" about customer financing addresses a key concern with Nvidia's current business approach. While it may mean somewhat slower growth, the prospect of reduced financial exposure should an AI bust occur is likely a wise move in the long run.
