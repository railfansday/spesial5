Fantasy Football 2026: Week 9 preview — Start/sit and more

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Welcome to Week 9. Somehow, we are already at the halfway point of the 18-week NFL season. That was fast.

Week 8 was strange. Tampa beat Atlanta 16-0 without completing a single pass. Dallas and Kansas City both blew double-digit fourth quarter leads. Joe Burrow accounted for six total touchdowns. Running backs absolutely ran wild on Sunday. Baltimore lost two quarterbacks to what might be season-ending injuries in the first half at Buffalo. Minnesota held Detroit to its lowest point total of the season, but also scored the fewest against them, and absorbed its first loss. The Giants grew their NFC East lead without playing. It was that kind of weekend – eerie and weird — kind of fitting for Halloween.

What's in store this week? Six teams – Philadelphia, Seattle, Baltimore, Minnesota, fCleveland, and Arizona – are switching to a different starting quarterback. That raises a bunch of questions and decisions for fantasy managers.

Stats of the Week, Bucs-Falcons (sorry, this HAS TO get its own section – one day you'll tell your grandkids about that time in 2026 when two NFL teams played a high school football game):

Tampa became the first team since 1974, and the third since the forward pass was invented in the 1930s, to win a game without completing a single pass.

Tampa is the first team in NFL history to have two players (Bucky Irving and Kenneth Gainwell) with 25+ rushes and 125+ rushing yards in the same game.

Tampa's defenders caught four more passes than Tampa's offensive players.

Despite not completing a pass, and rushing for just 14 yards with no TDs, Baker Mayfield had more fantasy points than, and triple the passer rating of, Michael Penix, Jr.

Over their past two games (both wins, with 59 total points scored), the Bucs called 16 passes and 104 runs, and Baker Mayfield had seven completions for 64 yards.

No team in AFC North is over .500. Every team in the NFC North is over .500. Every team in the NFC South is under .500.

The NFC North is a combined 22-3 in non-divisional games.

Bo Nix has engineered 13 fourth quarter comebacks – the most in NFL history for a quarterback in his first three seasons.

Since the start of last season, the Broncos are 3-3 in games where they trailed by 11+ points during the fourth quarter. The rest of the NFL is 11-215 in such situations.

For the first time since 2003, six running backs had at least 140 rushing yards on Sunday.

The Browns have lost 23 straight games in Pittsburgh, which ties the NFL record for a road losing streak vs. one team.

The Bears have 17 rushing touchdowns, which is the most for a team through their first eight games since 1956.

Since 2000, Arizona was 1-147-1 when trailing by 14 points or more in the fourth quarter, and Dallas was 69-0 at home when leading by 14 points or more in the fourth quarter. That all changed on Sunday with the Cardinals' late comeback.

On to Week 9. Note: CIN @ ATL is being played in Spain. Set those lineups early!

For those who are familiar with this column, you know the drill. For everyone else: The Rides, Fades, and Sleepers is an analysis of players that I think are primed for an especially strong or poor performance, often as compared to consensus expectations. This isn't a straight-up Start/Sit exercise. As a general rule, start your studs. I'll rarely list the most obvious names at a position as "Rides" because those players are almost always expected to have strong performances. You don't need me or anyone else telling you to start Joe Burrow. While this analysis is intended for season-long play, it works for DFS formats too. Half PPR scoring and Expert Consensus Rankings (ECR) are used for the column.

Each week, I'm picking a Ride, Fade, and Sleeper of the Week. The rules: The Ride of the Week can't be a truly elite option, the Fade can't be someone who nobody is starting anyway, and the Sleeper must be an actual sleeper, from down in the rankings.

Last week, my Rides, Fades, and Sleepers did well, with a lot more hits than misses. You can check my work here.

My biggest miss was my Ride of the Week, Zay Flowers. He scored zero points. I'll repeat that – A bagel. A donut. Zero point zero. I obviously couldn't foresee the quarterback injuries, but I'll take the L. My Fade of the Week hit (Bryce Young, 11.4 fantasy points, QB18) and so did my Sleeper of the Week (Geno Smith, 19 fantasy points, QB13).

Ride of the Week: Matthew Stafford (@WAS). Stafford is ranked outside the Top-10 QBs this week and I think that's too low. The Commanders can score behind Jayden Daniels and they also give up points. This game's Vegas total of 50.5 is the second highest on the board and I think it goes over. Washington is tough to run on, but generous in the passing game. They're one of seven teams that has allowed at least 15 TD passes so far this season, and they've given up the third most fantasy points per game (FPPG) to opposing QBs. Stafford is coming off his second worst fantasy output of the season and I think he'll rebound. He came out of last week's game with a sore elbow, so keep an eye on that.

Fade of the Week: Zay Flowers (vs. JAC). Well, this is a first for me, as Flowers goes from Ride of the Week to Fade of the Week in just five days. UDFA rookie Joe Fagnano gets his first NFL start for the Ravens, on a short week and a bad ankle. If the Ravens get to ten points, I'll be surprised. Their implied total is 15, which is the second lowest on the Board, and they haven't topped 13 points in any of their last three games. Jacoby Brissett will help – in Week 10. Jacksonville's defense must be licking its chops after it throttled another dead team last week. Flowers is a terrific receiver who was having a great season before last Sunday, but this QB situation is too much to overcome. He's ranked inside the Top-20 at the position this week and I have him outside my Top-30.

Sleeper of the Week: Drew Lock (vs. ARI). The Cardinals are coming off that improbable comeback in Dallas, while Seattle will look to rebound after getting spanked by the Bears at home. This should be a get-right game for the Seahawks, and a potential let-down affair for the Cardinals (who had a let-down two weeks ago, after their big upset win over the Rams in Week 6). Seattle may struggle to run the ball against a defense that just stymied Javonte Williams. Arizona has allowed the most TD passes (18) and second most FPPG to opposing QBs. Lock is ranked as the QB29 this week, which is absurd. He's behind Fagnano, Gardner Minshew II, and Shedeur Sanders? No. None of them have JSN to throw to. Obviously, he shouldn't be started over a Top-12 option, but I like him as a QB2 this week. In his only start of the season in Week 2, he put up 21 fantasy points at…you guessed it…Arizona.

Elite options this week – Josh Allen, Lamar, Drake Maye, Brock Purdy, Jared Goff, and Joe Burrow;the analysis starts below them.

Three QBs ranked in the Top-10 this week who should smash:

Dak Prescott (@IND). The Colts have given up, or at least, they don't have the ability to compete at the moment. Dak should have his way with this mediocre defense, which has allowed the tenth most FPPG to opposing QBs. Dallas can't afford another stupid loss.

Trevor Lawrence (@BAL). The Ravens have a decent defense that's middle of the pack against opposing QBs, but there's almost no scenario where they don't lose this game by a lot. Their offense won't be able to stay on the field, so Lawrence should have plenty of scoring chances.

Patrick Mahomes (vs. NYJ). Mahomes threw for 321 yards and three TDs at Denver, to break a mini-cold streak and this week he gets a much softer defense, at home. Kenneth Walker might run wild, but I think Mahomes will get his too. The Jets have allowed 14 TD passes this season and have only one pick (ahem, that's since the start of last season).

Malik Willis (vs. DET) is in play this week as a low-end QB1 streamer. He's thrown for two TDs in two straight games and added a rushing TD in Week 7. The Lions' defense is showing improvement over the last two weeks, but they're still vulnerable and while they got their safeties back, now they're without stalwart LB Jack Campbell. They've allowed the most passing yards, passing TDs (18) and FPPG to opposing QBs, and they've also yielded two rushing TDs to the position.

Other QB2-types to consider if you need it: C.J. Stroud (@LAC), Kirk Cousins (@SF), Bo Nix (@CAR), and J.J. McCarthy (@PHI). Carson Wentz (@BUF) is risky, but it's an excellent matchup.

There are some higher-end starters with less-than-ideal matchups, but none are bad enough for me to list them as Fades. However, there are some QB2s who fantasy managers could be considering this week, but that I'm avoiding if I can: Baker Mayfield (@CHI, and I'm not sure how much he can really throw right now), and Michael Penix, Jr. (vs. CIN, and if you started him last week, I'm sorry for your loss).

And don't start Justin Herbert (vs. HOU). This is now a running joke.

Elite options this week – Jahmyr Gibbs, Bijan Robinson, Kenneth Walker, and Christian McCaffrey; the analysis starts below them.

Three high-end backs in smash spots this week:

Javonte Williams (@IND) barely averaged two yards per carry last week, a week after setting a career high in rushing yards. Which Williams will we get this week? A good one, I expect. Indianapolis's run defense isn't as stout without DeForest Buckner and allows the tenth most FPPG to opposing RBs. The Colts are reeling and offered very little resistance to Jacksonville's ground game last week (the first without Buckner). Williams is among the league leaders in carries inside the five and should bounce back nicely in this spot.

Bucky Irving (@CHI) and Bhayshul Tuten (@BAL) are coming off monster games and should rack up some good totals again this week. Chicago is tougher up the gut with Jeffery Simmons in the middle, but Tampa will run it and then run it some more. The Ravens are a tough run defense, but game script is a big factor here. Jacksonville is a 10-point favorite and should dominate time of possession and play with a big lead. If Tuten is out, Chris Rodriguez, Jr. is an OK play if you need him.

Others ranked inside the Top-25 this week who I think will outperform their rankings: Chase Brown (@ATL, who now allow the third most FPPG to opposing RBs after getting run over for three straight hours on Sunday), Chuba Hubbard (vs. DEN), Jonah Coleman (@CAR, allowing the second most FPPG to opposing RBs), and Quinshon Judkins (@NO, most FPPG allowed to opposing RBs). If Chase Brown can't get the ground game going this week, I'll start to get worried.

A few to consider if you're stuck: Woody Marks (@LAC), Alvin Kamara (vs. CLE), Mike Washington, Jr. (@SF), Dylan Sampson (vs. CLE), and J.K. Dobbins (@CAR).

I've got some household names on the list this week.

Derrick Henry (vs. JAC) is a tough call this week. The Ravens are in a terrible situation for this game, on a short week. Jacksonville has been a buzzsaw for running backs. They allow the fewest yards and FPPG to opposing RBs. Henry is outside my Top-15 for the week. Henry scored last week, but his fantasy output has taken a huge hit since Lamar Jackson got hurt.

Is Jonathan Taylor (vs. DAL) really going to be active on Sunday? It's a tasty matchup (see, e.g., Allgeier, Tyler), but if he plays he could see limited snaps while potential buyers consider their offers and the Colts try to protect the asset. Start him if he's active, but with caution.

It's tough to start Saquon Barkley (@NYG), who is dealing with multiple injuries and isn't a lock to get a decent workload. Yes, he has two straight games with a TD, but in each of those games he played fewer than 20 snaps. I'm sure he still gets a little extra motivation against his old team, but they've been pretty good against running backs (Top-12 fantasy run defense).

Josh Jacobs (@NE) struggled to find running room last week and looked slow. He got bailed out with two short touchdowns, and that's his upside. Green Bay hasn't run the ball well all season, and New England allows the fifth fewest FPPG to opposing RBs. Pass.

The Seahawks struggled to run the ball consistently against Chicago, and it won't be all that much easier against Arizona, who is decent vs. the run. Throw in that Seattle is using multiple backs in rotation and I'll fade Zach Charbonnet and Jadarian Price (vs. ARI).

PSA: Don't start any Chargers RBs. Of all players who don't have injury as an excuse, Omarion Hampton is currently the single most disappointing player who went in the first two rounds of fantasy drafts.

Other RB Fades: Cam Skattebo (@PHI, if he plays), Ashton Jeanty (@SF), and Blake Corum (@WAS).

Elite options this week – Puka Nacua, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Ja'Marr Chase AND Tee Higgins, Amon-Ra St. Brown, CeeDee Lamb, and Malik Nabers; the analysis starts below them.

I've listed seven "elites" above. Beyond them, here are six Rides this week, all ranked inside the weekly Top-20: Christian Watson AND Matthew Golden (@NE, tough matchup but they can't run the ball and will have to throw a lot), Parker Washington (to bounce back, @BAL), Rashee Rice (vs. NYJ), Justin Jefferson (vs. BUF, they've allowed the most TD catches and fifth most FPPG to opposing WRs, and the Vikings will actually need to score to win this game), and Luther Burden III (vs. TB).

Moving down to those ranked between 21 and 30 for the Week — I like these guys as WR2/WR3 or Flex plays: D.J. Moore (@MIN, tough matchup, but Moore will get a lot of 1-on-1s), Stefon Diggs (vs. LAR), Ladd McConkey (vs. HOU), and Mike Evans (vs. LV, assuming he plays).

WRs ranked outside the Top-30 this week that I think you can plug into lineups if you're in need comprise a pretty big list this week: Deebo Samuel, Sr. (vs. LV, if he plays), K.C. Concepcion, Jr. (@NO), Malik Washington AND Caleb Douglas (vs. DET), Jordan Addison (vs. BUF), Rome Odunze (vs. TB, he's making a few big catches each week and is overdue for a score), Makai Lemon (vs. NYG, and Joe Milton could be a nice upgrade for this passing game), and Ryan Flournoy (@IND).

Tet McMillan (vs. DEN) had a monster game in Week 4 but hasn't topped 12.5 fantasy points in the rest of his outings. The Broncos are a tough matchup, too (sixth fewest FPPG and just four TD catches allowed to the position).

The Jets have more weapons these days, so Garrett Wilson (@KC) is not seeing his usual massive target share. It's a tough matchup (second fewest FPPG and a league-low two TD catches allowed to opposing receivers).

I know he scored a TD and got a 2-point catch last week, but Jaylen Waddle (@CAR) is hard to trust. He played a season-low 45% of snaps last week, a troubling development. He also only had 39 receiving yards and was bailed out by the scoring plays.

I'll fade A.J. Brown (vs. GB) if he plays. He's an auto-fade to me until he looks like some semblance of himself.

Other WRs ranked inside the Top-30 and/or who should be starting options this season and who I'm lower than consensus on this week include Jameson Williams (@MIA, third fewest FPPG allowed to opposing WRs, and he's a bit boom/bust), Michael Wilson (@SEA, with a QB change and going against the #1 fantasy defense vs. WRs) and Tre Tucker (@SF), who is as boom/bust as it gets.

Elite options this week – Brock Bowers, Trey McBride, George Kittle and Isaiah Likely; the analysis starts below them.

Harold Fannin, Jr. (@NO) is coming off a monster game after a mostly quiet start to the season. I think the QB change won't matter much for him, and he'll keep it going against a defense that hasn't covered tight ends well (fifth most FPPG allowed to the position).

Others in the Top-12 this week who I like for strong performances: Tucker Kraft (@NE), Travis Kelce (vs. NYJ), Sam LaPorta to bounce back after two down games (@MIA, third most FPPG allowed to opposing tight ends), and (gulp) Kyle Pitts, Sr. (vs. CIN).

Greg Dulcich (vs. DET) gets the gift that is the Lions' defense, which has allowed the most everything to tight ends. He's also been busy the last few weeks, with at least five targets in four straight games. Miami should need to throw a lot in this contest.

Mike Gesicki (@ATL) is mostly TD or bust, but the TDs keep coming (five straight games). He's there if you need him.

Others to consider if you're in need of a streamer: T.J. Hockenson (@BUF), Brenton Strange (@BAL), Tyler Higbee (@WAS, second most FPPG allowed to the position, but Rams' tight ends are the ultimate roulette wheel), and Mason Taylor (@KC, he's been on the rise and the Chiefs allow the fifth most FPPG to opposing tight ends).

Thank you, Dalton Kincaid (@MIN) for showing up last week! Unfortunately, this week's matchup is brutal. The Vikings have allowed the fewest catches, yards, FPPG, and touchdowns (zero) to opposing tight ends.

Jake Ferguson (@IND), are you still in the NFL? If he plays, he might be limited with an ankle injury.

More Fades: Mark Andrews (vs. JAC), Juwan Johnson (vs. CLE, and after a fast start he has faded fast, with just five catches for 23 yards over the past three games).

That's all I've got this week. Good luck in Week 9!
