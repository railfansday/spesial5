Martin Odegaard bails out comically wasteful Arsenal

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

For much of this sticky night in Naples, Arsenal appeared to be starting the new Champions League campaign the same way they finished the last one: by erroneously smashing the ball into the stands behind the goal.

Memories of Gabriel Magalhães’s ballooned penalty in the final against Paris St-Germain remain fresh in the mind, and here his team-mates were lining up to produce their own versions. Bukayo Saka and Piero Hincapié, especially, delivered astonishing misses which were closer to entering orbit than the opposition net.

Thankfully for Arsenal, though, there was one player who was in no mood to relive the frustration of that shoot-out loss 14 weeks ago. Martin Odegaard has started this season in truly fabulous form and here he scored a truly fabulous late goal, fizzed into the corner from range, to cap another inventive individual performance and earn three points for his team.

In five matches this season, Odegaard has already scored four goals. That is three more than he managed in the whole of last season. The Arsenal captain is fit again and he is flowing. As ever, when Odegaard flows, Arsenal flow.

“That’s what we need, the players with that mindset to decide the game and play with that personality,” said Mikel Arteta. “He has got that edge. He’s getting into those positions much more in recent games.”

In the end it was a thoroughly deserved victory for the Premier League side, who were superior to Napoli in every department. Were it not for their comically bad finishing, they would have guaranteed the result long before Odegaard’s 75th-minute strike.

Their expected goals figure suggested they should have scored more than four goals, compared to Napoli’s 0.22. In total, they registered 26 shots – the most of any Arsenal team on record in a single Champions League match, and the most on record a Napoli team has ever faced.

“With the amount of chances we generated, the scoreline has to reflect a very different result,” said Arteta. “The number of players that got into this situation, the amount of situations that we generated in various ways was something that pleased me a lot.”

There was, almost inevitably, another clean sheet for Arteta’s side. They had the best defence in the Champions League last season, conceding only seven goals in 15 matches, and they were largely untroubled here. Ezri Konsa was once again excellent, in only his second start for the club. He successfully wrapped up Rasmus Hojlund, the former Manchester United centre-forward, and was as calm in possession as the absent William Saliba would have been. A potential injury to right-back Ben White was a concern, though.

The Champions League is the one that Arsenal have never won and, you suspect, the one that Arteta really wants. Success in this competition would elevate Arsenal to a higher level as a club and fully establish Arteta as one of the great coaching minds of his generation.

To lose last season’s final in such painful circumstances clearly hurt the Arsenal manager, who was visibly distraught after that shoot-out defeat despite the obvious delight of winning the Premier League title. If you could give him the choice between retaining the league this season or leading Arsenal to their first European Cup, he would surely pick the latter.

Arsenal have now won THIRTEEN UCL League Phase games in a row 🔴🔥 pic.twitter.com/UmcfMYO13r

A trip to Naples in the first round of the league phase made for a lovely opening tie: atmospheric, intense and competitive. Napoli have had a difficult start to their campaign, losing two of three Serie A games so far, but they possess real quality and have a top manager in Massimiliano Allegri.

Even at the age of 35, the great Kevin De Bruyne still has power in his boots and schemes in his mind. When he tested Arsenal goalkeeper David Raya from range in the first half, it would have triggered horrible flashbacks in the minds of the travelling supporters. In his previous 17 starts against Arsenal for Manchester City, De Bruyne had scored eight goals and registered four assists. Thankfully for Arsenal, it was the only moment in which De Bruyne really threatened.

Throughout this game, Arsenal moved the ball around with precision and purpose. They created an extraordinary amount of chances, with Saka and Hincapié missing the best of them. Saka’s effort in the first half, when one-v-one with the goalkeeper, was strangely erratic. Hincapié’s opportunity, meanwhile, was laughable. Two yards out from goal, he somehow put the ball onto the running track that surrounds the pitch at the Stadio Diego Armando Maradona.

Despite all the wasted chances, Arsenal kept going. Odegaard, in particular, kept probing. His goal was gorgeous: a swift exchange of passes between him, White and Christos Tzolis resulted in the Arsenal captain firing his effort from the edge of the area. Delicate build-up, ferocious finish.

In keeping with the theme of the night, Arsenal should have scored more. Noni Madueke, for example, missed a one-v-one when it seemed like he had all the time in the world. But Odegaard’s intervention eventually proved to be enough, as he once again stood forward as Arsenal’s match-winner.
