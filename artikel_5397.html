10M transactions show off-MLS private listings discount

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A 10M-transaction review found coming soon listings that later hit the MLS sold for higher prices than comparable MLS-only listings. Listings kept private and off the MLS sold for less, with larger discounts reported in majority non-white and Hispanic neighborhoods.  AI Summary

Compass wants buyers to see it first. A study of more than 10 million home sales suggests some sellers would do better if everyone saw it at once. The gap between the marketing and the money is the real story.

On September 22, Compass launched “See It First,” the centerpiece of its largest Compass.com redesign in a decade, pulling coming-soon and private exclusive listings from its nine brands into one early-access showcase. “With See It First, we’re giving consumers the opportunity to discover more homes earlier,” Julie Keef, Compass’s head of consumer search, told HousingWire. Compass cited 56% year-over-year traffic growth on its site in August and a multimillion-dollar ad campaign behind the launch.

The timing was awkward. The day after the redesign, HousingWire reported on a study from the Association of Real Estate License Law Officials (ARELLO) that reviewed more than 10 million residential transactions between January 2024 and June 2026. The numbers complicate the private-listing story.

The study drew a line most consumers never hear. Coming-soon listings, the kind that get pre-marketed and then hit the MLS, sold for about $9,200 more than comparable MLS listings in the mid-market and close to $17,000 more at the top. Listings that stayed private and off the MLS did the opposite, coming in roughly $5,000 below MLS in the mid-range and about $3,500 below at the high end.

Powerfact: The premium belongs to exposure, not exclusivity. Pre-marketing plus full MLS reach won. Staying hidden lost.

The trend lines tell the same story. Coming-soon volume climbed 13 percent in the year ending June 2026. Private listings fell 3 percent over the same stretch. The market is quietly voting with its feet, even as the marketing points the other way.

Then there is the finding no fiduciary should skim past. The private-listing discount landed hardest in non-white neighborhoods. Private listings sold about 0.88% below MLS in majority-white areas, 2.21% below in majority non-white areas, and 3.33% below in majority-Hispanic areas. ARELLO stressed the gaps “do not establish causation or unlawful discrimination” but warrant “further examination.”

Powerfact: A smaller audience is a smaller check, and the sellers with the least room to lose lost the most.

Compass CEO Robert Reffkin has made no secret of where he stands. He has called the Clear Cooperation Policy “anti-competitive and anti-consumer” and said Compass “has not and will not adhere” to it. He has a fair point that the industry defends old rules on autopilot. But this study is not an association talking point. It is transaction data, and it says the gated model Compass is now marketing at scale tends to cost the person who owns the house.

Give Compass its due. It built a genuine consumer destination, kept the listing agent’s name and photo front and center instead of selling leads back to them, and made pre-marketing easy. The coming-soon half of the strategy is exactly what the data rewards. The gated half is where sellers should slow down.

Lead with the numbers, not the logo. Before you take a listing, walk the seller through both roads in dollars. Pre-market to build demand, then open the home to the full market through the MLS. That combination is what the study rewards.

If a seller wants to stay private, document it. Put the estimated cost of reduced exposure in writing and let them decide with eyes open. That single habit protects your client and protects you.

And sharpen your language. “Coming soon” is a launch runway. “Private” is a closed door. Selling them as the same thing is how good sellers quietly lose money.

Portals will keep spending to own the first click. Your job sits at the other end of the deal, on the seller’s final number. The agents who thrive from here will be the ones who can explain exposure, price it honestly, and prove the point with data instead of a slogan. The tools are changing fast. The fiduciary duty underneath them has not moved an inch.
