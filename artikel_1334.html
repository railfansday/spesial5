Video Assistant Referee (VAR) has its fair share of critics. Introduced to the Premier League at the start of the 2019/20 season, the technology was designed to help the on-pitch officials and ensure matches were officiated seamlessly and without controversy.

However, it has not had the impact that it was intended to have. If anything, the number of refereeing controversies has gone up, and many high-profile names, including Roy Keane and Wayne Rooney, have lost patience and want to see VAR scrapped.

But what would the 2026/27 Premier League table look like if every refereeing decision was correct? After every matchday in England's top tier, Squawka asks their followers to vote on controversial VAR decisions and whether the right decision was made or not.

Using that data, they have created a Premier League table without VAR errors following matchday 10, and it makes for interesting reading.

The table has been compiled on the basis that penalties, should they have been awarded, would have been converted.

Sunderland have had a dismal start to the campaign. The Black Cats, who are competing in the Conference League after finishing seventh in 2025/26, are currently rock bottom of the Premier League, having picked up five points from their opening ten games. They can't blame VAR for their troubles, either, as they would remain in the same position without its errors.

Crystal Palace would be second bottom, with Manchester United rounding out the relegation places. Nottingham Forest, who are currently 16th in the Premier League, would be two points better off and would be up in 14th, above Palace and United in the table, had VAR been perfect.

Back in September, Forest and Tottenham Hotspur drew 0-0 at the City Ground. VAR is adjudged to have wrongly intervened to disallow Neco Williams' goal for the home side. The Premier League released a statement explaining the goal had been ruled out for handball, but many felt the footage was inconclusive and not clear and obvious. Had VAR made the right decision, Forest would have won 1-0.

Coventry City currently sit 17th in the table, as is correct for them. But Fulham actually find themselves one place higher than they should be, as a result of Forest's issues.

Hull City have had a tremendous start and currently sit 13th in the table, but they would be a point and two places worse off without VAR errors.

Tottenham Hotspur would find themselves two places worse off down in 13th if VAR decisions were correct, while Ipswich Town would be two places and two points better off up in 12th. Newcastle United currently find themselves in 12th in the table, but the Magpies would be up in 11th with an extra point and a +6 goal difference swing in their favour.

Aston Villa are currently the team propping up the top half of the table with 13 points and a -3 goal difference from their opening ten games. But while their league position wouldn't have changed, the VAR decisions being correct would have them two points better off.

Leeds have had a strong start to the season, but they would be one point worse off in ninth in the table without any VAR errors, while Bournemouth would be dropping down to 8th in the table, with a -1 swing in their goal difference.

Everton would find themselves in 7th in the table, one place worse off, although they would still have 16 points and a +2 goal difference. Brentford are the biggest losers in the top half of the table, as they would be two places better off up in 6th place with two more points.

While the top five would be unchanged in terms of their standings, the points totals would be widely different. Chelsea and Liverpool currently sit fifth and fourth in the Premier League table with 16 and 18 points respectively, but with VAR errors being corrected, both teams should be on 19 points.

Manchester City would be two points worse off in third place, also on 19 points if VAR errors were fixed. They won 1-0 at Old Trafford thanks to Erling Haaland's winner. ProRef released a statement revealing that the goal should have been ruled out due to Enzo Fernandez being in an offside position. Without that goal, the match would've ended in a goalless draw.

Brighton would still be second place on 22 points with a +19 goal difference, but they should be two points closer to league leaders Arsenal.

Arsenal have picked up 27 points from their opening nine matches, winning nine and losing one. They wouldn't have beaten Liverpool without VAR errors, though.

Liverpool felt aggrieved that they weren't given a penalty when Alexander Isak's shirt was tugged by Jurrien Timber in the box, preventing him from having a header at goal. If VAR had made the right decision, Liverpool would've been given a spot-kick, and it is assumed they would have scored and that the spoils would have been shared.

Despite that, Mikel Arteta's side would still be well clear at the top of the table without VAR errors.
