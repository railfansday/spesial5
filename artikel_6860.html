4 Things to Fix Before Selling Your Home, According to Real Estate Experts

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

No, you don’t need to redo your kitchen—but a coat of paint won’t hurt.

Thinking of selling your home? You may be wondering whether to tackle those cosmetic upgrades you’ve been dreaming about—or just leave them for the next owner. Leaving clear maintenance issues or glaring holes can alter your home’s value and the price buyers are willing to pay, but deciding what’s worth fixing beyond the usual wear and tear is ultimately up to the homeowner. To help navigate the options, we reached out to experts from leading luxury brokerages across the country as they advise what sellers should prioritize before listing.

Megan Sullivan, a Douglas Elliman broker based in Greenwich, Connecticut says that potential sellers should first take care of any deferred maintenance, including water intrusion, drainage issues, and roof repairs, plus any aging mechanical systems or compromised windows. “Luxury buyers may appreciate original details and patina, but they do not want to inherit uncertainty,” she says.

North Carolina–based Liza Caminiti, who works for Ivester Jackson | Christie’s International Real Estate, agrees that sellers on the high end of the market should first address anything that could create doubt about how the property has been maintained. These include HVAC and plumbing issues. “Deferred maintenance should take priority because sophisticated buyers will investigate them carefully,” she says. “The goal is not necessarily to undertake a major renovation. It is to remove distractions, demonstrate exceptional care and allow buyers to focus on the architecture, lifestyle, and value of the home.”

If the seller would rather not make such investments, Etta McLendon of Aspen Snowmass Sotheby’s International Realty, has a suggestion. “If there is anything a seller is not going to fix, like installing a new roof, we will get trusted contractors to provide a bid,” she explains. “This way, the seller can be armed with knowledge and control the narrative before we even get to the negotiating table.”

The door that squeaks when it closes. The one floorboard that creaks underfoot. The A/C unit that's too loud. All of these seemingly minor inconveniences items fall under what New York City agent Wen J. Shi of Douglas Elliman calls “sensory” issues. “I always tell sellers to fix the these before the typical wear and tear,” she says.

Once the big-ticket items and deferred maintenance are handled, buyers that go the extra mile with small improvements can come out ahead. For instance, a quick paint job does wonders. “It’s really incredible what a fresh coat of paint can do to a home,” says says Katie Schaller, a Greenwich-based agent with Coldwell Banker Realty. Adds Washington, D.C.–based Lyndsi Armenio, of Douglas Elliman, “I’d rather see a seller spend money on fresh paint, great lighting, landscaping, beautiful hardware, and taking care of the little maintenance items that scream ‘to-do list.’ You don't necessarily need to gut the kitchen or renovate every bathroom before you sell.”

New York City–based Compass agent Nicole Hay agrees: “Fresh paint, updated hardware, clean windows and professional staging often deliver a better return than a $50K remodel,” she says.

Many brokers live by the maxim that you never get a second chance to make a first impression. “I always remind my sellers of their front entryway,” says Schaller. “Buyers tend to stand there waiting for the door to be opened. Make sure the cobwebs have been cleaned, the door is freshly painted and the area feels welcoming. First impressions really set the tone for the rest of the tour.”

Armenio zeroes in on the underlying logic: “Never underestimate the power of the front door,” she says. “That first five minutes of a showing really matters, especially at the luxury level.” Adds Helene Barre, of Sotheby’s International Realty in Greenwich, “In terms of curb appeal, sellers should clean up, spruce up, and dress up—a necessary ingredient to get buyers interested enough to walk through the front door.”
