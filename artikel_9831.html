Crypto vs Stocks, Minute by Minute: What 4-5 November Shows About BTC and 24/7 Equity Perps

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

"Bitcoin trades like a tech stock" is usually argued from daily closes. Until recently that was the only honest way to do it: the stock market closed at 16:00 New York time and crypto didn't. Binance now lists USDⓈ-M perpetuals on gold, silver, crude, copper and a dozen US stocks next to BTC and ETH, all trading 24/7 on the same venue. That makes a minute-by-minute comparison possible, in every US session, including weekends.

The first week of November gave us a good starting point:

So on 4 November crypto went one way while stocks went nowhere, and on 5 November they fell together but by very different amounts. This article uses those two days as the case study, then measures what holds over 21 April – 7 November 2026: 139 trading days and 21 perps:

Code and data: tick-microstructure-research (tmr crossasset fetch / analyze). Everything comes from public Binance data.

FollowSM's feed now covers 18 Binance TradFi perps alongside crypto, with VPIN, ±1% book imbalance and per-event taker sweeps:

In this article "the stock basket" means an equal-weight basket of the 12 US-listed single stocks (all of the above except QQQ, BABA and the four commodities). Its log return over any interval is the mean of its members' log returns. The QQQ perp serves as a cross-check: on 5-minute returns in regular hours it correlates 0.94 with the basket.

Every minute is labelled with the session of the underlying US market, using the NYSE calendar from Part 3: regular hours 09:30–16:00 ET, pre-market from 04:00, post-market to 20:00, overnight 20:00–04:00 into a trading day, and weekend/holiday for everything else. The perps keep the stock market's rhythm: the basket trades 54% of its volume in regular hours and 4% on weekends. BTC trades 34% and 16%.

Fig 1. Percentage moves since the 3 November US close. Blue bands are US regular hours.

Tuesday evening (3 Nov). On the evening of US midterm election day, between 19:00 and 22:00 ET, after the cash close, BTC fell 1.8%, the QQQ perp 2.2% and the stock basket 3.1%. Minute by minute, BTC and the SPY and QQQ perps all hit their lows within two minutes of each other, at 22:08–22:09 ET. By 04:00 ET most of it was gone. We don't attribute moves to news here; the point is that crypto and the stock perps moved together while no US stock exchange was open.

Wednesday (4 Nov). Crypto and stocks split.

σ = the day's log return over the standard deviation of that perp's previous 60 US close-to-close returns.

The perps are the stock market here. Against the official closes (Yahoo daily bars), every single-stock and ETF perp in the table moved within a third of a percentage point of the listed share on each day from 4 to 6 November:

Why other reports show different crypto numbers. Many market summaries quote BTC +3.9% and −5.5% (ETH +6.6% and −7.7%) for these two days. Those are UTC calendar days, and we get the same figures from the perps on that basis. We use US close-to-close instead, so crypto and stocks are measured over the same 24 hours.

On 4 November BTC tracked the megacaps, not the chip stocks. Measured against the basket, the day was ordinary: its BTC-unexplained residual was −1.4%, larger than 62% of days. The basket's flat close came from two halves cancelling each other.

Thursday (5 Nov). The selloff came in two legs. At 07:00 ET, BTC, ETH, the QQQ perp and MU all dropped in the same 1-minute bar. Then the stocks broke at the open. The VIX closed at 28.18, up from 16.61 the day before.

Fig 2. Basis points since 09:29 ET on 5 November. The black line is the 09:30 open.

For six minutes the stock perps fell and BTC barely moved. Then BTC dropped 39 bps in a single minute on $145M of perp volume, nine times the minute before. It kept falling for the rest of the hour. On the chart it looks like a textbook lead-lag: stocks moved first and crypto caught up.

The stock side of that was the real market, not a perp running ahead of it. On Yahoo's 1-minute bars the QQQ ETF itself stood at −73 and −104 bps at 09:36 and 09:37, and the perp stayed within about 3 bps of the ETF at 09:36, 09:37, 10:00 and 10:30.

One day is an anecdote, so we turned it into a test. On every other US open in the sample (138 days, with 5 November excluded because it generated the hypothesis), we compared the basket's first five minutes with BTC's next fifteen. As a control, we ran the same measurement at 12:00 ET.

Nothing. On the days with the biggest opening moves, BTC continued in the stocks' direction only 36% of the time. 5 November was one day, not a rule.

Fig 3. Correlation of 5-minute returns with BTC, with 95% confidence intervals from a bootstrap over whole days.

ETH's numbers are within ±0.03 of BTC's everywhere. Three observations:

The post-market dip (0.28) is probably single-stock news, such as earnings, moving individual stocks on its own after the close.

We measured cross-correlation of 1-minute returns at lags from −5 to +5 minutes. A positive lag means BTC moved first.

Fig 4. corr(BTC 1-minute return, stock basket 1-minute return k minutes later). The lag-0 bar is off scale.

In US hours, nobody leads by a minute. The off-lag correlations are 0.04 or less, against about 0.4 within the same minute. The regular-hours asymmetry is +0.019 in the first half of the sample and −0.008 in the second, so we don't claim it. Anything faster than a minute is beyond what 1-minute bars can resolve. Part 5 showed how much can happen inside one.

On weekends the stock perps follow BTC. The weekend asymmetry holds in both halves:

The same shows up for NVDA (+0.034 [+0.014, +0.054]) and even for gold (+0.031 [+0.005, +0.056]). It also shows up in large moves. When BTC made a 5-minute move beyond 4 trailing standard deviations on a weekend (130 times), the basket moved 8.3 bps with it in the same five minutes and another +3.2 bps [+0.7, +5.8] over the next fifteen. A random-time placebo stays within ±1.1 bps.

A big move in either market shows up in the other within the same five minutes, and doesn't continue. Whatever the "gap" between crypto and stocks is, during the week it is not a delay you can wait for.

The daily figures use US close-to-close returns, with the perp's last minute before 16:00 ET as the close.

Fig 5. Rolling 20-trading-day correlation of US close-to-close returns.

The relationship is real, positive in every session and clearly non-zero. But it is loose and it moves. BTC accounts for about a quarter of the basket's daily variance. The 20-day correlation fell to about zero in late August and sits at its high now; one outlier day like 5 November can move a 20-day correlation a long way on its own. On 5 November, a BTC hedge sized by the regression would have offset about 2 of the basket's 8 points (log returns).
