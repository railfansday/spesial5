How much did home internet cost in 2001 vs 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

You're actually getting better value now than you did in 2001.

Everything's getting more expensive. Well, almost everything. From personal computers to video game consoles, prices for most gadgets have increased massively, in part due to the ongoing RAM crisis. Home internet costs, however, haven't changed much despite witnessing big improvements in performance and bandwidth.

Fiber-optic cables, advancements in routers and new standards like Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 7 continue to boost real-world internet speeds. Latency has also improved meaningfully, with split-second commands transmitted far more quickly and reliably today than two decades ago.

Comparing price per megabit, costs have actually plummeted since the early 2000s. You might pay a similar amount, but performance is significantly better than two decades ago. ISPs also generally offer several plans at varying price points, letting you scale up or cut down based on your needs. Let's take a trip down memory lane and see how much internet prices have changed since the early 2000s.

About half of America's households had access to the internet in 2001, according to the U.S. Census Bureau. At the time, consumers could get unlimited dial-up internet for as low as $21.95 a month from AT&T WorldNet, with EarthLink also charging users $21.95 as of June 2001. Other providers charged a bit more, although not egregiously: AOL (America Online) started the year at the same $21.95, but raised its fee to $23.90 per month in May 2001.

Unsurprisingly, today's internet networks blow older infrastructure out of the water. If we account for inflation, though, they aren't more expensive. Instead, users actually pay less. For instance, that $51.67 spent on DSL back in 2001 has the buying power of about $100 today, which is more than enough for an internet subscription. BroadbandNow, for example, found that the average monthly cost of American home internet in 2026 is $81.16, with many states well below that.

We're also getting more bang for our buck compared to 20 years ago. HighSpeedInternet's Cost of Internet by State in 2026 report found that the median download speed in the US was 116.34 Mbps. That's a massive improvement over DSL and early broadband without a commensurate increase in cost, and the availability of gigabit fiber internet in some regions widens that gap even further.

Ziply Fiber, for example, starts at 2 Gbps and offers up to 50 Gbps in some coverage areas, although the latter will set you back $900 a month plus a one-time $600 installation. While most people won't need those speeds, it shows just how far internet connectivity has come since the early 2000s.
