How to get warnings about speed cameras in Apple CarPlay

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

CarPlay is just the messenger, while your navigation app does all the hard work.

With all the rage and controversy over Flock cameras, speed cameras (or speed traps) have taken a back seat. Most of us have to deal with speed cameras (though they're banned in some states and limited in others), and Apple CarPlay is one way to handle them. While CarPlay itself doesn't send out speed camera warnings, alerts can be turned on for whichever navigation app you use. It stands to reason that Apple CarPlay users default to Apple Maps, but there are other, equally useful alternatives like Waze and Google Maps. There's always a caveat, however. Regardless of the nav app you use, mobile speed traps, by definition, move around a lot. Unless someone reports the new position on a given app, you won't get a warning.

Mobile speed detectors (in some form or another) have been around since the first speeding arrest in the US in 1899. A NYC taxicab driver named Jacob German was caught going 12 mph in an Electrobat (nearly the speed of light back then) in an 8 mph zone. The mobile units in those days were police officers on bicycles. Fortunately, modern technology provides drivers with information as it changes, but there's no such thing as a navigation app that knows where every camera is at all times.

In Apple Maps, there is no camera switch to toggle on or off. Camera alerts are automatic in supported regions. Waze, a compatible CarPlay navigation app, turns camera alerts on by default. You have the option to fine-tune them as well. In the Waze app, open Settings > Alerts and Reports > Reports > Cameras. Each camera type has its own toggles for "Show on map" and "Alert while driving." Make sure any changes made in the Waze app take place before connecting to CarPlay, because Waze only applies them the next time you connect and go for a drive.

Google Maps is similar yet has slightly different settings. On CarPlay, tap "Report" to pinpoint a mobile speed camera so other drivers can see when using the app. If you want information provided by other drivers, open Google Maps in CarPlay and tap Settings > Alert options > All alerts.

Some navigation apps will send warning alerts without actively navigating, as long as the app is running passively in the background. Radarbot sends alerts for fixed and red light cameras through CarPlay if you have a Gold membership. Roadar adds Flock cameras to the mix, along with other license plate readers mixed in, with speed and red light camera alerts. If you're more concerned about Flock cameras, FlockHopper is free and works with CarPlay as well.

It can, though it depends on what navigation app you're using. With Apple Maps, the Speed Limit toggle is in Settings > Apps > Maps > Driving. The posted limit will appear on screen while you navigate. With the Waze app, make sure you're disconnected from CarPlay, open Waze and tap Settings > Speedometer > Show Speedometer > Set Preferences > Show Speed Limit. Google Maps is similar to Waze, with the speed limit located in the speedometer settings. Open the Google Maps app, tap your profile picture and tap Settings > Navigation > Driving > Speedometer.

Stock CarPlay doesn't include a speedometer, but apps like Waze and Google Maps will display current speeds through CarPlay if you want. Both Google Maps and Waze will warn you if your speeds climb too high, and Waze will give you a heads-up if speed limits are about to drop sharply. In response to car manufacturers ditching CarPlay, Apple introduced CarPlay Ultra, which displays your current speed on the instrument cluster, unlike regular CarPlay. CarPlay Ultra is fully integrated, so it's not an informational speed display, as Google Maps makes clear about its own speedometer.
