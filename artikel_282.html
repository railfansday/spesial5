Data center emissions are worse than we knew, Harvard study confirms

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

This past election, Americans across the country rejected data centers, voting to restrict new projects in their communities. As voters push back against data centers, a new study out of Harvard reveals startling revelations about the severity of the pollution they create.

Pollution from data centers is three times higher than previously estimated, according to a new study from the Harvard Data Science Initiative.

The study examined 403 "hyperscale" data centers and found that emissions from these facilities were underestimated. In fact, the lead author Gianluca Guidi believes that their research only provides a "floor estimate." When factoring in the thousands of data centers across the country, many of which were not included in the study, the energy use is likely much higher.

The study found that the largest data centers in the U.S. use as much energy in a single year as the state of Michigan does. Carbon emissions from these data centers surpassed those of entire countries.

Researchers found that 2 percent of all electricity use in the U.S. during the study period came from data centers. A single data center included in the study consumed as much energy as 30,000 homes on average. Certain U.S. states are worse off than others in terms of climate pollution from data centers, according to Harvard researchers. For example, Virginia has the most data centers, and fossil fuel-burning power plants are most common there. Other states, such as Oregon, have green-friendly, eclectic grids, so data centers residing there are likely emitting less pollution.

Data centers have become a controversial topic across the U.S. Thanks to the AI boom, the tech industry is looking to build more data centers in the U.S. to provide compute for AI models and research. However, data centers are proving unpopular in many communities. Some state legislatures have taken action to restrict data center buildouts and stiffen regulations around the industry. As previously mentioned, 44 ballot measures to restrict data center buildout were passed by voters during the midterm elections.

And the movement against AI data centers appears to only be growing. For example, environmental activist Erin Brockovich launched her own data center tracking initiative earlier this year.

The tech and AI industries are currently struggling with a series of other issues. However, based on what studies are finding, it seems the anti-data-center movement will continue to be a thorn in the side of big tech and AI.
