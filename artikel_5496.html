NAMB opposes VA loan fee increases in Senate bill

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

NAMB launched an advocacy campaign urging mortgage professionals to oppose Section 104 of S. 4744, which would raise VA IRRRL and assumption fees and make them permanent. The group estimates $4 billion in added borrower costs and backs the Major Richard Star Act as an alternative.  AI Summary

The National Association of Mortgage Brokers (NAMB) is urging mortgage professionals to contact their senators and oppose proposed increases to fees for veterans using Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) home loans, arguing the changes would shift the cost of new benefits onto other veterans.

The advocacy campaign, posted on NAMB’s website, comes as the Senate considers S. 4744, its version of the Take Care of America’s Veterans Act (H.R. 9237), which passed the House by voice vote on Veterans Day. The Senate bill is on the calendar and is eligible for debate and a vote.

NAMB supports the legislation’s goal of providing additional benefits to combat-injured veterans but opposes Section 104, which would increase fees associated with VA loans.

“Our biggest issue is the fact that our veterans serving our country took an oath to serve and defend. They didn’t take or raise their hand to receive a bill to pay for benefits for their fallen or disabled brothers and sisters. That is not the obligation of the soldier. That’s the obligation of the taxpayer and the disbursement of those tax dollars on the appropriations,” NAMB President Michael Farrell said in a conversation with HousingWire.

Under the Senate proposal, the funding fee for Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loans (IRRRLs) would increase from 0.5% to 1.42%, while the VA loan assumption fee would double from 0.5% to 1%. The increases would also become permanent, eliminating a 10-year sunset provision.

“For the veteran doing the IRRRL and raising that 1.42% from 0.5% on a $400,000 average loan is taking [the fee] from $2,000 to $5,680. And the the concept of, ‘Well, it doesn’t affect their recoupment’ in regards to justifying doing the loan … it does affect and impact the cost of doing the loan,” Farrell added.

NAMB estimates the changes would add $4 billion in costs for veteran borrowers, or an average of $3,780 per veteran. The association also argues the higher IRRRL fee could conflict with the VA’s 36-month recoupment requirement, potentially making some veterans ineligible for the refinancing program.

“It seems like there’s other solutions that can be looked into to raise the funding to support the benefit. … But to alienate it just to one aspect of the IRRRL, when home affordability is a huge concern and veterans are looking to step down their rate or step down their payment to better their family living expenses, now we’ve tripled the cost of getting that done,” Farrell said.

Instead, NAMB is advocating for passage of the Major Richard Star Act (H.R. 2102/S. 1032), a bipartisan, standalone measure that would allow approximately 54,000 combat-injured military retirees to receive both full retirement pay and VA disability compensation.

The legislation was introduced by Rep. Gus Bilirakis (R-Fla.) in the House and by Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.) in the Senate. On Veterans Day, the House voted 245-175 to discharge a resolution to consider the legislation separately and, on Nov. 12, passed the act.

“We are supportive of every veteran receiving their benefit,” Farrell said. “And if this is going to help 54,000 combat injured military retirees receive both their retirement pay and their disability pay, we stand to support the standalone Major Star Act. We do agree with that, but [regarding] Section 104, we have huge concerns on the feasibility of putting this bill on the backs of fellow veterans.”
