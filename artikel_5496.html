Here’s who’s getting the richest from Anthropic’s $81 billion IPO

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Anthropic’s seven cofounders are each worth about $20 billion on paper after the company’s record-breaking IPO on Thursday, which also made thousands of its employees and some Silicon Valley investors extremely rich.

Dario and Daniela Amodei, as well as the other cofounders—Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, and Chris Olah—have all pledged 80% of their personal Anthropic equity to philanthropic causes. These commitments, though, are voluntary and not legally binding, according to Anthropic’s IPO filing.

The company behind the popular AI chatbot Claude debuted on the Nasdaq Thursday, initially raising $81.3 billion in a public offering that surpassed the previous record of $75 billion SpaceX’s IPO initially brought in over the summer. On Friday, Anthropic announced that underwriters had purchased millions more shares, bringing the offering’s total to about $93.5 billion.

After pricing its shares at $83 apiece on Wednesday, the company opened trading significantly higher and closed out the day at around $97. Despite shares falling to $85 on Friday afternoon amid a broader tech-fueled stock retreat, the company is still worth around $1.1 trillion—surpassing the market cap of Walmart.

The record IPO is great news for the cofounders and insiders who have been supporting Anthropic since it was founded in 2021. The biggest gains, though, belong to Amazon and Google, whose stakes are worth about $316 billion combined—more than twice the roughly $142 billion held by the seven cofounders. Here are some of the people and companies who stand to benefit the most.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and his sister, Anthropic’s president and chair of the board, Daniela Amodei, are among the figures most commonly associated with the company. But there are another five cofounders who are cashing in as well.

Each of its seven cofounders owns between 237 million and 241 million shares after the offering. At the stock’s price of about $85 on Friday, that means each founder has holdings worth approximately $20 billion, putting them (at least on paper) in the same league as Bridgewater Associates founder Ray Dalio, who has a net worth of $21 billion, according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index.

Each founder also sold approximately 3.38 million shares in the IPO, worth about $280 million at the offering price.

Despite owning a minority of the company’s equity, Anthropic’s cofounders control 50.1% of its shareholder voting power. The nearly equal distribution of shares among the seven founders is unusual for a Silicon Valley startup, Fortune reported.

Besides its cofounders, some Silicon Valley investors are also set to win big. Lightspeed Venture Partners, an early backer of Snapchat, led a $3.5 billion funding round in Anthropic in March 2025 when the company was valued at $61.5 billion. The VC firm owns less than 2% of the company, according to Bloomberg, yet its stake is still reportedly worth tens of billions of dollars.

Other VCs like Menlo Ventures also own a stake representing less than 2% of the company, Bloomberg reported. Iconiq has invested more than $6 billion in Anthropic since 2025, and its stake is worth more than $26 billion, the outlet reported. Anthropic in total has more than 400 venture investors, according to PitchBook, yet no VC firm owns more than 5%, Fortune reported.

The company’s historic IPO means even some smaller stakes are now worth billions. Sound Ventures, the venture firm cofounded by Ashton Kutcher, invested about $435 million in Anthropic and owns about 0.5% of the company, Bloomberg reported. It’s unclear, though, how much Kutcher himself benefited from the trade as the actor best known for his role on That ’70s Show stepped down as general partner at the firm earlier this year.

Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt also owns about 1% of Anthropic, according to Bloomberg, putting his stake’s value at about $11 billion at Friday’s share price.

One early investor who won’t be reaping the rewards is FTX founder Sam Bankman-Fried, who is serving a sentence of 25 years in prison after being convicted on fraud and conspiracy charges. Bankman-Fried has appealed his sentence to the Supreme Court. The trading firm he founded, Alameda Research, invested $500 million in Anthropic, but FTX’s bankruptcy estate sold most of its stake in 2024 to repay creditors. Among the buyers was Bankman-Fried’s former employer Jane Street, which stands to reap a multibillion-dollar windfall.

Many of the company’s roughly 5,300 employees are also getting rich thanks to generous stock-based compensation. Private-markets research firm Sacra estimated that 2,800 current and former employees could hold at least $1 million in pretax vested equity in the company at a $1.35 trillion valuation, Reuters reported.

Some of the earliest employees stand to benefit disproportionately. At least 400 early hires could hold equity worth $100 million or more, according to CNBC.

To be sure, it’s hard to estimate which employees hold how much stock because Anthropic’s IPO filing discloses aggregate equity awards but not their distribution among individual employees.

Now that Anthropic is public, non-founder employees are allowed to sell up to 10% of certain holdings, and up to another 35% following the release of Anthropic’s 2026 financial results.

Some of the biggest gains from the company’s IPO will go to two Big Tech giants, Amazon and Google. Anthropic’s IPO filing estimated that Amazon would own 14.6% of the company’s outstanding shares and Google would own 13.4%.

Amazon’s stake stems from billions of dollars in investments the company has made in Anthropic dating back to 2023, around the time it had just 160 employees. At the time, Amazon committed an initial $1.25 billion as part of a deal to ultimately invest $4 billion in Anthropic. In exchange Anthropic agreed to make Amazon Web Services its primary cloud provider for critical workloads and make Claude available to AWS customers.

In total, Amazon invested $8 billion in convertible notes between 2023 and 2025 and put in another $10 billion in preferred stock in 2026.

Google has also poured billions into Anthropic, including $10 billion it invested in a financing round earlier this year and another $10 billion in a private placement alongside the IPO.

At the company’s roughly $85 per share stock price Friday, Amazon’s 1.94 billion shares would be worth about $165 billion, while Google’s 1.77 billion shares would be worth around $151 billion.

Role: Reporter Marco Quiroz-Gutierrez is a reporter for Fortune covering general business news.
