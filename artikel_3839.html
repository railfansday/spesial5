Chronicles: Medieval devs Raw Power are bankrupt, but their sandbox RPG lives on at a new studio co-founded by Satisfactory devs Coffee Stain

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

It "ticks many of the boxes", despite the funding problems

Goat Simulator and Satisfactory outfit Coffee Stain Group have bought the intellectual property rights and assets for Chronicles: Medieval, the Tom Hardy-garnished strategy RPG that was originally in development at now-bankrupt Raw Power Games. The game will now be made by a new developer, owned 25% by a few former Raw Power devs and 75% by Coffee Stain. Coffee Stain will fund the game's on-going development.

In development since 2022, and slated for Steam early access release in 2027, Chronicles: Medieval is a game about building vast armies of men in metal hats and horrifying doublets, then fighting alongside them in real time. It sees you positioning archers behind spears and finessing your cavalry flanking actions, all the while occupying a fleshy human body that can be hacked and stabbed. It's set during the Hundred Years' War between England and France over the 14th and 15th centuries, and gives you a volatile sandbox world of warring factions and patrols.

The original devs included people who worked on The Witcher 3, Total War, and Crusader Kings III, which may not surprise you from the foregoing descriptions. It's not entirely clear who has made the leap to Coffee Stain's newly founded studio.

Raw Power declared bankruptcy on 30th October, subsequently calling on players to help them find funding. During the bankruptcy proceedings, Coffee Stain successfully bid 36.75 million Danish Krone for Chronicles: Medieval, which comes to around four million pounds or 5.4 million dollars. According to CEO Anton Westbergh the project "ticks many of the boxes that we believe to be fundamental for a great game, and we think it has a good chance of becoming a strong long-term IP for the group." The game yet sits at 26th on SteamDB's most wishlisted, despite Raw Power's funding troubles.

Gaming doesn't want for simulations of shrieking feudal folk, but Medieval has a certain swagger, even when you remove Tom Hardy from the picture (and you should - he only ever voiced that one trailer). Here's hoping the new team can find their feet under Coffee Stain, and that whoever hasn't made the transition finds good work elsewhere.
