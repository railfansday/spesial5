The Rings Of Power Kills Its Version Of Samwise Gamgee

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Warning! Spoilers for Rings of Power season 3, episodes 1-4 ahead!And just like that, The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3 has done away with its version of Samwise Gamgee. Of course, this Hobbit himself won't even be born for several thousand more years, but Rings of Power has found a variety of intriguing ways to create threads of connection between its Second Age characters and those of The Lord of the Rings. This is done through archetypal roles, character dynamics, and parallel storylines, and Rings of Power season 3 has carried this idea further.

In the lead-up to the premiere of The Rings of Power season 3, promotional materials really centralized Galadriel, Arondir, and their orc guide, Marnûkh. Showrunner Patrick McKay noted that the trio is "a little bit like Frodo, Sam and Gollum," and now that the first four episodes of this season have arrived, we can see just what he meant. Galadriel is determined to trust Marnûkh, but Arondir is aggressively suspicious of the orc. Even the circumstances of their journey (departing Rivendell as a fellowship, an escape on Eagles) line up beautifully.

Of course, if Galadriel, Arondir, and Marnûkh are Rings of Power's meaningful reflection of Frodo, Sam, and Gollum, then season 3, episode 4 has killed off a rather key figure. In those very last moments of the episode, Arondir is stabbed and killed by none other than Theo, who has become a Nazgnagôl. Given that overt Sam parallel, this is a pretty intense cliffhanger.

Rings of Power season 3 did something interesting by killing off Arondir, especially considering Sam Gamgee's original role and fate at the end of The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King. When Frodo, Sam, and Gollum ultimately arrived at their destination, Sam turned out to be right about the fact that Gollum couldn't be trusted. The creature betrayed Frodo, but Sam swept in to save his friend in Shelob's lair and then again by the fires of Mount Doom. Then, once the Ring was destroyed, Frodo and Sam rode away on the backs of Eagles.

Things go a bit differently for the trio in Rings of Power season 3. This time, the primary hero (Galadriel) turns out to be right about her seemingly villainous guide (Marnûkh), while her faithful companion (Arondir) has to admit he was wrong. It's the complete inverse, an intentional twist on the original trope, and that should have been the first sign that Rings of Power's version of Frodo and Sam wouldn't both fly safely away on the backs of Eagles this time around.

It's quite the tragic shift. Rings of Power used our expectations, built from a more familiar Lord of the Rings story, against us. All those thousands of years before Frodo and Sam followed Gollum to try to destroy the One Ring, Galadriel and Elrond had followed Marnûkh to prevent the thing from being made in the first place. They ran similar journeys with comparable intentions but reached very different conclusions.

The impact of this concept is only slightly lessened by the fact that Rings of Power already attempted to establish a Frodo-Sam parallel. Back in seasons 1 and 2, we had the Harfoots, Nori (Markella Kavenagh), and Poppy (Megan Richards). These are Hobbit ancestors, and, in Rings of Power's version of the story, they were the first to greet Gandalf upon his arrival in Middle-earth. The dynamic between the two was certainly similar to that of Frodo and Sam, but this parallel was ultimately dropped.

And at the end of Rings of Power season 2, Nori and Poppy left Gandalf to return to their people. We haven't seen them since, which is rather a shame since it seems as if Prime Video's fantasy TV show could have done a whole lot more with this parallel. There have been at least some hints that Nori and Poppy will appear in future episodes of Rings of Power season 3, so we might get more on this front. For now, we can only mourn Arondir, who tried every bit as hard to protect his friends as Sam Gamgee.
