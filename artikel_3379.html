‘The Rings of Power’ Has Been Hiding Its Biggest Reference to the One Ring’s Creation in Plain Sight

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Rings of Power Season 3 Episode 7.

With the release of the final two episodes of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3, Sauron (Charlie Vickers) has finally crafted his One Ring to Rule Them All. This moment has been years in the making, and although the other Rings of Power have already made their way to the screen, the One Ring brings this prequel one step closer to J.R.R. Tolkien's original high-fantasy tale. But while the Prime Video series has only just unveiled the Dark Lord's secret weapon, The Rings of Power has teased the One Ring's creation from the very beginning.

The Rings of Power's opening credits have told the story of Middle-earth, leading to the Second Age, through sound. Set to a song composed by Howard Shore, the intro uses cymatics to mold and transform swirling particles, recounting the events of creation as conducted by Eru Ilúvatar through the Music of the Ainur. This eventually leads, as the Season 1 opening credits depict, to the creation of the nine rings of Men, seven for the Dwarves, and three for the Elves, along with Sauron's master ring itself. Season 2's intro does something similar. Although the credits themselves change each season, with the sand and dust forming different (and timely) constructs with every renovation, the process itself remains the same. In fact, it's in this process of visual sound that Sauron forges his own Ring of Power.

In the penultimate episode of Season 3, Sauron crafts the long-anticipated One Ring, a moment that has been teased since the series first began. As the Dark Lord works in the fires of Mount Doom, he uses Fëanor's hammer — stolen from Celebrimbor (Charles Edwards) — to strike the anvil containing his forging materials (gold and silver from Valinor), creating a massive vibration throughout the volcano. As the shock wave hits the walls of the mountain and falls back, each blast shapes the particles, eventually forming the Ring itself before Sauron imbues it with his dark power. The visual similarities are evident in the way the soot and dirt shift and move, and though it's not as seamless or smooth as the opening credits, it's most certainly by design.

The cast and creators break down the show's most personal storyline yet for Tolkien's most iconic wizard.

If the slipping and swirling sands in the opening credits are meant to echo the work of the angelic Ainur themselves, then Sauron's frantic, angry, and desperate attempts take a clunkier, less polished approach. Indeed, there's a reason why he argues with the ghost of Celebrimbor — be it in his own warped mind or a genuine appreciation, it makes no difference — in his quest to forge the master ring. Unlike the Ainur, he is not so naturally in tune with creation that he can properly sub-create such a simple vessel of power, and this is clear in the Season 3 credits themselves, which are more explosive and expansive regarding the borders of Mordor than they are life-giving and generative, focusing solely on the One Ring at the expense of all else.

While The Rings of Power depicts the One Ring's forging at the end of Season 3, Tolkien originally wrote it occurring at a different point in time. As detailed in The Silmarillion, Sauron first deceived the Elves into forging rings for Men and Dwarves under the guise of Annatar, Lord of Gifts. He aided Celebrimbor in the making of these initial 16 rings, but left Eregion to forge the One Ring in secret, leaving the smith to craft the three Elven rings on his own. These rings, on the Elves' part, were, per Tolkien's description in correspondence collected in The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien, made to prevent the "decay" of Middle-earth. Since Sauron had no hand in making them, they were left unmarred, but the moment Sauron put on his master ring, the Elves became keenly aware of the deception and removed their rings. We see this occur in The Rings of Power Season 3's penultimate episode as well.

Unlike the Prime Video series, the exact details of Sauron's forging are left unknown. However, it's quite doubtful he needed to ask a Balrog to master the secrets that he, having studied under Aulë, Smith of the Valar, already knew and imparted to others in the forging of the other rings. While the One Ring was meant to rule the others, it was certainly within his own ability to craft. Sauron also imbued it with his own fëa (the Quenya word for "soul") and thus linked himself to it directly, which is why, when the ring is eventually destroyed in The Return of the King, Sauron's body is permanently vanquished, and his spirit becomes a shadow over Middle-earth, one effortlessly blown away (no, he's not a giant flaming eye). We may be a long way off from that, but The Rings of Power has set the stage.

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 is available for streaming on Prime Video, with Season 4 premiering on a date yet to be announced.
