Canva, Block and Meta layoffs in 2026 came with different severance terms

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Tech layoff severance packages from Canva, Block and Meta show how far some employers go beyond base pay, from equity and laptops to extended healthcare

Sixteen weeks of base pay is a common reference point for severance at major tech companies. Outplacement figures from Challenger, Gray & Christmas and tracking by Simple Severance both put typical tech severance pay at around that level. Several layoffs in 2026 produced terms that differed from that baseline in the amount of pay, in what came with the pay, or in both.

A severance offer is rarely a single number. The packages in this list combine several parts: base pay, extra weeks tied to tenure, continued healthcare coverage, treatment of unvested equity, cash stipends or grants, company equipment and career support. Each employer assembled those parts differently.

Layoffs at Canva, Block $SQ and Meta $META in 2026 show that range. One company let departing staff keep their company-issued laptops. Another extended healthcare to employees' families. Block cut jobs twice in the same year, and the terms of its second round differed from the first.

Comparing tech layoff severance packages means looking past the headline count of weeks. Tenure formulas raise the total for long-serving staff. One company in this list adds two weeks for each continuous year of service, while Block adds one. Healthcare terms vary in length and in who is covered. Equity rules can decide whether a recent hire keeps shares or forfeits them. Some packages also include money aimed at what comes next, such as a transition stipend or a grant to start a venture.

The five entries that follow are a selection, not a ranking. Four describe individual layoff rounds: Canva in November, Block in February and November, and Meta in May. The fifth looks at the 16-week baseline and how the reported terms sit beside it. Each entry sets out the terms as reported. Where reporting on a round left out a detail, such as a headcount, the entry says so.

Reporting on these rounds does not say whether the base pay was delivered as a lump sum or as continued salary. The entries describe pay in weeks or months only, without assuming either method.

Canva promised every employee affected by its November 2026 layoffs at least six months of base pay, wherever in the world they worked. The cuts reached 22% of the company's workforce, which HR Brew puts at 1,271 employees.

The six-month figure is a floor, not a fixed amount. Each departing worker was promised no less than that much base pay.

Canva also changed how it treated equity for its newest employees. Vesting is the process by which granted stock becomes the employee's to keep. Many tech stock grants vest on a schedule that starts with a cliff. Under a one-year cliff, an employee earns no shares at all until the first anniversary of their start date. After that date, the remaining shares usually vest in smaller portions over the rest of the schedule. Someone laid off before reaching the cliff would normally walk away with nothing from the grant.

Canva removed that one-year vesting cliff for affected employees. Recent hires who had not yet reached their first anniversary keep their shares instead of forfeiting them.

The package extended to hardware. Departing employees could keep their company-issued MacBooks rather than return them.

Canva also put money toward what departing staff do next. It offered $10,000 grants to employees starting either a business or a community venture.

Healthcare was part of the offer. Canva provided one year of coverage "where applicable," a qualifier the reporting does not define further.

Job-search support rounded out the package. Affected workers received 12 months of LinkedIn Premium, along with career coaching and help with their résumés. LinkedIn Premium is the paid subscription tier of the professional networking site. It adds tools such as direct messages to members outside a user's network.

Canva announced other changes alongside the cuts. The company said Sydney would become the main base for more of its product teams. It also said some employees would be asked to move to centralized hubs.

Block $SQ's February 2026 layoffs came with 20 weeks of salary for departing employees, plus one additional week for every year of tenure. The package also included six months of healthcare coverage and a $5,000 transition stipend.

The salary portion has two parts. The 20 weeks act as a floor that applied to departing staff whatever their length of service.

The tenure component sits on top of that floor. Each year at Block added one more week of salary. A longer-serving employee therefore left with more weeks of pay than a recent hire.

Healthcare coverage ran for six months as part of the package. Reporting on the round does not say whether that coverage extended to employees' families. It also does not name the program or mechanism used to provide it.

The $5,000 transition stipend is a separate element. It was reported as a single cash amount, not as a figure calculated from salary or tenure.

Reporting on this round covers three elements: pay, healthcare and the stipend. It does not describe how unvested equity was treated. It also does not say whether staff kept company equipment or received career coaching or similar support. That limits any comparison of this severance package with others to the three elements that were reported.

Reporting on this round gives no headcount and no share of Block's workforce.

Block $SQ's November 2026 layoff package started at seven weeks of base pay. Affected employees also received one more week of pay for each year they had worked at Block.

The package had a fixed starting amount and a variable one. Every departing employee began from the same seven weeks. The tenure weeks then added to that figure. A worker with longer service at Block received more than a recent hire.

Block also gave departing staff a 30-day pay-without-working period. During that window, employees remained on pay without being expected to do their jobs. With the 30 days added, the November package had three separate pay elements: the seven-week base, the tenure weeks and the 30-day period.

Reporting on the round does not give a combined total that reconciles with these parts. This entry lists each element separately for that reason.

Healthcare for the November round ran through COBRA. COBRA is a U.S. federal law that allows eligible workers to keep their employer's group health coverage for a limited time after leaving a job. The reported terms name COBRA but do not say how long coverage lasted under Block's package. They also do not say whether Block paid any part of the premiums. That gap matters for departing staff. COBRA premiums are generally paid by the former employee unless an employer agrees to cover part of them.

The November terms differ from those of Block's February 2026 round. February's package started at 20 weeks of salary. November's started at seven weeks of base pay. Both rounds added one week for each year of service. With a smaller starting amount, the tenure weeks made up a larger part of a long-serving employee's package in November than in February.

The reported November terms do not mention a transition stipend.

No headcount or share of the workforce has been reported for Block's November cuts.

Meta $META's May 2026 layoff round affected roughly 10% of its workforce. Departing employees received 16 weeks of base pay, plus two additional weeks for each continuous year of service.

Reporting on the round gives the cut only as a share of staff. It does not say how many people lost their jobs.

The 16-week base equals four months of pay before any tenure is counted. Every employee in the round started from that same floor. The tenure weeks then build on it for each continuous year an employee spent at the company.

The formula ties those extra weeks to continuous service. That makes the length of an unbroken stretch at Meta the deciding factor in the final payment. A longer uninterrupted run brings more weeks of pay. A shorter one brings fewer. Reporting on the round does not explain how Meta treated a break in service.

Healthcare continuation came alongside the pay. Meta continued health coverage for 18 months. That coverage reached employees' families as well as the employees themselves.

HR Brew reports that the 18-month term tripled the healthcare coverage Meta offered in previous layoff cycles. The publication does not give the length of that earlier coverage.

The 18-month period runs longer than the stated healthcare terms in two other 2026 packages. Canva offered one year of coverage where applicable. Block $SQ's February round included six months. Block's November round named COBRA but gave no duration. Its length cannot be set beside Meta's.

Severance at major tech companies has typically landed around a 16-week baseline, according to outplacement figures from Challenger, Gray & Christmas and tracking by Simple Severance. HR Brew draws on both sources to set recent packages against that common reference point. It does not name a specific report or date from either source.

A baseline describes what is typical, not what is required. Each employer still sets its own formula for base pay, tenure credit and benefits. That makes the 16-week mark a yardstick for reading individual offers.

Against that yardstick, HR Brew treats three parts of Canva's November 2026 package as outliers. The first is the floor of at least six months of base pay for every affected employee worldwide. The others are the decision to let staff keep their company-issued MacBooks and the $10,000 grants for startup or community ventures.

Block $SQ's February 2026 round started above the benchmark. It paid 20 weeks of salary before any tenure credit. It then added one week for each year an employee had worked at the company.

Meta $META's May 2026 round started at the benchmark itself. Its formula paid 16 weeks of base pay. It then added two weeks for each continuous year of service. That formula lifts the total above the 16-week mark for anyone with at least one continuous year.

Block's November 2026 round set its base pay starting point below the benchmark. That package opened with seven weeks of base pay plus one week per year of tenure. It also included a separate 30-day pay-without-working period.

Weeks of pay are only one part of a layoff severance package. Comparing offers on that measure alone leaves out other terms. Healthcare terms vary by company. Meta continued coverage for 18 months for employees and their families. Block's February round gave six months of healthcare, and its November round named COBRA without a stated duration. Canva offered one year of coverage "where applicable." Block's February round also added a $5,000 transition stipend.
