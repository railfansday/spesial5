From store outlets to methane leaks: AI’s hunt for power

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

AI factories are outgrowing traditional data centers as models grow and distributed inference moves from concept to deployment. The infrastructure behind those models now has to span racks, sites and even scattered pockets of power.

Dell Technologies Inc. is betting that the answer lies in building complete systems rather than components, from rack-scale management to fully liquid-cooled designs. The size of modern AI clusters dwarfs anything high-performance computing has handled before, according to Ihab Tarazi (pictured), senior vice president and chief technology officer of AI, compute and networking at Dell.

“The difference with AI is the scale is massive: tens of thousands, hundreds of thousands of GPUs that are all on a single fabric. It may be multiple clusters, but they end up being a single fabric at the end of the day,” he said.

Tarazi spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and host Rebecca Knight at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed Dell’s 100% liquid-cooled networking system, distributed inference and turning stranded energy into AI capacity. (* Disclosure below.)

Dell’s latest move takes liquid cooling beyond the graphics processing unit. The company is shipping what it calls the first 100% liquid-cooled networking system, with switches, optics and cabling pretested in its factory and delivered as a single rack, Tarazi explained.

“The fact that it’s pretested as a single-rack system means [that] instead of having to assemble hundreds of pieces on-site and take maybe two weeks or three weeks to find the bugs, it all comes in, you install it [and] we turn it up in hours,” he said.

The networking launch follows Dell’s debut of liquid-cooled storage for AI racks. Compute, networking and storage can now operate within a common cooling and power architecture.

“Instead of building two data centers — one for GPU, one [for] network — being able to do one is a massive reduction in time and cost for us,” he said.

Software is the next lever for distributed inference. Customers want a single control plane that can work across different GPUs and CPUs and link as many as 1,000 small sites, each with some spare power, Tarazi noted. That approach turns stranded energy into AI capacity for companies that can’t quickly build a massive data center.

“You’ll be surprised what power availability means,” he said. “It doesn’t mean just that you have power in your store. It could also mean methane coming out — every source of power possible. People are going backwards to say, ‘How do I leverage that power for compute and enable inferencing in a distributed manner?'”
