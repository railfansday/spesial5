IRS raises 401(k) limit for 2027 to $25,500

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The IRS increased the 2027 401(k) deferral limit to $25,500 and raised catch-up limits to $8,500, or $11,750 for ages 60 to 63. IRA limits remain at $7,500, while multiple IRA and Saver’s Credit income thresholds increased.  AI Summary

The IRS has increased the annual contribution limit for 401(k) plans in 2027 to $25,500, up $1,000 from the 2026 limit of $24,500, the agency announced on Thursday.

The higher limit also applies to employees participating in 403(b) plans, governmental 457 plans and the federal government’s Thrift Savings Plan, according to guidance issued by the IRS in Notice 2026-66. The notice outlines cost-of-living adjustments for retirement plans and other retirement-related tax provisions for the 2027 tax year.

The catch-up contribution limit for employees ages 50 and older increased to $8,500, up from $8,000 in 2026. That brings the total annual contribution limit for most eligible participants ages 50 and older to $34,000.

Employees ages 60 through 63 are eligible for a higher catch-up contribution limit under the SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022. For 2027, that limit increased to $11,750, allowing eligible participants to contribute up to $37,250.

The annual contribution limit for individual retirement accounts remains unchanged at $7,500. The IRA catch-up contribution limit for individuals ages 50 and older also remains at $1,100.

“Along with the Working Families Tax Cuts, this increase in the pension limitations will help American families keep more of their hard-earned income,” IRS CEO Frank J. Bisignano said in a statement.

The IRS also increased several income thresholds used to determine eligibility for tax deductions and retirement account contributions.

For traditional IRAs, the income phase-out range for single taxpayers covered by a workplace retirement plan increased to $83,000 to $93,000, up from $81,000 to $91,000 in 2026.

For married couples filing jointly, the range increased to $133,000 to $153,000, up from $129,000 to $149,000, when the spouse making the contribution is covered by a workplace retirement plan.

For IRA contributors who are not covered by a workplace retirement plan but are married to someone who is, the phase-out range increased to $250,000 to $260,000, up from $242,000 to $252,000.

The income phase-out range for Roth IRA contributions also increased. For single taxpayers and heads of household, the range is now $158,000 to $173,000, compared with $153,000 to $168,000 in 2026. For married couples filing jointly, it increased to $250,000 to $260,000, up from $242,000 to $252,000.

The IRS also raised the annual contribution limit for SIMPLE retirement accounts to $17,500, up from $17,000. Certain eligible SIMPLE plans have a higher limit of $19,100, compared with $18,100 in 2026.

Catch-up contribution limits for most SIMPLE plans remain unchanged at $4,000 for employees ages 50 and older. The limit is $3,850 for certain applicable SIMPLE plans and $5,250 for employees ages 60 through 63.

The income limits for the Saver’s Credit, which benefits low- and moderate-income workers who save for retirement, also increased. For 2027, the limits are $83,000 for married couples filing jointly, $62,250 for heads of household, and $41,500 for single taxpayers and married individuals filing separately.

The IRS said the adjustments are intended to account for cost-of-living changes and help taxpayers and employers plan for the coming year.
