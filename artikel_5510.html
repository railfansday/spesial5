U.S. charges Canadian drug importer CanaRx's treasurer in yearslong smuggling scheme

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Company president Bob Howard is wanted in the federal case, and CANARX's general counsel says both Howards deny the charges

John Thomas Howard, treasurer and a director of Canadian prescription drug company CanaRx, faces smuggling and conspiracy charges after his arrest on Nov. 8, according to CNBC.

The arrest warrant adds two further counts: putting misbranded medications into interstate commerce and importing drugs unlawfully, according to CNBC. No plea is on file.

His brother, Bob Howard, is CanaRx's president and a director. He is sought as part of the federal probe and remains at large, according to CNBC.

FDA special agent Dustin Dobbs wrote in an affidavit that "there is probable cause to believe that (CanaRx) illegally imported or caused the illegal importation of prescription drugs into the United States," according to CNBC. The affidavit does not name the medications the agency believes were imported illegally. The criminal complaint covers Feb. 22, 2017, through April 15, 2026.

CanaRx general counsel Joseph Morris responded in a statement. "The Howards deny all the charges against them, will defend vigorously, and expect to be vindicated in court," Morris told CNBC.

CanaRx is based in Windsor, Ontario. On its website, the company says it sources medications from Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand, according to CNBC.

The FDA has scrutinized the company for several years, according to CNBC. The agency sent CanaRx a warning letter in 2019 saying the company was replacing medicines that U.S. clinicians had prescribed and the FDA had approved with unapproved ones. Morris countered that CanaRx was not deceiving patients, though the company consented to end imports of medications requiring administration by a physician. In 2023, the FDA confiscated a CanaRx shipment of medication that, according to the agency, might have been mislabeled and fake, based on an email a patient received, which CNBC obtained.

CanaRx is one of a group of businesses known as alternative funding programs, which offer high-cost specialty drugs to employers at lower prices. These programs frequently buy drugs abroad and deliver them straight to patients, which federal authorities have called unlawful and a safety hazard, according to CNBC. The FDA permits Americans to bring in drugs for their own use under certain circumstances, provided no domestic supply exists.

Gilead $GILD Sciences has a pending civil lawsuit that names CanaRx among several firms it alleges unlawfully brought Biktarvy, its HIV drug, into the country, according to CNBC. CanaRx has not responded to that suit. A Gilead spokesperson told CNBC on Wednesday that the company welcomes "the government's efforts to hold accountable those whose conduct may put patients at risk."
