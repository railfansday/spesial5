KORA builds AI models and agents into a new Linux OS bound for Qualcomm Snapdragon PCs

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Desktop software company KORA Systems Inc. today unveiled KORA, a Linux-based operating system that builds artificial intelligence models and agents into the system itself.

Qualcomm Technologies Inc. has spent close to a year working with the company to bring it to Snapdragon-powered PCs.

The launch goes after what KORA sees as a ceiling on AI in current desktop computers. Most AI software on macOS and Windows runs inside a browser tab or an app sandbox, according to the company, with little view of the computer underneath.

Because both platforms are closed, KORA argues, the integration stops at that point. Linux, by KORA’s account, is the only operating system open enough to be rebuilt around models and agents from the kernel up.

The core of that rebuild is an inference engine KORA calls Cortex. It pools the central processor, graphics processor and neural processing unit in a PC, plus any other machines on the network, and treats them as one computer. Models run locally on the user’s hardware by default, and frontier models get pulled in for heavier jobs.

On screen, the usual layout of folders and apps is gone. What KORA calls neural workspaces take its place, each one arranged around the task a user is working on. The user sets the permissions for any applications and agents sandboxed inside a workspace. Under all of it sits a neural file system, and the company says memory and context stay on the local machine.

Outside developers can build on the same pieces KORA’s own applications use through its software development kit, which opens up primitives such as memory, semantic search and email. Any model, AI service or agent will be able to integrate at the system level, the company said. An agentic service could even run as part of the operating system and take its identity, memory and compute from KORA.

Chief Executive Kirt McMaster called the desktop operating system “the ultimate harness.” About 45 million people account for 80% of spending on AI, he said, and almost all of their work happens on desktops running operating systems built 40 years ago. By 2033 the group will top 150 million people spending more than $400 billion a year, according to KORA’s own estimates. “Nobody has built an operating system for them,” McMaster said.

KORA grew out of Playtron Corp., a company McMaster founded to build a Linux alternative to Windows for gaming PCs. Playtron’s engineers wrote GameOS as a complete Linux operating system and shipped it on gaming devices, and the same team has been building KORA on that foundation since November 2025. GameOS carries on inside the new system as GameFrame, a one-click play mode that turns the desktop into a console for PC games.

With Cyanogen, Android and GameOS behind them, the founders count KORA as their fourth operating system. Chief Business Officer John Lagerling led Android business development at Google LLC, and he said he joined when the platform had 100,000 users and stayed until it had a billion. Openness got it there, in his view, and AI needs the same treatment.

The company believes “50 superpowered engineers can take on Windows,” according to Chief Technology Officer Franck De Girolami. His engineers now work side by side with AI models and agents, he said, to the point that “KORA is built inside KORA.”

The Qualcomm work is laid out in a separate joint release today. The result so far is a complete Linux desktop running on PCs with Snapdragon X2 Elite chips, the two companies said, and Cortex now spreads AI workloads across the whole chip. Qualcomm engineers worked on the project throughout, and the chipmaker is also pushing X2 Elite support into the mainline Linux kernel.

Those machines can run a 26 billion-parameter language model locally, according to the release. Kedar Kondap, Qualcomm’s senior vice president and general manager of compute and gaming, said the X2 Elite was designed to run advanced AI on the device. KORA has also built translation technology that lets selected Windows x86-64 applications run inside the Linux environment, so people switching over can keep using software they rely on.

The first machines to ship will run HUMAIN OS, an enterprise operating system KORA is co-developing with Saudi Arabian AI company HUMAIN. Those PCs are due in 2027, and the companies bill them as the first from hardware makers to pair a Linux-based desktop operating system with Snapdragon X2 Elite. KORA itself follows later in 2027, with pricing to be shared closer to availability.

KORA is a venture capital-backed startup that counts Samsung Next, Square Enix Holdings Co. Ltd., Polychain Capital LP, Van Eck Associates Corp., Circle Internet Group Inc. and Alumni Ventures among its investors. The release did not say how much the company has raised.
