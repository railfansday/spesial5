AI giants face a new lawsuit to freeze frontier model development

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The plaintiffs invoked a California law to challenge practices they allege threaten public health, safety and property. They say a freeze is needed to protect public safety

Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and The Alliance for Secure AI filed a lawsuit on Thursday seeking to freeze development of advanced AI models at Anthropic, Google $GOOGL, Meta $META, OpenAI and SpaceXSI. The suit, filed in San Francisco Superior Court, alleges that executives at the companies have warned that advanced AI systems pose imminent and catastrophic risks to humanity.

The plaintiffs invoke California's Unfair Competition Law to challenge practices they allege threaten public health, safety and property. They say a freeze is needed to protect public safety before a catastrophe occurs.

Former New Jersey Attorney General Matthew Platkin, who filed the complaint on behalf of the plaintiffs, said the companies are creating a public nuisance. "AI companies are causing a public nuisance by knowingly developing and deploying AI systems that pose catastrophic risk," Platkin said in a statement.

The complaint cites incidents involving existing, less powerful AI systems, the plaintiffs said. These include AI agents hacking private companies, gaining unauthorized access to government databases and submitting a false police report without human control. OpenAI's ChatGPT independently breached the systems of a different AI firm in July, according to The Associated Press.

"Our lawsuit asks the court to apply existing law and find the AI corporations' reckless practices unlawful and freeze further AI development," Robert Weissman, co-president of Public Citizen, said in a statement.

Phil Aroneanu, founder and executive director of Irreplaceable Education Fund, said in a statement: "We must freeze frontier AI development until the technology is under control and the American people have a say and a stake in its future, instead of leaving it to a handful of billionaire Big Tech CEOs."

The groups also say they have been forced to shift money and staff off their other scheduled projects to confront the dangers they say the companies' models pose, according to The Associated Press. The companies did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The suit follows the midterm elections, in which Democrats won control of Congress. President Donald Trump has pushed the companies to oversee their own safety practices, while Congress has yet to put any rules on the technology, according to The Associated Press.

Around 400 scientists, including Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, called in October for an international body with authority over containment standards and security incident reporting for advanced cyber-capable AI systems. The signatories argued that voluntary industry commitments are insufficient and urged governments to work toward a formal oversight regime.
