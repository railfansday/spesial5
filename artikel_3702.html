Boy, 14, Allegedly Killed Great-Aunt Who Had Been 'Afraid' of Him

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A 14-year-old Massachusetts boy is accused of fatally assaulting his great-aunt, who was found dead inside her kitchen after police were told she had fallen and was bleeding.

Brayden Lee, of Lowell, is charged with murder and assault and battery in connection with the death of Sheila Ingebritson, according to the Middlesex County District Attorney’s Office.

Authorities arrested Lee in Lowell on Thursday, November 19, more than three months after he allegedly killed Ingebritson, 69, the district attorney’s office announced in a news release.

Lee is being charged as an adult, as “all juveniles aged 14 and older charged with murder are automatically treated as adults” in Massachusetts, according to the state.

The charges against the teen stem from August 11, when police were called to a report about a woman who was allegedly bleeding after falling in her Lowell home, according to prosecutors.

Officers found Ingebritson dead in the kitchen with multiple injuries subsequently documented by a medical examiner, including a broken jaw, broken neck bone and skull fracture, prosecutors said.

Ingebritson lived with Lee and had custody of him, according to investigators. She was allegedly fearful of him.

“On several occasions the victim had allegedly expressed that she was afraid of the defendant and that he was volatile,” the district attorney’s office said.

Investigators learned Lee and Ingebritson had argued the day she died, based on a review of photo records and surveillance footage from Ingebritson’s home, according to prosecutors. The details of the argument weren’t specified.

They “were the only two people inside the home at the time of the fatal assault occurred,” the district attorney’s office said.

It wasn’t immediately clear whether Lee had an attorney who could comment on his behalf.

He was due to appear in court for an arraignment on Friday, November 20.

As a longtime Lowell resident, Ingebritson was born in the city in 1957 as one of 14 children from a “large, well known Irish Catholic family,” according to her obituary.

“Sheila was known for being a caretaker and for her huge heart,” the obituary reads.

The obituary mentions she was survived by Lee, who’s described as her foster child, in addition to two sons, a daughter and other family members. Ingebritson’s husband, Gene M. Ingebritson, died in 1997.

Lowell is located about a 30-mile drive northwest from Boston.
