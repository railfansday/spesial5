The Top Supplements for Women in Every Decade of Life, According to Experts

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Your nutrient needs change as you age—here’s how to fill in the gaps.

Even if you pride yourself on eating whole foods and cooking healthy meals, it can still be super hard to get all of the vitamins and minerals your body needs. Plus, your nutrient needs change as you age.

For instance, “as women navigate through the wonderful world of the menopause transition, there are changes, health-wise, that happen,” says Leslie Bonci, RD, a sports dietitian based in Pittsburgh. As your hormone levels start to decrease, you may be more likely to lose bone density and muscle mass. When these changes happen, “there are certain nutrients that are going to be needed more than others,” she adds. That’s when it can be helpful to turn to a supplement.

Meet the experts: Leslie Bonci, RD, is a sports dietitian based in Pittsburgh. Navya Mysore, MD, is a primary care physician, women's health expert, and WH advisory board member based in New York City.

Knowing which supplements you should take in each stage of life may be complicated, but we found the right experts to simplify everything. Consider this your supplement roadmap.

First, have a conversation with your primary care physician—they should know about all of the medications and supplements you’re currently taking or are considering. Your doc will help you ensure supplementation is the best course of action after considering your holistic health care plan. Then, they can rule out any potential medication interactions and decide which dosage is best, says Navya Mysore, MD, a primary care physician, women's health expert, and WH advisory board member based in New York City.

You also might want to get blood work done before starting a supplement, especially if you think you may have a deficiency. “Checking things like iron stores (ferritin), vitamin D, or B12 can tell you if you actually need a supplement and how much,” says Dr. Mysore. However, you don’t need blood work if you’re just taking a basic prenatal or multivitamin.

Just remember: “Supplements are a complement to—not a replacement for—eating,” Bonci says. They’re just one piece of the puzzle; in order to live your healthiest, longest, best life, you’ll want to make sure you’re eating nutritiously and exercising consistently.

Sorry to our gummy lovers out there: “Capsules usually win—they tend to absorb better and have fewer fillers,” says Dr. Mysore. “Tablets are fine but a bit more variable.” Unfortunately, gummies are often underdosed and may not include key nutrients like iron, she adds. So, your safest bet is a capsule.
