The 8 Best Slip-On Walking Shoes, According to Our Editors

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Looking to get out the door quicker? Don’t let laces slip you up.

SLIDING INTO A pair of slip-on walking shoes might be the easiest thing you’ll do all day. This footwear was built for speed and convenience, making it an accessory that deserves valuable real estate by your door.

Slip-on walking shoes have a rocky history—older versions had a looser fit, raising the risk of instability and friction, especially with higher mileage. But newer designs use no-tie laces, sock liners, pull tabs, and collapsible heels to create a custom fit that rivals what you’ll get with traditional laces.

These shoes are an obvious choice if you have dexterity issues, but they're also just convenient for everyone looking for an easy pair of walking shoes. Slip-on walking shoes also offer more structure than other laceless shoe designs, which has earned them a fanbase with podiatrists. “They are typically a much better option than flip-flops or slides because they provide more stability and keep the foot properly secured,” says Melissa Lockwood, DPM, a podiatrist at Heartland Foot and Ankle Associates in Bloomington, Illinois.

Nicole Graf, DPM, founder of Lexington Podiatry and associate professor at the University of Kentucky, also calls slip-on walking shoes a “huge step up” from flip-flops and slides.

Slip-on walking shoes aren’t for everyone. The fit can still throw some people off, and some simply prefer the tighter feel that laces deliver. But if you want travel-friendly shoes you can toss on in a second, these are a solid choice. As people who value quality, supportive footwear, Men’s Health editors have put in plenty of miles in slip-on walking shoes. The below check all the boxes.

The On Cloud 6 is an excellent example of how good a slip-on shoe can be. You can get in and out of this sneaker with no issue thanks to its wide opening, but the shoe’s elastic “speed” laces and sockliner create a snug fit once you’re in. (Those elastic laces can be adjusted to find your perfect fit.)

The sneaker uses Zero-Gravity CloudTec, which is On’s signature lightweight foam with hollow pods below for a cushioned, yet stable, feel underfoot. (Just be aware that those pods are prone to trapping small pebbles and debris.) A built-in flexible plate, called a Speedboard, helps push you forward with each step. This shoe also comes in a waterproof option, if you’re into rainy strolls. With a wide range of color options and an unobnoxious design, you can pair these with just about anything.

It’s tough to find a quality shoe under $100 right now, let alone one from a big brand. But the Adidas Cloudfoam Cuxxion Sock Shoes have some colorways under $50, making them an ideal choice for budget shoppers who don’t want to mess with laces.

This shoe uses Adidas’ signature Cloudfoam technology at the midsole for a plush, cushioned feel. Its Adiwear outsole is durable and grippy enough for most needs, too. But the real standout in this shoe is its elastic sock upper. It molds to your foot to create a surprisingly snug feel, even for narrow feet. The elastic sock collar supports that secure feel, although it may be too tight for people with wider ankles. The engineered knit upper is highly breathable, lowering the odds you’ll end your walk with sweaty feet. We appreciate the oversized pull tabs in front and back, making the process of getting these on and off seamless.

Feet come in all sizes, making it tough to find a slip-on shoe that works for everyone—and putting adjustable features at a premium. The Nike Pegasus 42 EasyOn has a specialized EasyOn lacing and bungee system up top to help you get the level of tightness you want, before locking it into place. (The lock and bungee combination takes some practice to get right at first.)

The shoe has a collapsible heel that springs back into place once you’re in, making the process of getting these on seamless. It’s also crafted with the same ReactX foam and Air Zoom unit as the standard—and popular—Nike Pegasus 42 running shoe, delivering an energetic feel under your feet. Convenient pull tabs at the heel sit flush against the shoe when they’re not in use, adding to the shoe’s overall sleekness.

Stability shoes are designed to guide your foot with each step and prevent too much inward rolling as you go. But choosing a stability shoe usually means sacrificing a high level of cushioning, since a shoe that feels too plush may not be overly supportive. Hoka’s Skyward Laceless manages to create a stable, locked-in feel while still delivering plenty of the brand’s signature cushioning underfoot.

The Skyward Laceless has a stretchy upper that gently hugs your foot, creating a sock-like feel. The ankle and instep are equally snug, which can be a good or bad thing, depending on your foot size and width. This shoe has plenty of foam below for a slightly bouncy feel, but it still corrects your stride as you go. The integrated tongue pull tab is a nice touch, making for easier entry.

The Under Armour SlipSpeed Mega is packed with features you won’t find in most slip-on walking shoes, creating a versatile, unique sneaker. For starters, this shoe converts from a sneaker design to a recovery slipper with the help of a collapsible heel. (Under Armour refers to this as going from “speed mode” to “slip mode.”)

The shoe skips elastic laces or a sockliner upper in favor of Under Armour’s specialized BOA fit system, a 12-point dial that you can turn to adjust the width across the midfoot. The wide base creates a stable feel, while lots of foam cushioning underfoot delivers a plushness you’ll appreciate. A heads-up: This shoe is slightly heavier than others at 11.7 ounces. That’s a difference you may start to feel on long walks.

After a grueling workout, it feels borderline pampering to slide into the right recovery shoe. The Kane Footwear Active Recovery Shoe is armed with features that should make it a fixture in your gym bag.

This slip-on has strong arch support and a slight rocker shape to help ease pressure on the bottom of your feet as you walk. Internal nodes in the footbed help to promote blood flow and circulation down below, while the shoe’s surprisingly secure design helps prevent slipping. The Active Recovery Shoe is water-resistant with an airy design that allows you to take this in and out of the gym shower without ending up with sopping feet. Heel tabs in back make it simple to get in and out.

Merrell is known for its rugged, outdoor footwear and hiking shoes. While the Merrell Jungle Moc isn’t exactly a hiking shoe, it’s a solid option when you want good traction, whether you’re navigating rocky ground or a slippery surface. The Jungle Moc has been a bestseller for years, with its simple slip-on design and high level of cushioning.

Dual side stretch panels and an exaggerated heel tab make it simple to get in and out of these shoes. The suede upper is breathable, but water-resistant to keep you dry in nearly all conditions. The Jungle Moc has a cushioned EVA midsole for stability and comfort, too. While these shoes are easy to get on, the curved heel makes it harder to slide out—just something to be aware of.

Clogs admittedly aren’t the right style for everyone—some offer less support than sneakers and may slip around more than a standard shoe. But the sleek Crocs Dylan Clog is a solid option for people who want a clog style without some of the common issues that come with it.

The Dylan Clog has a deep heel cup to help lock your foot in, lowering the risk it’ll move around as you step. It’s also made from one piece of Croslite (Crocs’ signature material) to prevent wear and tear over time. This shoe looks like leather thanks to its subtle pebbled texture, but it’s water-resistant and dries quickly when wet. The only major downside is that it’s not very breathable in front. If you’re prone to sweaty toes, this may not be the shoe for you.

Not all shoes are created equal, but the best slip-on walking shoes have all of the supportive features you’ll find in standard lace-up walking shoes. “You mostly get what you pay for in these,” says Richard H. Graves, DPM, a podiatrist at Sol Foot & Ankle Centers in Long Beach, California. He suggests taking a pass at dirt-cheap slip-on walking shoes. “The cheaper ones are not going to provide enough support in the long run, even if they might feel good at first,” Graves says. “There should be enough structure to support your arch and the midsole should be firm and not too flimsy.”

Many high-quality slip-on walking shoes provide similar comfort to regular sneakers. Look for elements like a breathable upper, pull tabs to make them easy to get on and off, and a roomy toe box. Structured heel panels and extra padding in this area help to keep your foot secure, even without standard laces.

Traction for slip-on walking shoes will vary depending on the type of shoe you choose. Most will offer decent traction—meaning, enough so you don’t feel like you’re slipping on wet or damp pavement. But a more rugged shoe like the Merrell Jungle Moc has a grippier sole to help provide extra traction over rockier ground.

Yes, slip-on walking shoes can provide arch support. If this is an important quality for you, look for features like contoured footbeds and a stable midsole. You’ll also want a shoe that fits well and stays put once it’s on to ensure your arch support is consistent. “Make sure that you're not getting them too loose just to make it easier to slide on and off—then you'll be sacrificing support,” Graves says.

Because these shoes are designed for walking, not just comfort, durability was also a key factor.
