“Trump was in bed with Jensen”: Critics balk at Huang’s wish to return to Nvidia

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

“I love our country”: Jensen Huang’s apology comes amid escalating Nvidia probes.

On Monday, large trucks began circling the streets of Washington, DC, carrying billboards urging Congress to investigate the Trump administration’s ties with Nvidia’s disgraced CEO Jensen Huang.

“Jensen was in bed with a spy from China,” the billboards created by the Tech Oversight Project (TOP) alleged. “Trump was in bed with Jensen.” TOP is a nonprofit that describes itself as “dedicated to reining in Big Tech.”

Currently, Trump is supporting a campaign to bring back Huang as Nvidia’s CEO—despite many Republicans calling it a “horrible idea.” Breaking his silence, Huang issued a statement Tuesday saying he would be “honored” to return, but the board said reinstatement is not under consideration as an internal investigation into US spying claims remains ongoing. Huang used the moment to say that his interests align with the US’s, saying:

I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an American citizen is one of the greatest gifts and proudest moments of my life. Nvidia has been my life’s work. Building it into the company it is today alongside my colleagues has been a privilege and an honor. Nowhere but in America could a company like Nvidia be possible. I love our country, and I love Nvidia.

Trump may have learned as early as this spring that Huang was potentially the target of Chinese espionage, but the president claimed recently that he didn’t know Huang was potentially exposed to a Chinese spy as he was becoming a prominent part of Trump’s inner circle, CNN reported.

If Trump wasn’t adequately briefed, it raises question about whether the White House acted with “sufficient urgency” to address a serious espionage threat, CNN said. But if Trump is deflecting, an even “graver question” will emerge about why Trump didn’t act on information and instead continued to keep Huang close, CNN said.

Instead of distancing himself from the Nvidia CEO, Trump invited Huang to sit at the head table during a state dinner with China’s president Xi Jinping, and last month, Trump awarded Huang the National Medal of Science, the nation’s highest scientiﬁc honor, for his AI achievements.

“Not only has he continued to do nothing, he’s invited that very CEO into the White House and on state visits to China with him,” Sacha Haworth, TOP’s founder and CEO, told Ars. “And the question we all have to ask is ‘why on Earth would he do that?’”

What Trump and Nvidia knew—and when—have become central questions for some Republicans, as well as top Democrats who plan to investigate the scandal when they assume control of key committees in Congress next year. Andrew Yoon, head of research for a nonprofit called CivAI, which warns the public of dangers from AI, told Ars that Huang doesn’t need to be compromised by China to have endangered the US. Prioritizing boosting profits over national security alone is “pretty concerning.”

“I think the most notable thing here is that Jensen Huang has been at the absolute forefront of lobbying to be able to sell chips to China,” Yoon said. “He has leveraged his very deep relationships with government, especially President Trump, in order to be able to sell as many Nvidia chips to China as he can get away with. If he could be selling Blackwell chips to China, I’m sure he would be.”

Yoon noted that evidence suggests some Chinese AI models have been trained on Blackwell chips. Even with China’s push to prioritize domestic chipmakers like Huawei, Nvidia chips remain “indispensable” to China’s frontier AI goals, Yoon said. And national security experts fear that access to high-end chips will help China advance its military AI or gain economic ground.

The White House isn’t commenting on Trump’s relationship with Huang. Among the lawmakers accusing the Trump administration of “corruption” is Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.), The Wall Street Journal reported. He has already asked the White House to “preserve all communications” between Trump and Huang.

Other lawmakers are “seeking more information about what Trump knew about the relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang,” the former Megaspeed CEO whom Huang admitted to having an affair with. They also want to know more about “the administration’s failure to stop artiﬁcial-intelligence chips from going to China,” WSJ reported, and Nvidia’s reportedly $25 million donation to the White House ballroom.

“It is not just an intelligence issue; it’s also a potential corruption issue,” Blumenthal said. “There needs to be a really penetrating, comprehensive investigation of all the possible compromises of our intelligence that may have occurred, but also… that Donald Trump is so high on the head of Nvidia, and now is potentially linked to intelligence compromises.”

Trump has profited off Nvidia’s success while in office, buying “millions of dollars worth of Nvidia shares in his personal brokerage accounts since returning to oﬃce in 2025,” the WSJ reported. The White House maintains that the president has no role in managing his stock portfolio.

Other officials advising Trump on AI also benefited from relaxing export curbs, TOP said. On Tuesday, the group issued a report summarizing what the public knows about potential conflicts of interest of two Trump officials: former White House AI Czar David Sacks and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Haworth, TOP’s founder, told Ars she’s concerned that Sacks and Lutnick abused their power to convince Trump to let Nvidia sell cutting-edge technology to China, while standing to “gain astronomical amounts of money.”

“The public is outraged by Nvidia’s CEO for compromising our national security, but we didn’t get here because of Jensen Huang’s missteps alone,” Haworth said. If “Nvidia was knowingly doing business with companies tied to illegal chip sales and smuggling with China,” then “the Trump administration was asleep at the wheel,” she said.

“White House AI Czar David Sacks and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick were protecting their own fortunes rather than looking out for Americans,” Haworth said.

If the US fails to hold them accountable for alleged conflicts of interest, “it would send a signal that powerful men once again cannot be held to account and that powerful men are protecting powerful men,” Haworth said.

Detailing Sacks’ alleged conflicts, the group cited New York Times reporting that the former AI czar had long supported Huang’s stance that allowing Nvidia to sell more chips in China would help the US stay ahead in the AI race. After Sacks became Trump’s key AI advisor, Huang quickly forged a “tight bond” with him, and “both stood to benefit” from their push to sell chips to China, TOP reported.

Also notably, while advising the White House, Sacks opposed policies such as the Trump administration’s recent move to cut off China’s remote access to US-made chips through now-restricted Southeast Asian data centers. Citing Sacks’ alignment with Huang, TOP reported that after just one meeting with the two men, Trump decided to allow Nvidia to sell AI chips to China that the Biden administration had previously blocked.

Huang valued his alliance with Sacks, TOP noted, citing reporting that showed Huang declaring last year that Nvidia was “lucky” to have Sacks advising Trump. But TOP is concerned that the alliance was built on mutual financial interest and that Sacks took a part-time role in the Trump administration to avoid divestments or disclosure requirements that might have revealed the extent of his possible conflicts of interest.

TOP flagged two areas it believes warrant Congressional investigation. While advising Trump to embrace AI deregulation, Sacks and his firm, Craft Ventures, continued investing substantially in AI. Sacks also stood to benefit from pushing Trump to integrate AI into warfare and national security tools, TOP’s report said. Craft Ventures has invested in Palantir, “a Peter Thiel-founded government contractor seen as a ‘rising star’ in the US Defense Tech World and holding hundreds of millions in US government defense contracts,” TOP said.

Even within the Trump administration, officials have turned on Commerce Secretary Lutnick, seemingly looking to blame him for mishandling the Nvidia spying investigation and allowing Trump to maintain close ties with Huang despite apparent intelligence risks.

Lutnick has bragged publicly that he promised Huang “really easy access to the President” after joining the administration, TOP reported. That alliance helped push Trump to approve the sale of Nvidia H200s to China, TOP said. And that seemed to directly benefit Lutnick, TOP said, because before he was confirmed as secretary, he was CEO of Cantor Fitzgerald, which held $1.2 billion in Nvidia stock.

“In his role as Secretary, Lutnick is responsible for policing exports of sensitive technology to adversaries and global rivals, like China,” TOP reported. “Lutnick and the company he controls benefited greatly from opening sales of Nvidia chips to China.”

After Lutnick was required to hand that company over to his son, Cantor Fitzgerald “maintained extensive commercial ties to Nvidia, helping arrange billions of dollars in financing for companies in its AI business universe and repeatedly hosting its top executives,” TOP noted.

Those investments shifted substantially around the same time that Lutnick began facing pressure to “crack down on Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company suspected of funneling Nvidia’s advanced artificial intelligence chips to China,” TOP reported. Cantor Fitzgerald “reduced its Nvidia holdings by nearly 50 percent between September 2025 and March 2026,” TOP said.

However, when Trump was reportedly first told of the spying allegations this spring, Cantor Fitzgerald again continued to boost Nvidia. Most prominently, the company helped “finance the expansion of one of Nvidia’s newest business partners, IREN,” facilitating a five-year, $3.4 billion deal between Nvidia and the Australia-based cloud computing provider.

Among those now criticizing Lutnick for weak enforcement of export controls that appeared designed to protect Huang’s bottom line is Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. But even with scrutiny coming from within the Trump administration, not everyone is convinced that Lutnick did anything wrong. Yoon suggested that, given Lutnick’s long career, there may be normal explanations for potential financial conflicts.

“Maybe a separate question is ‘should people with a long storied history in business and industry be working in government at this level?’” Yoon said.

Haworth said the next 48 hours will be critical in determining whether Huang succeeds in returning to Nvidia.

“I think it’s going to be incumbent on the incoming Democratic leadership to insist that whether or not he returns, investigations will follow,” Haworth said.

But Yoon thinks Nvidia is unlikely to bring Huang back any time soon, despite current evidence suggesting that “Nvidia’s actions here have been perfectly legal” and that “influencing a political system for your financial interests is very much legal in the United States.”

It seems “untenable for Nvidia to continue doing what they’re doing,” Yoon said. Zooming out even more, he suggested the scandal showed that the entire AI industry can no longer afford to operate under a profit motive alone, so long as governments continue positioning the technology as a strategic asset.

If US investigations expose corruption, bribery, treason, or other crimes, Nvidia risks losing some of its dominant stake in the GPU market. On Tuesday, The Information reported that some Nvidia customers had already lost trust and were looking to Google and AMD for more stability.

Nvidia has also been accused of intervening in a Commerce investigation, which Yoon said could also be “pretty consequential” if any evidence bears that out.

“I think it’s pretty unlikely that Huang makes it back to Nvidia any time soon,” Yoon said. “Maybe he goes on a redemption tour and comes back in five years or something, but I’m not really seeing a path forward for him right now.”
