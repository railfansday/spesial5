‘Primetime’ Cinematographer & Editor On Making Robert Pattinson Look Like A “Superhero” & Breakdown Viral Meme Catchphrase

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

In A24’s Primetime, the first predator confrontation unfolds in a bright, colorful home. For cinematographer David Bolen, that contrast was central to the scene’s tension. “This was like, we all felt a lot of anxiety about shooting this because we were like, ‘OK, this is the first predator encounter. This sets the tone for everything. So we have to make sure it’s good,’” he tells Deadline.

Director Lance Oppenheim was drawn to “really intense things happening in really brightly lit, beautiful homes,” Bolen says. “Someone’s life is kind of over, but it’s happening in this really bright, colorful space. So there’s an interesting dynamic there.”

As the film re-creates the To Catch a Predator standoff, Chris Hansen (newly minted James Bond actor Robert Pattinson) confronts a man (Tony Revolori) who thought he was meeting a teenage girl. Bolen says the brightness leaves the man nowhere to hide. “The light is shining directly on him, and no matter what, the cameras are going to capture him,” he says. “There’s an uneasiness about this happening that there’s no place for this predator to hide. Right now, Chris Hansen has him exactly where he wants him.” Watch on Deadline

Bolen and his gaffer pushed direct sunlight through the windows as Hansen descends the stairs, giving him a halo. “This in many ways is his superhero shot,” Bolen says. “He’s coming down from the stairs; he’s presenting himself as the full Chris Hansen for the first time. So early on with my gaffer, I decided, ‘OK, we’re going to backlight him really, really heavy with direct sunlight. There’s these windows above him right now, and we’re pushing giant sunlight streaks through him to give him that kind of angelic feel. And that was to play into this like, yeah, he’s a superhero in this moment. This is Chris Hansen.’”

Editor Daniel Garber found another way to stretch the unease in a moment that went viral on social media when Hansen confronted the predator, saying: “You see how this looks right?” Was Garber surprised by the internet craze or did he have an inkling beforehand? “In the edit, we’re also trying to establish a lot of that unsettling atmosphere through the rhythm of it,” he says. “There are a lot of moments where we’re holding on Chris as long as possible, really trying to milk the silences. And I think that draws out a lot of the uncanniness of this whole encounter.”

Garber adds: “Rob’s performance is one of the most amazing things that I got to deal with in this film. He was, I think, always thinking about Chris Hansen not necessarily as a real person, but as the persona who exists as a meme online at this point. And so a lot of that process of studying the meme of Chris Hansen, I think comes through in the way that he portrays Chris in these moments.”

Further describing the iconic line, Garber continues: “I think it went viral in part because Rob had developed all of these very specific physical performances that he knew would be the kind of moments that really stick in people’s heads. Rob probably knew from the get-go which moments were going to go viral, and I don’t know if I knew exactly, but I think we had a hunch.”

The kind of experimentation that this film required depends on deep trust. In The Process interview, Bolen and Garber explain that they have worked together for nearly a decade, building a shorthand that lets one department inspire the next. “We’re going to experiment, and we’re going to try things, and we’re not always going to be perfect, but we all support each other,” Bolen says. “Filmmaking’s really hard, and it can be tough on you personally, and when you feel like you have family around you in the collaborators, that’s really special.”
