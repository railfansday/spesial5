CMA Awards Predictions: Expect Ella Langley to Get Everything but Male Vocalist, and Maybe Even That’s Not Safe

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Variety is picking the winners for the 2026 CMAs, with plenty of easy-to-place bets on 9x-nominated Ella Langley. But it ain't over till the EOTY sings.

Senior Music Writer and Chief Music Critic

The suspense is unbearable: Will the 2026 CMA Awards see Ella Langley enjoying a total sweep, or just a near-sweep?

That is a real question, not a rhetorical one. But before we start trying to anticipate the math, let’s remember that, although Langley had a leading nine nominations, the most she can win is seven, because she is competing against herself in two categories, single of the year and song of the year, where “Choosin’ Texas” is up against “Be Her.” Normally that would leave room for vote-splitting, but rest assured, Langley and her two-stepping two-timers have the means to overcome that little problem. So with best single and best song utterly in the bag, that leaves five more open to see if she can run the board with the Country Music Association’s voters.

Entertainer of the Year, especially? There is some real suspense there. In some matters, it does take a crystal ball to see.

Here are our predictions for the winners of the 60th annual awards, which air live from Nashville’s Bridgestone Arena from 8-11 on the east coast and delayed for prime on the west coast, also at 8.

Luke CombsCody JohnsonElla LangleyChris StapletonLainey Wilson

Will win: Ella LangleyCould win: Cody Johnson

This top honor used to be regarded as something closer to an ongoing lifetime achievement award for a currently hot superstar than a recognition of pure, recent artistic achievement that could go to someone in the earlier stages of a long ascent. But it felt like that was starting to change when Lainey Wilson got it in 2023 without having developed any severe road wrinkles. (She also got it again in 2025.) All of which is to say that there is a question that could be asked with Lainey and this honor: “Too soon?” And the likely answer to that is: “Nope, but thanks for asking.”

Still, there is a lane open here for anyone to bust through if voters should decide that an armful of “lesser” awards are good enough for Langley this year. No one would greatly begrudge the well-love Wilson getting it for the third time in four years. Combs is probably the longest shot, being a two-time winner, like Langley. Neither Stapleton nor Johnson have gotten it even once. Stapleton has the “seriously, overdue” factor in his favor, having been nominated 11 times without ever taking it home. But if the enthusiasm for keeping it fresh continues but it’s just not Langley’s time for it, Johnson feels most like a presumptive second-place — and possibly first-place — finisher.

Still, the vast mainstream/crossover audiences watching the show at home will scratch their chins if it’s anyone but Langley, who has two of the biggest songs of the year in any genre in “Choosin’ Texas” and “Be Her” and has undoubtedly become country’s biggest ambassador to the world for this particular time frame. Choosin’ anyone else would just seem strangely insular.

“Be Her” – Ella Langley“Choosin’ Texas” – Ella Langley“I Knew It, I Knew You” – Taylor Swift“Somewhere Over Laredo” – Lainey Wilson“The Fall” – Cody Johnson

Will win: “Choosin’ Texas”Could win: Get real. Are you insane?

If “Choosin’ Texas” didn’t exist, we might be supposed that Langley would still be a front-runner for the award, but for “Be Her.” According to data provided to Variety by Luminate, “Be Her” is the all-around third most-consumed song of 2026 among all genres, not just country. But “Choosin’ Texas” is one of the biggest hits (use your Kanye voice here) of all time, as it currently enjoys a record-extending 28th week at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100. Moreover, there is no resentment in the country community against its success, and certainly none against Langley for crossing over when the song America can’t stop falling for is as country as country can be. Seeing a shoo-in get shooed in never felt any better.

But, needless to say, boy, are the CMAs producers glad Taylor Swift is nominated here. Surely she knows she is not going to beat Langley here, and she is happy to show up and perform anyway… as a thank-you to the ones who brought her to the dance, and also, maybe, a campaign stop on the way to a place where more is at stake.

Ain’t In It For My Health – Zach TopDandelion – Ella LangleyMiddle of Nowhere – Kacey MusgravesParker McCollum – Parker McCollumThe Way I Am – Luke Combs

Will win: “Dandelion”Could win: “Parker McCollum”

It’s hard to imagine the scenario where an album as across-the-board as good as “Dandelion” doesn’t follow “Choosin’ Texas” into the winners’ circle. But there’s a case voters can reasonably make for casting their ballots for anything else in the category. Musgraves’ “Middle of Nowhere” was the most critically acclaimed country album of the year overall, and marked a definitive return to country after some musically farther-afield records from the star. It would be wonderful to see Kacey finally get it, many of us can agree. But only occasionally do voters go for an album that didn’t have a huge impact at mainstream country radio, as evidence by the fact that Musgraves’ often left-of-center approach has landed her four prior nominations in this category but only one win (for “Golden Hour”).

Still, this is the category where voters are most apt to sometimes break ranks with bestselling stuff and pick something cool to make a statement. (Think Lee Ann Womack’s “There’s More Where That Came From” in 2005.) So that could mean it’s back to being Musgraves’ time… or it could mean they’d have a hankering for Parker McCollum’s critically acclaimed, self-titled album, in which he was seen as going for artistry more than hits. The fact that the ACM Awards gave McCollum this award back in the spring definitely should be taken into account in reading the tea leaves. But realistically, it just seems like we’ll never know which of those albums came in second… because how can Langley lose?

It’s hard to remember when the CMA Awards had a greater crop of songs up for this honor than this year. Can they make it a five-way tie and everyone gets a prize? No, they cannot. So as much applause as we might want to hold in reserve for Musgraves, Megan Moroney or Stephen Wilson Jr., this particular prize has had Langley’s name on it since before the creation of the world. Not to get biblical.

Miranda LambertElla LangleyMegan MoroneyKacey MusgravesLainey Wilson

Will win: Ella LangleyCould win: No other option. The need to get real persists.

If you were making a list of the five most outstanding artists in country music in the 2020s, you could pick these five without any shame or need to move on… even if we might eventually worry we’d overlooked a guy or two. But honestly, what a field. Anyway it’s Langley’s to lose.

Luke CombsCody JohnsonChris StapletonZach TopStephen Wilson Jr.

Will win: Ella Langley. No, just kidding. Cody JohnsonCould win: Stephen Wilson Jr.

We should probably look at which three artists from this field are also nominated for entertainer of the year, and narrow it down from there. Of those three, Johnson has the freshest momentum, even though Combs and Stapleton are not a bit less popular than they ever were. You could never go wrong with virtually anyone in the world by naming Stapleton the male vocalist of this or any year, but he might have simply won it too many times. It would be a news-making upset if Wilson should get in — the odds are really stacked against him amid these proven heavyweights, but there is so much appreciation for him that a big cheer would go up if he crashed through that ceiling.

Lady ALittle Big TownMidlandOld DominionThe Red Clay Strays

Will win: The Red Clay StraysCould win: Old Dominion

The vocal group and vocal duo categories tend to have the least fresh blood and the most predictable year-by-year streaks The Red Clay Strays were a surprise winner last year — no one was more surprised than them — so it’ll be interesting to see if that was a one-off or the start of yet another CMA streak. I’m betting the latter, not least of all because they’re basically a rock group, and country people love having a real rock group — a good one — to bring some rootsy energy and fierceness into the genre.

Brooks & DunnBrothers OsborneDan + ShayMuscadine BloodlineThelma & James

Will win: Brooks & DunnCould win: Brothers Osborne

Just as much as CMA voters have liked having some new blood to vote for, in the form of the Red Clay Strays, they have enjoyed going back to Brooks & Dunn to celebrate old-home week. They’ve won this 16 times, and finally returned to the winners’ circle last year after previously last getting it in 2018. Their comeback as awardees here still feels fresh, in that sense, even if it’s a feat as old as time immemorial for them.

“A Song To Sing” – Miranda Lambert (with Chris Stapleton)“Horses and Divorces” – Kacey Musgraves (feat. Miranda Lambert)“I Can’t Love You Anymore” – Ella Langley & Morgan Wallen“If I Don’t Leave I’m Gonna Stay” – Carly Pearce & Riley Green“McArthur” – HARDY, Eric Church, Morgan Wallen & Tim McGraw

Will win: “I Can’t Love You Anymore”Could win: “Horses and Divorces”

This one is actually a tough category to call. The Langley/Wallen duet will probably get swept in on the wave that is pushing Ella toward so many other noms, and there continues to be support for Wallen in many pockets of the industry, even though you wouldn’t know it from the lack of nominations he’s gotten since winning entertainer of the year two years ago. But although their song served its purpose as something for them to sing together on his tour, is it really up there with Langley’s best songs or more of an agreeable time-passer? If it should fail to ride through on her long coattails, it’s tough to know where things go from there. I’m partial to the sensual Pearce/Green duet, and wish Pearce was up for a lot more for her excellent album.

But damn it, “Horses and Divorces” actually was an event, if you’re going to take the title of the trophy seriously. After a long, low-simmering feud no one denied, Musgraves had the brilliant idea to call Lambert up for a peace-pipe session, in the guise of a songwriting section about their feud… to great comedic-yet-real effect Talk about working it out on the remix. As Clint Eastwood once said, though, deserve’s got nothin’ to do with it. So expect Langley to need to build an extra shelf.

“Choosin’ Texas” – Ella Langley“Dry Spell” – Kacey Musgraves“Gary” – Stephen Wilson Jr.“The Fall” – Cody Johnson“White Horse” – Chris Stapleton

Will win: “Choosin’ Texas”Could win: “Dry Spell” or “Gary”

“Choosin’ Texas” isn’t really the most interesting video of all of these, but it gets the job done, and that should be enough to have it two-step its way into the winners’ circle. Still, maybe this is the place for voters to exercise a fondness for Musgraves, via her amusing video about sexual frustration in the supermarket. As Freud said, sometimes a cucumber is not just a cucumber.

Avery AnnaCarter FaithEmily Ann RobertsTucker WetmoreStephen Wilson Jr.

Will win: Tucker WetmoreCould win: Stephen Wilson Jr.

Wetmore is the only one here with No. 1 hits, which ought to be enough to push him across the line as voters face the inevitable question of how they think their choice will hold up 10 or 15 years from now. He’d be a worthy winner, but so would any of the others, most of all the critically beloved Faith and Wilson. Faith just picked up a key Grammy nomination… which of course means little in this context, but just saying… she’s interesting and she’s a keeper. So is Wilson, who has a much larger profile at present, including a lot of left-over love from how he killed it on last year’s telecast, twice. Wetmore is the horse to bet on, but this might be closer to a photo finish than most would suspect.
