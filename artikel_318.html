Best Early Black Friday Espresso Machine Deals (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

If the barista at your neighborhood coffee shop knows your order before you even walk up to the counter, it might be time to consider learning to make that double Americano at home. With today being National Espresso Day and no shortage of espresso machines on sale ahead of Black Friday, that opportunity is particularly apt.

Each deal below is for a machine that a WIRED reviewer has tested and vetted in their own kitchen. If you take advantage of the savings now, your machine may arrive in time for Thanksgiving, allowing you to impress everyone with your post-dinner cappuccino-making skills.

For more deals, check out our Best Early Black Friday Deals, as well as early deals at Amazon and Home Depot.

Updated November 23: I’ve added new deals on machines from Diletta, Fellow, and De'Longhi, plus some accessories. I've also ensured up-to-date links and prices.

WIRED reviewers have tried and liked a couple of machines from Diletta, a line of Italian-made, classic semiautomatics exclusive to Seattle Coffee Gear, and they’re all on sale today through December 14.

We reviewed the Bello+ in 2024, appreciating the stylish, industrial design (handmade in Milan) and easily maneuverable steam wand. Our reviewer found it a bit finicky, but Seattle Coffee Gear says it does have a programmable pre-infusion for repeatable recipes.

Best Traditional Espresso Machine in our guide to the best, the Mio is stylish and built to last, with an analog design and a two-year warranty. It also makes for a solid first “real” espresso machine with its built-in shot timer and pressure gauge.

Fellow set some pretty lofty expectations with the release of its sleek Aiden coffee maker in 2024, and according to former WIRED reviewer Matthew Korfhage, the brand didn't disappoint on its first attempt at an espresso machine. The ability to adjust flow rates and pressure curves was once the province of, as Korfhage put it, only the “richest and nerdiest of nerds.” But Fellow now allows you—yes, you, at home in your kitchen—to fine-tune how water moves through the grounds, unlocking previously unforeseen dimensions of flavor.

When we tested this machine, we liked it for a variety of reasons, including an easy-to-use milk frother and two swappable bean hoppers, which are especially helpful if you like to switch to decaf in the afternoons. It comes in a variety of finishes, but note that Beige is the cheapest of all right now, at only $915.

This flashy, design-oriented semiautomatic (“a steampunk gasworks of a thing,” according to Korfhage) is quite dramatic in person. It has a dual boiler—meaning you can froth milk at the same time you’re pulling a shot—and it comes with a nice set of accessories, including a tamper and milk pitcher. Note that this sale is on the 2nd Gen machine, which features expanded grinder settings and a new portafilter.

Formerly the Linea Classic, De’Longhi’s Classic is the best bare-bones espresso machine we’ve tried, with a pressure gauge, 15-bar pump, and updated thermoblock system. I used one for a full year and, while it’s not as foolproof as a semiautomatic, it was easy enough for morning espresso shots and cappuccinos; I even pulled off some rudimentary latte art with its steam wand.

The Vertuo Pop+ is our favorite pod maker for espresso. The Deluxe model adds a larger, side-mount water tank (37 ounces to the original’s 25 ounces) and new finishes, including this white one, a matte black-and-gold, and a sort of gunmetal gray.

We appreciated this semiautomatic’s slim profile, consistent shots, and built-in burr grinder. The cold-brew setting is more of a gimmick than a way to make actual cold brew, and some design choices are a bit quirky. Even so, it’s a beautiful, industrial-looking machine that takes up only 12 inches of counter space.

Ninja sometimes has a reputation for doing too much, but we liked this semiautomatic as a beginner-friendly option that pulls some solid shots. It’s got a built-in grinder and milk frother, the latter of which can make cold foam and even has a setting for plant-based milk. In true Ninja fashion, it also has settings for drip coffee and cold brew, but neither impressed our tester, so I wouldn’t buy it for those features alone.

Former WIRED reviewer Matthew Korfhage tested Terra Kaffe's newer Demi and said it was perhaps the “dead-simplest fully automatic espresso machine I've yet encountered that still makes good coffee,” with just a dial and a button. Right now it's $200 off, or $300 off with a coffee subscription (Terra-Kaffe offers a house blend specifically made for use on its machines). Its bigger sibling, the TK-02, impressed our tester with its convenience, ease of use, front-facing water filter, and milk carafe. Though it didn't come out on top during a blind espresso taste test we did earlier this year, it's still a great-looking machine that's easy to use and is a whopping $400 off (or $500 with a coffee subscription) for Black Friday.

Maybe you or your recipient already has an espresso machine; in that case, they’ll need some quality beans. Our favorite coffee subscription, Atlas Coffee Club, has a new gift option on offer for Black Friday: a quarterly subscription of three bags of unique, globally sourced coffee every three months for $59. You can choose any roast you want, including espresso roast and even Nespresso pods. If you’d rather buy an ongoing subscription for yourself, your first bag is free with Atlas’ Black Friday sale.

Former barista and WIRED contributing reviewer Pete Cottell likes this little scale that includes three magnetic add-ons: a platform, a metal dosing cup, and a catch cup that fits directly onto almost any portafilter.

If grounds are sticking to the plastic components of your coffee grinder, Cottell recommends adding this durable, stylish spray bottle to your espresso setup.

Cottell likes situating this mat underneath your espresso setup, as the lip contains spills and the textured portion is great for drying tools post-washing.

Some machines come with a tamper; others don't. If yours doesn’t, Cottell recommends you get this one. The spring-loaded base unloads 15 pounds of force to create a perfectly flat puck inside your portafilter. (A click lets you know when to stop pushing, so it’s great for beginners, too.)
