The U.S. Must Reject Russian Narratives on Ukraine

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

As negotiations approach, it’s clear that Russia is not reasonable, and its victory on the battlefield is not a foregone conclusion.

A Ukrainian soldier from the Striletskyi special forces police battalion of the National Police in the Zaporizhzhia Region prepares 155 mm shells for a 2S22 Bohdana self-propelled howitzer to fire at Russian positions in Donetsk Region, Ukraine, January 23, 2026.

Dmytro Smolienko/Ukrinform/NurPhoto via Getty Images

The United States will try to mediate another round of negotiations between Ukraine and Russia starting tomorrow. If it is to be successful, the U.S. must depart from its current strategy. In President Trump’s eagerness for a deal to end the war in Ukraine, there has been a spate of U.S. actions that are favorable to Russia. U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff visited Russia and flattered Vladimir Putin, calling him a “humanitarian.” President Trump lifted sanctions on Russian diesel, waived enforcement of much of the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, and publicly pressured Ukraine’s president to take a deal.

This approach appears premised on two false narratives, which Russia is proffering to the U.S. special envoys: one, that the Russian government’s demands are reasonable and therefore open to compromise for the sake of peace, and two, that Russia’s defeat of Ukraine on the battlefield is only a matter of when, not if. The State Department, rather than the special envoys, should lead the effort to mediate an end to the war, and it should soundly reject both Russian narratives if it is to develop a strategy that could work.

Rebeccah Heinrichs is a senior fellow at the Hudson Institute and the director of Hudson’s Keystone Defense Initiative.
