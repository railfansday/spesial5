JetBlue Leads The Industry In Raising Checked Bag Fees… Again!

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

For whatever reason, JetBlue tends to be the industry leader when it comes to raising checked bag fees, and competitors tend to follow. Earlier this year, JetBlue was the first major US carrier to raise checked bag fees, and now the airline is raising checked bag fees again…

Effective immediately (for tickets purchased as of November 3, 2026), JetBlue has increased checked bag fees. JetBlue has a rather complicated fee structure for checked bags, with both off-peak and peak pricing, plus different pricing based on whether you pay for the bag 24 hours or more in advance.

Below is a chart with JetBlue’s new checked bag fees.

As you can see, an off-peak first checked bag on a domestic flight will now cost you $50-60 (depending on whether you pay in advance), while a peak first checked bag on a domestic flight will now cost you $55-70 (depending on whether you pay in advance). For context, below are the dates that are considered peak for the purposes of bag fees.

It goes without saying that paying $50-70 for a first checked bag is a pretty substantial cost, especially when you consider how little checked bags used to cost. On a one-way, you’re looking at $100-140. For a family of four, you’d be looking at $400-560.

When JetBlue raised checked bag fees earlier this year, the airline released a statement claiming it was increasing checked bag fees to be “more competitive,” and that “while we recognize that fee increases are never ideal, we take careful consideration to ensure these changes are implemented only when necessary.” I imagine this time around, the logic is much of the same.

The simple reality is that oil prices are way up. Airlines have actually done a solid job of being able to make fare increases stick. But that’s not enough to fully offset the increase in the cost of jet fuel.

JetBlue is in a rough financial spot, and it hasn’t turned a profit since before the pandemic. The airline is hoping to turn an operating profit in 2027, though that strikes me as being particularly optimistic. So the logic for raising checked bags is threefold:

JetBlue has just increased checked bag fees once again, and you’ll now pay $50-70 for a first checked bag on a domestic flight. This is JetBlue’s second price increase on checked bags so far this year. When airlines are trying to increase revenue, checked bag fees are often low hanging fruit.

When competitors match, it also becomes something that’s unavoidable for consumers, other than getting a credit card… which also has upside for the airline! I’d be shocked if other airlines don’t match this move from JetBlue.
