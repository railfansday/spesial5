Deranged American Airlines Passenger Screams “I Want To See Jesus” And Tries To Rip Open Emergency Exit Door Before Fellow Travelers Restrain Him With Zip Ties And Duct Tape

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Passengers and flight attendants worked together to restrain a man with zip ties and duct tape after he allegedly tried to open an exit door on an American Airlines flight to Chicago. The aircraft diverted to Phoenix, and the FBI says the man is now in custody. A frightening start to Thanksgiving travel, but also a reminder of how quickly strangers can come together when help is needed.

American Airlines flight 1494 was traveling from San Diego (SAN) to Chicago O’Hare (ORD) when a passenger disturbance forced a diversion to Phoenix (PHX) early on November 25, 2026.

According to Phoenix police, the man left his seat, moved toward the front of the aircraft, refused instructions to sit down, and attempted to tamper with an exit door.

Flight attendants and passengers restrained him until the aircraft landed safely in Phoenix around 1:00 am. The man sustained a minor injury and was taken to a hospital for evaluation. No injuries were reported among the other passengers or crew. Take a look at these videos:

Passenger Sean Bennett, who posted the videos above, said that he had been sleeping when a flight attendant’s shouting woke him. Another passenger, Hunter Glegory, recalled the man saying:

“I want to see Jesus, get me off this plane.”

.Passenger video captured the struggle and the use of zip ties and duct tape. One witness also described a flight attendant using a beverage cart to keep the confrontation from moving farther down the aisle. The FBI subsequently confirmed that the man had been taken into custody. It is interviewing witnesses and consulting federal prosecutors about possible charges.

Passengers eventually continued to Chicago later Wednesday.

Imagine waking up on a redeye to a flight attendant shouting at someone to get away from a door (I had one such experience many years ago…).

You are tired, perhaps disoriented, and suddenly trying to understand whether the commotion ahead is something that threatens you or your family. I would find that deeply unsettling and am thankful passengers were willing to help the crew.

Flight attendants are trained to respond to disturbances, but physically restraining someone who continues to resist can require assistance. People who had boarded simply planning on waking up in Chicago found themselves helping contain a dangerous situation instead.

We often focus on the selfish behavior we encounter onboard, and there is certainly no shortage of material. But here we saw passengers stepping forward to help people they did not know. Others described a remarkably calm response around them.

As we celebrate Thanksgiving in the USA, that is something to appreciate.

The reported remark about wanting to see Jesus is disturbing in this context, but it is not clear whether the man was experiencing a medical crisis, was under the influence of something, or acted for another reason. Whatever the explanation, the crew had to address his conduct immediately. Compassion for someone who may be in distress and firm action to protect everyone onboard are entirely compatible.

I hope the investigation establishes what happened and that the man receives appropriate care if he needs it and faces justice if this was due to a controllable choice like drugs or alcohol. I also hope the passengers and crew who intervened are doing well. AA should also send this guy the bill for the extra fuel, crew time, and anything other related expenses.
