Rail To The Austin Airport Is Back On The Table—After Costs Soared And A Voter Bait-and-Switch

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Austin’s airport used to be super convenient but it’s outgrown itself. There are just too many passengers for the size of the facility, at peak times it can be nearly body to body as you navigate through the terminal. What was once curb-to-gate in minutes, security wait times on peak travel days can be quite long. And traffic gets backed up heading into and out of the airport. There’s a long trek across the road and through a parking garage, up and down levels, to get to Uber or taxis. They’re building a new terminal and a new headhouse. The airport’s solution is to get (a lot) bigger. It will never recapture the ease of a smaller airport, and it’s going to mean more walking and showing up even earlier. One thing Austin doesn’t have is rail to the airport. Voters approved higher property taxes in 2020 to pay for it, but the cost of the project ballooned and train ambitions remained – but the airport connection got axed to save money. Now the mayor wants to start the process of planning again for a train to the airport. The much smaller light rail system now being developed will cost nearly double what was once estimated, even though it won’t reach the airport when it’s supposed to open in 2033. But since the airport is building an entirely new face to the facility, it makes sense to plan for how a train would approach and where the station would go. Plus, they’re going to be looking to have the airport pay for a portion of it. That’s part of what the mayor means when he talks about rail reaching the airport without new tax money. Mayor Kirk Watson lays out a three-mile extension of the planned train, mostly elevated, with a station at the airport. He says the Federal Transit Administration has agreed to a streamlined environmental review, and the next nine to twelve months will be spent working through costs and funding. His challenge is to deliver it “within our existing budget approved by voters – no new taxpayer dollars.” Latest #WatsonWire: It’s time to start the process to get rail to the airport. That’s a sign of success, and a reminder that we can’t build Austin’s future relying on infrastructure designed for its past. We need to envision a successful future and prepare for it. The “Journey With AUS” expansion project is delivering significant improvements in building the kind of airport a growing, globally connected city like Austin needs for decades to come. As we build a better airport, we also need to think about how we’re going to get people there to catch their flights and how to get them out of there to participate in our city. A world-class airport needs a multimodal, world-class transportation system to connect to it. Sure, we need to improve roadway access. And we also should give people more choices about how they travel. We need to have the same long-term vision for Austin light rail that we’ve had for ABIA. An airport rail connection has been part of Austin’s long-term transportation vision for years. With Journey with AUS, we have a chance to plan these projects strategically rather than trying to connect them after the fact. Until now, Austin Transit Partnership (ATP) has appropriately prioritized navigating the Federal Transit Administration (FTA) process for phase 1 of our system. This year, ATP received the important Record of Decision (ROD) from FTA and completed, in record time, the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) process for phase 1. In addition to getting the ROD and completing the NEPA process, I’ve wanted all major design and construction contracts in place and assurance that all of our financial decision-making is guided by a financial stewardship protocol before ATP takes any other big next steps. We have the ROD, the NEPA process is complete, the major contracts are in place, and the financial stewardship protocol is being followed. With these important milestones met, we can now take a major step: ATP will start the planning activities for a potential extension to take Austin light rail all the way to the airport. For years, our Austin Bergstrom International Airport (ABIA or AUS) operated beyond the capacity it was originally designed to accommodate. That’s a sign of success, and a reminder that we can’t build Austin’s future relying on infrastructure designed for its past.

We need to envision a successful future and prepare for it.

The “Journey With AUS” expansion project is delivering significant improvements in building the kind of airport a growing, globally connected city like Austin needs for decades to come.

As we build a better airport, we also need to think about how we’re going to get people there to catch their flights and how to get them out of there to participate in our city.

A world-class airport needs a multimodal, world-class transportation system to connect to it. Sure, we need to improve roadway access. And we also should give people more choices about how they travel.

We need to have the same long-term vision for Austin light rail that we’ve had for ABIA.

An airport rail connection has been part of Austin’s long-term transportation vision for years. With Journey with AUS, we have a chance to plan these projects strategically rather than trying to connect them after the fact.

Until now, Austin Transit Partnership (ATP) has appropriately prioritized navigating the Federal Transit Administration (FTA) process for phase 1 of our system. This year, ATP received the important Record of Decision (ROD) from FTA and completed, in record time, the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) process for phase 1.

In addition to getting the ROD and completing the NEPA process, I’ve wanted all major design and construction contracts in place and assurance that all of our financial decision-making is guided by a financial stewardship protocol before ATP takes any other big next steps.

We have the ROD, the NEPA process is complete, the major contracts are in place, and the financial stewardship protocol is being followed.

With these important milestones met, we can now take a major step: ATP will start the planning activities for a potential extension to take Austin light rail all the way to the airport.
