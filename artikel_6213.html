Ryanair Cancels All Flights In Europe On Christmas Day

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Christmas Day is fast approaching. On that festive day, airlines around the world will collectively cut 16% of flights, with Europe especially affected (-37%). Generally, it is much more common for carriers to scale back activity significantly than to cut flights completely.

One of the exceptions is Ryanair, Europe's largest carrier. OAG data shows that the ultra-low-cost carrier will have zero services on December 25. This is not unusual, as that's the same every year. This could be to give staff a break or because the airline does not think that it'd be worth flying.

Analysis of Ryanair's schedule for Saturday, December 19, to Sunday, December 27, shows that the pan-European operator plans 25,936 movements. Compared to the equivalent period in 2025, activity has jumped by a substantial 24%. Europe's already-largest operator grew by virtually a quarter in 12 months.

What is unchanged is that no flights will operate on December 25. 2,090 movements will exist on Christmas Eve 2026, which has jumped by 19% year-over-year. The volume will surpass the 2,000 services milestone for the first time. Ryanair will continue to have the most short-haul flights in Europe on that day.

It'll operate one in eight of Europe's short-haul flights on December 24. Naturally, flights will be down substantially compared to the prior Thursday, at 22% lower. As easyJet's Christmas Eve offering will be virtually unchanged (-1%), the gap between the two European behemoths will narrow significantly.

On a typical Thursday, the ultra-low-cost carrier's first flight of the day is usually around 5:45 AM. On Christmas Eve, schedule analysis shows that 63 routes are scheduled to leave between 5:45 AM and 5:55 AM. Covering 1,599 nautical miles (2,961 km), the longest link will be from Milan Malpensa to Tenerife South. At just 167 nautical miles (309 km), the shortest service will be from Leeds Bradford to Dublin.

Things will be very different in the evening. On the prior Thursday, the last departure of the day is down to be the 11:45 PM service from Seville, in southern Spain, to Rabat, the capital of Morocco. Not so on Christmas Eve. It'll be FR305, which is due to leave Salzburg, Austria, for London Stansted, Ryanair's top airport — including on December 24 — at 6:00 PM.

Usually, Ryanair's final arrival of the day is at around 1:15 AM, which is based on aircraft that took off the day before. On Christmas Eve, the latest scheduled arrival time is at 6:55 PM, with 31 routes due then (shown in the map above). Once the crew (and everyone else working for the airline) have finished their duties and gone home, there will be enough time to celebrate before midnight.

The airline will return to the air on Saturday, December 26, the so-called Boxing Day. 78 departures are scheduled to leave between 5:45 AM and 5:55 AM. 26 of them will be from UK and Irish airports, including some short legs, such as Edinburgh to Cork. It is likely that some passengers will be worse for wear from the festive day.

Ryanair plans a total of 3,448 movements on December 26, up by 27% compared to Boxing Day 2025. 1,175 routes will be available: 1,054 international and 121 domestic. With 11 outbound flights, Dublin to Gatwick will have the most activity.

Ryanair's movements across its entire network will increase from December 27, before dropping slightly for New Year's Day. But while the carrier's flights on Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, and Boxing Day will be higher than in 2025, New Year's Day will have fewer services than on January 1, 2026.
