Ahead of potentially signing for Newcastle United, Omar Marong and Alieu Saine flew to Tyneside at the start of November.

The net cast ever wider for young talent and the pair arriving for what was reported as a month-long trial for Newcastle United to evaluate them.

Omar Marong is the captain of the Gambia Under 17s and Alieu Saine is their star attacking player. The duo made headlines in September when they were instrumental in their country winning the WAFU A U-17 Tournament in September. Omar Marong, a 16-year-old central midfielder, captained Gambia Under 17s to that success, and winger Alieu Saine scored four goals to be joint top scorer in the tournament. The Baby Scorpions winning the tournament by defeating Senegal in the final.

United signed Gambian winger Yankuba Minteh (pictured at the top of this article) as an 18-year-old in June 2023, he was slightly further along his young career than Omar Marong and Alieu Saine. Minteh had played a relative handful of matches for Danish club Odense before moving to Newcastle United for a reported £7m transfer fee. He spent a year on loan at Feyenoord before a PSR forced sale for £33m to Brighton before he’d even played a single minute of football for Newcastle.

However, it has now been revealed that Omar Marong and Alieu Saine will not be following the path of Yankubah Minteh.

An update from The Mail saying that their information is that after a couple of weeks on trial with NUFC, the Gambian teenagers haven’t done enough to convince the club to progress with any deal(s).

The media update stating that the young duo impressed across the last couple of weeks but the club’s staff deciding that the existing young Newcastle United Academy players have greater potential than Omar Marong and Alieu Saine.

A friendly was set up last week, with Alieu Saine contributing a couple of assists in a kick about against the i2i International Soccer Academy, but neither of the trialists really standing out.

Newcastle United are continuing to grow their recruitment team around the world and it is expected that many more young players will arrive from Africa and elsewhere in the future, to allow club staff to assess them at close quarters on Tyneside.
