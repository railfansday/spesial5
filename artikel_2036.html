Thomas Tuchel’s 3 Lions levels above the World Cup winners as it ends Spain 2 England 3

The home fans inside the Riyadh Air Metropolitano stunned to see their World Cup winners outclassed.

This wasn’t a fluke, England were by far the better team and it was Spain who got so lucky to lose only by one goal, on home soil.

This victory meant it was 10 away wins in a row, the longest ever winning run on the road in their history.

England produced a truly superb display and the only puzzle in the opening half is how it took to just before the break for the Three Lions to take the lead.

From the first whistle England pressed high with numbers and could and should have been ahead in the opening minutes as the home goal led a charmed life and Simon in the Spanish goal made some brilliant saves.

This opening half was electric from Thomas Tuchel’s side and so exciting to watch, a world away from what we saw with Gareth Southgate.

It was of course Harry Kane who eventually made the breakthrough, very clever play saw him win the penalty and he dispatched it with the keeper having no chance.

Any thoughts of England sitting back to try and defend the lead were quickly dispelled, as the visitors continued in the same attacking vein after the break.

Eight minutes after the restart it was 2-0, Konsa powering home a header from Rice’s corner.

Twelve minutes into the second half it became 3-0, as a brilliant team move was finished off by Ben White.

Everybody had played well and Spain lucky to be only three down.

Always in football though, one moment can potentially totally change a match.

Quansah made an error to gift the Croatia equaliser on Thursday, he made just as bad a one here.

On 63 minutes he dawdled on the ball in the middle of the pitch and gifted possession,

Elliot Anderson had been excellent up to this point but then had a moment of madness himself, Lamine Yamal looked as if he had maybe overran the ball and got too close to James Trafford as he was left with a one on one after Quansah’s blunder. However, Anderson pulled Yamal back and it was a double disaster, a red card and a spot-kick.

Oyarzabal scored the penalty and from 3-0 up (should have been more) it suddenly was 3-1 and England a man down with around 30 minutes left to play.

It was now of course Spain on top and when Anthony Gordon repeated Quansah’s mistake with 15 minutes of normal time remaining, 20 including added time, it looked like this one was going to get away from England. Gordon caught in possession when dawdling, Yamal fed Williams and now only 3-2 to England.

It was then nailbiting stuff and some heroic defending and top notch saves from James Trafford got the job done.

The Spanish players and fans will no doubt be kidding themselves that in the end they were unlucky to lose.

Reality is though that for me Thomas Tuchel is a class manager and it was only those individual mistakes by Quansah, Gordon and Anderson that prevented a total annihilation of the World Cup winners by England.

This could and should have easily ended a four or five goal winning margin for England and they now look capable of beating anybody on their day.

Apart from those three individual errors that threatened to undermine this excellent team performance, everybody played well.

Pickford has been decent down the years for England but Trafford now looks ready to be number one.

Whilst Ben White did really well and one thing for sure, he is so much better at right-back than media heartthrob Alexander-Arnold, a defender who can’t defend. If Tino Livramento can ever stay fit, the Newcastle defender will be a real contender for that spot moving forward.

This win meant England topped their Nations League group.

The quarter-finals of the Nations League will take place in March 2027 with the draw taking place this Friday (20 November).

After topping their group it means England will take on one of the teams who finished second in the other tier A groups, so it could be one of Belgium, Greece or Denmark.
