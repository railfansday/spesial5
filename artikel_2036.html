Xabi Alonso has experienced an inconsistent start to his time in charge of Chelsea.

The Blues won their opening three games of the season but have won just four of the last 10 across all competitions since then.

The Spaniard's appointment attracted huge excitement when he arrived in the summer, having left Real Madrid in January, but his team selections have prompted frustration.

Alonso initially started with a back-three but has switched to a back-four, with Chelsea seeming to gain slightly in defensive solidity since then but also lose plenty going forward.

Players like Estevao have struggled for game time while the likes of Jordan Henderson, who it was thought had been brought in for dressing room presence alone, has been ever-present of late.

But looking back at Alonso's start to life at Bayer Leverkusen, there is cause to be optimistic for Chelsea fans.

No Chelsea boss in their last seven appointments has picked up fewer points after 10 games than Xabi Alonso 🫣📉 pic.twitter.com/mILWx3n1RB

By the time Alonso left Leverkusen, he was a legend. He had led his team to the Bundesliga title, winning it for the first time in club history.

And he had done it in style, almost winning the treble in an season in which Leverkusen remained unbeaten until the Europa League final, setting a remarkable 51-game streak.

But upon his arrival, in 2022, Alonso was regarded as a risky appointment after almost all of his experience had come with Real Sociedad's B team.

The opening months were difficult. Leverkusen won just one of the Spaniard's opening seven games, conceding 14 goals in the process.

The former midfielder experimented with his system but struggled to shake the inconsistent results until February, when Leverkusen were on a streak of four losses in their last five games.

Then, something clicked, and Alonso's side won 10 of their next 13. The next season would mark their unbeaten campaign.

There is no guarantee such a transformation happens at Chelsea, but slow starts are not out of the ordinary for their new manager.

Clearly, Alonso takes time to embed his tactical approach when he is appointed at a new club.

Though he actually started well at Real Madrid, winning 24 of the 34 matches he oversaw, there was little patience for the Spaniard's methods.

At Chelsea, it appears that he will be given more backing and more time to implement his ideas.

The Blues will hope to see the benefit of that in the coming months.
