It appears we may not have long to wait now for the much anticipated new Newcastle United stadium decision to be announced.

A media exclusive on Wednesday giving a headline making update.

Craig Hope at The Mail revealing that talks over a period of time between the Newcastle United hierarchy and Goldman Sachs reached a key point this month.

With senior representatives from the American investment banking organisation travelling to Tyneside this month.

The Mail reporting that their informed sources have told them that this significant meeting was to finalise ‘the proposed funding structure for a spectacular new 65,000-seater home on Leazes Park’ for Newcastle United, rather than an attempt to rebuild/restructure parts of St James’ Park.

The report talks of a brand new Newcastle United stadium costing £1.3billion super stadium – the strongest indication yet that the club are preparing to leave St James’ Park.

I have never thought that a major redevelopment of the existing stadium was viable, the cost would be huge compared to how few extra seats it would allow and just as importantly, the limitations on upgrading the existing ‘normal’ seating areas and the hospitality ones to the level of modern new stadiums that have been built in recent times. Not to mention the fact that if say the Gallowgate end was redeveloped, it would lead to a hugely reduced capacity for a significant amount of time.

The man from The Mail has also revealed that the meeting coincided with Jacobo Solis having arrived on Tyneside. The report detailing that ‘the influential Public Investment Fund executive and club director who is rarely seen in the city, was also present. The Spaniard’s involvement is viewed as extremely significant by those close to the project, and we understand an announcement on the club’s stadium plans is now ‘very close’.’

Back in September 2025 The Times revealed: ‘There is now a core of PIF executives who have been involved with Newcastle United since day one of its ownership, including Jacobo Solis, who, along with being head of European direct investments at PIF, is now also on the Newcastle board, having joined at the start of 2025.’

The Mail report that: ‘The proposed funding model combines borrowing, owner investment and potentially outside investors, with the debt sitting with Newcastle rather than the ownership. That makes the involvement of Goldman Sachs particularly significant as the club look to turn their ambitious plans into a financially viable reality.’

The media exclusive concludes with an expectation that an official announcement on the new Newcastle United stadium will be made early in 2027.
