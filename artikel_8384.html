3 Unprepared Hikers Rescued From Continental Divide After 11-Hour Search

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

An 11-hour overnight search and rescue operation in Colorado’s high country brought three stranded hikers safely off the Continental Divide early on the morning of November 15 after winter weather caught the group unprepared.

Grand County Search and Rescue (GCSAR) received the initial call at 5:01 p.m. on Saturday, November 14, according to a social media update. The hikers had lost their way amid heavy snow and treacherous trail conditions near the High Lonesome Trail, which sits near the Continental Divide Trail mile marker 1,340.

The group pitched camp at about 11,600 feet without adequate overnight winter gear or fire-starting supplies.

An initial response crew traveled up Rollins Pass Road on ATVs before continuing on foot deeper into the backcountry. Upon locating the trio, rescuers found them cold and exhausted and immediately provided warming gear, food, and support.

Rather than waiting until sunrise, field leaders determined that keeping the hikers exposed at 11,600 feet in worsening conditions posed too great a danger. A second team was deployed to help walk the group out in the dark.

Rescuers hike into the night. Photo via GCSAR.

Rescuers and hikers reached the ATVs by 3:10 a.m. on Sunday, and all personnel cleared the field by 4:18 a.m.

Backcountry Conditions and Safety Reminders

In their report, GCSAR notes that full winter conditions have already arrived at upper elevations, bringing deep snow, high winds, freezing temperatures, and rapidly shifting weather that can easily obscure trails above treeline.

Officials advise anyone venturing into the mountains to pack proper winter layers and carry the ten essentials.

GCSAR is staffed entirely by unpaid volunteers. The organization never bills rescue subjects and funds its gear, training, and rescue operations through public donations.
