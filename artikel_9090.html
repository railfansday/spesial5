New York City’s Transit System Has the State’s Greatest ‘Underground Museum.’ Here’s Why It’s Worth Visiting.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

New York has the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Museum of Modern Art, the Whitney Museum, the Frick Collection, and countless other great art institutions. Yet one of the city’s most well-rounded ones is an “underground museum” that can be found largely in the subway, with certain pieces also visible at Metro-North and Long Island Rail Road train stops.

That was the terminology used by Tina Vaz, director of the MTA’s Arts & Design program, in a talk held this past Friday at the PMC Studio as part of Art Week NYC, a festival of programming put on across the city by ARTnews, Artforum, and Art in America. “This is a dynamic transit environment, but even in all of the noise and movement and motion, the works get through and people connect with them,” Vaz said. She cited a survey in which 80 percent of respondents said they noticed the artworks at MTA, Metro-North, and LIRR stops, which have been done by artists ranging from William Wegman to Yayoi Kusama. That people were aware of these works, Vaz said, is “double proof of concept.”

Vaz’s latest commissions are by artists Ivan Forde, Trevor Paglen, and Howardena Pindell, who are respectively in the process of making new works for the Broadway Junction subway stop and the Metro-North’s Southeast and Botanical Garden stops. Forde and Pindell joined Vaz on Friday to discuss their commissions.

Forde’s, a mosaic called Bedtime Story for Brownsville: The Fish Who Carried the Moon (2026), grew out of his sketches and poetry. In his mosaic, the Moon is what he called a “main character.” “The Moon has lost her way, and she fell into the sea,” he said. “These fish, they come and they help her, and they guide her back to the sky … It’s really thinking about structures of epic poetry.”

Pindell is making a glass installation based on photographs she took over the course of a day at the New York Botanical Garden. “Once they were developed, I cut them into sections and laid them out along part of the station wall,” she said. “Then, there were arrows put in.” Through the installation, “you are seeing the inside of the garden, both the domed building as well as along the paths.”
