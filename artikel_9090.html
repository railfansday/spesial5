Rachel Whiteread Has Designed the 2027 Brit Awards Trophy

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Dame Rachel Whiteread, who was the first woman artist to win the Turner Prize in 1993, has been selected to design the 2027 Brit Awards trophy. The trophy has previously been designed by the likes of Damien Hirst, Zaha Hadid, Es Devlin and Anish Kapoor.

The trophy will be bestowed upon musicians nominated for the prestigious award ceremony taking place on February 27, 2027. Each trophy takes inspiration from the image of Britannia, a tall, elegant female figure that personifies Britain. For her design, Whiteread has inverted the statue, exposing its silver lining with punched holes throughout. “I exposed the interior of the statue—as an artist would do with their music,” said the artist in a statement. “I accepted the invitation to design next year’s Brit Award trophy, as I saw this as a natural extension of my work—to continue to celebrate and recognize British art and creativity, and to have an opportunity to apply my own perspective within such an iconic British cultural institution.” Related Articles Nnena Kalu Wins Turner Prize, Becoming First Learning-Disabled Artist to Take UK's Top Art Award Rachel Whiteread's Collaboration with French Silversmith Company Inspired by Corrugated Cardboard

Whiteread said she also agreed to design the statue in an effort to safeguard arts and culture, which she described as being “under very real threat.” “The Brits have celebrated musical creativity and artistry for decades now. We have some fantastically creative musicians who have produced music that lays their souls bare, provides hope in times of darkness, or speaks directly about a moment in time,” she added.

In designing the trophy, Whiteread joins a prestigious list of other artists who have given the statue new life: Dame Peter Blake, Tracey Emin, Vivienne Westwood and Yinka Ilori. In 1997, Whiteread became the first woman to represent Britain at the Venice Biennale, and in 2001, she became the third artist to have her work placed on the Fourth Plinth in Trafalgar Square, which took the form of an inverted resin recreation of the plinth.
