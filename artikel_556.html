Can GTA 6 teach men to be better boyfriends? We asked a relationship therapist.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Since Rockstar released previews of Grand Theft Auto VI this summer, social media has buzzed about the relationship system at the heart of the game. While some critics jumped to say that playing GTA 6 is cheating on your significant other, others speculated that playing the game might actually make players better at IRL connections — especially considering that young adults have less experience.

Now that GTA 6 is out, discussion of the latter has resurfaced on X:

But can making small gestures of affection in a video game actually teach players anything about being a better partner IRL? To answer that question, I reached out to sex and couples therapist Ilana Grines, whom I previously spoke to about whether playing the game is cheating.

"We have to be really careful about what we're claiming that video games can actually do for us," Grines said. It's useful when individuals are exposed to behaviors that are typically associated with relationship satisfaction as a whole — like spending time together — but, she said, "your girlfriend doesn't have a meter on her forehead that increased the more you...show her attention."

Part of the online conversation stems from the relationship between GTA 6 protagonists Jason and Lucia, whose connection develops through the player's actions. While players can choose to keep their relationship platonic, the game still tracks their bond. Holding hands, buying flowers, giving compliments, sending texts, and running errands together (which may or may not involve committing crimes) can all improve their relationship score.

The relationship meter gives players a sense of where Jason and Lucia stand with one another, Mashable's editorial director Eric Miller explained to me, a self-professed non-gamer. While previous GTA games have featured romantic relationships and family dynamics, GTA 6 takes things a step further by allowing players to actively shape the protagonists' relationship. Depending on their actions, Jason and Lucia can be running on empty (1/4), partners in crime (2/4), crazy in love (3/4), or hot together (4/4).

Grines is skeptical that simply performing these actions in a game can teach emotional intelligence. "You're literally playing a game when you could be participating in your relationship," Grines continued. That's another line of thinking that's echoed on social media today: That people (mostly men) are doing chores for Lucia instead of their IRL girlfriend.

One of Grines' clients told her that his girlfriend isn't letting him buy the game — but not because of Lucia. It's actually because of the strip club scenes and how realistic the characters look. The girlfriend isn't comfortable with him going to strip clubs in real life, so why would she be comfortable with him going to one in a video game? As in our discussion over whether playing GTA 6 is cheating, Grines said she's entitled to set a boundary.

Real relationships, of course, are more complicated than any in-game relationship meter can capture. And Grines warns against having unrealistic expectations for how video games can improve romance. While seeing a healthy relationship modeled in front of you can be useful, she said that modeling doesn't always create behavioral change. Being a good boyfriend in the game doesn't factor in a real partner's own preferences, emotions, and agency. (For example, your real girlfriend may hate flowers!)

That doesn't mean GTA 6 can't bring couples closer together. Gaming can be a shared experience, as PCMag's Jordan Minor demonstrated when he played the game with his wife.

"It's great that people can experiment and they can repeat actions that may improve their relationship satisfaction," she said. "The real test will be if they can take it out of the video game and into their real lives."

Not everyone likes this element of the game. Right-wing (or right-wing adjacent) influencers Asmongold and Clavicular (who was indicted on two rape charges yesterday) expressed their distaste for GTA 6. The former called the game a "cuck simulator" while playing it on stream, and Clavicular said the game is "insanely cucked."

Then again, GTA 6 is the highest-rated game of all time, so the haters are in the minority.
