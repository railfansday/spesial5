Breakfast News: What’s Nvidia’s Biggest Risk?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Nvidia falls as board meets to discuss Huang, AMD and Broadcom gain in pre-market, and more…

Nvidia (NVDA +2.87%) fell over 6% in pre-market trading after the WSJ reported that the board is preparing to oust co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang. Bloomberg Law further reported that the board is working with an outside firm to find successors and is weighing a temporary chief.

Nvidia's artificial intelligence (AI) dominance hasn't suddenly disappeared, but its key-man risk is real. The Nov. 17 quarterly earnings report and conference call may be among the most closely watched in the company’s history.

During a special Fool24 session last night for members, Team Rule Breakers analyst Dan Caplinger compared the situation at Nvidia to a familiar case, and added a warning.

Think back to when Steve Jobs handed Apple (AAPL +2.04%) to Tim Cook. It looks like an easy call now, but it didn’t feel that way at the time. “Don’t forget just how uncertain things were at the time,” Dan reminded Fools.

Nvidia may come through this in good shape. Even so, shareholders will probably face months of open questions. Investors who can live with that uncertainty and stay focused on the business will be in a better position to make clear decisions, whichever way this goes.

Nvidia shares fell, but Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +7.32%) rose more than 4% after hours Wednesday while Broadcom (AVGO +3.99%) gained over 2%. Investors are already weighing who could benefit if Nvidia loses its longtime leader. Both stocks are recommended by Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers.

Nvidia may have a Jensen problem, but its rivals could suddenly be looking at a bigger market opportunity.

Team Rule BreakersSay you’ve set aside $3,000 for a stock. Split it into three $1,000 pieces. Buy the first today.

Buy the second on the same date next month or next quarter, at whatever price it trades. Buy the third one period later, on the same terms.

Because the next move can’t be reliably predicted, the method doesn’t ask you to try. The calendar decides when you buy.

If prices rise, part of your money is already working. If they fall, your remaining thirds will likely buy cheaper and bring down the average you paid. Motley Fool co-founder David Gardner calls the approach “an incredibly effective win-win way to get invested in any volatile stock that’s trading high.”

We think buying in thirds is worth its small cost when two things are true. You believe the company will be worth meaningfully more three or more years from now. Without a plan, you’d keep waiting or put everything in at once on a hunch.
