Energy Transfer vs. Enbridge: Which High-Yield Pipeline Stock Could Make You Richer Over the Next 10 Years?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Both pipeline companies offer attractive dividends and long-term growth opportunities, but one could make you richer over the next decade.

Energy Transfer (ET -0.03%) and Enbridge (ENB +0.15%) are two of North America's leading energy infrastructure companies. They own extensive pipeline networks that generate recurring cash flow, providing them with the funds to reinvest in expanding their operations and pay attractive dividends. Energy Transfer currently yields more than 7%, while Enbridge yields over 5.5%.

Those high payouts could make both companies compelling wealth-building investments, particularly for investors who reinvest their dividends. However, their growth prospects, business models, and risk profiles differ. Here's how they compare and which could make more money for investors over the next decade.

Energy Transfer is a large, integrated master limited partnership (MLP). The company's diversified operations span the U.S. and include crude oil, natural gas liquids, refined products, and natural gas pipelines and related assets. Its assets generate very stable cash flow, with about 90% coming from fee-based sources.

The MLP generated $2.5 billion of distributable cash flow during the third quarter, an eye-popping 31% increase from the prior-year period. That covered its 7%-yielding distribution with $1.3 billion to spare. The company can easily afford to grow its distribution, which it has increased for 20 straight quarters and by over 3% in the past year.

Energy Transfer's robust cash flows also enable it to invest heavily in expanding its operations. It currently plans to invest about $5.5 billion in growth capital projects this year and another $7 billion in 2027. The company has growth capital projects secured through 2030. These investments support the MLP's plan to grow its distribution by 3% to 5% each year.

Energy Transfer combines a higher starting yield with a substantial growth pipeline, giving it attractive total-return potential.

Enbridge is a diversified pipeline and utility company based in Canada. It operates North America's longest crude oil and liquids transportation system, moves 20% of all the natural gas consumed in the U.S., operates the largest gas utility by volume, and is a leading renewable energy producer. These businesses generate very stable cash flow, with 98% of it coming from government-regulated rate structures or take-or-pay contracts.

The Canadian energy infrastructure giant pays out 60% to 70% of its stable cash flow as dividends, retaining the remainder to fund its continued expansion. That excess free cash flow, along with its strong balance sheet, provides it with more than 11 billion Canadian dollars ($7.7 billion) in annual investment capacity. That gives it the funding capacity to support its multi-billion-dollar growth capital project backlog, with projects scheduled to enter commercial service through the early 2030s. Enbridge estimates that it can deliver around 5% annual cash flow per share growth, which could support similar annual dividend increases. The company has increased its dividend for 31 consecutive years (in Canadian dollars).

Enbridge's diversified infrastructure portfolio provides greater earnings stability and a long history of dividend growth.

Energy Transfer and Enbridge should both deliver steadily rising cash flows in the coming years, enabling them to continue increasing their high-yielding payouts. They're both targeting mid-single-digit dividend growth rates, which should support similar annual stock price appreciation. Thanks to Energy Transfer's higher starting yield, the MLP has the potential to deliver higher total annualized returns compared to Enbridge over the next decade. I did assume the MLP would deliver slightly higher annual price appreciation (4.9% vs. 4.4%) due to its lower current valuation multiple.

Investors are taking on a bit more risk by investing in Energy Transfer because it doesn't have quite the diversification and predictability as Enbridge. There are also some tax complications with investing in an MLP, as it sends a Schedule K-1 Federal tax form each year. However, investing in Enbridge outside an IRA can create its own tax issues (including a 15% Canadian withholding tax).

Energy Transfer and Enbridge both have the potential to make long-term investors wealthier through their high-yielding payouts and growing energy infrastructure businesses. However, I'd give Energy Transfer the edge for investors seeking to make more money over the next decade. Its higher starting yield and expanding asset base could produce greater wealth, especially when you reinvest its lucrative distributions. Enbridge is an equally attractive alternative for investors who prioritize more diversified and predictable cash flows, especially in an IRA.
