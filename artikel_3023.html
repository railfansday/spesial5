How Amari Allen, Alabama basketball rebounding 'saved' Tide vs Arkansas State

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Arkansas State coach Ryan Pannone came into Coleman Coliseum knowing how Nate Oats wants his players to operate better than most who might brave his annual non-conference gauntlet. When the former Alabama basketball assistant met his predecessor for the first time on opposite sidelines, Pannone seemed determined to make sure his old boss couldn't play his game.

For much of the first half, although the No. 18 Crimson Tide emerged plus-12 on the glass, Arkansas State made the home team appear more vulnerable than bound for victory. While Alabama ultimately established a dominant 101-86 win to go 2-0 on the season, it was overshadowed by a wanting shooting performance that defied the odds.

Here are three takeaways from how it all went down.

The Crimson Tide trailed for 11:15 of the first half. In the final four minutes or so leading up to halftime, the team put together a 21-2 run − highlighted by a buzzer-beater three from Boise State transfer Drew Fielder. Still, Alabama went into the locker room with a 29.8% shooting percentage.

Volume threes were in full force, but Alabama converted just 11 of 36 attempts as former Nate Oats assistant Ryan Pannone opted for a more efficient perimeter approach. The Red Wolves finished the first half 7-for-18 on threes.

After the game, Oats indicated that he wasn't interested in discouraging his players from taking open looks. His halftime message?

Oats recalled telling the team, "We're not passing up any open looks. Just keep shooting them."

Yes, in fact, Oats said "...our rebounding saved us." In the same answer about effectiveness on the boards, Oats said rebounding had been an emphasis, but the team is "still not very good at it right now."

Pannone, however, believed he saw one of the best offensive rebounding teams that Oats has had in his tenure at Alabama.

Alabama collected 20 offensive rebounds in the first half, generating approximately 24 second-chance points. The Crimson Tide registered only five more O-boards in the second half and still won by eight on the offensive glass.

"I did feel like we let up a little bit in the second half," Oats said. However, he gave credit to Kentucky transfer Brandon Garrison, who claimed his second hard hat for his performance against Arkansas State after taking the first of the year against Sam Houston.

In addition to dominating blue-collar points, Garrison supported lead rebounder Amari Allen with 10 boards.

While Oats admitted having Aden Holloway, "the best shooter in the country," would've helped during the first half, Alabama learned its offense doesn't have to be firing on all cylinders for Allen to take charge.

The Crimson Tide struggled to find its touch, but the sophomore guard had it just about everywhere else. Allen finished with 16 points, shooting 5-for-11 with three triples, as well as 13 rebounds. Of Alabama's 19 assists against the Red Wolves, Allen accounted for 11 to notch the fifth triple double in program history.

"Think about all the NBA players that have come through Alabama throughout the years," Oats said. "To be the fourth player in program history to have a triple-double, you put yourself in pretty elite company."

Oats credited Allen's decision-making and selflessness with helping Alabama generate better offensive opportunities.

"I thought he really led the charge in the ball movement," Oats said.

For a Crimson Tide team still searching for consistency from its revamped roster, Allen's ability to contribute across the stat sheet continues to be every bit as important as his role as a scorer.

And with Holloway returning against Oakland on Monday, Nov. 9, Allen's all-around production gives Alabama another dimension as its rotation approaches full strength.
