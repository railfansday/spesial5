Pharmaceutical ads may influence which drug AI recommends, new research finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Next time you ask AI for medical advice, you may want to consider whether it’s been influenced by Big Pharma. Prescription drug advertisements are everywhere and may affect AI’s recommendations, according to a study published in The Lancet Digital Health this week.

The study authors conducted experiments with a dozen consumer-facing large language models (LLMs), including versions of Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT—made by Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI, respectively. 

Clinical guidance or commercial influence?

The models were tested across 33 scenarios that each included a clinical case description and multiple treatment options. In some cases, the AI models were also given an ad for one of the treatment options. The ad’s content included information about the drug and its uses, but did not specifically direct the AI model to recommend the drug.

When exposed to an ad, AI models recommended the treatment in question an average of 47.6% of the time, compared with 35% when no ad was present. However, the size of these shifts varied among the 12 models.

Although the models often changed their recommendation when an ad was present, in most cases they still selected one of the appropriate options. The outputs only disclosed the ad’s presence about 30% of the time. 

“An answer can be clinically reasonable and still raise an important question about why the AI arrived at that answer,” study author Dr. Girish Nadkarni, a professor of medicine and system chair of the Windreich Department of Artificial Intelligence and Human Health at Mount Sinai’s Icahn School of Medicine, said in a statement. “As AI becomes part of how people get health information, we need to be able to distinguish clinical guidance from commercial influence and be transparent about when advertising is part of the interaction.”

Pharmaceutical companies spent more than $6 billion on TV advertising alone in 2024, according to a report by the pharmaceutical data, analytics, and consulting firm IQVIA, which said that direct-to-consumer advertising “directly influences patient requests and prescription rates, driving real action in healthcare.”

Most countries ban drug ads

All high-income countries, save for the U.S. and New Zealand, ban direct-to-consumer ads for prescription drugs.

In the U.S., prescription drugs consume more than 30% of commercial time during major networks’ evening news programs, according to a 2025 press release from the U.S. Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions.

Last year, ranking member Sen. Bernie Sanders introduced the End Prescription Drug Ads Now Act, co-signed by six other senators, which would ban such advertising, including on social media. 

In practice, the Lancet study said, AI models could be exposed to drug ads “through user prompts, application-added context, retrieved online material, or inadvertent or malicious insertion,” and that, “unlike television or print advertising, which is subject to U.S. Food and Drug Administration oversight, AI-mediated promotion might not fit existing categories.” 

However, the study did not examine whether these measured effects translate into real-world changes in patient requests or prescriptions.

The chatbot will see you now

In a recent Doximity survey of 3,379 U.S. physicians, 54% said they had moderate or high levels of trust in AI’s ability to recommend treatment or management. Three-quarters of respondents also said patients had become more likely to propose a diagnosis or treatment plan suggested by AI over the past six months. 

“Clinicians might defer to algorithmic recommendations even when they conflict with clinical judgment,” the study’s authors said, and an appropriate but ad-influenced suggestion “might provide little reason to question it.”
