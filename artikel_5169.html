The crypto market remains under pressure, with Bitcoin dropping to a four-month low around $62,000 on November 15. SHIB, SOL and XLM all printed new lows on November 14, yet selling volume stayed well below earlier spikes, and oversold RSI readings suggest a recovery could be near, even though fundamental catalysts are still missing.

Thankfully, Shiba Inu's bearish rally might be fading sooner rather than later. The price's most recent red candle wasn't followed by a surge in selling volume, which could hint at a potential reversal.

On November 14, a single daily candle took SHIB below the July bottom around $0.0000041, with the wick reaching $0.0000037. At press time, the asset is trading at $0.00000387, the lowest level on the chart since March. Selling volume that day stayed well below the late July and August spikes.

Usually, when the price hits the bottom like that, the point of previously growing demand might become a source of additional selling pressure. For SHIB, it's the July zone, where buyers stepped in last time.

If we dive inside the 4HR price chart, we can clearly see a price/volume divergence, which is a good sign for bulls. Moving averages are diverging, so a quick retrace isn't yet possible. There's also potential for a quicker recovery thanks to RSI, which is sitting in the oversold zone on the daily chart.

SHIB is trading below all of its moving averages, with the 200 EMA hanging at around $0.0000054. Bitcoin's drop to a four-month low around $62,000 on November 15 didn't help either.

Despite the death cross on the chart solidifying, there's a good chance it's not critical. The price is showing negative performance one way or another, but it's not going to add more fuel to the bearish furnace.

The cross formed near $96, where a shorter moving average dropped below the 200 EMA, and another moving average is about to follow. SOL is trading at $71.70 after a wick to around $67.50 on November 14. Since its late September peak near $125, Solana has lost over 40% of its value.

First of all, death crosses aren't as scary as they used to be. Second, the signal is inherently lagging, so it only reflects what was already priced in. It's more of a guideline for those who are looking for an entry.

The chart shows a negative dynamic indeed, but, in a similar fashion to Shiba Inu, the bearish rally printed the first descending volume bar in a long time. Though it's only a single candle, it might be the start of a recovery.

The price is already oversold, but the bottom might be closer to $62, which is also reinforced by RSI. Back in June, $62 was the point where SOL bottomed out before the summer recovery.

Stellar has reached a potential price bottom where it might finally find ground for a retrace. The volume is relatively low, but the main issue is the lack of foundational recovery fuel.

XLM is trading at $0.1384 after dropping to $0.13 on November 14. That's below the May low around $0.142, the zone from which XLM spiked toward $0.30 in June. The RSI dipped below 30 during the drop. Since the late September high near $0.235, XLM has lost around 40%, while selling volume stayed far below the June and July spikes.

Short-term moving averages are crossing longer-term moving averages, and the issue here is that it's a signal of short-term aggravation. All of them are now above the price, with the 200 EMA at around $0.184.

The XLM update coming on November 23 is most likely priced in and unlikely to change the price direction fundamentally. Protocol 30, codenamed Bell, goes to a mainnet validator vote that day.

It adds post-quantum signature checks and muxed addresses for Soroban smart contracts and prepares the network for faster ledger timing. The upgrade has already been live on testnet since early November.

The key here is the crypto market recovery in general.
