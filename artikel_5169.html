TL;DR: Bitdeer posted record third-quarter revenue of $320.7 million, up 89% year over year, while reporting a $125.5 million net loss. Its stated AI-cloud backlog approached $2.9 billion, highlighting demand potential but not revenue already earned or cash already collected.

Bitdeer has delivered one of the clearest examples yet of a Bitcoin mining business trying to transform itself into a provider of broader computing infrastructure. Its latest quarter brought record revenue and a sizable artificial-intelligence cloud order pipeline. It also remained deeply unprofitable under standard accounting measures.

In its November 10 third-quarter results announcement, the company reported $320.7 million in revenue, an 89% increase from the year-earlier period. Adjusted EBITDA rose to about $69.6 million, while the GAAP net loss reached approximately $125.5 million.

Those measures differ, and the gap requires careful reading. Management highlighted changes in the fair value of digital-asset-related borrowing, including a $54.6 million noncash item, as one factor in the accounting result.

The figure likely to attract the most attention is Bitdeer’s reported AI-cloud backlog of approximately $2.9 billion. A backlog represents contracted or expected future services under the company’s reporting definitions, rather than cash already received or recognized quarterly revenue.

The amount eventually converted into sales will depend on contract delivery, customer demand, capacity coming online and the terms attached to commitments. Investors should seek disclosures about timing and any conditions that could delay or reduce the total.

Nevertheless, a substantial backlog can signal customer interest for specialized infrastructure. That is particularly valuable in a business where constructing or equipping data centers involves significant upfront spending.

Bitdeer reported production of 3,855 Bitcoin during the quarter and said it held 47 Bitcoin at quarter-end. Those figures help distinguish operating output from Bitcoin retained on the balance sheet. Production can be high even if a firm sells most of the coins to fund operations, equipment or other projects.

The company also reported approximately $213.7 million in cash, cash equivalents and restricted cash. A cash balance should be considered alongside capital commitments, financing costs and planned expansion rather than treated as unencumbered profits from AI.

NewsBTC has reported on Riot’s decision to sell Bitcoin as it pursues major AI infrastructure contracts. The miners are pursuing different projects, but both illustrate a sector balancing near-term funding needs with investments that may generate future revenue.

Our coverage of Soluna and Bitdeer’s Texas infrastructure agreement adds a concrete example of why access to powered sites and commercial partnerships remains important as the competition for computing capacity grows.

Adjusted EBITDA may help investors evaluate operating performance before certain expenses and valuation changes. It is not identical to net income or free cash flow. A company can report a positive adjusted measure and still incur losses, financing costs and heavy investment requirements.

That is why the $125.5 million net loss deserves to stand alongside the $320.7 million revenue headline. Both are true, and neither alone gives a complete picture.

Bitdeer’s next reporting periods need to show when AI-cloud backlog turns into delivered services, how those services contribute to margins and how much capital they consume. Improvements in mining efficiency and power costs will also remain relevant.

The latest results point to striking revenue momentum and a potentially meaningful new business line. They do not yet demonstrate that the shift toward AI has resolved the financial volatility historically associated with Bitcoin mining.
