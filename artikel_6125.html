Buy Southwest Rapid Rewards Points For 50% Off (1.5 Cents Each): Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Looking to buy Southwest points? Rapid Rewards is currently offering up to a 50% discount on purchased points, which is an opportunity to acquire them for just 1.5 cents each. Is that a good deal?

While there has long been value in strategically buying points, the concept of a revenue based frequent flyer program selling points can be tougher to rationalize. So that I don’t bury the lede, let me say that I’d only buy points in a very narrow set of circumstances, like if trying to top off a points balance for a specific redemption.

Link: Buy Southwest Rapid Rewards points with this promotion for 50% off

Between 12:01AM CT on November 19 and 11:59PM CT on December 31, 2026, Southwest Rapid Rewards is offering a discount on purchased points. With Southwest, it’s common for different members to be eligible for different offers, so you’ll have to log into your Rapid Rewards account to see what you’re eligible for.

However, the best version of the offer to buy Southwest points provides up to a 50% discount, structured as follows:

Ordinarily you can buy Southwest points for 3.0 cents each, and that price is inclusive of the 7.5% federal excise tax. Given the limit of being able to purchase up to 100,000 Rapid Rewards points per day, that means if you maxed out this promotion, you could buy 100,000 points for $1,500, which is a cost of 1.5 cents per point.

Southwest sells points in increments of 1,000 points, with 2,000 points being the minimum. Ordinarily there’s a daily maximum of buying 60,000 points, but during this promotion, that cap has been increased to 100,000 points.

Just in the interest of being thorough, some Rapid Rewards members may be eligible for smaller percentage discounts, so let me crunch the numbers on those potential offers:

Southwest has no annual limit on how many Rapid Rewards points you can buy. The only restriction is that you can ordinarily buy at most 60,000 points per calendar day. During this promotion, that cap has been increased to 100,000 points, though.

Note that the purchase of Southwest points is non-refundable, and buying Southwest points doesn’t count toward Rapid Rewards elite status or Companion Pass qualification. Purchased points typically post nearly instantly, but in some cases, it can take up to 72 hours for them to post.

Southwest Rapid Rewards frequently offers promotions on purchasing points. When the program does have a buy points promotion, the 50% discount offer is pretty standard. So I’d recommend buying points based on when you have a relatively immediate use for redeeming them, rather than anything else.

If you’d like to buy Southwest Airlines points, note that purchases are processed by points.com, meaning they don’t count as an airfare purchase for the purposes of credit card spending. Therefore this wouldn’t be treated as a Southwest purchase if you’re using a Rapid Rewards credit card.

I’d recommend using a card on which you’re trying to reach a minimum spending requirement, or otherwise, a credit card that maximizes your return on everyday spending. See this post for more on which credit cards are best for buying points.

Southwest Rapid Rewards is a revenue based frequent flyer program, and the number of points required for a flight is roughly correlated to the revenue cost of a flight. However, there is a dynamic element to the pricing, whereby the value you get per point varies significantly, based on the flight and fare class you redeem for. With this, we’re seeing redemption values as low as 1.1 cents per point, and as high as 1.5 cents per point, give or take.

As you can tell, there are probably few situations where it makes sense to buy points for 1.5 cents (or more) each, when they can’t be redeemed for that, best case scenario.

Let me cut to the chase — there are few situations where it makes sense to purchase Rapid Rewards points. Even at the discounted cost, there’s not usually value in buying points for 1.5 cents each when you can typically only redeem them for less than that. So don’t buy these points speculatively, unless you know exactly what you’re doing, and have a redemption in mind.

There are three potential scenarios I’d like to address, including the main scenario where I think it could make sense (but not really), which is to build up a balance of Southwest points, given the flexibility this provides.

Some might buy Rapid Rewards points in order to top off their account so they have enough points for a Rapid Rewards award ticket. I guess this might be worth it to some if you need a small number of points, though personally I’d just keep collecting points and then redeem with your next ticket.

The Southwest Airlines Companion Pass is one of the best values in domestic travel. If you qualify for this, you can always have a companion travel with you for just the standard taxes, regardless of whether you’re booking a cash ticket or redeeming points.
