British Airways Launches Major New 24-Hour Route In 7 Weeks: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

British Airways will finally reintroduce service to Melbourne in early January. Melbourne is the capital of the Australian state of Victoria and is now the nation's second most populous city based on the significant urban area population. It is also Australia's fastest-growing state capital by people added.

The carrier last flew there more than 20 years ago, in March 2006. That was when the UK's largest long-haul operator deployed the now long-retired Boeing 747-400. Things will be very different this time.

OAG data shows that the oneworld member will take off from London Heathrow to Melbourne on January 9, 2027. 20 years ago, flights operated via Singapore, but they'll now do so via Kuala Lumpur. This may also help to shore up the performance of Heathrow-Kuala Lumpur flights. Covering 9,134 nautical miles (16,916 km) each way on a great circle, but not real-world, basis, the new route to Melbourne will be the airline's new second-longest offering.

BA will deploy the Boeing 787-9 to Victoria, with both the 215-seat and 216-seat configurations used. This might not seem like a big difference, but it perhaps is, especially on such a long trip. The 216-seater has the airline's older business seats, while the 215-seat alternative has the newer cabin.

The configuration with the better product is due to operate 57% of the time between January and October next year. This includes the inaugural service. The 215-seat so-called Dreamliner, to use the Boeing moniker, has eight seats in first class, 38 in business, 39 in premium economy, and 130 in economy. Despite not having the newest cabins, this layout is slightly less premium. 21% of capacity is in first/business, against 23% on the 216-seater.

In the 12 months to August 2026, booking data shows that 1.4 million round-trip passengers flew between Europe and Melbourne. Excluding seasonality, around 3,800 people did so daily. Nearly half of the market flew with Emirates, Etihad Airways, and Qatar Airways via the Gulf or with Singapore Airlines via Changi. The quartet collectively benefits from lots of flights to Melbourne, while the Middle Eastern operators, in particular, have big European networks. Their dominance may reduce slightly, at least in specific markets, as four carriers will now fly between Melbourne and Europe, with three being European.

They include Finnair, whose debut in Australia, as unusual as that might appear, was in late October 2026. The oneworld member runs daily (which is even more surprising) on the Airbus A350-900 via Bangkok, with fifth freedom rights.

In the northern winter, Finnair has three daily flights from Helsinki to the Thai capital. As such, extending one flight to Australia during the cold season in the north with greater aircraft availability is less dramatic than might otherwise be the case. Significantly, it is the only EU airline to fly to Australia.

Turkish Airlines, whose Istanbul Airport hub is on the European side of the Bosphorus, also operates. Despite having lots of unused traffic rights to Australia, the carrier's frequency remains unchanged compared to when the long route began. This could be a sign of insufficient performance or a lack of widebodies. BA's return rounds out the European side of the equation. All three operators fly via a different city in Southeast Asia to benefit from the local traffic.

Qantas has a long history of flying from Melbourne to Europe. For example, the oneworld member — there's a trend here — flew to Heathrow via Singapore (QF9/QF10) or Hong Kong (QF29/QF30) for years on the 747-400 and later via Singapore on the A380. The Kangaroo Route then switched to flying via Dubai on the A380 in partnership with Emirates (QF9/QF10).

In 2018, when the 787-9 appeared, the routing changed again, flying via Perth on a same-plane basis (QF9/QF10). Late last month, Qantas reintroduced a one-stop, same-plane, same-flight-number operation from the UK's busiest airport to Melbourne via the capital of Western Australia.

Due to the ongoing war in Iran, the 787-9 does not currently begin in Melbourne on a same-flight-number basis. Avoiding Iranian airspace means that the aircraft does not fly nonstop from Perth to Heathrow, stopping in Singapore instead. The total operation is Perth-Singapore-Heathrow (as QF219, not QF9), and then Heathrow-Perth-Melbourne (QF10). While subject to change, starting and ending in Melbourne is due to return on March 28, 2027.
