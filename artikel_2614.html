One of Walker Kessler’s greatest skills? Helping the Lakers build chemistry

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

No one thought the Lakers would gel this quickly.

But in the Lakers' locker room, one of those new guys has expedited the process by being a generator of good vibes.

Walker Kessler is a beast on the court who's known as one of the best rebounders and rim protectors in the league.

Walker Kessler of the Utah Jazz walks out during a game against the Los Angeles Lakers in Los Angeles, Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

But off the court, he has done something equally if not more important: he has made it so that the Lakers look forward to coming to work every day.

"We all love Walk," Ziaire Williams said. "He's the anchor of our defense. You know, he's one of the nicest guys I think I've probably ever played with. He's a great dude, man. Love Walk to death. It's always good to know, you know, you have this paint presence behind you, top 1% of the 1% in rim protection. So, yeah, he's been great for us."

Kessler is averaging 14.3 points, 10.7 rebounds and 2.1 blocks per game for the Lakers, who are tied for the best record in the league (14-3) with the Spurs and 76ers and are considered title contenders.

But his impact extends beyond the box score.

He takes responsibility, such as when Lakers coach JJ Redick lit into him after he had only six points and one rebound at halftime against the Kings on Friday. He went on to finish with 15 points, seven rebounds and three blocked shots.

Dylan Cardwell #32 of the Sacramento Kings plays defense on Walker Kessler #14 of the Los Angeles Lakers in Los Angeles, California, Friday, Nov. 20, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

"I wasn't playing physical," Kessler said after the Lakers' 106-104 win. "I wasn't going up to dunk the ball. I was missing some more touches around the rim. And then defensively, being undisciplined. Jumping at stuff I don't need to jump at. He definitely lit into me. It was good. I needed it. That's what makes a good coach. Knowing when guys need that and when guys don't."

Kessler encourages those around him, such as letting the perimeter defenders know they can take risks and aggressively go for steals because he has their back if a player blows past them.

It has led to the Lakers leading the league in steals with 10.6 per game.

"Walker absorbs my mistakes," Matisse Thybulle said.

When Kessler was told his words, he balked.

"I promise you he's absorbing my mistakes, too," Kessler said. "There are times when I'll overcommit on a float or something and he'll back down on the big or get a steal in the passing lane or play the pocket or swipe down. So it goes both ways."

Walker Kessler of the Los Angeles Lakers shoots a 3-point basket against the Sacramento Kings on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

There's just a symbiosis between the Lakers' defensive leader and everyone else. He wants to do well for them. And the feeling is reciprocated. He enters rooms smiling. He always thanks reporters.

It's funny because Kessler began generating that type of good will as soon as the Lakers acquired him from the Jazz in July in a sign-and-trade for two first-round picks and two first-round pick swaps on top of a four-year, $180 million deal.

When Kessler was asked about the Lakers emptying their treasure trove of draft assets to acquire him, he didn't hold back.

"I'm somebody that if I know that they have that belief in me, I'm gonna run through a brick wall for them," Kessler said. "That's just how I've been wired my whole life."

Kessler is still finding his rhythm after playing only five games for the Jazz last season before suffering a torn labrum in his left shoulder that required surgery. After finishing with fewer than 10 rebounds in his first five games, Redick challenged him to be more aggressive on the boards, including rebounding outside his space.

The big man responded by having double-doubles in nine of his next 11 games.

Walker Kessler #14 of the Los Angeles Lakers and Matisse Thybulle #4 play in the fourth quarter on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. Getty Images

"It's not an easy thing to come back and be thrown in that fire, especially to do the things that he's doing," Collin Sexton said. "It's amazing, you know, to see. So we all have to make sure that we're uplifting him because it's not easy to just be out eight, nine months and just go back out there and do what he's doing. So I always tell him like he's doing a hell of a job."

Still, there's a feeling that Kessler hasn't even reached his potential yet, even though he's every bit of the franchise center the Lakers hoped to acquire.

But through Kessler's ups and downs on the court, one thing has remained consistent.

Kessler wants this to work. He wants the Lakers to be great. And it's obvious that he's inspiring everyone around him to deeply share that desire by how he carries himself and encourages those around him.

"Yeah, I mean, yeah, it's often overlooked, you know?" Williams said. "There are guys in the league who don't have the best interest [of others] and people skills and are just [in] their mind. Just focused on just whatever they got to do, you know? But Walk, he's a team-first player and just makes the vibes and the locker room and just the flow of the game just so much easier."
