Aiyuk will likely need a significant ramp-up period to get back into football conditioning and get acclimated to the Commanders' offense, and as a practice-squad player, he'll be afforded fewer practice reps than the receivers on the active roster.

What I can offer you that others can't is a look at what kind of receiver he was when he was last on the field prior to getting hurt. It's very hard to say what he is now because we've just nott seen him on a football field in two years, but we can look at what he was before the injury as a glipse of what his ceiling might be.

So what was Aiyuk before the injury? In no uncertain terms, Aiyuk was an elite receiver in 2023, racking up 1342 yards and seven touchdowns on 75 catches, with the second highest average yards per catch in the league that year with 17.9. What made him so good in 2023? The list is long but lets cover the basics. His biggest strength is his route running. Aiyuk, at least in 2023, was one of the top route runners in the league, giving him the ability to separate against even the best corners in the league.

Aiyuk understands that he can't always run every single route the same way, so he has different methods of running routes that he can go to in order to keep things fresh, but he's also capable of making different routes look very similar, which is the real sign of an elite route runner.

McAfee's show is the place for stars like Rodgers and LeBron James to open up–and take shots at their enemies

There was only one winner from Aaron Rodgers's scorched-earth takedown of Steelers coach Mike McCarthy. And that was Pat McAfee.

McAfee's years-long cultivation of Rodgers resulted in a spectacular interview that dominated the sports world Wednesday. The NFL, media, and fans will be talking about it for days as the league takes over the Thanksgiving holiday with a slate of five games on Thanksgiving Eve, Turkey Day, and Black Friday.

You want to know why ESPN's content czar Burke Magnus is negotiating an extension with McAfee that could pay him $60 million plus per year? This was Exhibit A. Sure, McAfee reportedly was paying Rodgers $1 million a year to appear on his show before Rodgers dropped his regular appearances last year. But name another media interviewer that could have gotten this much out of Rodgers? Or any pro athlete?

Stars like Rodgers and LeBron James know they'll be greeted by McAfee and Co. with the TV equivalent of a backrub. They can use McAfee's ESPN platform to craft their own narrative with no pushback–while setting scores with enemies.

Rodgers calmly dissecting McCarthy like a frog was incredible TV. It was raw, it was savage, it was beyond the beyond-do. I've covered the NFL for decades and never seen anything like it.

Whether or not any of the NFL's media partners will be interested in hiring Rodgers after today is an open question. Probably not.

In theory, you'd think the NFL's TV partners would clamor for the benched Steelers quarterback. He's a Super Bowl winner, an all-time top-10 QB, and a surefire Pro Football Hall of Famer.

The Steelers quarterback has demonstrated he can be incisive and entertaining on The Pat McAfee Show. On his worst day, the outspoken Rodgers would be looser than Brady, more insightful about current NFL defenses than Manning, and more interesting than Brees.

But Rodgers can also come off as an insufferable, self-absorbed crackpot with his "darkness retreats" and conspiracy theories.

The thin-skinned Rodgers uses media appearances to settle scores. McAfee had to apologize after Rodgers used an appearance on his show to imply comedian Jimmy Kimmel was tied to the Jeffrey Epstein documents.

On Wednesday, I talked to several network executives and agents about whether they'd be interested in Rodgers. As FOS previously found in March 2025, there was little interest in the controversial QB, who turns 43 next week.

"I think everybody in the business thinks he'd be good at it. But everybody is concerned at what would come with that," says one network executive who declined to be named. "He can be difficult off the field. There's the rub."

Rodgers' first stop would likely be a return to McAfee, who licenses his weekday show to ESPN. But if Rodgers wants to call games from the broadcast booth or serve on a traditional NFL studio show, he'd have to compete in a crowded marketplace next season.

The question of whether Rodgers' next career stop will be sports media could be moot. He's earned an eye-popping $417 million over his long NFL career with the Packers, Jets, and Steelers, per Spotrac. Rodgers himself has said he has no plans to enter the media.

"When this is done, it's Keyser Soze, and you won't see me," he told McAfee. "You won't see me. I'm not doing TV. . . . I'm not gonna be out and about."

The Steelers' halting level of dysfunction was brought to light late Tuesday night when Burt Lauten, the team's accomplished senior director of communications, issued a public statement: Mason Rudolph would be the starting quarterback for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos, and Rodgers would be inactive.

Dropping that dirty bomb at 11:10 p.m. was a screechingly loud signal that something seismic and strange had happened.

Sure, Rodgers, who'll turn 43 next week, hadn't resembled his four-time-MVP self during a recent two-game losing streak. But he'd just played against a Vic Fangio-coordinated defense, ending his afternoon with a gorgeously placed touchdown pass to a practice-squad receiver. And benching him for the 31-year-old Rudolph, he of the 9-9-1 career record as a starter, made the abrupt and weirdly timed move seem even more bizarre.

In a development that should have been entirely predictable to anyone aware of the actual circumstances, Rodgers responded by making an unscheduled appearance on McAfee's show — and airing out more dirty laundry than Fiona Gallagher in Season 7 of "Shameless."

The Steelers, and McCarthy, will have to try to clean up that mess in the coming hours, days and weeks. Good luck with that.

Maybe Rodgers has played his last down in the NFL, but he played his last serious down over four years ago with the Packers. Rodgers in his 40s has always been more of a media spectacle than a capable starting quarterback with the New York Jets (2023-24) and Steelers (2025-26). And my gut tells me that with more time on his hands than ever, Rodgers won't be going anywhere. Most content executives want that from Rodgers because they can translate it into money, and a lot of it. To me, it's more evidence that Rodgers is a proud, spoiled brat born on third base.

Rams make a statement. Entering this game, it was fair to expect the Packers could prevail as part of their playoff push. The Rams were playing with an offense held together by duct tape and chewing gum and had defeated a quartet of opponents (the Raiders, Chargers, Commanders, Cardinals) that weren't exactly the NFL's version of The Expendables. In terms of reputation, the Packers entered Wednesday night with more respect for their potential than the opposition could boast. The Rams decided to shove that skepticism right back in the direction from which it came Wednesday night by leaning deeper into their new identity, riding the running game to dominate time of possession and grind the Packers down to a pulp. Matthew Stafford only needed to complete 16 of 23 passes for 140 yards, threw an interception that could have been pivotal, and ended up not worrying about it for more than roughly 55 seconds. The Rams won the physical battle in the trenches against the Packers' beleaguered offensive line, shut down Green Bay's offense entirely in the second half and walked away with a resounding win that not only moved them to 7-4, but will likely make the rest of the NFL take them seriously again.

Love is on the struggle bus. I'm not sure if the Vikings broke Jordan Love mentally or if he needs more practice field work, but the ball isn't coming out very well, especially in key moments. What appeared to be a miscommunication with Matthew Golden resulted in an interception that looked notably worse from the wire camera angle, in which we saw Love fail to put enough velocity on his throw and instead offer a fly ball to Rams safety Jaylen McCollough for a pick that rendered Stafford's prior turnover moot. That sequence essentially doomed a lifeless Packers offense, which ran just 16 total plays in the second half and lost time of possession 23:45 to 6:15 in the second half. Love nearly threw another interception later in the half when he rolled left and sent a nosediving pass toward the arms of Trent McDuffie, well short of Love's intended target in another example of how consistent pressure (Los Angeles registered 12 pressures on the night) or the mere threat of pressure (as seen in the existences of Myles Garrett, Aaron Donald and Byron Young) can completely derail the talented passer. In the last two games, Love has rarely looked settled, which spells all kinds of bad for the Packers' pass-reliant offense. He needs to reset and get back on track quickly, or else their playoff hopes will evaporate before Christmas.
