The clubs opened the season against one another on Oct. 21 in Los Angeles and the Lakers rolled to a 127-105 victory behind 28 points, eight rebounds and seven assists from Luka Doncic, who didn't play the fourth quarter in a blowout. Yet he was upstaged by big man Walker Kessler in his Lakers debut as the 7-footer was 12-for-12 from the field for 26 points to go with nine boards. Those were the second-most shots without a miss in Lakers history, trailing only a 14-for-14 performance by Wilt Chamberlain in March 1969.

Curry led the Warriors with 23 points, while Lendeborg had a fairly quiet 12 points and three boards in his debut, but the No. 11 overall pick out of national champion Michigan has dominated since. Scouts were mixed on Lendeborg because he was so old coming out of college (was 23, now 24) and played power conference basketball for just one season after transferring from UAB.

If Kristaps Porzingis and Jimmy Butler were healthy, Lendeborg probably wouldn't even be a starter. But now he's averaging 22.6 points, 9.2 rebounds and 4.0 assists per game and is up to a +280 second-favorite to win NBA Rookie of the Year behind the Grizzlies' Cameron Boozer (+240), the No. 3 overall pick. No player has taken more ROY action in the past week at DraftKings than Lendeborg, however. He was +2800 right after the draft and +1800 when the season tipped.

He's already become the first rookie since Curry in February 2010 with at least 30 points, 10 rebounds and five assists in a game. It took Curry 51 games to accomplish the feat. While the Warriors are five games in, Lendeborg joined future Hall of Famers Patrick Ewing, David Robinson and Shaquille O'Neal as the only NBA players with at least 85 points and 35 rebounds in their first four career games in the draft lottery era (since 1985-86). Finally, Lendeborg has joined Victor Wembanyama as the only rookies since 1993 to tally 100 or more points and 40 or more rebounds in their first five career games.

"His composure. I mean, he just came off of an NCAA championship run," Curry said of Lendeborg this week. "He's just got composure, feel. His size on the court; he's a matchup problem. He's never really in a hurry and he's starting to see the pictures really well. Distributing, being aggressive, getting to the free-throw line, offensive rebounding."

Lendeborg is the first Warriors rookie with three consecutive 20-point games since Klay Thompson had three in 2011-12. With Butler recovering from his torn ACL, the Warriors just didn't have anyone not named Steph Curry who could create offense. The 6-foot-9, 241-pound rookie can. For tonight, his PRA is set at 29.5. The SportsLine props model has him projected 27.8.

"The addition of Yaxel," coach Steve Kerr said, "and the ability to kind of do what Jimmy does — slow the game down, be fouled, create shots for himself or others — it's a game-changer for us."

Remember that one-touted "second timeline" the Warriors thought they could accomplish in essentially rebuilding a championship team on the fly? That never happened, in large part because they blew the No. 2 overall pick in the 2020 draft on James Wiseman. Maybe getting Lendeborg at No. 11 is akin to landing Curry at No. 7 in 2009.
