Seahawks owner Vinod Khosla: "We’re very happy with both the team and the location right now"

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A pattern has emerged during the early months of Vinod Khosla’s ownership of the Seahawks.

First, he’ll make a less-than-unequivocal comment about the future of the team in Seattle. Two, people will hear his words and repeat them. Three, he will complain about that.

Most recently, Khosla was asked directly during an appearance on CNBC whether he’d consider moving the team.

“I’ve not made any plans or even discussed -- I know the press like to make a big deal of it,” Khosla said, via Sports Business Journal. “We’re very happy with both the team and the location right now.”

And there it is. “Right now.” Why add “right now”? Just end the sentence at “location.”

The same thing happened last month, when Khosla was interviewed by Axios. Asked point-blank whether he’d make a commitment to keeping the Seahawks in Seattle over the long term, Khosla said, “I will never say never. Will this team be in Seattle 500 years from now? But I have no current plans to change anything about the team, including the coach, president, operation or Lumen Field. It all functions well.”

It’s not difficult (although apparently it is). The question is simple. Will you move from Seattle?

While there’s an element of taxpayer-funding leverage that arises regarding future stadium renovations and/or construction in Seattle, there’s a way to answer the question that doesn’t sacrifice future bargaining power.

“We have every intention to remain in Seattle” is the easiest reply. With no further elaboration or explanation.

Frankly, his latest comments are more ominous than his response to the Axios question. Beyond the fact that he now knows anything he says will be repeated (indeed, he prefaced his response by saying “the press like to make a big deal of it”), Khosla added the ultimate caveat that will cause local consternation.

Right now isn’t “500 years from now.” Right now is subject to change. Because right now applies only to — wait for it — right now.

Right now is later from the last time I typed “right now.”

Here’s what Khosla should do: Write a check right now to someone who will give him a script to recite whenever he’s asked that question in the future right now.
