Tigers and Cubs: Week 13 LSU vs. Tennessee

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

November continues to haunt the LSU Tigers in year 1 under Lane Kiffin. On Saturday, they dropped their 3rd straight game, falling to Tennessee by a final score of 27-13.

The recent trend of collapsing in the 4th quarter happened, once again. Despite some horrific offensive play, LSU somehow found themselves tied at 13-13 nearing the halfway point of the 4th quarter. That was when Faizon Brandon took over and Sam Leavitt and the offense could not get out of their own way, still failing to have scored a point in the final quarter through 3 games this month.

The loss secured consecutive 5-loss seasons during the regular season and was a low point for a rocking first year in Baton Rouge for Kiffin.

There was only one player on the offensive side of the ball that was worthy of being even considered and that was Green.

7 receptions for 72 yards and a touchdown is a pretty standard game for the junior tight end, but considering this sluggish performance from everyone else that was on the field at the same time, it was one of the most impactful games that he has had.

Keys seems to be the lightening rod for the defense when things don't go well. Aside from Blake Baker, that is. But on Saturday, Keys was fantastic.

He led the team with 14 tackles, 6 more than the next closest. He added in 2 tackles for loss, a sack, an interception, pass breakup, and another QB hit.

Tackling has never been a question for him, though. It's his coverage skills that get him in to trouble. He was phenomenal in that department against the Volunteers. His pass coverage grade from Pro Football Focus was 75.1, which was 3rd highest on a defense that played excellent all day. His overall grade of 76.8 was also 3rd best on the entire team.

There aren't many players on this roster that can be impactful enough with just one half on the field to make this list, but Benefield is one of them. A week after what I phrased the "Ty Benefield game", in which he terrorized Arch Manning and Texas, the senior was on his way to another career performance before he was ejected for targeting late in the first half.

But before that, he had already racked up 8 tackles, which was tied for second most behind Keys. The most impressive play of the day for LSU was his strip sack, recovery, and short return to try and set his offense up for a 1st quarter touchdown. He also added a sack, tackle for loss, and a pass breakup.

Saturday against Arkansas will likely be the last game we ever see of Benefield in purple and gold, unfortunately, but he has an extremely bright future ahead of him in the NFL.

Although he didn't throw 3 interceptions like he did against Texas last week, Leavitt was simply not good enough again on Saturday. His stats don't look bad, finishing 27-39 for 222 yards and a touchdown, but that clean line is deceiving.

As we've seen throughout this losing streak, Leavitt looks panicked and unable to process what's happening in front of him. He either bails out of his reads early or he refuses to step up in a pocket, instead running backwards and putting himself in a nearly impossible position to make a play.

There were high hopes for Ja'Keem Jackson when he transferred from Florida prior to the 2025 season. With a loaded cornerback room that included Mansoor Delane, PJ Woodland, DJ Pickett, and even Ashton Stamps, his path to the field was tough last season.

He came back for 2026 and with 2 of those 4 gone and very little to replace them at the position during the offseason, the opportunity was there for Jackson. Still, he's had a hard time getting much time on the field this season and when he does, he struggles.

Jackson was out there for some key situations during the first half, but had the wrong kind of impact. In just 6 snaps, he was called for 2 penalties in coverage that resulted in big first downs.

With a PFF grade of 29.1, the cornerback was by far the lowest graded player on the entire team.

Not only was the performance by the offense downright disgusting, but the discipline of the team was even worse.

LSU committed 16 penalties for 98 yards. Of those 16, the offense was penalized 12 times. There was also one that was declined.

In particular, the offensive line was atrocious when it came to discipline. Yes, it's very loud in Neyland Stadium, but there's still no excuse for that many penalties. The preparation and coaching deserves some of that blame, as well, considering their gameplan was to use the sound clue of claps until it unraveled in the 1st quarter.

Tylen Singleton, also, had a bad moment. Late in the game on Tennessee's go-ahead touchdown drive, LSU made a tackle for a loss of 3 yards to set up a 3rd and 8 with the ball barely in FG range at the 37 yard line. But, Singleton lost his cool and took a swing at a Tennessee player after the whistle, resulting in a 15 yard unsportsmanlike conduct penalty and giving them a 1st down at the 22 yard line.

Two plays later, Faizon Brandon scampered around the left edge for an 18 yard touchdown run and the Vols would never look back.

Singleton is a highly-talented young linebacker who was finally getting his opportunity. All he can do is make it a learning experience going forward.
