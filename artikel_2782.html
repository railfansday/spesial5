Will Shane Steichen be back in 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

After the 2025 season ended, Colts owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon put both G.M. Chris Ballard and coach Shane Steichen on notice that much was expected in 2026.

Then, one day after the Colts lost to the Titans in Week 6, she fired Ballard and kept Steichen.

Nearly five weeks later, Steichen remains. After next Sunday’s game against the Giants, the Colts will enter their bye. That’s typically the best time for an in-season coaching change, since it gives the interim coach maximum time to get up to speed before the next game.

Creating a vacancy with six weeks left in the regular season carries a separate benefit. The team can get a head start on searching for the replacement. And if ownership already knows the coach will be fired after the season ends, it makes no sense to delay that part of the process.

So here’s the question. Will Irsay-Gordon bring Steichen back in 2027?

It makes no sense to keep a new G.M. from hiring a new coach. Every G.M. arrives with a wish list of coaches who would be pursued, if/when the position is vacant. It makes plenty of sense to give a new G.M. an immediate vacancy to fill.

But what about the Colts this year has made sense? The firing of the G.M. left the team without a seasoned, experienced executive to navigate the strategy of making, or not making, trades. They quickly moved guard Quenton Nelson and defensive tackle DeForest Buckner. They rejected offers for running back Jonathan Taylor and quarterback Daniel Jones. They moved two starting receivers — Keenan Allen and Josh Downs — on the day of the trade deadline.

Steichen has been operating for much of the season with one hand tied behind his back. Now, he’ll have both wrists secured. Whatever the decision will be as to Steichen, it’s unfair to base it on the team’s overall performance since the Nelson and Buckner trades.

Before that point, they took the Chiefs to overtime in Kansas City. They beat the Texans. They manhandled the Commanders in London. They took the Vikings to the limit in Minnesota.

Although the signs point to Steichen eventually being fired, the fact that he’s still there now means he could still be there later. The new G.M. may be expected to work with him.

It’s ultimately the owner’s call. And the owner may have strong feelings about Steichen’s skills and abilities, based on the fact that she’s on the sideline every week.

If she was upstairs, she’d likely be hearing criticism of the head coach during the game. Instead, she’s watching him in his element. And she hasn’t fired him yet.

Throw in the fact that a new coach may not be as welcoming to the owner’s presence in the bench area, and she may be even more inclined to give Steichen (who’s fine with it) another chance. Especially since she systematically began making his job much more difficult the moment the Colts traded Nelson and Buckner.

Moving forward, it’ll be virtually impossible. The fact that Steichen has survived this long could mean that he’ll survive whatever comes next.
