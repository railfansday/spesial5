A trading strategy to ride out the Nvidia uncertainty and generate income in the process

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation has sent shockwaves through the tech sector, dragging Nvidia's (NVDA) stock down roughly 30% since Oct. 6. The market despises uncertainty, and losing a visionary founder, particularly one without a well-telegraphed succession plan, makes it worse.

However, for opportunistic investors, this panic has made the stock cheaper (obviously) while options have become significantly more expensive. Trading in the $160s, Nvidia's valuation has taken a big haircut, yet its underlying business fundamentals remain largely intact. While Huang was the architect of Nvidia's AI dominance, the company's moat — superior GPUs, the CUDA software ecosystem, and networking expertise — does not walk out the door with him. The company's structural advantage in the global artificial intelligence rollout persists.

Buying good companies at a discount is investing 101, but the current market dynamics may do something else for those who use options.  Huang's departure guarantees that Nvidia will be operating under a cloud of transition for the foreseeable future. Executing a permanent CEO search while navigating global regulatory scrutiny takes time. Consequently, Nvidia's implied volatility (IV)—the market's pricing of expected future price swings — has spiked to highs not seen since the tariff tantrum in the first half of 2025, and this elevated IV is highly likely to persist for several months as the leadership drama slowly unfolds.

This prolonged period of high implied volatility can be a goldmine for option sellers, specifically those executing options overlays such as covered call strategies. When you buy NVDA shares at these depressed levels and simultaneously sell out-of-the-money call options against your position, the premiums you collect are directly tied to IV. Because fear and transition-related uncertainty have dramatically inflated these option prices, the income generated from selling covered calls is now significantly higher than usual.

This lowers the risk profile of holding the stock considerably over time. Consistently collecting these inflated premiums systematically lowers your overall cost basis in the underlying shares. If the stock trades sideways or recovers slowly during the CEO search, you are generating an outsized cash yield on a stagnant asset. If the stock experiences further downside turbulence, the heavy premium income acts as a substantial downside buffer, meaningfully reducing the strategy's overall risk over time.

By deploying a persistent covered call strategy, investors can leverage the exact fear driving the stock down to generate robust, risk-reducing income while maintaining exposure to the undeniable future of AI.
