Tale of 2 tight ends: Browns' Harold Fannin Jr., Raiders' Brock Bowers

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

BEREA ― There's a number of players Todd Monken has coached over the years for whom he clearly has an affection. There may be few that reach the level of Las Vegas Raiders tight end Brock Bowers.

Monken coached Bowers ― whom he said they affectionately nicknamed "Buddy the Yellow Lab" ― during his three seasons as the offensive coordinator at the University of Georgia. The nickname, the Browns coach said this week ahead of their "Sunday Night Football" game against the Raiders, came from the tight end's seemingly endless reservoir of energy.

To prove his point, Monken told a story from the first day of winter conditioning in 2021, Bowers' first season with the Bulldogs. The team was going through conditioning drills by positions inside Samford Stadium when it was the tight end group's turn to alternate running the length of the field and walking the width of it.

Bowers, immediately, took the lead in the pack. By a full 10 yards, in Monken's retelling of events.

"Now, I could have never done that," Monken said. "Here you are, first day, you're going to run with the pack. Everybody's going to say you're showboating, you know, you're trying to impress the coaches. That's all he knows. All he knows is one speed and work, and he gets that from mom and dad that were Division 1 athletes. And he's wired the right way. Like, you know, he's just unique."

Monken, though, appears to have found himself another "unique" talent at the tight end position. Since being hired with by the Browns in January, he has routinely compared Harold Fannin Jr. to Bowers in a number of ways.

There's the obvious positional comparisons, but also in the ways they play. Much like Bowers, there's a versatility to Fannin that makes him every bit as dangerous when he's aligned wide in the formation as when he's set up in the traditional tight end spot with his hand in the ground.

"I mean, it kind of makes sense," Fannin said on the first day of training camp in July. "I remember in college, me and Brock Bowers were kind of up there with the yards, so it makes sense."

That hasn't changed with both moving up to the NFL. Bowers came into the week second in the league in receiving yards for a tight end with 658 yards, while Fannin was sixth with 544.

Both are also big-play tight ends. Bowers leads all tight ends with receptions of 10-plus yards with 32 and 20-ust yards with 11, while Fannin is sixth in the former with 21 and fourth in the latter with eight.

Since 2025, Bowers is second in receiving yards for a tight end with 1,437 yards. Fannin, meanwhile, is fourth with 1,275 yards.

The one thing hanging over the potential tight end duel Sunday is Bowers' availability. The third-year pro missed the Raiders' Week 11 loss at the Denver Broncos due to an ankle injury, and has been limited in the first two practices of the week.

"We're expecting him to play and he's their go-to guy," Browns cornerback Denzel Ward said this week. "So when he's on the field, we know that he's going to get a lot of the targets and a lot of the touches. So just knowing where he's at on the field is going to be crucial for us and trying to win this football game. He's a really good player."

The Raiders still have one more full practice and a walkthrough before they depart for Cleveland. They will announce injury designations for the game later Nov. 27.

The competitor in Monken couldn't help but come out after he spent a long time praising Bowers, capping it off with a joking, "hopefully he's still hurt."

Fannin's had his own injury issues, specifically a groin injury that popped up ahead of the Week 9 loss at the New Orleans Saints. He didn't miss any time, however, due to injury, and has even turned into one of the best stretches of his admittedly-brief career.

The Canton native and Bowling Green State University product had just two catches for 42 yards against New Orleans, but one of those was an impressive 39-yard touchdown in overtime that saw him stiff-arm a pair of Saints defenders on his way to the end zone. He followed that up with a seven-catch, 91-yard performance in the Week 10 win over the Houston Texans, including another angry run after the catch on a 30-yard reception that set up the Browns' go-ahead touchdown with less than a minute remaining.

Fannin's last four games, dating back to the Week 7 win at the Tennessee Titans, have seen him catch 25 passes for 378 yards and one touchdown. The key to carrying that forward after the bye?

"Simple.," Fannin said Nov. 24. "Stay in rhythm, things like that. Obviously you're going to go out here and practice, work on it, continue to grow on it and keep getting better at it."
