Why a Data Center Slowdown Would Reach Utilities and Electrical Suppliers Before Chipmakers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

If the data center build-out starts to hit delays and speed bumps, it will affect the various industries involved differently.

Voters across the United States supported restrictions on data center development or rejected proposals favoring new projects in 40 of 41 local ballot contests on Nov. 3, according to Ballotpedia. The results included five full construction bans and three six-month moratoriums, raising the likelihood that some planned data centers will be delayed or canceled.

A slowdown in construction could reduce future infrastructure spending by electric utilities and cut into orders for electrical equipment.

However, chipmaker Nvidia (NVDA -0.86%) could continue selling chips to existing data centers and new projects in other locations. Hence, the earliest signs of a slowdown in the build-out may appear in electricity contracts and equipment orders rather than chip sales.

Data center developers often request electricity capacity from utilities while their projects are still in the planning stages. However, not all of these requests lead to actual construction.

Developers initially requested more than 30,000 megawatts of electricity capacity from American Electric Power's (AEP -0.41%) Ohio subsidiary. After American Electric Power Ohio introduced requirements for paid engineering studies and binding financial commitments, requests totaling 13,023 megawatts proceeded to studies. Developers had signed new contracts for only 5,642 megawatts by February 2026. Another 12,219 megawatts had already been contracted before American Electric Power Ohio's new data center tariff took effect in July 2025.

This does not mean actual electricity demand declined. Instead, many developers that initially requested power capacity did not proceed with binding contracts. A similar reduction in new commitments could signal slower future construction without immediately affecting electricity sales to existing customers.

Growing electricity demand from data centers can create new investment opportunities for utilities. Large projects may require new power plants, substations, and transmission lines, allowing regulated utilities to earn returns on additional infrastructure investments.

This opportunity is reflected in American Electric Power's expansion plans. The company expects 84 gigawatts of incremental contracted load by 2031. To support that growing demand, the company plans to invest about $100 billion from 2027 through 2031, primarily in power generation, transmission, and distribution. These investments are expected to increase its rate base, or the value of regulated assets on which it can earn returns, to $162 billion by 2031.

However, if some of those planned data centers are delayed or canceled, American Electric Power may need to postpone some of its own infrastructure investments. This could slow its earnings growth, although lower capital spending would also reduce its borrowing needs. Hence, a reduction in new data center projects would affect utilities' future growth opportunities before reducing their current electricity sales.

Data center construction also drives demand for transformers, switchgear, and other electrical equipment. If new projects are delayed, suppliers could receive fewer orders while continuing to deliver equipment already booked.

Eaton (ETN +0.18%) manufactures electrical equipment used in data centers and other industries. In the third quarter, it reported that orders from data center customers across its electrical businesses more than doubled over the preceding 12 months.

Eaton ended the third quarter with $26.2 billion in firm orders awaiting delivery. About 72% of these orders are targeted for delivery within the next 12 months. The backlog includes orders from industries other than data centers.

Hence, a slowdown in new orders might not immediately affect Eaton's revenue. However, if orders continue weakening, the company could eventually face slower sales growth and lower factory utilization.

At its Nov. 12 closing price, Eaton traded at about 26 times its expected 2026 adjusted earnings. Slower earnings growth could put pressure on this valuation. However, demand from utilities, industrial customers, and aerospace could help offset weakness in its data center business.

Chipmakers may not face the same immediate impact from delayed data center construction. Their customer will likely continue to buy chips to upgrade existing facilities or install them at other locations with available electricity and cooling capacity.

Nvidia is scheduled to report its fiscal third-quarter results on Nov. 17. The company was guiding for total revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, up from $96.2 billion in the second quarter. However, its actual results will provide a more recent indicator of demand for its artificial intelligence (AI) chips.

Microsoft (MSFT -1.18%) has already demonstrated how chip spending could remain strong despite data center construction delays. In its recently reported quarter, Microsoft's capital expenditures totaled $45.2 billion. Approximately three-quarters of this spending was for shorter-lived assets such as CPUs, GPUs, memory, and storage. This spending continued despite delays in third-party data center capacity.

However, prolonged construction delays could eventually affect chip demand, as new servers require sufficient power and cooling capacity. Nvidia had $279 billion in supply and manufacturing capacity commitments at the end of July, although some agreements can be adjusted before becoming firm orders. If customers delay chip purchases due to limited data center capacity, Nvidia could face risks associated with these commitments.

Hence, Nvidia's upcoming results and management's outlook could help investors assess whether data center construction delays are beginning to affect semiconductor demand.
