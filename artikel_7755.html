Kansas Realtor Indicted for Tax Evasion, Failure to File Tax Returns, and Pandemic Relief Fraud

A federal grand jury in Kansas City, Kansas, returned a superseding indictment yesterday charging a Louisburg, Kansas, woman with tax evasion, wire fraud, and willful failure to file personal and corporate income tax returns.

According to the superseding indictment, Michelle O’Connor, also known as Michelle Yi Lee, owned and operated a realty company based in the Kansas City metro area. For the years 2008 through 2015, O’Connor allegedly filed federal income tax returns, self-reporting that she owed a total of approximately $300,000 in taxes. According to the superseding indictment, despite reporting to the IRS that she owed taxes, O’Connor did not pay the amounts owed.

Starting in 2010, the IRS allegedly began trying to collect the outstanding taxes from O’Connor and sent her a total of more than 50 notices. From 2011 through late 2020, however, O’Connor allegedly stymied the IRS’s collections efforts by, among other things, filing false or frivolous bankruptcy petitions and reducing her bank balances by purchasing more than $250,000 in cashier’s checks to prevent the IRS from levying those accounts. According to the superseding indictment, O’Connor closed some of her personal bank accounts and maintained minimal balances in others in an effort to further obstruct the IRS’s collection efforts. O’Connor also allegedly paid for personal expenses using her business’s bank accounts. By 2020, O’Connor allegedly owed the IRS nearly half a million in taxes, penalties and interest.

In 2020, O’Connor also allegedly submitted 34 fraudulent Economic Injury Disaster Loan (EIDL) applications on behalf of her real estate business and seven other corporate entities she created for the purpose of applying for these credits. Under the EIDL program, a small business could receive a loan of up to $150,000 from the Small Business Administration to cover six months of working capital. In total, O’Connor allegedly received nearly $300,000 from her fraudulent EIDLs, which she used for personal purposes, including purchasing $115,000 of cryptocurrency. According to the superseding indictment, O’Connor also did not file individual and corporate income tax returns for the years 2020 through 2025 as required by law.

O’Connor was charged with one count of tax evasion, two counts of wire fraud, five counts of willful failure to file an individual tax return, and nine counts of willful failure to file a corporate tax return. If convicted, she faces maximum penalties of five years in prison for the tax evasion count, 20 years in prison for each count of wire fraud, and one year in prison for each count of willful failure to file a tax return. She also faces a period of supervised release, restitution and monetary penalties. A federal district court judge will determine any sentence after considering the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines and other statutory factors.

Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald of the Justice Department’s National Fraud Enforcement Division and U.S. Attorney Ryan A. Kriegshauser for the District of Kansas made the announcement.

IRS Criminal Investigation is investigating the case.

Trial Attorneys Dominick Giovanniello and J. Parker Gochenour of the Fraud Division’s Tax Section are prosecuting the case.
