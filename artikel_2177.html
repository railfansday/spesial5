West Ham have given Cesar Peixoto a major Wolves headache after Nuno decision

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

West Ham and Wolves started the Championship season as the two firm favourites for automatic promotion.

Yet, it’s fair to say neither side has hugely impressed in the opening months of the campaign, even if they are both certainly in the hunt to finish in the top two.

Despite their decent positions, the Hammers have seen enough to make a change in the dugout, with Scott Parker replacing Nuno Espírito Santo as head coach.

That shows just how important winning promotion is to the Irons, and Wolves are in a similar boat.

Firstly, it should be said that there’s absolutely no suggestion Peixoto could lose his job, and Wolves will rightly point out that they are ahead of West Ham in the table.

Some will say it’s extremely harsh that Nuno has been sacked with his team just four points away from second, so it would be on another level if Peixoto was dismissed, as Wanderers are third, and just two points behind Swansea going into the midweek fixtures.

Nevertheless, there’s no denying that Peixoto still hasn’t got the backing of all Wolves fans, which is largely down to their inconsistent performances.

There is no clear pattern of play from the Portuguese head coach, and his constant changes to the XI have been baffling.

As well as that, some fans are not happy with his preference for certain players and his substitutions, which is why chants of ‘you don’t know what you’re doing’ were aimed at Peixoto in the recent game against Watford - which Wolves were winning at the time!

The challenge for Peixoto will be to replicate what Nuno did at Molineux by taking the club back to the Premier League.

The circumstances are very different though, so the reality is that even if they do win promotion, Peixoto will not receive the adulation of his former Porto teammate.

Nuno’s appointment at Wolves in 2017 was the start of a remarkable journey for the Midlands outfit, during which time they hit heights that few thought were possible.

As a result, Nuno is viewed as a legend at Molineux, as he built the best team seen in decades, as Wolves returned to the European stage and regularly bloodied the noses of the best clubs in the country.

Plus, he also bought into what the city was about, and he had a special bond with the supporters.

Following on from that, it feels that any Wolves manager since is, perhaps unfairly, compared to Nuno, even if there is an understanding that the ambition of the owners is nowhere near what it once was.

So, with news of his sacking, it’s inevitable that some fans will call for Nuno to return to the place they feel is ‘home’ for the ex-Nottingham Forest chief.

Of course, that won’t be the view of every fan, but Nuno’s departing message in 2021 was that this is ‘not a goodbye, it’s see you soon’, suggesting that he too would be open to returning at some point.

Despite his mixed start to this season with West Ham, Nuno would be seen as a coup for any Championship club, so if Peixoto does struggle at any point, then it’s not hard to imagine that calls for the former manager to return will grow, especially as he is available.

You do have sympathy for Peixoto, because this is none of his doing, and it’s giving him a headache and distraction that he doesn’t need just months into his Wolves career.

Furthermore, it’s not like he is actively disliked by Wolves fans, more that he hasn’t fully won them over, whilst some will be delighted at their league position.

But, rightly or wrongly, Nuno’s availability will naturally add a bit more pressure, and it’s undoubtedly going to be brought up to Peixoto at some point.

However, that doesn’t have to be a bad thing, and, if he embraces the pressure, it will allow him to try and build his own relationship with the supporters.

Ultimately, they both want the same thing - for Wolves to reach the Premier League, and preferably by playing stylish football.

So, instead of Nuno’s shadow lurking in the background negatively, he must use it as motivation to make his own mark at Molineux, even if it’s unlikely he will manage to match his legacy.
