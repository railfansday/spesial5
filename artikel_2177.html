Charlton Athletic told to avoid signing ex-Spurs star - 'salary' issue raised

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Charlton Athletic bolstered their midfield options late in the summer transfer window with the arrival of Oliver Skipp on loan from Leicester City, but not many Addicks fans are hoping he'll stick around on the basis of his first few months at the Valley.

It's been a difficult campaign so far for Nathan Jones' side, and that's despite being the final side in the Championship with a 100% winning record, after coming away with nine points from nine in the first three games.

But with two of those wins coming in home games against Derby County and Preston North End, the two worst teams in the division, that's not exactly a start that's aged well, and an 11-game winless run following the win over the Lilywhites has seen them sink down the table.

Skipp, who came in on Deadline Day, hasn't won a game as a Charlton player, nor has he done much in Addicks fans' eyes to try and turn this poor form around.

It's usually around two to three months into a loan spell that a fanbase begins to start dreading what life may look like as and when a player moves back to their parent club and starts to hope that a permanent move can be made.

When it comes to Skipp and Charlton, though, that doesn't seem to be the case, but even if he were excelling at the Valley, FLW's Addicks fan pundit, Chris Woodward, doesn't think his side would be able to pull off a permanent deal for the ex-Spurs academy man if they wanted to.

"I don't see a world where we try and sign Skipp permanently. His salary makes it a complete non-starter," he said.

"What it would take to cover his current contract at Leicester is far more than we could offer financially, whether that be in League One or the Championship, depending on where we end up next summer."

Woodward is a bit more lenient towards Skipp than most other Charlton fans are, but does concede that the fact he's not excelling in what is, on paper, quite a poor engine room at the club is a slight concern.

"He hasn't been as bad as what some fans make out, but he's hardly set the world alight," he continued.

"What makes things worse is the fact that his competition is an injury-prone [Nathaniel] Chalobah, a 20-year-old [Alan] Mwamba with no senior experience, [Timothy] Ouma, who we couldn't genuinely tell you if he's in the country or not, and Conor Coventry, who was dropped for Conor Coady last season, who hadn't played in midfield for 10 years.

"The chances are, if you're struggling to shine in those circumstances, you're not worth considering for the long term."

What's arguably more frustrating for Woodward is that he knows there is a player there in Skipp. After all, the 26-year-old was bought by Leicester for £20 million just over two years ago after making over 100 appearances for Tottenham.

There have been games where the midfielder has showcased his quality, especially in defence. His defensive contributions per 90 are in the 90th percentile amongst defensive midfielders in the second tier.

But there simply needs to be more from him if Charlton were to consider signing him permanently, before they'd have to navigate the amount of money he's currently earning on his Leicester wage.

"We've seen flashes of brilliance, and there has to be if you cost £20 million and played for Tottenham," he concluded. "But he hasn't shown enough consistency for us to contemplate a permanent move."

It's been a horror couple of years for Skipp, ever since leaving Tottenham for Leicester.

That £20 million price tag put some unnecessary pressure on the 26-year-old, as did his situation in joining the former Premier League champions, who were desperate to remain in the top flight after winning the Championship the year prior.

Ultimately, he hasn't lived up to the bill, and now has two relegations on his CV. Pound for pound, he's quickly becoming recognised as one of the most disappointing signings that the Foxes have ever made, and a real indication of where things went wrong in recruitment.

This loan spell has provided Skipp with a chance to revive his career, almost, back in the Championship for a side many expected to be battling against the drop. He's been fine throughout his first couple of months, but Charlton at this moment needs a bit more than fine.

There's still plenty of time left in the season for both the 26-year-old and Nathan Jones' side to turn things around and generate some good feeling at the Valley. But for now, things are looking a little bleak, and not many fans are on board with keeping Skipp at the club come the end of his loan spell.
