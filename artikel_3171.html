If I Were Building a Portfolio From Scratch With $10,000, These 3 Consumer Stocks Would Come First

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Of course, these are also some of the simplest but most reliable names that plenty of experienced investors with long-lived portfolios are also holding.

By James Brumley – Nov 19, 2026 at 3:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Procter & Gamble ›

As the adage goes, the gift of hindsight is 20/20. That just means most of us wish we knew then what we know now. It's even true when it comes to picking stocks.

Although I've got no complaints about how my portfolio's fared since I first began investing three decades ago, if I were starting over with a clean slate, I'd do things differently. I'd do something simpler, with more of the patience that I'm glad I learned in the meantime.

With that as the backdrop, if I were starting a new portfolio from scratch with a respectable $10,000, here are the three safe, resilient, predictable, and low-maintenance names I'd start with that just might become the proven pillars of my portfolio.

As Warren Buffett has so famously suggested, invest in businesses you understand so you can reasonably evaluate their potential.

And there's arguably no business easier to understand than Coca-Cola (KO -2.04%) (which Buffett actually first added to Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio all the way back in 1988). Beverages are just water with some sort of flavoring added to it before it's purchased. One of the keys to success in this industry is simply figuring out which products consumers want most and then marketing them effectively.

The Coca-Cola Company does tremendously well on both fronts, having built one of the world's best-recognized and most-loved family of brands, including Minute Maid juices, Gold Peak tea, and of course, its namesake cola.

Although this quality is a key bullish feature here, it's not the crux of the reason KO is a solid pick for almost anyone's portfolio regardless of their experience as an investor.

The chief argument for buying and holding a long-lived stake in Coca-Cola is the reliable income it offers through a dividend that reliably grows. The beverage behemoth has not only paid dividends like clockwork for decades now, but has raised its annual dividend payment for 64 consecutive years. (And any company that achieves this is rightly considered a Dividend King.)

At its current price, you'd be plugging into this stock while its forward-looking yield is a respectable 2.1%.

In many regards, retailer Walmart (WMT -1.04%) enjoys the same basic business benefits that Coca-Cola does. Chief among them is the fact that consumers repeatedly buy what it sells.

Investors don't reap the same sort of benefit from these two seemingly similar companies, though. While Walmart technically pays a dividend too, it's a pittance, translating into a forward-looking yield of less than 1%.

Rather, the company grows shareholder value another way -- generous stock buybacks. Over the course of the past 20 years, this retailer has spent a healthy portion of its persistent profits to repurchase more than one-third of its outstanding shares, while also successfully investing in its own growth (and its penetration of the e-commerce arena).

Since 2006, the company's annual top line has improved from $345 billion to the current fiscal year's expected $753 billion. By retailing standards, that's huge, even if much of that growth stems from overseas expansion.

That's still not quite the reason I'd make this name a mainstay of a brand new portfolio, however. Although I like the idea of a value name in a value industry capable of performing like a growth stock, more than anything I like Walmart's dominance of North America's brick-and-mortar retailing landscape, with over 5,000 locales in the United States alone.

While it hasn't always managed this dominance as well as it could have -- remember the frequently empty shelves back in 2013? -- its overwhelming physical presence means it's got several levers it can pull to remain connected with customers and maintain this dominance. Its rivals just don't have these same options.

And that matters. See, I don't want the companies I own to have to fight a fair fight. I just want them to continue beating the competition.

Last but not least, I'd add consumer staples giant Procter & Gamble (PG -1.43%) to a portfolio being built from the ground up with $10,000.

You've heard of the company, but are you aware of just how broad its product portfolio is? Pampers diapers, Tide laundry detergent, Bounty paper towels, Gillette razors, Cascade dishwasher detergent, and Crest toothpaste are just some of the brands in the P&G family, several of which are the leading or second-best-selling brand in their category. Being the single-biggest name in the consumer goods business, you may be a regular user of its products without even realizing it.

And that's a big reason this name has earned a starting position in my new hypothetical portfolio. Much like Walmart and Coca-Cola, Procter & Gamble enjoys the sheer size and scale to do whatever it wants to do that its competitors just can't.

Yes, this includes advertising. Procter is often one of the world's very biggest advertisers, in fact, shelling out more than $10 billion on marketing alone during the fiscal year ending in June versus $87 billion in sales.

It's not just a matter of outspending its competition, however. Procter & Gamble is also an important supplier to retailers, as the retail industry knows P&G's products are not only a big reason consumers step foot in their stores; the company also helps promote sales of its own goods. So, its retail partners tend to prominently feature these goods as well.

You'll never get great growth from this company; last year's low-single-digit growth is in line with long-term norms. What you will get is consistent forward progress even in tough economic environments and a dividend that's proven amazingly resilient. Not only has the company paid at least one every year for over a century now, but has raised its annual dividend payment for over 70 consecutive years.

P&G is clearly a Dividend King. You can step into this resilient dividend growth while the stock's forward-looking yield stands at 2.5%.
