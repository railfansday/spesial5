Nvidia's Board Chairman Says Jensen Huang Isn't Coming Back, but He May Have a Poker Move for the Ages Hiding up His Sleeve

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia's Board Chair, Harvey Jones, says the company does not plan to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:08PM EST

Although Nvidia (NVDA -2.90%) just delivered another incredible earnings report, investors are preoccupied with the unexpected departure of founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang. During the company's earnings call, board chairman Harvey Jones made it clear that Nvidia intends to move forward without its former leader. Jones said the board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him.

However, Huang may have another way to influence Nvidia's future. According to a recent Reuters report, Nvidia's corporate governance rules grant the former CEO the ability to nominate two candidates for election to the company's board of directors.

At this time, there is no public evidence that Huang intends to exercise that right. Nevertheless, I think Tesla (TSLA +0.95%) CEO Elon Musk and CrowdStrike (CRWD +7.95%) CEO George Kurtz make two compelling hypothetical candidates. Here's why.

Nvidia's definitive proxy statement from May 12, 2026 outlines a shareholder nomination mechanism known as proxy access. Under the company's bylaws, shareholders who have controlled at least 3% of Nvidia's voting power for three consecutive years can nominate candidates for inclusion in its annual proxy materials. The number of candidates is limited to the greater of two directors or 20% of the board.

Huang has maintained ownership above that threshold for more than three years and currently controls approximately 3.5% of Nvidia. Importantly, this authority comes from Huang's position as a qualifying shareholder rather than a special privilege granted with founding Nvidia.

The proxy statement specifies that nominations for Nvidia's 2027 annual shareholder meeting must be submitted between Feb. 24 and March 26, 2027. It is important to note that nominating directors does not guarantee their election. Shareholders still need to vote on the candidates, and nominees must meet Nvidia's independence standards and other governance requirements.

Nevertheless, board composition matters greatly because directors oversee corporate strategy and executive succession planning. If Huang nominates candidates who share his vision for Nvidia's future, they could potentially influence other directors over time.

Elon Musk is easily one of the most provocative hypothetical nominees. Musk's companies are major buyers and suppliers within the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure ecosystem. Moreover, his ambitions extend beyond purchasing Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs). Musk's proposed Terafab semiconductor manufacturing initiative underscores his interest in developing new types of compute capacity to support future AI development.

Sitting on Nvidia's board could theoretically give Musk an opportunity to help shape the direction of one of the world's leading AI infrastructure companies. His experience building Tesla and Space Exploration Technologies also brings an unconventional perspective on manufacturing, capital investment, and innovation roadmaps.

However, commercial relationships between Musk's businesses and Nvidia, as well as potential competition with Terafab, would require scrutiny under the company's independence and conflict-of-interest rules.

George Kurtz is a different but equally interesting possibility. His professional relationship with Huang is supported by a strategic partnership between CrowdStrike and Nvidia. In September, Huang joined Kurtz at CrowdStrike's Fal.Con conference to introduce SafeMind, an agentic cybersecurity system incorporating Nvidia's Nemotron technology.

Huang has previously emphasized that cybersecurity will become one of AI's most compute-intensive applications, positioning CrowdStrike as an important partner in Nvidia's expansion beyond AI model training.

Of course, Kurtz's relationship with Nvidia also creates potential conflicts that would need to be evaluated before any nomination. Still, his experience in enterprise software and established working relationship with Huang make him an interesting candidate for a board seat.

Corporate boards are rarely unanimous on every single decision. For this reason, newly elected directors could potentially encourage the board to reconsider a decision that initially appeared final.

However, there are some obstacles to this scenario. Board members have fiduciary obligations to Nvidia and its shareholders. This means they must evaluate strategy and leadership decisions independently rather than simply following the wishes of the shareholder who nominated them.

Timing presents the biggest challenge, because Nvidia could appoint a new CEO well before its 2027 annual meeting. This means that even if Huang nominated two successful candidates, reversing the company's leadership transition would require support from additional directors.

Regardless, Huang's proxy-access provision introduces a governance consideration that investors shouldn't overlook. His substantial ownership still gives him an indirect avenue to influence Nvidia's future, even after resigning. Whether he chooses to exercise that right is not yet known, but even the possibility could keep speculation surrounding his return alive.

For investors, the big-picture issue is whether Nvidia can preserve its innovation, strategic relationships, and competitive advantages established under Huang's tenure. Ultimately, Nvidia's ability to execute its AI strategy under new leadership is more important than the possibility of a dramatic boardroom comeback.
