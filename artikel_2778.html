Bears vs. Saints: Inside the Snap Counts, Stats, and What Was That Game Plan?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Did this feel like a Chicago Bears preseason game plan to anyone else?

I'm not saying Ben Johnson looked past the New Orleans Saints, but the offensive game plan was run-heavy and fairly generic, and Dennis Allen's defensive game plan was fairly vanilla. Perhaps, with the short week ahead to face the Detroit Lions on Thanksgiving, they spent some of last week's prep on their NFC North rival because they were confident they could beat the Saints.

Chicago's running game had a few explosives, but it was mostly a plodding, grind-it-out day, and Johnson stuck with it because I think he wanted to keep the clock moving. After they went up 16 to 0, it seemed like Johnson never felt the Saints had a chance to come back, and with the way his defense was playing, he was content to coast to the 21 to 13 win.

Speaking of the defense, Allen was content to rush four most of the day and force the Saints to methodically work their way down the field. According to Next Gen Stats, the "Bears defense generated 24 pressures and 6 sacks against the Saints, their most pressures in a game since Week 9, 2018. Five of Chicago's sacks came with a four-man rush, tied for the second-most by any defense this season."

Even though the score was closer than we all expected, the Bears controlled this game from start to finish. The Saints made it a one-score game before the half, but the Bears forced a three-and-out on New Orleans' first possession of the third quarter, then went on an 11-play, 63-yard drive that ended in a field goal to push it to 19 to 10.

Again, I don't think they looked past the Saints, but back up two scores, this coaching staff felt confident they could ride it out because they were the better team, which is why they (possibly) went into it with that game plan in the first place.

Tight end Colston Loveland alluded to a lack of enthusiasm.

"I think we started slow and we didn't really have the enthusiasm, I feel like, on a couple drives and then had turnovers and penalties, which hurts," he said in his postgame presser via ESPN's Courtney Cronin. "But I think there's some good to take away for sure, too. I think it's just in the run game, finishing, straining. I had a couple I fell off of that could have been two, three, four more yards. So I think that's the difference."

There wasn't any panic from the sideline during the game, and even in Ben Johnson's postgame presser, he didn't give off a disappointed vibe. The Good, Better, Best locker room speech, which was low-key after the narrow win over the Buccaneers, felt just as upbeat and as fun as usual.

Again, I don't think they looked past the Saints; I think they gave the Saints the attention they felt they deserved, with an eye toward Thursday's battle for the North.

The Bears had more total yards (311 to 307), more first downs (18 to 17), and had a better time of possession (30:28 to 29:32).

Chicago's 311 yards is the fewest they've had in a win since September 28, 2026 when they only had 271 in a 25 to 24 win in Las Vegas over the Raiders.

The Saints ran more plays (71 to 58) and had a better third-down conversion rate (47.1% to 41.7%).

Both teams had two turnovers, and the Saints were flagged 7 times for 30 yards, while the Bears were flagged 3 times for 15.

Let's look at the Bears' playing-time breakdown and a few individual stats from the game.

Caleb Williams had a ball tipped into the air and intercepted, and he was sacked twice, but I thought he played a solid, low-risk game. He wasn't asked to do much, but he made a few plays when he needed to, and scrambled when he had to.

Kyle Monangai's return didn't jump-start the running game as we hoped, but he seemed to be on a pitch count.

D'Andre Swift had the ball ripped out of his hands as he fell to the ground on a nice play by the defender, but that's still his third fumble this season, tying a career high.

Rookie Zavion Thomas had a 29-yard kickoff return, along with 86 yards on offense, and he's beginning to look like a player worthy of more touches. He also looked really excited to talk with Tom Brady!

Jeffery Simmons had a great debut for the Bears, and his second game was more of the same, but yesterday against the Saints was incredible. According to Next Gen Stats, Simmons had 6 pressures in the game, and those came against four different Saints offensive linemen. All 6 pressures came in one-on-one matchups, tying for the most by a defensive tackle this season. He also lined up on the edge on two of his sacks.

Montez Sweat recorded the first safety of his career, and this was Chicago's first safety since Khalil Mack in 2020.

Clark Phillips III got the start at nickel and picked up the first sack of his four-year career.

Before this game, Jordan ven den Berg had only 2 total tackles on the season.

Cairo Santos was 1 of 2 on extra points and 2 for 2 on field goals.

Tory Taylor had 3 punts with a 46.7 average (40.0 net), with 2 dropped inside the twenty-yard line.
