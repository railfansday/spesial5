Celtics still struggling to develop chemistry with Mitchell Robinson

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

BOSTON — Mitchell Robinson and the Boston Celtics still haven't figured each other out, and the disconnect stood out on Monday night.

Robinson's latest opportunity came against the Orlando Magic, with starting center Neemias Queta sidelined for a rest day. Robinson checked in roughly six minutes into the contest but lasted less than five minutes before being replaced by Luka Garza.

"It's a bigs-by-committee," Celtics head coach Joe Mazzulla said after Boston's 115-105 defeat at TD Garden, its first home loss of the season.

It's been 14 games. Robinson has played in every single one. He's made five starts and logged more than 17 minutes per night. Still, the 28-year-old hasn't gotten on the same page with his new Boston teammates.

Keeping Robinson on the floor became increasingly difficult, especially on the second night of a back-to-back in which the Celtics shot just 25 percent in the opening quarter. Teammates struggled to connect with him on several lob attempts, while Robinson had trouble making enough of an impact to warrant an extended look off Boston's bench.

Even though the effort from Robinson was apparent, it wasn't translating.

Boston's inability to connect with Robinson on clean lob passes predates Monday night. It hasn't mattered whether the pass comes from Jayson Tatum or Derrick White. The Celtics have yet to iron out the wrinkles in their alley-oop execution with their new center.

Mazzulla admits it's not all on Robinson, to be fair.

"We threw a couple over the backboard a couple of times, I think also," Mazzulla said. "So I would say start there. Keep it below the backboard. I think that's a little bit of a timing thing. The lob play is a 2-on-1 read. It's a risk-averse situation. There has to be a great timing to it. The defensive big does a great job of timing it up. So it's not a bang-bang play. It's not an easy 2-on-1 read. It's something that you want to try to take advantage of."

Robinson finished with four points, five rebounds, two assists and a steal against Orlando. He also missed two free throws and picked up five personal fouls.

Boston's bigs combined for just seven rebounds. Franz Wagner alone grabbed eight, while Orlando's Goga Bitadze finished with six off the bench.

Not having Queta against a Magic team that leans into its size took a toll on the Celtics. With Robinson unreliable to deploy, Boston turned to Luka Garza and rookie Chris Cenac Jr. to fill the void. Garza didn't grab a single rebound and took five of his six shot attempts from 3-point range, while Cenac showed (limited) flashes of promise, finding his way into Boston's offensive flow. He finished with four points and two rebounds in just over 12 minutes.

Tatum spoke Cenac's flashes into existence, claiming the 19-year-old is "really, really going to be good," following Sunday night's win over the Dallas Mavericks.

He offered the Houston Cougars product some praise in the locker room again, speaking to the team's belief in their 27th overall selection in this past summer's draft, which Mazzulla also touched on.

Hearing about Tatum's vision for Cenac's future, Mazzulla expanded.

"He's got a lot of work to do, but I like his attitude," Mazzulla said pregame. "I think since Day 1, since he came in the summer, he's been over open-minded to just working and getting better. That's all I care about, especially at this age and stage of where he's at, and I thought that before [Sunday night vs. Dallas] because of his work ethic and his mentality and how he approaches everyday."

Cenac's lobs got TD Garden roaring and helped him start assembling a strong first impression as a pro.

"Chris stepped up," Tatum said, "especially with Neemi out. When his number was called, [he] was active on defense. Got out running in transition. He's a lob threat."

Still, that couldn't prevent the Celtics from getting outclassed by the 6-7 Orlando.

The Magic put the Celtics' frontcourt to shame, outscoring them 54-32. Orlando's final play — a fast-break dunk from a wide-open Jalen Suggs — sent the entire team into a frenzy. The Magic celebrated their first win in Boston since Dec. 18, 2022, while snapping the Celtics' six-game winning streak in the process.

Robinson was a liability, but Mazzulla remains optimistic for what's ahead for No. 4 in the still-young regular season.

"He'll continue to get better and better," Mazzulla said. "That's really the only thing is just the process of growth and him continuing to play the role that we need him to."
