Knicks Bulletin: ‘It’s going to be bittersweet’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Here's the latest before visiting the Wolves in still-not-overly-cold Minnesota.

On Rick Brunson's decision to bring Jose Alvarado into the game against Toronto:

"Just a great call. Rick Brunson made the call of throwing Jose in to see if he can give us a spark. So, just a heckuva call by Rick Brunson and just a fantastic job by Jose producing on both sides of the ball when his name was called."

On Karl-Anthony Towns improving his passing decisions and avoiding risks:

"It's a fine balance because he's capable of making some beautiful passes in tight windows so you want to let him be him. But I thought earlier in the year — and we talked to him about it, and said it a couple times — he was trying to make the home-run pass a little too much.

"And he's gotten to a point where he's mixing it up now. We're going to trust him because he's obviously done it for many years now with good results. We'll lean on him quite a bit in that regard but if we turn the ball over too much, we'll let him know."

On Towns' basketball intelligence and ability to read the game:

"KAT's got tremendous feel. He's a smart, smart dude when it comes to a lot of things, but especially when it comes to the game of basketball."

On Towns' coachability and the impact of assistant coaches Brendan O'Connor and Darren Erman:

"It's easy to see where he is, because everything we've asked him to do, he's tried to do. So, you're extremely coachable. You got a great feel. And you got two guys in (assistant coaches) Brendan O'Connor and Darren Erman that are teaching you the game defensively at a really high level, and you're starting to see the results of it."

On the Knicks' execution and contributions throughout the roster against Toronto:

"Our execution was at an extremely high level. We had a lot of guys contribute in a lot of ways tonight."

On why the NBA must continue penalizing dangerous closeouts and landing-space violations after Brunson's injury scare:

"They got to keep track of it because it's going to happen. Everybody wants to try to block [Brunson's] shot and every other guy's shot. Some guys go forward too much and, if they do, there's got to be a heavy penalty. Because if there's no heavy penalty, then guys won't be conscious of it. They'll just keep sliding underneath guys."

On why his shooting technique sometimes exposes him to dangerous situations:

"I fade a little bit. Taller guys are usually guarding me, so I got to be able to fade a little bit. Sometimes they're not jumping straight up. They're jumping towards me. It happens."

On prioritizing getting buckets over worrying about landing on a defender's foot:

"I'm not worried about falling on his foot. I'm more worried about if he's close enough to block it. If he is too close, I probably won't shoot it. If he's not, ball is going up."

On the Knicks rediscovering their preferred style of play against Toronto:

"We just played to the best of our abilities. I know the early season hasn't looked like it, but that's how we're most comfortable."

On the disappointment of not winning a championship during his nine seasons in Minnesota:

"I wanted to win one there. I didn't accomplish that. But it was great to have those moments, to bring Minnesota back to the playoffs and be able to do a lot of things."

On returning to Minnesota as an NBA champion with the Knicks:

"It's going to be bittersweet. I'm just happy that I have such amazing fans and had such an amazing time in Minnesota. I love it to death. I love that place so it's going to be cool to go back."

On becoming a minority owner of Napoli's basketball team:

"Napoli is a great underdog story and reminds me of what we have built with the Knicks in many ways. It is a project built around hard work, big dreams and the incredible passion Napoli fans have for their team. I've been coming to Naples and the Amalfi Coast for years, and I know what a fan base like this can mean to a team. Seeing this club already making noise in the EuroCup tells you where it's headed.

"Matt Rizzetta and the team at Underdog Global Partners have high ambitions with a real plan for where this club is going. The Napoli journey is still very much in the first quarter, so I am excited to be a part of building this into something truly special in the years ahead."

On Instagram after being demoted from starting center to the second unit:

"Nobody gone save me, gotta save my damn self."
