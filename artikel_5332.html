‘Aether & Iron’: Seattle-area indie game studio builds a noir detective RPG in the sky

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

by Thomas Wilde on Nov 26, 2026 at 8:51 amNovember 26, 2026 at 8:51 am

Some video games exist at the confluence point between a surprising number of genres, which can make them difficult to explain to people who aren’t hip-deep in the hobby. That’s why several of the most popular categories are currently nicknamed after the most visible games in them, such as the “Metroidvania” or “Soulslike.” Sure, this isn’t anywhere near the biggest problem in the video game industry at the moment, but we’re going to have to figure this out sooner or later.

Aether & Iron, created by the Issaquah, Wash.-based developer Seismic Squirrel, with co-developer Chaos Theory Games, is at one of those weird genre points. It’s described by its developers as a “narrative RPG,” which works well enough, but that also doesn’t exactly say that much.

It’s worth saying up front that Aether & Iron is on a slow boil. It’s a 30-hour game that unfolds at a careful pace, building out an elaborate science-fiction setting from first principles. If you’re looking for fast thrills, it’s not the experience for you. Instead, think of it like a long novel with points of potential failure, where every opportunity for interactivity is also a chance for you to take its story in a different direction.

Aether & Iron is set in an alternate universe where a strange substance called aether was found beneath Manhattan in 1871. Sixty-seven years later, the study and exploitation of aether has become its own branch of science, which led to a great technological leap forward. Much of New York has been transformed into a three-tiered flying city, split between the Lowers, the Uppers and the Heights. The higher a neighborhood flies, the higher its society.

You enter the story in 1938 in the role of Gia Randazzo, a smuggler from the Lowers who’s hit hard times. She’s so desperate for work that she takes a job she shouldn’t, as a driver and bodyguard for a young scientist from the Heights named Eleanor Reinhardt. It should be a milk run, but Dr. Reinhardt has enemies she doesn’t know about.

In short order, an assassin tries to kill them both, someone steals Gia’s car, and she and Dr. Reinhardt end up stuck together in the worst parts of the Lowers. With no money, a loaner vehicle and few friends left, Gia has to figure out who wants Dr. Reinhardt dead and why. That puts her on a collision course with a revolutionary movement, several of New York’s power brokers and a conspiracy that could knock the city back out of the sky.

You could call Aether & Iron something like “airship noir.” Despite its science-fiction trappings, it’s a dark detective novel at heart. Yes, humanity’s built up into the sky, but the street is always the street. Mickey Spillane meets William Gibson.

During dialogue and investigation sequences, Aether & Iron uses a pair of virtual six-sided dice to determine whether you succeed or fail at a given task. As you get further into the game, you can acquire items that let you reroll those dice on a failed check.

More importantly, you earn experience points for accomplishments and victories, which grant you skill points that can be spent in one of nine categories. You can customize Gia to follow several distinct character tracks, such as a detective, a back-alley brawler or a fast-talking con woman. That influences the path you take through the game and the options that become available to you.

No matter how you choose to play Gia, you can’t always avoid a fight. All the combat in Aether & Iron takes place behind the wheel, with Gia and her crew driving customized vehicles. You can acquire a whole fleet of cars and trucks, then kit them out with special weapons, utility devices, armored plates and matching paint jobs.

Each fight consists of a turn-based tactical battle through the streets in and around New York. In addition to simply shooting up an enemy, you can sideswipe them to force them off the road or into other cars.

Many of the city’s thoroughfares are falling apart or prone to sudden lane changes, so most fights will have their own set of additional hazards. Fortunately, they’re as dangerous to your enemies as they are to you, so you can exploit the road hazards if you think fast enough.

If I had to point at one problem I’ve got with Aether & Iron, it might be that the combat has a couple of unlabeled difficulty curves. Specifically, a few fights are built around specific types of damage to such a degree that the only real way past them is to reload an old save and modify all my vehicles for that particular encounter.

If you like the idea of constantly tweaking your vehicle collection for specific combat purposes, like heat shielding or knockback resistance, you’ll have some fun here. If you’re like me and you tend to rely on set-and-forget general-purpose builds, you’ll run into some roadblocks.

Other than that, you might know already whether or not you want to check it out. There’s a lot of care and art in Aether & Iron, mixed with a unique science-fiction setting that isn’t quite like anything else in video games at the moment.

Aether & Iron is currently available for PC and Mac via Steam for $24.99. Seismic Squirrel plans to release the game for PlayStation and Xbox in 2027.
