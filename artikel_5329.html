Washington state regulator issues temporary stop on new business for Directors Mortgage

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Washington’s Department of Financial Institutions issued an emergency order for Directors Mortgage to temporarily cease accepting new business in the state. Examiners said the company failed to provide requested records and showed signs it was not meeting financial obligations to employees and vendors.   AI Summary

The Washington Department of Financial Institutions (DFI) has ordered Directors Mortgage to temporarily stop taking on new business in the state, including the origination of loans for Washington residents or for properties located in the state, citing exam interference and concerns about the company’s finances.

According to the DFI’s order, examiners who conducted an on-site review at the Oregon-based lender’s main office from Nov. 17 to Nov. 19 were not provided requested information and records. During that period, the department served a subpoena on the company’s CEO and owner to obtain the production of original books, accounts, papers, records and files.

The department also said it obtained information during the examination indicating that the company was not meeting its financial obligations to employees or third-party vendors.

“Respondent’s failure to comply with the Department’s examination authority and Respondent’s current financial condition pose a serious risk of injury to the public,” the order states.

A representative of Directors Mortgage did not immediately respond to HousingWire’s request for comment.

The emergency order, signed on Friday by Brian J. Guerard, Washington DFI’s acting director, requires Directors Mortgage to deliver detailed financial information, including balance sheets and bank account data, and to fully respond to the department’s subpoena. The order also directs the company to satisfy outstanding financial obligations.

Under Washington law, companies may contest such orders in an administrative hearing. If a respondent does not seek a hearing, the order becomes permanent when DFI issues a final order.

Directors Mortgage, founded in 1998 by president and CEO Mark J. Hanna and headquartered in Oregon, has originated about $235 million in mortgages year to date, according to InGenius. The company had roughly 40 loan officers and nine active branches as of Monday, per data from the Nationwide Multistate Licensing System.

Washington’s DFI has been active recently regarding mortgage enforcement. Newrez recently agreed to pay roughly $3 million and implement wide-ranging mortgage servicing changes to resolve allegations by the department that it engaged in unfair or deceptive acts or practices.

Earlier this year, Washington state regulators issued a $75,000 fine and a $3,000 investigation fee against West Capital Lending, alleging multiple violations of the state’s consumer loan act. The company was accused of allowing unlicensed individuals to conduct mortgage-related activities and of failing to meet certain disclosure and reporting requirements. Per the agreement, WCL did not admit to the allegations nor any wrongdoing.

Meanwhile, Rocket Mortgage and Mr. Cooper Group recently agreed to pay a combined $6.75 million to the New York State Department of Financial Services to resolve allegations of cybersecurity violations tied to data protection, risk assessments and incident reporting.
