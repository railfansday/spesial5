Fabian Hürzeler sounded a little way of Hull in his pre-match press conference, citing the Tigers’ narrow 1-0 defeat to table-topping (and possible title rivals) Arsenal as a template for how today’s game might go.

“I think they do an impressive job,” Hürzeler said. “They had a brilliant start in the Premier League. They are very structured. Against Arsenal, I think Arsenal suffered. They [Arsenal] didn’t create a lot of big chances, and therefore we need to be patient because we also know that Hull are really good in transition.

“They also have individual quality in their team. Very well structured, very well organised, good in transition, good in set pieces, and I think it’s not an easy place to go to.”

Hull obviously beat Manchester United 2-0 in their opening home game and have drawn four successive matches at the MKM Stadium since, so that proves Hürzeler’s point somewhat.

My personal two-pennies-worth is that Brighton will probably fall away from the title race at some stage after Christmas. Their fixture list in the short term is fairly inviting, so I see their lofty position being sustained for a little while.

But realistically, a top five finish and Champions League qualification should be their primary objective. That’s really not impossible when you consider the struggles of Manchester United and Chelsea, and the possible fate of Manchester City if and when a verdict/punishment arrives re their 115s charges.

The fact Fabian Hürzeler could achieve that just after his 34th birthday would be an astonishing feat in itself.

The stats don’t lie when it comes to Brighton’s brilliant form. They are well clear as the Premier League’s top scorers with 26, despite many of their rivals having played a game more. They are also level with Arsenal (7) in terms of goals conceded, again having played fewer games than the Gunners.

Pascal Gross also leads way in the league this season for chances created (29).

They don’t have one outstanding goalscorer, but Gross is on four so far this season, with Yasin Ayari, Jack Hinshelwood and Charalampos Kostoulas all chipping in with three.

It looks like Hull could line up in a 4-4-2 today. It remains to be seen where Mohamed-Ali Cho will play, either on one of the flanks or pushed up alongside Oliver McBurnie. Mohamed Belloumi has been their primary creator this season, so watch out for him.

Brighton will surely shape up in their standard 4-2-3-1 formation, fluid as that is, with the likes of Diego Gomez, Yasin Ayari and Pascal Gross rotating between the midfield lines.

Peter Bankes (from Merseyside) is the referee today, with Andrew Kitchen in the VAR booth.

Hello and welcome to our live coverage of the solitary early kick-off in the Premier League today. Many will view Hull City versus Brighton as a mere hors d’oeuvres to the main dish of Liverpool against Manchester United later, but there is plenty riding on it.

Hull have not won a game in any competition since claiming back-to-back victories in a shock opening burst after winning promotion, while Brighton have lost just twice across 17 matches in all competitions. So this is a nailed-on Seagulls win, right?

Maybe, though Fabian Hürzeler will be guarding against complacency, especially because Hull have not lost by more than one goal in any game this season and have not conceded more than two. They are the proverbial hard nut to crack, albeit scoring goals is a problem for the Tigers – despite sitting 14th in the table, only two teams (Nottingham Forest and Sunderland) have scored fewer.

Team news has already dropped so we’ll bring you that in the next post. Kick-off is at 2pm (GMT) so please do stay tuned. I’m predicting this will be a tighter game than many would imagine.
