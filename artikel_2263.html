Celtic revival or one-off resistance and is Duran the best player in Scotland?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Welcome to The Burning Questions, our new daily debate where we take one major subject in Scottish sport and tackle the three questions everyone is talking about.

Each day, our writers will offer their verdicts on the biggest issues and arguments dominating the agenda. And Celtic are back under the spotlight after Martin O’Neill’s side delivered a much-needed European victory over Besiktas.

O’Neill appeared visibly emotional at the full-time whistle after watching his team produce an inspired display, but was this the start of a genuine turnaround or simply a singular act of resistance in a difficult season?

Then there’s Camilo Duran. Celtic’s recruitment has taken plenty of criticism, but the £5.5million Colombian continues to deliver and his latest goals have strengthened his claim to be the best player in Scottish football right now.

Read more: Who says Martin O'Neill is out of touch? Besiktas win was a tactical masterclass

And what about Europe? Despite their setbacks, Celtic’s hopes of reaching the Europa League knockout play-off round remain alive. Can O’Neill’s men build on the Besiktas win and collect enough points from their remaining four games to squeeze into the top 24?

Matthew Lindsay and Graeme Young give their answers to today’s three Burning Questions.

Matthew Lindsay: Time will tell. The 7-1 mauling of Hearts last month certainly proved to be a false dawn for the Scottish champions. Defeats against Celta Vigo and Falkirk and a draw with Dundee United followed that impressive result.

Celtic were excellent against a very good Besiktas team last night. They contained opponents who had beaten Hoffenheim and Marseille and drawn with Crystal Palace extremely well, while creating a raft of scoring chances in the final half-hour. It was a deserved victory and they should have prevailed by a more comfortable margin.

But I still fear Celtic are short of the quality they need in key areas. They desperately need a physical, defensively-minded central midfielder and I suspect their lack of strength in depth will prevent them from going on an extended winning run in the weeks ahead.

That said, I didn’t expect them to produce such an inspired display against Vincenzo Italiano’s men. The performance lifted the Celtic supporters and the players can tap into that feelgood factor going forward.

Graeme Young: I have a feeling this might still prove to be the start of a roaring second half of the season that defies convention. There was every reason to be perplexed by Celtic’s starting XI, but naming the likes of Kasper Hogh and Sebastien Tounekti allowed thrilling talents such as Camilo Duran and Callum Osmand to enter the game as if they had been fired out of a cannon.

But it would be remiss to downplay Celtic’s patterns of play, or lack thereof, in the biggest matches. Besiktas turned out to be a bit of a Turkey. It’s unlikely the better teams they face will be quite so welcoming as the fixtures continue to pile up.

Matthew Lindsay: Yes. For me, the Colombian is the form player in the country just now. His brace against Besiktas took his tally for the 2026/27 campaign to 13. Considering he has often been played out of position out wide, it is some return. He has been £5.5million very well spent indeed.

There is still an awfully long way to go before the Player of the Year awards are doled out and much can change. But if Celtic improve at home and abroad in the weeks and months ahead, and their midweek win augurs well for the future, then Duran will have even more scoring chances.

There have been a few standouts in Scottish football this term. Josh Fowler at St Johnstone is the leading marksman in the top flight, Ryan Naderi at Rangers has been inspired at times and Josh McPake has forced his way into the Scotland set-up. But the former Qarabag forward is the pick of the bunch.

Graeme Young: Yes. Rangers star defender Olwethu Makhanya looks every inch a superstar in the making, but this beautiful old game of ours is ultimately decided by the players who put the ball in the net.

Duran brings goals, versatility and an uncanny knack for making the difference in the biggest moments. His connection with the aforementioned Osmand is electric and feels like Celtic’s best shot at getting themselves firing in the right direction.

Matthew Lindsay: The Scottish champions' fine win over Besiktas moved them to within a point of the clubs occupying the knockout play-off spots in the Europa League league phase table. But going through remains a tall order.

They have three difficult away matches against Torreense, Omonia Nicosia and Royal Union Saint-Gilloise coming up in the months ahead. They will need to win on the road to progress.

Second-tier Portuguese outfit Torreense, who are up next in three weeks, showed they will not be easily overcome once again last night by beating Ararat-Armenia at home to move to six points.

If Celtic maintain the levels they hit last night, they could do it. But that is a big if. I fear their opening losses to Ferencvaros, Benfica and Celta Vigo may come back to haunt them.

Graeme Young: Do you know what? They might just do it.

O’Neill landed wins against Feyenoord and Utrecht, as well as a point in Bologna, during the second half of their Europa League campaign last season and looks a good bet to do something similar again.

Torreense, the Portuguese second-tier side, feels like a danger mission. Damned if you do, condemned if you don’t. Omonia doesn’t look the most challenging assignment and then it’s Marseille, who are also toiling, before Celtic finish against Union Saint-Gilloise, who may already be safely inside the top eight by the time they face the Premiership champions.

If Celtic land seven points from their final four games, it might just be enough to squeeze them into the top 24. But only just.
