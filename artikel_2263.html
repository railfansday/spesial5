Ally McCoist left in stitches as Derek McInnes makes Rangers admission on-air

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Rangers hero Ally McCoist was left in stitches as Derek McInnes issued an on-air one-liner over his start to life as Rangers manager.

The Ibrox hero couldn't contain his laughter as the Rangers boss admitted he faced a rocky opening to his tenure in Govan.

Rangers had exited the Europa League to Jagiellonia Bialystok, then the Conference League qualifiers to Jablonec before a draw and defeat in their opening two league matches.

However, since then Rangers have gone on a run of 11 matches unbeaten, advancing to the Premier Sports Cup final with victory over Aberdeen at Hampden this weekend.

But McInnes hasn't forgotten the early struggles as he joked with Jeff Stelling and McCoist on talkSPORT on Monday.

In a quip in response to Stelling's introduction discussing the challenges in the early stages of the season, McInnes said: "You've underestimated that slightly rocky part I think!"

The live stream then cut to McCoist who was doubled over at McInnes' honest assessment.

McInnes continued: "When you get a new job, it's normally for a reason – nothing is normally perfect. We had lots of work to do, but underneath it all, I felt if we came out the window with a strong enough squad, then we could compete. That's where we are at the minute.

"We needed a bit of confidence. The run started up at Aberdeen, going 11 games [unbeaten], and we're pleased with what we're doing. There's still a lot of improvement [needed]. But as Ally knows, at Rangers, we need to keep winning.

"The noise and everything that comes with it when you're not winning makes things so much more difficult.

"But I'm delighted with the response of the players. The game on Saturday was another case in point. We were the better team, we created more chances, we look as if we're scoring, but it took us too long to get the winning goal. But when it came, it was a great moment for us, and we look forward to the final now."

On there being progress to be made by his Rangers side, McInnes added: "There has to be (more improvement to come). No team is perfect at this stage of the season, the season is still in its infancy.

"People are so quick to judge and assume this is how it's going to be, but it's so early on. There's loads of improvements to come from us, but that's the same with most teams.

"We're a very young team, particularly in the forward line.

"A lot of stats will back that up. Sometimes we could do with a bit more nous and an older head up there just to try and make better decisions. But the potential is exciting, and the reach of some of these players is evident. But we need to try and win as we search for that potential."
