What we learned as Keegan Murray game-winner snaps Kings' historic home skid

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

What we learned as Keegan Murray game-winner snaps Kings' historic home skid originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SACRAMENTO — It came down to their final possession for the second straight night for the Kings on Saturday at Golden 1 Center.

Trailing 96-95 to the Boston Celtics with 11.8 seconds left, Darius Acuff Jr. missed a driving layup. But Keegan Murray was there for the offensive rebound over former teammate Devin Carter, and his put back gave Sacramento a 97-96 lead over the team with the second-best record in the Eastern Conference.

Boston had a chance to win it at the end. But Murray stayed glued to Celtics guard Payton Pritchard and forced a tough miss, giving the Kings their first home win of the 2026-7 NBA season.

And it was Acuff's first time lighting the beam as the Kings snapped the longest home winless streak to begin a season in franchise history at nine games. Sacramento improved to 2-14 on the season.

Acuff led the Kings with 21 points on 7-of-23 shooting, while De'Andre Hunter had 20 points and six rebounds.

For Boston, they got a season-high 19 points from Mike Conley Jr. and 19 points with eight rebounds from Paul George. The Celtics shot just 33.7 percent while missing star Jayson Tatum, who was given the night off for rest.

Murray's five-year, $140 million contract he signed last November kicked in this season. And to this point, there were questions as to whether the Kings made a mistake by inking Murray to such a lucrative long-term deal.

He came into the game averaging 10.3 points while shooting just 25 percent from 3-point range, and had been dealing with ankle capsulitis stemming from an injury last season.

Murray finished Saturday's game with 15 points and 12 rebounds, and made the game's two biggest plays.

Sacramento was sloppy offensively after playing Friday night against the Los Angeles Lakers. They allowed the Celtics to take a 54-41 lead into halftime despite Boston shooting just 37.3 percent.

That was because the Celtics had 16 more shot attempts, thanks to Sacramento's nine turnovers leading to 14 points. Domantas Sabonis had five by himself, with four coming during a rough stint early in the second quarter.

Sabonis finished with 15 points, 17 rebounds and a game-high seven turnovers. Nine of his points came in the third quarter. He helped Sacramento outscore the Celtics 56-42 after halftime.

The Kings cut Boston's lead to just two while starting the second half on a 17-6 run. The run came in contrast to Sacramento's season-long trend of being the NBA's worst third-quarter team.

They came into the game ranked dead last in third-quarter point differential at minus-88 in 15 games.

Sacramento tied the game at 60 on a fast-break dunk from Leaky Black. And Acuff gave the Kings the lead with a pull-up 3 moments later.

In all, the Kings outscored the Celtics 34-13 in the frame and took a nine-point lead into the fourth. Boston made just 5 of 27 shots (18.5 percent) while missing all 13 of their 3-pointers.

Four Kings starters – Acuff, Hunter, Zach LaVine and Sabonis – played all 12 minutes in the third.

The Kings up next will host the San Antonio Spurs on Monday at Golden 1 Center.
