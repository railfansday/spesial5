Blizzard adjusts popular World of Warcraft: Forever Battleground to stop players power-grinding rewards

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

"The overall intent for all of these changes was for games to feel more meaningful."

Many World of Warcraft: Forever players are currently in the midst of a substantial PvP reputation grind. However, this process has been disrupted due to a sudden change to one of the game's battleground PvP arenas.

The battleground in question is Alterac Valley, a 40 person-a-side clash between Alliance and Horde factions, where both sides need to push their forces across a snowy, mountainous map into the opposing faction's base to take down the NPC General waiting there. Alterac Valley is being grinded by max-level players for its gear rewards that are given once reaching "exalted" reputation there. Currently it's some of the best gear available ahead of raids being introduced next month.

Played the intended way, Alterac Valley is a lengthy battle. The Generals on both sides are fearsomely tough but by rushing to capture certain structures on the way to this battle, you can weaken the Generals enough to make them easier to overcome. As such, players have been fast-tracking this PvP instance, skipping smaller objectives and rushing core objectives to get to the Generals as fast as possible. This bypassed a lot of the incidental skirmishing allure of Alterac Valley, but it was an efficient way to quickly gain reputation and Honor points (PvP currency).

But this strategy has been pretty much killed outright as of today. In a post made to the official World of Warcraft forums, community manager Kaivax revealed that the reputation gained from killing the Generals has been drastically reduced, while the reputation gained from killing other NPCs and capturing previously ignored objectives now grants more. Generals are also granted a buff that makes them incredibly strong for the first 20 minutes of a match, which is then reduced for each objective taken.

This is a good change in regard to the experience of playing Alterac Valley. While rushing through was good for players looking to gain reputation quickly, they weren't really playing the Battleground in the way it's meant to be played. The dev post reads: "The overall intent for all of these changes was for games to feel more meaningful while trying to maintain a similar amount of rep gained over a similar playtime within the battleground, even with longer match lengths. There will be fluctuations depending on how successful you are at destroying towers and holding capture points at the end of a match however."

So how has the community reacted? Badly! Taking a gander at community hubs following this change shows plenty of frustration. Take this comment by Reddit user Athrek: "I'm fine with the changes if they give it Rank Points and calendar events. But they've gutted the rep for both sides, extended the time it takes to play, and it still gives zero Rank Points. Now you may as well get to the win condition and then sit there for a few hours, gaining the passive rep cause there's nothing else of value to be had."

It appears as of writing that the reputation gained isn't as much as it was previously, so those playing purely for reputation have been hit. There's another element of frustration which is that those who rushed to level 60 and ground Alterac Valley reputation and rewards before the changes won't be affected by this. Perhaps that's the allure of rushing to level 60!

"This is why the WoW mantra is 'exploit early, exploit often'," writes user Lanienah12. "Some people are already exalted with AV rep and will receive no punishment for it; meanwhile new people will be spending 3x as long." Especially in the wake of the Blackmaw Hold gold exploit being discovered by these frontrunners, there's a growing belief that those taking their time or that are limited by real-life factors are feeling the brunt of these changes.
