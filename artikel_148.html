Under-threat lidos get lifeline as Andy Burnham sets up fund to rebuild pools

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Move reflects growing demand for outdoor swimming as Britain heats up and follows campaign by MPs

Andy Burnham is setting up a public fund to rebuild the UK’s lidos after a revival in outdoor swimming.

Outdoor pools were built across the UK in the 1930s. The often beautiful, art deco constructions were made as a cheap way to provide access to healthy activity during the summer.

In the following decades the lidos fell into decline and by the end of the 1980s nearly two-thirds were derelict or demolished. Britain had more than 300 lidos in the 1930s but only about a third were still open in 1990. Some survivors are still at risk of closing down today and communities are fighting to keep them open.

Now, the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport has committed to creating a dedicated national fund to reinvigorate the UK’s lidos, after a campaign by MPs.

The move reflects the growing demand for outdoor swimming as summers in Britain heat up, and the fact that many communities do not have a place for cooling off. Better, the biggest leisure centre group in the country, which runs 10 outdoor swimming centres, said 2026 was by far its busiest year for outdoor swimming, with trips to its lidos between June and August rising by 28% to 692,471 visits.

Lisa Nandy, the culture secretary, said the investment would be “the first dedicated fund for lidos that this country has produced” and would form part of a wider public leisure facilities programme.

MPs who have campaigned to keep lidos open in their cities welcomed news of the fund.

Andrew Pakes, the Labour MP for Peterborough, who campaigned to keep the city’s lido open after the council’s budget plans suggested it could be mothballed, said the announcement was welcome news.

This year, Peterborough’s Grade II-listed art deco lido celebrated its 90th anniversary and its best ever season, which Pakes said echoed the experience at outdoor swimming facilities in many other cities. “This makes protecting and supporting our pools even more important,” he said.

He has been leading a group of MPs who have been calling for funding to build or reopen lidos in their local areas. “For places like Peterborough, the lido is far more than somewhere to swim. It is a much-loved part of our city’s heritage, bringing people together across generations,” he said.

The Carlisle MP, Julie Minns, who founded the campaign to save the city’s Grade II-listed Victorian and Turkish Baths, said: “These are places that bring communities together, support physical and mental wellbeing and form an important part of our shared heritage. In Carlisle, our Victorian and Turkish Baths are one of the city’s most distinctive historic buildings and have served generations of local residents.”
