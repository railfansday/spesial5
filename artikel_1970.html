Hearts sporting director addresses January transfer plans, ‘unprecedented’ injuries and recent form

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Sporting director Graeme Jones has pinpointed European football and a new playing style among the key reasons for Hearts' "unprecedented" injury situation.

The Tynecastle side were missing 11 first-team players for most recent match - a 5-2 home defeat by Falkirk before the international break - and then lost another when Malachi Fagan-Walcott limped off.

The absences have coincided with a run of just one win in eight matches across all competitions, leaving Wouter Vrancken's side fourth in the Scottish Premiership and 32nd in the Uefa Conference League.

"I need to look beyond the bad luck theory and dig into possible circumstances or reasons as to why we find ourselves where we are," Jones said in a club interview. "We've got a new head coach that's brought in an intended new front foot style philosophy of how we play. We have new players who are adapting to a more competitive league than what they've been used to previously.

"We have European football which is becoming the standard we want every season but the demands that Thursday-Sunday brings on the team. And then there's also the part of the bad luck which is we've suffered a huge amount of contact injuries so far this season, more in this short period than the whole of last season.

"So, unfortunate as it is, it's a convergence of all these factors. But I also think it's important to realise that there is light at the end of the tunnel. We have players returning for the game coming up on Saturday [away to Dundee].

"We're hoping to have up to six, maybe even seven players possibly back into contention for first team training. Whether they're included in the squad, it just might be too soon, but we'll manage that accordingly. I can be confident that things are going to improve in the second half of the season, I've got no doubts on that."

Jones reassured fans that Hearts are "not sitting on our hands" with regards to addressing their injury issues.

He added: "We conducted an external review of our performance area at the turn of this year. We utilised one of the top performance practitioners in the the UK and he conducted an in-depth review that came with a numerous recommendations of things that we needed to improve. We've implemented all of these things.

"We have invested heavily in our medical department and even though it might not feel like it from the outside looking in, we're in a much better place to deal with this unprecedented level of injuries than we would have been a year ago."

Hearts came into the campaign looking to build on last season's runners-up finish in the Scottish Premiership after narrowly missing out on a first title in 66 years to Celtic on the final day. However, after 12 matches, they have collected 10 points less than they had at the same stage last season with 12 more goals conceded. They also lost their 18-month unbeaten home record against Falkirk and were beaten by rivals Hibs at Hampden for the first time in the semi-finals of the Premier Sports Cup.

"It's not been good enough," Jones admitted. "I know that, Wouter knows that, the players know that and we're doing everything during this international week to recalibrate and rest and put things right as best we can.

"It's important to have the big picture in mind when you're in my role and looking towards the future. But I totally understand the short-term results have a massive effect on the general harmony across the board, particularly with our fans.

"I would never sit here and tell fans to be patient and calm during these periods. I am a fan, I've sat in the stand during some of the worst times throughout the club's history so I do understand. But we as a club and me as the sporting director have to remain focused on the objectives and the long-term future of the club and the success of the club that we are striving for."

Addressing Hearts' plans for the January transfer window, Jones, who was appointed sporting director two years ago, said the focus would be on balancing the squad.

"We've got a very large squad with double figures number of players that are unavailable recently so as they return back we will naturally have to look at creating balance within the squad," he said. "So we will look to decrease the size of the squad but also only add with quality. So it will be an active window like all transfer windows are but as we all know January is notoriously difficult.

"We've got plenty of evidence in the last two years that the players that sign in January don't always come in and hit the ground running. So we have to focus our attentions on the quality that can make a difference but we will be very much looking to create a bit of balance within the squad. Once all our players come back are fit and available and then looking to add quality and also help people move on that aren't getting the opportunities."
