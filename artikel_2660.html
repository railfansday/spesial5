Ohio State falls in fourth College Football Playoff rankings

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Well, that was the fall everyone saw coming, but maybe not as far as what we thought. It still landed hard because of what it means.

The College Football Playoff selection committee released its fourth rankings of the season on Tuesday night, and Ohio State is down to No. 21. That's a seven-spot tumble from No. 14 a week ago, after the 31-28 loss at Nebraska dropped the Buckeyes to 7-4.

USA TODAY Sports had predicted Ohio State would fall to No. 20, but many thought the Buckeyes might drop out of the rankings completely to fall in line with the US LBM Coaches Poll. The committee went one spot further. And honestly, that's about right.

This is what four losses gets you in November. The Buckeyes aren't in the playoff field. They aren't close to the field. At 7-4, the playoff conversation is over, and it has been over since the final whistle in Lincoln.

Let's be honest about how we got here. Ohio State has now lost three of its last four games. The Nebraska loss was the one that ended it -- a 31-28 gut punch on the road in which the Buckeyes fought back to tie it in the third quarter and still found a way to lose.

The committee noticed. Last week, Ohio State jumped six spots to No. 14 after beating Northwestern, and for a moment there was a path. A faint one, but a path. Win out, get some help, maybe sneak into the Big Ten championship picture. That path is gone now.

And here's the thing: The committee didn't just punish Ohio State for losing to Nebraska. It punished the Buckeyes for the whole month. The loss at USC. The loss to Oregon. The loss at Nebraska. Three losses in four games will do that to a team, even one with Ohio State's schedule strength and its win at Indiana back on Oct. 17.

The Buckeyes still own that win over the Hoosiers, 19-15 in Bloomington. That is still the best thing on the resume. It just doesn't carry the weight it used to, not when the losses keep piling up behind it.

If there's any consolation, it's that the committee was kinder than the polls. The AP dropped Ohio State all the way to No. 25, and the Buckeyes fell out of the Coaches Poll entirely for the first time in 15 years. At No. 21, the committee still thinks more of this team than the voters do. Small comfort, but it's something.

Everything, actually. Just not what it used to mean.

Ohio State vs. Michigan kicks off at noon Saturday at Ohio Stadium, and the Buckeyes are still playing for something real. A win over Michigan doesn't rescue the playoff hopes -- those are gone, and no one should pretend otherwise. But it does decide whether this season ends as a disappointment or a disaster.

Beat Michigan, and Ohio State finishes 8-4 with a rivalry win to carry into the offseason. Lose, and it's 7-5 with a third straight loss to end the regular season and a fan base that will spend the whole winter asking how a team this talented lost five games.

That's the stakes now. Not the playoff. The rivalry. And in Columbus, that has always been enough.

If there's one reason to believe Saturday goes Ohio State's way, it's the same reason there has been all season: Jeremiah Smith.

Smith was brilliant again at Nebraska -- six catches, 165 yards and two touchdowns -- and in the process became the first Big Ten receiver ever to reach 4,000 career yards. He also tied Terry Glenn's Ohio State single-season record with 17 receiving touchdowns.

Michigan knows all of that. Michigan will plan for all of that. And Smith will probably still make a play or two that makes you shake your head.

The question is whether the rest of the team can match him. Freshman quarterback Luke Fahey has been starting since Julian Sayin went down at Indiana, and he threw for 259 yards and two touchdowns in his first road start at Nebraska. He'll need to be better than that on Saturday. The defense, the one that has been the story of this slide, will need to be a whole lot better than that.

Ohio State wasn't the only Big Ten team to take a hit on Tuesday night. It was a rough week for the whole league.

UCLA fell five spots to No. 9 after the triple-overtime loss at Michigan, 18-16, ended the Bruins' unbeaten season. UCLA is still in the field at 10-1, but the margin for error is gone -- and the Bruins are dealing with an injury to quarterback Ty Dieffenbach on top of it. They close with USC on Saturday.

Indiana dropped six spots to No. 14 after its own overtime loss, 27-24 at Washington. The defending national champions are now on the outside looking in, behind three Big Ten rivals. They finish with Purdue.

And that's the other story of this release: Oregon and Iowa both moved up past Indiana. Oregon climbed one spot to No. 12, and Iowa jumped two to No. 13. The Hawkeyes now sit ahead of the Hoosiers despite having the same 9-2 record -- and Ohio State's win at Iowa back in the season suddenly looks like one of the few results still holding up for the Buckeyes.

Oh, and one more twist: Nebraska is ranked. The Cornhuskers entered the top 25 at No. 25 after beating Ohio State, their reward for handing the Buckeyes their fourth loss.

At the top, Georgia stayed No. 1, followed by Utah, Texas and Notre Dame. Alabama slid three spots to No. 6.

The rankings told Ohio State what it already knew. This isn't a playoff team. Not this year. Not at 7-4, not after losing three of four, not with the way the second half of the season has gone.

But Saturday still matters. It always matters. Michigan is coming to Columbus, and the Buckeyes get one more chance to salvage something from a season that slipped away from them in November.

Win, and the story of this season has a decent final chapter. Lose, and there is nothing left to say except wait until next year.

At some point, this team has to show it can finish. Saturday is the last chance to do it.
