Kyla Shanahan vs. Brian Flores is an all-time coaching battle. Who has the edge in 49ers-Vikings?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The San Francisco 49ers and Minnesota Vikings have been two of the best teams in the NFL so far this season, and on Sunday night they will do battle in Mexico City. Not only will this game bring some bragging rights as to who is the best team in the NFC and the NFL, but it will go a long way toward deciding who the No. 1 seed in the NFC ends up being when the playoffs roll around.

The 49ers have been arguably the best team in the NFL all season, despite a number of injuries affecting them on both sides of the ball. The offense has been the best in the NFL, Brock Purdy is an MVP candidate, and the defense has been surprisingly good and opportunistic enough to keep them ahead in most games. The Niners have navigated a very tough NFC West and have a big lead in the division heading into Week 11 as a result.

The Vikings have had a somewhat tumultuous season for a variety of reasons. On one hand, Minnesota was the final unbeaten team in the NFL and has arguably the best defense in the league led by Brian Flores. On the other side of the ball, however, things have been choppy at best. The Kyler Murray experiment didn't pan out, as the former No. 1 overall pick was inconsistent and banged up throughout the year, leading the Vikings to bring back Kirk Cousins in a deadline deal with the Raiders.

The first game of the Cousins reunion was a good one, as the Vikings got a very impressive 30-10 road win over the Green Bay Packers to get to 7-2. Now, they get another test against San Francisco in Week 11.

Of course, much of the attention coming into this game is on the battle between Kyle Shanahan and Brian Flores, arguably the two best play-callers in the sport. Which buttons will each of them press in this game, and who has the advantage?

Kyle Shanahan has built an offense based on timing, efficiency, and precision. All of his starting quarterbacks, all the way back to Matt Ryan's MVP season in Atlanta in 2016, have played with that, and Brock Purdy is no different. The structure of the Shanahan system is as reliable as it gets, and he has had elite offenses with varying levels of quarterback play — from Ryan to Jimmy Garoppolo to Purdy — as a result.

But Purdy has unlocked a new element of this offense with his playmaking ability. Sure, he plays with great timing and anticipation from the pocket on most plays, and that allows him to run the Shanahan system and take what's there from the play design. But when the design doesn't give him anything and he has to extend the play and find something on his own, he is able to do that in a way that Shanahan's other quarterbacks have not been. Some of the biggest plays from the 49ers' Week 10 win over the Dallas Cowboys came from Purdy extending the play before finding a big chunk down the field.

Purdy's playmaking and escapability is the trump card for the 49ers offense. It is what takes it from an elite unit to the best in the NFL, and the Vikings will have to limit it on Sunday night if they want to get the win.

Of course, what Flores wants to do on defense is no secret at this point. The Vikings are blitzing on 72.3% of opposing dropbacks. their time to pressure is 2.49 seconds, the second-quickest mark in the NFL. Their 46.5% pressure rate and 11.9% sack rate both lead the league by a massive margin. Flores wants to heat you up quickly, scheme up looks to beat your protection and get free runners, and make the quarterback uncomfortable in the pocket.

When the Vikings do bring pressure in this game, which will likely be almost all the time, they have to get Purdy on the ground. In 2024, the last time these two teams played, Purdy was sacked six times and had one of his least efficient games of the season against the Vikings, a 23-17 Minnesota win. These are different teams than they were back then, of course, but Purdy went viral after the game for telling Flores on the field that his scheme is "insane."

Purdy has been pretty tough to get on the ground this season. His pressure-to-sack ratio, just 14.3%, is the eighth-lowest number in the NFL per PFF. He is being pressured just 27.9% of the time, least in the NFL. Only Bo Nix, Patrick Mahomes and Michael Penix Jr. have been sacked less.

So Purdy is a pro at extending plays. His efficiency numbers are elite no matter which way you slice it, and that is true for extended dropbacks. On dropbacks over 2.5 seconds, Purdy's +0.15 EPA per dropback ranks tied for second in the NFL.

The good news for the Vikings is that they have completely shut down any passes on those extended plays this season. On those same dropbacks over 2.5 seconds, the Vikings are allowing an incredible -0.52 EPA per play and 3.3 yards per play, almost a full yard better than second place. Extending the play has been death for any quarterback going against Flores' defense this season, and now they get to test it again against one of the league's best.

If extending the play against the blitz isn't an option, the 49ers will have to find quick answers when Minnesota brings pressure. This is an area where the 49ers can get after the Vikings if they are consistent and efficient with it. But Purdy will have to be on top of his reads and know where to go with the ball almost every time, because the one time the Vikings catch you confused, the consequences are massive.

When teams have gotten the ball out in under 2.5 seconds this season, they have had success. The Vikings rank just 21st in the NFL in EPA per dropback against quick passes (+0.13). They are allowing 7.1 yards per play on those throws, many of which are screens or quick-hitters, but it is an area where you can get after them a little bit.

On the San Francisco side of things, Purdy has been elite when he gets the ball out of his hands quickly this season. His +0.47 EPA per dropback on passes under 2.5 seconds is the best number in the league by a mile, and it is the foundation of the 49ers' elite passing game. And Purdy isn't just dumping the ball off on these plays either. On quick passes, Purdy has the second-highest air yards per attempt in the league among full-time quarterbacks (5.3).

The issue in this matchup for the 49ers comes with the consistency of those plays. The Vikings are going to bring blitz after blitz on Sunday, and Purdy has been prone to holding onto the ball against the blitz this season. While he does lead the league in EPA per play (+0.30) against the blitz, his average time to throw on those plays of 2.94 seconds is the fourth-longest in the NFL. Avoiding some of those extended dropbacks in this matchup is going to be key for Purdy and the 49ers to have success.

Of course, there's no matchup exactly like playing Flores and the Vikings, so it's hard to draw from previous games to see what Sunday is going to look like for the 49ers offense. But if Purdy is running around in chaos mode while Flores sends the cavalry at him, that plays right into Minnesota's hands.

Shanahan will have a number of designed quick throws ready to go to counter the Vikings' blitz, but few teams are as good at batting down some of those short throws and getting in passing lanes as the Vikings are when they blitz. Keep an eye out for Andrew Van Ginkel and others to cause havoc in that area on Sunday night.

The Vikings, for as good as they have been on defense this season, haven't played many teams that can run the ball downhill at them over and over and over again. As a result, a lot of the teams on Minnesota's schedule have been stuck in obvious passing situations where Flores can really get after them.

This isn't a critique of the Vikings. They have shut down a few solid running games like the Bears and Buccaneers, though a rainstorm in Chicago and a backup quarterback in Tampa Bay limited the passing games in both of those outings. Arguably the two best running games that the Vikings played this season, the Lions and the Bills, both got wins over them.

The Lions and Bills had success in different ways against the Vikings, but both were able to make some inroads. Buffalo only averaged three yards per rush and generated two runs of 10 or more yards, but the Vikings posted their lowest stuff rate of the entire season in that game. The Lions, on the other hand, ripped off 10 runs of 10 or more yards and posted nearly six yards per carry.

The 49ers have arguably the most efficient run game in the NFL this season, and they generate explosive runs on top of that. For all of the praise Purdy and the passing game has received this year, the running game has ranked near the top of the NFL in a ton of important categories. San Francisco ranks third in yards per rush (4.9), third in EPA per rush (+0.10), second in rushing success rate (49.0%), and fourth in runs of 10 or more yards (37), per Next Gen Stats.

It will be interesting to see if Shanahan, using all of his misdirection and unique motion that he has become famous for, can help his team find a consistent groove running the football on Sunday night. If he can, Purdy and the passing game will be able to attack on top of that and the 49ers can keep rolling on offense.
