SEC plans to consider ejecting LSU on December 3

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The supposed “chaos” in college sports could still result in the SEC kicking LSU out of the conference.

Via Mark Schlabach of ESPN, the conference plans to consider giving LSU the boot at a meeting on December 3.

The disclosure came in court papers filed Monday in connection with a legal battle arising from LSU’s placement of guard RJ Luis Jr. on the roster, after he played in preseason games for the Boston Celtics.

“The lawsuit seeks to protect the Conference’s ability to govern itself and to enforce the rules and policies its member universities have voluntarily adopted,” the SEC said in a statement. “The Conference maintains its right to associate only with institutions that meet its established standards and expectations of membership and that demonstrate appropriate institutional control of their athletics programs.”

The fight first emerged when the LSU football team considered adding to the roster players who had spots on 90-man NFL rosters in the offseason, training camp, and preseason. LSU eventually backed down.

The overriding question is whether players who have signed what are essentially tryout contract are “professional” players. And whether college athletes who are now being paid are not “professional.”

The entire dynamic arises from players making the strategic decision to pursue opportunities deemed to be more lucrative. College athletes in the high-revenue sports are essentially pro players. The fact that a small handful of players who have attempted to become pro players have chosen to return to college for business reasons creates a convenient — but disingenuous — talking point.

It’s easy, and phony, to frame it as “pro players” returning to college. They’re ALL pro players at this point, even if the NCAA and its members continue to covet an antitrust exemption that will allow them to restrict the amount of money the players can make, thereby diverting the finite booster funds toward the program, and the coaches.

It’s really that simple. The powers-that-be want to reduce the revenue stream for player, because that money can otherwise be funneled to the schools. But they won’t admit that, because that makes it harder to get public (and in turn political) support. It’s easier to frame it as “pro players playing in college sports.”

Again, they’re all professionals. As they should be. For decades. a corrupt system kept the players from getting their fair share. Now that they are, the fat cats who control college sports want to ensure that the players will get less. So that they can get more.

The fact that LSU isn’t participating in the ruse makes them an obvious target of the folks who are trying to perpetuate it. At some point, LSU should just stand up and tell the SEC to GFY.
