Fantasy Basketball Rankings (9-cat leagues): Risers and fallers through Week 5

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

We're onto Week 6! Here is the 9-cat version of my risers and fallers list. These rankings are for rest-of-season 9-cat leagues, and I'm leaning on Yahoo's rankings from the last two weeks and last month. Efficiency matters more here than in points leagues, and it's the main thing separating these six players. Let's get into it!

Ausar is averaging 10.8 points, 8.7 rebounds, 5.4 assists, 1.6 steals and 1 block on 55% shooting through 16 games, and he's been even better lately. He's eighth over the last two weeks and 30th over the last month. He leads the Pistons in rebounding and had a 17-board game against Philadelphia on Nov. 4. The weak spots are well known. Those with punt-3 builds get the most out of him, since he positively adds value in rebounds, assists, stocks and field goal percentage. The jump to 73% from the line is another win, as he doesn't hurt you nearly as badly as he used to.

I have him 57th, as I anticipate Jalen Duren's return will impact his numbers to some degree. Either way, he's exceeding expectations in what could be a breakout campaign.

Smith is averaging 14.1 points, 7.5 rebounds, 2.1 3s and 1 block in 25.3 minutes, shooting 55% from the field with 0.9 turnovers per game. That mix of 3s, blocks, field goal percentage and low turnovers is why he grades out well in 9-cat without much volume. He's 14th over the last two weeks and 26th over the last month. He was drafted in just 8% of Yahoo leagues, so if you scooped him up off waivers, you're playing with house money.

I'm moving him up to 65th, from 99th, but not higher because much of this production came with Nic Claxton sidelined. Caleb Wilson is also out, and Claxton is still ramping up, so Smith's role should hold for now. I could be too low on him. Continue to hold.

McDaniels is 11th over the last two weeks and 18th over the last month.  The bump in usage comes from Anthony Edwards' absence. Edwards is out until late January after knee surgery, and McDaniels has absorbed a bigger share of the offense without losing efficiency. He's averaging 18.4 points, 6.2 rebounds, 3.7 assists, 1.2 steals, 0.9 blocks and 1.6 3s in 33.9 minutes, shooting 56% from the field and 85% from the line.

He's positive in eight of nine categories, with turnovers (2.2 per game) the only negative. I don't value turnovers that much, especially when he's so productive across other areas of fantasy. Some of that scoring should come back down when Edwards returns, which is why I have him 30th, up from 43rd. I wouldn't sell, even though his play is on another level right now.

Hart is 233rd over the last two weeks and 176th over the last month. His scoring is down to 6.6 points per game from 12.0 last season, and he's shooting 34% from the field and 60% from the line. He's still contributing 6.7 rebounds and 4.0 assists, which keeps him rosterable, but his steals have dipped recently and he's listed as questionable with a right shoulder sprain. In 9-cat, the low percentages make it a bigger problem. The good news is that his minutes appear to be trending back up. Landry Shamet's been eating into his minutes because of his shooting, but over the past two games, Hart's averaging 37 minutes per gam. He's also coming off a 10-11-10 triple-double, which could be a nice selling point if you're concerned about his ROS outlook.

I have him 120th, down from 72nd. I'd hold in 12-team 9-cat leagues for now since his shooting should improve, but he's not as bad as Mikal Bridges, who was near drop status.

Gobert is 239th over the last two weeks and 289th over the last month. His rebound and block rates are both down: he's averaging 10.6 rebounds and 1.0 block in 29.2 minutes, compared to 11.5 and 1.6 last season. His scoring has dropped from 10.9 to 7.6 points, and his field goal percentage is down to 49% after he shot 68.2% last season. That FG% was the main reason to roster him in 9-cat. He's also shooting 46% from the line on 4.7 attempts per game. That's way too much volume for someone shooting so poorly.

The former four-time defensive player of the year is more of a rebound specialist, so I'd look to move him if he can string together a few big double-double outings. I have him 150th, down from 110th. I'm probably still too generous.

Reid has the volume in his first season in Charlotte: 17.7 points, 8.3 rebounds and 2.5 3s in 31.5 minutes. The problem is efficiency. He's shooting 38% from the field on 17.5 attempts and 66% from the line, well below his career norms (45.6% and 73.2% last season in Minnesota), and he's adding 2.5 turnovers. That's why he's 149th over the last two weeks and 197th over the last month.

I have him 96th, down from 86th, because I expect his percentages to rebound once the Hornets get healthier and he isn't carrying as much of the offense. The volume makes him an easier hold in points leagues, but fantasy managers have every right to be concerned about his numbers.
