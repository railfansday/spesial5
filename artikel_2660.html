Chase Harrington scores go-ahead, insurance goals late in third; Spokane Chiefs outlast Kelowna 5-3 | WHL hockey

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Three times on Wednesday the Spokane Chiefs took a one-goal lead. And three times the Kelowna Rockets tied it up.

Chase Harrington scored with a little less than five minutes left — after a video review — and added an empty-net goal and the Spokane Chiefs outlasted the Kelowna Rockets 5-3 in a Western Hockey League game at Numerica Veterans Arena.

The U.S. Division and Western Conference leading Chiefs (16-3-1-1, 34 points) won for the second time after a brief two-game losing streak.

Goalie Carter Esler made 26 saves. The Chiefs went 0-for-5 on the power play and 1-for-2 on the kill. Both teams generated 29 shots on goal.

The teams will run it back Saturday at the Arena at 6:05 p.m.

"They're a team that works hard," Chiefs coach Brad Lauer said about Kelowna. "They've got a good identity to their foundation, and you know that's in their DNA. They're going to push you to the end, and you know we have to push back. You've got to be ready to play. You got to play a hard 60 minutes against them."

Tied at 3-3 late, Harrington thought he had a goal with 4:55 left in the game, stuffing one under Kelowna goalie Harrison Boettinger on a second chance attempt — but the referee immediately waived it off, much to the consternation of the home crowd. The in-house replay showed a clear goal, and the crowd roared again.

After a brief review by the officials, they referee signaled a good goal. Harrington picked up his ninth of the season and the Chiefs went ahead by a goal once again.

Harrington knew the goal would stand up to review.

"I got some speed through the neutral zone. Thought I'd drop a shoulder, take it to the net, and banged her home."

Kelowna pulled its goalie with 1:55 to go. The Chiefs twice narrowly missed long-range attempts at an empty-net goal but with 48 seconds left they cleared the zone and Harrington streaked down the left wing and dumped the puck in the yawning net to seal the win.

"(Kelowna is) a tough team. They play hard," Harrington said. "They're never going to back down, and neither are we, so it was a good win."

The Chiefs dominated possession early, and just 1 minute, 40 seconds into the game they got on the board. Parker Colmer tapped a puck out from along the boards to Luca Jarvis at the top of the left wing circle. The rookie used a defender as a screen and beat Boettinger for his fourth goal of the season.

A few minutes later Chiefs defenseman Nolan Saunderson tried an ill-advised pass from the corner through the center of the ice and it deflected off Vojtech Cihar and straight to Tomas Poletin in the slot, who sent a wrist shot low stick side past an unsuspecting Carter Esler for his 13th goal of the season.

"I thought we were on our toes. I thought we were moving. We got a good goal, nice goal. Did a lot of good things," Lauer said of the start. "Then we turned pucks over before that goal. We talked about turnovers against this team, and then it just kind of snowballed from there. And we kind of got put on our heels, and then we kind of struggled a little bit of executing plays."

Kelowna (9-5-4-3) was granted the game's first power play opportunity a few moments later after Harrington was sent off for interference on a heavy shoulder check in open ice.

The Chiefs killed it off and almost immediately received their first chance with the extra skater as William Matte was called for hooking a Chiefs forward down in the slot. But the home team couldn't generate any sustained pressure and it stayed tied.

With 3:29 left in the period Chiefs defenseman Nathan Mayes delivered a clean check along the wall and a trio of Rockets took exception. Mayes ended up fighting Dawson Gerwing, and Gerwing was also assessed minors for roughing and unsportsmanlike conduct, giving the Chiefs a four-minute power play.

But the remainder of the period went without as much as a shot on goal and it was tied after one.

The Chiefs didn't score on the final 31 seconds of the four-minute power play, but did score not long after that. At 4:03 of the second Marek Howell crept in from his point position to bang home a rebound of Ethan Hughes' sharp angle shot for his third goal of the season and 12th of his WHL career spanning 249 games.

Owen Martin was called for slashing at 8:18 of the period and Kelowna cashed in when Gerwing tapped in a backdoor pass from Ryan Oothoudt for his 10th goal of the season.

Late in the period, Harrington picked a fight with Nate Corbet at the end of a Chiefs power play to try to fire up the squad.

"I just thought the team needed a little bit of a spark, and he's a tough guy," Harrington said. "So, yeah."

"That's part of finding ways to create momentum for your team.," Lauer explained. "There's different ways of doing it, whether it's on a power play or maybe you get a shorthanded goal or moments in the game. I think Chase being the leader, being the captain understood maybe changing momentum."

It stayed that way until 3:44 of the third period when Gavin Burcar received a tipped pass from Matěj Tománek, deked a defender and sent a wrister past Boettinger for his sixth goal of the season

Just two minutes later the Chiefs received their fifth power play opportunity of the game, but it went empty and Spokane retained its slim lead — but not for long.

Just 30 seconds after the power play ended Kelowna's Jakub Frolo redirected Peyton Kettles' shot past Esler for his eighth of the season to tie it up for the third time.

"We just find a way to win," Harrington said. "Today, we could have easily packed it in, but we just have a lot of heart, we got a lot of care, a lot of juice."
