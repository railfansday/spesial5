Vitalik Buterin Wants Crypto to Be More Like Buying a House

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin has said that crypto needs some of the features that made housing a safe investment for earlier generations. "We need the equivalent of those good properties that housing investment has, for modern digital assets," Buterin wrote in a post on X on Nov. 18.

The post came a day after economist Peter Tulip published his own post on housing in Raleigh, North Carolina. According to Tulip, looser zoning rules in the city set off a construction boom and pushed real rents down 15% between 2022 and 2025.

In his post, Buterin first pointed to a benefit of young people putting their savings into stocks and crypto instead of real estate.

"One benefit of younger generations investing more in stonks and other digital things instead of housing is that it reduces perverse incentives that drive people to push to keep housing expensive, instead of rejoicing when prices drop as we do for all other life necessities," he wrote.

Still, the Ethereum co-founder said that housing has had one advantage over stocks and crypto. He described it as "a culture and policy-encouraged default of investing in a safe non-crazy thing that you can't easily liquidate."

"Modern platforms encourage the opposite of this, leading to more irresponsible behavior," Buterin added.

Ether was trading at $1,808 on Wednesday.

Buterin has written about housing and urban planning before. He has supported the YIMBY movement for denser development in cities for several years.

In 2022, Buterin argued for a land value tax. According to him, such a tax would lower the upfront cost of buying a home and discourage speculation on vacant lots.

In 2025, the Ethereum co-founder called the housing market a "confusing market of peculiar NFTs." According to Buterin, what is really being sold under the label of square footage is the right to live in a particular city. At the time, he also said that legalizing dense development speeds up the economy and benefits even people who prefer single-family homes.
