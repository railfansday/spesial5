Spain win World Cup in triumph of football over Argentine thuggery

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Football 1 Felons 0. It took extra time to get there but, thank goodness, we got there. The sport prevailed. Spain won the World Cup and we are all better for that. Football is better for that. It desperately needed Spain to win this final. Anything else would have felt like a crime.

Even at the final whistle Argentina showed their thuggishness with Nahuel Molina throwing a punch at Rodri and coach Lionel Scaloni – usually so calm – getting involved. Leandro Paredes, who might want to try to play football rather than manhandle his opponents, grabbed Eric García by the throat and pushed over Gavi. What a sight.

Leandro Paredes was sent off after the final whistle amidst ugly scenes from some of the Argentina players 🔴Nahuel Molina also appeared to throw a punch towards one of the celebrating Spain players 😬 pic.twitter.com/LVItPtx08n

And Lionel Messi? For all the brilliance of the world’s greatest ever player – as England can sorely testify from the semi-final – his most memorable contribution was to try to get Spain’s Marc Cucurella sent off for covering his mouth.

That was in retribution after Enzo Fernández was rightly dismissed in added time of the regulation 90 minutes to become the third Argentina player to be sent off in a World Cup final, after two in 1990. They used the same dirty tactics as all those decades ago.

After he collected his runners-up medal there were tears from Messi, 39 now and surely at his last World Cup. But no one need cry for Argentina.

Now the deposed world champions, they deserved nothing because they did nothing, becoming the first World Cup finalists not to even have a shot until the 121st minute, when Giuliano Simeone fired over from a corner when they were frantically chasing the game.

It's a late, late chance missed for Argentina! 😱The ball drops to Giuliano Simeone in the middle of the box but he's leaning back and he sends the ball sailing over the bar. pic.twitter.com/TISiWHBLiE

Instead, they pushed and shoved, fouled and stood over opponents on the ground with a few choice words. And tried to pressurise the referee, as they had so successfully done against England. But at least this time the official, Slovenian Slavko Vincic, eventually intervened with Fernández’s red.

ENZO FERNANDEZ IS SENT OFF 🟥Argentina will play the rest of the World Cup final with 10 men after Fernandez got his second yellow for a very late tackle. pic.twitter.com/OZFDiTc2oV

In the first half, Argentina committed more fouls – nine – than they completed passes in the final third of the pitch – eight. This was nasty, depressing anti-football. And it got what it deserved.

The only goal came in the second period of extra time with Spain’s brilliant winger, Nico Williams, who should have started and made such a difference, cleverly heading back a Pedro Porro cross for another replacement, Ferran Torres, to lash high into the net. What a relief.

Ferran Torres rifles the ball into the roof of the net and Spain lead in extra-time 🇪🇸 pic.twitter.com/uzlNFQ8QUX

Spain should have won far more comfortably, but the European champions lived up to the claim made by their coach Luis de la Fuente and proved they are the best team in the world, having beaten the favourites France in the last four.

Spain won this tournament for the second time, 16 years after the first. Now there is just one nation chasing a “second star”, the one beaten by Argentina in Atlanta last Wednesday.

If England ever want to win this again then here is the blueprint. This is how the game should be played. Get the ball, keep the ball, love the ball, move the ball. Try to play. Be brave and stay brave. It is not complicated, but it requires a re-education.

Maybe one day English football will grow up and cherish ball-players of their own, rather than Manchester City signing Rodri, who was magnificent here until he tired, or Arsenal with Martín Zubimendi, who successfully replaced him.

Rodri was named player of the tournament, in recognition of the football his nation played and the 30-year-old’s importance to to their system. He is their heartbeat and metronome. England do not just need to find a Rodri, but cherish whoever that is.

Spain lift the World Cup for a second time! 🏆🏆🇪🇸 pic.twitter.com/d4DFJdjCj9

Argentina will point to the fact they lost three-quarters of their back four to injury. But that would be a diversionary tactic. Their approach was clear from the first whistle and did not deviate as they attempted to rough up Spain the way they did England.

Except Spain are more evasive because they pass the ball quicker and better than England. There is such a debate about possession-based football in the Premier League, the attempt by coaches, often unsuccessful, to replicate Pep Guardiola. But that is what the best teams do; the winning teams. Especially in international football. We need to reduce the pace and physicality and remember what the purpose of the game is. Thomas Tuchel got it wrong and should admit it.

Spain again showed the way as they did in beating England in the final of the last European Championship, and they will be the team to beat when it comes to retaining that crown in two years’ time. Which will largely be held in England.

This was not a great final. It was a hard watch, especially after the referee gave Argentina licence to play as they wanted as he somehow failed to book Alexis Mac Allister for a horrible lunging tackle on Dani Olmo. The ball was an afterthought for the Liverpool midfielder.

Finally the chances came, late in normal time, with Torres heading straight at Emiliano Martínez, who also denied Pedri before Aymeric Laporte headed weakly. There were two more headers from Williams and Mikel Merino, all good chances.

The ball was in the net, from Williams, but those conspiracy theorists will have been in overdrive as it was ruled out for a non-existent foul on Nicolás Otamendi, who threw himself to the ground after contact with Merino.

Good afternoon and welcome to live coverage of the 2026 World Cup final. Football’s finest talent descends on New Jersey for the showpiece event of this month-long North American sojourn, with places in sporting immortality on the line. Argentina are looking to become the third nation to win back-to-back World Cups while Spain – perhaps surprisingly – play only the second final in their history; the other being in 2010, when Andrés Iniesta’s extra-time winner against the Netherlands secured a breakthrough global crown for an era-defining Spain side. The two nations have only faced off once before at the World Cup: a 1966 group game at Villa Park which La Albiceleste won 2-1.

Just eight miles separate the MetLife Stadium and Broadway, and today the spotlight falls firmly on football’s leading protagonist, Lionel Messi, with this final potentially being his World Cup curtain call. The gilded Argentine appeared to have scaled the summit by lifting the trophy that had eluded him in Qatar, but he has returned and – at 39 – has dazzled on stages across the continent all summer. Winning a second World Cup would be to achieve something Diego Maradona could not, and would only cement his legend further. Kylian Mbappé’s double in last night’s madcap third-place play-off means Messi must score two to win the Golden Boot and reclaim sole ownership of the tournament’s all-time scoring record. There has been much intrigue in the match-up between himself and Lamine Yamal, the talented Spanish teenager treading a similar path at Barcelona, not least because of that remarkable photo-shoot 19 years ago. But to reduce this final to “Messi vs Yamal” would be to diminish the talents of players such as Rodri and Julián Alvarez who are match-winners in their own right.

This will be the first World Cup final contested between the reigning Copa América and European Championship winners, and the first disputed by two countries that share a language since the inaugural 1930 competition in Uruguay, when the hosts defeated Argentina. There are precious few shared aspects, though, between this year’s finalists’ route to New Jersey. While Spain have conceded just one goal in 630 minutes of football and controlled their semi-final against tournament favourites France, Argentina have huffed and puffed their way to the final. Extra-time was required against Cape Verde and Switzerland, and Lionel Scaloni’s side had to produce late heroics against Egypt and – grr – England.

Donald Trump will present the trophy to the winning team’s captain, and his attendance has prompted the biggest security operation in US sporting history. The US president will be joined in the stands and VIP boxes by a glut of A-listers, and I wish my colleague who will be guiding you through the match later the very best of luck in identifying them all. This new-look final will also have a 30-minute half-time show starring Madonna, Justin Bieber, Shakira, BTS, Burna Boy, Gustavo Dudamel and the PS22 Chorus featuring Coldplay.
