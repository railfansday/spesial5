Is Coca-Cola a Millionaire-Maker Stock?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Sometimes boring stocks have the brightest futures.

By Will Ebiefung – Nov 20, 2026 at 5:05PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Coca-Cola ›

Few businesses exemplify American business success more than Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%). Since its founding in the late 19th century, the multinational beverage maker has expanded globally to serve billions and become one of the world's most recognized brands. Investors have profited substantially along the way, and this shows little sign of stopping.

Over the last 12 months alone, shares have jumped by an impressive 35%, substantially outpacing the S&P 500's return of just 8% over the same time frame. This is a big deal for a company that is already large and mature. Let's dig deeper into the pros and cons of Coca-Cola to decide if the company still has millionaire-maker potential.

To understand Coca-Cola's appeal, it can pay to listen to the philosophy of one of its biggest backers, Warren Buffett. His Berkshire Hathaway portfolio owns a whopping $38.4 billion worth of Coca-Cola stock, making it his third-largest bet behind American Express and Apple.

Buffett likes to invest in companies with simple and easy-to-understand business models that enjoy a strong economic moat. A moat is a competitive advantage that protects a company's market share against competition and allows it to enjoy stable or elevated pricing power. Coca-Cola is arguably the quintessential example of this type of business.

You can walk into a convenience store or restaurant practically anywhere in the world and find Coca-Cola's flagship beverage Coke (or one of its many subbrands like Sprite, Fanta, and a variety of localized options).

The company's ubiquitous shelf space has created unparalleled habit creation among its consumers, which is something new rivals will struggle to disrupt, even if they manage to create better-tasting products. This advantage also makes Coca-Cola's sales remarkably recession-resistant. It has allowed the company to protect its margins amid soaring global inflation in raw materials like aluminum and sugar.

Coca-Cola's gross margins have hovered around 60%, despite the soaring prices for its products. This suggests the company is passing on the vast majority of inflation to consumers, while protecting its own profitability.

Coca-Cola isn't without some long-term challenges. The company is already a global brand name, and that means there is very little room for organic growth. Management will be increasingly tempted to rely on price increases to juice investor returns. While the company's gross margin of over 60% was easy to justify back when a 12-pack cost $4.00, it is much harder to stomach when prices have doubled, and more American families are struggling to make ends meet.

Soda prices have far exceeded the U.S. inflation rate, despite boasting a disproportionately middle- and lower-income consumer base. It's hard not to wonder if this situation is as sustainable as it may look on the surface.

Over the long term, there could be a generational shift away from brand-name soft drinks as people become more comfortable buying lower-cost store brands from major retailers like Walmart, which offers alternatives like Sam's Cola and Twist Up.

Coca-Cola's recent performance is impressive, and its strong moat can help set the company up for more market-beating growth over the long term. That said, with a market cap of $422 billion (in a mature industry with relatively little room for organic growth), Coca-Cola's days of explosive millionaire-making growth are likely behind it. Shares are also somewhat expensive at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 28.

Investors who buy the stock now should look at it as a safer alternative to more speculative opportunities like generative AI. The company's dividend (currently boasting a yield of 2.21%) will likely represent a sizable percentage of its long-term returns.
