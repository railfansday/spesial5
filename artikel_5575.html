Housing groups urge FHFA to delay condo reserve rule until 2028

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Five housing and mortgage trade groups urged FHFA to delay a planned condo reserve requirement increase from 10% to 15% until Jan. 4, 2028. They cited budget cycle constraints, unclear implementation details and potential GSE financing disruptions.  AI Summary

Five housing and mortgage industry trade groups are asking the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) to delay a planned increase in condominium association reserve requirements by one year, citing implementation challenges and potential financing disruptions.

In a Nov. 19 letter sent to FHFA Director Bill Pulte, the organizations asked the agency to direct Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to postpone the changes from Jan. 4, 2027, to Jan. 4, 2028.

The new requirement would increase the minimum reserve allocation for applicable condominium project reviews from 10% to 15% of annual budgeted assessment income.

The letter was signed by the Community Associations Institute, Community Association Roundtable, Community Home Lenders of America (CHLA), Housing Policy Council and National Association of Mortgage Brokers (NAMB).

The groups said they support adequately funded condominium reserves but argued that associations, lenders and homeowners need more time to prepare for the change.

Some have asked for change before. In June, NAMB asked the FHFA to delay upcoming Fannie and Freddie condo review and insurance standards by 12 months. But Fannie Mae retired its limited review process for condominium projects on Aug. 3.

“Condominium project eligibility has already declined following recent changes to project review requirements, including the elimination of Limited Review options,” the groups wrote.

The coalition identified three primary concerns with the planned increase, stating that “the change could have unintended consequences for condominium financing and housing availability.”

First, many condominium associations operate on annual budget cycles and cannot easily adjust their funding midyear. Some may also need updated reserve studies, board approvals or homeowner consultations before changing their funding practices.

The groups also said questions remain about how the new requirement will apply to existing budgets, recently completed reserve studies, major capital improvement projects and alternative funding arrangements.

Lenders, servicers and condo management companies will also need time to update their systems, underwriting procedures and project reviews.

Second, the groups warned that associations unable to meet the higher reserve requirement or qualify under an alternative reserve study provision could lose eligibility for conventional financing through the government-sponsored enterprises (GSEs). The groups raised concerns that this could reduce the pool of potential buyers, make it more difficult for existing homeowners to sell or refinance, and put pressure on condo resale values.

The impact could be particularly pronounced in older buildings, smaller communities and associations with more “modest-income” homeowners, the letter stated. Projects that lose conventional financing eligibility could become more reliant on nonagency or portfolio loans, which may be more expensive or less readily available.

Lastly, the coalition said higher reserve contributions could add to housing costs at a time when homeowners are already facing elevated mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance premiums, maintenance expenses and association fees.

Fannie Mae has estimated that the higher reserve requirement could add an average of $13 to $14 per month to condominium owners’ costs, but the actual increase would vary by association, according to the coalition. Its accompanying analysis illustrated how the additional five percentage points could affect homeowners if the entire increase were funded through regular assessments.

As part of the proposed one-year extension, the coalition urged FHFA to convene industry stakeholders to evaluate the financial and operational effects of the change, clarify how existing reserve studies and funding arrangements will be treated, and develop transition provisions for associations working toward adequate reserve funding.

“We believe that strengthening condominium financial health and maintaining housing affordability should be complementary objectives,” the letter said.
