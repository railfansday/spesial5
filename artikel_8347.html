When your credit card’s chip won’t read, wipe its gold contacts with a soft cloth and try again. If it still fails, tap or swipe the card instead, since neither uses those contacts, and request a replacement in your issuer’s app, usually at no charge.

A chip that fails when you insert the card isn’t necessarily dead. Inserting reads it through that gold contact plate, and grime or a scratch on the plate can keep the card reader from reaching the chip.

Tapping reaches the same chip through an antenna inside the card, without touching the plate. So when the plate is the problem, a card marked with the contactless symbol’s four curved lines can still pay at any reader showing the same symbol.

Swiping skips the chip altogether, and after the chip fails three times, a card reader usually asks you to swipe instead. Tap when you can, though, since a swipe reads the magnetic stripe on the back, which skimmers hidden in card readers can copy. And if the card reads fine but is still declined at checkout, the chip isn’t the cause.

Capital One and American Express replace a damaged card free when you ask in the app or online, and American Express offers free next-day shipping. Until the new card arrives, keep paying with the old one, or add it to your phone’s wallet, which pays without the chip. The new card usually keeps the same card number, though the expiration date and security code can change.

When the new card comes, update any changed details wherever the card pays a bill automatically. Then give it a wallet slot of its own, away from keys and coins, and keep it out of hot cars and the laundry.
