Jito’s Solana data feed puts a price on seeing trades early

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Solana traders can pay to see transactions earlier through BAM without gaining the right to change their order within a committed batch. The product turns information timing into a saleable service, even as its execution rules protect the sequence of an already scheduled batch.

Helius's current subscription terms and BAM's validator revenue rules show how that earlier view is priced and who earns from selling it.

A BAM preconfirmation appears only after the leader has accepted a scheduled batch and committed to executing it in order. Unscheduled pending transactions are excluded. The stream therefore reveals committed activity earlier than a shred-based feed, rather than opening a window to rearrange that same batch.

BAM announced the service on Sept. 9, 2026 through Helius and Triton. CryptoSlate's July 2025 BAM coverage concerned the proposed sequencing architecture; the commercial data stream adds a separate question about access.

Jito does not sell the stream directly. Its distributors handle pricing, billing and onboarding.

Helius currently offers preconfirmations on Developer, Business, Professional and Enterprise plans, excluding free accounts. For each subscription in a slot that delivers at least one message, the charge is the greater of 300 credits or 10 credits per delivered message. Zero-message slots and uncovered slots cost nothing.

That means 10 delivered messages cost 300 credits, while 100 cost 1,000 credits, according to its published table. Concurrent subscriptions are billed separately. These are credit charges, not a stated dollar price per trade, and Helius says pricing is subject to change.

Customers also need to distinguish the two signals Helius includes by default. Its own preconfirmations appear when the leader executes a transaction and include its local execution status. BAM's arrive earlier, when the leader commits to execution, and carry no execution status in Helius's stream. Both precede shreds, but they describe different stages.

A fixed execution sequence can coexist with paid access to earlier information.

The ordering guarantee concerns the committed batch. Preconfirmations are not finality. Sequence spacing is not time. The split is a published allocation, not measured payouts, profits or losses. Helius's own execution-stage signal is outside this BAM-only path.

The commercial arrangement reaches the supply side too. BAM's published split assigns 35% of preconfirmation revenue to BAM validators, 35% to the Jito DAO and 30% to distributors.

Under the current validator rules, AgaveBAM and FireBAM operators are automatically opted into streaming. They can forgo the revenue and opt out, but self-service controls are not available yet. Opting out currently requires a ticket through Jito Developers Discord.

The validator portion is shared through eligible leader slots rather than paid solely to whichever validator produced a particular stream. BAM describes payments over the month after distributors pay the DAO, using priority fees attached to market-tick transactions. Skipped or disconnected slots earn nothing.

This gives participation a revenue incentive alongside an execution obligation. BAM says its Verifier checks produced blocks against commitments and that a validator that deviates can be disconnected.

BAM's operating model therefore puts a price on earlier information while enforcing a committed execution sequence. Assessing access means watching distributor eligibility, usage charges and actual stream coverage alongside those ordering safeguards. A fixed place in a batch does not by itself give every trader an equally early view.
