Bitcoin investors could pay the price as the Federal Reserve tightens bank lending rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The Fed's Nov. 2 survey shows tougher approvals alongside stronger nonbank demand, with no evidence of forced Bitcoin sales.

Banks tightened loan approval standards for nonbank financial firms while reporting stronger demand from business lenders and private-equity funds, according to the Federal Reserve's Nov. 2 lending survey.

That combination points to a financing constraint that could limit investors' appetite for Bitcoin beyond changes in the Fed's policy rate.

For business lenders, classified as business credit intermediaries in the survey, 12 of 50 responding domestic banks tightened standards, one eased, and 37 left them unchanged. The response table shows 24% tightening versus 2% easing, for a net tightening of 22%.

The October Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey covers the preceding three months, generally the third quarter. Responses were due Oct. 5, well before the late-October spread widening.

Domestic banks reported net tightening across all five nonbank borrower categories surveyed. They most often cited a less favorable or more uncertain economic outlook and deterioration in borrowers' financial strength.

At the same time, banks reported stronger demand from business credit intermediaries and private-equity funds in a question measuring funds actually disbursed.

Among banks reporting stronger nonbank demand, common reasons included working-capital or liquidity needs, refinancing, and financial investment opportunities. Banks can become more selective even as borrowers seek financing for both cash needs and investments.

A minority of banks tightened business-lender standards, producing a 22-point net balance. The survey and market indicators describe different periods.

Approval tightening is real, but neither its bank-share measure nor these mismatched dates establishes Bitcoin selling.

Survey covers Q3, with responses due Oct. 5. NFCI describes the week ending Oct. 23; spreads compare Oct. 28 and 30, the latest data exposed Nov. 3 morning. These separate measures do not establish Bitcoin selling.

Method: One square represents one of 50 banks answering Q29B: 12 tightened, 37 unchanged, 1 eased. Verified arithmetic: 12/50=24%, 1/50=2%, and (12−1)/50=22 percentage points net. Spread change: (3.44−3.14)×100=30 basis points. Date positions are schematic, not a time-proportional or causal scale.

Approval standards determine access to credit, so financing can become harder to obtain without a new policy-rate increase. The Fed held its target range at 3.75% to 4% on Oct. 28, a separate decision from the earlier lending conditions captured by the survey.

If financing becomes harder to secure while liquidity needs rise, investors may have less capacity or willingness to hold risky assets. A 2023 IMF working paper found that US monetary tightening historically reduced a common crypto-price factor through that channel.

The ICE BofA US high-yield option-adjusted spread rose from 3.14% on Oct. 28 to 3.44% on Oct. 30, a widening of 30 basis points.

The Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index was -0.487 for the week ending Oct. 23, indicating conditions looser than average.

A stronger signal of spillover would be continued widening in credit premiums alongside weakening Bitcoin demand, while narrowing spreads and resilient demand would weaken that case. For now, the survey shows a financing constraint worth watching.
