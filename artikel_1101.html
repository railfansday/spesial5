Drone footage showing Mauricio Pochettino leading his first training session as Manchester United manager has been posted online.

The 12-minute clip, which was filmed above United’s Carrington training complex, was uploaded to YouTube.

In the video, Pochettino can be seen working alongside the squad, which had been split into three separate groups.

Addressing the upload, the drone operator wrote: "For the people asking about legality. It is unrestricted airspace, meaning anyone can fly over it.

"The drone remained in line of sight at all times, it didn’t fly over anyone or even near anyone so was a perfectly legal flight going by the CAA rules that regulates drone usage.

"The drone is correctly labelled, and I hold all the relevant licences, public liability and insurance required. As for leaking team news/tactics, it was half an hour into the manager’s first training session, there are many media there so certainly won’t be the only footage of training released."

Manchester United have declined to comment on the footage.

The session marked Pochettino’s opening training session following his appointment as United’s new head coach last week. He succeeds Michael Carrick, who was dismissed during the international break with the team currently in the relegation zone after winning just one of their 10 Premier League matches.

After overseeing his final match with the United States - a 2-0 victory over Haiti that concluded early on Wednesday morning UK time - Pochettino arrived in Manchester later that day.

His first match in charge will see United take on old rivals Liverpool at Anfield on Sunday afternoon.

Andoni Iraola’s side are currently 10 points above United in the table, and will be hoping to spoil Pochettino’s Premier League return.

Pochettino is taking charge of his fourth Premier League club, having previously managed Southampton, Tottenham and Chelsea.

However, he has made it clear that he has been an admirer of United for a long time, and was cheering them on when they completed the Treble by winning the 1999 Champions League final against Bayern Munich in Barcelona.

“I was in Barcelona in that final, that was an iconic final,” he said in his first interview as manager. “We were shouting for Manchester United.

“From there, it was so exciting to see Manchester United, and more with the admiration of Sir Alex Ferguson – for me, the best coach in the history of the sport. I always admired him.”
