U.S. won court order to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

U.S. and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the funds North Korean hackers took from the exchange

The U.S. Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion worth of cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the department said on Thursday.

The department attributed the theft to members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls "TraderTraitor." It said they stole more than $6 billion worth of virtual assets from the exchange on or about Oct. 29. Government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion, and the $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that total, the department said.

"North Korea's government bankrolls its rogue regime and illicit weapons program through cryptocurrency theft," Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General for National Security M. Scott Proctor said in a statement. "The National Security Division will use every tool at its disposal to seize and return North Korea's ill-gotten gains."

Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Criminal Division said in a statement that the division and its law enforcement partners froze about half of the stolen assets before the funds moved out of reach. U.S. Attorney Jeanine Ferris Pirro for the District of Columbia said the seizure of more than $1.2 billion in stolen assets shows the government has met the North Korean regime head-on.

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. It said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

Last week, the FBI, the U.S. Secret Service and the Internal Revenue Service's criminal investigation unit issued a Cyber Alert attributing the theft to TraderTraitor. The alert urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses the actors are using to launder the Binance funds, the department said.

When the government seizes assets in such cases, it typically submits a forfeiture claim to the court, and a judge then rules on which parties are legitimately entitled to the money that was recovered, according to The Associated Press.

The Justice Department also announced the disruption of cloud-based infrastructure used in a North Korean scheme the FBI calls "FaceStealer." The department said the scheme, which uses recorded videos and deepfakes to lure targets into fake video meetings and install malware, has cost victims more than $400 million in digital assets during the past year and a half.
