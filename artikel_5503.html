China's trade surplus topped $1 trillion for a second year, two months early

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Customs data showed a $1.04 trillion surplus through October, as imports rose 41.6% and exports 35.2% from a year earlier

China ran a trade surplus of $1.04 trillion in the first 10 months of 2026, topping $1 trillion for a second straight year with two months left, customs data showed on Tuesday.

The total stands against the $1.2 trillion record set across 2025, the year the surplus first went beyond $1 trillion. The 10-month figure is roughly 9% above where it stood a year earlier, according to Bloomberg.

October exports rose 35.2% from a year earlier, while imports climbed 41.6%. Analysts had expected a 32% rise in imports, according to CNBC. The October surplus came to $105.3 billion, the smallest since May, according to Bloomberg.

Chinese exports to the EU increased 24.4% in October, whereas purchases from the bloc fell by over 4%, which pushed the monthly surplus with the EU up to $33.4 billion. The year-to-date surplus with the bloc exceeds $310 billion, close to a third of the overall total, according to Bloomberg.

Exports to the U.S. were up 32.9% in October while imports from there increased by roughly 35%, producing a bilateral surplus of $32.7 billion.

Worldwide demand for artificial-intelligence equipment has underpinned Chinese shipments, according to CNBC. Chips and computer parts supply 40% of this year's rise in the value of exports compared with a year earlier, according to Bloomberg. Exports of high-tech goods rose 67% in October, the fastest pace since early 2021.

Imports of high-tech products were 72% higher by value in October, according to CNBC's calculations of official figures. China brought in just under 42 million tons of crude oil during the month, a level last topped in March, according to Bloomberg.

China's cumulative surplus stood at $938.11 billion through September, including a record monthly surplus of $133.5 billion that month.

China's Customs Tariff Commission said on Monday it would keep suspending a 24% additional tariff on U.S. imports for two months while retaining a 10% tariff. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping are set to meet in Shenzhen this month at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit.
