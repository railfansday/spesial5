National Lifeline Association Urges Senate Commerce Committee to Strengthen Lifeline Fraud Prevention Measures

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

NaLA Supports the No Lifeline for Dead People Act and Calls for Improvements to the National Verifier

WASHINGTON, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The National Lifeline Association (NaLA) sent a letter to Senate Commerce Chairman Ted Cruz (R-TX) expressing support for S. 3954, the No Lifeline for Dead People Act, introduced by Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA), while urging Congress to strengthen the legislation to improve fraud prevention and protect the integrity of the federal Lifeline program.

NaLA has long supported efforts to eliminate waste, fraud, and abuse while ensuring that low-income Americans maintain access to connectivity.

"We appreciate Chairman Cruz's leadership and commitment to ensuring the integrity of the Lifeline program," said NaLA Chairman David B. Dorwart. "Preventing waste, fraud, and abuse is essential to protecting Lifeline for the millions of Americans who depend on it. We strongly support the goals of this legislation and believe additional improvements to the National Verifier will help ensure these safeguards are as effective as possible."

Specifically, NaLA urged Congress to amend the No Lifeline for Dead People Act to require the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) to initiate a rulemaking to improve the National Verifier's identity verification processes, ensure more timely and accurate identification of deceased enrollees, and establish real-time communication of death determinations with Lifeline service providers. While acknowledging that no database is perfect, NaLA believes a improved National Verifier used by all providers in all states will better protect Lifeline rather than relying on multiple program administrators.

These improvements would enable providers to identify and address potential issues, reduce improper payments, and strengthen protections for the Lifeline program.

NaLA remains committed to working with Congress and the FCC to advance meaningful reforms that safeguard taxpayer resources while preserving the Lifeline program's mission of keeping low-income Americans connected.

The National Lifeline Association (NaLA) stands as the sole industry trade group dedicated exclusively to supporting the Lifeline program. NaLA works to preserve and advance Lifeline and other low-income connectivity programs through stakeholder engagement, education, collaboration, and advocacy. NaLA supports all stakeholders in pursuing a common goal: voice, text, and broadband connectivity for all Americans. Through NaLA's ecosystem-wide membership that includes service providers, distributors, network access aggregators, compliance and software solutions vendors, device manufacturers, program supporters, and beneficiaries, NaLA provides a platform to enable and realize the goal of getting and keeping every low-income American connected to essential communications services. For more information on NaLA, visit https://www.nalalifeline.org.
