NAR advances strategic plan with new tools, MLS outreach and housing initiatives

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The National Association of Realtors released its third-quarter 2026 update on its 2026–2028 strategic plan, highlighting expanded engagement with nearly 500 MLS executives, early implementation work on the new 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, and the launch of a fraud-prevention hub and AI-focused training.   AI Summary

The National Association of Realtors (NAR) is moving from policy wins to implementation as it rolls out fraud-prevention tools, MLS engagement and housing supply efforts tied to its 2026–2028 strategic plan, according to a third-quarter progress report released Wednesday.

The update, covering the quarter ended Sept. 30, outlines how NAR is trying to convert advocacy wins and market research into practical support for agents, brokers and MLSs as the industry adjusts to commission lawsuits, changing rules and tighter housing affordability.

NAR said its work in the third quarter focused on five areas: MLS engagement, executing new federal housing-supply law, fraud prevention, commercial market intelligence and broker support. The association also highlighted technology and data initiatives, as well as developments in ongoing antitrust litigation.

NAR said it has now “expanded MLS executive engagement” to nearly 500 MLS leaders and formed a new MLS work group made up of industry experts. The group is publishing resources focused on maintaining a “pro-competitive, transparent marketplace,” according to the association’s update.

The outreach sits on top of an ongoing feedback effort that includes surveys, focus groups and in-person meetings with members, NAR said.

Why it matters: MLSs are reassessing rules, data access and listing practices in light of settlement-driven policy changes and regulatory scrutiny. More structured engagement between NAR and MLSs could influence how quickly and consistently local marketplaces adapt and how much operational risk brokers face during the transition.

NAR’s report puts significant emphasis on follow-through after the passage of the bipartisan 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, which became law July 11. The association said it “spearheaded” the push for the bill and is now working with state and local associations to turn the federal statute into local supply and affordability gains.

Beyond the ROAD to Housing Act, NAR said it continued advocacy for the More Homes on the Market Act and secured another “credit score modernization” change at the Federal Housing Administration. Details on the FHA move were not included in the high-level summary.

Why it matters: With mortgage rates still elevated and inventory tight in many markets, the near-term impact of any federal housing law will depend on how quickly local governments change zoning, permitting and infrastructure processes. Lenders, builders and brokers should watch how NAR and its state partners translate the law into specific local reforms and incentives.

NAR launched a central fraud-prevention hub and a consumer-facing guide aimed at helping members, associations and buyers and sellers spot red flags, protect sensitive information and safeguard transactions. The association also rolled out new fraud- and AI-focused education, including NAR NXT conference sessions and a Broker Power Hour on AI voice cloning and deepfake scams.

Why it matters: Wire fraud, business email compromise and emerging AI-enabled scams are driving higher operational and reputational risk on transactions. Centralized resources and training from NAR could influence how brokerages standardize verification, identity and communication protocols at the office level.

For commercial practitioners, NAR launched the Commercial Real Estate Demand Index and expanded the members-only Metro Market Dashboard. The association also released six local market assessment reports intended to give commercial Realtors a clearer view of where demand is growing across major property types.

Why it matters: With office valuations under pressure and higher rates reshaping cap rates, local demand data can help investors, lenders and brokers price risk, underwrite deals and identify resilient sectors within each metro.

NAR said it connected with more than 5,000 brokers in the quarter to discuss business challenges and share broker-focused resources. It also expanded broker programming and peer engagement, including a national broker networking session and planning for the first Broker Innovation Forum, scheduled for the NAR NXT conference.

The association said it is also building out data, technology and security resources, including expanded AI and emerging technology programming and new member research via the 2026 Realtors Technology Report, which tracks how agents are using tools in their businesses.
