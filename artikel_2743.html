Steelers grades: Aaron Rodgers, Mike McCarthy trying too hard to relive their glory years

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

We're going to do something a bit different this week. Usually, you'd see Joey and I going through individual performances or position groups and giving our thoughts. In Week 10, though, the Pittsburgh Steelers showed nothing but negatives, and we'd just be shouting into an echo chamber. So instead, we decided to just share our thoughts on the negative performance as a whole.

Joey Bray: It would be hard to give any single position group or player better than a "C" grade for their performance last night. I have been trying to think of the worst Steelers loss I have ever witnessed, and this is certainly up there, if not at the top of the list. I would argue that this was even worse than the team's most recent playoff loss to the Houston Texans last January. At least in that game, the team had a fighting chance going into the final quarter down one point.

I think we learned what the Steelers really are last night. Pittsburgh was always bound to get off to a hot start this season due to the first half of their schedule being fairly easy. However, this is going to be a bumpy second half of the season. Sure, the team will probably pick up a few more wins against teams like the Eagles, Panthers, and Titans, but they have not proven they can beat any kind of quality opponent yet.

They are a team that is fighting for a playoff spot, but still firmly below the class of the AFC. With an offense quarterbacked by a 42-year-old who is 28th in EPA/play, coupled with a lackluster and injured receiver room, there is only so far you can go. A quality team cannot have complete and total failures on offense at this point in the season, especially against divisional opponents.

The Steelers pass rush could not get their hands on Joe Burrow last night, and he picked apart their secondary all night long. The Steelers have major holes on both sides of the ball that they will need to address in the offseason. For now, they are playing the patchwork game while trying to limp into the playoffs for another year with a team that has desperately needed a reset for a long time. Pittsburgh's ceiling is the same as it has been for years now, good enough to make it to the postseason, but not good enough to hang with the teams that are serious about winning a Super Bowl.

Jarrett Bailey: You can tell how a Steelers game will go from their first offensive drive. Look at what they did against the Saints. Jaylen Warren ran the ball down the field and they were in the red zone in four plays. They ran the ball for just under 200 yards and averaged just under five yards per carry, which is how they won the game. Against the Browns, they ran the ball four out of five plays on their second drive (after seemingly learning from their first series that went three-and-out) and scored a touchdown to go up 10-0. Despite being one of the best rushing teams in the NFL, the Steelers came out in empty on their first play against the Bengals. A bobbled snap and rushed throw led to an incompletion, followed by the shotgun pitch that never works and then another incompletion. Going empty right away just felt like the Steelers thinking they could beat the Bengals at their own game, which they absolutely cannot do. This isn't an offense capable of being a spread system. There's a stubbornness to the offense. Mike McCarthy and Aaron Rodgers want to be the 2010 Packers. They don't have the bandwidth to do so, and their pride and/or egos get in the way too often. And look, we all have that. I used to be a guy who got up at 7:00 AM after going to bed at 1:00 AM. That works when you're 19, not so much almost a decade later. You have to adjust. And for Rodgers, he has to accept that he isn't the star of the show anymore. He isn't the main event wrestler that is going to be winning world titles – it's okay to be Adam Copeland winning the TNT Championship and Tag Team Championships or John Cena getting a late-career run with the United States Championship. Rodgers has to be willing to allow that to happen, and at times it feels like he's trying to relive the glory years.

This isn't all to bash on Rodgers because everyone loves to find excuses to do that. The Steelers have just proven to be a much better, and much more efficient offense, when they are a run-first team. It's who they are when they are most effective, and they need to accept it rather than run from it.
