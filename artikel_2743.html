The Bills and Chiefs are strikingly similar

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

I've spent an inordinate amount of time studying this Bills and Chiefs Thanksgiving game, digging deep for specific exploitable tendencies, team and player trends entering the contest, and anything I could find that could hint at who or what may swing this heavyweight bout.

In this enormous but expedited process, it dawned on me — these teams are ridiculously similar.

Maybe not in every way imaginable, of course.

But they're entering this contest about as close to being mirror images as we'll see in the NFL, particularly for two Super Bowl contenders.

Cook is third in the NFL in rushing yards per game at 106. Walker is second at 109.

These teams are each coming off three-straight wins over 17 or more points, which marks the first occurrence of a matchup between clubs entering a contest against each other with those type of win streaks since 2007 when the 8-0 Patriots — yes, those Patriots — traveled to Indianapolis to face the 7-0 Colts.

Both teams have sturdy, veteran-laden offensive lines.

The Bills are currently third in non-garbage time EPA per dropback at +0.213. The Chiefs are fifth at +0.194.

Buffalo has the league's eighth-best third-down conversion rate at 42.6%. Kansas City isn't far behind, in 10th at 41.8%. Andy Reid's club has converted 65.8% of their red-zone trips to touchdowns, the eighth-best in the NFL. The Bills have scored a touchdown on 63.4% of their trips into the red zone, which is the league's 12th-highest.

Points per drive? Bills are currently in fourth at 2.86, while the Chiefs are seventh at 2.50.

Overall offensive success rate? The Chiefs are sixth in the NFL at 49.9% of their plays deemed "successful" based on down and distance. The Bills are eighth at 47.9%.

Defensively, the Chiefs are seventh in EPA per play allowed at -0.035. The Bills have climbed to 13th at -0.002.

Steve Spagnuolo has dialed up a blitz on 28.5% of opponent dropbacks, per Fantasy Points Data. Jim Leonhard has sent a blitz on 30.1% of opponent dropbacks.

Kansas City's defense has 20 sacks on the season at a 5.1% rate. The Bills defense has 23 sacks at a 5.6% rate.

And even the road to 9-1 and 8-2, for the Bills and Chiefs, respectively, have closely resembled each other.

The Bills have played the following quarterbacks: C.J. Stroud, Jared Goff, Justin Herbert, Drake Moss (loss), Matthew Stafford, Kirk Cousins, Lamar Jackson/Tyler Huntley/Joey Fagnano, Carson Wentz, Geno Smith/Cade Klubnik, and Kyle McCord

The Chiefs have played these quarterbacks: Bo Nix, Daniel Jones, Malik Willis, Kirk Cousins, Justin Herbert, Bo Nix (loss), Sam Darnold/Drew Lock (loss), Geno Smith, Michael Penix, and Josh Johnson

And these clubs have played each other a whopping 10 times (!) since the beginning of the 2020 season, which basically equates to divisional-opponent type familiarity.

For as much as I want to zero in on the differences — and I may later this week before the game — this colossal Bills-Chiefs matchup is truly a standalone contest between two strikingly similar teams and quarterbacks that know each other exceptionally well.
