UCLA fans sent Bob Chesney a message in the rain he won’t soon forget

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

PASADENA, CA — The best moment of Bob Chesney's night happened before UCLA's game even started.

The coach walked onto the Rose Bowl field and looked around, soaking in something he hadn't seen yet in his first season with the Bruins.

A full stadium, in full force despite the heavy and unusual rain.

"When we walked out there, I was like damn," Chesney said. "Yes. I knew we were on the brink of it."

It literally cannot rain on UCLA's parade.

It took some time, but finally, UCLA isn't just changing its narrative on the field. It's changing the discourse in the stands, too.

Bruins fans do exist, and they're finally out of hibernation to prove it.

As for the football itself, the crowd that stuck it out through the rain was treated to the only thing Chesney and company have done in 2026: win. This time, UCLA beat Illinois 22-14.

UCLA is now 10-0 for the first time since 1998 and needs to win just one of its final two games to clinch a spot in the Big Ten championship game, inching ever closer to its first College Football Playoff appearance.

It wasn't an entirely convincing win for the Bruins. That's become a common theme and a major reason why, despite being one of only two undefeated teams left in the country, there are still doubts about how far UCLA can go.

The defense again wreaked havoc, shutting out the Fighting Illini in the first half. It marked the fourth time UCLA has done that to an opponent this season. The Bruins added a safety in the third quarter in what was shaping up to be another dominant defensive performance.

Once again, though, the offense struggled.

After marching right down the field on the opening drive, UCLA had trouble moving the ball, with its excellent rushing attack getting stuffed. Then, just before halftime, disaster appeared to strike when Ty Dieffenbach, already playing in place of the injured Nico Iamaleava, went down in a deja vu moment for Bruins fans.

Iamaleava's younger brother, Madden, struggled, and a measly 12-0 lead suddenly looked vulnerable.

But then came Dieffenbach, running out of the tunnel in a moment straight out of WWE, with the crowd popping like Stone Cold Steve Austin had just arrived. It injected some much-needed life into UCLA.

"The noise, I didn't know what it was," Chesney said. "And then I saw up on the screen, him coming down the sideline. That was pretty impressive."

Right after Illinois scored to cut the lead to five, Dieffenbach was back on the field. He led UCLA right down the field, capping the drive with a beautifully placed ball into the hands of Semaj Morgan for a touchdown.

That score proved to be too much for Illinois. In the final minutes, UCLA's defense regrouped and did what it has done all season: own the fourth quarter. The Bruins picked off quarterback Katin Houser for the third time, then forced a fourth-down stop to seal the win.

None of it, however, happens without the jolt of electricity pumped into a quarterback dealing with shoulder and ankle pain.

"That crowd reaction was unbelievable. That was one of the best moments of my life hearing that crowd," Dieffenbach said. "You just can't not want to play when you hear that. I think just showing everyone I was good, like let's go, I think that brought some fire to everyone else too."

The announced attendance was 45,800, which isn't necessarily something to brag about for the undefeated No. 4 team in the country playing in a stadium capable of holding considerably more.

But it was UCLA's highest home attendance of the season, and it came with the Bruins battling some brutal factors. Not only was rain in the forecast all day, but Friday night traffic in Los Angeles is unlike any other. Some members of the media spent more than two hours trying to get to the Rose Bowl.

Everything screamed that this would be another disappointing showing from the UCLA crowd.

Instead, fans not only made it inside the stadium, they stayed through the constant rain for the entire game.

"We were worried about the weather, but ultimately, what a great job by them," Chesney added. "Really proud of the way that they showed up."

It fits the old cliché: If you build it, they will come.

Nothing is said about the speed at which they will arrive, but the fans are finally coming back to the Rose Bowl.

Since the beginning of his tenure, Chesney has pleaded with the fanbase to come out and support the team, trying to make it as accessible as possible for them to do so. Maybe the majority were waiting for a reason.

Now, it's becoming impossible to ignore the hottest show in town.

Especially when you consider how many disgruntled fans across the country are begging their programs to poach Chesney away, trying to entice them with their full stadiums.

Well, UCLA is letting its coach know it has no plans to let him walk away.

Chesney smiled and thrust his fist into the air.

"Obviously, hearing you own your name is pretty fun," Chesney said. "It's pretty special. I appreciate them as much as they appreciate our program."
