3 Iowa wrestling things we learned at Hawkeyes' media day

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

IOWA CITY − The Iowa wrestling season is fast approaching, with the season-opening dual against Bellarmine coming on Nov. 13.

The program's annual day was held on Nov. 4, and reporters heard from coach Tom Brands and his wrestlers before the season begins. The Hawkeyes aim to build off of a fourth-place finish at last season's NCAA Championships and will be replacing several key figures — Drake Ayala, Michael Caliendo and Patrick Kennedy.

Brands praised wrestlers like Gabe Arnold and Ben Kueter for their accomplishments last season, while also emphasizing they need to continue to make leaps if they are to catch Penn State and other top programs in the program's chase for a national title.

So far, Brands said Iowa is attacking the challenge well.

"Our veterans have been conscientious about getting better," Brands said. "That's a good characteristic. Why is that important and why is that a good characteristic? Because a lot of times when you have accomplished something more than once... like Kueter is a two-time All-American, he's got two years left, he might think he's an automatic. He's the No. 1 heavyweight in America right now, ranked, at least in one or two polls that I've seen. That doesn't mean he's the No. 1 heavyweight in America. He still has to prove that every time out."

Here are the three biggest things we learned from the Hawkeyes' media day.

The Hawkeyes had several injuries last year. Add in Lehigh transfer and NCAA All-American Ryan Crookham to the mix, who had his previous two seasons cut short by injury, and there was a lot to be learned. Ultimately, Crookham, DeLuca, Ferrari and Kueter all expressed confidence in how they were feeling going into the season-opener.

DeLuca said he felt "strong, fully healthy," and is feeling added gratefulness as he returns to the mat. He said his rehab went well and allowed him to return in an efficient manner following his season-ending injury against Missouri last November.

For Crookham, Brands praised how he is looking in practice. Crookham said he felt good and is taking his recovery day by day with the coaches. He also confirmed he will have two years of eligibility remaining.

Ferrari said that he felt "really healthy," and "fantastic," as the season approaches, while also having an added fire for falling short in the NCAA semifinals last season.

Finally, Kueter said he is fully healthy coming into the season. He had meniscus surgery right after the NCAA Championships, but got on the mat and lifting two to three weeks later. He said it's the first time since he's been in college that he could train an entire summer to get ready for the season.

It appears that Iowa has a relatively clean bill of health entering the year, which will be key with limited depth of a 30-man roster cap and the need for some of the program's heaviest hitters to be their best come March.

Ayala, Caliendo and Kennedy were all cornerstones of the program last year, both in how they wrestled and how they led the team. It brings up a key question of who may be taking that mantle now.

Brands credited his veterans like Kueter, Ferrari, Arnold, Ryder Block, Nasir Bailey and Crookham for upholding the standard Iowa expects of itself and making sure all 30 wrestlers are marching toward the same goals.

"To keep answering your question about how have these guys changed or what characteristic does this team have as a whole, they're hearing more from their teammates on, 'Hey, that's not going to fly,'" Brands said.

That idea of everyone marching to the same drum was a topic new assistant coach Jacob Warner talked about in an interview with the Des Moines Register in September. One of the traits his national title-winning team had in 2021 was a roster in lockstep about what they wanted. He felt that was one of their main reasons why they could win a team title.

It's something the entire team is embracing, Arnold said. He mentioned an instance where DeLuca challenged him for slacking off during a hill-running workout. While Arnold jokingly denied taking a rep off, he loved the challenge from someone like DeLuca, who is only in his second year of the program.

"Everyone's stepped up a little bit, it's really cool to see," Arnold said.

It'll be interesting to see if that attitude translates into more wins for the Hawkeyes, but it is a trait embraced by Hawkeye teams in the past that led to success.

One of the larger questions of the Hawkeye lineup was left in the air to an extent at 149 or 157 pounds, but some questions were answered. Jordan Williams, who was charged with operating while under the influence in September will serve a one-match suspension, missing the dual vs. Bellarmine on Nov. 13, according to Brands.

Aside from the off-the-mat incident, Williams had an intriguing summer as well. He initially entered the transfer portal, but later returned to the Hawkeyes. He also had an inspired run of freestyle action, making the U23 World team for the United States and finding success at Senior-level events.

"He chose to stay and the standard is in place," Brands said. "We all have to do better and he knows he has to do better. We're going to help him do better, but in this sport, which is individual by nature, you have to do better."

Ambiguity remains on how lineup battles will shake out. Williams, Block and Kael Voinovich are all listed at 149 pounds. Miguel Estrada is at 157 pounds. Block is a returning NCAA All-American at 149 pounds, but it appears Williams and Voinovich may challenge for the spot. Brands said they are approaching the end of their wrestle-offs and should have more clarity on the lineup soon, but said all four contribute to solid depth at the weights.

'We have our best depth at 149 and 157," Brands said. "There's four guys there with Voinovich and Estrada, then you have Block who's a returning all-American and you have Jordan Williams. Those four guys at 149 and 157, they're going to factor in and we're going to need them all."
