Cathedral tops Bloomington South football in semi-state battle for ages

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

INDIANAPOLIS — There was no doubt in their minds, they were going for the win.

It was how the whole game had been played on both sides. Bloomington South and Cathedral football were not bowing to the will of the other.

So after Jonathan Winstead made one last spectacular catch at the 2-yard line and Duncan Combs spiked the ball with 3 seconds left, Combs got under center, but Cathedral called timeout. Head coach Gabe Johnson and his seniors had a decision to make down three — go for the tie, or go the glory of a long-awaited final four win.

The offense went back out. The ball was snapped to Combs, who pitched to senior running back Hayden Clark on his right, but Cathedral clogged all the escape routes and began celebrating after a hard-fought 24-21 win in their IHSAA Class 5A Semi-state game at Arsenal Tech on Friday, Nov. 20.

"He asked us, 'Do we got it?' I believe in our team, I said, 'Yes, let's go for it.'" Combs said. "To come up short by a yard sucks, but I wouldn't have had it any other way.

"Either go out trying your best to win it all, or lose hard with it. It's tough."

The heartache and tears were real. South (10-3) was making a fourth straight semi-state appearance, and none were as close as this one. South could taste it when they broke the sideline huddle and lined up at the 2.

"(The field goal) was for a moment," Johnson said. "But when you have 11 guys who say, 'Let's go for it. Let's go win it.' Then you've got to do it.

"I thought we were going to score. We had momentum at the at point."

"We just wanted an opportunity to play for a state title," Johnson said. "It was the right call to go for it and we didn't get it. There were a lot of calls I think I made that maybe didn't contribute to us being in a better position tonight. That's on me. But our kids fought hard and we gave ourselves to play for a state championship."

Cathedral will now get a chance to add to its haul of 14 as the ghosts of 1998 live on for South.

"They're hurting right now," Johnson said. "There nothing I can tell them to make it feel better. It just has to work its way through your body and leave. It's going to take some time because I know they wanted it badly. And they gave everything they had against a great football team."

The game was everything to be expected from the quality of teams on the field.

"They're a really great team and I think they would say the same about us," Combs said. "When two great teams come together, that's what happens, you get great football. I'm sure they'll do well at state."

Easton Bellini's third-down sack forced Cathedral's punt and set up the dramatic finish.

South got the ball back on its own 15 and needed a fourth-and-1 conversion by Clark to start the drive. It was everything a three-year senior starting QB dreams of and Combs delivered, going 7-of-8 for 77 yards.

First, 5 yards to Connor Parker, 17 to Winstead, 13 to Mayson Lewis, 3 to Clark then 17 to Parker, who just drug his left to the 18-yard line with 13 seconds left. Then 16 to Winstead at the 2.

"Our defense did a great job of holding them there," Combs said. "And when we got the ball, we only had one thing to do and that was move the ball down the field.

"I put faith in all my guys, my O-linemen, my receivers and we did a great job marching down there. I appreciate the coaches for putting faith in us for going for it on the 1-yard line."

Cathedral's starting offensive line goes 275, 285, 290, 310, 330 pounds and leaned on it well, rushing for 155 yards.

But the Irish also freely use junior QB Luke Finn (7-10, 102 yards passing) and sophomore QB Emmett Knowles (13-17, 138) interchangeably throughout the game. Knowles' live arm gave some pep to the passing attack, though both tossed for TDs critical to the win.

The first came after South punted with 1:21 left in the first half. Starting at his own 28, Finn hit a big 48-yard completion and connected on two more passes before finding Ollie Lewis open for a 5-yard score that made it 14-7 at halftime.

"That was not a great series for us," Johnson said.

Cathedral responded in quick fashion again in the third, after Mayson Lewis flew 99 yards with the second half kickoff to tie it.

All night, both teams took chances, going for it on fourth down in their own territory but this time, it backfired spectacularly for the Panthers, who gave the ball back on their own 34.

Next snap, Lewis, a thorn in South's side all night with 11 catches for 119 yards, was wide open and just like that, it was 21-14 Irish.

"I did not like my call to go for it on fourth down there," Johnson said. "I felt good that we could get a yard or two and we didn't."

Cathedral had its own fourth-down letdown when Finn was stopped at the Irish 44. But South couldn't take advantage. The Irish mixed run and pass well in a 14-play drive that started at their own 20 and ended with a 25-yard field goal and a 24-14 edge.

South stormed back, with Clark sneaking out of the back field for two big completions, the last of 30 yards, to score with 6:22 left.

"It was a great group of seniors," Johnson said. "It's as good a four-year span as there's been at Bloomington South minus the state championships.

So, it says a lot for them. It's completely on them. They put in the work."

It was getting tougher for Johnson to hold back tears. South went 29-10 over the past three years thanks in part to this year's seniors.

"We knew we had a great chance coming into this week," Combs said. "And leave it all out there on the field. We've been brothers for years now, going back to middle school, elementary, I've known these dudes my whole life. And I wouldn't want to do it with any other class."
