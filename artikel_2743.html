7 smart reasons to sell a stock—and my ‘golden rule’ for offloading shares, says hedge fund CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nobody is born knowing anything innately about the stock market. And if you feel like you've missed the investing class, you didn't. Usually there isn't one.

So responsible people who are trying to do the responsible thing by building a portfolio for themselves and their families often find huge gaps in their knowledge and don't know where to turn.

As the co-founder and CEO of the hedge fund Metropolitan Capital Advisors and a longtime panelist on CNBC's Fast Money, I know that there's no shortage of advice when you're getting started about great investments to buy. It can feel easy and gratifying, especially when your portfolio is doing well. But then things get tougher.

One of the hardest things to do as an investor is to figure out when to sell. You buy a stock because you believe it will go up in value. But the decision to sell is rarely so straightforward. Personally, I've made just about every selling mistake. But over time, I've developed a framework for knowing when to sell — and when not to.

Selling decisions are often rooted in emotion and cognitive bias rather than logic. One major culprit: anchoring to past performance, a flawed reference point that clouds clear thinking.

Behavioral economics, particularly prospect theory, shows that we experience the pain of a loss more intensely than the joy of a gain. This leads investors to cling to losers ("just need to get back to even") and sell winners too early ("lock in the gain").

We also place too much emphasis on recent events, a phenomenon known as recency bias. If your last tech stock soared, you're more likely to chase another. If your last trade flopped, you may avoid the market altogether. And when recent trades go well, people tend to trade more — often to their detriment.
