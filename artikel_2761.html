Billie Jean King honored with handprint ceremony at Hollywood's TCL Chinese Theatre

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Tennis legend Billie Jean King Thursday became the first American-born woman athlete honored with a handprint-footprint ceremony at the TCL Chinese Theatre.

"I sincerely hope I'm not the last," King said in her acceptance speech. "My parents and my brother, who have passed, would be so happy today if they were here, and they would remind me this is a really big deal."

King's brother, Randy Moffitt, was a relief pitcher for three major league teams from 1972-83. He died in 2025 in Long Beach following a long illness at age 76.

King also recalled childhood trips with her family from Long Beach to what was to then Grauman's Chinese Theatre "to walk among historic hand and footprints."

"It was very exciting," King said. "We watched ceremonies on the local TV news. We talked about it at the dinner table. Our family really loved this world, and because of that, today is very special for me. "

The other woman athlete honored in the theater's forecourt at 6925 Hollywood Blvd. is the Norwegian-born three-time gold medal-winning figure skater Sonja Henie, who imprinted her ice skates at her 1937 ceremony.

King's ceremony was the final handprint-footprint event in the forecourt before the historic theater celebrates its 100th anniversary in 2027, according to organizers.

In the era before Thursday's computerized rankings, experts selected the Long Beach Poly High School graduate as the world's No. 1 women's tennis player from 1966-68, 1971-72 and 1974. She won 39 Grand Slam titles -- 12 in singles, 16 in doubles and 11 in mixed doubles.

She led the "Original 9" women players who signed $1 contracts in 1970 to launch the first major women's professional tennis tour, challenging the sport's disparity in prize money.

King helped found the Women's Tennis Association three years later.

Her victory over Bobby Riggs in the 1973 "Battle of the Sexes" remains one of the best-known events in sports history. That same year, the U.S. Open became the first major sporting event to award equal prize money to men and women.

King was also among the founders of World TeamTennis, the first co-ed professional sports league. In 1971, she became the first woman athlete to earn $100,000 in a year.

Last year, she became the first woman honored with a Hollywood Walk of Fame star in the sports entertainment category, which was created in 2021 to recognize athletes who have made significant contributions to entertainment.

King was honored in part for serving as an executive producer of the 2023 PBS documentary series "Groundbreakers," which paired legendary women athletes with current stars.

Her other entertainment credits include serving as an executive producer of the 2013 documentary "The Battle of the Sexes," along with appearances as herself on "Fresh Off the Boat," "Arli$$" and "The Odd Couple."

In a recent Guardian interview, King said she kept quiet about her sexuality early in her career because she feared the women's tour would lose sponsorships.

"This is when it's not about yourself sometimes," King said. "It's about the future of the sport."

King publicly came out in 1981 after being outed, and she said she lost every endorsement overnight.

In 2009, King became the first woman athlete to receive the Presidential Medal of Freedom, recognizing her advocacy for women and the LGBTQ+ community.

She also founded the Women's Sports Foundation and the Billie Jean King Foundation, which support girls, women and future leaders through sports, education and activism.

Her documentary, "Give Me the Ball!," will premiere Sunday on ESPN, the ESPN App and Disney+ in the United States.

The ESPN Films documentary, directed by Liz Garbus and Elizabeth Wolff, includes archival footage and interviews with King, Serena Williams, Elton John and Chris Evert.
