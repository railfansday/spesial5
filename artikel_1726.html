The wild life of Peter Storey who died aged 81: An Arsenal double-winning legend who was jailed for three years for his role in a criminal counterfeit operation, once ran a brothel and was caught trying to smuggle adult films into the UK

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

By LUKE AUGUSTUS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 11:40 EST, 18 November 2026 | Updated: 11:55 EST, 18 November 2026

Those connected with Arsenal and England in particular, as well as Fulham, were in mourning on Tuesday following news of Peter Storey's death, aged 81.

The former right back and defensive midfielder died in hospital after a short illness, surrounded by his family.

Arsenal confirmed that the club will pay their respects at their Champions League match against Borussia Dortmund - which is their first home match post the international break - which takes place on November 24.

A club statement read: 'Everyone at Arsenal is deeply saddened by the passing of 1970-71 double winner, Peter Storey.

'All our thoughts are with his family and loved ones at this incredibly sad time. Rest in peace, Peter.'

Storey enjoyed a stellar career at Arsenal, becoming a bona-fide legend in north London and earning a reputation as a tough tackler.

Arsenal legend Peter Storey died aged 81 on Tuesday in hospital after a short illness

He played 501 games for Arsenal - placing him eighth on their all-time appearances list

Joining the club as an apprentice in 1961, he made his first-team debut against Leicester City in October 1965. That outing was the first of 501 appearances for the club - a tally which places him eighth on their all-time list.

A tough tackler, Storey moved from right back to central midfield - playing a key role in their Fairs Cup triumph in 1970.

During the 1970-71 campaign, Storey was part of the Arsenal side which completed the double and played an important part in their FA Cup success.

He scored a stoppage-time penalty against Stoke in the semi-finals to force a replay and the Gunners subsequently went on to lift the trophy.

Storey left the Gunners in 1977, scoring 19 goals across his time at the club, and moved to Fulham before retiring in 1978.

On the international stage, Storey was called up for England in 1971 and was capped 19 times - winning those between 1971-1973.

Post-retirement, Storey turned his hand to being a pub landlord. He owned a pub called the Jolly Farmers, now renamed De Beauvoir Arms, which was situated just over a mile away from Highbury - Arsenal's former home.

However, as his own autobiography - True Storey: My Life and Crimes as a Football Hatchet Man - later details, Storey - who was married four times - was a 'confirmed ladies' man who loved a drink' and his establishment 'became a magnet for north London villains'.

Storey, who played at right back and in midfield, won 19 caps for England between 1971-1973

Post-retirement, Storey got on the wrong side of the law multiple times for various offences

Becoming acquaintances with criminals 'the Barry brothers' ended up seeing Storey serve time in prison as a result.

Storey aided the siblings' counterfeit operation by providing the finance and storage of the cast die for them to do so.

The trio were eventually caught and arrested - with Storey serving three years in prison at Wandsworth and Spring Hill for his actions.

However, the story does not end there. Far from it.

While on bail for conspiracy to produce counterfeit money, Storey looked to raise quick capital to flee to Spain.

Storey sought to do so by starting a brothel.

Called the Calypso Massage Parlour, he set it up with three associates. That operation was short-lived though as he was arrested in December 1979, handed a £700 fine and given a six-month suspended sentence.

There were other run-ins with the law too. He was also briefly jailed for contempt of court after failing to appear at his bankruptcy hearings.

After his run-ins with the law, Storey moved to the south of France later in life (pictured in 2010)

In April 1982, he was given a 12-month suspended sentence for stealing two cars he had on hire purchase whilst running his minicab firm.

Eight years later, he spent 28 days in jail for attempting to import porn videos from Europe. However, he was caught when the videos were found in a spare tyre smuggled into the UK.

Storey, who later in life moved to the south of France, sold the majority of memorabilia for £20,000 years ago. However, he leaves behind plenty of memories as a player and post-retirement.
