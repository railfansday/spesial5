Sabre Wants to Be the Transaction Engine Behind AI Travel Bookings

AI platforms could become a new front door for travel bookings. Sabre wants to handle the transactions behind them, even if someone else controls the customer relationship.

Sabre spent years modernizing the shopping, booking, and servicing systems that AI applications can use. Now it's pursuing the major AI platforms themselves, aiming to keep travel transactions flowing through its systems even as the customer relationship moves elsewhere.

Sabre's new Mosaic Travel Connector for Grok Bot is the latest step. Announced this week and still in private beta, it is designed to bring flight and hotel shopping, booking, and servicing into the AI assistant.

The stakes are high for airlines and hotels. AI assistants could send them customers without travelers ever visiting their websites, but the platforms could also decide which handful of offers consumers see.

Sabre CEO Kurt Ekert explained why that matters: "the consumer will not paginate through results. They're going to get back one, two, maybe three options and choose from that."

Sabre is positioning itself as the transaction engine behind AI assistants, starting with Mosaic Travel Connector for Grok Bot, announced this week and still in private beta, which is designed to bring flight and hotel shopping, booking, and servicing into the AI assistant. The groundwork started in 2025 with agentic APIs and a Model Context Protocol server, and Sabre says more than 80 partners are in pilot or production with its MCP services, a mix of experiments and live deployments rather than 80 high-volume booking businesses. The company plans to extend the connector to ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini throughout 2027, though it gave no platform-specific dates. The economics are the open question: CEO Kurt Ekert said the agency incentives Sabre pays today would disappear when it sits behind an AI platform, replaced by "a revenue share of sorts with the LLM," and he believes per-transaction revenue and margins could be comparable or higher. Sabre has not disclosed commercial terms with individual AI platforms, and most of the potential upside from conversational commerce is excluded from its multiyear financial forecasts. For suppliers, Ekert's point about display is the sharp one: travelers will see one, two, or maybe three options rather than paginating through results.
