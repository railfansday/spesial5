Can Trump’s Agenda Survive the Blue Tsunami?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The core question is whether Republicans can preserve institutional gains long enough for them to become permanent.

President Donald Trump speaks in the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., November 9, 2026.

The catastrophic midterm results dealt to Republicans two weeks ago threaten but do not ultimately negate the conservative policy landscape as it stands two years into President Trump’s second term. As I wrote before the election, many aspects of this administration are clearly good from the conservative perspective, not just by comparison to the Biden administration, but to Trump’s own first term and previous Republican administrations since the days of Ronald Reagan.

The border has been secured, deportations have been carried out at a high rate, federal agencies have been forced to abandon much of their institutional commitment to DEI and financial support of the left-wing NGO complex, universities have been put on notice that federal funding is not unconditional, and the administrative state has undergone its most serious shake-up in generations.

President Donald Trump speaks in the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., November 9, 2026.

Inez Feltscher Stepman is a senior policy and legal analyst at the Independent Women's Forum.
