MGM Dubai’s second delay in 18 months leaves Wynn even longer as the UAE’s only licensed casino operator. MGM is still holding space for a casino it may never get to run.

MGM Dubai resort will open in early 2029, about a year later than the March 2028 date CEO Bill Hornbuckle gave investors in February.

Ari Kastrati, MGM Resorts’ chief content, hospitality and development officer, announced the new timeline alongside Dubai-based developer Wasl promoting the project’s structural topping out, which means the main structure is complete. “We are committed to keeping this momentum going as we head toward our early 2029 opening,” he said in a statement on Monday. The announcement did not say why the date moved by a year.

MGM Resorts' Dubai project will now open in early 2029, roughly a year later than the March 2028 date CEO Bill Hornbuckle gave investors in February. Ari Kastrati, MGM's chief content, hospitality and development officer, announced the timeline alongside Dubai developer Wasl as the project topped out structurally. The announcement did not say why the date moved. It is the second slip in about 18 months: on the first-quarter 2025 earnings call Hornbuckle said the resort would be ready in the third quarter of 2027, and on the first-quarter 2026 call he said the project "remains on schedule." MGM did not mention Dubai on its second- or third-quarter calls this year. The 280,000 square meter island resort next to the Burj Al Arab combines MGM, Bellagio, and Aria hotels with 1,500 rooms, 40 restaurants, a beach club, a theater, retail, and wellness facilities, and MGM manages it under a non-gaming agreement with Wasl Hospitality. MGM is holding 23,000 square meters on a podium floor for a potential casino, but Hornbuckle said in April that MGM has not heard whether gaming will be permitted. The delay gives Wynn a longer lead: Wynn Al Marjan, the UAE's first licensed integrated casino resort, is set to open in September 2027, and CBRE estimates the UAE casino market at upwards of $8.5 billion.
