Under Pressure From Struggling Members, SAG-AFTRA Vows to Advocate for Universal Healthcare

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The union has created a healthcare task force as its members talk openly about having troubles qualifying for its health plan and paying dues.

Actors have become increasingly vocal in recent months about their struggles amid the slowdown in production of film and television shows, drawing attention to performers’ hardships in qualifying for their union health plan and even paying their dues.

Facing pressure and online criticism to do something more for floundering members, on Friday the actors’ union SAG-AFTRA announced that its national board had voted unanimously to create a public policy-focused task force to advocate for universal healthcare. The group’s new President’s Healthcare Task Force will “mobilize SAG-AFTRA’s collective strength, public influence, and institutional capacity to advance universal access to affordable, comprehensive, and reliable health care,” the union stated. According to union president Sean Astin, the SAG-AFTRA Health Plan is on strong financial footing but “a subsantial majority of our members do not qualify” by either not earning the requisite minimum of $28,090 or working 108 applicable days per year.

“This Taskforce seeks a new level of participation in the national movement to expand care for everyone,” Astin added. “We will actively engage with policymakers, gather allies and provide a structured way for our members to participate formally in the process. The President’s Healthcare Taskforce will be an instrument of support in the movement for universal healthcare. The President’s Healthcare Taskforce will be an instrument of support in the movement for universal healthcare.”

It’s a step, though far from a quick-fix solution to the problems that members have grown louder about in recent weeks. In late October, American Horror Story and Glee actor Cheyenne Jackson launched the “One Job Away” campaign to draw attention to actors in need of one or a few more jobs to qualify for health insurance. “This is systematic. This is like across the whole swath of SAG actors working right now,” he told The Hollywood Reporter at the time.

According to the latest terms the union negotiated with studios and streamers earlier this year, aimed at addressing healthcare inflation, eligibility premiums and the earnings threshold for health insurance will rise slightly in 2027, with more increases coming in the years after.

Some members have also recently faulted the labor group for dropping its COVID-era policy of keeping performers who were behind on their dues in the union. As of Nov. 1, SAG-AFTRA returned to terminating the memberships of actors who owed dues and, according to Variety, thousands have been dropped from the labor group as a result.

As these concerns were developing, its national board extended union staff leader Duncan Crabtree-Ireland’s contract until 2032 on Oct. 24 and reportedly gave him a 15 percent raise, drawing some further scrutiny. Prior to the reported raise, Crabtree-Ireland, whose title has been expanded to deem him the chief executive officer of the union, was already the highest-paid labor leader in Hollywood with a published gross salary of $1,068,535. The union explained at the time, “The extended term provides for added stability and continuity for the union during a time of unprecedented industry disruption.” But some members found the timing insensitive.

Once seen as a progressive pipe dream, universal healthcare may be becoming more politically palatable after a midterm election that hinged on affordability concerns and amid rampant healthcare inflation. But achieving it would, to say the least, be politically complex and require immense support from many quarters. Still, at a time of major healthcare challenges across the country, the union can at least point to its advocacy as a form of action.
