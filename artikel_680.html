Marvel’s ‘Blade’ Director Exited Failed Movie Because ‘Life’s Too Short to Make S— Films’ and It Was ‘Dangerous’ Working ‘Without a Proper Script’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bassam Tariq revealed in a new interview with The New Arab that the biggest lesson he learned as the original director of Marvel’s failed “Blade” movie is “life’s too short to make shit films.”

“It was funny and strange to start casting and building without a proper script,” Tariq said abot his short-lived Marvel career. “You’re sitting there and explaining to actors what their role will be, and they just have to believe you. That’s also very dangerous because if the text comes and it doesn’t add up to what you were saying, your credibility gets completely shot. All I have is my integrity, and I’ve been thinking about that a lot.” Related Stories ‘Chicago Fire,’ ‘Med’ and ‘P.D.’ Set New Crossover Episodes on NBC in January

“If you’re the bearer of bad news to your actors, but you’re not the one making any of these decisions — and it’s actually coming from a corporate entity — that’s not a great way to be a director,” the director continued. “I know some people can take that and run with it, but I learned I was losing credibility with my actors, and it wasn’t a healthy way to operate as a filmmaker. Life’s too short to make shit films, so that was my big lesson: I have to have full control over my script. If we disagree, it’s between them and me. It’s quite sad because we had such high hopes for what we wanted to build.”

Marvel originally announced “Blade” at Comic-Con in July 2019 with Oscar winner Mahershala Ali set to star in the title role. Tariq (who went on to direct Ali in “Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother”) was the first director to join the movie, but he exited in September 2022 roughly two months before it was set to begin shooting. Yann Demange stepped in as the movie’s director after Tariq’s exit, but Demange ended up dropping out in June 2024.

Variety reported in November 2023 that “Blade” had gone through at least five different writers while in development, from “Watchmen” scribe Stacy Osei-Kuffour to Michael Starrbury. The movie was ultimately scrapped and never went into production.

Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige told the “Happy Sad Confused” podcast over the summer that he was “feeling like a gigantic loser and failure” for failing to get Ali’s “Blade” made, to which Ali agreed with during a later interview with GQ magazine.

“That’s a Kevin Feige question,” Ali said when asked why Marvel never got to the point of putting “Blade” into production. “They had, as you said, a two-time Oscar-winning actor under contract and you got billions of dollars. That’s a question for them. I don’t mean anything personal to you, but I don’t know why it didn’t. To this day, I don’t know why it didn’t happen. I just know that it was—Kevin, he said it was the biggest failure of his career and I agree.”

Amid the film’s development struggles, Ali’s interest in playing Blade never wavered. He told Variety on the “Jurassic World Rebirth” red carpet in 2015: “I’m ready. Let them know I’m ready.” In an interview with GQ earlier this year, Ali called out Marvel point blank by saying: “You had me under contract, they have billions of dollars, if they wanted to do the movie, we would’ve done the movie. So we’re not doing the movie.”

Feige explained in July 2025 how the prolonged delays on “Blade” were a symptom of Marvel’s “over-expansion” into Disney+ streaming content where “for the very first time ever, quantity trumped quality.” When Marvel began to reevaluate its development process, the “Blade” movie was one of the projects that stalled.
