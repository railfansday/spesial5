Celtic defend head of security amid Green Brigade row

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Green Brigade occupy a standing section at Celtic Park

Celtic have defended their head of security and the action taken against the Green Brigade last season after the fan group declared four supporters had been cleared over the incident that sparked a lengthy ban.

A Green Brigade statement said the four had been "finally cleared of wrongdoing" over the occurrence, which took place during a game against Falkirk on 29 October last year.

The Scottish champions released footage at the time which they said showed "around 100" supporters verbally abusing and assaulting a steward and two police officers.

The alleged flashpoint came after a ticket inspection at the rail seating section of Celtic Park, the club explained, as they outlined a ban on about 250 supporters, which lasted nearly six months.

The Green Brigade say Celtic's "irresponsible and vindictive claim" was intended to "smear" them.

A statement from the group said: "Not guilty pleas were accepted by both Procurator Fiscal and sheriff for breach of the peace charges before the trial commenced.

"The four supporters cleared were subjected to dawn raids, damage to homes and harassment of family members including confiscation of personal devices, this included a vulnerable adult who was left alone in the cold with no means to contact anyone after the front door was removed."

Celtic lift suspension of Green Brigade fans group

The Green Brigade called for the club to apologise for the collective ban and to lift suspensions on two supporters who remain banned over their interaction with head of security Mark Hargreaves at last year's abandoned annual general meeting.

"This week's outcome represents significant vindication for all supporters concerned in these events over the past year," they added.

"It also calls into question the credibility of Mark Hargreaves' subsequent allegations against two Green Brigade representatives."

Celtic pointed out they play no part in criminal proceedings and added that they were not aware all had been completed. And they stated that the outcome of the four cases did not alter their assessment of the incident.

The club said: "The conduct of the group witnessed that day was unsafe and completely unacceptable â it has no place at Celtic Park or anywhere in football. The club took reasonable and proportionate steps to deal with this conduct.

"Such was the seriousness of the incident that the club was called to attend a special meeting of the Safety Advisory Group of Glasgow City Council."

Celtic accused the Green Brigade of continuing a "campaign of personal harassment and abuse" towards former senior police officer Hargreaves.

"Mark is a highly-qualified, dedicated professional who plays a crucial role at the club in ensuring the safety of our supporters.

"Where conduct presents an unacceptable risk to that safety or demonstrates an unwillingness to co-operate with it, the club has to take appropriate and proportionate action."

BBC Scotland have contacted Police Scotland for comment.
