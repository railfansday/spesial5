Before Moving Contracts Into Revenue Hub, Prove Who Will Send the Next Invoice.

Importing a contract and moving its billing are different operations. A team can reproduce an agreement's dates and line items accurately while leaving two systems prepared to charge for the same future period.

That is why a Revenue Hub migration should begin with the next invoice, not with the import file. The most consequential question is which system owns each future billing instruction, especially when the customer renews, expands, or changes terms during the transition.

I would treat contract migration as a controlled transfer of responsibility. A successful import is one piece of evidence. The full acceptance test is whether the team can explain the active agreement, the next bill, the collection method, and the system responsible for each action without creating duplicates or losing history.

HubSpot's direct-contract guide documents direct creation and import under a beta path. It distinguishes ordinary imports without billing schedules from a separate billing-continuity migration path. It also documents billing and collection configuration. Do not treat a record import as proof that recurring billing has moved.

The implementation model below is a proposal. Example registers, states, and test cases are not native HubSpot objects or a claim that every account can perform the same migration. Verify supported paths, eligibility, payment processing, and permissions in the account before planning a production cutover.

A visibility-only contract can be valuable. It can give customer-facing teams a view of the agreement while the established billing system remains authoritative. That is a legitimate architecture if the boundary is explicit and the record does not misleadingly suggest that HubSpot owns collection.

For every agreement in scope, record the authoritative system for commercial terms, billing schedule, invoice issuance, collection, adjustments, and accounting export. Include a named owner and the evidence required to transfer each responsibility.

Do not use a single migration-complete flag for all six. A contract may be visible in HubSpot while collection remains external. An accounting connector may be reconciled while payment-method transfer is still unresolved. Those are separate states, not minor details beneath one green status.

The chosen owner may remain the same for some rows. Migration does not have to mean moving every responsibility at once. It does require avoiding two active owners for one billable obligation.

Capture the agreement as of a defined checkpoint. Include customer identity, billing entity, currency, term dates, recurring and one-time items, billing cadence, open invoices, approved adjustments, renewal terms, and pending amendments.

The baseline should distinguish committed terms from disputed or proposed changes. A salesperson's planned expansion is not an accepted amendment. A draft renewal is not the next active contract. An overdue invoice is not a reason to rewrite the commercial record to match the collected amount.

Reconcile the baseline with the people authorized to interpret it. Technical teams should not invent the treatment of unusual terms or decide how revenue should be recognized. Route accounting and legal questions to the appropriate owners, while documenting the resulting implementation decisions.

Freeze only the scope necessary for the cutover, and specify how urgent changes will be handled. An undocumented freeze creates shadow updates. A controlled exception process can preserve continuity without pretending the business will stop changing for the convenience of an import.

An expansion, contraction, date correction, billing-contact update, and renewal are not interchangeable edits. Classify the change before choosing the mechanism that applies it.

HubSpot's contract overview describes change quotes and direct-edit paths with different requirements. It also states that an accepted renewal quote creates a new contract associated with the prior one. Those lifecycle relationships should be preserved rather than flattened into a single continually overwritten record.

For a hypothetical expansion, retain the agreement before the change, the approved amendment, the effective date, the affected line items, and the expected billing consequence. A proposed amendment can have a future effective date without changing what the customer owes for the current period.

Use separate evidence for agreement acceptance and system execution. The customer may approve a change before the billing system applies it. Conversely, a system edit may occur without sufficient business approval. Both mismatches should be visible in an exception queue.

Proration is a policy implemented through software. The result can depend on effective dates, billing periods, currencies, rounding, credits, and the treatment of previously issued invoices. A short arithmetic formula is not enough to describe every commercial arrangement.

HubSpot's contract setup documentation includes proration and renewal settings. Verify the actual configuration and supported behavior before modeling expected results. Do not assume an example from another account proves your account's outcome.

Build synthetic fixtures for a mid-period increase, a mid-period decrease, a future-dated change, a change near a renewal boundary, and a change after an invoice has already been issued. For each fixture, finance should approve the expected commercial treatment before the engineering test is judged.

Retain the calculation inputs and expected document sequence. When a result differs, classify whether the issue is a setting, an unsupported scenario, an incorrect expectation, or an implementation defect. Do not repair a test by manually changing the total until it looks plausible.

A billing correction does not necessarily amend the agreement. A contract amendment does not necessarily explain every historical invoice correction. Keeping both records prevents the team from erasing the distinction.

The contract management guide allows editing eligible manually associated invoices, but excludes subscription-created invoices and invoices automatically associated after quote acceptance. An eligible invoice amount edit does not update total contract value. That separation should inform reconciliation rather than be treated as a defect to eliminate through forced synchronization.

For example, imagine a hypothetical billing error that is corrected without changing the customer's agreed package. The correction trail should explain the invoice adjustment while leaving the approved commitment intact. If the customer also negotiated a package reduction, that is a second business event with its own effective date and approval.

Reporting should be able to represent both. If the system cannot explain why billed value differs from committed value, the team will eventually reconstruct the story from messages. That is a fragile substitute for a maintained evidence trail.

Choose a specific boundary for the transfer: which periods remain owned by the old system, which begin in the new system, and how straddling amendments will be handled. Document the treatment of open invoices separately from future billing.

The plan should identify each account's next expected invoice date, amount basis, responsible system, and collection path. Where exact amounts are not yet known, state why and identify the authoritative calculation source. Do not invent certainty for the sake of a migration checklist.

Before enabling new billing, verify that the old system will not independently issue the same obligation. Use supported controls and a reviewed procedure. Do not delete historical records to prevent future activity; history and scheduling are different concerns.

If the old system's state cannot be confirmed, stop the affected cohort. A delayed transfer with a named owner is safer than two teams each assuming the other has disabled billing. Preserve the obligation and its due date while resolving the uncertainty.

A payment method visible in one system is not proof that another system may use it. Availability, processor support, customer authorization, and migration constraints require separate verification through the approved payment-provider process.

Do not export or copy sensitive payment details into spreadsheets, prompts, or ordinary CRM fields. Keep payment operations within approved systems and involve the provider and finance owner for supported transfer paths.

Where the payment method cannot move through a supported process, the migration plan may require another collection arrangement. That is a customer-facing and financial decision, not a technical workaround an engineer should invent to complete the import.

Similarly, a sandbox demonstration cannot prove that production collection will behave correctly. Define what was tested, what remains unknown, and who must authorize any controlled production verification. No real charge should be initiated merely to make a technical checklist green.

A renewal should retain its relationship to the agreement it succeeds. Otherwise the business can count both as active commitments or lose the ability to explain the transition between them.

Create fixtures for a standard renewal, an early renewal with changed terms, a renewal while an amendment is pending, a canceled renewal, and an evergreen arrangement. Define which dates, values, and associations should be visible at each stage.

Do not assume that every agreement has a meaningful end date. A finite-term test suite is not enough to validate an evergreen population. Segment the migration by commercial shape and verify each supported path separately.

Also test the downstream account view. Customer success should be able to distinguish current entitlement from a proposed future package. Sales should see the renewal opportunity without interpreting it as already committed. Finance should retain the link between issued documents and the agreement revision that produced them.

A rollback plan should state what can be reversed and what cannot. A draft record may be editable. An invoice already delivered or a payment already collected may require a formal correction process rather than deletion.

Prepare an exception queue for duplicate schedules, missing associations, uncertain collection outcomes, mismatched terms, and unprocessed changes. Each exception needs an owner, a containment action, and a verification requirement.

If a timeout leaves the outcome of a consequential operation uncertain, inspect the owning system before repeating the operation. Retrying a read is different from retrying a create or charge. A migration tool should reflect that difference in its recovery procedure.

Keep a cutover journal with the cohort, checkpoint, decisions, verification evidence, and unresolved items. It need not be elaborate. It must remain usable when the engineer who performed the migration is unavailable and another person needs to explain the next bill.

The migration is ready when the team can answer five questions for every in-scope agreement: what is currently committed, which revision is active, who issues the next invoice, how collection is authorized, and where corrections are recorded.

Verify those answers after an amendment and a renewal, not only after the first import. Reconcile a sample forward from agreement to bill and backward from bill to approved terms.

Revenue Hub's newer contract capabilities expand the options for connecting commercial history with customer context. The implementation succeeds when those options produce one clear responsibility for the next obligation. A populated contract record is useful. A defensible billing handoff is the actual cutover.
