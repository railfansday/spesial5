We’d Rather Spend the Holidays in These 7 Gorgeous Ski Town Rentals

I’ve planned most of my ski trips around one thing: the skiing. How’s the terrain? What’s the vert? How deep is the base? When’s the next storm?

Where I’m sleeping has generally been a secondary consideration. Give me a comfortable bed, a place to dry my gear, and a clean bathroom, and I’m happy.

But this holiday season, I’m dreaming bigger. What if, instead of the usual marathon of cooking, cleaning, and navigating family drama, we packed up our skis, gathered our favorite people, and disappeared into the mountains for a few days?

Of course, if I’m going to convince everyone to trade their usual holiday traditions for a ski trip, a cramped motel room and a communal boot dryer probably won’t cut it.

A gorgeous cabin with a roaring fireplace, a proper kitchen for holiday dinners, and a hot tub waiting after last chair? Now we’re talking.

Apparently, I’m not the only one who likes the idea.

According to Airbnb’s latest winter travel report, travelers are increasingly looking to smaller mountain towns for their holiday getaways. Searches for holiday stays in Hunter, New York, are up 37 percent year over year, while ski destinations like Brian Head, Utah, and Mammoth Lakes, California, also made the company’s list of trending winter escapes.

And with Airbnb’s new checkout rules, which generally prohibit hosts from requiring guests to strip beds, start laundry, or wash and put away dishes for bookings made on or after November 20, what’s not to love? Sorry, family and friends, the same rules do not apply at Chalet de Shafer. Here’s a sponge.

From a sprawling Mammoth chalet that sleeps 12 to a romantic mirrored hideaway in the Catskills, these seven properties offer a compelling alternative to the usual holiday routine: beautiful mountain settings, plenty of character, inspiration to make a few new traditions, and, most importantly, nearby skiing.

Some ski trips are easy; others require a group chat, a shared grocery list, and a spreadsheet. This five-bedroom Mammoth Lakes townhouse was made for the latter.

Located near Canyon Lodge, the spacious retreat sleeps 12 and offers easy access to one of the West’s most impressive ski mountains. The townhouse puts skiers near several lifts, with access to beginner terrain, intermediate cruisers, and connections to Mammoth’s more challenging slopes. There’s also a free gondola connecting Canyon Lodge to the Village, making dinner and après plans a lot easier.

I’ve been on enough group ski trips to know that getting 12 people out the door at the same time is an unachievable goal. That’s why staying within walking distance of multiple lifts accessing Mammoth’s sprawling terrain is such a gift. Everyone can ski on their own schedule, and nobody has to organize a carpool.

Mammoth ranked No. 7 in the West in SKI’s 2027 Resort Guide and landed in the Top 10 for snow, grooming, and terrain variety. With more than 3,500 acres of skiable terrain, 3,100 feet of vertical, and a summit elevation above 11,000 feet, it’s the kind of mountain that can keep even the most ambitious skiers occupied for days.

Inside the townhouse, you’ll find a wood-burning fireplace, private steam room, arcade table, and a well-equipped kitchen designed to feed a crowd. There’s ski and gear storage, too, which anyone who has shared a vacation rental with 11 other skiers will appreciate.

And because the Canyon Lodge lifts are within walking distance, you can leave the cars parked and focus on the important stuff, like who forgot to buy coffee and whose turn it is to make breakfast.

New Yorkers don’t have to travel far to find a proper ski weekend. Hunter Mountain, about 2.5 hours from the city, offers 1,600 feet of vertical and some of the most substantial lift-served skiing in the Catskills. And while Western skiers might turn their noses up at skiing in New York, anyone who’s spent time on the East Coast knows there’s a particular satisfaction in making the most of whatever winter throws at you. Hunter’s extensive snowmaking helps with that.

And just down the road is an architecturally stunning 225-square-foot mirrored tiny home that practically disappears into the surrounding forest. There’s a private wraparound deck with a hot tub, hammock, and outdoor fireplace, plus access to an all-glass sauna. It’s the kind of place that makes a romantic ski weekend feel considerably more romantic.

And if you’re a true New Yorker without a car, take a tip from my friend Abby and take the $59 round-trip ski bus from Manhattan to Hunter Mountain. You’ll still need to figure out transportation between the mountain and the cabin, but it’s a pretty compelling alternative to renting a car for the weekend. Check the bus schedule before planning an overnight stay.

This ski-town rental has one amenity that immediately caught our attention: a private sauna overlooking a Vermont river.

Located along the Brewster River near Smugglers’ Notch, this two-bedroom cottage is surrounded by 20 acres of mountain scenery. Large windows frame the water, while a wood-burning fire pit and sauna make post-ski recovery a major draw.

The fully equipped kitchen and riverside dining area are ideal for slow mornings, though there’s plenty of reason to head out early. Just a short drive away, Smugglers’ Notch Resort spreads across three mountains, with 78 trails and plenty of tree skiing.

Smugglers’ Notch has a reputation as a family resort (it earned the No. 2 spot for family friendliness in our 2027 Resort Guide), which might lead you to imagine an endless procession of pizza wedges and children in matching ski-school vests. But over on Madonna and Sterling, the mountain has some legitimately challenging terrain, including steep trails and tree skiing that demand your full attention.

Smuggs ranked No. 11 in the East in SKI’s 2027 Resort Guide and earned the No. 3 spot for value and No. 5 for terrain variety. Not bad for a mountain that still feels refreshingly old-school. In other words, you can spend the day skiing some legitimately challenging terrain and the evening recovering in a sauna with a view, with absolutely no obligation to do anything productive.

One thing to know: The hosts require four-wheel or all-wheel drive for winter access, and Route 108 through Smugglers’ Notch Pass closes seasonally.

Some ski trip lodging is designed for little more than getting some sleep between ski days. Others feel like the main event.

The Cabin at Bear River, a 560-square-foot retreat tucked into 3.5 wooded acres near Sunday River, falls firmly into the second category.

The fireplace, radiant-heated floors, and well-equipped kitchen, complete with a fondue pot and KitchenAid mixer, make a compelling case for a particularly civilized après experience.

When you’re ready to ski off that Gruyère, Sunday River is just down the road. Spanning eight interconnected peaks and 884 skiable acres, the Maine resort offers everything from long groomers to challenging steeps. Expert skiers can test themselves on White Heat, one of the East’s famously steep double-black-diamond runs, while intermediates will find plenty to explore across the mountain.

Sunday River ranked No. 9 in the East in SKI’s 2027 Resort Guide and earned top-10 honors for grooming, lifts, and terrain variety.

Back at the cabin, there’s a private sauna and outdoor hot tub waiting. Call it recovery if you want. Personally, I don’t need a sports scientist to justify cranking those jets all the way up.

Utah’s ski reputation has been built largely on the Cottonwood Canyons, and for good reason. But there’s a whole other ski experience waiting four hours south of Salt Lake City, where the mountains look nothing like the Wasatch, the base elevation is a lofty 9,600 feet, and you don’t have to spend your morning sitting in the red snake of traffic wondering whether everyone else in the state also called in sick.

Brian Head Resort is one of Utah’s highest-elevation ski resorts. Its two mountains, Giant Steps and Navajo, offer 72 trails and 1,320 feet of vertical. Navajo is a good place for beginners to get their ski legs, while Giant Steps has more challenging terrain, including intermediate and expert runs and terrain parks.

The surrounding landscape is a world away from the Wasatch, too. Think high-desert scenery, red-rock country, and the chance to tack on a visit to nearby Cedar Breaks National Monument.

And tucked among the trees, just a short drive from the lifts, is this striking Scandinavian-inspired chalet that I think I might actually take off this list so I can have it all to myself. (Quickly Googles “squatters’ rights in Utah.”)

Inside, expansive windows let in plenty of natural light, while the clean, modern interiors make an excellent case for doing very little after skiing beyond enjoying the private hot tub and fire pit right outside.

With room for eight guests, it’s a great option for a group trip, especially if your friends appreciate a good at-home après setup as much as fresh snow.

If your ideal winter vacation includes a wood-burning fireplace, a good book, and excellent New England skiing, let us introduce The Conscious Cabin.

This renovated 777-square-foot log cabin sits on three wooded acres in New Hampshire’s White Mountains, less than 10 minutes from downtown North Conway.

Inside, exposed timber and cozy furnishings deliver exactly the kind of atmosphere you’d hope for from a New England cabin. Outside, there’s a spacious backyard with a fire pit, gazebo, and a short trail leading to a brook.

Nearby Cranmore Mountain, just outside downtown North Conway, offers 1,200 feet of vertical and 60 trails, and is particularly well suited to families and skiers who appreciate cruising groomers. For more challenging terrain, head to Attitash, where two peaks offer more than 300 skiable acres, including classic New England trails, glades, and steeper runs.

Both resorts made SKI’s 2027 Resort Guide, ranking No. 17 and No. 19 in the East, respectively. Cranmore also took the top spot for lodging and ranked No. 4 for dining, making North Conway an especially appealing base for a winter getaway.

After all, this charming cabin is considerably more appealing than spending the holidays sleeping on your aunt’s pullout couch in the basement.

If your ideal ski vacation includes a little European charm without the transatlantic flight, Mont-Tremblant makes a pretty convincing case.

The Québec resort is known for its colorful pedestrian village, lively après scene, and some of the most substantial lift-served skiing in Eastern Canada.

With more than 100 trails spread across four mountain faces, 763 skiable acres, and 2,116 feet of vertical, Tremblant has enough terrain to keep everyone from beginners to expert skiers entertained. Its extensive snowmaking system is another welcome advantage for early-season trips.

Perched on the mountainside, Horizon 115-204 is a modern three-bedroom condo with ski-in/ski-out access, so you can go from breakfast to the slopes without a complicated transportation plan.

Inside, there’s a gas fireplace, a fully equipped kitchen, and enough room for eight guests. A balcony offers mountain air and space for a post-ski drink, while an on-site hot tub gives you another great reason to get out of your ski boots quickly.

And if you’re not quite ready to call it a night, the resort’s pedestrian village is accessible by shuttle, with restaurants, bars, and enough après activity to keep the evening going.
