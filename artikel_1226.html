Aussie mum's well-meaning act to get kids off their screens sparks council crackdown and $800 fines - after 'joyless' neighbours complained about her soccer pitch on public land

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 03:16 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 03:16 EST, 26 November 2026

An Aussie mum who set up donated soccer goals on a vacant block of council land to create a thriving neighbourhood pitch says she has been threatened with huge fines.

After complaints from neighbours, described by one local as 'joyless', Brisbane City Council dispatched its Rapid Response ranger team to the site.

The mum behind the initiative, Graceville resident Madeline Ruffino, told Daily Mail she had been ordered by the council to remove the goals after every game or face an $834 fine for each one left behind.

She is now leading a neighbourhood revolt against the council, with more than 1,000 people signing a petition calling for the donated goals to be allowed to stay.

What began with a handful of children kicking a ball around on an empty block on Long Street East quickly grew into a popular after-school and weekend gathering spot after Ms Ruffino installed the soccer goals.

Residents maintained the grass, marked out two pitches and secured donated equipment from Brisbane Paralympic Football Program director Jay Larkins.

Parents were thrilled to see their children spending hours outside with friends rather than glued to their screens.

For many children living in townhouses with little or no outdoor space, the vacant block became their shared backyard.

Ms Ruffino said the council's order to remove the goals after every game was impractical.

'We live in a townhouse complex. We don't have sheds or large backyards, and we simply have nowhere to store multiple goals, particularly the larger ones that were so generously donated to these kids.

'They weren't hurting anyone. They were just playing.'

But after being threatened with fines, Ruffino reluctantly packed away the larger goals and made smaller ones available from her front yard for children to borrow and return.

Since then, she said the number of children coming out to play had dwindled, with some arriving with their football boots and balls but quickly leaving when they discovered the goals were gone.

Ms Ruffino said that older children in the neighbourhood already had limited options when it came to playing outdoors.

'We've got security guards walking school grounds on weekends to stop kids playing there,' she said.

'Parks are fantastic for younger children, but older kids need somewhere to go too. Somewhere they can kick a ball, meet their friends, meet new friends and feel welcome.

'We encourage kids to get off their screens and spend time outside, but we need to give them places where they're actually allowed to do that.'

The vacant land was acquired by the council through property buybacks following the Brisbane floods of 2011 and 2022.

Tennyson Ward councillor Nicole Johnston told Daily Mail that children were permitted to play soccer on the vacant land, but council policy prohibited sporting equipment from being stored there.

'Children can play soccer on the open space in Long St East, Graceville. I have communicated this message clearly to residents for many months,' she said.

'However, council policy does not allow sporting facilities to be stored on parkland or open space, and sports fields and facilities do not adjoin residential properties due to adverse amenity impacts and property damage to neighbours.'

Cr Johnston said formal sporting fields were generally maintained to a higher safety standard for organised sport.

She suggested residents store the goals on private property, bring them onto the land when children wanted to play, and remove them afterwards.

She also pointed to several alternative recreational spaces, including Bert St Clair Oval, about 500 metres away, a park in neighbouring Sherwood and Gordon Thompson Oval in Chelmer.

But Ms Ruffino has vowed not to give up on the neighbourhood children.

'They may be making us remove the goals, but I'm not going to stop helping these kids play.'
