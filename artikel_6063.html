Canada's second-largest airline will quietly pull out of Tulum. The airport, which serves the Southern Riviera Maya, will lose another airline despite strong travelers' interest in the region. Many carriers have scaled back operations at the airport, focusing instead on the region's primary gateway, Cancun. United Airlines was the latest major airline to announce it would terminate operations at the airport.

WestJet's withdrawal from Tulum will leave just two other Canadian airlines serving the airport, with Air Canada maintaining flights from Montreal and Toronto while Air Transat continues serving Tulum from Montreal and Quebec City.

On January 9, Montreal will lose its WestJet link to Tulum, as the airline terminates its Boeing 737 MAX 8 service to the Mexican airport. WestJet currently operates the route up to three times a week, with the link being operated for the carrier's package holiday subsidiary, Sunwing Vacations.

Mexico in general, and especially Cancun and the Riviera Maya, continues to be a popular year-round destination for Canadians looking for vacations to the south. WestJet's decision to drop its services to Tulum raises questions about the route's load factors, as the airline abruptly stopped flying there in the middle of the Canadian winter, usually a popular time to travel south.

With WestJet removing its flights from Montreal to Tulum, it leaves just Air Canada and Air Transat operating the service nonstop to Tulum. From Montreal to Cancun, Air Canada, Air Canada Rouge, Flair Airlines, Air Transat and WestJet all maintain services either seasonally or year-round.

Montréal-Pierre Elliott Trudeau International Airport (YUL)

Canada's largest city will also lose its WestJet link to Tulum, as on January 9 the carrier will axe its service to the Southern Riviera Maya airport. This will leave Air Canada as the sole operator on the city pair, making it yet another carrier unable to make operations at Tulum viable.

Instead, flights to and from the state of Quintana Roo will now be focused on Cancun, where WestJet maintains year-round flights. From Cancun, Toronto is served by Air Canada, Air Canada Rouge, Air Transat, Flair, Porter Airlines, and WestJet.

Toronto Pearson International Airport (YYZ)

WestJet, up until January served Tulum seasonally and has not maintained a year-round service. The same is true for Air Canada and Air Transat.

WestJet's home at Calgary is the third and final Tulum route the airline has cut. WestJet maintains a seasonal service to Tulum, flying up to twice weekly with a mix of the 737-800 and MAX 8. On January 14, the service to the Mexican tourist destination will be shelved, leaving no flights to Canada's west.

Despite Tulum continuing to be a world-class tourist destination, the local airport has struggled to gain momentum since its opening. WestJet joins a long list of airlines that have opted to withdraw service to the airport in recent months, most recently United Airlines, which will terminate its routes from Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) on January 2 and George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) on January 4. Domestic carrier Volaris will also end its nonstop flights to Tulum from Guadalajara on January 7.

Remaining airlines serving Tulum after January will be Air Canada and Air Transat from Canada, and American Airlines and Delta Air Lines with services to the US, as well as Mexicana de Aviación and Viva with domestic services within Mexico.
