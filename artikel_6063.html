Judge fires back at Fani Willis' office in courtroom clash over Trump case: 'Due process doesn't apply to you'

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Willis’ office argued it should be allowed to defend its budget as President Donald Trump and other former defendants seek nearly $17 million in legal-fee reimbursements, but an appeals judge said the office was not entitled to due process.

A Georgia appellate judge pressed Fulton County District Attorney Fani Willis’ office Tuesday as it pushed for permission to fight millions in legal-fee requests from President Donald Trump and other former defendants in her collapsed election interference case.

Before a three-judge panel, Willis’ office argued it should be allowed to protect its budget from payments stemming from a prosecution she and her office were disqualified from handling in December 2024.

"What process are you entitled to? You’re the government. Due process doesn’t apply to you," Judge J. Wade Padgett told Donald Wakeford, a chief senior assistant district attorney representing Willis’ office.

Trump and other former defendants are seeking nearly $17 million under a Georgia law passed last year, with Trump alone requesting more than $6 million. The law allows defendants to recover legal fees if their prosecutor is removed for improper conduct and their charges are later dismissed.

Tuesday’s hearing focused on whether Willis’ office could participate in challenging those requests.

The state is represented in the reimbursement fight by Pete Skandalakis, executive director of the Prosecuting Attorneys’ Council of Georgia, who took over after Willis’ disqualification and moved to dismiss the charges in November 2025.

Willis’ office argued he cannot adequately protect its financial interests because his own decision to drop the case opened the door to the reimbursement requests. Wakeford also argued that Skandalakis’ authority in the case ended when the prosecution did.

"This statute has created a mess and that is no secret," Wakeford said of the new Georgia law allowing defendants to seek reimbursement for their legal fees.

The judges pressed him on whether Willis’ disqualification still bars her office from participating.

"So let’s talk about the elephant in the room then," Padgett said, pointing to the court’s earlier ruling declaring the district attorney wholly disqualified.

"The criminal prosecution has ended," Wakeford responded, arguing that the fee dispute is a separate matter.

"Is the disqualification over? Is she no longer wholly disqualified?" Judge Ken Hodges asked.

Wakeford said that Willis’ disqualification applied to the criminal prosecution, arguing it did not bar her office from participating in the separate legal-fee dispute.

But the panel also questioned whether that finding satisfies the new law’s requirements for reimbursement.

"The trial judge found an appearance of impropriety and found that actual misconduct had not been established," Padgett said.

Willis and her office were removed from the case after an appeals court found that her romantic relationship with Nathan Wade, the special prosecutor she appointed, created an appearance of impropriety.

In March, Fulton County Superior Court Judge Scott McAfee denied Willis’ request to intervene, finding that Skandalakis could adequately represent her office’s interests. Her office is asking the appeals court to overturn that decision.

The court announced no ruling during Tuesday’s hearing, leaving unresolved whether Willis’ office can directly challenge the fee requests.
