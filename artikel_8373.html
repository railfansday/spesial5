The platter is very well-protected in many hard-drives (vibration isolated) so it's quite possible the platter remains intact. If so, it is trivial to transfer the platter into a similar drive and extract data (e.g. testdisk).
No, not trivial as transfer needs to be done in a dust-free environment into another drive similar enough to work. I think there would also be a need for precise alignment.

I do not fear anything that requires clean rooms, exotic equipment, and a considerable amount of time, expertise, and determination.

By the way, I don't feel bad about discarding or destroying rather than re-using. At lest so far, continuing progress toward larger and larger drives makes old ones almost useless.

Fracturing the platters is sufficient for all of thus who don't possess military-grade information. By the way, most of them seem to be glass.

I've transferred platters myself to help others recover data without the use of a clean room or any special tools.

https://www.wikihow.com/Swap-Hard-Disk-Drive-Platters

While it's true that helium-filled enterprise-level drives require precise alignment and special tools garden variety drives are typically easy to transfer and spin up again.
