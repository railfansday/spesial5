This AI Power Stock More Than Doubled Its Revenue and Still Burned Cash. Which Number Matters More?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Bloom Energy is growing rapidly, but it saw negative free cash flow in the third quarter. Should investors be concerned?

By Courtney Carlsen – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:23AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bloom Energy ›

Hyperscalers need electricity fast, and Bloom Energy's (BE -11.79%) solid-oxide fuel cells can bring power on-site in as little as two months. That demand helped send third-quarter revenue soaring 138% year over year to $1.2 billion. But there's one catch: Bloom still burned $181 million in free cash flow.

So which number should investors focus on: the explosive growth or the cash drain? Both numbers give us insight into where Bloom's business stands today. Here's what investors need to know.

Bloom is seeing incredible demand for its solid oxide fuel cells and has inked major agreements with Oracle, American Electric Power, and Brookfield Asset Management in recent years, adding significantly to those deals this year. Delivering on these deals explains the company's incredible revenue growth.

However, Bloom Energy is still an up-and-coming industrial manufacturer scaling operations, not an established one like GE Vernova. While GE Vernova commands large up-front customer deposits due to its market dominance, upstarts like Bloom Energy use more customer-friendly, milestone-based billing arrangements, with payments tied to delivery, installation, and commissioning.

Bloom is spending cash up front to expand capacity while waiting for sold units to hit billing milestones. This dynamic isn't uncommon among industrial start-ups. As they scale, cash outflows for capital expenditures and manufacturing capacity expansion increase, while cash inflows are more uneven, causing free cash flow to fluctuate.

CFO Simon Edwards noted that Bloom's free cash flow burn is "not the beginning of a trend" and that the company expects to finish 2026 with positive free cash flow. The company has $2.5 billion in cash and equivalents and generated $439 million in positive free cash flow over the last year.

Bloom Energy stock has delivered, and investors will want to watch whether the company can consistently convert revenue growth into free cash flow to fund further growth in the quarters ahead.
