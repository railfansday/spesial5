Is everyone on Nets roster available for trade? Mostly yes

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Is "everyone" on the Brooklyn Nets roster available for the right price as Sam Amick reported Friday in a round-up of trade rumors?!? Even 20-year-old Mikel Brown Jr. and Egor Demin, the organization's two highest draft picks over the past 30 years?!? Seems a bit much, but with the Nets record disappointing as they get ready to play the Memphis Grizzlies at the end of a four-game road trip, the general sentiment appears true, as the The Athletic reporter noted.

If you call the Brooklyn Nets (3-8) about any of their players, they will listen. Per a team source, it's just that simple.

Such is life when you're a rebuilding team, not to mention an organization that has two veterans — Michael Porter Jr. and Julius Randle — who could help any number of contenders.

Those two players are widely discussed as top targets in league circles, with Porter (21.5 points and 7.5 rebounds per) above all the rest of the Nets. The 28-year-old small forward who won a title with Denver in 2023 is in the last year of his deal ($40.8 million). Randle, the 31-year-old forward who helped Minnesota reach the West finals in 2025, has a player option worth $35 million for next season.

Two days ago, Jake Fischer of The Steinline offered a similar assessment.

An expectation persists in some corners of the league that Brooklyn, which owes its first-rounder in June to Houston as a lingering vestige of the acquisition of James Harden in January 2021, might indeed decide to shop veterans such as Michael Porter Jr. via trade between now and Feb. 11 to see if it can capitalize on the anticipated lack of true sellers in the marketplace.

So, it's increasingly a given in NBA circles that the Nets will be a bazaar come February 12 at 3:00 p.m. EST or maybe even before, say December 15, the unofficial start of trade season when the dozens of players signed or re-signed over the Summer become available for the first time. The larger the pool of players, the more possibilities that GMs and capologists can noodle. Of course with all the injuries across the league, it's possible teams will want to find replacements earlier than that.

MPJ is the MMP of the group == Most Mentioned Player — for a number of reasons. He is having yet another good season as Amick notes and is on an expiring deal. If the Nets don't want to extend him — they can up till June 30 — then they'd better do something or lose him without a return. That is not going to happen. Randle is in a bit of a similar situation but he isn't expiring. He's got a player option and while he will be four years older than Porter Jr. by the deadline, his contract load is lighter. MPJ is owed $40.6 million this season, compared to the $33.3 million Randle makes now and the $35.8 million he'll earn if he doesn't exercise his option. He is expected to.

Otherwise, what's the Nets rush? That's where it gets interesting. For one thing, the Draft and off-season are when Sean Marks & co. makes their biggest moves and it's also when teams examine their futures a lot more closely. They know what the need. It wouldn't be surprising if the Nets look to add more assets for a big trade, even if they already had 13 first rounders and 19 seconds available. Do they have their eye on someone? Almost certainly, they have a list, but as Marks admitted on Brooklyn's Media Day in September, there's a lot that has to be considered, starting with who other teams are willing to part with.

Marks was specifically asked at Media Day when he might "push all the chips to the middle of the table" and make the big move for that player, as he has said, who will "absolutely and automatically change the trajectory of the franchise." He was generally non-committal, but did identify the factors that will come into play.

"We've got a couple more rookies this year to add into the fray as well," Marks said, suggesting he wants to see how his young players do this season. "But then it's also a combination of what becomes available, right? I can't predict when players from other teams become available, what free agency looks like down the road and so forth. So, I think that's where you just keep your flexibility and you know, like, okay, when's the right time to go for this?

"We're always listening. I'm not sure we're going to be any more active than we have been in the past, but that doesn't mean we're not listening. That doesn't mean we're not shaking trees and seeing what's out there. I think a lot will be predicated first and foremost on what this roster looks like, and we'll get a good feel for that pretty early." (Emphasis added.)

Of course, the Nets has been very very active in the recent past with the two franchise-defining trades with the Knicks and Rockets just before the the Draft in 2024, or the multiple salary dump deals just before the Draft in 2025 that got them MPJ and the Nuggets 2032 pick and the Hawks No. 22 pick. or just before the Draft in 2026 that got them Randle in the T'Wolves first round pick.

Bottom line, we think, is that the Nets are holding their cards close, maybe even closer than usual (which is saying something.) It makes sense, of course, because you can't keep accumulating assets forever. You have to move.
