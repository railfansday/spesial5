Sooryavanshi hits 28 sixes while smashing fastest double ton in List A cricket

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Vaibhav Sooryavanshi broke more records on Tuesday as the 15-year-old cricket sensation guided India A to a seven-wicket win vs UAE with an extraordinary knock; the opener made an astounding 204 runs off a mere 53 deliveries, including 28 sixes,

Vaibhav Sooryavanshi has broken more records as the teenage cricket sensation smashed his way to the fastest century and double century in List A cricket during India A's seven-wicket victory against the United Arab Emirates on Tuesday.

The 15-year-old produced an extraordinary innings, making 204 off a mere 53 deliveries, including 28 sixes, during the Asia Cup Rising Stars group match in Doha.

Sooryavanshi, who became the youngest debutant for India's senior T20 side in July, first raced his way to a 28-ball century, the fastest ton hit in List A cricket, surpassing Jake Fraser-McGurk by one delivery.

The Indian prodigy then went one better, advancing to his second ton off 24 balls to obliterate the record, previously held by Chad Bowes, for the fastest double century struck in List A cricket [103 balls].

His 40-ball 150 was also the fastest in List A cricket too, with the 15-year-old going on to smash four consecutive sixes.

It came after UAE had set India a target of 250 to win and thanks to Sooryavanshi's feats, Tilak Varma's side would go on to seal victory inside 14.2 overs.

Sooryavanshi completed the feat by smashing an incredible 28 sixes and seven fours, with a strike rate of 384.90.

That surpassed Western Australia's D'Arcy Short's record tally of 23 maximums, which he managed during a match against Queensland in 2019.

Known as a ruthless ball striker, Sooryavanshi was named the IPL's MVP for the 2026 season, after leading the league with 776 runs and a strike rate of 237.30.

In February, he set a new record for the highest score made at an Under-19 Cricket World Cup final, making 175 runs from 80 deliveries, as India went on to seal a 100-run win against England.
