Making sense of Liam Coen's nonsensical decision to kick a late field goal

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Jacksonville’s loss to the Giants on Sunday included a nonsensical decision with 3:43 to play. Down by eight points and facing fourth and goal from the New York eight, Jaguars coach Liam Coen decided to kick a field goal.

After the game, Jaguars coach Liam Coen justified the decision to take the three points by calling an effort to score a touchdown a “hope play.”

We hope he’s not serious. It was eight yards, not 80. Regardless of what the analytics said, it was time to go for the touchdown, which with a successful two-point conversion would have tied the game.

As explained on Monday’s PFT Live, I think the two-point conversion was the deciding factor for Coen. When the score was 23-9, the Jaguars scored a touchdown that cut the margin to eight. After the touchdown, Coen played the all-too-common analytics game, opting to go for two in the hopes of cutting the margin to six and ideally avoiding overtime if they had scored again.

When it was time to go for two, Coen utilized his best two-point play based on the specific matchup of the Jacksonville offense against the New York defense. It failed. Which meant that, if/when the Jaguars scored another touchdown, they would need to rely on Coen’s second-best two-point play.

After Plan A failed, why would Coen believe Plan B would succeed?

And so instead of going for a touchdown which may or may not have been capped by a successful two-point try, Coen opted to take the three and kick off. Which pissed off the New York offense, and which helped set the stage for the Jaguars never getting the ball again.

If the Jaguars had gone for the score, the Giants would have gotten the ball deep in their own end. The Jaguars would have been likely to get another shot at a touchdown and a two-point conversion.

Still, the Jaguars would have had to convert a two-point play after their best option at going for two had failed. In the moment, that reality may have been the difference for Coen.

Which brings us back to the analytics-driven trend to go for two after scoring a touchdown when trailing by 14. If the two-pointer fails, the coach needs to be confident in the second-best two-point play for the week. Coen likely wasn’t, which contributed to the decision to not try to gain eight yards in a one-score game.

That’s the most important factor when it comes to relying on math. Once teams start down the go-for-two path, their confidence (or lack thereof) in the potential for successful two-point plays becomes critical.

Coen’s best two-point play failed. Which may have shaken his faith in his second-best two-point play. Which may have prompted him to opt for the three points in lieu of rolling the dice on scoring from the eight — before rolling the dice on scoring again from the two.
