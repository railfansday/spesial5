Salesforce vs. ServiceNow: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Salesforce trades at a lower forward P/E than ServiceNow, yet ServiceNow is growing revenue faster, forcing investors to weigh price against momentum.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 20, 2026 at 4:27PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Salesforce ›

The shift toward enterprise automation and artificial intelligence has created a fierce battle between two cloud software titans. Investors choosing between Salesforce (CRM -1.47%) and ServiceNow (NOW -1.42%) must decide which platform offers better value.

Salesforce dominates the customer relationship management space, helping businesses track every client interaction. ServiceNow specializes in streamlining back-office workflows, from IT support to HR tasks. Both companies are now racing to integrate generative AI into their ecosystems, making this a pivotal comparison for investors seeking growth in tech stocks.

Salesforce provides cloud-based software that connects sales, marketing, and IT on a single platform to serve over 150,000 companies. It has recently prioritized AI integration through a major partnership with Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) for "Copilotforce," which combines customer context with AI experiences. The company continues expanding its reach through acquisitions such as Braze (BRZE +0.09%) to deepen engagement. Salesforce serves businesses of all sizes globally, and no single customer accounts for more than 10% of its total revenue.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached approximately $43.9 billion, representing growth of nearly 11.2% over the prior period. The company also generated net income of roughly $9.7 billion, which reflects a net margin of close to 22%. This growth trend has been supported by a steady increase in revenue from $34.9 billion just two years ago. The business focuses on maintaining profitability while scaling its cloud ecosystem and proprietary AI tools.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.3x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio, a measure of how well a company covers short-term debts with short-term assets, is approximately 0.8x. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $14.4 billion. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 23.4% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

ServiceNow offers an AI platform designed to automate enterprise work across departments including IT, human resources, and finance. The company focuses on large enterprise clients, ending recent periods with over 600 customers spending more than $5 million annually. It maintains strategic partnerships with Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) and Accenture (ACN +1.19%) to drive AI-driven workflow integrations for its 8,700 total customers. This strategy targets a growing presence in the public sector and large organizations seeking efficient digital transformation.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached close to $15.4 billion, marking revenue growth of approximately 21.8% over the preceding year. ServiceNow reported net income of nearly $1.4 billion for this period, yielding a net margin of approximately 9.4%. While its net margin is lower than its rival, the company has consistently increased its top line from $9.0 billion two years prior. This expansion reflects robust demand for workflow automation tools across global markets.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.2x. The current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term obligations using liquid assets, is roughly 1.0x, and free cash flow reached nearly $4.6 billion in the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 35.9% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Salesforce faces intense competition from established software vendors and AI-native firms entering the customer relationship management space. Integrating large-scale acquisitions like Braze involves execution risks that could disrupt operations if not managed carefully. The company also navigates cybersecurity threats and potential volatility from shifting its pricing models toward consumption-based structures.

ServiceNow must prove it can continue competing effectively as the AI market evolves rapidly. The company relies on third-party cloud providers and faces regulatory scrutiny related to its growing government contracting business. Additionally, any failure to realize benefits from its aggressive merger and acquisition strategy could impact future growth prospects if integration costs exceed the value gained.

Salesforce appears more attractively priced on a relative basis, carrying a lower Forward P/E based on future earnings estimates and a lower P/S ratio measuring value against sales over the past twelve months.

I'd go with ServiceNow. Management expects its AI products to reach $1.7 billion in yearly contracts by the end of this year, and they run on software that big companies rely on for everyday work like IT and HR requests. That software is a very sticky product, with an impressive 98% renewal rate, meaning almost all customers sign up again.

ServiceNow's AI business is growing fast, and overall sales are still rising rapidly. The stock plunged after its latest earnings report, as investors worried about slower growth in future contracts and about AI hurting software companies. But management has raised its AI goal twice this year, and the drop leaves the shares well below where they traded before.

To be fair to Salesforce, it is the cheaper stock and it is buying back a lot of its own shares. Its AI products are also catching on. But its sales are growing more slowly, and part of that growth came from buying another company. For long-term investors, ServiceNow offers a fast-growing AI business on top of software customers rarely leave, at a lower price after the recent sell-off.
