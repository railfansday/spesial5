Nebius Posted Superb Earnings and Crashed Anyway. Buy the Dip.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nebius' post-earnings dip is a compelling buying opportunity for patient investors.

By Marc Guberti – Nov 18, 2026 at 5:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Nebius Group ›

Nebius Group (NBIS -3.66%) stock lost 17% of its value after delivering earnings on Nov. 12, which looks like a good buying opportunity. Broader artificial intelligence headwinds have been dragging down the entire AI trade, with Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia acting as one of the latest gut punches.

The bad earnings reaction does not change the long-term fundamentals, which have continued to strengthen. While Nebius may have negative short-term sentiment, the future continues to look bright, warranting a closer look at current levels.

Naturally, the earnings report wasn't perfect. There were some real issues, but they are short-term problems that the company will solve. For instance, some of the capacity from the $17.4 billion, five-year Microsoft (MSFT -0.69%) infrastructure deal was delayed to Q2 2027. Previously, all of that capacity was supposed to be available by the end of the year.

It's not a problem with Nebius' execution. That's a good thing and a bad thing. The good thing is that when Nebius builds its own data centers, it's usually on time. However, the bad news is that some of Nebius' revenue is tied to colocation sites. In other words, Nebius does not own the facilities for those data centers.

Colocation introduces more execution risks and also increases expenses in the future. Nebius doesn't own the entire stack for all of its AI build-outs in the same way that Iren (IREN -4.59%) does. The company hinted at that in the Q3 shareholder letter, saying that future gigawatts will come from sites it owns while citing third-party delays as a major reason for the shift.

In its Q2 results, Nebius guided for $7 billion to $9 billion in annual recurring revenue (ARR) by the end of the year. The company pushed that target to $7 billion-plus by February and believes it will be in that range before the New Jersey site tied to the Microsoft deal is at full capacity.

This delay caused some concerns and is part of the reason why the stock dropped by 17% on earnings. However, does a delay for this lofty ARR target from December 2026 to February 2027 really mean anything if you zoom out five years?

It's a small snag in timing that does not change the thesis. Furthermore, the delay is due to a colocation facility, and Nebius is increasingly moving away from that model for future AI data centers. The problems are small rather than structural, which makes the current price drop a good buying opportunity.

Some investors were worried about Nebius projecting $40 billion to $45 billion in capital expenditures in 2027, but that's the nature of building large data centers that require AI chips. Some of the best parts of the business model suggest that Nebius can comfortably cover its capital expenditure requirements.

For instance, customer prepayments now cover all of its capital expenditures tied to Q3 agreements. These expenditures include GPUs, data center construction, and leasehold improvement costs, according to Nebius' Q3 shareholder letter. In 2027, 75% to 85% of Nebius' capital expenditures are expected to be covered by prepayments and available investment-grade asset-backed financing.

Nebius' cash balance, credit facilities, and revenue surge may be enough to cover the gap. Its megawatt values continue to climb, with Q3 deals averaging more than $25 million per megawatt, compared to a range of $20 million to $25 million per megawatt in the second quarter.

Rising megawatt values have also reduced the payback period of future deals from 1 year and 10 months to 1 year and 7 months. Trimming the payback period by three months in a single quarter highlights Nebius' strong traction, and this payback window excludes prepayments.

Nebius is targeting $12 billion to $13 billion in revenue next year. It implies that revenue will quadruple from current levels, and if that growth continues, this dip can be a generational buying opportunity.
