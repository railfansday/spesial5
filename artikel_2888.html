Trump Says Inflation Could Help Pay Down the National Debt. Here's What That Means for Your Retirement Savings

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Investors must work a little harder to protect their wealth.

By Will Ebiefung – Nov 24, 2026 at 8:00PM EST

According to Bureau of Labor Statistics data, the average price of a can of soda in the U.S. is around $0.60 when bought as part of a 12-pack -- up 80% from its price in 2019. While this is an extreme example, it highlights the devastating impact inflation can have on your purchasing power and the real value of your stock market returns.

Unfortunately for investors, inflation remains a problem. And a devastating warning from President Donald Trump suggests it could get worse before it gets better. Let's dig deeper into the market's challenges and discuss strategies investors can use to stay ahead of the curve.

For many investors, the Trump era has been a bit of a headscratcher. The administration has deliberately pursued disruptive policies, such as massive tariffs that can make it harder for many U.S. companies to do business. But instead of crashing, stocks have gone gangbusters, with the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.50%) jumping 25% since his election victory in November.

To be fair, some of this is luck. Trump has been in the right place at the right time to oversee the rise of generative artificial intelligence (AI), which has single-handedly added trillions to the value of America's tech industry. That said, White House policies have also played a role.

Trump's One Big Beautiful Bill Act helped lock in corporate tax cuts while also slashing regulations in the energy industry and introducing goodies such as allowing companies to write off certain categories of research and development expenses for tax purposes. Big tech is also happy with the administration's generally pro-AI stance, which is crucial at a time when public pushback against data center construction is growing.

That said, while Trump has managed to keep Wall Street happy so far, the administration has struggled to rein in other macroeconomic challenges that could undermine investor returns over the long haul. The biggest challenge is inflation. Tariffs and the Iran war have helped push the U.S. inflation rate to around 4% as of October, which is well above the Federal Reserve's target of 2%.

Higher inflation hurts investors' real returns and encourages the central bank to continue raising interest rates. This generally hurts stock valuations by making it harder for businesses to access the capital they need to invest in future growth.

Image source: Official White House Photo.

When asked about the situation earlier last month, Trump's response was alarming. In an interview with Time magazine, he suggested "certain levels of inflation" could help pay down the national debt "very rapidly." The logic may be that by lowering the value of the dollar, inflation will also devalue America's $40 trillion in borrowings, making it easier to manage. These remarks suggest the White House is willing to tolerate high inflation for much longer than investors will be comfortable with.

Trump's warning suggests that the U.S. government doesn't have a strong plan to bring down inflation or manage the national debt. In fact, both of these problems could get worse over the coming months as the war in Iran (along with the war in Ukraine) continues to drive up military spending and create lasting bottlenecks in the global economy.

That said, this isn't the time to panic. And it can pay to look toward the wisdom of investing legend Warren Buffett, who advised investors to never bet against America in his annual shareholder letter during the height of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2021. Over the long haul, the country has consistently bounced back from crises only to emerge stronger than ever. And there is no reason to assume this time will be the exception.

The recent midterm results have also already eased fears in the oil markets, as many traders expect the new Democrat-controlled Congress to impose more checks and balances on Trump's foreign policy.

The economic situation remains uncertain, but the long-term outlook is bright. And investors can stay ahead by increasing portfolio diversification to avoid being overly exposed to underperformance in any specific asset class.
