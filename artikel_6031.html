If you fly American Airlines frequently, one of its cobranded Citi cards could save you money on your trips.

The (see rates and fees) is the only credit card that includes a full Admirals Club membership.

It also offers statement credits, a free first checked bag (on eligible domestic itineraries) and bonuses that can help you reach American Airlines AAdvantage elite status.

The card has a $695 annual fee. Whether it’s worth paying comes down to how often you’ll use the lounges and how much you’d otherwise spend on purchases covered by the card’s credits.

Apply for the : Earn 80,000 bonus miles after spending $8,000 on purchases within the first three months from account opening.

Here’s how the value adds up in your first year and beyond.

New holders can currently earn 80,000 bonus miles after spending $8,000 on purchases within the first three months from account opening.

This bonus is worth about $1,160, based on TPG’s November 2026 valuations. We recommend holding off until at least 100,000 miles are on offer.

A significant portion of the card's overall value comes from its bonus, so earning it and redeeming the miles wisely are crucial to offsetting the card's high annual fee.

Related: The Citi / AAdvantage Executive's new benefits are live: 6 things to do now

Arguably the most valuable ongoing benefit of this card is the complimentary Admirals Club membership you'll receive every year that you hold it. When you have the , all you have to do is walk to the lounge and present a same-day boarding pass for an eligible flight.

American charges $750 a year for a single membership, which covers Admirals Club access for one person. A full membership (with two guests) and partner lounge access costs $1,200 to $1,400 a year (depending on your AAdvantage status). If you’d otherwise pay for a full membership, the card’s $695 annual fee can be worth it for lounge access alone.

With this card, you'll be able to access almost 50 Admirals Clubs and more than 60 partner lounges worldwide when flying American or one of its Oneworld partners. Notably, this includes Alaska Airlines lounges in hubs like Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), Oregon's Portland International Airport (PDX), San Francisco International Airport (SFO) and Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SEA).

You can bring in either your immediate family members (spouse or domestic partner and children under 18 years of age) or up to two guests. They don’t need to be on the same flight as you — just any American or Oneworld flight with a same-day boarding pass.

The offers valuable perks when flying American, including priority check-in at business-class counters (where available) and priority boarding privileges (where available) for you and up to eight travelers on the same reservation.

You also get your first checked bag free on domestic itineraries with American Airlines, saving you at least $45 each time. (Note that this is only for domestic flights; I had to pay out of pocket when I traveled to Mexico, forgetting the restrictions of this perk.)

Additionally, you'll receive a statement credit of up to $120 for Global Entry or TSA PreCheck (once every four years). I suggest maximizing your credit by applying for Global Entry, as it includes TSA PreCheck anyway.

The inKind dining credit is worth close to face value if you already eat at participating restaurants. Check the map for places you’d actually visit near home or on a trip.

I’ve had issues with the car rental credit, so I wouldn’t rely on it for value. Previously, a Budget rental in Cabo San Lucas, Mexico, was not coded correctly, and Citi wouldn’t manually apply the credit. Later, I completed another Budget rental in Ireland and waited the obligatory 10 weeks. Again, it didn't post, and again, Citi refused to apply a manual credit.

I don’t value the Lyft perk because you don’t get the $15 credit until after you’ve taken three (nondiscounted) rides in a month. Instead, I use my (see rates and fees) to earn 5 points per dollar spent, plus a $10 monthly in-app credit on Lyft purchases (through Sept. 30, 2027).

The inflight (and Admirals Club) credit is useful if you regularly buy onboard refreshments or snacks.

AAdvantage miles are more valuable (at 1.45 cents each) than Delta SkyMiles and United MileagePlus miles (both 1.2 cents), according to TPG's November 2026 valuations.

I redeemed part of the welcome bonus I received on this card for a luxurious flight. I used 70,000 miles (plus $174 in taxes and fees) for a one-way business-class flight in Qatar Airways’ fantastic Qsuite from Doha to Houston.

Given that this flight retails for $3,910 one-way, I got a very high 5.3 cents per mile from this redemption, more than triple TPG’s valuation of AAdvantage miles.

Finally, the is a great option if you want to extend Admirals Club access to friends and family affordably.

You can add up to three authorized users for $175 total (about $58 each), with each additional authorized user costing $175.

Every authorized user you add receives Admirals Club access (with a same-day boarding pass for an eligible flight) for themselves and up to two traveling companions. However, authorized users only get Admirals Club access, not a full Admirals Club membership.

If you’d otherwise pay for a full Admirals Club membership, the card’s $695 annual fee is already less than the cost of joining directly.

The welcome bonus can add substantial value in your first year. At renewal, base your decision on how often you used the lounges and how much the credits and travel perks actually saved you.

If lounge access isn’t important to you, consider one of Citi’s lower-fee AAdvantage cards instead.
