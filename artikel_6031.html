Becoming a savvy award traveler requires mastering two skills: earning points and miles, and redeeming them effectively.

By taking advantage of the best travel rewards credit cards, you can master the first skill. However, the second can be more challenging.

At TPG, we help you redeem transferable points at the maximum value. However, some travelers frequently run into issues finding the award availability they need.

There isn’t a general rule you can follow to make searching for award space easier. Each airline and award search engine has pros, cons and quirks to understand.

Fortunately, you don't need to search every airline's website individually. Several innovative third-party tools can now do much of the hard work for you. Knowing which airline programs to search can also save you time and points.

These are the best ways to find award availability for points and miles across major airlines.

Several apps and websites let you search across multiple airlines, alliances and programs at once, rather than worrying about the best loyalty program to sift through. Two of our favorites at TPG are: Seats.aero and Point.me.

Seats.aero currently searches more than 20 airline loyalty programs, including major programs like American Airlines AAdvantage, Delta SkyMiles and United MileagePlus. This powerful tool lets you search all these programs simultaneously across multiple routes, dates, classes and transfer partners in seconds. You can also search individual programs across an extended date range to identify available award seats. There are even handy filters so you can limit factors such as the maximum number of points or surcharges.

The platform has also developed several innovative tools to help you find those harder-to-book seats, such as Lufthansa first class, Delta One, All Nippon Airways first class and Qatar Airways Qsuite. You can set alerts across multiple routes, dates, programs and carriers to be notified by text or email if a seat you seek becomes available. There's also an AI Assistant if you don't know where to start.

Point.me searches more than 150 airlines across over 30 loyalty programs, generally checking one departure date at a time. That's particularly useful when you're trying to compare all the ways to book a specific flight, especially through programs Seats.aero doesn't fully support, such as British Airways Club, Iberia Club and Aer Lingus AerClub.

Unlike Seats.aero's cached results, Point.me uses live award searches. It also shows which transferable credit card currencies you can use, highlights applicable transfer bonuses and provides step-by-step instructions for transferring points and completing the booking.

Be aware that neither tool can actually book the ticket for you, so it's still worth confirming the award directly with your chosen airline program before moving any of your valuable transferable points.

The major North American airlines have reasonably comprehensive award search engines, so starting with the airline you want to fly is often the simplest option.

However, you'll sometimes find better value through a partner program. Here are some useful places to search.

It's worth comparing prices before booking. American, Delta and United all use dynamic pricing, so the number of miles required for a flight can change considerably depending on the date and demand.

Booking through a partner might save you thousands of points or miles, but don't assume that partner pricing is always fixed.

For example, Aeroplan now uses variable pricing for United-operated awards, so booking the same flight through Air Canada won't necessarily be cheaper than booking directly with United.

European loyalty programs can offer great redemption deals, whether you are taking, say, a business-class flight to Europe for just 40,500 credit card points or an economy flight to London for just 6,000 points.

Now that you know where to search for award space for your next trip, here are a few tricks to make the process easier.

This is an odd quirk of most award search engines, but you’ll often get different results if you search for your complete itinerary instead of one flight at a time.

If your complete itinerary isn't showing any award availability, try searching for each flight separately. This can help identify which segment is causing the problem, although finding each flight individually doesn't guarantee that the airline can combine them into a single award. The complete journey still needs to meet the program's routing and ticketing rules.

Sometimes, airlines will offer more seats for indirect itineraries than nonstop ones. Why? Airlines believe passengers will pay more for direct fares with cash (compared to indirect ones), so with less demand for connecting itineraries, they may offer additional seats.

Using this married-segment logic in your next search, you may be able to find long-haul, premium-cabin seats you did not think existed. Consider widening your search to connect in another city using a catch-all external tool like Seats.aero.

You might be surprised by the additional availability if you are willing to fly a little farther than your original destination.

However, don't assume you can remove a connecting segment and keep the same award price or availability.

One particularly frustrating issue when booking partner awards is phantom availability. This happens when an airline or search tool displays an award seat that can't actually be booked.

Unfortunately, this remains a problem across several major loyalty programs.

Before transferring points, select the exact flight and proceed as far as possible through the booking process. For an especially valuable redemption requiring a large points transfer, consider contacting the airline to confirm availability first.

Many airline customer service agents are good at their jobs, but you’ll get much better results when you do the work yourself.

Before you call to book an award flight, you should have the exact dates and flight numbers you want. Make your specific requests known early in the conversation.

As soon as you tell the agent where you’re trying to go, say something like, “I have the flight numbers here whenever you’re ready for them.” This way, they don’t waste any time trying to search on their end.

If you’ve done your homework to ensure that there is award space and your routing is valid, these calls will go much more easily.

Ask the agent to confirm the complete itinerary, the number of points or miles required, and the applicable taxes and fees. Once booked, check that the reservation has been ticketed and that every flight is confirmed in the cabin you wanted.

Learning how to find and book award flights is one of the hardest things about award travel — especially when booking premium-cabin seats on a long flight.

Fortunately, tools like Seats.aero and Point.me make it much easier to compare loyalty programs and identify flights that might otherwise take hours to find. Both platforms offer free features, while paid subscriptions unlock more comprehensive searches and other useful tools that can be well worth the cost when hunting for a hard-to-find, long-haul premium-cabin award seat.

If you have a specific airline you wish to fly, use the tables above to search for award space and compare prices across programs before transferring any credit card points and booking your trip.
