Florida Christians rebel against Imam's prayer opening a city meeting with a message of their own

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A group of Floridians showed up to a city commission meeting in the town of Bartow to protest an Imam’s invocation, praying the Lord’s Prayer in response to the Imam’s invitation. (Credit: Citizens Defending Freedom)

An imam who was scheduled to deliver the invocation at a city commission meeting Monday evening in a southwest Florida town was interrupted by Christian protesters — who had a prayer of their own to share.

Footage from inside the meeting obtained by Fox News Digital shows dozens of Christian activists crowded into the back of a Bartow, Florida city commission meeting room. As Imam Sajid Bhuiya of the town's Masjid Al-Nur Mosque stepped up to the lectern to give the invocation, they began reciting the Lord's Prayer in unison. Some chants of "USA!" broke out, as well.

According to Robert Goodman, the southeast regional director of the conservative activist group Citizens Defending Freedom, news of Bhuiya's scheduled appearance began making the rounds on social media last weekend. He was among those who shared the news on social media. Goodman said about 100 people from different groups ended up attending the meeting.

Christian protesters pack a city commission meeting in Bartow, Florida, as a local imam attempts to deliver the invocation. (Citizens Defending Freedom)

"Citizens Defending Freedom believes Christian voices deserve to be heard on matters affecting our communities, families, and freedoms, which is why we joined many other concerned citizens and organizations Monday night," Goodman told Fox News Digital.

He said the Quran teaches forced submission and permits men to strike their wives, while viewing the opinions of women as lesser than the opinions of men.

"We have every right to challenge teachings we believe conflict with human dignity, equality, religious liberty and America's founding principles, because we must be intolerant of intolerance while respecting every person's right to believe differently," he said.

Video from the city's official livestream of the event quickly went viral online.

Some protesters made off-color comments, including telling Bhuiya to go back to his own country, which Goodman clarified that neither he nor his organization support.

"The disrespectful remarks made by two or three individuals Monday night do not represent me or Citizens Defending Freedom, because as Christians, we are called to speak truth with conviction and respect, not insults," Goodman said.

Goodman said Bhuiya was invited to deliver the invocation by a city official. Members of the commission mostly sat quietly, observing the commotion while police handled the crowd.

Police eventually escorted the Christian protesters out of the building, though no arrests were made.

Imam Sajid Bhuiya of Masjid Al-Nur Mosque delivering the invocation at a Bartow, Florida city commission meeting after being interrupted by Christian protesters. (Robert Goodman)

Bhuiya then spoke, quoting passages from the Quran that he said are meant to unify people. "This is a reminder that our differences in background, culture and faiths should not prevent us from treating one another with dignity," he said.

Fox News Digital reached out to the Masjid Al-Nur Mosque for comment on the disruption of Bhuiya's invocation.

The city of Bartow released a statement explaining the Imam's appearance at the meeting.

The statement said that residents, "joined by individuals from other cities, attempted to prevent the imam from speaking and the Mayor from proceeding to conduct the business at hand."

A holy Christian cross laying on an open Bible with an American flag in the background. (iStock)

"For years, it has been tradition to open Bartow City Commission meetings with a prayer offered by either a faith-based leader or one of the City's elected officials," it said. "While historically the opening invocation/prayer is one based on Christian traditions, the opportunity is open to all faiths. This is consistent with the United States Constitution."

In the statement, city officials called for residents, "including religious leaders of all faiths, to come together to ensure that Bartow continues to be a welcoming place for all who live, work and play in Polk County's capital city."
