LIVE – Crypto News Today: Is Bitcoin Bottoming Out? BTC Bounces Back After PCE Shock

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Bitcoin bounced back toward $60,000 this morning after news on hotter-than-expected US inflation broke and sent the crypto tumbling to $57,300, its lowest level in more than two years. The rebound has brought back speculation that Bitcoin may finally be approaching a bottom, but ETF outflows and bearish signals continue to cloud the outlook.

October’s Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) inflation came in at 3.8% annually, while core PCE reached 3.3%, above the 3.2% expected by economists. These figures strengthened concerns that the Federal Reserve could keep monetary policy restrictive for longer, which then adds pressure to risk assets like Bitcoin.

Bitcoin nevertheless recovered after briefly dipping below $58,000, as we gauge and predict its next move.

Bitcoin remains down 23% this November, putting it on track for its worst monthly performance since June 2022. Spot Bitcoin ETFs have also suffered nearly $5 billion in net outflows this month through November 24, adding another source of selling pressure.

BlackRock’s IBIT has accounted for more than $4 billion in redemptions during the month, showing how quickly institutional demand has reversed.

However, not all investors are moving in the same direction. Data cited by Nansen shows wallets that rarely move Bitcoin received a net 10,000 BTC from exchanges over the past month, while ETFs collectively sold 70,000 BTC during November.

Nansen data shows large, infrequently moving wallets absorbed a net 10,000 BTC from exchanges over the past month.Meanwhile, spot Bitcoin ETFs sold 70,000 BTC in November.The imbalance is stark. Long term cold wallets are still accumulating, but nowhere near enough to offset…

Bitcoin’s rebound also comes after an arguably huge derivatives-market reset, with leveraged positions being flushed out during the recent slide. That could reduce some immediate selling pressure.

Holders have realized $8.4 billion in net losses over the past 30 days, according to CryptoQuant data, as investors are increasingly capitulating during the selloff. But the figure remains well below the $30 billion recorded during February’s bottom and $18.4 billion during June’s low. Selling pressure has intensified, but the scale of realized losses has yet to match previous capitulation events.

Some analysts believe Bitcoin’s recovery could be running ahead of the underlying market conditions. CoinMarketCap research head Alice Liu warned that the rebound may largely reflect short sellers covering positions, and not fresh demand, leaving prices vulnerable once that buying subsides.

Meanwhile, 10x Research’s bearish signal issued in October correctly anticipated Bitcoin’s decline toward $57,300. The firm has cautioned that oversold conditions can trigger short-term rebounds without necessarily signaling a reversal in the wider trend.

“Bitcoin has reached our $57,300 target. Short-term indicators were oversold for much of the decline, and tactical bounces were always possible. Our market structure work, however, kept pointing to a bearish primary trend.” – 10X Research

Bitcoin faces a difficult combination of persistent ETF withdrawals, uncertain news from Federal Reserve policy, and weakened market sentiment. The selloff has removed substantial leverage, but convincing evidence of renewed demand remains limited.

The $57,300 low is now a key level to defend. Holding that support while ETF outflows begin slowing will likely strengthen the case that Bitcoin is forming a bottom.
