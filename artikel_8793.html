The 6 Best On Running Shoes for Every Kind of Runner

On has come a long way since the days of cobbling together a running shoe from cut pieces of garden hoses and a dream for a springier shoe. The Swiss shoe manufacturer has evolved into a powerhouse of a brand with design and innovation leading its strategy. Founder and retired athlete Oliver Bernhard, along with Caspar Coppetti and David Allemann, has driven On forward with determination to make exceptional gear that’s high-performing and also sustainable and approachable for runners at all levels of experience and types. Whether you’re a casual road runner or hardcore trail trekker, On wants to match you with one of its pairs.

I have had the opportunity to test several On shoes, as has our Runner-in-Chief Jeff Dengate, alongside runners in our wear-tester program. We have narrowed it down to our favorites that we highlight in this list.

Many of the On shoes below have been through our extensive testing process. That means getting each model on the feet of our team of more than 300 avid runners who then put at least 100 miles on the shoe before providing their feedback. We’ve relied on our own in-depth research and experience in the running shoe industry, personal consultation with brand representatives, and nearly a decade of lab data and tester feedback to select the best models below.

On has put in serious work on the upper this time around, and the fit on the Cloudmonster 3 versus the 2 is noticeably more dialed-in, hugging the midfoot without that sloppy, I-might-lose-this-shoe feeling you’d get midrun. The engineered mesh breathes well and moves with—instead of against—your foot.

Fair warning, though, out of the box, the ride is a bit stiff, but give it a couple of runs, and you can sink into it like your favorite hoodie you definitely did not steal from your husband.

Once you’re about 15 miles deep, the CloudTec cushioning opens up and the signature CloudTec pods really carry you, giving you an energetic feel and a snappy return. What surprised me the most was how stable the shoe felt in spite of its impressive stack height. Thanks to the relative firmness of the cushioning, I felt surefooted and stable even on uneven surfaces like gravel and rocky terrain.

At $160, the On Cloudsurfer 2 lands right in the sweet spot of On’s lineup—priced well for a brand that often hits past $170 or $180, and a good value pick if you want On quality without stretching to the top of its usual range. So what does that money get you? A firm ride, first and foremost. This is noticeably firmer than the softer original, so if you’re chasing a super responsive, bouncy daily trainer, this likely isn’t the one to win you over.

Explosive energy return has never been On’s calling card. But that firmness is the whole point. It’s solid, consistent, and thanks to the chunked-up outsole rubber, it’s built to ride those miles. If durability and a dependable, no-drama ride are what you want, the Cloudsurfer 2 has it.

If you’re a heel striker looking for protection and comfort on daily runs, the Cloudswift 4 will keep those heel pads from getting wrecked. With a roughly 10mm drop and a 32.7mm heel stack, the geometry naturally favors a rearfoot landing, giving you a smoother heel-to-toe transition. The dual-density Helion midsole layers a softer foam up top for impact absorption over a firmer base for stability, and the newly shortened three-quarter Speedboard plate adds a stable, speedy feel for heel and midfoot strikers.

That said, a couple of caveats. The foam runs firm by 2026 standards, so “cloud-like” oversells it, while it’s great for mid-distance runs; long-haul runners may want more cushion. The engineered-mesh sock upper is breathable and comfortable, though the toebox is on the narrow side.

This shoe is one of our new favorites from the brand. It is the first shoe from On featuring their new, proprietary Surreal supercritical foam. It’s been in development for years and now it’s live. It provides what On refers to as “their softest cushioning yet,” and I’d agree. It’s a structured softness that doesn’t lose shape like some marshmallowy foams are wont to do.

This is a daily trainer with a major stack of foam that’s made to keep long miles easy. It’s not a speed shoe, but it is one of those shoes I can see myself grabbing for again and again because it’s simply just reliable. Gear Editor Amanda Furrer says it’s “near perfect” with “comfort all around.” It’s equally wonderful for runners with wide feet, or runners with high insteps looking for a roomy, secure fit.

I’ve been wearing and training in these for a few weeks, and I’m a big fan. They are redesigned from the first model to be lighter, snappier, and less prone to the foam condensing. They have a smooth, natural motion flexibility in the midfoot that allows you to rock forward without instability, and the plate gives you propulsion to see the kick through. Altogether, they’re bouncy and fun, and definitely fast.

The fit is solid for most foot types, but if you have flat or very wide feet, these are not for you. Also, if you have a narrow heel like I do, definitely employ the last lace eyelet for a heel lock, as otherwise you’ll probably get a bit of slip. They’re breathable and airy without feeling like they’re too insubstantial, but the texture of the upper itself does take some getting used to.

Competition: On’s racing shoes have snappy Helion HF, a Pebax foam, and rocker shape to glide you through transitions. The supershoes, including their Cloudultra Pro trail shoes, have a fiberglass speedboard that propels without losing stability.

In 2025, On introduced a new marathon-racing shoe, the LightSpray (LS) version of its Cloudboom Strike. Our editors witnessed On’s robot spraying plastic material as it twirled around the shoe, creating the laceless upper. The shoe was worn by 2025 NYC Marathon women’s champion Hellen Obiri.

Cushioned: On is known for its CloudTec cells, which saw an upgrade for this year’s Cloudmonster 3, which help absorb shock across the trunk.

Support: Their wider bases offer a bit of stability, but they’re not a brand that leans into framing for guidance.

Trail: On’s outsole compound, Missiongrip, is a sticky rubber material that is molded into lug formation for the base of the shoe. Depending on the shoe, it can tackle any manner of terrain.

The reason On was designed to be innovative and bring that “cell” formulation to market.

CloudTec: This is the bread and butter of On. They utilize open tubes or cells of rubber or foam form sandwiched between the top and outsole. They’re made to act as springs and shock absorption that both cushion and propel.

Speedboard: A light and snappy (typically) fiberglass layer in the midsole designed to help propel you forward while keeping you stable.

Helion: On’s proprietary cushioning material is made from a blend of EVA and OBC (olefin block copolymer) foam that provide you with firmness, responsiveness, and helps legs defeat fatigue.

Helion HF (hyper foam) is a Pebax version used in On’s racing shoes, like the Cloudboom Strike and Cloudmonster Hyper.
