Try This Variation of Sun Salutation if You Have a Tight Back or Hamstrings

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

In Yoga Journal’s Archives series, we share a curated collection of articles originally published in past issues beginning in 1975. This article about a Sun Salutation variation for tight hamstrings poses appeared in the September-October 1990 issue of Yoga Journal. Find more of our Archives here.

Sun Salutations are a means of aligning your breath and movement in a cyclical, graceful way. The series of poses unfolds in a way you can get lost in, creating the type of flow state you’re often after on your mat.

This Sun Salutation variation provides some opportunities for altering poses for anyone who experiences tightness in the back or hamstrings while retaining the strengthening along with the calming breath and movement of a classic Sun Salutation (Surya Namaskar). More specifically, it skips Plank but includes poses such as Chaturanga and the ferocious Upward-Facing Dog (Urdhva Mukha Svanasana), inviting you to further work your arms, legs, and abdominals.

Looking for even more of a challenge? Try hopping or jumping between poses when it makes sense for you.

Begin with your hands by your sides or at heart center in Mountain Pose.

Stretch your arms over the head and look slightly toward the sky in Upward Salute. If you want, bring your palms together and press.

Keep your knees bent as you bend forward with your arms extended over the head. Feel as if you are lifting your soft frontal body (the abdomen, organs, and lungs) into the shell of your back body (the pelvis, spine, and rib cage) so your spinal muscles do not overwork.

As you hang in Standing Forward Bend, begin to lift your sit bones upward, so the legs begin to straighten a little and your pelvis tips farther forward over your feet. You will feel an increased stretch along the back of the legs as the knees straighten. If you feel strain in the back of the knees or the lower back, keep your knees bent.

On an inhalation, step your left leg back behind you in Low Lunge. Allow your left knee to bend slightly or lower to the floor and make sure that your right knee is directly over your right ankle. If this pose puts too much strain on your lower back or groin, rest your hands on two blocks placed on either side of your forward foot.

Exhale to place your hands on the your mat and step back to Downward-Facing Dog. In this version, bring the feet hip-width apart, lifting the sitting bones and keeping the knees bent if needed. Keep the sitting bones lifted as you lower the heels toward the floor.

The most common mistake in the practice of Chaturanga is to bring the shoulders all the way forward over the wrists without bending the elbows. Lowering the chest from this position requires tremendous arm strength and almost invariably results in lowering the belly first and then collapsing the chest onto the floor. Lowering the knees to the floor can help.

On an exhalation, gently lower the knees to the floor and shift the chest forward until it is directly over the hands. Keep the elbows close to the rib cage as they bend, lowering the torso toward the ground without bending through the middle of the spine. If your arm strength is insufficient to prevent the middle of the spine from collapsing, you may want to do Plank Pose instead.

keep the abdomen lifted into the lower back as the chest lowers to just above the floor. Press back through your heels to elongate the spine.

Keeping the knees lightly on the floor, inhale and press the hands into the floor to straighten the arms. As the arms straighten, come onto the tops of the feet and broaden the shoulders, rolling the base of the shoulder blades down and forward in Upward-Facing Dog. Draw the belly back into the body, to support the lumbar spine, and lengthen the abdomen upward toward the chest. This variation creates a less extreme curve in the lumbar spine than the straight-legged version. You can always lower your chest closer to the floor if the backbend feels too extreme.

Move through Downward-Facing Dog, Standing Forward Bend, and Upward Salute, finishing with your hands together at heart center in Mountain Pose.
