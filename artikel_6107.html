Beond Airlines Suspends Flights For The Rest Of The Year: Better Luck In 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Back in April 2026, we saw Beond Airlines suspend all scheduled operations for the summer, as the airline claimed it would resume flights as of late October. Then in October, it announced it would return in December. There’s now another update, as the airline has just announced that it will resume flights in January. That doesn’t sound so good, does it?

Beond Airlines launched operations in 2023, and it brands itself as the world’s first premium leisure airline. The company is headquartered in Dubai, but primarily operates flights to and from the Maldives. I reviewed the airline in 2024, and the onboard product was super pleasant. The airline currently has a fleet of two aircraft, including one A319 and one A321.

Anyway, in mid-April 2026, the company announced that it would suspend its scheduled operations for the entire summer. The airline promised to resume flights in October, for the winter season.

Then in October, the airline announced that it would suspend operations through November 30, 2026, meaning it will resume flights as of December 1, 2026. Then just yesterday, the airline announced that it would suspend operations through December 31, 2026, meaning it will resume flights as of January 1, 2027.

So I guess it remains to be seen if we actually see a resumption of flights as of January, or if the timeline will get pushed back again. If you can’t make all-business class flights to the Maldives work for the festive season, then prospects seem pretty grim.

Beond Airlines does have its “commitment” to customers when plans change, which includes the ability to change destinations or travel dates, receive a travel credit valid for 18 months, or to receive a full refund. A change of destinations or dates, plus a travel credit, isn’t worth a whole lot for an airline that keeps pushing back its relaunch.

Interestingly, but perhaps not surprisingly, earlier this year the airline deleted its customer promise webpage, which used to guarantee rebooking on another airline in business class in the event a flight is canceled, or else the airline would offer a free flight. That page is now gone, and there’s no mention of rebooking on another airline.

So the airline will “support you every step of the way,” unless you actually want to take the vacation you booked, in which case you’re out of luck (which is less than ideal, given that many Maldives properties have very strict cancelation policies).

So I feel bad for Beond, and I’m not trying to do a “told you so, I was right” thing, because these are of course exceptional circumstances. The thing that made me suspicious about Beond for so long is that the airline would repeatedly make outlandish growth announcements, only to rarely follow through on them. That’s neither here nor there, though.

My assumption is that Saudi Arabia is to some extent funding the Red Sea flights, given how the country is throwing money at any airline willing to fly there. However, one wonders if that’s enough money to make the math work, or if Beond even has the general funding right now to keep things operating.

It’s just all so strange, because it feels like half of the announcements from Beond Airlines are really targeting prospective investors, constantly promoting how it’s going to open new subsidiaries all over the world, launch dozens of new routes, etc.

As I said in April, I didn’t think it was a sure bet that flights would resume in October. Now we’ll see if flights actually resume in January. I wouldn’t count on it. One bizarre angle to this is that Beond has claimed it will launch flights to London Heathrow (LHR). Best I can tell, there’s no public record of the airline getting slots for that. And if the airline did have slots, it would be under a “use it or lose it” clause, so not operating the route would make the airline lose them.

In the spring of 2026, all-business class airline Beond suspended scheduled operations through October 2026. Then the airline announced it would resume flights in December 2026. Now the airline claims it will relaunch flights in January 2027. Over the summer, we learned that the airline wasn’t paying many staff for months, and the airline is now pivoting to operating service via Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea Airport.

We’ll see what happens in the coming weeks. Presumably the Red Sea service gets the airline some money from the Saudi government, but it remains to be seen if that’s enough to keep this operation afloat.

It’s understandable that the current situation is really challenging, between the price of jet fuel, plus overall sentiment about the Middle East. However, I have to imagine the airline didn’t have great financial health before this whole mess started, and suspending flights in some ways only makes things even worse.
