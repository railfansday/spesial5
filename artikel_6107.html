flydubai Cleared To Return To Israel 2 Months After Boeing 737 Hijack Attempt

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Earlier this year, a Tel Aviv-bound flydubai flight was the subject of a hijacking by one of its own pilots. While his efforts were thwarted, with fellow flydubai pilots traveling on board the service able to regain control and divert the jet to Saudi Arabia, it prompted a strong response, with Israeli authorities banning the airline until at least mid-December. Now, however, the carrier has been granted approval to return.

Just under two months after the attempted attack, a delegation of Israeli authority figures traveled to the UAE to meet with their Emirati counterparts and discuss the next steps regarding the resumption of flights between Dubai and Tel Aviv. This was, after all, a route that flydubai had previously served extensively. While its return is subject to clearance, the news marks a strong step forward for flydubai.

According to One Mile At A Time, the Israeli delegation attended presentations by Emirati authorities in Dubai, where the latter party detailed the new steps that it had put into action following the attempted hijacking in order to improve aviation security. While the finer details have not yet been made public, the presentation was compelling enough for Transportation Minister Miri Regev to take action.

Specifically, he and his ministry have now approved the resumption of flydubai flights between the United Arab Emirates and Israel, having previously barred the carrier until at least December 15, 2026. Israel's Transportation Ministry said that this was "subject to clearance from security authorities," and "a significant step toward restoring air connectivity between Israel and the UAE." flydubai told Simple Flying that:

"We continue to work closely with the relevant authorities and look forward to resuming our flights between Dubai and Tel Aviv. Updates will be communicated on our official channels."

Should flydubai's flights to Tel Aviv resume in due course, this will be a key step forward for the airline as it regains access to what used to be one of its top destinations. According to The Times of Israel, the carrier used to operate as many as ten flights a day in each direction between Tel Aviv and Dubai. These served both point-to-point traffic and connecting itineraries serving destinations further afield in Asia.

Of course, with Israel and the UAE both being sizeable aviation markets, flydubai is not the only carrier that had previously had a strong presence on routes between the two countries. For instance, The Times of Israel notes that "security issues have also delayed Israeli airlines El Al, Arkia, and Israir from resuming flights to the UAE," leaving Etihad as the only airline currently linking the two countries.

In the wake of the attack, Israel imposed stricter screening procedures regarding the crews operating flights that either serve destinations in the country or traverse its airspace. Elsewhere, The Times of Israel notes that UAE flag carrier Emirates has yet to resume its own flights from Dubai to Tel Aviv since Emirati authorities halted them in February after US and Israeli forces began their attacks on Iran.

It was just under two months ago that the events of flydubai flight FZ1073 made headlines all around the world. While flying from Dubai International Airport (DXB) in the UAE to Tel Aviv Ben Gurion (TLV) in Israel, the flight's First Officer attacked the Captain with an axe and attempted to crash the plane, which descended sharply. After passengers fought off the attacker, deadheading pilots regained control.

The aircraft involved, a Boeing 737 MAX 8, descended more than 14,000 feet in under 30 seconds, prompting US President Donald Trump to praise the American planemaker on account of the jet having withstood forces "far beyond" its design. After diverting to Prince Sultan bin Abdulaziz Airport (TUU) in Saudi Arabia, the First Officer was taken into custody, with investigations revealing the full extent of his plans.
