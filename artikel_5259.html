Ethereum’s quantum-safe upgrade may have found a faster route forward, but the technical work isn’t finished.

Vitalik Buterin has shifted his support toward leanPCS under EIP-8288, arguing it could deliver much of leanSTARK’s flexibility without forcing developers to standardize a new instruction-set architecture first.

That’s a significant change in direction. It isn’t a completed upgrade, though.

EIP-8288 aims to reduce the amount of cryptographic data Ethereum must carry on-chain. Instead of publishing every signature or proof, nodes aggregate verification results into STARK proofs, which can then be aggregated further.

The chain ultimately receives compact claims and a master proof, rather than every underlying cryptographic object.

The motivation is becoming harder to ignore. Current 65-byte ECDSA signatures could grow to 1–6 kilobytes under proposed quantum-safe schemes.

Meanwhile, zero-knowledge proofs could expand from roughly 200–1,000 bytes to around 128 kilobytes when using STARKs. Putting all that data on-chain isn’t a practical scaling strategy.

Buterin’s latest explanation separates three approaches. leanSPHINCS supports a specific hash-based signature scheme, offering quantum-safe accounts with a proposed cost of 4,096 gas for 64 bytes.

leanSTARK is more flexible. Users could prove almost any computation, but Ethereum would first need to agree on a programming language or instruction-set architecture. That’s a difficult standard to lock in.

leanPCS offers a middle path. A polynomial commitment scheme proves a statement about a polynomial, and Buterin estimates STARK verification through this mechanism could cost around 20,000–40,000 gas.

It may require more claims on-chain than leanSTARK. In exchange, developers retain greater flexibility over off-chain proof systems.

The published EIP-8288 draft currently specifies leanSPHINCS and reserves leanSTARK, while leanPCS isn’t yet included as a verification mode. Its reference implementation remains listed as TBD, and no activation date is confirmed.

That gap matters. An endorsement isn’t a specification, and a specification isn’t a working network upgrade.

For now, Ethereum’s quantum-safe upgrade has a potentially faster technical direction, but leanPCS still needs formal integration, implementation and approval before it can become part of the network.
