Nvidia Stock Falls 5.6% as Board Investigates CEO Jensen Huang Over China Spy Allegations

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Home » Crypto Market News » Nvidia Stock Falls 5.6% as Board Investigates CEO Jensen Huang Over China Spy Allegations

Nvidia’s independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang has raised leadership concerns, putting additional pressure on NVDA shares.

Nvidia (NVDA) shares fell approximately 5.6 % in after-hours trading on Monday, November 9, after the company’s board announced an independent investigation into reports that CEO Jensen Huang had been targeted in a foreign intelligence operation involving China.

The investigation has raised questions about executive oversight and potential leadership uncertainty at the world’s leading AI chipmaker

Nvidia’s board announced the investigation on Sunday, following reports that its founder and CEO Huang had been targeted in a foreign intelligence effort.

A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the investigation.

The board emphasized that U.S. national and economic security remain priorities. However, it stated that it currently has no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia’s confidential information was compromised.

Huang supports the investigation and has committed to cooperating fully. The board also expressed confidence in his leadership team and promised further updates when appropriate.

The controversy follows a New York Times report published in October concerning Alice Huang, a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed, a company that purchases AI chips from Nvidia.

According to the reporting, U.S. intelligence agencies believe Alice Huang was an asset of China’s Ministry of State Security, the country’s principal intelligence agency.

U.S. authorities reportedly investigated allegations that she helped illegally divert advanced AI chips to China. The report also described a close relationship between Alice Huang and Jensen Huang.

The NYT also claimed U.S. intelligence agencies discovered last year that she illegally smuggled advanced AI chips into China while developing a “close relationship” with Huang.

Jensen Huang acknowledged in a November 8 message to Nvidia employees that he had been unfaithful to his wife and apologized for hurting her and their children, though he did not name the woman involved. “There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry,” he wrote.

Nvidia-linked perpetual futures on Hyperliquid and after-hours trading on the Blue Ocean system traded around $176.13 on Monday, approximately 5.6% below Friday’s Nasdaq closing price of $186.64.

Nvidia shares had gained 3.91% on Friday, November 6. The company’s market capitalization stood at approximately $4.5 trillion, with Apple ahead of Nvidia in market value, according to the supplied market data.

Gary Black, managing partner at The Future Fund, which holds NVDA as one of its largest long positions, expressed concerns about Huang’s ability to remain CEO.

In a November 8 post on X, Black argued that the board could question Huang’s judgment and conduct even without evidence of criminal wrongdoing. He also highlighted the absence of an obvious internal successor.

Black expects portfolio managers to reduce their Nvidia exposure in the coming weeks because of uncertainty surrounding potential succession. However, he does not anticipate long-term damage to the company’s underlying value.

In a follow-up post, Black said he hoped his assessment was wrong, describing Huang as one of the greatest CEOs of the current era.

Nvidia remains a central player in the global AI infrastructure market, making uncertainty surrounding its leadership relevant to technology stocks and broader risk sentiment. Since AI-related companies also influence investor appetite across crypto markets, sustained weakness in Nvidia could add pressure to AI-linked digital assets.
