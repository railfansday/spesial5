Same Price, Different Stellar: 69% Growth Outruns XLM On a Red-Day

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Home » Altcoins » Same Price, Different Stellar: 69% Growth Outruns XLM On a Red-Day

Stellar continues to add regulated issuers, infrastructure partners, and measurable on-chain volume.

Stellar keeps stacking real-world asset activity and on-chain usage even as its token trades near multi-month lows.

On a day when the broader crypto market shed roughly $120 billion and XLM dropped about 9%, the network still ranked first among tracked chains in tokenized fund growth, adding $291 million.

The $291 million single-day print was not an isolated event.

Over the past ten days the network has added several institutional markers: Janus Henderson launched the first Nasdaq-licensed tokenized fund on Stellar, DTCC went live with Stellar as the first public chain in its setup.

MetaMask began giving every account a Stellar address, Securitize signaled plans to expand, Bell went live on testnet, and Franklin Templeton’s presence on the network reached $1.1 billion.

These moves sit on top of longer-term figures. Stellar has repeatedly led or ranked near the top in tokenized fund inflows across 24-hour, 90-day, and year-to-date windows, even while XLM itself has lagged the network’s asset growth.

Separately, Stellar’s decentralized exchange volume rose 69% over the past week. Nearly the entire increase came from Aquarius, the network’s leading liquidity management protocol, according to DeFiLlama data.

Aquarius has been the dominant DEX by both volume and transactions for some time. Its 30-day volume recently sat near $95 million with cumulative volume above $800 million, and it has held the top spot by total value locked among Stellar DEXs.

The rest of the chain’s DeFi activity remains concentrated as well: a handful of protocols account for most of the locked value and trading.

The volume jump is real, yet total value locked on Aquarius has stayed relatively flat even during stronger trading months. That pattern suggests more activity is flowing through existing pools rather than a broad influx of new capital.

XLM is trading back near levels seen before some of the recent institutional announcements.

The gap between network metrics and token price has drawn direct questions from observers: who is required to buy more XLM as tokenized assets and DEX volume grow, and what mechanism turns that activity into sustained demand for the native token?

So far, Stellar’s progress in regulated funds and concentrated DeFi has not produced a matching lift in XLM.

Institutional products on the network often settle in other assets, and much of the on-chain trading runs through a small set of protocols whose fees and incentives do not automatically translate into broad XLM buying.
