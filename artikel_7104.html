Wood, Glass, Porcelain, and Beyond: Jewelry’s Alternative Material Crush

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Glass and wood are eclipsing metal for quirky cool jewelry.

Every item on this page was chosen by an ELLE editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

One of the greatest things about the best kinds of jewelry is that it has no limits. Unlike clothing, it can pretty much be made out of anything, so long as it’s durable. Glass, in jewelry, dates as far back as around 3500 BCE in ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt, via beads and carvings. Yet all these years later, designers are revisiting the humble material, along with wood, porcelain, and beyond. It makes sense: We’re definitely in an era of experimental jewelry, and materials that push beyond the typical precious metals and gemstones allow for bolder shapes and silhouettes, with major impact. Enter: wooden pendants, glass rings, and porcelain chains.

On the runways recently, we’ve seen Chanel and Dries Van Noten toy with glass necklaces, Margiela play with porcelain (in the form of an ASMR-worthy dress that was an undercover nod to the house’s famous ’90s vest made of the material) as well as multilayered tidbits in standout pieces like Celine’s charm necklaces.

Likewise, fine jewelers are integrating various materials into their work. Marla Aaron, for instance, reimagined her Lock connector pendant in an official collaboration with Nymphenburg porcelain. Marie Lichtenberg makes beautiful dice pendants rendered in black ebony and diamonds. Anna Maccieri Rossi recently released an entire collection dedicated to wood, including chunky necklaces and carved bangles set with gemstones. “I love wood because it is a living material, warm and natural,” she said of the collection. “This material offers me the chance to dream and work on a large scale, with a bolder approach.”

The cost of gold rapidly ascended in 2025, reaching an all-time nominal record high of $5,589.38 per troy ounce in January 2026. Driven by historic market shifts and a broader surge in precious metal prices, this sharp economic rise has dramatically reshaped how fashion insiders, collectors, and everyday jewelry lovers approach their collections. As traditional solid gold pieces become increasingly rare financial splurges, buyers are actively diversifying their wardrobes with creative, tactile alternatives. In addition to gravitating toward vintage sterling silver and archival heirlooms, jewelry aficionados are now getting their fix with these alternative materials—ranging from hand-carved wood and delicate hand-blown glass to sculptural porcelain and unconventional metals.

Some designers, too, are looking to build and reinvent the legacy of those who came before them. “I think when most people hear ‘glass jewelry,’ a pretty specific image comes to mind,” Colin Keane Lynch of Keane New York tells ELLE. “I’m interested in building on that history while exploring some of the less familiar possibilities.” Lynch, who has devoted his life to working with glass, designs sculptural glass petal rings and sinuous cuffs, all made to order. “Another thing I love about glass is its ability to take on the appearance of so many different materials through explorations of form, texture, and color. It also allows me to create things that might be difficult or even impossible to achieve in other materials.”

Much like the language and energy of crystals and gemstones, others are interested in these materials because of what they represent. Take Thatch, for instance. Founded by Amy Goin and Laura Laing, the brand sells huge wooden heart pendants as well as fish-shaped wooden bangles and charms. “We feel that wood holds energy the same way gemstones do,” says Goin. “A different kind of energy. Something quieter and more grounded. It’s soulful and raw. We love the bohemian beauty of wood, paired with a touch of gold and sparkle.”

For today’s designers, the most precious thing about jewelry is the possibility.
