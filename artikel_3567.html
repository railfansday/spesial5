The Head Of GTA 6’s Parent Company Helped Make One Of The Best Christmas Movies Of All Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

One of the most memorable and beloved Christmas movies is 1990's Home Alone, but it almost didn't happen, and the head of GTA 6's parent company played a role in bringing the movie to market.

Strauss Zelnick, the current CEO of Rockstar Games owner Take-Two, was the president and COO of 20th Century Fox at the time--he was among the youngest-ever Hollywood CEOs at the age of 32.

Warner Bros. was originally backing Home Alone and had actually begun production on the movie until budget concerns prompted the studio to pull the plug. "Four days into production, they shut down the picture and canceled it. That never happens," Zelnick told David Rubenstein.

Fox, under the leadership of chairman Joe Roth and Zelnick as president, swooped in and gave the filmmakers--including director Christopher Columbus and writer John Hughes--the money to make Home Alone.

That turned out to be a fantastic investment for Fox, as Home Alone ranked as one of the top five highest-grossing movies of the year in the US. It grossed more than $476 million globally against a $13 million budget, and it picked up two Oscar nominations. Home Alone also helped launch the career of Macaulay Culkin, and his role of Kevin remains his most memorable part.

So why didn't Zelnick keep making movies?

Zelnick was on top of the world as a film executive after Home Alone. However, he said he saw a bright future in the interactive entertainment business, so he left Fox in 1993 to join Crystal Dynamics where he served as president and CEO.

"I left Fox voluntarily, which no one ever does, to move to Silicon Valley, take a 95% pay cut and become the pre-revenue CEO of a little tiny video game company called Crystal Dynamics in 1993," he said in the Rubenstein interview.

After Crystal Dynamics, Zelnick shifted to the music business in the role of CEO as BMG Entertainment from 1994 to 2000. During that time, BMG started a gaming unit, BMG Interactive, which published the original GTA in 1997.

After that, Zelnick and an investor group took over Take-Two, which was in a tough financial spot at the time and was sued by the US government for accounting fraud.

Since the takeover, Take-Two has thrived. Its share price exploded from about $10 in 2007 to more than $220 today, and Take-Two today has a market cap of over $40 billion. The GTA franchise is a juggernaut at Take-Two, but it actually only accounts for 15% of the company's revenue in a non-release quarter. Take-Two today owns and operates a number of big non-GTA franchises, including NBA 2K, Borderlands, Mafia, BioShock, Civilization, and more.

GTA 6 launches on November 19 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. It's reportedly already sold close to 15 million copies and generated nearly $1.5 billion in revenue.
