Mini Review: KuloNiku: Bowl Up! (Switch 2) - A Satisfying, Tactile Cooking Sim

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

I have a real soft spot for games that simulate cooking. A strange thing given my aversion to crafting mechanics, but cooking? I could do that all day.

KuloNiku: Bowl Up! Is right up my alley, then. Developed by Gamebir Studio, it sees you take on the role of a new meatball restaurant owner. Your job is to pay close attention to your customers’ orders to ensure maximum satisfaction, thus increasing your reputation and revenue.

Your workstation is separated into sections, and you’ll need to tab between them to complete your order quickly and accurately. Someone orders a Classic Bowl with extra chilli? You’ll need to grab your bowl, go straight to the cooking pan, add three meatballs and one noodle nest, then tab over to the chopping board, grab your chilli and finely slice it. Once that’s added, tab back to your pan, chuck the meatballs and noodles into your bowl, add a ladle of broth, then go to your seasoning station. Add a blob of sweet soy sauce and your cutlery, then serve.

If you nail your customer’s order, they’ll shower you with praise and chuck a few coins into your tip jar. Get something wrong and they’ll let you know, but there generally isn’t any major penalty. If you really mess up, your customer may ask for a full refund, lowering your reputation.

As you complete orders and memorise your recipes, you’ll complete tasks a lot faster. It’s such a tactile experience, too. Using an on-screen cursor, you have to manually select each ingredient while having direct control over your knife when slicing through veg. It serves as the perfect game for mouse controls on Switch 2, giving accuracy that feels otherwise limited via analogue controls. In handheld, the touchscreen can be used to cycle through dialogue but not for cooking.

In addition to cooking, you can decorate your restaurant, visit the shop to purchase new ingredients and items, and nurture your relationships with NPCs during your time off. This usually involves visiting new areas of the neighbourhood and engaging in dialogue. Each area is a static background, so don’t go expecting to wander around; the focus will remain on your conversations.

Finally, you have Meatball Brawls, or ‘Meatbrawls’. These are scheduled competitions in which you showcase your cooking skills to be judged against an opponent. You’ll need to be on your toes with this, as the judges and audience throw in fresh requests in the heat of battle, forcing you to reconsider your flow to accommodate new ingredients. Win the brawl, and you’ll gain an even stronger reputation.

Overall, I found all of the activities outside of the kitchen to be fun enough, but I was constantly eager to get back to cooking. If the game is missing a key ingredient, it’s a mode in which you just cook recipes over and over. The main campaign limits you to just three customers per in-game day, which is a shame. Otherwise, it’s a great time for budding restaurateurs.
