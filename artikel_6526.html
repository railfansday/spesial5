37 Thanksgiving Cocktails and Drinks for Every Guest

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Whether you’re pouring a cup of cheer or drinking to steel your nerves against Great-Aunt Edna, these Thanksgiving cocktails cover every angle. There are the cranberry, apple, and maple flavors we crave this time of year, plus big-batch drinks that won’t keep you playing bartender all afternoon. We’re talking freezer-ready martinis, bourbon punch, and a cranberry mule that guests can spike themselves. After all, your to-do list is long enough.

And since Thanksgiving is something of a marathon, we’ve included lighter aperitifs and nonalcoholic drinks for anyone looking to pace themselves—or skip the booze altogether. When dinner gives way to dessert, warm up with a hot buttered rum, mulled wine, or spiced apple cider. Just don’t forget to set out a few Thanksgiving appetizers while you’re at it.

We love a Paper Plane as much as the next discerning cocktail drinker, but shaking them up one at a time? Hard pass. Batch the bourbon, Aperol, and amaro for this Thanksgiving cocktail ahead, then stir in the lemon juice and water about an hour before serving.

The “upside-down” refers to the inverted ratio of gin and vermouth in these martinis (not what they’ll do to you). The result is a lighter-drinking cocktail you can batch ahead and stash in the freezer until guests arrive.

All the steeped, spiced flavors you love in a hot toddy, with none of the warmth. This icy twist keeps pre-dinner cocktail hour refreshingly chill. Relatives, take note.

Apple cider, tart cranberry juice, and spicy ginger ale give rum punch a seasonal makeover. Make the cranberry-studded ice cubes ahead of time; they’ll keep the punch cold and make each glass look party-ready.

Sure, a cold beer works fine while you're watching football or tending the turkey fryer. But a pitcher of crisp Mexican lager punched up with margarita citrus makes the occasion feel a little more festive. For another low-effort beer cocktail, try a Spaghett—just lager, Aperol, and lemon.

For a big-batch cocktail with a distinctly autumnal spin, combine rum with dry hard cider and fruit. Prepare the rum base ahead, but save the bubbly cider until just before serving to preserve its fizz.

Bourbon, cognac, floral Earl Grey tea, and an herb-honey syrup make a party-ready punch. For an eye-catching presentation, add an ice ring decorated with lemon slices and rosemary sprigs.

Mezcal, orange liqueur, honey, and chocolate bitters make a lightly smoky cocktail that's particularly good with dessert. Even better, you can batch eight servings and stash them in the freezer up to two weeks ahead. No last-minute shaking required.

Feeling a little gung-ho about Christmas’s fast approach, are we? A big-batch punch packed with cranberry, citrus, nutty sherry, and warming spices makes a delicious bridge between Thanksgiving and the winter holidays.

A seasonal riff on the French 75 swaps gin for bourbon and adds a homemade pear syrup spiced with cinnamon, ginger, and cloves. We like it for a sparkling toast before dinner or alongside pie afterward.

The season of Hallmark’s flannel-clad himbos calls for a drink to match. Enter this lower-ABV sipper made with apple cider and amaro. A little bitter, a little sweet, and just fizzy enough—rather like the male lead in whatever movie “your aunt” is watching.

Homemade cranberry-thyme syrup gives this sparkling cocktail its ruby-red glow. A little Campari adds a pleasantly bitter edge, while Champagne makes it worthy of a holiday toast. The syrup can be prepared up to two weeks ahead.

If you want to start Thanksgiving with a little bubbly, this three-ingredient, lower-ABV aperitif is a fine place to begin. The recipe serves four, and the ginger syrup can be made well in advance.

Replace the gin in a classic Negroni with sparkling wine and you’ve got a lighter, fizzy aperitif. The bubbles mellow Campari’s bitter edge, making this a friendly introduction for the Negroni-curious.

Two ingredients, one very good drink. Fino sherry and ginger ale make a lightly boozy, pleasantly dry cocktail with just enough ginger bite.

Botanical gin, bittersweet grapefruit, and sparkling sake make a bubbly cocktail that’s bright enough to cut through a rich Thanksgiving spread. It serves six, so you can pour a round without mixing drinks one by one.

We love a margarita riff no matter the season. Ginger and rosemary bring a distinctly autumnal edge to this fizzy version, with just enough sparkle to make it feel like a holiday toast.

No punch bowls, cocktail shakers, or complicated mixology required for this two-ingredient drink. You’ve got better things to do between hosting duties and getting all those Thanksgiving sides to the table.

A lower-ABV sipper is your friend during a long day of polite frivolities. The Adonis sounds hella sexy, and we love the earthy nuttiness of sherry and sweet vermouth, made spritzy with club soda.

On a holiday where cranberries overflow-eth, pour a drink to match. Tart, citrusy, and just sweet enough, this vodka cocktail gets its signature pink hue from cranberry juice. A bright counterpoint to all that rich Thanksgiving food.

Think of this as an apple-brandy riff on the Manhattan. Sweet vermouth rounds out the fruit’s warmth, making this a particularly fitting fall cocktail.

Not quite a whiskey sour, not quite a cider cocktail, this autumnal drink mixes apple brandy with lemon and orange juices, maple syrup, and a dusting of nutmeg. It tastes like fall without leaning too heavily on baking spices.

Impress your whiskey-loving dad with a Prohibition-era cocktail he may not have heard of. Juicy grapefruit and floral honey give bourbon a bright, citrusy lift, just as winter citrus season is getting underway.

Bourbon, lemon juice, and simple syrup make a tart, boozy drink that’s just sweet enough. It’s ideal for anyone with a minimally stocked bar and a preference for the classics.

Think of the Boulevardier as the Negroni’s bourbon-loving cousin. Whiskey takes the place of gin, bringing warmth and richness to the bittersweet combination of Campari and sweet vermouth. A fine choice for sipping before dinner.

A smoky mezcal cocktail with Earl Grey tea syrup and grapefruit liqueur, this bright, aromatic margarita riff is an intriguing choice for Thanksgiving brunch—or for guests who prefer tequila to bourbon at cocktail hour.

A homemade cranberry-orange jam gives this tequila cocktail a distinctly Thanksgiving twist. Lime juice keeps it tart, while a sugar-salt-and-five-spice rim adds a fragrant finish. Make the jam ahead to streamline cocktail hour.

Whether for your Thanksgiving pregame or tomorrow morning’s hair of the dog, a well-made Bloody Mary is always welcome. Worcestershire, horseradish, hot sauce, and fresh lemon give this savory classic its signature kick.

A packed to-do list is no match for this make-ahead drink. Simmer red wine, cider, fruit, and spices ahead of time, then add tawny port and gently reheat when your guests arrive. Keep it warm in a slow cooker for self-service away from the kitchen.

We’d welcome a pot of mulled apple cider at any fall gathering. It’s nonalcoholic, serves a crowd, and fills the kitchen with the scent of warming spices. Guests can add a tipple of brandy, bourbon, Calvados, or rum if they wish.

Can’t decide between hot cider and spice-rich chai? Have both. This cozy, nonalcoholic drink brings tea and warming spices to apple cider, making it a particularly good companion for a second slice of pie.

For those who’d rather drink their Thanksgiving dessert, a warm mug of dark rum, butter, sugar, and spices delivers the goods. It’s rich, fragrant, and an excellent reason to linger at the table after the pie plates are cleared.

Blood orange, ginger, cinnamon, and cloves make this sweet-tart, nonalcoholic drink feel right at home on Thanksgiving. Prepare the shrub ahead, then top with sparkling water when guests arrive.

Who says a nonalcoholic spritz can’t have a little attitude? A bittersweet alcohol-free aperitif and a splash of olive brine give this savory, citrusy drink plenty of complexity. Serve it very cold, with an olive garnish.

Green tea, lemon juice, fresh mint, and honey make a soothing tonic after the Thanksgiving bacchanal. Alcohol-free on its own, it can take a splash of mezcal if anyone wants to steer it into cocktail territory.

Spiced maple syrup and cranberry juice reimagine the typical Moscow mule as a Thanksgiving drink that skips the vodka. Note that Angostura bitters contain alcohol; for a completely alcohol-free version, use an alternative like All the Bitter.
