How Brandon Williams’ expected return can energize Stephen Curry-less Warriors offense

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

How Brandon Williams' expected return can energize Stephen Curry-less Warriors offense appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

SAN FRANCISCO – To say that the Golden State Warriors' offense has been wayward with Stephen Curry sidelined due to his oblique tear is an understatement. In their last six games, the Dubs rank dead last in the league in offensive rating (99.0), field goal percentage (38.5%), and 3-point percentage (28.4%), cracking the 100-point barrier just once in that stretch.

But with sparkplug point guard Brandon Williams on track to return to the lineup Wednesday versus the Sacramento Kings after exiting in the first quarter of the Warriors' 138-87 victory over the New Orleans Pelicans on November 9, the 27-year-old microwave scorer will provide some much-needed juice and speed to an offense in sore need of another ballhandler.

"He practiced today. He's still day-to-day, but we're hopeful that he'll play," head coach Steve Kerr said Monday after the Warriors' morning practice.

"It's great to have him just because of his ability to put pressure on the rim and just the energy that Brandon brings," veteran center Al Horford said of Williams' impending return to the lineup.

Though Williams was initially viewed as a solid add to the Dubs' point guard depth, signing a one-year, $2.62-million veteran minimum deal this past offseason, his change-of-pace, lightning-quick on-ball skills had become an important part of how Golden State's offense functioned in the non-Curry minutes, minutes that typically dictate whether the Warriors can be serious contenders in the Western Conference arms race.

In the somewhat limited 158 minutes Williams has been on the floor, Golden State has scored an additional +12.1 points per 100 possessions, which ranks in the 94th percentile among all players in the league and ties Curry for most on the team, according to Cleaning the Glass.

Additionally, there's been very little drop-off from Curry to Williams. With Kerr staggering Curry and Williams to ensure at least one of them is on the floor at all times, the Warriors only suffer a 3.5 point/100 poss. drop-off, as Curry's +13.9 net rating to Williams' +10.4 net rating remains well into the positives according to Databallr.

And perhaps the most eye-opening advanced metric is how the Warriors are a -29.1 net rating when neither Curry or Williams are on the floor. They are -17.2 points/100 poss. worse offensively with neither of them on the floor (97.2) and somehow surrender 11.7 more points on defense (126.3) despite neither being known for their defensive prowess.

"Him going out at the same time as Steph was a big blow," Kerr acknowledged. "Another reason for kind of this offensive disruption that we've had."

Given his history with hamstring injuries, the Warriors may opt to ease Williams back into the lineup so as not to re-aggravate that kind of sensitive injury. But the second he steps on the court, Golden State will need Williams' speed and on-ball creation the moment he returns to the lineup, two things they've been sorely lacking in those areas and can speak to the lack of rhythm the offense has had as of late.

"Biggest thing is the rhythm of the offense," Kerr said, addressing the offensive woes as a whole. "We've lost our rhythm here on the road trip. And last night was a little clunky, but we're getting there. We're making some strides, and it's a huge adjustment when Steph goes out. It changes everything, and so we're making some adjustments on the fly. And I think we're going to be much better."

Even just the simple fact that Williams is a point guard and has tangible on-ball skills will settle the clunkiness of the Warriors offense. They've been forced to funnel the offense through Yaxel Lendeborg and Brandin Podziemski, and while each has made strides with that enormous responsibility, it's neither's forte.

Which is why the offense has looked so passive and destitute– Golden State doesn't have a true hub to dictate the flow of the game and pressure opposing defenses. In fact, it's been the opposite: opponents have realized they can press the hell out of the Warriors because they don't have players comfortable with their handle. But that style won't fly against Williams.

"On the road trip we got bothered by ball pressure by a couple of the teams. They took us out in San Antonio, Dallas, Memphis in particular," Kerr explained. "Those last few games, ball pressure really hurt us, kind of put us on our heels. And you can't do that with Brandon; he'll just blow right by you."

The Warriors have an ounce of momentum following their narrow victory over the Boston Celtics on Monday. They're hoping Williams can carry it forward.
