‘It’s wrong’ – Sheffield Wednesday and Barnsley slammed for ticket situation

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

This article is part of Football League World's 'Terrace Talk' series, which provides personal opinions from our FLW Fan Pundits regarding the latest breaking news, teams, players, managers, potential signings and more…

By the time Sheffield Wednesday travel to Oakwell to face Barnsley on the 20th November, the Owls squad will be hopeful of finding their feet under the guidance of new boss Dave Challinor.

After parting ways with Henrik Pedersen, the Owls moved swiftly to bring the former Stockport County boss to Hillsborough, who took the Hatters from the National League into the third tier in just five seasons.

The 51-year-old will be keen to have further success in the Steel City, taking on massive expectations and pressure that comes with leading such a huge club in English football.

But his reign got off to a very poor start last weekend as they were one of the first upsets in the FA Cup, with Wednesday dumped out of the competition at the first hurdle by losing 2-1 away at League Two outfit Oldham Athletic.

The Owls return to cup action on Wednesday evening by hosting Accrington Stanley in the group stage of the EFL Trophy, while they have another home game this coming weekend in League One as they clash with promotion rivals Plymouth Argyle.

After a meeting with the Pilgrims, it leads into a Yorkshire derby away at Barnsley, with Friday night football originally expecting to host a raucous Oakwell crowd.

However, there are fears now that Oakwell may not be as full and lively due to the club's ticket prices for both home and away supporters.

Barnsley v Sheffield Wednesday is the Friday night football on the 20th November, with Oakwell hosting a Yorkshire derby.

But there’s been criticism aimed at Barnsley for their ticket pricing for the fixture for both home and away fans.

Wednesday announced last week that the Tykes are charging £35 for an adult ticket, with the Owls handed an allocation of 4,810.

Plenty of supporters are bound to be viewing this as extortionate, particularly when the game is being broadcast live on Sky Sports.

Sheffield Wednesday were defeated by League Two opposition in Dave Challinor's first game as manager

Along with the adult ticket pricing, seniors (65+) are charged at £25, young adults (19-21) are also set at £25, while under-19s are £16 and under-14s are set at £10.

So this in mind, FLW’s Sheffield Wednesday fan pundit has given his thoughts over Barnsley’s decision to charge adults £35 a ticket.

Speaking to Football League World, Sheffield Wednesday fan pundit Patrick McKenna has blasted Barnsley for charging £35 per adult ticket for the clash against the Owls, labelling it as ‘wrong’ and a problem in football that keeps on rearing its ugly head.

Patrick told FLW: “To be fair, as Sheffield Wednesday fans, we really can’t say much about club’s charging away fans too much.

“Throughout the Chansiri era, we consistently charged high prices, and away fans had to pay them.

“Now it’s wrong, we charged too much, and it’s wrong when Barnsley are doing this. You see across football high ticket prices, which can’t be justified.

“Unfortunately, as fans, there’s not much we can do. All we can really do is pay the ticket or not go, but we can’t really, as a fanbase, criticise because there would be that hypocrisy on our part.

“Talk to any Wednesday or Barnsley fan, and we’d both be hoping for lower prices, so yeah, it’s disappointing that this sort of thing is still happening in football, but it has done for a long time and, unfortunately, it’s not something that’s going to go away.”

A massive problem with football throughout the country is pricing, with tickets getting more and more unaffordable as the cost of living gets tougher and tougher.

And Barnsley are offering little help to either their own or Sheffield Wednesday supporters through their latest price sting, with some simply unable to pay such hefty prices for a League One standard football match.

In what should have been a fiery cauldron of noise given it’s a local derby, there will be collectively fewer supporters at Oakwell on that Friday evening, with some electing to watch it on TV.

Because of this, supporters from both sides are being driven away from a game that has the potential to turn both Barnsley and Sheffield Wednesday’s seasons around, with the two sides currently enduring slumps that could be snapped out of through a derby day victory.

Unfortunately, these latest ticket hikes in the third tier reinforce the sad state that football is in at the minute, with clubs failing in their duties to make football an affordable escape at any professional level of the game.
