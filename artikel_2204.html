Scott Bain on Scotland No.1 hopes, beating Celtic and his love for Falkirk

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Falkirk's stunning 2-0 win over Celtic was built on discipline, resilience and a goalkeeping performance that Scott Bain admits ranks among the very best of his career.

The former Celtic keeper was in inspired form against his old club, producing a string of saves as John McGlynn's side claimed a famous victory over the Premiership champions. The result stretched Falkirk's winning run to four games and delivered a third consecutive clean sheet, with Bain at the heart of it all.

Having spent seven years at Celtic, where he was often a spectator for long spells, the 34-year-old relished being called into action and believed the display showcased the best of his shot-stopping ability.

"It was a busy day. It was an amazing result, four wins in a row and three clean sheets in a row. Amazing for the team, amazing for the club and the manager and, for myself, just delighted with the performance.

"We can take so much belief from that game. I really enjoyed the game. I had an early save and then one just before half-time from Duran. When we came out in the second half I thought it was going to be backs against the wall.

"I thought we defended excellently. We put our bodies in front of things, we cleared things and I just felt it was one of those games where they weren't going to score. Thankfully they never.

"When it got to about the 77th minute I thought we were going to be fine. They put us under a lot of pressure and put balls in the box, but I thought our togetherness, our team spirit and also our quality when we won the ball back at times was excellent.

"We outfought them at times and we also outplayed them at times. It was really good."

When asked if it was the finest display of his career, Bain admitted Sunday's performance was certainly in the conversation.

"It was probably the most saves I've made in a game. I had one against Dundee United that was similar this season where we never really managed to hold on.

"But I thought there we looked really comfortable in the end, although I'm having to make these saves.

"When it got down to the nitty-gritty of the last five minutes I thought we looked quite comfortable."

Bain's form has not gone unnoticed. Falkirk supporters repeatedly sang his name during the victory and even hailed him as Scotland's No.1, although the goalkeeper is not interested in making grand claims about displacing those ahead of him in the international pecking order.

The keeper, who has four Scotland caps, hopes his performances can earn him another call-up from Sebastien Pocognoli and admits he thoroughly enjoys being involved with the national squad.

"The fans have been brilliant with me," he said. "When I came here it probably took me a couple of games to get going and they stood by me. I feel really comfortable going into games and I feel like I'm performing really well, so it's nice that they sing that.

"I hope that I'm in the next squad. I really enjoyed working with the manager and seeing his ideas and how he works.

"As long as I go away and put my best foot forward, I'm happy to give my best account of myself out there."

Bain is widely regarded as one of the best ball-playing goalkeepers in Scotland, but against Celtic it was the more traditional side of his game that took centre stage.

"We didn't really play out from the back as much as we normally would, but I think it's a given that people know it's one of my strongest attributes.

"I've got most of the clubs in the bag, but sometimes I slice it off the tee.

"We never really did that. It was more about the nitty-gritty and I managed to make a lot of saves, so it was a good showcase in terms of saves. I'm really enjoying my time here and I feel really comfortable in the goals at the minute."

For now, Bain knows any Scotland ambitions depend on maintaining the level he has reached in recent weeks for a Falkirk side enjoying a strong spell of form.

"It's difficult because with international football you can only do what you can do for your club and hopefully the manager sees that and makes a decision.

"I don't ever go there expecting to play. I go there to represent myself the best I can and I play these games for Falkirk to represent myself the best I can.

"Whether that turns out with the manager giving me some game time when we're away then I'll do my best if I get the opportunity. But mostly, international football for me is about what I do here. Can I put in good performances to be in the conversation? That's really what it's about."
