Raptors Lose 15th Straight To Knicks: 4 Takeaways From Eastern Conference Thriller

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Just two nights removed from their overtime loss on Sunday, the Toronto Raptors had a chance to set things right with a rematch against the New York Knicks. This time, to their credit, the Raptors came out firing with a 29-point first quarter that took the champs by surprise. By the end, however, it was the Knicks that overwhelmed, riding the play of their stars to win their second straight game (111-102) and 15th straight against the Raptors in particular.

As usual, Jalen Brunson led the way for New York. In 34 minutes, he finished with 31 points, five rebounds, five assists, zero steals, and one block on 45.5% shooting from the field (4-11 from three). At shooting guard, Mikal Bridges dropped 17 points, five rebounds, and one assist on 63.6% shooting from the field (2-5 from three), while center Karl-Anthony Towns dropped 16 points, 16 rebounds, eight assists, and one steal on 77.8% shooting from the field (1-2 from three).

For the Raptors, Ja'Kobe Walter led the way with 25 points, one rebound, two assists, and two steals on 60.0% shooting from the field (7-12 from three). Veteran swingman RJ Barrett, meanwhile, contributed 22 points, five rebounds, five assists, zero steals, and one block on 52.9% shooting from the field (2-8 from three). Finally, there's Scottie Barnes: 20 points, four rebounds, two assists, and two blocks on 50.0% shooting (1-2 from three).

Whether it was the support of their home crowd or the desperation of not wanting to lose to the same team in back-to-back games, the Raptors came out with a lot of energy in this game, playing with an almost desperate urgency during the early minutes of the game. Eventually, though, that urgency turned to frustration as the Knicks got increasingly comfortable and expanded their lead down the stretch.

In the grand scheme of things, this game likely won't be remembered for long, but it told us a lot about where these teams are headed going into the holiday season (NBA Christmas and Cup Finals). For the Knicks especially, it's a step in the right direction that could signal an upcoming shift in the hierarchy.

To say Mikal Bridges was struggling this season would be an understatement. He looked borderline unplayable at times, and head coach Mike Brown was facing growing calls to bench him. He started showing signs of life against the Pacers on Friday (18 points in 28 minutes), and he showed a familiar spark in today's win, finishing with 17 points, five rebounds, and one assist on 63.6% shooting from the field and 40.0% shooting from three. Bridges held a steady rhythm all game, and that's when the Knicks are at their best.

Of course, even when Bridges was at his worst offensively, he still made a positive impact on defense. Tonight, that defensive touch was massive in containing the Raptors' shooting and regularly contesting their attempts. Bridges was active on this end throughout the game, making that quiet impact even when he wasn't scoring.

It's unclear what had Bridges in such a funk to begin with, but everything is easier for the Knicks when he's making shots. By easing pressure off Brunson, spacing the floor, and punishing teams for leaving him open, it answers so many problems, and we saw how it worked out for them against the Raptors.

If there's one specific time when the Knicks are prone to elevate their game, it's the fourth quarter. Tonight, after stumbling through most of the first half, they sealed the deal with a 31-point fourth quarter that ultimately decided the game. New York's best moments came in the final three minutes, when Brunson single-handedly willed his team to victory after making a four-point play to put them up by four. Even Mikal got in on the action, delivering a dagger three with roughly 45 seconds left.

The Knicks started the season 1-6 in clutch games, but we can assume that was an outlier now. As we were reminded today, this team is loaded with players who are not afraid of the moment. When the pressure was highest, they played with a sense of calm and poise, leading to smart decisions and crisp passes that always found their man. At this point, the Knicks have seen it all together, and they aren't fazed by the pressure.

What makes the Knicks so hard to beat is that they only get better in the clutch, and it's not just Brunson who beats you. Tonight, practically everyone had their moment, highlighting the depth and versatility of the defending champions.

As Kawhi Leonard deals with swelling in his left knee, his status remains a mystery, leaving the Raptors without their best player. They clearly missed him tonight, but how much will his return actually change for this team? Other than Scottie Barnes and RJ Barrett, the Raptors don't have nearly enough depth or talent to keep up with the likes of the 76ers, Celtics, or Pistons in the East. They traded away their best perimeter scorer (Brandon Ingram) and gave up a promising young athlete in Gradey Dick.

What the Raptors really lack is a center. Jakob Poeltl came off the bench tonight in favor of Collin Murray-Boyles, who finished with just eight points, one rebound, three assists, one steal, and three blocks on 66.7% shooting from the field. Sensing their advantage in the frontcourt, the Knicks were feasting inside, going in for easy layups and controlling the glass for a 44-23 rebounding advantage.

While there's no doubt the Raptors will be better off for Kawhi's return, they cannot rely on him to come back and just resolve their issues. To salvage this season, more proactive measures are required, and the options are limitless.

The Knicks' bench was a cause for concern going into the season, and it didn't build confidence when their best bench scorer from last season (Jordan Clarkson) started seeing reduced minutes. Fortunately, veteran Landry Shamet has been filling the role just fine, and he gave the Knicks another fine showing tonight. In 24 minutes off the bench, he put up 12 points, one rebound, and one assist on 57.1% shooting from the field (2-4 from three).

As the Raptors struggled to get anything from their second unit, Landry Shamet carried the Knicks' bench to ensure his team never lost momentum. His versatility let him fit into various lineups, and he made few mistakes while providing a steady boost on the court. So while Shamet may not be the hero Knicks fans were expecting, he's becoming a big storyline for their turnaround this season, and his importance is undeniable.

Ultimately, the Knicks will always lean more on their star-studded starting lineup, but having that boost off the bench makes everything easier. Tonight, we saw the impact Shamet could have (+26) and why we can expect to see more of him over the course of the season.
