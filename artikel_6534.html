31 Pumpkin Purée Recipes From Cake to Pie

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

As soon as fall arrives, canned pumpkin purée earns a permanent spot in the pantry. After all, it’s shelf-stable and can be turned into a trillion recipes, sweet and savory alike. Just be sure you’re using plain canned pumpkin purée, not pumpkin pie filling, which already contains sugar and spices.

Some recipes, like our Pumpkin Spice Crumb Cake, call for just half a can, while others, like Weeknight Pumpkin Chili, use the whole thing. But if you have leftover pumpkin purée hanging out in the fridge, consider it a good thing—it just means another pumpkin recipe is on the horizon. And if you’ve gone the extra mile to make pumpkin purée from fresh pumpkins, you overachiever, check the recipe before swapping it in: Homemade purée can be wetter than canned. So grab a scarf and let’s take a stroll, shall we?

These pumpkin pancakes are tall, light, and warmly spiced with tangy buttermilk and fresh ginger in the batter. Top with maple-syrup-spiked butter and crunchy pecans for a breakfast worth getting out of bed for.

As if pumpkin muffins with a crunchy pepita streusel weren’t enough, these have a hidden surprise: a pocket of sweetened cream cheese. They’re part breakfast, part cheesecake, and entirely worth getting out of bed for.

Tart cranberries punctuate these tender pumpkin scones, while a sprinkle of raw sugar adds a crisp finish. Serve them warm with the accompanying cinnamon-maple butter, which you can make up to four days ahead.

Part pancake, part custard, this Dutch baby comes with a bonus pumpkin purée recipe: ¼ cup goes into the batter, and the rest of the can becomes a silky, spiced pumpkin butter to spread on top. Leftover butter is excellent on toast.

Fresh ginger gives this super-moist pumpkin bread a little kick, while pumpkin seeds add crunch. The secret to its tender crumb? Extra-virgin olive oil. And don’t skip the salted maple butter for slathering on every slice.

Sweet, mellow pumpkin and sharp, salty cheddar are a perfect match in this hearty strata. Assemble it the day before to let the bread soak up the savory custard, then bake until golden and crisp on top.

A pumpkin spice latte you don’t need to leave the house for. Unlike many café versions, this one actually contains pumpkin purée. Don’t have an espresso machine? Swap in strong coffee instead.

This tender, deeply spiced pumpkin cake comes together without a stand mixer and gets a generous swoop of tangy cream cheese frosting. A drizzle of maple syrup is optional but highly encouraged.

This plush chocolate-pumpkin cake draws inspiration from Mexican chocolate, with cinnamon and a pinch of cayenne for warmth. The pumpkin glaze doubles down on the seasonal flavor.

With nearly as much brown sugar streusel as plush pumpkin cake, this New York–style crumb cake takes its topping seriously. If there is a more comforting afternoon snack, we need not know it.

Cinnamon-spiced pumpkin cake wraps around walnut-studded cream cheese frosting in a pretty spiral. The batter comes together in one bowl, but be sure to roll the cake while it’s still warm to prevent cracks.

Pumpkin and chocolate cake batters combine for a dramatic marbled effect in this striking Bundt cake. A glossy chocolate ganache finishes the job. It looks like a project, but you don’t even need a mixer.

Classic crumb cake meets chewy, bouncy mochi in this pumpkin-spiced mash-up. Sweet rice flour gives the cake its distinctive texture, while a buttery pecan streusel adds crunch. It’s particularly good for sharing at Friendsgiving.

Silky filling, flaky crust, a whisper of spice: This is pumpkin pie done right. And there’s no need to roast and purée a fresh pumpkin—our favorite canned variety delivers the consistency we’re after.

With its deeply browned top and creamy, pumpkin-spiced center, this Basque cheesecake looks impressive but skips the fussy water bath. No crust required, either. A full can of pumpkin purée goes into the filling.

The caramelized notes of bourbon and tang of sour cream balance this classic Gourmet dessert. Chopped pecans give the graham cracker crust a little extra crunch.

Can’t choose between pumpkin pie and cheesecake? These maple-sweetened bars make a pretty convincing argument for having both. Swirls of creamy cheesecake and spiced pumpkin filling make them especially handsome on a dessert platter.

Imagine a pumpkin pie with pecan pie’s best feature—obviously, the candied nut topping. A splash of rye whiskey adds a little malty complexity to this two-for-one Thanksgiving dessert.

This positively fluffy pumpkin pie gets a little nutty depth from tahini, whose toasty, slightly bitter flavor balances the sweet pumpkin filling. Whipped cream keeps things light and airy.

This autumnal riff on Puerto Rican flan de queso gets its exceptionally velvety texture from cream cheese. Pumpkin purée and warm spices bring the seasonal flavor, while a glossy caramel topping makes it hard to resist.

What’s more satisfying than cracking through that caramelized sugar topping? Finding a luscious, pumpkin-spiced custard underneath. This recipe uses an entire can of purée, and you can finish the sugar under the broiler if you don’t own a kitchen torch.

Everything you love about classic tiramisu, with the warming spices of a pumpkin spice latte. Ladyfingers dipped in bourbon-spiked espresso alternate with creamy pumpkin mascarpone filling. Make it ahead and dust with cinnamon and cocoa just before serving.

This streamlined recipe turns three hardworking ingredients—curry paste, coconut milk, and pumpkin purée—into a cozy, flavorful soup in just 30 minutes. Proof that a pantry dinner doesn’t have to feel like a compromise.

A whole can of pumpkin purée becomes a silky pasta sauce with garlic, Parmesan, and a kick of chile. Just the thing for a weeknight when you want something comforting without committing to an afternoon of cooking.

A full can of pumpkin purée becomes the sauce—no simmering required—for these cheesy black bean enchiladas. A quick turn under the broiler melts and browns the cheese; lime and crunchy pepitas finish the job.

A full can of pumpkin purée adds richness, body, and an autumnal hue to this speedy chili without making it taste like pumpkin pie. Ground beef, beans, and savory spices keep the flavors firmly in chili territory.

Adding pumpkin purée to Bolognese might not seem obvious, but it gives the sauce rich body without hours of simmering. Cooking it with tomato paste brings out its nutty, caramelized flavor.

Rolling the filled noodles into spirals adds a whimsical touch and ensures every bite has the perfect balance of spinach-ricotta, tender pasta, and creamy pumpkin sauce.

Canned pumpkin purée gives no-knead focaccia a golden hue, subtle sweetness, and plush crumb. Finish with rosemary and flaky salt (both optional, both recommended), and serve with a dish of olive oil for dipping.
