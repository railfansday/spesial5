Newcastle fans turn on Jaissle as defeat triggers relegation concerns

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Newcastle United did not just lose to Everton, they lost the home crowd, whose enthusiasm to back their team has drained from them at alarming speed.

This was a performance that belonged in the pre-takeover era, those bad old days under Steve McClaren, John Carver and Steve Bruce. It was a hugely satisfying evening for Everton manager David Moyes, who insisted there was “a way back” for Jack Grealish, even though he was once again left at home after he missed training last week without permission.

The atmosphere went flat early and gradually turned into anger and frustration. The crowd initially tried to stay with their team but were repeatedly punched in the face by the grim reality of the spectacle in front of them. They do not like the style of play, they do not like the application of the players and they cannot accept the results. They are fed up with pretending otherwise.

This was another miserable performance from Matthias Jaissle’s side, who did not manage a shot on target until the 86th minute. He has only been in the job three months, but nothing seems to be getting better.

“I have to take the responsibility,” he said. “We did not transfer the things we did in training. Hitting long balls all the time, that was not the plan. We were not brave enough to play short passes.

“As soon as the referee blew his whistle, the plan disappeared. We could feel the anger and the frustration, I felt it on the touchline. We are low on confidence and it shows.”

Newcastle looked listless, clueless and alarmingly devoid of belief in what they were being asked to do. They have the unmistakable whiff of a team in decline.

If they do not start playing a lot better, they will be worried about a relegation battle. It is four defeats in their last five games and the confidence in Jaissle to find solutions is fading. Rather than change what he is asking his team to do, he has doubled down on the formation and tactics.

Concerns have been creeping steadily upwards since August. There is a lack of confidence in the players, and worries about the quality of the eight new signings made over the summer.

In particular, there are serious misgivings about the implementation of the “vertical football” Jaissle has imported to the North East of England from the Austro-German Red Bull coaching school.

A night under the lights at St James’ Park should be a boisterous affair, but things went flat quickly. Newcastle, as they have tended to do this season, resorted to long balls punted forward. More often than not they were poorly directed – only 22 per cent were accurate over 98 minutes.

There was no fluency and no threat. The first groans were heard inside 15 minutes as another pass was hoisted harmlessly into the air. They grew more widespread and more frequent.

Everton, just as they had in their Carabao Cup win last week, looked comfortable. Recognising how easy it was to nullify Newcastle’s game plan, they offered an attacking threat of their own.

A rising shot from Merlin Röhl briefly had goalkeeper Lukas Hornicek worried. A free-kick into the Newcastle box caused panic. James Tarkowski won the first header, but Thierno Barry swung and missed with his left foot.

The groans began to turn into boos and whistles. Jaissle’s side could barely string more than two passes together. It felt precarious.

This is not an easy place to play football when the crowd airs its displeasure. As the first half limped on, there were players in black and white who looked like they could not handle the pressure.

It was a horrible game of football. With the last kick of the half, Everton should have taken the lead, Harrison Armstrong picked out Barry inside the box, but he made a mess of the finish. The referee’s whistle was accompanied by boos and a scream of “sort it out” from behind Jaissle’s technical area.

He did not follow those blunt instructions. Everton took the lead in such simple fashion and all Jaissle and his coaching staff could do was shake their heads at each other. Tyrique George was played in far too easily from a free-kick but his shot should have been saved by Hornicek. It was straight at the Czech, but it went through him and it left Barry with a tap in. Newcastle appealed for both a foul on Fabian Schar and an offside but got neither.
