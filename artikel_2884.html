'That's our dude:' Jalani Culpepper finally unleashed on offense, East View makes playoffs

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

CEDAR PARK - Is there anything Jalani Culpepper can't do?

If Thursday's game is any indication, then the answer is no.

East View running back Jalani Culpepper (8) hurdles Rouse defensive back Miles Burrell (1) to score a touchdown during the game at John Gupton Stadium on Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026 in Cedar Park, Texas. (Aaron E. Martinez/Austin American-Statesman)

Whether it was rushing for 85 yards and a TD, making numerous tackles or blocking a punt, Culpepper shone in every phase as the Patriots clinched a playoff spot with a 31-0 win over Rouse at Gupton Stadium in what was a play-in game for the last postseason spot out of District 24-6A.

"This is my senior year, and you want to go out with a bang," said Culpepper, who has 12 Power Four offers, including Texas Tech and SMU, as a defensive back. "We all knew that we couldn't let this game be our last. We've got too much of a brotherhood. … And being in the playoffs for two years in a row is phenomenal."

Culpepper hadn't played much on offense this season, which was intentional to save him for November. And when you combine him with Jayvion Clayter, it gives the Patriots an impressive combination in the backfield.

"When we were moving the ball on that second drive, I told our offensive coordinator, 'Put him in. That's our dude. Hand that kid the ball,'" East View coach Bill Robertson said. "We wanted to wait as long as we could to keep him healthy. But it's go time now."

All of Culpepper's rushing yards - East View ran for 235 as a team - came in the first half. And though he was seemingly involved in every play defensively as part of an effort by the Patriots that kept Rouse to less than 100 yards, his punt block late in the third quarter helped put the game away.

Justin Wright picked up the loose ball and sprinted 31 yards to give East View a 21-0 lead.

"I was kind of getting held up and blocked with all the other punts," Culpepper said. "Coach (Ryan) Dubelbeis told me to just give him (a move) so he'll run right past your face and you'll be unblocked. So I just trusted what he said, came in free and blocked it."

Robertson made it clear that he isn't happy just getting to the playoffs. The program did that last year in his first season as head coach. He wants to win a postseason game to "keep the program moving forward."

And with Culpepper now an option for every play, it certainly gives the Patriots (5-5, 3-3) a chance for their first playoff win since 2020.

"He does everything," Robertson said. "That's my dude. Since I've been here, I call that guy 'captain' because he's the dude."

East View picked a good time to have its best performance of the season. The Patriots caught three interceptions, didn't allow a first down until the second quarter and allowed less than 100 yards.

Caden Bruton recorded three sacks and was part of a defensive line that was key in limiting the Raiders to barely more than 2.0 yards per carry.

"That's the defense I felt we could be all year," Robertson said. "We play anywhere from eight to nine underclassmen on that defense, and there's been some tough sledding. But those guys keep showing up and keep getting better. And the last five games, our defense has really, really improved."

On the only true drive Rouse had all game, midway through the third quarter, when East View still only led 14-0, Erick Jones produced the team's first interception.

A few minutes later, Landon Wallace and Nicolas Kaje made interceptions on back-to-back Rouse offensive plays, with the latter returning it 40 yards for a TD.

"I haven't had a pick all season," Kaje said. "The play before, I was like, 'God, please let me get a pick, because everybody got a pick before me. … Then all of a sudden, on that play, I just stuck a hand out, and I was just trying to deflect it. And then it just ran right into my hand. So I just thank God for that."

Raiders coach Mark Hopkins wasn't holding his head afterwards.

In his first year leading the program, he had Rouse (4-6, 2-3) on the verge of a playoff berth, and he appreciates what his players did this season.

"Change is hard on everybody, and the kids bought in to what I was asking them to do," said Hopkins. "They embraced the change and attacked the challenge … and I'm proud of that."

Hopkins was hired in late February, and he said having his first full offseason will be important.

"We're going to keep getting better from here," he said. "This set the foundation. … We had a lot of young guys play their first varsity game the first week of the season, and they're coming back, so we're excited about the future."
