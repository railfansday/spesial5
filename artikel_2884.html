Preview: Surviving a pack of Wolves

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

We won't deny it: the last week or so has consisted of games that have been a 'must-win' for Utah. And tonight is included.

The team's play against Dallas was more on the promising side: fans saw Darryn Peterson start to find his groove and play like the #2 pick he is, Lauri Markkanen hit a mind-boggling eight triples, but it feels like Utah needs to play a perfect and synchronised game of basketball just to win a game of basketball.

The Jazz road trip concludes with a visit to see our divisional foe, along with former friends in Minnesota. The roster overhaul over the 2026 offseason added star LaMelo Ball to the mix, designed to alleviate the facilitating role for Anthony Edwards. They've even got a brand-spankin' new rebrand as the league adapts to the dying trend of minimalism. The Wolves are so unrecognisable that they even have Rudy Gobert looking like Montel Williams.

The addition of LaMelo and Kuminga has given them an early season boost. They hold a +5.1 rebound-per-game improvement compared to the baseline average of 49.2 rebounds per game last season. They've also maintained their defensive core to post a 106.6 defensive rating, second-best in the Association. After a hot 3-0 start, the Wolves have sizzled to a comfortable 7-3 record to sit at the 6th spot in the Western Conference.

Minnesota's rebounding is arguably Utah's biggest concern if they're chasing a win. They rank 2nd in the NBA in offensive rebound percentage (35.6%), making them a formidable foe for a team already missing Jusuf Nurkic. The team needs to stay disciplined, ensuring that every player sticks to boxing out.

It's especially important that the Jazz shooting woes need to be put on hold, or else they'll get punished on the other end. They have converted just 28% of their three-point attempts over the last three games (34-of-121). While they demonstrated minor improvement against Dallas, the overall efficiency continues to be a lingering issue. Missed shots could quickly translate into transition opportunities for the Wolves, while their offensive rebounding allows them to compensate for their own missed attempts.

And really, I don't know if it's possible to hold your own if Mo Bamba is another healthy scratch from the rotation, with a DNP-CD against Dallas, in favour of Tony Bradley. Perhaps his ankle flared up before tonight, but either way, Utah needs some size advantage.

It feels like it's been years since the Jazz were a bottom-10 team in turnovers. The lack of winning experience, combined with inconsistent offensive flow, has left the Jazz with a 17.2% turnover rate, ranking them 26th in the league.

This was evident when the Jazz coughed up 21 turnovers in their NBA Cup loss to the Mavericks. Will Hardy specifically highlighted the team's concerns about handling the ball and maintaining an offensive flow:

The ball movement that was promised still hasn't come to fruition. Things seem stagnant, relying heavily on Keyonte and Lauri to create their own shots rather than having them come to them naturally. Yes, a large part of it is them missing their distributing big man, Nurkic (whom will likely miss the rest of November), but it seems like they're trying to force-feed their top guys more than anything else.

Minnesota already rank eighth in opponent turnover percentage (16.04%), with McDaniels and Kuminga pressuring opposing teams on the perimeter. The Jazz can't lose focus. They will need to become more decisive, make quicker reads, and avoid unnecessary dribbling or else they risk the Timberwolves running away with this one.

The serious advantage in Utah's deck of cards is Minnesota's absence of Anthony Edwards for the next couple of months. When the Wolves win, it comes from contributions from several players rather than relying on a dominant scorer. Utah should look to disrupt the Timberwolves' flow by forcing their second and third options into difficult situations and preventing them from getting comfortable in the early going.

Utah's job tonight will be limiting the role of All-Star LaMelo Ball along with Minnesota's array of perimeter shooters, including Terrence Shannon Jr. and Bones Hyland. Dosunmu exploded for 26 points in New Orleans a couple days ago, so he'll be expected to carry that momentum into tonight.

It's also important to highlight the progression of Jaden McDaniels, posting a career-high 17.1 points per game and a 51.1% field-goal percentage if the season ended today. McDaniels also ranks first with the best offensive rating in the fourth quarter (141.3).

There's little room for error if the Jazz hope to beat a proven Playoff team. It feels like this has been the story for weeks, but seriously, it's do-or-die time for the Jazz to save this season.
