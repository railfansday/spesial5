Can Washington steal the NFC East crown? Hogs Haven readers weigh in after Thursday’s in over the Giants - poll results

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Here's what Ben Standig had to say earlier this week:

The 2026 NFL regular season is past the halfway point, but the finish line remains too far away to make sweeping declarations about how the NFC East race will unfold.

Suggesting that the eventual division winner will have at least eight victories doesn't seem like a stretch, in theory.

There's a solid argument that the Washington Commanders are the best all-around team among the remaining contenders. (Step aside, Eagles.)

When Washington sat at 1-3 following a dismal overseas loss to Indianapolis in Week 4, talk of an NFC East title felt like pure fantasy. Yet six games later, the burgundy & gold have quietly stitched together a 3-2-1 stretch highlighted by Thursday night's 24-21 win over the New York Giants at MetLife Stadium that has breathed a sense of renewed optimism into at least part of the fan base.

Now at 3-5-1, Washington still occupies third place in the division, trailing New York by 2 games. Even so, the underlying progress is real. Quarterback Jayden Daniels engineered a clutch late drive last Thursday night — connecting with Stefon Diggs on a 32-yard touchdown with 1:05 remaining — while Washington converted 7 of 14 third downs and held New York to 4-of-12 on third down.

Moreover, over the past 6 games, the Commanders have largely protected the football — carrying a +7 turnover differential on the season while leaning on young contributors like tight end Ben Sinnott, wideout Antonio Williams, and cornerbacks Trey Amos and (the not-so-young) Amik Robertson.

In this week's Hogs Haven Reacts survey, we asked readers two questions to gauge the community's temperature entering Week 11. The first was our weekly benchmark: Are you confident in the overall direction of the team?

This week, 46% of respondents answered "Yes," while 54% voted "No".

That 32-point surge from last week's 14% approval mark mirrors the 21-point jump we saw in Week 9 after Washington beat the Eagles. Nothing cures midweek angst quite like beating a division rival in prime time!

Still, the fact that 54% of voters remain in the "No" camp shows that skepticism runs deep. As several commenters and fans have mentioned, Washington has yet to beat a clear playoff contender outside of Seattle in Week 3, and they let winnable games against the Colts and Giants slip away earlier in the fall. Until the Commanders can achieve sustained winning, a majority of the fan base is keeping its guard up, and the heat will stay turned up on head coach Dan Quinn and GM Adam Peters.

Our second question asked which team respondents believe will win the NFC East division title in 2026. Despite Washington sitting behind both New York and Dallas in the standings, the results were fairly decisive:

That 54% vote represents a dramatic reversal from earlier in the season, but it stems less from sudden perfection in Ashburn than from the vulnerabilities of Washington's rivals.

First, Washington owns the head-to-head tiebreaker over the Giants (5-3-1) after Thursday's win. Second, the schedule-makers did New York no favors. Over the final 8 weeks, the Giants must run a gauntlet that includes Jacksonville, San Francisco, Seattle, and Detroit, whereas Washington gets much softer matchups against Arizona, Tennessee, and Atlanta.

Meanwhile, Dallas (4-6) has been unable to win back-to-back games since November 28, 2025, and its defensive struggles reached a boiling point this week when head coach Brian Schottenheimer stripped first-year coordinator Christian Parker of play-calling duties in favor of senior defensive assistant Mike Pettine. To beat out Washington for the division title, the Cowboys will have to win as many times in their remaining 7 games as the Commanders win in their final 8. Don't forget that the Commanders still have a chance to inflict a loss on the Cowboys when they meet in Landover for the final game of the regular season in Week 18.

The math is straightforward: if Washington can match the win totals of both the Giants and Cowboys, the Commanders will be the division winner!

Washington holds the tiebreaker over the Giants, and, because of the Week 5 tie, matching Dallas in the win column would put Washington a half-game ahead of the silver & blue.

If, for example, the Commanders can win 5 of their final 8 games to finish 8-8-1, they would be division champs if the Giants go 3-5 and the Cowboys, who have only 7 games remaining, finish out the season 4-3. Of course, with 8 weeks to go, there are sure to be a lot of twists & turns before things are decided, but the Cowboys' games in Week 17 vs the Giants and Week 18 on the road against the Commanders could prove to be decisive in determining the ultimate division champ.

Readers in the survey article comment thread broke cleanly along familiar lines.

The optimistic Pollyanna camp — voiced by Savo_UK, Redskins21Life, and BillWard among others– pointed directly to the schedule imbalance. Their consensus is that if Washington takes care of business against Arizona, Tennessee, Atlanta, and Dallas (in Week 18), stealing just 1 upset against Cincinnati, Houston, Minnesota, or Jacksonville would be enough to reach 8 wins, which could be enough to claim the division in this 2026 season.

The skeptical Cassandra camp — led by theFan4Life, WillThomas, and Greengmork among others — warned against mistaking a single prime-time escape against the Giants for a turnaround. They argue that Washington's defense still struggles to generate consistent pressure, that the roster lacks the talent to beat playoff-caliber offenses, and that finishing around .500 risks trapping the franchise in draft-position purgatory without building a true contender.

I think the Commanders defense has looked completely different since the return of Nick Cross and Trey Amos to the lineup.

In the first 5 games of 2026, the Commanders surrendered an average of 29.8 points per game. Since Week 6 in San Francisco, when Amos and Cross took the field together for the first time, Washington has given up 18.5 points per game. While that stretch did include games against the struggling Eagles and last week's road trip to New York, it also included games against the Niners and Rams.

While passing yards surrendered since Week 6 are not dramatically improved (255 per game in Wks 1-5; 232 per game in Wks 6-10), the number of passing TOUCHDOWNS surrendered has changed dramatically, from 2.8 per game (14 total) in the first 5 weeks to 0.75 per game (3 total) in the four most recent games; the secondary has not allowed more than one passing touchdown in any of those contests.

Anyone looking at the full-season stats for Washington's pass defense would question the Commanders' ability to limit the Bengals' high-powered attack this week. But a look at the most recent 4 games — which include playing against Matt Stafford & Puka Nacua as well as Brock Purdy and the Kyle Shanahan offense — might well bolster the confidence of Commanders fans with respect to improvements in Washington's pass defense since Cross and Amos took the field together.

If you only look at the scoreboard, it is easy to argue that Washington's offense is still spinning its wheels. To my eye, though, the underlying efficiency and rhythm of David Blough's unit have taken a noticeable step forward over the past month.

Across the four-game stretch straddling the Week 7 bye, the Commanders have pushed their average to 7.6 yards per pass attempt—a marked jump from the 4.8 to 6.3 yards per attempt they managed during the opening month—while completing 66.7 percent of their throws. Even in the Week 9 loss to Los Angeles, Jayden Daniels posted a 101.4 passer rating (24-of-35 for 253 yards) with explosive strikes of 42 yards to Stefon Diggs, 21 yards to Van Jefferson, and a 45-yard burst from Rachaad White, before following that up on Thursday night in New York by going 23-of-33 for 235 yards and three touchdowns in a 24–21 road win over the Giants.

That progress reflects a deliberate coaching emphasis on sharper situational execution; prior to Week 10, head coach Dan Quinn noted that the staff added extra practice repetitions specifically targeting red-zone spacing against man and zone looks so that "the decision-making process happens quickly" and the offense stops settling for field goals.

Just as encouraging has been the growing trust between Daniels and his first-time play-caller, paired with Daniels' emergence as the unit's vocal leader. There were plenty of justifiable questions early on about Blough's learning curve, yet Daniels emphasized ahead of the Giants game that their weekly game-planning loop is actively tailoring the playbook to his strengths: "I think just stuff I don't like throughout the week, he'll take out. Stuff I really do like, he'll call… If we got all 11 people going in the same direction, we have a chance."

After some early-season failures, that buy-in has shown up recently when it has mattered, most notably during Week 8's 27–10 win over Philadelphia, when Daniels gathered the offense during a sluggish 10–0 start—calling their opening series "complete BS"—and sparked 27 unanswered points.

Rookie wideout Antonio Williams credited Daniels' command in the huddle for uniting the group ("All the guys respect what he has to say, because we know the work he puts in"), while Quinn praised the quarterback's resilience in Thursday night's comeback after overcoming a turnover on downs and two interceptions to engineer the game-winning drive, saying, "To see us battle and stay in the fight, that's what I was looking for."

All in all, I see a team that struggled early and is still finding its feet, but one that is on the rise. The Commanders still haven't put together that full 60-minute game that was the hallmark of the 2024 season, but I believe that they're moving in that direction, and, with 8 games left to play, there's plenty of time to finish putting it together.

Meanwhile, in a different SB Nation poll this week:

Outside the DMV, barely one in six voters (15.7%) wanted a piece of Washington at +350—a stark contrast to the 54% of Hogs Haven readers projecting a division crown, and a gap the Commanders can start closing under the lights on Monday night.

The final 8-game push begins Monday night at Northwest Stadium against Joe Burrow and the AFC North-leading Cincinnati Bengals.

Anyone who watched the last regular-season meeting between these clubs on Monday Night Football in September 2024 remembers a clinic in offensive efficiency. Washington won 38-33 in a game that featured zero punts and zero turnovers — a feat the NFL hadn't seen since 1940.

If Washington wants to get the win again this Monday, the Commanders may need to be ready for another track meet. Burrow has Cincinnati rolling on a five-game winning streak, averaging 30.9 points per game behind Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins. For Washington to pull the upset, pass rushers Dorance Armstrong, Odafe Oweh, and K'Lavon Chaisson will have to disrupt Burrow's timing while the secondary continues to improve, and the Daniels-led offense keeps pace on the scoreboard.

Moneyline: Bengals -270 / Commanders +220

Winning on Monday night is a tall order, although, with Arizona and Tennessee up next, even a loss wouldn't derail Washington's plan for a division title. Still, catching fire under the Monday night lights would likely turn the current cautious optimism into genuine belief across the DMV.
