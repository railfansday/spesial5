U.K. weight loss drug users are cutting more than £1 billion in grocery spending

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Households with a user bought 415 million fewer food items in the year after starting, and 8.5% of U.K. households have one

Worldpanel by Numerator found that U.K. households with a weight loss drug user trimmed their grocery bills by over £1 billion ($1.3 billion) during the 12 months after starting the medicine, according to Bloomberg.

The consumer research firm said those households took home 415 million fewer grocery products over that span, according to Bloomberg. The firm's previous tracker, published in June, showed about 300 million fewer items.

The £1 billion total works out to £389 ($510) less in groceries bought every year by each household with a user, according to Reuters. Worldpanel drew on survey data from a sample of 9,629 households.

Households home to someone taking a GLP-1 medicine, like Eli Lilly $LLY's Mounjaro or Novo Nordisk's Wegovy, climbed from 6.3% of U.K. households in February to 8.5% in August, according to Reuters. Worldpanel expects that share to pass 10% in 2027.

Pills, which gained regulatory approval in June, make up 11.2% of U.K. users of the drugs, according to Reuters. Men account for 32% of users, a share that stood at 23% in February.

Buying habits have shifted among users. Outlay on crisps and alcohol dropped by an extra 15% in user households compared with households without a user, according to Reuters. Volumes of frozen ready meals sold jumped 30% in households with a user.

"This is not a flash-in-the-pan trend, but a seismic change that requires retailers and manufacturers to rethink their strategies," said Chantel Kennaugh, head of public sector and nutrition at Worldpanel, according to Bloomberg.

Retailers are responding, according to Bloomberg. Morrisons and Tesco have launched smaller, protein-rich meals aimed at users, while Boots, the health and pharmacy chain, has brought out items geared toward digestive health.

Some of the money leaving grocery baskets is going elsewhere, according to Bloomberg. Users put £78 million ($102 million) into health and beauty items beyond what Worldpanel had forecast.
