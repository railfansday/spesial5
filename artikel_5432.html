Inflation is pushing U.S. shoppers to spend more on Black Friday — even as they planned to scale back

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Shoppers who held off on large purchases in recent months are now bunching that money into sale periods such as the holiday season

PwC projects U.S. holiday spending will rise about 5.4% from a year earlier while shoppers say they plan to scale back gift budgets. After adjusting for inflation, the projected increase is about 2.8%, PwC said.

PwC's October survey found planned gift spending down 10% since June. Of the consumers surveyed, 85% intend to use a budgeting strategy. In PwC's survey, 67% plan to buy less-expensive alternatives, 56% plan to look for gifts under a set price and 40% plan to shop more for vintage, resale or used products.

Households with children plan to trim gift spending 4% since June, compared with 14% for households without children, PwC said.

Prices are adding to the total. The personal consumption expenditures price index rose 3.8% in October from a year earlier, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said Wednesday. Shoppers are also facing higher starting prices on discounted goods.

"Even though the discounts will be there, they're starting from a higher original price point in a lot of cases," said Neil Saunders, managing director of GlobalData, according to Bloomberg.

Americans are on track to rack up nearly $13 billion in web orders on Black Friday alone, according to Bloomberg. Shoppers polled by Circana expect their budgets to average $779, a drop from $796 a year ago. A quarter of them attributed the lower budgets to their own money situation and the state of the economy.

Shoppers who held off on large purchases in recent months are now bunching that money into sale periods such as the holiday season, Brian McCarthy, retail strategy lead at Deloitte, told CNBC. Shoppers are hunting for additional bargains while pushing some of their buying to a later date, McCarthy said.

Bain & Company forecast in September that U.S. holiday retail sales would top $1 trillion for the first time, with 4.5% nominal growth. The firm said more than half of that growth would come from price increases rather than shoppers buying more units.
