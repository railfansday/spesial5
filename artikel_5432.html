House Democrats are probing whether Lutnick shielded Cantor Fitzgerald's Nvidia stake

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Three ranking members are demanding records from the firm his son chairs, and Cantor Fitzgerald says the allegations are baseless

Bonnie Cash/UPI/Bloomberg via Getty Images

House Democrats are investigating whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick used his office to protect the Nvidia $NVDA holdings of Cantor Fitzgerald, the investment firm chaired by his son, and are demanding records from the firm.

Rep. Jamie Raskin, a Maryland Democrat and ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee, Rep. Gregory Meeks, a New York Democrat and ranking member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, and Rep. Sydney Kamlager-Dove, a California Democrat and ranking member of the Foreign Affairs subcommittee on South and Central Asia, sent a letter to Cantor Fitzgerald Chairman Brandon Lutnick and announced it Wednesday. They want to know whether Howard Lutnick blocked efforts to stop suspected smuggling of advanced Nvidia chips to China while the firm held hundreds of millions of dollars in Nvidia stock.

"[T]he American people deserve to know: Did the Secretary of Commerce seriously and perhaps irreversibly compromise U.S. national security interests to make money for his adult children and Cantor Fitzgerald?" the lawmakers wrote.

The lawmakers are seeking records of every Nvidia trade the firm has made since January 2024 and an accounting of its business in China and Southeast Asia. They also want communications about export controls and suspected chip smuggling, communications with the Commerce Department about Nvidia, Megaspeed and Asian data center projects, and records on President Donald Trump's reported Nvidia investments.

The letter asks Cantor Fitzgerald to preserve all relevant records, including encrypted messages and business communications on personal devices. It sets a Dec. 1 deadline for written confirmation.

Before Howard Lutnick became commerce secretary, Cantor Fitzgerald had disclosed holding more than 3.5 million shares of Nvidia stock, according to the lawmakers. After his confirmation, he agreed to transfer ownership of the firm to trusts benefiting his adult children. While U.S. officials scrutinized Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer, Cantor Fitzgerald cut its reported Nvidia holdings by close to half. It held about $310 million in Nvidia stock at the end of March.

The letter follows the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who admitted to extramarital affairs, including with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive suspected of spying for China. The lawmakers say Howard Lutnick resisted pressure to cut off Megaspeed’s access to Nvidia chips. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said four federal agencies had backed blacklisting Megaspeed for months while Commerce, which controls the blacklist, held out.

Cantor Fitzgerald told CNBC the letter contained errors and that any suggestion the firm's business swayed decisions made by the government has no basis. The firm said it has no meaningful position of its own in Nvidia, and that the shares it carries mostly stem from equity swaps it entered into on behalf of clients, with the drop coming as hedges were wound down once clients exited their positions. A Cantor Fitzgerald spokesperson said the firm plays no part in deciding U.S. export controls or which companies land on the entity list, according to Bloomberg. The Commerce Department and Nvidia did not respond right away when asked to comment.

Bessent said on Nov. 13 that the Commerce Department decides whether Megaspeed is added to the export blacklist, and that the Treasury, State and Defense departments and the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative all supported listing it.

Howard Lutnick has agreed to appear before the Senate Banking Committee. Committee Chair Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, has requested his testimony on Dec. 10.
