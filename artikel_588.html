BAFTA 2027 Entries Revealed: 126 Best Film Contenders, 728 Performances and No Spot for ‘You Can See Everything’ Star Amanda Seyfried

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

That’s how many titles appear on BAFTA‘s draft Entered Films List for the 2027 EE BAFTA Film Awards, with submissions across every category open to them: best film, director, screenplay and all nine craft races, right down to special visual effects.

There are 126 best film entries, up from 109 eligible titles a year ago. Across best film, director, the two screenplay races and the nine crafts, 213 features are in play. Count the titles entered only in specialty races such as Documentary, Animated Film or Outstanding Debut by a British Writer, Director or Producer and the list climbs to 235. The list isn’t final. Distributors and producers have until noon GMT on Monday, Nov. 30, to request amendments and the official Entered Films List arrives Friday, Dec. 4, the same day Round One voting opens. Expect a few small corrections.

Among the list of films submitting in every category are Denis Villeneuve’s “Dune: Part Three,” Christopher Nolan’s “The Odyssey,” Emerald Fennell’s “Wuthering Heights,” Steven Spielberg’s “Disclosure Day,” Robert Eggers’ “Werwulf,” “Project Hail Mary,” “Spider-Man: Brand New Day,” “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping,” “28 Years Later: The Bone Temple” and “Peaky Blinders: The Immortal Man.”

Also swinging in all 12 categories are the Tom Cruise-vehicle “Digger,” “Verity,” “Send Help,” “Crime 101,” “The Death of Robin Hood,” “Heart of the Beast,” “Pressure,” “Ink,” “Tenzing,” “Wicker,” “The Sheep Detectives” and “Ghosts: The Possession of Button House.”

Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s Elizabeth Holmes documentary is entered in best film, director, documentary and seven of the nine craft races, including costume design and makeup and hair. What it doesn’t have is a single performance entry, and that’s because BAFTA rules deem it ineligible.

Amanda Seyfried, who won an Emmy playing Holmes in Hulu’s “The Dropout,” returns to the role in the A24 film’s dramatized reenactments, a performance Variety currently projects to win the Oscar for supporting actress. BAFTA joins the Golden Globes and Actor Awards (formerly the SAG Awards) among awards bodies where Seyfried is ineligible for recognition. Critics Choice stands alone in deeming her eligible for supporting actress, making it the only televised stop on the precursor circuit where she can be nominated. Critics Choice Awards nomination voting is underway.

The four performance categories have 728 entries.

Six performers are entered four times apiece. Zendaya is submitted in lead for “Dune: Part Three,” “The Drama” and “Spider-Man: Brand New Day” and in supporting as Athena in “The Odyssey.” Her “Drama” co-star Robert Pattinson matches her, entered in lead for that film and for “Primetime,” where he plays Chris Hansen, plus supporting turns in “Dune: Part Three” and “The Odyssey.”

Anne Hathaway is entered in lead three times, for “The Devil Wears Prada 2,” “Mother Mary” and “Verity” andd in supporting as Penelope in “The Odyssey.” Sandra Hüller carries lead for “Rose” and supporting roles in “Project Hail Mary,” “Digger” and Paweł Pawlikowski’s “Fatherland,” where she plays Erika Mann.British stalwarts are also spread across multiple films. Stephen Graham leads “The Good Boy” and is entered in supporting for “Animol,” “Bunker” and “Peaky Blinders: The Immortal Man.” Johnny Flynn leads “The Idiot(s)” and is entered in supporting for “& Sons,” “Elsinore” and “Tenzing.”

Eleven more performers are entered three times: Caitríona Balfe (“A Long Winter,” “Sense and Sensibility,” “Tenzing”); Javier Bardem (“The Beloved,” “Bunker,” “Dune: Part Three”); Penélope Cruz (“Bunker,” “The Invite,” “La Bola Negra”); Monica Dolan (“The Lost Children of Tuam,” “Elsinore,” “Onwards and Sideways”); Claire Foy (“The Magic Faraway Tree,” “Savage House,” “Ink”); Andrew Garfield (“The Magic Faraway Tree,” “The Uprising,” “Artificial”); Jessica Gunning (“The Christophers,” “The Magic Faraway Tree,” “Sunny Dancer”); George MacKay (“Rose of Nevada,” “& Sons,” “Sense and Sensibility”); Bella Ramsey (“Sunny Dancer,” “The Liberation,” “The Sheep Detectives”); Mark Ruffalo (“Being Heumann,” “Crime 101,” “Spider-Man: Brand New Day”); and Tramell Tillman (“Ebenezer,” “Spider-Man: Brand New Day,” “Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother”).

The acting races also have new voting rules.

Casting chapter members now vote alongside the acting chapter in Rounds One and Two. The chapter vote determines the first five of 10 longlist spots in each category, while a jury selects the final five from performers ranked sixth through 15th. Each category still yields six nominees.

Not everyone is going wide. “Masters of the Universe” enters eight of the nine craft races, skipping original score and bypasses best film, director and screenplay entirely. “The Bride” is entered in production design alone. “The Super Mario Galaxy Movie” goes the other direction, entering best film with no craft, directing or writing submissions, alongside its animated and family film bids.

The Documentary category itself lands at 67 entries. Eight of those films are also entered in best film: “Epic: Elvis Presley in Concert,” “Musk,” “Oasis: Don’t Look Back in Anger,” “Everest: The Other Side,” “You Can See Everything,” “Super Nature,” “Derek vs Derek” and “Everybody to Kenmure Street.”

Outstanding British Film draws 79 entries, with the longlist expanded from 15 to 20 films. Film Not in the English Language climbs to 46 entries, and 15 of them are also competing in best film. Among them: “Kokuho,” Cristian Mungiu’s “Fjord,” “Fatherland,” “Rose,” “La Bola Negra,” “Ramayana” and “Godzilla Minus Zero.”

Animated Film jumps from 10 entries to 15, and 11 of them are also chasing Best Film. Pixar arrives with “Toy Story 5” and “Hoppers,” alongside “The Super Mario Galaxy Movie,” “Minions & Monsters,” Laika’s “Wildwood,” Aardman’s “Shaun the Sheep: The Beast of Mossy Bottom,” “Ray Gunn,” “The Cat in the Hat” and “Goat.”

The jury-voted Family Film category, renamed this year from Children’s & Family Film, rises from 14 to 21 entries.

Round One voting opens Friday, Dec. 4. It closes Dec. 29 for the four acting categories and Jan. 4 for everything else. Longlists are published Jan. 7, when Round Two voting begins. Nominations are announced Jan. 19, two days before the Oscar nominations. Round Three voting runs Jan. 26 through Feb. 16, and the 80th EE BAFTA Film Awards take place Sunday, Feb. 21, in London.
