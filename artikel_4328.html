The Witcher 3 Is Using AI for Geralt’s Voice, But There’s a Very Good Reason

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

Artificial intelligence has become one of the most controversial topics in the gaming industry, and it's not hard to understand why. From studios experimenting with AI-generated assets to developers finding new ways to automate creative work, players have plenty of reasons to be skeptical.

However, CD Projekt Red has found a rather unusual way to use the technology in The Witcher 3, and this time, there's a surprisingly touching story behind it.

The studio has recently confirmed that it is working with an AI company to adjust Geralt's Polish voice in the upcoming Songs of the Past expansion, which raised some players’ eyebrows. But don't worry, Jacek Rozenek, the actor who has portrayed the White Wolf in Polish since the original The Witcher, isn't being replaced.

In fact, he's still performing all of Geralt's new lines himself. This time, AI is actually being used as a supportive tool and is simply helping his voice sound closer to the one players remember from the 2015 RPG.

Amanita Design, the Czech studio behind Machinarium, recently called out an AI-generated project made using assets from the game.

As reported by Game File, Rozenek suffered a stroke in 2019 that left him unable to speak for months. His recovery was an incredibly difficult process, requiring him to train his voice for six or seven hours every day before he could return to acting.

He eventually returned to the role of Geralt, recording additional lines for The Witcher 3's next-gen update in 2022. However, the process took a significant toll on him. Rozenek told Game File that he was exhausted after those recording sessions, despite having worked hard to regain his ability to perform.

Jacek Rozenek, the actor who has portrayed the White Wolf in Polish since the original The Witcher, isn't being replaced.

When CD Projekt invited him back to record dialogue for Songs of the Past, the studio noticed that his voice had changed considerably. It was now lower and rougher than it had been when he recorded the thousands of lines featured in the original game.

Since the new expansion takes place around the same period as the base game, hearing Geralt suddenly sound different between old and new dialogue could not make sense to the player’s experience. So, rather than replacing Rozenek, however, CD Projekt decided to find another solution. A quite smart one, actually.

The studio partnered with Respeecher, an AI company specializing in voice technology that it had previously worked with on Phantom Liberty, the DLC of Cyberpunk 2077. With their help, Rozenek recorded all of Geralt's new dialogue as usual, and the recordings were then processed using an algorithm trained on his previous performances.

CD Projekt Red has just released Patch 5.01 for The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt Remastered.

In other words, the technology isn't generating Geralt's lines from scratch or replacing the actor's performance. Rozenek still delivers the dialogue, including its emotions and acting, while AI helps bring his voice closer to the version players heard over a decade ago.

Most importantly, the actor gave his full consent to the process and expressed his trust in the developers. "The point is that this voice should be as close as possible to my voice from 12 years ago," Rozenek explained.

CD Projekt's localization producer, Mikołaj Szwed, also acknowledged the concerns surrounding AI in the industry, but believes this is an example of how the technology can be used ethically. He described the situation as a special case, emphasizing that the decision was made because of Rozenek's health circumstances.

And honestly, it's difficult to argue with the sentiment behind it. Rather than using AI to cut costs or avoid hiring a performer, the Polish studio is using it to help an actor continue playing a character he's portrayed for decades. Songs of the Past is currently scheduled to launch in 2027, and Geralt's Polish voice will once again be in the hands of the person who helped make him so iconic!
