Trump held as much as $62 million in Nvidia stock as his team eased China chip rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Washington Post says the stake is well above the $615,000 to $1.3 million Trump reported at the end of 2024

President Donald Trump outside the White House. (Tasos Katopodis/Getty Images)

President Donald Trump owned Nvidia $NVDA shares worth somewhere from $13 million to $62 million at the close of August, according to an analysis of his financial disclosures by The Washington Post.

The disclosures leave out the most recent two months of trading because filings run behind, according to The Washington Post. The paper's range far exceeds the Nvidia holdings of between $615,000 and $1.3 million that Trump disclosed as of the end of 2024.

Trump's annual disclosure for 2025, which the Office of Government Ethics received on June 29, lists Nvidia stock valued at $1,000,001 to $5,000,000 in one investment account. The form shows $2,501 to $5,000 in capital gains income from the holding.

Reuters had not verified The Washington Post’s findings, and the White House and Nvidia had not responded to its requests for comment.

In December, Trump said the U.S. would permit Nvidia to sell China its H200 chips, the company's second-most-advanced AI processors, with a 25% cut of those sales going to the government, according to Reuters. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick also declined to impose curbs on a firm suspected of redirecting Nvidia chips into China, according to The Washington Post.

The Washington Post also said Jensen Huang, who has since left his post as Nvidia's chief, flew aboard Air Force One with Trump when the president last visited China.

The administration began permitting certain H200 shipments to China in January, with each application reviewed individually. That policy left in place separate restrictions covering organizations on the Commerce Department's Entity List. Nvidia's board has also opened an independent probe into whether a foreign intelligence operation singled out Jensen Huang.

Jensen Huang, a co-founder of Nvidia, stepped down as CEO last week after controversy erupted over his involvement with Alice Huang, a businesswoman from China, according to Reuters. U.S. authorities suspect Alice Huang of intelligence ties to Beijing.

Jensen Huang ranks among the president's nearest allies in the tech industry's California hub, and their ties go back to at least 2025, according to Reuters. A Reuters reporter noticed Jensen Huang's name pop up on the screen of Trump's phone while the president was on a December call.

Nvidia is scheduled to release its fiscal third-quarter results on Tuesday, Nov. 17, according to Reuters.
