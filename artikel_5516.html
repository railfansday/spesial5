October CPI rose 0.4%, but a funding lapse last year leaves most annual rates unpublished

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

BLS published 12-month changes for a few items, including gasoline up 37.4%, after missing October 2025 survey data

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Tuesday that the consumer price index for all urban consumers rose 0.4% in October on a seasonally adjusted basis, after a 0.6% increase in September. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3%, matching its gains in September and August.

Economists surveyed by Dow Jones had forecast a 0.3% overall increase and a 0.2% core gain, according to CNBC.

The bureau collected no survey data for October 2025 because of a lapse in appropriations, so it could not publish October 2025 indexes for most CPI series. The agency is therefore unable to publish 12-month changes for October 2026 for indexes that lack that data.

A few annual changes were published. Gasoline rose 37.4% over 12 months, used cars and trucks fell 3.3%, and new vehicles rose 0.7%. Prices for all items rose an estimated 4% over 12 months and core prices rose 2.8%, according to calculations by The New York Times.

The shelter index rose 0.3% in October and accounted for over 30% of the monthly increase in all items. The food index rose 0.5%. The energy index rose 0.7% after a 5.2% jump in September.

Gasoline rose 1.0% on a seasonally adjusted basis, though prices fell 0.7% before seasonal adjustment. The electricity index rose 0.9%, while the natural gas index fell 0.8%.

Used cars and trucks fell 1.2% in October after a 0.7% gain in September. The communication index fell 0.1%. Recreation rose 0.8%, motor vehicle insurance rose 0.7% and medical care rose 0.3%.

The two-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.69%, a gain of more than a tenth of a percentage point.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said in remarks on Tuesday that a half-point rate increase in December could be warranted if core inflation came in at 0.3% or higher, according to The New York Times. The Fed meets Dec. 8-9.

The bureau will publish the November CPI report on Dec. 10 at 8:30 a.m. ET.
