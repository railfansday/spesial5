Job Growth Slows Nationally, but Some Local Markets Tell a Different Story

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Job growth is one of the most important signals for home builders, as employment security helps shape consumer confidence, household formation, and the willingness to make a major purchase. And right now, the latest employment picture is a mixed one for the housing industry.

While employment growth remained positive, the U.S. added just 29,000 positions in September 2026, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Meanwhile, unemployment edged up to 4.2%. The slowdown fits a broader pattern of relatively modest hiring rather than a sharp deterioration in the labor market.

The composition of growth is equally important. Education and health services remained the strongest employment engine, expanding 1.7% from a year earlier. Information employment, by contrast, fell 4% year over year, while financial activities declined 1.1% over the same period.

These differences matter for builders. Two markets can add a similar number of jobs but generate different levels of housing demand depending on the industries driving growth and the incomes associated with those positions.

The national slowdown also masks significant geographic variation.

Salt Lake City led the major markets tracked by Zonda with 2.8% year-over-year employment growth in September, followed by Raleigh at 2.1% and Las Vegas at 1.9%.

The picture changes when looking at the number of jobs created since last year. Dallas added 36,200 positions despite employment growing a more modest 0.8%. New York City added 29,800, Los Angeles 28,600, Houston 25,100, and Phoenix 24,400.

That distinction is also important: percentage growth can identify accelerating economies, while absolute job creation provides another measure of the potential scale of household demand.

At the other end of the spectrum, Washington, D.C., lost 66,800 jobs and employment declined 2% since September 2025. Indianapolis fell 1.1%, Portland 0.9%, and Atlanta 0.3%. Still, several markets with declining employment have not experienced unusually high unemployment rates, suggesting builders should avoid treating negative job growth as a standalone demand signal.

For builders making multiyear land decisions, the outlook through 2031 may be even more important. The strongest projected employment gains cluster in Texas, the Mountain and Desert region, and the Carolinas, per Moody’s. Raleigh is forecast for 9.9% total employment growth from December 2026 through December 2031, followed by Austin at 9.7%, Orlando at 8.4%, and Las Vegas at 7.9%.
