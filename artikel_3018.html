If I Were Building a Portfolio From Scratch With $10,000, I’d Anchor It With This Unassuming Value Stock

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

This stock has beaten the S&P 500 over the past 25 years and outperforms during downturns.

By Dave Kovaleski – Nov 22, 2026 at 8:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than McDonald's ›

If you're building a portfolio from scratch with $10,000, there are some must-haves. You'd probably want broad market and large-cap exchange-traded funds (ETFs) as core holdings.

You'd certainly want some more aggressive growth stocks or ETFs to provide long-term alpha. And you would definitely want stocks or an ETF to provide some ballast when the broader markets or growth stocks are down.

One value stock that would serve that role is McDonald's (MCD +0.33%).

McDonald's is one of the largest fast food restaurant chains in the world and has been for a long time. It has also been one of the best defensive stocks on the market, buoyed by its consistent earnings in most markets and its excellent dividend.

The proof is in the performance. If you go back 25 years, encompassing the dot-com bear market, the global financial crisis, and everything since, McDonald's stock, on a total return basis, has an average annualized return of 12.4%. As the chart shows, that beats the S&P 500 and other top consumer stocks, like Walmart (WMT +1.51%), Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%), Target (TGT -0.97%), Proctor & Gamble (PG +0.91%), and PepsiCo (PEP +1.11%).

But when you drill down a bit further, you see that during most bear markets and downturns since then, McDonald's stock has not only beaten the benchmarks but had positive returns in some of the worst downturns.

For example, in 2008, when the S&P 500 was down 37%, McDonald's stock was up 9% for the year. In 2018, when the S&P 500 dropped 4%, McDonald's stock rose 6%. And in 2022, when the S&P 500 dropped 18%, McDonald's stock rose 1%.

So, it has the long-term performance that beats the benchmark, but more importantly, it performs well when the market doesn't. That is an important quality to have in your portfolio, as it helps smooth out overall returns during bouts of volatility or negative market sentiment.

McDonald's consistency is why it has been able to raise its dividend for the past 50 years in a row, making it a Dividend King, which is any company that has raised its annual dividend for at least 50 consecutive years. McDonald's currently pays out a high yield of 2.80% with a very manageable 49% payout ratio, so it is in a good financial position to keep boosting its dividend.

McDonald's is also in the beginning stages of a multi-year turnaround plan called NEXT, which management hopes will set the brand on a course for growth over the next decade. The NEXT plan aims to gain 1.5% market share in both the chicken and beverage categories, increase sales, raise the operating margin to the low- to mid-50% range, make restaurants more efficient, and have free-cash-flow conversion in the mid-to-high 80% range, among other goals, all by 2030.

It seeks to do this by improving the menu options and prices, capitalizing on the brand, modernizing restaurants and operations to make them more productive, and invest in people to improve the customer experience.

There are myriad challenges facing McDonald's including diet changes, competition, and the economy, but it has been able to anticipate changes in the past to maintain its market share. The new plan should allow McDonald's to maintain its leadership as a low-cost option for consumers.

That is why investors looking for a good value stock to balance out their portfolio should consider McDonald's.
