Game Preview #17: Wolves vs Grizzlies Thread

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Remember when there was a "budding rivalry" between the Minnesota Timberwolves and Memphis Grizzlies? A drama filled first round playoff matchup between future faces of the league, Anthony Edwards and Ja Morant. Karl-Anthony Towns Sr. and Tee Morant became the cutest "Dads of the NBA" bromance. Dillon Brooks, Mike Conley, Kyle Anderson…just so many storylines.

Now these two teams couldn't appear to be any further away from that. The Grizzlies have zero players remaining on their 2022 roster. The Wolves roster just Anthony Edwards and Jaden McDaniels from their respective squad. The connection these two franchises share is more about who gets to join the Eastern Conference in a few years. In the meantime, contrary to what many thought, Memphis is standing right above Minnesota in the Western Conference standings!

Who: Minnesota Timberwolves (11-5) vs. Memphis Grizzlies (12-4)

When: Saturday, November 21 at 7:00 PM CT

If you haven't been paying attention, the Memphis Grizzlies have been one of the feel-good stories of the NBA so far. They completely, and rightfully, ditched their previous oft-injured core of Ja Morant, Jaren Jackson Jr., and Desmond Bane. Many pundits believed that Memphis would be among the worst teams in the league as they rebuild from the ground floor this year (not me, as I was wise beyond comprehension).

Instead, they were shot out of a cannon to begin the season, starting 6-0. No, I'm not talking about a 6-0 scoring run. I'm talking about a 6-0 team record. They follow Tommy Ice/T.I.'s (head coach Tuomas Iisalo) lead, employing a strategy where he spreads minutes across a 10 to 12-man rotation. 11 different players average over 15 minutes per game. They're led by early season MVP candidate Ty Jerome (missed previous four games, probable to return Saturday) and 2026 third overall draft pick, Cameron Boozer (ruled out with ankle injury Saturday).

They fell back to Earth by losing four of their next five games, but have already bounced back to win five straight (against admittedly relatively easy competition). When you zoom out further, the only "good teams" they've beaten are the Trail Blazers and Cavaliers. They've had the easiest strength of schedule by a considerable margin.

So are they for real? More real than most thought.

Equal opportunity to all. That might be on the Grizzlies' whiteboard before every game. 11 different players average over 5.0 field goal attempts per game, compared to just eight for Minnesota. Nine different players average 9.0+ points per game, compared to just seven for Minnesota.

You cannot just guard one player to stop Memphis.

For Minnesota, this means their league-best defense will need to be on a string. Everyone is dangerous and they have to be treated as such. As a team, the Grizzlies make the third most amount of triples per game (15.6) and drain them at a league-best rate (39.6%) – most of these being open attempts. They are third in assists per game. They will spread things around and make themselves unpredictable.

Don't be surprised if a Cam Spencer or Javon Small pop off for 16 points. It's very possible Jerami Grant has a MPE (medium player explosion) and suddenly scores 24 points out of nowhere. Where did Taylor Hendricks come from, and why is he so buff and athletic now? Memphis also has no shortage of quality role players like Cedric Coward and Jaylen Wells, who will muck the game up on both ends of the court.

What has happened to LaMelo Ball's offense? (Did you see what I did? I removed the "O" from his name above. Like he's playing so bad offensively he doesn't have "O" in his name. Do you get it?) After I spent some time developing (more) creative headlines so that I could gush about his defense, it's been quite jarring to see his drop-off in what's supposed to be his main talent.

This month of November has been a seesaw of production so far. If you compare the numbers in the first five games of the month when the Wolves went 5-0, to his most recent four game stretch, it's eye-popping.

If you've been watching the games, you'll see that Ball is clearly trying to shoot his way through this slump. In fact, he's taking three more shots per game during this most recent stretch. Is this something more than just a rough patch? LaMelo had some tape around his left hand which he tugged at during throughout Thursday's game. There hasn't been any reports of ailments, at least not to my knowledge.

We're not at Rudy Gobert levels of offensive concern yet, but I'm keeping me eye on this.

When's the last time the Wolves had a blowout win? Over a week? Yeah, that's too long. It's time for Minnesota to bump up that point differential and pad some stats. LaMelo Ball finally breaks out of his slump and busts up the Grizzlies for a cool 33 points thanks to eight three-pointers. Rudy Gobert gives Cam Boozer his "Welcome the NBA" moment by dunking all over his head off an alley-oop.
