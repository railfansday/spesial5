5 questions with the enemy ahead of Steelers-Eagles

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Steelers are looking to rebound this week as they head on the road to take on the 2-7 Eagles. We talked with Brandon Gowton of Bleeding Green Nation to figure out what's gone wrong with Philadelphia's season, and what we can expect for this game.

1. This Eagles roster is hardly recognizable from the squad that won the Super Bowl just two seasons ago. I think most fans are aware of the Jalen Hurts situation. Outside of that, can you summarize for Steelers fans what has gone wrong for the 2-7 Eagles this season?Brandon Gowton: For starters, I'd recommend checking out Jimmy Kempski's "Eagles season from hell" tracker.

The TL;DR version is that the Eagles have been really bad and injured. The new-look offense is even worse than it was last year … and it can't just be blamed on injuries. The Eagles also looked bad on offense when they were healthy (see: the team scoring seven points in a Week 3 loss to the Chicago Bears). The Eagles' defense was supposed to be the strength of the team, but Vic Fangio's unit has largely underwhelmed.

Howie Roseman didn't have a great offseason. In the past, the coaching staff really benefited from the Eagles' general manager doing the heavy lifting. With the coaching staff now being required to do more with less, Nick Sirianni hasn't been able to answer the bell.2. It's only been one game, but what can you tell us about the Joe Milton experience so far? What can we expect from him and from the offense in general this game?BG: It's probably not going to be pretty for the Eagles. He's inconsistent.

He has the arm talent to make big throws. But his accuracy and touch were lacking in his first Eagles start. It was good to see the offense ran more vertical patterns than ever this season. But he didn't actually take a lot of deep shots against the New York Giants. That might change this week, especially if DeVonta Smith and/or Dontayvion Wicks are able to return from injury.

Milton's mobility is also a nice element for the Eagles after watching Tanner McKee be a statue in the pocket. Philly leads the NFL in unblocked pressures this season. While it's not ideal to often ask the QB to bail out the offensive line, it's the reality the Eagles are currently facing. He might be able to run around a bit on Sunday. And if the Steelers' defense has to respect that threat, Saquon Barkley could stand to benefit.

But he's probably just not going to play a very clean game. I expect sacks and turnovers.3. The Eagles' defense has seemingly slipped some this season, and the team just traded away Jordan Davis. What's been happening on that side of the ball? How do you think that unit will play the Steelers?BG: The Eagles' defense falling off is one of the bigger mysteries of this season. There was an expectation that this could be a top-five unit, if not challenge for the title of best defense in the NFL. This group clearly hasn't come close to that standard.

They have had some good stretches in games. But that usually looks like them holding up while the offense goes three-and-out over and over … before the dam ultimately breaks in the fourth quarter. Other times, they've just been bullied, especially in the trenches.

There are multiple factors why the defense hasn't lived up to the hype. Jalen Carter has been hampered by injury and isn't playing up to being paid as the top interior defender in the NFL. Cooper DeJean unexpectedly regressed after being an All-Pro defender last year. The Eagles have struggled to rush the passer. Riq Woolen, a volatile talent, has had more downs than ups as the cornerback across from Quinyon Mitchell.

It's also just difficult for defenses to be dominant year over year … unlike the case with elite offenses.

I do expect the defense to put up a respectable effort. Fangio's group played fairly well against the Giants, allowing just 19 points. And six of those points came on very short field goal drives (23 yards and four yards). The defense also produced six sacks, 14 TFLs, and nine QB hits. Given Aaron Rodgers' struggles, I think the Eagles could cause some problems for the Steelers … for a time, at least. Pittsburgh will probably do enough to make the game winnable for them.4. Philadelphia seems poised to completely reset after this season. Be honest, how likely are Nick Sirianni and Jalen Hurts to be back, respectively?BG: If we're judging Nick Sirianni by Jeffrey Lurie's historical precedent, he will not be back in 2026. The Eagles' owner simply does not tolerate offensive failure. And it's not just one bad season. Philly led the league in three-and-out percentage last year when the team was much healthier than they are in 2026. With a 5-12 record in their last 17 games, things are clearly trending in the wrong direction. It's hard to argue that Sirianni has answers or that he's doing his very best with the talent he's been given. Even if the Eagles rattle off some wins in the second half of this campaign, I can't see Lurie really buying into those as an indication of positive progress carrying over into 2027. When the Eagles won four games in a row to finish 8-8 after a 4-8 start in 2011, Lurie called that stretch "fool's gold." It became clear that he held onto Andy Reid for a season too long as the Eagles went 4-12 in 2012. And if the Eagles continue to lose, well, I just don't see how Sirianni survives that. The Eagles are four losses away from tying their longest in-season losing streak in franchise history at 11 games. If they lose out, they'll have 15 straight losses … the longest losing streak in franchise history (accounting for losing across multiple seasons). It'll be a total embarrassment that not even a Super Bowl-winning head coach can survive.

As for Hurts, that's a much trickier evaluation. We don't even know with 100% certainty that he'll ever play football again since he might have to be on blood thinners for the rest of his life. That's probably an extreme and unlikely scenario … but we have to acknowledge the non-zero possibility there. Assuming Hurts IS healthy enough to play again, there's still so much to sort through. Does the team want him back? Does he want to be back? How is his contract situation handled? Who does the new head coach (if there is one) desire at quarterback? Where do the Eagles pick in the first round? Do they like any of the college prospects? How many of those QB prospects actually declare for the draft? I just don't know how to project his future at the moment. I'm guessing he'll play for the Eagles again, even if the Super Bowl LIX MVP isn't going to be a long-term fixture here. But I really don't know how anyone could feel confident about what's going to happen.5. What's your final score prediction for this game? In what scenario do the Steelers win? In what scenario do the Eagles win?BG: For the first time since 1965, the Steelers are going to win in Philadelphia. It won't be pretty for Pittsburgh. Aaron Rodgers will struggle against an inspired Eagles defensive effort. But T.J. Watt is probably going to destroy Eagles rookie right tackle Markel Bell, causing multiple Milton turnovers.

Nick Sirianni will drop to 0-10 as a home underdog as the Eagles lose this game, 19 to 9.

The scenario where the Eagles win? Milton hits on some deep shots, and the Eagles' defense and/or special teams score at least once. Rodgers turns the ball over three times.
