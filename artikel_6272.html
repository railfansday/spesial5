United Airlines Finally Plans Boeing 757-300 Makeover With Seatback Screens And Bigger Bins

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

United Airlines plans to give its Boeing 757-300 fleet a long-overdue cabin update, adding larger overhead bins, entertainment screens at every seat, and power throughout the cabin. These aircraft have needed attention for years and were always included in the United Next refresh plan, though I am somewhat surprised to see United keep these workhorses in the fleet.

United Airlines 757-300 aircraft will be modified in accordance with the United’s “United Next” strategy, which includes installation of new Boeing Space Bins, Panasonic Audio Video On Demand (AVOD), In Flight Entertainment at every seat location, full cabin In-Seat Power Supply System (ISPS), new class dividers and cabin headers, and new UAL branding elements. Additionally, all seats and monuments will be refreshed throughout the cabin.

As I mentioned above, the surprise is not that United is refreshing its entire narrwobody fleet, which it has promised for years now, but that it is actually moving forward with the 757-300s rather than phasing them out. However, I also understand that an A321neo or 737 MAX is not necessarily a direct replacement for this workhorse.

My understanding is that one reason this project took longer to get moving was the lack of a certified larger-bin option for this particular aircraft. United had to work through that certification hurdle rather than simply order an existing installation, which puts the delay in context.

United listed just 21 Boeing 757-300s in its year-end 2025 fleet disclosure, each with 234 seats. Undertaking a specialized modification for a fleet that small is a more substantial commitment than fitting the same equipment across hundreds of aircraft. For passengers, however, the size of the subfleet is beside the point. If your flight is operated by a 757-300, the overhead storage and cabin condition are part of your United experience.

More useful bin space should make boarding less stressful: currently there are far too many bags that must be gate checked because the smaller overhead bins force larger (but standard) carry-on bags to be placed on their sides.

I will also be happy to see entertainment screens included throughout the aircraft. When traveling with my children, individual screens are especially useful.

JonNYC also mentions power and it will be great to have USB-C, but the aircraft already have universal A/C power outlets in every cabin.

But what JonNYC does not mention and I consider most important is that these aircraft will get Starlink. Currently, most 757-300s still have an ancient Panasonic Wi-Fi system that often does not work. I specifically avoid the 757-300 for this reason and have been burned almost every time I have flown it with non-operable Wi-Fi. Some 757-300s have been upgraded with Viasat, but I am unwilling to fly on the 753s unless absolutely necessary. The new cabins will be welcomed, but Starlink is the most important upgrade.

I do not object to an aircraft simply because it is older: Delta has shown that a 30-year-old aircraft with mood lighting, fast Wi-Fi, and seatback screens can feel just as comfortable as a more modern aircraft. Refreshed seating and better amenities can change that experience substantially, even when the aircraft itself has been flying for decades.

This investment suggests United sees a continuing role for the 757-300 for many years in the future.

The Boeing 757-300 is finally in line for the kind of cabin attention it has long needed. Larger bins, seatback entertainment, and Starlink Wi-Fi will make a noticeable difference. I figured these aircraft would just be retired, but it appears they will fly the “Friendly Skies” for many years to come.
