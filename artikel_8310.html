Preparing for 2200….Because One Does Not Simply “Pop Out for a bit of a Hike”

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Preparing for a thru hike on Appalachian Trail truly begins with just one burning question;  “What will I actually need to walk 2200 miles?”   That’s pretty simple and straightforward right?  Theoretically, yes………that is, of course, until you discover the mind-numbing multitude of content creator gear videos.

Suddenly, you find yourself manically comparing sleeping bags by their temperature rating, researching water filters like you’re prepping for the apocalypse, and obsessing over the ratio of weight and R-rating of every single sleeping pad in existence. Your hike is still months away, but you’ve already downloaded four GPS trail apps, scheduled transportation,  meticulously planned your daily mileage to the foot and plotted every daily campsite  down to the hour of  arrival.

Relax……You’re definitely not the first, or the last hiker to become immersed (obsessed?) in the vortex that is thru hiking prep!  Perhaps I can break it down and make it a little more palatable

First comes the backpack. It should be light, durable, comfortable, and large enough to hold everything you need—which, according to differing opinions, is both nothing and several highly specialized pieces of equipment. First, try the pack on in the store. Bring your gear, (yes, really do this) load up the pack you’re interested in, and then look yourself wearing it the in the mirror. Pay attention at how it feels.  Walk around to see how securely it sits.  Then, ask yourself….do I look ready to summit a mountain or preparing to flee a small country?  Adjust your choice according to what you see and feel.   Lastly, never, ever choose your pack based on the popularity of it, the aesthetics, or because the unsolicited opinion of some random internet blogger.

Then there’s your shelter. You want something weatherproof, spacious, easy to set up, and light enough that you won’t begin to question your life choices by mile four. Picture this;  You finally pull the trigger on the tent;  the eager  salesperson assures you that it’s really simple to pitch. You proudly take it home and then spend twenty five minutes trying to decipher the pitching instructions, and ultimately end up with what, oddly, looks like a very expensive kite!  Key advice: Research. research, research!  Take your time, debate with your eager brain. and finally choose the “one” based solely on what YOUR hiking style looks like. With so many options and opinions out there, only your opinion matters!

The art of  deciding if a sleeping bag or quilt…..down or synthetic …….or temperature rating, is for you, is another deeply personal decision.  When and where are you starting?  Average temperatures where you’ll be at that time of year?   Lightweight or no?  Inflatable pad or closed foam?  Answer these questions, imagine scenarios,  and the decision will be much clearer.

Food actually takes up surprisingly little space…..that is,  until you remember you’ll need to eat it every day. Focus on a balance of carbs, protein, and pleasure.

As a novice, I bought some of those fancy dehydrated meals labeled “creamy pasta primavera,” or “Spaghetti with meat sauce”.  I added the correct amount of boiling water, and soon I discovered that the words “creamy” or “meat” were undoubtedly creative writing! Still, in all fairness , it did have calories, and, in the end, calories are the fuel of the trail.

Clothing……now this decision becomes just another expedition into the unknown. Basically, you need layers for warmth, rain protection, sun protection, and the full understanding that your clothes may never again smell like laundry.

I pack two shirts; one for hiking, and one for sleeping, two pair of socks, two underwear, and one pair of long pants and one pair of shorts.  That’s it, because apparently I’m going into the wilderness, not to a four-day business conference.

Finally, you weigh your pack. You realize that it weighs enough that to suddenly start to reconsider your plans, question all of your hiking friendships, and immediately begrudge the person who told you that hiking was “just walking.” You remove a book, a camp mug, and a backup pair of pants. Then you eventually load them back because what if you want coffee while reading in clean pants?  I mean….that makes sense right??

Pick the gear you bring and the total weight of the full kit solely on what you’re honestly willing to carry.

The key, you’ll learn, is to pack what you need, to test it BEFORE you leave, and accept that your first big purchase may be a tiny scale. The Appalachian Trail doesn’t care whether your gear matches. It cares if you can carry it safely, use it efficiently, and still keep walking when your feet have begun filing formal complaints !

So, you now shoulder your pack, you take a few determined steps around the block, feeling great about your newfound expertise, and feel like you’re ready for anything……..but then you remember your loaded food bag is still sitting on the kitchen table.  Ugh!
