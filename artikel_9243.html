Nomad art and design fair lands at Abu Dhabi’s Modernist former airport terminal

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

“Rather than placing conventional booths inside it, we wanted to work with the building as we found it,” says Nomad’s founder and director Nicolas Bellavance-Lecompte of Zayed International Airport’s decommissioned Terminal 1 © N. Berenzhnoy; courtesy NOMAD Circle 3

The UAE is home to a trove of Modernist building designs thanks to the country’s founding in 1971 and the ensuing construction boom. One such building is the decommissioned Terminal 1 of Zayed International Airport, which was designed by the French architect Paul Andreu and opened in 1982. Now a designated heritage site, it is the unique venue for the Abu Dhabi edition of the itinerant art and design fair Nomad (18-22 November), which returns to the city for a second time in partnership with Abu Dhabi’s Department of Culture and Tourism.

“Architecture has always been fundamental to Nomad,” says the fair’s founder and director, Nicolas Bellavance‑Lecompte. “[Terminal 1] has a very particular architecture, but also a history as a place of movement and exchange. Rather than placing conventional booths inside it, we wanted to work with the building as we found it—its terminals, corridors, scale and different spatial conditions—and allow those qualities to become part of how the works are experienced.”

Simone Crestani’s Forsythia (2026), on show with Lebanese art and design gallery PIK’D at Nomad Abu Dhabi Courtesy PIK’D and NOMAD Abu Dhabi 2026

This year, the fair is expanding that mission with ambitious site-specific takeovers of four of the airport’s private terminals by Nilufar gallery, Gallery Fumi, Galerie Gastou and the jewellery advisory firm A2Z. There are 50 exhibitors taking part this year (compared with 40 last year) including local returning participants such as Leila Heller Gallery and the A/P Room as well as international spaces like Paris-based Galerie BSL, Milan’s Galleria Rossana Orlandi and Carpenters Workshop Gallery from London.

Highlights at the fair will include Maison Intègre’s bronze objects made using the ancient technique of lost wax, cast from recycled metal at its foundry in Burkina Faso; Seoul-based gallery Vein’s pairing of contemporary Korean practices with traditions including ottchil lacquer and mother-of-pearl inlay; and Polish design from Craftica Gallery.

Bellavance‑Lecompte says that jewellery will also be in the spotlight—as well as the 450 sq. m exhibition by A2Z, there will be presentations from the designers Studio Renn, Mattar jewellers and the gallery and advisory Ars Belga/Matthu-Boghossian. The Belgian jewellers Levuma will collaborate with the London gallery JCRD Design to bring together fine jewellery, diamonds and Brazilian design.

Another important development for Nomad this year is its expanded roster of institutions and non-profit initiatives on show in the old airport’s departure hall. Joining Irthi Contemporary Crafts Council—a Sharjah-based organisation that works with female artisans from the UAE and the wider Middle East, North Africa, South and Southeast Asia regions—which returns for this edition, is the recently established Design Commission Abu Dhabi and a collaborative presentation from the Institut du Monde Arabe (IMA) in Paris and House of Artisans, a cultural centre in Abu Dhabi that preserves traditional Emirati crafts. The project will bring together the French Sudanese fashion designer Abdel El Tayeb, who won the Confirmed Talent category of IMA’s 2024 Design Prize, and Emirati artisans to create a site-specific installation. “[This project] speaks to an idea at the heart of this edition: craft is a living practice, and something new can emerge when makers from different places work together,” Bellavance‑Lecompte says.

For Nomad’s inaugural Abu Dhabi edition, galleries reported sales exceeding $20m and many are “returning this year with more ambitious projects”, Bellavance‑Lecompte says. He expects that Abu Dhabi’s expanding cultural calendar and soon-to-open Guggenheim museum will bring more international collectors, curators and attention to the city and to Nomad. “Last year showed us that there is serious interest in collectable design, and this year we can build on those relationships,” he says.
