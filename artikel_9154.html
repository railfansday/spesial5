Mitakon’s $599 Medium-Format 65mm f/1.4 II Is Lighter and Upgrades Its Bokeh

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Mitakon’s $599 Medium-Format 65mm f/1.4 II Is Lighter and Upgrades Its Bokeh

Way back in 2018, Zhongyi (ZY) Optics launched the Mitakon Speedmaster 65mm f/1.4 manual-focus lens for the Fujifilm GFX mirrorless camera system. Now there’s an upgraded Mark II version, which is still ultra-fast, manual-focus, and very affordable. It’s also available for Hasselblad cameras.

The Mitakon Speedmaster 65mm f/1.4 II is lighter than its predecessor, has a new aperture diaphragm, and features an upgraded metal design.

The optical design is unchanged, with the lens still sporting 11 elements arranged across seven groups. ZY Optics says the lens, which has two High Refractive Index (HRI) and two Ultra-Low Dispersion (UD) elements, delivers “exceptional image quality” with minimized chromatic aberrations.

However, behind the glass is a new 11-bladed aperture diaphragm, up from a nine-bladed unit in the original Speedmaster 65mm f/1.4. ZY Optics says the new circular diaphragm delivers “rounder bokeh highlights.”

With its f/1.4 aperture, the lens is still among the very fastest available for the Fujifilm GFX system. Fujifilm itself doesn’t make any lenses quite as fast—its fastest options are the GF 55mm f/1.7 R WR and the GF 80mm f/1.7, both of which cost over $2,000.

Over on the Hasselblad X system, it’s a similar story. Hasselblad’s fastest lens is the XCD 1,9/80, or, put in more common terms, an 80mm f/1.9 lens. The company’s 65mm prime is the XCD 2,8/65.

Granted, Fujifilm’s and Hasselblad’s lenses are also native and equipped with autofocus, so there’s that. ZY Optics targets a different market, one less concerned with autofocus and looking to save some money.

The manual-focus prime can focus as close as 0.7 meters (2.3 feet), resulting in a maximum magnification of about 0.25x. Its 65mm focal length delivers a full-frame equivalent of just over 51mm.

Compared to its predecessor, the Mark II lens is significantly lighter. Weighing 900 grams (31.7 ounces), it sheds about 150 grams (5.3 ounces) compared to the original. The lens measures 95 millimeters (3.74 inches) long, accepts a 67mm filter thread, and features a built-in retractable lens hood.

ZY Optics says the new lens has an improved, precision-machined metal barrel. It looks a little different overall but retains the same general design, with a wide, ridged focus ring and an aperture control ring near the front of the lens. The depth-of-field scale markings have changed slightly, too.

The ZY Optics Mitakon Speedmaster 65mm f/1.4 II is available now for Fujifilm GFX and Hasselblad X mounts for $599.
