Trump's H-1B crackdown is pushing skilled tech workers to leave the U.S.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

IBM is ending visa sponsorship for most consulting jobs while the Labor Department proposes tighter permanent residency rules

Yash Sharma, a Google $GOOGL software engineer from India, plans to leave the U.S. for the U.K. next month after six years on an H-1B visa, according to Bloomberg. Sharma, 36, cited repeated changes to visa rules and slim odds of becoming a citizen.

The U.K. approved Sharma for a Global Talent visa that lets him move without a job offer, according to Bloomberg. His application for a U.S. "extraordinary ability" visa was turned down following a lengthy wait, and he has avoided traveling abroad for the past 18 months out of concern that he would not be allowed back in. He told Bloomberg the U.S. feels like a "golden prison."

The Labor Department proposed a rule earlier this week for the permanent labor certification program. It would strengthen recruitment standards and recordkeeping requirements and eliminate administrative exemptions to the labor market test, the agency said. The department called it the first comprehensive set of reforms to the program in nearly 20 years, and comments are open through Jan. 19, 2027.

Employers are adjusting as well. IBM $IBM said it is ending visa and green card sponsorship for most roles in IBM Consulting, its largest division. Affected workers can either continue with IBM from a location abroad or depart the firm before the year ends.

Boston-based Flywire disclosed in its quarterly report this month that it is considering shifting its hiring and product development to cheaper locations as a way to absorb rising H-1B visa expenses, according to Bloomberg.

The Trump administration started by issuing a proclamation that demanded $100,000 for some would-be employees living abroad, according to Bloomberg. Even though a court had halted that payment, it announced a different $103,000 charge on new H-1B applications in August.

The Labor Department last month barred Microsoft $MSFT, Adobe $ADBE and a set of large IT outsourcing companies from the Permanent Labor Certification program because of ongoing federal investigations, according to Bloomberg. Deputy Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling announced the suspensions.

The U.S. handed out roughly 260,000 fewer short-term, non-immigrant visas in the 2025 budget year compared with the previous one, according to Bloomberg, citing Migration Policy Institute analysis of federal data. That year still counted the final three complete months of former President Joe Biden’s time in office. New foreign student enrollment at U.S. universities is down an estimated 15% this fall, according to Bloomberg, citing the Institute of International Education.

According to Bloomberg, the administration contends that for years both employers and workers have exploited the immigration system, which it says has depressed wages for Americans.

Chris Thomas, an immigration lawyer and partner at Holland & Hart, told Bloomberg that some corporate clients are saying "it's too much, it's time to start offshoring." Kate Angustia, supervisory policy and practice counsel at the American Immigration Lawyers Association, told Bloomberg: “We are creating a brain drain.”
