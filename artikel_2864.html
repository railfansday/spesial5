Giants DC Dennard Wilson: ‘The sacks will come’ for Abdul Carter

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

When the New York Giants made Abdul Carter the third overall pick in the 2025 NFL Draft, it was believed that they had selected the next Micah Parsons.

After all, the two wore 11 for Penn State, were linebackers who became edge defenders, and have similarly incredible athletic traits. And like Parsons, Carter has quickly become one of the most feared pass rushers in the NFL… Except Carter hasn't produced the sacks expected of him.

Carter's lack of sack production became a hot topic of conversation around the Giants' media ecosystem this week. So much so that they prompted questions about the second-year defender when defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson spoke to the media on Thursday.

"I think he's played extremely well," Giants' defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson said when asked for his assessment of Carter's play through nine games.

"If you watch him, he's been extremely disruptive," he continued. "He's making a lot of plays."

Of course fans want to see him make plays, but what they want to see even more is Carter putting the opposing quarterback on the ground. That, however, hasn't happened much to date and he has just 2.0 on the season. But it's also difficult to watch a Giants' game and not see Carter have an impact.

"The sack numbers aren't there, but he's affecting the game," Wilson said. "A lot of holding calls, a lot of pressures. He's getting the quarterback off the spot, so the sacks will come. We're trying to put him in positions for him to get some touches on the quarterback, but he's doing everything he's supposed to do. In the run game, he's been excellent. He's holding force. The ball isn't circling outside of him. So, I love where he's at. I just wish the production was a little bit more for him right now, because he deserves it, especially the work that he's putting in."

Wilson is adamant that the sack numbers will improve as Carter continues to be disruptive during games. He also continued to praise Carter's work ethic.

"He's still a young player," he said. "He's progressing. I think he's at a good point in his career right now, and he still has more to come. So I'm excited where he's at. He just going to continue to get better. Man, you should see the guy. He works so hard in the meeting rooms, trying to understand the game plan, and he's taking pride in just being a disciplined football player. So everything else will take care of itself."

The discipline Carter has shown is of particular importance to the Giants' defense. Dennard Wilson has become one of the most aggressive defensive coordinators in the NFL, and is in the Top 5 in the NFL in blitz rate. Wilson's blitzes aren't just sending an extra man and letting everyone do their own thing. No, instead he thinks in terms of patterns to create opportunities for specific players, and every defender has his role to play in the blitz.

"We present a lot of different looks," he said, "but for us, from a conceptual teaching standpoint, guys are doing the same things from different spots at times. The blitz patterns can change. I'm a firm believer in 'you can mess with the front in terms of plugging people in different places, but you don't mess with coverage'. So in the cover standpoint, we don't change our cover structures because teams do certain things.

"But we'll do things up front to attack their known protection and their standard operating procedures on how they're going to protect certain looks, how they're going to slide," Wilson said. "So that's what we come up with our blitz patterns off of kind of what they do. We try to check their water a little bit, and if they have another answer, we get something for it. That's the attack."

Wilson added that that his blitz patterns are driven by a variety of factors that can change on a weekly basis. However, the Giants do have blitz packages designed to make use of Carter's prodigious athleticism, as well as that of players like star rookie Arvell Reese.

"There are certain things that we'll have set up for him [Crarter], but you kind of move to pieces around," Wilson said. "Because if you move to pieces around, they never know which ones are the known rushers. You have certain things we'll have for certain players depending on the blitz pattern. Some days it might be (Inside Linebacker) Arvell (Reese). Sometimes it's matchup driven. If we feel that we can attack a certain player on the line of scrimmage, we might put a guy there, because he can win fast right now, or whatever it is.

"It's a little bit of skill set. It's trying to get some of these guys some blitzes where they can come free, so they feel good about the blitz plan, and they attack it a certain way. So it's a little bit of both. With the guys that we have, we try to make them be interchangeable, so you don't know where we're come from."

It's also a bit difficult at times to remember just how new Carter still is to the position he plays. This is only his third year as a full-time edge defender, having only transitioned from off-ball linebacker in 2023 at Penn State. And while he might not be stuffing the box score in a way that's easy to write about, his incredible get-off makes him one of the most disruptive players in the NFL. That, combined with his unselfishness as a teammate, has helped make the Giants one of the best pass defenses in the NFL.

Though Carter has been under a bit of fire lately, Wilson had nothing but praise for him or the standards to which Carter holds himself.

"One thing about Abdul is this, he has high expectations," Wilson said. "I hope every player that we have has high expectations. I hope everybody in the building has high expectations. But Abdul is a team player. And that's what sometimes doesn't get noticed. And what Abdul is doing right now, he's doing his job to the best of his ability. And other things will happen for him. He's still progressing. You look at him from last year to this year and how he's playing, how dependable he is, how accountable he is. He's doing the little things correct.

"Everything else is going to take care of itself because he has that dynamic ability and that dynamic trait. It will turn on. And when it turns on, watch out."
