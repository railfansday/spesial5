What we learned from the Spurs’ loss to the Pistons

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

If you were to design the perfect game plan to defeat the San Antonio Spurs, it would probably look a lot like what J.B. Bicketstaff drew up for the Detroit Pistons. San Antonio was picked apart and played right into the Pistons' hands in a 105-93 loss that snapped the Spurs' 12-game winning streak.

San Antonio jumped out to a 14-point lead thanks in part to Victor Wembanyama playing like an alien inside, tossing the ball off the backboard and dunking all over the Pistons. Then, once the otherworldly display ended, the Pistons' game plan took hold.

On defense, the Pistons packed the paint, overplaying Wembanyama's dives to the basket, playing him incredibly physically. They dared the Spurs' guards to take threes. San Antonio obliged, throwing up 40 three-pointers, making only 9 (22%). Detroit's pressure sped up San Antonio, threw off their offensive rhythm, and led to 15 Spurs turnovers.

Offensively, the Pistons countered San Antonio's strategy of putting Wembanyama on Ausar Thompson so he could roam the paint and protect the rim. They turned Thompson into a playmaking hub, using him in perimeter dribble handoffs. So when Wembanyama sagged off, the Pistons got easy mid-range jumpers. When he played up, Detroit found cutters in the paint. The Pistons outscored the Spurs 60-44 in the paint.

This is the game plan that good teams will deploy against San Antonio. Pack the paint, don't let Wembanyama get lobs, and force the three-guard lineup to hit threes. The Spurs have to find a counter to this strategy. Teams will implement it as long as Wembanyama dominates the paint.

Wembanyama shouldn't be exempt from criticism in the loss. Yes, he led the team with 25 points and 18 rebounds, but he only scored 8 points in the second half. That's because he settled for difficult jumpers instead of getting into the paint as he did in the first half. Wembanyama was taking what the defense gave him, but they were giving it to him for a reason. He is letting teams off the hook when he takes deep threes or pull-ups. The Spurs need more ways to get him the ball inside the arc with room to score.

One way Wembanyama has improved his scoring this year is in the post. His baseline turnaround jump shot has become one of his go-to moves. It's been falling, but he only took one of them against Detroit.

The bench unit continued to struggle. The scoring falls off a cliff when Wembanyama goes to the bench for a lot of reasons. San Antonio insists on playing Dylan Harper and Stephon Castle together, letting Fox go to the bench. Those two do not playmake at the same level as Fox, so it ends up turning into the guards going one-on-one, with Luke Kornet playing essentially no offensive role. If Fox were to stay in alongside one of Castle or Harper, at least the Spurs would have a true point guard who could set up one-dimensional offensive players like Kornet and Julian Champagnie.

Defensively, the units with Kornet and Keldon Johnson have struggled. That two-man lineup has a defensive rating of 116.8 and a net rating of -17.1

Devin Vassell's shooting slump has hurt San Antonio. The three-guard lineup only works if he can make defenses pay for packing the paint. Vassell went 2-for-6 from three on Tuesday night and is now shooting 31.4% from deep this season.

Harper left the game with what seemed like a non-contact groin injury. If it's serious, that could mean the guard will miss at least a few weeks, at most a month and some change. That's a big problem for San Antonio, which has relied on Harper's scoring particularly in close games. Expect a combination of Jordan McLaughlin, David Jones-Garcia, and the rookie Ja'Kobi Gillespie to fill in. My two cents: give the rookie a chance. Gillespie would give them some much-needed pull-up shooting in bench lineups.
