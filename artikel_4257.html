Trees Hate You Review: You’ll Enjoy Hating Them Back

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Trees Hate You uses unpredictable traps and cartoon-style humor in a short, silly rage game, though its bugs and limited challenge may frustrate some.

It’s a very odd time in gaming where we’re seeing a lot more games coming out than ever before. Some are better than others, but many seem to simply have some fun and interesting premises that draw you in.

One such game would be Trees Hate You, which is a ridiculous rage game about being targeted by trees. Sounds like it shouldn’t be very good, but surprisingly the game’s Looney Tunes-esque approach to mechanics makes it an enjoyable game.

I played the game for around four hours, though a speed run could be significantly quicker, and came away enjoying most of my time with the game. It isn’t a perfect game, but it is a fun game, especially if you’re just looking for a silly little rage game.

It’s not a complex or complicated game in all reality, but that’s part of its charm. You play as a person who’s being hunted by trees for some reason or another and must navigate through several locations to finally reach your destination.

There’s very little in the way of story since virtually all of the information presented is through the visuals of the game. You’ll need to make your way through many dangerous traps that will change, sometimes seemingly at random.

It isn’t a perfect game, but it is a fun game, especially if you’re just looking for a silly little rage game.

There are four “levels,” three of which are separated into smaller sections to make it more digestible. Each section has a central theme and introduces some new trees while finding creative ways to make you scream obscenities.

It’s a pretty short and sweet game that culminates in a very, very odd ending. There aren’t really any abilities to get or anything to make the game easier. From the outset of the game, you’ve got your movement buttons and whatever brain power you can muster.

For a lot of rage games, each section of the game is stagnant so that you can learn from your mistakes and grow smarter. In this game, that entire ideology is completely thrown out the window, and it makes it much more interesting to play.

For almost every section of the game, when you die you end up back at the last checkpoint, but you’ll notice that something might be a little different. Sometimes it’s a new tree popping up along the path or a new path entirely.

This seemed to change at random upon death, and figuring out how to handle the changes added a new layer to the game. For some, it’s going to be frustrating in a different way, since it does change, meaning memorizing the path isn’t particularly helpful.

I thoroughly enjoyed that change of pace. As a big fan of great roguelikes, seeing that sort of game design appear here gave me pause at first until I got used to it. It managed to keep the game fresh despite the short run time.

There was a lot about this game that I thoroughly enjoyed, but there is no denying that the game felt way too short. Perhaps that was because I simply had played through some other rage games recently that prepared me for this, but still I felt it could’ve been a much longer experience.

Being a short experience isn’t too bad, as there are a few things that you can grind for if you really want, such as coins and hats, but at the end of the day this is a game you could beat in a single session. I suppose I was hoping to have a bit more oomph out of the game, though what we did get was more than fine.

There is a challenge mode that changes up some of the levels and makes things a bit more complicated with more traps and threats, but the levels will always remain the same between deaths. It was nice to provide the option to give players more bang for their buck.

The game doesn’t really waste any time on things that aren’t humorous or torturing you, which is a big bonus. As I approached the end, though, I had hoped the game would surprise me and get a bit longer or more ridiculous, but there was no such luck.

Video games are not often known for their sense of humor, and even less so for their ability to utilize visual gags. Trees Hate You has decided to right this wrong by making what effectively feels like a Looney Tunes episode into an entire game.

At first it was just some slight things, moments that felt naturally funny because of the tension and ridiculousness of the game. That changed after a few levels when trees began to do whatever they pleased, seemingly just to mess with me.

There is one tree in particular that embodies this ideology entirely. One tree blocks your path early on in the game by bending at insane angles to force you in another direction. This same tree will do this for the entirety of the game.

You’ll come across a path sometime later, and suddenly out of the sky the tree will reach down and block your path. This was incredibly idiotic and amazing. I had to stop and try it again just to make sure I could see what exactly was going on.

It’s a pretty hilarious game, especially when you run into those annoyingly funny moments that make you feel like you’ve been outsmarted by the trees. Even if you’re not a big fan of this kind of humor, it’s still enjoyable to see all the unique ways the trees manage to get you.

This is going to be something that I think gets to be a little divisive amongst people who play the game. On the one hand, the game isn’t quite as difficult as it could be, but on the other hand, the changing mechanics could make it more difficult for some.

I would’ve liked to see the game be a bit more difficult the first go around, but there is a challenge mode. It sort of balances out, giving everyone a chance to enjoy something about the game, but there were only a few sections that really got to me.

It’s a pretty hilarious game, especially when you run into those annoyingly funny moments that make you feel like you’ve been outsmarted by the trees.

It feels like the game is more about trial and error rather than conquering any difficult mechanics, and that’s more than okay, but it does take away some of the feeling of accomplishment. In reality, it feels like the game is more designed to watch others fail than to be played itself.

Ironically, there’s a lot of time to sit with what happens when you die in some crazy fashion. You’ll respawn at a checkpoint with virtually no risk, meaning you’ll never need to complete a past section a second time.

Trees Hate You is an interesting rage game that leans more heavily into the absurdity of the genre than the actual rage. Still, it’s a hilarious and ridiculously fun game. You’re going to walk away from the game having enjoyed yourself immensely despite some of the more pressing issues. There are annoying bugs that will force you to start from a prior checkpoint, but all in all, the game is a very good time.
