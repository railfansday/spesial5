Bitcoin Struggles to Hold $64,000 as ETF Outflows Hit $2.9B in November

Bitcoin is struggling to hold the $64,000 level as investors continue to pull money from spot Bitcoin ETFs and brace for another interest rate hike from the Federal Reserve.

The leading cryptocurrency was trading at around $63,800 on Thursday, down 1% on the day and 13% over the past week, according to Decrypt data. It has hovered between roughly $63,000 and $65,000 since the weekend, briefly dipping below $63,000 and touching a 24-hour low of $62,068, per CoinGecko data.

"Bitcoin remains under selling pressure," Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com, told Decrypt, noting that it has fallen roughly 26% from its late-September peak near $87,000. "Elevated Treasury yields, dollar strength and expectations of further Fed tightening are weighing on risk appetite, while the broader technology selloff has added to the pressure."

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $306.4 million in net outflows on Wednesday, after $239.9 million on Tuesday, according to SoSoValue data. The funds have seen outflows on nine of the past 10 trading days, with Monday's small $8 million inflow the only exception, and have lost about $2.9 billion so far in November. Their total net assets have fallen to $77.7 billion. Bitcoin miners have added to the selling, with major miners including Riot and MARA selling some of their holdings to fund a shift into AI infrastructure.

Monday's brief inflow "does not signal a market inflection point," Tim Sun, senior researcher at HashKey, told Decrypt. Net short positions in Bitcoin futures held by leveraged funds on CME have fallen to their lowest level since early 2023, and futures basis yields have narrowed, he said, indicating that hedge funds have largely unwound the basis trade that paired spot ETF purchases with short futures. That means the current outflows are mainly driven by investors actively cutting their Bitcoin exposure, Sun said.

"If macro expectations do not improve, investors may shift from buying the dip to selling into rallies, creating additional overhead resistance on subsequent rebounds," he added.

"Recent Bitcoin ETF outflows and leveraged liquidations" have amplified the decline, Hathorn said. "Despite stronger Nvidia earnings, the market has struggled to stage a significant rebound, suggesting confidence remains fragile. A sustained recovery may require renewed ETF inflows and a more supportive interest-rate outlook."

Some traders have also pointed the finger at Anthropic's upcoming IPO for drawing money away from crypto, though analysts have disputed that the listing is draining crypto liquidity, attributing it instead to ETF outflows, forced selling and rising interest rates.

Traders see a 90.7% chance that the Fed will raise rates by a quarter point at its December 9 meeting, and a 9.3% chance of a half-point hike, according to CME FedWatch. The odds of a half-point move have risen from 6.5% a day earlier. Fed Governor Christopher Waller has called for an even bigger, half-point increase, unless policymakers signal another rise in January.

Hathorn said $64,000 is the immediate level to watch, followed by support around $62,000 and the psychological $60,000 mark. A sustained recovery above $66,000 to $68,000 could ease selling pressure, "but the broader trend remains bearish while lower highs persist," she said.

Onchain analytics firm Glassnode said in its weekly report on Tuesday that the crash had pushed investors’ realized losses to levels seen only near past market lows in 2018, 2020, 2022 and early 2023, a sign of capitulation. Bitcoin is now holding a dense block of recent buyers between $62,000 and $66,000, which grew by about 66,000 BTC during the two days of heaviest selling, Glassnode said. But a daily close below that block could set off a fast drop toward the June low of $58,500, and the Realized Price, the average price at which all Bitcoin last moved, at $53,700.

Traders on Decrypt Predict see a 43% chance that Bitcoin will fall to $60,000 at some point before the end of November, and a 12% chance that it will drop as low as $55,000. They give just a 2% chance that it will climb back to $82,500 this month.

There are some signs of support. About 1.1 million BTC, roughly 5.5% of supply, last changed hands at prices between $60,000 and $64,000, and that band has grown by more than 25,000 BTC in recent days, according to Neil Steinhardt, head of Nexo U.S., in a note shared with Decrypt Wednesday. A dense base of holders with costs near current prices "can act as a support zone, though it is not a floor," he wrote.

Bitcoin has also held up better than gold. The amount of gold one Bitcoin buys rose above its 200-day average this week, to about 17.1 ounces, around 40% above its March low, Steinhardt said.
