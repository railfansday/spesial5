After the loss to the Bills, it feels like Aaron Glenn’s fate is sealed

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

"I am out of carrots. I am out of sticks. Mr. Scott has time and again proven himself to be an unmanageable employee, and a poor branch manager. I recommend he be removed from that position and re-assigned to sales, where he belongs."

That quote is from a performance review of Michael Scott from the television series, The Office.

Michael Scott didn't end up losing his job as branch manager of Dunder-Mifflin in the end. Nevertheless, the review remains relatable.

Sometimes people can improve their job performance, but other times you reach a realization that they just aren't capable of doing it.

We are past that stage with Aaron Glenn. We can point to any number of dubious marks of his resume as Jets head coach. He is now 5-22, sporting a worse winning percentage as an NFL head coach than Hue Jackson. Glenn now has more than twice as many double digit point losses than he has wins. The latest defeat was the ugly 31-12 blowout at the hands of the Buffalo Bills in Week 10.

There isn't much drama left surrounding Glenn. Barring an unexpected late season surge, he will not retain his job as Jets head coach for a third year. Even if there is a late season winning streak, it still might not be enough.

NFL teams don't reassign their coach to a different department. Coaching contracts aren't written that way, and practical considerations make the sort of demotion recommended for Scott unviable.

Glenn will be let go. He will make enough money to leave football if he so chooses. If he decides to continue his coaching career, he will be an in demand candidates for teams seeking a new defensive coordinator.

In the meantime, we will have to endure seven more games. That will be no easy task.

I am sure the Jets will invent new ways to lose along the way.

In some ways, the Jets' Week 10 loss to the Bills was familiar. Like a week earlier against the Chiefs, the Jets stayed competitive through one half of football. Their offense settled for field goals instead of scoring touchdowns. Then things fell apart in the second half.

This loss still was in many ways different from what we have seen before. Leave it to the Aaron Glenn Jets to lose by three scores in a game where they had an equal number of first downs to their opponent and only 13 fewer total yards.

The Jets showed many of the coaching flaws that have plagued the team throughout the season.

On offense this team shows no understanding of how to use its talent to the greatest effect. At this point, it feels like salary is the main consideration driving the decision to lean on Breece Hall as the lead back over Braelon Allen. It certainly isn't performance. The Jets can lean on saying, "A starter can't lose his job to injury," all they want. Whether or not Hall missed time from injury, Allen has outplayed him.

The Jets struggle just as much to play to their strengths in the passing game. It's easy to dismiss Kenyon Sadiq's lack of a role as a developing rookie showing inconsistency. But when backup wide receiver Isaiah Williams gets 7 targets, and a mismatch like Sadiq only gets 2, it speaks to mistaken priorities in the game plan.

The Jets can't put the ball in the end zone. There are no simple explanations for that. I'm sure it didn't help that they had a fourth round rookie quarterback with zero first team reps through the week playing half the game, but the inability to score touchdowns has been a season-long problem. Anyone watching the team would likely conclude that play calling has been a factor.

On the defensive side of the ball, the Jets are simply playing bad football. Aaron Glenn referred to play calling as his superpower, but Glenn's inability to orchestrate this defense has him looking like a mere mortal.

I think there's a danger for the Jets in blaming all of their problems on coaching. The roster isn't good enough just yet. Part of this is that the team had a long way to rebuild after their attempts to win a Super Bowl around Aaron Rodgers failed. Part of it is the team has made too many personnel misfires. The Jets need to ask themselves hard questions about the way they do business on many levels as they chart out their future.

For now, though, the coaching is the obvious and glaring problem at the center of everyone's attention.

Seven games remain, and unlike binging a television show, we can't just skip ahead to the next season when we don't like what we are seeing.
