Alex Pereira's first coach warns he's too heavy for Sergei Pavlovich fight at UFC 335

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

AlexPereira's first coach, Belocqua Wera, believes the formertwo-division Ultimate Fighting Championship champion should enter hisheavyweight bout against SergeiPavlovich lighter than planned.Pereira is scheduled to face Pavlovich in the UFC335 co-main event on Dec. 12. He told Ariel Helwani he weighed245 pounds and intended to compete at around 240 to 243 pounds.Wera told Sherdog that would be too heavy.

"His main mistake against Gane was going in heavy, losing the speedand explosiveness that are his greatest qualities. He has to facePavlovich between 231 and 236 pounds, where he keeps his agilityand explosiveness," Wera said.Wera, who gave Pereira his "Poatan" nickname, believes weight wasnot the only factor in his loss to Ciryl Ganeat UFC Freedom 250."It's easy to point out mistakes from the couch at home, but Ithink he got dazzled by that big show at the White House andabandoned the warrior posture. He made a mistake by not feintingthe jab in that moment when he took a counter jab that atheavyweight carries the power of a straight right. Obviously it isa fact that when he went down the opponent had a foul posture thathampered his recovery, but he was already damaged," he said.Wera nevertheless expects Pereira to rebound against Pavlovich."The first thing is the warrior posture. He has to face the Russianand little by little impose his better technique. Alex is going towin."

Despite his concerns about Pereira's weight, Wera believesheavyweight is the right division for him."He has every condition to be champion at heavyweight. Especiallybecause he faced heavyweights his whole life. What he can't do istry to gain a lot of weight to be a forced heavyweight," Werasaid.The coach also recalled encouraging Pereira to explore hisIndigenous ancestry."As a Brazilian, I've always been interested in my Indigenousroots. In fact, my kickboxing style is based on them. Poatan becamecurious and asked his mother if there were any indigenous people inthe family, and his mother confirmed that his grandparents wereindigenous. When he told me, we started working on that," Werasaid.Wera said he incorporated Tupinamba war rituals into Pereira'straining to cultivate a warrior spirit. Pereira's ancestry andpunching power also inspired his nickname."I took him to sign a contract with his first sponsor, and the guyasked for a nickname, and I said 'Poatan,' which doesn't mean'hands of stone.' If it meant 'hands of stone,' it would be Po(hand) Ita (stone)," Wera said.Wera said he also suggested "Itsari" by Sepultura, which Pereiracontinues to use as his walkout music.

During six years with Wera, Pereira won São Paulo state, Brazilianand WGP kickboxing championships before attracting interest fromGlory."They wanted him to spend some time training Dutch kickboxing, andI didn't allow it," Wera said. "I believed they would take himthere and remove the characteristics of Brazilian kickboxing.Instead, I took him to my brother's farm, and we spent a monththere, training."According to Wera, their partnership ended after Pereira's earlyGlory victories because of a sponsor's offer. Pereira has continuedto acknowledge his former coach's role in his career and visitedWera's gym in São Bernardo do Campo after winning his first UFCtitle.Wera told Sherdog he is facing financial difficulties and hasstarted an online fundraisingcampaign to help him continue teaching."I want to be able to keep fulfilling my dream, not only of formingchampions but also of helping people with depression, alcoholism,panic syndrome. I count on everyone's help to continue this story,"he said.
