Around the West: Will the Spurs ever lose again?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

I'm back with another update about the rest of the Western Conference. Just like last week, both the San Antonio Spurs and Los Angeles Lakers are on lengthy winning streak joyrides. The Spurs have won 12 straight while Los Angeles has won eight in a row. Anyhow, let's get to it.

Net ratings are courtesy of Cleaning the Glass and exclude garbage time.

Led by Victor Wembanyama and Dylan Harper, the Spurs have been absolutely dominant since an 0-2 start. They rank third in both offensive and defensive rating and are pummeling virtually every team they face. Their matchup with the Lakers on Nov. 27 could be quite illuminating for both sides.

An undefeated 4-0 week helped Houston wash away the taste of a 2-3 stretch following its 5-0 start. After a three-week absence, Steven Adams returned to the lineup during the Rockets' 147-119 rout of the Washington Wizards Sunday evening while Alperen Şengün dropped 43 points, 12 rebounds and five assists in a win over the Utah Jazz on Friday to continue his career-best campaign.

The Blazers rebounded from back-to-back losses against the Spurs and Lakers to twice knock off the shorthanded Oklahoma City Thunder before scoring an easy road win over the Sacramento Kings. Damian Lillard's shooting has really invigorated this group and he looks pretty dang good for a 36-year-old coming off a torn Achilles.

The Timberwolves were en fuego before the Chicago Bulls surprisingly beat the brakes off them Saturday night. Still, they've done quite well without Anthony Edwards, largely thanks to LaMelo Ball, Rudy Gobert, Jaden McDaniels, Ayo Dosunmu and Jonathan Kuminga. They'll be tested this week with a home back-to-back against the Blazers before welcoming the New York Knicks and Memphis Grizzlies to town.

After losing three in a row, the Grizzlies have won three of their last four against an undermanned Toronto Raptors team and the lottery-bound New Orleans Pelicans and Brooklyn Nets. Wins are wins but we'll get a better gauge of their quality this week when they face the Cleveland Cavaliers and Minnesota Timberwolves on the road. What I do know is Cedric Coward absolutely rules.

The Thunder had a tough week, losing both ends of a road back-to-back against Portland before seeing Darius Garland dash their NBA Cup dreams with a game-winning and-one Friday night. They did snap their three-game skid Sunday with a tidy win over the Kings, though, as Shai Gilgeous-Alexander tallied 37 points on 13-of-20 shooting. Down three starters, including two All-Stars, Oklahoma City remains ravaged by injury and trying to stay afloat.

The Nuggets have oddly played a league-low 11 games thus far while other teams have already played 14. Regardless, they've looked a bit better since a 3-3 start and have won four of five. Nikola Jokić is gradually rounding into form and Jamal Murray has been sensational. Duels with the Rockets and Boston Celtics this week should challenge them.

How quickly circumstances change in the NBA. The Warriors had won eight in a row entering Wednesday's showdown with Houston. Then, Stephen Curry exited early because of injury. He's expected to miss around one month with an oblique abdominal tear, according to ESPN's Shams Charania. Golden State dropped a close one to Houston Wednesday then was a dead man walking against the Spurs on Friday and lost by 38. They'll hope to shift the vibes Monday night against New Orleans.

Speaking of circumstances changing quickly, hello, Dallas Mavericks. Last week, I heaped praise upon Cooper Flagg and the new-look Mavs. Now, they've lost three straight games by a combined 86 points — including one to the second-string Cleveland Cavaliers — and Flagg's shot 16-of-46 from the floor over that span. Woof.

The Suns are just a bit of a jumbled mess. They rarely seem competitive against fellow playoff hopefuls, are severely undersized defensively and often find themselves in rotation with no way to counteract it. Devin Booker is scoring the heck out of the ball, however, averaging 24.8 points on 60.9% true shooting.

Following a 1-7 start, the Clippers have established a bit of traction with three wins in four tries. They'll look to push their winning streak to three games Tuesday night against the Jazz.

The good vibes of a four-game winning streak are a total relic now. New Orleans has lost six straight and Dejounte Murray recently requested a trade. The Pelicans are in a disarray and play such sticky, incongruent basketball because of a funky roster makeup.

The Kings are winless at home and rarely even keep games close. But rookie guard Darius Acuff Jr. is a legitimate bright spot, and I'm intrigued by Dylan Cardwell's bouncy, energy-infused skill-set. He and Maxime Raynaud could be a fun front-court pairing whenever Raynaud returns from injury.

The Jazz are bad and injured. It's a terrible place to be. The absences of Jaren Jackson Jr., Lauri Markkanen and Jusuf Nurkić leave them incredibly depleted up front. They do not get to the rim, are a poor, low-volume outside shooting team and rarely tilt defenses with their passing. It's a tough recipe. Darryn Peterson does seem to be finding his stride, though, and that's among the most crucial storylines for Utah this season.
