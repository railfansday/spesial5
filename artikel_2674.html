T.J. Lang blasts "despicable" Aaron Rodgers comments about Mike McCarthy

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

T.J. Lang blocked for Aaron Rodgers for eight seasons in Green Bay. Currently, Lang would be more inclined to knock Rodgers on his ass.

Lang sharply criticized Rodgers for his decision to go public with issues that should have stayed in the Pittsburgh locker room.

“I think it’s despicable,” Lang said on 97.1 The Ticket in Detroit, via Sean Keeley of Awful Announcing. “I think that Aaron Rodgers just became everything that he has bitched about and moaned about over the last 10 years with former coaches, former teammates, airing grievances out, giving opinions out in the media.

“He has blacklisted, I don’t know how many, former teammates from just simple comments telling the truth because he just — he was sick of it. Honestly, I don’t even know if I want to watch the whole thing. It’s just unnecessary. It’s a victim mentality. It’s a ‘poor me, woe is me. Oh, I thought this, and it wasn’t that.’ And I’m sick, man.

“I’ve been in a group chat with a whole lot of former teammates that feel the exact same way that I do.”

Most football players would feel that way. There’s a code in the locker room. Rodgers violated it.

“Mike McCarthy was my head coach for 8 years, and I love Mike McCarthy,” Lang said. “And I talked to Mike McCarthy on Monday night, and still have a great relationship with him.

“I haven’t talked to Aaron Rodgers in probably 5 years. I can’t remember the last time he returned a text. I don’t have a relationship with him. And I think it’s just absolutely despicable what he just did to Big Mike.”

Rodgers isn’t a whistleblower. He’s a troublemaker. He didn’t get his way, so he emptied a can of gasoline and tossed a match over his shoulder on the way out the door.

How does any of it help his now-former teammates? Rodgers’s decision to air out dirty laundry has created a massive distraction that will do nothing to help the team win.

Accurate or not, the task at hand becomes harder now that everyone is buzzing about Rodgers’s comments.

That said, no one should be surprised. It’s part and parcel of doing business with Rodgers. When it ends — and end it will — it will end poorly. Like it did in Green Bay. Like it did in New York. Like it did in Pittsburgh.

Like it will end for his next team, if there is one. And if there is, the message is clear.
