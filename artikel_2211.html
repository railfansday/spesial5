What teams AI thinks will be in the EFL Championship in the 2050/51 season

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

It's always fun to predict how things will look in 20, 50 or even 100 years time, but how will the EFL tables look in 2050?

The Championship is ever-changing. The likes of Lincoln City and Wrexham have made their returns in recent years after decades away, while certain established Premier League clubs are always threatened with dropping into the second tier, too.

Predicting how things will look in the 2050/51 campaign, or in 24 seasons, is almost impossible. The second tier in the 2002/03 season saw Portsmouth win the title and be promoted alongside Leicester City and Wolves, but the likes of Gillingham, Walsall and Grimsby Town were all alongside them in the division.

So, which clubs does AI believe will be in the Championship come 2050? FLW has consulted ChatGPT to find out.

In an ideal world, Birmingham City would be in the Champions League, rather than the Championship, in the future, given the ambition of owner Tom Wagner and the amount of investment put into the Blues side in recent years.

That ownership and investment may not be there in 24 years, though, which could lead to this project crashing and burning.

By 2025, Birmingham will be more than settled in their new 62,000-capacity 'powerhouse stadium', but AI feels that it will be hosting second-tier football.

Blackburn Rovers are one of the longer-serving Championship clubs right now, with this their ninth successive campaign since they were promoted out of League One.

Recent years have seen them bounce between relegation battles and play-off pushes, and under Tony Mowbray, they look to be pretty steady. AI feels that they'll level out back in the second tier come 2050.

Bolton Wanderers' seven-year exile from the Championship came to an end this season, with their long-awaited promotion from League One serving as the final stage of their fightback from near liquidation back in 2019.

The passionate Trotters fanbase will be hoping that the coming years will see them become an established second-tier outfit with the prospect of making a return to the Premier League, and perhaps they will do that in the next couple of decades.

But by 2050/51, AI feels that Bolton will once again be a Championship side.

The first non-current Championship side in the list is Bradford City. The Bantams haven't been a second-tier side in over two decades, but AI feels that they will be in the next 24 years.

Many felt that the West Yorkshire outfit could have gotten into the Championship next season, and they still could, but the departure of Graham Alexander has definitely wounded them, and new boss Michael Duff still has a lot of work to do to get fans onside.

But this is a well-supported club on the rise, and even if it isn't this season, that promotion from League One doesn't seem too far away.

Bristol City being a Championship club at any point feels inevitable. This year marks 18 of the last 20 seasons spent at this level, so perhaps 24 years on from this term, they'll still be finishing 12th in the second tier.

The Robins are at the start of a project led by Michael Skubala, off the back of a summer with some decent investment in promising players across the field, most of whom have come from League One.

If all goes well, perhaps Bristol City could exceed this expectation and be a well-run Premier League side?

Cardiff City haven't historically been a side famed for sticking around in one division. Since the 1974/75 season, the longest stint they've had in one league was a ten-year one in the Championship before their promotion in 2012/13.

Their last second-tier run lasted just six years before relegation in 2024/25, and they answered that with promotion back at the first time of asking.

With Brian Barry-Murphy at the helm, the focus right now is on going back up again, but there'll be no doubt there will be a different direction and another man at the helm come 2050.

Bluebirds fans hope that they'll be able to take on what they hope is a Premier League job after BBM leaves, but AI sees them back in the second tier by 2050.

Charlton Athletic are in a bit of a sticky situation in the Championship right now, failing to win any of their last 11 games and losing four straight. Nathan Jones is praying that Burnley don't get any better anytime soon, else they risk falling into the bottom three.

24 seasons is a long time. Perhaps there's a drop back into League One on the horizon for the Addicks, but ChatGPT at least believes they'll get over that hump and get back into the second tier.

Derby County are another side in real peril at this moment in time. They're also a team that has become synonymous with the Championship over the past couple of decades.

Since being relegated from the Premier League in 2002, 24 years ago, they've spent one year back in the top flight, two in League One, and 21 in the second tier. Given the Rams have been a play-off side in quite a few of those seasons, too, it's quite an achievement for them not to have gone up more than once.

So, in the coming 24 years, it makes sense to put Derby in amongst this Championship lineup.

The second non-current Championship side in the 2050/51 lineup is a club many expect to be a second-tier outfit in 2027/28.

Huddersfield Town have enjoyed a brilliant start to life under Martin Drury, dominating both the attacking and defensive metrics. They have a duo in Joe Taylor and Derensili Sanches Fernandes who have 25 goal involvements between them, and they sit atop League One, looking less and less likely to relinquish that position anytime soon.

With ambitious owner Kevin Nagle overseeing things, he'll be hoping to be a Premier League regular by the time he's ready to relinquish control. By 2050, though, AI feels that the Terriers will be back in the second tier.

Both Bradford and Huddersfield will be joined by West Yorkshire rivals and the first current Premier League side on the list, Leeds United, which will make for some tasty matchups over the year for sure.

The Whites put in a brilliant showing in the second half of last season to retain their top-flight status, and after starting this year on the front foot, some Leeds fans are expressing some frustration at the number of draws their side are seemingly settling for.

Again, 24 years is a long time, so perhaps Daniel Farke can turn things around again and bring the Yorkshire giants back to being European contenders. But the German won't be in charge come 2050, and ChatGPT believes that they'll have slid back into the Championship by then.
