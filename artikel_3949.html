Every time the very concept of a GTA 6 has come up in the past eight years, I've had just one wistful opinion to contribute: "I hope they make it more like Red Dead Redemption 2."

I'm not a huge GTA guy, but I am a Red Dead head. The sequel is Rockstar's best work (maybe second best now?) because of all the ways it prioritizes roleplay and choice-making by simulating the little things: Mixing tonics, crafting bullets individually, grooming Arthur's mustache. God, I love that stuff.

There was no way Rockstar would repeat all of these life sim-y touches for the louder and faster GTA 6, but I am surprised and elated that my favorite Red Dead 2 feature of them all made the cut: Your car is your horse.

That is to say, Jason and Lucia's personal vehicles are their mobile bases—an essential extension of them. You can't pet or name it, sadly, but it's important to keep track of and take care of your whip, because it's where you keep all your guns and outfits. It's all literally in the trunk: If you want to fetch your shotgun, you have to walk to the back of the car, open it up, and fish it out of a box.

Gone are the days of keeping seven assault rifles, ten SMGs, and four rocket launchers in your back pocket—you can only equip two "long" guns on your back, and large melee weapons like bats have to be stowed away, too. Aside from how cool it is to fish a shotgun out of my trunk like a disgruntled Jason Statham character, the limitation is forcing me to think twice about how to approach missions.

And unlike in Red Dead's lawless towns, the NPCs of Leonida seem to react negatively to the guy walking around with assault rifles on his back. The game tells you early on that police pay closer attention to you when openly carrying firearms—another reason to stow them away when you don't need 'em.

The car trunk is also where extra outfits can be stored. You can bring along five by default (more than Red Dead's saddles allowed), and even this isn't just for vanity. GTA 6's wanted system goes further than police chases: if you lose the cops, your wanted level will linger and cops identify you based on your description, clothes, and your car. The easiest way to go incognito after raising cain is to pull over, pop the trunk, and change outfits.

It's all very smart and cool and I love it. Though I can also understand why others wouldn't—it's a significant break from what GTA has been up to this point. The two gun limit means murderous rampages might end earlier than they used to, and I can foresee getting annoyed the first time I bring a sniper rifle to a shotgun fight and have no way to correct that. But I think this reflects how Rockstar designed GTA 6 to be just as much a life sim as a crime fantasy.

GTA 6 wants me to constantly make choices and consider what a real human would do. Like in one mission last night, I was left alone without my personal vehicle for the first time. Instead of Rockstar suggesting I grand theft an auto, a tutorial popped for calling a cab. So yea, I took a yellow cab back to my car a few miles away, and it was a chill ride, except for the cabbie who rudely shot down my attempts to make small talk. He's lucky my guns were in my car.
