Regal Cinemas May Switch From Pepsi To Coca-Cola Next Year, Insiders Reveal

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Coke or Pepsi? These days, we don’t have much choice.

Rumors are swirling that Regal Cinemas plans to end its partnership with Pepsi and start serving Coca-Cola products at theaters nationwide starting next year. The chain hasn’t publicly announced the switch, but one Regal employee on Reddit appeared to confirm that it’s not just hearsay.

“As a current employee, I can confirm this,” they wrote. “We had some Coke reps visit our location and take photos and see our soda layouts in the concessions area.”

Regal has bounced between the two beverage companies over the years. The movie theater chain served Pepsi products from its founding until 2002, when it pivoted to an 18-year exclusive contract with Coca-Cola. Regal reverted to Pepsi products in 2020 and called the partnership “an epic love story destined for the big screen.” Now, it seems like there’s no love lost.

Moviegoers are largely excited by this development. “Oh this is absolutely the best news possible!! It's literally been the singular downside to becoming such a Regal goer the past few years,” one X user wrote. Another added, “I am a Pepsi enjoyer, but if this is what it takes to get a cola-flavored Icee back in my Regal then I am all for it.”

If true, Regal would join a growing list of chains dropping Pepsi as its beverage partner. Last summer, Costco ended its 12-year contract to start serving Coca-Cola in its food courts. And, just last month, KFC announced that it, too, would start serving Coca-Cola products.

Anybody who keeps tabs on the drink company is probably unsurprised. PepsiCo has shuttered two bottling plants in Maryland and Tennessee since September. In a third-quarter earnings call last month, CEO Ramon Laguarta said that the company isn’t “competing well in soft drinks” and that he doesn’t “feel good” about how its beverage business is performing.

The company is also planning price hikes across its food and beverage portfolio to compensate for the rising costs of aluminum and diesel. With increasing prices and shrinking distribution, it appears serving Pepsi is losing its luster for some foodservice chains. Whether or not it stops with Regal remains to be seen.
