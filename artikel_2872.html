The Nasdaq Is Approaching Bear Market Territory: 2 Stocks Down 22% and 37% to Buy Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Investors continued to sell stocks leading into the Thanksgiving holiday.

By Bram Berkowitz – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:42AM EST

On the eve of Thanksgiving, selling resumed as investors returned to risk-off sentiment.

As of 11:39 a.m. ET, the Dow Jones Industrial Average had fallen roughly 785 points, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite declined over 4%. The Nasdaq is now down over 17% from its Oct. 6 peak and is nearing bear-market territory.

On Nov. 25, the October reading of the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge, the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, came in hotter than expected.

It rose 3.8% year-over-year on a seasonally adjusted basis, in line with estimates. However, core PCE, excluding more volatile food and energy prices, rose 3.3%, or 0.1% above what economists had expected. The latest inflation data suggests a high likelihood that the Fed will raise interest rates at its final meeting of the year on Dec. 9.

Furthermore, the Trump administration just imposed new tariffs on 14 major trading partners, including China and the European Union.

While sentiment remains depressed, take advantage of the dip and buy these two tech stocks down 22% and 37% this year right now.

The streaming giant Netflix (NFLX -2.53%) has had a difficult year, as the market has grown concerned that artificial intelligence and shifting consumer preferences will displace the sector.

Multiple reports of declining user engagement among viewers have rocked the investment thesis. Some investors believe AI will lower the barrier to entry for content creation, while consumers seem more interested in short-form content, whether on social media or YouTube.

Netflix Co-CEO Greg Peters essentially acknowledged on the company's recent earnings call that engagement among U.S. viewers, Netflix's most mature market, could be better. Yet the company's results remain quite strong. In the third quarter, revenue grew nearly 12% year over year, while diluted earnings per share grew 5%.

Management lowered its full-year ad revenue outlook by $300 million, citing a slower rollout of ad product capabilities. But this shouldn't lead investors to immediately assume this has to do with engagement.

Furthermore, Netflix has the ability to adapt, whether it's bringing on new forms of content to its platform, which it has already begun to do, or leveraging AI to improve its content recommendation and ad targeting capabilities. I also think the company still has a strong moat in creating high-quality long-form content that performs well.

Trading at 16x forward earnings, Netflix is now valued at a five-year low. While no one can predict the full impact of AI and short-form content on the streaming sector, very little has to go right for Netflix stock to rebound.

Additionally, the company has the expertise and resources to adapt to shifting consumer preferences. This makes the risk-reward proposition very favorable right now.

Like Netflix, the ride-hailing giant Uber (UBER -0.63%) continues to deliver strong financial results. In the third quarter of the year, Uber grew revenue by 10% year over year, while free cash flow and adjusted earnings rose by 14% and 35%, respectively. Gross bookings surged 20%.

However, management's fourth-quarter outlook for adjusted earnings per share of $0.95 to $0.99, representing annual growth of 34% to 39%, came up short of Wall Street estimates.

As with Netflix, investors remain concerned about AI disruption, particularly about what would happen to the world's largest ride-hailing fleet if autonomous driving becomes the norm. However, one reason Uber's fourth-quarter outlook disappointed was driver shortages and higher driver incentives.

This is a problem that autonomous robotaxis could solve. Furthermore, Uber has partnered with many autonomous companies. Uber's operational and regulatory expertise can help bring these driverless vehicles onto the roads. In its earnings release, Uber noted that it expects to have 700 fully driverless vehicles on its platform by year-end, and over 2,000 by the end of 2027.

Trading at roughly 19x forward earnings estimates, Uber is now at a multi-year low valuation. The company's financials remain strong, and Uber is partnering with AI and is likely to benefit from adding more driverless vehicles to the platform.

As with Netflix, I like the risk-reward profile and don't think much has to go right for the stock to rebound.
