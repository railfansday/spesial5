Sunday Patriots Notes: Mike Vrabel’s team faces a test of adversity

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The New England Patriots are off on a much needed bye week after their season quickly took a turn for the worse over the last two week.

It has been a quieter week than usual here on Pats Pulpit. But still things to clean out the notebook from the week. Welcome to the latest edition of our Sunday Patriots Notes.

As New England Patriots players take their seats in the team auditorium, head coach Mike Vrabel's four pillars of his team identity are plastered on the walls to their left and right.

In Germany last Sunday, those qualities were few and far between as the Patriots suffered another embarrassing defeat overseas, falling 35-10 to the Detroit Lions. The Lions didn't just win on the scoreboard. They also won the battle of effort and finish.

"They played harder than we did, so that's not why they beat us. They certainly outplayed us. They outcoached us, and they played harder," Vrabel said.

It's a troubling trend that has emerged over the last month under Vrabel. Players have acknowledged a lack of energy in back-to-back weeks. Linebacker Christian Elliss pointed to a lack of trust on the defensive side of the ball.

Vrabel's other three pillars — ball security and ball disruption, details, technique and fundamentals, and making great decisions — are also nowhere to be seen. The Patriots rank 31st in the league in turnover differential (-10). The defense has cratered into one of the league's worst units amid persistent issues with technique and fundamentals.

Even after a week in which Vrabel appeared to challenge his players publicly, his message didn't seem to get through. Asked whether he views his group as mentally fragile, Vrabel didn't directly answer.

"Again, adversity is a part of professional sports," Vrabel said. "So, I've shown opportunities where we've responded … The consistency of that and understanding and showing them one example of saying, 'Here you are stopping them four plays and getting an interception,' and on the other side, 'Here's just not good enough run defense and allows them to score.'

"So, I don't know which one it is. I just know that we need the more positive actions and the ones that we believe that somebody's going to make a play as opposed to what could possibly go wrong."

The Patriots will face another significant test of their ability to respond to adversity after quarterback Drake Maye suffered an ankle injury that will keep him out indefinitely. Vrabel noted that Maye will not be available coming out of the team's bye week, leaving backup quarterback Tommy DeVito to take over the offense.

At 5-5, however, the Patriots still find themselves in the playoff race with everything in front of them. Their season is far from over, but they'll need to show an ability to overcome adversity that has been missing for much of the year.

"When they make a play, it's our response that's critical. We can't let one play affect the next one," Vrabel said.

Sunday's loss in Germany offered another example of that problem. After Jahmyr Gibbs broke off a 50-yard reception, exposing season-long issues with both the Patriots' coverage and pass rush, the defense offered little resistance on the next play as Gibbs scored on a five-yard handoff.

"If they make a play, they make a play, and I think that those are things that happened to us the other day, things that we did to ourselves," Vrabel said. "If they make a really nice play, a contested catch or they break a tackle, we've got to line up again and go play. Sometimes we let the play before, unfortunately, determine maybe what happens on the next play."

For the Patriots to get back on track, it starts with Vrabel.

The head coach must find a way to revive his struggling defense, particularly with a backup quarterback now leading the offense. But just as importantly, he needs to get his team playing to the standard he has set since arriving in New England.

The four pillars plastered on the walls of the team auditorium and emphasized in meetings mean little if they aren't showing up on the field on Sundays. Vrabel has made it clear what he expects from his players. Now, he needs to find a way to get them to respond when things don't go their way.

The Patriots have seven games remaining to show they can do just that.

New England currently sits as the AFC's No. 8 seed, with a 36% chance of making the playoffs. If the Patriots show fight and make a push down the stretch, they'll likely need to surpass the six-win Browns or Steelers.

The most realistic path to putting themselves in playoff position would be winning their three games against the Chargers, Jets and Dolphins while stealing at least one win against the Bills, Vikings, Chiefs or Broncos.

If the season goes in the other direction, the Patriots will begin turning their attention toward their draft position in what continues to project as an extremely talented 2027 class. At 5-5, New England currently holds the No. 16 overall pick, with 12 teams sitting at three wins or fewer.

The Patriots have two weeks to get quarterback Tommy DeVito ready for his first start in Los Angeles in Week 12. It will be interesting to see how offensive coordinator Josh McDaniels adjusts his offense with DeVito under center instead of Drake Maye, with the first though being a less aggressive downfield passing attack, considering Maye ranks fourth in the league in average depth of target (10.1 yards).

DeVito showcased an impressive deep ball during training camp this summer, but it would be unrealistic to expect him to replicate the explosiveness Maye brings to the passing game.

One positive moving forward is that there were plenty of opportunities for DeVito to make plays on tape against the Lions, as New England's pass catchers consistently found ways to get open. DeVito struggled seeing the field and with ball placement, however, areas the Patriots will hope to see improve now that he has gotten his first extensive regular-season action since December 2024.

"I think there were some things that he missed, but also not having a ton of live action since training camp, and really, I think he'll be ready to go coming out of the bye," Vrabel said. "I think that he'll be able to look at some things and understand, whether that's being able to transfer up into the pocket, get us in the right play, get the ball out quicker or look at protections or rotation, whatever that may be. I think that'll all improve."

The Patriots have used five of their eight return designations from injured reserve this season, leaving them with three remaining for the rest of the regular season. Barring a setback, left guard Alijah Vera-Tucker is on track to take one of those spots. Eligible to return next week, Vera-Tucker has ramped up his rehab in recent weeks as he works his way back from a hamstring injury.

Wide receiver Romeo Doubs is also eligible to return next week after suffering a scapula injury in Week 6.

Patriots' second round pick Gabe Jacas continued to be one of the team's few bright spots last Sunday. Jacas tallied two pressures, one being a clean-up sack, and also discarding All-Pro left tackle Penei Sewell to make one of his three run stops.

On the year, Jacas ranks second among rookies in pressures (28), trailing only No. 2 overall pick David Bailey, and leads rookie edge defenders in run stops.

"Now you're getting to this thing where you're getting a little bit banged up. We're about halfway through the season. College ball's starting to finish up, and he's consistent. He's got his routine. He's staying on his routine, which is big," outside linebackers coach Mike Smith said.

"And he's getting comfortable, right? He's out there. He's communicating more. He's talking more. It's starting to slow down a little bit more. Still got a ways to go, but he's just feeling comfortable.

The Patriots will be back in the New Balance Athletics Center on Monday as they fully turn the page to the Los Angeles Chargers. The schedule will then be slightly altered with Thanksgiving on Thursday.

This means Mike Vrabel media availability on Monday will be followed by the first practice of the week on Tuesday. Players will have a typical Wednesday and Friday but will be off for Thanksgiving. New England will then head out to the west coast on Saturday where they will visit the Chargers on Sunday at 4:05 p.m. ET.
