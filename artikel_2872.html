49ers spare no expense for Mexico City trip with hope of taking team to new heights

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Kyle Shanahan gets to decide what he believes will best prepare the 49ers to play football.

That arrangement is particularly appreciated this week, with the 49ers setting up shop in Colorado Springs. Colo., to acclimate to elevation before facing the Vikings on Sunday in Mexico City.

San Francisco 49ers head coach Kyle Shanahan watches the first half of a game against the Dallas Cowboys in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

Moving an NFL operation for a week requires more than plane tickets and hotel rooms. Especially when the goal is to make a temporary home feel as much like the real thing as possible.

The 49ers brought the people, equipment and resources necessary to keep their routine intact. That includes the staff responsible for feeding them.

At some point, someone has to approve the expense.

Shanahan appreciates that his bosses have consistently approached those decisions by asking what helps the team, giving him the freedom to make recommendations based on football.

"That's been pretty amazing, because it's always just, 'What do you guys think helps you for football?'" Shanahan said. "And that's what I always try to think about."

That trust takes a concern off the coach's plate when weighing how to prepare for an unusual trip.

"No one's ever said, 'Yeah, but try to go this way because one's 100 times more than the other," Shanahan said.

There is plenty for Shanahan to consider before taking his team to another country for a game. The Vikings still require a full week of study. Players still need treatment, meetings and practice. Every hour spent sorting through a disruption is time that could have gone toward getting ready for Sunday.

The players appreciate the commitment, too, even if Christian McCaffrey is reserving judgment on how much practicing at elevation will help Sunday.

He has no such uncertainty about the organization's willingness to give its players every opportunity to prepare.

"One thing I know about our organization, and that I really respect, everybody from the top down, is they'll do anything to try to help us get acclimated to whatever adversities or things that we have to face throughout the week," McCaffrey said. "So if we're going to play at altitude, to come here and getting acclimated is part of that. So, you know, I appreciate it."

For the players and coaches, the investment shows up in the details. The meals remain familiar. The tools needed to study opponents, lift weights and recover from injuries travel with them.

Christian McCaffrey of the San Francisco 49ers leaves the field after a game against the Dallas Cowboys on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Arlington, Texas. Getty Images

"When we do travel, we do it pretty right. Like, we bring our cooks, we bring everything," Shanahan said. "Our offices look the same when we get to our hotel rooms. There's not one video thing that's not here. We bring equipment for all the strength and conditioning stuff, the rehab stuff."

The objective is to give the 49ers the benefits of preparing at elevation while preserving the infrastructure that supports a normal workweek.

That takes planning well beyond the practice field. Bringing the equipment is one part of it. Having the people who know how each player uses it, and what he needs to get through the week, helps make the arrangement work. The familiarity matters when so much else about the trip is different.

It also gives players fewer logistical concerns as they work through their football responsibilities.

San Francisco 49ers linebacker Fred Warner (54) reacts against the Dallas Cowboys at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

Shanahan's appreciation also comes with perspective from conversations with peers and his experience elsewhere.

"Even talking to some other coaches who have traveled before and done stuff like this, I just think it's a little bit different how our organization does it," Shanahan said. "Just with how they do whatever they think is right for the players and to give us an advantage, anything we need.

"I'm not saying the places I've been at and did it bad by any means, but if I started here first, I probably would have thought that."

For Shanahan, the willingness to fund all those details makes an expensive week away feel like a worthwhile investment in preparation for Sunday.
