How the Bears’ defense grades after their bye week

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Chicago Bears had their bye week this week, using the weekend to recover and regroup heading into their final eight games of the 2026 season. All told, their 7-2 record is one to be proud of, and though their offense gets a lot of the national praise, their defense has certainly surpassed expectations.

In particular, the way the Bears' defense has played in the two games after they traded for Jeffery Simmons has been remarkable. Even before then, though, their defense was better than many anticipated, thanks largely in part to creative pass-rush scheming by defensive coordinator Dennis Allen and strong play from this offseason's additions. This defensive improvement has seen the Bears project as a likely playoff team by Cloudbet review and a potential winner of the NFC North for the second year in a row.

All year, I've been recapping how the Bears have done analytically based on PFN Impact grading. Coming out of the bye week, I wanted to recap how each of their defensive and special teams players have ranked at their respective positions throughout the course of the season.

Neville Gallimore: 68.8 (108th of 120 DTs)

Tyrique Stevenson: 63.3 (96th of 102 CBs)

As a team, the Bears rank No. 11 in the NFL with a 76.8 PFN Defense Impact grade after Week 10. They rank in the top ten in the league in percentage of drives allowing a score, lowest EPA allowed per play, and lowest success rate allowed.

The obvious standout here is Jeffery Simmons, though only two of his nine games have been played with the Bears this season. He only comes in second narrowly behind Quinnen Williams not just among NFL defensive tackles, but among all NFL defensive players, regardless of position. He has established himself as a genuine contender for Defensive Player of the Year between his short time with Chicago and his previous seven games with the Titans.

None of the Bears' current top three performers by PFN Impact grading were on the team last year. Dillon Thieneman has been fantastic as a rookie, allowing a targeted passer rating of just 67.3 this season with a well-rounded analytical profile that grades above average among safeties in coverage, run support, and overall efficiency. Cam Lewis had a shaky start to the year but has improved his standing in recent weeks.

In addition to Simmons and Thieneman, the third of those top three defensive players this year has been Devin Bush. His 84.3 run defense grade ranks seventh among all NFL linebackers going into Week 11, and he's tied for fifth at his position with seven tackles for loss. Neither of the Bears' other two primary linebackers have been fantastic in run support or in rushing the passer, but D'Marco Jackson ranks third among all linebackers with an 88.6 coverage grade, giving him the slight edge over T.J. Edwards in the grading scale.

Along the defensive line, Austin Booker has grown into a quality starting edge rusher with 5.0 sacks through his first nine games. He and Simmons are the only defensive linemen with an above-average grade at either defensive end or defensive tackle, though it's worth noting that Montez Sweat has been on an upward trajectory since the Bears acquired Simmons from Tennessee.

In the secondary, fourth-round rookie Malik Muhammad has surprised as an average starter, which is a very impressive start to an NFL career for a Day 3 draft pick. Kyler Gordon hasn't played in enough games to qualify for a PFN CB Impact grade, though his presence in the nickel has been a welcome one. Chicago remains shaky at cornerback along the perimeter, though Jaylon Johnson boosted his stock with a 94.9 grade in Week 9 against the Buccaneers amidst an otherwise disappointing season.

Cairo Santos ranks No. 21 in the NFL with a completed field goal percentage of 83.3% going into Week 11. He's gone 3-for-4 from beyond 50 yards, which is a better conversion rate than many kickers, but he doesn't have as large of a sample size kicking from deep, and the blocked field goal he had from 23 yards out in Week 2 against the Vikings looms large. From a punting standpoint, Tory Taylor doesn't grade as elite in a particular aspect of his game, but he ranks No. 13 in the NFL in both net yards per punt and gross punting average, making him slightly above average for his position.
