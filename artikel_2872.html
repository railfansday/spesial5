2 Asset Manager Stocks to Buy That Each Oversee More Than $1 Trillion

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

BlackRock and Blackstone offer buyers very different setups on growth, valuation, and risk.

By Rick Orford – Nov 23, 2026 at 8:38PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Blackstone ›

BlackRock (BLK +3.88%) and Blackstone (BX +4.57%) each oversee more than $1 trillion in assets, making them two of the largest asset management firms worldwide. But with one trading closer to its 52-week high and the other down sharply this year, the question is: Which stock offers the more attractive opportunity for buyers?

BlackRock offers exposure to one of the most diversified businesses in asset management. With $15.5 trillion in assets under management (AUM) at the end of the third quarter, up 15% from the previous year, the company has built an enormous platform spanning index funds, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), and private markets.

On top of that, the company sees broad demand, attracting massive inflows across equities, fixed-income investments, alternative investments, and cash. For example, its iShares ETF business remains an important growth engine, while its active investment strategies attract capital from investors seeking opportunities beyond passive index investing.

Its third-quarter performance further strengthened the bull case in favor of the stock. Its third-quarter revenue rose 19% year over year, while adjusted earnings per share (EPS) climbed 38%. Looking ahead, its ability to attract new money while expanding its technology and private markets businesses gives it several avenues for continued earnings growth.

The main drawback is valuation. So far this year, BlackRock stock is up about 5% and trades at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio based on estimated earnings performance of about 18, which means investors are paying about $18 for every $1 of expected earnings. That leaves less room for error if market conditions deteriorate or inflows slow down.

Another company has also built its business around alternative assets, giving investors exposure to private equity, real estate, and infrastructure.

Blackstone has also established itself as one of the biggest names in alternative-asset management, with $1.4 trillion in AUM as of the third quarter. More broadly, its latest results suggest that investor demand remains healthy despite a challenging backdrop for private markets. Specifically, the company attracted $63.3 billion in new money, bringing its trailing 12-month (TTM) inflows to $271.6 billion.

Beyond inflows, one encouraging development is the growth of its perpetual capital business, which reached $562 billion. These investment vehicles are designed to provide capital without the fixed fund life and fundraising cycles associated with traditional private equity funds. This gives Blackstone a larger pool of relatively stable capital that can support more predictable fee revenue over time.

Blackstone is also benefiting from several major investment themes, particularly artificial intelligence (AI), energy infrastructure, and private credit. After all, building data centers, expanding electricity capacity, and financing AI infrastructure requires enormous amounts of capital. And Blackstone has established investment platforms across all these themes, allowing it to participate in the biggest opportunities of the next decade.

Blackstone shares have fallen almost 27% this year, but that has made its valuation more reasonable, with the stock trading at a forward P/E of about 16. Looking ahead, if transaction activity improves and fundraising accelerates, the stock could recover well.

Overall, both BlackRock and Blackstone offer exposure to the growing asset management industry, but their valuations and growth drivers present different opportunities for investors.

BlackRock offers scale, diversification, and strong earnings growth, making it appealing to investors seeking steady long-term growth, although its premium valuation limits the margin for error. Blackstone offers exposure to private markets and AI infrastructure at a considerably lower price, potentially giving investors greater upside if the company recovers. In other words, these giants appeal to different buyers: BlackRock is steadier, Blackstone is riskier. But each looks like a buy.
