'I've never revealed the whole story': England fans wouldn't boo me if they knew the truth, says Ben White... as he defends World Cup walkout and rejecting call-ups

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Published: 15:00 EST, 9 November 2026 | Updated: 18:20 EST, 9 November 2026

Ben White believes England fans would not boo him if they knew the truth behind his 2022 World Cup walkout.

The Arsenal defender left the camp in Qatar after the group stages citing 'personal reasons', although a fallout with Gareth Southgate's assistant, Steve Holland, was later reported. He then declined a return to the England set-up while Southgate and Holland were in charge but was included by Thomas Tuchel in March of this year.

However, White was booed by fans inside Wembley when he came on in the second-half of a friendly against Uruguay, only to score his first international goal and then concede a late penalty in a 1-1 draw. He was not selected for the World Cup in North America but is back with the squad ahead of this week's Nations League fixtures against Croatia and Spain.

And the 29-year-old, addressing those jeers for the first time, said: 'It was hard to hear, especially because they didn't know the situation, which is kind of what I put my mind to.

'They don't know my situation and it is not really about what I have done, because they don't know why, and if they did they would not be booing or saying these things. I just think about maybe (them) not knowing the full story, and if they did, maybe they wouldn't (boo).'

He added: 'Obviously, a load has been said. I've probably seen all of it. I just think people don't know the full story, because I've never said anything, and I won't put anyone under the bus or share any of that.

'There were loads of things that happened and communication wasn't great. I don't want to throw anyone under the bus. A lot of people try to do that to me so it's something I wouldn't want to do myself. I think it's probably the best way. It's a bit tricky and not straightforward, but it's amazing to be here now. It's a privilege to be called up.'

White, however, is adamant that he would make the same decision today and leave the World Cup, even if he will not divulge the reasons as to why.

'I don't think I would (do anything different),' he said. 'I think, as a person, I'm quite real, and I made the decision that I would probably continue to make. It's one that I'm not happy about. It wasn't ideal. But it happened, it's time to move on.

'I don't think I am looking to win anyone over or say anything that helps them get back on my side. It is what it is. There are reasons for it and I am just looking forward to playing for England and doing well and looking for success.'

White has spoken publicly about not watching football, and this, too, has been used against him by some supporters.

'It doesn't mean I don't like football, because I do it every day, so I must like it a little bit!' he said. 'It's because I don't watch it, which I think is quite normal for someone that does a job and then doesn't go home and then continue watching that job.

'I don't think anyone knows my personality. I keep that to my loved ones and family. It's all noise when people have an opinion on that stuff. It is not actually me. People don't know me and I like it that way. You give so much to the game, which is intense. I think it's important to keep some bits for yourself. I keep the energy for the people I love.'

England new boy Josh King believes his aptitude for academia gives him an advantage at the start of a burgeoning career.

The Fulham star – who has two Premier League goals this season – linked up with Thomas Tuchel’s squad on Monday morning as one of two fresh call ups alongside Jobe Bellingham.

King was a straight-A student at the renowned independent Hampton School in south London, with alumni including Queen guitarist Sir Brian May and golf pro Paul Casey.

The teenager’s mother, Michelle, is a university lecturer and finishing a PhD on sickle cell disease while his father Steve is a personal injury lawyer.

‘The most important thing about being good academically and education-wise is you’re able to take on information much more quickly,’ King said.

‘You can take on information which might take a bit longer for other people. I think that definitely helps.’

While the 19-year-old was invited to take part in a pre-World Cup training camp in Miami, he is hoping to make his international bow against Croatia on Thursday.

Although a central figure in Fulham’s midfield, a confident King could be utilised wider by Tuchel were he to be given an opportunity across the two-game Nations League camp.

‘There was a buzz around Fulham when I got called up [this week],’ he added. ‘I got off the phone with the boss, ran into the kitchen and started cheering with my dad. I called mum at work and she was screaming in the office. This felt slightly unachievable when I was younger.’

Jude Bellingham and Harry Kane were namechecked as those who helped him settle when introduced to the group in Florida, with King having also studied the likes of Jamal Musiala and Pedri to elevate his own game.

‘The main thing you take is the standards they set,’ he said when reflecting on joining the camp in America. ‘The way they conduct themselves, the way they perform every day, the way they train and the way they take everything seriously.

‘The little things, technical things, the way some people strike the ball, the way some people pass, just little things which you almost take without thinking about it - they also have an impact.

‘On a few occasions I asked “how do you finish this way” or “what do you do in this scenario” and it always helps to add to my game.’
