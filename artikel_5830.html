1. Which group kicked off its leaders’ summit in Manila, Philippines, on Sunday?

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations

U.S. President Donald Trump attended the summit for the second year in a row, but not much came of it—other than an introduction to Filipino fast-food chain Jollibee, Foreign Policy’s Joseph Rachman writes in Southeast Asia Brief.

2. Which object struck Pope Leo XIV in the face on Monday before he celebrated the final Mass of his trip to Peru?

The pope later forgave the worshipper who threw the rosary—continuing the Vatican’s strong postwar tradition of peace promotion, which Vanessa R. Corcoran wrote about in April.

3. Which landmark partly collapsed on Tuesday after Ukrainian drones and missiles struck it two days in a row?

The bridge is both a symbol of the Russian occupation of Crimea and a major chokepoint for the transport of military and civilian supplies, FP’s Alexandra Sharp reports in World Brief.

4. On Tuesday, the Pan American Health Organization announced that Bolivia, Mexico, and the United States had lost their designation of having eliminated which disease?

A measles outbreak has also been worsening in recent months in Bangladesh, which recorded around 200,000 suspected cases between mid-March and early October, FP’s Michael Kugelman wrote in South Asia Brief in October.

5. On Wednesday, Iranian forces targeted a U.S. air base located in which country?

The tit-for-tat strikes between Iran and the United States this week marked an end to a roughly two-month lull in such attacks, FP’s John Haltiwanger and Rishi Iyengar write in Situation Report.

6. The United States signed a military partnership agreement with which Latin American country on Wednesday?

U.S. officials said the deal was intended to help the two governments battle drug traffickers, but analysts believe that it could also be an opportunity for Washington to rein in the power of the Venezuelan military, FP’s Catherine Osborn reports in Latin America Brief.

7. On Thursday, the International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar had committed genocide against the Rohingya people. Which country brought the case before the court?

This is the first time the court has found a state directly responsible for genocide, and it’s likely to have major implications for future cases, Andrew Nachemson writes.

8. U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin held their first-ever trilateral meeting on Thursday. In which city did it take place?

The meeting was no Yalta Conference, but it did provide the leaders with plenty of attention and a few photo ops, FP’s James Palmer writes in China Brief.

9. Also on Thursday, Somalia expelled the ambassador of which country?

The decision was partly prompted by Abu Dhabi’s relationship with several Somalian regions, including the breakaway province of Somaliland—whose quest for international recognition has been picking up steam, Joshua Keating wrote in September.

10. On Wednesday, which spacecraft became the first human-made object to reach a distance of one light-day away from Earth?

Voyager 1 has now traveled more than four times the distance from the sun to Pluto—and as of next September, it will have been in space for 50 years, Smithsonian magazine reports.
