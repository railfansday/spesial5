ASRA NOMANI: Far-left US groups battle Trump plan to rival China's tech dominance

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

As President Donald Trump attends the 49th ASEAN Summit in the Philippines, communist and far-left organizations are mobilizing internationally against an American initiative designed to reduce reliance on China.

As President Donald Trump attends the 49th ASEAN Summit in the Philippines, American communist and far-left organizations are mobilizing internationally against an American initiative designed to reduce reliance on China.

According to a Fox News Digital investigation, a network of 120 mostly communist, socialist and far-left U.S. groups – many of them pro-China – are joining protests in Asia opposing the Trump administration's effort to build an artificial intelligence and semiconductor hub in the Philippines that would reduce U.S. dependence on China in the AI war.

The target is the Pax Silica tech industrial hub in the Philippines, which Undersecretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg, a former senior adviser at Palantir Technologies, said last year would strengthen American control over AI infrastructure, semiconductor production and critical mineral supply chains. Two days ago, Helberg described Pax Silica as key to the world's "superintelligence revolution."

The dispute centers partly on the proposed Clark Advanced Manufacturing Park, a roughly 4,000-acre development in New Clark City, Tarlac, in the Philippines, that would be a hub for the Pax Silica project.

A Fox News Digital investigation traced support for the protests to a transnational pro-China activist network stretching from Manila to New York, Washington, D.C., Los Angeles and Canada, including the Democratic Socialists of America East Bay, Calif., chapter and organizations with ties to American-born, Shanghai-based Marxist tech millionaire Neville Roy Singham. The protests also include indigenous groups in the Philippines that allege Pax Silica is being built on ancestral land.

About eight of 10 of the organizations in the network of 120 are openly communist, socialist or "anti-imperialist."

In addition, most of them are openly "anti-Zionist," opposed to the existence of a Jewish state of Israel, according to a Fox News Digital analysis of the groups in the network.

CodePink, a pro-communist, pro-China nonprofit co-founded by Singham's wife, Jodie Evans, has used its media programming to criticize Pax Silica as a "military-industrial-surveillance complex."

For Washington, experts say, the Pax Silica plan is about securing the technology and resources needed to compete with Beijing. For opponents, the protests are a way to curb American economic and military domination.

The campaign also reveals how opposition to Trump's China strategy is being organized not only overseas, but through pro-China activist networks inside the United States.

The U.S. network, organizing behind a campaign called "Shut Down Pax," includes familiar faces in the Singham network's protest circuit: BAYAN USA, a pro-communist Filipino American alliance helping lead the campaign; Anakbayan USA, a pro-communist youth organization; and the International League of Peoples' Struggle, or ILPS, founded with the help of Jose Maria Sison, the late founder of the Communist Party of the Philippines.

The campaign has also reached the U.S. Congress. A letter, headlined "Shut Down Pax Silica in the Philippines" calls on lawmakers to halt the initiative pending review of environmental protections, labor rights and the treatment of Philippine communities.

The activism intersects with other nonprofits in the network funded by Singham, including the People's Forum and BreakThrough News, that have promoted opposition to American foreign policy and challenged Washington's confrontation with Beijing.

On Sept. 20, the U.S. chapter of the International League of Peoples' Struggle, the International Coalition for Human Rights in the Philippines-US and Bagong Alyansang Makabayan USA, or BAYAN USA, launched the "Shut Down Pax Silica Campaign Network" in Queens, New York, with organizers claiming about 500 participants in person and online.

An article, published by Bulatlat, invoked a term popular in communist, "anti-imperialist" circles, claiming that a movement had begun in "the belly of the beast," with "resistance" to Pax Silica "rising."

"We unite to confront and oppose the Trump/U.S.-led Pax Silica Initiative in its competition with China to dominate the AI/tech supply chain," the coalition declared in its organizing platform.

"We Filipinos in the U.S. must also take action," BAYAN USA declares on its campaign website, urging opposition to U.S. investments associated with Pax Silica.

A month later, on Oct. 24, CodePink Radio published Episode 374, with host Marcy Winograd interviewing sociologist William Robinson about his new book, "Pax Silica: The New Ruling Class," examining the "rise of a new transnational capital complex" that combines big tech with global finance and the "military-industrial-surveillance complex" to "colonize the world unless we continue to protest, disrupt, divest and withhold our labor."

The Black Alliance for Peace, which participated in CodePink's November "Taxpayer Uprising" campaign in Washington, D.C., last week, also promoted the anti-Pax Silica campaign's launch.

Anakbayan USA, its local chapters and GABRIELA USA, a Filipino women's organization, appear among the American coalition's participants. Their Philippine counterparts are involved in the demonstrations surrounding Trump's visit.

The coalition's published platform also identifies "Migrante USA," student organizations and other activist groups.

The overlapping campaigns bring together Filipino American organizations, socialist activists and groups that have previously organized against U.S. military operations, Israel and American foreign policy.

Singham, an American-born technology entrepreneur who sold his consultancy ThoughtWorks for an estimated $785 million in 2017, has financed an extensive network of U.S. nonprofits.

A Fox News Digital investigation traced $278 million into six core American nonprofits, including CodePink, People's Forum, Tricontinental and BreakThrough BT Media Inc., which operates BreakThrough News.

In July, the House Ways and Means Committee subpoenaed three organizations as lawmakers investigated the financial network and potential foreign influence. The organizations have challenged the investigation.

One connection to the anti-Pax Silica campaign from the Singham network is "Nodutdol for Korean Community Development," an American group listed among the coalition's endorsers.

Nodutdol member Winter Oh participated in the September launch in Queens.

BreakThrough News has amplified the Philippine protests and criticism of Pax Silica, this summer promoting a BAYAN and Kilusang Mayo Uno demonstration with the slogan "The Philippines is not for sale!" As Trump was still in flight to Manila, BreakThrough news highlighted growing opposition to Pax Silica with a story headline, "ASEAN summit in Manila met with protests over Trump’s participation."

Philippine officials have previously accused organizations such as Gabriela, Anakbayan, Karapatan and Kilusang Mayo Uno of acting as communist fronts. The groups have denied those allegations.

The ideological roots of the campaign extend to the Communist Party of the Philippines, founded by Sison in 1968.

The United States designated the Communist Party of the Philippines and its armed wing, the New People's Army, as a foreign terrorist organization.

In a Nov. 7 editorial, the party's official newspaper, Ang Bayan, urged Filipinos to organize "widespread protests" against Trump and to "reject and drive him out."

The editorial accused Trump of advancing Pax Silica to strengthen American control over Philippine resources.

The publication also reported that clandestine organizations associated with the National Democratic Front of the Philippines staged demonstrations. One teachers' group, KAGUMA, reportedly chanted, "Join the New People's Army!"

The American campaign includes explicitly communist organizations, including the Workers World Party, a Marxist-Leninist organization, and the International Action Center.

Separately, "Fight Back! News," published by the Freedom Road Socialist Organization, a Marxist-Leninist group that has organized many anti-ICE protests this year, republished an editorial from Ang Bayan criticizing Trump's Pax Silica initiative.

The International League of Peoples' Struggle called for global protests under the slogans "Trump Out!" and "Reject Pax Silica!" The organization said groups planned demonstrations in 23 cities worldwide, including locations in the United States, Canada, Europe and Asia.

American organizers identified planned actions in Los Angeles, Oregon, Washington, D.C., New York and Honolulu.

In Ottawa, International League of Peoples' Struggle Canada, Anakbayan Ottawa, Migrante Ottawa and "Ottawa for Palestine" announced a demonstration for Wednesday outside the U.S. Embassy under the slogan "Shut the Pax Down!"

In an April proposal to the U.S. State Department, Philippine officials outlined plans involving critical minerals, industrial infrastructure, investment and technology development.

Protesters allege the project could displace Ayta Indigenous communities, expand mining, and strain electricity and water supplies.

Philippine officials dispute allegations that the project would create a U.S.-controlled enclave or include the massive AI data centers described by activists. They say the development remains subject to Philippine law.

As Trump arrived, demonstrations escalated.

Reuters footage showed students marching with anti-Trump banners. SunStar reported more than 200 arrests during demonstrations in Metro Manila, while GMA News reported that activists condemned police dispersals and disputed the government's response.
