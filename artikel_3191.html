Better Buy: Bitcoin vs. Ethereum

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Which one of the world’s top cryptocurrencies is a better investment today?

Bitcoin (BTC +1.74%) and Ether (ETH +3.75%) are the world's two most valuable cryptocurrencies. But after setting record highs in 2025, both tokens tumbled in 2026.

Bitcoin, which reached $126,000 last October, is now worth roughly $60,000. Ether, which topped $4,950 last August, trades at about $1,700. Let's see why these top "blue chip" cryptocurrencies crumbled -- and if either one is worth buying in this choppy market.

In the past, Bitcoin and Ether were both mined using the energy-intensive proof-of-work (PoW) consensus mechanism, in which miners used powerful computing hardware to solve cryptographic puzzles and earn tokens as rewards.

But there was a key difference: Bitcoin was primarily designed as a digital commodity, while Ethereum (the blockchain behind Ether) was built to support complex smart contracts, which are used to develop decentralized apps and tokenized assets. Bitcoin supported only simple smart contracts, and its limited programmability made it less attractive to developers.

In 2022, the Ethereum network -- whose native token is Ether -- transitioned to the more energy-efficient proof-of-stake (PoS) consensus mechanism. Under PoS, validators stake (lock up) their Ether tokens to help secure the network and validate transactions, earning rewards for their participation rather than simply mining new tokens. The bigger Ethereum's developer ecosystem grows, the more valuable Ether -- which funds the "gas fees" for those transactions -- becomes.

Today, Ether's value is closely tied to Ethereum's developer growth, while Bitcoin's value is still driven by its scarcity, utility, and value to institutional investors. Bitcoin has a maximum supply of 21 million tokens. More than 20 million of those tokens have already been mined, and its blockchain undergoes a "halving" every four years to cut its mining rewards in half.

Ether doesn't have a fixed maximum supply, and it fluctuates between being an inflationary token and a deflationary one based on Ethereum's network activity. Ethereum is upgrading its network to make it more scalable and secure, but it also faces intense competition from faster PoS blockchains like Solana (SOL +1.71%) and Cardano (ADA +1.41%). To stay ahead of these competitors, Ethereum is supporting more Layer 2 (L2) applications that bundle together transactions and process them off-chain at higher speeds than its Layer 1 (L1) blockchain.

Bitcoin and Ether have more long-term catalysts than many other smaller altcoins, but they aren't immune to the macro headwinds for the broader crypto market. Last year, many crypto investors expected interest rate cuts, clearer, crypto-friendly regulations, and lower Treasury yields to drive investors back toward cryptocurrencies and riskier investments.

But the opposite happened. The Iran war exacerbated inflation, driving the Fed to raise its rates for the first time in three years. Treasury yields spiked, and the CLARITY Act -- which would have provided a clearer regulatory framework for cryptocurrencies -- died in the Senate.

The Democratic sweep of the U.S. midterm elections also suggests that more restrictive regulations could be passed against the crypto industry. All of those challenges chilled the crypto market and drove investors toward safer, higher-yielding investments again.

Bitcoin and Ether will both remain under pressure for the foreseeable future. But if I had to pick one over the other, I'd buy Bitcoin because it has more growth potential as a hedge against expansionary monetary policies and the debasement of fiat currencies. Once its price stabilizes, it could become more comparable to "digital gold" than other cryptocurrencies.

Ether also has a bright future as the "digital oil" of decentralized applications. Still, that future is murkier due to intense competition from Solana, Cardano, and other PoS blockchains with faster transactions and lower fees. Unless Ether widens its moat against those competitors, it could have less upside potential than Bitcoin when the crypto winter finally ends.
