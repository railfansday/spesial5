Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Stroke trial hit its interim milestone as cash runs into late 2027.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

DiaMedica Therapeutics Inc. (DMAC -5.79%) reported progress on its clinical trials for DM199, which targets vascular dysfunction in stroke and pregnancy-related disorders. Management indicated that the 200th participant has been enrolled in the ReMEDy2 stroke trial, which triggers a planned interim efficacy analysis in the first quarter of 2027. The company stated that enrollment is complete for the first two dose cohorts in its phase 2 fetal growth restriction study, with preliminary data expected in late November. The company reported that regulatory authorizations from Health Canada and the European Medicines Agency support the expansion of its global phase 2 preeclampsia trial. Management stated that it is currently generating additional rat toxicology data required to support an investigational new drug application with the United States Food and Drug Administration.

We are evaluating whether and to what degree DM199 can favorably affect vast vascular physiology to improve vascular health and blood flow and ultimately support improved outcomes for both the mother and the baby. The ongoing Phase 2 FGR IST trial is evaluating repeated subcutaneous dosing of DM199 in women with early onset disease. Key assessments include safety and tolerability, and umbilical artery Doppler indices, that reflect the degree of resistance to blood flow from the fetus to the placenta. Abnormal Doppler findings, particularly absent or reversed end diastolic flow, help guide the timing of delivery in pregnancies complicated by fetal growth restriction.

Changes in these physiologic parameters will provide important information about the pharmacologic activity of DM199 and help inform further development. At the November 24 KOL event, we will review preliminary observations from the 2 6-participant cohorts tested at DM199 subcutaneous dose levels 5 and 10 micrograms per kilogram, respectively. Although this is an early open label study involving a small number of patients, the findings will provide an initial opportunity to assess DM199 safety, pharmacologic activity, and potential clinical effects in women with early onset FGR. And importantly, the experts will help put the open label data into context by providing benchmarking data on outcomes in patients receiving current standard of care treatment.

In Part 1a of the investigator sponsored trial, patients with late onset preeclampsia were studied. If you recall, that data showed rapid and sustained reductions in maternal systolic and diastolic blood pressure following DM199 treatment. We also observed pharmacodynamic effects including trends suggesting reduction in the uterine artery pulsatility and a measure of resistance and an indirect measure of uteroplacental blood flow. Together, these observations support dose selection and provide a strong rationale for conducting additional studies to determine whether DM199 can deliver meaningful clinical benefits, particularly in early onset disease. I would also like to highlight the clinical rationale for DM199 in acute ischemic stroke. Unlike thrombolytic therapies, which dissolve blood clots or mechanical thrombectomy, which physically remove them.

DM199 is designed to improve collateral blood flow within the ischemic penumbra the surrounding brain tissue that are adversely impacted by the clot, and that remain viable for a period of time but are at risk of irreversible injury if adequate blood flow is not restored. By improving hospital delivery to the threatened tissue, we believe DM199 may help preserve neurologic function ultimately leading to improved recovery following stroke. REMEDY 2 is a randomized double blind placebo controlled study evaluating DM199 in patients with acute ischemic stroke who begin treatment within 24 hours of symptom onset. Participants receive DM199 or placebo over 3 weeks with functional recovery assessed at day 90.

The study focuses on patients who are not candidates for mechanical thrombectomy, a population with substantial unmet medical need. Across these programs, our goal is to determine whether DM199's effect on vascular function and blood flow can translate into meaningful clinical benefits for patients with these serious conditions.
