You can build an entire website in about 15 minutes with OpenAI's latest model

I’ve tested plenty of AI tools that promise to make life easier, but I usually end up spending more time figuring out how to use them than I save. That’s why one particular feature of OpenAI’s new GPT-6.2 Astra caught my attention.

According to OpenAI’s announcement, the company’s newest frontier model can build more attractive, functional websites from a written description. You don’t need to know how to code, design a website, or understand what happens behind the scenes.

As a working mom of three, I immediately thought of something I could actually use. Between school schedules, activities, dinner plans and the endless list of things everyone needs to remember, keeping a family organized can feel like a full-time job.

So I decided to give GPT-6.2 Astra a single prompt and see whether it could build a family command center to keep everything in one place.

My goal wasn’t to create another pretty dashboard. I wanted a website where I could move things around on a calendar, plan dinners, automatically generate grocery lists, and track chores without jumping between apps.

And I wanted ChatGPT to build the entire thing without asking me a dozen follow-up questions.

OpenAI introduced GPT-6.2 Astra on Tuesday, alongside GPT-6.2 Sol, a faster, more efficient model designed for everyday tasks.

Astra is the more powerful of the two, with improvements in coding, visual design and complex projects that require multiple steps.

One of the more interesting claims is that the model can create websites that not only look better but actually work better. OpenAI says Astra has improved visual reasoning, helping it choose layouts, images and interactive elements that fit the purpose of a website.

This matters because building something useful takes more than generating a page with a few buttons. Those buttons need to work, information needs to update correctly, and the experience should make sense to someone who isn’t a developer.

For this test, I wanted to see how close Astra could get to a finished website using nothing more than one detailed prompt.

I opened ChatGPT Work, selected GPT-6.2 Astra and entered the following prompt:

Build me a fully functional, interactive family command center website that helps a busy household with three children stay organized.

I want a weekly calendar where I can add, edit and move events, including school activities, appointments and family plans.

Include a meal planner for breakfast, lunch and dinner that automatically generates a grocery list based on the meals I select. Let me check off grocery items as I shop.

Add a chore tracker where I can assign tasks to different family members, mark them complete and see everyone's progress throughout the week.

Create a homepage dashboard showing today's events, upcoming activities, tonight's dinner and anything that still needs to get done.

Make the website beautiful, modern, mobile-friendly and simple enough for children and adults to use. Use realistic sample data so I can test everything immediately.

Every button, calendar interaction, checkbox and calculation must function. I don't want a static mockup.

Store changes so my information doesn't disappear when I refresh the page, if the available environment supports persistent storage.

Do not ask follow-up questions. Make reasonable design decisions yourself and build the complete experience.

Before finishing, check the functionality, test the interactions and fix any problems you find. Tell me what you were unable to verify.

I deliberately included several features that depend on one another. A meal planner is useful on its own, but it becomes much more interesting when the meals automatically populate a grocery list.

I also wanted to see whether Astra could handle the less glamorous parts of building a website, such as saving changes and making sure everything still worked when I returned.

As Astra worked, it kept me updated on its progress with responses like, "I’ll build a complete family organizer with a shared calendar, meal planning and grocery lists, chore tracking, and a dashboard, then test the main interactions and saved data."And then it went on to say, "The design is taking shape as 'Hearth,' with a calm dashboard and a color for each family member. I’m connecting the calendar, meal planner, grocery quantities, and weekly chore progress so changes carry through the whole app.At any time I could have interrupted the model and suggested it go a different way. For instance, if instead of a calm theme, I could have had it use bold or even neon colors to reflect the chaos of a home with three young children. Of course, I stayed with calm. Every little bit helps. It's important to note, if you're going to ask GPT-6.2 to create a website, it isn't as fast as Gemini Hydrogen. That's not because it's not as good or not working correctly, each model just works differently, and that includes latency. After 11 minutes, I asked, "Are you almost done?" It then responded with, "The visual layout is ready, and I’m finishing the interactive features. I still need to test adding and moving events, generating groceries, checking off chores, and saving through a refresh before it’s ready to use."When it finally completed the task, a beautiful, fully functional website in the browser appeared. I was truly surprised by how nice the website turned out.

Astra built a site that handles multiple connected tasks, remembers my changes and works without constant troubleshooting, that's a much more compelling demonstration of what AI can do than another chatbot answering questions.

GPT-6.2 Astra is available through ChatGPT Work for eligible Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users, with access depending on the rollout and workspace permissions. The model is not being added to the regular Chat model selector as part of this launch.

To try it, open ChatGPT Work, select GPT-6.2 Astra if it's available and paste in the prompt above. You can customize it for your own household, changing the number of family members or adding features you'd find more useful.

You can also adapt the same approach to build a vacation planner, homework tracker, personal budget dashboard or other simple web app.

The best way to judge the result is to try using it. Give it a try and let me know in the comments what you think.
