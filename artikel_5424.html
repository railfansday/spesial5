Intel signed its first outside customer for advanced chip manufacturing

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

CEO Lip-Bu Tan did not name the client, which signed up for the 18A-P version of Intel's manufacturing process

Intel $INTC has landed what CEO Lip-Bu Tan described as its first external client for its 18A manufacturing process, he said Wednesday at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin, Texas, according to CNBC. Tan declined to identify the client.

Intel Chief Technology Officer Pushkar Ranade wrote on X $TWTR that the customer had chosen 18A-P, a variant of the process that reached the risk-production stage in June, according to CNBC. Risk production refers to a pilot phase in which manufacturers run limited volumes to check that the technology holds up before full-scale manufacturing, according to The Next Web. Intel says 18A-P can handle designs built for 18A with full compatibility.

Intel stock gained 2.6% in after-hours trading following Tan's remarks.

Until now, Intel has lacked a big-name client for its foundry unit and has largely produced silicon for its own product lines, according to CNBC. The company has spent five years trying to compete with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.

Intel announced a foundry deal with another customer earlier, according to The Next Web. Japanese chip designer Socionext said in August that it planned to build its next chips on 18A-P. Intel offered no explanation of how the new client's arrangement differs from that agreement.

Reports have connected Apple $AAPL with 18A-P production for upcoming Mac processors, but no deal has been confirmed by either company, according to The Next Web.

Tan also said Wednesday that Microsoft $MSFT has become the earliest customer Intel has publicly identified for its EMIB advanced packaging technology, according to CNBC.

Intel stock fell 45% in October, its largest one-month decline on record, after the company forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion. On the earnings call, Tan said "a couple of customers" would be ready to announce over the next two quarters.

Intel said at the time that it was redirecting part of its Oregon factory output from 18A wafers to wafers built on the older Intel 3 process for its data center products. In July, Intel announced a collaboration with cybersecurity company Fortinet $FTNT to make a security processor on Intel 4, a more mature process than 18A.
