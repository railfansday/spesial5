What drone footage revealed about Mauricio Pochettino's Man United tactics: How he'll set up with and without the ball, a change of role for Bryan Mbeumo and his intriguing use of Joshua Zirkzee

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 07:00 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 07:10 EST, 20 November 2026

While thinking over the last few days about his first press conference as Manchester United boss, Mauricio Pochettino would not have expected drones and the new Grand Theft Auto video game to be top of the agenda.

Having touched down in Manchester on Wednesday, taking a private jet laid on by United in the immediate aftermath of the United States’ game in Cincinnati against Haiti, it has been a whirlwind 48 hours for Pochettino and his staff.

On Thursday, he took charge of training for the first time against the backdrop of question marks around Bryan Mbeumo’s decision to post late into the night about popular new video game GTA VI.

By the time the day ended, the issue at hand was far more pressing as a drone pilot filmed and uploaded to YouTube a 12-minute video from Pochettino’s first session at Carrington.

How worried Pochettino actually is about the footage – which has since been pulled from YouTube, with a grovelling apology issued – remains to be seen but only this summer did the Argentine concede that ‘we are in an era of spies’.

The footage wasn’t a tactical golden ticket for Liverpool, but what was there to take from it?

A drone pilot filmed and uploaded to YouTube a 12-minute video from Mauricio Pochettino’s first training session as Manchester United manager

The players get to work, with United's coaching staff seemingly unaware that their every move is being followed from the skies

Pochettino sides have had a consistent set-up with and without the ball over the years.

The Argentine and his staff have, more often than not, leaned into a back-three in possession, shifting to a back-four without the ball.

In the drone footage we could see the United squad divided into three groups with one XI out on a different training pitch with Pochettino and coach Toni Jimenez.

That group were lined up in what appeared to be that preferred in-possession set-up, with academy defender Reece Munro tucking in from right back to form a back-three alongside Harry Maguire and Ayden Heaven, traditional centre backs in a back-four.

Pochettino likes to have at least one full back push on extremely high to provide the width and in this snippet of his first training session, that responsibility landed on the shoulders of teenager Harry Amass, who has struggled for minutes this season, getting a total of just 29 under Michael Carrick.

The United players are put into position by Pochettino at Carrington

Similarly, Pochettino has often shown a desire to play No 10s – or attacking midfielders – quite narrow. And that is what we saw here, with Amass and Mbeumo providing the width. Mbeumo was on the right of a 4-2-3-1 shape with Matheus Cunha and 17-year-old academy talent Jaume Camacho playing narrower and Joshua Zirkzee up front.

The formation becomes a 3-2-4-1 in possession with the full back pushing on to the wing, creating two No 10s behind the lone striker.

The footage was significant in that it showed Pochettino is implementing his ideas from day one and is wasting no time in trying to embed a style he needs players to get up to speed with quickly.

But little should be read into the personnel given it was an amalgamation of young players and senior regulars.

We could see academy players Amass, Camacho and Munro among the 10 outfield players, along with Cunha, Heaven, Maguire, Kobbie Mainoo, Mbeumo, Andrey Santos and Zirkzee.

Of the 10 that were put to task by Pochettino, only four have started more than five league games this season.

It is highly improbable that the personnel used will be anywhere close to the starting XI at Anfield on Sunday.

The footage also showed games of one-touch rondos going on between the two other groups which were divided between the rest of Pochettino’s coaching team, which includes his son Sebastiano and Miguel D’Agostino, as well as trusted assistant Jesus Perez.

Pochettino greets his new players including Bryan Mbeumo, who lined up out wide in the first training session

United's players start a 'rondo', where one defender stands in the middle and tries to pinch the ball from the players stood around the edge - who are allowed one touch at a time

One group included Diogo Dalot, Leny Yoro, Lisandro Martinez, Matthijs de Ligt, Youri Tielemans, Mason Mount and Noussair Mazraoui. The other was academy-led with Bruno Fernandes leading a rondo that featured Shea Lacey, Jack and Tyler Fletcher, Dan Armer, as well as Jaydan Kamason.

But the use of Mbeumo in Pochettino’s work was one of the most interesting aspects, largely because he was back operating in a wider role.

With injuries hampering Benjamin Sesko, Mbeumo has had to spend a lot of the season deployed through the middle. That could well have to continue given Sesko is not anticipated to return until late January, but Pochettino’s decision to use Zirkzee as the figurehead in his set-up was intriguing.

Pochettino’s principles and set-up won’t vary too much; the onus now is on players to adapt. But Pochettino and Jimenez did look to have players in the correct positions. There were no obvious experiments – which makes sense when time is of the essence.

Amass plays off the left, Munro is a centre back, Maguire is comfortable in the middle of any back three, Camacho plays as a No 10 in the academy, both Mainoo and Santos have played together in a double pivot at the base of midfield and Zirkzee is a centre forward.

Pochettino likes a centre forward who can drop in to allow No 10s to run beyond and that suits Zirkzee’s style well, allowing him to be involved as a link man and not isolated with his back to goal.

Mbeumo works better running in behind as a counter-attacking striker, playing on the shoulder of the last defender.

How Pochettino uses Mbeumo over the longer term remains curious because swap out Camacho for Fernandes and Amass for Dorgu and there is a plausible set-up for success, with Mbeumo holding width on the right, Cunha and Fernandes in those central pockets they crave most, and Dorgu holding width off the left.

Lisandro Martinez (left) and Leny Yoro also meet Pochettino and his staff at Carrington

Pochettino’s track record of working with and improving young players became immediately apparent, with so many given an introduction to his staff and their methods on day one.

While both Jack and Tyler Fletcher, along with Amass, Lacey and Bendito Mantato have made their first-team debuts, it was a golden opportunity in particular for Camacho, the stand-out player in the academy this season following the exit of JJ Gabriel.

United routinely promote multiple academy players each day to bolster numbers in first-team training sessions and it’s a good way for staff to bridge the gap in speed, physicality and intensity between senior and youth football.

Every manager that comes in likes to take on a project and when Ruben Amorim walked through the door it was Godwill Kukonki that caught the eye of staff.

Gabriel became a training mainstay in the months prior to his bombshell request to terminate his contract, and now Camacho, Munro and Armer are working towards first-team debuts instead.
