BBC Apologizes After ‘Strictly Come Dancing’ Is Interrupted By Sex Noises

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The BBC has apologized to viewers after lewd noises could be heard during the Strictly Come Dancing live show at Blackpool’s Tower Ballroom.

A recording of sex noises appeared to be played from the audience as Strictly judge Motsi Mabuse delivered her remarks about the performance of Delta Goodrem and partner Kai Widdrington.

Microphones picked up the noise. Mabuse turned to the audience to establish what was going on, before facing the camera again and exclaiming: “It’s not me.”

Emma Willis had to apologise at the end of tonight’s Strictly Come Dancing for ‘anything offensive’ that was heard during the show. Turns out, it was sex noises played by someone in the background. The episode of #Strictly is currently still not available on iPlayer. pic.twitter.com/fPxDW45h1V

At the end of the show (the format on which ABC’s Dancing with the Stars is based), presenter Emma Willis apologized to viewers “if you heard any audio disruptions”. In a statement, a BBC spokesperson added: “We apologise to any viewers offended during the live broadcast of Strictly this evening. We are investigating how this happened.” The noise was edited out of the version of the show available on iPlayer. Watch on Deadline

The police were called to the incident. A Lancashire Police spokesperson told The Guardian: “We are aware of an incident that took place during the filming of Strictly Come Dancing in Blackpool.

“We have been in contact with the event organisers, and a formal complaint has been made to us. A mobile phone has been seized and we are now looking into the matter to see if any criminal offences have taken place.”

It is the second time a BBC live show has been interrupted by sex noises. Coverage of an FA Cup tie between Wolves and Liverpool was plagued by an apparent pornography prank in 2023.

Gary Lineker was anchoring live build-up to the replay match at the Molineux Stadium when he was interrupted by what sounded like sex noises coming from the studio.

The former England player looked perplexed by the noise in the studio, joking to fellow presenter Alan Shearer: “I don’t know who’s making that noise … It’s toasty in this studio, it’s a bit noisy as well. I don’t know if somebody is sending something on someone’s phone, if it’s a joke. I don’t know whether you heard it at home.”

Lineker later tweeted a picture of a phone, which he said had been taped to the back of the set in an apparent act of sabotage.
