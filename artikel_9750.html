Business processes will include several different tasks, departments and systems. This can become time consuming and cumbersome to manage manually and can cause delays, double work, miscommunication and human error. Understand your current processes, identify where improvements can be made and implement automation in a structured way. By mapping out each step of the process, your business can be more organized, dependable and efficient.

Whether you’re looking to automate customer queries, payments, employee records or general internal processes, having a plan in place can help reduce manual work and improve consistency.

Business process automation mapping is when you produce a visual representation of your business processes so you can identify which parts can be automated.

When combined, this gives your organization a greater understanding of their processes and where automation can be most beneficial.

Before beginning to automate your processes, it is important to understand your current operations. If you try to automate an inefficient process without taking a look at the structure, you could just speed up your problems.

Creating a dependable automation strategy requires more than just choosing the right software and linking your systems together. First you need to understand what your business needs from its processes.

To help improve your processes and make them more efficient, there are some best practice tips to consider:

The first thing to do is understand what areas within your business processes are taking too long or requiring too much manual work.

Speak to your employees who regularly carry out these tasks. They can give you real insight into any issues.

By knowing what the issue actually is, you won’t waste time automating processes which don’t need much changes.

Each process map should be created with a clear objective. This means understanding what you want to achieve. You may want to reduce the time taken to process invoices, improve customer onboarding or make it easier to approve employee leave.

By identifying clear start and end points, you will avoid any unnecessary tasks.

Being clear with your scope will also help you know which departments, employees, systems and resources are involved.

One of the most important things you can do is document your current process. Make sure you document each task in the order it happens including decisions, approval, handover and exceptions.

Your basic process map could look like: Start of task->Gathering information->Verifying data->Approval/decision making->End of task-> Keeping records.

By documenting your current processes, you can identify any unnecessary tasks and understand how information flows between departments.

You’ll also have a reference point to compare your original process to your improved automated process.

Not every task in your business will be suitable for automation. Focus on processes with predictable patterns and repeated tasks.

Some examples could be: Sending confirmation emails, Creating invoices, Updating customer information, Scheduling regular notifications, Processing standard application forms, Organizing & transferring business data.

Jobs which require complex decision making, negotiating or personal interaction may still need human input. Starting with simpler processes will help you understand how the technology works and identify any issues.

Although you are automating processes, you will still need human oversight. Make sure your employees understand what tasks have been automated.

By knowing who has responsibility, you will avoid confusion and know who to refer to if there are any issues.

Your process map should be easy to understand for everyone in your department.

Use the same icons, labels and layout when mapping your processes. Different methods such as flowcharts and swim lanes can help you map different types of processes.

Swim lanes are great to use if you have multiple departments involved as it shows you who is responsible for each task.

Ensure you are not providing too much information. Too much can make it confusing. However, keep it simple and you may miss out on important decisions and responsibilities.

Many businesses will have multiple systems and databases. However, to make your automation more effective, you need to ensure accurate communication between systems.

Before implementing your automated process, look at the systems you need to use and see if they can communicate with each other.

If the systems aren’t integrated correctly, your employees may still have to manually transfer information. Consider the compatibility of your systems, accuracy of data, permissions and integration.

Testing your process is important to ensure it works how it should. Before implementing your automation throughout your organization, test your process in realistic scenarios.

Gather feedback from employees who regularly use the process and make any adjustments.

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) are written instructions for carrying out specific tasks. This is important to ensure consistency.

Having everything documented will help new employees understand the process and reduce reliance on verbal instructions.

Business process automation mapping is an important part of improving your modern business. It will help you understand your current processes, identify any inefficiencies and identify where automation can help.

Following best practices will help you clearly define responsibilities, keep processes consistent, improve integration and reduce any unnecessary manual work. However, you will need to continually focus on your processes. Test your processes, provide the right guidance and keep your process maps updated.

By using effective process mapping, careful planning and regular monitoring, you can create more reliable processes, improve productivity and create a strong base for your long term growth.
