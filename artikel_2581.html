What's next for the Twins? Three things Minnesota must do to make a postseason push in 2027 season

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

It has been a surprisingly productive week for the Minnesota Twins. First, catcher Ryan Jeffers accepted the $23.15 million qualifying offer on Tuesday. Jeffers turned down a multi-year offer from the Twins, according to The Athletic, so he's betting on himself. Betting that he'll at least maintain his free agent stock and get paid next offseason.

Then, on Wednesday, the Twins brought back veteran righty Sonny Gray with a two-year contract worth $55 million. There's an opt-out after the first year. Gray, 37, finished fifth in the AL Cy Young voting this season and pitched wonderfully for the Twins in 2022 and 2023. He joins a rotation that, on paper, looks like one of the better starting units in the American League.

"I really don't take it lightly that he chose us," GM Jeremy Zoll told the Associated Press about Gray's return. "He had an abundance of options, and given where he's at in his career, I know how important it is to him. He expressed it quite directly how important it is to win, and this isn't just about having a good season or a winning season. It's about winning and going deep in the playoffs and making a real playoff run."

Matthews could land in the bullpen next season and that might be his long-term home. His stuff should be well suited to a relief role. Gray makes the rotation better and potentially the bullpen better as well by bumping Matthews to relief. Jeffers accepting the qualifying offer keeps a key middle-of-the-order bat in place. A good week for the Twins, this has been.

After a disappointing 77-85 finish this year, Minnesota has already improved significantly in the early days of the offseason. They play in the AL Central. With all due respect, it is not the most imposing division. There's no reason the Twins can't be in the division and wild card races next season. Here now is what's left on Minnesota's offseason to-do list.

Teams had until 5 p.m. ET Friday to offer their pre-arbitration and arbitration-eligible players contracts for 2027. They didn't have to sign them Friday, but they had to tender an offer; otherwise, the player immediately became a free agent. Dozens of players were cut loose at the non-tender deadline, as usual, and two players stood out as non-tender candidates for the Twins: Trevor Larnach and Royce Lewis. Larnach was indeed non-tendered, while Lewis was dealt to the Dodgers.

It's not often a player gets non-tendered after posting a .352 on-base percentage and 120 OPS+, but the writing was on the wall. The Twins had an agreement in place to trade Larnach at the deadline (Astros owner Jim Crane nixed the deal) and they have a very crowded outfield. Also, Larnach had wrist surgery earlier this month. That created some questions going into next season, and the Twins opted to cut him loose.

Larnach had one more year of control remaining and was expected to make over $6 million through arbitration in 2027. I reckon the Twins tried again to trade him prior to Friday's non-tender deadline. In the end, he was non-tendered rather than risk tying $6 million or so up in yet another outfielder, particularly one who is coming off wrist surgery.

Things got so bad this summer that Lewis, who has a .288 on-base percentage in over 1,200 plate appearances the last three years, was sent to Triple-A for a spell, and also moved across the diamond from third base to first base. The Twins could not count on him to provide offense and now he's playing a position with a high offensive bar. That made him expendable.

Lewis, 27, was under team control through 2028 and that extra year was an important consideration for the club. His salary would have been in the $5 million range next season through arbitration, which is pricey but not exorbitant. Now, Lewis will receive a fresh start with the Dodgers, where he can be one of the guys, not the former No. 1 overall pick and expected face of the franchise.

The Twins presumably did not want to give up on a player with as much talent as Lewis. At the same time, they kept trying, and it never worked.

The Twins got a head start on their offseason bullpen shopping when they acquired Jeff Hoffman at the trade deadline. He has one more year remaining on the contract and will be in the team's late-inning mix in 2027.

Morris is not yet a household name, but he has a great arm and was dominant at times in 2026. The Twins have themselves a nice little late-inning trio in Gómez, Hoffman, and Morris. A spare starter like Matthews or Prielipp could wind up in the bullpen as well. Given the quality of their stuff, they have a chance to make a real impact as relievers.

Clearly though, the middle relief could use some help, and the Twins wouldn't need to spend big dollars to improve it. A second-tier free agent like, say, Andrew Kittredge would be a solid pickup. It's worth giving the Miami Marlins a call to see what it would take to get Anthony Bender and/or Michael Petersen. Point is, the bullpen can be improved, for sure.

The Twins have way more outfielders than outfield spots and that is in no way a bad thing. First and foremost, it allows them to use Byron Buxton as the full-time DH, which could help keep him on the field. Secondly, there's no such thing as too many good players. Depth is important and the Twins certainly have plenty of it in the outfield.
