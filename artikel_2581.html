‘You’re Trying To Reset Culture’ – Draymond Green Rips Eastern Conference HC for Benching Stars in OT Loss vs. Lakers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Charlotte Hornets have been on the cusp of making the Eastern Conference playoffs multiple times in the last few years, but have fallen short. The team has a clear defensive identity under head coach Charles Lee.

However, NBA champion and current Warriors forward Draymond Green questioned Lee's recent decision-making.

The Hornets had the opportunity to get a signature win against the Los Angeles Lakers last Tuesday, but Lee opted to bench key players Brandon Miller and Coby White during the game's most important moments.

Green reacted to Lee's decision on his show, saying, " I don't understand that. They traded Miles Bridges and LaMelo Ball in the same year. When you do that, you're trying to reset culture. And when you're trying to reset culture, a win over the Los Angeles Lakers, that's a win where it can change the direction of your season."

Green continued, "To say, 'Oh, they've been working back from injury, these guys in game 8 and 12, and you talking about we watching their minutes? Nah."

Lee elected to keep Miller and White on the sidelines during overtime of the Hornets' 118-114 loss to the Lakers, and later confirmed that both players were on minutes restrictions as they worked their way back from injuries.

The fallout from the benchings is the latest frustration in Lee's difficult third season. The Hornets are near the bottom of the East with a 4-12 record. While Lee has maintained the team's defensive identity, the Hornets are one of the worst offensive teams in the NBA.

Some of the team's offensive struggles are directly related to injuries. Kon Knueppel emerged as one of the best shooters in the league last season as a rookie, but has missed significant time due to a left hamstring strain.

With Ball and Bridges gone, Miller is potentially the Hornets' new franchise player. However, he's already dealt with hip, groin, and knee pain this year.

Miller is averaging 19.9 points per game this season, but has been inefficient, shooting 35.8% from the field and 29.4% from three. His three-point struggles are particularly alarming because he's attempting over 10 per game.

Miller starts a five-year $208,257,925 extension next season, so Lee and the organization are carefully monitoring his health even if Green doesn't approve of the methods.

The Hornets signed White to a three-year, $74 million contract to essentially replace Ball, and he's also started the season slowly after suffering a left calf strain during training camp. White has dealt with recurring calf issues, which is likely why the Hornets are being cautious with his minutes.

While Green and others question Lee's decisions, the Hornets need their top players at full strength to move up the Eastern Conference standings.
