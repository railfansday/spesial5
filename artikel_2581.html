‘That was freaking insane’: Chicago Bears stay ‘super resilient’ to rally and pull off a 39-35 win

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

When Chicago Bears wide receiver Rome Odunze toe-tapped in the end zone for a 6-yard go-ahead touchdown catch with 3 seconds left Thursday, it transported Sam Roush back to the frenzied fourth-quarter rallies of last season — and the rookie tight end wasn't even on the team then.

"I remember being in college (at Stanford) watching (the Bears) last year and being like, 'How is this even possible?'" he said after the Bears' 39-35 comeback thriller over the Detroit Lions on Thanksgiving Day at Ford Field. "And now I get to be a part of it."

Odunze's score was the first time the Bears led all game.

"Obviously, I'm not the guy that made those big plays today, but seeing Rome do that was incredible at the end of the game," Roush said. "And (quarterback) Caleb (Williams), like, that's why they call Caleb 'Ice Man.' Like, that was freaking insane.

If there's a word that describes the Bears on Thursday, it's "dogged."

There were several points in the game that looked like backbreakers, and some of the setbacks that seemed almost insurmountable.

• Midway through the first quarter, tight end Colston Loveland lost a fumble after his only catch.

• Later in the first, the Bears lost Loveland for the rest of the game after he injured his knee while blocking on a run play.

• The Bears' chance to mount a 2-minute drill before halftime ended with Williams taking a strip-sack and a safety.

• When Jahmyr Gibbs sprinted 70 yards for a fourth-quarter touchdown, it looked like a wrap.

Bears running back D'Andre Swift said they remained unfazed, even as the Lions built an 11-point advantage after Gibbs's scamper pushed Detroit's lead to 35-24 with 8 minutes, 20 seconds left in the game.

"We (were) always in the game, and I feel like the game never really got away from us," Swift said. "We gave up some stuff early on and kind of made it hard for us.

"But it was a heavyweight fight. Back and forth, back and forth. Went down to the wire."

Swift had a 30-yard touchdown run, and Kyle Monangai ran in 1- and 2-yard touchdowns, scores that helped keep the Bears engaged in a seesaw battle in the second and third quarters.

Odunze's two touchdown receptions both came in the fourth quarter.

The Bears defense coughed up 205 rushing yards and three touchdowns to Gibbs, but also came up with a pair of crucial plays.

"Defense came up in critical situations," Swift said. "It's how we fight as a team.

"That's the one word I would use for that win is just 'fight.'"

Rookie safety Dillon Thieneman caught his third interception of the season (and second in consecutive games) after Jared Goff's fourth-quarter pass bounced off receiver Jameson Williams' hands and into Thieneman's chest.

The offense couldn't convert that pick into points, but it didn't stall a Lions drive that reached the Chicago 34-yard line and likely cost Detroit at least a field goal try.

"I felt like that was a big change, and then giving it to the offense and all them to build some more momentum with that," Thieneman said. "I felt it was really important."

On another crucial defensive play, T.J. Edwards wrangled Gibbs to the ground after a 1-yard catch on fourth-and-4, where nothing was backing up Edwards but a lot of green turf.

Edwards' stop forced the Lions to turn the ball over on downs.

The offense then marched 68 yards on 13 plays in 2 minutes, 14 seconds, with Odunze's toe-drag touchdown capping it off.

Like Roush, Thieneman isn't used to these kinds of wild finishes.

"It's awesome, he said. "Definitely a lot of ups and downs. You gotta control yourself, gotta stay level in it all."

Throughout the game, various players and coaches would make uplifting comments such as "don't forget we've got a game to win, we're still in this," Kalif Raymond said.

"I remember, I want to say, (quarterbacks coach) J.T. Barrett came out on the sidelines, like, 'Hey, man, we're in this. This game is down to the wire.'"

Last season, the Bears engineered seven late-game comebacks between the regular season and playoffs.

This season, they've taken more leads and spent more time fending off comebacks, so Thursday's game was the first true nailbiter.

"Man, I'm really proud of our guys," Ben Johnson said. "I think about how far we've come since we've been in the building the last year and a half. Just the professionalism with which they conduct themselves, the hard work they put in all season long, but certainly on a short week like this.

"And then the belief that they had in a game like that, particularly when we gave them some momentum at halftime. And yet to be able to weather that storm."

Linebacker D'Marco Jackson said the team had heard the chatter about the Bears' struggles in the NFC North (2-6 under Johnson heading into the matchup), so to steal a win on the road against a division opponent, under those circumstances, was "super huge."

"I think a lot of people wrote us off thinking we weren't going to be able to come in here and pull it off," Jackson said. "We did just that and we put everybody on notice."

Roush also learned about the makeup of the team.

"Super resilient," he said. "Lot of playmakers. … It really is incredible. We're still hungry."
