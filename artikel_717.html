Holloway sacked by Swindon after two seasons

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Ian Holloway surpassed 1,000 games managing in English football during his time at Swindon

Manager Ian Holloway has been sacked by Swindon Town after just over two years in charge.

The Robins' 2-0 loss at Newport County on Saturday left them 17th in League Two and was their fourth successive defeat in all competitions - conceding 10 goals in the process.

They have won only two of their past 15 matches, and Holloway last month described himself as a "dead man walking" if Swindon's form did not turn around.

"Since joining the club towards the end of 2024, Ian has played an important role in stabilising our on-field position and helping the club make progress season upon season," read a Swindon statement.

"However, following careful consideration of recent results and performances, the club believes that now is the right time to take a different direction.

"This has not been an easy decision, but the board firmly believes that it is important to make difficult decisions when necessary, always with the best interests of Swindon Town at heart."

Coaches Gavin Gunning and Steve Mildenhall will take charge of the first team on an interim basis, with David Carney assisting them.

Holloway was appointed in October 2024 until the end of that season, with Swindon one place above the relegation zone.

He surpassed 1,000 games managing in English football two months later and signed a two-year contract in March 2025, having steered Swindon into play-off contention.

Promotion was the aim last season, with Holloway issuing a rallying call at the start of the campaign for Swindon to be the "team I believe we can" be.

They were in the automatic promotion spots in February, but only three wins in their last 13 games, including a 2-1 defeat by Chesterfield in a must-win final match, meant they missed out on the play-offs.

Although Swindon won three of their first five league games this season, they are without a win in their past five and have scored one goal in that time.

This season has largely consisted of feeling Swindon are two games away from a crisis and Australia-based chairman Clem Morfuni's arrival in the country to see deserved defeats by Newport and Grimsby finally made it one.

The team have consistently struggled to create goals and chances despite having League Two's player of the year Aaron Drinan, a rare exception to a chronic injury list.

The fans' energy for Holloway had grown thin, and sometimes vice versa.

Being ushered away by a steward at Rodney Parade as his last act may be a summary of how both felt about each other.

But there is a clear feel of stagnation at Swindon - and that means putting another manager on the chopping block is no guarantee of improvement.
