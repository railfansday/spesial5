Home listings are piling up in October, but price cuts are close to last year's level

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Homes under contract fell 10.5% from a year earlier, the steepest drop since October 2023, as mortgage rates averaged 7.33%

Realtor.com, a unit of News Corp, said active listings rose 7.4% from a year earlier in October to 1,183,500 homes, its quickest year-over-year pace in eight months, even as new listings fell 7.5%. Active inventory has grown for four straight months, narrowing the gap to typical pre-pandemic supply to its smallest since the pandemic.

Sellers did not pull listings in large numbers. The delisting rate held at 4.4% of active listings, compared with 4.6% a year earlier, Realtor.com said. Price cuts appeared on 20.5% of active listings in October, down 0.3 percentage point from September and 0.2 percentage point above a year earlier. That was the highest October share since 2022. The national median list price was $415,000, down 2.2% from a year earlier, the 12th straight month of annual declines.

"The market is cooling on both sides, but in different ways," Danielle Hale, Realtor.com's chief economist, said in a statement. "Buyers are stepping back as higher mortgage rates cut into their buying power, while sellers are listing less often without a broad increase in delistings or price cuts."

Jake Krimmel, a senior economist at Realtor.com, said in a statement: "On the seller side, we see more of a pause than an exodus." Krimmel said a seller pulling a listing around Halloween or Thanksgiving does not necessarily show the same distress as one who exits in midsummer.

Buyers pulled back further. Homes under contract fell 10.5% from a year earlier, a third straight monthly decline, Realtor.com said. Thirty-year mortgage rates averaged 7.33% in October, 110 basis points above a year earlier.

Zillow reported a 16.8% drop in newly pending listings from a year earlier in October. That marked the sharpest year-over-year fall since August 2023, and October activity had not been this weak since 2018. Zillow's count of homes for sale reached 1.4 million, up 3.4% from a year earlier.

Zillow said 26.5% of listings had a price cut in October, down from 26.9% a year earlier and 27.3% in September. The monthly payment for a typical American home came to an estimated $2,004, which is 12.8% more than a year earlier and 4.8% above September, the largest single-month increase in four years.

"November is shaping up to have an even larger contraction, but the silver lining is that next month is likely going to be the worst of it," Mischa Fisher, Zillow's chief economist, said in a statement, according to CNBC.

Mortgage rates had fallen for a third straight week by the week ending Oct. 30, to 7.30% on the 30-year fixed contract rate from 7.37%. The rate reached 7.50% in the week ended Oct. 9, the highest since November 2023. The housing market has weakened since rates returned to 7% in mid-September, and an index tracking contract signings for previously owned homes hit a record low in September.
