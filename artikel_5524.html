Getting Over the Down Payment Hurdle

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Is saving for a down payment the biggest thing standing between you and buying a home right now? You’re not alone in that.

It’s easy to get discouraged when you think it’s going to take years to save up the money you need. But guess what? You might not have to save it all yourself.

There’s help out there for homebuyers that a lot of people don’t know about. It’s called down payment assistance – and it could help you buy a lot sooner than you think.

Down payment assistance (DPA) is designed to give your down payment savings a boost. In some cases, it can also be used toward your closing costs. It’s funded by cities, nonprofits, state housing agencies, and other organizations to promote homeownership.

And just how much money is out there? The figures are pretty impressive. According to Down Payment Resource, qualified buyers could get close to $20,000 they could use to help them buy a home:

“With an average benefit of $18,000, down payment assistance (DPA) remains one of the most essential tools for addressing the nation’s affordability challenges. Programs continue to expand in scope, serving a broader range of incomes, property types and borrower needs, including first-generation, military and repeat buyers.”

While you may think this type of program is only meant for first-time buyers or buyers with limited income, that’s a common misconception. Here’s a breakdown from Down Payment Resource of what’s currently availableacross their over 2,800 programs:

74% provide down payment or closing cost assistance.

62% are available to first-time homebuyers.

While 62% of DPA programs are designed for first-time buyers, repeat buyers can be eligible for help too – partially thanks to how “first-time homebuyer” is defined.

The federal government – and most loan and DPA programs – define a first-time buyer as someone who has not owned a property they’ve lived in for the past 3 years. That means even repeat buyers could qualify, depending on their situation.

This just goes to show there are DPA programs out there for almost every kind of buyer and new options keep being added.

In fact, in Q3 2026, the number of programs available hit an all-time high. Up 8% from the prior year. So, if you’ve been thinking: “This sounds great, but it’s not for people like me,” you may want to reconsider.

There are different down payment assistance packages available, depending on where you live, your financial situation, and your goals. Your professional loan officer can give you specifics on how different DPA programs work and help find the right fit for you.

Just over half of down payment assistance is given as a second mortgage on top of your regular mortgage. Many of these loans are considered “silent” second mortgages, which means you aren’t required to make payments on them right away.

Different programs may even offer the ability for these second mortgages to be “forgiven” – meaning you don’t have to pay them back at all – if you live in the home long enough.

In fact, grant programs (which require no repayment if terms are met) now make up 8% of all available DPA programs.

The takeaway? Help is available – and there are a lot more options out there than you think.
