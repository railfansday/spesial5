Oil prices fall more than 4% as Iran peace talks ease supply fears

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Brent crude for January delivery fell 4.2% to $108.68 a barrel, reversing some of last week's gains ahead of Tuesday's U.S. midterm elections

Oil prices tumbled by upwards of 4% Monday, with negotiations to bring the U.S.-Iran conflict to a close soothing worries about Middle East supply.

Brent crude futures expiring in January slid 4.2% to $108.68 per barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate contracts due in December declined 4.3% to $99.61, while the January contracts shed 4.2% to reach $97.90.

The declines wiped out part of the previous week's rally. Brent had closed out October at a six-month peak.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Sunday that negotiations with mediators from Qatar about reopening the Strait of Hormuz were still under way, though no deal had been reached. He added that Iran had sent a delegation headed by a deputy foreign minister to Qatar, while no face-to-face meeting with U.S. officials was on the schedule.

Tim Waterer, chief market analyst at KCM Trade, described the move as a technical pullback after last week’s strong gains, according to Reuters. He cited continued optimism about a diplomatic breakthrough, along with a weekend that passed without significant fighting escalating.

The risk of renewed fighting remains. According to The Wall Street Journal, the U.S. has instructed a number of Gulf Arab states to get ready for a possible brief but intense military operation against Iran once Tuesday's midterm elections are over. President Donald Trump has yet to reach a conclusive decision about resuming strikes.

Seven OPEC+ producers decided Sunday to leave their targets for December output as they were. The group, whose members include Saudi Arabia and Russia, will next convene on Nov. 29 to assess output for January.

Regional attacks also continued over the weekend. The Saudi-led coalition announced Sunday an operation against assets that the Iran-backed Houthi militants in Yemen depend on for their drone and missile efforts.

Vice President JD Vance wrote last week to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, to try to revive peace talks. Trump said he had approved the outreach, which was meant to outline a possible structure for talks rather than present a full peace plan.

Qatari mediators also offered a new plan to end the fighting among Iran, the U.S. and Israel. A high-ranking Iranian official dismissed the offer as unacceptable. The U.S. and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding on June 17, but the agreement collapsed.
