England clung on to beat Spain 3-2 and top Nations League Group A3 in a thriller in Madrid, inflicting the first defeat on the world champions at home in 39 years.

Harry Kane deservedly opened the scoring with a composed penalty shortly before half-time, before Ezri Konsa doubled the Three Lions’ lead in the second half with a free header from a Declan Rice corner.

Ben White then marked his full return to the England fold - having started for the first time in this campaign - with a brilliant finish through Simon’s legs to make it 3-0.

But the game flipped entirely when Elliot Anderson was sent off and conceded a penalty for hauling down Lamine Yamal 65 minutes in, with Mikel Oyarzabal slotting home the spot-kick to make it 3-1.

Anthony Gordon was punished for losing possession cheaply as Yamal raced away to tee up Nico Williams for Spain’s second, setting up a grandstand finish in Madrid.

James Trafford was the hero as he produced several fine saves, including a double in extra time, and England held on to make a major statement and end Spain’s 43-game unbeaten run.

England hold out, for a raucous win that will rightly be held up. Sure, it might not have been what it could have been as Ben White surged in for a sensational third in Madrid… but that last word remains key.

England beat the world and European champions 3-2 in their own capital, to end the longest unbeaten run in international history, that stretched back to March 2024.

Spain couldn’t make it 44 games, as England just about made it to 94 minutes. That was ultimately down to a brilliant double save from James Trafford, who was one of many positives on another Nations League night of enjoyably entertaining drama. England manager Thomas Tuchel refused to get drawn into debates around his starting goalkeeper after James Trafford helped the Three Lions to a famous win over Spain in Madrid.

Tuchel’s side put on a superb display at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano, with Harry Kane opening the scoring from the spot before Ezri Konsa broke free to nod in from a corner when unmarked early into the second half.

The Three Lions were in dreamland not long after, with a superb team goal ending with Ben White sliding an accomplished finish in for 3-0 in the 58th minute.

The Three Lions had already confirmed their place in the quarter-finals in March with victory over Croatia earlier in this window, but have given themselves the best chances of a favourable draw by beating Spain 3-2 on Sunday and proven they are one of the teams to beat.

Here is how the players rated at the Metropolitano:

Declan Rice, 7: Conceded a needless free-kick and yellow card for tripping Yamal, and sent a poor free-kick straight at Simon in the 34th minute. Redeemed himself with an excellent delivery for Konsa’s goal and was a physical presence in midfield.

Morgan Rogers, 6: Didn’t quite get the pass right to Bellingham after a fine first-half team move. Outshone by the rest of the attack tonight.

Harry Kane, 8: In the right positions but flashed an early chance narrowly wide and off-target with a close-range header which was flagged offside. But led the line as always, won the penalty and put England in front with a brilliantly composed spot-kick. Replaced by Myles Lewis-Skelly.

Anthony Gordon, 6: A tale of two halves: 9 for the first half, 4 for the second. Fast and relentless; had the beating of Marc Pubill for pace and was a constant threat down the left flank. As Declan Rice is discussing how he had an “average World Cup”, and talks at length about the challenges modern players have in ensuring prime fitness, a seemingly more trivial topic comes up: the punditry of Graeme Souness. The former Liverpool captain has in recent weeks criticised Rice for playing “old man’s football” and “cheating” in his midfield role, while insisting he’s a “centre-back”.

Rice, of course, handles it diplomatically – “there would be no disrespect there, I’d shake his hand, I’d chat with him” – but with some edge, too. The England vice-captain asserts this is what the modern game demands, and it’s completely different to Souness’ time.
