Vogue Editors Predict the Hottest Coats of the Coldest Season

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

While winter technically starts on December 21, we’re calling it now: the season has officially begun. We feel confident in this pronouncement because, here in New York, all around us, jackets are finally manifesting. On sidewalks, in subway cars, hanging on crammed racks at the entrance of restaurants, on the backs of stylish passersby—everywhere we look, there are trenches, puffers, shearlings, bikers, blousons, sleek gorpy jackets, and so many more. Every shape, color, fabric, and mood a person can imagine is represented by a fetching piece of outerwear. Naturally, of course, we have some thoughts on what will be crowned the coat of the season. With that in mind, we asked Vogue editors around the office to take a stab at what they think the hottest coat of the winter 2026-27 season will be. Here are a few very educated guesses.

“In the spirit of the forthcoming Met Gala, a coat that is more than ordinary. Caroline Bille Brahe is brilliant at taking a classic, like a winter tweed or a classic duffel and working her magic on it by adding strong shoulders and a yoke, or storybook decorations.” —Laird Borrelli-Persson, senior archive editor

“Conner Ives certainly did not invent the fur-trimmed chinoiserie coat, but his spring 2027 runway—plus its cadre of celebrity fans—sent everyone from the ladies who lunch to the louche downtown set clamoring for opulent outerwear. Don’t have six grand for one of Ives’ coats? There are already plenty of mass market interpretations in the works—plus a host of enterprising TikTokers sharing how to make your own.” —Hannah Jackson, senior fashion writer

Connor Ives Reconstituted Vintage Fur and Silk Robe Coat in Black, £6,500

“Coat necklines have been going higher and higher every season, so this winter, I predict the full-on funnel neck coat will be huge. I love how structured-yet-sleek the silhouette is. Phoebe Philo has luxe trench or leather versions, but there are also affordable ones too, like this gray Mango wool style. And the best part? They cover your neck entirely—meaning, one less layer you have to pile on in the winter months.” — Christian Allaire, senior fashion and style writer

“As a Prada devotee, I saw this jacket go down the runway back in January and was immediately obsessed—it was pure Prada: subversive, distinctive, slightly off-putting and totally amazing. Its classic khaki cotton edges are fraying and crumbling away, revealing hints of traditional houndstooth at the hems and seams (and, in a particularly brilliant move, replacing the triangular logo at the yoke). But it’s also the most classic of shapes, easy to wear with practically anything. An amazing coat, 10/10. The only drawback (for me, at least) is that five-figure price tag. At least it’s a nice round number …?” —Max Berlinger, senior fashion news editor

“I’ve been wanting a lightweight plaid mackintosh for a minute now. I want something really long and with a classic approach to the pattern, and I found this on Massimo Dutti last week. I’ve been seeing these out on the streets a bit, it’s just such an easy way to add a little energy to an outfit, especially if you tend to wear darker colors (like me).” —Max Berlinger, senior fashion news editor

Massimo Dutti Long Plaid Trench Coat, $490

“There is no more dominant jacket style than the mockneck bomber at this very moment—and you can thank Phoebe Philo for that. Still, despite her undeniable influence, whether the jacket is suede, leather, or technical, the most important part is the uber-studied, ultimately effortless Olsen tuck.” - Alexandra Hildreth, fashion news writer

Phoebe Philo Rib Neck Black Leather Jacket, $8,000 Nour Hammour Quince Soft Suede Jacket in Mocha, $1,885Cos Leather Mock Neck Leather Bomber Jacket, $549

“Decadent, novelty overcoats are causing a frenzy. They might not be the most practical (or keep you very warm, for that matter), but they sure will make the outfit. They range from Colleen Allen’s velvet opera coats to Kallmeyer and Chan Luu’s jacquard dusters. Special shoutout to the Conner Ives vintage fur-trimmed satin coats that have been breaking the internet. Even Nour Hammour’s shearling and faux fur-trimmed coats are making outerwear a little more extra this winter. Here’s to making the everyday more extraordinary or, simply put, making your coat the look.” —Madeline Fass, fashion director, Vogue Shopping

“The Vogue girls cleared house and won at Arma’s mahjong night a few weeks ago, which means that a few of us will be wearing our field jackets for the next few months. But while we may have won our jackets, the brand has been on fire since their presentations in New York and Paris last season. In fact, eagle-eyed Vogue.com readers may have even clocked their leopard coat, which has been photographed by Phil Oh multiple times in each Fashion Week city. But I like how the field jacket feels oversized and casual enough to wear with a plain sweater or even with a dressier jacket.” —Irene Kim, editorial production manager

“Now that we’ve dredged all that we can from grandma’s attic, I think the next place to pull from is another house … the house of god. Stay with me, here: I think with Tom Ford’s Cry to Heaven on its way, and Pope Leo’s cultural dominance, we’ll see some monastic silhouettes beginning to emerge. Think the buttoned and buckled capes of Lanvin and Ann Demeulemeester, Nanushka's cheek-grazing collars, and the ecclesiastical severity of Mame Kurogouchi or Max Mara’s fall 2026 collections. It doesn’t have to mean austere—I love the drama of a mass! More like medieval monk meets modern minimalist, let’s say. I particularly love this La Veste Veste red and black cape that feels made for delivering the best homily of your life.”

“I’ve also come to embrace the bitchy little blazer—The Gilded Age’s Louisa Jacobson being a big reference here. She loves some sexy Tom Ford suiting with a sharp-as-hell lapel. New kids on the block, Catalogue NYC are making slim, gently cinched versions while Antwerp’s La Collection offers up decadent texture with their Margaret blazer. Brenda from accounts, this is not.”

“Despite their more tempered winters, Spanish women really know how to do good outerwear. And La Veste (which has been hosting a pop-up in New York) transitioned from a summer of sparkly short-shorts and circus tent-striped sets into sumptuous, candy-colored wool blazers and car coats. Violet, chartreuse, and funky combo stripes abound! Pro tip: La Veste co-founder Blanca Miro has collaborated with IYKYK Italian vintage dealer La Soffitta di Lulù on a stunning mini collection of fur-lined robes in sunshine yellow, sky blue, and unexpected prints.” —Anna Cafolla, news editor

“I think we’ll see something rich, opulent, decadent emerge this season, a response to the sleek, streamlined and, indeed, minimal vibes that have had a stronghold on winter fashion for a few years (see above: Connor Ives’s fur and silk robe coat). I can think of no better example than this plushy Dries Van Noten coat in an acid tone with a sumptuous surface and a regal collar. In fact, if I were a betting man, I’d say this exact model may just be THE coat of the season.” —Max Berlinger, senior fashion news editor
