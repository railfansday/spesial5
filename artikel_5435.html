If a home were designed specifically to deliver work-life balance, what would it look like? This was the challenge put to Studio MM Architect for this project, nestled in the foothills of the Catskills. Adding a twist, the project is the architect’s personal residence.

“For me, the most important part of the design was balancing living and working in my home,” says Marica McKeel, founder and principal of Studio MM Architect.

The design brings together a home, studio, and separate bunkhouse, each with distinct functions and stylistic identities.

“We specifically designed the house in two volumes—living and work/guest/other. The most intentional aspect of the design is having two separate staircases,” says McKeel.

The gallery doubles as exhibition space for the owner’s art collection, viewable from multiple vantage points. The bunkhouse is a comfortable, compact area for guests, with glass walls that extend views beautifully.

Collaboration with local artisans and subcontractors was an important part of this project, and resulted in several unique interior elements, such as the wall-drained teak powder room sink and dual kitchen islands with integrated joinery.

The team was willing and able to pivot when inspiration struck, as it often did during the construction process. Some of the most striking interior details were designed and built onsite, after major structural elements were installed, including the office stair railing and the woven railing near the primary suite.
