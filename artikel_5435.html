Nvidia's board is investigating CEO Jensen Huang's ties to a suspected Chinese spy

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Huang apologized to staff for being unfaithful to his wife, and the board said it has no reason to believe company secrets were compromised

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang (I-Hwa Cheng/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

Nvidia $NVDA's board of directors opened an independent investigation on Sunday after reports surfaced that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation, the company said. A panel made up of independent board members, with support from outside legal advisers, will supervise the inquiry.

Huang apologized to staff in a message for his infidelity toward his spouse. "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did," he wrote. Huang said he supports the investigation and will cooperate.

Jensen Huang disclosed in a note to employees that Alice Huang was the woman involved in his affair. Alice Huang is a business executive believed to be an operative for Chinese intelligence, according to The Wall Street Journal. She is no relation to the Nvidia CEO.

The board said it has seen nothing suggesting that U.S. security interests or Nvidia's proprietary information were put at risk, and that its review will draw on whatever information it can gather. The board said it has confidence in Jensen Huang and his leadership team and will provide updates as appropriate.

"Today's news will not slow Nvidia. Jensen is committed to continuing to lead Nvidia as CEO without interruption," a Nvidia spokesperson said in a statement to The Journal.

U.S. officials concluded that Alice Huang, then a top executive at Megaspeed International, passed information to the Chinese government during her work with Nvidia on building data centers in Asia, according to The Journal. Megaspeed has said it is a "compliant and legitimate company." The Chinese Embassy in Washington rejected the spying allegations.

Jensen Huang said last month that Nvidia's business with Megaspeed was "normal and not unusual". He said Nvidia had verified that Megaspeed did not ship its AI processors to China in violation of the law. Nvidia spokesman John Rizzo said at the time that Jensen Huang's interactions with Alice Huang were "not unusual or unexpected."

Three House Republican committee chairs wrote to Jensen Huang on Wednesday requesting that he appear for a private session with lawmakers on Nov. 18. Sen. Chris Coons (D., Del.) vowed to look into the issue.

Nvidia, which has a market capitalization of $4.5 trillion, is scheduled to release its results for the third quarter on Nov. 17.
