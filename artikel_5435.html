Trump demands probe into GDP revision, falsely calling it a 'record setting error'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Bureau of Economic Analysis raised third-quarter growth to 4.3% from 3.4%, a 0.9 point revision that was not a record

President Donald Trump on Thursday demanded a probe of the revised third-quarter GDP numbers issued by the federal government, labeling the change a "RECORD SETTING ERROR" in a post on Truth Social.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis on Wednesday raised its estimate of third-quarter real GDP growth to an annual rate of 4.3% from 3.4% in its advance estimate. The agency said the 0.9 percentage point revision mainly reflected upward revisions to investment and consumer spending. It said the investment revision was led by software, based on revised producer price data.

Trump linked the earlier estimate to the Nov. 3 midterm elections, in which Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress. "Why wasn't the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation," he wrote. He also called the revision a "massive, and purposeful, error" and said the corrected figure came "three weeks late."

The BEA put out the updated numbers on a timetable it had set in advance, according to Axios. The agency issues a quarterly GDP reading three times across a three-month span, and the next release is scheduled for Dec. 23.

The revision was the biggest such change since 2020, when the April-to-June quarter's growth figure was adjusted by 1.2 percentage points, according to CNBC, so it was not a record. The 0.9 percentage point change tied for 16th largest among advance-to-second-estimate revisions since 1990 and was about double the 0.43 percentage point average, CNBC said.

White House spokesman Matthew Middleton told CNBC the higher figure is evidence that Trump's policies are "delivering results." The Commerce Department, which oversees the BEA, and the BEA did not immediately respond to requests for comment, according to Axios.

Trump fired the Bureau of Labor Statistics chief in August 2025 following a weak jobs report. Trump called that report "phony" and "rigged."
