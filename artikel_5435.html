Booking Holdings’ travel startup Vayo starts booking trips with AI agents, even on rival Expedia

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Chronicling the Seattle and Pacific Northwest startup scene. SEE MORE

by Todd Bishop on Nov 17, 2026 at 6:20 amNovember 17, 2026 at 6:21 am

Vayo, the Seattle-based AI travel startup launched by Booking Holdings last month, is expanding beyond its initial focus on tracking travel plans — starting to use AI agents to book flights, hotels, rental cars and activities on behalf of users in its invite-only beta.

That includes bookings on Expedia, the flagship site of Booking Holdings’ biggest rival, Expedia Group. Vayo confirmed that its AI agents book there by navigating Expedia’s website, without a direct integration or partnership for those bookings.

The company says Vayo recommends the best option for the traveler, wherever that option is booked, not just through its parent company’s brands.

Vayo is led by Mike Fridgen, a longtime Seattle entrepreneur with deep roots in travel. Booking Holdings, the parent of Booking.com, Priceline, Kayak and OpenTable, said last month that it plans to invest $100 million in Vayo, depending on its progress and market conditions.

Companies are still working out how to handle AI agents that use their sites on behalf of customers. In September, Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse agent from shopping on Amazon.com, saying the agent didn’t identify itself.

When Vayo launched last month, Fridgen said it was relying on APIs and direct partnerships with travel companies, in part to avoid the risk of being blocked. Vayo says no site has blocked its bookings so far.

How the bookings work: Vayo’s new booking features work in two ways. It can book hotels, rental cars and activities through a direct connection with sister company Booking.com, and it has similar links to OpenTable for restaurants and SeatGeek for event tickets.

For flights and other bookings, its AI agents navigate the booking websites, signing in to the traveler’s existing accounts where needed and completing the purchase. Every booking requires the traveler’s approval, the company says.

If a site doesn’t allow AI agents, Vayo leaves that booking to the traveler.

Vayo uses 1Password to sign in to travelers’ accounts and, on sites other than Booking.com, Stripe’s Link agent wallet to pay, without seeing their passwords or card details, the company says. It reports a reservation as confirmed only after the supplier confirms it.

Payments and pricing: Travelers pay whichever company they book with, and Vayo is not the merchant of record. The company declined to share details on commissions, saying its partner arrangements are still taking shape and won’t factor into its recommendations.

When Booking.com has the best option, Vayo users get Genius Level 3 pricing, Booking.com’s top loyalty tier, with discounts of up to 20% on select stays.

The service is free to use during the invite-only beta. Responding to GeekWire’s questions, Vayo didn’t say how many people are using it or how many have completed a booking.

Who’s behind Vayo: Fridgen, the Vayo co-founder and CEO, was on the founding team of Expedia’s vacation-package business, led Alaska Airlines’ digital business and was founding head of product at airfare-prediction startup Farecast, before leading Decide.com and Madrona Venture Labs.

His co-founder and CTO is Allen Chen, the former CTO of Fanatics Collectibles, who previously co-founded Seattle software startups CloudPress and DataBlade. Ian Fliflet, OfferUp’s former chief growth officer, is head of growth. Vayo had about 20 employees, all working in downtown Seattle, when it launched last month.

Next on its product roadmap, the company says it plans to work inside the messaging apps people already use, and to let groups of travelers plan trips together.
