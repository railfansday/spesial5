Manchester United Managers' Worst First Games (Ranked)

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The debut of a new Manchester United manager is usually back page news, and it ensures that all eyes will be on whoever is in the dugout when it comes to their first outing.

With that comes major scrutiny and intense pressure, as every result is put under the microscope. For Mauricio Pochettino, the latest United gaffer, an intense 4-0 capitulation at Anfield against bitter long-term rivals Liverpool has meant he has matched the heaviest loss any United manager has suffered in his first game.

A poor start, yes, but he's certainly not alone in the annals of history when it comes to poor United manager debuts: here's the worst five first games for new managers.

Sir Alex Ferguson arrived from Aberdeen after Ron Atkinson's sacking to find a side in the bottom four of the First Division. His first afternoon at the cramped Manor Ground offered no new-manager bounce. John Aldridge's penalty on the stroke of half-time and a late Neil Slatter strike sealed a 2-0 defeat, and United had to wait until a 1-0 win over QPR on 22 November for their first victory under the Scot.

The rest is history. United recovered to finish 11th, and Ferguson stayed for 26 years as he created one of the greatest managerial dynasties in the sport's history. Winning 13 league titles and two Champions Leagues among a 38-trophy haul. And yet, even one of the greats of the game faced sacking claims.

Scott Duncan's opening day in the Old Trafford dugout ended with Joe Mawson scoring twice as Stoke won 2-0 in the Second Division.

It foreshadowed a turbulent five years. Despite heavy spending, United came within a whisker of the third tier in 1933/34, surviving only by beating Millwall 2-0 on the final day to send their opponents down instead.

Duncan led United to the Second Division title in 1935/36, but they were relegated straight back down a year later, and he departed for Ipswich Town in November 1937.

John Chapman, appointed from Airdrieonians after Jack Robson stepped down through ill health, watched his new side concede five at Old Trafford in his first game.

It set the tone for the campaign as United finished bottom of the First Division and were relegated — one of only four times since their inception in 1878.

Chapman did rebuild, guiding United back to the top flight in 1925, but his reign ended abruptly in October 1926 when the FA suspended him for "improper conduct" – a charge that was never publicly explained.

Chapman's suspension left United without a manager two days before a trip to Burnden Park, and the club handed temporary control to wing-half Lal Hilditch.

The result was a 4-0 thrashing, which stood for a century as the heaviest defeat suffered by a United manager in his first game.

Hilditch, the first player-manager in the club's history, was reluctant to pick himself, and the side suffered. He won only nine of his 30 games before Herbert Bamlett took over in April 1927.

Pochettino became the first United boss to begin his reign against Liverpool, and the afternoon unravelled almost immediately.

Leny Yoro was sent off in the eighth minute for a last-man foul on Alexander Isak, the earliest red card shown to a United player in Premier League history. Dominik Szoboszlai scored from the resulting free-kick, and Florian Wirtz doubled the lead within three minutes.

Rio Ngumoha, who became the youngest scorer in the fixture's history, and substitute Víctor Muñoz completed the rout after the break.

Pochettino inherited a side that had won once in 10 league games under Michael Carrick, who had guided them to third the season before. The defeat left United 19th in the Premier League table with eight points from 11 games, their lowest tally at that stage of a season since 1972/73.

The post-Ferguson era has produced false dawns in both directions. Many have tried and failed to bring sustained success to the club in what has been a faltering 13 years since Ferguson departed.

Louis van Gaal lost 2-1 at home to Swansea City in 2014, United's first opening-day home defeat since 1972. Erik ten Hag went one worse, losing 2-1 at home to Brighton before a 4-0 hammering at Brentford made him the first United manager in more than a century to lose his first two games. He still went on to win the League Cup and the FA Cup.

Former Man United defender Gary Neville hasn't held back.

At the other end of the scale, Ole Gunnar Solskjaer's 5-1 win at Cardiff City and David Moyes's 4-1 victory at Swansea in his first league game both preceded reigns that ended in the sack. Ruben Amorim, meanwhile, opened with a 1-1 draw at Ipswich Town and endured a torrid time.

If United's history proves anything, it is that a first result says little about what follows. Ferguson lost his opening game and built a dynasty. Pochettino now needs a recovery of his own to ensure United drag themselves out of a relegation battle, and start to build a team in his image.
