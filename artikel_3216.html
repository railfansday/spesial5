Horror Box Office, Driven 50% By Blumhouse Atomic Movies, Hits Near $2.5 Billion YTD

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The horror box office around the globe has hit $2.49 billion, +8% from last year’s $2.3 billion.

Blumhouse Atomic accounted for literally half of that figure off hits including but not limited to Focus Features’ highest grossing movie ever Obsession ($524.4M WW), A24’s highest grossing movie ever, Backrooms ($400M WW),Sony’s Insidious: Out of the Further ($172M WW), Universal’s The Other Mommy ($120M WW) and New Line’s Lee Cronin’s The Mummy ($90.5M WW).

Since Blumhouse and James Wan’s Atomic Monster merged, the combined production studio has seen $1 billion annually at the box office since 2024. In regards to admissions, horror was the only genre to see an uptick over 2025 of +7% where as romantic comedies were down -78%, the biggest drop for a feature genre. Another notable, 50% of Gen Zers prefer to watch movies in theaters. Watch on Deadline

The stats were unveiled today at Blumhouse Atmoic’s The Business of Fear 2026 symposium which had the attendance of Blumhouse Atomic partners Jason Blum and James Wan, Blumhouse President Abhijay Prakash, and NRG’s EVP and Head of Film, Ray Subers.

Prakash told attendees that when it comes to a studio and its return on investment (ROI), Blumhouse Atomic has the best: Backrooms had an ROI of 40x in regards to box office and its production cost ($10M) while Obsession had a ROI of 670x ($750K cost).

“I think the reason why horror has had a massive explosion in recent years is that it does not have the same type of stigma with younger generations as it did back in the days,” pointed out Saw architect Wan.

“That stigma is gone. Horror has crossed over from being the black sheep of the household and has crossed over into the mainstream in a big way,” he added.

“The mainstream audience is accepting horror movies of different types,” said Blum, “Indie horror —Obsession, Backrooms— are performing better than studio horror. I think those movies ten years ago would have done 90% less, they would have made $25 million at the box office.”

“After Covid, audiences ran back to sporting events and ran back to concerts, but kids had been fed movies so much at home, that it took longer to discover and remember the movies,” said Blum, “If there’s any genre that’s close to a sporting or a concert event, horror is more than anything else because it’s so participatory.”

Horror will continue to slay into 2027 has Blumhouse Atomic has such pic on the schedule including but not limited to New Line’s Conjuring universe, The Revenge of La Llorona on Feb. 26, The Exorcist reboot on March 12, Lionsgate’s Blair Witch revival on Sept. 24, Sleepover on Oct. 8 starring Michelle Monaghan and Justin Theroux, Paranormal Activity on Oct. 22 as well as Curry Barker’s next big screen feature via Focus Features, Anything But Ghosts on May 7.
