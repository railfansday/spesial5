SWIFT’s own framing confirms a multitude of XRP-tied banks are inside the core design of this pilot.

A popular software engineer has voiced his opinion on Ripple’s XRP Ledger’s ties to SWIFT and the possibility of integration for cross-border remittances. XRP’s chain is designed to primarily address cross-currency conversion and liquidity challenges via XRP as a bridge asset, rather than basic payments.

That’s exactly what SWIFT is looking for. The ability to process transactions in a matter of seconds instead of several business days serves a crystal-clear advantage over traditional financial messaging systems. The fast on-ledger cross-currency payments in 3-5 seconds using its built-in DEX and AMM for conversions.

XRP abd the ledger solve cross currency y conversion problems, not payment problems. SWIFT will eventually adopt XRP currency settlement in its backend, using XRP as a settlement intermediary. https://t.co/RKHWWDubeu

Another crucial factor is Ripple’s On-Demand Liquidity (ODL), constantly providing on-demand liquidity without pre-funded nostro accounts. That kind of duality enables XRP’s use both as a bridge crypto currency and an infrastructure layer for immediate payment settlements. SWIFT started their own 24/7 ledger in November, testing it out on multiple Ripple-affiliated banks.

HSBC, Standard Chartered and BNP Paribas and DBS Bank have all participated in on-chain transactions via Ripple’s tech stack one way or the other. These Ripple connections relate to custody infrastructure & tokenized assets. The SWIFT pilot itself uses bank-issued tokenized deposits orchestrated on SWIFT’s permissioned ledger not via XRP.
