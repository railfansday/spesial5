What Are Solana Privacy Rings? Inside Helius’ Privacy Protocol for Solana

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Every transaction on Solana is public by default. Anyone can look up a wallet address and see its balance, what it has sent and received, and when. That transparency is useful for verifying the network, but a problem for anyone who doesn't want their finances on display: a business paying salaries, a trader building a position, or an individual who simply doesn't want strangers seeing their savings.

Rings are programmable, onchain environments for encrypting money, markets, and finance - without ever leaving Solana

Now live on mainnet. pic.twitter.com/ezCIUCjcbI

Rings support SOL and other tokens on Solana, including SPL and Token-2022 tokens. Users keep custody of their funds throughout, since every transaction needs the owner's signature.

Crucially, the private balances live on the same network as everything else. Private transactions execute directly on Solana and encrypted data is stored on Solana's ledger, with no separate layer-2 network or sequencer in between. That means private balances sit alongside the same stablecoins, tokenized assets and DeFi liquidity as public ones.

Each private transfer comes with a zero-knowledge proof, a piece of cryptography that lets the network confirm the transfer is valid, for example that the sender actually had the funds, without revealing the numbers involved. Rings use a type of zero-knowledge proof called a Groth16 zk-SNARK, which Helius says can be generated in tens of milliseconds, either by a service provider or on the user's own device. Each private transfer settles in a single Solana transaction.

Users read their own encrypted balances with a viewing key. They can decrypt their balance locally, or let a service provider decrypt it and serve it to them, and they can share a key for a single transaction with an auditor or counterparty without exposing the rest of their history.

Moving funds into a Ring from an ordinary public wallet, or back out again, is visible onchain, including the asset and amount. But users who on-ramp from fiat currency straight into a private balance, through a supported provider, keep the asset and amount hidden from the start, according to Helius.

Because Custom Rings interoperate with the default Ring, regulated firms can keep their activity confidential from the market while still sharing the same liquidity as everyone else.

At launch, Decrypt, mining and staking app ORE and cross-chain service Aurora Intents were using the default Ring, while Backpack, DFlow, Jupiter, Squads and Titan were among the teams working on integrations.

Helius says the core programs and zero-knowledge circuits have been audited by security firms Certora, OtterSec and ZK Security. Engineers from the Solana Foundation, Blueshift, Zcash and other teams took part in the "trusted setup," the one-off ceremony that generates the parameters the proofs rely on.

The code is open source, published on GitHub under the Apache 2.0 license, so anyone can inspect how it works.

Formal verification, a mathematical check that the circuits behave exactly as intended, is still underway and expected to finish by the end of 2026. Solana's own documentation currently describes Rings as being in beta.
