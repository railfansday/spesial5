Two billion credentials publicly exposed, including everything needed for identity theft

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A security researcher has discovered a publicly exposed database containing approximately two billion credentials. The database was neither password-protected nor encrypted.

The scariest part of it is around 400,000 documents came from separate folders for individual victims, creating a detailed profile with all the personal data needed to “own that person” …

Cybersecurity researcher Jeremiah Fowler says that the database contains a huge array of information, including login details for Tor .onion addresses used to access the dark web.

I also observed thousands of Tor .onion addresses associated with account names and passwords. The Tor .onion services are generally categorized as part of the dark web, and these credentials could potentially provide access to private forums, marketplaces, communication platforms, or other hidden services. While .onion accounts alone do not imply criminal or malicious activity, it is estimated that as much as 60% of dark web sites host illegal material and illicit activities. This is the first time I have seen a large number of dark web accounts in breached credentials. It should be noted that legitimate organizations, journalists, whistleblower platforms, and news organizations also operate .onion services to provide anonymity and bypass censorship.

But the most frightening aspect of it is separate folders for individual victims containing all of the information needed for identity theft.

Fowler told Wired that this data appears to have been obtained through advanced malware.

The “scariest” thing in the database, Fowler says, was around 400,000 documents that appeared to come from “more advanced malware” compiling information about individual victims […] that create a detailed profile of an individual and their digital life. “If you had that, you own that person,” Fowler says.

There’s no information about the specific sources of the data, but it seems likely to be aggregated from a range of data breaches. Fowler contacted the hosting company to have the database removed, but it’s not known how many people may have had access to it.

The best protection is to use a password manager like Apple’s Passwords app to ensure you have strong, unique passwords for each individual website and service you use, with two-factor authentication enabled wherever possible. When given the choice of 2FA code delivery, you should always opt for an authenticator app rather than a text message to protect yourself against SIM-swap attacks.
