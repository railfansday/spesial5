Singapore Airlines Unveils New First & Business Class Cabins For A350, 777-9

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

The wait is finally over — Singapore Airlines has today unveiled its all-new cabins, which were first teased two years ago. Most significantly, this includes a brand new business class, plus the expansion of first class to more planes. This really represents the carrier’s next generation product offering, since these cabins will be installed on future Boeing 777-9s, as well as retrofitted on dozens of Airbus A350s.

Let’s first talk briefly about which planes will be getting this product, and then we’ll cover the details of the cabins. Keep in mind these changes will also include an updated soft product, including a new dining concept as well as new amenities across cabins.

Singapore Airlines’ new cabin products will be coming to all of the carrier’s upcoming Boeing 777-9s, as well as to select Airbus A350-900s. This includes a new first class, business class, premium economy, and economy, along with updated inflight entertainment and the installation of Starlink Wi-Fi.

Singapore Airlines has 31 Boeing 777-9s on order, which are expected to eventually be the carrier’s flagship aircraft. These will come delivered with the new cabins. As a matter of fact, Singapore Airlines had initially planned to debut the new cabins on these 777-9s all the way back in 2020. However, delivery of those planes has now been pushed back to late 2027 at the earliest.

More immediately, Singapore Airlines also plans to invest 1.1 billion SGD (~835 million USD) to update the interiors of its entire long haul Airbus A350-900 fleet. This consists of a total of 41 jets, and includes the carrier’s seven A350-900ULRs, with the “ULR” standing for “ultra long range,” as these planes operate the world’s longest flights.

The only A350-900s that won’t be reconfigured are the ones that are specifically designed for regional flights, featuring the same cabin products you’ll find onboard Boeing 787-10s.

Below is a chart with the basics of Singapore Airlines’ new first class product.

Singapore Airlines’ new first class seat is designed by PriestmanGoode, and manufactured by Collins Aerospace, and it’ll be available exclusively on Boeing 777-9s and Airbus A350-900ULRS (so not on standard A350-900s, and it won’t be retrofitted on existing aircraft).

Entertainment will be available on a 32″ 4K monitor. As you’d expect, tech will be cutting edge beyond that, with bluetooth audio, wireless, AC, and USB-C charging, etc. There will also be a touchscreen handset that provides easy access to KrisWorld, plus a panel with seat controls and flight information.

One of the nice things is that the center suites have a retractable divider, potentially creating a large, expansive space, so that guests in the center seats can dine and relax together. Singapore Airlines claims that when combined, the double bed becomes the biggest bed in commercial aviation, as it measures 80″ long and 60″ wide.

There won’t be overhead bins in first class, so bags can be stored in a cabin baggage compartment underneath the seat. There will also be a personal wardrobe, and a leather-lined vanity drawer.

Singapore Airlines’ new business class seat is designed by BMW Designworks, and manufactured by RECARO, and it’ll be available exclusively on Boeing 777-9s and Airbus A350-900s (both long haul and ultra long haul).

Entertainment will be available on a 20-22″ 4K monitor. As you’d expect, tech will be cutting edge beyond that, with bluetooth audio, wireless, AC, and USB-C charging, etc. The airline also highlights how the seat has multiple storage options, which is appreciated.

Perhaps what’s most interesting is how in every other row, the center seats will be very close together, with the ability to form a double bed, which is a feature that customers always appreciate.

When can we expect Singapore Airlines’ new premium products to debut? The expectation is that we’ll see a long haul Airbus A350-900 reconfigured first, and the first of those planes should now enter service in the second quarter of 2027. We don’t yet have any details beyond that.

The expectation is that the first retrofitted A350-900ULR (the ultra long range plane, operating the world’s longest flight) will debut at some point later in 2027, though that’s still up in the air. Meanwhile the 777-9s should start to join the fleet in late 2027 or early 2028 — only time will tell, given never-ending delays with Boeing.

Singapore Airlines should finish retrofitting its 41 A350s with the new cabins by either 2030 or 2031, though that timeline also remains subject to change. The delays with these new products reflects industry-wide supply chain constraints, as well as delays in the certification of the new seats (a common industry problem). The aircraft will be retrofitted by SIA Engineering Company in Singapore.

Which routes are likely to get the new cabins first? While we’ll see how this plays out, based on historical precedent, it’s likely that Sydney (SYD) and London (LHR) will be the first two destinations, since those are the first destinations where the airline has sent all of its new long haul products.

Singapore Airlines has just unveiled all-new cabins, which includes a new first class and business class. These cabins will be retrofitted on select Airbus A350-900s, and installed on all factory fresh Boeing 777-9s. These new products are long overdue, and I can’t wait to see them in service.

In first class, Singapore Airlines opted for a product that feels on brand, with warm colors, and a functional design that even allows for a double suite in the center seats. Meanwhile in business class, the airline chose a staggered product that looks solid, but I just can’t get over the cabin finishes… maybe it’s just me.
