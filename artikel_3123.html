I used to save poached eggs for lazy weekends. This microwave method made them part of my weekday routine.

My weekday breakfasts used to come down to a protein smoothie or scrambled eggs, mostly because both are fast.

Poached eggs, my favorite version, stayed on the weekend list because the stovetop method takes more babysitting than I can manage before work. A microwave was the key to changing all that.

Now, with just a small bowl of cold water and about a minute of cooking, I get a poached egg with a firm white and a soft yolk, ready to slide onto toast or greens. And it’s the best prep method I’ve found thus far.

This method demands just one minute, one bowl and leaves no mess, save for the quick rinse of a wet mug. The results are surprisingly good and the process is impossible to flub. You can easily tinker with timing to get the exact consistency you’re after.

So ditch the frying pan or sluggish pot of boiling water. Here’s how to make perfect poached eggs in under 60 seconds.

Step 1: Fill a ramekin with a half-cup of salted, room-temperature water. It must be enough to submerge the egg.

You’ll want enough water to submerge the egg but not spill over the top of the ramekin.

Step 2: Crack one egg and carefully drop it into the water, trying not to disturb its structural integrity.

Step 3: Place the ramekin in the microwave and cook on high for about 45 seconds, longer if you prefer a firmer yolk.

Cook the submerged egg for 40 seconds for a runny yolk and closer to a minute for a firm one.

Step 4: Remove the egg gently from the water with a slotted spoon or wire skimmer.

Lift your egg carefully out of the water with a slotted spoon or mesh strainer.

Step 5: Serve over toast or a bed of greens with avocado, hot sauce, chili crisp and other accompaniments.

In one or two tries you’ll be able to fine-tune your poached egg to your perfect doneness and then nail it every time.

Note: The base temperature for any microwave will vary, and thus, cook times may also. Try 40 to 45 seconds for your first effort, then add five-second increments for subsequent tries until you achieve the desired doneness.
