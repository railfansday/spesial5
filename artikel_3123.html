Alphabet Could Be the Safest Way to Own Anthropic. Here’s the Math.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Alphabet owns a piece of Anthropic and also gets paid to supply its computing power, which is a nice way to be on both sides of the AI boom.

By Patrick Sanders – Nov 26, 2026 at 12:27PM EST

It didn't take long for some of the early enthusiasm surrounding Anthropic (ANT +1.08%) stock to wear off. Shares of the artificial intelligence (AI) company opened at $111.50 on their first day of trading on Nov. 19, but fell below their initial public offering (IPO) price of $83 on just the fourth day of trading.

I'm not surprised. Tech IPOs have a history of volatility, and it's particularly difficult to put a valuation on a company growing as quickly as Anthropic.

But there's another way to invest in the company that developed Claude. And the more I look at the numbers, the more I like the idea of buying Alphabet (GOOG -2.02%) (GOOGL -2.13%) stock instead.

Alphabet has a sizable ownership stake in Anthropic, and it's also well positioned to earn tens of billions of dollars by leasing computing capacity to the AI lab.

At the time of this writing, Anthropic has a market cap of about $1 trillion. That puts the value of Alphabet's 13.7% ownership stake in it at $137 billion. Every time Anthropic gains $100 billion in market cap, Alphabet's investment increases by $13.7 billion, assuming the tech stalwart doesn’t sell any of its shares.

If Anthropic grows into a $2 trillion company, Alphabet's stake would be worth $274 billion. And if Anthropic becomes a $3 trillion company, Alphabet would be sitting on an investment worth $411 billion.

Alphabet only becomes a stronger, wealthier company as Anthropic grows. That's a nice position to be in. And it doesn't even factor in that Anthropic also will be paying a lot to Google Cloud for its services.

Anthropic's S-1 filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission revealed that the company is committed to spending at least $121.3 billion for Google Cloud services between April 2026 and July 2033.

That works out to roughly $16.7 billion annually over the life of the agreement, although the actual payments won't necessarily be spread out evenly over those years.

What's particularly attractive about this agreement from Alphabet's perspective is that Anthropic is obligated to pay for the contracted computing capacity regardless of how much it actually uses. If Anthropic's needs fall short of its commitment, it generally still has to pay Google the difference, subject to certain contractual adjustments and exceptions.

And that's only part of the business relationship.

Anthropic also estimates that it will spend another $125 billion to $150 billion for additional computing capacity powered by Tensor Processing Units (TPUs). They are the custom AI chips that Alphabet designs in conjunction with Broadcom (AVGO -0.07%).

Anthropic can obtain that computing capacity directly from Google or through third-party providers. We don't know how much of that money will end up in Alphabet's coffers, and the payments depend on several factors, including how much capacity is made available.

But Alphabet has an opportunity to do considerably more business with Anthropic than the $121.3 billion commitment suggests.

There's an important distinction between investing in Anthropic and doing business with Anthropic.

The AI company generated $17 billion in revenue in the third quarter, but it has also committed to spending approximately $900.9 billion, nearly all of it related to computing and infrastructure.

Anthropic needs to generate enough revenue from Claude to justify those expenditures. Alphabet, meanwhile, is being paid to provide some of that infrastructure.

And most importantly, Alphabet isn't entirely dependent on Anthropic for its success. It has its own AI platform, Gemini, a fast-growing cloud computing business that serves customers beyond Anthropic, and a highly profitable advertising business built around Google Search.

I think Anthropic could be an outstanding company over the next decade, but I'm much less certain about what its stock will do in the next year or two.

That's why I like Alphabet stock here. Anthropic could be a disappointing investment for its shareholders yet remain an extremely valuable customer for Google Cloud. I don't need to get Anthropic's valuation right to make money by owning Alphabet stock.
