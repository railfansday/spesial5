10 Ways to Strengthen Your Overlooked Arm Muscles in Yoga

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Longtime yoga teacher Amber Burke, graduate of two YogaWorks teacher trainings and former leader of a teacher training program at the University of New Mexico-Taos, shares some of her favorite yoga arm strength exercises. You can also explore other ways to strengthen your arms in yoga.

Have you ever walked out of yoga class with noodle arms? That’s likely because a typical vinyasa class, with its repeated Planks, Downward Dogs, and arm balances, works your chest, fronts of your shoulders, and triceps—aka, the muscles responsible for “pushing” movements. So much so, it’s become a truism that yoga tends to overlook the “pull” muscles, including those of the upper back, backs of your shoulders, biceps, and forearms.

Yet there are plenty of yoga poses that could strengthen these muscles of the arms if you know how to adapt them. In poses that usually allow your arms to rest, you can strengthen your underutilized muscles by creating a little resistance.

The following poses engage muscles that your usual yoga class might overlook. Bonus: In many of these, your legs are working a little harder, too, as your lower body supplies resistance for the arms. No gym or weights required. Although you will need a strap or belt and, optionally, a block.

Always work within your own limits, starting with less intensity and fewer repetitions.

Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the mat. Hug your right knee toward your chest and keep your left leg bent or straighten it on the mat. Hold your right shin with your hands or wrap a strap around it in Half Knees-to-Chest Pose (Ardha Apanasana). Press your shin against your hands or strap and, at the same time, pull your leg or the strap toward you. Draw your shoulder blades toward each other. Spend several breaths here, then switch sides.

Lie on your back and draw both knees toward your chest, keeping your hips on the mat. Grab hold of the outer edges of your feet or calves or hold the backs of your thighs. Keeping your knees bent, reach the soles of your feet toward the sky and widen your legs as you draw your knees toward your underarms in Happy Baby (Ananda Balasana). Push your feet or the backs of your thighs against your hands and, at the same time, pull down against your feet or thighs with your hands for several breaths, then release.

Sit tall with your legs straight in front of you. Bend your right knee and lean forward to grab hold of the outer edge of your right foot with your right hand or a strap. Lean back as you lift your right leg so your shin is about parallel to the mat in Archer Pose (Akarna Dhanurasana). Think of drawing your right foot back toward your right shoulder (similar to Happy Baby). Press your foot against your hand or the strap and, at the same time, pull your foot toward your face. Stay here for a few breaths, then release and switch sides.

Sit tall with your legs straight in front of you. Bend your right leg and cross it over your left leg, stepping your right foot to the mat outside your left thigh. Bend your left leg and draw your left foot toward your right sitting bone as you lower your left knee toward the mat. Lean forward and grab your right foot with both hands or a strap. Lean back and lift your right leg in front of you, playing with how much you can comfortably straighten it while keeping your chest lifted and your back straight in a variation of Heron Pose (Krounchasana). Reach your heel away from you and, at the same time, pull your foot toward you with your hands or the strap. Draw your shoulder blades toward each other. Stay here for a few breaths, then release and switch sides.

Stand with your feet hip-distance apart or wider and fold forward into Standing Forward Bend, bending your knees as much as you need. Slide your hands, palms facing up, underneath your feet so your fingers are pointing toward your heels in Hand to Foot Pose (Pada Hastasana). Shift your weight into your feet and, at the same time, press your hands upward against your feet as if you were trying to lift them off the mat. Draw your shoulder blades toward each other. After several breaths here, step off your hands, and slowly roll up to standing.

Stand facing the short edge of the mat and grab a strap. Step your left foot back and turn your toes out slightly. Lift the ball of your right foot so your toes point toward the sky. Loop the strap around the ball of your right foot, then stand upright. Slide your hands up the strap until your hands are at your hips and the strap is taut. Press into the strap with the ball of your foot and, at the same time, use your arms to keep the strap back against your hips for several breaths in this Pyramid Pose (Parsvottanasana) variation. You can intensify the stretch in your front leg by folding your chest forward with a flat back, adjusting your hands so that they remain alongside your hips and your strap stays taut. Continue to pull the strap toward you for a few breaths, then return to standing upright. Release the strap and switch sides.

Stand facing the long edge of the mat and step your feet wide apart. Place your hands on your hips and lift your chest. Forward fold with a flat back, keeping your knees bent as much as you need, then hook your big toes with your index and middle fingers or place a strap underneath both feet in Wide-Legged Standing Forward Bend (Prasarita Padottanasana D). Press your feet down toward the mat and, at the same time, bend your elbows out to the sides as you pull the strap as if you were trying to lift it toward you. Draw your shoulder blades toward each other and away from your ears. After a few breaths here, release your grip, then slowly roll up to standing.

Stand facing the short edge of the mat. Find a steady point straight in front of you at which to gaze. Press your left foot into the mat as you bend your right knee and lift your foot behind you toward your right sitting bone. Grab hold of your inner right foot with your right hand or wrap a strap around your right ankle and hold both ends in your right hand. Press your right foot toward the wall behind you as you lean your chest forward into a slight backbend and reach your left arm forward in Dancer Pose (Natarajasana). Press your right foot toward the wall behind you and, at the same time, try to pull your foot forward with your right hand or strap. Stay here for several breaths, then switch sides.

Lie on your stomach with your legs straight, the tops of your feet against the mat, and your arms alongside you with your palms facing down. Lift your head and chest off the mat, keeping your gaze a few inches in front of you on the mat. Press your thighs against the mat as you bend your knees, draw your feet toward your sitting bones, and grasp your ankles with your hands or wrap a strap around them. Press your shins toward the wall behind you and, at the same time, try to pull your feet toward you with your hands or the strap as you lift yourself into Bow Pose (Dhanurasana). Stay here for several breaths, then release.

Come to your hands and knees. Draw your toes together and widen your knees. Sink your hips toward your heels as far as feels good, walk your hands as far out in front of you as you can, and lower your forehead to the mat or a block. Press your hands against the mat and imagine you’re sliding it toward you, as you lift your elbows off the mat and draw your shoulder blades toward each other. Stay in this variation of Child’s Pose (Balasana) for several breaths, then slowly release your arms.
