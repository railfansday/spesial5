Riyadh’s hotels lost their peak season when the airport shut. With BA, Cathay Pacific and others suspended into the new year and Houthi strikes still hitting the capital, the recovery hasn’t started.

Riyadh hotels had one of their worst months of the year in October. A temporary shutdown of the city’s main airport cut off inbound demand just as peak season started.

Occupancy fell 34.7 percentage points year over year to 34.3% from 69%, according to CoStar’s STR. Only March had lower occupancy, at 28.3%. Average daily rate dropped 29.9% to $190 and revenue per available room (RevPAR) fell 65.1% to $65.

“October and November are usually the peak season months for Riyadh, so the impact is much bigger,” said Kostas Nikolaidis, associate account director for Middle East and Africa at STR.

The October demand collapse traces back to an attack on King Khalid International Airport. The airport closed after an attack on Oct. 10 that Saudi authorities said killed 13 people and injured more than 300. Riyadh hotels recorded one of their worst months of 2026 in October after an Oct. 10 attack on King Khalid International Airport shut the city's main gateway at the start of peak season. Occupancy fell 34.7 percentage points to 34.3%, ADR dropped 29.9% to $190, and RevPAR fell 65.1% to $65, according to CoStar's STR. Cirium data show 86% of scheduled Riyadh departures were canceled between Oct. 8 and late October, and most foreign carriers remain suspended: Etihad and IndiGo through Nov. 30, British Airways to Jan. 11, 2027, Cathay Pacific through Jan. 31, 2027, with Delta pushing its Atlanta-Riyadh launch to March 2027. Saudi carriers have shifted flights to Al Qassim airport, a 4.5-hour drive away, and bus passengers into the capital. Attacks have continued, with a Houthi missile or debris striking the Public Investment Fund tower, the second landmark hit in Riyadh's financial district in two weeks, and the events calendar has already lost the Saudi Grand Prix, the Future Investment Initiative, and the Red Sea International Film Festival. Dubai held up better: occupancy was 68.4%, down 16.5 points, with RevPAR down 42% to $124 — still nearly double Riyadh's. Within Dubai, luxury held rates but lost the most occupancy, while upper midscale and midscale cut rates hardest to stay full.
