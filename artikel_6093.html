What Happens When the Destination Comes Second?

Tourism marketing has to account for travelers whose plans begin with an experience rather than a place.

The conventional view of leisure travel starts with a destination. Persuade someone to choose a place, and the flights, hotels, and activities follow. But a growing part of the travel business is being built around the reverse: win the customer through something they want to do, then help determine where they go.

That gives hotels, event companies, airlines, and tour operators a role in shaping demand that destination marketers cannot afford to overlook. The competition begins before the traveler has a place in mind.

I asked Brian Chesky about the accommodation version of this on stage at Skift Global Forum in September 2021. Travelers had historically picked a destination and then looked for somewhere to stay, I said. He explained that accommodation had become the first choice for many people, which was why Airbnb had introduced its “I’m flexible” search.

Five years later, the commercial implications extend well beyond Airbnb.

The traditional sequence of leisure travel — pick a destination, then book flights, hotels, and activities — is being inverted by companies that win the customer through the stay, the event, or the experience first, and help determine the destination afterward. Airbnb reorganized search around categories of homes in May 2022 so the accommodation itself could supply the reason for the trip, while Skyscanner's Search Everywhere and easyJet's Inspire Me reach travelers who know their budget but not their destination. Live tourism makes the pattern clearest: Live Nation research executive Alisha Strother said at Skift's Live Tourism Summit that seven in 10 surveyed fans planned trips around events in places they had never visited, and that research put 60% of average trip spending outside the ticket and venue. Booking.com's October 2025 StubHub partnership places travel offers in front of ticket buyers, and Trip.com reported concert-package bookings up 64% year over year in the first half of 2026. Expedia's July acquisition of Layla and Google's Flight Deals, introduced in August 2025, push the same contest into AI-assisted discovery, though Skift Research's AI discovery study found greater involvement at the inspiration stage than at booking. Destination marketers are spending heavily against this backdrop — Brand USA plans to spend $165 million in fiscal 2027, and the Las Vegas Convention and Visitors Authority approved a three-year, $309 million marketing agreement in May. For suppliers, the open question is whether being inside someone else's purchase path fills unsold inventory or simply adds commissions and discounts to bookings they could have won directly.
