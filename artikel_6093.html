Delta Pilot Reportedly Fired For Answering His Phone While Taxiing—Similar Stories Go Back At Least 5 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

A Delta Air Lines first officer allegedly answered his cellphone while taxiing out in Atlanta on his first trip at the airline. The captain returned to the gate, had him removed, and the new hire lost his job, according to an account shared by the Black Box Aviation Podcast and covered by Live and Let’s Fly. The first officer was reportedly flying trips under a check pilot’s supervision as part of their initial operating experience. The Black Box Aviation Podcast’s account, November 17, 2026 There’s no identified pilot, flight number, aircraft type or date for the alleged incident. The podcast’s companion article repeats the allegation but doesn’t fill in those details. A Similar Delta Story Was Being Told In 2021 In a July 3, 2021 discussion on Airline Pilot Central, a moderator responded to a comment about how hard it was to get fired at Delta: Like answering your cell phone during taxi. On OE. In a follow-up, he said he’d heard the story from a line check pilot. That version included the employee defending his need to stay connected during a subsequent meeting with management. By July 2025, another forum version had the Delta pilot answering because FedEx was calling about a job. Other posters mocked how often this same kind of story gets told about different airlines. And in an older Reddit discussion of pilot firings, one commenter said they’d heard a first officer using Tinder while taxiing during training story at all four airlines where they’d worked. The comments at Live and Let’s Fly reflect a split between calling it longstanding airline folklore versus supporting strict enforcement of the phone prohibition. I had much the same reaction to September’s claim that 120 pilots had been grounded for using inflight Wi-Fi. There’s a real rule against personal device use, but the particular claims weren’t well-supported. The First Officer Has Work To Do During Taxi Even when the captain has control of he aircraft, the first officer needs to monitor the aircraft’s position, clearances and other traffic. Taxiing through Atlanta isn’t downtime before the flight starts. The sterile cockpit rule covers taxi. Federal regulations prohibit distracting, nonessential activities during critical phases of flight. Those include taxi, takeoff and landing, as well as other operations below 10,000 feet except cruise. Personal phone use isn’t permissible at cruise Wireless devices and laptops are restricted at a flight crewmember’s duty station throughout flight time. (Approved electronic charts and manuals are different. It’s the device’s use, rather than who owns it, that controls. ) I’ve written that pilots should be allowed to be human, rather than expected to behave like robots throughout a long flight. That doesn’t mean taking a personal call while moving a passenger jet through an airport. Phone Distraction Has Caused Real Problems I covered a Jetstar phone-distraction incident involving a Darwin–Singapore flight where the crew hadn’t lowered the landing gear when a warning sounded at 720 feet. Their go-around began at 392 feet. The Australian investigation found that incoming message alerts sounded, the first officer saw the captain occupied with his phone, and the captain said he was unlocking it and turning it off. Jetstar added a reminder to switch phones off to its takeoff checklist and moved its landing checklist completion requirement from 500 feet to 1,000 feet, and incorporated the incident into pilot training. Similar stories were socialized among airline pilots much earlier than that. This doesn’t mean it isn’t happening – it’s just that it’s not new.
