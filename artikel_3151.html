Micron Stock Fell More Than 40% From Its June Record. History Says What It Did After Its 5 Other Drops This Deep.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Will the memory maker's newest drop follow its quick recoveries or its long slumps?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 21, 2026 at 5:17AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

Memory chipmaker Micron Technology (MU -13.89%) has lost a big part of its 2026 gain since early summer. On Nov. 5, the stock closed around $656, 46% below the $1,213.56 it ended at on June 25, its highest close ever. Yet at about $712 as of this writing, it's still up about 150% this year, after starting 2026 near $285.

I looked back through the stock's daily closing prices since 1984 and counted a drop at the first close at least 40% below its highest close until then.

By that standard, Micron has done this five times before -- in 1985, 1995, 2000, 2022, and 2024. The newest one started at the Nov. 5 close.

What came next varied a lot. And I think the difference had less to do with the stock than with memory chip prices.

Three of the five earlier drops kept going. A year (252 trading days) after the January 1985 drop began, the stock was another 63% lower, and it didn't close at a new record until July 1993. The November 1995 drop left shares 42% lower a year later, with no new record until February 2000.

The drop that started in September 2000 was the worst of all. Shares were 68% lower a year in and around 97% lower by the late-2008 low. They didn't reach a new closing high until January 2022, over 21 years later.

The two latest drops went the other way. A year after the June 2022 drop began, Micron stock was up 22%, and it hit a record in March 2024. The August 2024 drop was followed by a 24% rise in the next year and a record close in September 2025, around 13 months later.

But every one of the five got deeper before it turned. Even in the two fast recoveries, the shares lost another 11% (by September 2022) and another 28% (by April 2025) after first hitting the 40% mark.

Two of the long slumps lined up with memory chip price busts. From December 1995 to August 1996, the average selling price of Micron's 4-megabit DRAM chip fell 78% as industry supply outran demand.

The 2000 bust was arguably even worse. Micron's sales in fiscal 2001 (the year ended Aug. 30, 2001) shrank 38% as average selling prices for its memory chips dropped around 60%, and a $1.5 billion profit a year earlier became a $625 million loss.

The 2022 drop came with a bust, too. Two weeks in, Micron reported record quarterly revenue of $8.64 billion but forecast around $7.2 billion for the next quarter as industry demand softened. Revenue in fiscal 2023 then dropped 49% to $15.5 billion, and the company went from an $8.7 billion profit to a $5.8 billion net loss.

But that bust was short. Fiscal 2024 revenue climbed 62% to $25.1 billion, and shares were back at a record before that fiscal year ended.

The 2024 drop came with no bust at all. Micron's fiscal 2024 fourth-quarter revenue surged 93% year over year, and fiscal 2025 revenue rose another 49%, to $37.4 billion.

So, shares tended to recover within around two years when the memory slump that followed was short or never happened.

I'd say today's drop looks more like 2024 than 2000, at least so far. Micron's fiscal 2026 fourth quarter (which ended Sept. 3) was its sixth straight quarter of record revenue, at $54.2 billion, and management's forecast for the current quarter is around $61.5 billion.

And its prices keep rising. DRAM prices went up a high-teens percentage from the previous quarter in fiscal Q4. With industry demand growing, much of it driven by artificial intelligence, management expects memory supply to be "much tighter" in 2027 and 2028 than in 2026.

Of course, growth is cooling. The forecast calls for around $7 billion more revenue than fiscal Q4 pulled in, after a jump of almost $13 billion from fiscal Q3 to fiscal Q4. And Micron plans to boost its capital spending in fiscal 2027, with most of the increase going to build new factory space, which could in time loosen the tight supply driving today's prices.

Investors already look to be bracing for that. Micron's price-to-earnings ratio is around 10, based on the $74.33 in earnings per share it posted for fiscal 2026.

Has the stock bottomed out? If history is any guide, likely not. But the last five drops also show the year ahead can depend on memory prices, and Micron's own outlook suggests tighter supply in 2027. If I were adding shares today, I'd buy bit by bit.
