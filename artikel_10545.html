Elon Musk once again promises Tesla’s new autonomous driving is better than humans

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Elon Musk has a new promise: Version 15 of Full Self-Driving drives better than a real driver. “Completely new architecture that deserves being called ‘super’ intelligence, as it drives better than human intelligence,” he wrote on X after Tesla started sending the update to a first batch of owners.

That’s great news.

It was great news back in November 2023, too, when he wrote: “Over time, Version 12 (end-to-end neural nets) will far exceed human safety even when unsupervised.” Also in August 2025, when he said: “v14 will be better than human for sure, but I don’t know if it will be 10X. Maybe 2X to 3X. v15 has a shot at 10X.” Back then, he offered no data, and no third-party study to support his claim. It’s the same thing now.

Tesla says that the new FSD v15—which Musk expects to roll out widely in December—upgrades the neural network that processes the camera sensors into information for the car to drive. It has also swapped the old lo-fi grayscale schematic 3D view for an AI-generated live street scene that looks GTA VI-ish. The latter is the only thing that we know is real—and I have to admit it looks pretty neat—but nobody knows if the new driving engine works as well as Musk claims.

Same song, new version number

It may not matter too much. The new v15 is still Level 2 autonomous driving, which means drivers will need to watch the road at all times. While Musk says it drives better than a human, it must be a very dumb human because Tesla’s own support page says the software “does not make the vehicle autonomous” and is meant for use “only with a fully attentive driver.” That matters because when a person has to be ready to take over the wheel at any second, every avoided crash is just as much a win for the human as for the machine.

Tesla claims that the new FSD offers “25x better handling of safety critical cases.” Better than what? Phil Duan, who leads self-driving at Tesla, said on X that “v15 is 25x safer than v14 across the fleet.” Musk answered an X user, too: “Some people thought we meant 25% improvement; no we mean 25 times!” But we don’t really know about how much “safer” v14 was either. As electric mobility site Electrek points out, without real published figures or a peer-reviewed study—like this one on Waymo reducing its crashes with injured people by 85%—that “25x” claim is hard to substantiate.

In a post, the company explains that its new vision encoder—the part of the network that interprets the information from the car’s video cameras—is the reason for that “25x better handling.” Now shared by FSD, Robotaxis, and Optimus humanoids, the engineering team says the new software improves “the critical intervention rate by 25x.” Tesla says the new encoder was trained on “millions of internet videos.”

According to Tesla, v15 adjusts its speed better in bad weather and school zones, steers clear of deep standing water, and is quicker to spot signs, potholes, small animals, chains, and junk on the road. It doesn’t say anything about fake Looney Tunes-painted tunnels.

It can ease off for speed cameras, too. And it learns from you, so if you cancel the slowdown at the same speed camera twice, by accelerating, it will stop braking at that point of the road. The company also claims that the car’s interior camera—which checks if you are paying attention to the road or not—now works more often when you wear hats and sunglasses.

Neat!

As part of the update, Tesla Reality View has changed. It now looks very similar to the live situational awareness screens you get in some Chinese cars: a hyperrealistic view of the road and your car as seen from behind at a 45-degree angle. You can see vehicles and people in 3D hyperrealistic renderings. Even the buildings are textured and lit like the real thing. It feels like a high-end car racing game. Musk claims “it’s like GTA.”

It looks neat, but it doesn’t seem to convey information better than the older user interface (UI), which showed a much clearer representation of reality. Seen from a high angle, it was a simplified, very stylized version of the road populated with semitransparent versions of vehicles and nearby people. In the old version, despite its perception problems, it was easy to get instant situational awareness. In the new v15, the realism obscures the information with too much darkness and glitz.

The interface also features a blue ribbon that traces the route FSD intends to drive, so you can correct it. Chevron arrows float overhead before every turn. And the map now shows several parking spots at your destination. Some Chinese car manufacturers are already displaying this information directly on the windshield, projecting computer-generated wide-screen augmented-reality (AR) graphics over the real world.

Fortunately, you can switch to a split view or just pick the GPS map view. Musk claims “you can tap any point on the reality revisualizer to make your car go there.” 

Musk says that the new UI uses the same visual features the FSD v15 driving model uses: It’s all AI-generated. The AI model renders short bursts of eight frames at 36 frames per second, using just half of one of the accelerators in the car’s AI4 computer.

Still, the video may lag behind reality because the AI is not fast enough. Musk admits the lag is “a bit high due to compute constraints.” He claims Tesla has a way to trim it by about 35%. More promises.

If December’s wide release arrives on time (which may or may not happen), it will put the new FSD in a lot more hands. In 2027 we will be able to test the real value of Musk’s promises once again. Let’s stay positive. Maybe this will be it. You know what they say: 15th time’s the charm.
