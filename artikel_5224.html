The FAA lifted its nationwide ground stop at 8:15 p.m. ET after a communications failure.

Reuters reported 6,600 cancellations and 8,600 delays, with further cancellations expected Sunday.

Airline shares fell as investors weighed the disruption and a proposed $50 billion system overhaul.

The FAA nationwide ground stop lasted more than four hours. The fallout, however, could keep U.S. airlines dealing with disrupted schedules through the weekend.

The Federal Aviation Administration reopened U.S. airspace at 8:15 p.m. ET on November 13 after a communications failure disrupted air traffic control centers. But restarting departures didn’t put thousands of displaced aircraft and crews back where they belonged.

A credible source reported earlier that more than 6,600 cancellations and 8,600 delays, with another 1,500 flights already canceled for Sunday, citing FlightRadar24. That’s a costly mess for carriers heading into a busy weekend.

The NYSE Arca Airline Index closed lower on Friday as airline shares dropped late in the regular session.

American Airlines, Delta and United also fell between 1% and 2% in after-hours trading. JetBlue lost about 3%, adding pressure as investors assessed the disruption.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said overloaded routers and switches produced inconsistent data across air traffic control centers. The FAA halted departures as a safety precaution.

Duffy called for $50 billion to modernize the aging air traffic control system. Earlier that day, the White House and Transportation Department had separately urged Congress to approve $30.1 billion for aviation infrastructure upgrades.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said investigators had found no evidence of a cyberattack so far.

The ground stop is over, but the recovery isn’t automatic. Aircraft remain out of position, crews face duty-time limits, and passengers must be rebooked.

Airlines could spend days rebuilding schedules. The FAA nationwide ground stop has exposed how one failure in shared infrastructure can disrupt the entire industry.
