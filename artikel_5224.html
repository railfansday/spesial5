Bitcoin staking allows holders to earn rewards on their BTC without selling the underlying asset. In 2026, Stacks, Babylon, Core, Starknet and Stacking DAO offer different approaches, with varying rewards, custody models and staking requirements.

Stacks targets approximately 3% annualized rewards in BTC, while Babylon, Core and Starknet distribute rewards in their native tokens. Stacking DAO offers liquid staking with BTC-linked returns.

Choosing a platform requires looking beyond advertised yields to understand token requirements, custody arrangements and withdrawal conditions.

Rates as of November 10, 2026. Stacks quotes a target annual yield, Babylon quotes APR, and other displayed rates may use different calculation conventions.

The main differences concern reward sources, custody arrangements, additional capital requirements and withdrawal flexibility.

Bitcoin staking rewards vary by source, denomination and additional capital requirements.

Stacks is a Bitcoin layer where BTC holders earn BTC.

Stacks miners commit BTC through Proof of Transfer (PoX) to compete for the right to produce Stacks blocks and earn STX rewards. The committed BTC is distributed to staking participants, providing the source of their BTC rewards. PoX has distributed over 4,200 BTC since January 2021.

Direct Bitcoin staking targets approximately 3% annualized yield, with weekly BTC rewards. Since September 2026, three staking bonds have sold out, bringing total BTC staked to 1,740 BTC.

Babylon distributes BABY rewards funded by newly issued tokens through the protocol’s inflation mechanism. Its displayed APR ranges from 0.04% to 0.52%, with the higher rate requiring participants to co-stake BABY alongside BTC.

Returns depend on reward distribution and BABY’s value relative to Bitcoin.

Core distributes CORE rewards to participants who delegate BTC to validators securing its blockchain. Rewards come from newly issued CORE block rewards and transaction fees.

Its displayed top-tier yield is 0.79%, with higher reward tiers requiring participants to stake CORE alongside BTC.

Starknet allows holders of supported tokenized Bitcoin assets to delegate them to validators securing the network.

Participants earn newly minted STRK tokens, with a displayed yield of 2.91%. Actual returns depend on network participation and STRK’s value relative to BTC.

Stacking DAO provides liquid Bitcoin staking through stBTC, a token representing an sBTC-based staking position. BTC rewards generated through Bitcoin staking on Stacks accumulate in the position, increasing the amount of sBTC backing each stBTC over time.

Its displayed yield is 2.31%, with rewards accruing automatically rather than requiring separate claims.

A 4% annual reward paid in a token that loses 50% of its value against BTC would be worth approximately 2% of the original BTC position, assuming other factors remain unchanged.

BTC rewards avoid this specific reward-token conversion risk, although staking still involves other costs and risks.

Stacks, Babylon and Core support native Bitcoin staking on Bitcoin L1, while Starknet and Stacking DAO use Bitcoin-backed assets on other layers.

In direct self-custodial staking, BTC remains timelocked on Bitcoin L1 under the participant’s custody. Institutions can also access this route through qualified custody infrastructure, including BitGo and Anchorage Digital.

BTC is locked on Bitcoin L1 and delegated to finality providers supporting the Babylon ecosystem. Bitcoin remains native, but the staking position is subject to protocol slashing conditions.

BTC holders timelock their Bitcoin on L1 and delegate staking weight to Core validators without transferring BTC to another chain.

Participants stake supported tokenized Bitcoin assets on Starknet, introducing dependencies on the underlying token’s backing and smart contracts.

Participants can deposit sBTC, a Bitcoin-backed asset on Stacks, or BTC, which is converted into sBTC. In return, they receive stBTC, representing their staking position and accruing BTC rewards. Part of Stacks’ Bitcoin staking capacity is allocated to this pooled staking route. Unlike direct staking on Stacks, this route is not self-custodial.

Native Bitcoin custody avoids certain bridge and wrapper dependencies but does not remove staking-specific risks.

Some protocols require additional token commitments, while others use them to increase rewards.

Stacks uses STX as staking capacity. Direct participants commit STX worth approximately 5% of their BTC position, locked for the staking term.

Babylon offers increased rewards through BABY co-staking, while Core uses CORE dual staking to determine reward tiers.

Starknet does not require participants to pair STRK with their BTC for standard delegation. Stacking DAO handles the underlying STX commitment without requiring participants to provide it separately.

These requirements affect capital efficiency because additional tokens introduce further price exposure.

Withdrawal conditions vary across staking models.

Stacks uses approximately six-month bonding periods for direct staking. Participants can exit early and recover BTC, but forfeit remaining rewards, while paired STX stays locked until the original term ends.

Babylon supports unbonding, subject to protocol-defined waiting periods and transaction requirements.

Core allows participants to choose how long to timelock their BTC on Bitcoin L1, with a minimum staking period of 24 hours. Once the term expires, BTC can be redeemed through a Bitcoin transaction.

Starknet allows participants to unstake tokenized BTC, subject to a seven-day withdrawal period during which rewards stop accruing. Liquid staking providers may offer alternative exit routes.

Stacking DAO supports instant stBTC-to-sBTC withdrawals when sufficient liquidity is available, subject to fees. Alternatively, participants can use a standard withdrawal with an approximately four-week cooldown. Converting sBTC into native BTC requires a separate withdrawal process.

Liquid staking provides flexibility, but instant redemption depends on available liquidity and protocol conditions.

Bitcoin staking risks depend on custody, reward assets and protocol conditions.

Token exposure: Stacks requires STX for direct staking, while Babylon and Core offer higher rewards through BABY and CORE commitments. Token price changes affect overall returns.

Slashing: Babylon can penalize staked BTC when delegated finality providers violate specified security conditions. Stacks’ self-custodial path does not apply BTC slashing.

Smart contracts: Starknet and Stacking DAO rely on tokenized Bitcoin infrastructure, introducing smart contract and asset-backing risks.

Liquidity and lockups: Native staking can restrict withdrawals, while liquid staking positions depend on redemption mechanisms and market liquidity.

Quoted yields should therefore be evaluated alongside custody arrangements, token requirements and withdrawal conditions.
