8 Game Boy JRPGs Still Trapped on Original Hardware

The Game Boy changed lives. Some of you reading this might feel like handheld gaming has always been there, but for those of us who grew up with a Game Boy or a Game Boy Color, it was truly something special. I remember sitting on the top of a toy box shelf in my closet, playing Pokémon Gold and Silver Late at night when I was meant to be at school that morning. I’d also rush to finish Pokémon battles because we had just pulled up to school that aforementioned morning and I’d have to get out of the car. But I can’t save right now, Mom, or even pause!

The Game Boy was a surprisingly complex console. There are many features and accessories that players may not know about.

With that said, you now know my history with the JRPG genre started out humble, and it’s only grown over the years. Through my line of work, I love to dig up old retro games that I overlooked when I was younger and try them out. With JRPGs, the catalogue is as vast as its age in the industry, which means it had its place on the Game Boy even if you might not recall it. A lot of prominent Game Boy JRPGs made it off the console, but today, I want to look at some games that were left behind.

As much as we look fondly on older console life cycles and libraries, none of them are completely free of strife. There are some interesting developmental stories and struggles from back then, but something that baffles me is that Lufia: The Legend Returns was originally meant to be a PlayStation game. It survived a studio closure to return to the rights of the original developers, and that team quickly shifted gears and made it for the Game Boy instead. Wild stuff!

If you’re thinking that shift probably led to some awkward decisions, you’d be right! Lufia: The Legend Returns didn't get the best reception when it came out. It’s a bit confusing for Lufia regulars because puzzles and typical dungeon crawls are replaced with randomized maps that leave a lot to luck above everything else, but the story is well worth picking the game up for.

The Lufia series is one of the more obscure classic JRPG franchises out there, so if you haven’t played it, it’s worth checking out. Especially since, for all of its flaws, Lufia: The Legend Returns has a unique grid-based battle system and plenty of classic JRPG elements like airships you’ve probably been missing, before Final Fantasy Resonance proved how that kind of stuff just can’t be beat. Like many of the games on this list, Lufia: The Legend Returns can only be enjoyed on original software these days unless you get lucky. It was released on the 3DS Virtual Console a few years back, but unfortunately we all know the fate of the Nintendo 3DS eShop.

Back when the Game Boy was around, a lot of RPGs and adventure games were still trying to find the dividing line between them. One of the games for this little handheld that made that line difficult was Bomberman Quest. The story goes that Bomberman is attacked while flying through space, and when he crashes down on an unknown planet, he finds every single engine of his ship missing. Getting it back requires playing a game that feels like the Zelda Game Boy titles.

Bomberman Quest is a game that I feel like hits a lot of highs, but also hits a lot of lows. Might be why it never caught on. It’s got a true ending waiting for you if you take on all of its challenges, but its also got the most repetitive music. There’s cute NPC interactions between every enemy possible and unique dungeons you don’t see often, like a cloud dungeon. But it runs so slowly. It’s probably why it didn’t catch on, but it’s a fun lost treasure for fans of the old school Bomberman games under Hudson Soft.

Great Greed to me is a good game to look at if you want something historically relevant, fun, and a bit ridiculous at the same time. It’s a game where you get teleported to a strange world where most of the characters are named after food, while also honing in on themes of lobbying and the dangers of pollution. It’s a strange one, but it’s the kind of fun that most games don’t dare to be these days, so I’ll always shout it out when people look for recommendations.

Against the surprising food-themed names, including one named Microwave of all things at the very start, the most wild thing about Great Greed is how it hasn’t caught on sooner. Great Greed reviewed fairly decently back in the ’90s. Considering how so many people don’t know about this game until a list like this highlights it, no one played it despite that. I think it's because no one gave it a try, but Great Greed has been left to biodegrade on the Game Boy all this time.

Pokémon was the most well-known JRPG for the Game Boy. That’s not even up for debate. With a juggernaut like that, naturally a few other companies wanted to try and cash in on something similar. Capcom launched their Pokémon rival Metal Walker in 1999. Despite what this might suggest, Metal Walker is rather creative in execution, and if you ask me, it deserved more spotlight than it got back in the day. It’s as if there was a Pokémon: Special Eevee version from the start. And that Eevee was a robot.

That’s not even a joke! One of the cool aspects of Metal Walker is that you might only have one robot companion, but this companion can literally upgrade and evolve. You can gather cores you find in towns and dungeons that change the form of your robot on the fly. There’s also another elemental dynamic with different land, sea, and sky forms you can use to your advantage. This sounds a lot like Pokémon, doesn’t it? Well, a lot of critics agreed, and it didn’t exactly drum up interest on shelves because of it. So it’s just been left alone all this time.

If you're one of those people who love Pokémon, but wish it would try and be better than it is today, these games might already fit that bill.

Look. I love Mystery Dungeon games. I got into the games, and roguelikes, with Pokémon Mystery Dungeon when I was a kid. Yeah, I know. You saw that coming. Randomized dungeon floors with items to find and plenty of strategy to get to the bottom (or the top depending on the dungeon) is one of my favorite ways to pass the time and challenge myself. There are a lot of Mystery Dungeon games out there, but there’s one I’d like to nominate as an honorable member: Cave Noire for the Game Boy.

Cave Noire is a Japan-only game that was literally Mystery Dungeon before Torneko’s Great Adventure would create Mystery Dungeon as we know it. Like many games that would come later, namely the Pokémon Mystery Dungeon games, you play as an adventurer aiming to join the local guild. To join, you have to be strong enough to get through four randomly generated dungeons with scaling levels. Beat each dungeon six times, and you join the guild and beat the game, but there’s still plenty of dungeons to conquer after that. Its short and sweet size on the Game Boy keeps the game tight and challenging, which is a great retro recommendation for those who want a newer Mystery Dungeon game. Like the Pokémon ones. Sigh.

If you’ve heard of this game before, you’re likely either thinking that I’ve made a mistake somewhere and added a PS1 game to this list, or you already know this interesting factoid. Yes, Azure Dreams is a PS1 game, but it was also ported to the Game Boy Color of all things. Unlike the developmental shift of Lufia: The Legend Returns, Azure Dreams is a decent port of the PS1 game, even if it had to cut a few features to make it happen. One change you might notice is that there's a more Pokémon-like battle system now, which probably says a lot about why this game was brought to the Game Boy Color, huh?

Truthfully, Azure Dreams is one impressive GBC game. It features some fantastic sprite work, a very lofty start that matches its upbringing as a PlayStation title, and some in-depth systems you’ll need to learn to navigate and balance. The catch is that the tutorial is pretty heavy-handed, taking up an entire floor of the first dungeon, being explained in dialogue with every step. Oh. Yeah. It’s another game with randomly generated dungeon floors. Seems like Game Boy developers were really ahead of the times with these mechanics.

Ayakashi no Shiro is probably as obscure as they come for Game Boy titles. The title literally means “Castle of Ayakashi,” giving away the game’s simple premise. The Ayakashi are monsters that you’re in charge of defeating. So, you crawl through dungeons, fight ghosts, rats, bats, and more. And that’s it. Literally. Ayakashi no Shiro was considered a game without much depth by those that reviewed it back in Japan, but nowadays, it might be a breath of fresh air for those of us bogged down by more modern, overstimulating games.

Out of a lot of games on this list that could probably thrive from some sort of remake or classic release on Nintendo Switch Online or something, Ayakashi no Shiro is probably better off as the retro gem it is now. While a lot of other Game Boy games spawned franchises or at least have a good number of fans, Ayakashi no Shiro is one of the few titles by the now defunct SETA Corporation. As a result, this game’s fate is to haunt the Game Boy’s catalogue like the ghosts within its dungeon walls.

Yes, this really is a vegetable-based JRPG made by Atlus for the Game Boy. Spud’s Adventure is one of my favorite games on the Game Boy just for the humor in that sentence alone. Like Ayakashi no Shiro, there isn’t much to this little portable adventure, so it isn’t one of the most iconic Game Boy games or anything, but seeing Persona-creating Atlus doing something so charming and cute during its humble beginnings is never not surprising for anyone who didn’t know about it already. So I’ll mention it any chance I get.

If you’re looking for a quick and easy Game Boy title to blow dust off of and enjoy for a little while, Spud’s Adventure is the perfect game. As Spud the potato, you’ll traverse a tower to save Princess Mato from her kidnapper. You’ll fight enemies and solve puzzles along the way like every other small adventure game before RPGs were more widely defined. It isn’t too hard, and some of the power-ups are a bit zany. It might not be a game to write home about, but it’s definitely one that I think Atlus’ biggest fans should at least try. You know. For the fact that something this charmingly goofy exists at all. You’ve gotta love it.
