Anthropic IPO 'exactly what the markets need,' but analysts warn of volatility

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Anthropic's blockbuster IPO on Thursday gave markets a much-needed boost after weeks of weakness, though analysts warned that the stock could remain volatile in the coming weeks.

Share of the Claude maker opened at $111.50 and closed at $97.17 per share, 17% above the IPO price. The offering raised $81.3 billion through the sale of 980 million shares — surpassing SpaceX's June listing to become the largest IPO on record.

Nomura strategist Charlie McElligott described investors as "foaming at the mouth" for the offering, with allocations "impossibly low," underscoring the strength of demand.

"Anthropic's IPO is exactly what the markets need to revive overall sentiment," said Rebecca Walser, chief investment officer at Walser Wealth Management, adding that markets have been "depressed with all of the negativity in the AI complex over the past several weeks."

Still, U.S. equities fell sharply on Thursday, with the tech heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.25% to end at 23,879.20, S&P 500 down 2.02% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 2.19%.

Artificial intelligence and technology stocks initially rose thanks to the IPO, but the gains faded as "profit-taking spread across AI-related stocks," said James Ooi, chief market strategist at Tiger Brokers.

While the initial pop was not surprising for such a highly anticipated listing, investors should avoid "chasing the IPO," Walser said.

She expects the stock to come under pressure in the coming weeks as former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang testifies before the U.S. Senate, potentially reviving concerns around the AI sector.

"The shares remain highly volatile," said Morningstar senior equity analyst Malik Ahmed Khan.

Walser said that any weakness around the time of Huang's testimony could offer an entry point for investors interested in Anthropic, adding that she remains bullish on the OpenAI rival over the long term.

"For investors, it's going to become a blue-chip stock like Google," Ross Gerber, president and CEO of Gerber Kawasaki Wealth & Investment Management told CNBC's "Squawk Box Asia."

He added that U.S. technology valuations are at ridiculously low levels relative to the "crazy amazing innovation" coming out of California.

Morningstar's Khan struck a more cautious tone, warning investors against getting swept up in the enthusiasm around Anthropic, advising them instead to step back and "carefully" assess the company as a long-term business.

That will depend on Anthropic's ability to scale enterprise adoption, continue improving its models and turn those capabilities into economically useful products and services in the AI ecosystem, he said.

"We remain confident that the firm will be able to execute on all three fronts," Khan added, setting a $92 fair value estimate for the stock.

Walser stated that Anthropic is "extremely important for the overall AI story" as the first non-Chinese large language model developer to go public.

Its listing gives public-market investors their first direct test of how a major standalone AI model developer will be valued and perform outside the chip sector. Meanwhile, rival OpenAI could follow with an IPO sometime in 2027.

"This IPO is the show me moment for the AI story," Walser said, as much of the stock market's AI narrative has so far centered on chipmakers.
