Ford Motor vs. Stellantis: Which Automaker Titan Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ford generates far more cash, while Stellantis trades at a steep discount, and the trade-off between the two is sharper than the headline suggests.

Will the shift to electric vehicles and software-driven services benefit traditional giants? Investors are weighing whether to back Ford Motor Co. (F -1.99%) or Stellantis (STLA -1.89%) as the industry evolves.

Ford relies on its iconic truck brands and a dedicated commercial unit to drive loyalty in North America. Stellantis operates a vast global network with brands like Jeep and Ram, providing massive scale across Europe and the Americas. Both face the challenge of modernizing production while maintaining profitability in a rapidly changing market.

Ford operates through its Ford Blue, Model e, and Ford Pro segments, focusing on internal combustion engines, electric vehicles, and commercial fleet services. Ford operates as a key player among consumer discretionary stocks, serving a wide variety of retail and commercial clients. The company does not depend on any single customer for its revenue, which helps diversify its client base.

In FY 2025, revenue reached about $174 billion, representing growth of under 1%. Despite this increase, the company reported a net loss of approximately $8.2 billion. This shift in the bottom line highlights the significant costs associated with its ongoing strategic transition toward electrified and software-connected vehicles.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was nearly 4.7x, meaning total liabilities are significantly higher than the value of its shareholder equity. The current ratio of roughly 1.1x indicates the company has sufficient short-term assets to meet its immediate obligations, which measures the ability to cover short-term debts. Free cash flow, which is the cash generated from operations minus capital expenditures, was approximately $12.5 billion for the year. This cash generation provides flexibility as the company continues to invest in its long-term initiatives.

Stellantis manages a diverse portfolio of 14 iconic vehicle brands and two mobility brands, serving customers in more than 130 markets worldwide. Its operations cover major regions like North America, Europe, South America, and Asia. The company leverages this global scale to produce vehicles ranging from luxury cars to heavy-duty trucks. By maintaining a presence in multiple segments, the company seeks to capture demand across different economic environments and consumer preferences.

During FY 2025, the company reported revenue of around $169.7 billion, which was a decrease of approximately 2.1% from the prior year. This revenue decline coincided with a net loss of about $24.7 billion for the fiscal period. The substantial loss and the drop in sales reflect the difficult environment the company faced during the year. These figures emphasize the volatility inherent in the global automotive manufacturing business during periods of intense competition. (Note that figures have been converted from euros to U.S.. dollars as of the publication date).

According to its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.9x, indicating that its total debt is slightly lower than its shareholder equity. The current ratio of approximately 1.0x suggests that its short-term assets are roughly equal to its short-term liabilities. Free cash flow, defined as cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was negative $17.9 billion for the fiscal year. A negative free cash flow means the company spent more on its operations and investments than it collected in cash.

Ford Motor faces several operational hurdles, including supply chain disruptions and labor issues that can impact production schedules. The company also deals with complex recall campaigns for safety defects, which can lead to significant remediation costs and reputational damage. Additionally, legal and regulatory risks from environmental emissions standards and autonomous vehicle scrutiny remain prominent. Macroeconomic factors like geopolitical instability and inflationary pressures also pose risks to its capital allocation plans and the execution of its long-term strategic goals.

Stellantis navigates a challenging competitive landscape where it must defend its market share against both legacy automakers and new electric vehicle entrants. The company faces potential trade barriers and high costs associated with shifting its global manufacturing footprint toward more sustainable production methods. Volatile consumer demand and the cyclicality of global vehicle sales can also impact its financial performance. Furthermore, managing 14 distinct brands across diverse international markets requires significant resources and presents ongoing integration and operational complexity.

Stellantis appears significantly cheaper than its peer given its lower Forward P/E ratio, which compares the stock price to future earnings estimates, while Ford maintains much stronger cash flow.

European auto giant Stellantis continues to struggle to make its scale into a consistently profitable business. The company reported 1% revenue growth in its most recent quarter, weaker than prior quarters sequentially this year, with cost savings of about $3.4 billion for the quarter, below the $5.6 billion target set by management

Mainly, Wall Street is concerned that the business is seeing spotty strength across its brand portfolio, rather than broad-based strength in its primary markets of North America and Europe. Analysts project sales growth of 5% for the year in Stellantis' reporting currency, with a swing to net income of $1.05 billion, which would be a highly encouraging swing from 2025's deep net loss.

The skepticism surrounding Stellantis is shown in its low P/S ratio of 0.06x. That reflects little belief right now that the automaker can convert its revenue into a profitable business.

The venerable U.S. automaker Ford has advantages: a well-known brand and strong customer loyalty. But the company has to balance maintaining its existing combustion engine business with funding the expansion of its toehold in EVs. It's not always easy, as seen in the company's recent decision to mothball its electric F-150 model.

Still, after a decent 2025, Ford has now tallied five straight years of sales growth and continues to demonstrate vehicle leadership positions in internal combustion engine brands, with some green shoots in EV and hybrid brands. In addition, Ford is growing its higher-margin software and physical services business, led by Ford Pro.

For its third quarter of fiscal 2026, the company posted revenue of $46.1 billion, down about 3% from the year-ago period. Its net income of more than $2 billion was down 20% from the prior-year period. Still, for the full year, 2026 will end up better than 2025, according to analysts, with a slight sales gain to $177 billion and a $12 billion swing from a net loss to profit of around $4 billion.

Both of these stocks are bargain-basement finds, with forward P/E ratios reflecting the struggles of the traditional auto industry. Ford Motor gets the pick here, with its business showing a bit more sustained perk than Stellantis.
