Bears vs. Saints Game Balls: Big Jeff Dominates Again

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Chicago Bears continued their recent winning streak on Sunday afternoon, taking their fifth game in a row (and third since acquiring Jeffery Simmons). This time, it was a 21-13 victory over the New Orleans Saints at a dreary Soldier Field.

Much like last year's game against the Saints, there was some lingering rain and gray skies throughout. But that didn't take away from the newly acquired ray of sunshine on the Bears defense, Simmons, who once again was a force of nature that couldn't be denied by the opposing offensive line.

The Bears offense started hot, jumping out to a 16-0 lead by the middle of the second quarter. But much like the team's previous game against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers, the offense cooled off considerably down the stretch and relied upon the defense to seal the victory.

Most expected the Bears running game to get back on track in this one. The Saints came in with one of the worst rushing defenses in the league, but neither D'Andre Swift or the returning Kyle Monangai could really get things going. Salvon Ahmed, recently brought up from the practice squad, was the only running back that found much success (6 carries for 33 yards).

But the real shining star on offense on Sunday was rookie WR Thomas, who has had an up and down season thus far. Two weeks ago, he had a kick return called back on a holding penalty, while also receiving a brutal unnecessary roughness penalty (and later a fine, too) that stalled one of Chicago's scoring drives.

Thomas made up for it this week, hauling in two passes for 56 yards (tops on the team), both of them convert on third down. He also had one rush for 30 yards, and a 29-yard kickoff return.

Thomas' first catch was this 40 yard reception on 3rd and 17 early in the third quarter.

Previous to this, Thomas showcased his elite speed with a 30 yard pickup on an end-around in the second quarter.

And then there was this crucial third down conversion and wild catch in the fourth quarter.

Many Bears defenders had good outings on Sunday, but when it comes down to it, El Jefe showed why he's in the running for Defensive Player of the Year.

Simmons recorded three sacks against the Saints, taking his total up to five in his three games with Chicago so far. He finished the day with three tackles, three sacks, three tackles for loss, and three quarterback hits.

You cannot stop Jeffery Simmons. You can only hope and pray to contain him. But you'd probably fail.

It seemed as though any time the Bears needed a big stop, Simmons showed up. He now has nine sacks on the season, the most in the league among defensive tackles. As previously mentioned, he has five with the Bears through just three games. Last year, that number would have ranked third on the Bears for the season. He's done it in 12 quarters.

Simmons' total of nine for the year so far would already rank second among the 2025 squad (Montez Sweat had 10 last year), and Simmons still has seven games to go.

Honorable mentions on defense: DE Austin Booker, CB Clark Phillips III, S Dillon Thienemann, and LB Devin Bush.
