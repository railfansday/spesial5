Fernando Mendoza’s painful NFL debut could hold answers to next test vs Broncos

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The best thing Fernando Mendoza has going for him Sunday in Denver might be the same thing the Broncos have going for them. Both sides got a good look at what happened to the Raiders rookie against Seattle.

Presumably, both sides hit the rewind button.

Fernando Mendoza (15) of the Las Vegas Raiders warms up before a game at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

For Broncos defensive coordinator Vance Joseph, the attraction is obvious. The Seahawks sacked Mendoza six times in his first NFL start. Any coach with a rested pass rush and a home crowd willing to make life miserable for a visiting quarterback would be interested in how they went about it.

For Mendoza, the part worth rewinding might come a second or two before he hit the ground. What did he see when he came to the line? What changed when he got the ball? And where could he have gone with it once it did?

The Broncos present some big-time challenges in that regard.

"It's one of the top defenses in the league for a reason," Mendoza said. "So they are very good, they're very sound and I respect them a lot."

Coach Klint Kubiak is confident the rookie will process and adapt accordingly.

"His approach has been excellent since he got here," Kubiak said. "He's a competitor. It's his first NFL start. I thought he played well enough for us to win, especially in that first half, and we didn't close it out in the second half."

A week ago, those were questions he had to answer against Mike Macdonald's defense without the benefit of a single NFL start. On Sunday, there's at least a chance Joseph asks him something he's heard before.

Consider what Denver can take from the way Seattle made Mendoza account for pressure. A linebacker walking up to the line gives the quarterback something to think about. Put one in each gap alongside the center and now the quarterback and his protection have some decisions to make.

Michael Mayer of the Las Vegas Raiders avoids a tackle from Josh Jobe of the Seattle Seahawks in Las Vegas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

The beauty of it for Denver is that neither linebacker has to come. If Mendoza hurries a throw over the middle to beat a blitz and finds a linebacker dropping into the very window he picked out, Joseph will have gotten what he wanted without sending anybody extra.

So where's the opportunity for Mendoza? Start with what happens if that linebacker comes.

Somebody just vacated a valuable piece of real estate. A receiver breaking inside behind him can give Mendoza a throw he can make with his feet set, before the rush has much say in the matter. Six or 7 yards on first down would do wonders for an offense that spent too much of Sunday trying to figure out third-and-long.

There's a bigger throw available, too, if Denver decides to crowd the line and leave a cornerback outside with no safety behind him. That's a chance to give Tre Tucker a ball down the sideline. The trick is confirming the safety really did come down. Seattle gave Mendoza firsthand experience with how much a picture can change between the time a quarterback calls for the ball and the time it reaches his hands.

Getting Tucker involved would go a long way, especially if Brock Bowers' ankle injury keeps him on the sideline. Tucker's ability to stretch the field, and Mendoza's willingness to take some chances on those long throws, could help open things up for the run game and the short and intermediate passing game.

Tyler Linderbaum, left, Fernando Mendoza, center, and Jalen Nailor celebrate a touchdown in Las Vegas on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

"There are a lot of variables and what our coach always emphasizes making sure we're taking the positive gain," Mendoza said. "However, those deep shots and those explosives are what can help us win the game. And so, I mean, it's really play-by-play, and I look forward to taking a look at the shots we have this weekend."

Lost in those six sacks is a reason to believe Mendoza can make some of those throws. He completed 11 of 13 passes in the first half against Seattle and got the ball to eight receivers. He already knows what it feels like to take what an NFL defense gives him and move on to the next play.

Kubiak can help him get back to that. Give Mendoza an early throw on a moving pocket. Change where Denver's edge rushers expect to find him. And if the Broncos are getting upfield in a hurry, a well-timed screen gives those rushers an invitation to keep right on going.

Some of which requires a seamless process well ahead of the snap.

"I was very pleased with him getting out of the huddle, him making the correct checks, the correct calls," Kubiak said. "That's what he has shown in practice, his ability to do that and to be able to run the show that way. There's room for growth, but in his first start, I thought that he operated the game like he does in practice."

Of course, there's a limit to what anybody can draw up if the Raiders run for 65 yards on 24 carries again. Joseph had a bye week to study the Raiders. He hardly needed all of it to see the advantage in making Mendoza throw on downs when everyone in the stadium knows he has to.

Denver figures to make the rookie prove he can solve some of the problems Seattle put on tape. Fair enough.

But if the Broncos want to borrow from the Seahawks, Mendoza gets something out of the deal, too. Somewhere in all that film of what went wrong is a throw he'd love to have back.

"The most important thing is how he's playing the game. We'll coach up everything," Kubiak said. "But like I said, and like I told Fernando, I was pleased because of the preparation that he put in. The things that he did in practice showed up in the game, and he's going to have a lot of things to learn and grow from."
