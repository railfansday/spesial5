JJ Redick reveals rarely-seen qualities that separate this Lakers team

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

One of the reasons why things are clicking so seamlessly?

Apparently none of the team's wings are keeping score of petty things, such as who starts, who gets more touches, etc.

Lakers coach JJ Redick has a theory as to why that's happening.

"I think they're all really grateful to be here, to be Lakers," Redick said after the Lakers' 109-94 win over the Jazz on Monday. "They're all really grateful to be on this team and be part of the group."

Redick pointed out that Matisse Thybulle, Ziaire Williams, Quentin Grimes and Jake LaRavia are all in the same boat even though different circumstances brought them to this moment.

In other words, they all know this is a rare opportunity.

They deeply understand that playing for a Laker team that is tied for the best record in the league at 15-3 and has a real shot at winning a championship is something worth sacrificing for.

"They've experienced adversity in their careers, in different ways, and I think that gratitude piece is like they're all in a situation [together]," Redick said. "…They're just all in a situation where they're putting the team first and they're putting their egos aside and that stuff matters and I think you know it's one of the reasons our group has had early success because we said on day one we wanted this season to be about the team and not any one individual one of us, and so far it's been that."

Los Angeles Lakers head coach JJ Redick looks on before an NBA basketball game against the Utah Jazz on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

Jake LaRavia started in 11 straight games before coming off the bench in the team's two contests prior to their win over the Jazz. He returned to the starting lineup Monday, finishing with 15 points, 10 rebounds and two steals.

"I've talked about it before, I think just everyone is willing to sacrifice for the same goal, and we just have a lot of adaptable players," LaRavia said.

"A lot of it's like lineup-based, matchup-based, so you know, whoever the coaches think should start that night is who's going to start. And I think as players we're all willing to be kind of thrown in and out of the lineup.

"And it doesn't really change much for us as players. You just, you know, keep the same mindset and keep the same goal and just go out there and play hard."

Jake LaRavia (12) of the Los Angeles Lakers dribbles during a game against the Utah Jazz on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

As for Redick, he said this roster lends itself to experimenting with different rotations, as he did by putting Ziaire Williams in the starting lineup for LaRavia in a loss against the Jazz on Nov. 18 and a win over the Kings on Nov. 20.

"This group is like malleable to lineup changes, you know, tweaking rotations," Redick said. "They understand that we're still trying to figure out the group and what's best for the group. So I don't think [Ziaire] or Jake was affected at all by you know the two games with Z in the starting lineup. And we were really good with Jake in the starting lineup. We were going to go back to it at some point. We went back to it [Monday], and he played a great game."

Keyonte George #3 of the Utah Jazz drives against Ziaire Williams #11 of the Los Angeles Lakers on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, in Los Angeles. Getty Images

The Lakers have won 11 of their last 12 games, including five of their last victories coming while Austin Reaves has been sidelined because of a left heel bruise.

Things are clicking. The chemistry is great. The locker room has good vibes.

And everyone seemingly is focused on the bigger picture instead of individual accolades or perceived slights.

"At the end of the day, we have a lot of guys who haven't been able to give it their all to win, and whenever we're able to, I feel like we're going to give it our heart and give it our all," Collin Sexton said.

"And I feel like [general manager] Rob [Pelinka] did a great job of bringing guys in that's ready to be selfless, but also ready to do whatever needed to win, and play whatever role that is. So, I think when you have a locker room full of guys who want to win more than themself, and no one cares about who's the hero at the end of the day, it's like we're going to give it our all."
