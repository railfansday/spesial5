Even John Harbaugh wonders ‘what are we going to do with this opportunity?’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

John Harbaugh has coached in 24 playoff games, fifth-most of all-time. His Baltimore Ravens teams made the playoffs in 12 of his 18 seasons as head coach. Patrick Ricard, Isaiah Likely, Jordan Stout, Malik Harrison, and Ar'Darius Washington — who came to the New York Giants this season to join Harbaugh — have been through plenty of annual races to reach the playoffs with Harbaugh.

Aside from the Baltimore transfers, though, there are not a lot of Giants used to these playoff stretch drives.

Kayvon Thibodeaux and Andrew Thomas were on the Giants' 2022 playoff team. Jason Pinnock was on that team, as well as with the San Francisco 49ers for two playoff games last season.

Running back Devin Singletary has been part of five playoff teams.

Backup guard Aaron Stinnie won a Super Bowl with the Tampa Bay Buccaneers in 2021 and has been on three playoff teams.

Guard Jon Runyan Jr. was a part of three Green Bay Packers teams that reached the playoffs.

Reserve cornerback Nic Jones was part of a Super Bowl team with the Kansas City Chiefs in 2024.

Right tackle Jermaine Eluemunor has played in two playoff games, in a 10-year career none since 2022 with the Las Vegas Raiders.

Defensive tackle Shelby Harris has played in two playoff games in a 13-year career, none since 2014.

Safety Jevon Holland and backup quarterback Jameis Winston have been in one playoff game.

Special teamer Elijah Campbell, tight end Chris Manhertz, and cornerback Greg Newsome have two games of playoff experience.

That isn't a lot. Quarterback J.J. McCarthy has won high school and college national championships, but hasn't played an NFL playoff game. What the Giants are about to experience is new to key players like Cam Skattebo, Malik Nabers, Abdul Carter, Arvell Reese, and plenty of others.

Even for those who have done it, playing important games down the strech of an NFL season is a new experience with this group.

"It's part of where we're at right now. We have a team and we're together. The way I look at it, we're a collective. We've got guys who've done things and different things or whatever, different levels of accomplishment, different ages, all those things. But as a collective, we haven't been there in these types of games," Harbaugh said this week. "So, that's our next step. What are we going to do with this opportunity? What are we going to do over the next few weeks? Where are we going to go? It's up to us to make the determination of who we are.

"So, there's a lot at stake, and we'll find out how we're going to handle it. I mean, you don't know. You can talk all you want, you can say all you want, until you get out there and you start doing it and you find out really where you're at. And we're going to learn a lot."

The lessons begin Sunday afternoon against the Jacksonville Jaguars.

The Xavier Gipson Experience begins Sunday for the Giants. It could be great. It could be a disaster. I just know that if you thought you had to hold your breath when the ball was kicked to Charlie Jones, you will be doing the same, maybe worse, with Gipson.

With no injuries keeping players out Sunday, I am curious to see what the Giants' inactives list looks like.

I have two big questions today. Can the Giants run the ball well enough to keep J.J. McCarthy from having to carry the offense with his arm? Who wins the blitz battle between Giants defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson and Jaguars quarterback Trevor Lawrence.
