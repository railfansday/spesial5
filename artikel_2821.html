Where the 49ers need to improve defensively vs. Vikings

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Last weekend was not a good day for the San Francisco 49ers defense, as they gave up 40 points on the Dallas Cowboys, yet still managed to come away with the 46-40 victory.

San Francisco's defense struggled on all fronts. The secondary especially had its issues, as Dallas's top three receivers all had quality games. George Pickens had 11 catches for 145 yards and three touchdowns, and that was with him dropping three passes. CeeDee Lamb had seven catches for 72 yards and a touchdown, while Ryan Flournoy had six catches for 71 yards and a touchdown.

Cornerbacks Deommodore Lenoir, Renardo Green, and Upton Stout entered the game banged up, and it showed, as both had their fair share of struggles against Dallas's elite offense. Of course, the matchup wasn't easy facing a high-caliber quarterback with two Pro Bowl receivers, but it was a rough day for a defense that had been quite good over the first half of the season.

That wasn't the only issue. The 49ers pass rush was also ineffective, failing to sack Dak Prescott, and Nick Bosa came out this week and took accountability for his play this season. Bosa has yet to record a sack, although his other numbers still indicate a productive season, especially with the double teams and chips he's facing.

Now, this week, the health is looking better. Stout remained limited in practice with his ongoing shoulder injury and is questionable, but Lenoir wasn't on the injury report and Renardo Green practiced in full on Thursday and is off the injury report for Sunday's game.

But the 49ers still have a tough challenge against the Minnesota Vikings, who put up 30 points on the Green Bay Packers in Kirk Cousins's return. Minnesota's offense suddenly looks formidable, and this battle should be a tough one on both sides.

After their struggles against Dallas, where do the 49ers need to improve defensively against Minnesota? Ahead of Sunday night, defensive coordinator Raheem Morris broke down the main issues he saw from the Cowboys game and what to take from it.

"Extending drives early with some of the penalties to get those guys to go down there, particularly on the first drive [was a problem]. We gave up three, I believe, on that first one," Morris said. "We got the big penalty to get those guys down there. They got a big-time field goal kicker. A couple of big plays in the first half. The two big plays were the ones that killed us there early. Got a pretty good grip on it after that and kind of held it down. It was at some point it was like 38-19. Then that finishing moments, we didn't do a very good job of getting the finishes off. Usually, the finishes happen when you're able to generate pressure with sacks or you're able to get interceptions.

"The Cowboys did a nice job, to give them credit, of getting the ball out of their hand. Did a nice job of getting the ball into their good players' hand, keeping drives going, extending drives. They had about three or four drives there where it was almost like overtime-like play for them, where it was going to be fourth down territory regardless and they were able to capitalize and go down and score on us. You would like to be able to stop some of those plays and be able to get those guys going and get off the grass at those moments and then that score wouldn't have got to where it was, but those things didn't happen.

"We didn't execute that way. And it's a collective group. I know [Nick] Bosa stood up in front of you guys and took all the credit and took all the accountability, but I really think it's just all of us. Some of it could be better play calls for them. Some of it could be better for us, what we can do for those guys. Some of it can be better plays made by us. Some of it, we just ran out a little bit of gas, got a little bit tired. Let's not kid ourselves. Human nature as well. So, fortunate to get out of there with a win. Definitely want to learn those lessons when you get a chance to do those things in those moments, when you get wins. Something that we can learn from and grow from and something that we definitely will."

One thing Morris did see from his team was accountability after the game, with the leaders on the team stepping up.

"They're such an accountable group," Morris added. "Most guys, you come in and you're just upset after a game. These guys, they hold themselves to such a high standard. They come in, they're able to speak very clearly right off the bat. So it's not going to be more vocal the next week because they were vocal the first week. Not necessarily Bosa. Bosa's kind of a to-himself guy, but for a guy like Fred [Warner], he's always going to speak up and do the things that happened. It wasn't like there was mistakes. They did some better job executing. There was a few mistakes. There's always going to be mistakes and there's always correcting mistakes. I'm not saying there's not mistakes, but there's always going to be correcting mistakes, but you correct those mistakes, you move on.

"There's some other things that, just you talking to as an individual player. The best thing about our guys, they always point at themselves first. Whether it's Fred, whether it's Bosa, whether it's DMo (Deommodore Lenoir), Renardo [Green], anybody on our football team. That's the cool part about being here with these guys is they point at themselves first. Then a lot of the times I'm able to say, 'Hey, I can help you here' or 'we can do these things better. We can make these calls. We can help you out, do a little bit better in these situations.' They are all so upfront and forthgoing with those things. That makes it really cool to talk to those guys that way."

Health seemed to be a factor last weekend, but the 49ers do need to be more disciplined and avoid the killer penalties (although some looked to be questionable calls).

But there is a lot at stake this weekend for the 49ers, as their No. 1 seed odds shift dramatically with a win or a loss. San Francisco's offense has always been up to the task, but they'll get their toughest matchup of the year with Brian Flores's defense. So, the 49ers defense will need to show up as well against this new-look Vikings offense with Kirk Cousins and execute on all three levels.
