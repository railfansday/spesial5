Shaun Wright-Phillips: Chelsea star must make himself ‘hard to ignore’ for Xabi Alonso

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Former Chelsea and England star Shaun Wright-Phillips has urged Estevao to fight tooth and nail for a starting spot at Stamford Bridge and make himself ‘hard to ignore’ under Xabi Alonso.

Estevao burst onto the scene across an eye-catching opening campaign with Chelsea having joined the club from Palmeiras in the summer of 2025, racking up eight goals and four assists for the Blues.

A serious hamstring tear brought Estevao’s debut season to a premature end, though, a devastating blow that saw him miss out on a place in Brazil’s squad at this year’s World Cup.

Just days after Estevao sustained his injury, Chelsea decided to call time on Liam Rosenior’s turbulent managerial spell – and it has proven difficult for the 19-year-old to work himself back into the side since Alonso took charge.

The teenager had been limited to only two starts in the Carabao Cup approaching late October and rumours were beginning to circulate that he had grown frustrated with his peripheral role, with Barcelona reportedly keeping tabs on the situation.

However, Joao Pedro’s absence over the last month-and-a-half has created an opportunity for Estevao in Alonso’s attack and the youngster has started two of Chelsea’s last three games.

A humbling 4-1 defeat at the hands of Leeds may not have been what Estevao had in mind for his return, though, following a substitute appearance at Sunderland, and he will no doubt be desperate to banish those memories with a strong performance versus Nottingham Forest on Friday night.

Asked if he felt that Estevao still had a big future at Chelsea under Alonso, Wright-Phillips told Metro, courtesy of kwiff: ‘It’s a hard one, because it seems like players at Chelsea outlive the managers, which is sad to say.

‘That’s where modern football is going at the minute. It seems like players are outlasting coaches.

‘But in general, that’s more of a tactical thing, in that Xabi plays more with wing-backs.

‘If you play Estevao, you want him in the front three. Chances come in random ways, so he will get his chance again, and he has to do what he did the other day.

‘When he came on, I thought he was flying: not the weekend just gone, the weekend before that [against Sunderland. He came on and played really, really well.

‘So that’s him saying, “Look, I’m good enough to stay in your squad.” If he continuously does that, it becomes hard to ignore.’

Another player whose development has hit something of a stumbling block in recent months is Malo Gusto, who, like Estevao, has found regular game-time hard to come by this season.

Reports just last week indicated Gusto could be open to the prospect of leaving Chelsea, with Manchester United understood to be among the right-back’s admirers.

But when asked if it could be time to move Gusto on should a suitable offer come in for the Frenchman, Wright-Phillips replied: ‘Who do you replace him with? I think Gusto is a really fantastic player.

‘He might not be happy, which is understandable, because before Xabi came, when the other managers were there, his playing time was a lot greater, because it was either him or Reece James.

‘Reece James has had bad luck, he’s injured quite a lot, so Gusto got to play a lot. Whereas now, I think he’s better than a bit-part player, to be honest.

‘I haven’t watched him enough this season to turn around and say he should definitely be starting, but from what I saw of him last year and the type of player he is, I’d have him in my team at the drop of a hat, because I don’t think there are many right-backs out there that are gettable of that standard.’

Chelsea splashed out in the region of £350million on new signings in the summer transfer window, including Morgan Rogers, who commanded a club-record fee of £117m when he joined from Aston Villa.

There were signs that the Blues were reaping the rewards of their big spending in a promising start to the 2026/27 campaign, with Alonso’s all-star front three firing on all cylinders.

An injury to Joao Pedro has coincided with a concerning dip in results, though, and Alonso’s men were targeted with a chorus of boos as they exited the pitch following their latest setback on Saturday.

‘I think at the very beginning he showed why he’s there, and at the minute it seems to have plateaued a bit. I don’t know what that’s from,’ Wright-Phillips said when asked what he had made of Alonso’s start to managerial life in the Premier League, with Chelsea languishing down in eighth.

‘Playing as many games as they have, with the internationals that have come in the way they’ve come in, it feels like the Champions League is every week; it feels like it’s the Carabao Cup or something every week at the minute.

‘It’s going to take a bit of time for the players to get used to that change, where every game is a big game now for everybody; literally every game comes around very quick.

‘I think those clubs will settle down and get it together because they’re lucky to have the players within that squad to do that.’

Pressed on whether there were any areas of the squad in need of strengthening in January, Wright-Phillips responded: ‘I’m not sure they need midfielders. I would say if they had two, or even one, solid defenders who can teach the other young ones there, that would be more than enough.

‘Chelsea have got an amazing squad. They’re missing that rock at the back; who will fight and die for everybody, because that rubs off on the team.

‘You might not like him, but that attitude and mentality will naturally rub off on you. Someone in the Thiago Silva mould.

‘They need to learn; they’re learning from each other. They’re good players, but to get to the top, most younger players and defenders learn off somebody with a lot more experience.’

Switching his focus to the title race, Wright-Phillips, who represented Manchester City, Chelsea and QPR during his playing days, picked out Brighton as this year’s surprise package.

And while they were beaten in stoppage time by Hull City at the weekend, the ex-England winger is confident the Seagulls remain one of Arsenal’s ‘main challengers’.

‘No disrespect to Arsenal, but the best club for me in the Premier League this season, to watch and for output, is Brighton,’ Wright-Phillips – a Premier League winner with Chelsea in 2006 – explained.

‘Brighton are probably playing the best, but they’re not at the top of the league.

‘It can change and go a different way. I don’t think Arsenal are playing that well, and I don’t think City have played that well, but they’re the two teams that know how to get results when they aren’t playing to the level they know their maximum is.

‘Unfortunately for City, that hasn’t happened in the last four games, which is something they have to deal with and turn around.

‘The Christmas period is the right time to possibly do it, because all the games come quick and fast and the league table can change a dozen times in that time.

‘The main challengers to Arsenal would be Brighton and Manchester City as it stands. They’re the ones with the points on the board.

‘Until people start putting the points on the board, which is turning around, you’re seeing Liverpool sneak back up the table.

‘There will be other challenges as well, but leading into this Christmas period, I would say Arsenal’s challenger right now is Brighton and Manchester City.

‘Brighton, I think, are the only team to beat both of those two teams and do it nicely as well. It wasn’t a smash and grab.’
