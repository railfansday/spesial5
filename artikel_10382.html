The Jensen Huang scandal could become a legal nightmare for Nvidia

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Nvidia’s troubles may be just beginning.

Along with stock market losses and massive reputational damage, CEO Jensen Huang’s extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy exposes the chipmaking giant to major legal risks.

The most clear-cut claim is for securities fraud, Stanford Law professor Robert Bartlett told TechCrunch, which would rest on whether the company duped investors by providing false or incomplete information about the relationship.

This angle has already led to at least one class action filing, which cites Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo’s statement in late October claiming Huang’s interactions with alleged spy Alice Huang were “not unusual or unexpected.” It also cites a subsequent comment by Huang himself that Nvidia’s ties to Alice Huang’s employer, Singapore-based Megaspeed, were “normal and not unusual,” and Huang’s May 2025 comment that Nvidia had found “no evidence of diversion” of its chips.  

At least the first two statements have since proven to be inaccurate. Rizzo’s comment was “clearly a misleading statement,” Bartlett told TechCrunch, adding that because Huang is Nvidia’s CEO, the court will consider his personal knowledge to be the company’s knowledge.

Along with liability for securities fraud, the company could also be exposed to governance liability, Bartlett said. Even if it initially had no knowledge of the issue, Nvidia’s board should have been informed—and acted—once the New York Times began reporting on the company’s ties to Megaspeed. Instead, the board did not announce an investigation until 10 days after the article was published.

However, the most explosive legal scenario would be if Huang somehow knew about Alice Huang’s intelligence connection. 

Fordham School of Law Professor Thomas Lee wrote today that this could expose Huang to criminal liability under the Economic Espionage Act, which punishes theft or misappropriation of trade secrets to benefit a foreign government.

Huang may also be exposed under the Export Control Reform Act of 2018 and the Export Administration Regulations, Lee added, which would consider any willful transfer of technology or source code to Alice Huang as an export to China.

Nvidia’s board has said it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised. President Donald Trump said yesterday that Huang told him that he didn’t know Alice Huang was a spy.

Three House committee chairs have asked Jensen Huang for a closed-door briefing next week, Bloomberg has reported.
