Alfonso Ribeiro on the “Wild” Ride of ‘DWTS’ Season 35, Reuniting With Tatyana Ali and Why He Won’t Compete in an All-Winners Season

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

To commemorate the finale of ‘DWTS,’ Ribeiro chats with THR about the ups-and-downs of season 35, his favorite part about hosting the show and why he can’t predict who will join him in the winners circle on Tuesday night.

Season 35 of Dancing With the Stars marks Alfonso Ribeiro’s fifth season hosting the show, and he has no idea how it will end.

After winning season 19 with Witney Carson, The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air joined the series as a co-host in its 31st season, a year that he believes laid the foundation for the show’s resurgence. “I think we owe a debt of gratitude to Charli D’Amelio doing the show. … Her being literally one of the most popular social media TikTok stars opened the door to an audience that previously didn’t care about this show,” he tells The Hollywood Reporter of what he pinpoints to DWTS’ spike in popularity (and ratings) in recent years. The TikTok star certainly brought new eyes to the series, but Ribeiro also refreshed it as host. After one year executing the job alongside Tyra Banks, Julianne Hough stepped in as his co-host, and the duo have remained at the post since. They both bring unique perspectives to DWTS: Ribeiro, as a celebrity who won the series, and Hough, as a former professional dancer and judge.

Ribeiro says there’s so many things that he loves about hosting the show, but one standout is the mentorship he’s able to offer the celebrities. This year, his Fresh Prince sister Tatyana Ali hit the ballroom floor, which he says has been “beautiful for me to experience with her.”

Ali is one of five couples still competing for a chance to win the Len Goodman mirrorball trophy on Tuesday night. Ribeiro admittedly doesn’t know what factors will hold more weight when determining this year’s winner.

“It is quite wild to me because this is a very different season than any other season we’ve ever had,” he says. “So, all of the normal things that we would be able to gauge doesn’t really exist this year.”

One thing Ribeiro hopes is that the portion of the audience spreading hate towards contestants will fade in future seasons. “At the end of the day, this is a dance competition show. This isn’t life or death. No one’s life is changing that greatly from this. It is designed for people to let loose, have some fun and truly enjoy these incredible celebrities,” he adds.

To commemorate the finale of DWTS, Ribeiro chats with THR below about the ups-and-downs of season 35; being in the ballroom and getting the chance to dance with Ali; his opinion on fans who believe celebrities with prior dance experience shouldn’t be cast on the show; and why you won’t ever see him compete on another All-Star (or all-winners) iteration of DWTS.

How does it feel to almost be at the finish line of season 35 of Dancing With the Stars?

This is an incredibly epic season. The numbers, the fans and the interest has been through the roof. We feel really blessed to do something no other show has really ever done in history, which is come back from the brink of extinction to turn around and become the No. 1 show on television. We’ve got a great cast, and they’ve done an incredible job of opening themselves up and allowing America to get to know them. Deena Katz and Conrad Green put a great team together.

The season 35 finale is going to be airing live coast-to-coast for the first time. What do you think that says about the growth and reach of the show that this was so wanted by fans, but also so much so that the affiliates came to an agreement for this to happen?

That’s a major move, right? The only thing on television that does that are sporting events, and the fact that we can move side by side with the NFL and NBA, and people are willing to, on a Tuesday night on the West Coast and Mountain time, come home a little early so that they could watch the show live is quite spectacular when you really think of it. That the affiliates and the network have that much trust in doing this as a finale says that they truly believe that this will work out great for the show and for the sponsors and the companies buying ad time. It says a lot about what they believe in the show.

Do you think that airing the finale live could be a potential test to see how the viewership comes in to see if the show would adapt this live format for a future season?

I can’t really speak on it, obviously, because I don’t know anyone’s thoughts. Anything I say would be conjecture, not anything real. I actually believe that it works the way that it works so far. Of course, we’d love for everyone on the West Coast and everyone in the Mountain time zone to be able to vote for their favorites. I think the interesting way would be to do it on Disney+ and not necessarily on ABC. But the question becomes, does the audience truly support it? And if the audience supports it, then why not, right?

If the numbers end up being as good or better, then of course let’s do it. But there are things that would severely be hampered by that. Technically speaking, ABC News Tonight is the No. 1 broadcast show on Tuesday nights, so you’d be giving up on that for the success of Dancing. The smarter people will make that decision, and we’ll see where that leads us. We’re super excited that we’re getting to experience this particular case. Hopefully all of America and everybody on the West Coast watches it live and we break ratings and voting records.

Tatyana has had such an incredible journey. What has it been like being able to be there and be a part of that journey?

I’ve been trying to get her to do the show for many years, and the timing just wasn’t right. And so happy she did, and really, mostly, so happy for her. It isn’t about me; it’s about her and what she is getting to experience. The little girl I met in 1990, [she’s] all grown up. She’s such a special person. What this show did for me was show America who Alfonso really was, not that character [from Fresh Prince]; same situation for her: getting to experience who Tatyana Ali is and people falling in love with the beautiful human she is.

What was your reaction when she came to you with the idea for you to be a part of her Dedication Night dance in honor of James [Avery]?

It started obviously with the producers asking me the question first. When we finally talked about it, it was already a yes. I expressed my very small concerns of, does that make it look like I’m favoring someone over everyone else, and will that be something that we ultimately have to deal with? And everyone came to the conclusion that that wouldn’t be an issue. The thing that took it over the top for me was that the dedication was to James Avery, and it was bigger than just this show. James Avery played my dad on three different shows, so dedicating it to him felt so right. It was a great opportunity for us to spend extra time together and go through the process of doing a competitive number on the show. I dance all the time on the show, but to be part of a competitive number made it extra special.

You have seen the growth the show has had in the past few years. What do you think it has been about these past two seasons that have really hooked a wider audience and opened the show up to more people?

I actually think it goes back further than the last two seasons. I think we owe a debt of gratitude to Charli D’Amelio for doing the show. Before her season, it was always an older audience. It wasn’t what it is today, and I think that her being literally one of the most popular social media TikTok stars opened the door to an audience that previously didn’t care about this show and always looked at it as, Oh, that’s my grandmother’s show. That’s my parents’ show. It wasn’t their show, and I think Charli D’Amelio made it OK for it to be their show. As the years went along, it just grew and grew, and more people talked about it, and it became this very cool energy that moved through the younger generation. Back in the day, we used to have this term, the “water cooler” TV shows, where people would stand around the water cooler at the office and talk about the show. Well, social media has become the water cooler, and this show has gained on that.

It’s beautiful to watch how the young audience is falling in love with the show. I certainly do hope that the younger audience can be fickle, and know as quickly as they fall in love with something, they fall out of love with it. I’m hoping that they stay in love with it for a while, and recognize that what we’re trying to do is bring love and joy to an audience. I will say that the audience out there that adds a lot of hate, negativity and bullying — I hope that goes away and it becomes more of just love and joy. At the end of the day, this is a dance competition show. This isn’t life or death. No one’s life is changing that greatly from this. It is designed for people to let loose, have some fun and truly enjoy these incredible celebrities.

There has been a lot of chatter and conversation online this season. Has there been one specific conversation online that has specifically stood out to you?

No, I’m not going to say anything specific. What I will say is everyone is entitled to their perspective of what they think the show is, and I would say that for me, it isn’t necessarily a show about people who have no dance experience learning to dance. You do that in Hollywood, it becomes a really short list of people. I look at this show as this is just a bunch of celebrities coming together to learn ballroom dancing. Whether they start with a lot of experience or they start with zero experience, it’s about the journey that they all go on.

What is your favorite part about hosting Dancing With the Stars?

I really do love being almost a mentor to a lot of our celebrities. I go in on Monday and I’m there all day watching every one of the performances and being there as a support system; I’m there for all of it. I’ve been the host of the show, [this year] being my fifth season, and yes, I was a contestant, but I was also a fan from season one on, so I’ve watched almost every person that’s ever done this show in all 35 seasons. That’s really the part that I enjoy: I enjoy being there for them, and it’s a gift that I get to be a part of their journey in some form.

Do you have a favorite number you performed on the show?

My favorite was my Paso Doble to “Turn Down for What.” That was my all-time favorite one. It was a lot of fun to do that. I mean, the music — songs make dances, and you would never think [of doing] a Paso Doble to that song, but yet Witney choreographed a great number having Lindsay [Arnold] on it with us.

Viewers always talk about how they want a potential second all-star season, but something I have always wondered about is an all-winners. If they ever did that and the idea was floated by you, is there any world where you would ever consider doing an all-winners season?

No, and that’s mostly because of the fact that I am the host of the show, and I certainly wouldn’t be giving that job to someone else while I dance again. The answer would be no. You know, we always talk about the all-star season. We always talk about how much fun that would be. The only problem with an all-star season, and especially now, think about how many of our new fans dislike the idea that they have dancers [competing on the show] that are really good dancers. So, you’d literally be making a show that all of those audience members wouldn’t ultimately want to watch. [It’d be] fun for the people doing it, right? But is it fun for that new audience that loves the idea of it being people who couldn’t dance in the beginning?

I think the two fan favorites from this season so far are Connor [Wood] and Tatyana. Tatyana, if she was to win, will be the lowest scoring winner in the history of the show on week one.

That’s this new audience. Tatyana doesn’t make the finale 10 years ago with that audience, because it was never about that. Now you have audience members that really want to see the Guillermos. They want to see the Tatyanas and the Connors and, going back to last season, the Andy Richters. They want to see those people go a long way because, at the end of the day, most of our audience can’t dance, so they’re excited about watching someone who reminds them of themselves achieve something that they would love to do.

I don’t know if I win if I’m on a season now, even though I’m not a trained dancer. Just the idea that I have dance experience, this audience is harder on those people, and I think that’s wrong, personally. That’s my own personal [opinion], not from the show’s perspective. I think it’s wrong because I think if we had 16 people on the show in the beginning who couldn’t dance, I don’t know if that’s an enjoyable show to watch.

I think watching some really great dances, some dances that are mediocre and dances that are not so good, I don’t think that the show would be as successful. I question that audience because I go, man, I think you kind of want your Jenna Dewan’s and your Harry [Shum Jr.]’s and your Charli D’Amelio’s, right? You you want those people on there, and you want to see them continue to get better.

Who do you think will be joining you in the Dancing With the Stars winner’s circle Tuesday night?

Not a clue. I really don’t know. It is quite wild to me because this is a very different season than any other season we’ve ever had. So, all of the normal things that we would be able to gauge doesn’t really exist this year. A lot of people online say that Connor’s getting the most votes, right? OK, but he’s also the lowest-scoring celebrity. So where does that fit? How does that work? How does the scoring system work? All of those questions leave us to, you know, if you were to look at scores through the season so far, Amber [Glenn] and Pasha [Pashkov] and Ezra [Frech] and Daniella [Karagach] are your hands down two of your favorites. Well, we don’t know where they are on the list, so I have no clue. I really don’t. What I will say is, what I’m hoping for is that every one of our celebrities and partners gives the best performances of the season, and then let it fall where it falls.
