German Giants Eye West Ham Star But Face £22m Obstacle

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

West Ham United midfielder Arne Engels is attracting interest from Bundesliga side VfB Stuttgart, although the German club could struggle to afford the £22m summer signing.

According to German newspaper BILD, as reported by Sport Witness, Stuttgart have identified Engels as one of several potential replacements for Angelo Stiller, who could leave the club next summer. Southampton’s Caspar Jander and Middlesbrough’s Aidan Morris are also under consideration.

However, Stuttgart reportedly regard the cost of signing Engels as unrealistic, with West Ham unlikely to accept a substantial loss on a player who only arrived from Celtic in August. The German club previously wanted the Belgian during his time at Augsburg, so this is not an entirely new interest.

Look, clearly Engels is somebody that West Ham head of recruitment Nils Koppen values extremely highly.

As we reported when the £22m agreement with Celtic was reached, the Belgian had been Koppen’s primary midfield target throughout much of the summer. West Ham pursued him for weeks before finally getting their man.

I thought very quickly he’d become one of the first names on the team sheet under Nuno Espírito Santo, and I suspect that will continue under Scott Parker.

So clearly he is a player who is admired.

That said, I’m not surprised Stuttgart are struggling to find value at £22m because I do think West Ham overpaid when we bought him from Celtic.

West Ham midfielders will play with more freedom under Parker which could unlock Engels value

Now, that price becomes fully justified if he helps get the team promoted this season. If not, then I’d have to say that so far he’s done little to justify the price tag.

That’s not to suggest Engels is a bad player. He’s a busy, high-energy midfielder who covers plenty of ground and contributes to the team’s overall play.

But there’s a difference between being a good Championship midfielder and looking like a £22m player.

Indeed, when I recently assessed Koppen’s summer recruitment, Engels was one of the players I felt remained something of an unknown quantity in terms of value for money.

But, and here’s the important thing, West Ham manager Scott Parker has a habit of getting central midfielders scoring goals.

And if he can get Engels further up the pitch than Nuno Espírito Santo managed to, and into goalscoring positions, I suspect his value will rise if he becomes a goalscorer rather than just a busy, high-energy midfield player.

We’ve already had a glimpse of what might be possible.

Engels scored his first West Ham goal during the extraordinary 3-3 draw at Cardiff City in Parker’s opening match, as the Hammers recovered from three goals down at half-time.

That was one of the positives from a remarkable second-half performance, which I discussed in my player ratings following the Cardiff comeback.

However, against Blackburn, Parker used Engels in a deeper midfield role, with Mohamadou Kanté and Divine Mukasa operating further forward. As our Blackburn match report highlighted, that arrangement helped protect the defence but offered Engels fewer opportunities to get forward.

It will be interesting to see whether Parker can find a balance between the two.

Engels has the energy to get around the pitch, and if he can add regular goals to his game, the argument over whether West Ham paid too much could begin to look very different.

For now, Stuttgart’s interest looks more like an indication of Engels’ standing in the game than an immediate threat to West Ham.

The Belgian is under contract until 2031, with the club holding an option for another year, as confirmed by West Ham’s official website.

And with the Hammers attempting to win promotion at the first attempt, I can’t imagine Koppen being particularly enthusiastic about losing the player he worked so hard to sign.

Personally, I still believe £22m was too much money for what Engels has shown us so far.

But if Parker can turn him into a midfielder who regularly arrives in the penalty area and contributes goals, that assessment could change considerably.

And if those goals help West Ham get promoted, nobody will be complaining about the transfer fee.
