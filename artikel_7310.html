Your buyers are sending agents. Will they recommend your brand?

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

For most of my career, marketing and sales have run on a single assumption: a person is paying attention. We wrote for human eyes, optimized for human clicks, and designed every interaction for human discernment. That assumption is starting to break.

Buyers are sending AI agents out ahead of them that have been tasked to read what you publish, compare you against the field, and hand the user a shortlist, often before that user lands on your site or talks to sales. At the same time, marketing and sales teams are building their own AI systems to keep pace. Both shifts are landing at once, and together they change how demand gets created and how trust gets earned.

This is the biggest shift in marketing since search itself. The brands that come out ahead will be the ones that get their foundation right first, because there is no AI strategy without a data strategy.

We have optimized for machines before; that was the whole idea behind search. What is happening now is a different order of magnitude. Agents are constantly reading, comparing, and recommending next steps based on users’ prompts.

That changes my job and yours. An agent now reads our story, determines whether our brand looks clear and trustworthy, and uses that read to decide whether we make the shortlist it hands to a decision-maker. Clear, consistent, well-structured content is what tips that decision and most brands will never know when they have been left off the list.

Enterprise marketing has always been episodic. We run a campaign, pass a lead, let sales run a play, and start over. Agent-driven buyers no longer move on that calendar, so our engines must evolve.

The shift now is toward an always-on system that reads each customer continuously, puts the best opportunities first, and evolves in the moment. This new process is rewiring how marketing and sales work together, from a handoff between two teams into one operation looking at the same customer at the same time.

But none of it works without a trustworthy foundation. If revenue or pipeline means six different things in six systems, layering AI on top will only scale the confusion. Governed, well-understood data is what lets an always-on engine move fast without getting reckless. When everyone has access to the same models, data becomes your differentiator because customer history and context are yours alone.

There is of course a grim version of this future as well. Infinite personalization collapses into infinite noise and every inbox fills with confident, hollow outreach. I think that outcome is a choice, not an inevitability.

Used well, AI takes the mechanical work off our teams: the assembling, formatting, and first draft nobody enjoys. When it gives back time, that time is only worth something if people spend it where machines cannot: understanding a customer, building a real relationship, making the call on what comes next.

That does not happen by accident. It takes leaders willing to rewrite roles, retrain teams, and give people room to experiment. But the judgment, creativity, and taste required to plan and execute differentiated marketing at scale will always remain human.

Your next customer may arrive as an agent or send one ahead. When that agent builds its shortlist, I want Snowflake on it, and I want your company on it too. That is the work in front of every marketing and sales leader now.
