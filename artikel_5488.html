Bronx real estate broker indicted for allegedly hiding felony convictions on license renewal

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

New York Attorney General Letitia James announced charges against Bronx broker Marcia Campbell tied to alleged false statements on a June 2026 license renewal. Campbell was indicted in Albany County on 1 class E felony count and 1 misdemeanor count.  AI Summary

Bronx real estate broker Marcia Campbell was arrested and indicted for allegedly failing to disclose felony convictions and pending criminal charges when she applied to renew her New York real estate broker license, New York Attorney General Letitia James announced Thursday.

Campbell, 64, who operates Extreme Realty Associates Inc. in the Bronx, is accused of falsely stating on a June 2026 renewal application that she had not been convicted of a crime and had no criminal charges pending since her previous renewal, according to the announcement from the Office of the Attorney General (OAG).

State regulators say that during that June 14, 2024, to June 13, 2026, period, Campbell had in fact pleaded guilty to two felonies in Westchester County and was facing 18 pending charges in Bronx County tied to alleged deed theft and real estate investment scams. Once the New York Department of State (DOS) discovered the alleged false statements, her broker license was administratively suspended.

The case underscores how aggressively New York regulators and prosecutors are now scrutinizing license applications and renewals for real estate professionals, particularly in deed theft and fraud matters. For brokers and agents, the case is a reminder that criminal history disclosures on licensing forms are not procedural formalities, but sworn statements that can carry separate felony exposure if falsified.

The new indictment builds on a series of criminal matters involving Campbell in multiple counties.

In October 2024, Attorney General James and Bronx District Attorney Darcel Clark announced two separate indictments against Campbell. In the first, she and her co-conspirators — including her husband — were charged in an alleged deed theft scheme involving the childhood home of a Bronx woman. Prosecutors say the group gained the homeowner’s trust, then used fraudulent contracts bearing the homeowner’s forged signature to transfer the property to a shell company they controlled and to obtain a $493,500 mortgage on the home. Campbell is scheduled to go to trial in that case on Feb. 22, 2027.

The second Bronx indictment accused Campbell of running a series of real estate investment scams. According to prosecutors, she induced clients to send her funds for purported real estate transactions, then kept the money and refused to return most of it. Those alleged scams took more than $250,000 from vulnerable New Yorkers, including first-time homebuyers, immigrants and a personal friend, the OAG said. A trial date in those cases has not yet been set.

Separate Westchester County prosecutions led to criminal convictions. On Dec. 18, 2025, Campbell pleaded guilty in two cases to Falsifying Business Records in the First Degree and Repeated Failure to File Corporate Taxes, both class E felonies. Authorities said those cases involved an alleged deed theft scheme that targeted three Westchester homes and involved roughly $2 million in fraudulently obtained loans. Campbell was sentenced to time served in those matters.

Despite those developments, prosecutors say Campbell submitted a broker license renewal application to DOS in June 2026 covering the period from June 15, 2026, through June 13, 2028. On that application, she allegedly answered “no” to questions asking whether she had been convicted of a crime or had criminal charges pending during the prior licensing period.

Attorney General James alleges Campbell knowingly omitted her December 2025 felony convictions in Westchester County and the 18 Bronx County charges that were already pending at the time she filed the renewal. When DOS identified the discrepancies and referred the matter to law enforcement, the agency suspended Campbell’s license.

For brokerages, the case highlights the operational risk of failing to monitor the criminal and regulatory status of principal brokers and associated licensees. A suspended or revoked license can jeopardize transactions, commissions and firm reputation, especially in markets where deed theft has drawn heightened scrutiny from lawmakers and regulators.

New York officials at the state and local level have invested significant resources into deed theft enforcement in recent years, particularly in New York City and surrounding counties where equity-rich but cash-poor homeowners are frequent targets. These schemes often rely on trusted intermediaries, including licensed real estate professionals, to gain access to property records and homeowners.

“New Yorkers depend on real estate brokers to help them make some of the most important purchases of their lives,” Attorney General James said in the announcement, adding that Campbell allegedly abused her role “to steal New Yorkers’ homes and savings, then tried to hide her crimes to maintain her license.”

For compliance officers at brokerages and franchisors, the case signals that state regulators are willing to cross-check licensing records with court and tax data and to bring independent criminal charges based solely on false or incomplete filings. Firms may want to revisit internal policies requiring prompt disclosure of arrests and convictions by licensed personnel and procedures for verifying those disclosures.
