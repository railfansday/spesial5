Home Depot Taps Longtime Exec for CEO as Company Faces ‘Frozen Housing Market’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Home Depot COO Richard McPhail was announced as the company's new CEO on Tuesday, ahead of the home improvement giant's third-quarter earnings call. McPhail will take over on Nov. 23.

McPhail is taking the reins from Ted Decker, who will stay on as chairman. Decker stepped into the role in 2022.

McPhail has been with Home Depot since 2005 and previously served as executive vice president and chief financial officer.

Meanwhile, Ann-Marie Campbell was named president and chief operating officer. Campbell began her career with the retailer 41 years ago, as a sales associate and most recently served as an executive vice president in charge of U.S. stores as well as Home Depot Canada, Home Depot Mexico, and Pro sales and services.

On Tuesday, during its third-quarter earnings call, the company reported better than expected quarterly revenue of $43.4 billion, up 4.9% from a year ago.

In remarks during the call, McPhail said Home Depot is “continuing to gain market share in a pressured housing market."

During the company's second-quarter earnings call in August, McPhail spoke of the "consumer uncertainty" around the housing market.

"While consumer uncertainty and housing affordability continue to pressure demand for larger home improvement projects, we remain focused on what we can control, our strategy of driving our core and culture, delivering a frictionless, interconnected experience, and winning the pro," he said.

When asked during the call whether higher interest rates were impacting the housing market, McPhail opined that it was actually a lack of housing inventory that was creating a sluggish market. At the time of the second-quarter calls, rates were 6.67% but have since climbed to 7.21%.

"With respect to rates, as we have said over the past few years, housing turnover, just as one point in the economy that we watch, has been at historical lows," he said. "It has never been lower as a percentage of the housing stock. Every time we have seen it hit the sort of 3% of the housing stock changing hands, over history, it has always bounced up relatively quickly. We have seen housing turnover at these low levels for four years now. I do not think that we have seen much volatility from the recent increase in rates. We do know that when we see step downs, we begin to see a little bit of life come into housing, but there is just no sign of an inflection point at this moment."

Following that meeting, McPhail acknowledged the "frozen housing market" in a conversation with CNBC.

“We continue to operate in what I call ‘frozen housing market conditions,’ but we also know that we’re taking share and that we’re serving our customers better every day,” he said. “It’s a reflection of the continued investment we’ve made and the focus on executing our strategy.”
