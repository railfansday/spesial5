BUILDER Reveals the 2026 BCDA Project of the Year

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

“The relationship to the earth and forest shifts from very grounded on the landward side of the house to a more perched position at the water side. The house is oriented so that the existing madrone tree is an essential element of the experience,” says PBW Architects’ Dan Wickline, who served as principal architect for the project.

“Our favorite aspect of the project is the connection between the protected outdoor space, the main interior living space, and the more exposed space adjacent to the water. The design creates a natural flow between these three areas, with transparency as well as physical connections that allow each space to blend into the others while serving its own distinct purpose,” adds Wickline. “The home is protected from the wind without sacrificing its water views, which is truly unique.”

Rather than relying on extensive glazing, the massing strategically frames key views while shielding occupants from the elements. The main living volume rises above the adjacent wings, allowing clerestory windows on three sides to bring filtered natural light deep into the interior. Generous openings on the north and south sides promote passive ventilation, eliminating the need for air conditioning.

Award: Project of the YearArchitect: PBW ArchitectsBuilder: Doug Guard ConstructionSize: 1,705 square feetLocation: Friday Harbor, Washington

Built by Doug Guard Construction, material selections reinforce the home’s connection to place. Alaskan yellow cedar clads portions of the exterior and interior, complemented by stained rift-sawn oak cabinetry, polished concrete floors, and black-framed windows. Together, the materials create a warm, durable palette suited to the Pacific Northwest coast.

With longevity in mind, the residence combines a compact footprint with resilient construction capable of withstanding coastal weather conditions. Native vegetation was protected throughout construction, and disturbed areas were restored with plantings that provide erosion control and natural filtration, with help from Garden Artisan Landscape. By working with the site’s ecology rather than against it, the Cape San Juan project offers a model for sustainable waterfront living.
