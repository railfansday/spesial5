The Bank of England hiked interest rates and warned inflation could hit around 5%

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1 to lift the rate to 4%, with one member pushing for a larger 0.5-point increase

The Bank of England raised its key interest rate by 0.25 percentage point to 4% on Thursday as higher energy prices from the conflict in the Middle East push up U.K. inflation, the bank said.

The Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1 for the increase. Eight members backed a quarter-point rise, while MPC member Catherine L. Mann voted for a half-point increase to 4.25%.

The bank published new forecasts alongside the decision. U.K. CPI inflation rose to 3.5% in September, and the bank expects it to climb to around 5% by the middle of next year. It then projects inflation to ease as the lift that pricier energy gives to annual readings wears off and tight monetary policy keeps weighing on prices.

The move is the bank's first rate increase since August 2023, according to CNBC.

The bank said there is limited evidence of knock-on effects from energy costs on other prices and wages. It judged, however, that "the degree of inflationary pressure in the pipeline is now sufficient to warrant an increase in Bank Rate." Activity data in recent months have generally been stronger than expected, the bank said.

Front-month Brent crude oil futures averaged $108 per barrel over the 15 U.K. working days ending Oct. 27, 38% higher than at the time of the July report.

The bank said monetary policy is not on a pre-set path. The committee will adjust Bank Rate as necessary to return inflation to the 2% target in the medium term.

In the minutes of the meeting, Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey said that "without news which softens the risks to inflationary pressures, I can see the argument for a further increase in rates and thereafter taking a more conditional approach, dependent on the evidence on energy prices and inflation dynamics."

MPC member Alan Taylor, who backed the quarter-point rise, wrote that "this is not the start of a hiking cycle." He described the move as a risk-management "one-for-now" insurance hike.

Mann said research points to non-linear inflation risks. She wrote that "from a risk management and credibility perspective, a 50-basis point hike in Bank Rate is 'good policy.'"

The Bank of England left its key rate unchanged at 3.75% in September, when six committee members voted to hold and three favored a rise to 4%.

The European Central Bank and the Federal Reserve each raised rates by 25 basis points in September, according to the Bank of England's report.
