Tokenized stocks are exactly where stablecoins were in 2020 – How?

Stablecoins started as a way to hold dollars on-chain. Then people started lending, trading, and earning with them. In time, DeFi grew up around them.

Tokenized stocks are heading down the same route now, and the resemblance is uncanny.

What’s worth noting here is how much of it is actually being used.

According to data from Token Terminal, tokenized stocks deposited into DeFi are now worth about $300 million. A year ago, that was at approx. $12.5 million.

This number didn’t move at all through late 2025. Things picked up in the spring, blasted through $100 million by around August, and then shot past a quarter million in September.

It’s also a bigger share of the market now. That $300 million is 6.3% of the $4.8 billion tokenized stock market cap. Last year, only 2.4% was being put to work.

Because once they’re there, holders can borrow against them, earn fees by providing liquidity, or chase yield.

And every new way to use them, is essentially one more reason not to cash out.

Tokenized stocks now have a market cap of about $4.8 billion. Stablecoins crossed that exact number in March 2020. Since then, stablecoins have grown, 59 times over! The number is at $285 billion now.

Tokenized stocks are moving fast too. They were worth only a few tens of millions in early 2025.

But why? A blockchain is basically a ledger for assets, and it gets more useful when it holds things people really want. U.S Stocks are about as in-demand as it gets.

Stablecoins didn’t get to where they are without the effort.

They made it through the COVID crash, the Terra collapse, FTX, and the USDC de-peg after SVB failed. Market cap fell by 28% between February 2022 and October 2023, then spiked up to a high by September 2024.

What kept them going though? Cash and T-bill reserves backed every token, plus people who actually wanted to use them.

Tokenized stocks follow the same 1:1 idea.

Except the risk factor is higher. So is the potential for gains.
