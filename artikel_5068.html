Bitquery counted 400 fewer BTC in Maple’s disclosed wallets than Maple reported

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Bitquery found 97.6% of the Bitcoin Maple reported for syrupUSDC on Nov. 18. It put the value of collateral it could count at about 152% of outstanding loans.

Maple Finance’s disclosed Bitcoin addresses held 400 fewer BTC than the lender reported for its syrupUSDC stablecoin lending pool in a Nov. 18 snapshot, according to the blockchain data firm Bitquery. Bitquery counted 15,961 BTC at those addresses against the 16,361 BTC Maple reported through its public API. That accounted for 97.6% of the reported Bitcoin.

In its investigation, Bitquery estimated that the collateral it could account for across assets was worth about 152% of the roughly $847 million in syrupUSDC loans at that point. Bitquery reported that the disclosed holdings of Ether, wrapped Bitcoin and XRP matched Maple’s figures in the same check. Bitquery read loan balances from Maple’s smart contracts and checked the totals against the pools’ books. It calculated coverage against outstanding syrupUSDC loans alone.

Depositors receive syrupUSDC when they supply USDC. Their yield comes from loans to institutional borrowers, who pledge crypto as collateral that can be sold to recover money if they stop paying.

Bitquery’s Nov. 18, 2026 snapshot measured Bitcoin balances at disclosed addresses and estimated the value of all collateral it could count against syrupUSDC loans.

The 400 BTC gap concerns balances at disclosed addresses. The coverage estimate compares assets counted across the pool with its outstanding loans.

Bitquery took the reported amounts and wallet list from Maple’s public API on Nov. 18 and checked balances on Bitcoin, Ethereum and the XRP Ledger. Four of the 26 Bitcoin addresses it checked were empty. Bitquery said coins from three had moved to an address outside the published list. The published amounts and addresses came from Maple. The blockchain data gave Bitquery a separate record of the balances at those addresses.

Bitquery published the investigation on Nov. 19 and said balances could have changed between Maple’s update and its read. The report leaves open how much of the difference those transfers explain and who controls the receiving wallet.

Maple Finance’s syrupUSDC dashboard showed about 14,162 BTC on Nov. 21 and listed 35 collateral wallets across several assets. The later dashboard leaves the Nov. 18 gap unexplained and does not establish whether Maple added the receiving address.

Maple’s dashboard also links to a automated LedgerLens check by The Network Firm Tech & Services, which reported about $1.316 billion in syrupUSDC collateral at 01:00 UTC on Nov. 17. That check predates Bitquery’s snapshot. The provider says the service relies on Maple and third-party data, including custodians and on-chain records, and offers no audit opinion or assurance. The verification dashboard reports a dollar total for the pool at the time shown. It does not break that figure down by wallet.

Published addresses give depositors a way to check what the wallets held at a given time. Establishing who controls the coins and what Maple could sell if a borrower defaults requires evidence about custody and the loan terms.
