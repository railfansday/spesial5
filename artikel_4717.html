For indoor storage, outdoor protection, or a little extra coverage while wrenching in the garage, these are the car covers worth buying.

A car cover is one of the simplest ways to protect a vehicle’s finish, especially when it spends more time parked than on the road. Dust, moisture, UV exposure, bird droppings, and tree sap can all take a toll on paint, even when a car is tucked away in a garage.

For collectors and enthusiasts, that protection can be especially important for seasonal storage, when a classic or weekend toy might sit untouched for months. A well-chosen cover helps keep the finish clean and reduces the work needed to get a car ready for its next drive.

The right cover depends on where and how you plan to use it. A soft, breathable indoor cover is ideal for keeping dust off a collector car, while outdoor models need to stand up to rain, sunlight, and wind without trapping moisture against the paint. Some covers are tailored to specific vehicles for a closer fit, while universal options offer a more affordable solution. There are also temporary covers for garage projects that provide a barrier against dust, overspray, and other messes during detailing, repairs, or restoration work.

I rounded up the best car covers for different vehicles, storage conditions, and budgets, focusing on the features that matter most: material quality, fit, weather resistance, breathability, and ease of use.

💡 Expert Tip: Always start with a clean, dry car before putting on a cover. Dirt and grit trapped between the fabric and paint can act like sandpaper as the cover shifts in the wind, leaving fine scratches and swirl marks. Even the best cover can do more harm than good if it’s used on a dirty car or traps moisture underneath.

Before you purchase a car cover, consider what kind of car you’re trying to protect and where it’ll most likely be parked. Covering a collectible in a climate-controlled garage is a far cry from keeping the bird poop off your street-parked beater—and the cost of a car cover always reflects that. Here are some other things to think about.

The old adage “you get what you pay for” really applies to car covers. High-end premium options will cost at least a few hundred dollars—even over $1000 for something like the vaunted CarCapsule—but solutions can be found for under $100 too. It boils down to the type of protection you’re purchasing and how much your vehicle is worth.

A custom-fit car cover is essential if you’re trying to protect a valuable car. Most manufacturers offer fitted covers, so if you’re concerned about protecting an investment—or if you live in a climate where you experience a lot of wind or accumulating sand or snow—buy a car cover that securely fits your vehicle.

Universal car covers are fine for most applications. Don’t be disappointed if the cover blows away in a storm or creatures build a nest in your tailpipe.

Outdoor car covers should be weather-resistant, UV-resistant, and water-resistant. Indoor covers are typically made from softer materials to prevent dust accumulation and minor dings.

Ensure the cover is waterproof or water-resistant for outdoor use to protect against rain and snow. Even for indoor use, a quality cover should protect against things like humidity and garage-roof leaks.

A good cover should offer UV protection to shield your finish, dashboard, and interior from fading or cracking due to sun exposure.

Consider covers with thermal protection if you live in areas with extreme temperatures. Some covers are designed to reflect heat to keep the vehicle cooler.

Some car covers use elastic to seal around a vehicle. These will be the most affordable, but after a while, elastic stretches beyond its usefulness. Some covers use straps that tie or connect with fasteners such as buckles, snaps, and hook-and-loop strips. These covers cost more but should provide extra security, and they’ll last longer. Some feature weighted hems to seal around the base of your vehicle.

For outdoor use, look for covers with adjustable straps or buckles to secure the cover and prevent it from blowing away. To deter theft, some covers come with lock and cable systems.

A well-made car cover with a soft inner lining and a good fit should not scratch your paint. Ensure the cover is clean and free from debris before putting it on your car.

Many car covers are designed to accommodate accessories, but it’s important to choose a cover that fits your specific vehicle setup. Look for covers with adjustable straps or custom fits if you have modifications.

Custom-fit covers are tailored specifically to the dimensions of your vehicle for a snug fit. Universal-fit covers, on the other hand, are designed to fit a range of vehicle sizes and shapes, which might not offer as precise a fit.

Yes, a car cover can trap moisture. It can’t create rust by itself, but it can keep metal damp long enough for corrosion to take hold. Whether a car cover contributes to rust depends on whether it traps moisture against the car for a long time—especially if the car is covered wet, the cover isn’t breathable, or road salt and grime are left on the surface. To lessen the risk, cover a clean, dry car with a well-fitted, breathable cover, and let the cover dry before storing it.
