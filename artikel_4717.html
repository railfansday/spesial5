Becoming Aware of Being Unaware

Every decision and move you make in this moment is determined by all your past experiences up to this very second. It is how every living creature stays alive. Is the situation safe or dangerous? It is how we evolved, and the species that ignored the cues or didn’t adapt didn’t survive.

Humans have an additional layer of issues, in that language and consciousness require another level of awareness to survive and thrive. Many threats, such as shaky finances, verbal abuse, being bullied at school or work, authoritarianism, racism, and a host of other issues, are real threats. However, if you step back, many situations that are perceived as threats are actually cognitive distortions. Although the threats are not real, they can feel real and still trigger your nervous system and fight-or-flight response. Since we cannot escape our thoughts, they are especially damaging because the reaction is sustained and your body breaks down. It is well-documented that chronic stress causes illness and disease. 1,2

A couple of factors to consider: We are programmed almost from the day we are born to be a certain way, according to the opinions of our parents, educators, peers, colleagues, society, and the marketing world. Marketing is especially problematic because it benefits from making you feel bad about yourself. Marketing efforts are focused on offering solutions to help you feel better about yourself—for a price. Unfortunately, the solutions are only temporary, and your brain keeps telling you (with ongoing messaging) that you are still not good enough. Then, you project the world’s opinion of you onto yourself, and onto others. (As a quick aside: When you are critical of others, you might as well carry a bullhorn telling the world how you feel about yourself. That is just how consciousness works.)

Ego is at the core of human suffering. It is one of the deadliest concepts, and it is unique to our species. It involves endless judgments of “better than” or “less than.” It extends our survival instincts into the emotional world. What makes it worse is that much of our ego is based on cognitive distortions, and with repetition, they become embedded in our brain as “reality.” Neuroscience research on consciousness has shown that our thoughts and abstract thinking become embedded in our brain as concretely as a car or a table. Consider the concept of colors. My cat probably recognizes red from white but doesn’t have a name for it.

The nail in this coffin is that anxiety and anger are powerful, hard-wired, and unpleasant survival reactions and sensations that are generated when you are in a fight-or-flight response, and hundreds of thousands of times more powerful than your conscious brain. They are necessary, the gift of life, and not subject to rational interventions. We spend much of our lives trying to run from these sensations and take them personally. One outcome is that we label ourselves and others. Labels, positive or negative, completely block awareness.

The skill of developing a “working relationship with anxiety and anger is another discussion. This article’s message is that using a stronger ego (identity) to compensate is a mismatch: ineffective, endless, exhausting, and eventually destructive. Any time you are anxious or frustrated, you have blocked true awareness of the variables contributing to a given situation. Something in the present triggered a memory from the past that either was dangerous or perceived as such. By definition, you are no longer in the present and have lost awareness. You can only see the world through your eyes; it is about you. Your brain’s blood supply shifts away from the thinking centers to the lower survival centers, and your behavior may not be your best. This is why we say, “no action in a reaction.”

It is not possible. The best you can hope for is to cultivate and nurture it. Each person views the world through his or her “programming,” and it is his or her reality. So, learning when you are unaware is a necessary first step. Then a deeper awareness can emanate and evolve. Awareness will beget more awareness.

We are all programmed from birth to be certain ways by our parents, peers, educators, and society. These concepts become our version of reality, as concrete as your concepts of a house or a car. Our brains interpret essentially everything. That is the reason true awareness is impossible, as each person perceives their view of life as the correct one—whether it is positive or negative.

The necessary step to see situations more deeply and clearly is understanding the clues of being unaware. You cannot change the scenarios when you are unaware. It is beyond your control. So, creating “space” and watching your reactions lets you see the world through others’ eyes. Noticing when you are unaware begins a process that opens your thinking and is far more interesting than rewatching your own projections. It is also the first step in reprogramming your brain, in that you cannot create change unless you know where you are starting from.
