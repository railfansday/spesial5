Looking at Paul George’s torrid start shooting the 3

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

It's true that in the Boston Celtics' most recent defeat, a road loss to the Denver Nuggets, Paul George couldn't hit water if he fell out of a boat. Boston's second-leading scorer so far in the fledgling stages of the season misfired on all but one of his seven long-range tries, even throwing up an airball after a reload off an offensive board.

The 1-for-7 shooting night from deep was far from the only blemish on George's body of work Wednesday, too. He fouled out of a game for the first time as a Celtic, and foul trouble limited him to just 23 minutes played. But for as much as the nine-time All-Star had an off night, his cold shooting performance stands out as an outlier rather than a point of concern. After all, the numbers tell a story that suggests George's 3-point effectiveness so far in his maiden campaign in Boston has been almost unprecedented for a player of his age.

A fitting place to start when talking about George's long-range shooting chops this season is that he's spearheaded the Celtics' 3-point-heavy offensive attack. As has been the status quo under head coach Joe Mazzulla, the 3-point shot is once again a linchpin to Boston's modus operandi on that side of the ball, and no single Celtic has reaped the rewards of that style of play quite like George. On a team that employs plenty of elite 3-point marksmen such as Jayson Tatum, Payton Pritchard and Sam Hauser, it's impressively the veteran who paces the Celtics in 3s made per contest at 3.4.

The team-high 3.4 3s George is knocking down on a nightly basis is an especially impressive number when comparing that mark to players in their age-36 seasons. The list of players who have averaged three-plus converted triples per game at such a late stage in their NBA careers is microscopically small. Only Stephen Curry and James Harden have accomplished such a feat, and they're two of the greatest long-range snipers the game has ever seen. They're No. 1 and No. 2 on the league's all-time made 3s list, for reference.

Now, George's high-volume scoring from deep thus far would only be so valuable if the efficiency wasn't there, but he's blended volume and accuracy beautifully to this point. He's not only taking a lot of threes, but he's also finding nylon on a sizable chunk of those attempts. The California native has connected on well over 40 percent of his already 104 3s attempted — 42.3 percent to be exact, a career-high. Moreover, he ranks in the top ten among all qualified players in made 3s this season with 44, ahead of esteemed shooters such as Curry, Jalen Brunson, Donovan Mitchell, Tyrese Maxey and Kyrie Irving.

The fountain of youth is apparently located in Boston, as he is shooting the 3-ball at such a high level that Celtics fans have spent much of the season rubbing their eyes, wondering if they're watching the George of yesteryear who could go off for 30 on any given night. Highlighting his 3-point prowess alone might be unfair considering all the other things he's done well for the Celtics this season (defend off the ball, rebound, function as a secondary playmaker), but it's perhaps where he's made the biggest positive impact, and hopefully he can sustain the red-hot shooting.
