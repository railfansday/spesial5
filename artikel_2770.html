Aljamain Sterling: Alexander Volkanovski 'schools' Kevin Vallejos

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Aljamain Sterling thinks UFC featherweight champion Alexander Volkanovski is a bad stylistic matchup for Kevin Vallejos.

Sterling (26-6 MMA, 18-6 UFC) was edged out by Vallejos in Saturday's UFC Fight Night 294 co-headliner at ABHA Arena in Doha, Qatar. The former champion argued that he should have won the fight after landing more strikes overall.

Vallejos (19-1 MMA, 5-0 UFC) called out Volkanovski after the win, but Sterling doesn't think the Argentinian powerhouse would have anything to offer for the champion.

"I think Volk schools him, and I'm not saying that maliciously by any means," Sterling said on "The Ariel Helwani Show." "I just don't see what he brings in terms of a skill set that would be problematic for Volk – especially what he did in his last performance, the way he did with Diego Lopes two times in a row, against a bigger, harder-hitting guy with a super granite chin in Diego Lopes.

"You can make an argument that because he fights similarly to Topuria, maybe he can catch him, but with the shorter reach and everything, I just don't really see it. It's not being a hater, it's just looking at it and trying to be objective as possible. You barely squeak by me, who's not a striker. Volk is a much better striker than I am."

After finishing Movsar Evloev at UFC 333, Volkanovski (29-4 MMA, 16-3 UFC) expressed interest in moving up to lightweight and challenging Justin Gaethje. Sterling doesn't think Vallejos' win is enough to keep Volkanovski at featherweight.

"I think after watching that fight, Volk is probably going to campaign even harder for the Gaethje fight because it's probably a fight that entices him a bit more just based on the recent performances," Sterling said. "I mean, that's what I would do, and maybe Jean Silva fights Vallejos for the vacant title. I think that's probably what's going to happen. Again, if Vallejos gets the fight, congrats to him – make the most of it.

"I just think that's where I lean to. I don't have a dog in the race. I like Volkanovski as a person. Kevin seems like a nice guy. But if I'm breaking down the fight as an analyst, I would just lean towards the guy that I think has the more proven track records against bigger guys, smaller guys and has pretty much had his way with everyone he's fought."
