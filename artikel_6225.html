FAA Lifts Nationwide Ground Stop, But The Airline Meltdown Is Far From Over

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The FAA halted departures nationwide Friday afternoon, November 13, following a communications failure affecting air traffic control centers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced Friday evening that the system had reopened at 8:15 pm ET.

That is welcome news for everyone waiting to travel. It does not, unfortunately, put aircraft and crews back where they need to be…

During the evening press conference, Duffy attributed the disruption to aging air traffic control communications equipment that became overloaded with data.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said officials were also examining how older equipment interacts with newer technology. That, of course, is a preliminary explanation, not a completed technical investigation. We still await a detailed account of what triggered the failure and why the system could not absorb it without such an extraordinary interruption.

Duffy warned that restoring airline operations would take time. The government and airlines would try to move flights Friday night, with recovery efforts continuing Saturday.

Mullin offered a more reassuring assessment of the security question:

“We have seen zero evidence this is a cyber attack.”

There has also been speculation about the FAA’s newly introduced SMART traffic-management tool. Its developer, Air Space Intelligence, denied that its software or integration caused the ground stop.

I would wait for the technical findings before assigning blame to a particular product or contractor, though the timing is certainly suspicious.

By 8 pm ET, FlightAware showed more than 10,000 delays and 5,000 cancellations involving U.S. flights.

The operational problem is obvious: a flight that could not depart may leave another airport without the aircraft needed for its next departure. Crews can reach duty limits while waiting. Passengers miss connections, and the remaining seats fill quickly.

Lifting the restriction allows airlines to begin untangling that mess, but of course does not instantly resolve it. Expect continued disruption tonight and check Saturday flights carefully too…this could go well into the busy Thanksgiving travel week.

The Department of Transportation explains that passengers are entitled to a refund when an airline cancels a flight and they decline the alternatives offered, regardless of the reason for cancellation. Qualifying significant delays also trigger refund rights when passengers choose not to travel.

Among the qualifying changes are scheduled arrival delays of at least three hours on domestic itineraries or six hours on international itineraries. You do not have to accept a voucher instead of a refund when a refund is owed.

That refund right is separate from whether the airline must pay for your hotel or dinner.

The FAA just froze every departure in the US over a communications failure at air traffic control centers. Here is what we know and what to do right now.

This is the kind of alert nobody in travel wants to see on a Friday afternoon. The FAA has ordered a nationwide ground stop for every airport in the country, effective 3:54 p.m. Eastern, according to the Air Traffic Control System Command Center as reported by Gizmodo.

The notice to operators is blunt. It cites “extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety” and says “All departing traffic should remain ground stopped until further notice,” per WCVB. Inbound international flights will be managed on a case-by-case basis.

Aircraft already in the air are not being ordered down immediately and can continue to their destinations, according to Reuters reporting cited by IBTimes. The hold applies to departures.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy put the cause in plain terms on X: a “communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers.” He added that “FAA technicians are investigating the issue.” Those centers handle high-altitude, en route traffic across the country, so a problem there is not a local hiccup. It touches nearly every flight plan in the system.

There is one wrinkle worth flagging. An FAA spokesperson described the problem as affecting “multiple” centers, while Duffy said “all,” per Gizmodo. Just before the national order, both Chicago airports were placed under a ground stop for an “equipment outage,” according to NBC Chicago. The government has not provided further detail, and as of this writing (5:12pm EST) nobody official has said whether a cyber event has been ruled in or out. I would not speculate in either direction until the FAA does.

The timing is awkward. Congress approved $12.5 billion in 2025 to modernize air traffic control communications, and the FAA and White House have asked for another $30.1 billion, as IBTimes notes. That money has not been applied to a solution yet so it’s unclear as to whether a future system would be able to avoid such a critical infrastructure system.

Extremely. This is the first nationwide ground stop since January 2023, according to the AP via ABC7. Reuters counts it as only the second nationwide halt of departures since September 11, 2001.

That 2023 event came from the failure of the NOTAM system, which pilots rely on for safety notices. The FAA lifted that order just before 9 a.m. Eastern, but the damage was done: more than 3,700 flights were delayed and more than 640 canceled, per the AP report at the time. That stop hit on a Wednesday morning. This one hit a Friday afternoon heading into a weekend, with aircraft and crews about to be stranded out of position. Even a short pause will ripple into Saturday.

For what it’s worth, SpaceX just started its launch channel ahead of a 5:45pm EST planned launch in which it will “double catch” both the booster and Starship. Space launches likely have its own windows but cross shared airspace.

If you are flying tonight, do not head to the airport on faith. Check your airline app first. Airports themselves are deferring to carriers, with Boston Logan telling passengers to “check with their airlines for the latest flight updates,” per WCVB. The Port Authority of New York and New Jersey could not even say whether planes were leaving JFK, according to Gizmodo. However, this amount of departures will take a long time to untangle and you’re unlikely to get out this evening.

Rebook if you can, and do it right now. The first people to grab the remaining seats on tomorrow morning’s flights are the ones who get out. If you have a cruise departure or a nonrefundable hotel tied to tonight’s flight, call the supplier now rather than after midnight. From the agency side, this is exactly when a travel advisor earns their keep, juggling the hotel, the ship, and the airline while you wait in line.

Keep your receipts for meals and hotels too. Airline policies on what they cover vary, and having the paperwork makes any claim easier. It’s unlikely the airlines will cover for an event out of their control but credit cards used to purchase this travel might.

A nationwide ground stop driven by a communications failure at the centers that run en route traffic is about as serious as an operational problem gets. The FAA has offered no time for departures to resume, and even when they do, Friday night’s schedule is effectively gone and Saturday will be messy. The bigger story will come after the planes are moving again: what failed, why there was no backup to keep traffic flowing, and whether the billions already earmarked for modernization are arriving fast enough.
