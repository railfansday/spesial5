JetBlue is Launching a New Domestic First Class—and We Got a First Look

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Flying domestic with JetBlue just got an upgrade. On Wednesday, November 11, the airline introduced BlueFirst, a brand-new domestic first class product, ahead of its debut flight on December 8. The cabin is a dedicated section in the first three rows, arranged two-by-two (read: no middle seats). It will be installed across the airline's fleet on planes that do not already have a Mint business class cabin, bringing a domestic first class to the shorter routes for which JetBlue is more commonly known. As Stephanie Evans Greene, the airline’s senior vice president of marketing and brand, tells Condé Nast Traveler, the idea is "premium, but not pretentious."

BlueFirst will begin rolling out across JetBlue’s fleet in early December, beginning with the carrier’s daily Boston–Fort Lauderdale flight and soon followed by its popular Fort Lauderdale–New York LaGuardia route.

The new cabin is a big shift for JetBlue, an airline that for most of its 26 years in business flew economy seats only. The one exception is Mint, a lie-flat business class available on the airline's transcontinental and transatlantic routes, like New York to Los Angeles and London. To be clear: BlueFirst is not a version of Mint, and the two will never appear on the same plane. Mint planes keep Mint, and BlueFirst goes on every other aircraft. Where Mint is a bed, BlueFirst is a recliner, built for the shorter leisure routes that make up most of JetBlue's network.

To test out the BlueFirst product, we checked out the first aircraft with the new cabins installed on the ground at New York—JFK. Here's everything travelers should know before booking.

The first three rows of the plane will be dedicated to BlueFirst, with 12 seats in total.

As for the seat itself, the first thing that will stand out to travelers is just how much more space there is. BlueFirst has only three rows—12 seats total—and each seat is about three inches wider than a standard economy seat, with room to spare in every direction. The seat is spacious enough to hold a passenger and a laptop bag next to them (or a large purse, in my case), and the space in front is just as generous, with up to seven extra inches of legroom compared with JetBlue's regular Main seats. It reclines five inches too, though the recline takes a bit of pressure (hold the button and push back on the seat firmly). Press the button again, and the seat pops right back up. In true JetBlue fashion—this is, after all, the airline that built its name on being the un-stuffy one—there's no curtain cutting the BlueFirst cabin off from the rest of the plane.

Mattress company Tuft & Needle designed the seat cushion using T&N Flex foam.

The seat cushion is made with Tuft & Needle's T&N Flex foam (yes, the mattress company), developed just for JetBlue. It's squishy enough to comfortably sink into, and it responds as you shift, in both the bottom cushion and the backrest. The adjustable headrest also has wings that fold in to cradle your head. "I'll go on record and say I think they're some of the most comfortable recline seats that are out there in domestic first class," Greene says.

BlueFirst passengers receive a blanket and an amenity kit with socks, earbuds, mouthwash, and OSEA Malibu skincare products.

Aside from the bigger, more comfortable seat, BlueFirst gives travelers a slew of other perks. At every seat, there's a 13.3-inch screen with Bluetooth for personal earbuds, USB-A, USB-C, and a regular outlet, plus JetBlue's free Wi-Fi. The overhead bins above the cabin are reserved for BlueFirst passengers, so boarding doesn't come with the usual scramble for space, and each large bifold tray table has a stand for your phone or tablet. Travelers also get a blanket and an amenity kit with socks, earbuds, mouthwash, and OSEA Malibu skincare products, including a lip balm, hand cream, and the fan-favorite calming pulse point oil.

What's more, the perks start before passengers even get on the plane: BlueFirst comes with priority check-in, a faster security lane at more than 30 airports, and Group 1 boarding. Most BlueFirst fares come with two free checked bags—even BlueFirst Base, the cheapest of the three, includes one checked bag.

The dining options in BlueFirst depend on how long your flight is. A bento-inspired box is served on flights of 899 miles or more, with an entrée, a side, and a dessert. The exact menu changes based on the route. Dinner might be a tortellini pasta salad with marinated green olives, a pastrami sandwich with a citrusy cabbage salad, or sesame noodles with crispy wontons and a Szechuan dip. The desserts are a highlight: the pasta comes with a carrot cake with a caramel icing top that's deliciously moist (my personal favorite), and there are colorful macarons and a pistachio chocolate cake on other menus. Breakfast runs from hazelnut-ricotta crêpes to a sweet potato and bean bowl with guacamole and hot sauce, a poached pear, and blueberry chia pudding. There are also kosher, vegan, and gluten-free versions of the boxes.

Drinks in this section of the cabin deserve their own mention, because BlueFirst's come with a twist you won't find on other US airlines. Everything is ordered right from the seatback screen in Mixologist Mode, which lets you build your own cocktail (or mocktail) instead of choosing from a list. You start with a base—a spirit, a soft drink, coffee, a mixer—and add up to four items in total from the full drink menu. The crew then brings the ingredients right to your separate pop-out cocktail tray, and you mix it yourself. There's a limit of two drinks per order, but once the first round lands, you can start the next. The drinks come in real glassware from Fable, on handmade watercolor coasters by New York-based illustrator Danielle Rose Fisher. For anyone not playing bartender, there's also wine from Parcelle, coffee from Cometeer and Joe Coffee, and Smith Teamaker teas.

The new cabin will first roll out on JetBlue flights from Fort Lauderdale to and New York LaGuardia and Boston.

The retrofit should be nearly done by the end of 2027, covering every JetBlue plane that doesn't have Mint.

BlueFirst tickets are currently on sale for travel dates beginning in December. The cabins will be added to more than 30 routes throughout 2026; you'll start seeing the new seats on flights from JFK to Cancún, Liberia, and San Juan beginning in January. However, even on those inaugural routes, not every flight will have BlueFirst right away (it depends on whether a fitted plane is operating that flight). Just over a dozen planes will have the cabin by the end of this year, with the rest of the fleet following through 2027.

By the end of next year, the retrofit should be nearly done, covering every JetBlue plane that doesn't have Mint. Until then, it's worth double-checking before booking since JetBlue notes BlueFirst may only be available on select flights or dates, and planes can be swapped at a moment’s notice depending on equipment needs and maintenance.
