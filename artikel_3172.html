Medical students should take paper exams, OpenEvidence CEO tells Semafor

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Daniel Nadler, whose AI tool sums up research for doctors, warned that leaning on AI risks weakening core skills.

Medical students should sit paper exams and master the material themselves, even if they also use AI, OpenEvidence chief executive Daniel Nadler said on Monday.

Nadler spoke at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World event, in an interview with Semafor’s Mohammed Sergie, Semafor reported.

“The risk of cognitive offloading is really, really acute,” Nadler told Semafor.

Cognitive offloading means handing mental work over to a tool. Nadler said the risk is greatest for young people who have studied with AI from the start.

Students should “learn the hard way, do paper exams,” he told Sergie.

OpenEvidence builds AI that sums up medical research for doctors. Semafor put the startup’s value at $15bn. Nadler also said all of the company’s engineers use many AI assistants.

Some research supports the concern. A 2025 Lancet study found that skilled doctors spotted fewer precancerous growths without AI after months of using it. Their unaided detection rate fell from 28.4% to 22.4%.

Separately, an MIT Media Lab study of 54 people found that those who wrote essays with ChatGPT showed the weakest brain connectivity. Both studies were small.

Benjamine Liu, chief executive of Formation Bio, also spoke at the event. His company uses AI to speed up drug development.

“AI systems can begin to now do the work of entire teams,” Liu told Semafor, speaking about the drug industry.

In September, OpenEvidence and Anthropic said they would offer free medical AI to doctors in about 100 countries.
