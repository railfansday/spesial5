Coca-Cola vs. PepsiCo: Pepsi's Revenue Dwarfs Coke's, but This Is the Number to Watch

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

PepsiCo's revenue runs nearly double Coca-Cola's, but Coca-Cola's steadier quarterly progression may signal stronger pricing power and operational discipline.

Coca-Cola (KO -1.07%) earns its revenue by selling nonalcoholic soft drinks, concentrates, and syrups to independent bottling partners.

During the most recent period, it concluded an operational review to formally select new agency partners tasked with managing its data and marketing operations, and it partnered with hospitality resorts to provide branded consumer experiences. A law firm also announced a formal legal investigation into a reported data breach.

PepsiCo (PEP -2.24%) earns its revenue by creating and supplying a global catalog of concentrated beverage syrups and packaged snack foods.

During the same time frame, it reached an 11-year operational agreement to formally extend its status as the exclusive beverage provider for Taco Bell, and it elected a new independent director to its board while simultaneously closing multiple regional manufacturing and warehouse facilities, resulting in localized workforce reductions.

Coca-Cola stock has returned more than 40% in 2026 as it continues to report strong business fundamentals. Renewed investor focus on consumer staples has also boosted the stock, though the same can't be said of PepsiCo, which is essentially flat year to date.

The graph above clearly illustrates Pepsi's larger absolute revenue, but that alone hasn't been enough to juice the stock this year. In its latest earnings release on Oct. 8, the company cut its 2026 core EPS outlook to 1% to 2% growth as North American beverage volumes fell modestly in the quarter. A week later, KFC confirmed it was switching from providing Pepsi to Coke products in its more than 3,700 U.S. restaurants by spring 2027. According to BofA, the account is worth about $500 million in annual sales. But beyond the roughly 3% hit to Pepsi's sales, it raises the possibility of other large accounts following suit. Earlier this year, Marriott announced it was replacing PepsiCo with Coca-Cola as its exclusive global beverage provider.

Those large accounts can move the revenue needle, but investors should also pay attention to operating margin, which measures the percentage of revenue that remains as profit after paying for the cost of goods sold (COGS) and regular operating expenses. Coca-Cola's operating margin was 33.4% in Q3, up from 32% last year. In contrast, PepsiCo's Q3 operating margin was 16.9%, an improvement over last year's 14.9%.

That difference is because Coca-Cola's business model is much more asset-light than PepsiCo's. It primarily makes and sells syrups and drink concentrates to independent bottling partners, avoiding the capital investment involved with things like building and managing factories and maintaining delivery fleets. That business model has served Coke well for years, and should allow the company to navigate macroeconomic pressures more easily.
