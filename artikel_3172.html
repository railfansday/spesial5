Teradyne: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Memory supply constraints are forcing chipmakers to increase test volume per wafer, a potential tailwind for Teradyne's core business.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 20, 2026 at 4:30PM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 28, 2026, Teradyne (TER -9.96%) announced total company revenue reached $1.3 billion as artificial intelligence demand expanded across all business lines. For long-term investors, the expansion of automated test equipment (custom hardware used to verify semiconductor chips) into data center infrastructure creates a multiyear growth runway.

Hyperscalers and chipmakers adopt dual vendor strategies (buying test equipment from two suppliers) to secure capacity and avoid supply chain delays. That shift gives Teradyne a direct path to capture market share in high performance compute chips where rivals previously held dominant positions.

Severe memory supply constraints force chipmakers to use lower stack heights in high bandwidth memory (specialized vertical memory stacks for fast data processing). Lower stack heights require more total stacks to process the same wafer volume, directly increasing the volume of performance tests Teradyne conducts.

As data center connections shift toward optical networking, handling and alignment requirements remain in flux. Teradyne forms open, nonexclusive partnerships with specialized equipment vendors to build complete test cells. This approach speeds high-volume production for next-generation data center architectures.

Quarterly sales holding above $1.2 billion reflect a structural step change in operational scale. Teradyne expands manufacturing capacity and dual-sources component suppliers to meet artificial intelligence infrastructure demand.

Heavy research investments and open partner ecosystems protect technical leadership across test and robotics segments. Management watches customer qualification pacing and supply lead times as semiconductor capital spending expands through 2028.
