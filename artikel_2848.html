3-man Fastbreak: Navigating without Duren

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Sitting at 8-4, Detroit finds itself right under Boston and Philadelphia at the top of the conference – having already knocked off both squads. What makes that start even more impressive is how much of it has come with Jalen Duren sidelined by a hamstring issue.

It shouldn't come as a shock to see the Pistons maintaining their place among the Eastern Conference elite, but the bigger story might be how long it takes the rest of the field to gel. Contenders like Indiana, Orlando, and Toronto entered the campaign with big ambitions, yet all three are currently stumbling below .500.

Here are a few key takeaways from this recent run of action.

Fresh off a impressive 60-win campaign, Detroit has quieted critics who viewed their summer moves with skepticism. Retaining their core talent with long-term deals has paid immediate dividends, as their roster continuity continues to fuel early momentum.

While the Pistons have routinely handled lesser opponents, their battles against heavyweights like New York, Philadelphia, and Los Angeles revealed familiar flaws. During offensive droughts, the ball movement stalled, resulting in over-reliance on Cade Cunningham to create individual magic while the surrounding unit remained static.

Is it a real cause for concern yet considering they've been down their second best player? Not in the short term.

This foundational identity guarantees postseason basketball and likely home-court advantage, yet doubts regarding their ceiling remain. Rumors of offseason trade pursuits suggest the front office recognizes the missing piece, but until a major acquisition materializes, lingering questions about their championship credentials will remain.

Detroit's identity remains largely unchanged from the past two seasons, but the offense is clearly evolving, especially without their All-NBA big man.

Last season, Detroit led the league with 57.9 points in the paint per game. This year, that figure has fallen to 47.2, around the middle of the pack. Duren's absence since the second game has certainly played a role, but the shift also reflects the team's offseason moves. Detroit moved on from Isaiah Stewart and Tobias Harris while adding better floor spacers in John Collins and Isaiah Jones. Duncan Robinson has also taken on a larger role, posting career highs with 17.4 points per game while shooting 43.1% from three.

That improved spacing has helped fuel Detroit's early success, but defense remains the team's foundation, with Detroit still leading the league in several defensive categories. Once Duren returns, it will be worth watching whether the offensive approach changes.

In the three games since Jenkins replaced Paul Reed in the starting lineup on November 8, Jenkins averaged 24.7 points, 5.7 rebounds, 4.0 assists, 2.3 steals and 4.3 three-pointers per game. The undrafted guard made occasional starts last season, but only when Cunningham was injured or resting. This year, the temporary promotion while Duren remains out shows that J.B. Bickerstaff trusts Jenkins with a larger role.

Since emerging in Detroit last season, Jenkins has gone from a promising two-way player to a legitimate contributor on a contending team. Jenkins has also shown a willingness to embrace any role and play with maximum effort. The question is no longer whether Jenkins can be a reliable role player, but how large that role can become as the stakes rise.

At 25, Jenkins still has room to grow. He is shooting 42.4% from three this season on 4.9 attempts per game and is averaging fewer turnovers than last year despite playing eight more minutes per night.

That chip on the shoulder may be Jenkins' greatest strength, and continued growth could be an underrated factor in how far the Pistons go this season.
