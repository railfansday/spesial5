“Buy now, pay later” can help stretch your holiday budget further, but pick your BNPL app carefully and avoid loan stacking.

Head of Content, Personal & Student Loans

As Americans compile their shopping lists ahead of the holiday season, I’ve been compiling my own kind of list — a list of 25 data points, gathered across six “buy now, pay later” (BNPL) apps, fed into a rubric of five categories and 22 subcategories, to produce a star rating for each app.

It’s a big list. I check it twice (or 10 times).

By the end, I know more about these apps than anyone should, and I’m eager to bestow my knowledge on the next unsuspecting person who utters “Klarna” in my vicinity.

I should have plenty of opportunities this year. Nearly 2 in 5 holiday spenders (38%) plan to use BNPL, according to NerdWallet’s holiday spending report. Concerningly, the same report found that a higher share of BNPL users expect they won’t be able to make their payments in full and on time compared to credit card users.

Using BNPL for holiday shopping isn’t inherently bad, but there are risks, including sneaky fees and the ease of loan stacking across many providers.

Here’s how to minimize those risks and set yourself up for success this holiday season.

Brenton Harrison, a certified financial planner in Nashville, says many of his clients are turning to buy now, pay later in the “messy middle of life,” when everyday expenses are high and budgets are tight. This is especially true during the holidays.

“They have kids they're providing for, or they’re trying to go travel and see family,” Harrison says. “When those expenses pile up, it can be very attractive to be able to spread out payments.”

Since BNPL divides your total purchase into smaller installments, typically due every two weeks, it feels easier on your cash flow than having to pay all at once.

While Harrison is open to his clients using BNPL, he emphasizes how important it is to understand the terms before opting in. BNPL apps only require a soft credit pull and a short application. When you can apply for a loan in just a few clicks, it’s easy to miss when payments are due or whether they’ll include interest.

But if clients understand the terms and are certain they can make the payments, Harrison says BNPL's set repayment schedule can be a better option than more open-ended financing.

“With a credit card, [a holiday purchase] might be something that they buy over and over and over again, because that interest just continues to accrue over time, and they never pay it off,” he says.

Not all BNPL apps are created equal. I measure the top apps across multiple categories, but I weigh affordability the highest. My thinking is if you’re already strapped for cash, it’s a bad idea to pay extra just to break up the payments.

There are providers, like Affirm and PayPal, that don’t charge interest with a common pay-in-four term (your total purchase, divided into four installments). They also charge no fees, including no late fees for missed payments.

Compare that to apps, like Sezzle and Zip, that may charge an automatic service or origination fee with your first installment, as well as late fees.

Long-term BNPL financing, which can stretch months or even years, usually includes interest, expressed as an annual percentage rate or APR. This is a more expensive payment option than a zero-interest pay-in-four.

Pay close attention to your checkout screen to see what you’re offered. Are the installment amounts equal or is the first installment higher than the others? That may mean there’s a fee. Do you see an APR listed? That means you’re paying interest.

Harrison points out that just because a store partners with a BNPL company doesn’t mean that’s your only option. Download a different BNPL app, search for the store and see if you get access to better terms, like a 0% offer.

Taking out multiple loans with different BNPL providers is known as “loan stacking,” and it’s risky.

Because you’re juggling different payment amounts due on different days, it’s hard to keep track of what’s coming out of your bank account. This is especially true if you set up automatic payments, which most BNPL apps require. If you overdraw your checking account, your bank will likely charge a fee.

According to research published in August by the Federal Reserve, 11% percent of BNPL users had a BNPL payment trigger an overdraft or nonsufficient funds fee from their bank in the prior year. People with less savings were even more likely to pay fees.

Before applying for multiple BNPL loans, look at a typical month of expenses and ask if there’s extra room in your budget for those payments, Harrison says.

“If you’re just assuming that because you’re spreading it out that you’ll be able to pay it, that's where I think people can get in trouble,” he says. “They set it and forget it, and they don't realize, ‘Oh, I am in no better position to pay that next month than I am this month, or I have three of these going at the same time, and collectively, it's more than I can manage.’”

Most apps send payment reminders, so you can potentially reschedule the payment or change your payment method.

Still, it’s best to avoid the headache to begin with. If you’re feeling pressured to spend more this holiday season, communicate your limits clearly with loved ones, Harrison says, and avoid any loans you can’t comfortably repay.

“Think about the purchase itself, especially around the holidays,” he says. “There's a big difference between something that I can pay off over three months and something that I should have never been buying in the first place.”
