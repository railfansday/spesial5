Is Sandisk (SNDK) Stock a Buy After Its 8-for-1 Split?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The split is a big one -- but it's not quite as meaningful as it seems.

On Oct. 29, Sandisk (SNDK -5.59%) announced that it would be splitting its stock 8-for-1. Whoever owned shares as of Nov. 9 will see their number of shares owned increase dramatically effective Nov. 19. Shares were recently trading around $885 apiece, pre-split.

If Sandisk has been on your radar, you might be wondering whether the stock will be a buy post-split. Here's my answer: Shares of Sandisk will be just as attractive (or not attractive) post-split as they were pre-split.

Here's a little stock-split math: Let's say that you own 10 shares pre-split, and that they're trading at $900 apiece just before the split. So pre-split, your shares are worth $9,000 (that's 10 times $900). Post-split, you'll have 80 shares (woo-hoo!) -- but at the same time that your number of shares is multiplied by 8, the share price is divided by 8. So post-split, you'll own 80 shares valued at $112.50 apiece (that's $900 divided by 8). Multiply your 80 shares by the new price of $112.50, and your stake's total value is... $9,000. See? Nothing really changed very much.

The company's revenue isn't increasing eight-fold. Its debt isn't shrinking by a factor of eight. Its business is pretty much the same, pre- and post-split.

Stock splits are mostly accounting events. They can make a stock seem cheaper, but remember that a $900 stock can be an undervalued bargain and a $9 stock can be an overvalued proposition, on its way to becoming a $2 stock.
