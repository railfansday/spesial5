Buy Wyndham Rewards Points With Best-Ever 100% Bonus (0.65 Cents Each): Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Wyndham Rewards has launched its latest promotion on purchased points, which is potentially in line with the best-ever offers that we’ve seen from the program. This could be a great value for unlocking discounted award stays, as the program is offering up to a 100% bonus, which is an opportunity to acquire points for 0.65 cents each.

Link: Buy Wyndham Rewards points with a bonus

Between 12:01AM ET on November 11 and 11:59PM on December 31, 2026, Wyndham Rewards is offering a bonus on purchased points. The best offer appears to be for up to a 100% bonus, though you’ll have to log into your account to see what you’re eligible for.

With this offer, you can unlock the highest bonus if you purchase at least 3,000 points in one transaction. If you purchase 1,000 or 2,000 points, you don’t receive any bonus. If you purchase points, they’ll post to your account within 72 hours, and purchases are non-refundable. Purchased points don’t count toward Wyndham Rewards elite status.

Ordinarily you can buy Wyndham Rewards points for 1.3 cents each, before any discounts or bonuses, and that includes all taxes and fees.

If you maxed out the promotion and were eligible for a 100% bonus, you could purchase 400,000 points (including 200,000 bonus points) for $2,600, which is a rate of 0.65 cents per point.

This is the best bonus offer I ever recall seeing from Wyndham Rewards, so if there has ever been a time to buy points, this is it.

Wyndham Rewards ordinarily lets members purchase up to 100,000 points per account per calendar year, before any bonuses. However, during this promotion, that cap has been doubled, to 200,000 points.

When Wyndham offers a promotion on purchased points, it’s most commonly an 80-100% bonus. So this 100% bonus is at the very top end of that scale. However, personally I’d decide on whether to make a purchase more based on if I had an immediate use in mind, rather than the exact cost per point.

More generally, there are definitely some properties where there’s value in outright buying points for the purposes of a redemption. Let me actually give a direct example, as I recently purchased some Wyndham points for a hotel stay, rather than booking with cash. I needed to be in Colorado Springs, and I booked The Mining Exchange Hotel, part of Wyndham Registry Collection (the hotel even has a Michelin key!)

The cash rate for a base room at the hotel would’ve been $307.57, inclusive of taxes & fees.

Meanwhile the hotel was charging just 30,000 points. At a rate of 0.65 cents per point, that’s like paying $195 for the stay, inclusive of taxes & fees. Not bad, eh?

Well, it gets better than that. Wyndham Rewards often lets you book premium rooms at no extra cost when redeeming points, so I was able to book a club room for the same 30,000 points.

If paying cash, that would’ve cost $438.54, inclusive of taxes & fees.

Now, the club rooms absolutely aren’t worth it, since you just get access to a pantry with some packaged snacks. But hey, if the cost is the same, I’ll take it. 😉

Wyndham Rewards has more than 9,000 hotels across 20 brands in 80+ countries. Some of the most popular Wyndham Rewards brands include Registry Collection, Wyndham Grand, Dolce, Wyndham, La Quinta, Wingate, Wyndham Garden, Ramada, Days Inn, Super 8, Howard Johnson, and Travelodge.

Everyone will value points differently. Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Wyndham Rewards points to be worth 0.6 cents each. Admittedly that’s a very conservative valuation, and there should be opportunities to get way more value than that.

Wyndham Rewards partners with a variety of transferable points currencies, including Bilt Rewards, Capital One, Chase Ultimate Rewards, and Citi ThankYou. The catch is that transfers are at a 1:1 ratio, so I wouldn’t at all consider that to be a good redemption value.

That’s because you could be getting way more value from your transferable points than moving them to a program where points can be purchased for well under a penny each.

Wyndham Rewards also has a portfolio of credit cards issued in the United States, which are potentially quite lucrative if you frequent the brand.

Wyndham Rewards is offering up to a 100% bonus on purchased points, which is an opportunity to buy points for as little as 0.65 cents each. While I most definitely wouldn’t speculatively buy Wyndham Rewards points at this cost, there are some situations where the math works out, so everyone should crunch the numbers for themselves.
