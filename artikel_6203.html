I'd heard rumblings about CardPointers for years before I finally started using it early last year. I've found it to be a very useful tool for auto-enrolling me in credit offers and helping me track my savings over time. It also helps me keep track of my various credit card perks (I have some 27 credit cards, so this is vital).

CardPointers is an app for both iOS and Android phones and a browser extension that automatically adds offers across your credit cards, including American Express, Chase and Citi cards.

It has everything from location-based reminders for nearby offers to alerts that tell you the best credit card to use for each store. It can also flag when a statement credit is available for a particular brand.

Best of all, it auto-enrolls my credit cards in all offers, so I never have to go hunting for specific credit card offers. In fact, I just shop normally and consider it a special occasion when a surprise statement credit hits my account.

I have all my Citi cards linked to CardPointers. Whenever I open up the Citi website on my computer, CardPointers automatically enrolls all my cards with any new offers. As you can see from this screenshot, CardPointers worked in the background to add 841 new merchant offers to my eligible cards.

A CardPointers extension sits in my browser and keeps a current tally of all the offers I'm enrolled in, in alphabetical order.

Just one example: I got a $5 statement credit for buying a $10 dinner at Burger King back in September. That was an offer I'd been auto-enrolled in by Cardpointers.

Here's another surprise credit I got on my American Express account after a stay at the Grand Hyatt Resort & Spa in Kauai, Hawaii, earlier this year.

And in another more recent example, I was looking to buy an Oura ring (currently on sale for Black Friday), and it showed me I had a $200 credit available that I hadn't used yet.

I'd conservatively say that Cardpointers has saved me several hundred dollars this year.

It's fairly straightforward to use CardPointers, but you'll need to input some data along the way. To start, download the mobile app, then you'll be prompted to create an account. Once you've been verified via email, you'll be able to input your credit card data. You won't need to input credit card numbers, but CardPointers will ask you for the card name and the date you opened it (to better track available offers).

You'll be prompted to input your cards. It's pretty straightforward, though a bit time-consuming.

I've only scratched the surface of everything CardPointers can do, but after using it for nearly two years, I'm a convert. With some 27 credit cards in my wallet, keeping track of all the available offers, statement credits and other benefits can feel like a full-time job. CardPointers takes much of that work off my plate.

What I love most is that I don't have to spend hours scrolling through my credit card accounts looking for offers to activate. CardPointers does the heavy lifting for me, automatically adding eligible offers and helping me take advantage of savings I might otherwise miss. There's nothing quite like getting a surprise statement credit for a purchase you were going to make anyway.

I've conservatively saved several hundred dollars this year alone, including credits on everything from fast food to luxury hotel stays. And it's not just about the money. The time I've saved and the peace of mind that I'm not leaving valuable credit card benefits on the table make the service even more worthwhile.

While the free version is a good place to start, I think the premium CardPointers+ membership is well worth the $100 annual price, particularly for anyone juggling multiple rewards credit cards. And with the current 50% off promotion, it's an even more compelling deal. In fact, I like the service so much that I'm planning to upgrade to a lifetime membership.

If you're serious about maximizing your credit card rewards, benefits and statement credits, CardPointers is one of those tools that can quickly pay for itself. It's become an essential part of my credit card strategy, and I can't imagine managing my growing collection of cards without it.
