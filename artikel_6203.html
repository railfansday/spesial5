American First Class Will Get Mini-Meals On Shorter Flights (700-899 Miles)

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

American Airlines is making a very nice change to first class service on some short haul flights that didn’t previously qualify for a meal service, and it’s something I’m sure passengers will be happy to see.

As things currently stand, American serves meals in first class on flights of at least 900 miles during meal times (900 miles roughly correlates to a little over two hours of flying, on average). In the US, this is basically the industry standard, as Delta and United have the same cutoff of 900 miles for serving meals. On flights shorter than that, airlines only offer drinks and a snack basket in first class.

It looks like American will soon be leading the industry when it comes to service in first class on shorter flights, as it brings back a program it used to offer. JonNYC was first to report that American will be bringing back the “light bites” concept on flights of 700 to 899 miles (700 miles correlates to a little over 90 minutes of flying, on average).

AA:"Light bites”/mini-meals coming back to short haul domestic first class flights 700-899 milesStarting on NYC/ORD.

As now confirmed by American, this is launching this month on flights between Chicago (ORD) and New York, including Kennedy (JFK) and LaGuardia (LGA). It remains to be seen when it expands to other routes. It must be a decade at this point since American offered this concept.

The expectation is that there will be a rotating selection of options, with two available on each flight, and they won’t be available via pre-order.

Breakfast selections may include smoked salmon, tropical chia overnight oats, prosciutto and melon, fruit and cheese plates, prosciutto and brie, or apple cinnamon chia pudding. Lunch and dinner options may include curry chicken salads, Thai noodle salads, turkey and gouda sandwiches, whipped burrata, poached shrimp with noodle salads, mezze plates, blackened chicken, or grilled vegetable wraps.

It goes without saying that expectations of premium cabin travelers differ massively around the globe. In the Middle East, it’s common to have a 30-minute business class flight with a lie flat bed, cappuccinos, champagne, and a substantial, tasty snack.

In Europe, you’re typically stuck with a pretty underwhelming hard product on short haul flights (just economy with a blocked middle), but regardless of the length of the flight, you’re almost always served something fresh, even if the flight time is well under an hour.

In the United States, we have the benefit of actually having a dedicated first class cabin with larger seats, but the soft product just isn’t great. You can be on a flight of nearly two hours and just be served packaged snacks.

We do have to give credit to Alaska for this, as it’s the only US carrier to serve some sort of fresh food on flights of under 900 miles. On most Alaska flights you’ll get a snack on shorter services, so kudos to the airline for that.

American Airlines will once again serve fresh food in first class on flights of 700-899 miles. This concept will debut on flights between Chicago and New York, and passengers will have the choice of two rotating options. This is really a resumption of something that American offered many years back.

Among the “big three” US carriers, American will be the only one to serve fresh food in first class on flights of under 900 miles, so kudos to the airline for that.
