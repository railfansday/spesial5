Top Gun, tears, thrills and the future script: Cardinale the leading actor in rollercoaster visit

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

When he touched down in Italy before the Udinese game, not even Gerry Cardinale himself will have foreseen the whirlwind week that followed.

The visit of the Milan owner was known about ahead of time, as were his motivations. Cardinale not only wanted to demonstrate his closeness to the team in a very important moment, but he also had a lot of off-field issues to tackle too.

He would end up staying in the country for one week, something that has not been common. This is a different version of the RedBird Capital founder that we are seeing though, one that seems more hands-on than ever when it comes to steering the ship.

Or rather, in this case, it was a bit of a rollercoaster that he was at the front of. It started with a climb, continued with a steep drop, and then came the ascent which ultimately led into take-off on his flight back to America.

Things started quite well for Cardinale in his latest visit, as Milan went away to Udinese and won 2-0 thanks to goals from Christian Pulisic and Omari Hutchinson, bouncing back well from the defeat in Bournemouth a few nights prior.

Attention then shifted to the midweek meeting with a resurgent Bologna side at San Siro, and things did not go as well. Alphadjo Cisse’s brace was ultimately outshone by Samuel Mbangula’s hat-trick, as the Rossoblu scored three unanswered second-half goals to win 4-2.

It was a crash back down to earth, and a further reminder that this team remains very much a work in progress, with obvious limitations and shortcomings that can be exploited in quite a ruthless way. With the loss, Milan moved seven points off the leaders Inter and Lazio.

A few days ago, after the win in Udine, Cardinale had praised both the team and Ruben Amorim in a post-match interview. After the Bologna game, the owner not only didn’t speak, but left the stadium looking rather glum to say the least.

Sometimes, though, the curses can be a blessing too. Perhaps this was a necessary reminder for everyone – from Cardinale down to the players – that there is no shortcut with this project, and that there will be growing pains.

It cannot be argued though that it set a rather gloomy backdrop to everything that would come in the next few days, which makes what actually happened all the more remarkable.

The big day finally arrived on Thursday, just 24 hours after the Bologna match: Milan and Inter unveiled the renderings of their new stadium, along with design studios Foster+Partners and Manica.

The timing of the event – which took place at the Sforzesco in the city centre – felt deliberate. Just two days before the derby, Milan and Inter got together to release the first images of how the new stadium will look.

Some details had been known for some time about the specifics of the project, but visualising it is another thing. Moreover, they dropped the bombshell before hand that a day one naming rights partner had been found: Generali, thus making it the Generali Stadium.

Leaving aside any subjective thoughts on the design, it was a very proud moment for Cardinale. The American had made no secret of his desire to construct a new home for the two clubs, and he shed some light on things in an interview with CBS.

“You know, when we bought the club four years ago, I started working on this right away. And part of what we’re trying to do here is bring the best of America to Italian soccer, and specifically to AC Milan.

“It’s extraordinary: most people didn’t think we could accomplish all this, or even get to this point. Not only did we announce the stadium this week, but we also announced the largest naming rights deal in history. I think it’s a wonderful thing for Italian soccer.

“The message to everyone is that we’re coming back. Italian soccer truly deserves to be at the top again, and we’ll do everything we can through AC Milan to make that happen.”

Something about the build-up to the derby, right up until kick-off, felt different. It seemed as though there was a conscious decision to try and create a connection with the past, something that Cardinale himself appeared to lead.

For example, Sky reported that Cardinale invited about 60 people to a dinner the night before the game. It wasn’t just an opportunity to catch up, but also for the American to explain what he has planned for Milan, seeking the support of some club legends.

The likes of Adriano Galliani, Ricardo Kaka and the Serie A president Simonelli attended the dinner. Other notable Milan legends present were Marcel Desailly, Serginho, Cristian Brocchi, Mathieu Flamini, Mauro Tassotti, Filippo Galli and Daniele Massaro.

At the Milan Store, racks of shirts adorned printing with names like Gattuso, Nesta, Seedorf, Inzaghi, Pirlo, Ambrosini, Kaka and even Maldini. The tag line behind the campaign was to ‘celebrate the heroes of Athens’, but again it felt like constructed timing.

Going back to that CBS interview, the bold proclamations that Cardinale made on the broadcast certainly turned some heads.

“I remember George Steinbrenner telling me about twenty years ago, ‘Don’t get too emotionally involved in all this, don’t go into the locker room, don’t do those things’. But in this case, I got emotionally involved,” he said.

“For me, it became a personal issue. And I don’t know what it is: maybe it’s just that… look around, listen. This means so much to the people of Milan, it has a huge impact on them, and I feel a huge responsibility.

“AC Milan were at the top of the world, and we’re going to bring them back there. This will probably be one of the greatest things I’ll do in my career.”

Speaking of that link with the past, Kaka was presented with a trophy before kick-off to mark his induction into the Rossoneri Hall of Fame. It was a moment filled with emotion for all the fans present who chanted his name, while the Brazilian couldn’t hold back the tears as a montage video played on the screen.

Those words might just have fallen very flat had Milan not delivered on the field, but they did. No doubt fired up by the emotionally-charged atmosphere inside the stadium, Gerry Cardinale got to watch on as his players delivered a show.

Diego Moreira – the man he reportedly pushed to sign personally for €51m – was the start of the show. He got two assists from Christian Pulisic and Adrien Rabiot, two of his other favourites, before bending in a free-kick of his own. It was enough to blow the champions away.

Just 72 hours earlier, Cardinale had trudged through the mixed zone barely able to make eye contact with reporters after seeing his team implode against (at the time) bottom-half Bologna. On Halloween night, he had the smile of a child who had just scooped all the candy.

Speaking to QSVS, he got emotional: “What did you think of the boys? Every three days playing a game, going through the injuries, we get our full team back, and look at those guys. Those guys are winners. It brings tears to my eyes, it’s great right? The best night, and we’re going to build on this.”

All of the pre-match hype, those ballsy interviews speaking of a desire to return to greatness, bringing legends back to invoke the nostalgia… it was a very risky move in hindsight. It could have backfired with a defeat, but the team delivered and gave everyone connected to the club immense pride.

So, within the space of four days and 180 minutes of football, Cardinale had witnessed both the floor and the ceiling of this team. The next mission – one he appears to be well aware of – is to bring the baseline up towards the obvious high potential Amorim’s group has.

Speaking to La Gazzetta dello Sport before the Inter game, the 59-year-old admitted that the squad is far from finished in his eyes, and that some further work is needed to try and get it up to the level required. He also invoked Top Gun to explain his bond with Amorim.

“We need to continue improving the squad because in the summer I wasn’t able to do everything I wanted. We’re rebuilding the team, and while I like the new arrivals, the ones who stayed, and the names I got rid of, it will probably take a couple more transfer windows to complete everything.

“We’re only a quarter of the way there, and the priority is to build a team that can deal with injuries. If you’re competing in European competitions and playing… Amorim Ball, which is high-tempo football, with quick transitions, where everyone runs between 9 and 11km per game, you have to rotate them.

“That’s why we’ll continue to be aggressive and opportunistic in the January transfer market, and I’ll continue to strengthen our bench. My style is to move decisively and quickly.

“What matters to me is getting what we want, players… that are Milan-worthy. I won’t waste money, but when I set my mind on signing a player, I do everything I can to succeed.”

The first big step will be to welcome a very important jigsaw piece to complete the management puzzle: Markus Krösche. The past few days also seemed to serve to iron out the remaining details to reach an agreement with the German, who will become the new Head of Football.

You may wish to remain sceptical, and that is absolutely warranted, but for the first time in a while there does seem to be the semblance of a path out of the rut. A more active owner, a talented squad with room to improve, a coach being backed, a key director incoming and a new stadium on the way.

Oh, and of course the bragging rights, which are heading back across the Atlantic Ocean with Cardinale while he no doubt draws up the itinerary for his next visit.
