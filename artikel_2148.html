Barnsley questioned for ‘really odd’ ticket decision v Sheffield Wednesday

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

This article is part of Football League World's 'Terrace Talk' series, which provides personal opinions from our FLW Fan Pundits regarding the latest breaking news, teams, players, managers, potential signings and more…

By the time Barnsley host Sheffield Wednesday on the 20th November, the Tykes will be hopeful that they enter the Yorkshire derby with some added confidence.

The Tykes have had a torrid start to the season under Daniel Stendel, sitting joint-bottom of the third tier standings, taking just nine points from their opening 12 league games.

Bromley are the only side below Barnsley by having a 10-worst goal difference, so results need to pick up quickly if they are to avoid the dreaded League Two trapdoor.

The Reds ended a six-game straight losing run by drawing 1-1 with promotion contenders Oxford United last weekend, and before their league clash against the Owls, host Aldershot Town in the first round of the FA Cup, while tthe following midweek sees them return to Oakwell to face Oldham Athletic in the EFL Trophy.

In an ideal scenario, Barnsley will be hoping to earn back-to-back victories, which would then stand them in great stead for what was expected to be a raucous Oakwell against Wednesday.

However, there are fears now that Oakwell may not be as full and lively due to the club's ticket prices.

Barnsley v Sheffield Wednesday is the Friday night football on the 20th November, with Oakwell hosting a Yorkshire derby.

But there’s been criticism aimed at Barnsley for their ticket pricing for the fixture.

It was announced in the last few days that the Tykes are charging £35 for a standard adult ticket against the Owls, with some viewing that as extortionate, given the game is being broadcast live on Sky Sports.

Barnsley's ticket prices for the visit of Sheffield Wednesday have NOT gone down well...

Along with the adult ticket pricing, seniors (65+) are charged at £25, young adults (19-21) are also set at £25, while under-19s are £16 and under-14s are set at £10.

With this in mind, FLW’s Barnsley fan pundit has given his thoughts over the decision to charge adults £35 a ticket.

Speaking to Football League World, Barnsley fan pundit Andy Symcox has questioned the Tykes about their decision to charge adults £35 a ticket for their meeting with Sheffield Wednesday, believing the pricing is ‘really odd’ and will keep some fans away from Oakwell for such an important clash.

Andy told FLW: “For me, the decision to charge £35 for a matchday ticket at Barnsley versus Sheffield Wednesday, on the surface, seems really odd.

“The game itself is on Sky, and thus, how many fans will decide not to go to Oakwell to watch it on their TV’s instead, quoting the excessive price.

“However, I wonder whether or not this is part of the reciprocal arrangement, as Sheffield Wednesday have notoriously charged very high prices in the past for games at Hillsborough, so much so that I personally refuse to go there due to the very high cost of tickets.

“On balance, however, given that it is on Sky, I find it a poor decision.”

A massive problem with football throughout the country is pricing, with tickets getting more and more unaffordable as the cost of living gets tougher and tougher.

And Barnsley are offering little help to their supporters through their latest price sting, with some supporters simply unable to pay such prices for a League One standard football match.

In what should have been a fiery cauldron of noise given it’s a local derby, Barnsley will see fewer supporters at Oakwell on that Friday evening, with some electing to watch it on TV given it’s being broadcast by Sky Sports.

Because of this, Barnsley are driving their supporters away from a game that has the potential to turn their season right around, and they will only have themselves to blame come that night if Oakwell has a soulless atmosphere and contributes towards yet another negative performance and result.

In what is such a tough situation for the club, they need every single one of their supporters to be right behind the team and creating an atmosphere that makes Oakwell a fortress again, but the club are currently killing any chance of that by charging supporters far too much money.
