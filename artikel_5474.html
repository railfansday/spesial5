Nvidia stock rebounds premarket as Morgan Stanley cut it from top picks

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Nvidia stock closed down 5.47% on Tuesday amid the scandal over CEO Jensen Huang's affair, with earnings due on Nov. 17

Nvidia $NVDA stock was up 5.3% in premarket trading on Wednesday as of 5:47 a.m. ET, bouncing back from two straight sessions of losses amid the scandal over CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with a woman suspected of spying for China.

Morgan Stanley $MS removed Nvidia from its list of top picks on Wednesday, according to CNBC. Nvidia stock had lost more than a quarter of its value since the bank first included it on Oct. 2. Analysts at the bank said in a note that Huang's leadership is "crucial" to where Nvidia is steering its strategy.

Nvidia stock ended Tuesday’s session 5.5% lower, with fresh reporting shedding light on the affair’s toll on the company.

Huang came back to work in person on Monday and sought to convey "business as usual," according to CNBC. He held meetings at the office on Tuesday, ahead of the company's earnings report on Nov. 17.

John Blank, chief equity strategist at Zacks, attributed the selloff to worries over possible new U.S. curbs and the chance that additional troubles could surface while the story unfolds, according to CNBC. "What else has Jensen done?" he said.

"It's not good for Nvidia. It's not good for Jensen Huang. It's certainly not good for the AI trade," Blank told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Wednesday.

On Tuesday, Nvidia retained law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead the independent investigation its board announced Sunday. Joon H. Kim, a former acting U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York, will lead the work, and a panel of independent directors will supervise it. The board said on Sunday that nothing it has seen suggests Nvidia's trade secrets or U.S. national security were put at risk.

Huang apologized to employees in a message on Sunday. “My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did,” he wrote. The woman, Alice Huang, is not related to him and is suspected by U.S. intelligence agencies of spying for China. China’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the allegation unsubstantiated.

The Justice Department is conducting a criminal probe of Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based firm suspected of illegally funneling Nvidia AI chips to China. Rosen Law Firm has filed a shareholder class action against Nvidia and Huang in federal court in San Jose, California. Three House Republican committee chairs have asked Huang to appear for a private session with lawmakers on Nov. 18.
