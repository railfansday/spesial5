Oil is rising toward its first weekly gain this month on Middle East supply risks

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

An attack on Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery and Sunday's OPEC+ meeting on 2027 output are keeping traders focused on supply

Brent crude futures are trading at $96.56 a barrel, and U.S. West Texas Intermediate has gained 1.8% to $85.15, putting both benchmarks on course to finish the week higher for the first time this month, according to The Wall Street Journal. Traders are assessing fresh dangers to oil shipments from the Middle East as OPEC+ prepares to meet on Sunday.

The Saudi Ministry of Energy said a projectile hit the Jazan refinery on Thursday, leaving one Nepalese worker dead and several other people hurt, and igniting a blaze that forced a partial pause in operations, according to CNN. Iran-backed Houthi militants carried out the attack, according to Bloomberg.

The strike followed the Houthis' claim, made the previous day, of an attack on Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's biggest oil refinery, according to CNN. Loadings of oil at the Ras Tanura terminal stopped in the wake of that strike, and it is not known if they have restarted.

A drone attack in Kuwait on Thursday killed two Italians, according to Bloomberg. Kuwait blamed Iran, which denied responsibility.

Oil moving through the Strait of Hormuz is running at 31% of its prewar volume, according to The Wall Street Journal, which cited early figures from Kpler. The premium that front-month Brent commands over the next contract has grown to $4.28 a barrel, compared with roughly $2.50 at the beginning of the week.

OPEC+ will meet Sunday to talk through how the group will collectively manage production in 2027, since current output deals lapse at the end of this year, according to The Wall Street Journal. The group is weighing a plan to leave existing production limits unchanged through January, since members are divided on what volumes they can maintain over the long run, according to Bloomberg.

Prices fell in mid-November. Brent slid to $91.38 a barrel on Nov. 18 as a larger U.S. crude stockpile and bets on oversupply outweighed strikes on tankers in the Middle East.

The International Energy Agency forecast a fourth-quarter surplus on Nov. 13 and said global supply exceeded demand in October for the first time since the war in Iran began. The agency expects fourth-quarter supply to average 105 million barrels a day, 1.7 million barrels a day above forecast demand.
