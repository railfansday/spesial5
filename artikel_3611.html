The Method-Dressing Madness Behind Mckenna Grace’s ‘Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ Press Tour Looks

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Before the Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping press tour began, Mckenna Grace knew she wanted to be method dressed for her appearances. Made a household term by Law Roach and Zendaya, method dressing is when actors wear looks that pay homage to the film or show they’re promoting: See Zendaya’s on-theme looks across red carpets for Dune, Spiderman, and The Odyssey for a slew of examples.

With the new Hunger Games film, it’s Grace’s turn to adopt the trend. A super fan of the Suzanne Collins book series and their film adaptations, Grace—who plays District 12 tribute Maysilee Donner—took a hand in the styling for her press run, weaving easter eggs into every look. Her stylist, Molly Dickson, was up for the challenge of translating Grace’s deep-cut references into custom and archival looks from Monique Lhuillier, Gucci, Celine and more.

In a wide-ranging conversation, Dickson breaks down all the looks that set the Hunger Games fans’ hearts aflame, from Rue’s blue dress to Katniss’ Mockingjay gown, and all the threads that came together for Grace’s most memorable press appearances yet.

Vanity Fair: How did you get into styling?

Molly Dickson: I went to college in Minnesota and my major was advertising. Two weeks before I graduated, I called my mom and said, "I'm not into this. I don't wanna do advertising." She said, "Well, what do you wanna do?" I told her, "I wanna do fashion. I wanna go to New York." She said, "No, absolutely not."

Right after I graduated, I went to LA, this was 2009 during the recession, and no one was hiring. I got an internship at Marie Claire in New York, and enrolled back into college to get school credit to intern at Hearst. I worked at Marie Claire as a market assistant for almost two years, and then I left and assisted Leslie Fremar for seven years. That's when I fell in love with celebrity. Then ten years ago, I went off on my own.

What would you say are your aesthetic influences as a stylist?

My personal style relates very similarly to Sadie Sink's style, very effortless. She always wants to be very comfortable and easy, never anything frilly or over the top. Just a simple, beautiful dress, and you let it speak for itself.

Mckenna is a big fan of the original film series, so it’s clear you’re pulling from there as influences when dressing her. Is it a collaborative process when coming to one of these referential looks?

She's obviously a very huge fan of the franchise. We actually started having conversations about this tour in April, which is very early to start having conversations. I could just sense her excitement around it. She started sending me mood boards, and I actually looked back today at the mood board and it was, it was a lot of Katniss Everdeen. It had Joanna on it, and it was filled with these really strong female characters from the franchise.

What she really wanted to hit was Katniss’ wedding gown look and the Mockingjay look with the wings. I was really stumped on how to pull that off without making it overly costumey.

Mckenna kept asking, "What's going on with those?" I knew the vision, but I didn't know how to execute it. I was working with Keke Palmer and the designer Michael Ngo. He was making some cool music costumes for Keke, and I asked, "Michael, could you make this silver piece?" 'Cause I really had no idea how to get it done.

He said, “I don't think I can do that. You know what? Just give me a day, let me play around with it." That night, he said, "I figured it out." We got the piece and I thought, "Okay, it needs to be a wedding dress under,” so I reached out to all the bridal houses. I wanted it to be vintage because it would tell a pretty story. Monique [Lhuillier] ended up sending a ton of wedding gowns, and the day before Mckenna came in for the fitting, I actually tried the wedding dresses on myself with the silver piece.

I sent Mckenna five dresses, and the one that she ended up wearing, she said, "That's it. I don't even need to try anything else. That's the one I'm gonna wear." Then, it was a matter of finding the right event to wear it to because it’s pretty nice, very dramatic.

She was in Paris for the Miu Miu show, and I brought it there because she had the Hunger Games Museum event. It was a whole conversation with her team, like, "Is it the right moment? Is it too much for this event?" Her PR said, "No, this is the perfect moment. It's the best way to kick off the tour." The response with the Hunger Games fans—I have never seen anything like it before, ever.

What was the experience of having her be such a big part of the styling process?

It makes my job so fun because she is so passionate about it and she loves Hunger Games so much, even just finding subtle nods back to the movie. She would say, "I wanna do a ladybug look." Thom Browne did that amazing ladybug dress, I thought, "That's so cool, but it's not really her personal aesthetic." So it’s finding ways to keep it still true to her personal style while maintaining the nods to the film.

The way you seem to understand Mckenna feels something similar to the connection between Katniss and Cinna in The Hunger Games. When pulling looks for her, what comes into play? How do you make her come through and not be lost in the lore of the stories or the dress?

Mckenna had said that she maybe wanted to do a red or a dark pink feather look because in the books [Maysilee] gets attacked by the red birds which are like flamingos. I'm pulling in all these red feather dresses and on the runway, this one dress looked amazing. Mckenna said, “That's perfect for the flamingo reference.”

But we put it on her and it was so big, and she's so petite so she was just getting swarmed. I said, “Let"s not force it.” The goal was for her to feel comfortable in the look rather than just getting each reference.

The public has been really excited about these looks. Are you thinking about the response to the looks when you’re putting them together?

There's been some conversations in a fitting, like, "Oh, the Hunger Games fans are gonna get this." They're gonna see the inspiration, they're gonna understand it.

But I wouldn't say we're choosing a look because ‘Oh, this is gonna go viral to the Hunger Games community.’ Mckenna is a super fan herself so if she's getting excited about the look, everyone else that's a fan of the franchise is gonna get excited about the look.

Do you feel any pressure to top the last outfit after each premiere?

Yes. I think it was Paris because that wedding dress did get so much press. In that fitting, it felt like, "We gotta keep our game up high." It's been really fun for my team and market assistants because a lot of the girls that work with me are huge fans of the movie. They're not even seeing it as work. They're like, "Oh my God, this is so exciting. Look at this. This looks like this from the movie."

What was the inspiration to do method dressing for this press run? What do you think is the value?

Mckenna knew back in April, she said, "I wanna do method for this." I personally did not read the books. I'm a visual person, so I've seen all the movies. Lionsgate was able to let me go with the cast when they first saw the movie because my agent was like, "Molly needs to go to the movie to get inspired before this press tour. She can't do a method dressing press tour without seeing the movie."

Even as the tour was going on, Mckenna kept sending me new references and quotes from the book and stuff like that.

What made you take it to the next level? From paying homage to not only characters, but the actual actors from the films like Jennifer Lawrence at the 2011 Oscars to even dressing like set pieces from the film.

That was all Mckenna's vision. On her first mood board, she had a lot of Jennifer Lawrence's red carpet moments because she's such a huge fan of Katniss and Jennifer Lawrence.

With the Rue reference, Mckenna thought that color was just very Hunger Games. It wasn't until she put the dress on, her mom said, "Wait, this is Rue's dress." So that was just fate. I would love to take credit for that.

Why do you think that this styling has generated so much attention?

I think it’s when you do method dressing for such a big franchise and you really are passionate about it. The thing is that Mckenna is so excited and passionate about this movie. She has said, “I didn't think that I would love doing fittings so much. I used to hate doing fittings.”

Because every look is so intentionally tied back to Hunger Games and she's such a big fan, it's literally like playing dress up with her. I think the fans see it, she's so relatable because she's a fan that all these fans think, "Wow, like, she's a fan and she got to do this." She’s the fangirl living out her dream, and the fans are excited for her that she finally gets to do this.

What do you think are the best kinds of projects to implement method dressing with/works best with? What does it work best with?

I think the big movies that already have a fan base, like Barbie or Wednesday, Being able to pull inspiration from existing books or films makes it so much easier for a stylist rather than a movie that no one's seen before. There's no curiosity or momentum about it because you're starting from scratch, whereas Hunger Games, Barbie, Wednesday, there's already a level of expectation.

The looks for this press run have been a mix of archival, new, and custom pieces. How do you make that choice? How important is it for you to create an archive for Mckenna?

It kind of is a mix of what we’re able to source. Mckenna really wanted to pay homage to Joanna's character with that scene where she's like, "My stylist is always putting me in trees." She's like, "I wanna do a tree reference." And I'm like, "How in the world am I gonna find a fashion reference that's a tree that's not a costume?"

I love the challenge of sourcing the market, and so do the girls on my team. I think it was 2011 Phoebe Philo where Celine did some tree references in the collection. But just because we find it online doesn't mean that we can get it. So then it's reaching out to every vintage dealer, every rental house, asking, "Do you guys have anything like this?" And they might have it, but they're like, "It's already gone, someone has it." I ended up actually finding the Celine tree dress on the RealReal, and I bought it. I love RealReal.

What is your approach to dressing different clients? For instance, you mentioned Sadie Sink earlier, can you walk me through how that process differs from Mckenna?

It's very different between each girl. I let them set the pace. If they wanna be involved, great. If they just wanna show up to the fitting and not even know what's gonna be there, I try to accommodate whatever direction they wanna go. I have one client that literally last week has sent me over 200 references of dresses that she wants, and another girl that's like, “I really don't care. Whatever you tell me to wear, I'll wear it.”

Every girl is different, and what makes my job so interesting is that it's not the same. Everyone has very different styles. I think the most important part of a job as a stylist is to keep their style true to them and not just force my style onto them. Mckenna's way more girly, Sadie is a little bit more casual, it’s just taking their style and elevating it a little bit.

Will you do any looks inspired by Mckenna’s character Maysilee? The looks have been mostly referential to things and people from past stories. Are there influences of Maysilee in the looks that fans haven't been able to pick up on because the film wasn’t out yet?

In a couple days, she’ll be in Miami for press, and she's wearing a very Maysilee inspired, purple Miu Miu dress that has a white collar. When she wears it, everyone's gonna be like, "Oh, that's Maysilee." She really wanted to have flowers and necklaces because Maysilee is really into jewelry, so it’s very light and flowery.

Can you break down the outfit for the New York City premiere?

We are doing the Mockingjay dress, the one with the wings, and that was another thing that I was very stumped on. I'm like, "What designer's gonna make these wings?" I had this woman in LA who does wings, she's done Halloween costumes and music videos.

In the first fitting, I pulled this navy Pamella Roland gown. The fabric is really, really cool. It's like a shimmery kind of organza. I thought, "That's the right color,” but I wanted it to be strapless. I went back to Pamella Roland and they were like, "We can make it in strapless." Mckenna was very keen on having some sort of embellishment to replicate Katniss because she had screen printing on the gown and it kind of looks like wings in a way.

Pamella then drew all these CADs with diamond wings on it, and then we got the wings made. We did the final fitting and I almost started to cry. Her mom's taking videos and it's epic. It's so exciting because the fans were doing polls on what she's gonna wear to the premiere.

It's full circle, we started with the wedding dress, Mckenna did the fire gown, and then we ended with the Mockingjay dress.

What was the hardest look to pull together?

I think design-wise, it was the fire gown.

Getting the fire printed on the fabric was really hard. I had a while back done a custom Cong Tri gown for Cami Mendes for the Masters of the Universe tour. It was like a gold beaded gown, and I remember I really liked the fabrics that they used for it.

So when I was thinking of who could do this fire gown, I reached out to them first, and I'm like, "Can you guys do something that looks like a fire? Like, she's literally on fire." And they're like, "No. We don't think we can." And again, they were like, "Give us some time." They came back and said, "We did all these testing on the fabric." I'm like, "Yeah, you did it. You did it."

What's really funny is we were at a fitting at Mckenna's house with the fire gown in a garment bag, and one of her friends stopped by. It was just a tip of the dress showing and her friend said, "Oh my God, you got a fire gown." Then I thought, "Okay, people are gonna get it."
