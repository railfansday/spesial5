Manchester United have Europe’s worst defence and the blame is clear

United have conceded 34 times this season in all competitions and have the worst record across Europe’s top five leagues having failed to strengthen the back line during the summer transfer window

Mauricio Pochettino exited Anfield complaining about a red card. This time, presumably, he will not be able to enlist Donald Trump’s help to get a suspension overturned. The Argentinian’s last three games in management have ended 6-0, 4-0 and 2-0; but, after bidding farewell to the United States with two wins, the 4-0 was a defeat, the heaviest suffered by a Manchester United manager on his bow since Huddersfield Town were the defending champions, Stanley Baldwin was Prime Minister and the future Elizabeth II was a few months old.

A 100-year low is saying something, even for United. Pochettino attributed Liverpool’s emphatic win to Leny Yoro’s eighth-minute dismissal, which he deemed unjust. “The circumstance, the circumstance,” he argued. But that did not render what happened afterwards invalid; or, indeed, what occurred immediately before.

There were two fundamental defensive errors: from Matthijs de Ligt, in leaving Ronald Araujo’s long ball to give Alexander Isak a clear run on goal, from Yoro in fouling the Liverpool striker. In their own way, they provided the story of the season: United’s defensive errors have abounded, they have four red cards, two to Yoro, and when Dominik Szoboszlai converted the resulting free kick, their habit of conceding early continued.

Pochettino tried to avoid broader questions about United’s leaky defence by citing the unique element: at Anfield, down to 10 men for more than 80 minutes. Perhaps it was a first attempt to protect the players who have failed a host of United managers.

Pochettino is a newcomer but problems predate him. It is increasingly evident there is no case for United’s defence. They have conceded 34 goals already this season, four in each of the last two games, 19 in total in the most recent six. Only Ipswich and United do not have a Premier League clean sheet this season.

Pochettino’s first attempt to add solidity was to give De Ligt his first top-flight outing in 357 days. Perhaps it was an attempt to wean United off their dependence on Harry Maguire, with his crippling lack of pace. But it went horribly wrong. The Dutchman looked rusty and uncertain. He delivered his entry for the worst performance given by a United defender this season; it is an incredibly competitive category.

De Ligt’s injury problems mean it is wrong to suggest he has even had a season. Some United defenders have and have had atrocious campaigns: Lisandro Martinez, Ayden Heaven and Diogo Dalot, to name three. Maguire has had a disappointing season, Yoro a distinctly mixed one. In goal, Senne Lammens has made mistake after mistake; at least he spared United a still heavier beating with a series of saves at Anfield. Liverpool had 28 shots, an xG of 4.38, 60 touches in the United box.

Which, besides the disorganised, dreadful defence, highlights the absence of a real defensive midfielder. Carlos Baleba might be one, but is injured. Andrey Santos was supposed to be one, but he has started seven games and United have lost six of them.

United can get overwhelmed in the middle of the pitch by anyone with energy. They look susceptible to every counter-attack. They are desperately weak whenever anyone runs at them, partly because most of their players lack speed; indeed, when Bradley Barcola burst clear to set up Liverpool’s third goal, it was Bruno Fernandes who chased him, while Martinez gave up.

Meanwhile, Dalot is enduring traumatic game after traumatic game: tormented by Aston Villa’s Alysson two weeks earlier, he fared no better against Rio Ngumoha. It is apparent the Portuguese, jeered by United fans in the Villa game, needs to be taken out of the firing line. As long as Luke Shaw is injured, he is likelier to remain in it. De Ligt probably needs to be removed from the fray, too. Yoro is already suspended for Saturday’s game against Brentford.

All of which underlined that an interview which was barely 48 hours old was ageing very badly. Chief executive Omar Berrada had explained on Friday why United did not buy a defender in the summer.

“We did come into it thinking: we have depth. We have nine players that can play in the back four positions,” he said. “So that's why we looked primarily at investing in the midfield.”

Now United have the worst of both worlds. They have bungled their midfield overhaul. They delayed the defensive makeover for too long. And if Michael Carrick wanted Lewis Hall, that was never realistic. United did not have enough money to do everything but it is worrying that they did not even consider bringing in a central defender or a right-back.

They underestimated their own requirements. They overestimated their own players. Now the numbers paint their own picture. Statistics means they can now be called Europe’s worst defence; no side in the five major leagues has been breached more often in all competitions. When United were Europe’s best team, in 2007-08, they only conceded 33 goals all season. Now they have let in 34 already. And that number looks like it will end up being much, much higher.
