'Somebody’s got to stand up to that guy' - Russell slams Verstappen after clash

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Red Bull's Max Verstappen and Mercedes' George Russell to start on front of Las Vegas grid, as Toto Wolff predicts "big cinema"; Russell hit out at rival after final practice run-in; watch Sunday's race at 4am (build-up from 2.30am), live on Sky Sports F1 and Main Event

George Russell launched a scathing criticism of Max Verstappen after a clash between them in final practice at the Las Vegas Grand Prix ahead of a race they will now start on the front row together.

​​​The pair, who have had run-ins in the past on track in recent years, clashed wheels in final practice as they fought over track position.

Verstappen first overtook Russell on the run down The Strip to Turn 14 before the Mercedes went back down the inside of the Red Bull in the braking zone, with the two cars brushing wheels.

Stewards investigated the incident and issued a warning to Verstappen, ruling he had been "driving erratically" under braking into the corner once ahead of the Mercedes, which had been preparing for a timed lap.

Verstappen and Russell went on to qualify second and third respectively, which becomes first and second on the grid for Sunday's race at 4am, live on Sky Sports F1, due to Q3 pacesetter Kimi Antonelli's five-place grid penalty.

Asked after qualifying about the decision made by stewards, Russell said "a warning was fine" before going on to issue a withering rebuke of his Red Bull rival's approach.

Russell told Sky Italy: "There's nothing in the rules to say you cannot overtake someone who is starting a lap but when you do this, you are screwing this person over and he does this week in, week out with many drivers and everybody accepts it.

"I'm not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they're bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody's got to stand up to that guy."

Russell's comments about wanting to "stand up to" Verstappen echoed those he made following an incident between them in qualifying at the 2024 Qatar Grand Prix.

The pair traded barbs in person and via interviews after that clash, when Verstappen was given a one-place grid drop for impeding Russell in Q3, with the Mercedes driver then launching an explosive attack on his rival ahead of the following week's race in Abu Dhabi.

After qualifying in Las Vegas, the pair appeared together in the post-race press conference and were asked about the impact and whether there had any confusion about the 'gentleman's agreement' between drivers regarding not disturbing a rival who was about to start a flying lap.

Verstappen said "nothing happened", while Russell said there was "no confusion, I think it was pretty clear".

Sky Sports F1's Jacques Villeneuve said of the incident itself: "Fun and exciting to watch! But we want to see this in the race, not in free practice. Unnecessary.

"Once you've lost your track position, too bad for you. If you're George, you got overtaken, it's practice. OK, you don't get it back, you lost your position and move on.

"That was a completely unnecessary risk. You don't want to make an enemy of Max."

Speaking after hearing Russell's post-qualifying comments, Sky Sports F1's Jamie Chadwick said: "George saying he'll stand up to Max, I like that. It's very reminiscent of what we saw in Qatar.

"With George we know he's never going to give to Max. I think Max does try his luck with that kind of thing and that is why he is able to find himself in good positions. It comes also from the pit wall, who are telling him to push through the traffic and fight his way to get clear air, and he's aggressive and he does bully people out of the way.

"Most people do give in to that and then someone like Liam Lawson [at the Mexico City GP] comes along, doesn't, and gets in trouble from the big team.

"I quite like the fact that George is standing up to him because Max does it intentionally, he drives like that. There is something good to be said for that, but there's also something that needs to be fought against."

With the pair now starting together on the front row for the short run to Las Vegas' first corner, Villeneuve added: "No love lost there, so that's good before the start for tomorrow! That will make it exciting.

"He shouldn't overtake someone who is about to start his lap, true, but how does that person know you're about to start your lap? That's one thing, and maybe you're about to start your own lap yourself.

"So that's a difficult one to judge, ultimately."

Sunday will be the first time that Verstappen and Russell have shared the front row of a Grand Prix grid this year.

Both drivers are in the five-way fight for second place in the Drivers' Championship, with Verstappen currently holding the position by 17 points from third-placed Russell.

Asked what he was expecting at the start of the race, Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff told Sky Sports F1: "Big cinema!

"If they try to go through the first corners together, we shall be watching."

Verstappen is chasing a third win in six races, and a third in four years on Las Vegas' Strip Circuit, and is ready for a challenge.

"Feeling good but, at the same time, also aware that Mercedes is quick," he told Sky Sports F1. "So we need to really try and fight.

"I think it's not going to be very easy, but I don't mind it to be tough. We'll see. I hope that just it is an exciting race."

Russell, though, believes the Red Bull has impressive pace to utilise too.

"I think on the whole we've been very competitive," said Russell, who had been fastest in final practice.

"But I think Max this morning actually had the fastest. I know I had a very strong session, but the lap times he was doing at the start of the session on the soft tyre, when it was 40 minutes before we did our fastest lap, we predicted that lap time he did was more competitive than that fast lap I did at the end of the session.

"So, I think between us and Max, it's very close."
