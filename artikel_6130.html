Progressive advocates call on House Democrats to force Cuba vote

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Rep. Nydia M. Velázquez, D-N.Y., is the sponsor of a Cuba war powers resolution filed in May. (Tom Williams/CQ Roll Call)

Progressive advocates are urging House Democrats to force a vote during the lame-duck session on a resolution aimed at preventing war against Cuba, arguing the issue is too urgent to wait until Democrats control the chamber next year.

“The Cuban people are suffering and dying, and cannot afford further months of inaction,” 50 progressive groups wrote to Democratic House leaders in a letter dated Wednesday. “In the midterm elections, U.S. voters resoundingly rejected a foreign policy of war and domination, and made clear their desire for Congress to act forcefully to rein in the Trump administration.

Holding a vote would demonstrate that Democrats recognize the urgency facing the Cuban people, and would help set the tone for an assertive agenda of peace, diplomacy and respect for international law in the 120th Congress, the letter added.

At issue is a war powers resolution introduced in May by Rep. Nydia M. Velázquez, D-N.Y., and House Foreign Affairs Committee ranking member Rep. Gregory W. Meeks, D-N.Y., that would call on the president “to remove the United States Armed Forces from hostilities within or against Cuba, unless explicitly authorized by a declaration of war or a specific authorization for use of military force.”

The letter was sent to House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York, Meeks and House Rules Committee ranking member Jim McGovern, D-Mass. It was signed by prominent anti-war groups such as Win Without War, Just Foreign Policy, the Friends Committee on National Legislation, Indivisible and Code Pink.

Advocates are frustrated that House Democrats have not forced a vote on a Cuba war powers resolution even as they have forced multiple war powers votes on Iran and see it as an example of leadership that is at odds with the progressive wing of the party. One advocate who requested anonymity to speak candidly said it’s their understanding Democratic leaders are hesitant to make potentially vulnerable Florida members of the caucus vote on the issue.

Spokespeople for Jeffries and Meeks did not respond to requests for comment Wednesday on whether Democrats will force a vote before the end of the year, what their reasoning is if they don’t and whether they plan to bring up a vote next year.

Senate Democrats sought to force a vote on a similar resolution earlier this year, but Republicans in that chamber successfully derailed the effort. Senate Republicans raised a point of order that the resolution was not entitled to privileged status under the War Powers Resolution since U.S. troops aren't actively engaged in combat against Cuba. The point of order was sustained in a mostly party-line vote of 51-47.

Senate Democrats later introduced another Cuba war powers resolution, which they have not tried to force a vote on and have not signaled they will do so before the end of the year.

The resolutions have come amid several veiled threats from President Donald Trump suggesting eventual military action against Cuba.

“I want to get Cuba done before I leave,” Trump told conservative radio host Hugh Hewitt last week when asked if he would order the use of military force against Cuba.

“Frankly, I’d love to be able to do it for the people, for the Cubans that came and were treated really badly, and they came to America,” Trump said, adding he would “certainly consider” taking military action.

Trump’s comments are on top of a pressure campaign his administration has been waging against Cuba. Since January, the United States has imposed a blockade of oil shipments to the island, causing fuel shortages that have led to lengthy blackouts and aggravated an economic crisis.

While the Senate vote in April did not go Democrats’ way, advocates still saw it as sending a strong message.

“Despite falling short of the majority, this was among the strongest votes in congressional history against the use of blockades, and one of the most important stands Congress has taken against U.S. economic sanctions toward Cuba in over six decades,” the letter from progressive organizations said. “The House should take a similarly forceful action at this crucial moment.”

The House vote, even if successful, would be largely symbolic since the measure Democrats can force a vote on is a concurrent resolution. Concurrent resolutions are widely considered to be nonbinding even if cleared by both chambers of Congress since they do not get a presidential signature, though anti-war advocates dispute that applies to war powers.

The progressive groups in their letter called for “decisive leadership” in forcing a vote on the issue.

“Across the political spectrum,” the letter said, “Americans do not want another regime change war, nor an open-ended, nation-building project abroad based on false pretenses — especially not when escalation risks exposing the United States to new national security risks, placing US servicemembers in harm’s way, stoking regional instability, and becoming the latest chapter in a long history of harmful U.S. regional policies that have fueled mass migration to U.S. shores.”
