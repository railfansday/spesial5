Why Microsoft is aiming to make Copilot the ‘OS for work’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

by Mary Jo Foley on Nov 16, 2026 at 5:44 amNovember 16, 2026 at 5:44 am

It’s been quite a while since Microsoft was “the Windows company,” meaning it derived the bulk of its revenues from Windows. However, Windows — along with Office — has helped Microsoft build a moat that has largely shielded it from forces that have battered some of its competitors as AI has risen to prominence.

These days, Microsoft executives have likened Copilot to an operating system, or as CEO Satya Nadella called it, “a new OS for work.” This doesn’t mean Microsoft is replacing Windows with Copilot (even though there was a Microsoft incubation project, codenamed “Project Aion,” that focused on the idea of a “Copilot OS”).

Plus, as the head of Windows discovered the hard way, many Windows users are dead set against Windows morphing into an “agentic OS.”

Instead, the Copilot as “a new OS for work” descriptor is a metaphor, but one with considerable history and significance.

Microsoft is betting that the race to bring AI into the workplace will be won by whoever controls the layer underneath the models — the data, permissions, security and management tools that businesses already run on Microsoft software. That’s the job Windows has done for decades, and it’s the part of the AI business where Microsoft starts out ahead.

Many of the components behind the OS metaphor are likely to be on display at the company’s annual Ignite conference in San Francisco this week. According to the session list for the weeklong event for customers and partners, Microsoft will highlight how Copilot acts as a platform for building, hosting and managing AI solutions.

The high-level pitch: Copilot can do it all. It can be the foundational layer for your agents, applications, and business processes.

Operating systems like Windows provide users with a way to interact with their applications and resources via a common interface across software and data. They orchestrate under the covers what’s happening when users perform specific tasks.

Taking the metaphor to the extreme, if Copilot can be seen as the new Windows, agents are the new apps; skills are the Win32 programming interfaces; and the Graph and IQ layers are like File Explorer.

The New OS Metaphor: Why Now? For the past year-plus, Microsoft has been working to get customers to think about Copilot as not so much a chatbot, but more a full-stack platform. Its recently introduced Copilot “super app” is meant to be the unifying interface across this platform.

Currently, the new Copilot app provides customers with Code, Cowork and Autopilot access. But beneath that top layer, Microsoft is adding other integrations to its core Microsoft Office experiences, Microsoft IQ intelligence backends, its CRM and ERP Dynamics capabilities, and agents and applications from other vendors and customers, as well.

Microsoft’s inclusion of its core Office capabilities in the new Copilot comes none too soon, as Anthropic last week introduced new Claude-based tools for spreadsheets and whiteboards, and previewed one for meetings, that compete more directly with Microsoft’s Office apps.

The “Copilot as New OS” metaphor also works when you consider that Microsoft is looking to the new Copilot app to raise the level of abstraction, or hide the underlying complexities, of how agentic computing works.

If you believe the AI story lines, we’re moving to a world where many customers expect to be able to ask a system to turn a bunch of slides into a business plan and then share that plan with a group of coworkers. In this scenario, users don’t have to think about which apps and data sources they need to pull together to achieve this; they will count on their AI system to figure it out and just do it on their behalf.

“We increasingly think of all our apps together as a new operating system for work that transcends traditional software categories to understand user intent and act on it,” said Nadella in his letter to shareholders, published as part of Microsoft’s annual report on Oct. 21.

The end goal? “As our apps move toward agent-first capabilities that can be called and orchestrated in the flow of work, the system becomes more valuable and more deeply embedded,” Nadella said.

The Slow and Unsexy Enterprise Race: In the enterprise space, being first and fastest doesn’t always win the race. While AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic vie to be the AI model kingmakers, Microsoft is content to focus on the less sexy but more difficult-to-deliver elements like context, permissions, skills, orchestration, governance and security, all hallmarks of operating systems like Windows.

Microsoft will try to pull all these OS-like pieces together at this week’s Ignite and show customers that it’s got what it takes with Copilot to make AI promises more than just demoware.
