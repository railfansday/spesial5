Mindseye developer Build a Rocket Boy - founded by ex-GTA veteran - has a mountain of debt, and the main plan for paying it back is litigation

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A week dedicated to all things Grand Theft Auto, as the long-awaited GTA 6 finally arrives.

"A coordinated campaign of negative online reviews and commentary."

Beleaguered Mindseye developer Build a Rocket Boy - the company created by former Rockstar and Grand Theft Auto producer Leslie Benzies - apparently owes approximately £62 million in debt. And a huge chunk of that appears to be owed to Chinese gaming company NetEase (£36.5m), though more than £1m appears to be owed to Benzies himself.

This came to light in a proposal issued by Build a Rocket Boy's administrators this week (spotted by Resetera), which lists both the moneys owed (right down to a £20 charge to OpenAI) and details what went wrong, as well as what Build a Rocket Boy intends to do to recoup money to pay the debts back. And the company's "largest" potential asset, according to the administrators, is litigation.

"Prior to the Company's entry into Administration, the directors had been investigating circumstances surrounding the commercial performance of the video game, MindsEye, which was released in 2025 following significant development investment," the administration proposal read. "The directors formed the view that the game's commercial prospects may have been adversely affected by a coordinated campaign of negative online reviews and commentary.

"The directors subsequently received information which they considered suggested the involvement of a number of third parties in organising and funding such activity and commenced litigation against some of those parties. The directors have indicated that any such claims may be substantial in value, although the extent of any recovery remains uncertain and will be dependent upon the outcome of ongoing investigations, legal proceedings and asset recovery actions."

This tallies with the narrative spun by co-company leader Mark Gerhard as the company waded into deep water. Gerhard blamed "organised espionage and corporate sabotage" for the studio's difficulties as multiple rounds of layoffs shredded Build a Rocket Boy. "People had their hands on the scale and deliberately tried to shape a negative narrative around the product," Gerhard said.

The situation he's referring to is likely the alleged smear campaign made against the game, which Gerhand reportedly claimed in an internal meeting this year was paid for by a "big American company" and then run by a UK company called Ritual Network, which apparently had influencers, journalists and staff at Build a Rocket Boy deliberately sabotage the game's release. That's according to an account of a videoed internal meeting reportedly seen by Insider Gaming earlier this year.

It had nothing to do with the dire state of Mindseye when it launched, then. Eurogamer awarded it one out of five stars, chastising its "ridiculous story, inconsistent writing, poorly designed mission scenarios, and utterly atrocious combat" in our Mindseye review.

Build a Rocket Boy was founded in 2016, and its first game, Everywhere - a sort of online gaming platform - was announced in 2022. It never materialised, but a part of that vision was carved out to become a standalone game experience of its own along the way, and that's how we got Mindseye - a take on a Grand Theft Auto formula. Whether or not the litigation is successful and can recoup any of Build a Rocket Boy's debts remains to be seen, but there's a financial mountain for the studio to climb if it hopes to operate again.
