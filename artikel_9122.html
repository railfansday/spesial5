Pair accused of murdering South African artist and curator Tamlin Blake appear in court as art scene mourns

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

As well as her work as an artist, Tamlin Blake was the chief curator for the Spier Arts Trust, a Cape Town-based organisation dedicated to building sustainable careers for professional artists

Two men accused of the murder of the South African mixed-media artist and curator Tamlin Blake appeared in the Riebeek West magistrate’s court on Wednesday (25 November). Blake was found killed on Sunday at her home in Riebeek-West, a small town 80km northwest of Cape Town. The 51-year-old artist was a well-loved member of the town’s prominent community of artists, among whom she lived for the past 20 years.

The two suspects, Pedro Fraser and Ronnie Von Wielligh, both aged 32, have been charged with murder and possession of stolen goods and will appear in court again on 1 December where they are expected to respond to the charges.

Angry protestors gathered outside the court, demanding justice with some chanting “no bail!”, reported the South African digital news platform, News24.

A representative for the state told the court that the murder was a Schedule 6 offence, which is one of the most serious categories of crime, according to News24. Prosecutors reportedly said she was attacked with a sharp object. Fraser and Von Wielligh will remain in prison until their appearance next Tuesday.

The police tracked down the suspects after tracing Blake’s cellphone, which was allegedly stolen during the attack, a source told another news platform, IOL Media.

“One suspect was arrested in possession of the cellphone. During police questioning, he admitted that he did not commit the offence alone. He then took police to his co-accused,” IOL Media’s source said.

The residents of Riebeek West and neighbouring Riebeek Kasteel are “completely shell shocked” by the news of Blake’s death, a fellow local artist and close friend of Blake’s, Kevan Moses, tells The Art Newspaper. He describes the area as home to many artists, “international, established as well as new” and that it is a “very nurturing creative environment”.

Blake, who was born in Johannesburg, obtained a master’s in fine arts at the University of Stellenbosch in 2001. She has exhibited extensively at home and abroad and is represented by Everard Read Gallery, which has locations in South Africa and London.

Blake was, according to her website, instrumental in developing beading techniques which are now used by various South African artists. Her recent works include tapestries woven from recycled hand-spun newspaper. She was also the chief curator for the Spier Arts Trust, a Cape Town-based organisation dedicated to building sustainable careers for professional artists. There Blake oversaw the acquisition of works of art for diverse collections and lead initiatives that supported artists at pivotal stages of their careers.

“Tamlin was a remarkable artist whose work reflected a deep sensitivity to the natural world and an unwavering commitment to her creative practice,” Everard Read Gallery said in a statement. “Through her distinctive voice and vision, she built a respected career and touched the lives of collectors, colleagues, and fellow artists in South Africa and abroad.

“We are grateful to have worked with Tamlin over many years, and to have shared her work with our audiences. Her contribution to contemporary South African art will be remembered with admiration, gratitude and affection.”

Spier Arts Trust described Blake in a statement as a “visionary artist” who “possessed the rarest gift: a boundless, ego-free generosity that put other people’s growth and dignity at the centre of her life’s work”.

“Those of us fortunate enough to have worked alongside her will forever remember her sharp eye, her quiet grace, her advocacy for authentic, exceptional expression, and the warmth she brought into every studio conversation,” the trust said.

Her friend Moses said: “Tamlin was a great artist, the gentlest person and a quiet thought leader. She was a mentor for many artists. She will be truly missed.”

Blake leaves behind her husband and two daughters, aged 18 and 20.
