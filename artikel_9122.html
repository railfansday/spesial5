Photographer Captures Caribou Carrying Around a Dead Rival in Its Antlers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Photographer Captures Caribou Carrying Around a Dead Rival in Its Antlers

A photographer captured extraordinary images of a bull caribou carrying its dead rival in its antlers.

Warning: The following photos contain graphic content that some may find upsetting.

Professional freelance photographer Peter Mather documented the astonishing scenes while he was filming caribou rut in the mountains around Whitehorse in Yukon, Canada, last month. During the rut, or mating season, male caribou compete for the chance to mate with females, often fighting each other using their antlers.

The photographer made the discovery after flying his drone to investigate a grizzly bear he had spotted near his camp. Mather initially believed the bear had killed a caribou, but he was stunned to discover that two caribou had locked antlers during a fight, leaving one of the animals dead.

“I spotted a grizzly bear at a location above camp on two separate locations, so I flew my drone up there, as I suspected the bear had killed a caribou,” Mather tells PetaPixel. “I was blown away to see that what actually happened was that two caribou had got their antlers stuck together when fighting and one was broken. A red fox and a grizzly were waiting nearby, patiently hoping to feed on the dead caribou.”

Mather says he watched the bull caribou for three days as it struggled to survive with its dead rival still caught in its antlers, while scavengers fed on the carcass.

“I spent three days watching the caribou. When the bear left, I walked up to the caribou a few times and fed it lichen and left water for him to drink,” the photographer explains. “Every day, golden eagles, ravens, and red fox were slowly picking away at the dead caribou, and it was getting smaller and smaller.”

He adds: “Every once in a while the grizzly bear would try and sneak up to eat, but the caribou would stand up and defend itself with its antlers, even though it had another caribou attached.”

Mather says he returned with a friend on the third day to try to free the caribou by cutting off the antlers of the dead one with a hacksaw, but was surprised by what he found from the animal instead.

“The final bits of the dead caribou had been pulled free of the bull. He only had the head and antlers of the dead caribou attached,” the photographer recalls. “When I approached, the caribou was able to stand up and run. The last I saw of him, he was trotting into the mountain with two heads.”

Mather works exclusively in Northern Canada and Alaska, focusing on long-term photography projects that document wildlife and the First Nations communities that have lived alongside these animals for centuries, often returning to the same locations over several years and using camera traps to capture unusual images.

Although Mather never learned what happened to the caribou, he believes the animal’s resilience and the fact that it would soon shed its antlers gave it a good chance of survival.

“I don’t know if he survived, but I suspect he did,” the photographer says. “He was the most resilient animal I’ve ever encountered, and I believe he would drop his antlers in a week or two anyhow. Bull caribou drop their antlers every year, shortly after the rut.”
