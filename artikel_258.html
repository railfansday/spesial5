Avi Davidson lost his left hand as a teenager. Since then, he's cycled through a few prosthetics. But his latest is different—it's wired up to nerves in his forearm and allows him to feel pressure. For the first time in years, he can handle delicate objects like grapes, eggs, or a loved one's hand.

Davidson is the ninth person to have this experimental device implanted, but he's the first to take it home and use it independently. Researchers hope his experiences will reveal how useful the technology really is in day-to-day life, and how it might affect his brain and nervous system.

In May 2025, my colleague David Rotman wrote about how AI could keep us dependent on natural gas. Just 18 months later, I've lost count of how many times he's been proven right.

Just one example: Plans for the Meta data center in Louisiana that David focused on have expanded, and the local utility went from three planned natural-gas plants to 10. Meanwhile, Meta's emissions are up 45% over last year's.
