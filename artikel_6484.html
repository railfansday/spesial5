Why ‘Blackout Wednesday’ Has Become One Of The Biggest Drinking Nights Of The Year

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Thanksgiving Eve is fun and nostalgic—but it can turn risky fast.

Wednesday isn’t typically a massive night for restaurants and bars—but the Wednesday before Thanksgiving is different. Most people have the next day off, and college students or young professionals are typically back in their hometowns for the holiday. What better way to kick off the long weekend than to get off your parents’ couch and hit up the local watering hole?

That’s how Thanksgiving Eve came to be known as “Blackout Wednesday” or “Drinksgiving,” one of the busiest drinking nights of the year. A 2023 study found that in 2022, liquor sales grew by 156% and beer sales increased by 85% on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving. Cocktail orders surged by 69%.

DoorDash has even named the day one of the busiest for alcohol deliveries. Among 10 more traditional holidays like New Year’s Eve and July 4th, Blackout Wednesday ranks right in the middle. “As families and friends return home for the winter holidays, the day before Thanksgiving has become the fifth most popular day for alcohol delivery,” DoorDash’s website reads.

Going out in your hometown is prime time for idly snooping on people from high school, witnessing some truly unlikely pairings (Tyler and Kayla?!). It’s social and nostalgic, but it’s also a day when one drink can easily turn into one too many.

It’s not all fun and drinking games, however. This unofficial holiday marks the start of so-called “DUI Season,” with checkpoints ramping up before Turkey Day. Your risk of coming into contact with a drunk driver also becomes 55% higher than on average days.

Short-term risks of heavy drinking and blacking out include vomiting, seizures, depression, and accidental injuries, as well as next-day anxiety and depression. Long-term, recurring binges are linked to liver damage, high blood pressure, and mental health issues. Alcohol-induced blackouts can also occur more rapidly after just a few drinks, especially if you are taking certain medications, drink on an empty stomach, or have a low body weight.

It’s totally valid to blow off some steam (or get out of chopping celery), just plan it smart. With actually-delicious mocktails, non-alcoholic beers, and ride-shares, there’s very little reason to put yourself—or others—in danger, especially during the holidays.

Hosts and guests alike can benefit from making a plan for keeping everyone safe until well after New Year’s Day by providing plenty of non-alcoholic options at gatherings, opting in on a “Dampsgiving,” checking in with loved ones (and designated drivers) before they hit the road, and always making sure there’s a spot on the couch for anyone who helped themselves to a few too many eggnog espresso martinis.
