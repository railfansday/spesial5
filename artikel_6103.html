American Airlines 737 Carrying US Senator Suffers Engine Failure, Diverts To Orlando

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

An American Airlines Boeing 737-800 carrying US Senator Ashley Moody made an emergency landing at Orlando International Airport (MCO) on Monday, November 16, after experiencing an engine problem shortly after takeoff. Flight AA496 was traveling from Tampa to Washington DC when its pilots declared a Mayday and shut down the aircraft's left engine. The aircraft landed safely, with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) confirming that it has opened an investigation.

The incident involved 178 people aboard the aircraft, which was identified as a 2013-built Boeing 737-800 registered N924NN. Flight-tracking records indicate that the aircraft spent just 47 minutes in the air before diverting to Orlando, where the crew had requested a longer runway and access to American Airlines maintenance facilities. Moody subsequently traveled to Washington on another flight, but missed Monday evening's Senate votes.

Flight AA496 departed Tampa International Airport (TPA) at approximately 1:57 PM Eastern Time. Aerocorner reports that while climbing through 9,300 feet, the crew reported abnormal indications involving the left CFM56 engine and requested permission to level off at 10,000 feet. The pilots began working through their checklists before deciding that the engine needed to be shut down. The aircraft was originally scheduled to complete the approximately 815 mile (1,312 km) journey to Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA).

Approximately three minutes after the initial report, the pilots declared an emergency and informed air traffic control that they were shutting down the left engine. According to ATC recordings reported by People, the crew requested vectors toward Orlando and one of its longer runways. The controller offered a return to Tampa instead, but the pilots maintained their preference for Orlando.

The aircraft remained at 9,700 feet for approximately 17 minutes while the pilots completed their procedures. It subsequently landed on Orlando's Runway 36L at approximately 2:44 PM. The cause of the engine malfunction has not yet been established.

Although the flight had only recently departed Tampa, the crew selected Orlando as their diversion, which sits approximately 81 miles (130 km) east of their departure point. Two factors reportedly influenced the decision: Orlando's longer runways and American Airlines' own maintenance facilities there. The pilots specifically mentioned these advantages during their conversation with air traffic control, suggesting the diversion was driven by operational considerations rather than geographical proximity alone.

Orlando has four runways, including two exceeding 12,000 feet, whereas Tampa's longest measures 11,002 feet. Flight AA496 landed on Orlando's 12,004-foot Runway 36L, providing approximately 1,000 feet of additional runway compared with Tampa's longest. Longer runways can offer greater stopping-distance margins during an abnormal landing, although an engine shutdown does not automatically increase the required landing distance.

Another significant advantage was American's maintenance presence at Orlando. The airport's June 2026 airline services directory confirms that American performs its own line maintenance there, while it has to rely on third-party maintenance providers at Tampa. This would have been critical to the pilots, knowing that if American's own technicians could inspect and repair the aircraft directly, it is likely to return to the air sooner. The airline subsequently dispatched another Boeing 737-800, N917NN, which reached Washington at approximately 7:20 PM.

The aircraft remains grounded at Philip Goldson International Airport while inspections continue.

Under 14 CFR §121.565, when an aircraft engine fails or is shut down to prevent possible damage, the pilot in command must land at the "nearest suitable airport" in terms of time. The regulation permits additional discretion for aircraft with three or more engines, but not twin-engine aircraft such as the Boeing 737-800. Consequently, continuing to Washington was not an option for the American Airlines crew once the engine was shut down.

The regulation does not define suitability solely by distance. Pilots must consider factors such as runway availability, aircraft performance, weather, terrain, and the ability to land safely. It also requires crews to report an in-flight engine shutdown and keep air traffic control informed until landing. Modern twin-engine airliners are designed to fly safely with one engine inoperative, allowing crews to complete checklists and prepare for a controlled landing, as occurred aboard AA496.

Moody is not the first US lawmaker to experience an aviation emergency in 2026. In May, Representative Tracey Mann was aboard an American Eagle regional jet that made an emergency landing in Kansas City after smoke was reported in the cabin. More recently, in September, a Coast Guard aircraft carrying Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin suffered an engine failure and landed safely at Washington Reagan National Airport. Moody, meanwhile, boarded the replacement flight to Washington but arrived after Senate voting had concluded.
