Owning the building made this easy. Minor didn’t need anyone’s money to put its wellness brand inside a Marriott-run hotel. Getting an outside owner to pay for one, and agreeing with another brand on who keeps the client, will be harder.

Minor Hotels has put the first city outpost of its new wellness brand inside a hotel it owns but doesn’t run. It is a test case meant to show other hotel owners that the concept can work under brands other than Minor’s own.

Bangkok Life, which was announced last month, pairs medical diagnostics with traditional medicine, nutrition and fitness. The new offering sits inside The St. Regis Bangkok, which Marriott manages under its St. Regis brand. The hotel is one of several Minor owns but other companies operate.

Thailand-headquartered Minor launched Life Longevity as a standalone wellness brand in October, building out its flagship, Layan Life by Anantara, at Anantara Layan Phuket Resort. Opting for a Bangkok roll-out in a hotel it owns also lets Minor invest its own capital first.

Minor Hotels has opened the first urban location of its Life Longevity wellness brand inside The St. Regis Bangkok — a hotel Minor owns but Marriott operates under its own brand. The move follows Layan Life by Anantara at Anantara Layan Phuket Resort, which Minor spent more than $11 million developing, and replaces the property's former Longevity Hub by Clinique La Prairie. Minor Chief Commercial Officer Ian Di Tullio said the brand was designed from the start to work independently of Anantara, giving Minor the freedom to place it in its own luxury properties or someone else's. Minor still owns or leases almost 70% of its portfolio under what it calls an asset-right strategy, and Di Tullio said that lets the company fund a concept itself, test the economics, and then approach third-party owners. He named three formats for the brand: a resort retreat, an urban hub, and eventually a residence. Di Tullio declined to say whether Marriott approved the concept under its brand standards or shares in revenue, citing confidentiality. Marriott did not immediately respond to Skift's request for comment.
