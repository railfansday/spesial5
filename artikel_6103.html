Hawaii Airport Video Shows Japanese ‘Hostess’ Handcuffed—Then Deported On A Flight Leaving In 30 Minutes

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A Japanese woman screams “Help me!” and “SOS!” as border officers handcuff her at Honolulu airport. Her Hawaii trip ends with a rush to get her onto a flight back to Nagoya leaving in 30 minutes because missing it would mean spending the night in federal detention. Newly circulating footage of the incident comes from Frontline Crime uploaded in July, and is from an episode of Airport Security: USA that appears to have come out two years ago. Officers restrain her as she cries out and insists she’s visiting on vacation. An officer says they were asking basic questions when she accused them of suspecting her of prostitution. He treats that reaction as evidence that she has something to hide. Questioning eventually turns to who paid for her airfare and hotel. She says a man covered the trip, but she doesn’t know where he is and hasn’t contacted him. The narrator first says officers suspect he has ties to a Japanese criminal organization, then reports an organized crime connection. The officer declares she hasn’t overcome the presumption that she intends to immigrate, and tells her she’ll be sent back to Japan because that makes her ineligible to enter as a temporary visitor. If she intended to work in a Hawaii club, even just serving drinks and talking with customers would be a problem because she was entering as a tourist and not someone work authorized. Newly circulating footage of the incident comes from Frontline Crime uploaded in July, and is from an episode of Airport Security: USA that appears to have come out two years ago.

A report on the clip describes the woman as a Japanese ‘hostess’.

View the airport footage shared by Grit Hiro

The woman refused to cooperate with a request to move to secondary inspection. Officers restrain her as she cries out and insists she’s visiting on vacation. An officer says they were asking basic questions when she accused them of suspecting her of prostitution. He treats that reaction as evidence that she has something to hide.

Questioning eventually turns to who paid for her airfare and hotel. She says a man covered the trip, but she doesn’t know where he is and hasn’t contacted him. The narrator first says officers suspect he has ties to a Japanese criminal organization, then reports an organized crime connection. The officer declares she hasn’t overcome the presumption that she intends to immigrate, and tells her she’ll be sent back to Japan because that makes her ineligible to enter as a temporary visitor.

If she intended to work in a Hawaii club, even just serving drinks and talking with customers would be a problem because she was entering as a tourist and not someone work authorized. On Japanese Twitter, Hiroyuki Nishimura mocked the idea that refusing questions and making a scene would get her admitted.

I’ve covered Molly Hill, who said she was strip searched and jailed after flying to Hawaii to visit her boyfriend. She had a ticket home after 88 days. Officials suspected she intended to stay, and she was brought back through the airport in handcuffs on her birthday.

And in 2022, an Australian traveler was detained in Honolulu over an onward-ticket problem. Jack Dunn said he was strip searched, placed in a cell, and left to sleep on a concrete floor with a paper bag for a pillow.
