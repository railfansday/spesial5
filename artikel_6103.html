United Nigeria Airlines Flew Passengers To The Wrong City—3 Years Later, Chairman Admits They Lied About It

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

United Nigeria Airlines flew passengers to the wrong city and blamed the weather. Three years later, they’re admitting it was all a lie. I covered this incident on November 28, 2023, including the conflict between the pilot reportedly acknowledging a wrong flight plan and the airline insisting on a weather diversion. Now, Live and Let’s Fly flags the airline chairman’s admission that the passengers’ explanation was right. Executive Chairman Obiora Okonkwo revisited the episode at the World Public Relations Forum in Abuja on November 17. A dispatcher had entered the wrong destination in the paperwork. The pilots flew there. The flight attendants apparently believed they’d reached the city everyone bought tickets to. He described the weather explanation as “just a lie” and offered this advice: “The best way of public relations is to tell the truth, but tell it responsibly.” Passengers Were Welcomed To Abuja While Sitting In Asaba On November 26, 2023, flight 0504 left Lagos with passengers booked to Abuja, Nigeria’s capital. It landed in Asaba instead, about 200 miles away. This was United Nigeria Airlines, not United Airlines. Passengers reported that the flight attendants welcomed everyone to Abuja but then everyone discovered where they actually were. The plane eventually continued to Abuja. The Investigation Uncovered The Truth The flight used an Airbus A320 operated by Bulgaria’s Fly2Sky for United Nigeria Airlines. Fly2Sky said at the time that United Nigeria had assigned it a Lagos–Asaba flight the previous day and hadn’t requested a change during the pilots’ preflight briefing. The Nigerian regulator identified failures well beyond one person entering a destination incorrectly. In its findings there was: Poor coordination between United Nigeria’s operations center and the company supplying the aircraft. No joint preflight briefing between the pilots and flight attendants. United Nigeria’s failure to monitor the leased aircraft through its operations center. Failure to follow the approved flight plan filed with air traffic control. The chairman story is about a dispatch error. The regulator, though, laid out several opportunities to prevent it. The passengers had bought a flight to Germany but the flight took them to Scotland. A few weeks later, Scandinavian Airlines sent a Copenhagen–Florence flight to Bologna. That was a deliberate decision made before departure because the pilots lacked the correct routing information for Florence. They flew to Bologna with plans to bus passengers onward. It’s just that they kept this information to themselves. The airline’s original statement blamed “poor destination weather”, said the pilot knew about the diversion, and attributed the confusion to an incorrect cabin announcement. The plane eventually continued to Abuja.

The flight used an Airbus A320 operated by Bulgaria’s Fly2Sky for United Nigeria Airlines. Fly2Sky said at the time that United Nigeria had assigned it a Lagos–Asaba flight the previous day and hadn’t requested a change during the pilots’ preflight briefing. The Nigerian regulator identified failures well beyond one person entering a destination incorrectly. In its findings there was: Poor coordination between United Nigeria’s operations center and the company supplying the aircraft. No joint preflight briefing between the pilots and flight attendants. United Nigeria’s failure to monitor the leased aircraft through its operations center. Failure to follow the approved flight plan filed with air traffic control. The chairman story is about a dispatch error. The regulator, though, laid out several opportunities to prevent it.
