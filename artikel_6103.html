Lufthansa Airlines will cut 1,000 full-time jobs at its Frankfurt and Munich hubs, according to a management letter seen by several news outlets. The board said in the letter that "this is the third year in a row of not being financially successful" and that "Lufthansa is currently not competitive in terms of its cost structure, efficiency and productivity," according to DW. The letter, reportedly signed by CEO Jens Ritter and four other board members, also outlines deeper capacity cuts on short- and medium-haul routes, a shift from Airbus A320 jets to the smaller A220, cuts to employee travel benefits, and a restructuring of hub operations to reduce crew overnight stays abroad. The airline posted an operating loss of €180 million ($197 million) in the first nine months of this year, even as the wider Lufthansa Group stayed profitable, with adjusted operating profit up 6% to €1.4 billion in the third quarter on strong premium demand and cost discipline. Fuel is the pressure point: the group now expects a €9.3 billion ($10.2 billion) fuel bill this year, up from €7.27 billion in 2025, amid higher jet fuel prices tied to the conflict in the Middle East. On the revenue side, the letter says Lufthansa Airlines plans to equip 10 aircraft per month with SpaceX's Starlink and monetize onboard internet access. It is unclear when the job cuts would take place.
