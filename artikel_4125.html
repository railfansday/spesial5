Baldur’s Gate 3 Prequel Novel Focused On Shadowheart Officially Confirmed

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Baldur's Gate 3 is so adored because of its companions, the poly troupe of brain-wormed misfits at each other's throats for half the game. Their backstories and inner turmoil are rich worlds worth exploring that rival even the Sword Coast and its legions of the Absolute, and with a cast of equally lovable rogues embracing their roles wholeheartedly outside of the game, it's hard not to get swept up in the hubbub of camp.

Wizards of the Coast understood the appeal, sort of — in the same way the fictional Mattel CEO went around chasing Margot Robbie's Barbie with dollar-signs in his eyes, trying to put them into neat boxes to sell beyond the game with an expanded universe of novel tie-ins. The first of these was the Astarion prequel, which missed the mark so much it triggered Goodreads' anti-spam protection and automatically began deleting negative reviews. Now, it's Shadowheart's turn, and considering how the last book turned out, it's easy to see why fans are skeptical.

The official BG3books website has announced a new prequel from Burning Kingdoms author Tasha Suri, set to release on June 8, 2027.

Crucially, just like with Astarion, there's no mention of the game's writers or Jennifer English, both of whom helped shape this character into the beloved companion that everyone fell head over heels for the second she washed up on the shores of the Nautiloid ship.

"A calculating priestess must investigate a brutal murder in this darkly fantasy [sic]," the description teases. "Her name is Shadowheart. As a devotee of the dark goddess Shar, she knows little more about herself than this. But she does know faith and power, and what it takes to keep them. In the end, all else is detail. Beneath the bustling city of Baldur's Gate and within the secretive underground church she calls home, one's past is a weakness best sacrificed to the fathomless Mirror of Loss. With oblivion comes strength, but without the bonds of memory, treachery festers among even the most ardent of Shar's acolytes.

To be fair, that's a title worth boasting about.

"When a member of their order is killed in the Upper City, Shadowheart and her so-called allies are called to investigate. She trusts none of them: not Mirie, the ambitious Griefguard; not Caldan, the cynical propagandist; and certainly not Nocturne, the charming quartermistress, who claims to have only Shadowheart’s best interests in mind. But the wealthiest district of Baldur’s Gate has secrets of its own, and it hides temptations far greater than any Shadowheart remembers facing. Torn between the familiar comforts of the dark and the thrill of new desire, she must solve the mystery to prove her devotion… or be cast into the light."

Even after Goodreads' alarms were triggered, and the page went into lockdown as 1-star reviews were wiped, the Astarion prequel novel hasn't fared well. As of writing, it has a score of just 2.93 — that might not sound disastrous, but in the world of books, where reviewers are often more generous, that's crushingly low.

Top reviews complain of the "awful depiction of Astarion's character," how his backstory misaligns with the game's canon, and that it was only saved by Neil Newbon's narration in the audiobook. Of course, that doesn't mean an entirely separate novel, from a different author, will be as disastrous, but it's up for debate whether the root cause of its failings were T. Kingfisher, or a WotC committee making asinine decisions — like wanting to remove the Gur from his backstory.

Still, considering Tasha Suri's expertise and history, there's room to be cautiously optimistic. While I admittedly haven't read her work, Burning Kingdoms is said to be a ruthless sapphic fantasy, with the trilogy's conclusion even being a finalist in the 2025 Hugo Award for Best Series. Reviewers often praise Suri's writing of calculating, angry, flawed, and politically sharp women. Her bread and butter is taking women under oppressive religious or imperial systems and letting them claw their way to power, which sounds ideal for a brainwashed Shar worshiper on paper.

But, again, it's unclear where the fault lies with the Astarion prequel, and the lack of involvement from anyone involved in the game, especially Shadowheart's writer, John Corcoran, does warrant concern.
