Nation’s largest grocery chain cuts thousands of jobs — sparks fury over who it’s hiring instead: 'Insanity'

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Fox News Digital spoke to Theo Wold, former White House Deputy Assistant for Domestic Policy under Trump, about the controversy surrounding Kroger’s move to offshore jobs.

Kroger, the largest traditional grocery chain in the nation, is facing strong blowback on social media over its plan to slash 3,000 jobs in the United States while advertising new positions overseas in India.

The controversy comes as the Cincinnati-based grocer begins executing a three-year plan it has previously announced to cut $5 billion in costs as part of a restructuring that will eliminate roughly 37% of its U.S. corporate workforce by 2029.

While Kroger maintains the reductions are aimed at lowering shelf prices through technology and leaner operations, simultaneous recruiting for a new hub in Bangalore has fueled online accusations that the company is offshoring American jobs while asking shoppers to bear high food prices.

"‘They’re doing the jobs Americans don’t want to do,’" Theo Wold, a former White House deputy assistant for domestic policy during President Donald Trump's first term, posted on X. "No. They are taking jobs from Americans because it’s cheaper for corporations to hire foreigners."

In an interview with Fox News Digital, Wold said situations like this need to "start to be an honest conversation about penalizing businesses that take jobs and send them overseas."

Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC) responded on X by saying, "Hire American."

"This insanity has to stop," retired Lt. Col. Buzz Patterson posted on X. "Reshore ALL US companies. We’re rapidly becoming India, and I didn’t serve 20 years defending that!"

"Kroger says these cuts are to ‘lower prices,’" workforce intelligence researcher Amanda Goodall wrote on X. "Classic. Do you think we will see lower prices at the grocery stores? I'm not holding my breath. I'm not a boycott person... but... damn. "

In a statement, a Kroger spokesperson said the notifications "are part of the corporate headcount reduction the company announced in early October to lower prices and improve the shopping experience by simplifying operations and better use of technology." Kroger declined to answer questions regarding how many U.S. roles are being shifted to its new Bangalore capability center, the projected headcount in India or which departments were most affected.

A young man shopping for groceries at the grocery store. (iStock)

The first wave of layoff notices went out to corporate employees last week, marking the initial phase of an overhaul announced in October by CEO Greg Foran that will pare Kroger’s white-collar workforce from roughly 8,200 to 5,200. Frontline store and warehouse employees are not targeted by the cuts.

In an October internal memo announcing the 3,000-role reduction, Foran pointed to expanding artificial intelligence and workflow automation as a central driver for trimming corporate overhead.

At the same time, job boards have listed nearly 30 corporate openings for Kroger’s new Global Capability Center in Bangalore across finance, supply chain, real estate and data analytics. Chief Financial Officer David Kennerley previously told investors the overseas capability center would deliver modest savings in 2026 before generating more substantial cost reductions in 2027 and 2028.
