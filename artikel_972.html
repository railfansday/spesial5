See a tardigrade shed its skin — October’s best science images

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The month’s sharpest science shots, selected by Nature’s photo team.

Gonzalo Quiroga Artigas/Nikon Small World

Gonzalo Quiroga Artigas/Nikon Small World

Just another day in space. NASA’s Astrobee, a cubic robot around 30 centimetres across, is designed to work in the microgravity environment inside the International Space Station (ISS). Each of NASA’s three Astrobees — working autonomously, or controlled by ISS astronauts or Earth-bound flight controllers and researchers —  float around taking on tasks such as drawing up inventories and recording experiments. The robots also function as experimental subjects to test microgravity software and hardware.

The Canadian-built arm of the ISS, Canadarm2, holds a Cygnus XL cargo spacecraft during undocking operations. It was released about 420 kilometres above the North Atlantic Ocean on 9 October.

Sting mechanics. A bee’s stinger was observed in action as it injected its venom, called apitoxin, into a gel that mimics skin1. Fiorella Ramirez-Esquivel, a sensory ecologist at the University of New South Wales in Canberra, and her collaborators detached the stinger from a honey bee (Apis mellifera) and studied the mechanics of its pumping mechanism. They discovered that it involves two collapsible cylinders that act as pistons.

Mourning ritual. In northwestern Namibia, Italian photographer Federico Veronesi followed tracks on a dry river bed to catch a rare glimpse of an elephant carrying a dead calf. His shot was the overall winner of the 2026 Wildlife Photographer of the Year competition, which is developed and produced by the Natural History Museum, London. Researchers have documented a range of mourning behaviours in African elephants (Loxodonta spp.), including carrying and guarding the dead.

A dung beetle (unidentified species) in Zimbabwe’s Zambezi National Park shot by Oscar Whitfield, who won the Young Wildlife Photographer of the Year competition.

Photo naturalist Tony Wu spent three years tracking the behaviour of eels off Japan’s southern island of Kyushu. Eventually he captured two specimens of Abbott’s moray eel (Gymnothorax eurostus) in what could be the first photo documenting their spawning behaviour. He used a red light, because the animals spawn at night and are sensitive to other colours of light. On his blog, he wrote that this was “the most difficult and challenging photographic endeavor that I have ever undertaken”.

Nature photographer Agorastos Papatsanis shone ultraviolet light on the forest floor at the foot of Mount Olympus in Greece to reveal the glowing stems of beautiful bonnet mushrooms (Mycena renati). Many fungi fluoresce under UV light — an effect that mycologists can use to help identify species.

Chilean photographer Francisco Negroni took this image of a dead puma (Puma concolor) displayed as a hunting trophy on a fence in the Patagonia region of Argentina.

The Wildlife Photographer of the Year exhibition is on until 11 July 2027 at London’s Natural History Museum.

Beautiful tech. This unusual view, of droplets of ink being squeezed through nozzles and onto paper during the printing process, won first place in the 2026 Nikon Small World competition. Ole Bielfeldt, an experimental photographer in Cologne, Germany, was partially motivated by his desire to understand how inkjet printers work.

This mesmerizing web is a cross-section of a mouse eye, showing retinal blood vessels and the optic nerve.

A tardigrade moulting and laying an egg in the old skin.

Skin cells from embryonic Xenopus frogs, show cilia (green), centrioles (a type of organelle, red) and cell membranes (white).

The nuclei in this 20-day-old sea urchin larva fluoresce blue to create a doodle-like outline.

A seal louse (Antarctophthirus sp.) attached to a hair from one of the aquatic mammals.

First responders. Rescuers carry an adult female orangutan (Pongo pygmaeus) across a palm-oil plantation in Laman Satong, Indonesian Borneo, on 30 September. Indonesia has been experiencing its worst wildfire season in a decade, blanketing southeast Asia in thick smoke.

Neanderthal hand. A dig in a cave in northern Spain has yielded the remains of a 46,000-year-old Neanderthal, including a well-preserved hand. The findings, which were presented in Tarragona, Spain, on 19 October and have not yet been peer reviewed, included analyses of what researchers described as well-preserved ancient DNA.

Space jellyfish. The 13 October launch of a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida produced this mesmerizing show as the exhaust glistened in predawn sunlight, and was caught by NASA photographer Bill White. The rocket was headed to the ISS on a resupply mission.

Storm vision. As Typhoon Surigae lingered off the coast of Japan on 28 September, the European Space Agency’s Copernicus Sentinel-1 pictured the sea underneath it using polarized radar. The orange in this false-colour image reveals rough surface conditions caused by strong winds; the calmer seas at the eye of the storm are dark blue.

Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2026), processed by ESA

Underwater treasure trove. A stunning 57 new marine species and 5 genera spanning corals, sea cucumbers and crustaceans were identified in just two weeks at an international ‘species discovery’ workshop in Buenos Aires in July. Scientists from across Latin America and Europe gathered to study marine samples, many of which were collected by the deep-sea remotely operated vehicle SuBastian in an expedition led by the Schmidt Ocean Institute to Argentina’s Mar del Plata Canyon in August 2025. The new species include ‘Loki’, a pale sea cucumber with a blob-like body that features two stubby appendages (Peniagone sp.).
