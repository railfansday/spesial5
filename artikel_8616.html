Save 20% of Your Take-Home Pay – or Start at 5% and Work Up

Aim to save about 20% of your take-home pay across retirement, an emergency fund, and other goals. If that much won’t fit your budget yet, start with what you can keep up, even 5%, and add a percentage point or two every few months and with each raise until you reach 20%.

That 20% covers everything you save, not only what goes into a savings account. Add the 401(k) contributions taken out of each paycheck back to your take-home pay, then count them toward the 20%.

It comes from the 50/30/20 rule, which splits take-home pay into 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and debt payments beyond the minimums. For retirement, Fidelity suggests saving at least 15% of your pay before taxes each year, counting any employer match. That rate assumes you start at 25 and retire at 67, and it rises to 18% if you start at 30 with no retirement savings.

Starting below 20% is fine as long as the share you save keeps rising. Save More Tomorrow, a plan designed by economists Richard Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi, has workers commit part of each future raise to retirement savings. At the first company to use it, participants went from saving an average of 3.5% of their pay to 13.6% in 40 months.

Put the money toward one goal at a time, in priority order: a starter emergency fund, enough in your 401(k) to get the full employer match, high-interest debt such as credit card balances, then the rest.

If 20% won’t fit your budget yet, start at 5% of take-home pay or even $25 a paycheck. Sort your spending against the 50/30/20 rule to find out whether needs or wants run over their share. Add a percentage point or two every few months, and put part of every raise toward savings.

Say your take-home pay stays at $4,000 a month. Starting at 5%, or $200, and adding a percentage point every three months gets you to 20%, or $800, in three years and nine months.

Schedule your first automatic transfer to savings for the day your paycheck arrives, and set a calendar reminder three months out to add the next percentage point.
