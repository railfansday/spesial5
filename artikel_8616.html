Before you take your phone abroad, check whether your plan already includes data where you’re going. If it doesn’t, download a travel eSIM from an app, since a week of its data can cost less than two days of Verizon’s or AT&T’s $12 day pass. Turn off data roaming on your U.S. line before you land.

With roaming on and no plan in place, Verizon charges its pay-as-you-go rate of $2.05 a megabyte, more than $2,000 a gigabyte. A $12 day pass from Verizon or AT&T still costs $120 for one phone over 10 days.

Verizon’s Unlimited Ultimate already includes data abroad, and T-Mobile’s Experience More includes 5GB of high-speed data in 215-plus countries and destinations, with 15GB on Experience Beyond. Frequent travelers get data in more than 200 destinations on Google Fi’s Unlimited Premium.

If yours doesn’t, buy a travel eSIM, which leaves your own number on the phone, from an app such as Airalo or Saily. For a couple’s 10 days in Italy, two Airalo eSIMs with unlimited data cost $64, against $240 for Verizon’s TravelPass on both phones, which also covers calls and texts. Calls and messages on WhatsApp or FaceTime use the eSIM’s data at no extra cost. For lighter use, Saily sells 10GB for Italy at $20.99, good for 30 days.

Before you buy, make sure your phone supports eSIM and isn’t locked to your carrier. On an iPhone, the About screen in General settings shows No SIM restrictions beside Carrier Lock when it’s unlocked, and only your carrier can unlock a locked one. Install the eSIM over Wi-Fi at home, then switch it on when you land, with data roaming on for that line only.

A local SIM card bought on arrival works too, but not in an iPhone 14 or later bought in the U.S., which takes only an eSIM. A day pass still makes sense for a trip of a day or two, since it keeps your own number working with nothing to install.

Turning off data roaming on your U.S. line keeps background apps from setting off a $12 day pass, but calls and texts still can. On AT&T, making or answering one call or sending one text abroad adds the pass automatically, as Verizon does on its Unlimited Plus and Unlimited Welcome plans. Call and text through WhatsApp or FaceTime instead.
