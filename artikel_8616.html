Before “cloud” was associated with On’s trainers and Phil Knight cofounded Blue Ribbon Sports, which eventually became Nike, there was Asics. The brand was founded over 75 years ago as Onitsuka Tiger, and would eventually merge with the Asics Corporation in 1977. The Asics “tiger stripes” logo on its shoes derives from the 1950s Onitsuka Tiger Mexico 66 shoe model. Its Gel-Cumulus and Gel-Nimbus legacy models were released in 1999.

Though the company is Japanese, the origin of Asics’s name is actually a Latin acronym, anima sana in corpore sano, for “sound mind in a sound body.” This ethos is exemplified when wearing, running, and racing in an Asics shoe. The brand’s dynamic cushioning provides comfort and a responsive ride for all running experiences. Well-received by wear-testers, we’ve included Asics trainers in multiple buying guides, including Best Running Shoes and the 2026 Shoe Awards.

Runner’s World has offered running advice and gear recommendations since 1966. We assembled a 300-plus-runner local group of varying experience levels, paces, and foot types to test the latest shoe releases and give their honest assessments on fit, comfort, and ride.

The shoes included on this list were all vetted by wear-testers and RW staff. I have also included my own experience testing some of these models during all kinds of workouts and in a couple of races.

RW contributor Michael Charboneau provided insight on Asics shoe tech and answered frequently asked questions about Asics trainers.

It makes this everyday trainer suitable for days when you want to pick up the pace.

Asics has cooked up quite a few proprietary features, and you’ll see many different terms when browsing the company’s running shoe lineup. To help you get more insight into these features, compare models, and find the right shoe, here’s a deeper dive on the main Asics running shoe tech to know.

This tech consists of a squishy, gel-like substance implanted in the midsole, and it absorbs impact forces for a smoother ride. The latest iteration is PureGel, and it’s formulated to be lighter, softer, and more flexible than previous versions of Gel.

Like other shoemakers, Asics has its own proprietary cushioning, called FlyteFoam (FF). Although it’s based on ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA), the traditional running shoe cushioning material, this foam is formulated to be lighter and more resilient than standard EVA. It doesn’t have quite the same springy feel as the other types of FF below, but it works fine for everyday running. It’s used in Asics’s more affordable shoes.

FF Blast Plus is the next level up. Asics hasn’t released details on the chemistry, but it likely includes olefin block copolymers, which make for a lighter, more flexible and more responsive foam. For a pillowy and springy ride that still holds up to everyday training, FF Blast Plus is the cushioning to go with.

Asics has also released an updated version, called FF Blast Max, that has a lower density, so it’s even softer and bouncier. It gives models a very plush ride. For maximum cushioning that doesn’t sacrifice responsiveness, look for training shoes with FF Blast Max.

Asics’s elite racing shoes have FF Turbo Plus foam. It’s made with polyether block amide (PEBA), an exceptionally light, soft, and bouncy material. The brand took this foam up a notch with the even bouncier FF Turbo Squared. Leap foam, which is made of Aliphatic TPU (A-TPU), is a super foam that’s softer and lighter than FF Turbo Squares. It is used in the brand’s top tier racing shoes. The shoes with this type of cushioning are pricey, but provide excellent shock absorption and springy responsiveness for a smooth, propulsive ride that helps you go fast. For race day, it’s definitely the top choice.

Asics takes a holistic approach to stability in its shoes, with several features working together to support the foot and guide it from landing to toe-off. The latest version of its stability feature-set is called the “4D Guidance System.” The 4D Guidance shoes feature a wide sole, a firm foam layer in midsole under the arch, and a flared sole shape at the heel to create a planted feel while counteracting overpronation, or excessive inward rolling motion in your step.

Some models have an earlier version of the system, called 3D Guidance. It has the same general features as 4D, but lacks the firm arch support foam in the midsole. If you know you need stability shoes, or simply prefer a more guided, supportive design, then you should look for these features.

Asics has developed a well-deserved reputation for manufacturing reliable shoes that can hold up through high-mileage training. Core models like the Gel-Cumulus, Gel-Nimbus, and Gel-Kayano have been around for decades (and continuously improved upon in that time), and they’re great picks if you’re looking for everyday trainers.

That said, the brand has also expanded into lightweight, responsive racing shoes and even trail shoes, too.

How do Asics shoes compare to shoes from other brands?

Historically, Asics shoes have been known for using multiple layers of cushioning to create a stable, well-cushioned (and somewhat stiff) ride. Core models like the Gel-Cumulus still exhibit this construction, with a moderate drop and layers of Gel and foam in the midsole for a plush feel that’s great for long runs and training miles.

However, newer shoes in the Asics lineup, like the Novablast, utilize more lightweight, bouncy midsole designs that mirror what other top brands are doing in their premium speedwork and racing shoes.

Has Asics created any key innovations in running shoe tech?

The short answer: Yes, lots of them. Asics has been experimenting with running shoe tech for well over half a century. In 1990, the brand opened the Institute of Sports Science in Kobe, Japan, where the company conducts materials tests and biomechanical research to fine-tune designs.

This lab has produced and refined many of Asics’s hallmark technologies, like Gel cushioning and super foams, like FF Leap and FF Turbo Squared. For runners, this means Asics shoes are built with proven designs that can help you meet your goals, from sticking to a training plan to nabbing a new PR on race day.
