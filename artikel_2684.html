Dylan Harper avoids serious hip injury in relief to Spurs

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Dylan Harper's feet went out from under him with a little more than a minute left, and for a few long minutes it looked like the San Antonio Spurs' red-hot start might go down with him.

Harper has a left hip rotator strain and avoided a major injury, both ESPN and the San Antonio Express-News reported. He is expected to miss from one to three weeks.

The second-year guard got hurt late in the Spurs' 105-93 loss to the Detroit Pistons on Nov. 17. With 1:07 left, Harper stopped to plant on a pass from Stephen Castle, and his feet slid out from under him on what appeared to be a wet spot on the court. Detroit's Ausar Thompson picked off the pass while Harper stayed on the floor, grabbing at his groin.

He was down for about a minute before a Spurs foul stopped play. Teammates and staff had to help him off the court. He had 20 points, four rebounds and two assists in 35 minutes before he left.

The loss ended San Antonio's 12-game winning streak. The way the Spurs talked after the game, they seemed braced for a long absence. Two days later, the mood has changed. The San Antonio Express News reported that Harper walked into the shootaround before Thursday's game against the Los Angeles Clippers without a limp.

The Spurs had already ruled out Harper for the Nov. 19 game against the Clippers because of left hip soreness. It's the first game he has missed this season. He had started and played in all 15 games alongside De'Aaron Fox and Castle in the backcourt.

The Spurs are 12-3 and second in the Western Conference, and Harper has been a big reason why. The No. 2 pick in the 2025 NBA Draft is averaging 18.4 points, 5.4 rebounds and 4.5 assists while shooting 50.5% from the field.

This comes as a dose of good news. The Spurs are talented and deep enough to remain competitive in Harper's absence, especially because of San Antonio's depth at guard.

Harper, 20, had been in the middle of a breakout season, his first as a starter, and has become one of the more promising young players in the NBA.

He excels in transition and does not shy away from getting to the rim, which helps open up space for San Antonio's perimeter shooters, so it's not like his absence won't be felt.

But the Spurs can actually return to the starting lineup that led them to an NBA Finals appearance last season, with Julian Champagnie sliding into small forward to replace Harper.

If Harper remains sidelined past the return of Tobias Harris (calf), that would give San Antonio further flexibility, as Harris is a floor-spacing forward known for his perimeter shooting. Harris is yet to make his Spurs debut, but he would mitigate the loss of Harper, albeit in a very different way.

And while the Spurs love the contributions of Keldon Johnson, they prefer him to come off the bench as the sixth man; don't expect that to change.

All to say: San Antonio should be breathing a sigh of relief because the continued development of Harper is necessary for any championship run.
