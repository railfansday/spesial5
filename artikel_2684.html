Red Sox acquire minor league infielder Jack Pineda in trade with Royals

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

He's Jack Pineda – Jumpin' Jack! – and he originally hails from Albuquerque, New Mexico, where they famously enjoy the bold flavor of mesquite grilled onions, jalapeño relish, and mango lime salsa on their ballpark hot dogs. The Red Sox acquired him last week in a trade with the Kansas City Royals in exchange for pitcher Angel Bastardo, a deal that was mostly triggered by the 40-man roster crunch.

He's a utility infielder who has moved all around the dirt, but he's played more games at short than anywhere else in the minor leagues.

The first thing to know about Jack Pineda is that he's 27-years-old, turning 28 next August. In most media reports about the trade, he's been described as a prospect, owing to the fact that he's yet to debut in the majors. But to be frank, it isn't really possible to be considered a prospect when you're 27. He's older than 15 players who took the field for the 2026 Red Sox. Hell, he's older than Triston Casas. So, unfortunately, Pineda officially is "just a guy" rather than a true prospect.

Having said that, there are some things to like about him, starting with his defense. Obviously we don't have the type of advanced fielding stats in the minors that we do in the big leagues, but scouts view him as above average all over the infield, including at short. And as you would expect of a utility infielder, he has good speed; in 81 games last year he swiped 18 bags and was caught just 4 times.

The issue with Pineda (and this is also something you expect of a 27-year-old minor leaguer) is the bat. Pineda is a free swinger who walked just 3 times in 22 AAA games in 2026. That's not quite Ceddanne-esque (Ceddy ended last season with just 4 walks in his last 50 games — yikes) but it's not that far off. Unfortunately, he's never come close to matching Rafaela's quality of contact.

Last year was the best offensive year of his career, so maybe he found something he can build on. After slashing just .235/.306/.312 in AA in 2025, Pineda returned to the same level to start 2026 and put up a robust .991 OPS+ in 59 games. That earned him his first call-up to AAA where, while still struggling to control the strike zone as much as ever, he did manage to keep his average over .300 and hit 2 homers and 10 doubles in 22 games. That's nice! But, again, we have to remember his age…

Showing scouts that he not only has a strong arm, but that he also has potential 60-grade hair. And considering that hair has been an important part of the Red Sox organizational identity dating back to Johnny Damon and continuing through the 2013 beards, the Sox could use more of that.

It looks like the Red Sox will enter 2027 with two primary shortstop options: the aging, oft-injured, and underwhelming Trevor Story, and the tantalizing but unproven Franklin Arias. And remember: this is coming off a season in which 5 guys played at least 100 innings at shortstop. Three of those five players (Marcelo Mayer, Isiah Kiner-Falefa, and Tsung-Che Chung) are no longer with the organization, while a fourth, Andruw Monasterio, proved to be a defensive disaster. So it's clear why Pineda is here: he's a glove-first depth piece who hopefully won't have to play for the 2027 Red Sox at all, but who could at least hold down the defensive side of the position for a while in an emergency, a la Tsung-Che Chung last July. Anything else he gives the team is a bonus.
