Power-hitter Lee Ivakina continues to be key to UAA volleyball's success as a senior

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Nov. 4—Since arriving on campus two years ago as a junior college transfer, Lee Ivakina has been integral to the University of Alaska Anchorage volleyball team's ability to thrive on the court.

In her first season with the team, she led the Seawolves in kills and points and finished third in digs, and she is on track to repeat all three feats heading into the final two weeks of the 2026 regular season.

Through 24 matches played, Ivakina is the only player on the team with 300-plus points and kills. She is averaging career highs with 3.48 kills and 4.03 points per set.

"She's been amazing for us and she's definitely one of our go-getters," head coach Morgan Hooe said. "Coming in as a junior college transfer is pretty tough to jump in and buy into the culture, but Lee has been that standard and kind of kept us that way."

Ivakina isn't the typical transfer student, as she is originally from Varna, Bulgaria.

With help from a recruiting agency back home in Bulgaria, she began her collegiate career at Navarro College, a junior college in Corsicana, Texas.

"After I was done with JUCO, my coaches helped me contact UAA, and now I'm here," Ivakina said. "I have enjoyed my time here; it's very nice."

Since the part of her home country where she grew up is close to the Black Sea, playing in Texas felt normal, but living in Alaska has been "quite different" and requires her to bundle up a lot more.

"It's getting cold here, so it's quite miserable, but it's been great and I've enjoyed it," Ivakina said.

She takes pride in being a leader on and off the court for the Seawolves as a playmaker and with her words, even though English isn't her first language.

"I love it, and I'm trying to do better every day, honestly," Ivakina said.

Her ability to uplift the team during matches goes beyond just her individual ability to score.

"Even if Lee has an off game, she's always contributing in some other fashion," Hooe said. "Sometimes we have to bring her out of the back row to give her a little break, but for the most part, she's our go-to gal."

Ivakina has recorded double-digit kills in 16 of 24 matches this season. Against Western Washington on Sept. 24, she tied her single-match career-high mark of 25 kills and set a new single-match career-high in points with 27.

"Lee is a phenomenal hitter," senior setter Hannah Trotter said. "She's one of those people who I know I can set her the ball no matter what and she's going to try her hardest to get the kill."

Trotter admires Ivakina's fearlessness and willingness to rise to the occasion when the team needs her the most, whether they're up, down or tied on the scoreboard.

"She's not afraid to hit the ball hard, she's going to make herself an option," Trotter said. "She's one of those hitters that every single ball, she comes to me and says, 'Set me the next ball,' and while I can't give her all of them, when you have someone like that (who) wants it that much for one, themselves and two, the team, I think it's a game changer because she has that competitive nature."

Both Trotter and Ivakina will play their final two home games as Seawolves this week when UAA takes on St. Martin's on Thursday night and Western Oregon on Saturday night.

Although she won't have any family or friends from Bulgaria in attendance when she takes the court in the main gym at the Alaska Airlines Center on Saturday night, Ivakina still expects it to be an emotional experience.

"I'm very, very excited; I'm not ready for senior night," she said. "I love the crowd, and I love playing at home; it's one of the highlights of my stay here."

Trotter also feels bittersweet about her Seawolf career coming to an end, but she's grateful and excited for the team's final two opportunities to prove themselves in front of their home fans.

"It's a fun arena to play in, it's a fun community to be in and it's just a fun environment," she said. "I think when we have things like this, it just kind of shows how special this school and this state are."

The Seawolves will close out the regular season on the road next week and are focused on winning, or at least earning another share of the Great Northwest Athletic Conference title for the second year in a row and qualifying for the Western Regional tournament.

"We're right on the cusp of potentially going to the West Regions, so if we can hopefully do our best for the next several games that we have, maybe we can see ourselves in the West," Hooe said.
