HubSpot is replacing its CEO and CFO as its co-founder returns to 'refound' the company

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Co-founder Brian Halligan takes over from Yamini Rangan, while Karen Burgess of Adobe will succeed Kate Bueker as CFO in February

HubSpot named co-founder Brian Halligan chief executive on Thursday, replacing Yamini Rangan, and announced that Chief Financial Officer Kate Bueker will retire in February 2027.

Rangan is stepping down as CEO and from the board effective Thursday and will stay with the company through Dec. 1 to help with the transition, the company said. Halligan has served on the board since 2005.

Karen Burgess, senior vice president and CFO of Adobe $ADBE's Creativity & Productivity business unit, will join HubSpot on Nov. 30 as executive vice president of finance. She will succeed Bueker as CFO on Feb. 11, 2027. Bueker will move into an advisory role through Dec. 31, 2027. The company described her retirement as long planned.

HubSpot reaffirmed the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 guidance it gave on Nov. 4 in its third-quarter earnings release.

Halligan co-founded HubSpot in 2005 with Chief Technology Officer Dharmesh Shah and served as CEO for 16 years. He plans to reimagine the company across product, go-to-market and culture, the company said.

"This is a refounding, and I can't wait to start!" Halligan said in a statement.

"The Board is united in its support of his refounding of the company," Lorrie Norrington, chairperson of the HubSpot board, said in a statement.

During Rangan's tenure, which began in September 2021, HubSpot's customer count grew from 135,000 to more than 311,000, the company said. Revenue rose from $1.3 billion in 2021 to an expected $3.7 billion in 2026.

"I don't want to see HubSpot swallowed up by a private equity firm or shrink — I want to rebuild it and reimagine it," Halligan told Bloomberg. He suggested HubSpot might have prioritized near-term financial goals over developing its products, according to Bloomberg. "I don't know if you looked at the last earnings, but the numbers aren't great," Halligan said.

HubSpot stock is down 50% so far this year, as of Wednesday.

The leadership change follows a restructuring. HubSpot announced in October that it was eliminating about 660 positions, roughly 7% of its global workforce. Rangan said at the time that the goal was a flatter organization with fewer management layers.
