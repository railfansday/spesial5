Absolute Home Mortgage targets growth through First Option strategic transaction

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Absolute Home Mortgage Corp. announced a strategic transaction with First Option Mortgage in a deal that will combine the lenders, along with Mortgage Equity Partners, into a platform positioned to originate about $4 billion annually after regulatory approval. Absolute Home Mortgage Corp. on Wednesday announced a strategic transaction with First Option Mortgage that positions the combined independent mortgage bank to grow its originations volume. The move was not considered to be a merger or acquisition.

New Jersey-based Absolute said in its announcement that Georgia-based First Option will migrate onto Absolute’s platform and continue to operate as a DBA unit after regulatory approval. The deal also brings First Option executives into Absolute’s ownership and governance structure, a move aimed at building out a dedicated growth engine in a consolidating market.

As part of the transaction, First Option leaders Alvin Shah and Kurt Nikolai will join Absolute’s ownership group and board of directors, pending regulatory approval. Shah will become chief strategy officer, with responsibility for strategic expansion and additional acquisitions that are actively pursuing, according to the announcement.

Combined with Absolute’s wholly owned subsidiary Mortgage Equity Partners, the platform is “positioned to originate approximately $4 billion annually” once approvals are complete, the company said. That level of volume would give the lender additional operating leverage at a time when many independent mortgage banks (IMBs) are looking to scale up or sell.

“This partnership is exciting. We have proven what we can do together in the face of adversity and tough market conditions,” Matthew VanFossen, CEO of Absolute Home Mortgage Corp, said in a statement. “Now we are 100% confident that we are strategically positioned to scale. There is no better time to capitalize on the foundation we have built.”

Data from Modex shows that Absolute Home Mortgage has closed roughly 3,000 loans and $1.13 billion in volume over the past 12 months, with 250 producing loan officers across 33 branches. First Option has produced $398 million across 1,300-plus units and has 91 producing LOs across 11 branches. Massachusetts-based Mortgage Equity Partners has 81 producing LOs across 19 branches, with total volume of $613 million on nearly 1,600 loans in the past year.

Absolute said Shah, Nikolai and VanFossen have worked in “close strategic alignment” for years, operating separate IMBs but maintaining similar approaches to operating models, culture and growth. Bringing these businesses and teams under one platform formalizes the relationship.

“We have been building toward this for years, on the operating model, on the culture and on the vision,” Nikolai said in a statement. “We have been strategically aligned for years, and we have seen firsthand how well our teams work together. Bringing our two lenders together makes natural sense.”

The First Option team will keep its existing leadership and sales culture while continuing to serve borrowers and referral partners in local markets under a DBA structure. That approach mirrors how some companies are structuring IMB rollups — centralized capital, compliance and operations paired with local branding and relationships.

Absolute framed the deal as another step in a longer-term consolidation strategy. The company said it intends to continue acquiring and partnering with independent mortgage companies that want to gain scale while retaining their brands and leadership teams.

Shah said the combined platform is designed to be attractive to other IMBs facing higher costs, thinner margins and increasing compliance demands.

“I am excited about the combined resources of this team and the scaling opportunity it brings to the market,” Shah said. “Scale creates opportunities for other mortgage companies to join our platform and become more profitable. We have the people, the capital alignment and the platform to grow aggressively, and we intend to.”

VanFossen said he expects scale and customer retention to be critical in the next cycle.

“The IMBs that win the next cycle will be the ones that operate at scale, retain their customers, and move decisively when the market opens up,” he said. “That is exactly what we are building. First Option is a major step, and we are not done. We intend to be the platform of choice for independent lenders who want to grow.”
