Uber's revenue growth slowed as driver shortages weighed on ride-hailing

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Mobility revenue fell 2% in the quarter, while Uber forecast fourth-quarter gross bookings growth of 18% to 22%

Uber $UBER reported third-quarter revenue growth of 10%, to $14.8 billion, as revenue in its Mobility ride-hailing segment fell 2%.

Uber did not have enough drivers to satisfy demand in markets including India, Brazil and Canada, and costlier fuel plus the Middle East conflict added to the strain, according to Reuters.

The 10% increase was the slowest revenue growth since the first quarter of 2021, according to Reuters. That compares with growth of 14% in the first quarter and 12% in the second.

Uber said business model changes reduced total revenue growth by 8 percentage points. Those changes were in the U.K., according to The Wall Street Journal.

"Elevated fuel prices and tighter driver availability weighed on supply growth in several international markets," Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said in prepared remarks, according to Reuters. Tighter immigration flows hurt Canada, where supply falls short of demand, he said.

Uber is putting money into recruiting, keeping and winning back drivers, and said its Mobility business picked up in October, according to Reuters. The Middle East conflict took 75 basis points off Mobility bookings growth in the quarter, and Uber builds in a 100-basis-point reduction for the final three months of the year.

Gross bookings rose 20% to $59.8 billion. Mobility bookings grew 18%, Delivery 22% and Freight 35%. Delivery revenue rose 23% and Freight revenue 34%. Trips grew 16% to 4.1 billion.

"Uber delivered its fifth consecutive quarter of 20%+ Gross Bookings growth, driven by record audience and consumer engagement," Khosrowshahi said in a statement.

Net income attributable to Uber was $512 million, down from $6.6 billion a year earlier. The year-ago figure included a $4.9 billion tax benefit. The latest quarter carried a pre-tax net loss of $476 million tied to repricing the company's equity investments. Non-GAAP earnings per share rose 35% to $0.88.

For the fourth quarter, Uber expects gross bookings between $63.25 billion and $65.25 billion, which would be growth of 18% to 22% from a year earlier after adjusting for currency swings. It expects non-GAAP earnings per share of $0.95 to $0.99, up 34% to 39%.
