Building-Permit Woes Tip Leading Economic Indicators Negative

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Leading economic indicators for the month of October took a slight downturn, as the latest Leading Economic Index (LEI) report from business research organization The Conference Board recorded a 0.3% drop to 99.3 from September.

The LEI is a predictive tool designed to anticipate the direction of the economy in the coming months. It is based on 10 component indicators, which, for example, include measures of average consumer expectations and the average weekly initial claims of unemployment insurance.

Justyna Zabinska-La Monica, senior manager of Business Cycle Indicators at The Conference Board, said that “The US LEI’s decline in October was mostly fueled by fewer building permits.”

Recent data on building permits has indeed been dismal. This month’s New Residential Construction report, for example, showed that permits were down 7.4% month-over-month and 8.7% year-over-year.

Zabinska-La Monica, for her part, emphasized that “(b)uilding permits data were at the weakest level since May 2020…Compared to September and one year ago, permits declined across all categories, including by type of dwelling and region. The only exception was the Midwest, where total permits were up slightly since September and 8.1% over the last 12 months.”

Zabinska-La Monica also singled out “consumer expectations for business conditions, average hours worked in manufacturing, and manufacturers’ new orders for nondefense capital goods” as “negative contributors” to October’s LEI. “Weakness in these components more than offset gains in others,” she explained.

The other two major indices in The Conference Board’s report, the Coincident Economic Index (CEI) and Lagging Economic Index (LAG), were both unchanged (apart from some minor September data revisions mentioned in the report).

The CEI, in contrast to the LEI, is considered a summary of indicators on the current state of the economy. The LAG serves as an after-the-fact confirmation of the state of the economy.

The overall outlook of The Conference Board, despite some disheartening October data, is still seemingly positive. Zabinska-La Monica noted that “the LEI’s six-month growth rate remained positive,” which “indicat(es) continued economic expansion ahead.” This economic expansion, however, will be “increasingly investment-led with softer household demand.”
