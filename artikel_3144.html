GE Vernova vs. Vistra: Which Industrials Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

GE Vernova boasts a strong balance sheet, while Vistra trades at a lower valuation but carries significant leverage.

GE Vernova (GEV +0.47%) and Vistra (VST +10.55%) both play critical roles in powering the global economy. As energy demand rises, choosing between these two leaders requires a close look at their different business models.

GE Vernova focuses on the technology and equipment required to generate and move electricity worldwide. In contrast, Vistra is a major power producer and retail provider serving millions of customers in the United States. While both benefit from the growing energy needs of data centers, they offer distinct risk and reward profiles for investors.

GE Vernova builds and services the equipment that makes electricity possible, ranging from gas and wind turbines to nuclear reactor technology. The company serves major global utilities and hyperscalers, maintaining an installed base that generates roughly 25% of the world's electricity. It also collaborates with Hitachi (HTHIF +1.54%) on the development of small modular reactors (SMRs) to meet future energy needs through nuclear power.

In its latest annual report covering FY 2025, GE Vernova reported revenue of nearly $38.1 billion, representing growth of roughly 8.9% over the prior year. The company achieved net income of close to $4.9 billion, resulting in an impressive net margin of approximately 12.8%. This performance reflects a steady improvement in profitability as the company scales its electrification and power segments.

Vistra operates as an integrated retail electricity and power generation company, selling both electricity and natural gas to approximately 5 million customers. Its diverse portfolio includes nuclear power, natural gas, and battery storage assets that support large load facilities like oil field operations and data centers. Key retail brands under its umbrella include TXU Energy and Dynegy Energy Services.

For FY 2025, Vistra generated revenue of approximately $17.7 billion, which was a 3% increase compared to the previous fiscal year. Net income for the period reached roughly $944 million, yielding a net margin of close to 5.3%. While these figures are lower than the prior year's results, Vistra remains a significant force among electric utility stocks today.

GE Vernova faces risks related to supply chain disruptions and the volatile costs of critical materials used in its turbines. The company often operates under long-term, fixed-price contracts, which can lead to project losses if labor or material expenses rise unexpectedly. It also faces intense global competition from other equipment manufacturers like Siemens Energy (SMEGF +3.67%) and Mitsubishi Electric (MIELF +2.54%).

Vistra is highly exposed to the price volatility of wholesale power and fuel sources, including natural gas and uranium. The company must also navigate complex regulatory environments regarding carbon emissions and climate change policies. Additionally, its high level of indebtedness requires disciplined financial management to maintain liquidity and meet collateral requirements in competitive energy markets.

Vistra presents a lower entry point based on its earnings multiples, while GE Vernova carries a premium.

Global electricity demand increased by 3% in 2025 compared to 2024, and that trend is only going to continue. And as that demand increases, GE Vernova and Vistra are each positioned to benefit. Both stocks are worth considering as investments, and each could fit together into a portfolio. But if I only had to choose one, I would choose GE Vernova. With increased power demand, GE Vernova serves more of a pick-and-shovel role, selling the infrastructure and components needed for power supply. That leaves it less exposed to regulatory risks compared to if it was directly supplying power.

It is also a quiet player in the nuclear energy space, working on an SMR project with Hitachi. Its BWRX-300 reactor is under construction in Canada, and it's expected to be completed by the end of 2029, with commercial operation before the end of 2030. It's once again positioning itself to be in the right place at the right time, as Precedence Research forecasts that the global nuclear reactor market is expected to grow from $31.5 billion in 2026 to $50.3 billion by 2034.

Also, GE Vernova's market it can serve is larger, as evidenced by its revenue and net income compared to Vistra. Also, its backlog alone, as of the third quarter of 2026, is $197 billion. Adding all that up- from its pick-and-shovel nature, which can avoid certain risks, to being at the forefront of building SMRs, to its backlog- I would rather own GE Vernova.
