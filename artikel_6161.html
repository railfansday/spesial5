KLM to Cut Workforce by Nearly 15% in €1 Billion Cost-Saving Drive

Despite improving profits, the Dutch airline says its future is far from guaranteed, with big changes needed to bring costs under control and restore competitiveness.

KLM is preparing to shrink its workforce by almost 15% as part of a major restructuring aimed at cutting costs and improving its long-term competitiveness.

The Dutch flag carrier announced the measures on Thursday alongside parent company Air France-KLM's third-quarter earnings. Around 5,000 positions have already been or will be affected, with jobs eliminated, outsourced or transferred within the wider airline group.

The overhaul aims to deliver an additional €1 billion ($1.1 billion) in annual earnings improvements, bringing the total targeted improvement to €1.4 billion.

Speaking on Thursday, Marjan Rintel, KLM's CEO, delivered a stark message about the need for change: "KLM's future is not guaranteed… That's why, at this pivotal moment for KLM, we're taking far-reaching, sometimes painful measures. Every department and every division will be asked to contribute."

KLM will cut, outsource, or transfer about 5,000 positions — close to 15% of its workforce — in a restructuring announced Thursday alongside Air France-KLM's third-quarter results. The plan targets an additional €1 billion ($1.1 billion) in annual earnings improvements, lifting the total goal to €1.4 billion. Roughly 1,250 catering jobs have already moved to Gategroup, 800 ground services roles are expected to go to third-party providers at Schiphol and for KLM Cityhopper, and 2,000 positions, including 1,500 at contact center Cygnific, will transfer to parent Air France-KLM. Another 1,000 reductions are planned through productivity gains, and KLM is seeking union agreements covering at least an 8% productivity improvement by 2028 and restrained wage growth. The fleet is being resized too: A321neos stay, Embraer E195-E2s replace previously planned A320s, and older 737s and E190s will be phased out, giving Cityhopper a bigger role in Europe. The cuts come despite improving numbers — KLM's third-quarter operating profit rose €106 million to €458 million, and group operating profit hit €1.35 billion, sending shares up about 12% — with CEO Marjan Rintel saying the cost base remains too high and that "KLM's future is not guaranteed."
