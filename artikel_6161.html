Air India CEO Tewolde Gebremariam thinks the carrier has been "punching below its weight." He is now planning for the airline he wants it to become, not the one it is today.

Air India is looking to increase its existing widebody aircraft orders as it seeks to expand its international network and regain market share from foreign carriers.

CEO Tewolde Gebremariam said the airline is in discussions with Airbus and Boeing about adding more widebody aircraft to support its long-term ambitions.

“We want to increase our order on widebody more,” Gebremariam said at the Aviation India and South Asia 2026 conference in New Delhi on Tuesday.

Despite an existing large-aircraft order book, he said Air India’s international ambitions require more capacity. Air India has 600 aircraft on firm order, of which 80 are widebodies. He said airlines must order aircraft years in advance to have capacity when demand arrives.

“Air India has been punching below its weight in the market. That is going to change,” he said.

Air India CEO Tewolde Gebremariam said the airline wants to add more widebody aircraft to its order book, even as the group posted consolidated losses of more than INR 220 billion ($2.3 billion) in fiscal 2026 — more than double the prior year. Speaking at the Aviation India and South Asia 2026 conference in New Delhi, Gebremariam said Air India has 600 aircraft on firm order, of which 80 are widebodies, and is in discussions with Airbus and Boeing about more. The airline is also rethinking its hubs: Pakistani airspace closure has hit Delhi harder than Mumbai, so more Europe and North America flying moves to Mumbai, Delhi grows eastward, and Bengaluru is under consideration as a third hub. Costs are the immediate problem. "Our cost is greater than our revenue," Gebremariam said, and a cross-functional team is looking for savings and shared corporate functions with Air India Express. He gave no timeframe for a return to profitability. The growth target is clear: win back third- and fourth-freedom India-international traffic currently flown by foreign carriers, grow international-to-international connections from about 10% of the business, and push the full-service Air India brand further upmarket while Air India Express holds the low-cost segment.
