Senators still see NDAA as chance for online safety legislation

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Sen. Josh Hawley, R-Mo., delivers his opening statement during the hearing on Tuesday. (Bill Clark/CQ Roll Call) (Bill Clark CQ Roll Call)

Online safety advocates are looking to the annual defense policy bill for action on legislation to expand accountability for online platforms in cases of child sexual exploitation, as they also introduced legislation to make sure those cases aren’t forced into private arbitration.

Senators and advocates expressed support Tuesday for legislation that would expand the ability of victims of online sexual exploitation to sue social media platforms at a hearing focused on dangers children face on the gaming platform Roblox.

The Senate Judiciary Committee’s panel on Crime and Counterterrorism held the hearing after its Chairman Josh Hawley, R-Mo., and ranking member Richard J. Durbin, D-Ill., introduced a bill Monday that would ban pre-dispute arbitration agreements from affecting cases where a minor was harmed online, including by sexual exploitation.

Hawley and Durbin also worked together on a separate bill to expand eligibility for victims of abuse to bring civil suits against online platforms that intentionally, knowingly or recklessly promoted or facilitated the abuse. It would also include penalties for certain platforms that fail to report child pornography violations on their sites.

That bill is included in a manager’s amendment to the Senate’s fiscal 2027 National Defense Authorization Act bill. Durbin encouraged Congress to pass the NDAA “in the closing days” of the session because “protecting children is an important part of it.”

“I have a few weeks left in my service in the United States Senate,” said Durbin, who is retiring at the end of his term in January. “I’m thinking each day it’s coming to an end, but I want it to come [to an] end the right way.”

The subcommittee has spent much of the 119th Congress focused on harms to children online, including from artificial intelligence chatbots.

Tuesday’s hearing included testimony from survivors and experts on child exploitation on Roblox.

Hawley said that victims are bound by pre-dispute agreements with Roblox to settle claims through private arbitration, rather than in court.

“They slam the courthouse door shut in their faces,” Hawley said. “We’re talking about children now, child victims of sexual exploitation online are denied their day in court by the very company that has exposed them to the content to begin with.”

A spokesperson for Roblox said in a statement that “safety has been central” to the platform since its launch.

“We require age checks for chat, place users under 16 in age-based accounts with protective defaults, and block images and video in chat,” the spokesperson said. “A legal position about arbitration is not a measure of a company’s safety record, and a hearing built around it fails to advance safety.”

Myriam Gilles, a professor of law at Northwestern University, told the panel that a 2022 law that ended forced arbitration agreements for victims of sexual assault has gaps. She said Roblox has argued it does not apply to online actions, just “completed physical assault” and does not apply to platforms.

She also said that Hawley’s new arbitration bill would enhance the effects of his measure regarding civil suits by making sure those suits can be heard publicly if families so choose.

“Congress can pass laws, but if [suits] just get shunted into private arbitration, they’re doing no good in the world,” Gilles said. “A right to sue that ends in secret arbitration is a right in name only.”

The hearing came the same day that advocates, including reality TV personality and activist Paris Hilton, held a press conference to urge House action on a bill (S 1837) that would allow individuals to bring civil suits over nonconsensual intimate depictions generated by artificial intelligence or for possession or production of deepfake porn if there was also intent to disclose the images.

That bill passed the Senate in January but has not been taken up in the House.
