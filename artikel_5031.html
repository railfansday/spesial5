Bitcoin’s next crash may come from holders, not leverage, as $10.7 billion gets liquidated

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Futures exposure has contracted sharply, but persistent ETF withdrawals and worsening holder profitability could keep Bitcoin under pressure.

Bitcoin futures open interest fell 12% last week to $29.3 billion, its lowest level since early March, according to Glassnode's Nov. 23 Market Pulse.

Over the same week to Nov. 22, the ratio of short-term-holder supply to long-term-holder supply rose from 15% to 15.5%, its highest since late February. Leveraged traders have thinned out, while recent buyers hold a larger share of the coins, leaving it unclear whether forced selling has ended or shifted to spot holders.

DWF Labs' coin-denominated data showed open interest falling from 712,800 BTC on Nov. 13 to about 639,900 BTC, a 10.2% decline over a slightly different window, which points to genuine position reduction.

Long-side funding payments fell 53.6% to $803,900, so traders pay roughly half as much to hold bullish perpetual positions. Perpetual cumulative volume delta (CVD) moved from negative $1.5 billion to negative $486.5 million, and spot CVD from negative $170.8 million to negative $120.9 million.

Both readings stayed negative, which describes slower net selling.

US dollar value of outstanding futures positions at the two weekly readings in Glassnode's Nov. 23 report. The decline reflects both Bitcoin's price and changes in outstanding contracts.

Source: Glassnode — BTC Market Pulse, Nov. 23

DWF Labs counted over $4.9 billion in crypto-wide liquidations in the week to Nov. 22, including $3.75 billion in longs, about 76%.

The prior week brought nearly $5.8 billion, so roughly $10.7 billion cleared in two weeks. Fewer leveraged positions mean a falling price has less collateral to wipe out. Options traders continue to pay up for protection, as the 25-delta skew rose to 17.4% from 14.4%, its highest since mid-July.

Glassnode's ratio compares short-term-holder supply with long-term-holder supply, a different measure from short-term holders' share of all coins.

The firm classifies entities by average coin age, with a smoothed transition around 155 days, so the ratio can rise as long-term supply falls, as short-term supply grows, or both. A 0.5-point rise is modest, but it arrived while prices fell and futures positions shrank.

Profitable supply fell 6.9%. Both readings come from Glassnode's Nov. 23 report and are rounded to one decimal place.

Source: Glassnode — BTC Market Pulse, Nov. 23

Supply in profit dropped from 54.2% to 47.3%, leaving 52.7% of circulating supply at a loss or near breakeven.

Short-term-holder net unrealized profit and loss sits at negative 18.3%, its lowest since mid-June. Realized capitalization contracted 0.93% over the month, against 0.06% a week earlier, its weakest reading since July.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.7 billion in the week, the heaviest outflow since early July, and Glassnode puts ETF MVRV at 0.97, which places the average ETF holder below estimated cost basis.

Glassnode's Nov. 17 report supplied the $62,000 to $66,000 cluster where many recent buyers entered, the $74,100 short-term-holder cost basis, the $58,500 June low and the $53,700 realized price as key levels.

From a price near $63,000, $74,100 sits 17.6% higher and $53,700 sits 14.8% lower.

If Bitcoin holds the $62,000 to $66,000 cluster while spot CVD turns positive, ETF outflows reverse, and realized capitalization climbs toward zero, the derivatives reset becomes the base for accumulation.

A move toward $74,100 would then test whether recent buyers can reach breakeven and avoid a rush to sell, while futures positions rebuild gradually behind spot buying.

If Bitcoin fails to hold that cluster while ETF outflows continue and realized capitalization keeps contracting, sellers are holders closing losses, and thin futures positioning offers little cushion.

A break below $58,500 would put the $53,700 realized price in view, and both paths are conditional scenarios built on Glassnode's reference levels.

The derivatives half of the reset is complete, while the spot half awaits evidence of buyers large enough to absorb coins that holders sell at a loss.
