Bitcoin ETFs Shed $582M in a Day as BTC Drops Below $62K

Bitcoin slid below $62,000 on Friday, extending a weeklong sell-off as investors pulled money from spot Bitcoin ETFs and braced for another interest rate hike from the Federal Reserve.

The leading cryptocurrency was trading at around $61,780, down 2.8% over the past day and 10% over the past week, after touching a 24-hour low of $61,273—its lowest level since early July—according to CoinGecko data. It has fallen more than $2,000 since struggling to hold $64,000 earlier this week, and has also formed a so-called death cross, a bearish chart pattern.

The drop triggered $727 million in liquidations across crypto markets over the past 24 hours, about 86% of which were long positions betting on higher prices, according to CoinGlass. Bitcoin accounted for just under $202 million of the total, with more than 151,000 traders liquidated.

Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $582.2 million in net outflows on Thursday, their largest single-day outflow since October 30, according to SoSoValue data. The funds have lost more than $1.1 billion so far this week and almost $3.5 billion in November, while their total net assets have fallen to $76.6 billion.

"Within crypto, the most telling sign is this month's outflow from spot Bitcoin ETFs, which suggests investors are choosing to stay on the sidelines for now," Neil Steinhardt, head of Nexo U.S., told Decrypt.

Treasury yields above 5% and expectations that the Fed will raise rates in December have weighed on assets that don't pay interest, including Bitcoin and gold, Steinhardt said. "Because the pressure appears tied to the rate backdrop, and that backdrop has shifted before, a turn in yields or the dollar would likely be the first signal that appetite is returning," he added, pointing to the December 4 jobs report and the Fed's December 9 meeting as the next checkpoints.

Fed Governor Christopher Waller has called for a larger, half-point increase at that meeting, unless policymakers signal another rise in January. Traders are betting on a smaller move: CME FedWatch puts the odds of a quarter-point hike on December 9 at 99.2%, with the chance of a half-point increase falling to almost zero from more than 9% on Thursday morning.

The decline has left the average Bitcoin holder only just in profit. Bitcoin is still trading above its realized price, the average price at which every coin on the network last moved, but the margin is thin, onchain analytics firm Glassnode tweeted on Friday. A break below would put holders in loss for the first time since early 2023, it said.

CoinShares head of research James Butterfill sees the slide as part of a longer downturn. "I see this as a continuation of the existing bear market rather than the start of another," he said in a note published on Thursday, pointing to selling by large holders since late 2025, expectations of higher rates and weakness in tech stocks.

CoinShares' base case is for Bitcoin to trade between $60,000 and $90,000 through much of 2027, until falling inflation gives the Fed room to ease, Butterfill said.
