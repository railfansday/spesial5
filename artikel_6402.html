Benchmade Just Knocked 20% Off Three of Its Most Popular Knives for Black Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Yes, that includes the Osborne and the Bugout.

We’re constantly testing and writing about pocket knives here at Popular Mechanics, and Benchmade is a brand that always tops our list of favorites. Its folders, automatics, flippers, and fixed blades are beautifully designed using all high-quality materials, and all are U.S.-made in its Oregon headquarters. But there’s no denying they’re pricey, costing upward of $200, and they rarely go on sale for much of a discount.

But right now, ahead of Black Friday, Benchmade is knocking 20 percent off three of its most popular knives on Amazon—the Osborne, the Bugout, and the Bailout—for a savings of roughly $70 each.

When the Osborne debuted in 2000, it was Benchmade’s first blade to sport the Axis lock, which been replicated in some form by nearly every major knife manufacturer since its patent expired in 2019. It’s designed for ambidextrous operation and fast, one-handed deployment, plus it keeps your fingers clear of the blade while you open and close the knife.

This seasonal version uses CPM-S90V stainless super steel, which offers great edge retention and reliable toughness for everyday tasks like opening boxes. It’s coated with a protective distressed finish to make the reverse tanto-style blade look rustic and has a rich-looking dark-green aluminum handle, with copper accents. It feels good in hand—a durable yet lightweight EDC knife.

The Bugout is the knife our resident expert Justin Park grabs first for backpacking, camping and hunting and says it’s “arguably the defining pocket knife of the explosion of modern EDC blades.” It’s strong, but ultra lightweight and hardly registers in your pocket or pack.

Like the Osborne above, the Bugout uses S90V stainless super steel, featuring a shorter drop-point blade, copper accents, and handle that combines the same dark-green aluminum with a grippy and rugged-looking canvas micarta.

The Bailout is lightweight and tough. It has a longer drop-point tanto-style S90V stainless steel blade covered in the same stonewashed protective finish as the Bugout and Osborne, along with the same dark-green aluminum handle and copper accents. While it’s another great choice for EDC, the Bailout is outfitted with tactical-style elements, like the lanyard slot at the rear and the deep-carry clip.
