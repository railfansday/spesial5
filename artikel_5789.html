Mullin defends TSA shoes-on policy after congressman calls classified briefing ‘very concerning’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin defended TSA’s shoes-on policy after Rep. Eli Crane said a classified inspector general briefing raised serious concerns.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin defended letting travelers keep their shoes on at airport checkpoints Wednesday after an Arizona congressman pressed him on why the TSA policy remains in place despite a classified watchdog review.

"We have never put anyone at risk at all," Mullin told Rep. Eli Crane, R-Ariz., at a House Homeland Security Committee hearing.

Mullin said the department evaluates screening technology daily and that he had personally met with DHS Inspector General Joseph Cuffari in a secure facility.

"In fact, we addressed three of the four concerns," Mullin said. He said the fourth, in his view, had nothing to do with security.

Mullin gave no specifics about what DHS had done to address the three concerns or what the fourth involved.

Crane said he had received a classified briefing from the inspector general and called it "very concerning." He asked Mullin to discuss the matter privately.

The exchange put renewed scrutiny on a July 2025 decision by then-Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem to end routine shoe removal at TSA checkpoints nationwide.

Travelers move through a TSA security checkpoint at an airport. (iStock)

Noem said at the time that advances in screening technology would allow the agency to speed up lines while maintaining security. TSA officers could still ask travelers to remove their shoes for additional screening.

Within weeks of the change, the inspector general sent DHS and TSA leaders a formal warning about a screening weakness, according to questions entered into the Senate record in July.

The watchdog issued its final classified report on Nov. 1, 2025. In a March memo reviewed by CBS News, Cuffari said his office was still waiting for the agencies’ formal response after the 90-day deadline had passed.

The Senate record also shows that Sen. Maria Cantwell, D-Wash., asked TSA in April to reverse the policy until the screening weakness was addressed. In a May response cited by Cantwell, then-acting TSA Administrator Ha Nguyen McNeill said "current intelligence and risk assessments indicate no direct, credible threats related to shoes."

Mullin’s testimony Wednesday left open whether the inspector general had signed off on the steps DHS says it took.
