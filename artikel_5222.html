American Bitcoin reported approximately $126 million in third-quarter net income and said its Bitcoin reserve increased through mining rather than new spot purchases during the period. The November 4 release also cites about $221 million in adjusted EBITDA and an approximately 5% increase in satoshis per share. Those figures put the focus on an increasingly important distinction among public Bitcoin businesses: the difference between growing a treasury and growing the amount of Bitcoin attributable to each share.

The company says it did not issue shares through its at-the-market program or purchase Bitcoin during the quarter. Instead, management credits mining operations with reserve growth despite seasonal electricity cost pressure. It also highlights a roughly 5% increase in sats per share. This metric is worth checking because a rising total Bitcoin balance can be less impressive if the share count rises even faster. Yet sats per share is not an accounting profit measure or a guarantee of shareholder return. Investors also need to consider debt, other assets and liabilities, operating expenses and the market price paid for the stock.

American Bitcoin reported net income and adjusted EBITDA of different magnitudes. Adjusted EBITDA can help explain management's view of operating performance, but exclusions and accounting policies matter. In mining and treasury companies, the price of Bitcoin, the valuation of held assets, depreciation of mining equipment and financing arrangements can all materially affect results. A single strong quarter should therefore be read beside the company's complete financial statements and reconciliation of non-GAAP figures. Comparing adjusted EBITDA from two miners is difficult unless the underlying definitions are aligned.

Management describes producing Bitcoin at a structural discount to market prices. Miners can achieve attractive unit costs when power contracts, fleet efficiency and site economics are favorable, but network difficulty and Bitcoin's price constantly change the equation. Electricity costs can be seasonal; new mining capacity can increase competition; older machines can become less efficient. A production model that looks profitable today may be less attractive after a difficult market shift. It is useful to track the cost of mining one coin, but readers should ask exactly which direct and indirect costs are included.

Public shareholders own claims on a company, not specific coins in its reserve. That provides exposure to mining operations and management decisions as well as the Bitcoin held. The company can create value through efficient production and disciplined capital allocation, but it can also face stock-market volatility unrelated to the daily Bitcoin move. The most meaningful comparison with direct BTC ownership is not merely whether its reserves rise, but whether shareholders benefit after dilution, corporate costs and valuation premiums are considered.

The next quarter should be assessed using mined BTC, treasury changes, production cost, network difficulty, share count and cash liquidity. The company's earnings release is a useful snapshot, but it does not make future mining profitability predictable. In a sector where several operators are selling Bitcoin to fund AI data centers, American Bitcoin's stated emphasis on accumulating coins provides a distinct business model for investors to evaluate.
