Google's new model beats its pricier one and is just as impressive

Google just launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen, and it could be one of the biggest upgrades to its free AI chatbot yet.

The new model, announced Thursday, is designed to be faster, more accurate and better at following instructions. It even edges out Google's more expensive Gemini 4 Argon on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, scoring 51 compared with Argon's 50 at Hydrogen's default high reasoning setting.

But the real reason to pay attention is what Hydrogen could mean for everyday Gemini users.

Google says the model is better at handling complicated requests, working with information across different apps and producing useful results without endless follow-up prompts. The company also claims it reduces fabricated information by more than fivefold compared with Gemini 3.8 Flash in its evaluations.

And unlike Google's most powerful Gemini models, Hydrogen is available to free users, although there's a catch. You won't necessarily be able to select it manually without a subscription. Instead, Google's automatic model selection determines when Hydrogen is appropriate for your request.

Here are five ways to put Google's newest AI to work.

Most AI models are good at summarization and pulling out the most important points from documents. But Google says this model in particular can process dense PDFs, spreadsheets, charts and other complicated material, pulling relevant details into organized reports and summaries. So, it doesn't only summarize, but it can then implement the information into something you can use. That means you could upload a lengthy insurance policy, school handbook or appliance manual and ask Gemini to find the information you actually need.You can also ask follow-up questions without having to explain the document all over again.

Prompt to try: Read this document and explain the 10 most important things I need to know in plain English. Highlight any deadlines, fees, restrictions or potential problems. Include page references for every important finding and create a table of anything that requires action.

One of the biggest frustrations with AI writing is getting something that sounds natural rather than a generic corporate announcement.

Google says Hydrogen is significantly better at following detailed instructions about tone, length, formatting and style. In theory, that means less time rewriting the same email five different ways.

Try giving it a difficult writing task, such as responding diplomatically to an unreasonable request or explaining something complicated without sounding condescending.The key is giving Gemini specific rules about what you want and what you don't want.

Prompt to try: Help me write a friendly but firm email declining a request. Keep it under 150 words. Make it sound warm, confident and conversational, not overly formal. Avoid corporate jargon, unnecessary apologies and long introductions. Explain my decision clearly and end on a positive note.

This is one of my favorite uses for Hydrogen because it uses the applications you use most within Google Workspace.

Google says the model is better at connecting information from uploaded files, Gmail, Google Drive and supported connected apps, including services such as Dropbox and Airtable. Just keep in mind that you'll need to connect the relevant apps and grant Gemini permission to access them first. Access to individual integrations and actions can also depend on your account and subscription.

Rather than searching through messages individually, you could ask Gemini to piece together information from several sources and turn it into an actionable plan. I've already used it for organizing my busy family schedule and pulling together information for an upcoming project.

Prompt to try: Look through my connected Gmail and Google Drive for information about my upcoming appointments and events over the next two weeks. Create a chronological schedule with dates, times, locations and anything I need to prepare. Flag missing information and tell me which emails or documents contain the details.

Hydrogen isn't just designed to answer questions. Google has also improved its ability to turn plain-English instructions into working interactive projects.

The company demonstrated the model building everything from an interactive 3D globe that visualizes lightning strikes to a virtual island where people can create structures using simple prompts. You don't need to attempt anything nearly that ambitious, but you could ask Gemini to build a household budget calculator, an interactive study guide or a simple game.What I think is cool is what happens after the first version. You can ask Gemini to change colors, add features or fix problems using ordinary language. Keep reiterating to get the best results. In fact, I recommend giving this a try in Gemini's Canvas so you can keep your project organized.

Prompt to try: Create a simple, interactive household budget calculator with fields for monthly income, housing, groceries, utilities, transportation and savings. Automatically calculate the amount remaining after expenses. Include a visual chart and make the design clean, colorful and easy to use on a phone. Show me how to preview and use it.

Hydrogen isn't limited to the Gemini app. Google is also bringing the model to Search, where it says users should see better responses to complicated questions.

Instead of requiring a perfectly worded search, Hydrogen is designed to understand what you're actually trying to accomplish, even when your request includes multiple questions or confusing details.

Google also says its upgraded Search system can discover a wider range of relevant sources and present information in more useful visual formats. The goal is to make Search better at understanding the entire request rather than treating it as a collection of keywords.

Search to try: I need a lightweight laptop for working from coffee shops that has excellent battery life, can handle lots of browser tabs and costs less than $800. Compare my best options, explain the trade-offs and show me reliable reviews.

Head to the Gemini site and sign in with your Google account.

Google is rolling out Hydrogen globally, but the exact experience depends on your subscription. Free users can access Hydrogen through automatic model selection, which chooses the model based on the complexity of the request. Most straightforward questions may still use a lighter model.

If you don't see Hydrogen listed in your model picker, that doesn't necessarily mean you aren't using it. With automatic selection enabled, Google can route appropriate requests to Hydrogen.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen is not Google's most powerful AI model, but I've already experienced what it can do for everyday tasks. Its improvements focus on the things people actually do with AI: understanding documents, writing emails, organizing information, researching purchases and creating useful tools for their life.
