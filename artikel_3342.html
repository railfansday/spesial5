Hailee Steinfeld, Jodie Foster, and Rashida Jones Reveal the TV Shows They Wish Would Come Back

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Just in time for the holidays, Walt Disney Animation Studios invites families to take a journey to the magical realm of Hexe in their original new feature Disney's Hexed. The movie stars Hailee Steinfeld as the voice of the unconventional Billie, a young girl who discovers she possesses incredible powers, alongside the vocal talents of Jodie Foster as the villainous Queen Celeste, and Rashida Jones as Billie’s overprotective mother, Alice.

In the movie, Alice has done everything she can to protect her daughter from a world that seems determined to call Billie back. But when Billie is a teenager, her powers won’t hold back anymore, and both mom and daughter are drawn back to Hexe, where they uncover family mysteries intertwined with the realm’s destiny.

In this interview with Collider’s Steve Weintraub, Steinfeld, Foster, and Jones look back on the moments from their careers they wish they could revisit, share the TV shows they want just one more season of, discuss the lessons learned after parenthood, and Foster reveals a crucial factor of being an on-screen villain. Don’t miss the full conversation in the video above with a transcript provided below.

COLLIDER: This movie is all about magic. If you could magically bring back one TV show for one more season, what would you bring back?

JONES: I never watched it. I can't believe I'm saying this. Did it end in a way that it could come back for another season?

HAILEE STEINFELD: But you just want it back.

You want another season in, like, the middle of the run.

FOSTER: Yes. With the same people. I just want it with the same people in the same circumstances. Great characters.

STEINFELD: I want to see Peaky Blinders. I know we got the movie, which was great, but I totally could’ve done another season of the show.

I'll let you know, they're filming a sequel series right now.

I think there are actually two series they’re working on.

STEINFELD: He’s like, “It's been in production for four years. That's three years ago.”

It's really coming. It takes place in the ‘40s or ‘50s.

JONES: I would say, and this is not because I had an involvement, but I really love the show Freaks and Geeks, and it was cut off extremely too soon after one season. I would have loved to see that show. I felt like that show kind of changed that genre. It was like a genre that hadn't been done before. It was like kind of like dark comedy, and it was so great. Like, what a great show.

JONES: It is. And by the way, to say, obviously we wouldn't want to mess that up with a second season, but I think there were a lot more stories to tell from what I heard.

Continuing in my theme of using magic, if you could magically go back and spend one more day on a previous job, what would you love to go spend one more day doing?

STEINFELD: Oh, wow, that's an amazing question.

FOSTER: I got all the answers quick. I would go back and do one more day of True Detective. That was my happiest place.

FOSTER: Yeah. Especially since by the time we finished, winter was over. So I would say, yeah, I'd like to do spring in Iceland again.

STEINFELD: Wow. I think I'd pick an extra day on True Grit. Maybe there's nothing to elaborate on there.

STEINFELD: I spent many months of my life with people coming up to me saying, “This is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity. Do you understand this?” And, of course, I was like, “Yeah, it's incredible.” And I now realize how much of a once-in-a-lifetime experience I was in. If I could get an extra day out of that one, I totally would.

JONES: I wish I had something with “true” in the title, but I just don’t. So I guess I'm not in this game. I would probably say, even though I got lots of time, I'd probably say Parks and Recreation because it was such a dream job, and we just had so much fun, and it didn't feel like work. I just loved going to work, and I love those people, and it’d be nice to be there. One day.

Jodie, I'm very curious about what took so long for people to see you as a villain, and for the two of you, you guys have this great mother-daughter relationship. What's something your own parents were right about that you refused to admit when you were younger?

FOSTER: Well, I think people didn't see me as a villain because I was too short. I think they thought I'd have to be much taller to be a villain. But the good news about animation is you have no idea how tall I am!

FOSTER: I’m super tall in the movie. I did one where I was a villain once in a movie called Elysium, and then I've had villainous qualities.

FOSTER: Oh, animated! Well, this is it. This is my one.

JONES: I mean, I would just say in general, I lost my mom seven years ago, right after I had my child, and every day I wish I could talk to her, and also just apologize for not understanding where she was. Like you were saying about your mom, you understand your mom so much more when you become a parent. It just makes me so grateful for how loving and patient she was with me, you know? Sorry. I don't mean to bring it down, but yeah, that's what I would say.

STEINFELD: That's so beautiful. I was kind of thinking along the lines of patience is a virtue for me. Like, you don't understand how they're human, too. You grow up thinking your parents are superheroes, and I don't know. That's such a big question. I'm gonna have to think a little bit more about that.

Hexed premieres in theaters on November 25.
