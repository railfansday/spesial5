Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Drought process, voting by proxy, and deceptive flashlights.

Please enjoy the latest edition of Short Circuit, a weekly feature written by a bunch of people at the Institute for Justice.

Oh boy! Oh boy! This Tuesday, Dec. 1, the Supreme Court is going to hear oral argument in an IJ case, Jouppi v. Alaska. After a passenger on his charter flight got busted for sneaking several cases of beer into a dry town, the state took bush pilot Ken Jouppi's $95k Cessna. The Alaska Supreme Court ruled that the forfeiture of his plane wouldn't be excessive even for a single six-pack. Click here to learn more.

New on the Short Circuit podcast: What does qualified immunity have to do with your paycheck? And how does the First Amendment intersect with payroll deductions? Clock in for some answers on the latest podcast.

Why don't more civil rights lawyers bring state constitutional claims in federal court? Because, like in an Indiana Jones movie, "there are jurisdictional pitfalls, abstention traps, and unsteady judges imploring you to just throw them the idol." See Center for Judicial Engagement director Anthony Sanders' explanation in State Court Report.
