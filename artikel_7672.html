New York State Bar Association tells lawyers not to bill for time saved by AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The New York State Bar Association is calling for restrictions on how much lawyers can bill clients for the amount of time saved using artificial intelligence tools or expended learning how to use them. (Image from Shutterstock)

The New York State Bar Association is calling for restrictions on how much lawyers can bill clients for the amount of time saved using artificial intelligence tools or expended learning how to use them.

Law360 reported Monday that the state bar association’s Committee on Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies issued a report classifying the cost of buying and maintaining such software as an overhead expense that can be passed along as a disbursement only when tied to a specific client matter and when the basis for the charge has been disclosed.

According to Law360, the report added that lawyers generally don’t have to tell clients about AI use for administrative tasks—such as word processing, spelling and grammar checks, calendaring or timekeeping—provided that the use doesn’t affect fees.

“Generative AI is indelibly changing the practice of law. As part of our mission to be the leading voice for the legal profession, we are providing clear guidelines on the use of AI tools as a resource, so attorneys can use AI responsibly and ethically in their practices,” said Taa Grays, the president of the New York State Bar Association, in a statement. “The guidelines can help attorneys avoid possible perils of using AI, including inadvertently practicing law in a jurisdiction where they are not licensed or failing to notify clients or the courts of AI use.”

The committee also flagged familiar hazards, such as biased training data that may deepen existing inequities. Confidentiality is another concern: If an AI tool learns from one client’s information in a way that makes it available in another client’s matter, lawyers must turn to the New York Rules of Professional Conduct.

The report cautioned against building tools that deliver automated legal services in ways that could amount to unauthorized practice or using AI to pass as a human with prospective clients.

Supervising lawyers were reminded that they remain responsible for their subordinates’ compliance and should not assume that a law firm-approved tool or policy removes the need to review work on each matter.

According to Law360, the state bar association’s governing body adopted the report Saturday.
