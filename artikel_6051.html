JetBlue Cancels 11 International Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

JetBlue is the USA's fourth-largest airline for international service. In November 2026, for example, the carrier has 2,723 departures beyond the US border, equivalent to 91 daily flights. Compared to last November, activity has fallen by 9%.

The international arena is a relatively large part of JetBlue's total offering. In the same month, 20% of services are international, more than double the rate for the US's ten largest operators. However, multiple cuts, including those mentioned in this article, have reduced JetBlue's share from 23% last November.

It is common to compare how things have changed between two periods of time, such as different months, quarters, half years, or years. Cirium Diio data was used to see which international routes JetBlue operated in November 2025 but which do not exist in November 2026.

11 links were identified, most of which existed from New York JFK — historically the airline's most-served airport — and Newark. While they could simply have become seasonal or, at least, focused more on the peak periods, that is not the case. They have all been removed. Moreover, as disclosed recently, JetBlue has pulled out of Antigua, Bonaire, Belize City, and Tulum entirely.

Routes Served Last November But Not This November

Following heavy losses, JetBlue removed many underperforming or less significant international routes network-wise as a part of its JetForward restructuring. Compared to last November, such flights from New York JFK have fallen by a considerable 24%, while Boston (a much smaller station for such activity) is down by 16%. Newark has decreased by 68%, with only one route now served, while Orlando is 29% lower.

Despite the reductions, New York JFK remains JetBlue's number one airport for service beyond the US border. It has 42% of the departures in November, reduced from half 12 months ago. What has really changed is the rise of Fort Lauderdale, at which the carrier is increasingly focusing (internationally and domestically), partly because of the exit of Spirit Airlines.

International flights from the South Florida airport are up 63%, with Fort Lauderdale now accounting for 29% of JetBlue's total, 13 points more than 12 months ago. It is developing a mini-hub, of sorts, between the US and the Caribbean and Latin America. Excluding US territories, 18 international routes are available in November, with more coming in 2027.

Several of the 11 routes were pulled after relatively little time, which is often the case, even without a program like JetForward. If the initial performance is not sufficiently good relative to expectations, and the network contribution is not important enough, it is sensible to redeploy the expensive aircraft to where they may perform better.

New York JFK to San Pedro Sula flights lasted for less than a year, influenced by a very low load factor of just 68.1%. The carrier decided to consolidate on its Fort Lauderdale service, which began in November 2025, with that route's traffic figures rising as a result.

Initial data from the US Department of Transportation demonstrates this. Between November 2025 and the following April, when JFK flights still existed, Fort Lauderdale's average load factor was 62.5%. Ouch. But from May 2026 to August 2026 (as far as the data currently extends), it jumped to 90.1%. Given that more and more lower-yielding transit traffic is probably carried now, yields may have fallen.
