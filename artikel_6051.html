Skift Commerce Summit 2026: Balancing Trust, Access, and Control in the Traveler Relationship

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Travel companies are now being forced to decide which parts of the customer relationship to pursue directly while allowing and even enabling intermediaries to have more control of the journey.

AI is already searching and booking on behalf of travelers, but travel companies haven’t settled who should control those transactions, who should pay for them, or who takes primary responsibility for customer service.

At the Skift Commerce Summit, executives said AI is moving faster than the industry can settle the economics or rules around it.

“The agents are here,” said Skift Head of Research Seth Borko. “But almost no one is ready.”

At the Skift Commerce Summit, executives said AI agents are already searching and booking travel, but the industry hasn't settled who controls those transactions, who pays for them, or who handles customer service when something goes wrong. United Airlines announced during the Summit that it would restrict AI agents from accessing its website, and MCR Hotels CEO Tyler Morse said MCR would ban them too if it could figure out how. Turkish Airlines took the opposite position: about 1% of its traffic already comes from AI, and digital chief Fatih Durmaz said the airline's stance is "very much to work with AI," while acknowledging that servicing and permissions still need substantial work. Skift Head of Research Seth Borko showed how an AI agent completed a United booking on a United credit card, then failed to earn miles because the transaction wasn't recognized correctly. Luxury Escapes CEO Adam Schwab said an AI customer-service system misunderstood a request and canceled a booking, costing about $10,000 to fix. The economics remain unresolved: Kalibri CEO Cindy Estis Green said U.S. hotels spent about $47 billion acquiring guests in 2025, roughly 25% of room revenue for most properties, and she was skeptical AI would lower that. JetBlue President Marty St. George said he fears intermediaries will eventually want to get paid, money airlines aren't spending today.
