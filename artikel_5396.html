GameStop's CEO said the shift to digital-only games has hurt consumers

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Ryan Cohen said all-digital releases have hurt the retailer too, as Grand Theft Auto VI is due out as a download-only title

GameStop $GME Chief Executive Ryan Cohen said publishers have erred by shipping games solely as downloads, with no physical disc option, according to Bloomberg. He made the comments as Take-Two Interactive $TTWO’s “Grand Theft Auto VI” is set for release on Thursday as a download-only title.

"When you buy a game, you should be able to sell it, trade it or lend it to a friend," Cohen told Bloomberg. "Unquestionably, the decision to move to all digital has hurt consumers, and it's also hurt GameStop."

Cohen would not single out any company by name, according to Bloomberg. A Take-Two spokesperson declined to comment. Retailers are selling boxes that contain download codes for the $80 game.

When questioned about the state of presales, Cohen told Bloomberg: "It's a code in a box. I mean, we're selling a lot of it."

Trade-ins are a core part of GameStop's business. Pre-owned and refurbished products accounted for 22% of GameStop's sales in the quarter ending Aug. 1, roughly $171 million, a 32% drop from the prior year.

Cohen told Bloomberg he has talked with "the obvious" publishers and that curbing trade-ins helps them sell more copies at full price. "Of course they don't like [trade-ins] because removing a used copy eliminates an alternative to buying new," he said. "They're taking away competition."

Sony $SONY Group said in July it will stop manufacturing physical discs for new PlayStation titles starting in January 2028, according to Bloomberg.

Cohen posted about the issue on Tuesday, according to Kotaku. "GTA. On a disc. In a case. On your shelf. Like if you still want a physical copy," he wrote on X $TWTR.

Cohen told Bloomberg he has met with Rep. Ritchie Torres, a New York Democrat who supports the Power to the Players Act. According to Bloomberg, the bill would make game makers guarantee buyers continued access to whatever they have purchased.

"When you pay for a game, you should own it," Torres said in an October statement, according to Bloomberg. "A game that can be taken away the moment a company pulls the plug is not a purchase. It is a rental disguised as a sale."

In Europe, the Stop Killing Games movement has secured 1.3 million signatures behind a European Citizens' Initiative that would require publishers to keep titles playable, according to Bloomberg.
