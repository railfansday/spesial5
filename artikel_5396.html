Yearly Inflation Measures Still Hot; Economics Professor Deems December Rate Hike ‘A Done Deal’

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, released monthly by the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), showed an increase of 0.3% in October. The PCE price index gained 0.5% in September, making October a very slight cooldown.

The “core” PCE price index, or the PCE price index with food and energy data excluded, held steady at a 0.3% month-to-month increase.

The PCE price index is widely considered to be the Fed’s favored measure of inflation, and thus a cooldown in PCE price index gains may also signal a cooldown in Fed rate hikes.

The year-to-year data, however, point in the opposite direction. Compared to October of last year, the PCE price index actually increased 3.8%, a 0.2% gain from the same year-to-year comparison in October.

The Core PCE price index also shot up 3.3% compared to October of last year. Navy Federal Chief Economist Heather Long called this “(a)nother hot inflation reading” on X and remarked that “Core came in hotter than expected. It’s yet another reminder that the inflation problem right now goes beyond a mere energy shock.”

University of Pennsylvania Economics Professor Mohamed A. El-Erian made the implications of the PCE report very clear in a post on X: “All this will leave markets feeling that a Fed rate hike on December 9th is essentially a done deal, disappointing those who were hoping for a quick repeat of 2018.”

A Fed rate hike in December is likely disheartening news for the housing market, which has already seen a “crater(ing)” of pending home sales due to a high October spike in mortgage rates.

Rates have already edged down (and back up again, albeit remaining lower than the highest of the October highs), but anticipation of a rate hike may not grant buyers the confidence needed to reenter the market.

In other news, though, the latest BEA report also recorded a current-dollar PCE rise of 0.3% in October, the equivalent of $63.8 billion. The increase in expenditures is downstream of increased spending on goods and services, in which the BEA’s category of “other nondurable goods” represented the highest increase. Motor vehicles and parts experienced the largest decrease.

Lastly, personal income increased $201.8 billion or 0.7% month-over-month. According to the report, this increase mostly reflected increases in compensation, government social benefits and personal income receipts on assets.
