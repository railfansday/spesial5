‘A complete evaporation of confidence’–6 critical things Nvidia, and interim CEO Colette Kress, need to nail on the first Jensen-free earnings call

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nobody—not even Nvidia’s board—knows who will lead the new regime at Nvidia now that Jensen Huang is gone. For now, longtime CFO Colette Kress is in the driver’s seat as interim chief, and she’s been handed Atlas’s job of holding up the sky during Nvidia’s fiscal 2027 third quarter results after the bell on Tuesday.

Kress has to be the “voice of stability” while she also attempts to get the investment community reoriented to the fundamental issues at Nvidia that impact the business and valuation of the stock, said Melissa Otto, global head of Visible Alpha Research at S&P Global.

First and foremost, analysts are still expecting a typically gargantuan quarter, with $109.3 billion in revenue, according to S&P’s Visible Alpha consensus—which is routine for Nvidia and will be mission critical for Kress. The quarterly figure is up about 92% from $57 billion last year, but the number has to be delivered in the right way. Nvidia’s gotten the world used to over performance and now the company needs to sizzle with confidence at one of the most vulnerable moments in its history.

“There’s a complete evaporation of confidence at the moment, and there’s so much uncertainty around a lot of qualitative issues,” said Otto. “The narrative needs to come back to fundamentals. How is the business doing? Is it achieving what it said it’s going to achieve?”

Nvidia’s stock is down 20% since the company’s last quarterly earnings on August 26, losing roughly half a trillion dollars in market cap, amid a broader selloff in AI and related tech stocks that have since rallied. Tuesday could be a moment for Nvidia to make its case (or at least, its first impression) as a post-Jensen business. Or it could be a bloodbath.

Here are six key things to pay attention on Nvidia’s earnings conference call.

Nvidia told investors in August to expect third-quarter revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, which puts us in the range of about $105.8 billion to $110.2 billion. That outlook assumed no data center compute revenue from China.

Visible Alpha consensus sits at $109.3 billion with earnings of $2.47 per share. The data center segment—Nvidia’s crown jewel—is expected to deliver $101.8 billion of the topline total, with estimates ranging from $99.7 billion to $110.8 billion. Nvidia’s third quarter ended in late October, which means the results were not affected by Huang’s ouster. Still, any signs of weakness in the business during Huang’s final days at the helm would likely add even more uncertainty to the company’s prospects.

Jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets, wrote in commentary that the earnings report “will be a major referendum on the AI trade following its leadership shake-up, particularly its outlook for demand, margins and financial commitments.” Colossal results “won’t resolve the questions surrounding Jensen’s departure,” he wrote.

This is Otto’s top issue. “Do they maintain their guidance for next year?” she said. “If that’s not the case, then there’s going to be some volatility.” That’s putting it lightly.

In August, Kress said Nvidia’s preliminary expectation was for revenue to grow about 70% in fiscal 2028 (roughly equivalent to calendar 2027), with supply remaining a bottleneck at least through the end of next year.

At the time, Huang said Nvidia had never guided financial figures a year in advance, but with demand running well above 70%, he felt confident about it. The true test will be whether that confidence outlives his ouster.

“It is the case that we’ve never forecasted or never guided to a year in advance,” said Huang at the time. “And even though our demand is much greater than 70%, our supply allows us to confidently deliver 70%.”

That means $699.2 billion in fiscal 2028 revenue, if Huang’s confidence holds up, according to Visible Alpha’s revenue consensus. In the official announcement of Huang’s resignation last week, Nvidia’s newly installed board chair, Harvey Jones, said the company expects “operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption.” We’ll see.

For now, it’s Kress and the board has said it will consider internal and external candidates to replace Huang, whether that’s one person, or a sharing of power in multiple new roles. Otto called the leadership transition “essentially a regime change” and said investors want to hear what comes next.

“What changes might be made and how might this affect their existing strategy?” she said.

There are also constituencies, mainly other tech founders, who want to see Huang return to his CEO role. Portfolio managers don’t love holding stock that tanks, especially a stock that has been such a rocket ship: Nvidia shares have surged more than 1,300% since ChatGPT’s late 2022 launch.

“For a company that spent 33 years under one CEO, this is about more than beating expectations,” Woods wrote. “Nvidia now needs to show that the business can continue delivering without the man who built it.”

Giuseppe Sette, cofounder and president of Reflexivity, wrote in commentary that a reinstatement “would most likely trigger a sharp, immediate relief rally,” but on a “less durable and more volatile path than the classic founder-return analogues, since this is a scandal-driven exit rather than a strategy-driven one.”

Nvidia has become an important financial backer of its customers, through direct investments and guarantees to backstop debt, with neocloud companies ranking among the biggest beneficiaries.

Total future commitments, spread over the next five fiscal years and thereafter, amount to $366 billion, with $120 billion falling in the remainder of fiscal 2027, which ends in late January. Supply and capacity makes up $279 billion of that total, up from $119 billion the quarter before.

John Belton of Gabelli Funds wrote that the initiatives are “polarizing for the stock giving ongoing concerns about ‘circular financing.’”

Recent media reports have suggested that Kress, and Huang himself prior to his exit, were rethinking Nvidia’s role as the AI industry’s “central bank” and were planning to tighten the purse strings.

Mark Malek, chief investment officer at Siebert Financial, said the AI trade splits two ways. “Those collecting checks for building the future and those writing the checks hoping the future eventually pays them back—investors had better know which ones they own.”

Nvidia’s market success rests on the strength of its technology, so it’s crucial that it flawlessly execute the baton pass from its current Blackwell product line up to Vera Rubin, Nvidia’s newer, next generation platform.

“This is the year,” said S&P’s Otto. “We’ve seen Blackwell doing quite well, and what we would want to understand is how should we be thinking about Rubin for the rest of this year and next year.”

In August, Nvidia said Rubin was ramping into full production, with server racks running at partners including CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle, and Nebius. In August, Kress said Rubin would account for about 20% of data center revenue in the third quarter, and that hyperscale growth would reaccelerate in the fourth quarter and into fiscal 2028 as Rubin supply spreads.

Otto said she’s got her ears pricked for “any kind of color” that shows whether expectations “are aligned correctly to the way that they’re describing the market.”

In August, Nvidia lowered the bar for gross profit margin expectations, blaming the industry-wide rise in memory prices. It guided to a third-quarter gross margin of 74%, plus or minus 50 basis points, down from 75% in the second quarter.

Kress told investors at the time that margins would “bottom” in the fourth quarter at 71% to 72%, then settle at 72% to 73% as Nvidia’s own product price increases take effect. “We want to be direct about this rather than let it linger as an open question,” said Kress.

Wall Street analysts currently expect 73.1% gross margin in the third quarter and 70.2% for the fourth, and then rising to 72.2% next year, according to Visible Alpha estimates.

A negative change in margin expectations could land pretty heavily on a stock that has seen some whiplash.

All six of these issues mean analysts on Tuesday’s conference call need to ask Kress the hard questions. And analysts—whether due to fear of losing access or a myopic focus on their short term financial models—have a reputation for shying away from tough questions.

Asked whether sell-side analysts will press on the leadership ouster and espionage scandal in front of a global audience, S&P’s Otto didn’t want to predict. She also didn’t want to guess whether Kress would be joined by new Chair Harvey Jones on the call to have a second hand on deck to field questions.

“It could go in a million different directions,” Otto said. “It’s probably more effective to ask Kalshi.”

Her advice for anyone listening: “Buckle up. I think it’s going to be an interesting call.”

Nvidia is scheduled to report at 1:20 p.m. PT on Tuesday, with the call slated for 2 p.m. PT.

Amanda Gerut is the west coast editor at Fortune, overseeing publicly traded businesses, executive compensation, Securities and Exchange Commission regulations, and investigations.
