Trump takes on Somali welfare dependency in Thanksgiving message, lays out who should stay in US

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

President Donald Trump said Rep. Ilhan Omar could be deported or face jail when asked about questions surrounding her age, calling the Minnesota Democrat "a disgrace to our country."

In a lengthy Thanksgiving message on Truth Social Thursday, President Donald Trump praised a small fraction of the Somali community, saying they should be allowed to remain in the United States, while calling for the immediate removal of those receiving government assistance while unemployed.

The holiday post mixed praise for "AMERICAN PATRIOTS" with criticism of the "Radical Far Left" and Minnesota’s Somali community, which Trump has frequently accused of public assistance fraud in the state.

"I want to thank the 2% of our Somalian population that have a job, and are actually willing to work, without receiving Government Assistance," Trump wrote in the post. "These are wonderful American ‘citizens,’ and should be allowed to stay in our Country, and celebrate Thanksgiving with us. The others should be thrown out, immediately, thereby saving the Great State of Minnesota, which has no chance if they are allowed to stay."

Ken Martin, chair of the Democratic National Committee, said Trump was spending the holiday "spewing hatred, attacking immigrants and bragging about his ballroom," saying he should instead be reminded that Thanksgiving is about "gratitude, generosity and community, not bigotry and interior decorating."

"As a proud Minnesotan, let me make something abundantly clear: our Somali American neighbors are part of the fabric of our state," Martin wrote on X. "They are small business owners, teachers, healthcare workers, parents, and hardworking Americans. They contribute far more to the spirit of this country than a man who can’t even get through a Thanksgiving greeting without demonizing an entire community."

"In Minnesota, we pull up another chair at the Thanksgiving table," he added. "Trump apparently would rather deport the people sitting around it."

Trump’s remarks echo ongoing criticism from some Republicans regarding public benefit usage and fraud cases within the community.

During a House Oversight Committee hearing in January, Rep. Brandon Gill, R-Texas, highlighted data on government support programs, noting that 54% of Somali-headed households in Minnesota receive food stamps.

Elsewhere in the post, Trump claimed that "murderers, drug dealers, and criminals" — who he said illegally entered the U.S. from foreign prisons and mental institutions — have largely been deported.

"They are mostly gone now, brought back to their point of origin and, hopefully, living a beautiful and idyllic life, and promising, ideally, never to kill innocent people again," he wrote.

"We won’t have to worry about that, because they are GONE — but, Happy Thanksgiving to them anyway, even though from Faraway Lands," he added. "We miss you dearly, but you will never be back, and America will never allow what you have done to us to happen again!"

Secretary of State Marco Rubio also shared a holiday message on social media, with a video focused on opportunities provided by the U.S.

"We know how special this country is. It's the place that gave us freedom. It's a place that gave us opportunity. It's a place that's given our parents, our grandparents, and many of you the chance not just to do well in life, but to leave our children better off than ourselves," Rubio said in the video.

In the attached caption, he added, "Being an American is a gift unlike any ever afforded to any other peoples throughout human history — we have so much to be thankful for. Happy Thanksgiving."
