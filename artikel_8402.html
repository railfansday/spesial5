Slow Mornings, Friends, & Hikers in Flagstaff

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

This morning I was surprised how early I woke up in the room. I was up pretty late last night doing some editing. Nevertheless we were up bright and early and were both ravenously hungry for some breakfast. Once we got up we decided to just walk down the road to a nearby breakfast spot to eat. I wound up getting a chorizo eggs Benedict that had avocado and a chipotle sauce on top. Plus a pumpkin spiced chai latte, which was absolutely delicious.

Slide and I got a delicious breakfast before heading out of Flagstaff.

After we had a wonderful breakfast, we headed back to the house and met up with Gorf. Then she drove us to the grocery store to do a quick resupply. I still had some food left at the house from when we went into the Grand Canyon. But definitely needed a bit more for this next stretch of trail which is going to be 130 miles long. I did such a bad job packing out food for the last stretch and now I’m determined to get it right this time. I knew I needed more crunchy snacks like chips or goldfish. I think this time around I definitely got some more exciting things to eat. Hopefully it will be enough and I’ll also enjoy eating all the goodies I got. Resupplying is always a struggle for me because my appetite and cravings really ebb and flow on the trail. It seems like I can never get it exactly right, even after all the miles I’ve hiked in the past.

This snack made me crack up. I know it’s just a random brand of meat stick, but I think it’s hilarious that Mr. Beast makes meat sticks.

While we were getting packed up, two other hikers named Leaf and Handy came by the house. Leaf is going to hike out with Strix and Woods and be on the trail for the next couple of weeks. They wound up heading out a little bit before us and I believe Handy drove them all to the trailhead. An hour or so later we were all packed up and Gorf drove us back to the same gas station from last night. From there we could walk a short distance and be right back on the trail.

Just after noon we got dropped off back at the gas station from last night.

I packed out a couple of slices of pizza from last night which I enjoyed in the first hour or so of the day. I never liked Hawaiin pizza in my entire life until last years calendar year triple crown hike. In Damascus, Virginia I had some Hawaiin pizza and enjoyed it for the first time in my life. And now I love it! That was a great way to start off another day of hiking. Plus the trail was really flat leaving town. Apparently we can expect some generally flat and easy terrain until we get closer to Pine, AZ, our next resupply location. That’s great news because we have some major mileage standing in between us and the next town. Hopefully if we can hike around 20 miles today and then do 3 more big mile days, then we could get into town the morning that the storm is going to hit. But that will require doing about 35 miles a day for the next few days after today. That’s not a bad price to pay though in my opinion if it means avoiding most of the weather.

A cool piece of art right on the trail. This was in a section of trail with numerous short scenic loops.

Early on into our day the trail passed through an area that had some small loop trails which are popular for day hikers. We actually wound up doing a small loop by accident instead of turning off and continuing on the Arizona trail. We didn’t even realize until we had looped back to a sign that I recognized from earlier in the day. Luckily we didn’t waste too much time doing that and it was pretty funny to be honest. Plus the trail was almost entirely flat, so it wasn’t too big of an error.

Bright happy yellow flowers alongside the trail.

Sometime after that we got onto a wider double-track dirt road. This brought us past a train track and we got to watch a train go by. Not long after that it began to rain on us lightly as well, which I wasn’t expecting. With an umbrella, though, I hardly even mind a light rain! It was comfortable enough. The timing was great actually and we wound up passing through a large tunnel a little while after the rain started. We had been going for a while so it was a great opportunity to stop off for a break and stay out of the rain. Otherwise we probably would have just continued on until the rain stopped. Taking a break on trail when it’s raining is not very enjoyable and I tend to avoid it unless I’m really in dire need of a rest.

Bright, colorful graffiti at a tunnel that the trail passed right through.

The trail itself looked stunning today thanks to all of the recent weather. The wildflowers were bright and blooming and all of the green foliage was so lush. I’m assuming that Arizona doesn’t typically look like this in the fall! A lot of hikers tend to do the trail in the spring to enjoy more water on trail and more lush plant life. Getting tons of rain isn’t necessarily great for us, but it sure does make for some beautiful hiking conditions. The plants are clearly very happy about it themselves.

Slide taking some photos of the passing train.

After our tunnel break we continued going past the trail tracks and saw another train go by. Then the trail brought us through a couple more long tunnels which was really neat. One was a double tunnel too! We passed through a first long tunnel, exited briefly, and then went right back into another. I enjoyed looking at some of the nicer graffiti on the walls of the tunnel. One particular piece was of a large meerkat or groundhog-type animal. Some of the graffiti was actually pretty dang good!

I liked this cute little guy that was graffiti’d on one of the tunnels.

Around this time the rain let us and ceased for the remainder of the evening which was delightful. The trail continued to be nice and easy and we were making pretty great time. Our only hope for the day was to try to go around 20 miles. That would leave us with 3 big days of hiking in order to get to town hopefully on Thursday morning. Today is Sunday, and the storm is looking like it is going to start on Thursday and continue on through Saturday evening. I can’t believe we are even considering taking another 2 or so days off-trail for another set of storms. But I do not mess around with major rain in the desert. Flash flooding is more common than people think, plus river crossings become quite dangerous when that much water is flowing down from higher ground.

It was a gloomy, rainy day but wound up being pretty perfect! Aside from a little bit of mud on the trail.

Just before dark the trail changed a bit and began bringing us past some tall rock structures. There was a continuous rock wall on our right side for a while as we made our way down the trail, enjoying the last bit of daylight. We had already made it quite far fortunately and it was looking like we would only have the hike until just after dark. We wound up making it about 18 miles and then began searching for a place to camp for the night. The spot where we got set up even seemed like it might have some great views of the sunrise in the morning, which would be lovely.

The trail followed along this rock wall for a while which was a nice change of scenery later in the day.

It didn’t take long to find a nice flat place for the two of us to get set up. Once my tent was up and everything was inside I got comfortable and had a bit of dinner. Then did a bit of scanning through the maps on Far Out to access how far we might have to go the next couple of days. It’s looking like we’ll have to do 35 miles or more for the next two days, then hopefully the third day will be a little bit shorter. If we really push, we might be able to make it to the trailhead and road Wednesday evening. If we could get into town before the rain started that would be great. But more likely we will have a few miles to hike on Thursday morning. Then we might just have to try and hitch into town while it’s raining. That is never an easy task, but I’m hopeful. After doing a bit of planning I wound up doing my stretching and then rolling over and calling it a night.
