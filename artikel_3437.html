24 Years Later, Star Trek's Most Controversial Movie Is Officially Free on Streaming

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Star Trek: Nemesis ended the Next Generation crew's big-screen adventures on a sour note. Thankfully, that wasn't their final goodbye.

Saying goodbye to beloved television characters is never easy. After spending years getting to know them, audiences have expectations about how their stories should end. Some get the send offs they deserve, while others leave fans wondering what might have been. Unfortunately, 2002's Star Trek: Nemesis falls into the latter category, bringing an unceremonious end to the big-screen adventures of the Next Generation crew.

The fourth and final film featuring the Next Generation cast finds Captain Jean-Luc Picard (Patrick Stewart) confronting Shinzon (Tom Hardy), a younger clone of Picard who has seized power in a Romulan coup and plans to destroy Earth with a devastating weapon. It's a promising premise that Nemesis squanders on a convoluted, action-heavy story that forgets what made these characters special.

Now streaming free on Pluto TV, Nemesis remains a disappointing conclusion to the Next Generation film series. Having revisited it recently, I can confirm that nearly 24 years haven't done much to improve it.

The best episodes of The Next Generation aren't necessarily the ones featuring massive space battles. "The Measure of a Man" explores Data's humanity, while "Darmok" focuses on two captains struggling to communicate. Neither requires Picard to jump behind the wheel of a dune buggy and race across an alien desert while shooting at the locals. Yet that's exactly what Nemesis gives us.

Director Stuart Baird, who previously helmed the action thrillers Executive Decision and U.S. Marshals, and screenwriter John Logan, an Oscar nominee for Gladiator, seem determined to turn Picard into an action hero. To be fair, the Enterprise-E ramming Shinzon's massive warship, the Scimitar, is spectacular. Unfortunately, impressive action doesn't make up for a story that barely knows what to do with its characters. Geordi La Forge (LeVar Burton), Beverly Crusher (Gates McFadden), and Worf (Michael Dorn) are reduced to background players. Deanna Troi (Marina Sirtis) endures an unpleasant psychic assault by Shinzon's Reman viceroy (Ron Perlman), while her wedding to William Riker (Jonathan Frakes) gets surprisingly little attention.

Then there's Data (Brent Spiner), who sacrifices himself to save Picard and destroy the Scimitar. It's supposed to be the film's emotional gut punch, but the introduction of B-4, a less advanced android into whom Data has transferred his memories, leaves the door open for a convenient resurrection. Even in death, Data can't escape the possibility of a sequel.

For a crew that spent seven seasons exploring humanity, friendship, and sacrifice, Star Trek: Nemesis was a remarkably unsatisfying goodbye.

One of the more fascinating things about Nemesis is watching a young Tom Hardy play Shinzon. Hardy was still relatively unknown when he landed the role, and according to Stewart, he wasn't exactly the most sociable member of the cast.

In his 2023 memoir, Making It So, Stewart doesn't mince words about the movie, writing that Nemesis "was particularly weak." He also complains about the material he was given, saying, "I didn't have a single exciting scene to play."

As for Hardy, Stewart describes him as a solitary young actor who rarely exchanged greetings with his fellow actors and spent his downtime in his trailer with his girlfriend. Stewart emphasizes that Hardy wasn't hostile, but getting to know him proved nearly impossible.

When Hardy finished filming, he left without much ceremony, prompting Stewart to turn to Brent Spiner and Jonathan Frakes and predict they'd probably never hear from him again. Considering Hardy would go on to star in Inception, The Dark Knight Rises, Mad Max: Fury Road, and the Venom movies, Stewart's prediction ranks among the more spectacularly incorrect assessments of a future Hollywood star. To his credit, Stewart has a sense of humor about the whole thing, writing, "It gives me nothing but pleasure that Tom has proven me so wrong."

For more than two decades, Nemesis remained the final big-screen adventure for the Next Generation cast. Its disappointing box office performance effectively ended their theatrical adventures, leaving fans with a finale that felt less like a celebration and more like a franchise running out of ideas. Thankfully, the crew eventually got another chance.

The third and final season of Star Trek: Picard reunited Picard, Riker, Troi, Worf, Geordi, Beverly, and Data for one last adventure. Picard Season 3 wasn't perfect, and it certainly wasn't lacking in explosions, phaser battles, or threats to the Federation. But unlike Nemesis, it remembered that the greatest strength of The Next Generation was the bond between its characters.

That difference is especially apparent in show's final scene. After saving the galaxy yet again, the crew gathers around a table for a game of poker. No superweapons, no evil clones, and thankfully, no dune buggies. It's a fitting callback to "All Good Things," the 1994 Next Generation series finale, which ended with Picard finally joining his officers for their regular poker game.

Nemesis may have ended the crew's theatrical adventures, but Picard gave them something better: a farewell that celebrated what made them special. And after everything these characters have been through, there's something satisfying about knowing their story ended not with another battle, but with one last hand of cards.
