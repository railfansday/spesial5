Netflix’s Star-Studded WWII Movie Officially Arrives This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Like other streaming services nowadays, Netflix has started to warm up to releasing its films in theaters — at least some of them. The streaming service strongly considered leaning into this route when it tried to acquire Warner Bros., despite having the streaming-at-home line for the longest time. Some of its films this year have received limited theatrical runs in anticipation of Academy Award nominations, and the service has one strong contender so far: La Bola Negra. The Spanish epic has received acclaim from fans and critics, putting it in a strong position for awards in various categories.

Apart from La Bola Negra, Netflix has also given limited releases to The Further Misadventures of Cliff Booth, INK, Ray Gunn, and more. However, one of these films hasn't performed well critically, a telltale sign that its Oscar campaign has reached a natural end. That movie is a World War II epic that made its theatrical debut last month and has grossed $815813 across 349 theaters. Now, The Mosquito Bowl is coming to Netflix this week, and it could be a streaming hit.

Directed by Peter Berg and based on a script he co-wrote with Mark L. Smith, the action thriller is set in the 1940s and follows four American college football stars in the aftermath of the attack on Pearl Harbor. These young men abandon their promising careers to enlist as Marines in America's war with Japan. "As they prepare for the brutal invasion of Okinawa, they'll play in a legendary game featuring some of the greatest players in history — a game that, for many, will be the last they ever play," reads the film's description. Cast members include Nicholas Galitzine (John McLughry), Bill Skarsgård (Bob Bauman), Ray Nicholson (Tony Butkovich), and Tom Francis (Dave Schreiner). The Mosquito Bowl streams on Netflix on November 25.

With a current 41% critics' score on Rotten Tomatoes, the film has not received the best reviews. Critics noted that The Mosquito Bowl feels quite hollow in more ways than one. Collider's Maggie Lovitt said that it is a bit pedestrian and "adds very little new to the canon of war epics," while ScreenRant's Lizzie Declan did not appreciate the violence used to shield the lack of an engaging story. RogerEbert's Robert Daniels said it is "lacking in humanity." However, critic reviews don't always reflect viewer consensus, and those who saw it in theaters gave it a favorable 91% rating. Netflix could be vindicated when The Mosquito Bowl finally enters friendlier territory.

Stream the film on Wednesday, November 25, and stay tuned to Collider for more updates.
