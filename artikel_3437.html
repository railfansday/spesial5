‘Event Horizon’s Original Director Officially Reveals 2-Part Spiritual Successor Franchise

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

For anyone who watched Event Horizon and decided that space exploration was best left to somebody else, Paul W.S. Anderson has more reasons to stay home. His 1997 sci-fi horror movie sent Laurence Fishburne and Sam Neill aboard a ship that had returned from a place no rescue crew should investigate. The resulting nightmare remains one of the filmmaker’s most memorable excursions, right down to its deeply unhelpful explanation of where they were going to need eyes.

Anderson spent much of his subsequent career building the Resident Evil movie franchise, but the prospect of him returning to cosmic horror carries its own appeal. Event Horizon understood that a spaceship could become a haunted house, with the added inconvenience of having absolutely nowhere to run. Nearly three decades later, he has found another expedition worth worrying about.

Anderson is attached to direct movie adaptations of Nether Station and Nether Vortex, the sci-fi horror novels by Kevin J. Anderson. Deadline first reported the projects, which will have screenplays by Jonathan Wakeham. The director will also produce alongside longtime collaborator Jeremy Bolt, John Harlacher, and Tony Eldridge. The connection to Event Horizon goes beyond sharing a director. Kevin J. Anderson cited the movie as an influence on Nether Station, making the adaptations an opportunity for the filmmaker to revisit the kind of horror he helped inspire. These are separate stories, with a new protagonist facing discoveries that humanity might have been better off leaving alone.

The novels follow Cammie Skoura, a brilliant, neurodivergent young scientist on a deep-space expedition. A trail of ancient ruins and extinct alien civilizations leads toward a discovery with terrifying implications. Cammie gives the story a central perspective through which to encounter its enormous mysteries, rather than asking audiences to absorb an entire fictional history before the trouble starts. “I couldn’t imagine a better director for this project than Paul W.S. Anderson,” the author said. The filmmaker, meanwhile, praised the cinematic possibilities of the material and expressed enthusiasm for Cammie’s place within its Lovecraftian horror. Casting and release dates have not been announced.

What makes the pairing promising is the sense of discovery that Event Horizon turned into such a dreadful mistake. Exploring abandoned places is an irresistible setup for horror because every answer encourages someone to keep going. Ancient alien ruins offer that temptation on a much larger scale, especially when the civilizations responsible are no longer around to explain what happened. Two connected books also give Anderson room to build beyond a single disastrous encounter. Whether both movies reach the screen remains to be seen, but the announced adaptations offer a substantial return to a genre that produced one of his most enduring movies.

Nether Station and Nether Vortex are in development.
