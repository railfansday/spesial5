Celsius vs. Hershey: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Energy drinks or candy bars? We put a high-growth upstart up against a 130-year-old sweets empire.

By Josh Kohn-Lindquist – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 6:59AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Celsius Holdings ›

Choosing between growth and stability often defines an investment strategy. You might consider whether the rapid expansion of Celsius (CELH +3.08%) or the enduring legacy of Hershey (HSY +1.64%) is the better buy.

Celsius represents the high-growth side of the beverage market, focusing on functional energy drinks that appeal to health-conscious consumers. Hershey remains a cornerstone of the confectionery world, offering massive scale and predictable returns through its dominant position in chocolate and snacks.

Celsius focuses on the functional beverage market, selling energy drinks designed to boost metabolism and provide sustained energy. It reaches consumers through major retailers and e-commerce platforms like Amazon (AMZN +1.61%), while competing among beverage stocks for shelf space. A critical component of its strategy is a distribution agreement with Pepsi, which provides the necessary infrastructure to scale quickly. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as Pepsi accounted for roughly 43.2% of revenue recently.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $3.2 billion. This figure represents revenue growth of approximately 49.1% compared to the prior twelve-month period. During the same window, the company reported net income of nearly $230.2 million, demonstrating its ability to maintain profitability as it scales its footprint across North America and international markets.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.2x. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity, indicating how much the company relies on borrowing to fund its operations. The current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term liabilities with current assets, is roughly 1.7x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $72.1 million. Free cash flow represents the cash a company generates after accounting for cash outflows to support operations and maintain its capital assets.

Hershey operates as a global leader in the confectionery and snacking industry, producing well-known brands ranging from chocolate bars to protein snacks. The company sells its products through wholesale distributors and major retail chains, with McLane Company, Inc. serving as its largest customer, accounting for approximately 27% of net sales. This customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business, though Hershey offsets this with a presence in over 65 countries. Its strategy emphasizes brand loyalty and consistent product innovation to maintain its market-leading position.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached close to $12.3 billion. This reflects revenue growth of approximately 6.7% over the previous year, showing steady progress in a mature market. The company reported net income of nearly $1.8 billion during this period, highlighting the strong cash-generating power of its established brand portfolio.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 1.2x. The current ratio is approximately 1.2x, suggesting the company maintains sufficient liquidity to meet its immediate financial obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $1.8 billion. This robust cash generation allows the company to support its operations and return value to shareholders through various capital allocation strategies.

Celsius faces significant revenue concentration with Pepsi, creating a high level of dependency on a single partner for distribution. The company also faces intense competition from global brands like Monster Beverage (MNST +1.11%) and Coca-Cola (KO -0.50%). Operational risks include a reliance on third-party co-packers and potential supply chain disruptions for aluminum cans and specific ingredients. Furthermore, the integration of new brands like Alani Nu could lead to operational challenges or cannibalize its existing product lines.

Hershey faces significant commodity price volatility, especially in cocoa and sugar, which can affect its gross margin. The company must also manage the risk of sales volume declines if price increases intended to offset rising input costs lead to consumer pushback. Competition from global giants like Nestlé (NSRGF -4.46%) remains a constant pressure, requiring continuous marketing and innovation. Additionally, Hershey faces complexities in international operations and potential cybersecurity threats that could disrupt its global supply chain.

Celsius trades at a slightly higher earnings multiple than Hershey, though its lower price-to-sales ratio might appeal to those prioritizing rapid revenue expansion.

While Celsius certainly offers the more exciting growth profile versus steady-Eddie Hershey, its organic sales growth likely lies closer to the high single digits as of its most recent quarter. Celsius's skyrocketing revenue in 2026 primarily came from adding Alani Nu to its operations via its $1.8 billion acquisition of the up-and-coming energy drink maker, rather than true organic sales growth.

That said, Alani Nu rapidly surpassed the company's namesake Celsius brand as its best-selling drink -- and its growth isn't slowing yet. Alani Nu grew revenue by 24% (product sold to distributors) and saw retail sales rise 58% (product sold at c-stores, or grocery stores, for example), highlighting the brand's growing popularity. On top of Alani Nu's success, Celsius grew international revenue by 78%, and these foreign sales account for just 5% of the company's total revenue, leaving a potentially decades-long growth runway ahead.

With a portfolio of Alani Nu, Celsius, Rockstar, and international sales, Celsius should provide reasonable growth upside over the foreseeable future -- just don't expect 2026's growth rates to repeat, barring another major acquisition.

Hershey, meanwhile, is a stalwart chocolatier and snacking behemoth paying a well-funded and growing dividend that yields 3.3%. The company's days of consistent double-digit sales growth are behind it, but management expects adjusted EPS to rise by roughly 7% annually over the long term. Leaning on its strategy as a serial acquirer, I fully expect Hershey to meet this goal as it builds out its steadily growing salty snacks portfolio (primarily popcorn and pretzels). GLP-1 medications certainly pose a threat to Hershey's business, but I believe the peptide may, counterintuitively, be a benefit to the company, as miniaturization and smaller packaging could boost margins.

Ultimately, I'm not in a rush to buy more shares of either stock, though I do own both. However, I would lean toward buying Celsius in 2027, given its stronger growth potential and similar valuation to Hershey. Investors seeking stability and passive income may be better off buying Hershey, though, so it comes down to any individual investor's goals and risk appetite.
