This Should Be Your First Retirement Savings Priority in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

This could be one of the best retirement decisions you ever make.

By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 27, 2026 at 6:05AM EST

You've probably got a lot on your mind with the holidays in full swing, but with only a few weeks to go until 2027, it's worth taking a few minutes to map out your priorities for next year, including your retirement savings goals.

Goals like maxing out your IRA or contributing a certain dollar amount to your retirement account each month are great. But if you have a 401(k), there's one goal that is worth putting above all others.

Claiming your full 401(k) match should be your top priority in 2027, unless doing so would leave you unable to pay your bills today. This match, which could be worth hundreds to thousands of dollars today, could easily grow into tens of thousands of dollars by retirement. That could be enough to cover several months of living expenses in the future.

But you only get those dollars if you first contribute money to your own 401(k). Fail to do this by the end of the year, and you miss out on the opportunity to earn that money.

Given the limited time available, it's worth focusing on your 401(k) contributions first, at least until you've claimed your full match for the year. Then, if you'd like, you can switch to funding a different retirement account, such as an IRA, which offers greater flexibility.

What you need to do to claim your full 401(k) match in 2027 depends in part on how your company's matching formula works. Most formulas either give you $1 or $0.50 for every dollar you put into your 401(k), up to a certain percentage of your income. For example, if you get a dollar-for-dollar match on up to 4% of your income and you earn $60,000, you'd save $2,400 of your own money and your employer would give you another $2,400.

Check with your employer to learn how its matching formula works. Then, calculate how much of your own money you must set aside to get the entire match. Divide this amount by the number of pay periods in the year to determine how much you must set aside per paycheck to get the entire match.

If you're not able to set aside the entire amount each month due to budget constraints, get as much of your match as possible. And if you get a raise later in the year, recalculate how much you need to save to get the full match and increase your contribution rate accordingly if you can.
