3 Things Sellers Need To Know About Pricing Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

If you’re getting ready to sell your house, you’ve probably heard how important it is to price it right. And that’s especially true right now.

Today’s buyers have more homes to choose from and they’re feeling pinched by today’s mortgage rates. That makes them more selective. And that means having the right pricing strategy matters more than ever.

That starts with understanding these three things. Because, with the right approach, you can still put your house in a great position to sell.

It’s natural to look at what your neighbor’s house sold for and think yours should sell for something similar. And those comparable sales (or comps) absolutely matter. But they only tell part of the story. Here’s why.

Buyers aren’t shopping against what sold three or even six months ago. They’re comparing your house to what’s available right now.

That’s why your agent will look at more than just recent sales. They’ll also consider the houses currently for sale, what’s going under contract, and how your house stacks up against those options to find the right price for your house.

The goal isn’t necessarily to price lower than everyone else. It’s to make sure buyers see the value in choosing your house over the competition.

As Jake Krimmel, Senior Economist at Realtor.com explains:

“Pricing to today’s demand is helping homes move and keeping more transactions alive in a high-rate environment.”

Understanding how your house compares to what’s available today can help you feel more confident in your asking price.

Getting your asking price right puts you in a stronger position from the start. But when an offer comes in, remember that price is only one part of the equation.

Today’s buyers may still negotiate on things like money toward closing costs or repairs, even when a house is priced well – and that’s not necessarily a commentary on your price, it’s just the norm for negotiation in today’s market.

According to Redfin, 44.7% of home sales involve seller concessions. That’s just under half. And 15.8% had both a price reduction and a concession.

Now, that doesn’t mean you have to agree to everything a buyer asks for. But sometimes offering a closing-cost credit or helping with a mortgage rate buydown could be what seals the deal. Other times, holding firm is the better move. Your agent will help you decide.

The best advice? Look at the whole offer, including what you’ll actually walk away with, to decide what’s right for you.

Even with a competitive price, some houses take longer to sell than others. And if yours isn’t getting the attention or offers you expected, that doesn’t mean your sale is off track. It just means it’s time to take a closer look at what’s happening.

Your agent can help you use buyer feedback and local market activity to figure out what adjustments, if any, could make a difference.

Are buyers seeing your listing online but not scheduling showings? Maybe the photos or presentation aren’t doing your house justice.

Are people touring but not making offers? It could be the condition, the price, or how your house compares to others they’ve seen.

And if buyers are making offers, but the terms aren’t working for you, that may call for a different negotiation strategy altogether.

While you may worry that means you have to drop your price, that’s not always true. A price cut can help, but only if it addresses what’s keeping buyers from making a move.

That’s why feedback, local competition, and an agent who knows how to interpret both are so important.
