History Says This Is What Happens to the Nasdaq After the Midterms

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Political uncertainty has been a source of volatility for stocks recently, and a striking historical trend could hint at what comes next.

By Keith Noonan – Nov 20, 2026 at 7:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Invesco QQQ Trust ›

The stock market has been volatile lately due to a series of macroeconomic risk factors and concerns about valuations for the companies in the artificial intelligence (AI) industry that has been so central to the market's gains in recent years.

Along those lines, some investors are worried that big wins for the Democratic Party in the recent U.S. midterm elections could impede data center buildouts, lead to the introduction of new regulations, and put some leading players in the AI space under greater scrutiny.

Stocks could continue to face some bearish pressures in the near term, but investors shouldn't overreact to the possibility of political headwinds. In fact, history says that the stock market typically enjoys strong performance in the year following midterm elections.

Due in part to uncertainty connected to the implications of election results, the stock market has tended to see more volatility and produce weaker returns in years that play host to midterm elections. Conversely, the year of trading following midterm elections has tended to produce strong returns.

Since 1950, the S&P 500 has delivered an average return of 15% in the year of trading following midterms. Meanwhile, the Nasdaq Composite has posted an average annual return of nearly 26% in post-midterm years since its founding in 1971. That kind of historical backdrop could suggest that now is a worthwhile time to put some investment dollars into the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ -1.46%) -- an exchange traded fund (ETF) that tracks the Nasdaq-100 index and includes the 100 largest non-financials companies on the Nasdaq exchange.

On the other hand, it's important to keep in mind that past performance is no guarantee of future results. There are some unique dynamics on the table right now that could cause a divergence from typical post-midterm stock market trends next year.

For starters, inflation has continued to run relatively hot -- and there's a fair chance that the Federal Reserve's Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) will vote for an interest rate increase this year and at least one additional rate hike next year. Higher interest rates help to combat inflation, but they also create a less favorable backdrop for the stock market -- and growth stocks in particular. Adding additional macroeconomic pressures, bond yields have remained stubbornly high.

Massive valuation gains for leading AI companies including Nvidia, Alphabet, and Microsoft in recent years also mean that indexes including the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite have become increasingly concentrated in recent years. This means that an investment in the Invesco QQQ Trust would likely face strong valuation pressures if sentiment surrounding top AI names shifts in a more bearish direction.

In summary, the historical trend suggests that the Nasdaq Composite, the Nasdaq 100, and the Invesco QQQ Trust should see strong returns in the year to come -- but investors also shouldn't treat that outcome as a given.
