Have Semiconductors Bottomed? Here's How the Current Chip Stock Plunge Compares With Past Bear Markets.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The current chip pullback is near where others have bottomed… with one notable exception.

After reaching all-time highs in June, the broader semiconductor sector has gone into a severe bear market.

The first big pullback came in July, with the release of powerful Chinese open-weight models and notable short-sellers making valuation-based calls. In October and into November, fears of a Democratic sweep of both houses of Congress and state governorships fueled concerns about a data center ban or slowdown.

The semiconductor sector has been the best-performing sector over the long term, but it's also the most volatile. So if chip stocks have bottomed, now could be a great entry point for long-term investors. But has the pullback ended? Here's how the current drawdown compares with past chip bear markets.

The chip sector, as denoted by the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX -3.67%), fell 39.5% from its June high to its recent low on Nov. 5. That is very severe, but roughly in line with the ETF's periodic declines over the past 10 years. In fact, the current 2026 sell-off puts it right in the middle of the average chip sector sell-off over the past decade.

As you can see, the current drawdown is right in the middle in terms of magnitude compared with the other four major drawdowns in the SOXX over the past 10 years.

The reasons for these past drawdowns were also somewhat similar to the current chip plunge. Like 2018, this year is a midterm election year during a Trump administration. Also, as in 2018, inflation had begun to tick up -- albeit off very low levels -- and the Federal Reserve was beginning to raise interest rates, which hadn't really happened since the global financial crisis of 2008.

The same sort of thing happened in late 2021, only to a greater degree. The post-pandemic shortages and stimulus spurred a wave of inflation not seen since the 1980s, prompting the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates at the fastest pace in its history. Rising inflation and interest rates in both cases caused companies and consumers to pull back on technology spending, which had been pulled forward during the pandemic. They had also been strong during the cloud computing boom in 2017-2018.

In 2024 and into 2025, government policy, combined with overly optimistic valuations at the beginning of that year driven by the AI boom, caused the pullback. The Biden administration tightened export controls on China during that period, while companies largely deferred IT spending on PCs and traditional servers to make room to buy expensive Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%) GPUs. That sell-off continued into the early second Trump term, when tariff worries, culminating in "Liberation Day," caused a final market flush and a market bottom.

Today, we are seeing a mix of all the different factors that colored prior sell-offs, aside from the unique COVID-19 crash. In these three other scenarios, a mix of high valuations, inflation, rising interest rates, and restrictive government policy led investors to question the high growth expectations embedded in chip stock valuations.

Of course, these four other examples only encompass recent history. Some investors compare the current AI pullback to the bust after the dot-com boom.

If the AI boom were to come undone similarly, the consequences would be more severe than even the severe pullback we've already seen. In the case of the SOXX, the semiconductor index never even recovered its prior high before a second crisis -- the housing bust of 2008 -- took it down even further than the dot-com crash.

Most know that the dot-com boom peaked in early 2000, which was actually before the SOXX ETF started trading in July 2001. However, after peaking in early August 2001, the SOXX eventually retreated 70.2% to its bottom in November 2008. The ETF didn't recover its August 2001 high until January 2014 -- 12.5 years after its prior peak!

If, in fact, the AI boom is one large "overbuild," as the dot-com build-out was, then this sell-off might not be over yet.

In this investor's opinion, the current situation seems more akin to more recent semiconductor bear markets than the dot-com bust. As Blackstone (BX -0.60%) CEO Jon Gray said in a recent interview with Bloomberg News:

If the current pullback is indeed a growth and supply-related scare, rather than a demand bubble bursting, then the chip sector has just about fallen in line with recent bear-market bottoms in semiconductors. That's why I have been buying more chip stocks over the past two weeks, and I would recommend that others with a long-term investing horizon do the same.
