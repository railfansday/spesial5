Is Jensen Huang’s Humble Cotton Coat His Way of Saying ‘I’m Sorry’?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Disgraced tech executive Jensen Huang has made a name for himself not just as one of the most influential figures in the booming AI industry, but for his vast collection of streamlined leather jackets. Indeed, in some circles, Huang, cofounder and CEO of NVIDIA, is known as much for his self-styled appearance as the rebel of Silicon Valley as for his shrewd business acumen. Leave Sam Altman to his schlubby Everyman athleisure and Mark Zuckerberg to his spiffy makeover featuring Prada and The Row, Huang’s choice of outerwear—and his obsessive dedication to it—was meant to convey his role as an insurgent force in the industry—no rank-and-file leader, but a rulebreaker and iconoclast.

Well, the rulebreaker part has come to vivid life in recent weeks, as Huang has made other news—namely for conducting extramarital affairs (plural) with an alleged Chinese agent and not one, but two of Nvidia’s employees. Not good, guys! While philandering is certainly frowned upon, it’s sadly not unheard of amongst the ranks of powerful men. It’s more the whole … putting our national security at risk and compromising the multi-trillion-dollar AI industry that is the real, and very serious, concern. Huang, as a result, resigned from his role on November 12, only to be reinstated unanimously by his board yesterday, a mere 11 days later. Trump was involved and … well, again, not good.

So… For his grand, Phoenix-rising-from-the-ashes moment, did Huang embrace that sartorial symbol of rebellion—the leather biker jacket? No, the CEO ditched the leather of yore and opted for a seemingly modest uniform: a lightweight zip-front jacket, slim black jeans, and a pair of slip-on leather sneakers. With the slightly modulated, distinctly softer look—call it “Redemption Dressing”—was he trying to transmit a softer, repentant version of himself (indeed, he offered up an apology to his employees who stood by clapping). In an era where optics often overshadow intention, Huang understood that he needed to visually distance himself from his former self-mythologizing posture as a bad boy and forge a new, remorseful persona. Some may scoff at the mere swapping of a piece of outerwear, and yet, Huang has hardly been seen without a leather jacket for years, so the choice, while subtle, certainly reads as considered (in the meantime, he’s single-handedly changed the meaning of the leather jacket). And yet, by adhering to his former preference for a dark palette and slim silhouette, he’s also splitting the difference, projecting—or trying to—an image of consistency and stability during a time of tumult.

New jacket, new Huang? Maybe. With those Hermès shoes costing a cool $1,600, he may be mistaken for regretful, but definitely not in need of cash.
