You Don’t Need Designer Sneakers. You Need These $95 German Army Trainers.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

We must admit that these ones from Quince are damn good.

Welcome to the Esquire Endorsement. Heavily researched. Thoroughly vetted. These picks are the best way to spend your hard-earned cash.

In my life, I lean toward luxury and secondhand clothing wherever possible, desperately trying to get away from all the cheap garbage being sold to me on Instagram ads and the Amazon homepage. I’ve spent years of my life hunting sales and eBay listings for clothes and boots. I’m like an edge-habitat pine growing in the direction of the radiant sun that is well-made clothing, trying to forget the fast fashion slop that lies in the forest behind me.

But I throw that out the window when it comes to sneakers. Designer sneakers are, for me, one of the dumbest things I could ever buy. Even when they are made with real leather, you are paying for a plastic sole that has a lifespan of a few years. I don’t think you should pay more than the $200 price of a pair of New Balance when it comes to sneakers.

Under that price point, I’ve never been able to find a good pair German Army Trainers, which I have always wanted. That changed when I found these. Quince (yeah yeah, I was just complaining about fashion slop, but life is full of contradictions) makes a perfect pair of GATs—and it makes them for $95. I honestly think every man in the world should buy a pair of these.

This is my real qualm with designer sneakers, and it totally undercuts anything you could tell me about the second-hand sneaker hype market. What you should pay for, when you pay expensive prices for clothing, is craftsmanship. When you buy a Gucci 1953 loafer or Loro Piana cashmere you probably arrived there because of the brand, but you are paying for the craftsmanship. I see no such correlation in designer sneakers, even the ones those brands make.

But a $95 pair of kicks? That’s honest. Even though I’m not bullish on Quince’s fast fashion take on quiet luxury (nor am I bullish on its caviar program), and the fact that Quince chose to closely replicate the Margiela GAT instead of the original German army models, I will let it slide. At $95, there are no claims being made about leather quality, or the footbed, or whatever is going on in the sole you can’t see. They look good and are easy to replace; it’s about as honest of a deal as you can find shopping these days.

The thing about the original BW Sport GATs—which you can get for about this price still, though used workout sneakers aren’t my cup of tea—is that they’re just about bulletproof. Those original mid-century designs made were first issued in the 1970s and stopped being issued around the end of the Cold War, a real feat of durability.

These shoes mimic that Army-issued durability. I’ve worn these sneakers for going on a year now. In that time, I’ve seen many pairs of more expensive kicks start to fall apart, get scuffed beyond recognition, and stink like hell. These have not. The pair I’ve worn all year is the pair pictured in this story. Nothing I say in this endorsement could be more convincing than that.

And really that’s the argument here. When I wrote about the Chuck Taylors I normally wear, it was a use-it-and-lose-it argument—they cost $45, who cares if the soles get holes within 12 months. But here, I fear I’m going to sound like a marketing mouthpiece for Quince. These shoes are $95, but they’ll last just as long as a pair that cost you $950. That’s all you can ask for these days.
