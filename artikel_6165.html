As someone who will log over 200,000 miles in the air this year, my travel strategy centers on maximizing value with minimal effort, especially when flying United. Even though I’ve earned their invite-only Global Services status, pairing that status with a United credit card unlocks even more value.

While United has several different credit card offerings, the United Club℠ Business Card is the clear choice for me.

The information for the United Club Business has been collected independently by The Points Guy. The card details on this page have not been reviewed or provided by the card issuer.

United Airlines offers discounted award pricing to elite members and cardholders. These discounts stack with each other to yield bigger savings on flights. The minimum discounts don’t necessarily sound substantial. Cardholders receive at least a 10% discount on United-operated award flights, while elite members get a minimum discount of 15%.

And while those are impressive, we’ve actually found much bigger discounts than that, sometimes exceeding 100,000 miles per ticket. We've even seen one-way international business-class awards drop from over 300,000 miles to under 100,000 miles.

A recent real-world example just came up on my podcast. My co-host had just opened a United credit card and was looking at a flight home from Europe for his family of four. Award tickets in business class were 80,000 miles one-way, per passenger (plus taxes and fees).

As soon as the new United credit card was linked to his profile, he saw a 15% discount, or 12,000 fewer miles per award ticket. So, less than a week after signing up for the card, he had already saved 48,000 United miles.

United recently changed how members earn miles on paid flights. Now, members without a United credit card earn significantly fewer miles than they used to. The good news is that adding a United credit card to your wallet can now boost your earnings by 30% to 50%.

For a top-tier 1K or Global Services member, that’s an extra three miles per dollar on every eligible ticket. For a road warrior taking two $450 round trips monthly, that could mean more than 30,000 extra miles earned per year.

United also changed access to United Club lounges. Its All Access membership, which includes guest privileges, can cost as much as $1,400, depending on your elite status

However, the United Club Business Card gives you two ways to significantly reduce your out-of-pocket costs for the All Access membership: Spend $50,000 on the card in a calendar year, or hold Premier Gold status or higher. With a $695 annual fee, the card can be a much cheaper way to bring guests into United Clubs.

I don’t spend much time in lounges when traveling for work, preferring to arrive as close to departure as possible. But family travel moves at a slower pace, making lounge access a welcome respite, especially with United's recent upgrades.

Access for the family used to be a “nice” plus. With the new and refreshed clubs United has opened recently, the lounge game has stepped up more than a notch.

It started with a beautiful refresh of the West United Club in Denver a few years ago. This was an existing club that shut down for a massive refresh, which gave us a peek into what the future holds for club design. Denver also debuted United’s new “grab and go” concept, United Club Fly.

Fast forward to 2026 and the airline has made two massive improvements on the United Club front. United recently opened a new, 800-seat club at George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) in Houston with an outdoor terrace. It also just opened a brand new club in the new E terminal at Washington-Dulles International Airport (IAD).

Not every club is as stellar as these new gems, but United is clearly upping its game.

United's recent changes make a compelling case for pairing elite status with a credit card. For business travelers, the United Club Business Credit Card offers more miles, cheaper awards and maybe even a quiet place to sit with the family on your next vacation.
