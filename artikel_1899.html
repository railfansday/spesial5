30 Greatest Soccer Forwards of All Time Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Correspondent AUTHORITY Football journalist with experience at Reach PLC and PA Media; University of Central Lancashire graduate. FOCUS Fan-engagement content and match analysis across the Premier League and Championship. THE INSIGHT Oliver utilises a network of club and industry contacts to deliver verified reporting and fan-first content. He provides the human stories behind the results to ensure fans get the full picture as it happens.

Goals win trophies, and the greatest forwards in soccer history have defined every era of the sport.

From Pele’s three World Cup triumphs and Diego Maradona’s brilliance to Ronaldo Nazario’s explosive prime, attacking icons have consistently captured global imagination.

In the modern era, Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo shattered every scoring record imaginable, taking their rivalry from European nights to Inter Miami and Saudi Arabia.

Football Insider has ranked the 30 greatest soccer forwards of all time—covering center-forwards, wide forwards, and second strikers—judged on peak dominance, silverware, goalscoring output, and lasting legacy.

Scroll down to see who claims the number one spot, and let us know in the comments who we ranked too low.

Kicking this list off is an enigmatic genius of a striker who had it all. Ibrahimovic was consistently dominant in the air, had outstanding technique and could score from anywhere on the pitch. He is one of the most recognisable soccer players ever, with his long ponytailed locks providing an iconic silhouette to a decorated career.

He cannot be any higher on this list because of a lack of success at international level and in the Champions League, but the Swedish superstar featured for several of the world’s biggest clubs and scored over 570 career goals across four consecutive decades. He even took his talents to LA in 2018 and became one of the best MLS players of all time.

One of the few active players in this list, and the youngest by a margin, Haaland has rewritten the rules of goalscoring in the Premier League and is well on track to being an all-time great of the sport. He utilises immense power and pace and otherworldly movement in the penalty area to score at an unbelievable rate every season, and is one of the biggest names in sport in this century. His recent World Cup exploits propelled him to being the fifth-most followed player ever on Instagram as it stands.

Haaland boasts a number of the biggest honours in the game and has already earned endless achievements despite likely only being halfway through his playing career. His record-breaking career is showing no signs of slowing down, and he may well end his playing days in the top 10 of this list.

The reigning Ballon d’Or winner has set the benchmark for being a complete striker over the last decade of soccer. He is England’s greatest ever player, when impact and statistics are considered, and the best part is that he was never even meant to be this good. Kane deserves his plaudits for putting in the work to dominate across numerous competitions, with clinical finishing and pinpoint passing being key parts of his repertoire.

He may be nearing the end of his career, but is arguably better than ever before and could see his stock rise even further with more trophies added to his cabinet. He is the only Englishman to score over 500 official senior career goals and the fastest player in Bundesliga history to reach 100 strikes.

Moving away from traditional number nines, Figo is the first winger to be included on the list. He had incredible speed and vision and remains one of the most controversial figures in the sport for his world-record move from Barcelona to Real Madrid in 2000. That switch is still remembered as one of the craziest events in soccer, and he was also a leading figure in Portugal’s Golden Generation.

Figo won the Ballon d’Or in 2000 and FIFA World Player of the Year in 2001, while also picking up several big domestic and European honours in his time in Spain and at Inter Milan. He was not particularly a prolific scorer, but did utilise amazing creativity to record 276 assists in 919 career appearances.

Salah has been one of the defining players of his generation following an unprecedentedly amazing career at Liverpool. He has broken the mould for African players in the sport, becoming a trailblazer for those from his continent in the 21st century by combining electric speed and trickery with outstanding finishing ability and movement.

The Egyptian star is the greatest African player to ever grace the sport, with countless trophies and exceptional longevity at the top level firing him to the top above other strong candidates. He remains a world-renowned name in soccer and boasts incredible records and achievements in the Premier League and Champions League.

No, this list is not only full of players who have featured in the 21st century. Kopa was one of the first household names in soccer as he scored prolifically across Europe in the 1950s and 1960s. His influence on the game as we know it is still prevalent today, with the ‘Kopa Trophy’ awarded to the best-performing under-21 player each year being named after him because he was such a fantastic player and because he won the 1958 Ballon d’Or.

The versatile French forward was also known for his outstanding dribbling and playmaking abilities, as he became the focal point of a Real Madrid side that won three successive European Cups between 1956 and 1959.

Regarded by many as the best Premier League player ever, and potentially the greatest in English soccer of all time, Henry comes in ahead of Salah due to the sheer rate at which he contributed to goals and the outstanding aesthetics that he carried throughout a decorated career. The Frenchman had style, grace and an unmatched aura that could only be summed up by his own “va-va-voom” catchphrase, coined in a 2002 Renault commercial.

His complete centre-forward abilities made him one of the most feared players of all time across his career, and he also had the trophy haul to match. World Cup and European Championship glory came alongside Champions League silverware, and he even ended his soccer days with a memorable MLS swansong at the New York Red Bulls.

Lewandowski is one of the greatest pure goalscorers in the history of soccer. His elite finishing, exceptional anticipation and underrated physicality have all combined to make him a serious force across several decades of playing. He will go down as one of the best players to ever play in Germany and certainly one of the finest strikers in this century.

He was robbed of a Ballon d’Or in 2020 after cancellation due to COVID-19, and arguably again in 2021 as Messi trumped him on reputation alone. The Polish superstar is his country’s greatest ever player and has scored over 750 goals across a 20-year career, while he is now winding down his playing days with Chicago Fire.

Neymar is perhaps one of the most divisive soccer players ever, given that he would have placed even higher on this list had he applied himself fully, but his amazing natural ability has still seen him become one of the world’s greatest. He is a modern-day Brazilian icon who has dominated social media and the wider sporting zeitgeist of the last 15 years, and holds the distinction of being the closest of any player to breaking up the Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo Ballon d’Or duopoly of the 2010s.

The ex-Barcelona winger is Selecao soccer royalty, even though the World Cup has eluded him despite his outstanding career. He is the all-time top scorer and assist provider for his national team and is one of five players ever to have scored over 100 professional goals with three different clubs. Neymar also remains the most expensive player ever after joining PSG for £200m in 2017.

Rummenigge was capable of playing across the forward line and dominated defences in Germany during his 10 years at Bayern Munich between 1974 and 1984. He was equally strong with both feet and a fine dribbler who boasted impressive finishing ability, while also becoming a global ambassador for his country as he established himself as a star for Inter after Bayern.

He won the Ballon d’Or in 1980 and 1981, and helped shape the German giants into a European superpower with two European Cup wins in the 70s. Rummenigge also won the European Championships with his country and finished as a runner-up twice in the World Cup.

Kocsis was an integral part of Hungary’s ‘Golden Team’ of the 1950s, which remains one of the most iconic national team sides in soccer history. The Budapest-born star became a sporting icon for his country throughout his playing days, as he scored at an exceptional rate for several sides and also became one of Barcelona’s greatest-ever players. His nickname was ‘Golden Head’ because of his exceptional heading ability.

He is second in the list of top single-tournament World Cup scorers, with 11 in the 1954 edition, and his 1.103 goals per game average is ranked number one for those with caps in FIFA class-A competitions, meaning he is effectively the most prolific international scorer ever.

An icon who is perhaps mostly defined by his off-field troubles and antics, Best is lauded as one of the best of his time and someone who consistently pushed fans to the edge of their seats whenever he had the ball at his feet. He was soccer’s original celebrity superstar in the West, and his mop-top hairstyle remains synonymous with those silky feet to this day.

Best changed the image of soccer players as a real household name in the UK, and had the undoubted ability to match his fame. Man United were already a huge club before his arrival on the scene in 1963, but he fired them to a maiden European Cup triumph and was a key player as they continued to rise. The Northern Irishman’s career tailed off but he still had time to become one of the first big-name stars to play in the USA with LA Aztecs, as he enjoyed numerous strong spells in the NASL.

Back to the modern day with a player who is currently starring in MLS, ‘El Pistolero’ has been one of the best soccer stars in the world since joining Liverpool in 2011 and is one of Uruguay’s greatest of all time. His outstanding technical ability has been flanked by an unstoppable determination to win and pure aggression over the last 15 years, with numerous high-profile incidents making him one of the most controversial players ever too.

Suarez was part of the best attacking trio of all time at Barcelona alongside Neymar and Messi, and has scored over 600 professional goals in his career so far. He holds numerous major records and is another who would have dominated even more if not for two standouts also playing in his generation.

Some may feel like Mbappe is too high on this list, but it is only right to celebrate him as one of the greatest forwards ever, even if he is nowhere near finished in the sport of soccer. The French superstar has reached incredible heights over the last 10 years and is a global phenomenon who consistently ranks among the best scorers and players every season. Mbappe is yet to lift a Ballon d’Or but, incredibly, has finished in the top 10 in all nine editions in which he has been nominated.

His remarkable collection of records includes: most World Cup goals in history (22), most World Cup final goals (4), France’s all-time leading scorer and the only player to win two World Cup Golden Boots. He is only likely to move further up this list as time goes on.

Matthews was one of soccer’s first genuine global superstars. Nicknamed ‘The Wizard of the Dribble’, he helped to define the traditional winger’s role and attracted hundreds of thousands of supporters to stadiums all by himself during his time with Stoke, Blackpool and England. Pele reportedly said that he had taught people how soccer should be played.

He became a landmark figure in the history of the sport after winning the first Ballon d’Or in 1956 at the age of 41. Matthews also remains the oldest professional to play in England, at 50, and he raised the profile of wide players as a whole for the way he decimated defences before retiring in 1965.

The San Siro’s official name is the ‘Stadio Giuseppe Meazza’; such is the significance of the Italian forward to soccer in the region and his country. It was named thus in 1980 following his passing, after Meazza represented both clubs between 1927 and 1942 and built his name as one of the sport’s first prolific scorers at the top level known across the world. He was regarded as an elegant player and a great leader who could feature across the forward line.

Meazza won numerous individual accolades during and after his playing career. His crowning moment was a World Cup Golden Ball award in Italy’s winning campaign of 1934, while he also featured as they won a successive crown four years later.

Puskas is another star of the past who has been able to transcend generations in soccer folklore. He was the crown jewel of Hungary’s Golden Team and a leading star in the sport across the world in the 1950s and 60s. His outstanding range of scoring ability and prolificness means his legacy still lives on through FIFA’s Puskas Award, awarded to the best goal each year.

He holds one of the best international scoring rates, with 84 in 85 Hungary games, the most goals scored in a single European Cup final (four for Real Madrid in 1960), and bagged a whopping 802 official career goals in 792 matches between 1943 and 1966.

Muller was such a good striker that France Football’s award for the most prolific player in that position for each season has been named after him since 2021. The six-yard box was home for the German star, who needed little space or time to find the net and was a defining figure for his country as well as a global star of the 1970s and 80s.

He is one of just eleven players ever to have won the Ballon d’Or, World Cup and Champions League. Muller also plundered home 68 goals in 62 international appearances, while he remains the all-time leading scorer in the Bundesliga (356 goals).

Romario was one of the most clinical forwards in soccer history during his playing days. He personified Brazilian skill and flair alongside a natural ruthless streak in the area to become one of the leading strikers of his generation in the 1980s, 1990s and 2000s. Romario only featured for eight seasons in Europe, and so his outstanding legacy is testament to a career that was not held back by a preference to play in his home country.

His explosive ability in the final third fired Brazil to 1994 World Cup glory in the USA, as he lifted the Golden Ball and was named the FIFA World Player of the Year. He scored over 700 professional goals in his decorated career.

Van Basten retired at just 30 years old in 1995, and so it speaks to just how much of an impact he had on soccer that he is so high on this list despite injury ending his career prematurely. The Dutch forward combined exceptional physical presence with amazing technique to create iconic moments aplenty. His volleyed goal in the Euro 1988 final against the USSR is widely regarded as one of the greatest ever.

He was a central figure in AC Milan’s run as one of the most successful European sides in that same period, winning two European Cups and the Ballon d’Or three times between 1988 and 1992. Van Basten’s career was an incredible one as he peaked higher than many in this list, but he can only rank here because it was cruelly cut short.

Ronaldinho brought Brazilian street spirit to the world’s biggest stages in his time playing soccer. He was the ultimate entertainer and became one of the most recognisable and most marketable sporting figures on the planet in the 2000s for his iconic swagger on and off the pitch. The extraordinary playmaker became an icon for his nation, as well as the likes of Barcelona, PSG and AC Milan.

His mercurial performances helped Brazil win the 2002 World Cup, and he also won the 2005 Ballon d’Or while spearheading Barca’s resurgence throughout the decade. Real Madrid fans were even drawn to applauding his performance in a particular El Clasico, while he was the world’s highest-paid soccer player ahead of David Beckham in 2006 too, owing to his wealth of commercial endorsements.

‘The Blond Arrow’ was an outstanding striker for his time and is held up as one of the greatest players to grace the sport. Di Stefano was a complete player who could drop deep and dictate play as well as hang around the box to score at will. He is attributed with pioneering such a role, as well as an early version of the Dutch ‘Total Football’.

His 1953 arrival at Real Madrid transformed the club into the global superpower we know today, with five consecutive European Cup wins, as he scored in each final, and eight La Liga titles in his next 11 seasons at the club. He ranks fourth in their all-time scorers list (308) after also winning two Ballon d’Ors and the unique Super Ballon d’Or award in 1989.

Garrincha pioneered and symbolised the Brazilian idea of ‘joga bonito’, as he wowed audiences throughout his career with incredible dribbling skills, balance and acceleration. He provided the blueprint for the winger role as we know it now and was a global superstar as Brazilians took the sport by storm in the mid-20th century.

He carried his country to the 1962 World Cup and won the Golden Ball, while Brazil also remarkably avoided defeat across all 40 international games that he played with Pele. The right winger is one of the greatest players to feature in the top flight of his home country, with 245 recorded goals in 612 appearances for Botafogo.

Eusebio put Portuguese soccer on the map during a similar era to Garrincha, and was the first African-born player to become a worldwide star in the sport. He was dangerous both inside and outside the penalty area while possessing excellent close control and underrated physicality. Eusebio was the biggest soccer export from Portugal before the 21st century and helped to break down barriers as a leading black figure in a time full of racial discrimination and inequality.

The 1965 Ballon d’Or winner’s achievements speak for themselves. He was central to Benfica becoming a powerhouse of a club as they won numerous league and continental titles with him in attack, while also winning the European Golden Shoe twice and the 1966 World Cup Golden Boot.

O Fenomeno is among the most technically gifted strikers on this list and was a generational talent during his soccer career. He was a completely unstoppable force on the pitch and became a global celebrity off it in the rising digital age of the early 2000s. No one can forget Ronaldo’s 2002 World Cup haircut, which may well be the most iconic style of any player ever.

An absolute nightmare for defenders, the Brazilian’s silky skills and devastating speed made goals almost easy for him to come by, even at the top level. He is a club legend for Barcelona, Inter and Real Madrid, a two-time World Cup winner and two-time Ballon d’Or winner, and still one of soccer’s biggest ‘what ifs’ as serious knee injuries disrupted his later career.

Cruyff remains a true pioneer and trailblazer for attackers throughout soccer history. The enigmatic Dutch star was able to destroy defences with exceptional intelligence and creativity while being the face of ‘Total Football.’ His long, swept-back hair and innocent look became iconic as it went hand-in-hand with the effortless class he showed on the pitch.

The number 14 is synonymous with Ajax icon Cruyff, who helped the Dutch side dominate European soccer in the 1970s and won three Ballon D’Ors in four years during that period. Cruyff is the greatest Ajax and Netherlands player ever, while he also holds legendary status at Barcelona.

Five-time Ballon d’Or winner Ronaldo has a seriously strong claim to being the most recognisable and well-known sportsperson of all time. That is no mean feat, but his incredible wealth, longevity and social reach all combine to make him a machine of marketing to this very day. He has built such an empire by becoming one of the most dominant and prolific soccer forwards ever over the last 20 years.

The Portuguese icon holds records galore for numerous competitions and clubs, with the most international appearances (234) and goals (146) of any player in the sport. He is approaching the incredible 1,000-goal mark and is the statistically greatest player in Champions League history too. Ronaldo misses out on the top three through no real fault of his own, but those above him were more naturally talented players.

Maradona is not only one of the greatest soccer stars ever; he defined the sport for numerous years and became a global phenomenon for his crazy antics on and off the pitch, while also building an almost cult-like following that spread across clubs he represented and the wider soccer world. He became a symbol of national pride in Argentina and even inspired the creation of a religion, such is the way he captivated those by how he played the sport.

The eccentric second striker enjoyed the single best World Cup campaign of all time as he dragged his country to glory in 1986, with his ‘Hand of God’ goal against England arguably the most iconic and well-known strike in soccer to this day. His best club achievement was undoubtedly transforming Napoli into Italian champions, as he became a hero in Italy, with his influence there still prominent to this day as their stadium is named after him.

Pele was long regarded as the number one on this list, but will have to settle for second place despite being a game-defining superstar who remains lauded as a soccer immortal nearly 50 years on from his retirement. The greatest player from the most decorated country in the sport, superlatives can barely describe his legacy after he was key to making soccer a global spectacle in the mid-20th century.

The Brazilian was the best player of his generation and would have been the greatest in those directly before and after him before Maradona came along. Pele remains the only player to win three World Cups, scoring in two finals, while the debate around his official career goals total rages on. He boasted over 1,000 strikes with friendly and exhibition games included, and was also a trailblazer overseas as he grew the game exponentially in the USA during his stint at the New York Cosmos.

Messi is simply the best soccer player ever. Forget forwards. The Argentine hero has defined a generation over the last 20 years and reached heights that no one thought could be found after Pele and Maradona. He also has a strong claim to be the most recognisable sportsperson of all time, with immense wealth and global reach making him a celebrity across the planet aside from his genius on-field achievements.

The iconic number 10 shirt worn by those before him is now Messi’s above anyone else. He has defied description in so many ways on countless occasions, yet has seemed to reach even greater levels in recent years towards the end of his career. The most Ballon d’Ors (8), most goals in a calendar year (91), the most official goals scored for one club (672 at Barcelona) and the most official team trophies (47) are just some of the seemingly limitless list of records he holds in the sport. It will be one of the most monumental days in sporting history when he decides to hang up his boots.
