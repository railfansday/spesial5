Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Binance said it secured a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing injunction from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts to help it recover assets stolen in its recent hack, tokenized Nvidia’s market cap surged 159% as the chipmaker’s stock sank, and reports of cryptocurrency theft involving tampered Ledger hardware wallets have emerged in Dubai.

Binance said it has secured a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing injunction from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts as it seeks to recover assets stolen in its record Oct. 29 hack.

The court orders target unidentified perpetrators of the attack and are intended to help issuers, exchanges and other partners freeze and return stolen assets, the company said in a statement shared with Cointelegraph on Wednesday.

The orders can be served via blockchain, according to M. Kendall Day of Gibson Dunn, which represents Binance. This allows legal notices to be delivered to wallet addresses rather than through traditional channels. Day said market participants would also be notified of updates to a “living list” of 26,000 wallet addresses identified as holding stolen assets.

Binance said some businesses require formal court orders before they can release, transfer or return frozen funds. The injunction’s $6.5 billion amount is not a tally of recovered assets, and the statement did not disclose how much had so far been returned under the orders.

Binance also said it obtained orders requiring stolen assets to be handed over. Separate interim orders cover assets frozen by certain named parties against whom no wrongdoing is alleged.

The combined market value of tokenized stocks tied to Nvidia shares rose about 159% from Nov. 8 to Tuesday, while the chipmaker’s Nasdaq-listed shares fell nearly 17% from their last close before co-founder Jensen Huang publicly admitted to an extramarital affair.

The value of Nvidia (NVDA) tokens tracked by Token Terminal rose from $155 million on Nov. 8 to $401.9 million by Tuesday.

Nvidia shed about $750 billion in market value, falling from $4.5 trillion at the Nov. 6 close to $3.75 trillion at Tuesday’s close, according to Yahoo Finance data.

Huang acknowledged the affair in a message to Nvidia employees on Nov. 8, nearly two weeks after The New York Times reported that US intelligence officials suspected Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang of spying for Beijing. China denied the allegations.

Tokenized stocks are blockchain tokens designed to track the price of a share. In one common setup, an issuer creates tokens backed by shares held by a custodian.

Binance, for example, lists an Nvidia token that can trade when Nasdaq is closed. It gives investors exposure to Nvidia’s share price without shareholder rights. The market value can rise as more tokens enter circulation, even if Nvidia’s share price falls.

Two Ledger owners have reported cryptocurrency thefts involving hardware wallets they say were purchased from an authorized retailer in Dubai, raising concerns that a hardware wallet tampering campaign had also spread to the UAE.

An X user posting as Rewinsky on Monday said they found their Ledger Flex had been tampered with after reporting the loss of about 1,300 Solana (SOL) Mark Karpelès’ Ledger Implant OSINT registry subsequently confirmed the device as containing a hardware implant.

A second owner, posting as New Babylon Order Worldwide, said Tuesday that 507,000 USDt (USDT) was stolen from their Ledger Stax on Saturday. They said the device was bought new at Virgin Megastore Dubai on Oct. 11, two days after Ledger publicly disclosed an investigation into crypto losses involving devices sold by Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis.

The reports suggest the Ledger device tampering has extended past retailers in just Asia and Europe. Ledger lists Virgin Megastores among its authorized retailers in the United Arab Emirates.
