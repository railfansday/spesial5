Verstappen wins wild Las Vegas GP as Ferraris collide again

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Max Verstappen wins wild Las Vegas Grand Prix; Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri claim double podium for McLaren after late Mercedes collapse saw George Russell and Kimi Antonelli drop back; Charles Leclerc penalised for causing collision with Ferrari team-mate Lewis Hamilton

Max Verstappen produced a remarkable drive to win a wild Las Vegas Grand Prix that saw him withstand extreme pressure from George Russell and Kimi Antonelli before a late Mercedes collapse.

Pole-sitter Verstappen retained the lead at the start but the enormous power of a slipstream on the long straights of the Las Vegas Strip Circuit made it impossible for the Dutchman to pull clear, and resulted in 34 changes of the lead and well over 300 total overtakes in the 50-lap race.

First Russell, and then later newly crowned world champion Antonelli were each able to pass Verstappen on multiple occasions but the Red Bull driver calmly retook the lead within a few corners every time, expertly managing the deployment of his power.

Russell, trying to pass his team-mate for second with three laps remaining, ran into a barrier after overshooting a corner and dropped down the order, before Antonelli made an error with his deployment at the start of the final lap that allowed Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri to snatch a double podium for McLaren.

"That was crazy. Really, really surreal and something I've never seen before in F1 myself," Verstappen said.

"I think we had it [deployment] set up well and it was a crazy amount of fighting, and the slipstream was so big that it was just impossible to drive off, and it was just crazy how many people were following."

There was more unwanted drama for Ferrari as team-mates Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc collided again as they battled for sixth with 12 laps remaining, with the latter given a 10-second penalty by the stewards for causing the incident.

Pierre Gasly took fifth ahead of his team-mate Franco Colapinto to secure a major points haul for Alpine, with Hamilton recovering to seventh, having served a 10-second penalty himself earlier in the race for overtaking Norris off track.

Carlos Sainz snatched eighth from Russell on the run to the finish line to equal Williams' best result of the season, while Leclerc claimed the final point in 10th.

The result all but secures second in the drivers' standings for Verstappen, who made it four wins in six races to pull 37 points clear of third-placed Hamilton with two rounds to go in Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

McLaren's late gains enabled them to close within nine points of Red Bull in the battle for third in the constructors' standings, with Verstappen's team-mate Isack Hadjar unfortunate to finish outside the points after a front-wing failure - moments after a Safety Car stoppage that had bunched the field - dropped him from third to last.

Despite Antonelli and Mercedes having already closed out this year's championships, there was plenty of tension on the grid as Verstappen and Russell lined up on the front row following a renewal of a long-term spat between the pair earlier in the weekend.

Following a collision between the pair in final practice that resulted in Verstappen being given a warning by the stewards, Russell launched a scathing criticism of the Dutchman, echoing comments he made almost exactly two years ago as he said someone needed to "stand up" to the Red Bull driver.

Despite those words, there was plenty of respect on show between the pair on track as they battled for much of the race after Verstappen had made a strong start to retain the lead.

Russell, who was already under constant pressure from Hadjar and then Norris, adjusted his deployment strategy to repeatedly find a way past Verstappen in the final sector. However, he could never find a way to keep the Red Bull behind him through the opening corners of the following lap.

The only real pause in the frantic action came on lap 19 when Cadillac's Sergio Perez and Haas' Rafael Camara collided as the former attempted an overtake.

The damage left Perez stranded on track, triggering the Safety Car. Its deployment was extended when Racing Bulls' Arvid Lindblad, who was running fifth at the time, spun and also came to a stop on the circuit.

When the race resumed at the end of lap 25, Russell continued to put Verstappen under pressure, retaking the lead several times. He even reclaimed first place after briefly dropping behind Norris.

Russell made his final move into the lead at the end of lap 37, but Verstappen quickly reclaimed the position. For the first time in the race, Russell then dropped behind team-mate Antonelli, who had fought back from a five-place grid penalty despite securing pole position in qualifying.

Russell was tyring to mount one last push for victory on lap 48, but his attempt to go up the inside of Antonelli at Turn 5 came unstuck as he locked up and nudged into the barrier.

Having seen the incident in his mirrors, Verstappen immediately came on team radio - apparently delighted at his rival's error - saying: "Haha. Well done! My god…"

Verstappen had already demonstrated his awareness midway through the race, opening discussions with his team over the radio about adjusting their deployment strategy to ensure Mercedes could not beat him in a drag race to the line on the final lap.

Ultimately, such tactics weren't necessary as Antonelli, by lifting off the throttle, triggered a loss in deployment that allowed both McLarens to overtake him and gave Verstappen a relatively stress-free final lap.

The four-time world champion wasn't finished taking a dig at Russell. Asked about the Brit during the post-race press conference, Verstappen added: "It was honestly fantastic when I looked in my mirror and saw what was happening. I was like, 'Fantastic, my day is only getting better.'"

"And of course, when I then crossed the line in first, my day was complete."

With Antonelli and Mercedes dominating this year's championships, much of F1's drama in recent weeks has been provided by the repeated battles between Ferrari team-mates Hamilton and Leclerc.

Hamilton was left furious following the Mexico City Grand Prix at the start of the month when Leclerc launched an ambitious attempt to take the lead from him on the opening lap, resulting in a clash that cost the team a likely one-two and gifted Verstappen victory.

Both drivers said the situation had been resolved following talks in Brazil the following week, but that didn't appear to be the case on Saturday night in Las Vegas.

Starting from 9th and 10th after a dismal qualifying, the pair made minor contact at the start, but far worse was to come.

Hamilton had driven impressively to fight his way back through the field after serving a 10-second penalty for going off track while passing Norris. He was ahead of Leclerc in the battle for sixth and pushing to retake the position from the McLaren when the pair came together.

Having seen Hamilton fail to get past Norris, Leclerc attempted to capitalise, making an ambitious move up the inside at Turn 7.

The result was a collision that spun Hamilton and saw him drop back through the field, with the stewards deciding Leclerc was to blame as he had come from too far behind his team-mate, which meant Hamilton had no obligation to leave space on the inside.

Hamilton was measured in his comments both on the radio and after the race, but Ferrari team principal Frederic Vasseur took the notable action as he summoned Leclerc to speak to him immediately after the race, saying over the radio: "Charles, I want to see you after the race before everything."

Vasseur later told Sky Sports F1: "We passed the message to Charles to not overtake because in this kind of race you have to overtake the cars in front of you or you are passed by the others. It means that you have to do your race and Charles did clearly a mistake that's penalising the team a lot. It's completely unacceptable today."

While the latest episode of the Verstappen-Russell beef will perhaps be the biggest talking point ahead of Thursday's media day at the Qatar Grand Prix, a new chapter of Ferrari's internal battle and Vasseur's inability to control the situation will be met with similar anticipation.
