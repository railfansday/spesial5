Whiff of arrogance as Arsenal unexpectedly implode

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Arsenal were cruising. This was life on Premier League easy street, but from complete control of this game against Newcastle United, they tossed it away. It was a spectacular, unexpected implosion. They were extremely fortunate to escape with a point.

You cannot switch off away from home in the Premier League and you certainly can never be complacent against a team with Newcastle’s home crowd behind them.

From control to disarray. From looking like champions, Arsenal, who also lost goalkeeper David Raya to a worrying looking injury, rumbled under pressure. To go from dominance to being grateful for a draw, this was a flaky side of the Premier League leaders we have not seen for a while.

“We have lost two points,” said manager Mikel Arteta. “We were exceptional in the first half, the intensity and the quality, we should have been two, three or four goals up.

“When you don’t do that, in the second half you cannot give the opposition belief and you cannot give them goals. We did both. We started to lose duels, second balls, we gave it away.”

Was it arrogance? Did the title-holders start to believe they were simply too good to be hurt or worried by a team with Newcastle’s obvious limitations? It looked like it. The way Arsenal fell apart as soon as the momentum in the game shifted in the home team’s favour in the second half was shocking.

Newcastle were dreadful before the break and managed just one attack before half-time. Perhaps it was all too easy for Arteta’s side. They have become used to ferocious battles on Tyneside, but this was a stroll for 45 minutes. When it turned into a proper test after half-time, though, they folded.

The visitors took a deserved lead, through Eberechi Eze, having barely had to break sweat, but the way they approached the second half was baffling. As soon as Newcastle got in their faces and made it a scrap, Arsenal were bullied, ruffled and rushed into mistakes.

To give the opposition one goal against the run of play can be forgiven, but to do it a second time, when the heat is cranked up, hints at a fragility we no longer thought existed under Arteta.

The first goal came from a blunder by captain Martin Odegaard. It was a lazy, sloppy swing of the leg to try to clear a throw-in and the ball sliced backwards. When it looped into the path of Will Osula, he squared to the unmarked Harvey Barnes who put away the equaliser comfortably.

It was Newcastle’s first shot on target, but Arsenal’s air of invincibility vanished and the crowd sensed it. Newcastle players responded to the noise, snapping into tackles and pouring forward in numbers, yet the second goal was also an Arsenal gift.

How do you leave the giant Dan Burn unmarked at a corner? Newcastle’s captain had so much time and space the crowd were able to scream in anticipation before his header flew goalwards, eventually bundled across the line by his centre-back colleague Malick Thiaw.

Newcastle deserved it. For half an hour in the second half they were the better side. From the depths of despair, from the apathy and boos of half-time, this was a completely different team.

If this is the vertical football Matthias Jaissle has preached since he arrived in August, Newcastle supporters can buy into it. There was speed, a physicality and a boldness in this second-half performance. It was fun and exciting. It was football played on the front foot. It was what we were promised when the German replaced Eddie Howe.

“That is Newcastle’s DNA, the second half is what we want this team to play like,” said Jaissle. “We knew there would be ups and downs this season, but if we can put that performance together for 90 minutes we can definitely say we are moving in the right direction.”

The problem is, Newcastle have not put a 90-minute performance like this together all season. We have only seen it in flashes and as good as the second half was, we cannot lose sight of the fact the first was abject. Newcastle’s football was aimless, clueless and dysfunctional. One attack before the break, one deflected shot wide is simply not good enough.

The transformation at half-time was unexpected. The substitutes helped; Tino Livramento was a massive upgrade on Amar Dedic at right-back, Jacob Ramsey improved the midfield and Anthony Elanga brought speed and, yes, a vertical threat to their attack.

But Jaissle’s side did not hold on for the win. They had the chances to score a third, but it was their ambition to do so that caused their downfall. Arsenal’s class shone again, passing through the lines to release Noni Madueke.

The winger still had lots to do, but having cut inside, leaving two Newcastle defenders on the ground, his shot squeezed through a crowd and through the arms of goalkeeper Lukasz Hornicek. The Czech international will not want to see it again, and this comes after a similar mistake in the home defeat to Everton.

Speaking to Sky Sports, the Arsenal boss said: “My feeling is that we lost two points today. I think we were exceptional in the first half. At every level. Our intensity, the clarity that we have, how to attack the jumps of Newcastle every time they wanted to go man-to-man, the composure that we have, the way we use our relationship to break their press. And it should have been 2-0, 3-0 or 4-0 up, clear.

“But it wasn’t. And when it wasn’t, then we expected in the second half that we have to again raise the level because the opponent is going to have a reaction. And the opponent did have a reaction and we were very below the level that is required to stretch the gap in a game like this in this stadium. And then we gave them belief.

“And I say we give them belief because we give them a goal, we give them a throw-in that we just give them the ball. We have no structure to control the opposite winger. We concede a goal and it’s game on. And from that it became a very chaotic game. Every set-piece, we know that they are exceptional at it and we concede from a set piece.

“And then you have to react. And then we went back to the team that the exceptional team that we were in the first half again for another 25 minutes and we scored a great goal. We create infinity of openings to create and score the third goal, but we lack some time or to be precise, to make that count.”
