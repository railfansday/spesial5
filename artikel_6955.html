Before &amp; After: Two Best Friends Turned a Stripped-Down Tudor Into a Colorful, Antique-Filled Dream Home

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Designer Anne Hulcher Tollett understood her client’s signature aesthetic better than anyone.

Anne Hulcher Tollett can’t remember a life without her best friend, Van. They met in a playpen as toddlers and quickly became an integral part of each other's lives, with many childhood memories taking place in Van’s house growing up. Tollett has been with her best friend through every milestone, including as her maid of honor at her wedding to her husband, Sean, and when the couple put an offer on a suburbanized Tudor home in Richmond, Virginia.

The five-bedroom house had good bones and an intuitive flow, but it needed help getting some of its charm back. When Van and Sean bought it for their family of four (plus their dog, Jelly Bean, and a small flock of chickens), it was whitewashed and lacked architectural character. In short, it wasn’t up to par with Van’s eclectic style, according to Tollett, but as the principal designer of Hanover Avenue, she reassured her bestie that she could easily take it from suburban chic to warm, layered, and personality-filled without having to completely rework the interior.

“Van’s aesthetic is diametrically opposed to ‘simple’ or ‘builder-grade,’ and although this house had been meticulously renovated and maintained by the previous owners, some of the charm she loves had been removed,” says Tollett. The designer and her team focused on lighter renovations and cosmetic upgrades, walking her best friend through every decision, from wallpaper choices to reworking the staircase to highlight the amazing treasures Van has collected on her travels over the years.

Throughout the renovation, Tollett and Van leaned into their sisterly shorthand, resulting in a project with very few disruptions. “Our biggest challenge was creating unexpected quirky moments without taking down walls or undoing all of the thoughtful and well-built upgrades that came with the house,” Tollett explains. “We found places on tables, walls, and bookshelves to highlight everything Van and her family have collected over the years. It makes the house feel like theirs and not the product of some design firm.”

Walls were covered with grasscloth, patterns were layered on throw pillows and vintage rugs, and anything that wasn’t antique or pre-owned was chosen to add a touch of patina to the space, allowing Van’s collections to truly sing.

Once Tollett was finished with the renovation, the house told the story of their friendship and a life well lived. Because the designer knows Van like the back of her hand, she was able to create a retreat that feels like a true reflection of who she is and how her family lives without editing out any of Van’s eccentricities. “We understood her design sensibility and took something well-built but fairly plain and made it fun, pretty, and interesting at every turn, which is exactly how anyone would describe Van!” Tollett adds. Besides, who better to design your house than the person who knows you better than yourself?

The barrel back and turquoise chairs were finds that Tollett sourced for Van’s previous home, proving that timeless profiles and accessible sizes can work in many different spaces throughout their lifetimes.

“A room like this with a vaulted ceiling and white walls creates a wonderful backdrop for bold pops of color and patterns because it can visually hold its own against furnishings with personality,” she says. “Van had the beautiful sideboard, and we loved pairing it with the tattooed table.”

Possibly one of the most remarkable makeovers in the entire house, Tollett coated the entryway in deep blue paint, topping it off with darling wallpaper on the ceiling for added dimension. “This is a great example of how a can of paint, a roll of wallpaper, and some strategically placed treasures can transform a room,” she says.

Large-scale grasscloth wallpaper instantly gave the once-all-white box character and rhythm, setting a serene backdrop for the rest of the unique finds in this dining room. The chandelier hanging over the table had long been on Tollett’s list to use in a project, and Van’s home was the perfect spot.

“The dining room is right near the front door, so it’s hung to look like it's being blown by the entrance breeze—a true classic with a twist!” the designer says.

Above the antique bar (whose natural patina plays so well with the wallpaper), are two framed photos of puppets, which Van found during one of her globe-trotting trips. She carried them around in her backpack, and Tollett loved the story behind them so much that she had them framed. On the opposite wall, behind the piano, is a portrait of Van’s grandmother in a vibrant green dress.

The kitchen was well-maintained when Tollett got her hands on it, but it didn’t feel all that fun, and it needed more than simple cosmetic refreshes like other areas of the home.

“We removed the big-box island lighting and reworked the storage, hood, and casework on the range wall,” Tollett explains. “We did the same on the wall opposite the range,” she adds, which was in fantastic shape, so they kept it.

To give the cook space more character, the designer installed a custom Delftware tile backsplash. The island was also in good shape, so Tollett upgraded the hardware, faucet, face frames, and lighting above it, opting for three Pinch pendant lights which bring an interesting, natural texture to the space.

“Open concept kitchens are tricky because they are seen from every angle, so we knew we needed to improve the storage to hide clutter,” she adds. Opposite the range wall, Tollett removed the open shelves, creating closed casework instead and topping everything off with pretty hardware and colors that play well with the adjacent dining and living rooms.

Tollett knew she was going to wrap this powder room in a beautiful butterfly-and-bird wallpaper, so she custom-matched a paint to fully drench the room in a whimsical emerald green hue. They even had their wonderful wallpaper installers cut out a few extra birds and butterflies to extend the design onto the ceiling, creating a more immersive experience. “We wanted the powder room to pack an equal punch to the bold, blue entryway it connects to,” she explains.

One of the mantras Tollett and her team at Hanover Avenue follow is “wonderfully weird,” which the hand sculpture extending through the wall to hold the soap perfectly captures. “The space feels not only more fun but also much more personalized to our clients!”

During the renovation, Van’s mother passed away, so Tollett transformed this almost-forgotten area into an homage to the matriarch. “We reused her beautiful plate rack and hung all her flawless collections of plates on the walls,” the designer says, officially turning this room, which overlooks the vegetable gardens, into the “plate room.”

They papered the walls in warm neutral grasscloth and hung relaxed Roman shades to softly filter the natural light, furnishing the room with many of the fabulous pieces Van already owned (and loved). “This is a wonderful example of ‘reimagining what you have,’ and finding ways to remember the ones we love in our own rooms,” Tollett adds. “Van’s mom was the coolest!”

“Decades of sleepovers in Van’s childhood bedroom allowed me to execute exactly what she was hoping to achieve,” Tollett explains. Though there are many layers of pattern in this sitting area, the designer managed to prevent it from feeling busy by keeping the same serene color palette for most of the textiles, minus a few pops of red for a warm balance.

“This room is a grown-up translation of Van’s entire design sensibility that began when she was young and was nurtured in her adulthood during her travels,” Tollett adds.

On the other side of the room is a calming wash of warm white paint and natural textures, giving Van a visual place of rest ideal for sleeping. Tollett calls it a “well-collected, well-traveled, layered space that seems to have evolved all on its own—a sanctuary for Van and Sean,” which makes the designer especially happy since she was the maid of honor at the couple’s wedding.

The primary bath really only needed some cosmetic upgrades, but they did a lot of heavy lifting. Airy Roman shades soften the space while Van’s mother’s bench and one of her existing rugs give it personality. By opting for two warmer paint colors and swapping out key fixtures, such as the lights, hardware, and faucets, Tollett created a serene, sophisticated escape that looks completely new, all with the same bones.

Tollett tapped into her childhood memories with her best friend when designing this room. “During slumber parties, Van and I fell asleep across from each other in twin beds, so it felt fitting to create another bedroom that fosters the same kind of childhood chatter,” she says, adding that stepping inside is like stepping into a time machine going back to 1983.

Along with the beds, much of the look was directly inspired by Van’s childhood bedroom, the round green pillow on the bed having been plucked directly from it. The pre-existing Tudor windows were perfect for the vintage vibe.

Tucked away at the far end of the home is a spot connected to the sunroom lounge. Dubbed the “dayroom”—an informal spot dedicated to relaxation and reading—Tollett wanted it to feel cozy and as collected as the rest of the home but still a bit private. They washed the walls in a soft gray-green hue and did the same to the custom day bed, finishing off the room with antique bookcases in a complementary gray tone. It’s easy for small rooms like these to feel overcrowded, but the pale color palette takes away some visual weight.

As founder and principal designer of Hanover Avenue, Anne Hulcher Tollett is intimately involved in every project the Richmond-based design firm takes on, from high-end residential to boutique commercial. Tollett finds a thrill in uncovering her clients’ design styles and endless joy in creating their dream homes that reflect who they are.
