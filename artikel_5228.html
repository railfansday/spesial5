NEAR Protocol Revenue Rises Despite 50% Token Sell-Off

NEAR Protocol (NEAR) fell 11.41% on Nov. 20 and traded at $2.84, according to TradingView data. In early October, the token was trading near $6, and it has lost about half of its value since then.

Over the same period, the volume of NEAR Intents went up. "NEAR went from $6 to $3 in about a month. funny thing is, NEAR Intents is doing more volume now than it did in october," NEAR ecosystem contributor Evgeny Haenko wrote on X on Nov. 20.

According to Haenko, NEAR Intents averaged around $129 million in daily volume in October, and in November it was processing around $134 million a day. "The price got cut in half, the business didn't slow down at all. hard for me to be bearish here," he wrote.

NEAR Intents lets users swap assets across more than 30 blockchains, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana and XRP. The service is built into wallets such as Ledger, Trust Wallet and Brave. Over the past year, its trading volume grew 7.1 times to $39.5 billion.

NEAR Intents has collected about $63 million in fees in total, and the network keeps about 29% of that amount. Fees on the NEAR Intents interface, payments from B2B partners and the spread on better-than-market trades accounted for 16.3%, 9.9% and 2.8% of the fees, respectively. Over the last 30 days, the protocol earned about $1.8 million, compared with $2.2 million in October.

Since February 2026, the protocol has used NEAR Intents revenue to buy NEAR on the market. More than 1.84 million NEAR have gone to the buyback wallet so far, and that is 41.6% of all distributed onchain revenue. Base transaction fees on the network are burned in full.

In early November, the NEAR community also voted to cut the maximum annual issuance from 2.5% to 1.6% over two years. The proposal, HSP-029, went through House of Stake, NEAR's governance initiative, and Haenko is one of its participants.

On Sept. 29, Bitwise launched the first U.S. spot NEAR ETF on NYSE Arca under the ticker NRR. Two days later, on Oct. 1, NEAR Intents paused its services after an exploit that cost about $3.8 million, and the team promised to compensate users in full. The exploiter later returned the funds. "The funds from the $3.8M NEAR Intents hack were sent back in full," NEAR Intents general manager Alex Shevchenko said at the time.
