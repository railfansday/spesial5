Ethereum’s “store-of-value” narrative is gaining traction lately.

Looking at the broader picture, there are several factors that are leading to increased risk sentiment in the U.S. market.

Whether it be fears over an AI bubble, high-interest rates, CPI figures, or the aftermath of the mid-term elections, Bitcoin is heading toward its worst annual cycle since the 2022 bear market, with year-to-date losses approaching 17% in 2026.

Keeping this in mind, while Ethereum has suffered nearly twice Bitcoin’s losses and is looking at its worst year since 2022, co-founder Joseph Lubin is bullish over the future potential of Ethereum as an inflationary hedge.

Highlighting several key trends on-chain, he says that Ethereum has long-term potential to outperform both Bitcoin and gold as a store of value.

One of the main reasons for his argument is because of the DeFi ecosystem, stablecoins, and smart contracts on Ethereum. These allow ETH to have a stronger utility base compared to Bitcoin and gold.

In addition, with AI adoption accelerating, Ethereum could also see rising demand for its network infrastructure.

Ethereum’s inflation-hedge narrative faces a key test.

As previously mentioned, the U.S. market is facing a range of risks. This has made this one of the most volatile times for investors. So far, Ethereum has had trouble attracting risk capital, falling almost 30% during Q4 compared to Bitcoin, which has fallen only 17%.

This puts Joseph Lubin’s thesis under pressure, as Bitcoin continues to lead the store-of-value narrative while Ethereum struggles to keep up.

Gold, meanwhile, is also losing steam. The metal is trading in a technical downtrend with losses of more than 5% so far in Q4, as it has failed to move above $4,000. The most recent fall below this level has caused a loss of around $480 billion in market value in just 10 hours.

That said, gold still has a clear “relative-strength” advantage over crypto.

As the chart above shows, the ETH/XAU ratio has lost more than 19% in Q4, demonstrating that Ethereum has failed to make any progress relative to gold. This becomes a key level to monitor. A breakout could result in better technical support for Lubin’s long-term thesis.

For now, Ethereum is struggling to outperform Bitcoin and gold currently. Despite rising staking participation and stronger network security, ETH is yet to translate these fundamentals into stronger capital inflows or relative price performance.

Ethereum is showing growing network participation, supporting Lubin’s store-of-value thesis.

Despite this, ETH continues to lag Bitcoin and gold, with the ETH/XAU ratio down over 19% in Q4.
