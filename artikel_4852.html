Puffer Jackets, Wetsuits, Headphones, and More: The Coolest Gear Our Editors Are Testing This Month

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Gear that can actually handle colder weather.

Welcome to the MH Gear Report, where Men’s Health gear and tech experts find the coolest new releases in gear, technology, home goods, and more. Plus, some insights into new gear we’ve been testing lately.

THE HOLIDAYS ARE fast approaching, and we’re already prepping for Black Friday and Cyber Monday here at Men’s Health. But that doesn’t mean our gear testing stops. In fact, we’re in a switch right now with the cooler weather and getting to finally test things like puffer jackets, midlayers, and wetsuits.

While we’re always testing products like headphones or running shoes no matter where the calendar is at, these seasonal shifts give us the chance to dig into some other activities and regimens. And then getting to translate all those experiences to you so you know what’s good, what’s not, and what’s just ok.

Nothing finally built a pro-level pair of cans and there’s a lot to love: physical buttons, great battery life (30+ hours), improved active-noise cancelling that’s really good, and the signature transparent look that the brand has become known for. They are a bit heavy (you expect that from pro headphones) and the ear cushioning is slightly less firm than we’d like, but these have been a pair we’re going to keep wearing most days.

The Adios Pro 5 is lighter and more responsive than the Pro 4, and has a reworked upper (that helps create a more locked-in fit) and a new midsole foam formulation that feels closer to firm than cushy. Lots of runners gunning for a race PR will love the Pro 5 for their goal event and their fastest training days.

“I love this lightweight insulated jacket,” Creative Director Jamie Prokell says. “It’s the perfect transitional coat—somewhere between a down puffer and a bomber, the Lapwing looks stylish on its own and provides the right amount of warmth without the bulk.”

Later in the season, its low-profile design also makes it a great liner under a shell. And you can feel good knowing that its REPREVE insulation and recycled performance fabrics work well while also being better for the planet.

Ciele makes more than just running hats, and this pair of run shorts is already a great offering with the 2-in-1 compression-like short underneath and a very light and flowy short on top. But given a more reflective detailing treatment for the Nite Rite capsule, these bad boys are perfect for the shorter days we’re now getting.

The U-zip wetsuit is a fairly new offering from a few brands, and Patagonia’s version is one we’ve just started testing given the season. Like all Patagonia wetsuits, it’s made neoprene-free (which is petroleum-based) with Yulex natural rubber and synthetic rubber. So this easier-to-get-into suit is also a bit better for the planet.

Utilizing Polartec’s Alpha Direct fleece (which was originally developed for U.S. Special Forces), this active quarter zip is built for movement. The weight-to-warmth ratio is serious, and the beauty of the open-mesh fleece fabric is that it’s great at insulating but also at dumping heat when things get too warm. While lots of brands are using this material right now, this is one of the more unique offerings we’ve tried in the QZ (most are hoodies with scuba-style hoods).

While the screenless fitness tracker trend certainly took hold in 2026, there’s still lots of folks who want some screen. The new Fitbit Edge is the successor to the Charge 6 and leans into the design that Fitbit became known for.

Out of nowhere last month, Yeti dropped a performance apparel line made up of four pieces: a down jacket, a rain jacket, a zip-up fleece, and a quarter-zip fleece. It’s a pivot into outdoor apparel we can’t say we expected, but it does make a bit of sense. We’ve just gotten pieces in for testing and plan to have a full review up soon, but these do look and feel the part. Yet they do come with the premium Yeti price tag as well.

Yes, Skechers has made a carbon-plated race shoe and it’s pretty solid. Lightweight at 5.9 oz, the woven jacquard upper is comfy and the closure system allows for a secure fit—it’s also got that slightly off-center lacing to help avoid hot spots on the top of the foot, which I find meaningful. The propulsion from this shoe is pretty decent, without it feeling unnatural. After some speed work, I’ve found the faster the pace the more the shoe settles in for me. I think it might be my pick for a 5K turkey trot later this month.

For guys who run warm (like lots of our editors), The Eight Sleep Pod mattress cover has been a game changer. With its temperature control and sleep tracking, it’s let us create personalized zones that match our physiology. The latest version improves the user experience and comes in at a lower price than past releases, plus it gives you the option to just get a half mattress cover for cheaper (for bachelors) or the full cover.

“I often wear 100% Merino baselayers for training in my garage in winter but they can be a little itchy,” says Executive Editor Ben Court. But as Ben has found out, the Worn 80Hr Tee has the thermal and sweat-wicking qualities of wool but is made from a blend of 85 percent merino, 12 percent nylon, and 3 percent elastane so it's a little softer. Plus, the extra stretch from the elastane means the sleeve fit is more snug and flattering on your biceps.

The Bose Ultra Open Earbuds are still a pair we’re constantly grabbing when we want a reliable, open-ear experience. The brand’s latest open-ear development leans further into the clip style and are truly built for active use. You get an IP54 water-resistance rating (opposed to a lesser IPX4) and physical, tactile buttons that are much easier to utilize when things get sweaty. The biggest appeal is these are $100 less than the Ultra ones, so it’s an easy choice if you’re looking for a pair for running, the gym, or any activity.

By now, it’s well known that Away makes a solid travel suitcase. If you’ve ever rolled a bag over a textured surface while holding coffee in one hand, you know how hard it can be to keep everything moving. Away has perfected the roll, making the walk to the gate feel effortless. “With its latest Series 3 case, the brand has upgraded the experience by adding locking wheels—so there are no more runaway bags while you pay at the counter or riding the tram,” Prokell says. “The internal compression system provides plenty of space and helps everything arrive in the same shape it left.”

The Carry-On Flex fits into nearly every overhead bin, regardless of the plane’s size. But if you usually fly on larger planes—or wear shoes bigger than a size 11—you might want to upgrade to the Bigger Carry-On Flex for a little extra room and a more comfortable fit for your footwear.
