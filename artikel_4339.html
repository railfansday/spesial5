Denver man traps Waymo robotaxis with cones, eggs them at least 30 times

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A man in Denver has found a cheap way to beat Waymo’s self-driving system: a few orange traffic cones and a carton of eggs.

Waymo says it has counted at least 30 egg attacks on its robotaxis in Denver, all believed to be the same man. And the Denver District Attorney’s Office has declined to file charges, calling it a civil matter, according to The New York Times.

The method is laid out in a Denver police report filed last month. The man pulls up in front of a Waymo somewhere in or around downtown, takes orange cones out of his trunk, and sets them around the robotaxi so it can’t drive off. Then come the eggs, sometimes thrown from his driver’s seat, sometimes after he gets out.

He leaves in a silver Toyota Corolla. Per the report, that car belongs to a fleet leased out to Lyft drivers. The man hasn’t been identified, and Lyft didn’t comment.

A Waymo investigator took seven of the incidents to police. Each egged vehicle had to come out of service for cleaning, and the detailing bill came to about $2,000. No permanent damage.

Police brought their findings to prosecutors, who decided “no criminal charges” were appropriate. A spokesperson for the DA told the Times the office is now collecting evidence on potential damage and will work with the city attorney to find “the most appropriate legal mechanism to make this behavior stop.”

At least one Waymo had a rider inside. Jeff Hunley was headed to the Union Station neighborhood in September when his robotaxi got hit. He filmed it, said he felt safe in the car, and got his fare refunded.

Waymo opened its Denver service to the public on September 1, alongside San Diego and Tampa. The company now runs more than 100 vehicles across 60 square miles of the city, and Mayor Mike Johnston is a fan. He called them popular and affordable, and couldn’t resist adding that “overall they’re a good egg.”

The rest of November hasn’t been as friendly. California’s DMV ordered Waymo’s driverless vehicles off state freeways on November 11 after robotaxis drove into active construction zones and one tried to get past a CHP traffic break. Waymo responded by pausing freeway rides in every US market.

And vandalism isn’t new for Waymo. We covered it when at least five of its I-Pace robotaxis were set on fire during protests in Los Angeles last year.

Putting a single cone on a Waymo’s hood to freeze it has been a known trick since San Francisco activists did it in 2023. Ringing the car with cones works for the same reason: the Waymo Driver is built to respect them, and it won’t push through.

A human driver getting pelted with eggs would just roll over a cone and leave. A robotaxi sits there and takes it.

That’s the correct default. You don’t want a driverless car deciding when a cone is optional, especially two weeks after regulators pulled Waymo off California freeways for ignoring work zones. But it means anyone with a trunk full of cones can pin a robotaxi in place for as long as they want.

We’ll try to keep the egg puns to a minimum. Mostly.

This is small-time vandalism. Two grand in cleaning, no injuries, no lasting damage. But it shows a real weakness in robotaxis: they’re designed to be the most cautious car on the road, and a determined person can turn that caution against them in under a minute. The car can’t escape, can’t honk its way out, and apparently can’t stop the same guy from coming back 30 times.

The DA’s call is hard to follow. Prosecutors have a repeat pattern, a vehicle tied to a known fleet operator, rider video, and robotaxis that are basically rolling camera rigs. And still no charges. If someone boxed in a regular taxi with a passenger inside and started throwing things at it, we suspect the answer would be different.

At the same time, I can’t help but respect the guy’s dedication.
