Hazlewood backs Cummins to remain as Test captain after sandpaper 'pile-on'

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Australia captain Pat Cummins has been scrutinised after Cameron Bancroft's damning account of the 2018 sandpaper scandal; Josh Hazlewood defended Cummins in a new interview and questioned how the Test team would react if he was forced to step away as captain

Josh Hazlewood has defended Pat Cummins over a “pile-on” around Sandpapergate and refused to rule out boycotting the Test team if he is made to step down as captain.

Hazlewood became the latest player to break their silence over his involvement in the 2018 ball-tampering scandal, which has dominated headlines again since comments from Australian team-mate Usman Khawaja in his new autobiography 'Sabr' claimed the whole team were aware of using sandpaper.

Cameron Bancroft plunged Australian cricket into further turmoil with a 4,000-word statement, backing claims "the whole team" knew about the plot and contradicting strong denials Cummins offered in his press conference a few days earlier.

Cummins had branded Khawaja's testimony "complete and utter nonsense" and "100 per cent wrong", leading Bancroft to go public with his story, while Australia's Test captain has had his future speculated after how he has handled the incident.

"It feels like it's been a bit of a pile on the last few days [at Cummins] from different people," Hazlewood told CodeSports. "I want to back Pat [Cummins] and everything he said in his press conference last week, everything he's done for the last five or six years in particular as captain.

"To get the group where it is now and in a position where something like that [sandpaper] is never going to happen again - I think is just a remarkable job. He's a great leader. If you went around and asked everyone in the team, you'd hear the same response.

"If we turned up for the first Test and he wasn't the captain and it was someone else, honestly, I don't know how that would go."

Australia are next in Test action when they face New Zealand in a four-match series, beginning on December 9 in Perth, where Hazlewood would consider whether to feature if Cummins was no longer captain.

"I'd have to be put in that position to make a decision, but you'd have to think about it, yeah," Hazlewood added.

Bancroft, Warner and then-captain Steve Smith all served bans for their role in the ball-tampering plot, while Hazelwood remains adamant on his position that the bowlers did not know about the use of sandpaper.

"We were interviewed straight after [Cape Town] and I trust what I said in that two-three hour interrogation more than anything because it was in the day or two after," Hazlewood explained.

"It was fresh in my mind at that point. Two things stand out for me from what's been said. Was I an architect in the plan to tamper with the ball with sandpaper? And was I aware sandpaper or foreign substances being used on the field?

"The answer is no. When it boils down to it, I think those two points are crucial."

On whether he noticed a change in ball condition during that Test, Hazlewood said: "I never remember looking at the ball and going, 'wow, this is not natural deterioration'. And obviously the umpires didn't either."

Former England captain Michael Atherton said on the Sky Sports Cricket Podcast he does not expect Cummins to resign from his role as Test skipper.

"He's obviously got a lot of credit in the bank in terms of performance as captain, having transformed the culture and kept Australia at the top of the tree, if you like," Atherton said. "So I don't think there's an immediate threat there."
