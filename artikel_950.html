Will Frank turn things around at Crystal Palace?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Thomas Frank takes charge of Crystal Palace for the first time on Saturday as the Eagles face Coventry at the Coventry Building Society Arena; Frank's new side sit bottom of the Premier League table after two wins from their first 10 matches

Football Journalist @DanLong__

One of the Premier League's most charismatic managers is back.

On November 16, Thomas Frank signed a deal running until the summer of 2029 to take over the vacant managerial role at Crystal Palace, following the conclusion of Pierre Sage's short spell at Selhurst Park.

His return to the top flight comes nine months after he was sacked by Tottenham after a run of just seven wins in 26 Premier League games.

Before that, he had four successful Premier League seasons with Brentford, after guiding them to promotion in 2020/21.

Here, Sky Sports takes a look at what Frank needs to change, what to expect in terms of style and shape and whether he can keep Palace in the Premier League...

Palace won the Conference League last season, so it's easy to forget they also only finished six points above the Premier League relegation zone.

Sage was unable to build on the legacy left by Oliver Glasner, so the Eagles again find themselves in a spot of bother.

The Premier League's bottom side have lost four in succession and have the worst defensive record in the division, having conceded an average of 2.4 goals per game.

They are through to the quarter-finals of the Carabao Cup, however, and have taken five points from four Europa League league phase matches so far, which has them currently on course to qualify for the knockout rounds.

Speaking in his first interview with the club, Frank identified the problems his new side need to address.

"There's no doubt - to state the obvious - we need to stabilise things. We need to work incredibly hard," he said.

"We are conceding too many goals, so we need to work on the defensive side of things, with principles, attitude. We need to work on set-pieces, which are key in both boxes. We need to work on defensive transitions, as we are conceding too many situations there.

"We need to bring energy, belief, confidence because this is a very, very good team and very good players, but they need to believe in themselves.

"We need to balance a lot of things with aiming high in Europe and two fantastic cup tournaments, but the Premier League is the most important, if we need to prioritise."

Analysis by Sky Sports News' Chris Reidy:

Frank will see areas all over the pitch he can improve for Crystal Palace, but undoubtedly his priority will be to stop them conceding as many goals and as many chances as they have so far this season.

Crystal Palace have kept one clean sheet in the Premier League this season and too often look vulnerable on transitions and weak on set-pieces.

Frank will have to review whether the personnel he has suits playing three centre-halves or if a change to four is necessary. Time and time again teams have exploited space between defenders against Crystal Palace which leads to shots or good chances created for the opposition.

Away from the defence, Frank will need to rejuvenate Adam Wharton. The midfielder has been way off his best this season, and perhaps finding the right partner for him will be key to that. Quinten Timber has shown plenty of energy and will in his early days at Palace perhaps without giving stability in the middle, and Daichi Kamada has been used higher up the pitch due to Ismaila Sarr's absence from the team.

What has gone under the radar in recent weeks because of Crystal Palace's defending, is their struggles up top. Eddie Nketiah's fortunate penalty with the last kick of the game against Liverpool spared Palace a record of four Premier League games without a goal.

Crystal Palace have spent the most time trailing in the league and have also only taken the lead in three games this season. A mentality shift is needed, because Palace have good players who aren't responding well to adversity.

Based on his previous jobs, Frank has shown he is a pragmatic manager, not wedded to one shape or style and comfortable finding different ways to win.

His most used formation in the Premier League is a 4-3-3 (59), but he has also heavily used a 4-2-3-1 (35) or - importantly for Crystal Palace - a 3-5-2 (53).

Across his four top-flight seasons at Brentford, the Bees scored 80 set-piece goals, with only Arsenal, Liverpool and Man City scoring more across that period.

He is reportedly close to adding Bernardo Cueva - his former set-piece coach at Brentford - to his coaching staff, which suggests a similar approach is likely at Selhurst Park.

Elsewhere, while the Bees became markedly more direct after promotion from the Championship, there was still a particular focus on high quality chances, meaning fewer shots were taken from outside the box.

From 1763 shots across their first four seasons in the Premier League, Frank's Brentford created 225.0 worth of Expected Goals, which equated to a league-high 0.128 xG per shot.

Analysis by Sky Sports News' Michael Bridge:

"It just didn't seem the right fit from the start. It didn't get off to the best of starts in terms of the press conference when he said: 'We will 100 per cent lose football matches.' Then he turned to the press office and said: 'Apart from Arsenal.' The problem is, when you say something at a club like Spurs, it stays.

"There were some really tough defeats for Spurs fans to look at. The defeat at home to Chelsea, when they generated 0.1 xG. Then there was the 4-1 reverse at Arsenal, 0.07 xG.

"Spurs had come off the back of winning the Europa League, but they'd finished 17th in the league with 22 league defeats. They weren't as good as the Europa League trophy suggested.

"It wasn't a great squad. It was a squad littered with injuries. It was also a squad of players who then thought, well, we've won the European trophy. I think a couple of them thought, 'Who's this guy?'

"Then he picked Cristian Romero to be the club captain. Romero spoke out on a number of occasions during that season, criticising the board, criticising the hierarchy. He was sent off in two key games against Liverpool and Man Utd, so there was a real discipline issue there as well.

"Then there was that really unfortunate incident away to Bournemouth where he was walking around pre-match holding an Arsenal cup because Arsenal played in the previous match.

"He needs to prove that Spurs was a one-off and not Brentford."

It's clear Frank and Palace have a significant battle on their hands.

They have collected 0.7 points per game this season - the lowest in the Premier League. Maintaining that average would see them pick up 20 more points, finish on 28 and almost certainly be relegated to the Sky Bet Championship.

Unsurprisingly, they are currently priced at Evens by Sky Bet to be relegated from the Premier League, while Opta Analyst's predicted table shows them finishing bottom of the pile with 38 points.

Frank's own points-per-game average in the Premier League, however, stands at 1.3.

If Palace replicate that average across the remaining 28 games of the season, they would collect another 36.4 points, taking them to 44, which would almost certainly be enough to survive. In a 38-game Premier League season, no side has ever collected as many as 43 points and gone down.

Frank has already shown he can build a successful Premier League team. He now has 28 games to show he can save one.
