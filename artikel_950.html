'It's the match people wanted' - Pegula to face Andreeva at WTA Finals

Jessica Pegula saved a match ‌point en route to a gritty 7-6 (7-2) 4-6 7-5 win over Linda Noskova to advance to the semi-finals at the WTA Finals and ​send fellow American Coco Gauff packing from the season-ending championships.

Pegula enjoyed a career-high 16 aces while Wimbledon champion Noskova suffered a career-high 15 double faults in a see-saw battle that spanned a remarkable two ​hours and 45 minutes in one of the matches of the year.

Noskova had already reached the semi-finals but Pegula's victory eliminated Gauff despite her 6-4 7-5 victory against Ukrainian Marta Kostyuk earlier.

Gauff, Pegula and Noskova all finished the group stage 2-1. Noskova went 5-2 in sets to win the group, followed by Pegula at 5-3 and Gauff at 4-3.

"I've learned that sometimes the serve feels good and sometimes it doesn't, and how to manage ​the days when it doesn't and to ride it when you are serving well," Pegula told reporters.

"I don't think I served that well in my first couple matches and I served great today. Sometimes there's no ​rhyme or reason."

The win sets up a grudge clash between Pegula and teenager Mirra Andreeva in ‌the semi-finals on Saturday.

"It's the match people wanted, well now you are going to get it," said Pegula.

Pegula called Andreeva "a diva" who "needs to be humbled" but the American didn't realise that her remarks were being livestreamed during ‌a practice session before the tournament.

Pegula also praised the world No 3, although she later apologised to those involved.

"She's fast, she has a great serve, she's strong, she hits the ball pretty hard and she's smart," Pegula said.

WTA chair Valerie Camillo later apologised to Pegula for the "regrettable" incident.

Noskova, meanwhile, will face the winner of Friday's round-robin match between Aryna Sabalenka and Elina Svitolina.

Earlier, Gauff rallied to beat Kostyuk on a sun-soaked Stadium Two at Indian Wells.

After sealing the first set with a volley ​that sent fans leaping to their feet, Gauff ​rallied from 1-5 down in the second, winning six games in a row.

"I was fully prepared ​to play a third set there," said Gauff, who needed treatment on her back during the second ⁠set.

"It's the last tournament of the season so I just wanted to finish it on a high note and give myself the best chance to play the semi-finals. Either way, I didn't want to be sad walking off the court."

Before Pegula and Noskova took the court, Gauff said she had a back-up plan for Saturday in Los Angeles, where Tyler, the Creator's Camp Flog Gnaw Carnival is taking place this weekend.

"But I'm hoping not to be there," Gauff said.

Kostyuk, who won ⁠the Madrid title and reached the French Open semi-finals, was ​already eliminated coming into her match having lost to Noskova and Pegula.

Kostyuk joined the ​eight-player tournament as an alternate after world No 1 Elena Rybakina withdrew due to illness, but failed to win a set in her three matches as she struggled with a ‌wrist injury.
