AI Helped Hospital Lower Readmissions, Deaths After Discharge

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

— EMR-based tool alerted clinicians to highest-risk patients in need of closer follow-up

Artificial intelligence (AI) helped one Texas hospital lower readmissions and post-discharge deaths, without adding any new staff, according to a case study presented at the HLTH conference in Las Vegas.

Using predictive AI to focus follow-up visits on its Medicare surgical patients at highest risk, Houston Methodist Hospital's 30-day post-discharge mortality fell about 35%, and readmissions fell about 18%, Brenda Campbell, MS, BSN, the hospital's senior innovation consultant, said at the conference.

Observed-to-expected (O/E) ratios -- which compare patients' results with those of Medicare patients matched for baseline risk -- fell from 0.48 in 2021-2022 to 0.31 in 2025-Q1 2026 (a partial period) for post-discharge deaths. Meanwhile, the O/E ratio for readmissions fell from 1.17 to 0.96 over the same period. A falling O/E ratio means the hospital moved faster than the benchmark, she reported.

Clinician follow-up visits within 14 days rose from 54.6% to 62.2% in the highest mortality-risk group and from 55.8% to 64.5% in the highest readmission-risk group during that time. Crucially, the hospital said this was accomplished without adding staff.

"We've done this without any additional resources," Campbell said. "Resources are tight. ... You have to really risk-stratify to get the right patients to the resources that you have."

The effort began in 2021, when Houston Methodist Hospital asked why its cardiovascular service line ranked well for inpatient mortality but poorly after discharge. The hospital brought in the Health Data Analytics Institute (HDAI), which had trained predictive AI on the entire Medicare data set, including 150 million Medicare lives.

HDAI found the cardiovascular service line ranked 7th for in-hospital mortality but 43rd for 30-day post-discharge mortality among more than 100 academic medical centers. About 70% of the 30-day post-discharge deaths occurred in the highest-risk quintile of patients.

"You can't manage what you don't know," Campbell said.

The hospital created a risk stratification plan, with predictions embedded into the electronic medical record. It asked clinicians to prioritize follow-up within 2 weeks for the highest-risk patients.

"Your limited team can't see everybody. ... So let's get to the people who are most likely to readmit," Carola Endicott, EdM, vice president of customer engagement at HDAI, said at the conference.

Timely follow-up can be life-saving, Endicott said in an interview after the session. A patient might be experiencing shortness of breath they don't feel is urgent enough to call about, she said. They may have forgotten to pick up a potentially crucial prescription medication, and need a friendly reminder. Or perhaps there was a surgical complication that wasn't caught.

John Brownstein, PhD, senior vice president and chief innovation officer at Boston Children's Hospital, who was not involved in the initiative, called it a "really concrete application of AI to predict high-risk groups that are worthy of intervention in a way that reduces the risk of readmission and mortality."

"AI can level the playing field," Brownstein told MedPage Today. "Well-funded health systems can hire staff to compensate for some of these challenges, but in a world where you are understaffed, AI can help reach those goals."

By working with clinicians to prioritize efforts, Houston Methodist Hospital's readmission ranking among 114 academic medical centers, among all medical and surgical Medicare fee-for-service patients, rose from 59th in 2022 to 7th in 2024, based on actual-to-expected rates, Campbell reported.

Mortality and readmission post-discharge is a major metric that hospitals track, said Brownstein. "It's a big cost to health systems and it is a big impact for patients. That's why so many health systems have invested in this space."

For Brownstein, this case study shows that implementing AI solutions doesn't have to be overcomplicated. "We want the right tool for the right problem. We over-index on these high-token use cases for generative AI. In this case, this use case is straightforward, and really high-value."

"We think it's the small things really that can have the biggest impact," Campbell said. She would like to see the follow-up rate climb further. "We're not where we want to be, but we know we're on the right trajectory to get there."
