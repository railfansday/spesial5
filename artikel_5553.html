STAT+: Before midterm losses, MAHA spent on itself instead of candidates

WASHINGTON — When Tony Lyons, leader of the largest Make America Healthy Again organizations in Washington, spoke to supporters the day after the election, he was sullen.

“MAHA was up against some of the most powerful and best funded interests in the world — they backed the candidates running against us,” he said on a Zoom call, books published by his company prominently arrayed behind him. “The forces that had been lined up against us are just monstrous.”

But filings show that the MAHA PAC Lyons leads, with more than $10 million to spend through the cycle, scarcely used its funds to push back on those forces. An examination by STAT found that the MAHA PAC was instead spending big on its allies, boosters, and an affiliated nonprofit.

Those investments often came at the expense of spending on political races, including for MAHA candidates in competitive districts, campaign finance experts suggested.
