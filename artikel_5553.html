Honda plans $3 billion Ohio hybrid plant, its first new U.S. mass-production plant since 2008

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The plant is expected to begin production in 2029 with capacity for 240,000 vehicles a year and more than 2,000 jobs

Honda $HMC announced plans to build a $3 billion factory in central Ohio, with production slated to start in 2029 pending final government approvals.

The plant will create more than 2,000 jobs and have capacity for 240,000 vehicles a year, the company said. It will sit on land between Honda's existing East Liberty and Marysville auto plants and become the fourth Honda auto plant in Ohio. The project is part of investments that will expand Honda's yearly vehicle-building capability in the U.S. to roughly 1.3 million units and across North America to roughly 1.9 million.

Honda said the plant will be able to build multiple vehicle types and powertrains, including hybrid, gasoline-powered and battery-electric vehicles. Honda would not name the models planned for the site. It would be the automaker’s first large-scale U.S. factory for assembling vehicles since an Indiana facility began operating in 2008, according to CNBC.

"We are making one of the most significant commitments in our history to position Honda for the next 50 years," Bob Nelson, executive vice president of American Honda Motor Co., said in a statement.

Nelson told CNBC that although tariffs affect the business, the plant mainly reflects a drive for expansion in the U.S. "Tariffs are an impact to us, even with the high localization rate that we have," he said. "But the real impact is that we have customers out there wanting Honda product and we can't give it to them."

Honda also plans investments at existing facilities in Ohio, Alabama and Georgia to expand production of hybrid vehicles, engines and hybrid systems. In Alabama, the company will add equipment for producing a hybrid V6 engine of a new design. In Ohio, it is retooling the Anna Engine Plant for a next-generation four-cylinder engine and a transmission plant to raise hybrid system capacity by 250% in the coming years. Honda also will update its Alliston, Ontario, operations and reintroduce engine manufacturing there.

In May, Honda unveiled a plan to roll out 15 more hybrids across its Honda and Acura lineups around the globe by 2030, with North America expected to be the primary market for many of them.

Honda had warned in August that it might not build a new North American assembly plant if the U.S., Canada and Mexico failed to extend the USMCA trade agreement. The agreement remains unextended as a deepening trade conflict pits the U.S. against Canada, according to Reuters.
