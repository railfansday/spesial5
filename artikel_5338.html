5 reveals from the final Avengers: Doomsday trailer, from Steve's son to Cyclops

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Steve's teenage son, the Fantastic Four, Doctor Doom's warning, Thor's confession and Cyclops's rallying cry: 5 reveals from the final trailer.

For years, Marvel Studios treated the Avengers story as finished, with its heroes retired or their roles handed to successors. Avengers: Doomsday is the studio's attempt to open that story back up. The previous Avengers film was Avengers: Endgame in 2019, according to Notebookcheck. Marvel is coming back to a series it set aside years ago, so it has to rebuild the team rather than resume where it left off. The filmmakers present at least one homecoming as certain rather than risky. Directors Joe and Anthony Russo have called Steve Rogers the character who changed their lives and described his return as inevitable, according to the Dallas Morning News. For viewers, the question is therefore not whether the old heroes will come back but what Marvel has to disturb to bring them back.

Marvel started reopening the past before its final trailer came out, and it did so by returning to the old film itself. The studio re-released the 2019 finale as Avengers: Endgame Encore with four minutes of new footage tied to Doomsday, Variety reports, which put the setup for the new film in theaters inside the old one. Audiences paid to see that setup, and the re-release opened at No. 1 at the domestic box office with $26 million. That means a large audience had already watched Doomsday's groundwork before Marvel showed its last trailer. It also leaves the trailer in an awkward position. To surprise people who have seen that much, Marvel has to reveal moments the film might once have saved for opening night.

The Hollywood Reporter highlighted the most startling moments shown in the last Doomsday trailer, from a glimpse of Steve's family life to a warning from Doctor Doom and an X $TWTR-Men rallying cry. Marvel will release the film on Dec. 18, 2026, and these moments give audiences their clearest look at it until then. Read together, they tell one story: heroes pulled out of lives they had settled into, and a villain who wants those lives back.

Before anyone throws a punch, Marvel shows the life Steve chose when he walked away from the fight. The final trailer starts at home with Steve and Peggy Carter, whose teenage son doesn't know that his father was once Captain America, according to The Hollywood Reporter. Endgame ended with Steve returning as an old man, The Dallas Morning News reports, and the trailer now shows the middle of the life that film only implied. His peace depends on keeping the hero buried, even from his own family, and by opening in that house, Marvel makes everything that follows a threat to the home.

Marvel's earlier footage left fans guessing about Steve's family. After the first teaser, Rolling Stone suggested that the baby in it was Steve's child with Peggy, according to The Dallas Morning News. The final trailer gives Steve and Peggy a teenage son, which fits that guess and settles a mystery Marvel could have kept until opening night.

Steve gave up the role for good instead of taking a break from it. He handed his shield to Sam Wilson at the end of Endgame, according to The Dallas Morning News, so a return now would mean leaving his family rather than stepping back into an old job.

The trailer doesn't say what Steve will have to give up once he is pulled back into the fight.

Four heroes show up at Steve's peaceful home to draw him out of retirement. Reed Richards, Sue Storm, Johnny Storm and Ben Grimm are the ones at his door, according to The Hollywood Reporter. Variety reports that Pedro Pascal plays Mister Fantastic. None of them served with Steve, so Marvel makes the recruitment a meeting between strangers rather than old friends.

The Four want more from Steve than an extra pair of hands. Variety reports that their aim is to put him in charge of the Avengers in the fight against Doctor Doom. The saga had let Steve go, and this visit returns him to the head of the team.

Steve's return also means a change of place. The Four take him back to the main timeline, according to Variety, away from the one where he settled down after Endgame.

Steve can rejoin the Avengers only by walking out of the house where the Four found him.

In the final Avengers: Doomsday trailer, Doctor Doom treats the heroes as thieves. Robert Downey Jr.'s Victor von Doom says they are living "stolen lives" and wants them returned, according to The Hollywood Reporter. The heroes have settled down since their last fight, and Doom's line is an ultimatum rather than a taunt.

The trailer makes the threat personal through the footage that plays while Doom speaks. His line runs over Steve leaving with the Fantastic Four and over scenes of Thor's daughter, Love, and Ant-Man's daughter, Cassie, according to Gizmodo. Heard against those images, "stolen lives" points at the families the heroes love, not a city or the team.

For all its menace, the trailer never shows how Doom would take those lives, though one shot may hint at it. In that shot two planets move toward a third, and Gizmodo suggests that the image may point to an incursion, a danger Marvel raised earlier in Loki and Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness. The film has not confirmed the incursion guess. If it proves right, Doom's demand would carry a threat from those earlier stories into the Avengers saga.

Doom has named what he wants returned, and the film will have to show how he means to collect it.

Thor's call for the heroes to unite comes with an admission that he can't tell who most of them are. Thor, played by Chris Hemsworth, tells the merging teams to drop their bickering, according to The Hollywood Reporter. "I don't know what half of you do. I don't even know who half of you are," Thor says, as reported by Notebookcheck. For viewers, that joke takes off some pressure. If Thor can't keep track of everyone, Marvel isn't asking the audience to keep track either.

The bickering Thor wants to end is a real fight between heroes from two franchises. His speech is cross-cut with footage of the X $TWTR-Men fighting the Avengers, according to Notebookcheck. That makes his plea the moment when separate teams with separate histories are pushed into one coalition against Doctor Doom.

Thor's joke acknowledges how crowded the cast has become, but audiences will learn whether they care about heroes they can't place only when the film opens.

The X $TWTR-Men arrive in the final trailer as a team called together by their own leader. Scott Summers gathers the mutants with the classic X-Men rallying cry, "To me, my X-Men," according to The Hollywood Reporter. In a crowded fight, the scene gives the mutants a leader of their own on screen.

The line matters because the man saying it is the Cyclops from the old films, not a new version of the character. James Marsden, who plays Scott Summers in the trailer, played Cyclops in the earlier Fox X-Men films, according to Reactor. He never said the X-Men's signature call in any of them, so the trailer is the first time audiences have heard him say it.

One line can't show how large a role Marsden's Cyclops will have in the film. Still, giving him the team's best-known call suggests Marvel wants longtime fans to notice his return.

What Cyclops does after he calls his team together is the part the trailer keeps back.
