Are Bitcoin Bears Priced In? Deutsche Bank’s Quiet Move Marks Major Counter-Signal For BTC USD

Deutsche Bank opened its digital asset custody service to institutional and corporate clients on Wednesday, November 11, starting with Bitcoin, Ether and three stablecoins. Germany’s largest bank now holds the private keys to clients’ coins itself, three years after it first applied for a German crypto custody licence.

The launch lands in a gloomy market. Bitcoin trades at $73,943, down 5.8% in a week. That is 41% below its October 2025 record of $126,080.

The crowd is gloomier still. Analytics firm Santiment counts more negative than positive social media comments on BTC, Ether and XRP. It reads that disbelief as a sign of a classic bullish counter signal, the idea that markets tend to turn when most traders expect them to fall.

We checked that idea against eight years of the Crypto Fear & Greed Index. It works, but only at the extremes.

The service does two things at the start. It stores clients’ coins and moves them on request, between the bank’s own warm and cold wallets or out to third parties. Trading is not part of it.

The bank’s custody site lists the details:

Clients reach it through Autobahn, the same platform they already use for the bank’s other services. That is the selling point for a corporate treasurer: no new crypto firm to vet, and no in-house wallet team to build.

The bank set out the plan in a September 16 release, with tokenized securities on its roadmap. “Digital assets are not a replacement for the traditional financial system but an important complement to it,” said Gerald Podobnik, co-head of Deutsche Bank’s Corporate Bank.

The same release warned that crypto held this way is not covered by a deposit guarantee like ordinary bank deposits.

Santiment tracks the ratio of positive to negative comments about each coin on social media. A reading of 1 means the two are even, and anything below 1 means the crowd is more negative than positive.

On Tuesday, all three large coins sat below that line on its Sanbase charts, which track Santiment’s daily positive-to-negative ratio:

The firm called BTC and Ether sentiment “very bearish” and XRP “extremely bearish”. Its view is that crowds tend to be most bearish near a bottom, so heavy pessimism can mark a turn higher.

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index tells the same story. It reads 29 on Wednesday, in “fear”, down from 32 on Tuesday. It has read 29 or lower on 192 of the 315 days so far in 2026.

So does fear pay? We matched every daily reading of the Fear & Greed Index since February 2018 with Bitcoin’s daily close on Binance 30 days later. That gave 3,172 days to test.

The counter signal Santiment describes has worked in deep panic, not in ordinary gloom. Moderate fear has been the normal state of 2026, and it has not marked a bottom on its own.

The Fear & Greed reading would need to fall another 10 points or more to reach the zone where buying the fear has paid. By that test, the crowd is bearish, but not yet bearish enough.

Bitcoin trades at $73,943 on Binance. On Tuesday, it touched $72,727, its lowest price since August 21.

On the daily chart, where each candle covers one day, BTC sits below both of its exponential moving averages (EMAs), averages that give recent prices more weight. The 200-day EMA stands at $76,451 and the 50-day EMA at $78,906.

Deutsche Bank’s custody desk is built for the long run, and it won’t move the price this week. For the counter signal, the history is clear: it has fired in deep fear, below 20, and the index sits at 29.
