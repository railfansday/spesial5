Trump floats a pardon for Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Huang is due to sit for a private session with House committees and a public Senate committee hearing next week

President Donald Trump said he is open to pardoning reinstated Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang for any potential crimes, but he does not think a pardon will be needed.

"It's always an option, I guess, but I don't think it'll be necessary," Trump said in an interview with the New York Post published Tuesday.

Huang is due to sit for a private session with House committees on Dec. 1, then face a public Senate committee hearing on Dec. 3. Trump urged Huang to "tell the truth" and said he does not think the CEO needs to "take the Fifth."

"I've always found him to be 100% aboveboard, and you know a very smart person," the president said.

Sen. John Kennedy, a Louisiana Republican, said Monday that he anticipates Huang will invoke his Fifth Amendment rights, according to CNBC. "I expect him to take the Fifth Amendment so he doesn't incriminate himself in criminal prosecution, but we're going to give him a chance to explain," Kennedy said.

The president's pardon power reaches only offenses under federal law, not state law.

Topics for the closed-door session include Jensen Huang's affair with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, who is suspected of spying for China (the two are not related), as well as the practices Nvidia uses to safeguard its information, according to Bloomberg.

Trump said he has weighed the idea of the federal government buying an ownership share in Nvidia. "It's probably not too late," he said.

Three House committee chairs have said the reinstatement does not end their demands for answers on chip smuggling to China. They said Jensen Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter confirmed they will appear for transcribed interviews on Dec. 1 and Dec. 2.

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to bring Huang back on Monday, less than two weeks after he resigned on Nov. 12 amid reports of multiple affairs. U.S. officials suspect one of the women involved of relaying information to China's intelligence services, and Beijing has dismissed the allegation.

Several Republican lawmakers criticized the reinstatement and Trump's role in it. Trump wrote on Truth Social that acting Attorney General Todd Blanche confirmed the government has no evidence Huang engaged in unlawful conduct.
