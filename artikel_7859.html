Carpet cleaning equipment often features various cleaning modes and includes several accessories. Hose and cord length affect how long the unit can continuously clean before unplugging and moving. Keeping these factors in mind when shopping can help ensure you pick the best residential carpet cleaner for your needs.

Spot-cleaning machines are lightweight, portable, and less expensive than whole-house cleaners, but their suction isn’t as strong as larger units. A handheld carpet cleaner is ideal for use on stairs, in homes with just a couple of carpeted rooms, or on area rugs. These small carpet cleaners are also good for cleaning the carpets in the car.

Whole-house or upright carpet cleaners have robust motors, powerful suction, and thick, rotating brushes that provide deep cleaning. Many models offer multiple cleaning modes and use their own cleaning solutions, making them better suited to larger homes with many carpeted rooms that see heavy traffic. A whole-house, heavy-duty carpet cleaner is ideal for lifting dirt and grime that’s settled deep into carpet or rug fibers.

Buyers will want to consider their physical limits, the amount of carpeting, and the number of levels in the home when making a decision. In general, the heavier the machine, the more powerful its suction motor and rotating brushes will be. Portable carpet cleaners, like handheld carpet cleaners, are lighter and easier to maneuver, whereas full-size upright machines capable of heavy-duty cleaning will be harder to move and store.

Cord and hose length both affect cleaning time; the less often you have to unplug and plug in, the more efficient the cleaning session. Long cords of 20 feet or more and hoses up to 10 feet are particularly helpful in large rooms and for cleaning stairs. Since some upright units don’t have hoses, buyers wanting to clean under heavy pieces of furniture will want to be careful when choosing.

Carpet-cleaner accessories are attachments that can be added to a hose or intake port for cleaning different materials, reaching difficult areas, or dealing with pet hair and odors. Some of the most common types include upholstery cleaners; crevice-cleaning attachments (handy for tight spots like the indent created where the baseboard meets the carpet); or spinning brushes that lift tough, stuck-on stains. Pet attachments include special brushes, guards, and even isolated tanks for trapping fur to prevent it from getting caught in the machine. Many machines include samples of the brand’s best carpet shampoos or cleaning solutions to help you start cleaning right away.

The price range in this lineup is $125 to $700. However, the average cost of hiring a professional carpet cleaner is about $50 per room and can be up to $600 for larger homes. This means that after just a few uses, your new carpet cleaner will have paid for itself. You also have the option of renting carpet-cleaning equipment and DIYing the job at an average price of $30 per day. Either of these routes may make sense if you have just one or two carpeted rooms, but if your entire house is carpeted or you live with small children or furry friends, it may be more cost-effective to compare carpet cleaners and invest in your own unit.

If regular vacuuming and moderate care are taken, a carpet can be cleaned once every 12 to 18 months. In an active household with pets and children, you’ll want to clean the carpet every 6 to 12 months.

The best carpet cleaner for pets can remove pet hair, odors, and stains from even the deepest fibers of the carpet. In our tests, the Shark CarpetXpert HairPro Pet With StainStriker did a great job on these tasks as well as on more serious pet messes.

Bissell recommends staying off carpet for 4 to 6 hours after cleaning, but total dry time depends on a variety of factors, including the type of carpet fibers, the pile, how much solution and water were used, room size, ventilation, humidity, and temperature. Carpet made from polyester takes between 6 and 12 hours to dry, but wool carpet takes at least 24 hours to dry. To reduce drying time, use a quick-cleaning mode, which releases less liquid, and increase ventilation by opening windows, turning on fans, and turning on heat or air conditioning.

If you want to remove surface stains or odors, shampooing your carpets will be better. To deep clean and sanitize carpet, a steam cleaner will be more effective.

How much you should spend on a carpet cleaner depends on your needs regarding features, size, and frequency of use. The carpet cleaners selected for this guide cost between $125 and $700, which reflects the typical price range for reliable models.
