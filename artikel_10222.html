The iPhone Duo’s rocky launch is a reminder of how far Android foldables have come

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

On Monday, I unboxed the iPhone Duo I pre-ordered back in October and experienced a disarming sense of déjà vu. It’s beautiful hardware married to a messy, often painful software onboarding experience atypical of Apple’s usual polish. It reminds me of the Galaxy Fold’s early years.

I was most looking forward to the combination of a foldable form factor and Apple’s superior app ecosystem, but even the apps that have been updated to support the new aspect ratio, on both the front and back, feel ungainly and buggy. In moving UI elements from the bottom to the right, even with the display open, the Duo forces a rebuilding of essential muscle memory that’s prevailed across mobile platforms, including Android foldables, for 20 years.

The front display, in particular, is a massive test of one’s patience: apps, even ones ostensibly optimized for its stout dimensions, often feel unusable, with UI elements that would be prominent on the iPhone 18 Pro hidden behind overflow menus on the Duo. It’s exasperating and, frankly, boggling.

But what’s interesting is the enthusiasm with which developers are approaching the new form factor. Reddit threads reveal dozens of Duo-optimized app recommendations that feel novel and, if not polished, then propulsive. There’s a fascinating delta between waiting for my bank, or Starbucks, or Kobo, to get its act together to support the Duo and seeking out net-new opportunities and experiences.

I spent most of the summer using the Galaxy Z Fold 8, and I didn’t expect going back to it to feel so stately and predictable: the showcase of a platform with fewer highs but none of the Duo’s current lows.

I’m not sure if I’m going to keep the Duo and wait out its colicky infancy. But I do know that until now I hadn’t given Samsung enough credit for the amount of work it did to make foldable software feel ordinary, and that’s the highest compliment I can pay.

Andrew’s look at the Boox Picco is a nice reminder that a gadget can be interesting because it does less. The $109 e-reader has a 4-inch screen, physical page-turn buttons, and an included magnetic case that can attach to a compatible phone. It competes directly with XTEINK’s excellent X4 Pro, which features a rudimentary touchscreen and backlight for around $100, though its magnets are built in.

Both products are wonderful examples of hardware companies adapting to the growing size and complexity of smartphones and seeing an opening for single-purpose products that do one thing really well. Plus, it’s fun to stick a thing to the back of your phone and… read it.

For something you can explore without buying another device, Paolo Mainardi turned his personal website into a point-and-click adventure. Articles live in books, links sit on a notice board, and his about page is a letter in a lighthouse. The Context Island is an incredibly thoughtful and unique approach to “web” design, and it’s another example of how AI can really help bring crazy ideas to fruition.
