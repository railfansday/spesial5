Brooklyn Nets’ defense falls flat in 142-116 loss vs. Timberwolves

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

There's only so much that can go right on one end of the floor when nearly everything is going wrong on the other. Great defense means little if you can't score, and excellent offense doesn't matter if the defense is weak.

Brooklyn Nets learned that lesson again on Monday night against the Minnesota Timberwolves, 142-116, dropping them to 3-13 and marking their eighth double-digit loss of the season. It was yet another season-high for Brooklyn's opponent and fifth straight loss.

It didn't take long for that imbalance to show.

By the end of the first quarter, Brooklyn trailed 41-28. In his first career NBA start, following Nolan Traoré's hamstring injury, rookie Mikel Brown Jr. scored eight points in eight first-quarter minutes, shooting 3-of-5 from the field and hitting both 3-point attempts.

Julius Randle also dominated his former team, posting 29 points in 35 minutes along with four rebounds and four assists.

Entering the night as the league's 16th-ranked offense, Minnesota had its highest-scoring quarter of the season to start things off on Monday, dropping 41 points. It wasn't a particularly sluggish quarter for the Nets offensively; they scored 28 and hit an impressive 5-of-6 from deep.

However, defense was the biggest issue, allowing the Timberwolves to hit seven threes and win the fast-break points battle 10-0.

The story remained much the same in the second quarter. While the Nets tied the quarter, with 33 points apiece, the same exact issues prevailed. By the end of the first half, both teams were shooting over 50% from beyond the arc. The Nets still lost the fast-break points battle by a large margin (18-2), committed 12 turnovers against Minnesota's eight, and allowed 18 points off those compared to their 10. They were also dominated inside, allowing 30 points in the paint compared to their own 18.

Regardless of how connected the team's offense is, it's difficult to win games with that level of struggle on the defensive end of the floor. From the perimeter to the paint to the fast break, Brooklyn's defense simply couldn't find a groove in the first half. Monday also marked the fourth time in the past five games that the team has allowed over 70 points in the first half.

Their best defensive outing in that stretch came Friday, when they surrendered 62 to the Orlando Magic.

After the third quarter, however, the Timberwolves took a 115-91 lead. The tale of the game continued. Minnesota also finished the third quarter by scoring on 18 of their final 20 possessions. That just isn't a recipe for winning games, even though Brooklyn shot 13-of-22 from 3-point range through three quarters.

It wasn't all ugly. There are certainly some individual performances to highlight.

In his first start, Brown Jr. impressed with 17 points in 29 minutes, coupled with five rebounds and seven assists. He led the team in first-half points but didn't score in the second half. The gripe was his turnovers, logging six against seven assists.

In his second straight start over Egor Dëmin, Danny Wolf also provided a meaningful all-around presence for Brooklyn. Wolf compiled 11 points, five boards, and four assists in 25 minutes, including a 3-of-5 mark from downtown.

At the end of the day, though, until the Nets find some semblance of defensive consistency, even their excellent offensive outings will be hard to turn into wins.

It hasn't been the easiest beginning for Mikel Brown Jr. After all, how could it be? He's been on on a minutes restriction to begin his career. He had himself a solid outing on Monday night in front of a national crowd.
