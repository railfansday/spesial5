Pacio Vs. Yamakita: 4 Keys To Victory In ONE Strawweight MMA World Title Fight At ONE SAMURAI 5

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A World Champion armed with every tool in the book locks horns with a submission-hunting challenger capable of turning any exchange into dangerous territory at ONE SAMURAI 5.

ONE Strawweight MMA World Champion Joshua "The Passion" Pacio defends his crown against Japanese contender Keito "Pocket Monk" Yamakita in the five-round main event, which goes down live at EBARA WAVE Arena Ota in Tokyo, Japan, on Wednesday, November 18.

The Filipino superstar's resume speaks for itself. The multiple-time divisional king unified the World Title with a second-round TKO of Jarred "The Monkey God" Brooks at the conclusion of their trilogy last year, then submitted Mansur Malachiev inside the first round at The Inner Circle 21 this past July.

Yamakita, meanwhile, carries home-crowd support and a five-fight winning streak into the bout. The former Pancrase Champion owns wins over former ONE World Champions Alex "Little Rock" Silva and Yosuke "The Ninja" Saruta, and he enters fresh off back-to-back submission wins over Ryohei Kurosawa and Koyuru Tanoue.

Here are four keys that could decide who takes the strawweight MMA throne.

Pacio holds a clear physical edge in this matchup. The Baguio City native stands four inches taller than Yamakita and owns a two-inch reach advantage, and he knows how to use every bit of it.

The jab turns that length into a weapon. Rooted in his wushu background, it keeps opponents at bay and disrupts their entries, buying him time to sprawl. Knees and straight shots await anyone who changes levels carelessly.

That range control carried him to the throne in 2018, when he outstruck Yoshitaka "Nobita" Naito over five rounds in their rematch to claim the belt for the first time.

Yamakita must cross that distance to start his offense. Length alone won't keep him out, though. Jeremy "The Jaguar" Miado held a clear height and reach advantage, yet Yamakita still found a single-leg against the fence and finished him in round one.

If "The Passion" manages the distance with his footwork, Yamakita may spend five rounds searching for a way inside.

Before MMA, Yamakita made his name on the wrestling mats, placing on Japan's national stage as a junior high student and later as a National Athletic Meet runner-up.

The Inabe native's 5-foot-1-inch frame works in his favor. A low center of gravity powers explosive level changes, and every takedown opens the door to the ground strikes, back-takes, and submissions that define his game.

Pacio's compatriot and former teammate Lito "Thunder Kid" Adiwang got an up-close look, as Yamakita took him down early and controlled the fight en route to a unanimous decision. In his ONE Championship debut, the challenger's tireless pace also overwhelmed a grappling specialist in Alex Silva.

That poses a real problem for the defending champion. Pacio's toughest nights have come against opponents who wrestled him, from Brooks' five-round decision win in 2022 to the repeated takedowns Malachiev landed in their first meeting.

Should the Japanese wrestler win the battle for position, the fight shifts into the world where he does his best work.

Five-round fights have been Pacio's norm since he was 22. Over eight years, the 30-year-old has built three World Title reigns and settled a trilogy with Brooks, nearly all of it with 25 minutes on the clock.

That experience shapes how he fights. He prepares for 25 minutes every time out, building a pace and composure that rarely fade in the championship rounds.

Yet that mindset has never stopped him from seizing an opening. He rallied from early trouble to stop Brooks with ground strikes in the second round of their unification bout, then needed less than one round to submit Malachiev in their rematch.

Yamakita, by contrast, has yet to fight beyond three rounds in ONE. He did win the Pancrase title over five rounds, and his grinding style seems built for long fights. Whether it holds up under 25 minutes of championship pressure remains to be seen.

If this one reaches deep waters, the Lions Nation MMA representative will be in familiar territory.

Yamakita's wrestling and jiu-jitsu converge in his signature finish, the bulldog choke. The rare head-and-arm strangle rewards the upper-body control he builds through every scramble.

His creativity makes it work. Against Miado, he switched from a no-hook rear-naked choke to the bulldog, then walked his feet up the Circle wall to tighten the angle and force the tap. "Pocket Monk" has even teased a new variation of his own, dubbed the "Cerberus choke."

At ONE SAMURAI 2 in August, he hunted an arm-triangle, took Tanoue's back, and closed the show with another first-round bulldog choke. Months earlier, he armbarred Kurosawa at ONE SAMURAI 1.

Pacio has grown into a dangerous grappler himself, as his submission of Malachiev proved. Even so, Japanese opponents have found his neck before. Earlier in his career, both Naito and Hayato Suzuki choked him out from the back.

Should Yamakita find the neck on November 18, he could topple a dynasty and return the strawweight throne to Japanese hands on home soil.
