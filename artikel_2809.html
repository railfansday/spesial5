Our Defense vs. Their Offense: Chicago Bears

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Chicago Bears and head coach Ben Johnson will be bringing the 7th-ranked offense in the NFL with them when they play the New Orleans Saints on Sunday. The Bears may have a young, budding superstar QB in Caleb Williams, but they are a ball-control, run-dominant offense. The Bears lead the league in time of possession at 34:31 and rushing attempts with 303, and they average over 4 yards per rush. They are second in the NFL with 17 rushing TDs.

The efficiency of this offense does not stop there. They are in the top 5 in rushing yards, averaging 142 yards per game, and third-down conversions at 45.3%. As dynamic as Caleb Williams is as a QB, the passing offense has not been highlight-reel explosive. That does not mean Williams cannot make those types of plays, but Johnson has done a very good job this year of keeping the offense on schedule, and Williams is creating when he needs to.

The passing offense ranks 12th in the NFL, averaging 241 yards per game. They only have 10 passing TDs so far this season. Part of the reason for this is that Williams was unable to play for three weeks due to a hamstring injury. He has not missed a game since returning against Atlanta, and the Bears have not lost since his return.

The Saints are bringing one of the worst defensive units in the league to Soldier Field on Sunday. The Saints' defense will have to enter this game with the mentality that they cannot allow Chicago to run the ball. This is easier said than done because the Saints are 27th in rushing defense, allowing 129 yards per game on the ground.

The advantage the Saints will have is when the Bears drop back to pass. The Saints have 26 sacks on the season so far and are tied for 9th in the NFL. If they can force Williams to throw the ball and keep him contained within the pocket, they will have a chance to cause negative plays.

The Saints' defense will have to be opportunistic and create chaos in the backfield for Chicago. Williams is getting better, but he also has 5 interceptions on the season with only 8 TDs. Can the Saints make him feel uncomfortable and force him to give the ball up?

The Saints will have to slow down D'Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai. This running back combination has accounted for over 900 rushing yards on 215 carries and 13 rushing touchdowns. This further stresses the importance of forcing the Bears into third-and-long situations and obvious passing downs so the edge rushers and Kaden Elliss can play to their strengths.

The Saints will look to shock the NFL and hand the Bears their third loss of the year.
