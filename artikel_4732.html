You often hear about setting boundaries with others, but if you struggle with anxiety issues, how about setting boundaries with yourself?

Understandably, anxious people do all sorts of things to try to feel less anxiety. The problem is that the things that we do to manage anxiety might work in the moment, but can ultimately backfire, and they may cement long-term anxiety. Consider the following examples:

Here are some steps for helping yourself reduce long-term anxiety.

1. Work on accepting anxiety. Often, for people to reduce their long-term anxiety, they need to accept that they might feel anxious and that they can handle experiencing anxiety. So many people have secondary anxiety, or anxiety about anxiety, which only escalates anxiety and makes everything worse for themselves.

How can you accept anxiety? Even just telling yourself that it’s OK to be anxious, that it’s not the end of the world, that it won’t go on forever, and that you can handle it is a start. Committing to reducing anxiety-driven behaviors is another important step. Also, many people would benefit from accepting that uncertainty is a part of life, and not a sign of something wrong.

2. Assess your anxiety-driven behaviors. To set some anxiety boundaries with yourself, you need to figure out what exactly you are doing. You can ask yourself, if I didn’t have issues with anxiety, what would I be doing differently? What would I be doing? What wouldn’t I be doing?
