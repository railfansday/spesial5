The Preview: Scottish Cup is the perfect proving ground for Clydebank and Co., as band of Lowland League clubs look to make their mark

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 15:17 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 16:44 EST, 27 November 2026

If Clydebank aren’t careful, getting the better of SPFL opposition will soon become something of an expectation rather than a hope. Then again, given their lofty ambitions, that’s exactly the kind of club they want to be.

It’s been 23 extraordinary years since its reformation, having gone bust following a series of events which left one of Scottish football’s great clubs — and great communities, for that matter — on its knees.

That it has taken less than a quarter of a century to get back within touching distance of the SPFL frankly beggars’ belief.

Win the Lowland League West — they currently sit three points behind leaders Largs Thistle with a couple of games in hand — and they will have a crack at earning promotion to the big time via a play-off.

Yet, as exciting a prospect as that may be, it’s something which will be a million miles from the minds of the players, staff and supporters as they rock up at Holm Park this afternoon for a Scottish Cup third-round clash against Championship high-flyers Livingston.

This, of course, is not uncharted territory. Indeed, it’s the third time in as many years that the club have made it to this stage of the competition, and even made it one round further in 2024-25 before bowing out to Hibs at Easter Road.

Clydebank will welcome Championship side Livingston to Holm Park tomorrow

Yet it wouldn’t necessarily be a great surprise if they were to reach that stage again this time around given the way in which they’ve gone about their business with such professionalism against teams playing much higher up in the football pyramid.

Their incredible run to the KDM Evolution Trophy quarter-finals saw them defeat Dumbarton, East Fife, Kelty Hearts and Alloa Athletic before progressing on penalties against Queen’s Park on Tuesday night.

That win marked the 77th consecutive domestic match in which Clydebank have found the net, incidentally, having surpassed Celtic’s record of 75 set in 2017 a few days earlier.

Pulling off a shock once, maybe even twice, could be put down to luck. To do so habitually is something different. Which is why this is a bit of a nightmare draw for Livi.

The days of non-league clubs simply making up the numbers at this point of the competition are a thing of the past, with the gap between the second and fifth tiers of Scottish football seemingly getting smaller with each passing year.

That the likes of Clydebank will genuinely believe they can beat a team chasing an immediate return to the top flight suggests as much.

They are not the only Lowland League West side in action today, with Cumnock Juniors eyeing up a shock as they welcome Championship outfit Arbroath, who know a thing or two about upsetting the odds having punched well above their weight for long enough.

You suspect Raith Rovers won’t be relishing their journey to face Troon at Portland Park, a stadium perhaps best known for hosting a few Brazilian chaps including Pele, Garrincha, Tostao and Zico ahead of the 1966 World Cup in England.

The Lowland League West side have beaten the likes of Alloa and Queen's Park this season

The names of today’s visitors may not quite carry the same gravitas, but nevertheless, it’s arguably the biggest game in Troon’s 80-year history.

One thing is for certain: there will be at least one Lowland League West club in the hat for the next round, with Renfrew — who have Argyll McCoist, son of Rangers legend Ally in their squad — going head-to-head with Cumbernauld Colts, with both chasing a money-spinning fourth-round tie against one of the country’s big guns.

Bo’ness United and Brechin City — both battling it out in the Lowland League East this season — are in the same boat ahead of their meeting at Newtown Park.

Fifty miles separate Ross County and Brora Rangers ahead of their clash in Dingwall, but in Highland terms, it constitutes something of a derby.

The visitors are looking to follow in their opponents footsteps by getting up to the SPFL this season, 22 years after County broke through. And they are going about it the right way too, having gone unbeaten in the Highland League so far this term.

Funnily enough, they are coming against the only other team in Scotland’s top five tiers who share that particular accolade.

And those in charge at Morton will be praying their side can survive their trip to League Two’s basement side Annan Athletic in the hope of pulling a lucrative fixture in the next round.

Amid all the financial uncertainty and concern regarding the future of the Cappielow club these days — even more so following the news earlier this week that main sponsor Dalrada Technology Ltd has been placed in liquidation — goodness knows they need some positivity around the place.
