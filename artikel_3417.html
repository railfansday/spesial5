5 Greatest YA Movie Franchises Of All Time, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Young adult stories have always made for compelling movie adaptations, especially when you look at the massive YA movie boom that began in the early 2000s. Magical worlds, dystopian futures, complicated love triangles, and the supernatural surrounding various coming-of-age stories will always have strong entertainment potential, though some are better than others.

The very best YA movie franchises turn relatable and familiar coming-of-age experiences into major cinematic experiences, balancing honoring the source material with offering new perspectives at the same time. As such, it's notable that our choices for the top five greatest YA movie franchises are all still ongoing with brand-new projects in some form or another, from Harry Potter to The Hunger Games.

This ranking will consider a few different key factors, including overall quality, consistency, how well it adapts and expands the source material, world-building, and cultural impact. Keeping that in mind, here are the five greatest YA movie franchises ranked from great to greatest.

Disney and 20th Century Fox's Chronicles of Narnia movies brought C.S. Lewis' beloved fantasy novels to life, starting with 2005's The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe. Audiences were introduced to the Pevensie children who discover the magical land of Narnia and ultimately join the fight against the White Witch.

Without question, the first movie remains the peak of the franchise, though 2008's Prince Caspian offered a compelling darker journey with the Pevensies' return to a very different Narnia. 2010's Voyage of the Dawn Treader also adapted the source material fairly well. However, the franchise lost momentum and stalled after the third film, so much so that the rest of the books in Lewis' series were never adapted in this iteration of the franchise. That's a large reason why this ranks so low on this list, despite still being one of the GOATs.

Looking ahead, it will be very interesting to see how Netflix and Greta Gerwig's The Magician's Nephew fares next year, a brand-new take on the franchise that looks to be incredibly unique.

DreamWorks' How to Train Your Dragon franchise is one of the most emotionally satisfying animated movie sagas ever made, with its focus on a young outcast Viking named Hiccup and a dragon named Toothless. Managing to change his entire village's hatred of dragons, the franchise gets better with every entry as Hiccup gradually evolves into a great leader and hero for his people.

There's an incredibly rich coming-of-age story baked into the entire franchise that is also full of some of the greatest dragon designs and animated action sequences of all time (not to mention one of the very best movie scores as well). Having expanded into television, short films, and most recently the start of a whole live-action adaptation, the enduring popularity and audience engagement are a clear testament to its GOAT status, though here's hoping the rest of the live-action franchise lives up to its animated counterparts as the first movie did.

It may not yet have the same cultural footprint as the top three from a live-action standpoint, but it still delivers one of the most satisfying and complete narratives on this list.

Speaking of cultural impact, few YA movie franchises have had quite as big an impact as Twilight. Based on Stephenie Meyer's bestselling novels, the supernatural romance series saw Bella Swan falling in love with Edward Cullen, a vampire struggling between his own feelings for her and his more dangerous impulses. The series only expands from there, featuring a love triangle with werewolves (Jacob), rival vampires, and major supernatural political conflicts.

Running from 2008–2012, the five-film franchise was a huge cultural phenomenon that is still talked about today, particularly as the key franchise that helped launch Robert Pattinson's ongoing stardom as one of the biggest stars today (who else has ever played Batman and James Bond simultaneously?) Twilight also inspired several other YA movie romance adaptations and supernatural stories. Despite the inconsistency between films and major melodrama throughout, its cultural impact and massive fanbase can't be dismissed.

Suzanne Collins' The Hunger Games novels became one of the most compelling YA movie franchises of the 2010s. Set in the dystopian nation of Panem created from the ashes of what was North America, the main four-film series focuses on Katniss Everdeen (Jennifer Lawrence), who volunteers to take her younger sister's place in a televised battle to the death, featuring children randomly selected from each of the nation's 12 districts for the entertainment of the wealthy elite living in the Capitol.

Following her survival in the first book/movie along with Peeta Mellark (Josh Hutcherson), what follows is Katniss' evolution into a major symbol of revolution known as the Mockingjay as the nation falls into civil war, examining the power of propaganda, class inequality, political manipulation and corruption, and the cost of revolution itself (there's also a major love triangle).

Running from 2012-2015, the original four films provided an incredibly satisfying adaptation of Collins' primary trilogy, with Catching Fire standing out as a very strong sequel. However, the franchise has since continued with 2023's The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, exploring the younger years of President Snow, while Sunrise on the Reaping has just been released this year and is now playing in theaters, starring a young Haymitch Abernathy and more legacy characters in a major prequel revealing the events of the second Quarter Quell.

Both spin-off prequels prove the ongoing narrative potential of the world of Panem and The Hunger Games as one of the strongest YA movie franchises ever...aside from one that absolutely holds the top spot.

It truly is difficult to imagine any YA movie franchise surpassing Warner Bros.' Harry Potter films. Beginning with 2001's The Sorcerer's Stone, audiences were introduced to a magical world and school for witchcraft and wizardry that is endlessly captivating and full of wonder, even as the eight-film series evolves into a major magical war against the dark forces of Voldemort.

Few cinematic trios are as beloved as Harry, Ron, and Hermione, and even fewer have been given as much time to grow and evolve on-screen across 10 years of storytelling. Likewise, the incredibly consistent cast overall throughout those 10 years is a huge advantage for the franchise. By all accounts, the main Harry Potter saga is one of the most emotionally satisfying journeys in all of cinema (Forget just YA movie franchises).

Beyond the original saga, the ongoing potential of the Wizarding World was shown with the Fantastic Beasts prequel series. Despite its mixed results, the films still maintained high fan engagement and added to the franchise's overall cultural impact.

Looking ahead, Warner Bros is set to release its new Harry Potter streaming series on HBO Max at the end of the year, a full reboot with an all-new cast that intends to adapt one book per season. On the one hand, it's a testament to the original movie franchise and its enduring cultural impact as a huge piece of IP. On the other, the Harry Potter movies are still so beloved that trying to reboot the franchise is a massive risk and is already facing controversies ahead of its premiere this Christmas.

At any rate, Harry Potter's iconic world-building, memorable performances, and extraordinary cultural influence all serve to make it the most complete and greatest YA franchise on this list. More than any other YA series, Harry Potter made audiences feel like they were essentially growing up alongside its heroes. As a result, it's highly doubtful there will ever be a bigger GOAT when it comes to YA movie franchises.

Harry Potter is a multimedia franchise about an orphaned boy who enrolls at Hogwarts School of Wizardry, where he learns the truth about himself, his family, and the terrible evil that haunts the magical world. Adapted from the novels, Harry Potter is an eight-episode film saga that follows the journey of Harry Potter and his friends, Hermoine Granger and Ron Weasley, as they navigate the tricky world of growing up, school life, and magic. Starting from year one and moving to their seventh year, the films chronicle the students' time at Hogwarts while unfurling a sinister plot that centers around the unsuspecting Harry. With the return of the dark wizard, Voldemort, the students and professors at Hogwarts will fight to carry on as the world around them may change forever. Harry Potter has expanded beyond the world of its films and novels with several video games, a spin-off film series titled Fantastic Beasts, and even attractions at Universal Studios.
