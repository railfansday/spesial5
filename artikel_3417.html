2026's Most Viral Movie with 97 Rotten Tomatoes Score Sets Streaming Date

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

You are not ready for what Nathan Fielder has in store

In recent years, Nathan Fielder has rightfully earned a place as one of the most provocative figures in modern comedy. Most recently, the actor, filmmaker, and pilot (it’s a long story) co-directed You Can See Everything, a three-hour, brain-bending documentary about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes and her partner Billy Evans in the days before Holmes began her 11-year prison sentence for defrauding investors.

Despite this seemingly obscure premise, the A24 movie has exceeded all expectations, grossing an impressive $93 million on a $7 million budget to become the 10th highest-grossing documentary of all time. The film has received widespread critical acclaim, with a 97% Tomatometer score and an even more impressive 97 average review score on Metacritic. Audiences are equally enthusiastic; the movie has a 95% user score on Rotten Tomatoes.

According to WhenToStream’s sources, You Can See Everything is coming to Video On Demand (VOD) and digital platforms on Nov. 24, 2026. This would put the movie on streaming right before Thanksgiving; one can hope it leads to many awkward Turkey Day meals. While we don’t know how much the movie will cost to rent or buy, it will likely be priced between $19.99 and $24.99 to rent, as has become standard for early digital releases. You Can See Everything is still in theaters in the U.S. and hasn’t opened worldwide yet. It’s coming to the U.K. on Nov. 20, and to France on Dec. 30.

It’s difficult to explain what makes You Can See Everything so mesmerizing without spoiling its bizarre twists and turns, but the gist is that the movie pitches itself as one thing, before slowly spiraling into something else. Fielder once again shows his ability to squeeze the absurdity out of people and situations, as he did on Nathan for You and The Rehearsal, leading to increasingly ridiculous, self-referential circumstances built around deception and performance.

The movie intersects with many contemporary issues, including the increasing right-wing radicalization of tech bros, and grows increasingly meta until it eventually calls into question whether documentaries can really uncover the “truth” at all. There’s a lot going on, and it’s best to see the madness unfold yourself.
