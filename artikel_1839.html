Speared Hearts’ craziness goes on as Gorgie boys give Monaco something to drop off at Louvre

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Eight goals at Dens Park is one thing. Going toe-to-toe with French aristocrats and being beaten, cruelly, at the death is quite another. Hearts’ growing reputation as the great entertainers was nevertheless burnished here in thrilling fashion.

The price the Hearts fans might have to pay for such swashbuckling play was also demonstrated. Defensive fragility was all too apparent, including at the end when substitute Mamadou Coulibaly brushed off a challenge in midfield and threaded the ball through for Folarin Balogun to score his second and the night’s decisive goal. We were already into the final minute of the four added on at the end of a quite breathless European tie.

Mon Dieu! What a match. AS Monaco won’t forget this trip to Scotland, and a vibrant Tynecastle, in a hurry. As for the hosts, they remain just about alive in the Conference League.

The point they were deprived in heartbreaking fashion would have made such a difference. As good as Monaco are, it wasn’t as if Hearts were undone by a moment of genius, which makes it harder to bear. The hosts also had chances to score their own third goal and were angered when referee Daniel Schlager waved away penalty appeals when substitute Josh McPake ended up in a heap after Jordan Teze’s challenge.

Perhaps the home supporters will simply have to put up with see-sawing emotions in a season that’s already shaping up to be one of the craziest in the club’s history. Perhaps defensive slackness, although costly, must simply be endured if goals continue to flow at the other end, especially ones as sensational as Hearts fans were treated to here. Drop them off in the Louvre on your way home, Monaco.

Jordi Altena’s opener was declared a “potential” goal of the season by the always judicious Tynecastle Tannoy announcer. Just as well because a better one was just around the corner. Sabah Kerjota’s curler on the stroke of half time was an immaculate finish that Lukas Hradecky, the experienced keeper, just had to stand rooted to his spot and admire.

Altena’s strike was not quite in the same unsavable part of the goal but it was still breathtaking in its execution. Kerjota, the little Albanian livewire, also played a part in it. His free-kick looked to be going in until Hradecky pushed the ball against the post. Claudio Braga made sure the ball was recycled and played it back to Kerjota, who passed it inside to Altena. The Dutchman is fast becoming a Tynecastle favourite. No wonder when he can strike a ball like this. Hradecky was left flapping at the air beneath his bar.

Since going 5-0 down to Falkirk after 47 minutes earlier this month at Tynecastle, Hearts had scored 10 unanswered goals. This was the 11th. The response was always coming. This was AS Monaco after all. They might be struggling currently in Ligue 1 but how can they be they be written off? The team remains packed full of talent. This was not the shadow side Hearts might have hoped to face. Eric Dier, the England international, was present and correct as was former Arsenal player – and Donald Trump’s friend - Balogun.

Monaco were the ones raising a quizzical French eyebrow at the home team sheet. Due to much publicised injury problems and lack of adequate club-trained academy players, Hearts couldn’t even fill the bench.

Matthew Gillies stepped in for his first senior start in midfield. Unless Monaco were familiar with Stenhousemuir's opening matches of this season, the talented 19-year-old might have been something of a mystery to them. The same applies to Finlay Pollock, who is something of a mystery to Hearts fans as well.

Pollock came on towards the end for his first Hearts appearance since 2024. The 22-year-old has been out on loan at East Fife and Raith Rovers in between times. He was given the last nine minutes in place of a drained Kerjota. The whole team, including substitutes, looked shattered at the end but they had contributed to a game that will live long in the memory. There was the drama of an overturned penalty for Hearts in the second half after an alleged handball by substitute Mathys Detourbet as well as the sight of Beau Reus galloping up field for a corner in the final seconds.

The visitors’ first goal of the night saw Balogun do the necessary – and no more - from the penalty spot after Stuart Findlay had clipped the heels of the dangerous Matthis Abline.

There was some dismay among home fans about Reus’ lack of movement. Balogun’s penalty lacked conviction and certainly wasn’t placed in the corner. Far from it. Nevertheless, it brought Monaco level. The French side took heart although they didn’t have it all their own way. Abline was booked for a foul on Laurent Mendy as Hearts forced the visitors into sometimes desperate measures.

Monaco did though take the lead for the first time in the night five minutes before half time after Edan Diop took advantage of a slack ball from Altena following a throw-in. After playing a one-two with Balogun, he slipped the ball beyond Reus.

Gillies was showing his worth in midfield. He might have had a goal to tell his pals about had Pierre Landry Kabore not taken the ball off his foot as he was preparing to unleash a shot from the edge of the box. Kabore, following his brace against Dundee, was in frisky form but often got a little too excited and forgot the basics, such as avoiding running into teammates.

Gillies was withdrawn at half time for Blair Spittal, back from injury and desperate to play a part. He also took the captain’s armband from McCart and helped drive Hearts on. Sadly, his and others’ efforts were in vain on an agonising night for the hosts.
