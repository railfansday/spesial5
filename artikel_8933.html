SHAU’s Microlibraries Initiative Expands with a New Project in Jakarta

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A new microlibrary project by Indonesian studio SHAU is under construction in Jakarta. The Microlibrary Taman Puring is part of the microlibraries initiative designed and organized by SHAU Rotterdam-Bandung (Florian Heinzelmann and Daliana Suryawinata), in collaboration with the City of Jakarta and the Microlibrary Foundation, a nonprofit initiative dedicated to expanding a network of sustainable, multifunctional community reading spaces across low-income neighborhoods in Indonesia. SHAU's first project, Kampung Mrican Phase 1, received the highest prize in the social architecture category of the Ammodo Architecture Award 2025, funding that made possible the realization of this second one.

SHAU's first project, Kampung Mrican Phase 1, received the highest prize in the social architecture category of the Ammodo Architecture Award 2025, funding that made possible the realization of this second one.

Microlibrary Taman Puring broke ground on October 24, 2026, and is expected to be completed and celebrated as part of Jakarta's 500th anniversary in June 2027. The project is set within a park in South Jakarta, with abundant vegetation incorporated into the design. The building is designed with spiraling, curved concrete that supports a lightweight green roof, creating shaded, semi-outdoor reading spaces between the spiral rooms. During the design process, consultations and workshops with different disability communities, including Yayasan Menembus Batas and Mitra Inspirasi Pendidikan Indonesia (MIPI), took place to integrate universal access into the design, including wheelchair accessibility and accommodations for users with visual impairments.

SHAU's Kampung Mrican Phase 1, recognized by the Ammodo Architecture Award 2025, also involved kampung residents in the design process. The project comprises a library, playground, public space, flood-control post, community garden, bridge, and sidewalk system. It was carried out in collaboration with the PKP Directorate under the Ministry of Public Works and Housing. The aim of the Ministry's program is to enhance living conditions by upgrading infrastructure, creating an opportunity to encourage reading in the area while accommodating community activities. Taman Puring joins seven other buildings designed by SHAU for the microlibraries network.
