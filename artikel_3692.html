Horde PVP character lock in WoW Forever, explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Having trouble making a Horde character in PvP lately? You're not alone.

If you’ve been eager to hop into World of Warcraft: Forever, create a Horde character, say “lok’tar ogar!” (victory or death!) after an intense PvP match, you’re going to have to wait. There’s an ongoing faction lock on Horde characters in PvP rulesets until further notice.

In a game like WoW, premised on being big on faction warfare, achieving some form of balance across player numbers ensures no one side dominates PvP. And since Forever does have a dedicated mega-realm (ruleset) for PvP players, this faction lock has become a source of frustration.

We’ll look into what we know so far, and update you once it’s finally been lifted.

The faction lock happens when you try to switch over to the PvP ruleset and create a Horde character. Chances are, you’ll be stopped by the game. The error box will state this is due to a “population imbalance.”

If you’ve ever encountered faction restrictions in WoW before, this is a similar situation. Just like the error says, it’s very likely that one faction probably had a lot more players than the other.

“While even the most extreme case in a region is currently at roughly a 55/45 split, that is not as close as we want it to be,” community manager Kaivax said in a post. “Our goal is to bring things as close to a 50/50 faction balance as possible.”

Perhaps the most common “answer” to the lock problem is to create a character in the other faction to help balance the population. Unfortunately, this will lock your account’s PvP ruleset to that faction.

If you already have a character in the PvE ruleset, you can hop on there and enjoy its more casual leveling experience. You can still toggle World PvP via the options menu.

Should you want to take the game’s adventuring fantasy more seriously, maybe the RP-PvP ruleset works for you. This just has the added requirement of actually roleplaying your character, so speaking in dialogue, following cues for anything out-of-character, and just fully immersing in Azeroth. Perfect if you want to roleplay your character’s heritage on top of high-stakes PvP.

Character creation restrictions in PvP rulesets weren’t just exclusive to the Horde, however. Blizzard also locked the creation of Alliance characters to some degree, in a bid to ensure character populations across each faction are as balanced as realistically possible.

However, Yryadorne from customer service clarified that their team isn’t able to unlock this manually.

Some players asked whether trial accounts also affected the count. These are accounts where you could only play for free until Level 20 (with restrictions) without a subscription. Kaivax replied that their testing concluded the trial accounts had nothing to do with the imbalance. They explained that the 55/45 split focused on the “active logged-in population” of players across all levels.

There’s no evidence that creating a lot of trial accounts would fix the faction balance, especially if part of the consideration is that they are “actively” logged in.

A lot of players expressed frustration with the inability to create characters in their favored Faction, especially those who want to sink their teeth into PvP in Forever.

The situation has been particularly difficult for players who also want to play with friends of one faction, only to find out it’s currently locked.

One Blizzard forum post even requested some form of compensation, as there’s no proper communication surrounding the faction lock. Other threads also called out the lack of any virtual queue, estimated waiting time, or any way of knowing how long until the restriction is lifted.

The idea of faction-locking isn’t new to Forever, but it certainly came as a shocker to players still getting used to the idea of having one mega-realm to play on.

Things were different in the OG release and Classic, where you could have characters in different realms. Back then, you could only be Horde on one PvP realm and Alliance on another. Faction rules only applied to new players when one faction was starting to crowd a specific realm, so newcomers had to adjust by joining the rival group.
