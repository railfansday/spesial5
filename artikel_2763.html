WTC points table scenarios after India vs New Zealand Test series: All possible changes

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

India went into the series fourth in the World Test Championship standings on 51.52%.

New Zealand are fourth in the points table after a draw in the first Test.

A series win would lift India's chances, which is the only outcome that keeps them in genuine contention.

India's attempt to reach a third World Test Championship final has reached the stage where arithmetic matters as much as form, and the final Test in New Zealand that begins on November 27 is the last chance to make the numbers work before Australia arrive in January.

Shubman Gill's side have played 12 Tests in this cycle and won five of them, which leaves them on 72 points from a possible 144 and a points percentage of 50.00. That is fourth, and a long way behind the two teams currently holding the final places.

New Zealand were one of those two teams at the top, however, with a draw at Wellington, they have now dropped to third position. Tom Latham's side sit have won four, lost one and drawn twice, and a series win in the final Test would increase their chance of reaching the final.

Here is a full breakdown of what is at stake and how the WTC points table could change on every possible outcome of the India vs New Zealand series.

For India, this was a close-to-must-win territory. They were beaten 2-0 at home by South Africa earlier in the cycle, which is the result that did the real damage, and they had only this series and the five-Test home summer against Australia left to repair it.

They have played the fewest matches of any side near the top, which makes their percentage both the strongest and the most fragile, because every result moves it a long way.

Two wins would have put them close to 80% and effectively settle one of the two final spots. After the draw in the first Test, they will have to win the final Test and also hope to do a clean sweap in the five-match BGT series in the new year.

The two-match series offers four possible outcomes, each with a very different impact on the standings. Below is how the table could shift under every scenario.

A 2-0 sweep was the only result that could have genuinely changed India's cycle. It would have taken them to 92 points from 13 matches and a percentage of 58.97, moving them above Bangladesh and into striking distance of the top three with five home Tests against Australia still to play.

A 1-0 win with the other Test drawn is worth far less, lifting India only to 53.85. New Zealand would drop to 58.33 in that case, which would still leave them ahead of India but no longer comfortable at the top.

A 2-0 home win would have effectively ended India's campaign. They would stay on 68 points across 13 matches for a percentage of 43.59, needing to win all five against Australia and still rely on results elsewhere. New Zealand, meanwhile, would climb to 79.17 and all but confirm a place in the final.

If New Zealand win 1-0 with one draw, India fall to 46.15 and Latham's side rise to 70.83, which keeps them narrowly ahead of South Africa at the top.

Two draws would leave India on 76 points from 13 matches at 48.72, which is lower than where they started, because a drawn Test returns only four of the 12 points on offer. It is the quiet killer among these outcomes.

New Zealand would slip to 62.50 and almost certainly surrender top spot to South Africa, although they would remain in a strong position with fewer matches played than most of their rivals.

A shared series, with each side winning one Test, would leave India on 80 points at 51.28, which is within a quarter of a percentage point of where they are now. New Zealand would fall to 66.67 and drop behind both South Africa and Australia.

This is the scenario that helps neither side, which is why both will be pushing for a decisive result rather than settling for a split across two matches.
