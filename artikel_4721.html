Pope Leo XIV Diagnosed With Lung Cancer After Surgery to Remove Part of Lung

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Pope Leo XIV has been diagnosed with lung cancer following a successful surgery, the Vatican said.

The Pope, 71, underwent surgery at Agostino Gemelli University Policlinic in Rome on October 13 to remove a nodule from his right lung. This was determined to be pulmonary adenocarcinoma, the most common type of lung cancer.

The surgeons removed the lower lobe of the Pope’s lung to “ensure a radical procedure; the lower lobe of the right lung was removed using a video-assisted technique,” said Dr. Luigi Carbone, the Director of Vatican Healthcare Services, and Professor Stephano Margaritora, the lead surgeon, in a press release.

“An intraoperative histological examination confirmed the diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma, as suspected from the preoperative examinations,” they said.

Pope Leo was in the operating room for 3 hours, while the operation itself lasted around 90 minutes.

The doctors noted that it was a successful operation with no complications.

According to Matteo Bruni, the Director of the Holy See Press Office, the Pope asked for dinner after the surgery and spent the evening with his secretaries and other Vatican staff.

In a press release on Wednesday, the Vatican said that the Pope’s postoperative recovery is progressing well.

According to Vatican News, Pope Leo had a calm first night following his surgery.

In the coming days, exams will be conducted on the removed tissue. There has currently been no change in the initially announced week of hospitalization.

On October 14, the Pope underwent blood tests and chest X-rays that were scheduled following his surgery. The results of these tests confirm that his postoperative recovery is progressing as expected, and the values are within a normal range for the type of operation performed.

Pope Leo has also undergone some respiratory physiotherapy and has begun walking around his room, the Vatican said.

It has not yet been announced whether he will require any further treatment.

Adenocarcinoma is the most common subtype of lung cancer globally. It accounts for around 40% of all lung cancer diagnoses.

Adenocarcinoma is a type of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which accounts for about 80–85% of all lung cancers.

Lung adenocarcinoma begins in the epithelial cells that produce mucus. It is the most common type of NSCLC.

Adenocarcinoma can be classified further by the genetic changes driving the tumor.
