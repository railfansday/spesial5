Marching bands scramble to cover costs after Thanksgiving parade rearranged

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

How New York reacted to postponement of Macy's parade

Touring musicians from across the US who descended on New York City to perform at the Macy's Thanksgiving parade have turned to fundraising to cover their costs, after getting stuck when the event was rearranged due to a storm.

A band from Texas told the BBC's US partner, CBS News, it had raised some $350,000 (Â£267,000) to pay for an extended stay for its 500 members. It saluted the kindness of its supporters.

A group of student musicians from Hawaii, meanwhile, voiced their relief after friends and family rallied around to cover their additional hotel, coach and meal costs.

The parade, a famous annual event, is now scheduled to take place on Saturday.

Earlier this week, organisers said they would it push it back by two days, in part due to the concerns around flying giant inflatables in strong winds.

The balloons were temporarily deflated for a few days - and so too were the spirits of those who had journeyed to the city to watch the action.

The move represented the first time the event had been postponed, although it was suspended altogether during World War Two.

Each year, a number of marching bands travel from across the country, some of them from high schools, to provide music for the event. This is considered a huge opportunity, as the parade is watched by millions of people in person and on TV.

The leader of the Texas band described his emotions as costs for his delegation were met.

"It's been exhausting and emotional to figure this out, but it has been incredibly humbling to see an outpouring of kindness," Ryan Demkovich, the director of the Pride of Pearland Marching Band at Pearland High School, near Houston, in Texas, told CBS.

Demkovich said contributions had arrived from businesses and individuals in the band's home community, with the help of a social media push.

Speaking to the BBC earlier on Thursday, the group's director Rickey H Badua described the time pressures. "We are racing to get this done," he explained.

The band is made up of hundreds of musicians from dozens of schools across the state. It had travelled almost 5,000 miles (8,000km) to get to New York.

"Getting the news that we got the parade postponed was not easy, and emotional, for our students," Badua said.

But the musicians sprang into action quickly, he said. "We come from a very far place," Badua said, adding that "to get so close to the finish line and not show up for it, we couldn't give up that easily".

The response from friends and family had been "incredible", he said.

Badua was hopeful his group would get the same experience he had when he first performed in the parade in 2003, as student drum major.

"The energy is palpable, people are just excited to see a band that is great."

Another musical act, from Illinois, told the BBC on Thursday it was trying to raise $175,000 to cover another night in a hotel, additional food, and bus costs back to the city of Champaign.

"Things are going well due to the very generous support of so many amazing human beings," said Prof Barry Houser, director of the University of Illinois Marching Illini.

While awaiting the parade, musicians and punters alike have sheltered from the heavy wind and rain that were brought by a storm to eastern areas of the US.

Hundreds of thousands of homes lost power, and coastal areas were flooded. The National Weather Service (NWS) expects conditions to improve gradually on Friday.
