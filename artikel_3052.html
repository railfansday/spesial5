The Peloton Turnaround Story: Here's the Good News, the Bad News, and Where Its Stock Could Go Next

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Peloton's revenue has declined significantly over the last five years, led by shrinking exercise equipment sales.

By Anthony Di Pizio – Nov 19, 2026 at 5:00PM EST

Peloton Interactive (PTON -5.23%) stock peaked at around $163 in late 2020, which was a whopping 462% increase from its initial public offering (IPO) price of $29. Consumers were buying the company's stationary exercise bikes, treadmills, and other equipment hand over fist at the height of the COVID-19 pandemic to maintain their fitness levels at home while gyms were closed.

But as lockdowns and social restrictions were gradually lifted in 2022, demand for Peloton's equipment crashed. The company quickly found itself in a fight for survival as its losses ballooned into the billions of dollars.

Peloton stock has since plummeted 96% from its peak. However, although equipment sales remain weak, management has made a series of changes to bring the company back from the brink of failure. Here's the good news, the bad news, and whether this might be a buying opportunity for investors.

Peloton's annual revenue peaked at $4 billion in fiscal 2021 (ended June 30, 2021), with equipment sales accounting for $3.1 billion of that total. However, the company's revenue has since declined for five straight years, coming in at $2.4 billion in fiscal 2026 (ended June 30). Equipment sales represented just $770.1 million -- or less than one third -- of the total.

Even partnering with third-party retailers like Amazon, Dick's Sporting Goods, and Costco hasn't been enough to revive Peloton's slumping equipment sales. Simply put, the pandemic triggered a unique, one-off spike in demand that the company hasn't been able to replicate, despite its best efforts.

The bulk of Peloton's revenue now comes from digital subscriptions. There is the connected fitness subscription, which gives equipment owners access to virtual classes and performance tracking tools. Then there is a separate subscription for the company's mobile application, which can be used by fitness enthusiasts who don't own Peloton's equipment. It helps them build workout plans and provides access to certain virtual classes.

Unfortunately, both the connected fitness subscription and the mobile app subscription saw a decline in paying members during the recent fiscal 2027 first quarter (ended Sept. 30), so Peloton is now experiencing a deterioration in sales across both hardware and software.

As a result, Peloton's business is likely to shrink for the seventh straight year, because management is forecasting a 4% decline in revenue during fiscal 2027 overall.

Following Peloton's excellent operating results in fiscal 2021, management positioned the company for more growth by increasing costs. But when that growth failed to arrive in fiscal 2022, Peloton suffered a whopping $2.8 billion net loss at the bottom line, and available cash was quickly running out.

Management proceeded to slash operating costs by 68% between fiscal 2022 and fiscal 2026, and Peloton is now profitable again. It generated a net income of $63.2 million in fiscal 2026, followed by a further $96.2 million in profit during the first quarter of fiscal 2027, after additional cost cuts.

After excluding one-off and noncash expenses like stock-based compensation, Peloton has delivered adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) of $517.7 million over the last four quarters. Simply put, the company is no longer at risk of going under.

If Peloton's revenue continues to decline year after year, the only way it can maintain profitability is by slashing costs even further. But, unfortunately, it will eventually run out of ways to do that -- and every time it pulls money away from areas like marketing, it becomes even harder to generate sales growth. As a result, the company could find itself in a downward spiral in the next few years that will be very challenging to reverse.

Peloton was sitting on over $1.3 billion in cash and cash equivalents as of Sept. 30, so it has some runway to continue testing different strategies to reignite equipment and subscription sales. However, the company is also carrying over $900 million in long-term debt, so it doesn't have an indefinite amount of time to deliver results.

Shrinking businesses tend to destroy shareholder value over time, so I don't think it's a good idea to buy Peloton stock right now, even after its 96% decline. It might be better to wait until the company proves it can deliver consistent revenue growth on an annual basis, because that's the big missing piece in the turnaround story. Until then, more downside in the stock could be ahead.
