Stock Market Today, Nov. 18: CoreWeave Slides as Nvidia's Blowout Earnings Fail to Ease Debt Concerns

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Booming demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure is doing little to alleviate investor concerns about the financial costs of keeping pace with the industry's rapid expansion.

By RJ Fulton – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:45PM EST

CoreWeave (CRWV -4.13%) fell 4.13% on Wednesday, November 18, closing at $47.83. Strong quarterly earnings from Nvidia (NVDA -2.90%) initially lifted CoreWeave shares in premarket trading, but those gains quickly evaporated as investors shifted their attention back to the company's substantial debt burden and ongoing financing needs. Despite booming demand for artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure, Wall Street remains increasingly concerned about the financial costs of sustaining that growth.

Trading volume reached 58.9 million shares, approximately 28% above its three-month daily average of 45.9 million. Despite recent selling pressure, CoreWeave shares remain up approximately 20% since the company's initial public offering in 2025.

The broader market delivered mixed results on Wednesday. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.23%) fell 0.24% to 7,323, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +0.42%) gained 0.42% to 24,430.

Despite modest strength in technology stocks, AI infrastructure providers remained under pressure. CoreWeave's peer IREN (IREN -3.58%) fell 3.61% to $15.24, underscoring growing investor concerns about the substantial capital requirements and financing costs associated with expanding AI computing capacity.

It is clear that demand for AI infrastructure remains exceptionally strong. Last week, CoreWeave reported year-over-year revenue growth of 168% and raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to between $13 billion and $13.3 billion. However, the market's reaction to Nvidia's earnings suggests investors are becoming less concerned about the strength of AI demand and more concerned about the financial costs of meeting it.

CoreWeave is targeting profitability in 2028, leaving the company with a considerable period during which it must finance its expansion. With the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, elevated borrowing costs present a significant headwind for a business that relies heavily on debt financing to acquire computing hardware and expand data center capacity.

Looking ahead, investors should pay particularly close attention to CoreWeave's financing arrangements, capital expenditures, and ability to convert contracted customer demand into cash flow. Long-term customer contracts and prepayments provide some financial stability, but the timing of those cash inflows relative to infrastructure spending remains critical.

While CoreWeave's revenue growth is impressive, the investment thesis increasingly depends on whether the company can generate sufficient returns on its substantial capital investments. Until there is greater visibility into sustainable profitability, strong revenue growth alone may not be enough to restore investor confidence.
