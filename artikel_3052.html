The Nasdaq Has Entered a Correction. Here Are 3 Space and EV Stocks I’d Buy if It Turns Into a Crash.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A correction tests your nerves, but a crash tests a company's bank account. Here are three space and EV stocks with the cash, the customers, or both to come out of a deeper sell-off stronger.

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% on Nov. 5 and closed at 24,315, more than 11% below the record it set on Oct. 6. That put the tech-heavy index in a correction per CNBC. The trigger was the Democratic sweep in the Nov. 3 midterms, which stirred fears that new limits on data centers could slow the artificial intelligence (AI) build-out.

Here's a simple way to think about the words getting thrown around. A correction is a 10% drop from a recent high, and it happens often enough that it's closer to bad weather than an emergency. A bear market is a 20% drop. A stock market crash is a rapid, steep decline, typically more than 20% in a matter of days or weeks.

The Nasdaq has since bounced, closing at 25,466.19 on Nov. 11. I'd rather make my shopping list before the next leg down. These three space and electric vehicle (EV) stocks are already about 40% or more below their highs, and each could come out of a crash stronger.

Rocket Lab (RKLB -3.58%) builds small rockets, satellites, and satellite parts. Its Nov. 9 report showed a record quarter: Revenue rose 73% to $268 million, and its backlog, meaning signed work it hasn't delivered yet, jumped 52% in three months to about $3.6 billion.

Then there's Neutron, its bigger reusable rocket. Its first launch slipped to no earlier than 2027, yet satellite operators signed more than $515 million in Neutron contracts last quarter, and Rocket Lab raised the base price to $70 million on several of them.

It's also buying Iridium Communications (IRDM -1.59%), the satellite phone and data network, for about $8 billion including debt, adding a steady subscription business. Rocket Lab ended September with $4.4 billion in cash and says the deal is already fully funded. Shares closed at $66.15 on Nov. 11, about 56% below their 52-week high.

BYD Company ADR (BYDDY -1.32%) is China's biggest maker of electric vehicles (EVs), and it makes its own batteries. With a brutal price war at home, it's selling abroad. In October, it exported 170,194 vehicles out of the 490,168 it sold.

That push is showing up in profits. Third-quarter revenue rose 13.6% to 221.5 billion yuan ($32 billion), and net profit climbed 28% to about 10 billion yuan ($1.5 billion). It's also bringing Flash Charging to Europe. It can refill BYD's second-generation Blade Battery from 10% to 70% in five minutes, about as long as a stop at the gas pump.

That's why I'd want it in a crash. A profitable carmaker that builds its own batteries can cut prices in a downturn and still make money while weaker rivals run dry. At $8.72, the shares are about 40% below their 52-week high and trade around 17 times earnings.

Rivian Automotive (RIVN -0.90%) finally has a cheaper SUV that people want. The R2 became its best-selling vehicle in Q3, helping deliveries jump 46% to 19,248 and revenue rise 38% to $2.16 billion.

The hidden strength is software. Volkswagen pays Rivian, through a joint venture, to build software for VW's future cars. That helped Rivian's software and services business earn a $256 million gross profit last quarter, more than covering its $80 million loss on building vehicles. Next up is "Point to Point," which drives a whole route while the owner supervises, plus an Autonomy and AI Day on Dec. 9.

The catch: Rivian's free cash flow was negative $1.1 billion last quarter, meaning it spent that much more than it brought in. It has about $7.1 billion available, including a $1 billion Volkswagen loan from October. Shares closed at $13.54 on Nov. 11, about 40% below their 52-week high.

Rocket Lab has the cash, BYD has the profits, and Rivian has a partner paying part of its way. In a crash, that's what separates the companies that buy cheap from the ones forced to sell cheap.
