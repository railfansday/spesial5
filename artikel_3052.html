BBVA Argentina (BBAR) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Margins compressed as rates fell, yet fee income and market share kept growing.

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 25, 2026 at 2:54PM EST

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

BBVA Argentina (BBAR -1.97%) reported a sequential decline in quarterly profitability as rapid interest rate reductions compressed net interest margins. Management indicated that while the bank faced a more gradual evolution of loans and deposits than originally anticipated, it successfully gained market share in both categories. Year-to-date performance remains positive, supported by double-digit growth in fee income and effective expense control. The bank observed early signs of improvement in asset quality, particularly in retail delinquency, and maintains a capital position significantly above regulatory requirements to fund future lending activities as the economic cycle gains momentum.

During the third quarter of 2026, the bank continued to operate in a dynamic macroeconomic environment marked by some short-term challenges, including a moderation in economic activity, inflation above initial expectations, and a more gradual evolution of loans and deposits than originally anticipated. Nevertheless, we remain confident in the medium and long-term outlook for the Argentine economy, supported by ongoing structural reforms and deregulation, solid financial and fiscal fundamentals, positive developments in the trade balance, and increasing investment opportunities.

We believe these factors are the laying foundations for a more stable, predictable, and sustainable economic environment, creating favorable conditions for the continued development and growth of our business. In this context, BBVA continues to strengthen its competitive positioning, consolidating its place as the second-largest private bank, leading financial intermediation growth among its main peers in a challenging credit quality landscape. Moving on to our financial highlights for the quarter, BBVA Argentina posted an inflation-adjusted net income of ARS 58.9 billion for the third quarter of 2026. This represents a 57.7% decrease quarter-over-quarter and a quarterly ROE of 4.9%.

The decline was mainly driven by a decrease in net interest margins stemmed from interest rates dropping compared to the first half of the year, which negatively affected asset repricing and also from lower yields on inflation-adjusted assets. As a consequence, NIMs declined from 18.2% to 15.1% in the quarter. It is worth mentioning that NIM adjusted by inflation cost, which in our view best reflects the evolution of margin over time, fell 260 basis points in the quarter to 12.2% and 10 basis points compared to 12 months ago, even with liabilities rate level approximately 2,000 basis points lower.

Regarding efficiency, our quarterly efficiency ratio stood at 49.6%, mainly affected by lower operating income, while personnel benefits and administrative expenses still proved a better performance. Nonetheless, year-to-date results show a positive overview, with a net income of nine months of ARS 294.4 billion, 14.4% above the same period in 2025. These figures arise from a 16% growth in net interest margin, a 17% improvement in net fee income, and a 6% reduction in personnel and administrative expenses, which, although largely offset by a 44% increase in loan loss allowances, lets us glimpse the potential of the business if credit situation continues to normalize as expected. Now, let's look at the dynamics of our balance sheet and credit portfolio.

Total financing to the private sector closed the quarter at ARS 18.4 trillion, increasing 2% quarter-over-quarter. While local currency loans increased 0.8%, our foreign currency private loans grew by 5.6% sequentially, equivalent to a 9.2% increase in hard currency. On the retail side, mortgage lending continues to gain momentum alongside pledge loans. Furthermore, we are successfully capturing business, mainly driven by the commercial segment and foreign currency loans. Our consolidated loan market share stood at 11.93%, signaling a total gain of 40 basis points over the last 12 months. On the funding side, total deposits reached ARS 19.8 trillion. Private deposit market share increased from 9.90% to 10.22% in the quarter, and up 14 basis points year-over-year.

In terms of asset quality, we are beginning to observe favorable signs in portfolio evolution, particularly in early delinquency indicators. This improvement is progressively reflected in lower loan loss allowances, which recorded a 5% decrease in real terms during the period, although there is still room for a more significant reduction. BBVA's NPL ratio at the end of September was 5.55%, a 54 basis point decrease quarter-over-quarter, while the system presented a ratio of 7.4% as of August, last available data as of the moment of this recording. Cost of risk shows a similar decline from 7.13% in the second quarter of 2026 to 6.58% in the third quarter of 2026.

Looking at solvency and liquidity, our liquidity ratio closed at a very comfortable 44.4%. More importantly, our capital position remains robust, with a regulatory capital ratio of 17.9%, representing 117.6% excess over minimum regulatory requirements. For subsequent periods, our expectation is to consolidate an upward path in profits once again, driven by progressive improvement in risk indicators and intermediation margins within a context of expense control. In this context, we continue to strengthen our capabilities to capture opportunities that arise when the economic cycle regains momentum, creating value for our stakeholders, clients, investors, and the country's productive development. Thank you for your time and continued support.

On business activity, despite the lower-than-expected credit demand, we grew our loan portfolio in real terms and strengthened our position in both loans and deposits. Over the last few years, we have achieved significant scale, with a market share of around 12% in lending and above 10% in deposits. We are now in a very good position to build on that scale. Our priority is to make the most of it by deepening customer relationships, increasing transactional activity, and improving profitability.

In retail, we continue to focus on secured lending, particularly mortgages and auto loans, and customers where we have greater visibility and repayment capability. On the corporate side, we see interesting opportunities in the more dynamic sectors of the economy and across their value chains. Our expectations for loan growth for this year is now more moderate, reflecting the slower recovery in demand. As economic activity and real incomes recover, we expect lending to gradually regain momentum, always with a focus on profitability and asset quality. Moving to margins, lower interest rates have put pressure on asset yields, but going forward, we expect margins to remain stable.

However, in Argentina, it is important to look at the margins and inflation together. As inflation comes down, the negative impact of inflation accounting in our results should also decrease, partially offsetting the pressure that lower interest rates have had on the net interest income. Our focus remains on pricing, funding mix, and business volumes. On fees, despite a weaker quarter due to some specific reasons, we have achieved solid growth of around 17% year-to-date in real terms compared to last year. Going forward, we want transactional activity to become an increasingly important source of revenues. On asset quality, we are starting to see the improvement in early arrears that we mentioned last quarter reflected in our NPL ratio.

We have also started to rebuild coverage. The encouraging point in that, the quality is our new origination is significantly better. We expect a gradual improvement in asset quality, and impairments will take longer to normalize. On efficiency, we continue to make progress on our cost base. Our priority is to make these savings sustainable. As activity recovers, we should also benefit from operating leverage. Finally, on profitability, we expect to close 2026 with a similar ROE to the one reported for the first nine months of the year. Looking into 2027, we see several factors that should support a progressive recovery: higher business volumes, a lower impact from inflation accounting, continued cost discipline, and a gradual normalization of impairments.

We remain cautious about how quickly these factors will materialize. What is important is that we are facing this environment with a stronger competitive position and with a capital and liquidity to support future profitability and growth.
