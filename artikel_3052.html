Could This Under-the-Radar, Cash-Rich Stock Be a 10-Bagger By 2030?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The neighborhood network is growing again, but a 10-fold return would require a lot to go right.

I won't sugarcoat it. Nextdoor (NXDR +0.49%) has been a tough investment to own since it went public in 2021 at $10 per share. The stock is down roughly 80% since then, and for a while it wasn't clear that the neighborhood social network could grow at all, at least profitably.

That's starting to change. Users are coming back, the company is close to breaking even, and it has a large cash cushion relative to its size. With a market value of about $790 million, it's fair to ask whether Nextdoor could be a 10-bagger from here. Here's the bull case, and the hurdles.

In the third quarter, Nextdoor's revenue rose 14% year over year, ahead of expectations. Platform weekly active users also grew by 14% to a record 24.5 million, the third straight quarter of sequential growth. What's more, management said the growth came mostly from returning users and product improvements, not paid marketing, and the number of people posting each week rose 27%.

CEO and co-founder Nirav Tolia believes Nextdoor is uniquely positioned to thrive in the AI age. In a world where more of the internet is becoming synthetic, he argues, real people verified to a real place become scarce. That's a compelling angle as AI-generated content floods the rest of social media. But could the stock be a 10-bagger for investors who buy at these levels?

In the third quarter, Nextdoor's adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) more than doubled to $10.3 million, and Nextdoor's net loss shrank to less than $1 million. It's worth noting that GAAP results still benefit from interest income on the more than $380 million in cash on its balance sheet, and that stock-based compensation remains high at 18% of revenue. The former adds to the bottom line, while the stock-based comp is the main reason Nextdoor is yet to achieve GAAP profitability.

Speaking of cash, the balance sheet is a major strength. Nextdoor ended the quarter with $382 million in cash and investments and no debt. That's nearly half of its market value, and the company has a $100 million buyback program in place to take advantage of weakness in the stock price

To be sure, a 10x return from Nextdoor would mean a market value of roughly $8 billion. If the market valued Nextdoor at about 5 times sales, a reasonable valuation for a profitable social platform, that would require roughly $1.6 billion in annual revenue, or a little more than five times this year's expected $294 million.

That's where the bull case runs into trouble. Average revenue per user rose just 1% last quarter. Nextdoor's audience is growing faster than its ability to monetize it, and it competes with much larger platforms for local advertising dollars. As a small-cap stock, it can also be volatile. However, the top-line growth has been accelerating, and if the company can figure out how to improve monetization, a 10x isn't outside the realm of possibilities.

In fairness, getting to $1.6 billion in revenue by 2030 might be pushing it, even if things go well. But this is a small company with tremendous upside potential if management can figure out profitable long-term growth (and the signs since Tolia returned as CEO have been mostly positive). After all, Nextdoor could 10x from its current price and have a smaller market cap than either Snap (SNAP +7.96%) or Pinterest (PINS +0.60%). Plus, keep in mind that if the company gets aggressive with its buyback program at the current level, it could reduce the share count and help drive per-share value.

Having said all that, Nextdoor is more interesting today than it's been in years. It has a unique asset in 15 years of verified local conversations, its users are growing again, and its cash pile gives it time to figure out monetization. I own Nextdoor and like what I've been seeing in recent quarters, and I think it's just a matter of time before the market starts to appreciate the company's positive momentum.
