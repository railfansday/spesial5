Amy Poehler’s ‘Parks and Recreation’ Replacement Is Officially a Streaming Hit

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Thanksgiving is a time for family, food, and reflection on all one has to be grateful for in a year. After the turkey and all the fixings are eaten, however, it's also a good time to catch up on everything on streaming. This year, the holiday week is packed with some big new releases, from Netflix's return to East Texas with The Hunting Wives Season 2 to its gritty World War II novel adaptation, The Mosquito Bowl, and Prime Video's return to Ridley Scott's classic cyberpunk world in Blade Runner 2099. For Peacock, most viewers are tucking into Amy Poehler's new comedy series following its launch on Thursday.

Over ten years after bidding farewell to Pawnee and Parks and Recreation, the Emmy winner has returned to small-screen humor with Dig, reuniting with her beloved Indiana-set sitcom's co-creator, Michael Schur, as both star and creative partner. Based on Kate Myers' 2023 novel Excavations, the series unfolds at a dig site in Greece, where four archaeologists make a discovery that changes everything. At the center is Poehler's Elise, the site's lead who longs to start her own dig, but who has long been forced to play second fiddle to British scientist Nigel (Hugh Laurie), who has a sincere love for the field, but simply refuses to retire and adheres strictly to tradition. Worse, he often gets the credit for anything interesting found. Her latest find with her fellow archaeologists seems like a chance to prove themselves, but instead, it jeopardizes all of their careers, pulling them into an international conspiracy.

Poehler and Laurie are joined by Geraldine Viswanathan, Antonia Thomas, and Fina Strazza on the dig site as part of the main cast. Dig also boasts a strong group of comedic actors among its recurring stars, including What We Do in the Shadows alum Kayvan Novak and Big Mouth star Jason Mantzoukas, alongside Bonita Friedericy, Jon Pointing, Brennan Lee Mulligan, and more. With so much talent on board, viewers showed up in force, immediately making the new show the #1 series on Peacock the day of launch. Reviews are also proving that viewers are digging the comedy, with critics landing at a solid 78% on Rotten Tomatoes and audiences going a bit higher at 81%.

Though Dig was based on Excavations, there were some major changes to the story for the screen, including to the central mystery enveloping Elise's archaeology team. As such, it also doesn't have to end after just one season. Speaking to TV Line, series writer J.J. Philbin teased that, despite a mostly clean ending, the team was thinking about what a Season 2 could look like throughout the process of penning the first season. Furthermore, he outright confirmed that "We are now writing Season 2" to be ready in case Peacock hands down the renewal order. It's far too early to say for certain what kind of chances the show has at a continuation, but its strong opening is a good indication that Elise and her colleagues turned friends could be heading to an entirely new site to continue doing what they're best at.
