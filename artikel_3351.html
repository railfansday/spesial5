Forget 'Game of Thrones,' These 10 Fantasy Shows Have Perfect Final Seasons

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Game of Thrones is undeniably one of the biggest television and cultural phenomena of the 21stcCentury, and its legacy is likely to be remembered for generations to come. However, the final season was rather divisive. Some fans defend it, while many others have dragged it through the mud, criticizing it for being rushed, nonsensical, and deeply unsatisfying.

It's a thing that happens sometimes: some shows just don't end on a high note, especially in the fantasy genre. On the other hand, there are plenty of fantasy shows out there that do have excellent finales, which is pretty widely agreed on. These finales didn't sow division amongst the fanbase; they satisfied long-time viewers and did everything they were supposed to.

Like it or not, franchise creator George Lucas says that Star Wars is more in line with fantasy than with sci-fi. Star Wars: The Clone Wars was the first animated TV show to be produced within the current canon, and it lasted seven seasons and 12 whole years. Set between the second and third prequel films, the saga follows various Jedi knights, Sith lords, bounty hunters, politicians, and clone troopers across engagements in the galaxy as the Republic faces off against the Separatists.

The sixth season aired much earlier than the seventh, due to Disney acquiring the brand around 2014. However, Disney still made the effort to release the final season during the height of the COVID pandemic, which is exactly what fans needed. The final season was amazing, too, featuring a climactic confrontation and wrapping everything up in a nice little bow, providing a smooth segue into the third film. This final season is one of the finest efforts Disney's Star Wars has undertaken.

The Dragon Prince is an animated series set on the magical continent of Xadia. The land has been divided in half following a catastrophic war, with humans segregated to the West and the Elves to the East. Dividing the two is a massive river of magma, overseen by the king of dragons. However, humans assassinate the dragon king, along with his egg, using dark magic, throwing both realms into chaos. In the West, two human princes team up with an elf when they discover that the dragon king's egg was actually not destroyed at all, which makes them embark on a quest to take it to the elves and remaining dragons.

The animation on this one is absolutely gorgeous and jam-packed with vibrant colours and detailed environments. The Dragon Prince also has a fascinating world, a simple but effective magic system, a grand sense of adventure, and some highly emotional moments. The seventh season wrapped everything up perfectly and proved that the showrunners never lost their mojo throughout its run, not even for a second.

His Dark Materials is the second attempt at adapting the novel trilogy by Philip Pullman, following a disastrous movie effort in 2008, which halted all chances of a sequel. This modern HBO effort was much better and was far more faithful to the book. Playing into themes of the multiverse, magic, and mythological creatures that impressed long-time fans of the original work.

The plot concerns a young girl living in an alternate version of England, who is the key to undermining and dismantling a shadowy corporation that runs much of the known world. She is accompanied by her dæmon, a small shape-shifting creature who serves as a sort of animal companion for her. His Dark Materials is clearly crafted with love, as the showrunners hid a ton of Easter eggs that only book readers will pick up on. The final season was not a cancellation, but rather the point that there was no more source material left to cover: all three books had been adapted. This means its conclusion is well-rounded and complete, as well as utterly amazing.

Shadow and Bone is based on the novels by Leigh Bardugo, which have a sizable fanbase. Unfortunately, it wasn't sizable enough to sustain the multi-million-dollar budget, resulting in the show's cancellation just two seasons in, ending on a cliffhanger, no less. This cancellation was not received well by fans, who actually adored it for how it embodied the atmosphere and humour of the original novels.

Rather than being a Medieval-themed fantasy, which the genre tends to favour, it went a little more steampunk, feeling more like Victorian England, instead. This ensured the show had a distinct identity separate from other fantasy TV shows. It's full of witty writing, heartfelt moments, and a shockingly detailed world for what was originally a YA series. The second and final season cranked things up to 11, doing everything just right, even if it didn't attract the numbers it needed.

Avatar: The Last Airbender is a classic Nicktoon that completely threw out the typical Nickelodeon formula. Instead of being a comedy with zero continuity, it was a distinct, beginning-to-end adventure, although it definitely had its comedic moments. As many know by now, the story is about four nations, each representing the elements of air, fire, earth, and water, which are in turmoil following a widespread invasion from the Fire Nation.

It is prophesied that an Avatar will rise, learn to "bend" all the elements, and bring harmony to the land. With an aesthetic inspired by Japanese anime and Medieval East Asia, the series was acclaimed for its profound moral wisdom, light-hearted humour, and action-packed antics. This show had everything going for it, including an explosive finale that never gets old, no matter how many years have gone by.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

Arcane is an animated series based on the popular MOBA video game League of Legends. The universe of the game has always blended elements of sci-fi, fantasy, and steampunk, and the show tends to follow in its footsteps. Ordinarily, video game adaptations are seen as shameless cash grabs, though Arcane was quite the opposite. It was actually created by some of the game developers and was crafted with such care that it even won multiple Emmys.

Arcane is only two seasons long, but it still became a major television event, with its final season becoming a highly anticipated entry in Netflix's library. Don't be fooled by the two seasons thing, though--it didn't get cancelled, and it was willingly brought to a proper close by the showrunners. This meant the final season wasn't rushed, and was crafted just as carefully as the first, making for an action-packed adventure that was surprisingly moving.

What We Do in the Shadows is a series created by Taika Waititi, based on his 2014 movie of the same name. Filmed in a mockumentary style, the idea is simple: what if a bunch of classic Gothic European vampires were abruptly thrust into 21st Century New York City? The result is every bit as absurd and horrifying as you could possibly imagine.

Concluding after six whole seasons, the horror/fantasy/comedy reached a proper conclusion that left very few, if any, loose ends. As it ended, it retained much of the same flair and quirky humour that made fans fall in love with it in the first place, proving that its final season could be just as good, if not better than its first.

The Twilight Zone is one of the most revolutionary television series ever made. Created by Rod Serling, this anthology series describes itself as featuring ordinary people in extraordinary circumstances. It tends to blur the line between genres, featuring episodes that stray into sci-fi, fantasy, and horror. Some are about aliens or robots, while some feature ghosts or demonic entities. Each episode has its own unique concept, cast, plot twist, and self-contained storyline, so no two episodes are alike.

After five seasons, the show came to an end, yet it felt like it could have gone on forever. Whether the writers were running out of ideas or not, The Twilight Zone maintained its momentum throughout its five-year run, never once dipping in quality or producing anything short of perfection. As such, the final season for this show is just as brilliant as the rest of it.

Kingdom is a South Korean Netflix series based on a webtoon-turned-graphic novel. Set at the end of the Middle Ages, the show features the historic kingdom of Joseon as it is plunged into chaos. This is due to the tyrannical Haewon Cho Clan making a violent bid for the throne by using a mythical resurrection plant, unleashing a zombie horde in the process. Now, the current royalty has to fight with their competitors on top of an army of the undead.

Kingdom received two full seasons plus one feature-length special episode before being soft-cancelled. Even though it's doubtful it will ever come back at this point (which is made even more infuriating by the fact that it ended on a cliffhanger), that doesn't change the fact that each and every episode is flawless. This fantasy/horror is absolutely singular in atmosphere, and brought a whole bunch of interesting concepts to the table. The final season is even better than the first, and is pretty much perfect in every sense of the word.

Based on the novel series by the late Robert Jordan, The Wheel of Time was unceremoniously cancelled right as it hit its stride. The first season was average, the second was good, but not great, and the third was absolutely stellar. This is probably why its abrupt cancellation prompted a massive online fan campaign to try and save it, which is a response that was quite unexpected.

Story-wise, the show is about a chosen one named the Dragon Reborn who is destined to either save the world or destroy it. The show had its own distinct identity, bringing Jordan's fascinating world to life in a way that nobody had seen before, and absolutely nailed the casting. It had such a unique vibe too, one that is completely unmatched by other fantasy shows. It's honestly such a shame that The Wheel of Time never got to see a proper conclusion, but at least its third and final season ensures it went out on a high note.
