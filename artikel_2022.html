“Planning approval secured” : Stadium twist could change West Ham’s ownership plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

West Ham United’s window of opportunity to acquire their rented home for next to nothing appears to be fading. With £20 million of losses each year, the ground is a massive white elephant with the main tenant paying next to nothing for it’s bullet proof lease.

Former vice Chair Karren Brady, say what you like about her,  did at least negotiate the deal of the century giving West Ham a rock solid indestructible tenancy for generations to come. As the club were relegated, so the rent diminished leaving the owners to pick up the tab for West Ham’s failure on the pitch: An estimated £2.5 million each year is added to the tax payer burden thanks to the clause existing to reduce costs when West Ham dropped out of the Premier League.

Opportunities existed this summer, so we were told,  for the club to negotiate to buy the stadium, with suggestions that it would take £50 million for the deal to be done.

In the apparent lack of any take up by West Ham’s shareholders, the stadium owners have sought to bump up the former Olympic venue’s value in the meantime, by seeking to increase revenues through obtaining permission from Newham Council for a wider array of entertainment. Meaning the revenue which can be generated from the Stadium will be significantly higher, theoretically at least increasing its value and likely heading off West Ham’s options of acquiring it for next to nothing. Possibly.

West Ham’s London Stadium at night: There’ll be more events, louder music and bigger attendances – still won’t make a profit though. Just saying.

However the huge maintenance costs and temporary nature of the upper part of the structure means that even with consent for more events, bigger attendances and louder music, London Stadium is never going to become a lucrative, marketable venue in it’s current configuration.

Someone is going to have to spend hundreds of millions of pounds on fixing it: Like a giant pass-the-parcel game with a huge ticking time bomb of debt at the core of the fancy wrapping, neither West Ham nor the owners want to be left holding the bill when the music stops.

The difference is, the status quo favours the club, not the Greater London Authority who, as it stands, will find it pretty near impossible to turn a profit even with new approvals granted and new events lined up. And as every year passes, the need to spend big, big money on maintenance grows greater. In this instance, West Ham’s board biding their time and kicking it into the long grass for a few years yet is probably the shrewdest course of action. From the football club’s perspective.
