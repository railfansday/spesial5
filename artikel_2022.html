Lewis Hall is the best English left-back by a country mile, Lewis Hall is the best left-back in the Premier League, Lewis Hall is going to join Manchester United in January 2027.

As we were reminded in this past week of internationals, Lewis Hall is indeed the Premier League’s and England’s best left-back.

The lifelong Newcastle United fan was the best player on the pitch when England defeated Croatia at Wembley on Thursday.

Then on Sunday he was up there as once again one of England’s very best players as they won 3-2 against World Cup holders Spain, with Lewis Hall outclassing their most dangerous player, Lamine Yamal.

So of course the natural outcome is that Lewis Hall will now sign for Manchester United in January…

Twenty years ago, Manchester United had won eight Premier League titles in the previous 14 seasons and never finished lower than third across that time period.

Back in November 2006, Manchester United were well on their way to reclaiming the Premier League title, they would win three in a row and five in seven, finishing runners-up in the other two.

From the 1991-92 season to the 2012-13 season, those 22 seasons saw Manchester United win 13 titles, six times runners-up, three times finishing third.

These last 13 seasons Manchester United have not won a single Premier League title, eight of the 13 they have finished outside the top three.

The last three Premier League seasons Manchester United have included a 15th place and an 8th place one and are currently sitting in the relegation zone after only one Premier League win in the last six months.

So of course Lewis Hall is desperate to join Manchester United…

The lifelong Newcastle United fan was eight years old when Man U last lifted the Premier League title.

Exactly why thirteen and a half years later would the best left-back in the Premier League would have even minimal interest  in signing for Manchester United?

Yet this is what the Manchester United fans, the Manchester United loving media, as well as other desperate idiots, want us all to believe.

Funnily enough, none of them give any reasons as to why this should be the case.

Nothing logical anyway, nothing that makes sense.

The best they would come out with is something along the lines of…”Well, it is Manchester United.”

Which ironically hits the nail squarely on the head, it is Manchester United.

It isn’t one of the successful clubs, it is one that used to be successful many years ago, during the first eight years Lewis Hall was on this earth, not the near decade and a half that has followed.

Lewis Hall now has the world at his feet and will have choices to make.

Will he stay forever at the club he and his family have supported all of their lives, or will he at some point join a club that appears to have a lot more chance of winning trophies?

Neither of those options feature Manchester United.

Yet I have seen so many journalists and pundits yet again embarrass themselves, after his stellar performances for England, talk about Lewis Hall set to move to Manchester United. Rather than talk about just how good he is for Newcastle United and vital to this current season (and hopefully beyond).

As an extra amusing aside, we also have the following…

On 29 May 2023 it was announced that Mauricio Pochettino would be appointed as head coach of Chelsea on 1 July 2023, on a two-year contract with an option to extend for another year.

On 22 August 2023, Lewis Hall joined Newcastle United on a season-long loan with an obligation to buy for £28 million, plus £7 million in add-ons (On 1 November, he scored his first goal for Newcastle United in a 3-0 League Cup win against Manchester United at Old Trafford. Then on 15 May 2024, Lewis Hall scored his first ever Premier League goal for United against Man U at Old Trafford…).

Amusingly, the new Manchester United boss Mauricio Pochettino was the Chelsea boss who sold Lewis Hall to Newcastle United. Pochettino selling Hall without even first playing him in a competitive match.

Of course, the reality is that at basket cases clubs Chelsea and Manchester United, the manager/head coach has no say in which players are bought or sold. So Mauricio Pochettino had no say on whether Chelsea sold Lewis Hall back in August 2023, just the same as Mauricio Pochettino will have no say in January 2027 if Manchester United embarrass themselves with an approach to try and ‘tempt’ (and fail, obviously) Lewis Hall to join Sir Jim Ratcliffe’s Old Trafford circus.
