Joel Piroe’s admission sums up what most West Ham fans are feeling this season

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Not everyone, it seems, is mossing the ‘glitz and glamour’ that participation in the Premier League claims to bring. Higher profiles, more ‘tourist fans’, tougher games, regular pastings by the top six – it is most definitely a double edged sword.

From hundreds of comments on our readers’ section in the summer, West Ham United fans actively looked forward to a season of winning more games in the Championship and hopefully dominating the league after two mentally exhausting seasons at the foot of the Premier League, living with the spectre of relegation seemingly for six months of the year.

The absence of VAR’s infuriating, pointless and often incorrect interference in games has been one of the massive bonuses of Championship football

And of course, the reality of relegation is very different from the perception: Life doesn’t stop when the team you love is booted out of the top flight. And it seems from a players’ perspective, it isn’t all bad either. West Ham’s Joel Piroe, reported by sportwitness.co.uk, has been waxing lyrical as to the benefits of Championship football and how it is tougher than many other European top tiers:

“It’s difficult to explain. Everyone knows the Premier League is considered the best league in the world,” he told VoetbalPrimeur.

“On the other hand, the Championship has the same intensity and the same tempo as the Premier League. It’s much closer to that level than the Eredivisie is.”

…And there is no VAR so it is more rewarding for players and a better, more entertaining spectacle for supporters….. he could have added.

And of course, there’s the proviso that the Championship is all well and good – providing your club gets out of it before financial armageddon condemns it to permanent exile from the top tier.

He is right, the EPL is the best league in the world, and he is also admitting that he is one of the many that is just not good enough to play in it. In fact, in Joel Piroe’s case he has not yet shown that he is overly comfortable in the Championship. As for the VAR discussion, yes, it is frustrating waiting for man to interpret the moving pictures, but without VAR we have just as many, if not more, errors of judgement by referees who do not see the truth. The reason we get mad is because we have the benefit of slow motion action replay and they don’t. Ironically, that is VAR.
