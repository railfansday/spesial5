All 8 Episodes Of Prime Video's Already-Renewed Neo-Western Are Perfect From Start To Finish

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Amazon can’t seem to help itself from spinning off its biggest hit shows into prequels, sequels, and offshoots. Bosch has been spun off into Bosch: Legacy; The Terminal List has been spun off into The Terminal List: Dark Wolf; Hazbin Hotel has been spun off into Helluva Boss. But it can be forgiven, because these spinoffs usually end up being worthwhile. The Boys’ animated spinoff Diabolical is one of the only anthology shows without a single weak episode, and its college-set spinoff Gen V showed us a whole new side of that universe, with a cast of characters worth investing in.

Earlier this year, Reacher debuted its own spinoff, Neagley, and not only was it a satisfying expansion of the franchise; it may have even joined Frasier and Better Call Saul in the ranks of superior spinoffs that outshine their predecessor. After Prime Video released Reacher’s season 4 finale, it came with the entire first season of Frances Neagley’s spinoff.

Neagley is Jack Reacher’s closest confidant and ally, and she’d shown up to help him save the day in every previous season of Reacher, but she was absent from season 4, because she was being promoted to series lead. While Reacher was fighting political corruption and international assassins, Neagley was fighting a sinister cult in Chicago.

Neagley follows a pretty predictable plot, but it’s a worthwhile ride. It’s well-paced, agreeably action-packed, and stuffed with great actors, including Sten herself, who gets to bring more depth and dramatic weight to the character here than she ever did in her supporting roles on Reacher. The title character in Neagley is every bit the stone-cold badass she was in her previous appearances, but when she takes center stage in her own show, she gets to dig a little deeper into the vulnerability and insecurity and fear being masked by that badass facade.

The series opens with Neagley’s childhood friend stepping in front of a train. His death looks suspicious, as does his recent residency with a local cult, so Neagley jumps on the case, determined to get to the bottom of it. From there, you’re hooked. If you’ve seen the last four seasons of Reacher, you know Neagley and her friends will eventually have enough evidence to storm the cult’s compound and blast them to kingdom come, but there’s a lot of great character drama and riveting action set-pieces on the way to that inevitable climax.

Reacher is one of the most formulaic shows on television, and that’s what we love about it. In an age of subversive, serialized TV dramas, Reacher is a refreshingly old-school procedural about a noble hero drifting from town to town, serving vigilante justice. Every season, you know what you’re getting — a new town, a new threat, a new supporting cast, etc. — and there hasn’t been a weak installment yet, but Neagley may have beaten Reacher at its own game.

Neagley follows the Reacher formula to the letter. A personal conflict draws the hero into a dark conspiracy, she amasses a merry band of misfit allies including a love interest and a plucky kid to mentor, and she follows a series of clues and shootouts to an explosive climactic showdown. It satisfies as a Reacher-style action-adventure, but it goes above and beyond the expectations of that formula.

Reacher famously never changes, but Neagley actually evolved as a character throughout the first season of her spinoff. She confronted her demons, overcame her haphephobia, and embraced all her new friends as a found family. Neagley was a much darker, weirder show than Reacher, but it also hit on a deeper emotional level.
