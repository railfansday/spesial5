The best career fields for the next decade pair fast projected job growth with high pay, mostly in health care, technology, and business. Before you train for one, weigh its pay against the years and tuition its schooling takes, since a longer program doesn’t guarantee a bigger paycheck.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects nurse practitioner jobs to grow 41% from 2025 to 2035, the fastest of any occupation, and the job pays a median $132,300 a year.

Getting there takes years of school, as it does in every field below, and more years in school don’t reliably mean more pay. Industrial engineers and physical therapists are both projected to grow 12% and earn nearly the same median, $102,440 and $102,760. Engineers start with a bachelor’s degree, while physical therapists add a three-year doctorate after one.

All 20 fields are projected to grow at least 7% over the decade, which the BLS glossary counts as much faster than average. Each also pays a median of at least $82,000. Pay is each field’s median for May 2025, so half its workers earn more and half earn less.

Eight of the 20 are in health care, and all but one of them need a graduate degree.

Four of these six start with a bachelor’s degree, and data scientists have the fastest projected growth on the list after nurse practitioners.

All three start with a bachelor’s degree, though the best-paid of them also takes years of experience.

These three share no industry, but each pays a median above $90,000 and starts with a bachelor’s degree.

Start with the school each field takes. A bachelor’s degree is the usual entry point for 11 of these 20, though financial managers and airline pilots also need experience first. Six call for a master’s degree, and three for a doctorate or a professional degree such as veterinary medicine.

Employer tuition aid and free training programs can cover part of a program’s cost before you borrow. If a four-year degree is more than you want, several high-paying jobs don’t require one.

For any field that isn’t on this list, look up its pay and job outlook. Also weigh which jobs hold up best in a recession and how much of a job’s work AI could take over.

Count the jobs the growth adds as well. Statisticians’ 11% projected growth adds about 3,400 jobs over the decade, while management analysts’ 10% adds about 109,200.

Then, for each field on your shortlist, add up the years of school past high school, price each year as its tuition plus the pay you’d have earned working instead, and set that total against the field’s median pay.
