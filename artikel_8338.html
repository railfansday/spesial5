Day 29: The Magic Before the Event

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Though I knew I’d need rest for my upcoming all-nighter, my body was ready to get started.

The day began with a short 17-kilometer walk to Astorga, a little warm-up for what was to come at night.

Even though I left at 6:30, I was surprised to see daylight begin almost right away. Daylight saving time went into effect the night before, so sunrise and sunset happened an hour earlier than I was used to.

I chose an alternative route leaving the town I slept in. Once again it was longer, but to me, the peace was worth it.

The sunrise was impeccable. And as the sky lit up with an array of colors, I heard the most lovely music coming from the crest of the hill I was climbing. There, I found a little shack that was a donation-based cafe. Over The Hill was run by a kind, Portuguese man who worked with his extremely affectionate dog by his side. The love, the view, the atmosphere, the sunrise—it was all so precious.

My Italian brother caught up to me there, and from then on we walked together.

I found a farm with baby cows in their pens. As one of my favorite animals, I ran up and gave one a kiss. It made me completely overjoyed. I skipped away squealing. Today was already so perfect.

To add even more to the magic of the day, my Italian brother and I stumbled upon El Jardín del Alma. It was my favorite type of atmosphere and decoration.

To me, I knew they each held spiritual significance. To be in a place that mirrored my beliefs felt like home.

Not to mention the giant table of fruit, veggies, crackers, eggs, cheese, coffee, and so many other foods, all self-service and donativo.

I grabbed a coffee, banana, and peanut butter, and I was in heaven.

While we sat there, my Italian brother told me that he decided he is not going to drink alcohol until Santiago. He shook my hand as an act of accountability, and I realized I loved the idea and wanted to vow to it as well.

Once we reached the city of Astorga, we split up on our own adventures.

I made my way to the magnificent structures of Palacio de Gaudí and Catedral de Santa María de Astorga. Directly next to each other, I sat down in the courtyard, took off my shoes, and took in the architecture around me while the bells chimed.

When I entered the cathedral, I paid €8 and started the tour with the audio guide. Turns out, the first several rooms of the building were a museum.

As an artist, I could appreciate the craftsmanship of the magnificent clothing, wooden sculptures, precious metal embellishments, and other works.

One legend I’d learned about was depicted in a painting.

The translation of the painting’s description stated that she was “a pagan and wicked woman who ruled Galicia.” She denied the apostles permission to give a place to bury Saint James until a miraculous series of events occurred and “she repented, became a Christian, and transformed her palace into a church.”

To me, this was symbolic of the traditions that were here before and the Christian faith that was built on top.

That directly correlated to the place I planned to go for sunrise on the next day, Cruz del Ferro. More to come about that later though.

Once I got into the cathedral, I didn’t want to listen to the audio anymore. I’d rather experience the art than listen to it.

What I felt in those walls wasn’t like León. It felt heavy.

I sat and considered meditating, but felt like I needed to leave.

Because of the experiences in those cathedrals, though, I knew I could appreciate the art and history without needing to put a story to it.

I made my way to meet the dads. They had an apartment for the night, and we all bought food in previous towns that we carried so we could make dinner together. In the lavish penthouse, I kicked up my feet and recharged to prepare myself for my all-nighter.

The brothers came to the apartment to join us for a homemade spaghetti dinner.

Having my Camino dads and my Camino brothers all in one place, sharing a homemade meal together, felt like it couldn’t get any better.

When my Argentinian brother arrived, though, he told me that he was going to do the all-nighter with me. The idea had been planted by a conversation he and I had two weeks ago on the New Moon. We were in Logroño, the first day I met him, and he said he wanted to do a hike throughout the night without a headlamp. I told him the date of the full moon, which had now arrived. That day planted a seed. Now, with a strong friendship, we got to embark on this venture together.

Even with two cups of coffee after our pasta dinner together, my eyes were aching with exhaustion.

But at 10:54, my Argentine brother and I left the apartment and set out on our night walk to Cruz del Ferro….
