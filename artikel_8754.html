Balanced Cushioning, which refers to the shoe’s heel-to-toe drop, or lack thereof. Altra designs most of its shoes with no drop, meaning your foot sits flat rather than sloping downward from heel to toe. According to Altra, this can help promote your natural stride and encourage a lower-impact landing—because the heel isn’t as beefed up, you’re less likely to land on it.

Altra also introduced its 4mm line of shoes so runners can ease into zero-drop with an offset that’s approximately two stacked nickels.

The first time I ran in Altras, I couldn’t help feeling like I was wearing clown shoes. Unbox a pair of Altras and you’ll see that the forefoot is much wider than on other shoes; the enlarged, anatomical toe box allows the foot to expand and gives the toes room to splay. Altra now has three different foot shapes: original (the widest), standard, and slim (the narrowest).

Wide-foot runners and runners with bunions appreciate the roominess in the toe area. But all runners can benefit from wearing Altra’s foot-shaped trainers. A narrow toebox that cramps your toes can negatively affect your performance (and set you up for injuries).

The company uses three main cushioning materials in its shoes: Ego, Ego Max, and Ego Pro.

Ego: Compared to traditional EVA, this foam provides a springier feel and more durability.

Ego Max: A foam made of compression-molded EVA and tuned for a softer feel than Ego, but it isn’t as responsive.

Ego Pro: Altra’s top-shelf foam made of thermoplastic elastomers (TPE) formed in a supercritical fluid process. It’s the brand’s lightest and most responsive cushioning material.

P35X: Altra’s new and improved version of Ego foam that’s lighter and more efficient.

Altra’s stability tech is its guide rail, which is a layer of dense midsole foam that is raised on the medial side. The guide rail serves as a “bumper” that helps counter overpronation during your stride.

Altra’s women’s shoes are built on a women’s-specific last to match the biomechanics of female feet. These shoes are narrower through the heel and midfoot and better accommodate women’s generally longer arches.

We recommend starting slow and short if it’s your first time running in zero-drop shoes. Shoes with a drop have more cushioning in the heel. If you’re a heel-striker, you may need to run in a zero-drop shoe with a higher stack height to provide more support under the heel area. Try Altra’s 4mm shoes first to become accustomed to a lower-drop shoe.

What is the difference between Altra’s guide rail and Brooks’s guide rails?

Altra’s guide rail runs along the medial side of the shoe, acting as a bumper and preventing your foot from collapsing during sharp turns or if your feet roll inward during your gait cycle (overpronation).

Brooks’s guide rails are on the medial and lateral sides of the shoe. Like Altra’s guide rail, the guide rails also provide stability when you need it, making the shoes supportive for overpronators and neutral runners. The guide rails act as bumpers, helping align the knee and ankle.
