I Always Choose Droptail Bibs for Long Rides. These Black Friday Deals Make the Best Ones Easier to Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Top deals on droptail bib shorts, cargo bibs, and tights that solve a big women’s cycling kit problem.

Gear-obsessed editors choose every product we review. We may earn commission if you buy from a link. How we test gear.

The best women’s cycling clothing doesn’t come cheap. Despite the cost, high-quality bib shorts and winter tights are worth their weight in gold, especially when they fit well and are comfortable. For long rides, I’m a big fan of droptail-style bibs. These specially designed women’s shorts make nature breaks quicker; you don’t need to take off your jersey or jacket. The feature is particularly handy on cold-weather rides.

Unfortunately, some of the best-performing droptail bib shorts and tights often cost more than $300. Luckily, many clothing brands and retailers are running great Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals this year—and it’s a great way to save big on some of our favorite models from the best names in bike apparel.

For the uninitiated, droptails are a style of cycling bib shorts that pull down at the rear (hence the name). Unlike traditional bib shorts, droptail models let cyclists who don’t pee standing up to use the bathroom without removing their kit.

Droptail bibs entered cyclists’ gear bags 15 years ago when Pearl Izumi introduced its first models “designed to help female riders take easier nature breaks without sacrificing the benefits of bib shorts.” In the decade and a half since, other cycling clothing companies—Velocio, Ornot, Rapha, Capo, and others—have released their spin on the droptail concept.

For roughly half the population, droptail bibs are a game-changer, and several cycling clothing brands incorporated the design into their women’s bib shorts. Some droptail designs use stretchy elastic straps or panels, while others use magnetic or plastic clasps.

It’s a super useful feature; without it, riders wearing bibs must remove their jersey and jacket (and other layers over the bib straps) for sit-down bathroom breaks. And the last thing you want is to fumble with zippers on a cold day, get wet taking off layers in the rain, or have your pricey jersey fall on a dirty gas station floor. I almost always pick droptail bibs over models without the feature when I shop for kits—it’s simply that useful.

Cycle apparel companies offer droptail bib shorts, riding tights, or cargo bibs (shorts with pockets). Each brand below currently has sale prices on at least one droptail model—some have several styles available. These prices won’t last long, and some sizes and colors may already be sold out.

Pearl Izumi is well-known for its entry- and mid-tier cycling clothing. The brand’s riding apparel is popular for its solid performance for the price. Pearl Izumi’s droptail design uses stretchy fabric with a front clasp.

Scotland-based clothing brand Endura makes high-performance cycling kit that works well in especially wet and gritty conditions—like gravel, mountain biking, bikepacking, and commuting. Endura calls its droptail design “DropSeat,” and it uses a stretch-band design.

While Pearl Izumi created the first droptail bibs, Velocio popularized the feature when it launched its brand. Now owned by component company SRAM, Velocio’s FlyFree droptail system uses stretch-bands with specifically cut panels. The brand uses FlyFree across its range of women’s bib shorts, cycling tights, and 3/4-length bibs.

Ornot is a small clothing company that makes some of my favorite droptail tights and bibs. The San Francisco-based brand’s products fit great and are high-quality. I especially like Ornot’s lightweight tights—they’re perfect for days when you know you won’t take off the leg warmers, but thermal tights are overkill. Ornot’s droptail system uses stretchy panels and straps.

Capo is a small brand that offers custom team apparel and a selection of performance-focused cycling clothing. Capo’s Super Corsa is lightweight and great for hot days outside or on the trainer. Its Italian-made droptail bibs and cargo bibs use stretchy panels and straps.

Performance apparel company Rapha is known for its highly functional, often pricey, understated cycling clothing. It offers two droptail systems: bibs with a clasp (“Detachable”) and cargo bibs and tights with high-stretch uppers.

Assos makes premium-level, race-inspired cycling clothing. It’s certainly not cheap, but it performs extremely well. The Swiss company makes one droptail bib model with a dual-clasp design.
