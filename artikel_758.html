Has Verstappen feud reignited at wrong time for Russell?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The feud between George Russell and Max Verstappen reignited in spectacular style in Las Vegas following a clash between the pair in practice; watch the Qatar Grand Prix live on Sky Sports this weekend, with qualifying at 6pm on Saturday and the race at 4pm on Sunday

The reignition of the long-running feud between George Russell and Max Verstappen has added an entertaining twist to the end of the 2026 Formula 1 season, but it may not be what the Mercedes driver needed at this moment.

Russell and Verstappen clashed during final practice at the Las Vegas Grand Prix when the Red Bull driver, slightly cheekily, snatched track position from his Mercedes rival as they both prepared for push laps.

Irked by Verstappen's actions, which he later said he felt breached a gentleman's agreement the drivers share, Russell attempted to reclaim track position from the weaving Red Bull, with the pair making slight contact.

The stewards summoned the two drivers following the session as they conducted an investigation, before handing Verstappen a warning.

The incident came less than a month on from Verstappen being involved a similar incident with Racing Bulls driver Liam Lawson during practice at the Mexico City Grand Prix, which resulted in both drivers being given one-place grid penalties.

Speaking to Sky Italy later that evening, Russell said: "There's nothing in the rules to say you cannot overtake someone who is starting a lap but when you do this, you are screwing this person over and he does this week in, week out with many drivers and everybody accepts it.

"I'm not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they're bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody's got to stand up to that guy."

Russell's comments came after the result of qualifying in Las Vegas dictated that the pair would start together on the front row, with Verstappen ahead in pole position.

That setup a Saturday night showdown, in which the pair engaged in a thrilling battle for the lead for much of the race, before Russell dropped behind Kimi Antonelli in the latter stages and then drove into a barrier attempting to pass his Mercedes team-mate.

Verstappen reacted on team radio with apparent glee having seen Russell's error in his mirrors, and then, after closing out a superb victory, further stuck the boot into the British driver during the post-race press conference for the top three.

The four-time world champion said: "The thing is, he speaks a lot next to the track. And honestly, for me, it doesn't really bother me because he's so far below my level that it doesn't really make any sense for me anyway to try and challenge that. So, it doesn't bother me.

"And today, karma is a b****. It was honestly fantastic when I looked in my mirror and I saw what was happening [when Russell crashed]. I was like, 'fantastic, my day is only getting better.' And of course, when I then crossed the line in first, my day was complete."

With less than five months separating them in age, Russell and Verstappen have been racing each other in karting since they were children.

While Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff reflected in Las Vegas that the feud may have stretched back to those days, the first notable F1 flashpoint between the pair came in the 2023 Azerbaijan Sprint when they made contact through several corners on the opening lap.

In a post-race exchange broadcast to the world, Verstappen barked at Russell to "expect next time the same" and swore at the Mercedes driver as he walked away. He would later refer to Russell as "princess George" in an interview with the Dutch media.

A spectacular fall out between the pair then occurred towards the end of the 2024 season, after Verstappen felt Russell had played an unreasonably key role in the Red Bull driver being given a grid penalty that cost him pole for the Qatar Grand Prix.

Verstappen said in Qatar that he had "lost all respect" for Russell, who then responded with a remarkable explosive verbal attack on the Dutchman the following week at the season-ending Abu Dhabi Grand Prix.

Russell said Verstappen had threatened to put him "in the wall" in Qatar, and said that "people have been bullied by Max for years". Verstappen questioned the accuracy of Russell's claims and labelled him a "loser" and "backstabber".

The pair clashed once more on track at the 2025 Spanish Grand Prix when Verstappen, enraged at his own team for instructing him to let Russell through to avoid receiving a penalty, appeared to wave the Mercedes past before lunging into the side of it.

Verstappen was penalised by the stewards, later admitted the move was "not right" and apologised to his team, but did not personally address Russell.

Less talked about than the pair's clashes is a potential further tension added by Russell's boss Wolff having very publicly tried to sign Verstappen from Red Bull. Russell told Sky Sports F1 in June 2025 that his own contract extension was being delayed by Mercedes' attempts to sign Verstappen.

The previous clashes between the pair came when Russell was in a position of strength at Mercedes, given he had performed hugely impressively during his first four seasons with the team from 2022 to 2025.

Russell had expressed belief on many occasions over the years that if Mercedes delivered a title-winning car, he was ready to grab the opportunity, and few doubted him.

However, when they finally produced such a car following the major regulation changes ahead of the 2026 season, it was his far less-experienced team-mate Antonelli who snatched away what Russell thought was going to be his title.

Russell has been tormented by his struggles throughout the season, repeatedly expressing uncertainty over what was causing his deficit to Antonelli before eventually appearing to decide that he must alter his driving style to be able to match the 20-year-old Italian in the sport's latest machinery.

If there was any doubt over how painful the season had been, it was crystalised by Wolff's decision to allow the Brit to leave the track in Mexico almost immediately after the race when Antonelli sealed the title, which the Austrian said was because he found Russell "distraught".

It was one thing Russell 'standing up' to Verstappen when he could claim that given a strong car, he would rival the Dutchman, but the way the 2026 season has played out has left Russell vulnerable, a reality which Verstappen attempted to exploit with his "not on my level" remarks.

Not only has Russell been comprehensively outperformed by Antonelli, but he has now fallen to fifth in the drivers' standings, with Verstappen's four wins in the last six races having put him firmly on course for second place.

While Mercedes have proven in the past that they will allow both their drivers to fight for championships, there is no doubt that Antonelli has made himself the team's clear top dog for the tie being.

Given both championships have already been decided, the Russell-Verstappen feud is going to attract huge attention at the final two rounds of the season in Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

The best-case scenario for Russell across the season-ending triple header was to put together some strong results and enter the winter with some moment to take into 2027.

Verstappen, who has finished on the podium at nine successive races, and his own team-mate Antonelli, were always likely to be the biggest obstacles to that.

While Verstappen insisted in Las Vegas that he wasn't motivated by Russell's remarks, his comments on radio and after the race suggested there was some extra fire in his belly, which may well remain there in the Middle East.

The sporting challenge of claiming a victory may have stiffened, but Russell has also heightened his emotional load by putt himself back in the spotlight at a time where the relative calm offered by the titles having been decided may have been welcomed.

The drivers have in recent years established a tradition of all going for dinner one night at the final round in Abu Dhabi, where Verstappen famously offered Russell a seat next to him just days after their war of words in 2024.

While Russell won't want to completely back down after Verstappen's post-race comments in Las Vegas, a defusal of the situation might be the best thing for him.

Whether he takes that approach will become clear during Thursday's media sessions in Qatar, during which both drivers will speak to the press.
