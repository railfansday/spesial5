Millwall first club hit with partial closure over discriminatory chants

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Millwall have been punished for discriminatory chanting relating to disability and sexual orientation by fans in a game against Charlton in January; a two-game partial stadium closure (one game of which is suspended) is first of its kind at this level; Millwall also fined £70,000

Senior Football Journalist @psmithXI

Millwall have become the first club in the top four divisions of English football to be punished with a partial stadium closure for discriminatory chanting after a regulatory commission proved two FA charges.

The unprecedented measure is for Millwall failing to ensure their supporters didn't engage in discriminatory chanting that includes reference to disability and sexual orientation during a home match with Charlton in January.

As well as a two-game partial stadium closure - one game of which is suspended until the end of the 2027/28 season - Millwall have been fined £70,000 plus hearing costs, warned about their future conduct, and have had their FA Action Plan extended until the end of next season.

Blocks 19, 20, 47 and 48 of the Dockers Stand will be closed when Millwall host Sheffield United on December 8 - a total of 1,274 seats.

In their written conclusions, the commission found "the club's degree of culpability in relation to the incidents was significant".

In response, speaking exclusively to Sky Sports, Millwall managing director Mark Fairbrother said: "As a club, we didn't do enough and on behalf of the club I apologise to everyone who has been affected by that chanting because it shouldn't happen."

Millwall pleaded guilty to the charges but Fairbrother said Millwall were "disappointed" with the outcome.

He also pointed to new processes the club have since put in place to try to avoid a recurrence - but called on other football clubs, the FA, and organisations such as Kick It Out to come together to find preventative solutions.

"We challenge everything we do and look at if there are ways that we can improve? It's highlighted where we need to be better," he said. "We had already put mitigations in place and need to continue to do so.

"But this is one football club. These instances are happening across football. This is a challenge for the sport in general.

"We see it this [past] week with the charges to Chelsea and also the FA for the England fans in Spain. So, we can put measures in place but we're one club. We can't solve all of society's issues.

"We need to work with all key football stakeholders to look at how can we all do this better? What does good look like? What does best practice look like? And making sure that we embrace that. But everyone else has a collective responsibility as well."

Fairbrother says "the stick approach" used against clubs whose supporters are found guilty of discriminatory chanting just "punishes the fans who actually are just here to enjoy themselves". Instead, he asks "what can we do in terms of the carrot approach to educate people to eradicate this?"

He also noted that supporters identified in the discriminatory chanting against Charlton were all under 18 years old.

"That shows a lack of understanding of the implication of these words," he said. "These words obviously hurt and they have a significant impact on those groups that it affects. But there's that lack of understanding of the issue and the impact."

Asked if he would be interested in working more with groups such as Kick It Out to address this aspect, Fairbrother said: "I'd embrace it. From a personal perspective, I think that would be really good and I implore them to please come work with us, come and look at what we do, challenge what we do.

"If you don't think something we're doing is good enough, come and tell us but look at solutions. If we all have a solutions-focused mindset, we can actually start to make real difference."

The regulatory commission also wrote in their conclusions that "the reputation of the club and English football would have been tarnished" by the chanting during a game shown live on Sky Sports. Fairbrother feels that.

"It hurts all of us in the club. But I know it hurts our fans as well because that is not who we are," he said.

Millwall play Charlton again at the Den on December 19 in a Championship fixture. On how the club are working to avoid a repeat of the discriminatory chanting heard in January, Fairbrother said: "We've put in place a number of procedures already.

"We've already had West Ham here earlier in the season, which was probably our highest-risk fixture for a number of years. That went without incident. But we had a different approach to different fan groups in terms of just reminding them of their responsibility.

"But also for us as a club, making sure that we're on the front foot in terms of everything we do. We will have that same approach to Charlton. You can't predict the future, but we have a more robust process in place now."

This latest issue was the first case of discriminatory chanting at the Den since an FA Cup tie with Everton in 2019 - but follows three other cases involving Millwall supporters away from home since April 2023.

Asked whether he is confident of making a difference, Fairbrother said that would require the support of Millwall fans to call out discriminatory language.

"For each of those instances - and all three of them were away games - there were specific small fan groups who obviously engaged with that chanting. This is now the first instance we've had here at the Den," he said.

"So for all fans, we need to check and challenge this. We need to make sure it doesn't build up. Don't engage with this type of chanting.

"It's not fair on [other Millwall supporters]. We need to all do better. As a club, we need to do better and we will do better for them. But also, it's a collective one. If it happens, if people see it, call it out. We all need to be more proactive on this.

"I'm proud to represent Millwall and I think it goes against what this club's really about."

Sky Sports News has contacted the FA and Kick It Out for comment
