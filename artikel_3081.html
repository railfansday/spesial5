Palo Alto Networks: Accelerating Growth and a $4.5 Billion Bet on the Future of Cybersecurity

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Demand has never been stronger for the cybersecurity specialist.

By Danny Vena, CPA – Nov 18, 2026 at 6:14PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Palo Alto Networks ›

A quick look at the headlines is all it takes to understand the unprecedented need for robust cybersecurity. The emergence of next-generation artificial intelligence (AI)-powered threats has emboldened hackers, and the frequency of attacks has risen to new heights.

With that as a backdrop, investors watched closely as Palo Alto Networks (PANW +9.90%) released its quarterly financial report. Shareholders were pleased to find that the company has risen to the challenge, as robust demand has fueled record growth. Palo Alto also made a sizable investment to ensure that enterprises can adopt AI securely, regardless of what models they use.

For its fiscal 2027 first quarter (ended Oct. 31), Palo Alto Networks delivered record revenue that grew 39% year over year to $3.44 billion, driven primarily by robust growth in its Next-Generation Security annual recurring revenue (NGS ARR). The company also generated adjusted net income of $881 million and adjusted earnings per diluted share (EPS) of $1.05, up 33% and 13%, respectively.

To give those numbers context, analysts' consensus estimates called for revenue of $3.31 billion and adjusted EPS of $0.97, so Palo Alto Networks easily scaled expectations.

It isn't just the current quarter that looks strong. Remaining performance obligation (RPO) -- contractually obligated revenue that hasn't yet been earned -- grew 35% year over year to $21 billion, building a strong foundation for future growth.

Palo Alto Networks' customer metrics were equally compelling, as its net retention rate remained above 120% for its platform customers. Put another way, existing customers are spending 20% more than in the prior-year quarter.

Not only did Palo Alto Networks surprise to the upside, but it also signaled strong growth ahead, as evidenced by its robust outlook. For the fiscal second quarter, management is guiding for revenue of roughly $3.53 billion, or year-over-year growth of roughly 36%.

Investors were particularly inspired by the company's full-year guidance. For fiscal 2027, management is guiding for total revenue of $14.5 billion, up about 26% year over year, with NGS ARR of $11.5 billion, also up 26%. Given management’s tendency to underpromise and overdeliver, that forecast will likely rise over time.

In a surprise announcement, Palo Alto Networks revealed that it will shell out $4.5 billion to acquire Applied Compute, a leading platform for post-training and inference of open-weight AI models. This will allow Palo Alto to "help enterprises adopt AI securely, whether they choose open-weight models, closed frontier models, or both," according to the announcement.

Even as demand remains high, cybersecurity stocks have swooned as technology issues have fallen. Ahead of its Q1 report, Palo Alto Networks shares declined nearly 19% from their early October peak.

However, some investors will still balk at Palo Alto Networks' premium valuation, as the stock currently sells for 82 times forward earnings and 70 times next year's expected earnings. Historically, investors tend to reward high-growth stocks with premium valuations thanks to their consistently above-average, uh, growth.

I'm a firm believer in the old adage "You get what you pay for." Management has shown an uncanny knack for stoking Palo Alto's impressive growth, and recent technological advances make cybersecurity more important than ever.

That's why I'm confident in saying Palo Alto Networks is a buy.
