Briton swims length of strait of Hormuz to highlight environmental cost of war

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

UN patron Lewis Pugh saw ships ablaze and fighter jets overhead during three-day swim as Iran conflict continues

A British endurance swimmer has completed a three-day swim through the strait of Hormuz to highlight the environmental cost of the Iran war.

Lewis Pugh, a UN patron of the oceans, swam the 9.3-mile (15km) length of the waterway, which is a key route for oil shipments from the Middle East.

According to the Lewis Pugh Foundation, he witnessed several attacks on commercial shipping, explosions in the distance and fighter jets overhead.

An oil tanker was struck by a projectile about 2.5 miles from where he was swimming on the first day. On the second, he saw flames rising above a headland separating him from a burning liquefied petroleum gas carrier, Tara Gas, and later he passed close to the vessel while it was still on fire.

“My immediate thoughts were for the seafarers onboard,” Pugh said. “The flames were enormous. These vessels carry highly flammable gases, and an attack on one can have catastrophic consequences, not just for crew members but also on the surrounding marine environment.”

The 56-year-old undertook his first swim in a conflict zone to urge an end to the fighting, the reopening of the strait and action to address environmental damage caused by the conflict.

Believed to be the first person to attempt a long-distance swim in the strait since the conflict began in March, Pugh swam a route from Musandam Island, carving through the dramatic mountains and inlets of Oman’s Musandam peninsula and Iran’s spectacular mangrove forests, to reach the Cape of Hormuz at the peninsula’s northern tip.

He said: “War causes immense human suffering. Lives are lost, families are torn apart and homes, hospitals and schools can be destroyed in minutes. But there is another casualty that receives far too little attention: nature itself. And the damage can last long after the fighting has stopped.”

The small expedition team included a support boat and kayak that remained alongside Pugh during the swim.

Pugh said environmental damage caused by conflict often received less attention than the human cost. According to the UN, at least 175 vessels have been attacked since fighting began, and about 30 attacks have caused significant pollution.

The strait of Hormuz is one of the world’s most important shipping routes and is also home to wildlife including dugongs, sea turtles and whale sharks.

Pugh, who was born in Plymouth, said oil spills and other contamination could have long-term consequences for marine ecosystems including coral reefs, seagrass meadows and mangrove forests.
