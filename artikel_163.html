double quotation markI think it might be too tough, yeah. And a lot of people would say that it’s great. But a lot of people think it’s too tough.

The president’s comments were an unusually frank acknowledgment that one of the defining policies of his second administration had become a political liability. But they were seemingly at odds with his decision on Friday to elevate Stephen Miller, the architect of his immigration crackdown, to White House chief of staff.

But he admitted: “I get hurt because we also took people that had good jobs, that created one violation, or breaking of the law, and that was that they came into the country illegally.”

Public health leaders and politicians who made healthcare a central part of their platforms swept into office in the US midterm elections last week, setting the stage for national, state and local changes.

Voters, faced with an unprecedented rise in vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles, alongside devastating outbreaks of foodborne illnesses, chose national candidates who prioritized public health, healthcare affordability, reproductive health and the impeachment of Robert F Kennedy Jr, among other key issues.

“The American people are speaking out against President Trump and the policies that he’s been rolling out since the day he took office,” said Julie Scofield, a member of the coordinating committee at the grassroots organization Defend Public Health. “I think it’s a complete repudiation.”

Mallory Harris, a scientist in Austin, almost delayed having children because she was worried about accessing life-saving healthcare, such as vaccines and abortion, if she needed it. She had doctors check her immunity against viruses including measles, and sought assurances from her physicians that she would have access to appropriate care. Harris found out she was pregnant just as 2026 officially became the worst year for measles in the US since before she was born. Texas has no paid time off for maternity or paternity leave, but Harris and her husband will try to wait until the baby is six months old and can get the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) shot early before starting daycare.

Dealing with public health emergencies like these have been “a top issue” for her as a voter. After the election, “it suddenly became a lot less scary”, Harris said. “We’ve seen scientists and physicians getting elected in record numbers. We saw big shifts in places that have had measles outbreaks.”
