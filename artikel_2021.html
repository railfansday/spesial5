'Look at Clement Turpin...' - Ex-refs chief shares verdict on viral England penalty controversy vs Spain

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Elliot Anderson was "fortunate" to go unpunished in the build-up to England's opening penalty against Spain.

That is according to former PGMOL chief and ex-FIFA referee, Keith Hackett, who exclusively told Football Insider that under different circumstances, the Manchester City man could have seen a card.

On the stroke of half-time, Anderson played a beautiful ball forward to Harry Kane, who jinked into the penalty area and was taken down by Pau Cubarsi.

Clement Turpin conversed with VAR before allowing the spot-kick to stand, but the Three Lions' captain was forced to wait while Marc Cucurella received medical attention.

Replays showed a clear follow-through on the former Chelsea defender, catching him very high as Anderson made his pass forward.

The incident became heavily debated, but both Roy Keane and Ian Wright were happy with the penalty standing, rather than the referee getting his cards out.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Hackett understood Spain's frustrations, revealing that he thought the goal could, and maybe should, have been disallowed.

"I think that if you're going to disallow go the goal, you've got to be absolutely 100 per cent in what you've seen and what you've done," he said.

"Anderson was clever in that we've got this adage, haven't we, of 'He's played the ball, but he's then hit the man'. He's fortunate.

"He's really fortunate that the goal was allowed to stand. But we are, in my opinion, looking at here in Clement Terpin, the world's number one referee.

"And he demonstrated that. He demonstrated that with his body language, his communication skills, a friendliness that doesn't go away.

"It's a good debate. The reality is a player has a duty of care towards his opponent.

"Kicking the ball and then following through and kicking his opponent is an offence in the laws of the game. But I think he was given the benefit of the doubt."

In the Premier League, that kind of challenge is easily a red card, especially with how involved VAR likes to be in the division.

Asked about the nature of the challenge, Hackett said: "That's the tightness of calls of this nature. And that's why I think we were lucky that the referee, on his angle, stuck with his call.

"Because in England, right, we would have ruled that goal out, wouldn't we?"

Fans will likely to continue debating this for the next few days, and it wouldn't be a surprise to see this used as an example later in the season.
