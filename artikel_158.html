Ministry of Justice investigating Russell Brand message from prison claiming he is not guilty

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

PM’s spokesperson calls message, read out by convicted rapist’s wife on their YouTube channel, ‘clearly appalling’

The Ministry of Justice has launched an investigation into whether Russell Brand has broken prison rules after the jailed comedian shared a message claiming he is not guilty of sexual offences just days after his conviction.

Brand’s wife, Laura Brand, read a message from her rapist husband in a video uploaded to their YouTube channel in which he claimed: “I am not guilty of the offences I have been jailed for.”

It is understood a prisoner will be deemed to have broken the rules if they provide comments to a family member they know will be published.

Any inquiry could also examine whether the current rules need to be updated to stop comments from prisoners being broadcast via profit-making YouTube channels.

Asked about the comments, the prime minister’s official spokesperson said: “This is clearly appalling and is being urgently investigated. We expect action to be taken if he is found to have broken the rules. Prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims and speaking to the media is only permitted in exceptional circumstances.

“Prisoners cannot get around these rules by having someone else publish their comments … This is being urgently investigated. We expect action to be taken if the rules have been broken.”

Brand, 51, is serving a 10-and-a-half-year prison term after he was convicted of one count of rape and three sexual assaults at Southwark crown court on 5 November.

In his message, Brand said he had been moved from the category B Wandsworth prison in south-west London to the lower category C HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire – a prison for men convicted of sexual offences.

Reading the message from a notebook, Laura Brand said: “As you know, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening.”

His message added: “I am incarcerated in HMP Littlehey, little as in small, and hey as in hey, I’m in the biggest sex offenders’ prison in Europe. The good news is that I have already seen that the staff here are strong, they are kind, and they are dedicated. And as for my fellow prisoners, I now know that I am in no position to judge anyone.

“I don’t know which other men here are not guilty, and the ones that are, they are people that Christ died to save, like you and me, let us all love, pray for, and forgive one another as God forgave us.”

Responding to the video on Monday, the foreign secretary, Ed Miliband, told Sky News: “His crimes are heinous and awful. I think it’s revolting, by the way, that he shows no remorse today in his comments. No remorse, no sympathy for the victims. Just disgusting comments.”

According to official guidance, prisoners are expected to make a formal application through their governor if they wish to contact the media.

Prison sources said current rules may have to be updated to stop people from releasing comments on YouTube channels through which a prisoner or their family may profit via sponsorship or advertising. The rules say: “Correspondence which is intended for publication or for use by radio or television or for posting on the internet (or which if sent, would be likely to be published or broadcast) must not contain material which is for publication in return for payment (unless the prisoner is unconvicted).”
