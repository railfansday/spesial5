Manchester City nurse self-inflicted wounds again as Kevin De Bruyne’s return highlights decline

Manchester City 2-2 Napoli: Rayan Cherki, the most natural replacement for the great Belgian, rescued a point for the hosts as they twice came from behind in a dramatic second half

If replacing Kevin De Bruyne could appear the impossible job, there are times when Rayan Cherki looks a worthy heir to Manchester City’s greatest player.

As De Bruyne made perhaps his last return to the Etihad Stadium, there was a sign that the baton has been passed. The final say came from Cherki, powering into the penalty area, placing a shot perfectly past Vanja Milinkovic-Savic with the kind of expert precision that was De Bruyne’s hallmark. A high-class goal with four minutes to go showed the maverick has a substance.

So did his predecessor. For De Bruyne, the man who said his love for City will never change, there may be some satisfaction in the scoreline. For Napoli too, given that they had lost seven of their previous eight away games in the Champions League. For City, even the fact they twice equalised should not camouflage the sense they are stumbling and stuttering their way into Sunday’s clash at Arsenal.

“The performance overall was good,” claimed Enzo Maresca, but that was a flattering assessment. He picked the wrong team and Cherki suffered as a result. City were slow and soporific in the first half. Erling Haaland’s verdict had more credence. “Not good and we let them in too easy,” said the stand-in captain. City conceded twice more to mean they have let in 20 goals in their last 10 games. There could have been another, Gianluigi Donnarumma making a fine save from Sam Beukema’s header.

“Five games, 10 points,” reflected Maresca. But City have left themselves with little leeway in the Champions League, if they are to secure a top-eight finish, with five points dropped already and a trip to Barcelona next.

Yet if it was about City’s immediate future, it was about their past, too. There was an understandable focus on De Bruyne, even if the game did not revolve around him.

A footballer who left Manchester for Napoli in 2025 struck on his return. A midfielder who cost Manchester City a sizeable sum scored. Neither was De Bruyne. Instead, it was Rasmus Hojlund who put Napoli ahead, something the former Manchester United forward savoured, and Ayyoub Bouaddi, one of those charged with living up to the standards De Bruyne set in the midfield, who levelled.

De Bruyne had just gone off, to perhaps the last standing ovation he will get at this ground, when Noa Lang volleyed in Leonardo Spinazzola’s deep cross for a glorious goal. If City were hurt, Lang suffered a different kind of pain in his last game in England. The winger’s thumb became detached when it was caught between advertising hoardings at Anfield during Galatasaray’s March defeat to Liverpool. He required emergency surgery to stitch it back together.

The restorative element for City here came in the eventual result, and courtesy of Cherki. Josko Gvardiol almost equalised, volleying against the post, before the Frenchman did. It was a rescue job from him and Maresca, but the manager had to undo his own errors. Cherki had spent much of the match miscast, stranded on the right touchline. “We know his best position in the middle,” Maresca admitted.

But even with a crowd including Luis Figo, it seemed he forgot the importance of wingers. He benched Jeremy Doku and Iliman Ndiaye and City had no threat from the flanks. He first summoned the Belgian, replacing the utterly anonymous Allan, and then the Senegalese. Enzo Fernandez went off and Cherki, a fringe figure on the right, came alive when moved infield. Doku set up a landmark strike for Bouaddi.

The Moroccan had commanded an £85m transfer fee before he had scored a goal in professional football. He altered that statistic, opening his City account after a cutback from Doku. Much like Cherki’s later surge, it was the kind of well-timed run into the penalty area that De Bruyne made many a time over his decade at City.

Cherki perhaps had to make amends, given his role in Napoli’s opener. He gave the ball away, Billy Gilmour threaded a pass through and Hojlund turned past Marc Guehi to angle a shot in. City have now gone 10 games without a clean sheet and, not for the first time, caused their own problems.

For the former United forward, and boyhood United fan, it was a sweet moment. Hojlund raised a finger in celebration, shouting there was only one club in Manchester. “Good banter,” he said. His goal was a glimpse of the potential he sometimes showed at Old Trafford; perhaps, but for Ruben Amorim, he might still be there.

Meanwhile, in a strange orange kit, City scarcely looked themselves. Or not the team they were in De Bruyne’s heyday, anyway, the team who were anointed the best in Europe.

They had distinguished guests besides Figo. City chairman Khaldoon al-Mubarak was sat next to Uefa president Aleksander Ceferin; this is City’s 16th consecutive season in the Champions League but, given potential Premier League punishments, perhaps their last for quite some time. Like De Bruyne, Ceferin may not be seen again here soon.
