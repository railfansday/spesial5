McIlroy overcomes late stumble to take share of lead in Dubai

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Rory McIlroy surged to take a share of the lead at the DP World Tour Championship on Thursday, joining Tyrrell Hatton at the top of the leaderboard at five under par; watch the third round of the DP World Tour Championship live on Sky Sports

Rory McIlroy took a big step towards clinching his eighth Race to Dubai title on Thursday, rallying from a late stumble to take a share of the 36-hole lead alongside Tyrrell Hatton at the DP World Tour Championship.

McIlroy briefly held the outright lead on Friday after he went four-under through his first 11 holes, but a flurry of bogeys on his back nine saw the Northern Irishman drop down the leaderboard at the Jumeirah Golf Estates.

He then hit back with a birdie on the 18th, which saw the two-time Masters champion post a second-round 70 and move to join clubhouse leader Hatton at five under par.

Hatton began his round in emphatic fashion, making birdies on his first two holes, before mixing in a further three birdies alongside two bogeys to post a three-under 69 for his second round. He will tee up with McIlroy in Saturday's final pairing.

Overnight leader, Joost Luiten, also suffered a late stumble, conceding back-to-back bogeys on holes 14 and 15, but unlike McIlroy, he was unable to recover, going one over for the day to join Kristoffer Reitan tied for third at four under par.

McIlroy's hopes of equalling Collin Montgomerie's record of eight Order of Merit titles grew, as Race to Dubai Rankings leader Eugenio Chacarra battled to stay within touching distance of the leaders, mixing in six birdies with five bogeys to go one over through 16 holes.

However, the Spaniard showed resilience, carding back-to-back birdies on his final two holes, even chipping in from off the green on the 18th, to post a second-round 71, and move four shots off the leaders.

Patrick Reed, who had been vying to become only the second American to win the Race to Dubai title, endured a torrid day, posting a five-over 77, and slumped to second-bottom on the leaderboard after going six over in his final six holes.

McIlroy's resilient performance moves him to the top of the projected Race to Dubai rankings.

"I want to win the tournament," McIlroy said. "And if I win the tournament, that's all I can do. It doesn't really matter what any of the other guys do, if they play well enough.

"But if I go and I win the first trophy on Sunday, then the second trophy should take care of itself."

Late bogeys were the order of the day at the Earth Course, with Mikael Lindberg, who had also briefly moved to take a share of the lead on Friday, dropping two shots late on as he fell off the pace to join Freddy Schott at three under par.

Jon Rahm battled with bunkers throughout his second round, first taking two out of a fairway bunker on the seventh hole on his way to making a par, before smashing a wedge shot into the bank of another bunker on the eighth hole, as he posted a second-round 71 to finish three shots back from Hatton and McIlroy.

"I was making good swings, I just couldn't quite hit it close enough to have makeable putts," Rahm said.

"I hit loads of greens, I was just never constantly over 30 feet. It's going to be hard to make putts like that. So it's a bit of a survival day."

It took McIlroy a while to find his rhythm, with the Northern Irishman missing early birdie chances on holes one and two before opening his account on the third, flighting a wedge shot at the flag to set him up for a short birdie putt.

He added back-to-back birdies on the seventh and eighth, with McIlroy showcasing his exceptional iron play to hit the green on the former from 240 yards out.

His back nine was a different story, despite McIlroy again showcasing his elite accuracy to make a birdie on the 10th.

McIlroy came unstuck on the 12th, sending his tee shot left, before missing the green with his approach as he went on to card his first bogey of the day.

"Once you start putting yourself out of position on this golf course, this setup, these conditions, it starts to get very difficult," McIlroy said.

He then came a cropper to a fairway bunker on hole 16 and missed the green on the par-three 17th to make back-to-back bogeys late on.

But he responded with a birdie on the final hole, sending an approach from 267 yards out to the heart of the green to leave himself a 72ft eagle putt.

He couldn't make good his first attempt, with McIlroy ultimately wrapping up his second round with a birdie.
