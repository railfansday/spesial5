'I hate it' Brady reacts to stunning Rodgers claims

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Sky Sports NFL's Jason Bell says Aaron Rodgers is 'losing respect' after claims that head coach Mike McCarthy was 'snoring' in a quarterback meeting in an explosive interview; Steelers president Art Rooney II comes out in "100 per cent" support of McCarthy after team releases Rodgers

Has Aaron Rodgers played his final game in the NFL? Was he wrong to publicly criticise Mike McCarthy? Seven-time Super Bowl champion Tom Brady says he 'hates' the situation, insisting "nobody wins when the family feuds".

Rodgers sent shockwaves through the NFL on Thanksgiving Eve as he used an interview on The Pat McAfee Show to claim McCarthy had been "falling asleep in meetings" while suggesting his entire team believed a change was required on the sideline.

It came after Rodgers was benched for Friday's matchup with the Denver Broncos following two straight defeats, with Mason Rudolph due to start under center.

The Steelers later released Rodgers 11 weeks into what is set to be the final season of the 42-year-old's career.

"The sanctity of an NFL locker room is, it's a very sacred place and we shouldn't ever lose track of that as players, it's a privilege to be in that locker room," said Brady during the broadcast of Thursday's Thanksgiving clash between the Dallas Cowboys and Philadelphia Eagles.

"You're fighting with your teammates. You're fighting with your coaches. You're always acting as a team together. Shouldn't always be the place for private things to become public.

"A Hall of Fame career and he's a tremendous player and has nothing to do with his play style. I was fortunate to be around a lot of great teammates over the years and what made a great teammate to me was you cared about your teammates and your coaches and you cared about what the team was trying to accomplish."

Brady referenced his own relationship with former head coach Bill Belichick, with whom he won six of his seven Super Bowl rings as part of a historic New England Patriots dynasty.

"That supersedes any Individual aspect of your game that you want to promote. I was in a great locker room, I played for my teammates, I played for my coaches. Nobody wins when the family feuds," Brady continued.

"I wasn't a perfect player. He (Bill Belichick) wasn't a perfect coach. But we did some things in the game that we're so proud of now and even me being four years removed from the game the things that matter more than winning a Super Bowl or trophy is my relationships with my teammates my coaches.

"That's going to transcend anything you do on the field, so it's just unfortunate. I hate it and I hate to be even talking about it. I just think it's a privilege of the NFL, you want to inspire the next generation of athletes and you always want to do it the right way."

The four-time MVP quarterback is 'losing respect' following his explosive claims about the McCarthy, according to Sky Sports NFL's Jason Bell.

"First of all, I don't want to be on Aaron Rodgers' bad side. Let's start there. We all make mistakes, right? We all do things that aren't necessarily right in the moment," said Bell.

"But when I look at him as a player, he wasn't playing well. He wasn't helping his team.

"And you could could see him and his head coach arguing on the sideline with with each other. They weren't getting along.

"Whatever happens behind closed doors, that goes down, but the response to it is where I'm losing a little respect.

"In that locker room is a sanctuary. Some things we just don't let out. We don't have those conversations in public - it's a respect thing.

"If Rodgers was playing MVP football like he did before, he could say all these things and get away with it, but you have to know where you are in your career."

Rodgers suggested in his appearance on The Pat McAfee Show that the prospect of being benched had never been mentioned during recent conversations with McCarthy.

He accused McCarthy of snoring during meetings and rebuffing Rodgers' suggestions that he needed to make more of an effort of connecting with the Steelers' defensive players.

Rodgers added that the "final straw" came during half-time of a game when McCarthy closed a team talk by shouting 'one, two, three, Packers!'.

Rodgers and McCarthy spent 13 seasons together with the Packers from 2006-2018, reaching the playoffs nine times and winning one Super Bowl. They were reunited this past offseason when the Steelers hired McCarthy following the departure of Mike Tomlin.

"I'm not sure what's next for Aaron Rodgers. I think he will figure out that for himself over the next handful of months, or whatever his particular process is," said Super Bowl champion and Sky Sports NFL expert Ndamukong Suh.

"But I want to go back to what Jason [Bell] said in the way that you handle your business, especially from the perspective of a star. There's a way you go about handling things.

"Those things should be handled behind closed doors before trying to get ahead of the story - on either side. Have a conversation, have an understanding, and then go from there and address it.

"Going out to the media and doing all of these different things, The Pat McAfee Show, it's great for them and great for ESPN, but it's not the proper way I would want to handle that particular scenario."

Responding to Rodgers' comments, McCarthy admitted to his "embarrassing" moment when mentioning the Packers during half-time of their defeat to the Philadelphia Eagles in Week 11.

Steelers president Art Rooney II defended McCarthy on Thursday in an interview with the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, insisting he supports him "100 per cent".

"I just want to say that I have 100 per cent confidence in Mike McCarthy and I expect he'll be our coach this season, next season and beyond," Mr Rooney II told the Post-Gazette.

"The big thing, what happened this week, is insubordination can't be tolerated in any organization. When you start getting ultimatums, you can't live with that. There's maybe a time and place after the season if he wanted to kind of unload.

"He talks about wanting to be a good teammate, but you don't do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that's for sure."

Rooney II underlined his belief that events in the locker room should be kept within the locker room, and downplayed Rodgers' comments regarding concerns for McCarthy's health.

The Steelers are 6-4 as they prepare to face the Denver Broncos (8-2) on Black Friday.

Terry Bradshaw, who won four Super Bowls as quarterback for the Steelers, criticised Rodgers for his lack of leadership and turning on his teammates.

"I'm sorry that Aaron Rodgers had to do this," said Bradshaw on the Fox Sports coverage. "I'm sorry that he had to go public with all his gripes and complaints. Am I surprised? Absolutely not.

"To my opinion he's a strange guy anyway and the one thing you don't do, all of us athletes up here, none of us turn on our teammates, we don't turn on our coaching staff, we don't turn on our ownership, we show class on the way out. Aaron Rodgers didn't do that.

"He said 'when I'm through playing here in Pittsburgh, I'll disappear and you'll never hear or see from me again' and I hope this is one time he's telling the truth.

"When you're the quarterback, you are the leader, whether you want to admit it or not, you are the leader.

"Had trouble in Green Bay as their leader, had trouble in New York with the Jets as their leader and certainly in Pittsburgh."

Rob Gronkowski said that Rodgers does not prioritise his team unlike his former quarterback Tom Brady, who also had disagreements with coaches.

"I don't think he plays again in the NFL," said former Patriots tight end Rob Gronkowski on Fox Sports."When there's a guy like Aaron Rodgers that's available there's too much smoke. It would take away from the objective and that's to win games."Aaron Rodgers and Tom Brady they're both Hall of Fame quarterbacks. I've seen situations personally with my own eyes where Tom and coaches had disagreements but there's one thing that Tom did different. He would dissect it all and then he would hold it in and he would still do what's best for the team and for his teammates and for the organisation. And yes the disagreements, they were lethal sometimes."But then I would see him go out and still be the best player he can be in practice and in games to help win games and win Super Bowls for the team."
