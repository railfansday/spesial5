John Deere acquires Orchard Robotics, an AI farming startup with Seattle ties

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Chronicling the Seattle and Pacific Northwest startup scene. SEE MORE

by Lisa Stiffler on Nov 20, 2026 at 7:52 amNovember 20, 2026 at 9:14 am

Orchard Robotics, an agtech company using artificial intelligence to help growers monitor crops and make farming decisions, has been acquired by John Deere. The deal was announced Thursday and the terms were not disclosed.

The startup has developed a system of cameras mounted on tractors and other equipment that gather images as the machines traverse a farm. Orchard’s AI analyzes the images to generate data on vines, trees and crops, which is then used to track growth, yield and health.

“This acquisition connects crop-level intelligence with John Deere technology and equipment that can precisely execute the work in the field,” Tim Marquis, John Deere’s director of farm systems innovation, said in a statement.

That includes helping farmers target treatments and make better-timed decisions about thinning, spraying and harvesting.

Orchard was founded by CEO Charlie Wu, a Thiel Fellow who dropped out of Cornell University to launch his business in 2022.

“Over these next few years, our goal is to bring the power of Orchard’s industry-leading technology and AI Farming capabilities to every farmer out there,” Wu said in a LinkedIn post. “This is only the beginning of a long and awesome journey ahead.”

The startup is based in San Francisco and has an office in Seattle. Wu splits his time between the two cities and was a finalist for Young Entrepreneur of the Year at the 2026 GeekWire Awards.

Orchard has raised more than $25 million from investors, including a $22 million Series A round announced in September 2025. The startup has 45 employees, according to PitchBook.

The Pacific Northwest is home to numerous agtech startups. That includes other companies that collect and analyze data, such as Loftus Labs and innov8.ag, as well as IUNU and Koidra, which are focused on greenhouse crops. Businesses tackling field operations include weed destroyers Carbon Robotics and Aigen; rock-removing TerraClear; and irrigation startups FarmHQ and Verdi.
