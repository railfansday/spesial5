Prime Video’s Star Wars Replacement Is Hailed as the Best Space Show of All Time

Space may be the final frontier, but you don't need to go to galaxies far, far away to find some good space adventures. In fact, the best one is set right here, in our very own Solar System. Over its six seasons, Prime Video's The Expanse has quietly built its reputation as the best space series on television, surpassing even mighty sci-fi franchises like Star Wars and Star Trek, and amassing quite a following among fans of the genre. Having debuted in 2015, it was even brought back from cancellation, proving how beloved it really is — fans just wouldn't let go. All that wouldn't have happened with just any series, of course, so what is it that makes The Expanse so special, even among titans?

Although it is set right here on our cosmic doorstep, The Expanse also takes place roughly some 200 years into the future. It's a time of war, with three main factions fracturing the Solar System: the United Nations of Earth and Luna, the Martian Congressional Republic, and the Outer Planets Alliance. The latter comprises all the planets and stations working in the asteroid belt, which have been part of Earth, but are now fighting for independence. Tensions are at an all-time high, and the tiniest mistake can lead to all-out war.

At the center of all that is an unlikely group of heroes and their aptly named gunship, the Rocinante. Led by James Holden (Steven Strait), they start out working hauling ice to the dwarf planet Ceres, but a distress call from a derelict ship in the middle of nowhere suddenly puts them in the middle of a conspiracy that threatens the whole system. With a sort of "found family" dynamics, the Rocinante crew is at first only looking to stay out of trouble, but, as it often happens in these stories, they quickly grow into heroes.

Meanwhile, on Ceres, the disappearance of a young woman has police investigator Joe Miller (Thomas Jane) rattling some cages that are dangerously close to making the planet the place where the war actually starts. With resources like air and water being rationed because of all the tension, everyone is on edge, and, unbeknownst to everyone, the thread Miller finds himself pulling ties all that together. As this soon-to-be-very-hot war gears up, there are still some people on all sides trying to work a way through it.

Star Wars and Star Trek have a much wider scope than The Expanse, granted, as they deal with a galactic scale, while the latter stretches as far as the asteroid belt, at first. Still, after a while, distances don't seem to matter as much when you have technologies like hyperdrives or warp drives. In The Expanse, however, there is no faster-than-light travel, so distances actually matter. This is one of the ways in which the Prime Video series uses space to its advantage, making it feel much more real than any other sci-fi galaxy.

Considering this, it's only natural that there would eventually be a divide between Earth and Mars, as well as a secessionist movement in the belt. It's precisely there that the most interesting effects of space life are felt. So far away from the two other planets, life in space turned Belters almost into an ethnicity of their own, with physical differences and their own language, Belter Creole. Because of that, they are usually seen as a lower caste by both Earth and Mars, hence the OPA's struggle for independence.

Who doesn't want to hear a Deep Purple cover in a sci-fi dialect?

To go with this rich worldbuilding (or spacebuilding?), The Expanse also pays a lot of attention to production design, particularly ships and space stations. Instead of flat rooms and hallways, ships are built more like skyscrapers, with decks serving different purposes. This allows for acceleration to create a gravitational pull that's small enough for the human body to feel comfortable with, and maneuvers like "flip and burn" turning, and the effect of G-forces on the crew also make The Expanse the most scientifically accurate space series around.

All that makes The Expanse one of the best sci-fi adaptations on TV. Its six seasons adapt only two-thirds of James S.A. Corey's book series, though, so, despite Prime Video having concluded the series with Season 6, we still can't say we've gotten the whole thing. But it could have been even shorter, because, in 2018, The Expanse was cancelled by Syfy after only three seasons for its poor ratings on TV and its complicated business and distribution model.

However, the audience wasn't ready to say goodbye, and they made their feelings known. The resulting #SaveTheExpanse campaign quickly became one of the most memorable fan movements in recent TV history, including crowdfunding efforts, targeting Amazon executives and even Jeff Bezos directly, and even getting an airplane to fly a banner over Amazon Studios. It also helped to have notorious celebrity sci-fi buffs like Wil Wheaton, George R.R. Martin, and Patton Oswalt join in.

This kind of loyalty and dedication isn't seen often, proving just how good The Expanse really is. Amazon picked it up for three more seasons in 2018, raising the bar with production value and consolidating the series as a sci-fi phenomenon. Few series have managed to pull off what The Expanse did in terms of worldbuilding and storytelling in this genre, and, right now, it doesn't look like any other will any time soon, either. Not even Star Wars or Star Trek.

The Expanse is available to stream on Prime Video.
