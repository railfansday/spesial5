Former state official charged with tampering bar exam results

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

The former executive director of the Arkansas Supreme Court’s Office of Professional Programs faces 65 felony counts after an investigation found “extensive irregularities” with bar exam results from 2020 to 2025, according to the state supreme court. (Image from Shutterstock)

The former executive director of the Arkansas Supreme Court’s Office of Professional Programs faces 65 felony counts after an investigation found “extensive irregularities” with bar exam results from 2020 to 2025, according to the state supreme court.

During the course of at least eight bar administrations, Nancie Givens, who left her post in February, took points from some bar examinees and assigned them to others, moving some candidates to a minimum passing score, according to a Thursday press release.

In at least one case, according to the state supreme court, Givens changed a score of an examinee who earned a passing score to a failing one.

A criminal investigation initiated in April by the Arkansas State Police and the FBI found that 11,400 files were deleted or unavailable, most including bar examinee applications, profiles and exam score sheets that the Office of Professional Programs was required by the Arkansas Supreme Court’s rules to retain for 25 years, according to the press release.

“If these allegations are true, these actions constitute an unprecedented breach of public trust,” according to the press release.

The state supreme court, state and federal authorities are working to recover deleted and missing data, analyze the impacted test administrations, and determine the full scope of the crime, according to the press release.

The “vast majority” of Arkansas bar examinees who had been notified that they passed “did, in fact, pass the exam” according to the press release, but those impacted by the misconduct will ⁠be notified “as soon as possible upon definitive confirmation of their status,” the Arkansas Supreme Court said.

For the July 2025 bar exam, 244 people took the bar exam in Arkansas, with a 70% pass rate, according to the National Conference of Bar Examiners.
