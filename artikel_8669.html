It may be thirty years since Dwayne Johnson made his debut with the WWE, but the “Most Electrifying Man in Sports Entertainment” can still break out his signature “Rock Bottom” slam at a mere second’s notice—just ask Pat McAfee.

Sparks flew when Johnson attended ESPN’s College Gameday as guest picker on Nov. 21. During the live broadcast from the Lakeside Patio at the University of Miami, the Hurricanes took on the Virginia Tech Hokies, but the action off the field got just as intense when McAfee brought up a questionable call that had gone in Miami’s favor. Johnson, who played for the Hurricanes as a defensive tackle in the early 90s, took exception to those remarks and things got physical, fast.

Tension was in the air as Johnson made his way to the commentary position, complete with his WWE theme music on blast, and decked out in his number 94 shirt, because McAfee tried to pour cold water on the Hurricane’s 28-20 win. “Already talking trash about Miami,” responded The Rock.

“Didn’t it feel a little soft,” teased McAfee of the Hurricane’s performance. “I mean like, maybe, the softest I’ve ever seen Miami,” he went on.

At first, it looked like McAfee might have gotten a free pass as Johnson pulled him in for a close handshake, but in a moment that reminded us why The Rock is one of the most iconic pro wrestlers of all time, the former WWE champ grabbed McAfee and proceeded to execute the Rock Bottom, slamming the former football player straight through a nearby table.

With McAfee out for the count, “The Great One” then basked in the adulation of his home team. Of course, McAfee has dabbled in the world of grappling, and has even made wrestling appearances for WWE, but he certainly met his match in Miami.

Making his debut as “Rocky Maivia” on November 17, 1996, at the Survivor Series pay-per-view, Johnson went on to become one of the most popular wrestlers of all time. While the big man now concentrates on a full-time career in Hollywood, “The People’s Champion” did step through the ropes at WrestleMania XL in 2024. And, to celebrate 30 years of The Rock, Johnson recently confirmed that he will make an appearance at the 2026 Survivor Series on Nov. 28.

Whether or not he gets mixed up in the action there remains to be seen, but with a big money WrestleMania planned in Saudi Arabia next year, there’s plenty of hope that Johnson could have at least one more match. Either way, Pat McAfee can attest that The Rock’s still got what it takes.
