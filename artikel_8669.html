Named Executor? Pay the Estate’s Debts & Taxes Before Anyone Inherits

As executor, settle the estate in order: get the court’s authority, inventory everything, and pay valid debts, taxes, and costs from an estate account before you distribute anything. If you hand out inheritances first and the estate runs short, the IRS can hold you personally responsible for the tax it still owes.

Family members may press for their shares within weeks, long before it’s safe to pay them.

Federal taxes come first when an estate can’t pay all its debts, according to IRS Publication 559. An executor who pays heirs ahead of a tax debt they knew of, or should have known of, owes it personally, up to the amount paid out ahead of it.

Hire a probate lawyer if the estate can’t cover its debts or an heir contests the will. Skip the executor’s fee if you’re the only heir, since the fee is taxable income and an inheritance isn’t.

Say a daughter and her brother inherit their father’s estate in equal shares, and she’s the executor. Over nine months she pays $20,000 of his final bills and taxes from the estate’s $250,000, and only then splits the remaining $230,000, $115,000 each.

Send IRS Form 5495 right after you file the final returns. The IRS then has nine months to name any tax due, and paying it ends your personal liability for those taxes.
