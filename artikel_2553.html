Latest CFP rankings give Iowa multiple playoff options | Leistikow

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

There's a famous scene in the movie "Major League" where the Cleveland Indians players are having a clubhouse meeting and are made aware of the front-office's plot to sabotage the team. The despondent players turn to (fictional) veteran Jake Taylor who says, "Well then, I guess there's only one thing left to do."

Taylor's response: "Win the whole (bleeping) thing."

That probably should still be the rallying cry for Iowa football players and fans after the latest batch of College Football Playoff rankings on Nov. 24. Reach the Big Ten Championship Game and then win it, and you're definitively into the 12-team playoff as an automatic qualifier.

The CFP selection committee did offer the Hawkeyes some respect and a potential at-large path to the CFP if they can take care of business on Black Friday against Nebraska.

Iowa bumped up two spots, to No. 13, after a 34-17 win at Illinois on Nov. 21 to extend its winning streak to four games. The 9-2 Hawkeyes were the third-highest Big Ten Conference team in the committee's top 25, which added Washington and Nebraska to give the league eight representatives.

Indiana dropping from No. 8 to 14 in the rankings was the biggest development of Tuesday night for Iowa. The second-biggest development was seeing Washington (a team Iowa thrashed in Seattle, 41-24, and it wasn't even that close) and Nebraska jump into the ranking. Suddenly, Iowa has a ranked win on its ledger with the chance at another on Friday. (Of course, both teams could still fall out with losses this weekend, but still … it helps for now.)

Here's why: If the committee gets down to picking a second Big Ten team, Iowa already being valued ahead of Indiana is significant. The Hawkeyes have a tougher final game than Indiana (which hosts Purdue), so if both team wins the logical thought is that Iowa would stay ahead of the Hoosiers.

If Iowa doesn't make the Big Ten title game but finishes 10-2, that would give the Hawkeyes a potential case to jump ahead of a 10-3 UCLA or 10-3 Indiana or 9-4 Oregon if those any of those teams make the championship and lose.

Even though the Big Ten doesn't have a team ranked in the top 8 for the first time ever in the CFP rankings era, there was another beneficial thing league-wide as two Big 12 teams (No. 10 BYU, No. 11 Texas Tech) were the "last two in" — opening the door for any loss (including in the Big 12 Championship) by either school to potentially knock them out and push one of those other Big Ten teams into the field.

(Remember, the No. 12 team will be the highest Group of Six team, so Iowa needs to be in the top 11 to qualify.)

Bottom line, this was a much more encouraging night for Iowa than a week ago, when the Hawkeyes were kept at No. 15 leapfrogged by two Big Ten teams despite a 21-point win over Purdue. But Nebraska's 31-28 win over then-No. 14 Ohio State helped Iowa two-fold, as it knocked out the Buckeyes from the CFP and moved the Cornhuskers into the rankings.

So, we'll see a ranked matchup — No. 13 Iowa vs. No. 25 Nebraska — at 11 a.m. CT on CBS on Black Friday at sold-out Kinnick Stadium. Buckle up.

Iowa head coach Kirk Ferentz has been drilling into his players that if the Hawkeyes lose, all discussions end. If the Hawkeyes beat the Huskers, then they can scoreboard-watch and dream … for either a Big Ten title-game berth (likely vs. Oregon, if it happens) or for an at-large spot with the right losses by other teams.

"I've encouraged our players to spend their time and their energy focused on getting ready for this game, because it's another big challenge," Ferentz said. "None of them have been easy, and this one certainly won't. We're playing a really good football team. We'll have to be at our absolute best to have an outcome go our way. We're aware of that, and right now we're doing that work. That's really where our focus is as a team and as a program."
