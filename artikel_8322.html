House Hacking – 10 Ways to Cut Your Housing Cost, Sorted by What You Have

The biggest version starts when you buy. An FHA loan, a mortgage the Federal Housing Administration insures, lets you put as little as 3.5% down on a two- to four-unit building if you live in one unit and your credit score is at least 580.

The rent helps you qualify, too. The Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), which sets the FHA’s rules, lets the lender count 75% of the other units’ expected rent as your income. That rent is the appraiser’s estimate or the amount in the leases, whichever is lower.

Which ones you can use depends on what you have, so the ten below are grouped that way: a home you’re about to buy, a home with room to spare, or no plan to buy at all.

Your home is one unit, and the rent from the others goes toward the mortgage. A three- or four-unit building has one more test to pass. Under HUD’s self-sufficiency rule, the appraiser’s estimate of rent for every unit, yours included, minus at least 25% for vacancies and upkeep, has to cover the full payment of principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

The catch is that you become your tenants’ landlord and neighbor, the one they call when something breaks. HUD also requires you to move in within 60 days of closing and plan to stay at least a year. The contract HUD has you sign bars renting any part of the building for less than 30 days while the loan is insured, so short-term rentals are out.

Get preapproved by a mortgage lender that makes FHA loans before you tour buildings, and ask how it will count each building’s rent.

Buy a home that needs work, fix it up while you live there, and sell once you qualify for the home-sale tax break. The IRS lets you leave up to $250,000 of profit out of your income, or $500,000 on a joint return, if you owned the home and lived in it for at least two of the five years before the sale.

The tax-free profit is what pays you back for the years of mortgage payments. The risk is living through the work while repairs run over budget, which cuts into the profit.

Make your offer depend on a home inspection, and use the inspection period to get contractor bids, so you can back out if the work costs more than you planned.

A spare bedroom is the quickest start, since the room already exists. In SpareRoom’s rent index for the third quarter of 2026, average roommate rent across 26 metro areas ran from $756 a month in San Antonio to $1,543 in New York.

Price your room against listings near you, then run a credit and background check on each applicant and sign a written room agreement.

Short-term guests pay by the night, so the income rises and falls with your bookings. Airbnb says its typical U.S. host earned about $15,600 in 2025, and what you’d earn depends on your city, your price, and how many nights you fill.

Airbnb is putting its hosts on one combined service fee, which takes 15.5% of what most hosts charge for a booking out of their payout. Cleaning and restocking between guests take your time, and your city may require a permit, cap the nights you rent, or ban short-term rentals. An FHA loan on a two- to four-unit building or a home with a second unit rules them out too, under HUD’s 30-day contract.

Read your city’s short-term rental rules first, then price your space against similar listings nearby.

An empty garage, driveway, or shed can bring in rent from someone storing a car, a boat, or boxes there. On Neighbor, you choose the price and get paid each month, and the company takes a processing fee of 4.9% plus 30 cents out of each payout.

The risk is strangers coming and going on your property, so set access hours and list what can’t be stored. Neighbor bars renters from living or working in the space and includes $1 million in liability protection with each reservation, but check your homeowners association’s rules and your own insurance first.

To set your price, check what a self-storage unit of the same size rents for near you, and come in below it.

Whether hosting pays depends on the student’s program. A high school exchange student on a J-1 visa comes through a sponsor the State Department designates, and federal rules bar sponsors from paying host families.

Hosting a student in 12th grade or below under a written agreement with a qualified organization can earn a tax deduction instead. IRS Publication 526 lets you deduct up to $50 for each full month the student lives with you, if you itemize.

Some colleges pay host families to house international students in a homestay program. Green River College in Washington, for one, pays $1,000 a month for students 17 and older and $1,100 for 16-year-olds, and warns hosts not to count on it as income, since a student isn’t guaranteed.

Ask the colleges and language schools near you whether they run a homestay program and what it pays.

An accessory dwelling unit (ADU), a second, self-contained home on your property with its own kitchen and entrance, rents like any apartment and keeps the tenant out of your living space. The catch is the cost of building it. In California, a 2021 UC Berkeley survey of ADU owners put the median cost of building one at $150,000, though 37% cost less than $100,000.

ADUs there rented for a median $2,000 a month. At those medians, a $150,000 unit takes about 75 months of rent to pay for itself, before upkeep, taxes, and empty months.

Zoning decides whether you can add one at all, and permits, utility hookups, and a higher property tax bill add to the cost.

Before you pay for plans, ask your city’s planning office whether your lot allows an ADU and what it requires.

Buying a home that already has one skips the build. An FHA lender can count 75% of its expected rent toward your income, up to 30% of the income you qualify with, and the same 30-day rental contract applies.

Before you make an offer, ask the city whether the guest house is a permitted dwelling, since an unpermitted unit may not be legal to rent.

Apartment owners hire resident managers to live on site and run the property day to day, from screening tenants to handling repairs. Under a resident manager agreement, the apartment is part or all of the pay, rent-free or at a discount, and you agree to move out when the job ends.

If the job requires you to live there, the apartment can be tax-free. Under IRS Publication 15-B, lodging stays out of your wages when it’s on the employer’s premises, provided for the employer’s convenience, and a condition of the job.

The trade is that work and home are the same building, so tenant problems don’t wait for business hours.

Search job listings for “resident manager” or “on-site manager,” and ask each employer whether the unit is free, discounted, or paid for out of your wages.

Many other jobs come with housing, and the home ends with the job. Before you take one, add the rent you’d save to the pay, minus anything the employer charges for the room.

Rent is taxable income, usually reported on Schedule E, the IRS form for rental income. You can deduct the costs of renting, including depreciation, a yearly deduction that spreads what the rented space cost you over a set number of years. When you rent part of your home, IRS Publication 527 has you split shared costs like mortgage interest and heat between the rented space and yours, usually by rooms or square footage.

Short stays get a break under section 280A(g) of the tax code. If you rent out the home you live in for 14 days or fewer in a year, the IRS says you don’t report the rent, and you don’t deduct rental expenses either.

Selling brings two more rules from IRS Publication 523. A room you rented inside your home doesn’t cost you the home-sale tax break, but the part of your profit equal to the depreciation you took, or could have taken, is still taxed. A separate unit you rented out, like the other half of a duplex or a guest house, generally doesn’t get the tax break at all for that part.

Ask your city’s zoning or planning office whether your plan is allowed at your address. Read your homeowners association’s rules, which can ban rentals or set a minimum lease length, and ask your lender how renting part of the home fits your mortgage.

Then call your insurance company. The Texas Department of Insurance warns that most homeowners policies won’t pay for damage to a rental, or will pay only part, and that landlord insurance is built for long-term leases, not short stays.

Get the city’s zoning answer, the association’s rule, and your lender’s and insurer’s terms in writing before the first renter moves in or you make an offer.
