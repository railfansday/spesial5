Justice Cody Hiland Graduated Law School in 1998 (Or 1999?) And Was Admitted To The Bar in (February?) 2002

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Today Justice Cody Hiland appeared before the Senate Judiciary Committee. Senator Durbin quoted my blog post which called him unqualified, then totally botched the question. Durbin referred to me as a "conservative law professor," so he has the same problem as the New York Times. Alas, instead of asking why Hiland decided to air private conversations with law students, Durbin simply asked Hiland about temperament.

None of the other senators gave him even the slightest tough question. It's a shame, because he likely would have had trouble. This was one of the least polished circuit court nominees I've seen. Hell, Hiland made Todd Butler look like a pro. Is the process over? Well, I came across this posting from the retired senior editor of the Arkansas Times:

Would news that a nominee for a federal judgeship twice failed the bar exam change the tenor of loud opinions on the exam rigging scandal?

For starters, it isn't entirely clear when Hiland graduated law school. Here is the line from his questionnaire. Is it 1998 or 1999? The stray 1999 is odd. Maybe he received the JD in 1998 but needed some more coursework to graduate?

According to the University of Arkansas Little Rock Bowen School of Law, Hiland graduated in 1998. Anyway, something is off here.

According to his Senate Judiciary Committee Questionnaire, he was admitted to the bar in 2002, but he does not indicate if he took the July or February administration.

I was able to find an article from the Arkansas Democrat Gazette from March 30, 2002 which indicates that Hiland passed the February 2002 bar exam. As a general rule, people take the February bar when they either (a) had some sort of conflict for the July bar or (b) failed the July bar.

Between 1997 and 2000, Hiland held various positions in the office of Governor Mike Huckabee. None required a JD. From 2000 to 2002, he worked as a Staff Attorney at the Arkansas Transitional Employment Board. It isn't clear to me how he could be a Staff Attorney before he was admitted to the bar. In 2002, after Hiland was admitted to the bar, he began as an associate at a law firm in Paris, Arkansas.

Usually, law school graduates immediately take the bar exam. Perhaps Hiland was too busy serving as an aide to the governor to do so right away. Maybe he took it for the first time in February 2002, either two or three years after graduating from law school.

If only President Trump asked Katie Zacharia when she passed the bar. It might be worth asking Hiland about how many bar exams he has taken in a Question for the Record.
