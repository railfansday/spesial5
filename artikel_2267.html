Turki Alalshikh blame rejected as Derby County crisis deepens

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

It has been a nightmare start to the season for Derby County in the Championship.

After missing out on the play-offs on the final day of last season, Derby would have been hoping to challenge for promotion once again this campaign, but John Eustace's men currently find themselves at the wrong end of the table.

Derby owner David Clowes has made no secret of his desire to bring fresh investment into the club in recent years, and it looked as though a cash injection was imminent after Saudi billionaire Turki Alalshikh agreed to purchase a controlling stake in the club.

The proposed deal had been given approval by the EFL and the Independent Football Regulator, but Alalshikh unexpectedly withdrew from the takeover in August, claiming that, as the campaign had already started, he had "no time to prepare the team or the club for the season ahead".

While the Rams went on to make further signings after Alalshikh's takeover deal collapsed, including the permanent addition of midfielder David Ozoh for a fee of around £4 million, head coach John Eustace later claimed that the saga caused the club to lose "a lot of momentum on players that we were looking at".

After the off-field drama over the summer, Derby have made a disastrous start to the season on the pitch, and they head into the November international break sitting in the Championship relegation zone, with pressure building on Eustace as increasing numbers of supporters call for a managerial change.

When asked if Alalshikh's takeover falling through at the last minute is to blame for the club's poor start, FLW's Derby County fan pundit Shaun Woodward admitted that it is a contributing factor, as well as the recent injury crisis, but he insisted that a managerial change is the only way to get the Rams out of their current predicament.

"There is definitely an element of blame on Turki Alalshikh for pulling out so late in the day, but we can't be pinning it all on the takeover," Shaun told FLW.

"John Eustace still has a more than capable squad at his disposal.

"The majority of that squad was good enough to get us to an eighth-placed finish last season.

"Yes, it might have affected the transfers he had pencilled in, and we had to then go for plan B or plan C transfers, but the nucleus of that squad is still far too good to be in the position that we find ourselves in now.

"Yes, you can also blame injuries to our backline, but, again, the players that have stepped in should be doing better, they are still Championship players.

"They are not covering themselves in any glory whatsoever, and you can say the same about Eustace and his staff because they haven't been able to motivate the players, their tactics seem wrong, he doesn't sound very inspiring in his post-match interviews, and he looks defeated.

"The players look lost out on the pitch, but, all of that said, you can blame Eustace as much as you want, and he will lose his job because of this as the buck stops with him, the players have let him down massively as well.

"The last couple of performances especially at Millwall and at home to Norwich, the players have downed tools, they have given up.

"They are very much playing in individual mode, where they don't want to be made the scapegoat, they are hiding on the pitch, we can all see it as fans.

"No one is looking like they really care, and it's sad to see because there's still a nucleus of players out on that pitch that should be far better than 23rd in the league with the lowest goals scored and the highest goals conceded.

"We're not creating anything, we're gifting teams goal after goal, so I think there's an element of everything involved here.

"Yes, the takeover didn't help, maybe the transfers weren't the ones we wanted necessarily, but I still felt we had a good enough squad.

"Injuries haven't helped the backline, but the players that have come in haven't stepped up, and the more experienced players in our squad haven't led by example.

"It's just been a shambles, and, let's be real, the warning signs were there in pre-season, we looked lathargic and unfit, and that's carried on into the season.

"Until we get a new manager, I just can't see it changing, and, even then, it's going to be tough for someone to come in with the current group.

"The confidence is rock bottom, someone is going to have to come in and change formation, change personnel, rip up the script and start again because right now, we're only heading one way and that's League One."

While Alalshikh agreed a deal to invest in Derby and went through the EFL and IFR approval processes, Clowes and Eustace must have known that there was always a chance his takeover could collapse, particularly given that his previous interest in other Championship clubs, such as Bristol City and Millwall, had failed to progress.

Alalshikh's late withdrawal would inevitably have caused some disruption to the Rams' transfer plans, but Eustace still has a competitive squad at his disposal, which includes many of those who helped the club to an eighth-placed finish last season.
