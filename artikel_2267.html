Huddersfield Town and Middlesbrough won’t believe what's happening at non-league side

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

It's not uncommon for ex-EFL stars to wind down their careers in non-league, but what's happening at Harborough Town, especially, will catch the eye of Middlesbrough, Huddersfield Town and neutral fans, too.

Harborough are one of the fastest-rising non-league sides right now, having only just joined Step 6 in the non-league ladder in 2010. This season, they're competing in the National League North (Step 2) for the first time in their history, after winning the Southern League Premier Central last year.

With the likes of Morecambe, Macclesfield FC and Hereford now competing with them in league action, the need for extra quality and some ex-EFL professionals was needed in the summer, and continues to be needed as the year goes on.

And that led Mitch Austin's side to form quite the peculiar strike partnership.

Harborough hit headlines ahead of their first season at National League North level by securing the arrival of Britt Assombalonga.

The 33-year-old is still technically Middlesbrough's record signing, having arrived from Nottingham Forest for £15 million back in the summer of 2017. However, Will Lankshear will be set to break that once the add-ons in his move from Tottenham Hotspur are activated.

It's safe to say that Lankshear will probably be better remembered in a Boro shirt, but Assombalonga wasn't exactly a dud. He scored double figures in three of his four Championship campaigns at the Riverside, including 15 in his debut year to help his side reach the play-offs.

After leaving Teesside in 2021, the 33-year-old bounced around Turkish clubs, in between a short stint at Watford, before playing a small part in Barnet's season last year in League Two, and has now dropped a couple more divisions to play for Harborough.

Now, though, he has some competition up top in the form of Kyle Hudlin, the former Huddersfield man and widely recognised as the tallest outfield player in England, standing at six-foot-nine.

Hudlin signed for Huddersfield off the back of scoring in the National League play-off final for Solihull Moors in 2022. He made just 10 appearances for the Terriers, spending the majority of his time out on loan, including a stint at Newport County, where he scored six in 27 League Two appearances in 2024/25.

After a year in Vietnam, he's returned to England and has signed for Harborough.

Harborough have been on quite the meteoric rise, with four promotions in 15 years, the final three of which have come in the last five seasons.

Their first in the National League North has been steady, with Mitch Austin's side sitting two points above the bottom four in what is turning out to be yet another very competitive Step 2 campaign, after last season saw two sides relegated on 50 or more points.

Assombalonga has been in and out of the side, owing to a few niggling injuries, but has two goals and two assists in six league starts this season.

Harborough do have a small excuse for a slower start than in previous years, though, as they've gone through three qualifying rounds in the FA Cup to reach the First Round proper for just the second time in their history, and for the second time in three years.

Those who remember them from two years ago will know that they took League One side Reading all the way at the Select Car Leasing Stadium in the second round, losing in extra time after drawing 3-3 with them after 90 minutes.

This season, they travel to another third-tier side in the first round, with Mitch Austin's men heading to Leyton Orient. They'll be wanting to cause another scare, and even follow through on upsetting the O's this weekend.

It's not only Assombalonga and Hudlin who have EFL reps under their belts in this Harborough side. Behind them is former three-time League One champion Michael Jacobs, who has over 450 appearances in the Football League to his name and was playing at Northampton Town last year.

It's those players who will be called upon by the Bees to help them through difficult matchups, not only in the league against ex-league sides, but also in FA Cup bouts against teams three divisions higher.

With the amount of ex-EFL scoring power in the squad now, there'll be an expectation for the club to start firing more in the National League North, of which their 13 goals are a divisional low.

As and when they do, Huddersfield and Middlesbrough fans may keep a small glancing eye on whether it's either of their former pro players doing so. And if they both combine against Leyton Orient on Saturday, it's sure to get some traction amongst footballing circles, too.
