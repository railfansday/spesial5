#PostPulpit mailbag: What’s wrong with the Patriots defense?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Behind back-to-back blowout losses and losing Drake Maye to injury, the New England Patriots season has quickly derailed over the last two weeks. Now, they have plenty questions to answers after a much needed bye week.

So, let's get into some of those in this week's edition of the #PostPulpit Mailbag.

What has gone so wrong with the defense? They're relatively healthy and have a lot of the same personnel as last year. Kuhr did a great job last year and really earned the official promotion to DC. So what happened?

Along the same lines, do you think Gonzo is really the type of player who, now that he's gotten his payday, is going to sit on his laurels for the remainder of his career? – TDDsDad

Is the defense's biggest problem the square peg or round hole – scheme doesn't fit players or players don't fit scheme – spyponder90

The Patriots defense has a litany of problems right now. Here's how we'd break them down:

Zone coverage: Statistically, the Patriots are the worst zone coverage team in football, ranking dead last in both EPA and passer rating allowed in zone coverage. Defensive coordinator Zak Kuhr was a step ahead of opposing offenses earlier this season with his disguises in the secondary, but teams have caught on while New England's defensive backs have failed to live up to their potential.

Some of the issues in zone coverage also tie back to the pass rush. The Patriots rank 20th in the league in four-man pressure rate, giving opposing quarterbacks time to find the voids in coverage. To generate pressure, New England has had to blitz, which then leaves more space for the offense to attack with at least one defender taken out of coverage — as seen on Jahmyr Gibbs' 50-yard reception last week.

Man coverage: The logical follow-up would be to play more man coverage, especially with New England's talented cover corners. The Patriots already rank near the top of the league in man coverage rate, but no team can rely on man coverage for the majority of a game for several reasons. Without an effective changeup, opposing offenses have game-planned for man coverage and exploit it with beaters such as stacks and bunch formations. On top of that, the talented cover corners have again not been playing to their potential.

Effort/tackling: New England is not a poor tackling team by the numbers, but the defense often doesn't appear to be rallying to the football — a concern when Mike Vrabel has made effort and finish his No. 1 non-negotiable. Opponents are piling up yards after the catch and yards after contact against New England. That reached a new low last week, when the Lions generated 220 yards after the catch and another 135 yards after contact on the ground.

The effort doesn't always show up on film, either. The defensive line allowed Detroit to average 4.5 yards before contact (!). Defenders in the secondary often take poor angles and seem more focused on ripping the ball out than getting the ball carrier to the ground.

Coaching: Lastly, when it comes to Christian Gonzalez, there is too much evidence to suggest he isn't an elite cornerback in this league. The fact that he — along with other talented players such as Milton Williams and Craig Woodson — has regressed makes it difficult not to point the biggest arrow at the coaching staff. It might be time for the staff to go back to the basics to try and get things corrected.

Do you feel the coaching staff is getting a pass on this season's regression because of all the injuries? – Ricky Hot Pants

They shouldn't be, at least on the defensive side of the ball. This is the second most expensive defense in football that has largely been healthy outside of missing Christian Gonzalez and Carlton Davis for a handful of games. The results have been terrible over the last month.

And while I'm not putting Mike Vrabel on the hot seat and believe he will be the coach next season, it's concerning that his message does not seem to be getting through to the players currently as they are not playing to his identity, such as effort and finish. Some of his comments in the media the past two weeks have also raised eyebrows.

Give us some Pats Pulpit O-line grades. How have player performed and who can be positive contributors next year. – Chu

Big picture first: The Patriots' offensive line ranks sixth in PFF pass-blocking grade but drops to 23rd in run-blocking. That largely matches the eye test. As a pass-protection unit, offensive coordinator Josh McDaniels has done a good job assisting his linemen with chip help, moving pockets, etc. Still, the group ranks near the top of the league in unblocked pressures allowed.

It's been a difficult season for this unit from a health standpoint, with 13 different offensive line combinations contributing to some of the communication issues and miscues. However, here are some individual notes on the healthy linemen:

Will Campbell: Back healthy, the second-year tackle has taken a positive step forward, adding more variety to his pass sets over the offseason. He still has a handful of non-competitive quick losses that need to be cleaned up, and it's worth noting that his two worst performances this season have come against top competition (Bills, Lions). Campbell does remain a strong run blocker.

Jared Wilson: The move back to center has treated Wilson well, as the 23-year-old has developed into an impressive one-on-one pass blocker after some early-season struggles. We'd still like to see his eyes improve when he's left uncovered, but his athleticism has stood out in space and at the second level.

Morgan Moses: It's understandable that fans want to see first-round pick Caleb Lomu get an opportunity, but nothing Moses has done this season warrants benching him. The 35-year-old has battled through injuries as usual and has allowed just 14 pressures and two quarterback hits through 10 games.

Are you a tanker Brian? Do you feel sacrificing your integrity as a sportsman to cheat out another team for their earned draft spot is acceptable? – PatsHowYouDoIt

Let's face it the Pats are mediocre this year. There is no saving this season. Should the Pats just cut bait and prepare for next season? – luckyfukikentucky

I'm #TeamTank in the right situation. Back in 2023, piling up losses was the best thing the Patriots could do to put themselves in position to draft Drake Maye. But now, with the coach and quarterback in place, I'd much rather see some fight from this team over the final seven games.

Hey Brian. Easy one today: Will the AJ Brown trade go down as the worst trade in franchise history? – Pat in Agawam

I'm not ready to go there yet because I do believe A.J. Brown can still be productive next season. He still looked like a difference-maker before suffering the high ankle sprain, which I believe derailed his season completely. He'll be 30, and the injuries have started to pile up, so he might not be the 1,200-yard version of A.J. Brown anymore. Still, I believe he can be a useful player moving forward. That said, giving up a first-round pick for that isn't great business regardless.

Jimmy G coming back would at least be fun, right? In a Kirk Cousins in Minnesota kinda way? – slunkywontergreen

Considering Jimmy G. hasn't really played football in multiple seasons and wasn't good when he last did, I don't think I'd have much fun watching. I feel like the 2016 Patriots team would agree with that assessment.
