$1,000 Would Buy About 12 Anthropic Shares at the Top of the Expected IPO Range. Would That Be a Smart Bet?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

History says the first few days of trading can be the most expensive time to buy.

Anthropic's initial public offering is finally almost here -- the AI start-up behind Claude plans to go public on the Nasdaq exchange on Nov. 19 under the ticker ANT, and plans to sell shares for between $70 and $78 apiece.

At the top of that range, $1,000 would get you 12 shares of Anthropic stock. But I wouldn't be in a hurry to buy right away.

Don't get me wrong -- I think Anthropic is going to be a good company to hold, and I plan to invest at some point. But I also think it's a mistake to rush in during the first few days of trading. And it could be a very expensive mistake.

Big IPOs attract a lot of attention. Retail investors tend to run up the stock price because they want in on the action, but often, those gains are short-lived.

Meta Platforms (META -1.20%) is a perfect example. Then known as Facebook, Mark Zuckerberg's company went public in May 2012 at $38 per share. By September that year, the stock had fallen to $17.55, a decline of nearly 54%. Of course, Meta has been a fantastic company in the last few years and currently is one of the biggest in the world, with a market cap of about $1.6 trillion. But I'm sure investors who bought at $38 wish they had waited and gotten their initial investment at less than $20.

More recently, Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +3.16%) went public on June 12. It priced its IPO at $135, and shares began trading at $150. The stock surged above $225 in just a couple of days before falling apart. And today, you can get the stock for about $137.

Anyone who bought SpaceX at more than $200 is still licking their wounds.

Neither example means Anthropic will follow the same path. But they demonstrate why buying immediately after an IPO can be unnecessarily expensive.

Anthropic shows many signs of being a winning investment. It reported $17 billion in revenue for the third quarter, up more than 12-fold from a year ago. More than 11,900 of its customers generate at least $100,000 in annual revenue for Anthropic.

Then there's the other side of the coin -- Anthropic's latest S-1 discloses approximately $900.9 billion in infrastructure and other purchase and lease commitments extending over nearly two decades. That's a tremendous financial obligation, even for a company growing as quickly as Anthropic.

I like the company's business prospects, and its revenue growth gives investors plenty to be excited about. But buying a promising company and paying a sensible price for its shares are two different things.

I'd rather let Anthropic establish a trading history, allow the initial excitement to settle, and see where its valuation lands. There's no reason anyone’s $1,000 has to be invested on the first day.
