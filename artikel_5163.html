Protesters Arrested Outside Nasdaq as Activists Oppose Anthropic IPO Over AI Bubble Risks

At least four protesters have been arrested outside Nasdaq’s Times Square headquarters Thursday as Anthropic celebrated its $81.34 billion initial public offering, according to a press release shared with Decrypt by organizers of the #StopTechOligarchs demonstration.

The Stop Funding Billionaires campaign accused Anthropic and its early investors of benefiting from an AI boom that could leave ordinary retirement savers exposed if share prices tumble. Organizers called on pension funds and other large investors to stay away from the company's stock.

“Teachers, firefighters, civil servants will pay for that loss,” said Jose Gonzalez, an activist with Stop Funding Billionaires and New York Communities for Change, in a statement. He argued that workers have little control over how large institutions invest the savings they depend on for retirement.

The concern centers on faster rules for adding new companies to major stock indexes. Under Nasdaq’s fast-entry policy, an eligible IPO large enough to rank among the Nasdaq-100’s top 40 companies can enter the index after 15 trading days, allowing index-tracking funds to buy the shares sooner than under older rules.

That does not mean all pension funds or 401(k) plans must purchase Anthropic shares, let alone buy them during the IPO. Funds tracking a benchmark that admits Anthropic would normally adjust their holdings, while other managers can make different choices.

Protesters also raised concerns about AI-driven job displacement, the water and energy used by data centers, and AI applications in surveillance and military targeting. Their claims about specific military and law-enforcement harms were allegations made by the campaign, not findings established by the release.

“I’m proud to join activists and community members out here today to stand against Anthropic and the tech billionaires who put profit over people,” said New York state senator-elect Aber Kawas in the organizers’ statement. Kawas also criticized AI’s military uses and the prospect of companies replacing workers with automated systems.

One protester identified by organizers as among those arrested called for a freeze on what they described as reckless AI development. “Don’t bail out Big Tech when their bubble inevitably pops,” the protester said.

Anthropic priced 980 million shares at $83 apiece, raising $81.34 billion in the initial offering. That exceeded SpaceX’s original $75 billion IPO sale, although SpaceX’s final offering was larger after underwriters bought additional shares.

The IPO also followed a sharp change in market expectations. Earlier this month, an AI-stock sell-off had led some investors to question whether Anthropic could command a valuation above $1 trillion, before demand pushed its IPO price higher.

The protesters had called for Anthropic to halt its IPO, even as the offering went ahead. The campaign is also urging pension funds, university endowments and other institutional investors not to buy shares in companies it says undermine workers’ rights.
