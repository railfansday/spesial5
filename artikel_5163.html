CFTC Makes Historic Move With Blockchain Derivatives Approval

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) has opened historic doors for blockchain technology adoption in traditional financial markets as it approves exchange and clearinghouse applications for blockchain-based derivatives market infrastructure.

On Nov. 16, CFTC Chairman Michael Selig announced that the commission had given the green light to exchange (designated contract market or DCM) and clearinghouse (derivatives clearing organization or DCO) applications for a company using blockchain technology for the execution and settlement of derivatives contracts.

As per the official statement, Selig considers this a major step forward for U.S. financial markets. He said that blockchain-based market infrastructure is compatible with current U.S. financial regulations.

"This approval makes clear that there is a regulatory path for companies that want to innovate and use blockchain technology to modernize how derivatives are traded and settled,"  he elaborated.

Selig pointed out that the U.S. derivatives markets have seen a number of technological advancements, from physical trading floors to electronic trading platforms.

Now, with blockchain technology, he believes there is an opportunity to further modernize financial market infrastructure without losing regulatory oversight and investor protections.

Notably, there is no technical architecture requirement imposed on derivatives exchanges and clearinghouses under the CFTC's current regulatory framework.

Therefore, firms that leverage blockchain-based networks can apply for regulatory approval under the same set of rules and regulations that apply to traditional financial market participants.

However, Selig also clarified that blockchain adoption is not a license to escape regulatory oversight.

Companies will still need to comply with rules covering anti-money laundering (AML), client protections, market monitoring, risk mitigation and other measures designed to protect the integrity of financial markets.

Simply put, the CFTC is looking to bring blockchain technology into regulated derivatives market infrastructure while maintaining the same standards as traditional financial market participants.

For some time, the CFTC chairman has been pushing for a scenario where U.S. companies developing emerging financial technologies can operate in the country without having to look to international markets.

The latest developments further reinforce the message that the U.S. is open for business for companies building blockchain-based financial market infrastructure, according to Selig.
