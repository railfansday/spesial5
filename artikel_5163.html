eToro Q3 Profit Drops 74% as Crypto Trading Activity Slows

TL;DR: eToro reported third-quarter net income of about $15 million, down from $57 million a year earlier, as crypto-related activity weakened. Its funded-account base continued to grow, highlighting the difference between attracting customers and generating trading revenue from them in any particular quarter.

A trading platform can gain users even when the market becomes less profitable to trade. That tension runs through eToro’s latest financial results, which showed weaker quarterly earnings despite an expanding customer base.

According to the company’s November 10 earnings release, third-quarter net income was approximately $15 million, a decline of about 74% from $57 million in the comparable period. Net contribution came to around $186 million, down 13% year over year, with lower cryptocurrency trading activity contributing to the drop.

At the same time, eToro reported 4.39 million funded accounts, an increase of 18%, and assets under administration of approximately $20.8 billion. It also announced a $250 million share repurchase authorization.

Brokerage and trading businesses earn revenue from a mix of activity, product pricing and other services. When customers trade less frequently, a growing account base may not immediately offset the effect. The relationship is particularly visible in crypto, where transaction volumes can shift quickly as market sentiment changes.

That is why the funded-account figure and the net-contribution figure should be assessed together. One shows a measure of customer acquisition and retention; the other gives more insight into the contribution earned from services in the quarter.

Assets under administration are also distinct from eToro’s own cash and corporate assets. They reflect client-related asset values that can be affected by price movements as well as client activity.

Lower crypto activity at one trading platform does not prove digital assets are in a long-term decline. Users may trade through different venues, switch into other asset classes or simply become less active during a choppy period. The business may also earn different amounts from different types of orders.

Still, eToro’s numbers offer an operating-business perspective on the volatility seen in public-market crypto demand. NewsBTC has been tracking recent Bitcoin ETF outflows as well as uneven demand for spot Ether funds. ETF subscriptions and brokerage transaction revenues are separate metrics, but both help describe how investors are participating in the market.

A fall in net contribution can also reflect conditions in noncrypto products. Readers should avoid attributing every change in a diversified platform’s results to Bitcoin prices alone.

The $250 million repurchase authorization gives eToro permission to buy back shares under its program, subject to the company’s terms. It is not proof that the full amount was repurchased on November 10, nor does it guarantee future share-price gains.

Repurchases can alter the share count and capital allocation over time, but their effect depends on execution price, cash available and how the company balances other investments. They are best evaluated alongside earnings quality and strategic spending.

Investors should watch whether funded-account growth leads to sustained activity and improved net contribution in subsequent quarters. The product mix matters: strength in equities or other services could help offset a quieter crypto market, while increased marketing and operating costs could limit the benefit.

For crypto observers, the report is a reminder that adoption and profitability do not always move together. eToro gained funded accounts, but the softer trading environment made it harder to translate that scale into quarterly profit.
