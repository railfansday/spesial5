‘True Family’: Nile Rodgers Remembers Duran Duran’s Andy Taylor

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

"RIP my dear friend. We were always happy when we were together. I love you," Rodgers writes.

You can’t choose you family. But you can choose your true family. That’s how Nile Rodgers remembers Andy Taylor, the founding guitarist of Duran Duran and The Power Station, who died this week following a years-long battle with prostate cancer, at the age of 65. Rodgers, like millions of music fans around the globe, is mourning the Englishman’s passing. “Dear Andy Taylor RIP. How many countless great days and nights did we have?,” he writes. “I was sleeping last night when I heard the news of your passing. #RIP my dear friend. We were always happy when we were together. I love you.”

Rodgers and Taylor go way back. The Chic mainstay and elite record producer worked with Duran Duran on many of their hits, a friendship that began with the British new wave legends’ first U.S. No. 1 “The Reflex” from 1984.

Chic’s bass player Bernard Edwards produced the 1985 debut album from The Power Station, which gathered Duran Duran’s Andy and John Taylor, along with English singer Robert Palmer and U.S. drummer Tony Thompson. Rodgers provided some informal assistance to the project, which yielded the hits “Some Like It Hot” and a cover of Marc Bolan’s “Get It On (Bang A Gong)”.

“#BernardEdwardsRIP loved you, and #TonyThompsonRIP loved you,” adds Rodgers, a cancer survivor. “Our two bands will be forever linked in the most special way. True family. Peace and eternal love.”

Rodgers shared a happy snap of himself with Andy, from somewhere, sometime in the 1980s, slapped with the caption: “The way I’ll always think of us…guitarist brothers.”

It’s been a heartbreaking month for Duranies. Earlier, the band’s founding drummer Roger Taylor announced he was hanging up his sticks due to worsening tinnitus.

Andy Taylor (no relation with John and Roger) had multiple stints in Duran Duran, scoring 13 hits on the Billboard Hot 100, including No. 1s with “The Reflex” and “A View to a Kill” in 1985. The group’s beloved second album Rio album (which housed “Hungry Like the Wolf,” “Save a Prayer,” “My Own Way,” and the title track) hit the Billboard 200 in 1982 and remained on the chart for a whopping 128 weeks.

He had two stints with Duran Duran, including their all-conquering run through the first half of the ’80s, during which he gave true grit to the band’s sound, both with his guitar and rockstar swagger.

Taylor was inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022 in absentia due to his illness, which his bandmates revealed for the very first during their induction speech.
