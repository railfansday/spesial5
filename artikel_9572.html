Austin, TX, USA, November 17th, 2026/CyberNewswire/--SpyCloud, the leader in identity threat protection, today released its SpyCloud 2026 Forecast Report, outlining several predictions for 2027 drawn from the company’s experts across security research, product, investigations, and data science, plus a survey of 50 security professionals at large enterprises. The predictions are ordered by expert confidence, with the most certain listed first.

AI is speeding up attackers and defenders alike, but organizations are adopting it faster than they can govern it. Attackers are using that gap to automate phishing and evolve malware, shortening their path to the access they want.

“The clearest pattern heading into 2027 is that attackers are gaining access through vectors that organizations already trust: active employee sessions, vendor connections, and even insider threats,” said Aurora Johnson, Director, Threat Research & Intelligence at SpyCloud. “Security measures like multifactor authentication are good, but defenders need to be mindful that they’re not enough these days. Criminals have developed new tactics to effectively bypass these protections.”

Cybersecurity Threats SpyCloud Anticipates in 2027

Several of these predictions point to the same shift in what attackers want: not just employee passwords, but the tokens, keys, and service accounts that connect SaaS platforms, vendors, and AI tools.

“With the proliferation of more SaaS and less on-prem services, NHIs have become as valuable if not more valuable than everyday traditional username/email password pairs,” said Joe Roosen, Senior Director, Security Research at SpyCloud. “Because of this, commodity infostealers will pivot to seeking out NHIs as a preference.”

Drawing on more than one trillion recaptured identity assets, SpyCloud helps organizations identify compromised identities and automate remediation before stolen access can enable session hijacking, account takeover, ransomware, fraud, and other cyberattacks.

“AI is making familiar attacks faster and easier to scale,” said Jason Lancaster, Chief Investigations Officer at SpyCloud. “Stolen sessions, API keys, and tokens already give criminals access to business systems, and as AI tools and agents hold more of those credentials, organizations need greater visibility into what has been exposed and the ability to remediate compromised access before attackers can exploit it.”
