Second Air Force National Signing Day Held at U.S. Capitol

When an American joins any branch of the United States military, they repeat an Oath of Enlistment to make the commitment official.

I do solemnly swear that I will support and defend the Constitution of the United States against all enemies, foreign and domestic; that I will bear true faith and allegiance to the same; and that I will obey the orders of the President of the United States and the orders of the officers appointed over me, according to regulations and the Uniform Code of Military Justice. So help me God.

On November 12, 2026, 64 men and women ventured to the Rayburn Office Building of the U.S. Capitol to take that oath and enter the Air Force and Space Force Delayed Entry Program. This swearing-in was the climactic moment of the second annual National Signing Day, an event hosted by the Air Force’s 317th Recruiting Squadron.

The concept of this event came from retired Air Force Master Sergeant and M&F Senior Military Editor Rob Wilkins, who said the idea was inspired by watching his son’s announcement for where he was going to play college football.

Wilkins explained, “I was watching this take place and thought to myself, ‘We should be doing something like this for the military.’”

Wilkins’ idea became a reality thanks to several people he worked with, including Congressman Don Bacon of Nebraska, Air Force Senior Master Sergeant Matt London, and others within the 317th. It made what was already a significant moment in the lives of these future service members even more memorable for them and their families who accompanied them.

Staff Sergeant Maddalyn Secrist was one of the recruiters on-hand for the event and was very involved with making the 2026 event a success. Secrist shared that having this on Capitol Hill took something local to the DMV area and gave it a sense of national awareness.

“It gives it more of a public view and gives these Airmen and Guardians the recognition that they need.”

Prior to their swearing-in, each person’s name was announced as they went to the table to sign on the dotted line and have their photo taken, reminiscent of the high school athletes who announce where they will play in college. Each new recruit was cheered by family, friends, and fellow American citizens as well as members of the Trump Administration like Deputy Assistant Secretary of War for Military Community and Family Policy Stephen B. Simmons and recently appointed Executive Director of the President’s Council on Sports, Fitness, and Nutrition, Jacob Phillips.

Among those new recruits was Kevin McAllister, who was one of the older members of the group. At 35 years old and with a family tradition of service, McAllister knew that being physically prepared was a must, which is why he committed to longer runs and more intense training in preparation for the day. He also had to mentally dedicate himself to the process and suggests others who may be considering service do the same.

“I had to find the right mindset. There was a lot of running and calisthenics, but it taught me how to use different muscles,” he said. Even though he was fully prepared to take the oath, being in the Capitol definitely added a level of gravitas to the day.

“We are surrounded by the place where laws are made. The history of the United States is here. Just getting to add this to my family legacy makes me look at the flag differently.”

Bacon addressed the group and offered words of gratitude and encouragement that could be traced back to his own 30-year military career before entering the House of Representatives. He also shared wisdom that could be beneficial to people from all walks of life, whether they are in the military or not.

“Be the best at the job you’re going to get,” Bacon advised. “Learn that job inside and out so your boss can go, ‘I can trust that person.’”

There is no official word on future Signing Day events, but Secrist hopes that recruiters can use highlights or social media posts from this day to inspire their own applicants and create a similar experience to showcase why being a part of the Joint Force is so special.

Secrist said, “We think other recruiters can see this event and get inspired to do something like this in their own squadrons.”

To learn more about the Air Force or Space Force, go to airforce.com or spaceforce.mil.
