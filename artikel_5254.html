Stock market crash fears intensify as Nasdaq plunges 10% and S&P 500 drops 8.6% in its worst week since April 2025.

Dow and S&P 500 enter correction territory, falling more than 10% from their record highs as Wall Street's sell-off accelerates.

AI and semiconductor stocks lead the crash, with Nvidia, AMD, Micron and other technology stocks facing heavy losses.

The US stock market crash intensified this week as investors aggressively dumped technology and artificial intelligence stocks, pushing major Wall Street indexes toward their steepest weekly declines in years. The Nasdaq Composite plunged approximately 10%, marking its worst week since the March 2020 pandemic crash, while the S&P 500 lost 8.6%.

Both the S&P 500 and Dow Jones entered correction territory, raising concerns that the AI-driven market rally may be facing a deeper repricing.

With technology stocks leading the decline, investors are now questioning whether the latest stock market crash signals a temporary correction or the beginning of a broader downturn.

Wall Street’s losses accelerated on Friday, with major indexes closing sharply lower after another wave of selling hit AI and semiconductor stocks.

On Friday, the S&P 500 fell 3.7% to close at 6,910.39, while the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 949 points, closing at 48,147.33.

The S&P 500 and Dow are now more than 10% below their recent record highs, confirming corrections in both indexes. The Nasdaq has also erased its gains for 2026.

The latest stock market crash is primarily driven by growing concerns surrounding the artificial intelligence trade. Investors have poured enormous capital into AI infrastructure, chipmakers and data-center companies.

However, rising valuations and uncertainty over whether AI investments can generate sufficient returns are prompting investors to reassess the sector’s growth expectations. The sell-off intensified following the market’s reaction to Anthropic’s public debut, which failed to restore confidence in AI stocks. Investors also remain concerned about interest rates, inflation and the possibility of tighter monetary conditions.

The result has been a sharp unwinding of crowded technology trades, with losses spreading beyond individual AI companies into the broader stock market.

Semiconductor stocks have suffered the most severe losses as investors reduce exposure to AI-related companies. Nvidia fell approximately 23% for the week, marking its worst weekly performance since 2008. AMD lost around 20.5%, while Micron and other chipmakers also faced significant selling pressure.

The semiconductor sector’s 17.1% weekly decline highlights the scale of the repricing underway in the AI trade. Companies that previously drove Wall Street’s rally are now becoming the biggest contributors to the market’s losses

The immediate outlook for Wall Street remains uncertain. The latest correction has raised concerns about valuations, AI spending and investor positioning, but a correction does not automatically signal a prolonged bear market.

Investors will closely watch whether technology stocks stabilize, whether volatility eases and whether companies can deliver earnings growth strong enough to justify their valuations. For now, the stock market crash has exposed the risks of Wall Street’s heavy dependence on AI stocks.

If selling continues across semiconductor and technology companies, the broader market could remain under pressure heading into the next trading week.
