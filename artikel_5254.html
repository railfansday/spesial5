Bitcoin rebounded from below $76,000 to test the $78,500 to $80,000 range after election results.

More than $230 million in Bitcoin call options were reportedly bought near the daily low.

Bulls need a decisive move above $80,000 to $82,000; support sits at $75,000 to $76,500.

Bitcoin’s sharp V-shaped recovery has dragged prices back toward $78,500 to $80,000 after an election-night slide below $76,000. The reversal followed the reported Democratic sweep of both congressional chambers, turning political uncertainty into a fresh market catalyst. But one strong session doesn’t erase the risks.

The initial reaction was negative. A Democratic-controlled Congress is generally viewed as less favorable toward digital asset legislation, yet a finalized result removed uncertainty that had kept some capital sidelined.

Investors are also weighing potential political gridlock. With a Republican president and Democrats controlling Congress, major policy changes could face resistance. The market analysis argues that reduced government borrowing could pressure the dollar and Treasury yields, potentially supporting scarce risk assets.

Derivatives activity added fuel. Institutional traders reportedly bought more than $230 million in Bitcoin call options near the daily low, while short sellers covering positions accelerated the upward move.

The rebound also followed a difficult October, when Bitcoin lost roughly 7% amid macroeconomic pressure and institutional ETF outflows. Oversold conditions may have left prices vulnerable to a sharp bounce once the election uncertainty cleared.

The immediate hurdle sits between $80,000 and $82,000. Bitcoin needs a decisive reclaim and close above that zone to strengthen the recovery case.

On the downside, $75,000 to $76,500 is now the key trend-support region in BTC. Holding above it would keep the broader macro structure intact.

For now, the Bitcoin price recovery has momentum, not confirmation. A sustained move above resistance matters more than one dramatic rebound.
