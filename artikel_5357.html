Elon Musk calls Jensen Huang's ouster from Nvidia a mistake — and regrets all that election spending

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Tesla and SpaceX CEO said no one could replace Huang as Nvidia's chief executive, comparing it to kicking "Michael Jordan off your team"

Elon Musk said Nvidia $NVDA made a poor decision in losing Jensen Huang as CEO and that he regrets the amount of money he put into the midterm elections, according to CNBC.

The Tesla $TSLA and SpaceX CEO spoke Wednesday with Andrew Ross Sorkin during The New York Times' DealBook Summit. "You've got a winning streak ... and then you kick Michael Jordan off your team," Musk said of Huang's ouster. "Doesn't seem like a smart decision to me."

Musk said no one could replace Huang. He said President Donald Trump has voiced the view that Huang should have stayed on. "I think the president has voiced publicly that he thinks Jensen should have stayed as CEO, and everyone I know thinks the same ... this is, at the end of the day, the Nvidia board decision," Musk said. "We're not on the board, right?"

On the midterm elections, Musk said he felt "pretty foolish" about spending "so much money and not seeing much for it," according to Forbes. "If I could rewind the clock, I would have spent less money," he said.

Musk said he wanted to stop any single party from holding power over the White House and both chambers of Congress. He said "maybe it would have been much worse without that."

Musk spent at least $474 million on midterm campaigns.

Musk also distanced himself from the MAGA movement. "I'm not MAGA," he said, and he added that the meaning of the label was unclear to him.

Musk had backed a petition asking Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang as CEO. Huang resigned on Nov. 12 amid reports that he had been unfaithful with at least two women. U.S. officials suspect one of them, Alice Huang, who is not related to him, of relaying information to China's intelligence services. Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO, and the board said it had enlisted an executive recruiting firm.

Huang struck a deal on Tuesday with the leaders of three House committees to sit for a closed-door, transcribed interview on Dec. 1. The Senate Banking Committee asked him to appear on Dec. 3, and Chair Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, said Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick have both agreed to testify. No hearing date has been announced. Huang said on Tuesday he would be honored to return as CEO if Nvidia's board decides it serves shareholders, employees, the company and America.
