Dan Campbell won’t cut out celebrations despite Aidan Hutchinson injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

After Detroit Lions defensive end Aidan Hutchinson tore his ACL during a sack celebration, a huge debate ensued about whether Hutchinson was being reckless and selfish by putting himself in danger with the excessive celebration. A ton of Lions fans grew up in the era of Barry Sanders modestly handing off the football after touchdowns, so they've been accustomed to seeing players "act like they've been there before" and have a relative disdain for showboating. And given that this fanbase also witnessed a very similar injury to linebacker Stephen Tulloch in 2014, it's not surprising that some are mad at Hutchinson.

But don't count Lions coach Dan Campbell among them. During his radio appearance on 97.1 The Ticket this week, Campbell was asked if he believed he needed to talk to the team about celebrations, given both Hutchinson's injury and the amount of unsportsmanlike penalties the team has taken this year. He gave a pretty emphatic answer.

"No, I don't believe that. I think what happened speaks for itself. We're not talking to eight-year-olds here," Campbell said.

Campbell then proceeded to tell a story about how he thinks he tore his bicep celebrating a play with just an emphatic arm-pump celebration, and that didn't encourage him to tone down the emotions after a play.

"So if you say, 'Hey, don't pump your arm next time, make sure you stay stoic,' this game is built off of emotion," Campbell said. "If you have to contain yourself after a play is made—I'm not talking about in the moment, in the game, because you've got to keep your head—then you're really becoming something you're not."

Ultimately, Campbell seemed to imply that this was just a freak accident. He's seen Hutchinson do a ton of other incredibly athletic things that test the knee more than this celebration. He just got unlucky in the moment.

"The number of moves that I've seen Hutch make and the number of plyometrics, explosive movements, the spins, the one-leg—the lateral movements, lateral bounces," Campbell said. "I'm just like—it was one of those things, man. I hate it.

"But, no, I think we all understand what it is. Let's be smart, but, no, I'm not going to say, 'No one celebrates anymore.'"

As for how the team will respond with Hutchinson now out for the year, Campbell said they'll always be on the lookout for more talent to add to the roster—including claiming Tyrus West off waivers—but they are going to need several players to step up in his absence. But he's confident.

"We're prepared to handle this storm. We are suited to handle this storm actually I think better tahn we were in '24. That's the good news," Campbell said. "Now, what has to happen, what can happen, and what will happen is that somebody's got to pick up the slack. Somebody has to fill that void, and it's not going to be one person, by the way. One person's not going to fill the void of Aidan Hutchinson.

"So it's going to require (Alim McNeill) Mac to be at his very best. It's going to require (DJ) Wonnum to be at his very best. It means Derrick Moore and Sky(ler) Gill-Howard are going to have to freakin' step up and elevate their game. And Tyleik (Williams), we're going to ask Tyleik to do more, too. So that's really what you're looking at, man. So, we've got resources here to handle this, but guys are going to have to fill that void. We've got to do it collectively.

"The good news is we're playing as one on defense. That really is our strength right now, we're playing as one unit."
