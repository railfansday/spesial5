Recruiting Roundup: 5-star can see himself as ‘hometown hero’ for Michigan Football

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Welcome to the Recruiting Roundup, our twice-per-week series breaking down the latest developments on the recruiting trail for the Michigan Wolverines. We'll keep you up to date on the top targets, the visits they take, the predictions that analysts make, and everything in between. Here's what you need to know today…

Antijuan Wilkes Jr. has been to Michigan eight times this year, including four game days at The Big House. Two days after his latest game day visit, the 2028 five-star Detroit Cass Tech offensive lineman put the Wolverines in his top eight. For the No. 1 player in the state in the Rivals Industry Rankings, there's an appeal to playing close to home that goes beyond those Saturdays.

"I love Michigan, I can't lie," Wilkes recently told The Wolverine's Ethan McDowell. "I'm from Michigan, so it'd be cool to be a hometown hero for sure."

Offensive line coach Jim Harding is his primary recruiter, but he's got some help, as Wilkes said Harding and Ron Bellamy text him every day. Michigan will have to keep at it, too, as Wilkes has already visited Texas A&M, Nebraska and Washington this fall. He also has a Kentucky trip up next and plans to see the Wolverines from the other sideline when Ohio State hosts The Game.

For now, Wilkes has a state semifinal to play with Cass Tech. He is comfortable taking the spring and summer to figure out where he wants to go.

2027 three-star offensive lineman Conner Rutherford was still considering an official visit to Indiana when he came to Ann Arbor the weekend of Nov. 7. Then the Chillicothe, Ohio native got to watch Harding work with his linemen. The Indiana official never happened, and two days ago, Rutherford committed to Michigan.

"I saw how coach Harding coaches his players, and I could see myself playing for him," Rutherford told 247Sports' Brian Dohn.

Rutherford committed to NC State in the spring and reopened his recruitment after the Wolfpack made a coaching change. He is now Michigan's fifth offensive lineman in the class.

"It's the right fit because I feel like I can get developed into the elite ball player I can be, while I get a great education," he said.

Rutherford (No. 839 overall in the Rivals Industry Rankings) plans to enroll early this winter.

The biggest change to Michigan's visitor list is who's missing. 2027 four-star safety and Kentucky commit Tristin Hughes was set to return with his family, but The Wolverine's Ethan McDowell updated his report to say Hughes is no longer visiting this weekend.

That leaves 2027 three-star Pitt wide receiver commit Jacob Thomas as the flip target to watch this weekend. Thomas, a Miami High receiver who has run the 100-meter dash in 10.34 seconds, already took an unofficial visit to Ann Arbor this fall. Now, the Wolverines get him on an official visit for their home finale about two weeks before signing day.

Thomas told McDowell he wants more time around the staff, and McDowell believes Michigan has a chance to flip him if it makes a push.

Among the juniors, keep an eye on Mississippi receiver Joshua Parker, a high school teammate of Michigan running back commit Tyson Robinson. Illinois corner Logan Thompson and Saline defensive back Marquis Ray are also expected. Brother Rice receiver Tyler Trusel is still deciding between Michigan and Nebraska for a visit this weekend.

Jackson Doman, the tight end in the 2025 class — technically 2027 class because of his two-year mission trip — that Michigan offered after he reopened his recruitment in June announced on Tuesday he's headed to Utah.

Doman originally signed with BYU in the 2025 class before leaving on an LDS mission. His family helped manage his recruitment while he was away.

A family source told UteZone's Dan Sorensen that Utah's success on the field, Doman's relationships with the coaches and the way his faith fit with the program all played a part. The former state wrestling champion is due back from his mission in February and will join the Utes next season.

That's tough news for Michigan, which only has one tight end coming through the 2027 class in four-star Colt Lumpris. Another tight end commit in the 2027 class, Christian Hansawl will join the program in 2029 after his two-year mission trip.
