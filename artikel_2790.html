Brian Flores vs. Kyle Shanahan is ultimate 'Sunday Night Football' chess match | Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Like you, me and millions of others, Phil Simms can't wait to see how it all goes down in Mexico City on Sunday night, when the NFL's most dominating defense collides with the league's top-ranked offense.

The Minnesota Vikings (7-2) have allowed just six touchdowns all season, yielding an NFL-low average of 14 points per game. The San Francisco 49ers (8-1) have scored more touchdowns (37) than any team in the league while amassing the most yards.

Something's gotta give. And oh, by the way: those big-time units just happen to be directed by two of the sharpest minds in the NFL universe, with Minnesota's D created by coordinator Brian Flores while 49ers coach Kyle Shanahan pushes the buttons for the prolific offense.

Complete with that matchup of wits, it's a subplot for "Sunday Night Football" (8:20 p.m. ET) that the folks at NBC are undoubtedly tinkled pink about.

"Without question, this is the best D-coordinator and the best O-coordinator in the NFL," Simms, the former Giants quarterback and Super Bowl XXI MVP, told USA TODAY Sports from his home in New Jersey. "If you try to argue that I would just go, 'You're stupid.'

"All I know isc in that chair, and I can't wait for this game."

If the names aren't moving enough – the 49ers surround Brock Purdy with Christian McCaffrey, George Kittle and Trent Williams, among others, while the Vikings bring Harrison Smith, Andrew Van Ginkel and Dallas Turner, among others – let a few more numbers speak.

The Vikings lead the league with 37 sacks and hold opposing QBs to the lowest efficiency rating at 69.5. The 49ers have given up the third-fewest sacks (13) while Purdy ranks second in the NFL with a 115.2 passer rating.

That efficient knack for Purdy will undoubtedly face its toughest test the season because the Vikings blitz at the highest rate in years (maybe ever), bringing extra rushers on more than 80% of the snaps. Purdy, in his fifth NFL season, can vouch for that. He was sacked six times and threw an interception in the last meeting between the teams, a 23-17 Vikings win in 2024. In 2023, Minnesota collected just one sack but intercepted Purdy twice in a 22-17 victory.

Shanahan said this week that experience has facilitated Purdy's growth to the point nothing will surprise him because there is little he hasn't seen. We'll see how that plays out. Flores' knack involves unpredictability that comes with designing blitzes that may not have shown up on video.

"Look, I played 15 years," Simms said. "I think I know every blitz, have seen it or whatever. But that's just not true. I watch the Vikings every week and it never fails. There's something new every week.

"I don't know if you can get ready for it in a week. That's one of their little secrets of power."

Still, as sharp as Flores is, there are other master strategists all across the NFL. Why is Minnesota's architect able to get away with such a blitz-heavy scheme – remember the tried-and-true football axiom, "live by the blitz, die by the blitz" – without getting burned?

While Shanahan mentioned the commitment to Minnesota's aggressiveness as vital to the effectiveness, listen to another Super Bowl MVP quarterback.

"They have so many smart players," Kurt Warner, who led the Rams to a Super Bowl 34 crown as triggerman for the "Greatest Show on Turf," told USA TODAY Sports.

"He does a great job teaching his guys the nuances, those details," Warner added of Flores. "It's, 'If we're going to do this, this is how it has to go.' But it's also having players with a great feel, great instincts. They understand the big picture. 'I've got to rush the C-gap, but I've got to know what the quarterback is doing and what the formation is.' They can add extra layers to it."

That includes the versatility that plays hand in hand with disguising intentions. Maybe no Vikings defender illustrates as much like Van Ginkel, the outside linebacker who has generated his team-high 8 ½ sacks on just 175 rush snaps, while also logging 103 snaps in coverage – more than any edge rusher in the league.

Of course, the 49ers offense has its own brand of versatility. McCaffrey, the NFL's leading scorer (80 points, including 13 TDs), has long been one of the league's most lethal dual threats with receiving skills out of the backfield on par with many wideouts. Deebo Samuel Sr. is the powerful receiver who frequently lines up in the backfield. Kyle Juszczyk is not your classic fullback, aligning all across the formation as another type of Swiss Army knife.

In some regards, including versatility, the Vikings defense and 49ers offense provide mirror images.

Which gets us back to Shanahan, long one of the league's best play-callers. As much as he marvels at play designs that create wide-open windows for passing targets, Simms notes the resourcefulness.

The 49ers have produced their big numbers this season – and tied for the most victories in the NFL – despite having the wide receiver corps decimated by injuries. Last year, Shanahan took the 49ers to the playoffs despite losing Purdy for several games on top of setbacks to Kittle and McCaffrey (in addition to the season-ending injuries to defensive stars Fred Warner and Nick Bosa).

"Kyle, he's going to do whatever it takes," Simms said.

He thought about the flow that Mac Jones had last season while filling in for Purdy and asked: "Look at Kyle Shanahan's career and tell me which quarterback has ever played bad under him?"

The lineup would include Kirk Cousins, back in Minnesota to revive the Vikings' playoff hopes. Matched against San Francisco's defense on Sunday night, he represents another layer of a subplot. Shanahan was Cousins' first NFL offensive coordinator when the quarterback broke into the NFL with Washington in 2012.

Here's to a great chess match. One thing for certain: There is no shortage of respect between Shanahan and Flores.

"Where do I start?" said Flores, using the same word – elite – to describe the 49ers' rushing attack, pass protection and play design. "All of it is elite."
