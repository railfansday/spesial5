Stats Rundown: 4 numbers from Mavs 116-114 win

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Mavericks escaped Salt Lake City with a 116-114 win over the Jazz on Sunday night, surviving a fourth-quarter collapse that nearly erased a comfortable second-half lead. Dallas got another strong night from Cooper Flagg, who finished with 28 points, while Kyrie Irving added 16 points and eight assists in just over 27 minutes. The Mavericks also got important production throughout the rotation, with Caleb Martin scoring 13 points and recording four steals, Zaccharie Risacher adding 15 points and eight rebounds, and Dallas finishing with 32 assists and 16 steals as a team.

The game was close early, but Dallas gradually took control behind its defense and ball movement. The Mavericks forced Utah into turnover after turnover and started to create separation in the third quarter, when Irving repeatedly set up Flagg and Daniel Gafford for easy looks. Flagg scored nine points in a stretch that helped push Dallas' lead into double digits, and by the fourth quarter the Mavericks appeared to have the game completely under control.

Then Svi Mykhailiuk nearly stole it. The Utah backup wing finished with 39 points and an absurd 10 made threes, catching fire late as Utah turned a 16-point deficit into a one-possession game with just over two minutes remaining. Dallas struggled to execute down the stretch, committing turnovers and failing to put the game away, but Flagg finally gave the Mavericks some breathing room by knocking down two free throws with four seconds left. Utah's final three missed at the buzzer, allowing Dallas to survive 116-114 despite an ugly finish.

The Mavericks finished with 16 steals compared to just six for Utah, and that activity played a huge role in creating the separation Dallas needed in the second half. Naji Marshall led the way with five steals, Caleb Martin added four, and Dallas consistently turned Utah mistakes into opportunities before the Jazz could set their defense.

It was especially noticeable early in the third quarter. Martin picked off a pass, Daniel Gafford forced another turnover moments later, and Dallas quickly pushed the lead into double digits. The Mavericks did plenty wrong late, but their defensive activity was one of the biggest reasons they had enough cushion to survive it.

This was one of Dallas' better ball-movement games.

The Mavericks recorded 32 assists on 43 made field goals, with Kyrie Irving leading the team with eight despite playing just 27 minutes. Flagg added four, Martin and Gafford each had four, and Marshall chipped in four more off the bench.

That balance mattered. Dallas did not need Flagg or Irving to dominate every possession because the offense consistently created good looks through movement and quick decisions. Irving was particularly effective as a facilitator, including a stretch in the third where he assisted on a Gafford basket, a Flagg dunk and then Flagg's three that pushed the Mavericks ahead 75-62.

This almost became the entire story of the game.

Mykhailiuk finished with 39 points and went an absurd 10-of-15 from three, keeping Utah alive long after Dallas appeared to have put the game away. He scored 21 points in the fourth quarter alone, hitting threes from increasingly ridiculous distances as Utah turned what looked like garbage time into a legitimate game.

Dallas led 114-98 with just over two minutes remaining. Mykhailiuk then hit another three, scored three free throws, buried another three with 37 seconds left, and hit yet another with 15 seconds remaining to make it a two-point game. The Mavericks survived, but they'll probably want to avoid repeating a game where one player nearly erased a massive late lead.

Dallas somehow won despite getting destroyed on the glass.

Utah outrebounded the Mavericks 50-34 and grabbed 13 offensive rebounds compared to Dallas' seven. Mo Bamba alone finished with 10 rebounds, while no Maverick reached double digits.

Normally, losing the rebounding battle by 16 is a pretty reliable way to lose a basketball game. Dallas survived because it compensated everywhere else. The Mavericks forced 16 steals, moved the ball well, and shot 38.1 percent from three. Still, the rebounding disparity gave Utah extra possessions and helped make the late comeback possible. Against better teams, Dallas probably does not get away with it.
