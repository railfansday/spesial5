Beaks & Geeks: Cardinals, Chiefs Winners & Losers: Arizona taken to the woodshed – again

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Arizona Cardinals are on the road against the Kansas City Chiefs, who are one of the best teams in the AFC as well as in the league. They have a great offense. Perhaps they are looking beyond the matchup against the Cardinals since they are playing the one-loss Buffalo Bills on Thanksgiving Day.

The Cardinals came into this game starting their fourth quarterback of the young season. The NFL record is five, so, there's that.

Arizona At the final gun, the Cardinals were never in the game as they were beaten pretty badly in the first half, then allowed the Chiefs to coast to a 34-7 win.

So, who played well for the Cardinals? Who didn't?

LB Jack Gibbens – Mr. Dependable for the Cardinals had another good day along the defense as he led all defenders with 10 total tackles. Stopped RB Emmett Johnson for just a two yard gain in the first quarter that ended up being three points instead of six. As WR Xavier Worthy was rumbling downfield in the third quarter, Gibbens came from behind to tackle the receiver after a 20-yard gain that could have been a touchdown. On a third-and-five with 2:31 left in the game, Gibbens nailed RB Nate Carter for just a two-yard gain, which forced a punt.

P Blake Gillikin – Probably the offense's MVP today. Had three punts downed inside the 20 with one on the three and another at the one yard line. Six punts for 241 yards with a 40.2 average and a long of 55 yards. He was more focused on pinning the Chiefs deep than his net average. Nice work.

WR Harrison Wallace III – Somebody has to be listed as having a great game, so here is the undrafted rookie playing well despite not being named the starter and coming up with good plays. In the first quarter with Arizona driving on a third-six, Wallace turned a short pass into a 25-yard gain to the KC 26-yard line. He got 10 yards in the third quarter and then on the same drive facing a third-and-five, he caught the short pass to the right for 10 more yards to keep the drive going. Led all receivers with 7 targets, 4 receptions for 57 yards, and a 14.3 yards per catch average. Nice day at the office.

Third-and-two stop – It looked like Kansas City was going to run away with this game when they faced a third-and-two with 7:36 left in Quarter 3. The ball was handed to RB Emmett Johnson who was stuffed by DTs Walter Nolen and Roy Lopez for no gain that led to a punt.

Second drive of the game – KR Max Melton got a decent return to set up Arizona at the Cardinals' 37. Two short passes gained a first down. Then Wallace hauled in the 25-yarder to the Chiefs' 48. A sack, then two short passes and two short runs placed the ball at the KC eight yard line as TE Trey McBride converted a third-and-five by gaining six. QB Josh Johnson found WR Simi Fehoko crossing in the end zone for six. The score was now 14-7 Kansas City. It just felt good at that moment, didn't it?

Covering #87 – With TE Travis Kelce being the Chiefs main pass catcher and a fav target of QB Patrick Mahomes, it would make sense for DC Nick Rallis to come up with a great gameplan to eliminate the talented tight end from the game, right? So, whose responsibility was to cover Kelce? Well, nobody.

And nobody covered him all game. That was the gameplan? Passes to Kelce moved the chains all first half, not to mention he scored two touchdowns, both he was wide open. On his first touchdown in the first quarter, LB Mack Wilson was trailing Kelce the entire time so all Mahomes had to do was zip it in there. With 2:27 left in the first quarter, Kelce gained 13 yards but was not covered. He caught a ball that got 14 early in the second quarter, and was tackled after getting good running yardage but no coverage on the pass. 13 yards on a TE screen. Another TD catch that he was open in the second quarter. Kelce was not challenged all day, and chipped, then released and found open areas all game long. 7 targets, 6 catches for 74 yards and two touchdowns.

CB Kei'Trel Clark – With 7:14 remaining before the half, Clark played well off WR Josh Downs, then bit on the arm cock of Mahomes. Downs then simply ran past Clark for a 26-yard gain. In the first quarter, Clark dropped a sure interception. He gave up nine yards in the second quarter to RB Emmett Johnson by yet again, giving a large cushion and 13-yards to WR Josh Downs on a third-and-10. That drive ended in another Chiefs TD. Five total tackles, mostly from chasing down the receiver after he made a large gain.

Offense first half – The Chiefs had 152 yards on offense in the first quarter and 295 at the half. The Cardinals had 13 yards on offense in the first quarter and 78 yards in the first half. 21 first downs for KC, five for Arizona. Nine rushing yards for the Cardinals whereas the Chiefs had 69. Trey McBride three catches for 17 yards. Time of possession: Kansas City – 20:06, Arizona – 9:54. Score: 27-7. Yeah, all of this in just two quarters.

Fourth and one play – As the second quarter was winding down, the Cardinals had a third-and-2 at their own 36 yard line behind just 17-7. A Bam Knight run made just one yard. Run out the punt team, right? Their own 37 yard line, correct? Down just 10 points, correcto? That would be a negative. Instead, a jet sweep by WR Simi Fehoko was ordered who gained nothing. It was Fehoko's first carry of the year by the way. Turnover of downs, and a subsequent Chiefs TD in just three downs that moved the scoreboard to 24-7. One word: completely dumb. Okay, that's two words.

Best quote of the game – As the Chiefs moved up and down the field and scored on all five possessions in the first half, TV announcer Ian Eagle stated, "The Chiefs are doing whatever they want."

RT Josh Fryar – The backup tackle had no answer for #56, DE George Karlaftis, who was in the offensive backfield all game it seemed. Johnson was sacked three times, as Karlaftis had 2.5 sacks, 4 tackles, 2 tackles for loss, and a whopping 5 QB hits. Fryar just could not stop the outside speed of Karlaftis. His sack with 11:08 left in the third quarter killed a good drive which ended in yet another Gillikin punt.

Last three games – The Cardinals offensive production in the final three games: 3 points, 6 points, and 7 points. With the escalation, does that mean this offense is improving?

CB/KR Max Melton – Had two good kickoff returns, one for 37 yards while the other was for 36 yards.

Chiefs RB Emmett Johnson – At Nebraska, Johnson was a First Team All-American and was named the "Big 10 RB of the Year" plus voted First Team All-Big 10. He was taken in the fifth round. Instead of taking an elite pass rusher with the third overall pick, or an offensive tackle, the Cardinals took Notre Dame RB Jeremiyah Love, who is on IR with repetitive ankle issues and has played sparingly. Johnson just ran for his second consecutive 100-yard game where he scored in both contests and has a rushing touchdowns in four consecutive games.

DE Josh Sweat – Was very active with pass rush although the only stats he registered was two QB hits. The Walter Nolen sack in the third quarter was because Sweat forced Mahomes out on a third-and-six and into the waiting arms of Nolen.

WR Simi Fehoko – Okay, he had the game's only points with the TD catch in the first quarter, but he also had several bad plays. On the fourth down run in Cardinals territory, he never any attempt to go forward and was stopped for no gain. Then with 6:48 left in the third quarter, he dropped a pass that would have converted into a first down into KC territory. Had five targets, yet only caught one?

QB Josh Johnson – Okay, he had one day of practice. We get it. Maybe the offensive coaching staff should have placed tape on the front of the receivers and tight ends helmets with their name on it so that the new guy could at least call them by name. He had his moments, and had some decent pockets, but was also pestered a lot with a bad offensive line. 18 for 31 for 155 yards, a 5.0 average per reception, sacked three times with an 82.1 QB rating.
