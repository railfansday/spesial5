'Never Down, Never Quit' Whippets crowned regional champions in Richland County thriller

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

MANSFIELD — Shelby head coach Rob Mahaney said it perfectly.

"They're never down, they never quit, and they just keep going."

The Whippets overcame plenty of adversity in its 14-7 Division IV Region 14 championship victory over fellow Richland County and Mid-Ohio Athletic Conference rival Clear Fork.

It's Shelby's third regional title in school history, joining the 2017 and 2025 teams, and second consecutive title.

Defense wins championships? Well, both Clear Fork and Shelby have relied on its defense all season long, and that didn't change on Friday, Nov. 20 at historic Arlin Field in Mansfield.

Both teams only gained one first down each in the first quarter. Brady Bowman came away with the first turnover of the day when he intercepted a Colt pass at Shelby's own 49-yard line, stalling a momentous drive with 4:47 remaining in the quarter.

Shelby drove down the field but turned it over on downs inside the Clear Fork 20 with 8:27 left in the second.

Then, Clear Fork gained some momentum. Quarterback Marcus Hoeflich connected with Trey Sellers for 30 yards. A couple plays later, Hoeflich escaped a sack and threw a 28-yard bomb to Nash Evans, giving Clear Fork a 7-0 lead with 4:49 in the quarter.

The score remained that way until halftime.

The Colts D has been a strength all season long and proved worthy by holding Shelby scoreless in the first half.

"I thought defensively we played outstanding, flying to the football and making things very difficult for a high-powered offense," said Clear Fork head coach Aaron Brokaw.

Shelby got the ball first in the second half, and immediately completed a pass to Brady Bowman, the school's all-time leading receiver, who had just one catch in the first. Some offensive momentum finally came along after quarterback Michael Shepherd took off for a run of 51-yards, but the Clear Fork D once again forced a turnover on downs. Then, Shelby immediately forced another turnover on downs.

Bowman took the ensuing punt return to the 10-yard line, which seemed to create just the spark the Whippets were looking for. Gavin Baker ran it into the endzone on the next play. Tie game heading to the fourth quarter.

That's when viewers got everything a regional championship game could be.

The Shelby D hunkered down, once again forcing a punt. Luke Blevins got the run game going again with a 51-yard run, but threw an interception to the Colts' Jackson Brubaker on a trick play two plays later.

If that wasn't game-changing enough, Clear Fork took a shot down the field on the very next play which was intercepted by Conner Hartz of Shelby.

"Our defense comes in everyday and practices hard, and hope that we can keep doing this," said Hartz.

The Whippets did not convert and faced a fourth-and-6 at their own 37-yard line with 6:44 to go in the game. Despite being a senior-laden team, the Whippet coaching staff called the sophomore, Blevins, name again.

Blevins ran to the 47-yard line on a fake punt. The next play, Bowman caught the ball into space and ran 43 yards. The drive ultimately ended with a 9-yard touchdown pass from Shepherd to Griffin Yeager, giving Shelby the lead with 5:08 remaining in the game.

"The adrenaline, just looking up and seeing the whole town cheering for you. The adversity we had was the same thing last week, down going into the half. Then, to come out with a better game plan and take over the game," said Blevins.

Then, the Shelby defense stood tough again. The Colts went backwards, turning it over on downs. But Shelby did the same thing. The Colts had one more possession to try to tie or win, with everything on the line and just 1:35 to do it. And they came out firing.

Hoeflich completed a 15-yard pass to Sellers and then ran for 11 yards. He then found Alex Schlosser for 18 yards. A short gain and an incompletion left the Colts looking at 3rd and 8 from the Shelby 11 with a minute left.

Michael Shepherd, who had completed 12-of-20 passes for 149 yards and a score and carried the ball 11 times for 66 yards in the game, stepped in to play defensive end on the final possession, something he hasn't done a whole lot this season.

He decided making plays offensively wasn't enough, sacking Marcus Hoeflich for a six-yard loss on third down. Clear Fork quickly threw a heave to the endzone, but it fell incomplete.

The much-anticipated showdown managed to live up to the hype.

"To see both communities come out and shine, it was awesome. It was a great experience," said Hartz.

Clear Fork's season ends at 10-3, marking just the second time in school history the Colts have been to the regional final.

"I think it was the kind of game and the kind of atmosphere that a lot of people thought it was going to be. It's a testament to the hard work and dedication of our senior class. They mean the world to the program as a coach. They've done everything we've asked them to do, and I couldn't be prouder of the young people they've become," said Brokaw.

This is the second straight week Shelby has used two fourth quarter touchdowns to win.

"Obviously Clear Fork has a good defense, but we believe in our defense as well. We trusted those guys and they came up and made plays for us. The way this season has gone, we've had to rely on that defense. We brought back two starters, so I would have never guessed we would have made it back to the final four like this, relying on the defense. Teams grow and evolve, and I couldn't be prouder of them," said Mahaney.

The still undefeated Shelby Whippets will play Urbana next Friday in the Division IV state final four. The location is yet to be determined.

"We're going to play in the final four, and there were a lot of people who didn't think these seniors would be able to do that. What they did is trusted one another, found ways even when they were backed up, and found a way to make it happen. I couldn't be prouder of these guys."
