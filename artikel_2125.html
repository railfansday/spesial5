Wolves can solve £12.7m transfer issue with Cardiff City star who is ‘different’

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Wolves have had a strong Championship season so far, with Cesar Peixoto’s side currently fourth in the table, just one point off top spot.

There was a big expectation at Molineux going into the new season, and whilst the board didn’t deliver on the major squad overhaul that they promised, there’s no denying Peixoto has a talented group.

All connected to Wolves will believe there is enough in the ranks to win promotion, although the first few months of the campaign have also shown that a few glaring issues need to be addressed in the January window.

For most fans, there are two priority positions for the recruitment team to target in the New Year - a winger and a striker.

Whilst you can have some sympathy for Wolves’ striker situation due to the serious knee injury that Raul Jimenez suffered, the reality is that none of the wingers at Peixoto's disposal are truly convincing right now.

Rodrigo Gomes falls into that category, even if there is a lot to like about the Portugal U21 international in terms of his energy and enthusiasm.

However, all attacking players are judged on their end product, and Gomes has managed just two goals and one assist in 11 games, and both of those strikes came in the same game.

At 23, you would’ve expected Gomes to be doing more to deliver on his undoubted potential. There was a feeling that he could have benefited from Wolves’ relegation with a more prominent role, but, at this stage, he has failed to take that opportunity.

It would be unfair to single out Gomes, as all the wingers at Molineux are not hitting the numbers that Peixoto would want.

Mateus Mane and Rafiki Said are yet to find the net, and the duo only have one assist apiece so far this season, although the Comoros international has done reasonably well since returning to fitness.

Jean-Ricner Bellegarde has operated out wide at times, but he looks uncomfortable, and he is another that has a poor goal return, with Bertrand Traore yet to feature in the Championship due to injury.

Only Fer Lopez is producing in terms of goals and assists, and even though he is capable of playing from the right, he prefers to start in the middle.

Therefore, a quick direct winger is needed to improve the balance of the squad, and to make Wolves more ruthless going forward.

January is a notoriously difficult month for clubs, and it will be no different for Wolves as they seek attacking reinforcements.

With promotion the aim, Wanderers must try to bring in players that can hit the ground running, so it would make sense to go for those with Championship experience.

And, one man who could help Peixoto is Ollie Tanner, with the Cardiff winger having enjoyed a productive campaign so far for the Bluebirds.

A return of three goals and four assists is a total that only Lopez beats at Wolves, which shows just how well he has done.

More importantly, Tanner has all the qualities that Peixoto wants from his winger. He will hug the right touchline, he is direct, he has pace, and he looks to beat his man at every chance, and was something of an assist-machine by setting up 12 goals from just 28 League One appearances as the Bluebirds finished second in League One last time out.

Plus, he is also capable of going down the outside to put a cross in, or cut inside onto his left to have a shot.

Tanner’s talent has been known for years, which is why he has been likened to Gareth Bale in the past, but it took time for the 24-year-old to justify that hype.

Obviously, those comparisons still seem a long way off, but there’s no doubt that Brian Barry-Murphy’s appointment has helped Tanner’s career, with the Irishman having previously claimed that the ex-Lewes man is ‘different’ with what he can do.

Wolves need someone who can help them achieve their objective of winning promotion, but they won’t want to sign a player that they would just have to discard if they did go up.

But, there will be a belief that Tanner could make his mark in the top flight, so he could be seen as a long-term solution for the Midlands outfit.

There are obviously areas of Tanner’s game that he must improve, but he has all the tools to cause even the better defenders problems due to his pace, power and ability to go both ways.

There is evidence that his decision making and composure have developed in the past 18 months, and, when you see Tanner play, you get the feeling that he still has levels to go in his game.

It’s going to be difficult to convince Cardiff to sell Tanner in January, but Wolves may have the financial muscle to tempt the Bluebirds, and if they could reach an agreement, it may turn out to be a shrewd move for the Championship high-flyers.
