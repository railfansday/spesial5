Jackery Launches Explorer 100D Portable Power Station. Maximum Capability For Carryon Compatibility

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Jackery just launched the Explorer 100D portable power station. This is Jackery’s smallest battery, giving a new level of versatility to power many of the devices people rely on most. Meanwhile, it integrates  the durability and safety people expect from Jackery’s larger models into a compact case.

The Explorer 100D has a capacity of 96 Wh (30,000mAh) which puts it just below the carryon limit for domestic and international flights. Meanwhile, up to 158 W of output is more than enough to charge a laptop. The 100D is compatible with advanced protocols including AVS, PD 3.1 and PPS, providing precise charging, particularly for Apple products.

The Explorer 100D measures 4.6” X 3.4” X 3.4”(11.6 X  8.3 cm X  8.5 cm), or about the size of a softball. It weighs 1.87 lbs. (850 g). This is a bit larger than the typical power bank, but much smaller than the typical solar generator. It is also smaller and lighter than the Explorer 100 Plus that it replaces, while also increasing in power output.

On the test sample model, I found the form factor very convenient. It is not small enough to fit in your pants pocket, but the rectangular case with rounded corners was easy to fit in a backpack or carryon bag. A cloth handle makes it easy to hold, and a shoulder strap is available. I had a credentials lanyard from a previous media event on hand, and it worked fine as a strap for the lightweight battery.

On the front of the battery are two 140 W USB-C ports and an 18 W USB-A port. The left USB-C port can both charge the battery and discharge to another device. The battery can also be discharged through the other two ports while it charges. The battery does not come with a charger, although a USB-C cable is provided. I was able to charge the Explorer 100D on a wide range of chargers, although lower powered chargers take a while to charge the large capacity battery.

The front display default shows battery charge percentage as well as input and output power. However, you can switch the display to also show power output to each port or battery health, cycles and temperature.

Unlike some of Jackery’s larger batteries, the 100D does not have cooling fans or vents in the case. The LFP battery is rated for 3000 full charge cycles, good for 8 years of daily use. This is far more durable than many Li-ion power banks, which commonly only offer a few hundred charge cycles. It is also a 50% improvement over the previous Explorer 100 Plus model that it replaces. Of note, while this model does comply with China’s strict CCC (China Compulsory Certification), the logo was not printed on the test model I received, although that may change in the future. Without the logo present, it could be taken away if travelling in China. However, the battery did have FCC and SGS certification printed on it, which are more relevant to most US customers.

The portable battery can function as a solar generator by accepting direct solar input up to 100 W. This makes it a bit different than many power banks. Solar panels connect through the USB-C charge port. which may require the purchase of an adaptor, depending on the panel used. Jackery’s lightweight, folding SolarSaga 100 Air could charge the battery in less than two hours. The battery can also take 12 V or 24V input from a car, boat or RV. Using a traditional USB-C charger, the Explorer 100D can accept up to 140 W of input power.

The Explorer 100D  test model came with 30% charge and reached a full charge in about an hour. While the battery was charging, I was able to charge my phone and another power bank off the output ports while the battery was charging. I initially charged the battery off the HomePower 1000 Plus V2, which has a 140 W USB-C output. With similar styling, it looked like a baby of the larger battery when placed on top. Of course, the battery would take longer to charge on lower power. However, it could be used to accumulate charge over time and discharge more quickly for a device like a laptop that needs more power than what may be available from a charge jack at a hotel room or airport.

While the battery will often be used to charge phones, the Explorer 100D ran my T-Mobile Wi-Fi router all day, with capacity left to charge my phone and tablet. When used with a laptop with a USB-C charge port, the small battery could power a remote office.

I was also able to run a small personal fan off of the Explorer 100D. With peak power draw at 5 watts, it didn’t pose much of a challenge for the battery. This would come in handy at an outdoor event or while fishing on a hot day.

On Halloween, I used the battery to power an old Bluetooth speaker for hours of handing out treats, while keeping our phones charged. The black and orange design fits well with the holiday decorations. By the end of the night, the battery still had over 90% capacity.

I also used the battery on a trip to an apple harvest festival. While charging ports were in short supply in the car, the Explorer 100D kept phone and camera batteries topped up. The rubber feet kept the battery from shifting around when placed on the center console.

The Jackery Explorer 100D launches as a two-unit bundle for $179.99 at select Costco warehouse locations from the start of November 2026. Individual units will then become available at Walmart for $99 on November 15th, just in time for holiday travel.

Frost & Sullivan recently named Jackery the World’s No.1 Solar Generator Brand. Jackery leads in solar generator sales volume globally and has pioneered multiple innovations in portable power. The Explorer 100D is the latest example. The company has a focus on providing solutions for customer needs with durable products that have exceeded my expectations in testing. After trying the Explorer 100D, I also find it to be more useful than I originally expected, using it in a wide range of applications. While the Explorer 100D clearly is not going to solve all of the world’s energy problems, it provides another power option that meets the needs of many customers while integrating well with Jackery’s expanding range of solar and energy storage products. As Steven Wang, CEO of Jackery Americas puts it:

Power support is often most effective when it’s highly portable and backed by comprehensive compatibility and a long lifespan. Developed for peak productivity, the Explorer 100D represents years of honed design and feature implementation, while maintaining focus on the top-quality engineering and unmatched consistency that make Jackery portable power stations must-haves for work and travel. Thanks to that collection of refined expertise, it’s among the longest-lasting ultra-portable batteries ever.

I look forward to putting the Explorer 100D to the test during some upcoming travel over the next few months. I also look forward to seeing what Jackery comes up with next in versatile, portable power.
