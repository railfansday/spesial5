Chelsea's slump in form unpacked: The stars who badly need to step up from Emi Martinez to Cole Palmer, what defensive horror shows mean for Xabi Alonso's job security - and the players in line to leave in January

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 12:00 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 12:02 EST, 23 November 2026

Chelsea are lurching towards a crisis after yet another loss in which they were bullied, this time by Leeds United.

That 4-1 hiding saw Xabi Alonso heckled, and his players booed at both half-time and full-time, arguably deservedly so for such a disastrous showing.

It has led to tentative speculation around Alonso's future as Chelsea prepare to travel to Oliver Glasner's Nottingham Forest on Friday night, not to mention questions about where he is going wrong and how he might arrest his team's slump in form.

Here, Daily Mail Sport tackles all the burning issues at Stamford Bridge...

Chief among Chelsea’s problems is the fact they have conceded 20 Premier League goals this season. It is as many as they have scored at the other end. So, as much as we have enjoyed their freescoring forward line this season, so too must we criticise their disastrous defending.

We know Alonso can organise a defence. His Bayer Leverkusen side only conceded 24 goals when they won the Bundesliga as unbeaten champions in 2023-24. Chelsea are now on the verge of overtaking that total before we even hit December.

Liam Delap, their former striker now at Forest, will be desperate to add to that tally this Friday, too.

Xabi Alonso is coming under growing pressure, with his Chelsea side failing to back up their promising early-season form

Levi Colwill was all at sea against Leeds as Chelsea shipped another four goals, taking their tally to 20 in 11 games

This morning, I watched back all 20 of the goals that Chelsea have leaked this term. There have been so many individual mistakes.

Like on day one, in the 3-2 win at Fulham, when an out of position Robert Sanchez let in Josh King's shot. And when Levi Colwill missed a defensive header which should have been routine, which led to Gonzalo Garcia scoring.

Chelsea got rid of Sanchez after that game, but Emiliano Martinez has hardly helped shore up this defence, as was apparent in the 4-1 loss to Leeds, when Colwill and Co were again all over the shop.

Until they cut out the silly mistakes, it is hard to see Chelsea competing at the top end of the Premier League.

Every manager at every club is sackable. Nobody is immune. But that’s not to say Alonso is suddenly up for the chop at Chelsea. I haven’t heard anything which would suggest those inside the club think finding a fourth permanent manager in the year 2026 is the answer.

Let’s not forget the overwhelming optimism when news broke of Chelsea striking an agreement to hire Alonso. It was an hour or so after the club had lost the 2026 FA Cup final to Manchester City. Recently, I took a look at the comments section of the story I wrote that day. It was full of excitement from Chelsea fans, and anger from Liverpool supporters at missing out on him.

Anyway, I think it’s too soon for us to start talking about Alonso’s job. He is in a bad moment, obviously. A very bad moment. Losing at home to Tottenham Hotspur and Leeds is a complete no-no for any Blues boss.

The supporters are more likely to curse his name than chant it right now, and it is hard to identify the style Alonso wants, with few discernible patterns of play to Chelsea's football.

But let’s give the bloke a chance to get himself out of this jam as he heads into his 12th Premier League match.

Believe it or not, Chelsea don’t like changing managers mid-season if they can help it. Enzo Maresca resigned, exiting on January 1, 2026. Liam Rosenior was sacked, yes, but the club were effectively forced into that change as the players clearly gave up on him. Let’s see if Alonso can find some solutions.

Chelsea don’t like changing managers mid-season if they can help it and Alonso, remember, has only had 11 Premier League games in charge

We’ve tried to coax a little anger out of Alonso at press conferences, practically encouraging him to have a pop at his players for how they are performing, but he tends to resist the urge.

He’s clearly a nice guy. However, a Chelsea source does tell me he’s different behind the scenes. We may not see it on the outside, but internally, he is perfectly capable of being fiery with the players.

Which I hope is the case. Because sometimes, it can feel as if these Chelsea players need a good kick up the backside, rather than being coddled.

I wrote a piece after the 4-1 loss to Leeds about how superstars can be stroppy. It’s normal. They can be so good that when the game isn’t going their way, they show their sulky side.

Palmer did that both during and after Saturday’s defeat. It was pretty poor from him to not want to go around applauding the fans who had bothered to stick around at full-time, even if there had been boos. An apology has been attributed to Palmer on social media, but that’s just another case of fake quotes being circulated.

For me, Palmer has to look at himself as much as anybody. It’s been too long since we’ve seen him try to take a match by the scruff of the neck.

Cole Palmer put in another quiet display against Leeds - he needs to start taking matches by the scruff of the neck

WHAT HAPPENED WITH EMEGHA AFTER THE GAME?

Striker Emmanuel Emegha started for the first time and scored for the first time in Chelsea’s 4-1 loss to Leeds. Then he exited Stamford Bridge.

We have seen players leaving the stadium on foot before. Videos of Palmer being surrounded by supporters while being escorted to his ride home occasionally crop up on social media.

Emegha had possessions stolen from his car while he was in a restaurant after the match and the incident has been reported to police. He was not harmed.

From a footballing point of view, I'd like to see Emegha start away at Forest this Friday.

Not that I’m aware. He’s been linked with Paris Saint-Germain and I had an email from a PR person this morning trying to push a loan to AC Milan or Roma as a possibility.

Alonso has given the fans what they wanted in the last few league games. He started Estevao against Manchester United, brought him on at half-time at Sunderland and started him against Leeds.

Estevao, clearly an exciting talent, could do with taking these opportunities to show why he deserves more of them.

Estevao has been given a chance to shine by Alonso in recent weeks but has failed to grab his opportunities

I could see Malo Gusto leaving if there’s interest and Chelsea don’t keep demanding £75million, because it is hard to see who would spend that on him.

Gusto started in five of Alonso’s first six games in all competitions at the beginning of the campaign. Since then, he has been handed one start in eight, and that was in the Carabao Cup at Liverpool. Coming on as a substitute in the loss to Leeds, he tried and failed to play Brenden Aaronson offside and was then beaten by his man as the visitors made it 4-1.

I wouldn’t be surprised if a few other fringe players are linked with leaving in pursuit of game time ahead of January, such as Valentin Barco and Jamie Gittens, who is training again after his ankle injury.

As for incomings, I don’t need to tell you that Chelsea are interested in Alex Scott of Bournemouth. Arsenal are, too, but the word is the Blues are still the frontrunners as we approach January. Whether Scott is what this team needs is another conversation.
