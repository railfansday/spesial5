Ian Hart: Let’s keep Brighton’s defeat to Tigers in perspective – and get back on the horse quickly

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

No matter how much possession you have or how many chances you create, or what the xG is (whatever that is!), unless you put the ball over the line more times than your opponents, it ultimately counts for nothing.

But while Sunday’s defeat at Hull City wasn’t pleasant viewing, perhaps in almost a perverse way it might be the wake-up call the Albion needed.

Let’s admit it, in the lead-up to the most recent international break, we were almost getting carried away. Full disclosure I, among thousands of others, was secretly daring to dream, maybe not the title, but the words Champions and League had entered old Harty’s thought process.

And to be fair that wasn’t just from within the club and its fanbase, externally, Brighton were being lauded as legitimate title contenders.

While attending an excellent Seagulls Down Under function in Adelaide, one of the Albion’s ever-growing Aussie contingent showed me an online tabloid story stating that Brighton would be head to head with defending Champions Arsenal for the League!

Some weeks ago I wrote that I felt Albion more suited to the role of ‘EPL ‘andlords’ in that while they might not win the title, along with the likes of Bournemouth and Brentford they would ultimately dictate the outcome of the competition.

And after Sunday, that might also apply at the other end of the table.

Prior to kick-off, Hull City only had Manchester United below them in the form table. While there are still 27 games to go, that last-gasp and – many might argue – somewhat fortunate win for the Tigers, might be the difference between success and failure come the end of the season.

Given the Albion should have been home and hosed by half-time, it is extremely annoying, but it’s football.

And the next 270 minutes, with the Albion playing three games in seven days – the Conference League home tie against Romania’s Universitatea Craiova on Thursday night, before league games on Sunday and Wednesday, Newcastle at home and Bournemouth away – I believe we will see and learn more about the character of Fabian Hurzeler and his squad.

With those games and the welcome return of the talismanic Kaoru Mitoma from his long-term injury, it’s very much of a case of getting back on the horse immediately and moving forward again.

I do wonder how much the attendance on Thursday will be affected by the 5.45pm kick-off time, to accommodate the TV companies. However perhaps on the flip.side with it being a ‘school night’ with a sensible ticket pricing policy we might see a number of the next generation of young Albion fans attending the Amex for the first time.

We will know the answer to that around 7pm Thursday and hopefully by then the Albion will be well on the way to returning to winning ways.

As a footnote, and another opportunity to put things firmly into perspective, Sunday’s game on Humberside was a couple of weeks short of the 30th anniversary of perhaps one of the most memorable Brighton v Hull fixtures.

Saturday, December 14, 1996 – Steve Gritt’s first game as Albion manager, with his new charges firmly rooted to the bottom of the entire Football League.

Anti-Gritt graffiti outside the ground (with his name spelt wrong!), a fans’ protest involving 3,000 whistles to put the poor old ref on the back foot, but in the end a 3-0 and ‘The Great Escape’ was on!

So all these years later, despite Sunday’s defeat, the Albion are still second in the Premier League doesn’t seem that bad.
