Manchester United’s attack may be their best form of defence this season

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Is attack going to be Manchester United’s best form of defence this season?

There was plenty to like going forward, particularly once United had nudged their way in front after their second lucky break of what had hitherto looked like being a tricky afternoon.

Give this team a little space and they have the attacking players to inflict some serious damage, none more so than Bruno Fernandes who, just five days after collecting the PFA Players’ Player of the Year award, scored a hat-trick in what was another inspired performance.

Ipswich Town will testify to that after a sobering second half when it felt like United were going to score with every raid forward and, for a time, even threaten to equal the nine goals they scored against the promoted side at this ground 31 years ago.

In the returning Marcus Rashford, whom manager Michael Carrick likened to a “new signing”, Matheus Cunha, Bryan Mbeumo and, of course, Fernandes, United have an intriguing blend of attacking players. Throw in Kobbie Mainoo who, on another day, would have stolen the headlines with an imperious display in central midfield, and one thing United are not lacking for is creativity.

Their offensive problems tend to come when opponents sit in and ask to be broken down. Hull City, of course, proved as much last weekend when United looked desperately short of ideas in attack.

But you would probably bet on Carrick being more likely to find solutions to that particular conundrum than some of the defensive headaches that again reared their head here.

A week after that shock 2-0 defeat to Hull, United were given another scare when Ipswich took advantage of more meek defending to lead and could easily have scored more in that first half.

With a couple of days of the transfer window still remaining, it would feel remiss of United not to add a quality left-back before the deadline passes.

Those 15 minutes in the first half when Abdul Fatawu gave Luke Shaw a torrid time, not least in that moment he raced away from the United defender to cross for Leif Davis to score, should tell United’s hierarchy everything about the risks of not reinforcing in the position.

Defensive rebuilding is next summer’s big project but some things cannot wait and that is before we get to some of the issues in the other full-back position, which Ipswich also exploited. Fatawu aside, Daizen Maeda and Davis caused Diogo Dalot and Mbeumo problems down United’s right and the excellent Julio Enciso was a persistent nuisance until the game fell away from Ipswich.

For all the defensive concerns, though, United were exhilarating going forward, especially once they found themselves in front after benefiting from a curious ruling that dictated Harry Maguire’s “accidental” handball in the lead-up to the home team’s second goal was “not punishable” because he did not end up scoring.

In other words, had Maguire himself found the net and not seen his cross-cum-shot deflect in off the Ipswich defender Jacob Greaves, the goal would not have stood.

United were brilliant from that point on. There had been flashes of the threat they hold in the opening 45 minutes but thereafter Ipswich just found themselves routinely bludgeoned and unable to escape the tailspin. As the rain poured down, the goals poured in. Gary O’Neil, the Ipswich manager, talked about a lack of “emotional discipline” but plenty of teams would have found United hard to contain in that moment.

Rashford was a threat on his first start for United for 626 days. Harness his talent in the right way and he will be an enormous asset. Cunha’s withdrawn role, with Fernandes and Mbeumo routinely racing in behind United’s false nine, caused Ipswich no end of problems. Amad Diallo and Mason Mount were missing through injury and Benjamin Sesko, a substitute, is still working his way back to fitness after missing all of pre-season. Patrick Dorgu almost scored a couple after coming on. So Carrick has plenty of options.

Fernandes was at his irrepressible best, initially punishing poor Marcelino Núñez after his slip allowed Cunha to play in the United captain to equalise five minutes before the restart and then taking the game away from Ipswich in the second.

His last act was to send Mbeumo through to score with a delicious lob.

This was the first time United had scored five goals in a Premier League game since beating Leeds United five years ago and you had to go back 10 years to find the last time they had more shots in a league match.

“There was a good mixture [in attack] there,” Carrick said. “Obviously we’ve got to find the right balance and options. We can play slightly different ways across the front line and a lot of the front players can play different roles.

“It was a little bit different today playing Cunha a bit deeper, and Bryan and Marcus a little bit higher. So it’s up to us to try and get that balance right, but certainly there’s creation and there’s goals within the squad, for sure.”

There was little doubt United benefited from the return of Mainoo in midfield. Summer signing Andrey Santos was the one to make way and Mainoo in turn produced the kind of intelligent, inventive display alongside Youri Tielemans that makes you wonder how Thomas Tuchel did not give the midfielder a minute for England at this summer’s World Cup.

He recycles possession beautifully, excels in the tight spaces, has a nose for goal and an eye for a pass and a lovely ability to glide past players even though he is not quick. Ipswich could not live with him.

Asked where Mainoo could still develop, Carrick said, half in jest: “On today’s performance, not much, really. I thought he was pretty much all-around, very, very good for a first start back. I understand Bruno will get a lot of credit today, and he deserves that as well, but I thought Kobbie was immense in the middle of the pitch for us and underpinned the performance of the team.”

The Man Utd head coach told Sky Sports: “It was a good win. It was a really good win in the end. We played some really good football. The biggest thing today was the mentality of the boys tp overcomes setbacks. We’re going to have them and it depends on how you deal with them. I was really pleased with that. It’s easy to look at the result but the mentality and the stubbornness really pleased me.

“We controlled the game pretty well for the most part. A lot of our play was looking on the way to how we wanted it to look. The boys took it on really well and we were fabulous.”

And on Bruno Fernandes, Carrick added: “He showed his quality today. Bruno did what he’s good at. He’s had a big week winning awards. Three goals and a fantastic performance.

“There were a lot of positives with and without the ball but it’s the whole mentality dealing with it and trusting each other. Last week didn’t go for us and we accept that. Today was a lot better.

“We’re not getting carried away. We were down last week but everything wasn’t wrong. We can still get better, we haven’t cracked it. We’ll get ready for the next.”

Speaking with Sky Sports, he said: “Really unlucky to be 2-1 down, really unlucky - two of the most unlucky goals you will see, but then not good enough to get back into it. We got punished by a very good side.

“To beat Manchester United at Old Trafford you need the moments to go in your favour and they didn’t. Rules can go against you, or a deflection, there are lots of things that can happen in a game that are beyond your control.

“It is a complicated rule - handball if it’s the goal scorer but not if it takes a deflection and was heading off the target. I think that is tough for a referee.

“But no problem. I’m not here to moan about refs. We get no points, at Old Trafford a lot of people leave with no points but we leave with an understanding of where to get better.

“We have three points from two tough games. A good start for us. It is the Premier League, the lads understand there will be highs and lows. We don’t want there to be too many lows but the lads are fine in there. They understand.”

Good afternoon and welcome to our live coverage of Sunday’s late Premier League kick-off between Manchester United and Ipswich Town at Old Trafford.

Man Utd suffered an almighty upset last weekend as they slumped to a shock 2-0 defeat against Hull City in Yorkshire. The hosts scored twice after 38 minutes, leaving Michael Carrick’s men with a mountain to climb, but they simply had no answer so Man Utd were shambolic defensively and toothless in attack.

When the fixture list was released, everyone looked at Man Utd’s opening two games against newly-promoted sides as the ideal opportunity for maximum points. But now, there is serious pressure on today’s home team to deliver in front of their fans.

Reflecting on last week’s loss in a press conference ahead of this outing, Carrick said: “I think your success comes from how well you deal with setbacks in the end. In any position, or certainly any sport, particularly this one, going through a season, there are going to be setbacks, sometimes a bit smaller than others. It’s how you deal with that initially, how you react to it and what comes next. I think that ultimately decides what type of team you’re going to become.

“So far, we’ve dealt with those setbacks really well within games and game to game. We had one last week, there’s no getting away from it, and it’s disappointing, but we’re looking to bounce back. We’ve had a good week. The boys have re-focused, learnt from where we could have improved and kept pushing forward.”

Against the odds, Ipswich began their 2026-27 Premier League campaign with a 2-1 comeback victory over Sunderland, with summer signing Emersonn equalising before substitute Jack Clarke found a 90th-minute winner at Portman Road. They followed this game with another triumph at home in midweek, beating Leicester City 3-1 in the League Cup second round.

With all eyes on Man Utd after their stuttering start, Ipswich will see this as an ideal opportunity to ruffle the feathers of a Premier League heavyweight and further dent their sky-high ambitions.

Kick-off is at 16:30, with team news coming shortly.
