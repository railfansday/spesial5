Mike Plante, Sundance Film Festival Shorts Programmer, Dies at 56

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Mike Plante, a filmmaker and 25-year veteran of the Sundance Institute, died Tuesday after a brief illness. He was 56.

At Sundance, Plante worked as a Shorts Programmer and was a familiar, warm-hearted presence at festival screenings and events.

“Mike was a treasure to the Institute, the Festival, and our independent film community,” Sundance Festival Director Eugene Hernandez and Director of Programming Kim Yutani said in a joint statement. “His sense of wonder and humor were matched by boundless enthusiasm and curatorial excellence. Our Festival’s Short Film Program and the Sundance Film Festival Short Film Tour were shaped by Mike’s energy and superb taste in movies. Mike’s roots at Sundance Institute, and his relationships within our organization and festival, run deep. In addition to his work at the festival, Plante directed the 2011 feature documentary “Be Like an Ant,” which followed a Vietnam War veteran who builds a house around a mobile home to handle winter living. He also directed numerous shorts including “The Polaroid Job” (2016), which looked at his parents’ work running a paid event-photography studio in the 1980s. It is available to watch on the New York Times’ Op-Doc website.

Amy Redford, the daughter of Sundance founder Robert Redford and a board member of the festival, called Plante, “a visionary.” “Through his extraordinary curatorial talent, he launched countless storytellers through the vital medium of short film, many of whom have gone on to become some of the world’s most enduring artists,” she said. “Mike embodied the Sundance ethos and played a critical role in preserving the continuity of its values. He was unfailingly generous with his time and mentorship, and a talented creator in his own right. But Mike’s most enduring legacy is his kindness. It was, and remains, the first thing people say about him. His passing is a profound loss not only to the Sundance community, but to all those fortunate enough to have known and loved him. He will be deeply missed.”

Along with directing, Plante produced feature documentaries by other directors, including “Scrap Vessel” (2009) and “Giuseppe Makes A Movie” (2014). Plante started working for film festivals as a programmer in 1993, eventually joining Sundance in 2001.

In 1998, he started the film zine Cinemad, which he wrote for and produced a podcast. The site championed indie and under-seen movies. Plante also wrote “Sensation Cinema: The Audacious, Defiant History of Louis Sher and the Art Theatre Guild,” which looked at an influential arthouse theater owner and distributor. It was a world that Plante knew intimately. In 2024, he was named head of distribution at The Beverly Theater, an independent cinema in Las Vegas, Nevada. He also worked with the CineVegas Film Festival with Trevor Groth, another Sundance vet, as its Director of Programming.
