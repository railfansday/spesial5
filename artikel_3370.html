Stephen King Praises Hit 4-Part Thriller As "The Best Cop Show On TV"

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Stephen King just revealed what he considers to be the best police procedural on TV.

The bestselling horror author is known for publishing such hits as Carrie, The Shining, The Stand, The Dark Tower, Pet Sematary, It, Misery ,and The Green Mile. When he's not writing, King takes to social media to share his views on current events and gives recommendations for the books, TV shows, and movies he believes his fans should consume.

On that latter front, he has done it again. On X, King showered praise on the British series Blue Lights, calling it "the best cop show on TV," and singled out the season 1 episode "Full Moon Fever," which he believes "deserves all the kudos there are." He also acknowledged that a reviewer once compared Blue Lights to The Pitt due to the authentic way it depicts the field of law enforcement, similar to how The Pitt does the same for medicine.

"BLUE LIGHTS (Britbox): The best cop show on TV, and one episode 'Full Moon Fever'--deserves all the kudos there are. Some critic said 'It's THE PITT with police.' Smart critic."

Blue Lights premiered in 2023 and centers on three recruits—Grace, Annie and Tommy—who work for the Police Service of Northern Ireland at Blackthorn Station as they deal with gangs, feuds, and division in the city.

Sian Brooke, Katherine Devlin, Nathan Braniff, Martin McCann, Richard Dormer, Hannah McClean, Andi Osho, Jonathan Harden, and John Lynch star in the series, which was created by Declan Lawn and Adam Patterson.

With a critics' score of 91% and an audience score of 87% on Rotten Tomatoes, Blue Lights has earned positive reviews throughout its run on the air and won Best Drama Series at the BAFTA TV Awards last year.

Blue Lights is available to stream on BritBox and ended its fourth season earlier this month. While BBC One has not renewed Blue Lights for season 5 yet, if it were to come back, Lawn and Patterson won't be there as they recently revealed they've exited the production to work on other projects.

King is no stranger to the world of television, as many of his novels have been adapted for the small screen over the years, including It, 11.22.63, The Mist, Mr. Mercedes, The Outsider, The Stand, Chapelwaite, and The Institute.

His very first book, Carrie, was recently given the TV treatment on Prime Video, courtesy of Mike Flanagan, who previously adapted King's Gerald's Game, Doctor Sleep, and The Life of Chuck.

With Summer H. Howell in the title role, Carrie received positive reviews this fall from critics and audiences alike, and King, who serves as an executive producer, gave it his stamp of approval.

Adaptations of King's books have been hit or miss, according to the author himself. He hasn't been shy about expressing when a film or show hasn't met his expectations. On the flip side, he's always eager to share his recommendations from other creators and storytellers, like he has done with Blue Lights.

As viewers wait to find out whether the series gets renewed, perhaps Stephen King's positive reception will create new fans and help the network with its decision.

Blue Lights (2023) is a police drama set in Belfast, following three new PSNI recruits as they learn how dangerous and complicated frontline policing can be. Their training quickly becomes a test of judgment and survival as they face criminal gangs, divided communities, and the pressures that come with policing a city still shaped by its troubled past.
