Booking Holdings took a $900 million revenue reduction and accrued a $100 million loss in the third quarter tied to a potential Federal Trade Commission complaint against its Priceline brand, which the company said is in discussions with the FTC to resolve. In a quarterly filing Tuesday, Booking said the FTC may seek restitution and penalties exceeding $1 billion, plus litigation and similar claims in other jurisdictions, and that litigation remains possible. The company disclosed in the second quarter that the FTC intended to file a complaint against Priceline and a B2B affiliate partner over disclosures, fees, customer support, and billing practices; Reuters reported last month, citing anonymous sources, that the partner is Guest Reservations. The FTC matter runs alongside a European Commission investigation that has called Booking.com's practices for combating fake listings and phishing "substandard." Operationally, net income fell 1% to $2.7 billion on revenue of $8.9 billion, also down 1%, while room nights rose 7%, accelerating from 5% in the second quarter. CFO Ewout Steenbergen described the fourth-quarter travel environment as "mixed," with moderation in international demand and domestic travel holding up as travelers take shorter trips closer to home. Connected trip transactions — bookings across two or more verticals — grew almost 20% year over year and made up a low double-digit share of Booking.com transactions.
