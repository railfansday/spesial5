Air Canada Launches 22 New International Routes In Only 4 Weeks: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Air Canada, Wikimedia Commons, Simple Flying

Between November 21 and December 17, Air Canada and its lower-cost unit Rouge will collectively launch or restart 22 international routes. They won't just be from the Star Alliance member's primary hubs of Toronto Pearson, Montreal, and Vancouver. They'll also be from Calgary, Edmonton, Halifax, Ottawa, Quebec City, and Winnipeg.

For obvious reasons, none of the routes are to Europe. Instead, they're primarily to the Caribbean, Central America, and Mexico for warm winter weather. It is possible that some of the links to the Caribbean are materializing because the airline no longer flies to Cuba.

Half of the routes have not previously been served by the airline, at least in the last 22 years and possibly ever. Most of the others return after an absence of at least a decade. They include from Winnipeg to the ever-popular tourist destination of Punta Cana. At 2,334 nautical miles (4,323 km) each way, it'll be Air Canada's new longest route from the capital of Manitoba in more than a fifth of a century.

At 1,769 nautical miles (3,276 km), Vancouver to Monterrey will be Air Canada's new longest international Airbus A220-300 market from British Columbia. However, it will not be the equipment's longest-ever service from Vancouver past the Canadian border. The carrier deployed the narrowbody from Vancouver to Raleigh-Durham until June 2026 and Nashville until three months later, but those routes have since ended.

They include Vancouver to the growing Mexican tourist destination of Puerto Escondido, which is a market that has not previously had flights with any airline. It is, therefore, particularly notable. OAG data shows that Vancouver will have flights to 11 destinations in Mexico this winter, provided by Aeromexico, Air Canada, Flair, and WestJet. With 31% of the departures, Air Canada is the second-largest operator, after WestJet.

Some broader context is needed for Puerto Escondido, which is particularly well-known for surfing. Vancouver will be the third nonstop market from Canada, after those by Air Canada from Toronto (which launched in December 2025) and WestJet from Calgary (December 2025).

They followed the start of the first route from the USA to Puerto Escondido in April 2025. This was United Airlines from Houston, while American Airlines followed from Dallas in December last year. All the new routes were influenced by the airport's infrastructure improvements, with significant further development underway to accommodate the much greater traffic.

All but one of the following links will be new to Air Canada's network. At a considerable 2,976 nautical miles (5,512 km), Vancouver to the increasingly popular Costa Rican destination of Liberia will become the Star Alliance member's longest narrowbody operation from British Columbia. More than that, it'll be the operator's longest-ever service on the Boeing 737 MAX 8, exceeding the distance of all transatlantic routes on the variant.

The most notable market is, of course, from Vancouver to Sapporo, which will become the airline's new shortest route to Asia. Sapporo is known for winter activities, including skiing and other snow-based adventures. Air Canada will now have ten routes from Vancouver to Asia this winter, although flights to Singapore will end in January 2027.
