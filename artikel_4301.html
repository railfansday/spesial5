10 Open-World Games You Can Finish in 10 Hours & Under

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Elena Chapella is a Writer for DualShockers. Previously an award-winning journalist, Elena is passionate about writing what she loves.

When any gamer hears about open world games, they immediately (safely) assume that the game would proceed to take hours upon hours to complete. However, that’s not always the case with open world games.

In fact, some of these games can be completed in less than 10 hours.

If you enjoy exploration-focused titles that are free of filler, we recommend the ten fastest open-world games to 100%.

For players who are wanting a one-and-done experience with an open world game, being able to beat it within the same day or two days maximum, these games scratch that itch. You’re able to hop in and complete the game practically quicker than you can download it, and it’s just the right amount of content to tide you over until the next game.

While others are diving into open world games to sink hundreds upon hundreds of hours into them, you can rest assured that these games won’t eat up your schedule.

The thing about Outer Wilds is that the world itself is the final boss, with players only having a limited amount of time in order to save the universe – but it’s not done on its own. The game’s DLC, Echoes of the Eye, is about 10 and half hours long, obviously shorter for faster players.

The DLC doesn’t replace anything in the game, but it’s rather a separate and self-contained location, more than ready for players to dive in and unravel its secrets. This way, if players are wanting a taste of Outer Wilds without going through the whole game, they can easily do so.

This is especially the case if you’ve already beaten the game/solved the mystery of the main story. Since there isn’t really any replayability, the DLC offers a whole new mystery altogether – in a way, it’s like you’re playing the game for the first time all over again, and it can be solved pretty quickly.

Thankfully, there are also even more DLCs if you want more mystery, but they aren’t anywhere near as condensed as Echoes of the Eye.

There’s something particularly special about Shadow of the Colossus: the game is labelled as an open world, but many players would walk away from it thinking that it’s a semi-open world due to just how empty the world itself is.

Of course, that’s typically a bad thing for open worlds, but the unique thing about Shadow of the Colossus is that it would not work without that emptiness. Combine this with a linear order for the bosses themselves (which are platforming puzzles as well), a vague yet compelling story and a genuinely cool premise, and you’ve got a game that’s as fun as it is challenging.

One of my students refers to the game as “Rated T for Teen Mario,” and I'm bound to agree, if that helps get the idea across. And just like Mario, the game can be beaten pretty quickly: about 7 hours, in fact.

With that in mind, if this is a game that interests you, be prepared to spend most of those 7 hours climbing rather than fighting; you’re going to love it regardless.

Right now, The Legend of Zelda once again has the gaming community in a complete buzz thanks to the fantastic reception to the Ocarina of Time Remake.

The series is continuously revolutionary, constantly evolving and redefining the open world genre as we know it. However, many tend to forget that open world Zelda didn’t start with Breath of the Wild, it started with the very first entry in the entire franchise.

The nice part about the game is that it’s not long at all (especially when compared to its sequels that will come over the next few decades) – averaging only at about 8 hours to beat the game. Players who want a traditional Zelda experience in a short amount of time can’t find any other game that would be a better fit.

And as the game comes up on its 40th anniversary, it’s all the more reason to dive into where it all began.

Obviously, both Red Dead Redemption games are massive, telling all kinds of stories jam-packed in a rich world of historical fiction, so it would take a while to beat either entry. However, the first game’s DLC, Undead Nightmare, is a fantastic stand-alone story that only takes about 6 and a half hours to complete.

Undead Nightmare takes everything you love about the classic Red Dead Redemption, but with a twist: suddenly, everyone is trying to survive a zombie apocalypse that came about practically overnight. The world and everything you can do within it, naturally, is related to this instead of your standard robberies and bounties.

Since the world is so condensed, the DLC goes by much quicker than most, but it’s an absolute blast from start to finish. In fact, it’s widely considered to be one of the greatest DLCs of all time, simply for how refreshingly unique and fun it is.

With that in mind, the fact that this game can be completed quicker than a standard work shift just makes it all the better.

Of all the Assassin's Creed games, the shortest mainline entry (non-DLC game) is easily Assassin's Creed: Rogue, clocking in at about 11 hours to be beaten (again, it’s much quicker than that for veteran AC fans and seasoned gamers as a whole).

Surprisingly, the game has one of the more unique stories that the series has seen, focusing on an Assassin named Shay Patrick Cormac literally going rogue, slaughtering the brotherhood he knows and siding with the Templars.

And the best part of the story is the fact that the modern-day segments are thankfully incredibly short – the segments that drag and feel like a chore in all the other titles in the franchise don’t bog down this game.

Overall, it’s the perfect bite-sized game for Assassin’s Creed content, regardless of if you’re a fan or even brand new to the series. It’s truly one of a kind, especially for a series like AC.

From the titans of Xenoblade to the lawns of Santa Destroy, these Wii open-world games prove curiosity always pays off if you’re willing to get lost.

If you were given 10 hours to simply explore New York City and soak it all in, you wouldn’t even be an eighth of the way through your journey before you reach that time. The fact that Marvel's Spider-Man brought in NYC on a similar (still scaled down) scale cemented the game’s timeframe as you continue to whip around as The Friendly Neighborhood himself.

Yet, you don’t have anywhere near as many superhero duties in Spider-Man: Miles Morales, so obviously, it will take you a lot less time. It would take about 8 hours, in fact.

Since you’re playing the game as Miles Morales (duh) while Peter Parker is still around and kicking, you’re basically a superhero in training. This training will have you busy constantly taking down bad guys and saving the day, serving as the sole Spider-Man in NYC for several hours until Peter comes back from vacation with MJ.

Or you could just swing around New York City doing whatever you want for that timeframe, you’d get the same amount of enjoyment.

Just because a game is a horror game doesn’t mean it can’t also be open world, and in DREDGE, our open world is a vast, dangerous ocean. Even then, it’s nowhere near as dangerous as it will be once your sanity drains due to the Lovecraftian terrors you’ll encounter.

It sounds horrifying, but it’s actually the coziest fishing game you’d ever imagine. You fish, fill out your encyclopedia, discover aberrations with the marine life and upgrade your ship, but the real fun comes from the simplest mechanic: sorting the fish into your boat. Seriously, the inventory management is the most addictive part of the whole game.

The nice part is that it’s wrapped up quickly, too, taking only about 9 and a half hours to complete – but trust me, you’re going to willingly sink far more hours into the game (and your ship). And if that doesn’t sound like enough content in the game for how much I sing its praises, it also has two equally dense and fun DLCs.

When it comes to short, yet full open world games, you cannot get any better of an experience than with DREDGE.

The lovely thing about Eastshade isn’t just how stunning of a game it is, but also the fact that it can be beaten pretty quickly and pretty easily – just around 9 hours.

The whole game is about finding the most beautiful spots in the game to paint them (using a photography mechanic), so those entire 9 hours are filled with genuine beauty. Of course, since it’s open world, there are side quests (artwork commissions) and other things to do, but you’re going to be far more caught up in how pretty the world is.

And for those who love subtle, environmental storytelling, this world is full of it. You can learn more while you’re out and about exploring in it, or from the various other characters that you can talk to.

It’s one of the few open world games that keeps you busy without actively stressing you out; since, after all, the point of the game is to relax you, you’re going to find yourself in a comfortable state of bliss while playing.

If you’ve ever wanted to feel like you’re driving a pod around Tatooine (like any Star Wars fan would), you would have a fantastic time with Sable.

The game seems pretty surface-level and simple: just ride around on your sand-speeder and enjoy the game’s lovely art style, but once you start playing, you’ll see it’s a lot deeper than you’d expect. It’s supposed to be about the protagonist’s self-discovery on their home planet, but by the end of your adventure, you’re going to feel like you’ve gone on a self-discovery of your own.

While the game can be completed in about 7 and a half hours, it’s actually better if you take your time with the exploration – that’s the game’s strongest component, after all. Why not take the time to enjoy a world as pretty and interesting as this one?

It’s not perfectly polished; it’s actually pretty janky of a game at times, but if anything, it makes you love the game and all the charm it has even more. At the same time, if some of the previously mentioned jank (charm) is a little too much, then rest assured that you can get through this game very, very quickly.

A Short Hike is just that: short. It’s not the kind of short where it’s on the shorter end of a long game, no, the game is only about an hour and a half long. If you try to complete every single element within it to 100% the game, you’re looking at about 4 hours, an incredibly tiny open world game.

The premise of the game is simple: you’re hiking up a mountain. If you run into anyone or anything on the way there, you’re more than welcome to interact with them, but it’s optional if you’re wanting to just get to the top. Considering how short the game is, I’d recommend not skipping anything if you can help it, just to get the richest experience you can.

Don’t rush through this hike, either. The game is already short enough, and there is so much enjoyable content that you don’t want to miss out on while on the way to the top.

But as we’ve learned with games like these: it’s not about the destination, it’s about the journey, so don’t be afraid to go on that journey.
