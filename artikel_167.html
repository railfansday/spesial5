Hakeem Jeffries on brink of House history as he tells Democrats to ‘fight Maga, not each other’ – US politics live

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Democrats resoundingly nominated Jeffries to serve as speaker of the House when the new Congress is sworn in next year

Hello and welcome to the US politics live blog.

Hakeem Jeffries said it is the job of Democratic lawmakers to “fight Maga extremism” as he stood on the brink of becoming the first ever Black house speaker.

Democrats on Wednesday resoundingly nominated Jeffries to serve as speaker of the House when the new Congress is sworn in next year.

A New York congressman who has served as the Democratic minority leader since 2023, Jeffries is set to become one of Donald Trump’s most prominent opponents for the final two years of his presidency, when Democrats will control both the Senate and House of Representatives and are expected to undertake aggressive efforts to check his powers.

After his nomination was secured during closed-door voting, 251-11, ahead of a full House vote, Jeffries promised to take on the Trump administration’s Maga approach to governing the US. “Our job is to fight Maga extremism, not each other,” he said, “and to get big things done for the American people.”

Democrats nominated Jeffries despite concerns from some progressives that he represents a politically risky status quo for a party that is looking to capitalize on a surprisingly good performance in the midterms, where voters swept them back into power in the House and Senate with significant majorities.

He would become the first Democratic House speaker since Nancy Pelosi, who will retire from Congress at the end of this year.

Meanwhile today, president Trump has left China after hailing his trilateral meeting with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping, saying the three leaders were “really making history”. Trump said the US, Russia and China, the world’s largest nuclear powers, were “uniquely positioned” to discuss reducing their nuclear stockpiles.

The National Security Agency is seeing a third day of disruptions to its internal network, NSA Net, which allows staff to share highly classified information with each other, according to the New York Times.

The Times reports that employees at the NSA’s Maryland headquarters are under “code orange” for Thursday day shift, which requires non-essential workers are to stay home unless contacted by their supervisors.

Earlier this week the NSA said that it experienced a “temporary degradation” during scheduled routine maintenance, after reports that NSA Net had gone down.

In a statement, the agency said some internal systems were affected but that “mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational” and its ability to defend national security continues.

However, the NSA did not say what caused the outage, and whether the system was back to normal.

Over the coming weeks, Chuck Schumer and Chris Van Hollen, along with any late challengers, will vie for the party’s top Senate leadership post before lawmakers cast their votes. Here are four things to know about how the election works.

A secret, closed-door election: Unlike the public speaker of the House vote, where lawmakers cast ballots on the chamber floor, the Senate leadership contest takes place behind closed doors. Senators vote by secret ballot, and individual votes are not disclosed.

The winner only needs a caucus majority: The Democratic leadership race is decided solely by members of the party’s Senate caucus. Now that Democrats are set to have 56 seats in the new year, the winner will need secure 29 votes for the top role.

The vote is scheduled for next month: As things stand, the election is set for 1 December. Both incumbent senators and incoming Democratic members will participate, while retiring and outgoing senators will not vote, despite many having publicly weighed in on the race.

Republicans choose their leaders separately: Republicans hold their own closed-door leadership elections in both chambers. Earlier this month, House Republicans selected hardliner Jim Jordan as their leader after Mike Johnson opted not to run for minority leader. In the Senate, Republicans unanimously backed John Thune to continue leading the conference.

Following the news that Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries to be the next House speaker, leading to a full chamber vote which he is likely to win in the new year, it’s worth turning our attention to the ongoing battle in the upper chamber.

Here, top Democrats Chuck Schumer is vying to become Senate majority leader but is facing a challenge from progressive Chris Van Hollen. Despite the fact that Schumer is confident he has the votes to succeed, a number of lawmakers have pledged support for Van Hollen and say there needs to be change from leadership when Democrats take control of the Senate in the new year.

On Wednesday, Van Hollen said in a video on social media that he is continuing to meet with members one-on-one, and any discussion about expediting the closed-door vote to select the leader on 1 December has been quashed.

“We need to use this opportunity to change the way the Senate Democratic caucus does business,” Van Hollen said.

Attorney general Todd Blanche’s wife, Kristine Blanche, who was appointed earlier this year to a US government advisory board on alternative medicine, has a website on which she marketed dietary supplements and treatments with unproven medical and health claims.

Blanche is a licensed physician assistant who says she has a PhD in breast thermography from the University of Science, Arts and Technology in Montserrat, a British territory in the Caribbean. She is also an advocate for alternative medical cures and holistic health.

Her site also links to a page called “Dr Blanche’s Dispensary”, where patients can buy supplement pills she recommends. She suggested that one product, a liquid herbal compound, could be used to treat the Epstein-Barr virus, although she misspelled “Epstein”.

There is no medication approved by the FDA to treat or cure Epstein-Barr, and the product itself does not claim to treat the virus.

Blanche also listed five supplements in her store, priced up to $63 a bottle, which she said can help treat human papillomavirus (HPV).

Another treatment Blanche offers, priced online at $595, claims to help men “boost” their testosterone levels and lose weight.

An anti-vaccine group led for years by Robert F Kennedy Jr said Meta has “taken down” posts on its accounts, after a Guardian report found the group was sharing false and misleading information about vaccines.

The Guardian reported on Wednesday that Children’s Health Defense (CHD) had earlier this year been allowed back on Facebook and Instagram, four years after Meta removed it from Facebook and Instagram for repeatedly violating its misinformation policies.

CHD said in posts on X that within hours of the Guardian’s article running, “ALL of our accounts have been targeted”. It accused Meta of censorship.

“Our posts are being taken down AGAIN simply for sharing the truth about vaccines and the experiences of families whose children were injured by vaccines,” CHD posted on Wednesday night, adding: “People have every right to discuss vaccine injuries and share their experiences without being silenced by big tech.”

Meta and CHD did not immediately return emails seeking comment.

Among the content that was no longer up as of Wednesday evening was a video that the group had posted on Facebook that said the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine is “not so safe and effective”.

Another video no longer available had been titled “Vaccines: Your child could die”, and made the misleading claim that vaccines are deadly.

A third video that was no longer up said the HPV vaccines are “very dangerous”, have “caused hundreds, thousands of deaths”, and do not prevent cervical cancer.

All three posts had been visible on CHD accounts at the time the Guardian story was published on Wednesday.

Chinese state media has issued a four-point “peace declaration by the Chinese, Russian and US leaders” following their meeting.

The same statement was later issued by Russia.

Chinese president Xi Jinping, Russian president Putin and US president Trump held a meeting in Shenzhen on 19 November 2026. The Russian and US leaders expressed their gratitude to China for hosting this historic meeting. The three heads of state exchanged in-depth views on the current international situation and committed themselves to maintaining strategic stability and international security.

1. As permanent members of the UN security council, China, Russia and the US are committed to promoting UN reform so that it may respond more effectively to global challenges and fulfil its fundamental mission of maintaining international peace and security.

2. The three countries plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations. They reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows.

3. The three heads of state recognised that the principle of state consent and respect for the immunity of state officials are of paramount importance, and agreed that the international criminal court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction.

4. The three heads of state reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must not be fought. The three countries will engage in dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing nuclear proliferation.

Elon Musk denied being Maga on Wednesday, despite aligning himself intensely with Donald Trump’s Make America Great Again movement since 2024.

“I’m not Maga,” the world’s richest person told the New York Times Dealbook summit, which he attended via video call from China, where he is one of several US business leaders who have accompanied Trump on his trip to Asia.

“I’m not sure what Maga means, but if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican party, then I certainly don’t have that,” the chief executive of SpaceX and Tesla added.

Musk famously declared at a 2024 campaign rally in Madison Square Garden: “I’m not just Maga. I’m dark, gothic Maga.”

In a 45-minute interview with Andrew Ross Sorkin, a New York Times columnist, Musk also said it would be good for Washington to have split control between the two parties, and that he thinks “there are positives and negatives in both”.

He also accused Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, of “lying” about restrictions on access to his firm’s Starshield satellite system; praised Jensen Huang, the ousted Nvidia chief executive; and said that “no one I met at Anthropic set off my evil detector”.

Asked about his previous negative comments about Anthropic, Musk said that before he agreed to rent computing power to the AI firm: “I went to met with a lot of people at Anthropic. I didn’t just move forward with the deal.”

“My interaction so far with the Anthropic team is that they seem like good people and they seem principled,” Musk added. “They seem diligent and they seem seem to care about the future.”

Musk donated at least $250m of his own money to help Trump win the election in 2024, and recently spent nearly $500m in the US midterms to support Republicans, many of whom lost.

Denmark’s parliament on Thursday approved a hard-wrought agreement with Greenland and the United States to strengthen security in the Arctic and North Atlantic.

The vote follows months of pressure by president Donald Trump seeking to control Greenland, a semiautonomous territory of Denmark, a Nato ally.

The trilateral deal - between Trump and the Danish and Greenlandic leaders – paves the way for more US bases in Greenland and eases tensions over Trump’s repeated calls for the vast island to become part of the US.

The 109-0 vote in the Folketing amounts to a final step, with the deal expected to enter into force once Denmark and Greenland has signed on. Greenland’s parliament approved the deal earlier this month, also with no opposition.
