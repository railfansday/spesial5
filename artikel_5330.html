Malaysia Airlines is switching to Boeing widebody jets for the first time in 30 years

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Malaysia Aviation Group plans 10 787-10 jets with options for 6 more, to replace its seven Airbus A350s from 2032

Malaysia Aviation Group, the parent of Malaysia Airlines, announced the acquisition of 10 Boeing $BA 787-10 Dreamliners on Wednesday, with options for six more. The aircraft will replace the airline's seven Airbus A350s.

Boeing described the deal as the group's first 787 order and said the purchase is to be finalized. A signing ceremony in Kuala Lumpur was attended by Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, MAG said.

Deliveries are scheduled between 2032 and 2035, the same period in which the A350s are expected to be phased out, MAG said. The 787s will seat 319 passengers in three classes.

The order is MAG's first time buying widebodies straight from Boeing since 1995, according to Bloomberg. The 16 aircraft are worth $9.3 billion at list prices, with engines counted in and no discounts applied.

"The acquisition of the 787 Dreamliner represents a strategic investment in the future of Malaysia Aviation Group," MAG President and Group CEO Nasaruddin A. Bakar said in a statement. "These aircraft will strengthen our ability to serve key international markets with greater capacity, efficiency and flexibility."

The jets are expected to serve London, Paris, Sydney, Melbourne, Tokyo, Osaka and Seoul, based on future network planning, MAG said. Boeing said the order marks a return to Boeing widebodies for Malaysia Airlines, which previously operated 747 and 777 jets.

GE Aerospace said MAG selected its GEnx-1B engines to power up to 16 of the jets. It is the first time MAG has chosen GEnx engines for its fleet, GE Aerospace said.

Malaysia Airlines is not leaving Airbus widebodies entirely. It has placed orders for 40 A330neos over the past few years, and 11 of them have arrived, according to Bloomberg.

On the narrowbody side, the airline operates more than 37 Next-Generation 737s and 19 737-8s, MAG said. Its total order book is 55 Boeing 737s, made up of 43 737-8s and 12 737-10s, with the remaining aircraft expected by 2030.

Other Asian carriers have also turned to the 787-10. Philippine Airlines committed in July to order up to 20 of the jets in a memorandum of understanding covering 15 aircraft plus five options, with deliveries set to begin in 2031.
