No matter what happens when they face Portugal tonight, Wales know they will face a relegation play-off, but they will want to end their Group A campaign on a high (kick-off 7:45 pm, live on BBC Two and iPlayer).

Despite picking up just three points so far, from their 2-1 home win over Norway, Wales will not be automatically relegated from the top tier, with that fate going to the two bottom sides overall and both Turkey and the Czech Republic finished without a single point.

Instead, Craig Bellamy’s side will take part in a relegation play-off in March against one of the teams from League B, with their opponents to be confirmed when the draw takes place on Friday, 20 November.

Wales would love to go out on a high, despite the disappointment of missing out on a chance to be among the top seeds for Euro 2028 qualifying.

Portugal also know their Nations League fate, but the top spot is not yet secured; they know just a point will be enough to give them the advantage going into the quarter-final draw.

They are the reigning champions after beating Spain on penalties in the 2025 final, and they have won the tournament twice after beating the Netherlands in the inaugural final back in 2019.

Despite their history, they are only the fourth favourites to lift the trophy again, with the football betting sites behind Spain, France and England.

But they are the standout favourites for this one at 5/11, while Wales are 6/1 to secure only their second win over Portugal.

They have only met five times, and Wales’ only win came back in 1951, when they secured a 2-1 win in Cardiff. Since then, they have lost three games in a row without scoring a single goal, including back in September when Joao Felix scored the only goal of the game in Lisbon.

Wales managed just one shot on target in that one, and goals have been a problem for Bellamy’s side, with just three in their five Nations League goals and eight conceded.

Jorge Jesus’ side, on the other hand, have scored 10 but they do go into the game on the back of a surprise 4-1 drubbing at home by Denmark, and they will want to prove that they can cope without their talisman Cristiano Ronaldo, who retired from international duty earlier this month.

Wales have now lost five of their last six games in all competitions, which is as many defeats as they had in their previous 29 matches, and it’s hard to see them getting anything out of this one.

Former Chelsea midfielder Joao Felix has scored three goals in Portugal’s Nations League campaign, including the winner when the two sides last met.

The 27-year-old left Stamford Bridge in the summer of 2025, moving to Saudi side Al-Nassr for a fee of £43.7m, where he joined up with then Portuguese captain Ronaldo.

With a Ronaldo-sized void to fill Felix has shown what he is capable of and betting sites are offering 11/8 on him scoring at any time or 5/1 on him scoring first or last, and he has done twice each during this campaign.
