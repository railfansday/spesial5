Nuno’s West Ham Future Under Threat As Board Concerns Grow

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Nuno Espírito Santo’s position as West Ham United manager is under increasing threat, according to a new report from The Guardian. With the Hammers slipping to eighth in the Championship following consecutive home defeats, patience is reportedly wearing thin among key figures at the London Stadium.

Writing for The Guardian, Hammers fan Jacob Steinberg reports that although Nuno is expected to be given more time to turn things around, influential figures at West Ham are becoming increasingly impatient with the club’s disappointing start to the season.

The report comes just two days after the 3-1 defeat against West Bromwich Albion, a result which prompted further questions about Nuno’s tactics and his ability to get the best out of a squad assembled with the intention of securing an immediate return to the Premier League.

According to The Guardian, any decision regarding Nuno’s future would be taken collectively by the West Ham board, rather than by one shareholder acting alone.

Interim chief executive Karim Virani and recruitment director Nils Koppen are among those expected to have input.

The report also revisits the decision to retain Nuno following relegation, explaining that David Sullivan had wanted a change of manager while Daniel Křetínský favoured continuity.

However, that previous support does not necessarily mean the manager’s position is secure indefinitely.

West Ham have won just twice in their last six Championship matches, and the upcoming fixtures against Cardiff City and Blackburn Rovers could become increasingly important ahead of the November international break.

The Guardian also highlights concerns about Nuno’s inability to settle on his strongest starting eleven, the lack of balance in midfield and the failure of the club’s strikers to provide sufficient goals.

The report states that West Ham’s three recognised centre-forwards, Joël Piroe, Pablo Felipe and Taty Castellanos, have scored just five league goals between them this season.

Meanwhile, Jarrod Bowen has scored seven, leaving the Hammers heavily dependent on our captain.

The continued absence of recognised defensive midfielders Soungoutou Magassa and Edson Álvarez from Nuno’s preferred selections is another issue raised.

These concerns will sound familiar to Claret & Hugh readers. Following the West Brom defeat, I questioned whether Nuno’s entire West Ham project was becoming fundamentally flawed, particularly given the lack of cohesion and the manager’s constant tactical changes.

And while I appreciate that managers need time, I struggle to see how West Ham can continue performing so poorly with a squad that should be capable of competing for automatic promotion.

Perhaps the most important aspect of The Guardian’s report is the suggestion that Nuno will face difficult questions if performances fail to improve before the international break.

There is no suggestion that a dismissal has already been agreed, and the report specifically indicates that West Ham still hope the manager can turn things around.

Nevertheless, it appears the pressure is no longer coming exclusively from frustrated supporters.

The next two matches now offer Nuno an opportunity to demonstrate that he can produce a convincing response.

Because as I’ve argued previously, West Ham’s problems haven’t suddenly appeared over the last fortnight. The warning signs have been there for considerably longer.

And if the manager cannot begin addressing them, it is becoming increasingly difficult to see how the club can justify continuing with the same approach.
