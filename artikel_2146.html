Bristol City and Bristol Rovers fans both face the same issue right now

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The two Bristol clubs don't meet often, yet their rivalry is one of the fiercest in the English game. Right now, though, City and Rovers fans are united in their dislike of their current managers.

We're right in the midst of sacking season in the EFL, and both Michael Skubala and Steve Evans are edging closer to the trapdoor, having failed thus far, with both sides having lofted expectations ahead of the campaign.

Bristol City have had those expectations since their run to the play-offs under Liam Manning in 2024/25, but a transfer window which saw plenty of investment into the playing squad, with the likes of Dom Ballard, Lorent Tolaj, Lisav Eissat and others arriving, has only stoked that anticipation that they could make a run towards the top eight.

Meanwhile, all the good feeling that Steve Evans created in the latter stages of last season and in the first few weeks of this year has dissipated, with the Gas routinely beaten and looking over their shoulders, which wasn't the plan at all this year.

While Bristol City fans would like to have maintained their play-off level in the last couple of years, there was some potential leeway regarding some form of a project over the summer.

The Robins had a little shake-up in the board over the past year, with Charlie Boss appointed CEO in February, a few months after lifelong fan Richard Scudamore became a member of the board and Sporting Director James Ellis arrived this past summer. This is their first full season at the club.

Bringing in Michael Skubala and investing in some promising players from League One and abroad indicated that this would be a multi-year project to try and get into the Premier League, but as it stands, Bristol City are sleepwalking towards the third tier, winning just two of their last 10 games.

More importantly, though, Skubala's brand of football isn't giving fans the belief that things will get better. They've scored just four goals at Ashton Gate, and lost their last home game 5-0 against Norwich City. To many, that was the final straw.

Evidently, Robins fans aren't having Skubala in charge anymore, and once you lose the supporters, it's hard to see a route back.

While Michael Skubala's situation is very much a project yet to get going, the fall-off at Bristol Rovers for Steve Evans these last couple of months has been head-scratching and rather alarming.

It was just six months ago that Evans was celebrating amongst the fans in their 4-0 win over Cheltenham Town, marking their seventh successive victory at the Memorial Ground and their eighth straight in league action. If he went into the crowd now, they'd probably have a different reaction.

The Gas were many's pre-season favourites in League Two this year after they ended last season with 11 wins in their final 14 games, and they started this term with four wins from five. Since then, it's been one win and eight defeats in 10.

The credit that Evans built up through the latter stages of last season has left the bank, and Gas fans are calling for a change.

Saturday's home defeat against Chesterfield leaves Bristol Rovers sitting 18th in League Two, with the main concerns coming from the effort of the players during this dry spell of form.

Bristol City made their decision over the international break. With fans calling for Michael Skubala's head, CEO Charlie Boss sent a message of confidence towards their head coach.

Regardless of what Robins fans think, there can be some admiration that the hierarchy hasn't panicked immediately and is willing to give Skubala time to try and turn the ship around and oversee the multi-year project he signed up for in the summer.

The schedule doesn't get any easier for Bristol City after the break, though, with Middlesbrough, Wrexham, West Brom and Swansea City coming up in their next four games.

City fans are going to have to stick with Skubala through this difficult period and try to will their side on to collect some points. If not, the Robins could be in a relegation battle as we head towards Christmas, and a sacking then would only be seen as a decision made weeks too late at Ashton Gate.

While there hasn't been a direct vote of confidence at board level, Steve Evans may still have a small amount of credit left to try and turn this dreadful form around at the Memorial Stadium.

Evans' predecessor, Darrell Clarke, was sacked after losing 10 straight league games, so the higher-ups have shown patience where it may have been running thin in the stands previously.

There's also the possibility that things don't get better without Evans in the dugout, who has achieved success nearly everywhere he's been, especially in League Two, with him getting Rotherham United and Stevenage promoted from this level in the past.

Right now, both sets of fans in Bristol want their current head coaches to leave, but it doesn't seem like that will happen anytime soon. Only time will tell whether those decisions are the correct ones.
