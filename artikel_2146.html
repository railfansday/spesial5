I think this is the best Newcastle United team that is available now

It has not been an enjoyable season watching the Newcastle United team so far and that’s being nice about, it may be hyperbolic to say we’ve been as bad as some of what we saw under the dark days of Mike Ashley, but the cluelessness of our play at times this year has definitely brought up memories of John Carver and Steve Bruce for me.

For the record, I think persisting with a manager who is making Ruben Amorim seem flexible isn’t a great idea, but trying to think of how with the current squad we might be able to make the best of the situation.

Let us start with the issues for Matthias Jaissle’ s preferred system, firstly at the back where being honest I think our back five who started against Fulham are ok together. Hornicek has made a few mistakes but overall, he’s made some good saves, and his ball playing is a big help.

At LB Lewis Hall is an absolute Unicorn and could play in any system, at RB Dedic is a bit defensively suspect but has a lot of energy and gets up and down the line. CB is where the theoretical issue is, Jaissles system requires pacy ball playing CBs, who are good when left 1v1 and the only one of ours who fits that bill in some way is Thiaw, who is generally great but has the odd Titus moment.

Our other CBs just aren’t fits in this system, none have the pace, Schar is fading a bit even on the ball and Botman has never been the same since our medical staff got their hands on him. I’m not that worried about this area of the pitch too much though as despite Dan Burn being slow and not the greatest on the ball he won’t overplay, and he will put his body on the line and win as much as he can. I think there is always a place for an old school CB. So, keep that back five from the Fulham game together and we should be ok there.

Now moving into midfield and we need two for the double pivot, Joelinton gives you physically but he doesn’t have the guile or composure required to play there week in and week out (though what I’d give to have him vs Arsenal on Saturday). Ramsey is pretty anonymous there, Willock despite once every few years looking like he could be a great destroyer (Hull this season and the first half vs Forest (4-3) a couple of years ago) is way too inconsistent and finally Bamba who looks lost in his limited minutes and has hardly had a look in.

As such, it comes down for me to two of three, we have Nico who’s shown his quality in a couple of games (Palace/Spurs) but has ran around and fouled a lot in others, Miley who can look like a world beater but then also get overran and finally Steur who’s great on the ball but has defensive limitations at this point in his development. All three lack legs but from what we have they are definitely our best options.

I think when thinking of who is best in the double pivot we need to think of who is going to be in the 10 with them. So far this year it has been Wissa and Willock there the most with the odd moment of Ramsey.

Wissa after one good game against Liverpool looks like he’s put his jeans back on and just isn’t suitable, Willock has his moments but like in the pivot he’s incredibly inconsistent and can coast for larger parts of the game and finally Ramsey while not seeming like an awful player has so little impact. I think going for him rather than El Khannouss will be one that bites us far years.

So, not an inspiring group of options in that 10 role which is key to this system working, we need someone dropping between the lines, who can then turn and make quick accurate passes to our front line. None of the three mentioned can do that at any sort of decent level and it kills our attacking play. I do think we have one player who could thrive here.

That player is Sean Steur whose passing range is probably the best we have and his footballing brain belies his years. You may think it’s not his position. he plays deeper and he’s very good there, and to that I say we need to look to the south coast and to Brighton and one of this season’s best players in Pascal Gross.

You see Gross often lines up as the 10 for Brighton and his passing ability and nous causes chaos but he doesn’t just stay at the top end of the field, he has freedom to wander and drop deep and start attacks. I can see Steur not only being able to do this but be able to thrive in this role. The added bonus is as we have two other midfielders behind him his defensive limitations are covered.

Because of this, in the middle of the pitch I’d want to see Steur in the 10 and Miley and Nico in the double pivot, there is a risk of not having a runner in there but you’d hope with the technical ability of those three we can exert a degree of control on games and set a firmer base in front of the defence.

We then move to the wingers, where in Jaissle’s systems the wingers need to be comfortable coming inside and creating space for the full-backs to overlap leading to overloads of the opposition defence. One of our big problems is on the right-hand side we have two wingers who being polite are touchline wingers and being more blunt, Jacob Murphy puts in the occasional good ball but his all-round game is chronic and Elanga reminds me of Obertan and not because of the pace (I’m waiting for him to forget to take the ball with him when he’s running through at some point).

On the left Barnes always puts up a good stat line but his unwillingness to have a go at full-backs combined with his low success rate of beating his man puts us on the back foot a lot. The one thing Barnes does well is he makes a lot of runs, maybe with someone in that ten who can find him he would offer more.

Toure is so raw if he were a steak a Frenchman would send him back to cook some more, he has moments, but you just don’t know what you would get with him. I’d be tempted to play him more purely because he is one of our only options who will get at his full-back and I think he may actually be better playing on the right in the long run.

The last player to throw into the mix is Pardo, the lad wants to be a striker but that is very optimistic, and I think it’s clear he’s a winger and that’s where he needs to play most likely on the left. So again, on the wings not an inspiring bunch, I’d like to see Pardo getting game time on the left and it’s between Toure or Barnes for the right (Murphy and Elanga only look good when the other is playing). I think it would have to be Toure as we need to see what he can do, he’s a left footer so would cut inside more naturally, and he can make something happen.

The last position is probably the most worrying because at previous clubs Matthias Jaissle has used strikers like Toney and Sesko as his focal points, physical centre forwards who can put pressure on the opposition centre backs and hold the ball up. As mentioned before, Wissa often looks like he is playing in Jeans and even when he gets on the ball, he has been less than inspiring and doesn’t have the physicality to hold the ball up or the quality to link the play up. Our other option is Osula who can be chaotic and has moments where you think there might be a player there but then he does things like not run in a straight line when chasing a pass or get shrugged off the ball despite his physical gifts. There’s something there but he has a very headless vibe. At this point he’s clearly the better option but if Wilson gets his act together and does something in January a CF is a must.

So, for now a 4-2-3-1 Newcastle United team of:

Hornicek, Dedic, Thiaw, Burn, Hall, Nico, Miley, Toure, Steur, Pardo, Osula

This is what I think is the best we’ve got and I’d like to see this line up at some point soon, but I think the key thing we need to try is moving Steur into the free/10 role, to let him try and solve our creativity issues.

Anyway, back to reality and Saturday coming, fingers crossed for the oncoming onslaught of the Opulent Stoke. Fingers crossed and Howay the Mags.
