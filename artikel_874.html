Premier League: 10 things to look out for this weekend

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bruno Guimarães to feel the heat on Tyneside, Alex Scott’s return further cheers Cherries and Manchester City face favourite whipping boys

After three Premier League defeats on the spin, Manchester City need something or someone to come along and put some pep in their step, and Fulham look like just the thing – after all, in the past 15 years these teams have played 20 times in all competitions, and City have won precisely 20 of those games. Their current run of 17 successive league wins against Fulham is the longest run of victories by one club against a single opponent in English top-flight history. It is a reflection of the changing realities of English football, and the gulf that has opened between the teams that dominate it and those who seek to challenge them, that of the top 10 such runs all but one occurred in whole or in part after 2010 and six of them have been achieved by Sheikh Mansour’s Manchester City, with Chelsea responsible for another two, Manchester United one, and Portsmouth’s postwar, 13-game supremacy over Everton – a wild ride that included two 5-0s, a 5-1, a 6-3 and a 7-0 – the anomaly. Simon Burnton

Manchester City v Fulham, Saturday 12.30pm (all times GMT)

Positivity surrounds Aston Villa despite them failing to win in four matches, the last of which ended in agonising fashion; they had to settle for a 4-4 draw at Manchester United after twice squandering a two-goal advantage. Villa should have managed the final minutes better, but a home date with Sunderland presents an opportunity to return to winning ways. Unai Emery needs to eliminate the defensive naivety that has blighted some performances and the expected absence of Matty Cash, this time owing to concussion sustained on Poland duty, would deny the Villa manager his preferred defensive lineup. This fixture threw up a classic last season, Tammy Abraham scoring a stoppage-time winner to extend the visitors’ miserable record at Villa Park. Sunderland have won on only two of their past 17 league visits, their last victory coming in 2011 courtesy of the former Villa loanee Phil Bardsley. Ben Fisher

The last time Alex Scott played for Bournemouth, Marco Rose was yet to taste victory in the Premier League. When the 23-year-old returns to midfield he will do so with the team in a totally different place. Bournemouth have racked up eight straight wins in all competitions before hosting Nottingham Forest on Saturday and Scott, who has been able to build his fitness during the international break after a thigh problem, may have to wait his turn owing to the form of Tyler Adams and Ryan Christie. If Rose opts for momentum, then Eli Junior Kroupi, fresh from scoring his first France goal, could make his first start of the season after sustaining a freak foot injury in pre-season. Bournemouth have hardly been short of firepower, though, with Evanilson scoring seven goals in his past eight games. BF

Bournemouth v Nottingham Forest, Saturday 3pm

Chelsea left Wearside battered and bruised after being pushed around by Brian Brobbey. The Sunderland forward bullied Wesley Fofana and Levi Colwill, who had to be substituted at half-time, and won the game’s decisive penalty with a solo run that left Xabi Alonso angry with his side’s powderpuff defending. Alonso insisted Chelsea had prepared for Brobbey’s threat; the Chelsea manager will hope they are better at putting the plan into practice when they face another target man on Saturday. Leeds head to Stamford Bridge and will look to Dominic Calvert-Lewin, fresh from helping England get over the line against Spain last weekend, to unnerve Alonso’s defenders. Jacob Steinberg

Thomas Frank’s first assessment of his new charges after taking over at Crystal Palace this week included the nuggets that “we’re conceding too many goals … we need to work on the defensive side of things with principles, attitude, everything regarding that”, and in particular “we need to work on the set pieces, that’s key”. If he can’t inspire an immediate improvement this is where this match could be decided, being as it is an encounter between the league’s worst set-piece defenders – no one comes close to the 67 shots and xG of 7.44 coughed up by Palace at restarts – and the best. Though Coventry have let in one goal from set pieces, and neither Brentford nor Liverpool have shipped any, the hosts’ set-piece xG of 1.65 is a league low, and they were equally outstanding from dead-ball situations in the Championship last season. The home side will be boosted by the return of the 6ft 4in defender Bobby Thomas, who, despite a calf injury that meant he missed the last two games and the final third of the one before that, has still made more clearances than any other Coventry player. SB

It may have been a slightly strange week for Tottenham fans. They have watched as their former managers Mauricio Pochettino and Thomas Frank have been installed at Manchester United and Crystal Palace respectively, while their current head coach, Roberto De Zerbi, picked up the Premier League’s manager of the month award for October. That is reflective of the improvement De Zerbi has overseen in recent weeks, with Spurs happy to move forward with the Italian and avoid the chaos enveloping some of their rivals. The long-held assumption among some connected to Tottenham that Pochettino would one day return to save the club must now be banished; De Zerbi needs full buy-in from everyone to build his project. That’s the only way Spurs can ensure they steer clear of future crises. Dominic Booth

With the temperature in Newcastle forecast to dip to 0C at kick-off, Matthias Jaissle is perhaps hoping that his team’s guests from north London (where a balmy 9C is predicted at 5.30pm) freeze in the Tyneside chill. Otherwise it is hard to envisage his young side overcoming a formidable looking Arsenal on a day when Newcastle’s head coach seeks what would be only his second win in seven games. Home fans will not merely be hurt by the sight of their much-missed former captain Bruno Guimarães wearing Arsenal colours but decidedly nervous about a fixture that precedes a run of tricky engagements involving a trip to Brighton and home dates with Manchester United and Sunderland. Jaissle’s cause could be helped should Mikel Arteta name Guimarães in his starting XI and the crowd respond to the once adored Brazilian’s defection by creating the sort of hostile atmosphere that might just galvanise a Newcastle team expected to contain the former Arsenal midfielder Joe Willock. Meanwhile Jaissle’s defence must be mindful of the threat posed by another Newcastle old boy in Mikel Merino. Louise Taylor

Brighton are out to prove their early-season form has been no fluke and the fixture list for November and December looks promising for their chances of retaining their lofty position. Hull, having started the season with back-to-back league victories, now appear vulnerable and have not won since that August burst. Brighton follow the trip to Humberside with a home game against Universitatea Craiova in the Conference League, then host Newcastle, and face none of the big six until a visit to Tottenham on 29 December. With Brighton having won seven of their 10 league games, tentative talk of a title challenge may not seem so fanciful come Christmas. The challenge tactically for Fabian Hürzeler’s men will be to deal with the tag of favourites for most of their upcoming games, with teams perhaps starting to wise up to Brighton’s high-pressing, vertical style. DB

And so it starts all over again. Manchester United’s eighth permanent manager in 13 years – not forgetting the interim spells of Ryan Giggs, Michael Carrick, Ralf Rangnick, Ruud van Nistelrooy and Darren Fletcher – was impressively quick to commit to the cause when taking a private jet from Cincinnati to Manchester immediately after his final game as USA head coach. Mauricio Pochettino needs his new, floundering team to reciprocate at Anfield. United have conceded four times in the first four minutes of a game this season, seven goals in the opening 17 minutes in total, and will have had little time to absorb instructions from their latest coach by the time they face an expectant Liverpool. United’s defensive options might tempt many managers to board the next flight out but, as a decent record against the so-called big six since the start of last season demonstrates, the team are capable of showing up when they feel like it. That is just one of the many issues Pochettino has to address. Andy Hunter

Liverpool v Manchester United, Sunday 4.30pm

Fans of neutral officiating will appreciate the fact that both Brentford and Everton have won six games refereed by Craig Pawson, who will take charge of this match. But where the two clubs’ experiences diverge is that, while Brentford have only danced to the tune of the Pawson whistle 12 times, enjoying a 50% win ratio, Everton have toiled through 33 Pawson-helmed matches for an equivalent figure of 18%. Of Everton’s past 14 Pawson-patrolled matches there has been only one victory, and 10 defeats. Pawson, meanwhile, has taken charge of five Brentford home matches and they have won every one, thanks in part to the two penalties he has awarded them (both converted), compared with the one he gave against them (missed). Jack Grealish is back having sat out Everton’s past four league games because of illness, lack of fitness and a breach of club discipline, but given that, of the three Premier League matches he has missed, Everton won the two most recent – and the other one was at Arsenal – he may not go straight back into the team. Indeed of Everton’s four league wins this season, Grealish has missed three and played only eight minutes in a fourth. SB
