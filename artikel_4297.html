A survey found one in five people are taking tomorrow off work to play GTA. Will you be among them?

Running a business in this modern era is filled with challenges. How do you keep investors happy and optimistic for the future? What are the potential pitfalls of shifting cross-border trade deals? What if a really popular video game comes out and everybody takes Thursday off?

That last snag might sound ridiculous, but according to a survey from staffing software company BambooHR, nearly 20 percent of salaried U.S. workers are planning on taking the day off on Thursday, November 19, to play the new Grand Theft Auto VI game, which will launch at midnight (ET). Hype over GTA VI has been building steadily over the past few years, stoked by a couple of delays. Reportedly, presales of the game have hit the billion-dollar mark, which means that there are going to be a lot of sore thumbs over the next while.

For non-gamers, all of this furor can be hard to digest. However, in terms of storytelling, video gaming has proved a strong competitor for the movie industry, capable of weaving plot lines that extend over hours and hours of gameplay. Sure, nobody agonized over the paddle’s motivation in Pong, but these days you can make both a game and a movie about two Italian plumbers battling turtles, and both film and game will rake in huge profits.

Early reviews for the game are out, and the critics are almost universal in a chorus of praise for the blend of immersion and action, and the way the world of Vice City seems to be alive. The graphics look incredible, and unlike with some previous large gaming releases, the bugs seem to have been worked out in those previous launch delays.

Besides all the Grand Theft stuff, the Autos part of the series looks to be better than ever. Beyond police-spec Ford Explorer and Taurus Interceptors, there will be supercars, Rolls-Royce cabriolets, and all manner of high-dollar fare that you can nab on a whim. GTA VI doesn’t brand these cars, so it’ll be something called a Pegassi that you’ll be streaking down the highway in rather than a Lamborghini, but the looks are virtually the same.

The GTA series has always been slightly controversial for its blend of crime and violence, but then again, Goodfellas wasn’t popular as just a film about Joe Pesci offering art criticism on a painting with two dogs in it. Virtually robbing a bank and launching your getaway car over an apartment building is good fun, and it helps to blow off a little steam after a stressful day at work.
