Right-Leaning Tech Billionaires Are Prepping for a Blue Wave

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

It’s election Tuesday, and after years of lying low, the tech left is ready for its big comeback. “We’re gonna wipe the floor pretty aggressively,” one Democratic consultant who works with big tech donors boasted to me late last week. Meanwhile, a DC tech lobbyist I caught up with told me, “For my Republican friends, it’s like the fall of Saigon. Everyone’s trying to get the last chopper out.”

After the votes are all counted, I expect the postmortems coming out of Silicon Valley to start getting spicy. After all, the tech right hardly sat this one out. Elon Musk alone spent a reported $474 million on midterms races. A billionaire coalition including Sergey Brin, Patrick Collison, and Eric Schmidt spent more than $333 million to prevent the so-called billionaire tax in California. A swirling cadre of PACs and dark-money groups were caught using less-than-aboveboard tactics to combat a rising tide of public fury around AI. Spoiler: It didn’t work.

Over the last few weeks, I’ve been reporting on Greg and Anna Brockman, two of the tech mega-donors who’ve thrown the most money at this chaotic election cycle. While OpenAI cofounder and president Greg Brockman’s story has been told pretty extensively, Anna remains one of the last great mysteries on the AI frontier. As The Wall Street Journal put it this spring, “She is intensely private: Almost no information, including her maiden name, is available online.” Until now.

Read on to learn more about their AI-fueled love story, their slow-motion political misfire, and one of the more surprising twists in my journalism career: I went to college with her.

Mentioned in this issue: Citrini Research, Greg Brockman, Diane von Furstenberg, David Shor, Will DePue, Tina Brown, Alex Karp, Instacart, Darren Aronofsky, Kathryn Murdoch, Daniela and Dario Amodei, Elon Musk, Anna Brockman, Peter Thiel, Mira Murati, Donald Trump, James Blair, Niraj Pant, OpenAI, Sam Altman, DoorDash, Intel, Binance, Edna Mode, Subvocal, Meta, Ilya Sutskever, Y Combinator, Walter Isaacson, and more.

The buzzy macro research firm Citrini Research has started renting out the notorious “Torture Townhouse,” a luxurious eight-bedroom home in SoHo where two crypto investors allegedly kidnapped and tortured a man in an attempt to steal millions of dollars worth of Bitcoin. Over the weekend, Citrini threw a Halloween party in the now infamous apartment, where political data scientist David Shor, former OpenAI researcher Will DePue, and Ritual cofounder Niraj Pant stopped by. Dark.

Last week, Diane von Furstenberg, Tina Brown, Darren Aronofsky, Kathryn Murdoch, and others came out to fête Walter Isaacson’s new podcast The Builders, which features lengthy interviews with figures like Alex Karp, Daniela and Dario Amodei, Elon Musk, and Peter Thiel. “When we started this podcast, I was an AI optimist,” Isaacson told the crowd. But after learning more about the technology’s progress—and interviewing more than a couple of people he noted seem to “lack the empathy gene”—he became more fearful of what might happen when we unleash “swarms of agents that we allow to get out of our control.”

Buried in The Atlantic’s excellent profile of Donald Trump’s former White House deputy chief of staff and top Republican strategist James Blair was the tidbit that Meta had hired Blair to advise on its data-centers communications strategy. Meta did not respond to a request for comment.

OpenAI is already rolling out its post-midterms rebrand: Data centers are heretofore to be known as “compute campuses.” Good luck with that, guys!

DoorDash has acquired its grocery-delivery competitor Instacart for $17 billion—if regulators allow the deal to go through.

Frantic crypto investors are pulling their money out of Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, following a $6 billion cyberattack that the FBI attributed to North Korea. (About half that money was successfully frozen.) Binance has reported $3.64 billion in user withdrawals since restoring user access after the incident.

As if San Francisco’s street scenes weren’t surreal enough already, now you can expect to see people wandering around murmuring inaudibly to themselves while looking like Edna Mode. At least that’s the hope over at lipreading-to-text smart-glasses maker Subvocal, which just raised $1.3 million from Y Combinator and Afore Capital.

-25%: Chipmaker Intel’s stock took a tumble Friday along with other tech and AI stocks, as some investors began to hedge on big AI bets.
