Kilmar Ábrego García lands in Spain, ending deportation battle with Trump administration

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Kilmar Abrego Garcia lands in Madrid, Spain, after leaving the United States voluntarily amid a deportation fight.

Kilmar Ábrego García, the illegal immigrant from El Salvador whose case became a flashpoint for critics of President Donald Trump's immigration enforcement policies, landed in Spain on Wednesday after agreeing to leave the United States.

Ábrego García departed Tuesday for Madrid on a commercial flight and was swarmed with supporters and journalists after touching down and making his way through the airport.

His voluntary departure ends his battle with the Trump administration that began last year with his initial deportation over allegations that he was a member of the MS-13 gang. He ultimately plans to make his way to Mexico, The New York Times reported, citing his attorneys.

Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg, one of Ábrego García's lawyers, said in a statement late Tuesday that this is a "temporary departure" for his client, who has left the U.S. as a "free man."

"Mr. Abrego Garcia has many options to return to the United States as the husband of a U.S. citizen and the father of a U.S. citizen child. We don't expect that to happen under this administration, which we know will never give him a fair shake, but we expect that the next administration will prioritize reunifying this family and bringing him back to Maryland," Sandoval-Moshenberg said.

The White House celebrated Ábrego García leaving the U.S., maintaining that he is a gang member and a criminal.

"The American people can be thankful this Thanksgiving that this MS-13 gang member, human trafficker, serial domestic abuser, and child predator can no longer terrorize American citizens," said Lauren Bis, a White House spokeswoman. "This Salvadoran man chose to self-deport to Spain — a country that has been wrecked by open-border policies. Good riddance."

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Tuesday that he was "delighted" that Ábrego García would be on his way to Spain the next day.

Karoline Leavitt, formerly the White House press secretary, also weighed in, saying that Ábrego García leaving the country has "vindicated" the Trump administration.

"This was an individual who illegally entered our country, who has a very long and deep criminal record, by the way, and deserves and needs to be deported," Leavitt told Fox News host Rachel Campos-Duffy on Wednesday. "Kudos to the president, the Department of Homeland Security and Stephen Miller, who have been adamant about what's doing what's right."

A federal judge in Maryland ordered Ábrego García to leave the U.S. by Tuesday, according to two sources familiar with the matter. That order remains under seal, and the exact terms of his release were not immediately clear.

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) emphasized that Ábrego García did not receive any financial incentives through the Trump administration's voluntary self-deportation program, which offers migrants travel assistance and financial support to leave the country.

"To be clear: Kilmar Abrego Garcia departed the United States pursuant to a removal order. He paid for his own flight and received no incentives," DHS posted on X. "He will NEVER be allowed back inside the United States."

DHS also rejected descriptions of Ábrego García as a Maryland resident.

"Kilmar Abrego Garcia is not and will never be a Maryland Man—he is a criminal illegal alien from El Salvador and was a public safety threat to American citizens. He is not coming back, despite the efforts of sanctuary politicians who spent massive amounts of time and money defending this low-life," the department said.

The move comes after sustained efforts by the Trump administration to remove Ábrego García, who entered the U.S. illegally more than a decade ago. At one point during the legal proceedings, federal authorities sought to deport him to several African nations, one of them being Liberia.

Ábrego García had been living in Maryland with his wife and children when immigration authorities detained him in March 2025. He was subsequently deported to El Salvador in error and held in the Central American nation's maximum-security CECOT prison despite a standing court order barring his removal there due to the risk of gang persecution.

Democrats rallied around him despite his suspected ties to the MS-13 gang.

"A year and a half ago, the Trump Administration wanted to leave Kilmar Ábrego García to rot in a Salvadoran gulag. Today, Kilmar is traveling to a country of his choice, a free man — unharmed and with dignity," said Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who visited Ábrego García while detained in El Salvador.

"While the Trump Administration had been pursuing their vindictive prosecution against Kilmar and desperately trying to ship him against his will to countries known for human rights abuses, the Administration was forced to come to this agreement that respects Kilmar’s will and the due process rights guaranteed to him and to all in our nation," Van Hollen added. "I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free – including from this Administration’s constant persecution."

Kilmar Ábrego García and his wife, Jennifer, are seen outside the ICE Field Office in Baltimore on August 25, 2025. (Breanne Deppisch/Fox News Digital)

Federal officials eventually returned him to the U.S. to face human smuggling charges from a 2022 traffic stop in Tennessee. He pleaded not guilty to the charges, which a judge later dismissed.

Throughout the legal fight, the administration has claimed that Ábrego García was a gang member and pointed to a 2021 protective order his wife had filed against him over domestic violence allegations.
