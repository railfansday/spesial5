A Glorious Man

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Even now, Ben Sasse has not stopped thinking and speaking and writing about America.

Former senator Ben Sasse speaks during an event in Washington, D.C., June 2, 2026.

Listen closely, and you will notice that Ben Sasse uses the word “glorious” often. He uses it for the everyday goodness of Americans, the architecture of limited government they inherited, and now for his own rendezvous with God. Death by metastatic pancreatic cancer, Sasse says, is “what I need.”

This historian by training, turnaround specialist by trade, former senator by a felt obligation, is the rare conservative undeterred by fatalism, even — especially — when staring it in the face. In wisdom tucked into a footnote in his second book, Sasse doesn’t deny that the world is a “broken place always at risk of spinning out of control.” It’s just that he finds this unruliness quieting in a way, and responds with “profound gratitude for the gifts we’ve received and consequently aims to conserve or preserve those blessings.”

John R. Puri is the Thomas L. Rhodes Fellow at National Review.
