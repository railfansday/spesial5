Nectin-4 mediates rubella virus entry in airway and placental cells

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Rubella virus (RuV) causes rubella and, when acquired during pregnancy, congenital rubella syndrome1. Despite its clinical significance, the cellular entry mechanism of RuV remains poorly characterized, owing to limited identification of host receptors2, which hinders pathogenesis research and therapeutic development. Here, using proximity biotin labelling-based proteomics coupled with CRISPR–Cas9 validation, we identify nectin-4 as a functional RuV entry receptor. Ectopic expression of nectin-4 conferred susceptibility to RuV in non-permissive cells, whereas blocking or genetic ablation of nectin-4 reduced infection, results that demonstrate that nectin-4 is a major entry factor. Mechanistically, nectin-4 directly interacts with the RuV envelope glycoprotein heterodimer and facilitates receptor-mediated endocytosis following initial calcium-dependent viral attachment to cellular lipids. In polarized airway epithelial models, nectin-4 mediates basolateral entry of RuV and supports apical progeny release, a finding that provides a molecular mechanism for respiratory transmission. In placental models, nectin-4 is highly expressed on syncytiotrophoblasts and facilitates infection at the maternal–fetal interface, although additional nectin-4-independent pathways contribute to infection of other trophoblast populations. Together, these data establish nectin-4 as a key factor for respiratory transmission and early placental infection of RuV and highlight it as a promising therapeutic target for the prevention of rubella and related diseases.
