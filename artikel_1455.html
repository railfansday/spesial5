€130 million spent, the crazy compensation paid by Manchester United to fire its coaches

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The news finally broke a little earlier today. After three weeks of delay, Manchester United ended up dismissing Michael Carrick from his duties as first team coach. His situation was no longer tenable since the defeats in Como (4-1), at Old Trafford against Bournemouth (3-0), after having already lost the Manchester derby at home in September (1-0). We almost forget the tensions in the locker room with Lisandro Martinez and Bruno Fernandes. 18th in the Premier League with only one small victory on the clock against promoted Ipswich on the 2nd day, it was time for the adventure to end.

The former midfielder joins a very long list of managers dismissed since the departure of the emblematic Sir Alex Ferguson in 2013. He arrived last January, however, and had a good second half of last season, qualifying the Red Devils for the C1. Last May, its management then took the opportunity to extend it until 2028. A decision that it must regret 6 months later. To part with it, she had to sign a check for him and his staff for €6 million. Hiring Mauricio Pochettino, the likely successor, will cost €9 million.

In 13 years, MU has made a specialty of landing its coaches. The Telegraph even took stock of all the members of the technical staff who were fired before the end of their contract since David Moyes, Ferguson’s successor, at Carrick. The total sum seems barely believable: €130 million in compensation paid. First there was the staff attached to Ferguson that Moyes no longer wanted (€2.9 million), then a year later in 2014 the departure of the Scottish technician and his assistants (€5.8 million). Louis van Gaal arrived before being dismissed after two years and an extension signed halfway: €10 million.

The record (by far) belongs to José Mourinho, coach between 2018 and 2021 and left with a check for €23.5 million. Less prestigious, Ole Gunnar Solskjaer still cost MU’s finances €10.9 million in 2022. Ralf Rangnick took over, first as an interim before continuing the adventure. The German took the opportunity to make numerous internal changes, which had to be financed, before being asked to leave in turn. Management had to pay him more than €17.5 million to offer the position to Erik ten Hag. Manchester United thought they were getting off to a good start with the man who was coming out of an extraordinary adventure with Ajax.

He quickly became disillusioned. Initially weighed down by the incompatibility of mood and tactics with Cristiano Ronaldo, the Dutchman never managed to convince and ended up giving up, after two and a half seasons all the same. He left with the tidy sum of €12.4 million. Ruben Amorim was then recruited in the middle of the season from Sporting CP for €12 million, only to leave 14 months later with compensation of €9.8 million in his pocket. In the meantime, the sports unit had tried to reform with the recruitment of Dan Ashworth in the summer of 2024 for €3.6 million. At the beginning of December of the same year, the sports director left, recovering €4.9 million in the process.
