England's evolution: James Trafford and two other stars chisel their names into the XI, Thomas Tuchel learning from Argentina and Jude Bellingham playing like a captain!

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Published: 02:32 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 09:25 EST, 16 November 2026

England ended world champions Spain's 43-match unbeaten run in dramatic fashion with a 3-2 win on a memorable night in Madrid.

After Harry Kane, Ezri Konsa and Ben White scored to establish a three-goal lead, the visitors had to cling on after Elliot Anderson was sent off and the hosts scored twice in the final half hour.

The victory means Thomas Tuchel's side top their Nations League group and it provided a strong riposte to criticism of the manager following the World Cup semi-final defeat by Argentina in July.

Our chief football reporter, Craig Hope, was at the Metropolitano Stadium, and here are his FIVE big take-aways…

It would not have mattered had England conceded a late equaliser. To draw comparison with Argentina in the final half hour is unfair and inaccurate. The World Cup semi-final was 11v11 and so, when we draw conclusions from this game, let us do so on the same terms.

Thomas Tuchel was delighted with his England players as they won 3-2 away in Spain

And what did England do at 11v11 with a 1-0 lead? They attacked. And attacked again. This was a major change (and improvement) and is the reason for such encouragement. They have learnt from a mistake, as much as Tuchel will be loathe to admit.

Mexico would be a fairer parallel, but that was against opposition far inferior to Spain. At 11v10, we could not expect England to stay on the front foot here, as they were until the point of Jarell Quansah's needless mistake and Elliot Anderson's subsequent red card.

And that is cause for great optimism - because this game was not in the balance in any way when the teams were at equal number.

For me, and I don't think this is even close, this was the best performance of Tuchel's reign.

Do England suddenly have a pair of full backs?! After years of trawling and trialling, here in the most testing of surrounds, Ben White and Lewis Hall inked their names into Tuchel's XI.

We can forgive the concession of two goals when down to 10 men - because with 11, Spain barely looked like getting a shot on target. In fact, they didn't.

Much of that was down to the solidity of the full backs. Then, with backs to the wall, the pair became bricks in the resistance. One White block late on was heroic.

Before that, Hall's best work was not in containing Lamine Yamal, but in emulating him. His dribbles were daring and unnerved Spain, who thought they would spend the game in England's half and with the ball. They did neither early on and Hall was central to that.

White's goal had shades of Carlos Alberto on the overlap, and his England redemption arc felt like it reached completion here. Let him now move on now, for England need him.

No more boos, please, only cheers for White (and Hall).

It's time to stop the boos for Ben White after he starred with Lewis Hall at full back

Ezri Konsa is England's best defender. Build the backline around him.

Tuchel said on the eve of the game that he thought Konsa had grown for the move to Arsenal, and this was proof.

Boy do England need a mature, reliable presence at the heart of their defence. Konsa showed here why he can be that man. He scored a fine goal, which is always a sign of a defender ready to take wider responsibility, but it was his cool interceptions and desperate blocks at the other end that were most reassuring.

What Konsa has to do now is build on this - and a man-of-the-match performance at the home of the world champions is a pretty lofty and solid foundation from which to do so.

Tuchel will, and should, be giving serious thought to keeping James Trafford in England's goal for the Nations League quarter-finals.

Jordan Pickford has done nothing wrong. And yet, it feels like one position the manager wants to evolve - and here was his justification.

Trafford can be a little erratic and immature. What better way to grow, emotionally, than with a series of saves to preserve an historic win. He will feel like a different player, and person, to the one who started this game. Like White and Hall, this felt like a coming-of-age night in an England jersey.

Trafford has all the tools. He might just have used them to chisel his name into that team-sheet.

Spain's fans are left shocked as Ezri Konsa hails the brilliant Trafford in emphatic fashion

No goals, but England would not have won this game without Bellingham. He terrified Spain, perhaps given their intimate knowledge of his brilliance.

Beating the best is as much a mental challenge as it is physical, and Bellingham believed. He made England believe, too.

His swagger. His majesty. And yes, his skill. Of all the positives from this win, it is hard to look past Bellingham as the biggest reason for England being able to challenge Spain at the top of the world rankings.

Jude Bellingham played like a captain, dropping into midfield when down to 10 men

He won the corner for the second goal and created the third. He then dropped into midfield when down to 10 and played like a captain.

That is what England need - Bellingham the leader, not Bellingham the individual. He has bought into the collective under Tuchel and, with that, his team-mates are buying into his big ideas. They don't come much bigger than what England achieved here.
