THE BREAKDOWN: How Florian Wirtz suddenly became the most powerful creative force in the Premier League - from Andoni Iraola's perfect tactics to Liverpool's Bradley Barcola effect

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 21:00 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 05:37 EST, 25 November 2026

After Florian Wirtz trudged off the field just over an hour into Liverpool’s defeat by Manchester City in October, sacrificed following Ryan Gravenberch’s red card, Jamie Carragher had once again seen enough of the £116million man.

‘I think he needs to come out of the team,’ said Carragher post-match. ‘I said that last time out and I'm going to say it again.’

Fast forward six weeks, and Wirtz was running the show for Liverpool as they put four past Manchester United in poor Mauricio Pochettino’s first match in charge, topping a stellar performance off with a goal and an assist. Carragher hailed the German’s stunning first-half volley as ‘a goal Dennis Bergkamp would have scored’.

How things change. This was a performance, even against an abysmal 10-man United, that showed Wirtz was ready, at last, to seize the big occasions.

Because, for so long, he wasn’t ready. Carragher was not alone in his earlier assessment, one shared by countless frustrated Liverpool fans. There was a feeling that this slight, wispy technician had the tools to flourish in the slower-paced Bundesliga, or even for Liverpool in the Champions League, but would, perhaps, remain unable to shine amid the rough and tumble of the Premier League.

Wirtz managed five goals and four assists in the Premier League last season and added only one assist this term before that City game. Since then, he’s come to life. Four assists and a couple of goals in the league with another one of each in the Champions League, a tally he will hope to add to on Wednesday evening against Club Bruges.

Florian Wirtz has suddenly sprung to life as a Liverpool player and is making his critics eat their words

Wirtz scores a majestic flick against Manchester United on Sunday

Since that City defeat, Wirtz is getting into dangerous positions more often. He is having more touches in the opposition box, more shots in the opposition box, playing more passes into the opposition box and creating more than twice as many chances from open play.

Wirtz boasts a league-high 29 of those this season, and 20 of the chances have come since that City game.

The last player to create more than the 10 open-play chances Wirtz managed against United was David Silva in 2015. Wirtz created nine against Brentford, too, in the first league match after the City defeat, meaning he’s the only Premier League player on record (since 2003-04) to create nine or more chances from open play in multiple games in the same season. Wirtz did it twice in five weeks.

Let’s nip this one in the bud early. It is clear, of course, that Jurgen Klopp, the legendary former Liverpool manager now Germany head coach, has got a tune out of Wirtz in recent months. The pair, according to those who follow the national team, are very close.

‘Wirtz is a once-in-a-century talent,’ was Klopp’s assessment last year, while he later remarked that he was ‘shocked’ by how good Wirtz was in their first training session together.

The 23-year-old has shown it in matches, too, with four goals and an assist in his last four games for Germany, including a brace last week against the Netherlands.

This has fed a narrative of how Klopp has been the one to unlock Wirtz’s powers. Klopp deserves credit, naturally, but he is also helped by having the freedom to give Wirtz what he needs: space.

‘What do I need to be at my best? Ideally, a lot of space in every game so I can do what I want!’ said Wirtz after scoring one and setting up the other in Germany’s 2-0 win over Serbia in October.

He was half-joking, laughing as he said it, but he’s right. Technical creators like Wirtz thrive on space. He’s been able to have more of that in international football, with Klopp even playing him as a false nine to give him all the creative freedom he needs. He leads the press but the midfielders behind him do the bulk of the dirty work to allow Wirtz, the star, to shine.

Wirtz’s performances with his country have helped grow his confidence, but it would be deeply unfair on Iraola to suggest Klopp’s blueprint is the one now being copied.

Even if Liverpool and Germany play a similar style, it is much, much harder to give players freedom in the Premier League.

But if anyone was going to find a way to do it for Wirtz, it was Iraola.

Arne Slot could never work it out. By the end of his tenure, he’d played Wirtz as an attacking midfielder, stuck him on both flanks and thrust him up front. It was a conundrum he just couldn’t solve.

Iraola's Bournemouth side were one of the hardest-working, highest-pressing teams in the division but his system was still able to allow the likes of Justin Kluivert or Junior Kroupi to create and drift into pockets of space to set the attacking tempo.

For Iraola, it was always going to be Wirtz. The German has started every league game, all of them in the No 10 position.

He’s always led the way off the ball – only three players have won possession more often in the final third this season – but Iraola has been finding different ways to get the best out of him with the ball, too. Against Brentford, he shifted Rio Ngumoha inside almost to play as a second No 10 beside Wirtz, which helped create space for him to set up Liverpool's equaliser.

Having Alexis Mac Allister behind him allows Wirtz to concentrate on being the creative heartbeat, while even Dominik Szoboszlai has played noticeably deeper since returning from injury.

Andoni Iraola has started Wirtz in every league game, all of them in the No 10 position

Szoboszlai isn't the only one. Another aspect of Germany's game that has helped Wirtz is lining up alongside strikers who like to drop deep in Kai Havertz and Nick Woltemade, leaving space for Wirtz to attack.

In recent weeks, especially against United, Liverpool striker Alexander Isak has been doing the same. It was Isak doing exactly that which led to Wirtz’s goal.

Isak (below, in possession) drops in, plays the ball out wide to Ngumoha and Wirtz sprints into the box to get on to the end of the cross.

Alexander Isak dropped into midfield and sprayed the ball wide to Rio Ngumoha before Wirtz's goal against United

Isak missed much of last season through injury but his all-round play is much better attuned to Wirtz's game than those who played alongside him for most of 2025-26, including Hugo Ekitike.

One of the most devastating aspects of Iraola’s Bournemouth was how quickly players flooded forward when they attacked.

His Liverpool side is starting to do the same which, in turn, helps get the best out of Wirtz.

Last season under Slot, the likes of Cody Gakpo and Mo Salah would often tuck in instead of bombing forward out wide and Milos Kerkez, one of the most attacking full backs in the division under Iraola at Bournemouth, rarely did the same under Slot.

Now, Liverpool’s wingers are flying. This season under Iraola, Liverpool are averaging more ‘fast breaks’, how Opta define counter attacks, and shots from them than in any campaign under Slot or even the ‘heavy metal’ Klopp.

Wirtz has attempted seven through balls this season, one of the highest tallies in the division. Since the City game in particular, he has tried to play passes with purpose into the box.

Wirtz was creating chances in the first six games of this season but only a few of them were penetrating passes into the box

The German's chances created in the last five games, since the City defeat, show how many balls he is playing into dangerous areas

The rapid Bradley Barcola starting to find his feet since his £123m move has been a huge help to Wirtz.

Of Wirtz’s 20 open-play chances created since the City game, a quarter of them have been for Barcola.

Having those runners out wide like Barcola and Ngumoha is so helpful for the German. As he drives forward with the ball against United (below), he has three runners ahead of him with even Szoboszlai bursting forward. Wirtz has so many options and eventually slips in Isak for a great chance.

Liverpool had wide runners driving forward against United, helping Wirtz create chances and split their defence

You can see from the average position map from the game (below), just how attacking Liverpool’s wingers – and Kerkez – were, albeit, granted, against 10 men for most of the contest.

The average position map of Liverpool's players against United shows how high up the pitch they played

Wirtz has had a run like this before, around this time last year. During December and January, he racked up four league goals and three assists – having not managed a single one until then – only to notch one of each for the rest of the Premier League campaign.

You get the sense, though, with his confidence growing under managers for both club and country who want to play his way, that he might just keep shining this time.
