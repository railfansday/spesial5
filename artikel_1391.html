Bistable regulation of adipocyte heterogeneity governs thermogenesis

Brown adipocytes (BAs) are thermogenic cells that regulate energy homeostasis, and age-related declines in thermogenesis contribute to cold sensitivity1,2,3,4,5. We previously identified a BA subpopulation with low UCP1 expression (hereafter, BA-L cells) that coexists with the classical, high-UCP1-expressing BAs (hereafter, BA-H cells)6. Here we show that IL-1β secreted by neutrophils and macrophages stabilizes nearby BAs in the BA-L state. Specifically, IL-1β stabilizes hexose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (H6PD) protein from degradation to ensure the generation of active glucocorticoids, which activate the glucocorticoid receptor (GR, encoded by Nr3c1) and increase the expression of its target gene, regulator of G-protein signalling 2 (Rgs2), thereby suppressing UCP1 expression. Cold exposure reduces IL-1β levels, depending on sympathetic innervation, leading to H6PD degradation and the conversion of BA-L cells to BA-H cells, thereby enhancing UCP1-dependent thermogenesis. Rising environmental temperatures trigger the opposite events. BA-specific inducible deletion of Nr3c1 during cold exposure locks these cells in the BA-H state, leading to persistent thermogenesis and increased energy expenditure days after the mice return to room temperature. Notably, IL-1β is elevated in aged brown adipose tissue, resulting in a locked low BA-H/BA-L ratio and cold intolerance. Suppressing GR in aged mice restores the BA-H/BA-L ratio, resumes thermogenesis and increases energy expenditure. Our findings reveal a spatiotemporal neuronal–immune regulation of BA heterogeneity that governs thermogenesis and offers a fundamental understanding of the mechanisms underlying increased cold sensitivity in older individuals.

Functional cellular heterogeneity is well documented in hepatocytes, driven by liver zonation7, and in muscle fibres, shaped by local environmental cues, particularly motor neuron activity8. Single-cell technologies have revealed extensive new patterns of heterogeneity of specific cell types within the same tissue9, yet their biological importance remains largely undetermined.

Thermogenic adipose tissue undergoes adaptive thermogenesis and is crucial for energy expenditure and thermoregulation1,2. Its specialized metabolism and precise responses to environmental and physiological stimuli make it an ideal model for studying functional cellular heterogeneity. Thermogenic capacity declines substantially with age in rodents and humans, leading to age-related cold sensitivity and metabolic disorders3,4,5, yet the underlying mechanisms remain unclear.

BAs and beige adipocytes mediate UCP1-dependent non-shivering thermogenesis10. Although substantial progress has been made in identifying the key pathways that activate thermogenesis, most studies have been conducted at the whole-tissue level, leaving the mechanisms governing its activation, maintenance and deactivation incompletely understood.

The heterogeneity of BAs within the same brown adipose tissue (BAT) and beige adipocyte cluster within inguinal white adipose tissue (iWAT) has been characterized by our group6 and others5,11,12,13,14. We found that BAs heterogeneously express UCP1 and classified those with low expression as BA-L cells and those with high expression as BA-H cells6. At room temperature, BA-H cells constitute 30–50% of all BAs; BA-H cells predominate after cold exposure, whereas BA-L cells predominate under thermoneutral conditions6. This raises questions of why BA heterogeneity exists, and whether it reflects random variation along a thermogenic continuum or spatially organized, tightly regulated states within the tissue.

Here we identify dynamic bistable regulation of BA heterogeneity controlled by the spatial proximity of BAs to IL-1β puncta through a neuronal–immune regulatory mechanism. This regulation becomes dysfunctional during ageing, leading to cold intolerance in older adults. These findings highlight how different cell types collectively control cellular heterogeneity in a spatiotemporal manner, integrating environmental and internal cues to regulate tissue metabolism and function.

Reanalysis of our previous single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset6 revealed enrichment of glucocorticoid signalling and GR-target genes in BA-L cells (Extended Data Fig. 1a–c). Top BA-L-cell-enriched genes include H6pd, Rgs2 and thioredoxin-interacting protein (Txnip), whereas BA-H cells were enriched for thermogenesis-related genes, including Ucp1 and cytochrome c oxidase 6B1 (Cox6b1) (Extended Data Fig. 1a,b,d–f). Nr3c1 was similarly expressed in BA-H and BA-L cells (Extended Data Fig. 1e,f).

We generated Ucp1-Chaser-mT/mG mice for inducible labelling of Ucp1+ cells by crossing the Ucp1-rtTA, TRE-cre and Rosa-stop-mT/mG mouse lines (Extended Data Fig. 2a). After 1 week of feeding on a doxycycline diet at room temperature (24 °C), GFP+ and GFP− BAs showed a patchy distribution (Fig. 1a), consistent with BA heterogeneity in AdipoChaser mice6. The Ucp1-rtTA mice used a relatively weak 5 kb Ucp1 promoter15 so GFP is only expressed in UCP1high-expressing BAs (BA-H cells), while Ucp1-cre mice have a strong bacterial artificial chromosome-based Ucp1 promoter that drives Cre expression in all BAs16. Immunofluorescence (IF) staining confirmed selective H6PD expression in BA-L cells (Fig. 1a); 50% of BAs were UCP1+(GFP+)H6PD−, 45% were UCP1−(GFP−)H6PD+ and 5% were UCP1+(GFP+)H6PD+ (Fig. 1b). Similar heterogeneity was observed in iWAT after 1 week of cold exposure (6 °C) (Fig. 1c), with 45% of these beige adipocytes being UCP1+H6PD−, 49% UCP1−H6PD+ and 6% UCP1+H6PD+ (Fig. 1d). Human BAT showed a similar reciprocal UCP1 and H6PD pattern in multilocular brown–beige adipocytes (Fig. 1e), 45% of BAs were UCP1+H6PD−, 47% were UCP1−H6PD+ and 8% were UCP1+H6PD+ (Fig. 1f). Western blotting confirmed more than twofold higher H6PD in BA-L cells than in BA-H cells and more than fourfold higher UCP1 in BA-H cells than in BA-L cells, while H6PD was enriched in mature BAs relative to in the stromal vascular fractions (SVFs) (Extended Data Fig. 2b,c). Together, these data identify H6PD as a BA-L marker.

To enable real-time labelling of BA-H cells, we generated Ucp1-mGFP knock-in mice by inserting a P2A-mGFP cassette after Ucp1 exon 6, immediately upstream of the stop codon (Extended Data Fig. 2d). These mice displayed robust BAT GFP fluorescence without altered UCP1 expression (Extended Data Fig. 2e,f). GFP IF staining revealed a heterogeneous distribution of GFP+ and GFP− BAs (Extended Data Fig. 2g). In vitro-cultured BAs and beige adipocytes showed selective H6PD expression in GFP− BA-L cells (Extended Data Fig. 2h), validating our in vivo findings.

We next investigated UCP1-independent thermogenesis through the futile creatine cycle17,18,19,20,21. Tissue-non-specific alkaline phosphatase (TNAP) and creatine kinase B (CKB) protein levels increased 4.8-fold and 2.5-fold, respectively, in BAT during cold exposure and were enriched 2.9-fold and 2.0-fold in BA-L cells versus in BA-H cells (Extended Data Fig. 3a,b), consistent with higher Ckb mRNA in BA-L cells6. IF staining further showed TNAP enrichment in BA-L cells (H6PDhigh BAs) in BAT and H6PDhigh beige adipocytes in cold-exposed iWAT (Extended Data Fig. 3c,d). In vitro-differentiated BAs also displayed selective TNAP expression in BA-L cells (GFP− BAs) (Extended Data Fig. 3e), indicating that the futile creatine cycle is more active in BA-L cells.

Regarding the metabolic differences between BA-L cells and BA-H cells, BA-L cells have half the basal respiration, but similar maximal respiration compared to BA-H cells, indicating similarly high mitochondrial potential6. Using in vivo [U-13C]glucose tracing22, we recently showed that BAT has the most active glucose oxidation among metabolically active tissues, while in vitro tracing confirmed active glucose oxidation in differentiated BAs23. To characterize BA-L metabolism, we performed [U-13C] glucose and [U-13C] palmitate tracing in freshly isolated BA-H cells and BA-L cells, followed by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) analysis (Extended Data Fig. 4a). Fully labelled (m+16) free palmitate (C16:0) was about twofold higher in BA-L cells, indicating greater fatty acid uptake (Extended Data Fig. 4b), while fully labelled total palmitate was sixfold higher, suggesting preferential fatty acids storage in BA-L cells (Extended Data Fig. 4c). BA-L cells also showed lower citrate enrichment from both tracers, indicating lower glucose and fatty acid oxidation (Extended Data Fig. 4d). Thus, BA-L cells exhibit greater fatty acid uptake and accumulation but lower fatty acid and glucose oxidation, consistent with their lower basal respiration6.

We next examined whether H6PD is a functional marker of BA-L cells. H6PD depletion in BAs differentiated from BAT SVFs of Ucp1-mGFP mice increased Ucp1 expression sixfold (Fig. 1g), indicating that H6PD negatively regulates Ucp1. Conversely, H6pd overexpression by lentiviral injection into BAT of Ucp1-mGFP mice (Fig. 1h and Extended Data Fig. 4e,f) significantly reduced the proportion of BA-H cells (Fig. 1i,j). Thus, H6PD is a functional marker of BA-L that regulates UCP1 expression and BA heterogeneity.

H6PD localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum lumen and generates endoplasmic reticulum-resident NADPH, distinct from cytosolic glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD)24. H6PD primarily determines the reductase activity of endoplasmic reticulum-localized 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (HSD11B1), which converts inactive cortisone to active cortisol25. GR protein levels were similar between BA-H cells and BA-L cells, indicating that GR signalling is not regulated by receptor abundance (Extended Data Fig. 3b). We next investigated whether GR signalling regulates UCP1 expression. Simultaneous treatment of BAs differentiated from the SVFs of wild-type (WT) mice with 11-dehydrocorticosterone (11-DHC), a natural substrate for active glucocorticoid biosynthesis25, and noradrenaline (NA) substantially increased UCP1 protein (Fig. 2a). However, 24 h 11-DHC pretreatment blunted NA-induced UCP1 expression (Fig. 2b). Thus, prolonged activation of GR signalling suppresses BA responsiveness to NA, maintaining BA-L cells in a stable, low-thermogenic status.

Forskolin activates adenylyl cyclase (AC) to generate cAMP26. 11-DHC pretreatment also blocked forskolin-induced UCP1 expression (Fig. 2c), suggesting inhibition of AC activity or downstream cAMP signalling. To determine where GR signalling acts within the canonical β-adrenergic receptor–cAMP–PKA pathway that controls UCP1 expression27, we measured PKA activity in in vitro-differentiated BAs after 24 h of 11-DHC pretreatment, followed by NA, forskolin or 8-BrcAMP (a cAMP derivative). 11-DHC pretreatment markedly reduced NA- or forskolin-induced PKA activity (Fig. 2d,e), but not the response to 8-BrcAMP (Fig. 2f), indicating that downstream cAMP signalling is not affected by GR activation. Thus, GR signalling potentially suppresses UCP1 expression by inhibiting AC-mediated cAMP production.

Whereas 11-DHC requires conversion to active glucocorticoids, dexamethasone (DEX) directly activates GR. In contrast to the delayed effect of 11-DHC, DEX suppressed NA-induced UCP1 expression after co-treatment (Fig. 2g), an effect that was abrogated by the GR antagonist mifepristone (RU-486) (Fig. 2g). Similarly, in the in vitro-differentiated BAs, CL-316,243 increased Ucp1 expression about 3-fold, whereas DEX pretreatment reduced this induction to about 1.6-fold (Extended Data Fig. 5a). Together, these data support the conclusion that GR activation maintains BA-L cells in a low-UCP1-expressing state by suppressing cAMP production.

We next investigated how GR signalling regulates BA heterogeneity. DEX is commonly used in adipogenic differentiation cocktails; its withdrawal increased the BA-H/BA-L ratio by 2.8-fold in BAs differentiated from BAT SVF of Ucp1-mGFP mice (Extended Data Fig. 5b,c). Conversely, 11-DHC and the active glucocorticoid corticosterone decreased the BA-H/BA-L ratio compared with in the DEX-free control, independent of differentiation efficiency (Extended Data Fig. 5b–d). In WT mice, DEX induced BAT whitening and reduced the BA-H/BA-L ratio by 74% (Extended Data Fig. 5e,f). Thus, manipulating GR signalling alters BA heterogeneity both in vitro and in vivo.

To determine how GR signalling inhibits cAMP production, we examined other BA-L-enriched genes. Rgs2 was highly expressed in BA-L cells (Extended Data Fig. 1a,b,e,f). RGS2 binds to the C1 domain of AC III, V and VI, attenuating Gαs-dependent cAMP accumulation28. IF staining confirmed selective RGS2 expression in BA-L cells, with 40% of BAs being UCP1+(GFP+)RGS2−, 55% UCP1−(GFP−)RGS2+ and 5% UCP1+(GFP+)RGS2+ (Fig. 2h). RGS2 was also selectively expressed in GFP− BA-L cells in in vitro-differentiated BAs (brown and beige) (Extended Data Fig. 5g,h). Analysis of published GR chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) followed by sequencing datasets29 identified GR occupancy within the promoter and intronic regions of Rgs2 (Extended Data Fig. 5i). ChIP–quantitative PCR (qPCR) in in vitro-differentiated BAs confirmed GR binding at the Rgs2 promoter, which was enhanced by DEX (Fig. 2i). Lastly, DEX increased Rgs2 mRNA 2.2-fold in freshly isolated BAs (Fig. 2j), supporting Rgs2 as a GR target. Thus, RGS2 is a GR target in the BAs that inhibits cAMP production. Knocking down Rgs2 in in vitro-differentiated BAs (Fig. 2k) did not affect PKA activity in response to NA alone (Extended Data Fig. 5j,k). However, Rgs2 knockdown abrogated the inhibitory effect of 11-DHC pretreatment on NA-induced PKA substrate phosphorylation (Fig. 2l,m). Rgs2 is therefore a GR target in BAs that suppresses Ucp1 expression by inhibiting AC activity, and BA-L cells are stabilized in the low-thermogenic state through the GR–RGS2–AC signalling axis.

H6PD protein levels in BAT changed inversely with UCP1 across environmental temperatures, while GR protein was only slightly elevated at thermoneutrality (30 °C) (Extended Data Fig. 6a). Cold exposure reduced H6PD protein by 1.8-fold in iWAT, but not in gonadal white adipose tissue (gWAT) (Extended Data Fig. 6b,c), suggesting thermogenic-depot-specific regulation. CL-316,243 similarly decreased H6PD and increased UCP1 protein levels in BAT (Extended Data Fig. 6d). H6pd mRNA decreased rather than increased with rising temperature in BAT (Extended Data Fig. 6e), indicating post-transcriptional regulation. Thus, H6PD protein levels, but not mRNA levels, change dynamically in BAT in response to fluctuating environmental temperatures, which may be essential for altering the BA-H/BA-L ratio in response to thermogenic demand.

Sympathetic neurons secrete NA to activate BA thermogenesis. We next tested whether sympathetic innervation mediates cold-induced H6PD protein reduction using unilateral BAT denervation, validated by reduced tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) protein (Fig. 3a). Cold exposure increased BA-H cells from 31.1% to 85.6% (Fig. 3b), as previously reported6. Denervation prevented the cold-induced reduction of H6PD (Fig. 3a), and although there was an increase in the BA-H/BA-L ratio, the proportion of BA-H cells was 66% lower than the sham group during cold exposure (29.1% versus 85.6%) (Fig. 3b). The low BA-H/BA-L ratio was accompanied by BA whitening (Extended Data Fig. 6f), a feature associated with impaired thermogenesis30. Notably, H6PD consistently labelled BA-L cells regardless of the variable BA-H/BA-L ratio at different environmental temperatures (Fig. 3b). Thus, sympathetic innervation is required to reduce H6PD protein and raise the BA-H/BA-L ratio after cold exposure.

Cold exposure also downregulated GR target genes, including Rasd1, Fkbp5, Pik3r1, Tsc22d3 and Rgs2 in BAT, whereas denervation blunted these effects (Extended Data Fig. 6g), supporting the involvement of GR signalling in raising the BA-H/BA-L ratio. We next tested whether the opposite occurred when shifting mice from cold to thermoneutrality. H6PD protein increased 2.1-fold in BAT, while GR protein remained unchanged (Fig. 3c). Transitioning cold-exposed mice to 30 °C also restored GR target gene expression in BAT (Extended Data Fig. 6h), indicating that H6PD and GR signalling dynamically respond to temperature fluctuations to regulate BA heterogeneity.

We next tested whether blocking GR during the transition from cold to warmer temperatures prevents BA-H cells from switching to BA-L cells. We generated a BA-H-specific, inducible Nr3c1-knockout model by crossing Ucp1-rtTA, TRE-cre and Nr3c1flox/flox mice (Fig. 3d). Mice were first exposed to 6 °C for 1 week to raise the BA-H/BA-L ratio, followed by 1 week of doxycycline diet under cold conditions to induce Nr3c1 deletion in BA-H cells (Ucp1-Nr3c1flox/flox turning into Ucp1-Nr3c1−/−), and were then returned to room temperature with the doxycycline diet for another week. One week is long enough for the mice to reduce thermogenesis according to the rise in environmental temperature. IF staining confirmed Nr3c1 deletion in most BAs (Fig. 3e), consistent with 86% of BAs being BA-H cells during cold exposure (Fig. 3b), and western blotting showed reduced GR and RGS2 in isolated BAs (Fig. 3f). We previously showed that BA-H cells contain smaller lipid droplets and therefore have higher density, whereas BA-L cells have larger lipid droplets, lower density and float after mild centrifugation (like white adipocytes)6. Notably, BAT from Ucp1-Nr3c1−/− mice sank in PBS, whereas control BAT floated (Fig. 3g), suggesting a higher density and a higher BA-H/BA-L ratio. Consistently, Ucp1-Nr3c1−/− mice showed browner BAT with smaller lipid droplets, increased iWAT beiging (Fig. 3h) and a higher proportion of BA-H cells (Fig. 3i). Metabolic cage studies further showed increased energy expenditure and oxygen consumption in Ucp1-Nr3c1−/− mice, without changes in body weight (Fig. 3j,k and Extended Data Fig. 6i), body composition, food intake, respiratory exchange ratio or locomotor activity (Extended Data Fig. 6j–n). As the controls were metabolically healthy young adults, Ucp1-Nr3c1−/− mice showed no further improvements in glucose tolerance or insulin sensitivity (Extended Data Fig. 6o,p). These data highlight stage- and cell-subtype-specific requirements for GR signalling to regulate bistable BA states and thermogenesis. Inhibition of GR reactivation during the transition from cold to room temperature preserved a high BA-H/BA-L ratio and persistent thermogenesis despite reduced environmental thermogenic demand. These data not only confirm that we can block the BA-H-to-BA-L switch by inhibiting GR signalling, but also indicate that manipulating BA heterogeneity can increase thermogenesis and significantly boost whole-body energy expenditure.

To elucidate the sympathetic regulation of H6PD protein, we treated in vitro-differentiated BAs with NA but observed no change in H6PD protein (Fig. 4a), suggesting that other cell types and factors are involved in sympathetic regulation of H6PD. We next assessed cytokine expression in BAT and found that Il1b exhibited the greatest reduction (by 53%) after cold exposure (Fig. 4b and Extended Data Fig. 7a), and the greatest induction (by 3.3-fold) when mice were shifted from cold to thermoneutrality (Fig. 4c and Extended Data Fig. 7b). Cold-induced Il1b suppression was blunted by BAT sympathetic denervation (Fig. 4b), while NA reduced Il1b expression in BAT explants (Extended Data Fig. 7c). Il1b was predominantly expressed in SVFs rather than in mature adipocytes (Fig. 4d) and its expression changed with environmental temperature (Fig. 4d). qPCR with reverse transcription (RT–qPCR) analysis further confirmed the predominant expression of Il1b in CD45+ SVFs (Fig. 4e), indicating an immune cell source. By contrast, systemic IL-1β levels remained unchanged across environmental temperatures, suggesting local regulation of IL-1β levels in BAT (Extended Data Fig. 7d).

Notably, IF staining revealed that BA-L cells clustered around IL-1β puncta, whereas BA-H cells formed clusters further away (Fig. 4f and Extended Data Fig. 7e), a pattern that was observed across room temperature, during cold exposure and after denervation (Extended Data Fig. 7e). Quantification indicated that IL-1β puncta and GFP+ cells (BA-H cells) show a significant negative Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC = −0.5 ± 0.1), suggesting a mutually exclusive pattern between these two factors at room temperature (Extended Data Fig. 7f). During cold exposure, this pattern persisted but was weaker (PCC = −0.2 ± 0.1), as fewer IL-1β puncta were available for quantification (Extended Data Fig. 7f). BAT denervation prevented the reduction in IL-1β puncta, maintained a low BA-H/BA-L ratio and preserved a strong negative correlation with GFP+ cells (BA-H cells) (PCC = −0.45 ± 0.1). Thus, across all three conditions, BA-L cells were consistently close to IL-1β puncta, indicating a strong correlation between IL-1β and BA heterogeneity.

We next tested whether IL-1β regulates H6PD protein levels in BAs. Among the proinflammatory cytokines tested, TNF and IL-1β increased H6PD protein levels by 1.8- and 3.5-fold, respectively (Fig. 4g), without increasing H6pd mRNA (Extended Data Fig. 7g), confirming a post-transcriptional regulation of H6PD. Combined IL-1β, TNF and IFNγ treatment did not further increase H6PD protein beyond the effect of IL-1β alone (Fig. 4g). IL-1β increased H6PD protein in a time-dependent manner (Fig. 4h). During cycloheximide (CHX) treatment, H6PD abundance decreased by 30% and 50% at 6 and 12 h, respectively, whereas IL-1β prevented this decline (Fig. 4i), indicating increased H6PD protein stability from degradation. IL-1β is known to activate multiple downstream pathways, including JNK, MEK–ERK, p38–MAPK and IKK–NF-κB. Among inhibitors targeting these pathways, only the MEK and ERK inhibitor U0126 attenuated IL-1β-induced H6PD upregulation (Extended Data Fig. 7h,i), indicating that MEK–ERK mediates IL-1β-induced H6PD stability.

We next examined whether IL-1β regulates UCP1 expression. IL-1β did not alter UCP1 expression in the in vitro-differentiated BAs in the absence of 11-DHC, but reduced UCP1 protein levels by 53% in the presence of 11-DHC (Extended Data Fig. 7j,k), indicating that IL-1β suppresses UCP1 through GR signalling.

Adipose-tissue-resident immune cells can regulate sympathetic innervation31,32,33. However, TH and synaptophysin staining showed similar sympathetic innervation of BA-H cells and BA-L cells (Extended Data Fig. 7l,m), indicating that IL-1β regulates BA heterogeneity independently of differential sympathetic innervation.

To identify the immune cells that secrete IL-1β in BAT, we integrated multiple published BAT scRNA-seq datasets14,34,35,36,37 (Extended Data Fig. 8a). β-Adrenergic receptors were expressed in CD45+ immune cells (Extended Data Fig. 8a,b), suggesting potential sympathetic regulation. Subclustering analysis revealed that Il1b was enriched in neutrophils (CD45+CD11b+F4/80−CXCR2+), monocytes (CD45+CD11b+F4/80−Ly6C+), and macrophages (CD45+CD11b+F4/80+) (Extended Data Fig. 8c–e). Flow cytometry confirmed that IL-1β was mainly produced by CD45+ immune cells (Extended Data Fig. 9a,b), with IL-1β+ cells predominantly comprising neutrophils and macrophages, but not monocytes (Extended Data Fig. 9c–e). IL-1β+ cells represented 10% of neutrophils and 1% of macrophages, indicating that IL-1β production is restricted to specific subsets (Extended Data Fig. 9d). Despite the low frequency, the greater abundance of macrophages resulted in substantial numbers of IL-1β+ macrophages, with IL-1β+ neutrophils only 1.7-fold more abundant (Extended Data Fig. 9e), indicating that both cell types are major IL-1β sources. IF staining further confirmed IL-1β colocalization with subsets of CXCR2+ neutrophils and F4/80+ macrophages in BAT (Extended Data Fig. 9f,g).

Depletion of CD45+ immune cells from BAT SVFs increased the BA-H/BA-L ratio by 2.7-fold without affecting overall differentiation efficiency (Extended Data Fig. 9h–j), supporting a role for immune cells in regulating BA heterogeneity. To further confirm the role of local IL-1β in thermogenic regulation, we injected lentivirus encoding Il1rn (encoding IL-1RA), a naturally occurring inhibitor of IL-1 receptor signalling, or an mCherry control directly into BAT (Extended Data Fig. 10a). IL-1RA increased the BA-H/BA-L ratio twofold in mice housed at room temperature (Fig. 4j,k). In in vitro-differentiated BAs, Il1r knockdown attenuated IL-1β-mediated suppression of UCP1 expression, indicating a direct effect of IL-1β on BAs (Extended Data Fig. 10b). Moreover, in vivo pharmacological inhibition of IL-1β production using the caspase-1 inhibitor VX-765 increased the BA-H/BA-L ratio by 2.2-fold (Extended Data Fig. 10c,d), further demonstrating a functional role for IL-1β in regulating BA heterogeneity.

We next investigated how temperature fluctuations regulate IL-1β in BAT. Il1b expression in primary BAT SVFs was temperature-dependent (Fig. 4d), whereas IL-1β+ immune cell numbers remained unchanged across housing temperatures (Extended Data Fig. 10e–h), indicating regulation independent of immune cell recruitment. NA directly suppressed Il1b expression in BAT-derived CD45+ immune cells (Extended Data Fig. 10i), suggesting adrenergic regulation of IL-1β production. Consistent with adrenergic receptor expression in CD45+ immune cells (Extended Data Fig. 8a,b), scRNA-seq analysis showed selective expression of Adrb1 and Adrb2, but not α-adrenergic receptor genes, in Il1b-expressing immune cell populations (Extended Data Fig. 11a). We further examined adrenergic regulation of Il1b using bone marrow-derived neutrophils. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) induced Il1b expression dose dependently (Extended Data Fig. 11b), whereas NA pretreatment suppressed this induction in a dose- and time-dependent manner (Extended Data Fig. 11c,d). This effect was reversed by the non-selective β-adrenergic receptor antagonist propranolol, but not by the non-selective α-adrenergic receptor antagonist phenoxybenzamine (Extended Data Fig. 11e,f). Consistently, propranolol administration, but not phenoxybenzamine, increased Il1b expression in BAT in vivo (Extended Data Fig. 11g,h), supporting β-adrenergic suppression of IL-1β production.

Decreased BA thermogenesis has been reported with obesity38 and ageing3. However, high fat diet- (HFD)-induced obese mice were completely cold-tolerant, with 100% survival after 7 days of cold exposure (Fig. 5a,b). HFD feeding decreased H6PD and increased UCP1 protein levels (Fig. 5c), indicating preserved BAT thermogenic potential. By contrast, aged mice exhibited cold intolerance, with reduced body temperature and a 25% mortality within the first 2 days (Fig. 5d,e), suggesting impaired thermogenesis. Aged mice showed a 2.3-fold increase in H6PD and a 40% decrease in UCP1 (Fig. 5f), suggesting a reduced BA-H/BA-L ratio. GR was modestly reduced by HFD feeding but unchanged with ageing. These differences suggest that cold-induced thermogenesis is more severely impaired with ageing than with obesity at a young age, and that the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying impaired thermogenesis may differ substantially.

In young Ucp1-Chaser-mT/mG mice, BA-H cells increased from 28.8% at 24 °C to 61.7% and 89.7% after 1 and 3 days of cold exposure, respectively. In aged mice, the baseline BA-H/BA-L ratio at 24 °C was extremely low (0.31%), and BA-H cells increased to 15% and 50.9% after 1 and 3 days of cold exposure, respectively (Fig. 5g,h), suggesting delayed BA bistable regulation in response to cold stimulation. H6PD progressively decreased during cold exposure in both young and aged mice (Fig. 5i and Extended Data Fig. 11i), but its level at cold exposure in aged mice was comparable to that in young mice at 24 °C (Extended Data Fig. 11i). UCP1 was also reduced in aged mice, consistent with the lower BA-H/BA-L ratio (Extended Data Fig. 11i). Despite increased BA-L abundance, cold-induced TNAP expression was also blunted with age, although TNAP remained enriched in BA-L cells (Extended Data Fig. 11j), suggesting impaired futile creatine cycle activation. Aged mice also exhibited a 1.8-fold increase in BAT corticosterone (the active form of glucocorticoid in mice) in BAT, but not serum corticosterone (Extended Data Fig. 11k,l), and a sixfold increase in BAT Il1b expression arising from SVFs (Extended Data Fig. 11m,n). Together, these results suggest that elevated local IL-1β and H6PD-mediated overactivation of the GR–RGS2–AC axis lower the BA-H/BA-L ratio, therefore impairing cold-induced thermogenesis in aged mice.

Lastly, we investigated whether GR inhibition could restore BA bistable regulation and thermogenesis in aged mice. One week of RU-486 treatment increased the proportion of BA-H cells in aged mice from 0.5% to 8.3% at room temperature and from 10.6% to 32.8% after 1 day of cold exposure (Fig. 5j–m). RU-486 also reduced lipid droplet accumulation within BAs and improved BAT whitening (Fig. 5n), increased rectal temperature during the cold tolerance test and reduced body weight in the first few days of cold exposure (Extended Data Fig. 11o,p). Metabolic cage studies revealed that RU-486 treatment increased energy expenditure and oxygen consumption across the day–night cycle, without changes in respiratory exchange ratio, locomotor activity or food intake (Extended Data Fig. 11q–u). Thus, GR antagonism increases energy expenditure in aged mice, potentially due to elevated UCP1-dependent thermogenesis in BAT.

We found that the two BA subtypes are spatially organized and tightly regulated. IL-1β stabilizes surrounding BAs in the low-thermogenic state (BA-L cells) by extending H6PD protein half-life. H6PD facilitates active glucocorticoid production, which suppresses UCP1 expression through the GR–RGS2–AC axis (Extended Data Fig. 12a). BAs further from IL-1β puncta are stabilized in the high-thermogenic state (BA-H cells) with low H6PD protein levels (Extended Data Fig. 12b). During cold stimulation, activated sympathetic neurons secrete NA, which suppresses IL-1β production by immune cells. Low IL-1β levels promote H6PD degradation and switch BA-L cells into BA-H cells (Extended Data Fig. 12a,b). After cold stimulation, this process is reversed, restoring a lower BA-H/BA-L ratio. Age impairs the flexibility to alter BA heterogeneity due to IL-1β overload in BAT, which significantly contributes to the age-related decline in thermogenesis (Extended Data Fig. 12b).

The advantages of this spatiotemporal neuronal–immune regulation of BA heterogeneity are multifaceted. First, it enables a dual-layered response: an immediate, acute response through readily active BA-H cells, and a longer-term, chronic response that involves shifting cellular heterogeneity. Second, it facilitates communication between different cell types through spatial proximity, thereby integrating stimuli from multiple systems, combining environmental and internal cues, and enabling precise regulation of cell states. Third, shifting between BA-H and BA-L states preserves BAs from oxidative damage associated with sustained high thermogenesis.

Although this study primarily focused on UCP1-dependent thermogenesis, UCP1-independent mechanisms are also critical for thermogenic adipose tissue function and whole-body metabolic homeostasis39,40,41. We observed that TNAP, a key component of the futile creatine cycle, is enriched in BA-L cells. Cold exposure increased the proportion of BA-H cells while inducing CKB and TNAP expression in BAT42, reflecting enhanced futile creatine cycle activity either specifically in BA-L cells or across all BAs, with BA-L cells retaining higher activity. Importantly, cold-induced TNAP expression was compromised with age, while remaining enriched in BA-L cells. These results suggest that both UCP1- and futile creatine cycle-dependent thermogenesis are impaired with age.

BAT has long been recognized as a target organ for glucocorticoids, but their precise role remains unclear. Some studies indicate that glucocorticoids suppress UCP1 expression and/or BAT activity43,44,45,46, whereas others reach the opposite conclusions47,48,49. Glucocorticoids have also been implicated in warm-induced beige adipocyte whitening50. This study shows that GR signalling in BAT regulates BA heterogeneity by locking BAs in the BA-L state and reducing their responsiveness to NA. Active glucocorticoid levels are regulated locally, independent of systemic levels25. Thus, systemic glucocorticoid treatments may not alter active glucocorticoid levels in individual BAs, potentially explaining previous conflicting observations. Importantly, inducible GR deletion in BAs during the transition from cold to room temperature sustained the high BA-H/BA-L ratio and thermogenesis. The stage- and cell-subtype-specific requirement of GR signalling explains why Nr3c1-knockout mice do not exhibit altered BAT development or thermogenesis51,52, and indicates that targeting GR at selective timing could profoundly manipulate thermogenic activity.

Our study showed that BA heterogeneity is determined by communication from sympathetic neurons to immune cells, rather than by direct communication from sympathetic neurons to BAs. NA, the primary neurotransmitter from sympathetic nerves, has been shown to modulate immune cell response by regulating cytokine production by immune cells in the liver and adipose tissue53,54. We found that NA directly suppresses Il1b production in immune cells through β-adrenergic signalling, thereby modulating BA heterogeneity. Notably, a recent study showed that cold-induced β3-adrenergic activation transiently recruits neutrophils predominantly to white adipose tissue (WAT), particularly the visceral depot55. These infiltrating neutrophils produce IL-1β, which suppresses local lipolysis by attenuating cAMP-dependent signalling, thereby limiting excessive lipid mobilization. Based on our findings in BAT, IL-1β may similarly suppress lipolysis through the H6PD–GR–RGS2–AC axis in white adipocytes. Notably, Il1b expression in WAT exhibits transient kinetics, peaking at 12 h after stimulation and returning to basal levels at 24 h. Given that NA suppresses Il1b expression in BAT, it may similarly suppress Il1b expression in newly infiltrated neutrophils in WAT.

It remains unclear why in vitro-differentiated BAs exhibit heterogeneity. BAs may intrinsically differentiate into cells with varying UCP1 levels to preserve thermogenic flexibility or maintain metabolic balance. Cross-talk between BAs and IL-1β+ immune cells in vivo may further reinforce and spatially organize this heterogeneity. Alternatively, IL-1β+ immune cells in SVFs during differentiation may influence the local environment. Indeed, depletion of CD45+ immune cells from SVFs before differentiation substantially increased the proportion of BA-H cells, suggesting that SVF immune cells reduce the BA-H/BA-L ratio. Future efforts with cell type-specific receptor knockout mouse models will help to validate and further elucidate how sympathetic innervation regulates IL-1β secretion and how IL-1β regulates H6PD protein stability.

Decline of thermogenesis and age-related adiposity56 contribute to age-related metabolic disorders. Previous studies suggest that reduction in thermogenic activity may be due to impaired beige adipogenesis5,57, declined mitochondrial fuel oxidation3, reduced adrenergic receptors, altered other master regulators of thermogenesis58 and compromised sympathetic innervation due to infiltration of senescent immune cells59. Our study showed that Il1b is increased in aged BAT, consistent with previous studies reporting increased immune cell infiltration and upregulated cytokines in aged BAT59. Increased IL-1β stabilized large amounts of BAs in the BA-L state and suppressed thermogenesis in aged mice. Importantly, pharmacological inhibition of GR signalling by a GR antagonist raised the BA-H/BA-L ratio and improved cold-induced thermogenesis in aged mice, indicating that BA-specific GR signalling could be targeted to correct age-related cold intolerance and significantly increase basal metabolism. However, given the systemic effects of RU-486 (ref. 60), other tissues and cell types may also contribute. Future efforts will focus on the effects of targeted inhibition of GR signalling specifically in thermogenic cells.

Fluorescence microscopy images were acquired for three experimental conditions: room temperature, cold exposure and cold exposure with denervation. For each condition, dual-channel fluorescence images were collected with UCP1 labelled in the green channel and IL-1β labelled in the magenta channel, along with corresponding autofluorescence reference images for cellular region identification. All image analysis was performed using custom Python scripts using the tifffile, numpy, scipy and pandas libraries. To restrict analysis to cellular regions and exclude background artifacts, autofluorescence images were Gaussian-smoothed and used to generate binary masks with a threshold of 50 arbitrary units applied to autofluorescence intensity values65, ensuring that correlation analysis was performed exclusively on biologically relevant areas. To eliminate low-intensity noise and focus analysis on meaningful fluorescence signals, intensity-based thresholds were applied to both channels: UCP1 (green channel) at 70 arbitrary units and IL-1β (magenta channel) at 20 arbitrary units. Pixels were retained for analysis if they exceeded the threshold in either channel, using a logical OR to preserve regions with specific labelling while maintaining sensitivity to differential expression patterns. Co-localization between UCP1 and IL-1β was quantified using the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC), calculated pixel-wise across filtered cellular regions66. After masking and thresholding, images were vectorized by flattening two-dimensional pixel arrays into one-dimensional arrays. The PCC was computed using scipy.stats, providing both the correlation coefficient and associated P value for each image pair. Statistical comparisons between experimental conditions were performed using Welch’s t-tests for independent samples with unequal variances. This approach accounts for potential differences in variance between experimental groups while providing robust statistical inference. Results were compiled into structured data frames and visualized using bar plots with error bars.

scRNA-seq data from the non-adipocyte fraction of BAT of C57BL/6J mice maintained at room temperature were obtained and selected from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database under accessions GSE207707, GSE177635, GSE160585 and GSE161447. Data processing and analysis were conducted using Seurat v.4.0.6 in R v.4.1.2. Each dataset was imported, converted to Seurat objects, normalized and then variable features were identified. Integration anchors were computed to merge the datasets into a unified Seurat object. Quality control involved filtering out cells with fewer than 200 detected features and those with mitochondrial gene content exceeding 10%. The integrated data were scaled, and principal component analysis was performed. Clustering was performed using the Louvain algorithm with a resolution of 1.0. For visualization, t-distributed stochastic neighbour embedding was used to depict cell clusters. Identification of each cell type was determined by comparing cell cluster-specific marker genes with known cell type-specific markers from the literature. Immune cell clusters were further identified and subclustered based on the expression of Ptprc, and non-immune cell types, including erythrocytes (Hba-a1 and Hba-a2), capillary endothelial cells (Pecam1 and Gpibp1), smooth muscle cells (Acta2) and adipose progenitor cells (Pdgfra and Col3a1), were excluded from the analysis.

To identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and enriched pathways between BA-L and BA-H cells, scRNA-seq data deposited in the NCBI GEO database under accession number GSE125269 were analysed. Adipocyte clusters were selected based on Plin1 expression, while contaminating endothelial cells (Pecam1) were excluded from the analysis. BA-L and BA-H cell populations were identified as described previously6. These populations were further compared to identify DEGs using the FindMarker function, which applied the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with a log-transformed fold change threshold of 0.25 and a minimum detection fraction (min.pct) of 25%. To control the false discovery rate, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and genes with an adjusted P of <0.05 were considered statistically significant. Visualization of the top 50 upregulated DEGs in both BA-L and BA-H cells was generated using the DoHeatmap function. Pathway enrichment analysis was performed on the top 75 upregulated and downregulated DEGs in BA-L cells using Gene Ontology Biological Processes. The analysis used Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P values to identify significantly enriched pathways. Pathways with an adjusted P of <0.05 and containing at least three enriched genes were considered significantly enriched. The top 20 enriched pathways were determined based on adjusted P values. Dot plots of these pathways were generated to visualize the enrichment results, with specific pathways of interest highlighted for enhanced interpretability.

a, Molecular mechanism for the maintenance of the BA-L state. BA has two stable states, low and high thermogenesis, based on UCP1 expression. BA states are governed by their spatial proximity to IL-1β+ immune cells (mainly neutrophils and macrophages), which integrate signals from sympathetic neurons and immune cells to determine whether H6PD protein levels should be stabilized or degraded. Green arrows illustrate how BA-L cells are stabilized in a low-thermogenic state: IL-1β enhances the protein stability of H6PD in thermogenic adipocytes, thereby boosting the production of active glucocorticoids. Glucocorticoid activates the expression of its target RGS2, which inhibits adenylyl cyclase and reduces cAMP production, thereby reducing UCP1 expression. Red arrows illustrate how BA-L cells switch into BA-H cells upon cold exposure: cold stimulation activates sympathetic neurons, which decrease local BAT IL-1β levels; lower IL-1β levels lead to H6PD protein degradation, turning BA-L into BA-H. The purple arrow illustrates that in aged individuals, IL-1β is elevated in BAT. High IL-1β levels persistently prevent H6PD degradation, desensitizing BAs’ cold response by locking them in BA-L states. Illustration created with BioRender; Wang, Q. https://BioRender.com/basqx8x (2026). b, Bistable regulation of BA heterogeneity in a spatiotemporal manner governs thermogenic activity. We report that IL-1β-secreting immune cells, mainly neutrophils and macrophages, stabilize the surrounding BAs in a low-thermogenic state by preventing H6PD degradation; BAs farther from IL-1β+ cells are stabilized in a high-thermogenic state. Specifically, H6PD is a functional marker of BA-L cells and boosts the production of active GC. GC then activates the expression of its target, RGS2, which inhibits adenylyl cyclase, reducing cAMP production and, in turn, UCP1 expression. This bistable regulation dynamically and precisely controls cellular heterogeneity in response to signalling from sympathetic nerves and the immune system, managing thermogenesis at the tissue level. Increased IL-1β levels in the BAT of aged individuals lock BAs in a low-thermogenic state, thereby lowering thermogenic activity and leading to cold intolerance.
