Doug Christie provides honest explanation about being on hot seat for Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Doug Christie provides honest explanation about being on hot seat for Kings originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SACRAMENTO — The more the Kings lose, the more the big-picture questions arise.

The Kings fell to 1-11 in a 120-107 loss to the Portland Trail Blazers on Friday at Golden 1 Center, extending their franchise-record home winless streak to seven games to begin the 2026-27 NBA season.

To be sure, expectations weren't high surrounding Doug Christie's team coming in. General manager Scott Perry has made it clear his team is in early stages of a multi-season rebuilding project around promising rookie Darius Acuff Jr.

But Sacramento has the second-worst record in the NBA –– only the 1-12 Utah Jazz and Milwaukee Bucks are worse –– and currently is on pace to finish with seven wins, worse than the 1972-73 Philadelphia 76ers, who had the worst 82-game record in NBA history at 9-73.

That futility has led to a swelling conversation about the immediate future of Kings coach Doug Christie, who is 23-70 since having the interim tag removed from his title before last season.

Christie was asked Friday about that conversation and if he feels the growing pressure as the Kings dropped their fifth straight.

"I think the seat is hot, always," Christie said. "The nature of this business, it's all about winning. So that's where you think the seat is always hot."

Christie's answer reflected a sense of urgency that any NBA coach could relate to. But he also knows he's the Kings' eighth head coach since the current ownership group headed by Vivek Ranadivé assumed control in 2013.

Christie had a front-row seat to the firing of Mike Brown in December 2024, just less than two seasons after being voted as the unanimous coach of the year and helping end the Kings' 17-year NBA playoff drought, the longest in league history. Brown, of course, won a championship last season with the New York Knicks.

Christie was an assistant on Brown's staff, and leaped a handful of Brown assistants for the head job after Brown was fired while on the way to the airport to travel to a road game against the Los Angeles Lakers. Christie and Perry remade the staff the following offseason when Perry came on as general manager and made Christie the full-time head coach.

The Kings went 22-60 in the Christie's first full season. Landing Acuff with the No. 7 pick in the 2026 NBA Draft served as the silver lining in what proved to be a lost season while Domantas Sabonis, Keegan Murray and Zach LaVine missed most of it with injuries.

Christie indicated he understands why he's in the crosshairs of some Kings fans unhappy with the product on the court. But he's trying not to focus on that, he said.

"What's for me is going to be for me," Christie said. "I'm always going to do my best, continue to improve in every aspect of whatever it is I do. No one's going to outwork me when it comes to that. So that's not something I worry about.

"In moments, do you feel like, 'ugh'? Yeah, of course," he continued. "But then I go find my center and understand that I'm a leader of men and an organization, and I have to come with the proper energy, the proper messaging, all those things to continue to lead them forward."

Christie pointed to Friday's performance as a positive despite the final score. The Kings had good energy throughout the game, Christie said. They lost by getting outscored at the 3-point line by 18 points and the free-throw line by 11 more.

They lost while outscoring the Blazers in points in the paint (46-38), second-chance points (12-6), and fast-break points (20-10). It wasn't a blowout that has become too common early this season.

Sabonis rebounded from his scoreless performance on Tuesday with 21 points and 12 rebounds, while Murray had his best game of the year with 16 points, seven rebounds and four assists in his return to the starting lineup for the first time since the second game of the season.

The Kings were led in scoring by DeAndre Hunter's 24, while Acuff had his streak of 20-plus points snapped at seven games by finishing with 17 on 6-of-19 shooting. Acuff had just seven points through three quarters but scored 10 in the fourth.

Still, those developments were far from enough to overcome Portland. And there's little proof the Kings will bring the energy they brought Friday consistently as the season continues.

The schedule remains tough with the next game Sunday against the Oklahoma City Thunder, Wednesday versus the Phoenix Suns before a back-to-back on the road against the Lakers and home to the Boston Celtics next Friday and Saturday.

"But I'm always searching because leadership is something that I take seriously when it comes to (the players) and trying to find a pathway for them to be successful," Christie said. "…And if you've ever been through anything in life, you don't look at the totality of it. You look at it in bite sizes.

"A lot of people can be overwhelmed by that. I'm just not built that way. So therefore, I won't let my players be built that way, and that's how we get through."
