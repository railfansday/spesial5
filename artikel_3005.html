Chiefs-Cardinals: 5 Things to Watch in Week 11 matchup

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The (7-2) Kansas City Chiefs will host the (3-7) Arizona Cardinals this week in an interconference matchup between AFC West and NFC West teams. The game kicks off at noon at Arrowhead Stadium in Kansas City, Missouri, and will be broadcast on CBS.

With a 35-13 blowout win over the Atlanta Falcons, the Chiefs found themselves back in the driver's seat in the AFC West, taking a slight advantage over the Denver Broncos due to tiebreakers.

The Cardinals found themselves on the opposite end of the stick, losing 34-6 to the Los Angeles Rams to fall to dead last in their division.

The Chiefs are heavy favorites heading into this week and need a win, with much tougher matchups on the horizon.

Right tackle Jaylon Moore (ankle) and right guard Trey Smith (concussion) have been ruled out; Moore missed last week as well, and Smith went out in the first quarter.

Josh Ezeudu will likely start his third game at right tackle, while Mike Caliendo should make his third start at right guard.

Against the Falcons, the Chiefs' offensive line issues didn't matter, but they could limit how the offense calls plays.

Patrick Mahomes and the skill players overcame the injuries up front last week, but it will be interesting to see whether Arizona targets the right side this week when the Chiefs drop back to pass.

The Cardinals defense ranks 17th in the NFL in sacks this season with 17 total sacks. Cardinals defensive coordinator Nick Rallis was retained through the head coaching change from Jonathan Gannon to Mike LaFleur, and it will be interesting to see how he prepares to face the beat-up Chiefs offensive line.

This week, the Chiefs announced they signed veteran offensive tackle Fred Johnson to the team's practice squad. Johnson has 22 career starts, so it will be interesting to see whether the team shifts the line further if performance issues arise or more injuries occur.

Head coach Andy Reid commented on the line during his press conference on Friday.

"Well, we've got a variety there, but they're all going to have a shot to get in there and go, so we'll see how it all goes," Reid said. "But I think everybody has a lot of trust in Mike (Caliendo), if he's the one in there playing."

Despite a slow start to the season, the Chiefs' pass rush has delivered back-to-back solid performances, led by stars Chris Jones and George Karlaftis.

Karlaftis leads the team with 5.5 sacks this season and has at least one in the last three games. Jones has three this year, but has come alive with 2.5 through the past two games.

The slow start getting home hurt the team, but both have played their best football so far in November.

The defensive line has also seen the emergence of defensive end Ashton Gillotte, who has recorded at least one sack in the last two weeks, along with multiple pressures.

The Cardinals have allowed 28 sacks so far this season, tied for sixth-worst in the NFL, and will start multiple backup-caliber players on their offensive line on Sunday.

Rookie quarterback Carson Beck is unlikely to play, meaning journeyman backup Josh Johnson would start.

If the Chiefs pass rush can turn in another solid performance, they should be able to shut down the Cardinals offense.

General manager Brett Veach's latest in-season acquisition started off his tenure in Kansas City on a positive note despite playing limited snaps and receiving only five targets against Atlanta.

In only ten snaps, Josh Downs caught four passes for 53 yards and hauled in an impressive touchdown to end the first half.

In that limited action, he showed off his route-running ability and the sure hands needed in the Chiefs system. With a big game in the middle of next week against Buffalo, Downs likely won't see every snap out wide, but I expect to see him get around 25-30 as he gets more comfortable in the Chiefs system.

In his first start, rookie running back Emmett Johnson exploded for a massive game, compiling 170 total yards on the ground and through the air and scoring two touchdowns off passes from Mahomes.

With Kenneth Walker (groin) slated to miss his second consecutive game, Johnson will once again get the call to start.

With the touchdowns, Johnson took the lead for rookie touchdowns and has moved himself into the conversation for the Offensive Rookie of the Year award.

Johnson has impressed with a tremendous combination of vision and cutting ability that has helped him find running lanes and break tackles at a high rate on so few touches.

With 519 total scrimmage yards this season, Johnson has quickly become a difference-maker for the Chiefs offense, and even when Walker returns, Johnson should still see his role expand. I would expect to see Johnson in heavy doses on Sunday.

The Chiefs have once again found elite cornerback play in Mansoor Delane and Nohl Williams.

A former third-round pick, Williams has quickly become one of the NFL's best corners and has been among the league's most effective players in man coverage.

Opposite Williams, Delane has been equally impressive and is making a run at a potential defensive rookie-of-the-year campaign.

Critics questioned the decision to trade All-Pro corner Trent McDuffie in the offseason, but so far the trade has paid off.

Delane and Williams are both on rookie deals and appear headed toward bright careers. Against a stumbling offense and a journeyman quarterback, this week will give each player a chance to pad their stats and look to add to their interception totals.
