Anthropic Starts Trading Nov. 19. These 3 Industrial Stocks Already Are Linked to It -- and You Can Buy Them Right Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Anthropic plans to spend over $900 billion, and it is already working with these companies.

By Emma Newbery – Nov 19, 2026 at 7:22AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Caterpillar ›

Anthropic's (ANT +0.00%) initial public offering (IPO) looks set to be another blockbuster, possibly setting new IPO records and giving investors a chance to access a pure-play artificial intelligence (AI) stock. However, large IPOs can be speculative, and history tells us they often fall in their first year.

Anthropic's prospectus says a lot about both AI's huge potential and enormous risks. Putting the potential existential risks to humanity to one side, one figure struck me: It disclosed over $900 billion in planned compute and infrastructure spending. It says it will spend more than half of that before the end of 2030. As a relatively cautious investor, I'm more excited by that solid commitment than the initial volatility of an IPO.

While many of Anthropic's deals are not public or are subcontracted through its partners, these industrial stocks already have Anthropic funds and are set to benefit from further AI infrastructure spending.

Already an AI pick-and-shovel favorite, Caterpillar (CAT +0.51%) soared to an all-time high of over $1,070 in June. However, at the time of this writing on Nov. 18, the stock is trading close to $560 -- almost 50% down from its high and little changed for the year. The well-known brand manufactures industrial equipment, including gas turbines and power generators.

Its Q3 sales increased 17% from the year before to $20.7 billion, coming in just above analyst estimates. Some of that has been driven by the significant power demands of data centers and cloud computing. Indeed, one striking figure from its earnings is its record $78 billion backlog, reflecting robust demand across the board and giving the company a cushion against possible slowdowns in AI spending.

That backlog brings me to Anthropic, which essentially paid to jump the queue by taking over OnePWR's Caterpillar order for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty generators. Anthropic paid $3.4 billion to take over the orders, which are expected to be available in the next two years. The deal, completed in September, shows how important power-generation equipment is to AI firms, and bodes well for Caterpillar's growth.

Vertiv (VRT +2.00%) keeps data center equipment cool. It manufactures power management technology and cooling systems that allow data centers to process high computing workloads. This week, it expanded its 800-volt direct current (VDC) systems, which were developed in collaboration with Nvidia (NVDA +2.66%) and are designed to better support the needs of next-generation data centers. It is a key development that should help Vertiv maintain its dominant position.

Vertiv's modular designs can reduce the time it takes to bring data centers online -- an attractive proposition in the race to build massive AI infrastructure as fast as possible. However, fears that the pace of data center development may slow and questions about the sustainability of the AI boom have weighed on Vertiv shares. Its stock is trading around $197, 48% lower than its May all-time high. Despite its falling share price, Vertiv's Q3 results beat expectations with quarterly sales up 61% year-on-year to $4.3 billion. The firm also said that its Q3 orders were up over 100% from the year before.

Anthropic's $900 billion spending spree suggests the AI build-out has room to run, which would be good for Vertiv demand.

Crucially, Anthropic doesn't want to rely on leasing cloud capacity; it is investing in its own purpose-built data centers, powered by specialized chips. Vertiv is a major AI infrastructure player, but its connection to Anthropic is somewhat indirect. It is a partner in Hut 8's (HUT +4.53%) River Bend data center campus in Louisiana, which The Financial Times reports will supply 245 megawatts of computing power to Anthropic.

With a market cap of around $2 billion, ERock (EROC +1.22%), is smaller and significantly riskier than Vertiv or Caterpillar. The firm boasts its ability to provide reliable off-grid generators quickly, which can later transition into backup power.

ERock itself IPO'd in June, and has had a bumpy first six months. It went public with an offer price of $21.50 and is now trading at $7.58. Given its relative youth and size, ERock's contract to provide 470 megawatts of power equipment to Anthropic is significant. Its RockBlock generators run primarily on natural gas. It's worth noting that Anthropic points to the importance of natural gas several times in its IPO prospectus, specifically the increased competition for supply and pipeline capacity.

All three of these industrial firms have existing ties with Anthropic and are part of massive AI infrastructure developments already underway. The demand and strong order books are attractive, and the recent investor caution on AI means their share prices are now more reasonably priced. There is still risk, and caution is justified, but none of them depend only on the AI build-out, and all could benefit from Anthropic's huge spending plans.
