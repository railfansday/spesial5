The Most Important Number In Nvidia's Earnings Report Was 75%

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia’s next-year forecast says more about its post-Huang confidence than any quarterly beat.

By Patrick Sanders – Nov 18, 2026 at 8:28AM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -0.76%) had plenty to prove when it reported its fiscal third-quarter earnings after the market closed Nov. 17. It was the company's first earnings report since Jensen Huang's sudden departure. Investors wanted to know whether the artificial intelligence chipmaker could maintain its momentum without its longtime CEO.

The numbers were outstanding. But I think the most important figure in Nvidia's earnings report wasn't its revenue, earnings, or even its guidance for the current quarter.

That's how much Nvidia now expects its revenue to grow in fiscal 2028, up from its previous forecast of 70%. And that increase tells us something important about the company's confidence in its ability to keep delivering AI processors to customers – even without Huang running the show.

For the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (ending Oct. 25, 2026), Nvidia reported revenue of $115.84 billion, up 103% from a year ago, and topping analysts' expectations of $109.10 billion. Its data center business generated $107.6 billion in revenue, up 110% year over year, as demand for AI computing infrastructure continued to grow.

"Our momentum is real," interim CEO Colette Kress said. "Nvidia is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people. Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track, and our ambition has never been greater."

Nvidia also projected fourth-quarter revenue of approximately $130 billion, ahead of the $125.1 billion consensus estimate compiled by LSEG. While that number stands out, it's also important to know that Nvidia's fiscal fourth quarter includes an additional week of revenue. Melius Research estimates that accounts for approximately $9 billion to $10 billion of the roughly $14 billion sequential increase in Nvidia's guidance.

That's why I'm paying more attention to what Nvidia expects to happen next year.

The increase in Nvidia's fiscal 2028 growth forecast is what really got my attention. In August, Nvidia projected 70% revenue growth for its 2028 fiscal year, and acknowledged that its ability to supply enough processors would limit how quickly it could expand.

Now the company is bumping that projection up to 75% growth.

During the company's earnings call with analysts, Kress said that Nvidia evaluated both customer demand and the additional supply it expects to make available. The company believes it can deliver that growth despite ongoing supply constraints and new U.S. export restrictions.

Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress. Image source: Nvidia.

Nvidia's ability to grow depends on more than designing powerful chips. It needs manufacturing capacity, advanced memory, packaging, networking components, and customers with enough data center capacity to put its systems to work.

And just as importantly, Nvidia needs to keep those relationships going even in the post-Huang era. Huang was forced to resign Nov. 12 after the board began investigating allegations that he had a relationship with a Chinese businesswoman who U.S. intelligence officials believe is a Chinese spy. Other extramarital relationships with Nvidia employees also came to light.

There are some, including President Donald Trump and people at Nvidia, who suggest that Huang be reinstated. And Huang issued a lengthy statement on the social media platform X saying he would return if asked.

But Nvidia closed that door on Tuesday, stating that Huang would not be reinstated and citing his "personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms."

Huang's departure created legitimate questions about Nvidia's leadership and its relationships with customers and suppliers. Those concerns won't disappear after one earnings report. But the company's higher fiscal 2028 forecast suggests its manufacturing partnerships, customer relationships, and product roadmap remain intact.

I think the increased forecast is more significant than a quarterly earnings beat. While its first earnings report without Huang suggests the business can continue delivering exceptional results, a revenue growth rate of 75% in the next fiscal year shows Nvidia is hardly running out of momentum.
