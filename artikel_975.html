Harrison: The animosity Fury and AJ have is genuine

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Audley Harrison doesn't believe Tyson Fury vs Anthony Joshua has been left too late in their careers; "they're not in their primes but there's still enough there and the animosity that they have is genuine. So it's a fight definitely I want to see," the Olympic gold medallist said

Audley Harrison has backed Anthony Joshua and Tyson Fury to deliver when they fight on December 11 in Cardiff.

Harrison was one half of an all-British heavyweight grudge match when he boxed David Haye in a 2010 event that was much hyped but ultimately disappointed.

Harrison though doesn't think Fury and Joshua have left their fight too late.

"'Politricks' has always been there in boxing, always unfortunately messing up boxing," Harrison told Sky Sports.

"I will be sitting on the edge of my seat for Tyson Fury and Anthony Joshua. They're not in their primes but there's still enough there and the animosity that they have is genuine. So it's a fight definitely I want to see. While it's a little late, I can't argue with that.

"It's not going to be them in their primes but it's going to be good enough. We need to see it for sure."

He added: "Look at Mike Tyson-Lennox Lewis, everyone was complaining for that fight, that fight was like five years too late in many people's eyes.

"I was there in Memphis, it was a great fight, great atmosphere, exactly what we wanted.

"Lennox banged him out but the fight was so exciting."

Harrison preceded Joshua as an Olympic super-heavyweight gold medallist for Great Britain. But he did not scale the heights Joshua achieved in the professional ranks. Joshua has twice been a unified heavyweight world champion and has made a habit of selling out stadium shows in London and Cardiff.

"All of these guys came from nowhere. AJ went to the Olympics not as the favourite, same as Deontay Wilder, and they punched their way to glory," Harrison said.

"When Joshua turned pro he was raw as hell, you've got to give him credit he was able to go through.

"When he lost to [Andy] Ruiz they crucified him because he was meant to win, but he was still raw. He's always been really disciplined, he's strong and he's always come into the ring ready.

"Technically and tactically he's been off in some fights, obviously with [Oleksandr] Usyk, obviously with [Daniel] Dubois. But came back in the [Wladimir] Klitschko fight. When you look at his career, you've got to take your hat off. He wasn't perfect, but he's not supposed to be."

Harrison added: "He's still got the power, he's still got the punch but he doesn't have the punch resistance now.

"We're seeing that with Joshua, we're seeing that with Fury. We haven't seen that with Usyk yet. I haven't seen him start to flounder yet in terms of punch resistance.

"To be fair to AJ and Tyson Fury, they've both had a legendary career, when you look at what they've done, not just the money they've made, if you look at their legacy."
