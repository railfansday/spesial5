Better Chip Stock: Nvidia vs. AMD

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The things affecting Nvidia's stock price are not entirely related to the state of its business.

Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%) shareholders have had a rough few days. After a week in which news broke that CEO and co-founder Jensen Huang had engaged in an extramarital affair with a woman who is accused of being a Chinese spy, and another with an Nvidia executive, on Thursday morning, he resigned.

The exit of the man who has been the face of the company for decades has investors worried about its future. AMD (AMD -8.75%) shareholders aren't in the same boat, and the chipmaker is enjoying surging data center sales.

However, the question remains: Which stock is the better buy? It's a hard question to answer, because at face value, Nvidia is doing far better than AMD. But AMD doesn't have other questions surrounding the state of its business.

What matters is what you value most as an investor, as Nvidia is likely to be a tumultuous stock to own over the next few months.

Before comparing Nvidia and AMD stocks, investors must answer a different question: How capable will Nvidia be without Huang in the CEO role? If you’re of the view that the company’s future really rests on the caliber of the person in the top job, then Nvidia’s investment thesis can’t be properly analyzed until his permanent successor is picked. For now, respected CFO Colette Kress occupies the role on an interim basis.

If it doesn't matter to you who is leading Nvidia, then you're free to proceed to the next point.

Nvidia and AMD are both computing unit designers, although their businesses look a bit different. AMD's product lineup is a lot broader than Nvidia's, and so is its customer base. It has sizable businesses in the consumer electronics world as well as in embedded processors and data centers. However, almost all investors care about is the data center business, as that's what is growing rapidly right now.

During Q3, AMD's data center revenue rose 97% year over year to $8.5 billion. That's a strong result, and investors are excited. However, because AMD also has fairly large exposure to other segments, its overall growth rate was 45%, bringing its top line to $13.4 billion. The problem is it isn't anywhere close to Nvidia's growth and size.

Nvidia reports its fiscal 2027 Q3 results on Nov. 17. But until we get that update about the recent state of the business, the best we can do is to use its fiscal Q2 results. In that period, its data center sales rose 117% year over year to $89 billion. Because Nvidia's data center business makes up a far larger chunk of its total, Nvidia's overall revenue growth rate was 106% to $96.2 billion.

Nothing that has transpired over the past week will affect what Nvidia reports in Q3, and I'd expect another quarter of outperforming AMD based on historical trends. Furthermore, Nvidia has already told investors to expect 70% revenue growth in its fiscal 2028. Management is comfortable giving that guidance because Nvidia's business is practically locked in, with AI hyperscalers ordering its processors far in advance of when they'll be ready to install them in data centers.

I think this supports the premise that Nvidia can keep succeeding without Huang as CEO, because it's already got a lot of future growth on the books due to how strong a business it has built.

So, from a growth standpoint, Nvidia is still the better pick. And after this month’s sell-off, the stock is even cheaper.

The best way to weigh the value of these two stocks is to look at their expected earnings for next year. By that metric, AMD is about 3 times more expensive than Nvidia.

NVDA PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts

That seems like a huge premium to pay for AMD, and the reality is that regardless of who is running Nvidia, a pricing mismatch of that magnitude is too great to ignore. Nvidia's stock could triple from this point without looking too expensive, and even a double would leave the stock looking cheap compared to its growth rate.

I think Nvidia is the far better stock to buy here, mainly due to how cheap the stock is. Nvidia's business is strong, and its board should find a talented new leader to take it over.
