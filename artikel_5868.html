Democrat knocks off Republican Rep. Scott DesJarlais in Tennessee

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Democrat Victoria Broderick, seen here speaking to a reporter outside the Capitol on Nov. 11, has unseated Republican Rep. Scott DesJarlais in Tennessee's 4th District. (Bill Clark/CQ Roll Call) (Bill Clark CQ Roll Call)

Democrat Victoria Broderick has defeated Republican Rep. Scott DesJarlais in Tennessee’s 4th District, giving her party the 259th seat of its commanding House majority in the next Congress.

Broderick, a tech support worker, was leading by 361 votes when The Associated Press called the race Tuesday, three weeks after all votes were cast.

Broderick’s victory ranks among the most surprising Democratic wins of this year’s midterm elections. DesJarlais, who defeated Broderick in 2024 by more than 40 points, was not considered vulnerable heading into Election Day.

But the election two years ago took place under a previous version of Tennessee’s congressional map. Following this spring’s Supreme Court ruling that limited the use of race in redistricting, the state’s Republican-led legislature redrew the House lines to eliminate Rep. Steve Cohen’s Memphis-area district, the lone seat held by Democrats.

In doing so, though, the new map significantly altered the boundaries of the largely rural 4th District in Middle Tennessee, which now extends into Nashville’s Davidson County. Thirty-three percent of DesJarlais’ constituents were new to him, according to calculations by Inside Elections with Nathan L. Gonzales, as the district shifted from one that Donald Trump carried by 43 points in 2024 to one that would have backed him by 24 points.

Under the new map, Republicans struggled to win most of the state’s House seats earlier this month. They narrowly won Cohen’s 9th District, one of just three flips for the party this year nationwide. But Broderick’s win ensures that Tennessee will continue to have a Democrat in its congressional delegation.

DesJarlais had already conceded the race before Tuesday’s AP call.

“As the final votes have now been tabulated in this new and completely restructured district, it appears that we have a result showing my campaign has come just a few hundred votes short out of just under 200,000 votes cast,” he said in a Nov. 16 statement congratulating Broderick.

A member of the hard-line conservative Freedom Caucus, DesJarlais was first elected in 2010, unseating Democrat Lincoln Davis. While he’d been comfortably reelected in the years since, he faced competitive primaries in 2014 and 2016 after his divorce records came to light.

Broderick, a mother of two and a former chair of the Lincoln County Democratic Party, raised less than $100,000 for her campaign, compared with more than $650,000 for DesJarlais. She also defeated a better-funded opponent in the Democratic primary.

She told PBS NewsHour that she had essentially been campaigning for the seat since launching her 2024 bid and built relationships across the district that helped her win.

“I never stopped running for Congress. And when it was redistricted, I just essentially said, well, the district stayed the same and actually became more competitive,” she said. “So the work's going to start speaking for itself real soon. And we saw that happen.”

Three House races remain uncalled as of Tuesday afternoon. Louisiana will hold runoffs in December for two districts, including a Democrat-held seat that Republicans redrew in their favor. The race for Minnesota’s 1st District is heading for a hand recount, with Republican Rep. Brad Finstad trailing Democratic challenger Jake Johnson by 324 votes.
