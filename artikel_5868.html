Jeffries survives Dem vote to secure speaker position as progressive resistance clouds his chances

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries tells Fox News that he is going to speak to every member ahead of leadership elections to rally support to be selected as Speaker of the House.

House Democrats selected Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., as their nominee for speaker of the House in the 120th Congress following the party's sweeping midterm victories, a senior House Democratic staffer confirmed to Fox News Digital.

Despite growing opposition from progressives over his leadership and ties to the Democratic establishment, Jeffries survived the secret-ballot vote on Wednesday in a "landslide," according to the staffer, advancing him as the party's nominee to lead the majority in the new Congress. Only 11 Democrats voted against his speaker nomination.

The New York lawmaker must now win a full House vote in January to secure the position.

Jeffries ran unopposed for the nomination, although recent reports indicated Democratic congressional offices had been inundated with calls from frustrated voters urging lawmakers to select an alternative candidate.

After he cleared the vote, the committee room where the incoming class of Democratic members, made up of re-elected and newly elected caucus members, erupted in cheering and clapping immediately following the vote.

"It was a blowout number," Rep. Bennie Thompson, D-Miss., told Fox News Digital after the vote.

The Democratic leader has faced criticism from progressive members of his party over his support for Israel and his ties to corporate political action committees, which critics argue reflect the priorities of the Democratic establishment rather than the party's progressive base.

Rep.-elect Don Leonard, D-Ohio, who flipped a district carried by Trump in 2024, told local media that he would be "shopping for a new leader" if Jeffries did not commit to changing his position on Israel.

When asked by Fox News Digital if he voted against Jeffries on Wednesday, Rep.-elect Adam Hamawy, D-N.J., a new member of the progressive caucus, declined to comment. After the vote count was reported, it was confirmed that Hamawy voted against Jeffries’ nomination.

Rep. elects Darializa Avila Chevalier, D-NY., Frederick Haynes, D-Texas, Melat Kiros, D-Colo., Donavan McKinney, D-Mich., Chris Rabb, PA, and Claire Valdez, D-N.Y., joined Hamawy in opposing Jeffries.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., speaks with reporters as he walks to his office after a press conference at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., on March 27, 2026. (Samuel Corum/Getty Images)

Rep. Jonathan Jackson, D-Ill., told Fox News Digital that the members who voted against them were "unfair" as they had never seen his abilities as a legislator, minority leader and let alone Speaker of the House.

Jackson added that these new representatives probably voted against Jeffries because they were following what their constituents wanted them to do.

Rep. Josh Gottheimer, D-N.J., told Fox News Digital that these new representatives are members of the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA), not the Democratic Party, and have their own independent legislative agenda outside that of the establishment.

After the vote, the coalition who voted against Jeffries released a statement claiming that they opposed Jeffries' nomination because the minority leader does not represent the agenda they believe in, which includes abolishing Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), and Medicare-for-all.

Democrats are preparing to use their new majority in January to immediately launch investigations, challenge President Donald Trump's policies through legislative oversight and hold congressional hearings. If Jeffries advances to speaker, he will lead that effort.

Despite the opposition, Jeffries has maintained support from prominent Democratic leaders, including former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi, D-Calif., who publicly backed his bid for the speakership.

Rep. Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., and Rep. Nancy Pelosi, D-Calif., talk on the House floor on Jan. 6, 2023. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc. via Getty Images)

Jeffries' nomination does not guarantee that he will become speaker when the new Congress convenes in January. The New York Democrat must still secure enough votes from the full House to win the position, but with the overwhelming victory on Wednesday, the path to speakership appears clearcut.

The looming vote could test whether progressive dissatisfaction with Jeffries' leadership translates into opposition on Capitol Hill or remains largely confined to grassroots activists.

The controversy also raises questions about whether incoming Democrats who campaigned against the party's establishment will ultimately support its chosen leader or demand policy concessions in exchange for their votes.

During the secret ballot session on Wednesday morning, Democrats selected Rep. Ted Lieu, D-Calif., to become the party's next caucus chair.
