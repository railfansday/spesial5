Thinking About Taking Your House Off the Market? Read This First.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Between travel plans, family gatherings, and everything else this time of year can bring, selling your house may feel like just another squeeze on your calendar.

And if your house is already on the market, you may be wondering whether it’s worth keeping it listed. Maybe you’re even asking yourself: Wouldn’t it be easier to take a break and try again after the new year?

Before you decide, you should know there are some very real advantages that make staying on the market worthwhile.

Typically speaking, fewer people shop for homes toward the end of the year. And yes, that’s feeling even more true with rates where they are now. But here’s what you have to remember…

You don’t need dozens of people to fall in love with your house. You just need the right one. And the people who are still looking often have a pretty good reason they need to move.

Maybe they’ve accepted a new job that starts in early 2027.

Maybe their family needs more space and they’re trying to move during school breaks.

Or maybe something in their life has changed, and waiting until spring simply isn’t an option.

“Yes, things typically slow down in the weeks leading up to the holidays. But there are still people actively looking for homes and ready to pounce—or those who just entered the market on a short timeline and need to buy fast.”

The buyers looking now are exactly the types of buyers you want to see. They’re serious. They’re motivated. And they want to make this work.

Here’s another reason to think twice before taking your house off the market: other sellers may be having the exact same thought.

As the year winds down, fewer homeowners typically put their houses up for sale. And the latest data shows that’s happening again.

According to HousingWire Data, the number of new listings fell 13.4% from mid-October to mid-November 2026, and is down 7.1% year-over-year – the steepest annual decline since 2022. And that’s actually good for sellers who already have their homes listed for sale (see graph below):

For sellers who stay on the market, that means fewer of those fresh newly-listed houses to compete with. Sure, you may have other homeowners whose house has been listed for a while, but there won’t be nearly as many of those shiny, enticing “just listed” properties drawing your buyer’s attention away.

Keeping your house available gives it a chance to be seen while other homeowners are waiting for the calendar to turn. And that could mean more eyes on your house.

There’s also something to be said for keeping the momentum you’ve already built.

Your house is listed. The photos are done. Buyers can find it online. And your agent can continue marketing it to people who are actively looking.

If you take your house off the market, you could miss someone who’d be interested. But here’s the important part: staying on the market doesn’t mean you shouldn’t make adjustments.

If your house hasn’t attracted the offers you expected, this may be the perfect time to revisit your strategy.

Does the price still make sense compared to the competition?

Could better photos, staging, or a few small updates help your house stand out?

Would a price cut or upfront incentive be enough to get buyers interested?

Those are conversations worth having with your agent now, rather than assuming a new year will automatically bring a different result.

And you don’t have to put your life on hold for showings, either. Work with your agent to find a schedule that makes sense for you and your travel plans this season.
