Builder Sentiment Falls to 14-Year Low in November

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Builder confidence fell sharply in November as higher mortgage rates, persistent affordability challenges, and rising construction costs continued to weigh on the new-home market, according to the National Association of Home Builders (NAHB).

The NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index fell four points to 25 in November, its lowest reading since April 2012. The index stood at 29 in October. Any reading below 50 indicates more builders view market conditions as poor than good.

“Builders are confronting multiple challenges in today’s economy,” says Bill Owens, chairman of the NAHB. “Stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs. Higher fuel prices are prompting subcontractors to raise their rates sharply, and with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction.”

All three components of the HMI declined during the month. The index measuring current sales conditions fell six points to 28, while the component measuring sales expectations over the next six months declined two points to 30. Prospective buyer traffic dropped two points to 16, its lowest level in 15 years excluding the onset of the pandemic in 2020.

In response to weaker demand, builders continued to rely heavily on incentives and price reductions to attract buyers. The share of builders cutting home prices increased to 46% in November, the highest level of the post-Covid period. The average price reduction remained at 6% for the eighth consecutive month.

Meanwhile, 68% of builders offered sales incentives, also a post-Covid high. November marked the 20th consecutive month in which at least 60% of builders reported using incentives. That represents a further deterioration from October, when 43% of builders reported cutting prices and 65% used incentives.

“The housing market faces a range of supply-side challenges, including building material costs pushed higher by tariffs, an ongoing shortage of construction workers and excessive regulations that undermine housing affordability,” says Robert Dietz, NAHB chief economist. “Mortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence, and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back.”

On a three-month moving-average basis, builder confidence declined in every region. The Northeast fell four points to 34, the Midwest declined five points to 35, the South dropped three points to 27, and the West fell three points to 23.
