GTA 6 Withholds Your 100% Stats Until the End — For Good Reason

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Rockstar Games took a "no checklist" approach to design

Completionists beware: Grand Theft Auto 6 is going to take you a very, very long time to complete. Rockstar Game’s open-world game is filled to the brim with things to do, see, and collect. You can go golfing, collect undersea treasures, find collectible trading cards, rack up medals at shooting ranges, scan landmarks, and so much more. Rockstar knows that it could get overwhelming fast, so GTA 6 was designed to combat that in one key way.

When playing through GTA 6, you’ll see a completion percentage on your save file. That number will only tick up when you complete story missions, a little less than 1% at a time. The rest of your collectible and activity progress is tracked in separate menus. However, you’ll also notice a locked menu option, with a line of text saying that it will open once you complete the story.

That’s your completionist menu. It shows you everything you need to achieve to get a true 100% save file, from finding all the special cars to sticking up every location you can. Once I unlocked it, the completion percentage on my save file kicked all the way down to 66%. After 10 hours of additional exploration, it had only ticked up another 2%. Needless to say, GTA 6 is not something you should expect to fully clear in a long weekend.

In an interview with Polygon ahead of the game’s release, Rockstar North co-head Rob Nelson explained the decision to hide the completion menu until players got the story out of the way. Nelson said that it came down to not wanting to make players feel a compulsion to stop and do everything while playing. In fact, Nelson was insistent about fighting back against a lot of traditional open-world philosophies, saying that he took a “no checklists” approach to design. (Nelson admitted that he had to cede a little bit of ground and include a few checklists in the final game.) Notably, you won't unlock the ability to use cheats until rolling credits either. That further encourages you to explore in a normal way rather than cheating your way into tough end-game activities early.

It’s a small design consideration, but an important one. If you try to play GTA 6 like a normal open-world game, you’re bound to get lost in a sea of distractions. You’re constantly pulled into unexpected side quests, drawn towards roadside activities, or tempted into an easy robbery. But without a completion menu tracking your progression throughout the story, it’s easy to accept the fact that you probably can’t do it all at once. There’s no checklist to make you feel like you need to be robbing more banks or else you’re not doing enough.

I appreciate that as a notorious open-world completionist. If you give me a Ubisoft game with a map full of items to check off, my brain is going to feel a need to do everything. Heck, I did everything there was to do in The Division, a game that I didn’t actually like! My experience with GTA 6 felt significantly more natural, as my playthrough was guided by curiosity rather than objective markers. In fact, so many sights and quests in Leonida aren’t marked on the map at all. Discovery is a fluid process, and I’ve come to appreciate that more since finishing the story and seeing exactly how much I missed.

I don’t imagine that I'll be chasing a 100% save file anytime soon, but I’m glad that there’s still so much game left to pick away at — and I’m glad I didn’t burn myself out by trying to gorge on it all before I could savor it.
