10 Best Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Replacements

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

It's been several weeks now since Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave was released for Nintendo Switch 2, and fans around the world are still exploring this massive game. While you could easily sink hundreds of hours into playing through every Flame Lord's story, you may be wondering what to play after your adventures in Dagda are through. Worry not: we've compiled a list of games like Fortune's Weave to keep your strategic skills sharp while you wait for the story expansion DLC that's slated to be released sometime in 2027. While many of the games on this list are strategy RPGs, some incorporate other standout elements of Fortune's Weave, like its tournament-focused story or the opportunity to recruit dozens of allies.

If you enjoy negotiating tricky terrain and periodically swapping your character classes in Fortune's Weave, the 2025 remake of Final Fantasy Tactics will be right up your alley. While it's not quite as difficult as the 1998 PlayStation original, FF Tactics demands that you engage deeply with its job system and figure out which team compositions are necessary for a specific battle. You are very much expected to grind via "optional" skirmishes, even on normal difficulty, and that challenge leads to a satisfying sense of mastery. A surprisingly grim yet moving story about the exploitation of common people by the nobility makes this one worth revisiting again and again.

2023's Engage didn't manage to inspire a thousand ships like Three Houses, mostly because the story is pretty flat and forgettable. But it's still well worth playing, because the combat and maps are extremely fun. The central gimmick, Emblem Rings, allow you to equip anyone in your squad with the skills of a past Fire Emblem hero, allowing for some truly bonkers synergies and limitless possibilities for customization. Build a strong enough bond between a character and an Emblem, and they'll be able to inherit some of that Emblem's skills permanently. Engage has plenty of social-sim elements and overworld exploration, but for some reason I feel far less remorse about ignoring them here than I do in Fortune's Weave.

Are you the type of Fire Emblem player for whom permadeath is the only option? Then you'll vibe with the tough battles in this 2019 strategy game from indie developer Chucklefish. Wargroove features 12+ unique Commanders, each with a special ultimate ability called a "Groove" that charges during combat, like healing powers or building walls. My favorite commander is Caesar, a golden retriever in stately silver armor that's festooned with a plume of red feathers. In one mission, he and his troops enter an abandoned stronghold, only to find that it’s been overrun by rogues. These ruffians soon learn a harsh lesson: underestimate the dog general at your own peril. He's a beautiful boy and I will murder anyone who lays a finger on him.

This tactical RPG from Sega does away with grid-based maps, though it's similar to Fire Emblem in that you'll control an assortment of class types in turn-based combat. The watercolor aesthetics are unique and charming, but don't expect a ton of narrative depth from the game's anime riff on the Second World War. Thankfully, the combat is a far richer and deeper experience, thanks to elements like tanks, ordinance, snipers. The maps offer plenty of variety in terms of terrain, obstacles, and weather, and there are around 50 characters to recruit, each with their own unique abilities and perks. But don't get too attached; they can die permanently if they're wounded in battle.

A weekly cheat sheet to what you’ll eventually love to play. No buying, no commitment — just saving for later.

Sega's '90s entry into the strategy RPG genre hasn't enjoyed the decades-long success of Fire Emblem, but it still has plenty of memorable maps to enjoy, including a giant chessboard and a series of floating rafts. The story is bare-minimum hokum, even by '90s RPG standards: think "the magical seal has broken on ancient evil." But an assortment of truly unique characters — including a werewolf, a phoenix, and checks notes a flying jellyfish with ice magic make Shining Force 2 well worth checking out for retro game fans. If all the social sim elements and calendar management in Fortune's Weave stress you out, you'll find none of that here: just sweet, sweet grids punctuated by the occasional town. If you have Nintendo Switch Online + Expansion Pack, you can find it in the Sega Genesis library.

If you enjoyed gradually unlocking the world map in Fortune's Weave, Vanillaware's 2024 tactical RPG takes a similar approach to exploration as you liberate towns and gather resources. The main story is pretty standard — you're tasked with assembling an army to overthrow the evil empire — but the optional adventures and character-driven missions have more meat on the bone. Like Fortune's Weave, Unicorn Overlord features more than 60 characters to recruit across a variety of classes. In a fun riff on the typical tactics formula, you'll combine those characters in squads of up to five for turn-based combat. You can even customize the order in which your skills and passive abilities trigger to maximize their damage-dealing potential. Vanillaware's painterly environments and character designs help make the experience easy on the eyes, too.

The Hundred Line is a choose-your-own adventure story first and a tactical RPG second. Even so, there's plenty for Fire Emblem fans to sink their teeth into. While your characters can't die permanently for plot reasons, their best attacks only unlock when they're close to death. Limited turns and massive waves of enemies encourage a high-risk, high-reward approach. You can use all your action points in a given turn, or save up a bunch to unleash hell all at once. While there are plenty of intricate, grid-based battles to chew on, its greatest similarity to Fortune's Weave is getting to see a variety of perspectives on crucial events. The Hundred Line features more than 20 distinct story routes and 100 unique endings. (And it was one of our favorite games of 2025!)

Did you spend countless hours befriending the various warriors scattered around Dagsion in Fortune's Weave, even if some of those recruits never even made it to the front line? If so, you should check out Konami's Suikoden series, especially the first two games, which got a lovely remaster back in 2025. These turn-based RPGs allow you to recruit 108 characters to join your fledgling army, though not all of them are playable. Some will perform vital functions around your castle, like running various shops, manning the storeroom, and even enabling fast travel. If you only play one game in this series, make it Suikoden 2. It chronicles a continent engulfed by war due to the bloodthirsty ambitions of Luca Blight, one of the most loathsome video-game villains of all time. In terms of sheer brutality and love-to-hate factor, Luca gives Fortune's Weave's Divine Emperor Dagda a serious run for his money.

First things first: Atlus's 2024 RPG features turn-based combat, rather than the map-based battles in Fortune's Weave. Beyond that, though, there are a ton of similarities between the two. Metaphor also blends a massively consequential fighting tournament with a popularity contest, as your group of heroes must gain renown across the land by accomplishing daring feats of bravery. While the game features eight playable characters, you can befriend dozens more through the Persona-like Follower Bonds system, which opens up new job classes as well as combat and exploration perks. And speaking of exploration, like Fortune's Weave, there's an extensive overworld map to explore during your free time, which allows you to unlock side quests, take on optional battles, and complete bounties.

Square Enix hasn't made a ton of strategy RPGs, and honestly, we're not sure why. Every time the company dips a toe in this pond, the results are usually something pretty special. That's the case for this 2022 tactical RPG co-developed with Japan-based studio Artdink. There are some overlong cutscenes you'll want to button-mash through, but the maps here are unique and well-designed. If you're a clever tactician, elevation, bodies of water, and even environmental obstacles like mine carts can all be used to turn the tide of battle in your favor.
