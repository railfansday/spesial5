House effectively ends Hegseth impeachment. And, 3 men accused of plotting U.K. attacks

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The House has effectively killed Republican Rep. Thomas Massie's effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The chamber voted 211 to 204 to send the impeachment articles back to the House Judiciary Committee for review. The move circumvents any potential floor vote on the impeachment and virtually ensures it won't be addressed before the current Congress session ends. Massie had reintroduced the impeachment articles on Monday, accusing Hegseth of high crimes related to the war with Iran and U.S. strikes against drug boats in the Caribbean and Eastern Pacific.

Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio will be the new minority leader of the House. House Republicans voted yesterday to replace Speaker Mike Johnson, who is stepping down after last week's midterm losses. At 62, Jordan brings experience from the House Oversight and Judiciary committees. The two panels are poised to play a central role in the new Congress as Democrats plan investigations into President Trump and his administration.

U.S. federal prosecutors have indicted three men accused of being Iran-backed operatives allegedly involved in nearly 20 terrorist attacks and attempted attacks in Europe and the U.S. The charges include a plot in September against a British air base where American forces were stationed. The Justice Department says some of these attacks were directed live via FaceTime from Iraq.

A Navy admiral fired by the Trump administration is returning to Washington as a Democratic congresswoman. In Virginia, a former Army Colonel beat another former Army Colonel, and a female Navy vet won a rematch to unseat another female Navy vet. In Florida, a staunchly pro-Trump Air Force Vet narrowly fended off a former Army Brigadier General (both also women.) In Montana, two military veterans lost, splitting the votes against a Republican former attorney general with no military background.

What conclusion to draw? There isn't any!

Americans dependably say they admire military service, and political campaigns recruit vets to run on tough races. Whether it works remains an open question, though some research suggests Democrats in particular may benefit from veterans status with voters.

The next Congress includes at least 80 veterans, with a few races still too close to call. And according to With Honor, which supports veteran candidates from both parties who promise to sponsor legislation across the aisle, more than 50 vets in the next Congress have signed their pledge.

But the number of vets is shrinking, down to 15% from the 19% currently in office. That's probably just demographics. Relatively few Americans serve in today's all-volunteer military, while past generations were drafted by the millions to Vietnam and Korea. During World War 2, military service was almost a given for men. When that generation entered politics, both chambers had around 70% veterans for a decade, from 1965 to 1975.

The 120th Congress will host more female vets than ever, about double the last class. Analysis by the Veterans Campaign and the University of San Francisco found that Democrats chose veterans as candidates for the toughest races, in districts President Trump won in 2024 by an average of 22 points.

There's another way vets are serving: as poll workers. The group Vet the Vote recruits veterans and their families to help run elections. Starting from a goal of 150,000 volunteers during the 2024 presidential cycle, their campaign counted more than 175,000 for this midterm, from all 50 states, helping bolster the understaffed and overworked polling officials nationwide. The group says it had volunteers in 4 out of 5 zip codes across the country.

And veterans like to vote. Syracuse University D'Aniello Institute for Veterans and Military Families found that vets turned out in relatively higher numbers than civilians in every election since 2014, a difference especially pronounced in midterm elections.

Being honest with people can be tricky. Sometimes, deciding whether to say anything at all can be harder than finding the right words to say.
