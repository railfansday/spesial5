Mississippi Cessna 210 Crash Kills 6 After 10,000-Foot/Minute Descent

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A Cessna 210 Centurion six-seat light aircraft crashed into the Gulf of Mexico between Pascagoula, Mississippi, and Horn Island after descending at 10,000 feet (3,050 meters) per minute. All six passengers tragically died in the crash, including four adults and two boys, aged eight and 10.

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) have started an investigation into the accident. The NTSB will lead this work and provide updates as more information becomes available.

The Cessna involved in the crash took off from New Orleans at 2:00 PM local time and was bound for Fort Myers, Florida, with the tail number N29002. At 3:45 PM, the regional air traffic control tower in Mobile, Alabama, reported losing contact with the aircraft. WVUE, a New Orleans news station, has reported that those onboard were traveling back home after enjoying a fishing trip in Venice, Louisiana.

Coast Guard responders began searching the waters over which the aircraft had been flying. They found only three life jackets, an unoccupied life raft, and the plane's floating cabin. Divers then went into the Gulf of Mexico and recovered the bodies of all of those onboard.

Commander Trevor Blount, the coordinator for the Coast Guard's search-and-rescue operations in the Mobile area, spoke about the efforts.

"We are grateful for the strong inter-agency coordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families."

The FAA and NTSB have started their investigation into the accident. Some information has already emerged. For example, the Coast Guard has said the aircraft was falling at a rate of 10,000 feet (3,050 meters) per minute when it crashed. The FAA's database shows that the aircraft is registered to EAB Services, which is based in Cape Coral, Florida. It was active for the past week, safely completing a few trips.

This included Fort Myers to Buras, Louisiana on November 21, and Fort Myers to East Rutherfordton, North Carolina (outbound and return) on November 19.

Simple Flying has contacted the FAA, which confirmed the incident in a statement.

"A Cessna 210 crashed southwest of Pascagoula, Mississippi, around 3:30 PM. local time on Tuesday, Nov. 24. Six people were on board. The FAA and the National Transportation Safety Board will investigate. The NTSB will lead the investigation and provide any updates."

The accident occurred after the aircraft attempted to divert while en route to Austin.

Cessna accidents and incidents made headlines earlier this week. An Air Canada Boeing 737 MAX 8 received a Traffic Collision Avoidance System (TCAS) alert because of an unresponsive Cessna in close proximity. The 737 MAX was climbing out of Palm Springs International Airport (PSP) and bound for Vancouver International Airport (YVR).

The pilots received a resolution advisory at approximately 11,400 feet (3,500 meters) to climb an additional 600 feet (183 meters), allowing the two planes to reach a safe amount of separation. Luckily, the two aircraft were kept apart from each other. However, the Cessna's identity and the minimum distance that separated the two aircraft remains unclear. The Air Canada jet safely completed its journey to Vancouver, which took two hours and 40 minutes.
