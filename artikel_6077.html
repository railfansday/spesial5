Which Credit Cards Give You Hotel Elite Status, And Are They Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Hotel credit cards are among the most underrated out there, thanks to the strong perks that they offer. In this post, I’d like to specifically focus on credit cards that offer hotel elite status, and talk about under what circumstances they could be worth it.

I’ve already written about the best hotel credit cards offering free nights, either as an annual benefit or as a reward for reaching certain spending thresholds. In many cases, these anniversary free night certificates can more than justify the annual fees on cards, given that they can be redeemed at some great hotels.

Nowadays it’s possible to earn elite status with most major hotel group through credit cards, so let’s talk about the options there are, and under what circumstances this is actually worth it.

I’d like to take a look at all the ways that you can use US-issued credit cards to earn elite status with a hotel loyalty program. In some cases, you get status just for having a card, and in other cases, a certain amount of spending is required.

There are potentially all kinds of benefits to hotel elite status, ranging from free breakfast to late check-out to bonus points to suite upgrades. Obviously the higher your status, the better the perks.

If you’re going to stay with a certain hotel group, it goes without saying that having some elite status is better than not having elite status. At the same time, I don’t want to overpromise, and suggest that being an elite member is going to get you the most amazing treatment on earth.

After all, if you get elite status just for having a credit card, you can assume that you’re not alone in pursuing this strategy. 😉 That being said, there are definitely some credit card elite status opportunities that are more lucrative than others.

My single favorite hotel credit card for elite status is the Hilton Honors American Express Aspire Card (learn more), as it offers Diamond status in the Hilton Honors program. This gets you 100% bonus points, room upgrades subject to availability, breakfast (or a food & beverage credit in the US), lounge access at select brands, etc.

As is common with the major hotel groups, the further from the United States you get, the better the elite treatment, and the better the shot of a good upgrade. Hilton Honors is my third preferred hotel group, and the Diamond status on the card does the trick for me. Given all the card’s other perks, I also find it really easy to justify.

My second favorite hotel credit card is the Marriott Bonvoy Brilliant® American Express® Card (learn more), as it offers Platinum status in the Marriott Bonvoy program. This gets you 50% bonus points, room upgrades subject to availability, the option of selecting breakfast as your welcome amenity at most brands, lounge access at most brands, guaranteed 4PM check-out at non-resorts, etc.

Don’t expect all that much when it comes to upgrades (at least in major markets), but the guaranteed perks, like late check-out, breakfast at most brands, etc., really do add up.

Beyond that, I think there should be a special mention for all the cards offering Hilton Gold status, given how easy it is to earn. Most crucially, it offers breakfast at properties outside the United States (and a food & beverage credit within the United States), so at least it offers something in the way of guaranteed benefits, compared to other comparable tiers.

It’s pretty remarkable that nowadays you can earn valuable status with Hilton, Marriott, and Wyndham, just for having a credit card (without a spending requirement), and with Hyatt and IHG you can earn valuable status with credit card spending.

On top of that, there are many mid-range credit cards that offer status just for having the card, which can be valuable as well. For example, it’s really easy to earn Hilton Gold elite status, and that gets you perks like free breakfast at most brands, bonus points, and more.

If you are going to spend money on your hotel credit card to earn status, just make sure you consider the opportunity cost of that spending.
