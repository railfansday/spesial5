Obama-appointed judge paves way to unseal Ilhan Omar search warrant in blow to Trump's DOJ

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

White House 'border czar' Tom Homan joins 'America Reports' to discuss federal probes into Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., the threat of migrant caravans and more.

A federal judge in Washington, D.C. who has often ruled against the Trump administration dealt a blow to the Justice Department Tuesday, paving the way to unseal the criminal search warrant and related documents against Rep. Ilhan Omar.

Judge James Boasberg, who was appointed to the bench by former President Barack Obama in 2011, asked the DOJ's attorneys to submit proposed redactions to the documents, excluding the main affidavit, by Friday. Unless a higher court intervenes, the court will publish the redacted documents online Monday beginning at 5 p.m. after consulting with prosecutors.

After its release, the groups seeking the documents have until Nov. 30 to challenge any blacked-out sections.

While criminal warrants aren't typically unsealed until after charges are filed, 15 media organizations and watchdog group Judicial Watch sued to obtain the warrant. They have argued that releasing the contents of the warrant is in the public interest.

The plaintiffs argued specifically that it should be made public which statutes federal investigators think Omar may have broken.

Boasberg signaled that he would not release the warrant application, which is a sworn statement submitted to a judge explaining facts and evidence that establish probable cause for issuing a warrant.

The DOJ did not immediately return Fox News Digital's request for comment.

Omar is facing a probe related to whether she or her father fraudulently filled out immigration paperwork in order for her to obtain citizenship. The Somali-born congresswoman said last week that she wasn't privy to what exactly the DOJ is investigating.

During the raid, which was conducted by Homeland Security Investigations (HIS), Omar's personal cellphone, computer and passport were seized. Her DNA has also been obtained, and her sister, Sahra Noor, was questioned by investigators the week of the raid.

Homeland Security Investigations police search the home of Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., on Oct. 12, 2026, in Washington, D.C. According to reports, the search is part of an ongoing criminal investigation. (Andrew Leyden/Getty Images)

In a motion seeking to prevent the federal search warrant for the congresswoman’s home from being unsealed, the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia referred to Omar as "Ilhan Nur Said Elmi, a/k/a Ilhan Abdullahi Omar…" and sources later told Fox News Digital that Ilhan Omar is not the congresswoman's "real name."

That news caused speculation that Omar married her brother, Ahmed Nur Said Elmi, to resurface. The pair married in 2009, and officially divorced in 2017.

Omar has not been charged with a crime and has repeatedly and categorically denied any wrongdoing. She has characterized the investigation as politically motivated, saying President Donald Trump is targeting her.

But the DOJ under former President Barack Obama opened an investigation into Omar's marriage with Nur Said Elmi in 2016 — suggesting the nonpartisan nature of some of the inquiries into the Minnesota lawmaker.

The New York Times first reported that Obama's FBI and Department of Homeland Security (DHS) began the probe in the summer of 2016, and that officials "found some evidence that [the marriage] was fraudulent." The Times reported that the investigation concluded when officials decided the potential felony crime was past the statute of limitations.

That probe, an official told Fox News Digital, remained open until 2021, when it was closed under the Biden administration.
