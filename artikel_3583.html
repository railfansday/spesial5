I'm psyched for Fallout's third season whenever it comes out (Kyle MacLachlan says it could be May or June next year), but I'd be perfectly happy if it had an ending and the show was done. Better to tell your story and leave it there than drag out a show just because it's popular and end up finishing on a wet fart of a finale when you should have gone out on, er, whatever the opposite of a wet fart is.

Season 3 has finished filming, but right now not even Walton Goggins, who plays The Ghoul, is sure what the future holds for Fallout's TV incarnation. Talking about how the show's maintaining the "subversive humor and the satirical kind of elements" it brought over from the games at an awards screening attended by GamesRadar, he said, "I feel confident that we've done that in season three, and if we get the opportunity to come back for season four, that would continue to honor how sacred this is to people. It's not something that we take lightly."

Goggins as we know doesn't play videogames himself. And co-star Aaron Moten has sworn off playing Fallout, leaving Ella Purnell the only actor to roll a Vault Dweller out of the headline trio. But despite that, Goggins is still aware of what the games mean to people.

"I mean, my son is a gamer. I see exactly how and why he plays games. I'm not a gamer at all, but the game of Fallout has been around for such a long time. It's been a shared experience between parents and their children and friends for such a long time, but it holds a very special place in their heart," Goggins said.

"I think what they have responded to, at least what I've been told out on the road, is the reverence in which all of us approach the sacred nature of this game, and while still keeping it an original story in the canon of Fallout."

Fallout Season 3 will feature another definite videogame-enjoyer in its cast, though. Aaron Paul, star of Dispatch and some other stuff I guess, has joined the show 16 years after attending a launch event for Fallout: New Vegas.
