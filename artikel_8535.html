Discount sites, shoe chains’ online clearance sections, and brands’ own sale pages sell brand-name shoes for less, but the return terms decide whether a bad fit costs you. Before you order a size you haven’t worn, check the return window and the cost of a mailed return, since several sellers charge $8.50 to $9.99.

Shoes in the same size fit differently from one brand to the next, and even between models from one brand, so a pair ordered online is a guess until the box arrives.

Return terms split the eight sellers below into three groups. Three pay for a mailed return, though one does so only for members and another only in the lower 48 states. Three more take returns free in their stores but deduct $8.50 or $9.95 from a refund by mail, and the last two make you pay for every return you mail.

The sellers with free mail returns make it cheap to order two sizes and keep one. Say you’re between a 10 and a 10.5 in a $140 running shoe from Running Warehouse. Order both, try them on a clean floor, keep the pair that fits, and send the other back with the prepaid label from the box. You pay $280 up front, $140 comes back once the refund posts, and the return costs nothing. From a seller that deducts $9.95 by mail, the same order at the same price leaves the pair you keep costing $149.95.

Keep any try-on indoors, since most of these sellers refund only unworn pairs. Zappos also wants its shoe box back without tape or postal labels, so pack it inside a shipping box.

Buy a size you’re unsure of from Zappos, Running Warehouse, or Nike as a member, or from DSW, Famous Footwear, or Nordstrom Rack when one of their stores is near you. Save 6pm and Joe’s New Balance Outlet for a model you already own in the right size.
