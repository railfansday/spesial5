Tare discusses new Milan direction, limitations in his role and ‘jealousy’: “Better late than never”

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Igli Tare has spoken without holding anything back about his time at AC Milan, explaining his frustration with several things.

When Tare arrived, many Milan fans were happy to see someone with Serie A experience oversee the project and whilst his first season didn’t end how anyone had hoped, he wasn’t seen as a major issue.

So, when it was announced that he was part of a complete overturning of the management, there was a bit of a surprise, even if it did feel inevitable at one point.

With the overhaul of changes in the summer, one does wonder what Tare would have done differently, especially with suggestions that his hands were tied in the mercato last season. Nevertheless,

Speaking on Sky Sport’s ‘Calcio Unplugged’, Igli Tare revealed all to Gianluca Di Marzio and Stefano Borghi in an in-depth interview about his time with the Diavolo, as Radio Rossonera has relayed.

“Today is the first time I’ve spoken out after all these months. The wound of not having reached the Champions League is still enormous, and since I arrived at Milan – also questioning myself with the type of contract I signed – the objective couldn’t be only the Champions League; otherwise I wouldn’t have understood where I was.”

“It was great because he was the one who convinced me. He had such a huge desire to join Milan. I wanted to video chat before going to see him because I couldn’t believe it. But after five minutes, I knew in my head that he was the player Milan really lacked.

“Not just because of his qualities, but as a man and as a figure that that team didn’t have. He never said no. A photo with a child, an autograph, a meeting, talking to someone… He never said no. These are the things that humility is key to in life.

“I’m proud to have brought a great champion to Milan. In my opinion, if he wants, he can play another two seasons without any problem. He never missed a training session. Sometimes we were the ones to stop him because he was always there, ready. Loving football at that age is truly an honour.”

Is it a characteristic of the champion or is it a generational issue? Today’s champions seem to have a different mentality:

“Today we live in the age of social media. At 16, you’re already a footballer who can earn millions of euros. Living this way, you have the problem of not only managing a player, who is still a child, but you have to manage the business they bring with them: family, agents, girlfriends…

“These are obstacles that didn’t exist back then. We were a generation that played in the streets. Back then, our parents beat us to get us to come home. Today, however, it’s to get them out of the house because they spend all day in front of computers.

“This aspect has been lost a bit, but it’s our fault. Education and discipline are the key to success.”

Fullkrug. He performed poorly in Milan, but in the Bundesliga he’s scoring a lot and is the top scorer. How do you explain that?

“It’s simple. He came to us after a tough time in England, with a lot of scepticism. And then, right away, he broke his toe. No one ever said he’d been having injections on the bone for two months just to train, let alone to play.

“I knew he was a centre forward that the team was missing; given his characteristics and his interpretation of the game, his qualities were unquestionable. Just two years before coming to Milan, he’d played in the Champions League final, playing a key role in a competition with Borussia Dortmund.

“There are situations where, for a player, even a very important one, it matters how you feel. If you feel appreciated, valued, taken into consideration… When you play for a club like Milan, the weight of the shirt is completely different than when you play for other clubs.

“You feel it from the first day you join. I had no doubt that he could be what he’s demonstrating now.”

“We tried to sign him back in June. We made a first attempt. In the final days of the transfer window, another important name we tried to sign was Akanji. The Champions League was crucial for both of them. They turned him down, not because of the club’s importance, but because he wasn’t playing in the Champions League.

“Then the opportunity arose again at the end of August for Rabiot. Defeat against Cremonese? No, we had already decided to bring him. It was just a matter of contract details.”

“It was difficult for us because we decided to change the formation five days before the transfer window closed. Switching from a 4-3-3 to a three-man defense meant we were having a really hard time finding a quality player in the last five days.

“We were a bit unlucky, first with the Akanji story and then with Liverpool’s Joe Gomez… Guehi’s move from Crystal Palace to Liverpool fell through, and at the last minute they—we already had the deal ready, we were just waiting for this to happen—made a step back. And to avoid being left without a central defender at the last minute, we signed Odogu as a promising player.”

On not being able to reach the Champions League and not being able to continue his work at Milan…

“This is the first time today, after all these months, that I’ve spoken about this… The wound of not reaching the Champions League is still enormous. I think that from the first day I arrived at Milan, even in the way I signed—I questioned myself with the type of contract.

“I said, ‘If I have to come only for the Champions League objective, then I don’t understand where I’ve come from.’ Milan has a different story. I had to go through a trial. The disappointment is that those who run the club didn’t understand how much time people needed, which is now being done, rightly so.

“That is to say, it takes several transfer sessions to get a competitive team ready to win. At the beginning, I felt the responsibility to put the pieces together immediately, because after the defeat in the Coppa Italia final the year before, there were some important signs.

“I didn’t know where to start. But I think we did an important job last summer, we sold for a lot and also signed 10 new players. Then you need time to integrate, learn new philosophies and create a cohesive group, hungry to win.

“Until March-April, we were a group with difficulties, but we were at the top of the table. Then in the last 10 matchdays, we had a mental breakdown, also due to other external dynamics, which I don’t want to discuss here, which had a significant impact.

“Internal or external dynamics? External to the team. But we also have some blame: myself first and foremost, along with Max [Allegri].

“We had to be a little more attentive to certain dynamics. We needed to be more present with the team to ultimately lead them to the final result. Isolation is the wrong word; I don’t believe in these things. I believe in teamwork; I believe in cohesion: from the club to the team.

“When this happens, you transmit it to the team. When these situations become possible, you could also see it in the team’s results, because the players are very attentive. Sometimes we think they have other interests, but they pay attention to everything that comes out. They follow everything very carefully and transmit it.

“Instilling serenity… How can you convey serenity? When, in difficult moments, you show that you believe in the project more than before. Sometimes I missed this feeling, a lack of something that could give us the final push.”

But despite this, did you think you could continue? Does this farewell after only a year feel like a stab in the back?

“Look, I would have asked for a round table where we could all sit down together and analyze everything. Then they could have just said ‘no, thanks’… But I respect the decision they made. I accept it with great regret, but I’ve never questioned it because there’s someone above me who has to decide, including for the fate of the club.

“And when they decide to take a different path, you have to accept it. I know things could have gone differently, even much better. I’ll always be a Milan fan, even after an experience like that.”

Was AC Milan a team that needed the constant presence of a manager or coach to avoid letting itself go as it later did?

“The feeling I had at the beginning was that this leadership was a bit lacking. The arrival of Modric—and another of our goals was also Granit Xhaka, Leverkusen’s captain—was precisely to bring real players into the group, with important characteristics and capable of leading others.

“Granit didn’t arrive; Luka arrived, who has a different way of being a leader: he does it in silence and with his attitude on the pitch. I had the opportunity to meet Mike Maignan: an extraordinary guy, a soul who cares deeply about the AC Milan shirt.

“Gabbia is another extraordinary guy; he lives for this club. Rabiot too… His experience spoke for itself. The great disappointment is that I expected something more in terms of responsibility and leadership from Pulisic: he’s a top-class player with unquestionable qualities. But in terms of performance, things happened that I had never experienced in my career…

“Like Gimenez: an extraordinary guy with numbers that spoke for themselves; for a year he failed to score a goal. Then it happened that Pulisic didn’t score for six months. He scored a goal. Then Leao didn’t score for four months.

“Situations that are hard to come by in football have happened… It can happen that you don’t score for a month, two months, but that long a period of time also affected the team’s final performance.

“Losing a match against Cagliari the way we did, 2-1, I think was the worst match of my life. The feeling and the disappointment I felt towards the fans in the stadium that night is something I will never forget for the rest of my life.”

If Cardinale had been the Cardinale he is today, the one who is also physically present, would you still be AC ​​Milan’s DS?

“I can’t answer that question; he has to answer it. I can say that I remember when the Jashari negotiations were going on… I had just landed in Rome and I was very pleased when he called me because he wanted to understand what was happening in that negotiation.

“I explained all the dynamics to him and I really liked that he said, ‘Anything you need, if you have to spend a little more, let me know because I’m here.’ Then during that process I never had the chance to talk to him, even intentionally.

“There were other people above me who I never wanted to bypass. I never had a direct relationship with him, and the possibility of a discussion would have helped everyone. Not me, but everyone. By putting all the problems and dynamics on the table, we could have resolved things much sooner. But I think it’s a new experience for Cardinale.

“He’s had experience in other sports in America. Now he’s done something important, taking responsibility and putting his face on the line. I would have preferred to have had that presence even when I was there because it would have been very useful. But for the Milan, better late than never.

“Now, for better or worse, he will make his own decisions and responsibilities. I think that for him, too, the best interests of Milan come first.”

Was Leao a problem or a resource in your mind?

“I knew him from the outside. When I had the chance to meet him in person, I thought he was an extraordinary guy. I truly loved him as a person, like everyone else. But he was the player from whom we all expected something more. I think football was no longer a priority for him. That’s the key to understanding him.”

“I think a bit about his life outside of football. Music, fashion, things… But I never doubted the sense of belonging he had towards Milan. You could sense that he loved playing for Milan and was suffering from not performing.

“He had many physical problems… First a calf, then a bit of pubalgia… He always played with his hands under control. I always tried to have individual conversations with him more than with any other Milan player.

“Every time I spoke to him, I left with a positive feeling. I sensed that he wanted to. But sometimes wanting isn’t enough.”

Was there a chance that Leao would leave during your summer too?

“A concrete offer never came for him. Regarding the other transfers, it’s not just Tare’s job. When I arrived – even at Lazio – I was a bit alone, but I tried to involve all the members of the club to give him the importance he needed to build a sense of belonging and responsibility. I think I did a very good job, together with Furlani, Ibra, and Moncada.

“We achieved significant sales figures, in a year when we weren’t even in the Champions League. Thanks to the work we did last summer, investments were made this year, with the money we had at the time. I really believe in teamwork.

“I said it even before the Coppa Italia match against Bari; I asked to speak to convey the importance of the work of the people within the club because it was right that way. Then afterwards, it’s human…

“They were coming off a bad year, when two cumbersome figures like Tare and Allegri arrive, and everyone was talking about Tare and Allegri, some disappointment was understandable. I understood the feeling they had, but I could… There was little to do about it.

“For me, it was a new path, a new opportunity. It was essential to work as a team and present ourselves as a real company.”
