DynamoDB Incremental Backups in AWS Backup: What the Cost Example Shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

If you back up DynamoDB tables with AWS Backup, you are probably paying for a full copy of every table, every time. As of 20 November there is another option: incremental backups. AWS published a worked cost example, so I ran the numbers through to see how much it matters.

All of this is from the launch post, the AWS storage blog and the pricing page. I have not run it myself.

After the first full backup, each later backup stores only what changed. AWS Backup starts a new full backup every 15 days (the blog says "currently", so do not hard-code that). Every recovery point can be restored on its own: restoring an incremental one pulls in the full backup and the increments up to that point.

AWS's blog uses US East (N. Virginia) warm storage at $0.10 per GB-month for full backups and $0.14 for incremental. The scenario is a 1 TB table, daily backups, 30-day retention.

These are AWS's theoretical numbers, not measurements. Still, the shape is useful. Even at 30% daily churn you roughly halve the bill, and a low-churn table drops by a large factor. The per-GB incremental rate is higher than the full rate, so the saving comes entirely from storing far less.

One caveat: the pricing page extract I read did not include the rate table, so check current rates for your Region before you model anything.

Prerequisite: advanced DynamoDB backup features must be enabled. New accounts get them automatically. If your account already had advanced features on, incremental is not switched on for you. The blog's CLI check and enable:

All three must be true. Existing plans keep working. The next backup of each table is a full one, then incrementals follow, and older fulls expire on their original schedule.

Pull your last month of backup storage cost per table. Rank by size. Pick the three biggest, estimate daily change from your write metrics, and plug them into the blog's model. If you see a clear win, enable incremental in one Region first and compare the bill after a full 30-day retention cycle. Which of your tables has the lowest churn?
