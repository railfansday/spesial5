32 things we learned in NFL Week 10: Trade deadline already delivering big benefits

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The 32 things we learned from Week 10 of the 2026 NFL season:

0. The still-winless Los Angeles Chargers remain on pace to become the NFL's first-ever 0-17 team … unless they can notch their first victory of 2026 in prime time on Monday night in Baltimore, when Jacoby Brissett is set to make his debut as the Ravens' latest QB1.

1. The number of games every NFL team – save the Chicago Bears, Denver Broncos, Philadelphia Eagles and Tampa Bay Buccaneers, who were all on their bye week – has played since the Nov. 10 trade deadline. And those that delved into the market experienced almost immediate returns.

2. Once and present and future Minnesota Vikings QB Kirk Cousins only needed five days to get his former and current team back on track – leading the Vikes to a resounding 30-10 victory over the Green Bay Packers just six days after they were embarrassed by the Buffalo Bills on the "Monday Night Football" prime-time platform.

2a. And it was a meaningful win for an emotional Cousins, whose previous game with the Vikings had also been at Lambeau Field – one during which he tore his Achilles tendon in a game midway through a 2023 season that was shaping up as one of the best years of his career up to that point. Minnesota did not re-sign Cousins in 2024, and he joined the Atlanta Falcons.

3. New Kansas City Chiefs WR Josh Downs seemed to fit into his new circumstances snugly, catching four balls for 53 yards and a TD in his K.C. debut. Downs also assumed the punt return duties for his new team.

4. The number of NFL officials who worked a pair of games Sunday in the aftermath of the FAA's Friday ground stop, which caused travel havoc that rippled through the weekend. Referee Don Willard, umpire Terry Killens, field judge Jabir Walker and side judge Justin Larrew worked the Dolphins-Colts matchup at 1 p.m. ET in Indianapolis, then drove to Cincinnati for the "Sunday Night Football" showdown between the Steelers and Bengals. Willard, normally a side judge, not only was on double duty but in an unfamiliar role to boot.

5. The number of TD passes thrown Sunday in Atlanta by Chiefs QB Patrick Mahomes – the ninth time, playoffs included, he's reached that threshold in his career but first since Week 1 of the 2022 season.

5a. The number of TD passes thrown Sunday by Dallas Cowboys QB Dak Prescott – the second time, playoffs included, he's reached that threshold in his career … though with far less impact on this day than Mahomes. More on Dallas later.

6. The number of game-winning drives Cleveland Browns QB Deshaun Watson has engineered this season for the 6-4 Browns – i.e., he's done so in all of their games, including Sunday's 27-24 defeat of the Houston Texans, Watson's original club. Cleveland trailed 17-0 early in the second quarter.

6a. Also the number of NFL games the league staged in Europe this season, Sunday's matchup between the Detroit Lions and New England Patriots in Munich the final installment of that sextet.

7. Lions WR Amon-Ra St. Brown, whose mother is German, shone in the contest – which wasn't quite a homecoming for a man who has Deutschland citizenship, but close. He had his first multi-TD game since Week 2 in Detroit's 35-10 blowout victory. "Being in Germany, number one, was a blessing, it's been amazing," said St. Brown afterward, who answered many questions in recent days in German. "But being able to win like that − with the guys, my teammates in front of the fans here in Germany, it's like a dream come true."

8. The number of times Packers QB Jordan Love was sacked Sunday by the blitz-happy Vikings defense. Prior to that nightmare, Love had never been bagged more than five times in an NFL game.

9. Pretty cool that Jacksonville Jaguars head coach gave his new acquisitions, DT Jordan Davis and S Jessie Bates III, Sunday off given neither has had a bye week nor was in line for one given the Jags' remaining schedule.

10. And Jacksonville didn't need its new guys, rolling the Titans 40-23 – the Jaguars' fifth win this season by at least 17 points.

11. We'll know in two months whether or not the Jags overpaid for Bates and Davis, who will cost the franchise one first- and two second-round picks. But the 2026 squad's trajectory was, and continues to be, impressive.

12. While the Cousins deal worked as intended – so far – for Minnesota, Las Vegas Raiders rookie QB Fernando Mendoza maybe wasn't as ready to start as reports coming out of the desert suggested. He had some promising early moments Sunday against the Seattle Seahawks' "Dark Side" defense, but the Silver and Black wound up with fewer than 100 net passing yards in the first regular-season start for the No. 1 pick of the 2026 draft – Mendoza sacked six times for 45 yards in losses … and he didn't seem to see some of the pressure coming at him.

13. The number of penalties committed by the Texans on Sunday, costing them 112 yards. Bad teams, talented or not, find ways to beat themselves.

14. The jersey number of Seattle QB Sam Darnold, who's missed the past two games with various injuries. Sunday, backup QB Drew Lock improved to 3-0 this season and also shepherded the team to victory in Week 1, when Darnold was also hurt. Lock's wins have come against the Arizona Cardinals (twice) and Las Vegas Raiders, but you have to wonder if, at some point, the Seahawks opt for the hot hand. With their bye upcoming, they have another week to decide as Darnold continues to heal.

15. The jersey number of Seahawks WR-KR Tory Horton, who made one of the more heads-up special teams plays you'll ever see in a bid to get his team past the two-minute warning after the Raiders had pulled within one score but had to kick off with 2:04 left on the clock. He leapt high to swat the incoming kickoff forward and then directed teammate Rashid Shaheed to return it and get Seattle inside of the two-minute mark. Horton was flagged for illegally batting the ball on the very unique sequence, but Las Vegas declined it. Regardless, his play achieved its objective, and Seattle prevailed 21-13.

16. Lock completed 14 of 17 passes in Sin City for 240 yards and two TDs, giving him a nearly perfect 158.0 passer rating. However, he did fumble twice, turnovers the same bugaboo that has plagued Darnold.

17. The number of penalties committed by the Carolina Panthers on Sunday, costing them 152 yards – both franchise records. Bad teams, talented or not, find ways to beat themselves – though not necessarily in the abhorrent NFC South, Carolina surviving the New Orleans Saints 20-17 on the road.

18. Thankfully for the Panthers, they still have RB Chuba Hubbard, one of the league's more underrated backs. His 172 yards from scrimmage Sunday represented 61% of Carolina's offensive output. His two TDs similarly provided 60% of his team's points.

19. The Buffalo Bills got seven tackles and a pass defended by new S Amani Hooker, a player the Tennessee Titans were willing to part with.

20. And though you hate to glean too much from wins over the New York Jets (on Sunday), Vikings and Ravens – all teams the Bills have caught without their QB1s for at least a portion of those matchups – Buffalo has allowed just 28 points since its Week 7 bye. The AFC East leaders, which surrendered at least 29 points in five of their first six games this season, have permitted just one touchdown – total – in its past three games.

21. We'll continue to wonder why the Indianapolis Colts didn't trade RB Jonathan Taylor – though, apparently, they didn't get the Godfather offer they wanted, Round 2 draft pick compensation evidently insufficient.

22. Yet the Colts, who are clearly headed into rebuild with their soon-to-be 28-year-old bell cow under contract through the 2028 season, continue to seemingly manage his touches. Taylor had 18 in Sunday's 24-22 defeat of the Miami Dolphins, his third straight game with fewer than 20. (He had at least 20 in six of Indy's first seven game this season.) Maybe Indy is hoping its next GM can trade a fresh Taylor for more draft capital in a few months.

23. We'll also continue to wonder why the Titans didn't trade RB Tony Pollard, who scored his sixth TD in the past three games Sunday but likely only has eight games left with one-win Tennessee before he leaves in free agency. We'll see if the 2028 compensatory pick the franchise might get will be worth whatever it could have reaped for the versatile back, who will turn 30 in the offseason.

24. Los Angeles Rams legend Aaron Donald began Sunday still seeking his first post-unretirement sack. But he got Arizona Cardinals QB Carson Beck 1½ times in the rookie's first regular-season start.

25. And the injury-depleted Rams won their fourth in a row and have rushed for at least 120 yards in each of those victories – including a season-high 202 on Sunday.

26. The San Francisco 49ers' 46-40 defeat of Dallas was more convincing than the final score indicates.

27. The Niners' 584 yards were the most ever piled up against the Cowboys in their own building and, per Elias, San Francisco's three TDs from 60+ yards were tied for the most in a game by one team over the past half-century.

28. Christian McCaffrey's 66- and 74-yard TD catches – mostly runs after the catch – were the two longest of his 10-year career. They also gave him the league's career record for most TD receptions by a running back.

29. And San Francisco, which had already reacquired DT DeForest Buckner, got a nice initial ROI from its latest trade deadline acquisition – WR Keenan Allen catching five balls for 61 yards in his 49ers debut after coming over from the Colts just days before.

30. Is monochrome black slimming? Maybe in the win column. Just ask the Saints.

31. Is monochrome black slimming? Maybe in the win column. Just ask the Jets.

32. Not much to like about the Cowboys this season. But we appreciate them honoring veterans and Medal of Honor winners annually at this time of year by adding a red stripe to the centerline of their helmets as part of the NFL's "Salute to Service" program.
