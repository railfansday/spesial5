Nets vs. Raptors Preview: Can Brooklyn capitalize against a shorthanded Toronto team?

A much needed victory. The Brooklyn Nets continued their road trip with a matchup against the Milwaukee Bucks on Wednesday night. Brooklyn got back into the win column with a 104-97 win and hopes that it can be the start of a much-needed turnaround.

The Toronto Raptors are coming off perhaps their most frustrating loss of the season — a 116-111 loss at the hands of the lowly Milwaukee Bucks. That's a team the Nets just snapped their own five-game losing streak against. Toronto will be missing Kawhi Leonard and RJ Barrett in this one, so the Nets should really find a way to squeeze out with a win.

Who: Brooklyn Nets (4-13) vs. Toronto Raptors (6-11)

OUT – Nolan Traore (right hamstring strain)

OUT – Mo Wagner (left knee injury management)

OUT – Kawhi Leonard (left knee injury management)

OUT – RJ Barrett (right hamstring strain)

QUESTIONABLE – Alijah Martin (left knee soreness)

The Brooklyn Nets (4-13) travel north to face the Toronto Raptors (6-11) on Friday night in the front end of an international back-to-back. Once this one wraps up, they're flying back to the States for a matchup in Charlotte Hornets on Saturday night.

Ben Saraf was called up to the big club, so we'll see how Jordi Fernandez utilizes him. Mikel Brown Jr. has yet to play both ends of a back-to-back, but with Traore out, Brooklyn's guard room has even less margin for error. Brown Jr. will have to start playing both ends of a back-to-back eventually, so now would be a perfect time to start.

We expected a lot more from the Raptors this year, but circumstances have gotten in the way. Kawhi Leonard has only appeared in two games this season, though he is doing individual workouts and hopes to be back soon enough. In the meantime, the team will have to make do without the former NBA Finals MVP. From Julian Apolinario of Raptors HQ:

"Unfortunately, the Raptors look like they're not constructed in a way that they can win big games without Kawhi Leonard, at least, not yet. It's early in the season, and there are more games ahead of the Raptors than behind them, and given some more time with a clear starting lineup, the Raptors might be able to put it together.

A machine doesn't work while missing its biggest gear, and it seems that no matter how well the Raptors play, they remain a step behind the best teams in the league. Come playoff time, things will have to be different if the Raps want any real chance of success this year, but that doesn't necessarily mean that things are hopeless with the current roster — injuries just need to be on their side."

Michael Porter Jr. is still working through his slump. The Nets forward has been under 45 percent shooting from the field in four consecutive games and for a team that needs his scoring to be close to competitive, they can't afford extended cold stretches from him. Every day is a chance to get back on the good foot, so the Nets hope today is the day MPJ returns to his All-Star caliber form.

It wasn't supposed to be like this, but we're learning something exciting along the way. With Leonard and Barrett on the team, Scottie Barnes was not expected to be the focal point of the team's offense. However, with those two out, he's had to take on more of the scoring and playmaking responsibilities to keep the team afloat. Barnes is at career highs in usage rate, field goal attempts, and points per game. Also, he's been living in the midrange area a lot more as he's at career lows in three-point attempt rate and percentage of shots attempted inside of three feet. Barnes is a tremendous finisher at the rim so it'll be interesting to see how Toronto finds him more opportunities near the basket.

Julius Randle will draw the Barnes matchup and see if he can continue his recent shooting success. Randle has shot at least 45 percent from the field in nine out of the last ten games, and the Nets will need that to continue tonight. When Randle is assertive and making good decisions, the ceiling for the Nets offense is a lot higher. When he holds on to the ball too much, things get stagnant and frustrations rise.

Even shorthanded, the Raptors pose an interesting challenge for the Nets and we'll see how the team does on the front end of a back-to-back.
