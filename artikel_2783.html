NBA panic meter: Which teams should worry after first month of season?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The NBA season is long. Too long, according to some critics.

One month of basketball, particularly the first month, is rarely enough to make definitive conclusions about a team's outlook. But it is enough time to form first impressions, identify flaws that loom as bigger problems and spot the teams in danger of not meeting expectations. It's enough time for a fan base to start panicking.

The first month of the 2026-27 NBA campaign has been especially challenging for several teams with playoff aspirations, with injuries and underwhelming performances already threatening to derail some of those hopes. At the same time, the defending champion New York Knicks, Miami Heat and Cleveland Cavaliers are among a handful of contenders stumbling out of the gate to the point that it's beginning to cause alarm.

So what's actually worthy of pushing the panic button and what's simply part of the natural ebb and flow of an 82-game season? Here's a breakdown of the NBA's panic meter after nearly a month of the 2026-27 regular season.

The season is going off the rails quickly in Utah, with the Jazz mired in a 1-13 start and already dealing with injuries to several key players. Making matters worse, former cornerstone center Walker Kessler is flourishing with the Los Angeles Lakers after Utah opted to trade him this offseason rather than commit to a lucrative long-term extension. This was supposed to be the year the Jazz took a meaningful step forward and reentered the Western Conference playoff picture. Instead, they're already staring at a significant hole in the standings, and the front office is facing uncomfortable questions about whether its long rebuild is any closer to producing a contender.

Denver had won four of their previous five games entering Nov. 16, but their 7-4 start deserves closer scrutiny. The Nuggets' seven victories have come against opponents with a combined .383 winning percentage, while each of their four losses were by double digits against teams currently ahead of them in the Western Conference standings. Nikola Jokić has remained productive, but he hasn't looked quite as dominant as he did during his MVP peak, with a sharp decline in his 3-point shooting allowing defenses to guard him differently since his return from last season's wrist injury. Considering Jamal Murray is playing at a high level, Denver's inability to compete with upper-tier opponents is an especially troubling sign for a team that made no major roster upgrades after a first-round playoff exit.

The Cavaliers beat the Dallas Mavericks on Nov. 14 to get back to .500, but the circumstances only amplified concerns about Cleveland's underwhelming start. With the usual starters unavailable because of travel disruptions caused by the nationwide FAA ground stop, the Cavaliers' reserves stepped in and reinforced how poorly the team's top players have performed this season. Cleveland's projected starting lineup of James Harden, Donovan Mitchell, Peyton Watson, Evan Mobley and Jarrett Allen owns a -12.4 NET rating in 99 minutes together as of Nov. 16. Harden's slow start is concerning given his age, while Watson's struggles as both a shooter and defender have already sparked questions about his four-year, $88 million contract. This team reached the Eastern Conference finals last season and seems farther from returning than expected.

The Raptors were one of last season's biggest surprises in the Eastern Conference then doubled down on their win-now ambitions by acquiring Kawhi Leonard to provide the late-game scoring they lacked. But Leonard hasn't played since Oct. 23 after the team announced he would undergo a treatment and rehabilitation program to address swelling in his left knee. Toronto has gone 3-8 since then, including a current four-game losing streak punctuated by several blown leads. While an MRI revealed no structural damage, Leonard's lengthy injury history makes it difficult to assume a quick return to form. More concerning is that the Raptors still can't prove they can compete with the East's best teams. They are 0-7 against opponents with better records this season after going 5-22 in those situations a year ago.

The defending NBA champions opened the season 0-3, with an offense that has sputtered at times. New York's most-used lineup from a season ago – Jalen Brunson, Miles McBride, Josh Hart, Mikal Bridges and Karl-Anthony Towns – is also being outscored by 10 points per 100 possessions more than last year. But the Knicks have already climbed back above .500 thanks to a recent four-game winning streak. Towns is averaging a double-double and leading the NBA in rebounds, while Brunson remains one of the league's premier offensive orchestrators and shot creators. More importantly, this core proved during last year's playoff run that it can elevate its play when the stakes are highest. A slow start is worth monitoring, but New York has time to round back into championship form as the season progresses.

Plenty of NBA observers were ready to hit the panic button when the Heat opened the Giannis Antetokounmpo era with four consecutive losses. Since then, Miami has gone 7-2 while developing a lineup that appears to maximize the talents of both Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo after those initial growing pains. As of Nov. 16, the group of Antetokounmpo, Adebayo, Klay Thompson, Pelle Larsson and Davion Mitchell owns a 23.3 NET rating. Only the starting lineups of the Spurs and Lakers have played as many minutes and performed better. The Heat's climb up the Eastern Conference standings may not be perfectly smooth, but there are already indications that some of the early issues that plagued them are being resolved.

Tyrese Haliburton is already back on the injury report with a hamstring issue, and Ivica Zubac's integration into Indiana's up-tempo system hasn't been entirely seamless. Four of the team's five starters have already missed games. The Pacers learned last season how thin their margin for error becomes without Haliburton on the floor, and this year's success will depend heavily on whether he can consistently resemble the player he was before suffering a torn Achilles in Game 7 of the 2025 NBA Finals. Haliburton looked sharp during his first four games back, but Indiana's outlook could change quickly if his latest hamstring issue becomes a recurring problem.
