3 takeaways as the Mavericks’ comeback comes up just short, 112-111, at the San Antonio Spurs

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The Dallas Mavericks (4-4) nearly pulled off a ridiculous comeback Wednesday night, but fell 112-111 to the San Antonio Spurs (6-2) after a strange final possession. Victor Wembanyama completely dominated the game, finishing with 52 points, 24 rebounds, and eight blocks while controlling everything Dallas tried to do around the rim. Kyrie Irving led the Mavericks with 29 points, while Cooper Flagg recovered from a brutal first half to finish with 19 points, 10 rebounds and five assists. Naji Marshall was huge off the bench with 22 points on 9-of-12 shooting, helping Flagg lead a second-half comeback that gave Dallas a chance to win on the final possession.

The Mavericks had a rough opening quarter in San Antonio, mostly because Victor Wembanyama looked completely in control while Dallas' two best players could not get anything going. Wembanyama had 15 points, eight rebounds, three steals and two blocks in just over eight minutes, while Cooper Flagg missed all seven of his shots and Kyrie Irving started 2-of-8. Morez Johnson Jr. gave Dallas some good energy off the bench, but the Mavericks shot just 31 percent and struggled to finish anything around the rim. San Antonio led 32-26 after one.

The second quarter made the matchup look even worse. Wembanyama completely erased the paint, forcing hesitations, kick-outs, and bad attempts while blocking Flagg, P.J. Washington, and Daniel Gafford in the same stretch. By halftime, he already had 29 points, 15 rebounds, and five blocks. Flagg was just 1-of-11 with four points and no assists; Kyrie had 17, and San Antonio dominated the glass 44-31 with 15 offensive rebounds. Dallas trailed 68-51 and looked completely overmatched.

The Mavericks finally showed some life in the third. They won the non-Wembanyama minutes, Flagg started to look more aggressive, and the small-ball approach gave San Antonio more problems. Kyrie helped push the comeback, Walter Clayton Jr. hit another three, and Dallas won the quarter 29-22 to cut the deficit to 90-80. Wembanyama was still everywhere and finished with 40 points, but Dallas had at least made the game competitive again.

The fourth quarter was easily Dallas' best stretch. Naji Marshall and Flagg led the comeback, with Marshall hitting huge shots and Flagg finally getting downhill and creating offense. Dallas sped the game up, went small, and did about as good a job as possible neutralizing Wembanyama, though he still made several massive plays late. Marshall's three with 1:41 left tied the game at 111, and after Wembanyama split a pair of free throws with 24 seconds left, Dallas had one final chance to win. The possession went nowhere, ending with a difficult Kyrie turnaround fadeaway with three seconds left, and the Mavericks fell 112-111.

Cooper Flagg was awful in the first half, and aside from Victor Wembanyama completely taking over, he was probably the biggest reason Dallas got buried early. Flagg went just 1-of-11 before halftime, could not get to his spots, struggled badly against San Antonio's length, and started passing up shots he normally takes. He also didn't give Dallas enough defensively, which made the performance feel even worse. The second half was completely different. Flagg finally started attacking, got out in transition, made better reads, and became one of the main reasons Dallas even had a chance to win. He finished with 19 points, 10 rebounds, and five assists, and while the 7-of-19 shooting line still wasn't pretty, most of the damage came after halftime. He and Naji Marshall led the comeback, and Flagg looked much more like the player Dallas needs him to be down the stretch. The first half was probably his worst stretch of the season, but the response mattered.

Dallas had the ball down one with 24 seconds left and a chance to win the game, and the final possession was baffling. The Mavericks waited until roughly 10 seconds remained to really initiate anything, then basically just handed the ball to Kyrie Irving and asked him to make something happen. No screen, no dribble handoff, no action to force a switch, no attempt to get Flagg involved, just a Kyrie isolation that ended in a difficult turnaround fadeaway with three seconds left. It felt like one of those old Jason Kidd end-of-game possessions where the entire offense disappears, and the plan becomes "Kyrie, go save us." That cannot be the best option. Kyrie is obviously one of the best isolation scorers in basketball, but when you have an entire timeout to draw something up, you have to make the defense work harder than that. Dusty May has been creative offensively in plenty of situations already, which made this even stranger. Dallas had a chance to steal a game after being down big, and the final possession did not give them nearly enough of a chance.

There's really no more complicated way to say it. Wembanyama was incredible.

He finished with 52 points, 24 rebounds, and eight blocks on 16-of-28 shooting, while also getting to the free-throw line 23 times. The numbers are absurd, but his impact on the game was even bigger. Dallas could not operate normally offensively because every paint touch became a problem. Drivers hesitated, bigs had shots erased, and the Mavericks were constantly forced into kick-outs or difficult attempts because Wembanyama was waiting around the rim.

Dallas did a much better job neutralizing him in the second half by playing smaller, speeding up the game, and making him defend in space, but he still found ways to dominate. He hit a huge three in the fourth, finished possessions on the glass, and scored the free throw that ultimately gave San Antonio the winning point. This was one of those games where you walk away understanding exactly why Wembanyama changes everything. Dallas nearly stole it anyway, but for most of the night, he completely dictated what the Mavericks were allowed to do.
