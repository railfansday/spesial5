Slow Horses has been renewed well into the future

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Featured image credit:  Jack English/Apple TV+ via Rolling Stone

Yes. Slow Horses, the wonderful television series based on Mick Herron’s wonderful Slough House books series has been renewed. Season 6 just aired this fall, on its home network of Apple TV+, and work on the 7th season has begun.

But the series has been renewed even further than that. When Season 6 aired, we learned that the show had been renewed for an 8th season. This was great news.

And now, Apple has announced that the series will continue through Season 10! Yes, Seasons 9 and 10 have been announced! This means we have four more seasons to look forward to. That’s four more years of Jackson Lamb and his crew of misfits. That’s a whole American presidential term’s worth of Slow Horses.

Until Season 6, each season of the show has adapted a single one of Heron’s novels, all in chronological order. Season 6 broke the mold by adapting two: the sequential volumes Joe Country and Slough House. This means there are two extant novels left to adapt, Bad Actors and Clown Town, and early reports have confirmed that seasons 7 and 8 will undertake each of those novels, respectively. This means that Heron’s next novel will also be guaranteed a season, as will the next.

Will he indeed write more? Yes, it certainly seems so, though there have been no announcements about a new installment. The most recent novel, Clown Town, was released in 2025, and Heron’s novels typically have a 1-3 year gap in between them. But there’s still plenty of relevant Slough House material rife for adaptation; Heron has also published five Slough House novellas, none of which have been adapted yet. (Interested readers should note that all the novellas thus far have been collected in a volume called Standing By the Wall.)

The series Slow Horses stars Gary Oldman as the irascible, perpetually-disheveled, foul-mouthed, flatulent and yet thoroughly lovable MI5 agent Jackson Lamb, who oversees a notorious departmental backwater of the Secret Service called Slough House, an institutional repository for agents who have badly messed up (or pissed off somebody important). Time and time again Lamb and his team of oddballs, including Jack Lowden’s put-in-Slough-House-by-sabotage River Cartwright, wind up using their special skills, disregard for the rules, and occasional desire to prove themselves/not be messed with to save the day, often. It’s the best.
