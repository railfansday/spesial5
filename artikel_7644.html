My New Book Chapter on How to Curb Dangerous Emergency Powers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Now avaialble on SSRN. It is coauthored with Josh Braver, and will be published in the forthcoming Cato Handbook on Executive Power.

My forthcoming chapter on "Emergency Powers" is now available on SSRN. It is coauthored with Josh Braver (University of Wisconsin), and will be published in the forthcoming Cato Handbook on Executive Power, edited by Molly Nixon. Braver is a leading expert on the law of domestic use of the military and a liberal/progressive. I am a a libertarian. If we can come to agreement on these issues, I hope others can, as well. Here is the abstract:

Emergency powers are special types of government authorities that are only supposed to be used under extraordinary circumstances, in order to address a crisis. But, over time, many have become institutionalized as near-blank checks that the executive can invoke at will. This situation imperils civil liberties and our constitutional system.

This chapter goes over the major US emergency powers, including the National Emergencies Act, the Insurrection Act, the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), and others, and offers proposals for curbing the dangers they create. Effective reform requires a combination of new legislation and strong, nondeferential judicial review.

I recently covered related issues in an article for Just Security, which goes over potential legislative reforms. In the Cato Handbook chapter, Braver and I cover both legislative reforms and how judicial review can be strengthened.
