Can web users trust ad tech’s server-side shift?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Join us Dec. 1-3 in Nashville for the Digiday Programmatic Marketing Summit

“Trust” is one of those words that gets thrown around a lot in ad tech circles. But it’s going to become even more important if ad tech completes its server-side shift.

In response to Apple’s identity resolution crackdown, the online ad industry has rallied around server-side ways to sidestep browser-based controls. IAB Tech Lab’s Trusted Server is the biggest example of this. Not only does the open-source software promise publishers faster page-load speeds, it enables publishers to better control their ad tech stacks and minimize their exposure to browser-based blockades.

Taking ad calls out of the browser, however, has the effect of taking them out of users’ sights. A browser can see – and block – the calls a web page asks it to make. Once the calls run on a publisher’s server, though, users have to trust the publisher to a greater degree.

And this is where the trust in Trusted Server really matters.

Lam also noted that Trusted Server was designed from the start to support integration with consent management platforms. That would give websites a way to share a user’s consent status across the programmatic supply chain. But of course, that requires the user blindly trusting the site to do so.

Here are a few highlights from the conversation, which have been edited for length and clarity.

“There is a fear of just broadly server-side because it’s not automatically observable to the consumer’s browser and you can’t automatically inspect it from that point. But what we’ve done for Trusted Server in particular is we’ve made sure all of this is considered. What’s included in Trusted Server is pretty robust logging and auditing. Anyone who is looking to deploy Trusted Server gets that out of the box.”
