Binance said on Monday, November 16, that it will pass the next Microsoft and Applied Materials cash dividends to holders of its tokenized stocks, MSFTB and AMATB. It will snapshot balances on Thursday, November 19, the same day both stocks go ex-dividend.

Tokenized stocks are crypto tokens that track a real share, held by a custodian, and trade around the clock. Binance’s versions, called bStocks, do not pay the dividend in cash. Instead, the net amount is reinvested, and each holder’s token balance grows slightly.

We compared both tokens with the real shares since late August. During market hours, they tracked closely. Over weekends, their price called Monday’s opening move only half the time.

Here is what holders get, and what the token prices tell traders this week.

Both companies declared their payouts in September. Microsoft will pay $0.98 a share, up from $0.91. Applied Materials, a maker of chipmaking equipment, will pay $0.53 a share. Both go to shareholders of record on November 19 and are paid on December 10.

A dividend only matters if the token tracks the stock. So we compared both tokens with the real shares, using Binance hourly candles and Nasdaq prices since late August.

During market hours, the match is close. At the Nasdaq close, the tokens sat at a median 0.06% from the official closing price across 58 sessions. The widest gap was 0.28%.

Weekends are different. The tokens keep trading while Wall Street is shut, so we checked whether their weekend price predicted Monday’s opening move.

Thin trading in tokenized stocks explains part of it. MSFTB traded about $183,000 in the past 24 hours on Binance, and AMATB about $114,000. This weekend, AMATB stood 1.8% below Friday’s $370.50 close on Sunday night, while MSFTB was down 0.2%. On our numbers, those weekend quotes said little about where the stocks would open.

On the 4-hour chart, where each candle covers four hours, MSFTB trades at $527.61 on Binance. It sits just above its 50-period exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $526.02, and its 200-period EMA at $526.95. With both averages flat and close together, the trend is neutral.

For holders of tokenized stocks, the dividend adds about a tenth of a percent to their balance this quarter. The bigger lesson is in timing: on weekdays, the tokens follow the stock, but weekend prices are a weak guide to Monday.
