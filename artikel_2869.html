Kings hope Keegan Murray's late-game heroics vs. Celtics can get him on track

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Kings hope Keegan Murray's late-game heroics vs. Celtics can get him on track originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SACRAMENTO — For a night, Keegan Murray finally has something to build off of.

The Kings forward's early season has been marred by overcoming an ankle injury stemming back to last year's frustrating 22-win finish. The one-time sharp shooter came into Saturday's game hitting a paltry 25 percent of his threes.

And after signing a five-year, $140 million contract last fall, Murray has been tabbed either a building block for the Kings or an expensive player for another team to acquire. Both of which raise expectations and add pressure to the shoulders of the quiet 26-year-old who should be entering his NBA prime.

All of which made Murray's primal scream after forcing a Payton Pritchard airball as time expired Saturday night notable.

Murray clamped the Boston Celtics point guard as he tried to make a step-back game winner. It came after Murray leaped over his former teammate Devin Carter to secure an offensive rebound and game-winning put back with 5.0 seconds remaining, giving Sacramento the ugly 97-96 victory, its second win of the season and first at Golden 1 Center.

It ended a miserable nine-game home winless streak to begin the year, the longest run in franchise history. It moved the Kings record to 2-14.

But more importantly for Murray, it might serve as rare signature moment. The Kings desperately need Murray to look like the player they envisioned him becoming if they're going to trend back towards respectability.

His 3-point shooting has declined in every season since his standout first year when he set a then-NBA rookie record with 206 made 3-pointers while shooting 41 percent from deep. He hasn't shot above 34 percent from 3-point range since 2024-25, and shot 28 percent from distance last season while playing just 23 games due to injury.

He started the year missing the first 15 games after suffering a torn ulnar collateral ligament in his left thumb. Then he suffered multiple ankle injures that led to two prolonged absences, including the final 22 games of the season. Murray said he had a setback with the ankle during the offseason and already has missed seven games this season because of it.

Saturday marked his seventh straight game played, his longest streak since last December.

"To see him go through all the things that he's gone through and to come out on the other side of that with some success is really a shining moment for him and for us, to be quite frank, because we all love him and want him to succeed," said embattled Kings coach Doug Christie.

Murray finished the game with 15 points and a season-high 12 rebounds (his previous high was seven he reached three times).

His defense also proved helpful while Celtics star Paul George shot just 6 of 21 and Pritchard made 4 of 17. George had 19 points while Pritchard finished with just nine. They shot just 34 percent as a team.

Boston was without star Jayson Tatum, who was given the night off to rest while the Celtics were playing the first night of a back to back. Guard Derrick White was out with a shoulder injury. They travel to San Francisco to play the Golden State Warriors on Sunday.

Darius Acuff Jr. led the Kings with 21 points on an inefficient 7-of-23 shooting. And while building a foundation around Acuff is imperative for Sacramento over the next few seasons, Murray's future is a significant variable. It's unclear whether Murray will stick around or be moved as part of the team's ongoing rebuilding project if the right trade comes along.

"He's stayed himself throughout the whole way," Acuff said of Murray. "He never changed. He was always bought in, never had bad energy. Brought energy every day no matter what the circumstances."

Murray, of course, speaks in monotone and is known for keeping to himself. He wasn't made available to speak to reporters after the game despite being one of the biggest stories of the night while the Kings earned a much needed win.

Shying from the spotlight is entirely unlike him. But that's the push and pull with having Murray on your team. His level of aggression has been a point of emphasis throughout his time in the league after the Kings selected him No. 4 overall in the 2022 NBA Draft.

He often has faded into the background, but the Kings are now paying him to be a game changer, and Saturday was the best example of the young season.

"It's never his fault," Zach LaVine said of Murray. "He's had to deal with a lot of stuff, coming back (from injury), playing with minutes restrictions, trying to get his groove back, get hurt again, that's tough.

"I've been injured in that situation. You don't feel like yourself sometimes. It takes time. … But he sticks with it. I see him every morning lifting, running, shooting. He's very consistent with his work, the stuff that nobody else sees, and he continues to do it regardless of what he gets paid."
