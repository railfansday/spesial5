Raptors seek end to skid against faultering Wizards

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Toronto Raptors have struggled through their last stretch of games. Dropping games to Memphis, Boston, Philly, and New York twice has resulted in a five game skid that contributes to a forming pattern of the Raptors' inability to beat good teams this season.

Luckily for Toronto, tonight could be a good respite from this as they face the 4-11 Washington Wizards who have just dropped eight straight games.

In their first matchup of the season, Toronto handled Washington easily, coming away with a 112-102 victory, led by Scottie Barnes who had a 28-point, 14-rebound double-double. Washington's best performer was Trae Young who had a 22-point, 12 assist double-double of his own, but didn't have enough help around him to give the Raptors any trouble.

It wasn't a pretty victory either. The Raptors shot a mere 21% from the field, with RJ, Scottie, and IQ combining for an abysmal 2-17 from long range. They also had 16 turnovers, with Scottie personally committing 8 of those. In most situations, those kind of numbers would likely mean a loss, but Toronto managed to beat the Wizards in other ways, which they will have to do again to get out of this slump.

Toronto dominated the boards in their first contest, outrebounding Washington 53-40. Removing second chance opportunities goes a long way to building some separation. Even more helpful though were the 19 of those that were offensive rebounds, creating their own second chance opportunities to prevent empty possessions.

When you're making mistakes and turning the ball over, you need to make sure you are putting in that much more effort on the defensive end to try and win the possession back. Collin Murray-Boyles, who has been having a phenomenal season so far had 3 steals, as did Kawhi, helping contribute to the 12 steals and 6 blocks the Raptors had, outnumbering the turnovers they committed.

Despite their losing record, Toronto has shown a lot of growth this season. Without Kawhi in the lineup, they've given championship calibre teams a run for their money. Their youth has been a disadvantage, but the team sees all of these games as learning opportunities that they can look back on and use to get better.

The progress they've shown in the 12 games between their first matchup with Washington and now cannot be understated. Their shooting, while still not great, has shown improvement. Even if you only consider the last five games, all of which Toronto dropped (and three of those they played without Scottie), they had fewer turnovers, better shooting splits, and more assists in all of those games.

They're moving the ball better, playing as a team better, and shooting better. Of course Washington has had an opportunity to get better too, but this should be a good litmus test to see how the Raptors have grown so far this season.

The last time around, neither Anthony Davis nor Trae Young had particularly strong performances. Young shot 33% from the field and turned the ball over four times. Davis only managed nine points, by far his worst performance of the season thus far. While uncharacteristic for them, Toronto will need to force similar performances for them again. Murray-Boyles has a great opportunity here to shut Davis down and Shead could be a pesky and intriguing pairing against Young to slow him down, although Toronto's defence is best when they are working as a unit.

With any luck, they'll be able to move back into the win column, with a little boost in morale before they head to Detroit for another tough matchup.

Toronto: Scottie Barnes, RJ Barrett, Immanuel Quickley, Collin Murray-Boyles, Ja'Kobe Walter

Washington: Anthony Davis, Alex Sarr, Kyshawn George, AJ Dybantsa, Trae Young

Toronto: Kawhi Leonard, Nate Bittle, Kevin McCullar Jr.

Washington: Reece Beekman, Justin Champagnie, Felix Okpara, Tristan Vukcevic, Jamir Watkins
