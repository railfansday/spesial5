Day 33: The Most Present Moment

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

I woke up early, wanting to get a jump-start on the day, and took all my things to the common room with the fireplace. As I sat by the stove, diving into my journal, the legend of the property walked in. Though we didn’t speak the same language, Jesús’s precious smile and the way he moved around setting up for breakfast brought me so much joy.

This is the type of unconditional service that the Camino was teaching me.

And with the inspiration of that, I began walking before 6.

The climb out of that town was so steep, but the view overlooking it? Priceless.

Walking on the mountain ridges felt like a communion. The peaks and valleys surrounding me were visible in different shades of black and gray depending on their distance. The bright moon illuminated my path, casting shadows from the loose rocks so that I didn’t hurt myself. The stillness held a presence, like the mountains were embracing me in the same way they held so many pilgrims before. This is where I felt the divine mother most potently.

I got to an overlook and it was starting to get light on the horizon. This was the view I wanted for the Cruz del Ferro sunrise.

I sat my phone up for a time-lapse video and was forced to be completely present.

My habit as a writer is to have my phone out in moments like this, attempting to capture the feeling with words.

Words will never capture what I felt in that hour of watching the sunrise.

I sang to the sky and the majesty of the Creator who made all of it: the scene before me, my life, the opportunities I got to experience.

I cried in gratitude, finally able to express the emotions I was holding.

That image will be burned into the depths of my mind for the rest of my life.

Eventually, after a bit more walking, my Argentinian brother was the first person I saw all day. He was someone who could appreciate what that sunrise meant to me, and he was exactly the person I wanted to share the rest of that morning with.

We talked in Spanish about dreams and ones I’ve had that have changed my life. Those types of conversations with him, and how he teaches me by communicating challenging concepts, have helped me improve my speaking so much.

After we finally made it to the normal route and got off the mountain, we sat on a bench to take some layers off. He pulled out a bag of fruits that he gathered from the wild, like he was a well-trained forager.

Pears. Red apples. Multiple fruits I can’t pronounce.

They were unbelievably fresh and unique compared to ones at the store.

As if we were exuding some energy, two different groups of pilgrims stopped to video us or get a picture with us. We didn’t know them at all. It felt like paparazzi, and I think we both secretly liked it.

We walked and talked about having roughly ten days left and how much we would miss each other. This was a topic of conversation I’d had with both brothers independently, but the feeling was mutual among the three of us.

At the halfway point of the day, we stopped at a cafe for lunch. The news was blaring with something that neither of us wanted to see. We shared our meal outside, soaking up the sun and watching the fall leaves glisten while we talked about everything under the sun, all in Spanish.

We agreed to walk independently after our meal, so I went on ahead.

It was all road walking, but I didn’t mind it. The road had minimal cars, and the terrain went back and forth between a cow pen and a mountain village. Each was precious in its own way, even if my legs tried to detach me with their soreness.

Then started the toughest climb since day one.

I stopped at a bar long enough to chug a Red Bull and an Aquarius, and to say hi to the baby goat in the pen.

Then, right before town, I finally crested the ridge. And there, I saw it—the sign marking the entrance to the region I’ve long awaited:

The view had me breathless, along with the two long hours of climbing. It was so green, so lush, so rich in beauty.

And at last, I reached the town of O Cebreiro.

It’s like I’d been transported back in time. The gray stone buildings, Celtic symbols, and bagpipe music coming from inside felt like a traditional greeting from a time long ago.

I checked into my albergue, Xunta de Galicia O Cebreiro Pilgrims Hostel, then immediately went into the town to explore the two stores and two restaurants.

I met with the American dads and my Italian brother for burgers, but when we could see the colorful sky through the window, we ran to the sunset. The town was on a mountain peak, so no matter which side we went to, there were fantastic views.

The specific spot with the best views showcased the same exact neon colors I’d gotten to witness in the morning. I turned around to look for the dads and my brother, and all I saw were three paparazzi behind me. They were all capturing me with the sunset without me even having to ask.

From being solo at the sunrise to being surrounded by my people at sunset, today was one of my favorite days on the Camino.

I lay on my bed, exhausted from a full day of joy. A wave of grief hit me as I realized that the end was rapidly upon us. I sought out both of my brothers and asked for a hug. My heart hurt at how much I was going to miss these people who have become my family.
