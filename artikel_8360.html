A job with housing can be worth more than its pay suggests. It can wipe out your rent, and lodging you must accept on the work site can be tax-free. Before you take one, ask what the room costs you, how many hours you’ll be on call, and where you’d live after it ends.

Some of these jobs house you for free, and others rent you a room and take the cost out of each paycheck, so the first thing to learn about an offer is which kind it is.

The free kind can also be tax-free. Under IRS Publication 15-B, lodging stays out of your taxable wages when it’s on the employer’s premises, the employer provides it for a real business reason, and the job requires you to live there. A superintendent who has to live in the building can pass that test, while an apartment handed out as extra pay can’t.

The catch is the same in every job below. The home ends with the job, so a layoff, a firing, or the end of a season also means a move.

In these jobs, you live where you work. House-sitting trades care of someone’s home for a free place to stay while you travel, so it’s lodging rather than a job.

Housing here lasts as long as the season, the contract, or the tour.

These jobs come with housing by policy, though not always for free.

Teaching English abroad without a teaching license is a separate route, with housing deals of its own.

Put a number on the housing before you compare offers. Look up the rent on a similar place nearby, subtract whatever the employer deducts, and add the difference to the pay.

Then get the terms in writing: what you pay for the room, who can live with you, and how many days you have to move out if the job ends.
