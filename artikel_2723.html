Preview: Nets look to get on track against a Sixers team without LeBron

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

It's been rough for Brooklyn, and on Monday night, they'll have unwelcoming division foe at Barclays Center.

Jaylen Brown and the new-look Philadelphia 76ers are coming to Brooklyn, and with the Nets sitting at 3-9, there could be plenty of opposing fans making themselves at home in the stands. If you were looking for a reason to watch LeBron James, then you're out of luck. He's out of this one due to low back/sciatica management — it's also the first night of a back-to-back for Philly.

The Nets return home following a 129-110 loss to the Memphis Grizzlies on Saturday, dropping to 3-9. Meanwhile, Philadelphia enters at 11-3, riding a much different wave of momentum. If recent history tells us anything, the Sixers have enjoyed their trips to Brooklyn — they're 6-2 in the last eight games at Barclays Center.

Who: Philadelphia 76ers (11-3) at Brooklyn Nets (3-9)

Let's start with the obvious. The Nets haven't exactly had Philadelphia's number lately, dropping three of four meetings last season, including both games at Barclays Center. Not exactly fond memories for the Brooklyn faithful.

Unfortunately for Brooklyn, this version of the Sixers isn't looking any easier.

Philadelphia is coming off a 131-124 victory over Indiana on Sunday, with Jaylen Brown scoring 31 points and Tyrese Maxey adding 27. LeBron James contributed 15 points and 11 assists, passing former Net Jason Kidd for third on the NBA's all-time assists list. Not bad for a guy who's been in the league longer than some of Brooklyn's players have been alive.

The Nets, meanwhile, are hoping to bounce back from Saturday's loss to Memphis. Julius Randle led Brooklyn with 22 points, while Day'Ron Sharpe added 16 points and 11 rebounds.

The Grizzlies jumped out to a 38-23 lead after the opening quarter Saturday, leaving the Nets playing catch-up for most of the night. Against a Philadelphia team with this much offensive firepower, that's a dangerous habit. For Brooklyn to have a chance Monday, it'll need to bring more urgency from the opening tip, take better care of the basketball, and make Philadelphia work defensively.

The one advantage? The Sixers are playing their second game in as many nights. Maybe the Nets can take advantage.

And some late news: Josh Minott is indeed frustrated about his playing time. On Saturday, he threw up an obscene gesture at the Smoothie King Center which the NBA caught on camera and on Monday fined him $25,000.

We originally had LeBron James, but he was deemed out in the afternoon. But seriously, who else were we going to pick? The 'King' has been making life miserable for the Nets for more than two decades, dating all the way back to their days in New Jersey, averaging 27.6 points, 7.2 rebounds, and 7.6 assists against the franchise, compiling a 41-23 record.

As for Jaylen Brown, he's averaging 21.9 points per game — his lowest since he was 23 year old during the 2019-20 season. Context is key here, though. The Sixers are stacked with talent, so much to the point that James is crusing at 41 years old and Joel Embiid is far from his prime self, yet the Sixers are still 11-3.

Headlines surrounding Brown have been all about Jayson Tatum, but he's quietly having one of his most efficient season ever, shooting 49.5% from the field (2nd-best in his career) and 38.7% from three (3rd-best in his career). The 2023-24 Finals MVP is an asset no matter where he goes, no matter who he's surrounded by. He propelled a Celtics team who didn't have Jayson Tatum all last season. Now, he's bought into what Philly's provides, which varies depending on the night.

With James out and Embiid questionable, expect him to try and get hot early. Maybe there's added incentive against the Nets.
