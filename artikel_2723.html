The NFL season is always chock-full of surprises and disappointments. Despite a half-year of ranking, projecting and predicting how the year will go from fans, media and league personnel alike, the bull's-eye on the dartboard that we call a typical NFL season is nigh impossible to hit. And pulling a Luke Littler on those predictions is essentially unfathomable.

Now that we're past the halfway point in the season (enjoy those sloppy island games while you can!), I wanted to highlight a few of the most pleasant surprises, along with a few disappointments that have left us with a sour taste in our mouths a week before Thanksgiving.

With apologies to units like the Vikings' defense, Chiefs' offense or others at the top of the boards, I narrowed this down to units whose expectations weren't that high, or that fell way below their projected status. Yahoo Sports surveyed high-ranking league sources to better understand the debates from recent annual meetings as well as the most likely factors to motivate any changes.

Sources were split on whether they wanted any shift to the structure — and how that shift should materialize if it does unfold.

One high-ranking NFC executive who favors revamping the playoff structure was skeptical anything will change in the next five to 10 years, even if the schedule expands to 18 games.
