Charlie Villanueva, Raymond Felton debate Wemby vs. Michael Jordan scenario, because why not?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The debate over the NBA's greatest players has taken another unexpected turn, with former players Charlie Villanueva and Raymond Felton weighing in on a hypothetical choice between Victor Wembanyama and Michael Jordan. Their discussion focused on which superstar a team should choose based on the first four seasons of their careers.

On their To The Baha podcast, Villanueva and Felton shared opposing views on the Wembanyama-Jordan comparison. Villanueva argued that Wembanyama's early career deserves a direct comparison with Jordan's, asking, "And don't say MJ because it's no way you're picking MJ, MJ didn't win until year what?" Felton immediately challenged that argument, pointing to Jordan's scoring numbers and Wembanyama's lack of a championship. "Michael Jordan averaged 35 points. Look at his numbers. Did Wemby win? I don't care. He didn't win," Felton said.

Jordan's fourth NBA season in 1987-88 was remarkable. He averaged 35.0 points, 5.5 rebounds and 5.9 assists while winning the regular-season MVP and Defensive Player of the Year awards. Wembanyama, meanwhile, is averaging 30.3 points, 11.7 rebounds and 2.8 assists through 12 games in his fourth season.

However, Jordan had yet to win an NBA championship at that point. He captured his first title with the Chicago Bulls in 1991, during his seventh season. That timeline supports Villanueva's argument about judging players by their early-career production rather than their eventual championships.

Felton, meanwhile, puts greater emphasis on Michael Jordan's scoring dominance and individual accomplishments. The debate highlights two different ways of evaluating a player's career: individual production and team success. Victor Wembanyama still has time to build his legacy, but Jordan's fourth-season achievements give Felton a compelling case.
