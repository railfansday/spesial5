Scotland’s miserable night shows Sebastien Pocognoli is already running out of time

On Wednesday, 18 November, it will be a year to the day since one of the greatest nights in Scotland’s history: the 4-2 win over Denmark that sensationally sealed qualification to the following summer’s World Cup. The BBC will be celebrating by showing a full match replay on Tuesday and many Scotland supporters who tuned into see a wretched end to their Nations League campaign on Monday evening will have wished they had just waited to watch that instead.

How dramatically Scotland’s fortunes have fallen in the 12 months since, as a miserable 4-0 defeat to a second-string Switzerland side with nothing to play for raised serious questions about the new era under Sebastien Pocognoli. It remains early days –  there have only been six games since the departure of Steve Clarke – but Pocognoli has overseen just two wins, both of which came against North Macedonia. There has been little progress under the Belgian, and the complete absence of a statement result.

On what was a historic night for Andy Robertson, the record-breaking captain offered a damning assessment of their work so far in implementing Pocognoli’s new approach. “I don't think you've got time in international football,” he said. “Are they supporters that have travelled everywhere going to give us time? No. The people inside Hampden, are they going to give us time? No. Are the people outside Hampden that are going to be critical, and rightly so, are they going to give us time? No.”

There were some bright signs in the first half in St Gallen, but then Scotland were the side with something on the line as they chased a victory that could have given them an outside chance of reaching a Nations League promotion play-off. Switzerland, by contrast, were already through as group winners. The World Cup quarter-finalists were missing many of their first-choice team but a much-changed, inexperienced side remained cohesive and took a first-half lead through Lewis Ferguson’s own goal.

Turning into his own net was a horrible moment for Ferguson, but it should not have been a disaster for Scotland. They had time at the break to turn it around. But what was perhaps the most alarming aspect of their surrender to the Swiss was how sloppy they were at the start of the second half, and the way their heads dropped after conceding twice in five minutes following the restart. It did not reflect well on Pocognoli that his players looked as if their confidence was on the floor.

It was notable, also, that Pocognoli appeared to snap at a reporter in response to a gentle pre-match questioning of his tactics and 3-4-3 formation. He wants his team to be front-footed and take the game to their opponents by pushing high up the pitch, but it’s clear for all to see that Scotland are struggling to combine that with being creative from open play and retaining defensive solidity. The mistakes in possession and at the back, once they went behind, suggested a lack of belief in the system they are trying to play. Scott McTominay has been anonymous playing as one of two No 10s, next to John McGinn, in his two appearances under Pocognoli.

Scotland had these issues too under Clarke, increasingly towards the end of his tenure and especially at the World Cup, but in qualifying or the Nations League there was always the sense that the could rise to a challenge when they were being doubted. Scotland may have missed out on the Nations League play-offs even if they had won at Switzerland – as it happens, Slovenia won 3-0 at North Macedonia anyway – but beating even an inexperienced Switzerland side would have been a boost. Collapsing in the manner they did could be even more damaging.

As if to stress Robertson’s point, the Euro 2028 qualifying draw takes place at the start of the December, ahead of potentially staging the tournament on home soil 18 months later. Finishing the Nations League in third place in League B means Scotland will be stuck in Pot 3 of the draw. A potential four-team group could see them land next to Spain and Sweden and with just two automatic qualifying spots available.

And while there are two qualification spaces left in reserve, should any of the co-hosts fail to qualify automatically, there are only two, and they will go to the teams with the best qualifying record: Scotland could be left praying for England, Wales and the Republic of Ireland to coast through.

Should they land in a five-team qualifying group, Scotland could be playing their Euro 2028 qualifiers by as early as the next international break in March. The Nations League carried ramifications but the series of six games across September, October and November where the time for Pocognoli to settle in. Now, there are just more questions than answers, and the clock towards the home Euros ticking while a sense of apathy among the Tartan Army grows.

In that context, Robertson’s message felt even more pertinent. “We've not got time,” he stressed. “The players don't have time. We have to step up. Now we've got a long break until March, which is not a good thing. We really need to step up again because in international football, you don't have time.”
