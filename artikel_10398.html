Healthy futures start early

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

If you ask anyone what they want for their children, you’ll likely hear one of the same answers continue to pop up: good health.

However, childhood health outcomes are heading in the wrong direction. About one in five children and adolescents in the U.S. has obesity, and recent research published in JAMA found that several measures of children’s health have declined since 2007, including sleep health, mental health, and chronic physical conditions.

These trends have consequences that extend well beyond childhood. Research shows that many of the chronic diseases driving healthcare costs, straining employers, and reducing workforce productivity are influenced by habits and routines established during early childhood. These conditions include diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and hypertension. They also affect working families today, as parents navigate the challenges of supporting a child’s health while balancing work responsibilities.

If we want healthier adults and a more productive workforce tomorrow, we need to invest in healthier habits for children today.

A POWERFUL PREVENTION OPPORTUNITY

Organizations across healthcare, education, government, and the nonprofit sector have long recognized the importance of helping children live healthier lives. There are many longstanding health programs for elementary and high school-age children, such as Action for Healthy Kids and Abbott’s own Future Well Kids, along with federally supported programs such as Bright Futures. All are working toward the same goal.

While school-age health is important, experts increasingly identify early childhood, as young as three years old, as a critical window for development. This is when habits begin to take shape and can influence health outcomes for years to come. By the time children start kindergarten, many of the routines that shape their daily lives are already established.

That thinking led to Abbott’s collaboration with Sesame Workshop. By combining health expertise with a trusted voice in early childhood education, the shared effort aims to reach more families with practical tools and resources that can help make healthy habits easier to build and maintain from an early age.

For young children, healthy routines around nutrition, movement, and sleep may feel more approachable and fun when coming from a beloved character like Elmo rather than another reminder from an adult—including lessons about trying a new food or developing a bedtime routine.

To reach families with young children, it’s important to meet them where they are with tools they can seamlessly integrate into their everyday lives.

MAKE HEALTHY HABITS WORK FOR REAL LIFE

Most families are already aware that healthy habits matter. They understand that nutrition, movement, and sleep play an important role in lifelong well-being. The harder task is turning that knowledge into action for working parents amid work schedules, caregiving responsibilities, financial pressures, and the competing demands of family life.

A recent survey we conducted with Kantar earlier this year reflects this challenge. Nearly all of the 2,000 parents surveyed said childhood habits influence lifelong health, yet many also reported feeling significant pressure to model healthy behaviors while experiencing difficulty doing so consistently.

This gap between knowing and doing is one of the most persistent challenges in public health.

Information is essential, but it is rarely enough on its own. Lasting behavior change happens when healthy choices are convenient, accessible, and realistic for families.

INVEST IN THE NEXT GENERATION

That’s why successful interventions focus less on perfection and more on building small, sustainable habits that fit into everyday routines. The opportunity now is to bring businesses, nonprofits, educators, and families together to make health a natural part of the programs and spaces that already support young children.

Supporting children’s health is an investment in stronger communities, a healthier future workforce, and better long-term outcomes. Organizations that help remove barriers for families can contribute to healthier generations for years to come.

Every small step taken together can help set the foundation for lifelong health and reverse the rise of chronic disease.

Melissa Brotz is senior vice president of global marketing and external affairs for Abbott, and president of Abbott Fund.
