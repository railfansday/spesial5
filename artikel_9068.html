Cézanne ‘Arlequin’ Sells for $111 M. at Sotheby’s, Becoming One of the Most Expensive Paintings by the Artist

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Paul Cézanne made his name painting apples and mountains. But on Wednesday night, the artist’s somewhat severe picture of a teenager in costume became one of the most expensive Cézannes ever sold at auction, after selling at Sotheby’s New York for $111 million with fees.

Arlequin (1888–90), a colorful painting of a young man in a red-and-black diamond-patterned costume, hit the block at Sotheby’s during its dedicated evening sale for the Blaquier-Arrieta collection. It failed to beat the previous record of $137.8 million set by La Montagne Sainte-Victoire (1888–90), which sold at Christie’s in 2022 as part of Microsoft cofounder Paul Allen‘s collection. Still, once auctioneer Oliver Barker sold Arlequin tonight, a round of applause followed.

Sotheby’s didn’t publish an estimate for Arlequin prior to the sale, though the painting was expected to bring in around $120 million.

The figure in the painting is Cézanne’s son, Paul, dressed as Harlequin, the mischievous character from Italian commedia dell’arte theater. He wears a pointed hat and an elaborate white collar, and holds a slender baton as he walks across a sharply tilted floor. For someone dressed as a comic character, he looks surprisingly serious.

Cézanne painted the picture in Paris between 1888 and 1890, when he was experimenting with a series of theatrical subjects. There are only four paintings in the group, and Arlequin is the only one still in private hands. The others are in museums, including the National Gallery of Art in Washington, D.C., the Pola Museum of Art in Japan, and the Pushkin Museum in Moscow.

The painting comes with quite a history. It passed through the hands of several of the great dealers of the early modern art market, including Ambroise Vollard and Paul Rosenberg, before become part of Lord Victor Rothschild’s collection during the 1930s. His son, Lord Jacob Rothschild, later inherited it, and in 1975 it was sold through London’s Lefevre Gallery to Argentine collectors Carlos Pedro Blaquier and Nelly Arrieta de Blaquier.

Before leaving for Argentina, the painting appeared in major exhibitions at the Museum of Modern Art in New York in 1929, the Metropolitan Museum of Art in 1952, and the Tate Gallery in London in 1954. After the Blaquiers bought it, it all but disappeared from public view for half a century.

Sotheby’s had guaranteed the painting before Wednesday’s auction, promising the sellers an undisclosed amount regardless of the outcome. It also secured an irrevocable bid, meaning at least one prospective buyer had committed to bidding before the auction began.

Such arrangements are increasingly common at the top of the art market. According to an analysis by the art-market data firm ARTDAI, just 19 guaranteed Cézanne lots make up 63 percent of the hammer-price value in its database of 640 Cézanne sales. In other words, a small number of exceptionally expensive pictures drive much of the artist’s auction business.

The record-setting Cézanne, La Montagne Sainte-Victoire, hammered at $120 million in 2022, more than twice the price achieved by the artist’s next most expensive auction result, a still life that fetched $60.5 million at Sotheby’s in 1999.

Of course, that auction record doesn’t tell the whole story. A version of Cézanne’s The Card Players was reportedly sold privately to Qatar for around $250 million in 2011, a reminder that some of the biggest deals in the art world never reach a public salesroom.

Arlequin was one of 12 paintings offered from the Blaquier-Arrieta collection on Wednesday, alongside Vincent van Gogh’s Châtaigniers en fleurs (1890), which had been expected to fetch around $180 million and ended up selling for $190.4 million.
