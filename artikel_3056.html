3 Beaten-Down Stocks That Could Soar in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Each of these stocks has some big potential catalysts.

It's easy to find stocks that have fallen sharply. But the challenge is finding beaten-down stocks poised to bounce back. One important lesson all investors should learn is that a lower share price alone doesn't make a stock a bargain. Plenty of stocks that fall never recover.

When evaluating stocks that have declined, I look for specific catalysts that could potentially take the business to the next level, and the stock price with it. With that in mind, here are three stocks well below their highs with identifiable catalysts that could make 2027 a big year, from expanding profit margins to a record backlog to a recently closed merger.

SoFi Technologies (SOFI -1.34%) trades more than 50% below its 52-week high, even though its adjusted net revenue grew 44% last quarter.

SoFi's growth story has been extremely impressive for years, and through the first three quarters of 2026, it has been its most impressive yet. But the catalyst I'm watching in 2027 is profitability. SoFi's adjusted EBITDA margin reached 34% in the third quarter, and adjusted EBITDA grew 70%, outpacing revenue growth. As a bank, SoFi has many fixed costs, so each new member it adds should be more profitable than the last, which is especially true as the bank's cross-selling continues to dramatically improve over time.

Management's early outlook for 2027 calls for about $6.4 billion of adjusted net revenue and earnings of about $0.90 per share, up from roughly $0.61 expected this year. If it delivers, profits would grow much faster than sales. SoFi's stock trades for about 17 times 2027's expected EPS, which could be a bargain for patient long-term investors.

Forgent Power Solutions (FPS -5.15%) went public in February, reached a peak of $66 per share, and now trades for less than $22. To put it mildly, this stock performance doesn't reflect how quickly Forgent's business is growing.

Forgent makes electrical equipment that data centers need to manage power. In its fiscal first quarter, revenue rose 69% year over year to a record $479 million, and operating cash flow jumped to $136 million from just $6 million a year earlier. Management also raised its fiscal 2027 revenue forecast to $2.45 billion to $2.65 billion, representing about 80% growth, and says its backlog already fully covers that amount.

In other words, most of next year's growth is already on the books. The stock trades for about 16 times expected adjusted earnings, a reasonable price for this kind of visibility. It's also worth noting that management says Forgent has the manufacturing capacity to support up to $5 billion in annual revenue, roughly double what it expects next year, so it shouldn't need to spend heavily to keep growing.

To be fair, there are some valid reasons why the stock is under pressure. Recently, Forgent's CFO unexpectedly left the company. And there are legitimate concerns that a slowdown in the AI build-out could negatively impact AI infrastructure companies like Forgent. But for patient, long-term investors, Forgent could be a bargain at these levels.

Homebuilder Dream Finders Homes (DFH +2.85%), which I also own, trades far below its high. On Oct. 20, it closed its all-cash acquisition of Beazer Homes, valued at about $2.2 billion, including debt, creating the sixth-largest homebuilder in the U.S.

That makes 2027 the first full year as a combined company. Management expects more than $100 million in annual cost savings and says the deal will boost earnings per share by a double-digit percentage in its first year. The third quarter was a reminder that it won't be easy, though. Gross margin fell to 14.9% from 17.5% a year earlier, and CEO Patrick Zalupski said margins "remain under pressure."

To be sure, the real estate market is agonizingly weak right now. Homebuilders are sitting on more unsold homes in inventory than at any point since the financial crisis in 2008-09. Stubbornly high mortgage rates and overall economic uncertainty are major reasons why. However, if housing demand recovers in 2027, the larger company should have much greater earnings power. Dream Finders is a well-run company that has a lot to gain once the housing market normalizes; I own it in my portfolio, and I've added to my position several times this year at the beaten-down stock price.

To be sure, none of these is a sure thing. SoFi's growth depends on credit quality holding up, and September's weak jobs report is worth keeping an eye on. Forgent is a young public company with a handful of large customers. And Dream Finders is taking on substantial debt to buy a large competitor at a time when mortgage rates remain elevated, and home sales are under pressure.

Each of these stocks has a catalyst that's already in motion, whether it's expanding margins, a backlog that's already booked, or a merger that just closed. All three have significant risk factors, but all three could look very different a year from now.
