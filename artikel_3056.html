3 Stocks I'd Love to Buy at Black Friday Sale Prices

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Here are three excellent businesses I'd love to buy if their stocks hit Black Friday sale prices.

By Matt DiLallo – Nov 27, 2026 at 7:15AM EST

The stock market has been on sale recently, with the S&P 500 down more than 10% from its peak. Many stocks are down even more. However, we haven't hit Black Friday prices yet.

I have a long list of excellent companies I'd love to own -- or own more of -- if their share prices were a little more attractive. Three near the top of that list are Cintas (CTAS +0.14%), Waste Management (WM -0.43%), and PepsiCo (PEP +0.08%).

Here's why I'd love to see their stocks go on sale, and the prices that would make me seriously consider buying.

Cintas helps over 1 million businesses get ready for their workday. It provides uniforms, cleaning supplies, safety equipment, and more through long-term customer relationships. The business generates recurring revenue and grows steadily by offering existing customers more services and adding new ones to its routes.

The company is a compounding machine. Its revenue rose nearly 11% last quarter while its earnings per share jumped 15%. Cintas generates strong cash flow, which it uses to expand its business and to return cash to investors through share repurchases and a growing dividend (43 consecutive years of annual increases). It's in a strong position to continue growing, especially if it closes its $5.5 billion acquisition of Unifirst, which would enhance its capabilities.

While I love Cintas' business model, I don't like the price. At $225 a share, Cintas trades at more than 40 times forward earnings. That's a lot to pay for even a high-quality business like Cintas. I'd love to see the stock go on sale, and I would jump at the chance to buy shares at $185.

Waste Management is North America's leading environmental solutions provider. It has an irreplaceable network of transfer stations, landfills, recycling facilities, and other critical assets. They support the essential services it provides, which generate very recurring revenue (75% has annuity-like characteristics).

The company produces steadily rising revenue and strong, growing free cash flow. Waste Management uses its cash to reinvest in business growth through bolt-on acquisitions and sustainability investments, such as new recycling facilities and renewable natural gas projects. Meanwhile, it returns substantial cash to shareholders through buybacks and a growing dividend (23 consecutive annual increases).

Shares of the environmental solutions leader are currently around $245, putting its valuation at more than 30 times earnings. I'd love to pay closer to $200 a share to boost my position.

PepsiCo is a global leader in snacks and beverages. It benefits from resilient demand, as consumers continue to buy soda, water, chips, and dip during economic downturns.

While Pepsi is currently facing some headwinds in its North American business, it expects to deliver stronger long-term growth by continuing to invest in its brands. It aims to deliver high-single-digit earnings-per-share growth over the long term. That should support continued dividend increases. PepsiCo has increased its dividend for 54 years, qualifying it as a Dividend King, a company with 50 or more years of annual dividend increases.

I think Pepsi is a good value at its current price of around $140 a share. However, I'd love to buy even more shares at a lower price. My ideal target would be $130, which would push its already-satisfying dividend yield up to an even tastier 4.5%.

Great companies don't make great investments if you buy them at the wrong prices. While I'd be comfortable owning Cintas, Waste Management, and PepsiCo for many years, I'd rather buy them when their valuations provide a better opportunity to earn attractive long-term returns. A Black Friday-style discount in any of these stocks would get my attention. Until then, I'm happy to keep them on my shopping list and wait for the right opportunity.
