Are Stablecoins a Threat to Visa and Mastercard?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Benefiting from favorable regulation in the U.S., the blockchain-enabled payments innovation is the next hurdle for Visa and Mastercard to clear.

By Neil Patel – Nov 25, 2026 at 9:21AM EST

Investors associate digital assets with extreme volatility. Look at Bitcoin's (BTC -1.43%) price chart, and this perception is warranted.

But the industry also came up with stablecoins. These are a type of cryptocurrency usually pegged to the U.S. dollar, and they're meant to hold their value. They leverage the speed and 24/7 runtime that blockchain networks are known for. This makes them particularly interesting in the world of payments where those traits matter tremendously.

Visa (V +0.20%) and Mastercard (MA +0.00%) dominate the global payments landscape, each handling trillions of dollars in volume every single quarter. Both companies haven't hesitated to quickly lean into the opportunities that stablecoins might offer. They're playing offense in an effort to protect their wide economic moats.

Still, there's certainly concern among shareholders that this technology could be a disruptive force. Are stablecoins a threat to the robust competitive positions of Visa and Mastercard?

In a Nov. 17 speech, Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said that "stablecoins are starting to be used behind the scenes to settle obligations among participants in card networks."

Settlement is how the financial institutions on each side of a card purchase pay each other. Stablecoins are now part of that plumbing.

The stablecoin market was worth more than $300 billion earlier this month, according to research from The Motley Fool. That figure is up from $204 billion in January 2025. The GENIUS Act, signed into law in July 2025, established a federal rulebook for the U.S. stablecoins, adding a level of legitimacy to the industry.

Market cap isn't usage, though. McKinsey and Artemis Analytics estimate that reported stablecoin volume runs as high as $35 trillion a year, mostly from crypto trading and internal transfers. Real-world payments came to about $390 billion in 2025, just 0.02% of global payments. Business-to-business transactions, like paying overseas suppliers, made up $226 billion of that. Remittances and payouts are other practical uses.

Waller acknowledged that open blockchain networks "offer a new paradigm for competition with traditional payment rails." Established players are responding by building their own blockchains, forming consortiums, or partnering with emerging crypto firms. But he also called traditional rails "a reliable backbone for final funds settlement." Stablecoins are being absorbed into the system. They aren't replacing it.

Visa and Mastercard are leading that response. Visa's stablecoin settlement run rate, which annualizes recent activity, surpassed $25 billion in the fiscal 2026 fourth quarter (ended Sept. 30), up 10x year over year. It now settles in five stablecoins across 10 blockchains and supports more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programs.

Mastercard made a purchase. It closed its acquisition of stablecoin infrastructure provider BVNK on Aug. 3, a deal worth up to $1.8 billion. It has since launched a platform that lets institutions manage fiat and stablecoin accounts side by side. In June, it announced stablecoin settlement for card issuers and acquirers, even on weekends and holidays.

Keep all of this in perspective. Visa handled $18.8 trillion in annualized payments volume in Q4. The stablecoin run rate equals roughly 0.1% of that pace. Stablecoins might provide an upgrade to how money moves between Visa's partners, but they aren't stealing volume.

The economic moats are about more than technology. Waller noted that established firms "have amassed customer relationships and associated network effects, along with the power of market confidence." A new token doesn't come with Mastercard's 3.5 billion cards, merchant acceptance, or the fraud and dispute protections consumers trust.

And both companies have a long history of not shying away when new technologies emerge. They embraced e-commerce, mobile wallets, and tap-to-pay. Visa has issued more than 17.5 billion tokens, which replace card numbers with secure substitutes. Contactless now accounts for 81% of Mastercard's in-person purchase transactions.

A notable innovation to how money moves, stablecoins shouldn't be ignored by investors. However, they aren't a threat to Visa and Mastercard.
