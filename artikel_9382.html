British Museum and Victoria and Albert Museum extend loan of Asante regalia to the Manhyia Palace Museum in Ghana

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Nicholas Cullinan, the director of the British Museum, visits the Asantehene Otumfuo Osei Tutu II in Kumasi, the historic capital of the Asante Empire and capital of today's Ashanti Region in Ghana

Asante regalia gold objects on loan from the British Museum and Victoria and Albert Museum (V&A) will remain on show in Kumasi, Ghana until 2030 following the extension of loan agreements by the two UK museums. The British Museum has loaned 15 objects while the V&A lent 17 pieces, almost all of which were looted during military operations in the late 19th century.

The British Museum and the V&A signed a loan agreement in 2024 with Otumfuo Osei Tutu II, the 16th Asantehene (King of the Asante), to return the gold regalia to the Manhyia Palace Museum in Kumasi, the historic capital of what was then the Asante (Ashanti) empire. The loans will be renewed for a further three years at the end of the current term in April 2027. The two UK museums are legally unable to deaccession so the objects are being returned as long-term loans.

“The rollover of the loan agreement was always likely and from both the British Museum’s and Manhyia Palace Museum’s point of view, this has been a successful partnership with increased visitors, training and expansion at Manhyia. There is nonetheless enduring frustration from many in Ghana, both in Asante and beyond, that this is only a loan,” says Barnaby Phillips, author of The African Kingdom of Gold: Britain and the Asante Treasure.

The loan renewal announcement followed a recent visit to Kumasi by Nicholas Cullinan, the director of the British Museum, following an invitation by the Asantehene.

The gold regalia associated with the Asante royal court is on display in the Manhyia Palace Museum's Homecoming exhibition. Items shown include a small gold ornament in the form of a lute-harp (sankuo), one of the cast gold objects presented in 1817 to British diplomat Thomas Bowdich during his trip to Kumasi.

“The British Museum has about 220 pieces of Asante gold regalia and the Manhyia Palace Museum has been involved in the selection of objects [for loan]. There has been considerable input regarding what goes back to Ghana,” adds Phillips.

The idea of a loans deal, first discussed in the 1970s, was revived when the Asantehene visited London in May 2023 to attend the coronation of King Charles III.
