Three Lions’ win over Spain comes in two acts: the first superb, the second heroic | Sid Lowe

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Visitors take game to world champions and, though their hold on possession plummets, their rearguard is outstanding

In the final, absurdly tense moments before England finally did it and Spain finally fell, when Dan Burn had been sent on to do Dan Burn things, when the board had gone up with a 4 on but the clock wasn’t moving fast enough and the men in red kept coming back, Harry Kane had come to the side of the pitch to stand beside Thomas Tuchel. Palms out, Kane signalled and directed and appealed with his teammates to hold on to what they had: somehow, anyhow. What they had was special, but their grip on it was not as secure as it had seemed. Not as secure as it should have been too.

Tuchel gave Kane a nudge that looked pretty playful considering the pressure, before Marc Bollengier, the fourth official, ordered him back to the bench. But England’s captain was not gone for long and when he returned he was joined by almost all of them, awaiting the big moment. From there, he watched James Trafford make an astonishing double save, a moment of heroism that allowed them to hang on. Then, standing alongside the rest who could no longer just sit there, he saw the last header clear – Dan Burn things – before the whistle went. Then he and they went on one last run, back on to the pitch.

As they went there was celebration for them, applause for all of them, Spain too. What a game it had been, what a night. “We knew we would beat them,” Declan Rice claimed afterwards. Would? Maybe in the 60th minute they had known, England 3-0 up and playing their way to a superb and, yes, comfortable victory over the world champions – the first team to beat them, penalties aside, in 44 games and almost a thousand nights. But with half an hour left Spain had been revived, England reduced to 10 men and, as Tuchel said: “Thirty minutes against Spain feels like three hours.”

It did to him, to his players too. To everyone else, it flew by. This is the best game you can see in football, Spain’s head coach, Luis de la Fuente, had said, and he wasn’t wrong. When England got that third, the fans had sung: “You’ve seen the England, now eff off home.” Nah, you’re all right, thanks. Far better to stay and watch this; it had been hugely impressive from England; now, here were Spain too. Now, it was going to be wild. Nervy, noisy and fun.

Jarell Quansah lost the ball, Fermín López released Lamine Yamal and Spain won a penalty that Mikel Oyarzabal scored. It was some game. Twice Spain scored; countless times they came, the Metropolitano chanting: “Yes, we can!” In the end, they could not, but they had got close. They had really believed they might; as the momentum built, perhaps they had really believed they would.

This was history: made by England, perhaps; broken by England, certainly. Spain had not been beaten in 90 or 120 minutes since Colombia defeated them two and a half years ago, their only loss in that period coming on penalties against Portugal in last year’s Nations League final. They had not lost a competitive game since Scotland in March 2023. That, Fifa said, was the longest run ever and even if there was an asterisk, it was still mightily impressive, but it had to end some time. If not necessarily like this.

“With every passing game, defeat gets closer,” De la Fuente said. Now, it arrived. “They can seem invincible but we’re ready,” Tuchel had said. At the end Spain’s staff lined up to applaud their players off. England’s players stood before their fans, exhausted, arms raised.

Ultimately, this came in two acts. The first act was superb from England, the second heroic. “We can’t play like them and they can’t play like us,” De la Fuente had said and yet what was so striking about the way that this began was that England took the game to Spain on their own terms. They were the ones dominating the ball, not allowing their opponents out; theirs was the midfield, the control.

Ten minutes is not a reliable sample size it is true – and it turned out even an hour might not have been enough – but as the clock rolled into double figures, the possession stat showed 70% for England. While that number fell, when it reached 20 the shot count was 4-0. The first came after 39 seconds and before Spain had a touch, Unai Simón flashing out a right hand to Jude Bellingham’s shot inside the six-yard box. Martín Zubimendi immediately had to stop Anthony Gordon’s shot and then Simón stopped Rice. All that inside three minutes; three more and Kane was flashing a shot just wide.

England’s fans were enjoying this. A chorus of “the best in the world, the best in the world, Harry Kane, the best in the world” came down. They watched England control Spain’s place, Elliot Anderson and Rice excelling. They watched England take the lead, Kane scoring his 93rd goal with penalty No 28. They watched Bellingham burst through, responding by launching into a rendition of the Beatles which was still going when they watched Ezri Konsa head in the second. Then they watched Bellingham burst through again to set up Ben White. They watched England be brilliant for an hour but there was still plenty of Spain to watch as well. Nervously now and they will remember Trafford’s superhero rescue for some time, but it was still their night, and what a night.
