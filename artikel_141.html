‘Just wrong’: British right distances itself from Trump after Putin ‘friend’ post

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Reform deputy leader calls Russian leader ‘a murderous dictator’ after US president appears to endorse him

Reform UK and the Conservatives have distanced themselves from Donald Trump after the US president posted a photo of himself shaking hands with Vladimir Putin with the caption: “My friend.”

The US president had already used the term to describe the Russian president while at last week’s Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, southern China, where he also met the Chinese president, Xi Jinping.

But the photo with Putin, showing Trump leaning towards the Russian leader to talk to him as they met at the summit, posted by Trump on his Truth Social platform overnight, prompted condemnation in the UK.

Asked for his reaction, Richard Tice, the deputy leader of Reform UK, the British party most closely associated with Trump, replied: “Horror.”

He told Sky News: “It’s just wrong at every level … And it’s a grave mistake. He is not our friend, Putin. He is, as others have said, a murderous dictator. And no one is in any doubt about that.”

Asked whether Nigel Farage, the Reform UK leader, who is a friend of Trump, would speak to the US president about this, Tice replied: “Oh, there have been a number of occasions where one could agree with Trump, and then disagree with him.

“There have been more occasions recently when clearly we have disagreed with him and said so very explicitly. And in a sense, that’s the nature of long friendships. Sometimes you’ll agree and sometimes you’ll disagree. And, I think the disagreements are growing.”

Reform and the Conservatives under Kemi Badenoch, both of whom welcomed Trump’s re-election in 2024, have increasingly tried to dissociate themselves from the US president amid questions over his temperament and judgment, while his attacks on Iran have driven up global energy prices.

Polling last month for YouGov found that just 10% of British people have a favourable opinion of Trump, with 86% holding an unfavourable view. Asked about the “My friend” post, Alex Burghart, Badenoch’s deputy, said he was “very sad” about it.

He told Sky News: “I’m sad and I’m depressed, because the world that I grew up in was one in which the United States was a defender of freedom. I worry that we might be moving into a world where that isn’t the case any more.”

Asked about his party’s previous support for Trump, Burghart replied: “If you look at what the Conservative party is proposing in terms of defence and foreign policy, it’s very clear that we are still on the side of freedom, and we’re the party that has said that we will put up defence spending to what Nato wants it to be.

“So I think you can trust Kemi Badenoch and the Conservatives on this. But, I very much hope that we don’t enter a world in which large, aggressive powers feel that they can occupy their neighbours and get the approval of the United States.”

When asked about the Trump post, Hamish Falconer, a Foreign Office minister, called Putin “a butcher” and said Andy Burnham would make the UK’s views known.

Falconer told Sky News: “The prime minister speaks to the president regularly and is in absolutely no way pulling his punches about the strength of feeling that the UK has as a whole about Russia’s invasion of Ukraine.”
