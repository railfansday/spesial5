SuperDial revamps its healthcare voice agent platform to stop AI bots wasting time calling each other

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

SuperDial Inc., the creator of voice-first artificial intelligence agents for automating healthcare workflows, is overhauling its platform with a new Agent-to-Agent RCM architecture.

The goal is to eliminate the compute inefficiencies that have arisen as autonomous workers become more prevalent in the industry.

The startup, which raised $15 million in a Series A funding round last year, is best known for its voice agents that automate payer operations, revenue cycle management and administrative outreach. It aims to alleviate the time-consuming burden of having to call health insurance providers, waste time on hold, navigate phone trees and check online portals for information. Traditionally, SuperDial has seen itself as a voice-first platform that’s focused on call automation, but the shift to its Agent-to-Agent RCM architecture suggests it wants to deemphasize phone calls as much as possible.

The company is addressing the friction that has emerged from the slightly dystopian reality that both healthcare providers and insurance payers have largely replaced humans with AI agents in order to automate routine calls. But the fact that agents are handling the calls on both sides creates huge inefficiencies, resulting in those bots wasting time and compute resources as they engage in unnecessary robotic conversations. Rather than streamline insurance eligibility checks, claim follow-ups and prior authorizations, those agents find themselves stuck in multi-minute phone exchanges to relay structured data verbally that both sides already possess.

SuperDial founder and Chief Executive Sam Schwager refers to these scenarios as the “bot wars,” and says they’re increasingly engaging in relentless and destructive battles with one another that cost both healthcare providers and insurance companies money.

“One side deploys AI to secure approvals and payment for care,” he said. “The other deploys AI to review those requests and, at times, deny them. Neither has a reason to stop. Every extra round costs more in AI spend, pulls in staff on both sides to handle what the bots can’t settle, and can delay payment and care. The result is more friction, not less.”

With Agent-to-Agent RCM, SuperDial is trying to sidestep the bot wars by no longer defaulting to outbound calls. Instead, it acts as a kind of omnichannel orchestration layer, where success is determined by the number of requests resolved, rather than completed phone calls. When a revenue cycle team initiates a request, SuperDial’s agents will first check the relevant electronic data interchange feeds and application programming interfaces to find the information they need.

If they find it, great, but if not, they will then try the payer’s web portals. If they’re still out of luck after exhausting all options for digital retrieval, only then will they proceed to place a voice call. Those calls are more efficient too, since SuperDial’s agents will retain all of the context from their initial search, so they only need to query the agent on the other side of the line for the information that’s missing.

Schwager said Agent-to-Agent RCM puts more emphasis on resolution, allowing a provider’s agent and a payer’s system to connect and work together to resolve administrative requests with less work on both sides.

“The difference between this and the bot wars is the definition of done,” he said. “Two agents were optimizing for their own side of the exchange, but now those two agents are optimizing for a usable answer — that is, the information the provider needs to move the account forward, delivered in response to a request the payer can actually answer.”

For customers, life becomes much simpler. All they have to do is tell SuperDial what it needs to know, and its agents will work to get the answers in the fastest time and most efficient manner possible. “How we get the answer is our problem, not theirs,” Schwager said.

Schwager said the industry needs to move away from the idea that healthcare administration can be improved by having two bots argue with each other faster. “If an agent already has the information, that context should travel with the request,” he explained. “If a digital channel can answer it, there should be no call. The goal is fewer exchanges, not faster ones.”

The company’s long-term goal is to create an AI-native network that facilitates seamless, automated payer-provider communications. The goal is to eliminate repetitive inquiries and cut down on the administrative delays that can negatively impact patient outcomes, Schwager added.

SuperDial says it’s already having a big impact. Since it founded its platform in 2021, it has handled more than 10 million automated calls between healthcare providers and insurance firms – interactions that would once have been made by humans. Had humans made those calls, it would have been much slower, because SuperDial says its agents average about four minutes per call when dealing with insurers’ automated voice systems, compared with 17 minutes or more when reaching a human representative.

With its architectural overhaul, SuperDial is now targeting insurers, in addition to the healthcare providers it already serves. Earlier this year, it announced it had signed a key deal with Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America to modernize its dental provider data attestation workflows.
