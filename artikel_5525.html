Zebec ZBCN Buyback: 40.5M Tokens Held, Not Burned, as Supply Stays Unchanged

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

Zebec says it bought back 40.5 million ZBCN in the third quarter and is keeping them "out of circulation."

On-chain, those tokens didn't reach Zebec's buyback treasury wallet during the quarter. They arrived on October 21, three weeks after Q3 ended, the same day Zebec announced the buyback, sent from what Solscan labels a Crypto.com hot wallet.

The amount matches Zebec's claim almost to the token. But the wallet's history also shows something "out of circulation" doesn't usually suggest: it has moved 59 million ZBCN out before. Let's go through the wallet to check what Zebec's buyback claim actually covers.

Zebec Network announced on October 21, in an X post, that it repurchased 40,536,539 ZBCN in Q3 for about $100,000. That implies an average price of roughly $0.00247. The money came from Zebec Cards and payroll fees across Zebec Enterprise and the SuperApp.

Zebec's explainer, Inside the ZBCN Buyback Program, says buybacks are "funded by operating revenue" from payroll, payments, cards and related services, not from treasury reserves. That's a real point in Zebec's favor.

The scale is small. ZBCN has a 100 billion maximum supply, so 40.5 million tokens is about 0.04% of it.

Zebec says the repurchased tokens "will not re-enter circulation." They weren't burned. ZBCN's total supply is exactly the same as it was before the buyback.

That's a deliberate policy. The program began in 2023 under the old ZBC token and originally included burns. In early 2024, the Zebec DAO approved ZIP-4, which paused burns while keeping recurring buybacks. Zebec says the DAO expects to decide by the end of 2026 whether to propose bringing burns back. Governance happens through the Zebec DAO portal.

The practical difference matters. A burn permanently destroys tokens. "Out of circulation" depends on Zebec continuing to hold them and on data providers leaving them out of circulating supply. The blockchain doesn't enforce either one.

Zebec's blog says repurchased tokens are held "in the designated on-chain Buyback Treasury Wallet and with Uphold Institutional," but doesn't give the address. The account linked with the announcement is EkXEg6de172aoLvr7w86YzQ2rf5oEFD3iUt8uQsLQuQW. ZBCN's Solana mint address is ZBCNpuD7YMXzTHB2fhGkGi78MNsHGLRXUhRewNRm9RU.

As of October 22, Solscan shows the wallet holding 108,256,701.48 ZBCN, worth about $246,157 at $0.002273.

The Q3 check passes. On October 21 at about 14:11 UTC, the wallet received two ZBCN transfers from an address Solscan labels "Crypto.com Hot Wallet":

Together, that's 40,535,224.09 ZBCN, just 1,315 ZBCN (about $3) short of Zebec's figure. That's a 99.997% match. The small difference is likely due to fees or rounding.

One detail stands out: the account is off-curve. Solscan shows "isOnCurve: FALSE," which means the address is derived by a program and has no private key of its own. Accounts like this are usually controlled by a program such as a multisig, so moving tokens requires that program's approval. That's a better setup than a single person's wallet, but it isn't a lock.

The timing shows how the program works. The wallet's transfer history suggests a repeating pattern, with each batch arriving after its quarter has ended:

Those three batches add up to the wallet's current balance within 0.01 ZBCN. So the "buyback treasury" holds the last three quarters of deliveries and nothing else.

This means that during each quarter, the purchased tokens weren't in the on-chain treasury. The pattern points to buying on an exchange, with tokens sitting in an exchange account until they're withdrawn to the treasury around announcement time. The chain doesn't show when the Q3 purchases actually happened, so for July 1 to September 30, the "$100,000 Q3 buyback" can't be checked on-chain. Only the October 21 delivery can.

That's common practice and not a sign of wrongdoing. But it means the on-chain record confirms delivery, not the purchases themselves.

If the balance only covers the last three batches, what happened to earlier buybacks? The transfer history answers that, too. The wallet has sent ZBCN out twice, both times to the same address, shown on Solscan as 9Sq2nZ...4TEine:

That's about 58.97 million ZBCN moved out of a wallet described as holding tokens that "will not re-enter circulation."To be fair, Zebec says tokens are "periodically consolidated with Uphold Institutional for custody," and these outflows may be exactly that. There's no confirmation that 9Sq2nZ...4TEine belongs to Uphold. Once tokens leave this wallet, readers can't follow them using Zebec's disclosures. These transfers also show what a burn would prevent: tokens in this wallet can, and do, move.

The Q3 claim holds up better than I expected. The wallet received almost exactly 40.5 million ZBCN, and the balance reconciles to the token. What the chain can't show is when the tokens were bought, or where earlier buybacks are now.
