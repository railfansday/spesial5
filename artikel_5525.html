Physician satisfaction continues to decline and patient loyalty is up for grabs -- Bain & Company

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

BOSTON, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Physician satisfaction has fallen to its lowest level since Bain & Company started recording in 2017, and one in four now wants to switch employers, finds the firm's latest analysis of US health systems. On the patient side, while 91% of consumers have a preferred health system, one-third sought care outside of it in the past 12 months. Meanwhile, healthcare providers are spending heavily on AI to accelerate improvements, but only about 40% of administrators can clearly measure the returns on their AI investments.

Those are among the findings of Bain's US Front Line of Healthcare 2026 report, which explores industry trends based on the firm's surveys of consumers, physicians, nurses, and hospital administrators.

"Health system leaders have a tall agenda: revitalize relationships with clinicians and meet patients' rising expectations, while also redesigning care in an AI-native world," says Michael Brookshire, head of Bain & Company's Americas Healthcare & Life Sciences practice. "Successful health systems will transform the full frontline experience by involving physicians in decision making, smoothing out the end-to-end patient care process, and using AI strategically to benefit both groups."

Physician retention is driven by respect, recognition, and a voice in decision making

Among US physicians, the overall employee Net Promoter ScoreSM (eNPS), Bain's proprietary measurement of worker satisfaction, fell from 33 in 2024 to just 6 in 2026, the lowest recorded since Bain started tracking in 2017. Physicians who feel engaged in decision making have an 88-point higher eNPS than those who don't, while clinicians who feel excluded from decision making are 3.5 times more likely to consider switching employers.

Overall, a quarter of physicians say they want to switch employers, up 5 percentage points from 2024, and not just because of burnout and excessive workload. Half of physicians now cite not being treated with respect and dignity as a top non-pay reason to change employers, up from 30% two years ago, and 43% cite a lack of recognition and appreciation, versus 37% in 2024. The declining physician experience has put pressure on recruitment, with 55% of administrators saying that recruiting physicians has become more challenging, up from 35% in 2024.

AI can elevate the physician experience, though buy-in has its limits. Nearly one in three physicians uses diagnostic or treatment decision-support AI tools, and 56% of them use AI tools to support clinical decision making multiple times a day, with many reporting improvements across clinical decision support, patient-facing and administrative use cases. But human oversight remains a prerequisite for most clinicians and AI is a non-starter in some areas: for example, nearly 40% of clinicians reject using AI for clinical triage, regardless of whether there is human oversight.

"Leading health systems are cultivating an owner's mindset among physicians to foster a sense of shared stake in how the organization runs," says Erin Morrissette, MD, a partner in Bain & Company's Healthcare & Life Sciences practice. "They are involving physicians before decisions are made, giving them meaningful influence over technologies and workflows, recognizing their contributions, and building real clinician representation in leadership and governance."

Patient preference does not guarantee loyalty, human-delivered care is preferred over AI

Healthcare consumers are taking more control over their own care, raising the bar for their providers. More than 75% of consumers say they conduct their own medical research more frequently, expect greater doctor responsiveness, and take more interest in prevention and lifestyle changes than they did in 2024.

When patients' needs are not met, many seek care elsewhere. Nearly one-third sought care outside of their preferred health system in the past year, primarily for more convenience (32%), a provider's recommendation (32%), or shorter wait times (26%).

One source of patient frustration is a fragmented healthcare experience. Nearly half of respondents report needing to see multiple doctors for an accurate diagnosis and routinely being sent to multiple locations to treat a single health issue. About one in four say they still lack access to a single, complete medical record.

While nearly a third of patients consult AI on health questions, they overwhelmingly prefer human-delivered care and are less comfortable with AI in healthcare than they were last year. Among consumers, 56% most prefer in-person clinical care for non-acute visits, though that has dropped from 67% last year, and 37% most prefer virtual appointments, up from 27% in 2025. AI chatbots are the least desired care channel for 60% of consumers, up from 49% last year, and the most preferred type for just 4%.
