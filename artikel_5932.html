Even Putin Is a ’90s Nostalgist

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

But in this life, you can’t go backward in time.

Russian President Vladimir Putin observes Russia-Belarus joint military exercises, codenamed Zapad-2025 (West-2025), at the Mulino training ground in Nizhny Novgorod Region, Russia, September 16, 2025.

In Abu Dhabi for peace talks, Russian negotiators revealed that Vladimir Putin is just like the rest of us, a nostalgist for the 1990s. You and I might want to go back for the easeful sense of prosperity, the pre-9/11 sense of security, the pre-Instagram sense of contentment for the pleasures of your own life, or the still-unified popular culture that gave an entire diverse country something in common to talk about at work. Vladimir Putin’s men reiterated one of their primary pre-Ukraine war demands: Russia wants NATO to be back in its 1997 positions.

Well, sort of. The Russian demand is chiefly about geography. If it were done, it would mean removing nine battlegroups in total: Sweden, in Finland; the U.K., in Estonia; Canada, in Latvia; Germany, in Lithuania; France, in Romania; Spain, in Slovakia; Italy, in Bulgaria; and the U.S., which has 10,000 men in Poland now but only 300 of them permanently there. The U.S. has Aegis Ashore weapons sites in Romania and Northern Poland, and there is a multinational, NATO division-level command center headquartered in northeastern Poland.
