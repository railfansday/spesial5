Gap is cutting its full-year forecast as Banana Republic and Athleta stumble

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Gap cut its net sales outlook to "roughly flat" from growth of 1% to 1.5% as warm weather weighed on demand

Gap $GPS lowered its full-year outlook on Thursday after warmer weather weakened demand in October and early November, the company said.

The owner of Old Navy, Gap, Banana Republic and Athleta expects fiscal 2026 net sales to be "roughly flat," down from a prior forecast of growth of 1% to 1.5%. Adjusted earnings per share are projected at $2.29 to $2.34, compared with a prior range of $2.35 to $2.45. Adjusted operating margin is projected at 7.2%, down from a prior range of 7.4% to 7.6%. For the fourth quarter, Gap expects net sales to be flat to down 1%.

Gap said the outlook reflects a slower start to the fourth quarter, followed by improvement as weather normalized. The company furnished the earnings release to the Securities and Exchange Commission in a Form 8-K filing.

"While demand softened in October and early November, amidst warmer weather, we are encouraged by the more recent improvement in trends as temperatures have cooled," CEO Richard Dickson said in a statement.

Third-quarter net sales rose 2% to $4.0 billion, and comparable sales were flat. Net income was $311 million, or 86 cents per share, up from $236 million, or 62 cents per share, a year earlier. Analysts had expected comparable sales to rise 1.3%, according to CNBC.

Banana Republic comparable sales fell 3%, which Gap tied to shoppers' reaction to its women's fall assortment and to customers postponing purchases of heavier seasonal clothing. Athleta comparable sales fell 15%, a drop the company blamed on its attempts to refresh the product lineup and marketing, which gained little momentum with shoppers. Old Navy comparable sales were flat, while Gap brand comparable sales rose 9%, the 12th consecutive quarter of growth.

Dickson told CNBC that much of the lower guidance stemmed from warmer weather in the first two weeks of the quarter. He said the company anticipates "meaningful acceleration" as shoppers head into the busiest stretch of the holidays, according to CNBC.

Gap named Ashley Kechter, a Vuori executive, as Athleta's next president and CEO. She starts Dec. 14, according to CNBC. "Clearly, we're disappointed with Athleta's performance, and we recognize there's a lot of work ahead," Dickson told CNBC. Kechter will head a wide-ranging assessment of the brand, Dickson said.

Banana Republic stocked too heavily in sweaters and outerwear, according to Bloomberg. Michael Francis began as Old Navy's CEO on Nov. 2, according to CNBC.

Gap stock was up as high as 4.3% before the opening bell on Friday. As of Thursday's close, the stock was down 27% for the year.
