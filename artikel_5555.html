Cerebras stock soars about 16% in extended trading on earnings beat and Microsoft deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Cerebras lifted its 2026 core revenue outlook to $940 million-$960 million and expects revenue to triple in 2027

Cerebras Systems reported third-quarter core revenue of $230.9 million, up 70% from a year earlier, and raised its full-year revenue outlook on Thursday. Cerebras stock rose about 16% in extended trading.

Core revenue topped the $214.7 million analysts expected, and adjusted earnings of 12 cents a share compared with an expected loss of 14 cents, according to CNBC.

Cerebras announced a cloud deal with a top-three hyperscaler, without naming the customer in its release. Microsoft $MSFT signed the deal in October, CEO Andrew Feldman told CNBC. "We believe revenue begins to show up in Q1 '27," Feldman said. Financial terms were not disclosed, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Looking to the fourth quarter, Cerebras is forecasting core revenue in a range of $310 million to $330 million, with gross margin on that core business between 47% and 49%. Analysts polled by LSEG expected $271 million in revenue, according to CNBC.

For the full year, the company expects core revenue of $940 million to $960 million and a core gross margin of 42% to 44%. Previous guidance was $880 million to $890 million in core revenue. Cerebras also said it expects to triple revenue in 2027.

Total revenue under generally accepted accounting principles rose 55% to $210.8 million. The net loss came to $161.2 million, or 67 cents per share, versus $22.2 million, or 41 cents per share, in the year-ago period.

Core revenue generated by cloud and other services climbed 272% to $144.9 million. Core hardware revenue fell to $86.0 million from $96.8 million.

Cerebras and Oracle $ORCL announced plans on Oct. 27 to offer Cerebras' AI inference on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, with availability planned for 2027. In its earnings release, Cerebras said it is integrating its inference technology into the Oracle cloud.

Cerebras stock had touched its weakest level since its Nasdaq $NDAQ debut after reports that OpenAI had picked Nvidia $NVDA's graphics processing units, not Cerebras chips, to run the "Ultrafast" mode in GPT-6.1 Sol. Cerebras has a $10 billion agreement with OpenAI, struck in January, to deliver 750 megawatts of computing capacity over the period ending in 2028.
