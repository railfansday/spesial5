Personal Investments • Re: Another roth conversion question

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Does anyone ever consider that roth conversions can also be beneficial if one wants to early retire? Why is this always overlooked?
Roth conversions are useful in early retirement, but not normally while you are still working. The potential benefit is that you can do a Roth conversion in 2026, and spend the money in 2031 penalty-free; you can then do another Roth conversion in 2027, and spend the money in 2032 penalty-free, and so on. This is known as a Roth conversion ladder.

But you will rarely want to do that before the year in which you retire early. If you are retiring early in 2027, you likely have a high salary in 2026. If you add a Roth conversion to that amount, you will pay a very high tax rate on the conversion. Ideally, as an early retiree, you will have some other source of money for 2031, such as existing Roth IRAs or taxable investments. But even if you need to use tax-sheltered accounts, you might be better off doing an ordinary withdrawal in 2031 and paying the 10% penalty but a lower tax rate, rather than converting in 2026 at a high rate.

For any of these conversions, you can avoid the market growth issues by converting bonds, and keeping your stocks for future expenses. If you hold a five-year bond in your traditional IRA in 2026, you know exactly what the converted bond will be worth in 2031 after you convert; if you hold an intermediate-term bond fund, you have a good idea of what it will be worth in 2031. In contrast, if you convert $100K of stock in 2026, you don't know whether it will be worth $200K in 2031 and you converted too much and paid tax prematurely, or worth $50K in 2031 and you still have to withdraw more and possibly pay a penalty.
