Google will turn off its search engine for schools by default. Is it an AI ‘ultimatum’?

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Let K-12 Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The tech giant’s recent announcement comes as schools are pushing for more autonomy over what artificial intelligence tools students can use.

Google sent shockwaves through school communities nationwide Tuesday with an announcement that it would turn off Google Search and two of its other artificial intelligence features for all “K-12 student accounts” by default.

School administrators can, however, opt to turn Google Search, Gemini, Gemini in Classrooms or Gemini Notebook on for students at any time.

The tech giant said in a Tuesday post on X that the change is happening because schools are “asking for more control over student use of AI services.”

“We know there’s concern about students using AI to shortcut schoolwork, and schools have told us they want control,” Google said on X. “That’s exactly why we are making these changes today to address the feedback we’ve heard from educators in recent months.”

The Google Search feature in recent years has presented a tough challenge for some district leaders who have wanted to turn off the search engine’s AI capabilities, particularly for their youngest students. And critics of Google’s decision say the latest move puts schools in a difficult position: Either students can use Google Search with its AI feature or not at all.

Rich Calkins, superintendent of Pocantico Hills Central School District in New York, expressed skepticism over the Google announcement. Pocantico Hills CSD is waiting to see how Google will officially roll out this decision, Calkins said. Regardless, he added, the online safety and well-being of the district’s students is his main priority.

“I personally am very suspect of any sort of corporation, entity, or group that attempts to hold school districts hostage by illustrating to them that they do not have the ability to turn off a feature in a program that may be possibly harmful to students in any shape, manner and fashion,” Calkins said.

It remains unclear when Google will begin to turn off its AI tools by default for K-12 and if the feature can be controlled by a school administrator at the individual student, grade or school level.

Meanwhile, some parent advocacy groups that have successfully pushed this year for school policies on screentime limits and AI moratoriums are calling for district leaders to not give into Google and to divest from the company’s tools altogether.

“Google forced its AI products into classrooms and parents fought back," said Lila Byock, founder of Schools Beyond Screens in a Nov. 3 statement. "Now Google is refusing to take no for an answer. The company’s hostile ultimatum is a desperate attempt to maintain its stranglehold on public education.”

Earlier this year, New York City Public Schools announced it would enforce a one-year moratorium on student-facing AI tools at the elementary and middle school levels. Shortly after, Los Angeles Unified School District made a similar move to pause AI tools for students districtwide.

After Google’s Tuesday announcement, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani posted on BlueSky that “Google is trying to use its monopoly power to force AI into our public schools. No corporation should have the power to dictate how we teach our children.”

Earlier this week, InnovateEDU released a model policy for responsible ed tech use in pre-K through 5th grade that calls for all generative AI features to be disabled by default for schools. Under that model, districts would only enable such AI tools once they’ve been authorized for instructional and educational purposes.

Erin Mote, CEO and founder at InnovateEDU, said she thinks Google’s announcement is in response to the fact that schools are “overwhelmingly” asking to have more control around the AI tools that students use in classroom environments.

InnovateEDU called for AI tools to be turned off by default for the youngest of students, Mote said, because “it's really important to trust in the judgment of educators and schools and local context to be able to decide what their policy is around student-facing generative AI use.”

Mote said she views the Google Search development as positive, because it “invests in the trust of educators,” but it’s still a difficult situation since “people have come to rely on Google Search.” She added that one reason Google may be unable to turn off AI features from its search engine is because AI is “so deeply embedded in their search functionality now that it would be difficult to unstick it.”
