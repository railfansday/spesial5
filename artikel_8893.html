Base Design gives Visa a brand refresh "for the next generation"

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Design agency Base Design has created a more compact version of credit card company Visa’s logo and worked with Displaay Type Foundry on a new typeface, as part of a refresh of the brand’s visual identity.

While the 2021 Visa wordmark remains unchanged, the refresh included creating a shortened symbol for the brand, rolling out the new Visa Sans typeface, and creating a blocky hero graphic to market towards a younger generation.

“When you’re working with a brand as iconic as Visa, the challenge isn’t recognition – it’s relevance,” said Base Design executive creative director Min Lew.

“Our ambition was to build on Visa’s extraordinary equity while giving it a renewed sense of energy and expression, one that feels as relevant to the next generation as it does to the generations who have grown up with it.”

As part of this strategy, the studio cut the V out of the existing wordmark to function as its own symbol. It will be used on credit cards and appear across digital platforms.

The studio also wrapped it in colourful bar, which will also be used on graphic elements such as posters, and to frame text.

“More than a framing device, the hero graphic device brings movement, direction, and possibility to the identity,” said Lew.

“This simple gesture becomes the foundation for a visual language that can be energetic at a global sporting event, confident and bold in a B2B environment, or vibrant and elevated for affluent audiences.”

For Visa Sans, Base Design and Displaay Type Foundry also based the design on the existing logo.

The typeface features rounded edges akin to the rounded corner of the Visa V, and overall, extends “the logo’s character across the wider identity”.

Ultimately, the new identity was created to be used across different smaller, touchpoints according to the team, such as at tap-to-pay stations, at checkout, or online, as the brand appears “for only a moment” in day to day life.

“We have deep trust and recognition as a brand,” said Visa chief marketing officer Frank Cooper III. “This refresh gives us an opportunity to build on that.”

A more subtle approach to logo and branding updates has been popular in recent years, such as at Walmart and Google.

In contrast, the US restaurant chain Cracker Barrel recently took a hard swing at a logo update, which was immediately met with online backlash and promptly scrapped in favour of the original design.
