Good morning, Jason Mast here filling in from Pharmalot’s Midtown Bureau. Wishing you a happy Turkey week. I hope that it is joyful, or at least tolerable. In the meantime, the news marches on. …The FDA again rejected a hotly debated experimental Duchenne muscular dystrophy drug from Capricor Therapeutics, STAT tells us. The rejection letter came months after an advisory committee in which reviewers excoriated the company’s data, saying a Phase 3 trial had — contrary to Capricor’s claims — clearly failed. Some advocates and doctors pushed for the drug’s approval, noting it was for patients with a terminal disease and few other options.

Eli Lilly bought a U.K.-based Huntington’s disease startup for up to $725 million, Endpoints reports. The startup, called LoQus23 Therapeutics, is developing a pill aimed at an increasingly popular target in neurons called MSH3. LoQus23 planned to start a human trial in the coming months. This is Lilly’s 15th acquisition of the year, as its leaders look to pour obesity-drug riches into a new wave of powerful medicines.

Kymera said its closely watched Dupixent rival safely and effectively treated eczema in a Phase 2 study, STAT relays. The pill, called KT-621, targets an inflammatory protein called STAT6. It is among the first of a new kind of therapy called protein degraders that chemists and executives hope will let them target previously undruggable proteins. KT-621 has generated particular excitement among investors because of its potential to serve as an oral alternative to Dupixent, Sanofi and Regeneron’s mega-blockbuster immune drug.
