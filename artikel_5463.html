White House formed multi-agency team to prevent repeat of nationwide flight shutdown

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Officials from the FAA, Defense Department, Transportation Department and intelligence office are working on preventing a repeat of the Nov. 13 ground stop

The White House organized a "tiger team" that draws on officials from the Federal Aviation Administration, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, the Defense Department and the Transportation Department, with the aim of keeping another nationwide ground stop from happening, according to CBS News. The group has held its first meeting at the White House.

A tiger team, according to CBS News, brings together a handful of specialists to tackle one particular problem that routine operations are poorly equipped to handle.

The FAA issued the nationwide ground stop at about 3:30 p.m. ET on Nov. 13 after a technical problem, and air traffic resumed at 8:15 p.m. that day. About 3,500 aircraft were in the air and all landed safely. Operators canceled about 12,462 flights, though the agency said not all cancellations were directly caused by the ground stop.

The FAA said its legacy equipment became unstable when data flooded its network at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds. The initial investigation points to a radar site in Amarillo, Texas, and a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas. L3Harris, the FAA's network service provider, had been working at the El Dorado site, including deploying a new router.

The FAA said it has no indication of a cyberattack. It said it disabled the broadcasting function on the originating routers and on more than 60 similar routers, and that it and L3Harris are still investigating the root cause.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford provided an update on the restoration of airspace. Duffy said officials have not determined whether contractor error or a faulty router caused the flood. Bedford said the router replacement was routine maintenance of the existing telecommunications network and not part of the $12.5 billion modernization effort.

Duffy would not rule out another ground stop while upgrades to the system are under way, according to CBS News. Asked about a Government Accountability Office report on the modernization effort, Duffy told CBS News: “We are going, I think, at the right speed. We have to get off old network, old telecom.”

The GAO report, issued in September, found that the FAA has advanced the effort but may be favoring quick action over cost control and the risk of disrupting air traffic, according to CBS News. The GAO also found that the FAA has neither a total cost estimate nor a master schedule covering related projects. Duffy is asking Congress for $50 billion for the updates.

President Donald Trump blamed the previous administration for an "old and broken" FAA system and urged Congress to provide money after the ground stop. Nationwide ground stops are rare. The previous two followed the Sept. 11 attacks and a brief FAA computer failure in January 2023.
