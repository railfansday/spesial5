LeBron James is suing a startup over an AI ad that faked his endorsement

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The federal lawsuit seeks damages and a court order barring the use of his likeness, voice and the Bron name

LeBron James sued the AI-powered sports betting startup Bron, which is operated by PlayVision, and its two co-founders on Tuesday. He alleges they used his nickname and an AI-generated version of him in an ad without his permission, according to CNBC.

The complaint, filed in U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, names co-founders Anish Gupta and Marc Zoghby as defendants, according to CNBC. It accuses them of trademark infringement, according to Reuters.

The ad shows an AI-generated James inside a basketball team’s locker room, dressed in a No. 23 jersey printed with his nickname, covered in his familiar tattoos and speaking in a synthetic imitation of his voice, according to CNBC. The figure swipes through wagering choices for NBA games inside the app. Within 24 hours, the ad had been viewed about 1 million times on X $TWTR, according to the complaint.

The complaint says the depiction "falsely communicates that Mr. James wagers, or considers wagering, on NBA games and NBA player performance—conduct that NBA rules prohibit," according to Reuters. James says he has been "especially careful in the sports-betting and prediction-market space" and holds an exclusive endorsement deal with Polymarket, a platform for prediction markets.

James's lawyers issued a cease-and-desist demand dated Nov. 5, according to CNBC. The startup subsequently took the ad down from X and Instagram, and said it was in the process of renaming the app. The ad stayed viewable via reposts by other accounts, and the app still had the same name when the suit was filed.

James says the defendants used the letter to "gin up additional publicity," according to Reuters. NBA star Luka Doncic, who was also featured in the ad, sent a cease-and-desist letter of his own, according to the complaint.

The complaint calls James's endorsement "uniquely valuable," according to Reuters. It says the founders reasoned that the buzz would be worth more than whatever legal trouble followed, so that the alleged infringement of his rights amounted to just another marketing cost, according to CNBC.

James is asking the court for damages and an order that would stop the defendants from trading on his name, image, likeness or voice, and from continuing to call the app Bron, according to CNBC. The damages include unspecified compensatory and punitive amounts, according to Reuters. Attorney Alex Spiro represents James.

Apple $AAPL's App Store describes Bron as a "sports analytics and betting research app" and says it does not accept wagers, according to CNBC. PlayVision is based in California and went through the fall 2025 cohort at Y Combinator. Bron did not immediately respond to Reuters' requests for comment.
