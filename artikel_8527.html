Personal Investments • Re: How to incorporate an inheritance into retirement plan??

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

All you can really do is spend more now and if the inheritance doesn't pan out, work longer or spend less later.

The additional length of working you are willing to risk or reduced spending you are willing to endure can inform how much you could increase your spending now.

I'm in a similar situation and could probably quit today in my early 40's if I were to get half of the inheritance I will probably get. Current plan has us calling in rich in our mid to late 40's without any inheritance. But until that money is under my name I'm proceeding as if it wont ever be there and allow us to be perfectly happy with our current fairly liberal spending habits. If and when it does come (likely not for 10-15 years) the purse strings will loosen a little more.
I guess I should have been clearer in my post that I was looking for thoughts on how to model it in my retirement planning projections. The idea is to see how much it would impact our spending limits earlier in our retirement. And how we could use that for life opportunities, not only for us, but our adult children.
Consider both fund positioning now and the future tax ramifcations of various %'s of inheritance (taxable, Tax defferred, Roth). Similarly with AA commitments now and future income tax results.
Test various inheritance $$ by modeling the affects in the future - what if you recieve 100% of existing inheritance, then run 75%, 50%, 25% what do these models look like and how would those change your positioning now and in the next few years.
Additionally - the family may consider all of the affects of gifting well past the typical yearly maximums if that would be appropriate and positive.
