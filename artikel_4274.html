Tesla Cybertruck’s already impressive turning radius gets even tighter

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Tesla is rolling out an update that makes the Cybertruck turn even tighter, improving on what was already one of the best turning radiuses you can get in a full-size pickup.

But there’s a catch. It only works on 2027 model year Cybertrucks, and Tesla plans to stop building the truck by the end of next summer.

Tesla’s official Cybertruck account posted a short drone video early Saturday showing a truck doing circles in a parking lot, with a “before” and “after” comparison of its turning path. “Even tighter turns now rolling out to new Cybertrucks,” the account wrote.

In a follow-up post, Tesla said the improvement requires software version 2026.44 or newer and “only applies to model year 2027 Cybertrucks.”

Tesla didn’t share a new turning circle figure. In the video, the “after” circle is visibly smaller than the “before” one by what looks to be at least a foot.

The Cybertruck’s maneuverability comes from its rear-wheel steering paired with steer-by-wire. At low speeds, the rear wheels turn opposite to the fronts, which shrinks the space the truck needs to make a U-turn. We’ve been tracking the Cybertruck’s four-wheel steering since the prototype days, when Tesla was showing it off in parking garages.

Even before this update, the results were impressive. Tesla’s owner’s manual lists the Cybertruck’s curb-to-curb turning circle at about 43.5 ft (13.25 m). That beats the Ford F-150 Lightning at roughly 47.8 ft and the Rivian R1T at 44.9 ft, and it’s within about 4 ft of a Model Y. Not bad for a 6,600-lb truck that’s nearly 19 feet long.

And Tesla always said there was more to come. At launch, the rear wheels turned only about 3 degrees. In early 2024, a Tesla engineer said a software update would push that to 10 degrees, and Elon Musk said the company had improvements in the works to “tighten the turning circle significantly.”

Tesla hasn’t said whether this update delivers the full 10 degrees, or why it’s limited to 2027 trucks. If it comes down to hardware, owners of earlier Cybertrucks who were counting on this as an over-the-air upgrade are out of luck.

Credit where it’s due. The turning circle is one of the best things about the Cybertruck, and anyone who has driven one knows it. Steer-by-wire combined with rear-wheel steering is real engineering, and other vehicles are just starting to get that combo a couple years after Cybertruck had it. Making it even better is a nice update.

And 39.4 feet turning circle for the Cadillac Escalade which also has rear wheel steering. As does the iX5 (39.7'), MB GLC (36.7') and EQS (35.8'), the Lucid Gravity (38.4), Hummer 37.4', Cayenne (38.4) and a around two dozen other vehicles on the market today. So maybe this is not such big news after all?

Earlier this month, Musk announced that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas to make room for Cybercab. He has since pushed the end date to the end of summer 2027 to work through an “order backlog.” We estimate Tesla is delivering about 7,000 to 8,000 Cybertrucks a quarter, roughly 12% of the 250,000 units a year it planned for.

So Tesla is improving a 2027 model year truck that it will stop making next summer. And since Tesla now only sells the $109,990 Cyberbeast, the people getting this upgrade are the few still buying the most expensive Cybertruck in its final months. It does nothing about what actually sank the truck: pricing that never came close to the $39,900 promised in 2019, range and towing that fell short, and a design most truck buyers never warmed up to.

It’s a cool engineering flex on a product with an expiration date. If Tesla had delivered this kind of polish on the $40,000 truck it promised, would we be writing the Cybertruck’s obituary right now?
