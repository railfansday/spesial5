The pink tax is the extra charged for a product marketed to women when it differs from the men’s version only in color, scent, or packaging. Compare the price per ounce or per item of both versions, check that the ingredients match, and buy the cheaper one, whichever aisle you find it in.

When New York City’s Department of Consumer Affairs compared nearly 800 products sold in men’s and women’s versions in 2015, the women’s versions cost 7% more on average and 13% more for personal care. The widest gap was hair care, where women paid 48% more than men for shampoo and conditioner.

In a 2018 study of 10 personal care categories, the Government Accountability Office found women’s versions cost more in five, deodorant among them, but men’s cost more for shaving gel and nondisposable razors.

Some of the difference in the city’s study came from package size. It found men’s deodorant often sold in 2.7-ounce sticks and women’s in 2.6-ounce sticks at the same price, so compare the unit price, the cost per ounce or per item.

Say a pink four-pack of disposable razors costs $6 and the same razor in a blue six-pack costs $7.50. That’s $1.50 a razor against $1.25, so the blue pack is the cheaper buy. Then make sure the formulas match. For an antiperspirant, compare the active ingredient and strength on the Drug Facts label. For a shampoo, compare the first several ingredients, which are listed in order of amount.

In California and New York, a business can’t charge different prices for substantially similar products or services based on gender. A haircut or a dry-cleaning job can still cost more when it takes more time or skill. California’s Gender Tax Repeal Act also makes barbers, salons, tailors, and dry cleaners post prices for their standard services, and New York entitles you to a written price list on request from any service business.

Report a product priced by gender to California’s attorney general. In New York, file a complaint with the Division of Consumer Protection to seek back the difference you paid, or call its helpline at 1-800-697-1220.

Start with deodorant, the category both studies found pricier for women than for men, and compare the price per ounce of a men’s or unisex stick with the same active ingredient and strength.
