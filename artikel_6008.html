Trump officials reportedly at odds over tech titan's comeback as national security concerns linger

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

‘The Five’ weighs in on Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia following an affair scandal and his admission to additional affairs. The panel examines Democrats’ response, his welcome back to the company and questions about potential national security implications.

President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance helped bring Jensen Huang back to the helm of Nvidia — a move that has reportedly exposed divisions within the administration and prompted one expert to warn it is "sending the wrong message."

The push to get Huang his job back has reportedly put Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick at odds with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent over the handling of national-security concerns involving Huang’s relationship with a woman whose alleged ties to Chinese intelligence remain in dispute, as well as questions about advanced U.S. chips reaching China.

Vance reportedly worked with Nvidia's board over the weekend to get Huang his job back, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who reportedly warned Trump about intelligence concerning the relationship, took another approach. Rubio has not publicly weighed in on the CEO's reinstatement and his office did not respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.

The Vice President told CNN on Wednesday that Attorney General Todd Blanche assured him that Huang was not under investigation.

"But most importantly, at just, I think, a personal level, we received the assurances that he was not under any significant, he was under no investigation, and the accusations against him were based on anonymous reporting, not on actual evidence," he said.

Vance continued, saying that he didn't think it was appropriate anyone should lose their job "because anonymous sources accuse them of things in the New York Times."

The Nvidia saga reveals a "definitively split approach within the administration" toward China, as the company pressed for looser export controls, Max Meizlish of the Foundation for Defense of Democracies told Fox News Digital.

Nvidia said it received assurances from senior officials that Huang's reinstatement posed no national-security risk. But federal investigations were still underway into the Nvidia CEO's affair with a suspected Chinese spy and potential diversion of advanced U.S. chips to China when the president began his push.

The decision to reinstate Huang has left Republican lawmakers demanding to know what senior administration officials told Nvidia’s board that led them to reverse course less than two weeks after Huang's resignation.

The scrutiny intensified Monday when the Commerce Department added Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company tied to Huang’s former affair partner, to its trade blacklist after months of reported disagreement inside the administration over restricting the firm under investigation for allegedly helping divert Nvidia chips to the CCP.

Director of National Intelligence (DNI) Jay Clayton, however, said Sunday that investigations into Megaspeed and executive Alice Huang were ongoing, urging officials to "let that investigation play out" and "gather the facts."

Trump said Attorney General Todd Blanche confirmed the U.S. government had no evidence Huang engaged in unlawful conduct.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has pushed the Trump administration to ease restrictions on advanced AI chip sales to China, making the company’s technology a focal point in the U.S.-China race for AI leadership. (Kiyoshi Ota/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

But when asked Monday night whether Huang had been cleared of national-security concerns or violations of federal law, Blanche declined to say. He argued that the question assumed facts about what the DOJ was investigating and claimed "a lot of thoughtful process by administration officials" went into the decision to back restoring Huang.

Meizlish questioned the administration’s commitment to preventing chip diversion and told Fox News Digital that restoring Huang "is clearly sending the wrong message."

"The company itself certainly has a lot of questions to answer," he added.

Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive who had a relationship with Jensen Huang, is under scrutiny over alleged ties to Chinese intelligence. Megaspeed is being investigated over possible diversion of advanced chips to China.

Bessent said Nov. 13 that Treasury, the State Department, the Pentagon and the U.S. Trade Representative had "for months" supported adding Megaspeed to the Entity List, but conceded that "Commerce has the pen" to decide whether to take action.

Bessent said his deputy had been pushing for action. Prior reporting indicates Commerce officials resisted the move while other administration officials pressed for restrictions. Trump later acknowledged that Lutnick may have delayed action to avoid a "blowup with China."

Treasury did not respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment and Commerce did not respond when asked about reports the department dragged its feet on implementing export controls on Megaspeed.

Republican lawmakers are pressing for answers about what the administration knew before Nvidia’s board decided to bring Huang back.

Sen. John Curtis, R-Utah, wrote on X: "Who in our government weighed in? What did they tell the board? And on what basis did the board conclude there was no risk?"

Rep. Pat Harrigan, R-N.C., called Huang’s reinstatement a "mistake," warning it could "whitewash" unresolved national-security questions.

The Republican chairmen of the House committees on Intelligence, Foreign Affairs and China said the board’s "brief internal investigation and reinstatement" did not erase the threats and questions they are investigating. Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter are scheduled to appear for transcribed interviews with the committees Dec. 1 and Dec. 2.

The congressional scrutiny comes amid separate questions about why it took months to blacklist Megaspeed and whether the controls now in place go far enough.

Commerce added Megaspeed and six affiliates to the Entity List Monday, the same day Nvidia announced Huang’s return. The designation requires licenses for covered exports to the companies, with applications subject to a presumption of denial.

"Commerce will continue to vigorously enforce export controls, including these new Entity Listings," the department said. It explained the listing makes it unlawful to export U.S.-origin goods, software or technology to Megaspeed, and that advanced SI chips are also barred even when made overseas.

Former National Security Council official Chris McGuire criticized Commerce for not applying an entity-specific foreign-produced direct product rule, which can extend U.S. export controls to some foreign-made goods produced with U.S. technology. He said that without it, less advanced AI chips and components made overseas — including Nvidia H20 chips and high-bandwidth memory — could still be shipped to Megaspeed.

A Commerce official pushed back on McGuire’s characterization, telling Fox News Digital an entity-specific FDPR was unnecessary because the existing Advanced Computing FDPR already places advanced SI chips destined anywhere in the world under U.S. export controls.

Commerce’s on-the-record statement similarly said advanced SI chips are barred from Megaspeed even when made overseas. McGuire’s criticism focuses on what he says remains outside those rules, including less advanced chips and components.

Meizlish said resellers would still need a license to supply Megaspeed, with applications presumed denied.

Applying the entity-specific FDPR, he said, could further restrict the company’s access to foreign-made chips and components. Commerce did not specify whether H20 chips or the other components McGuire cited are covered by the existing rules.

The White House maintains that its policy has been consistent, with spokesman Matthew Middleton telling Fox News Digital that "America’s most advanced chips must go to U.S. SI innovators, not Chinese shell companies."

A White House official also said Trump has repeatedly ruled out allowing China to obtain Nvidia’s most advanced Blackwell and Rubin chips, and noted that Megaspeed has been under federal investigation since 2024 and Nvidia placed the company on its internal "no sell" list in June 2025.

Commerce said last week that the DOJ later took the lead in the criminal investigation. Neither department has announced charges against Megaspeed.

Lawmakers are set to question Huang and Nvidia’s top lawyer next month about the company’s handling of the controversy and the national-security concerns surrounding his return.
