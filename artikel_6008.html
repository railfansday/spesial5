Wyndham Rewards recently revised its fixed award chart, expanding from three to four redemption tiers.

The 15,000- and 30,000-point tiers remain, while free nights now start at 5,000 points instead of 7,500. Wyndham also added a 45,000-point tier for a select number of its highest-tier hotels and redistributed thousands of properties across all four tiers.

The new top tier is 50% higher than the former 30,000-point maximum, a devaluation in anyone's book. However, the new astonishingly low 5,000-point tier was what caught my attention. What exactly do you get for so few Wyndham Rewards points?

As someone who can't resist a great deal, I checked into one of these hotels to find out whether it was an amazing new sweet spot or a night I would rather forget.

On a recent visit to Kansas and Missouri, I checked the Wyndham Rewards website and saw that the area had several properties for just 5,000 points per night.

I chose the Super 8 by Wyndham Riverside/Kansas City, mainly for its proximity to the beautiful new Kansas City International Airport (MCI). The next morning, I was flying to Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) before continuing to Europe in a lie-flat seat for less than the price of a cup of coffee.

At the time of booking, the hotel had a 2.8-out-of-5 score based on 84 ratings on Wyndham's website, which did not fill me with confidence. However, the Wyndham Rewards member rate for my night was $96.21 including taxes and fees. Redeeming 5,000 points therefore gave me 1.92 cents per Wyndham point, about 3.5 times TPG's November 2026 valuation of 0.55 cents per Wyndham point.

I took a Lyft from downtown Kansas City, Missouri, to Riverside, a small suburb on the north bank of the Missouri River. First impressions of the property were that it was pretty remote, with nothing really nearby other than the Argosy Casino.

There was plenty of parking, and the 48 rooms were spread across a two-story motor-inn-style building.

While the property was less than 20 years old and was renovated in 2010, the rather garish lime-green lobby looked ready for another refresh.

I was quickly helped by a very friendly attendant, who I believe was the franchisee, and his wife, who also greeted me warmly.

There were several property rules listed on the counter with normal restrictions on smoking and pets in rooms.

What raised an eyebrow was the rule that visitors were allowed in guest rooms for only 60 minutes maximum, which made me wonder what kind of visitors might be passing through the doors and for what purpose in one-hour blocks.

The attendant thanked me for being a Wyndham Rewards member and quickly handed me my room key. So far, the experience had been better than the 2.8-out-of-5 rating suggested, but the room would be the biggest test.

I nervously opened the door to find a comfortable room with a king-size bed, plenty of pillows, a large flat-screen TV, a minifridge and a workspace.

Like the lobby, I doubt this decor will win any design awards, but it was clean, quiet and comfortable, and everything worked.

The separate bathroom had a tub/shower combo and a spotless toilet with several towels for me to choose from.

If I had one complaint about the room, it was that the showerhead was pulling away from the bathroom wall, but it still provided a steady flow of hot water.

The property was light on other amenities: There was no fitness center, swimming pool, bar or restaurant. However, the Argosy Casino was a few minutes' walk away, so there was plenty to keep me occupied for the evening.

After a good night's uninterrupted sleep, all guests even received a complimentary breakfast, with toast, cereals, juice and coffee available.

This is a great deal for a 5,000 point redemption with no resort fee.

The staff bid me a friendly farewell when I checked out, and I was on my way to the airport after a comfortable, peaceful and stress-free stay.

Wyndham Rewards are easy to earn, as they transfer at a 1:1 rate from the following transferable rewards:

But the best transfer option is for anyone with Wells Fargo Rewards points. This is because rather than transferring at a 1:1 rate, they transfer at a much more generous 1:2 rate. This means you would only need 2,500 Wells Fargo points to book a 5,000-point Wyndham Rewards hotel night, and Wells Fargo points can be transferred in any increments, unlike the strict 1,000-point increments of most transferable rewards.

Given that Wyndham Rewards properties offering 5,000-point nights regularly sell these nights for around $100 cash, this is an easy way to get around 4 cents per Wells Fargo point, more than twice TPG's valuation of Wells Fargo Rewards points.

Barclays also offers four Wyndham Rewards branded credit cards. Depending on the card and, in some cases, when it was opened, eligible cardholders receive a 10%, 20%, or 25% discount on free-night redemptions. That reduces a standard 5,000-point night to 4,500, 4,000 or 3,750 points, respectively.

Wyndham's new 5,000-point redemptions will not put you in a luxury hotel, but this stay was terrific value for money and delivered a clean, quiet room, a comfortable bed and a simple breakfast before an early flight.

The decor was dated, and the loose showerhead fixture needed attention. Even so, the friendly service and uninterrupted night's sleep were far better than the hotel's 2.8-out-of-5 rating had led me to expect. For a one-night stay near an airport, I would give it 4 out of 5.

Redeeming 5,000 Wyndham Rewards points instead of paying the nearly $100 member rate will give you 2 cents' worth per point. Even better, a traveler transferring from Wells Fargo Rewards would need just 2,500 points for the same award — I can't think of a more valuable way to use Wells Fargo points.

I would happily stay at a property like this again for just 5,000 Wyndham Rewards points per night.
