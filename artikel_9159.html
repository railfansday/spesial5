Drone Operator Films Manchester United Training Session and Leaks Video on YouTube

A drone operator filmed English soccer team Manchester United’s first training session with new coach Mauricio Pochettino and shared the “unethical” footage on YouTube.

Pochettino officially left the U.S. men’s national soccer team (USMNT) to become head coach of English Premier League club Manchester United. On Thursday, he oversaw his first training session with Man United’s first-team squad ahead of Sunday’s Premier League game against Liverpool at the club’s Carrington training ground.

However, a drone operator who runs the YouTube account CC Overview — which describes itself as sharing “drone footage of construction at football stadia and events affecting our environment” — flew his aerial device over the facility during Pochettino’s first training session. He then posted a 12-minute segment on YouTube on Thursday.

According to a report by The Athletic, Pochettino and his players, who were split into three groups, were identifiable in the aerial footage, which amassed over 600,000 views on YouTube. The footage showed Pochettino leading a training session with one group of players, while his assistant Jesus Perez oversaw two separate groups. The video, filmed at United’s Carrington training base, also captured audio of the YouTuber apparently controlling the drone saying “let’s get closer,” before zooming in on the new head coach.

The drone operator has since apologized for a “total error in judgment.” He says the aerial footage was legal under U.K. drone laws but has removed the “unethical” video from YouTube.

“A total error of judgement on my part led to me filming some of the Man United training session at Carrington, whilst the flight was legal in terms of drone laws I fully accept it was unethical on my part,” the drone operator writes in a post on YouTube.

The drone operator describes aiming to film Pochettino in the club’s training kit for the first time, but adds he had no intention of recording a “meaningful training session.” The YouTuber says he was operating under an incorrect assumption that it was open to media outlets. He also insists he acted independently and was not working on behalf of Liverpool or any other football club.

“I would like to apologize to Manchester United, Mauricio Pochettino and the players for my actions and sincerely hope those actions do not affect the outcome of any match,” the drone operator says.

Man United prohibits the use of drones at its Carrington training facility, where signs warn that anyone operating a drone could face civil action or prosecution.
