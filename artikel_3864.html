This week, amidst ongoing service disruptions, the US Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) disclosed that it is "investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems." The bureau, which collects and analyzes transaction data to combat financial crimes, says it has taken some of its e-filing systems offline "as a proactive measure."

FinCEN hasn't provided an estimate of when the investigation and system disruptions will be resolved, leaving the ability of financial institutions to report suspicious activity and interface with law enforcement indefinitely hampered. Meanwhile, as FinCEN is putting out fires, US Treasury chief information officer Sam Corcos has been busying himself with a different kind of work: He's been vibecoding a Civilization clone.

Corcos' LLM-powered game project was noticed by Bulwark economics editor Catherine Rampell: "The Treasury CIO appears to have spent the past three days vibecoding a Civilization game clone instead of working on this major Treasury database cyber breach," she wrote in a post on X. "As I type this, his Claude code is literally adding theme music."

A DOGE appointee who previously raised cybersecurity concerns at the IRS with an API "hackathon" and sought to cancel engineering efforts intended to modernize its systems, Corcos is hosting his little LLM project on Github. It's called Legatum, and he describes it as "historical 4X on a hex map, built for people who love Civilization."

As Corcos writes in its readme, Legatum is "largely" built with the use of AI coding agents, and all of its art is AI-generated. While FinCEN addresses its cybersecurity breach, Corcos and his Claude agents have been steadily pushing updates: At time of writing, the latest commit—added just six minutes ago—tweaked some of its AI art.

Among the other half-dozen or so commits in the last three hours are adjustments to map generation, music updates, and—let's see—attempts at stemming "a wave of political issues and README vandalism" left by Github users.

That automated moderation evidently still needs some optimizing, judging from the repo's Issue #220, which reads: "Lead developer is a giant asshole." Another issue notes that "late game American civilization feels broken," because "when you get the Large Language Model tech unlocked, it pretty much destroys any chance of a cultural victory because all your artists are gone, and all your government buildings become totally useless."

FinCEN isn't the only US government organization suffering cybersecurity issues this week. Yesterday, The New York Times reported that the CIA, the Office of the Director of National Security, and the Department of Defense's National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency had been impacted by a vulnerability in an open source intelligence system, which exposed unclassified information.

On X, Corcos has addressed the questionable optics of overseeing a vibecoded Civ knockoff while his department's facing a cybersecurity intrusion: "Just a heads up, the computers are doing it on their own," Corcos said in a reply to a user who compared the incident to Nero fiddling while Rome burned. "Mostly Opus 5.5 orchestrating a bunch of iterative loops."

In another reply to a more sympathetic user, Corcos was more enthusiastic: "These tools are getting so good it's blowing my mind. A brief voice note on my phone can kick off an amount of work that would have taken dozens of people weeks to deliver," he said. "So cool."
