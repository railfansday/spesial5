Tom Hiddleston's Near-Perfect Apple TV Miniseries Gets Better With Every Episode

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Period dramas span a wide range of shows, including everything from romances to war stories. This variety gives fans almost too many options to watch, and though several have become well known, others inevitably fly under the radar. One such example is Apple TV's The Essex Serpent. The 2022 miniseries is an unpredictable entry in the genre, but even four years out from its release, it's one that period drama fans shouldn't miss.

Adapted from Sarah Perry's novel of the same name, the Apple TV miniseries stars Claire Danes and Tom Hiddleston, whose performances set the show apart. Beyond its recognizable leading duo, the series distinguishes itself with a slowly building tension as the story unfolds and shifts, making it impossible to stop watching. Consisting of just six episodes, The Essex Serpent is an easy binge for genre fans that will not disappoint.

The Essex Serpent is a Victorian-era gothic romance centered around Cora Seaborne (Danes), a newly widowed woman and a paleontology enthusiast. Rather than mourning the death of her abusive husband, Cora uses her newfound freedom to indulge her scientific interests by investigating reports of the Essex Serpent, a mythical creature said to be preying on the village of Aldwinter. Packing up her son, Frankie (Michael Jibson), and her companion, Martha (Hayley Squires), Cora travels to Aldwinter to see what she can uncover.

There can never be too many period dramas.

Once in Aldwinter, Cora learns that the residents fully believe the creature is responsible for local tragedies — except for the village's skeptical vicar, Will (Hiddleston), who forges an intellectual bond with Cora. The possibility of anything more is risky, considering Will is already married to Stella (Clémence Poésy), yet the love triangle that forms between them holds its own surprises. As locals disappear and disaster mounts, the villagers grow suspicious of Cora despite her attempts to help. Rather than the creature story it initially seems to be, The Essex Serpent focuses on deeper themes to create a character-driven narrative with slowly building tension.

The Essex Serpent's main narrative obstacle isn't the potential existence of the titular creature itself, but the dangerous hysteria in Aldwinter as well as the complicated romance between Cora and Will, both of which take time to build. Ultimately, the characters are the story's greatest strength, with the series relying on their complex emotional journeys to carry the show. Thanks to the talented cast, especially standouts Danes and Hiddleston, the miniseries explores layered people that make The Essex Serpent a rewarding watch.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

While there's plenty to set The Essex Serpent apart, Danes' character is a highlight. Subverting the expectations of a traditional Victorian heroine, Cora's deep belief in science and natural skepticism make her compelling to watch. Additionally, the story makes no secret of her abusive marriage, but allows her to escape following her husband's death and pursue her own intellectual interests. The Essex Serpent may have been overlooked, but it isn't lacking in surprising twists and turns, and its short format rewards any period drama fan looking for a thrilling weekend binge.
