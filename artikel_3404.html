Florence Pugh’s Steinbeck Adaptation Officially Passes Massive Netflix Milestone

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

East of Eden has reaped a bountiful harvest for Netflix. Not only has the new adaptation of the classic John Steinbeck novel been a critical success, but it's still garnering new viewers. The seven-episode miniseries, which stars Florence Pugh, has just passed a major milestone.

Based on Steinbeck's bestselling 1952 novel, East of Eden is a generational saga that centers around Cathy Ames (Pugh) and Adam Trask (Christopher Abbott). The two meet in the late 19th century when Cathy is found half-dead by half-brothers Adam and Charles (Mike Faist). Trying to overcome their respective dark pasts, Cathy and Adam marry and travel west to California. However, Cathy can't defy her true nature, and abandons Adam with their twin sons, Caleb and Aron, shortly after their births. When the two grow to manhood, they must reckon with the ghosts of their past.

After being released on October 1, East of Eden has now spent six consecutive weeks on Netflix's top ten list. It's an impressive feat for a miniseries based on a novel that's already been adapted into an Oscar-winning film, back in 1955. In fact, there's a familial connection between the two adaptations: Zoe Kazan, who created, co-wrote, co-showran, and produced the miniseries, is the granddaughter of Elia Kazan, who directed the film. It currently holds an 88% Fresh rating on Rotten Tomatoes; Collider's Terese Lacson gave it a rave review, calling it "a daring new perspective on the familiar story."

Kazan and co-showrunner Jeb Stuart are moving on from East of Eden to take on a task that's almost as daunting: Larry McMurtry's classic revisionist Western Lonesome Dove. The Pulitzer Prize-winning 1985 novel was already adapted into a much-beloved miniseries in 1989 with Tommy Lee Jones and Robert Duvall. Stuart spoke with Collider about taking on McMurtry's magnum opus, and what the new Netflix adaptation will do that the original didn't: "I have great respect for the original miniseries, and obviously the novel is like East of Eden. It's a big, bold American novel that's...also something you could use as a doorstop, it's 900 pages. Because it is 900 pages, there's a lot they didn't get into that four-episode miniseries that was, you know, Robert Duvall's masterpiece."

East of Eden is now streaming on Netflix. Stay tuned to Collider for future updates.
