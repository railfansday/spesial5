6 Horror Series Better Than American Horror Story

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

From TV powerhouse Ryan Murphy, American Horror Story's first season, Murder House, drew immediate attention with its all-star cast that included Connie Britton, Dylan McDermott, Jennifer Lange, Sarah Paulson, and Frances Conroy. Even more importantly, Murder House was genuinely compelling, balancing horror, drama, and emotional depth in one self-contained season.

At the time, a horror anthology felt both unique and timeless, evoking nostalgia for series like The Twilight Zone while still delivering something modern and fresh. For the next several years, American Horror Story continued to successfully reinvent itself, attracting stars like Lady Gaga, Kathy Bates, and Angela Bassett, and making new ones out of the likes of Evan Peters and Emma Roberts.

In its prime, the anthology format seemed to promise that American Horror Story could live forever. Eventually, though, the show began to feel like a caricature of itself, relying on the allure of fan-favorite actors and buzzy pieces of stunt casting. The show's horror became more campy and less genuinely scary, and new stories increasingly connected to previous ones in attempts to recapture the original magic.

This reached an unprecedented level in the show's most recent season, 13, which was a massive crossover of American Horror Story's first five seasons, as well as Cult and Apocalypse​​​. The thinnest of storylines connected the ambitious mashup, and even as fan-favorite actors reprised their most iconic roles, 13 felt like it forgot the show's own plot, making it a shell of what American Horror Story once was.

Though American Horror Story was once a pillar of the horror television landscape, these six shows have far surpassed the example it set.

Today, Mike Flanagan is the king of horror television. With hits ranging from the original Midnight Mass to the latest adaptation of Stephen King's Carrie, the writer and showrunner has proven that he is far from a one-hit wonder when it comes to telling scary stories on TV. His very first TV projects, though, felt truly like an elevated American Horror Story.

The Haunting of Hill House, inspired by Shirley Jackson's novel of the same name, was an immediate hit that put Netflix on the map for its horror limited series. Both terrifying and emotionally poignant, Hill House took extensive creative liberties from its source material to tell a story that felt simultaneously gothic and contemporary.

The same could be said of Flanagan's next limited series, The Haunting of Bly Manor. While Bly Manor was technically its own, separate show, it shared a title format, theme music, and tagline ("You're expected") with Hill House, setting the stage for a Haunting of... anthology of modernized adaptations of gothic horror literature.

Like Hill House, Bly Manor took a character-first approach without losing the genuine chills of a ghost story. In the end, Bly Manor proved to be truly haunting in every sense of the word, making the two projects the gold standard of contemporary self-contained horror.

Something Very Bad Is Going to Happen was Netflix's first major horror limited series made in the wake of Mike Flanagan's migration to Prime Video. The limited series, produced by the Duffer Brothers, proved that the streamer was capable of maintaining the high expectations of quality set by Flanagan's works.

The creepy story of Jerry Poole and Coldies Frozen Custard plays a more important role in Something Very Bad Is Going to Happen than you may think.

At first, Something Very Bad Is Going to Happen relied primarily on its unsettling tone as Rachel (Camila Morrone) prepared for her wedding with a gut feeling that something wasn't right. As it went on, though, the show became a rich, intertwined story of love, family dysfunction, and tall tales coming to life.

Morrone's down-to-earth and utterly likable performance set the groundwork for her character's intuition to be taken seriously, while Jennifer Jason Leigh and Adam DiMarco rounded out the cast with similarly compelling performances. In the end, Something Very Bad Is Going to Happen delivered on the promise of its title in a way that felt earned and full of equal doses of heartache and horror.

Netflix's latest record-breaking acquired series deserves its place in the streamer's Top 10. Set in a quaint yet terrifying, inescapable town, From is among the most memorable folk horror series made in recent years. Complete with flesh-eating nocturnal monsters, time travel, and an ancient, shapeshifting trickster seemingly behind it all, From utilizes classic horror imagery in a completely new way.

While its strange premise and laundry list of unsolved mysteries have led to frequent comparisons to Lost, From is definitively scarier, giving just as much attention to the genuine terror that faces the townspeople as the overarching mystery of why they've been brought there in the first place.

While the vampire-adjacent monsters offer ample nightmare fuel in and of themselves, From also taps into a more psychological horror with the promise that if the townspeople were to fail to solve the show's mystery, their demise would be at their own hands. Throughout the series, the Township has worked to drain its residents of hope and instead sow the seeds of division and violence among them.

Bates Motel served as a prequel to Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho, yet it was set in the present day. The temporal disconnect gave the show an odd, off-kilter feel from the very start, and Bates Motel continued to keep viewers on their toes despite the conclusion promised by the 1960 movie.

Vera Farmiga, Nestor Carbonell, and Olivia Cooke all delivered memorable performances that greatly expanded the world created in Psycho, but the true standout was Freddie Highmore's work as Norman Bates. Highmore's Norman was complex, multifaceted, and equal parts endearing and dangerous.

His descent to the state the character is seen in Psycho was a slow burn of impressive proportions, and the subtlety of his mannerisms and behavior created a believable confusion around Norman's state of mind. Though it ran for five successful seasons, Bates Motel deserves a lasting legacy within the horror genre.

Its vampire-centric premise makes True Blood immediately brings American Horror Story's Coven​​​​​​ to mind, but comparing the two actually cements how much more gritty, grounding, and emotionally resonant HBO's 2007 series really was.

With a plot involving vampires, werewolves, shapeshifters, fairies, and witches, True Blood's premise was high fantasy to the extreme, yet in execution, the show was actually a powerful character study and cultural exploration of the American South. Even while dating vampires and werewolves, Anna Paquin's Sookie Stackhouse was, first and foremost, a resilient, likable, and completely believable Louisiana girl.

Paquin's performance gave True Blood its down-to-earth tone, but the show also broadened its scope as it explored themes of religious prejudice, queerness in the South, and female independence. True Blood was often silly, but it also created a serious and genuine world that felt like it could, somehow, exist within our own.

Arguably the biggest breakout series of 2026, Widow's Bay has offered an entirely new take on the horror-comedy genre mashup. Rather than watering down its scares with jokes, Widow's Bay​​​​​'s first season was both laugh-out-loud funny and genuinely eerie.

The cursed town of Widow's Bay poses a very real threat to its residents, yet the depiction of how those residents respond to that threat takes a tone akin to a workplace comedy. Matthew Rhys' self-proclaimed first major foray into comedy is a phenomenal acting feat as he imbues the bumbling Mayor Tom Loftis with pitch-perfect line delivery and a heartfelt, if not misguided, passion for bettering the town.

Widow's Bay's season 1 finale raised the stakes on both a plot and character level, setting the stage for the show's sophomore season to be just as much of an event as its first. All in all, Widow's Bay is a testament to the limitless potential the horror genre holds today.
