Armstrong is Everton's running man and Moyes believes he can get better

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In this exclusive interview with David Moyes, the Everton manager discusses the development of Harrison Armstrong since being recalled from a loan spell at Preston North End and the reasons why he believes the 19-year-old midfielder can still improve...

Football Features Writer @ghostgoal

Harrison Armstrong has started every Premier League game for Everton this season, ranking in the top three for distance covered in the competition going into the weekend. This has been some year for a player who started 2026 on loan at Preston North End.

There was talk of a big move to Nottingham Forest in the summer and interest from elsewhere remains as Everton try to persuade him to sign a new contract. Supporters will be desperate for one of their own to stay. And David Moyes feel the same way.

"He is a big player for us," the Everton manager tells Sky Sports. "For a 19-year-old young midfield player he is doing really well. I hope he can continue to do that." That recall from Preston in January already feels like a pivotal moment in Armstrong's career.

"I was watching him quite regularly. He was doing really well and we had to bring him back. They did not like it obviously because Preston were going really well. But we had got so many injuries around December time that we needed to bring him back."

In truth, it was not ideal for Armstrong in some respects. "I think if he had got another 20 games with them it would have been good for him." But there were opportunities off the bench and the fact that he did not have things all his own way will have helped him too.

There were substitute appearances against Arsenal and Manchester City when he was brought on to help tighten things up only for Everton to concede late. But even those experiences are useful. "He is a confident boy. He has got a lot of belief in himself."

Most importantly, he has responded. "Since then he has stepped up again." Speaking to Moyes at Everton's training ground, he acknowledges that it was Armstrong's work in the summer that made him sit up and take notice. "He was very good in pre-season."

Moyes explains: "There was no way we could really put him back out on loan this year. We knew that we would find more minutes for him. But his form in pre-season games was the reason which made me think that we were certainly centred around him."

Circumstances helped. "We lost Jimmy Garner with a hernia operation in the opening games of the season, which left Hayden Hackney, who had just arrived and was still carrying a bit of an injury from last season. So it gave Harrison the opportunity to start."

In Moyes' words, it was a chance "to show what he could do" and Armstrong has done just that. "He has taken it with both hands." The youngster's obvious enthusiasm instantly endeared him to an Everton crowd that demand work rate from their players.

Those distance-covered statistics stand out but Armstrong sets the tone for Everton with his off-the-ball work. "You have got to have some attributes which are really important and that one is really important for him and for us," Moyes explains.

The result is that even with Garner and Hackney fit, he has stayed in. "Without doubt he has earned the right to be in the team. He has earned the right by his performances every week because we have got competition. But if you are doing as well as he is…"

Of course, Moyes remains the archetypal tough-taskmaster and it is only natural that the 63-year-old Scot opts to couch his praise for the player with a reminder that Armstrong must add more to his game if he is to fulfil his potential in the long term.

"There are things we want him to improve on. He has still got a bit to learn." What exactly? "We would like him to do a bit better with his passing at times and I certainly want him to score more goals or get more opportunities. They would be the things."

But there is optimism that he can add these elements. "We must not forget that he is a 19-year-old midfield player and very much learning his trade." Moyes knows. He has overseen the development of top-class teenagers from Wayne Rooney to Declan Rice.

What is his verdict on how good Armstrong could go on to become? "I think that he can develop into a really top-notch midfield player," adds Moyes. It has been a breakout year for him in the Premier League. But this could be just the start for Harrison Armstrong.
