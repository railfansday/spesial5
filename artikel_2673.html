NFL Power Rankings entering Week 11: Can the Browns keep winning in dramatic fashion?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

NFC East: Cowboys | Giants | Eagles | Commanders NFC North: Bears | Lions | Packers | Vikings NFC South: Falcons | Panthers | Saints | Buccaneers NFC West: Cardinals | Rams | 49ers | Seahawks

AFC East: Bills | Dolphins | Patriots | Jets AFC North: Ravens | Bengals | Browns | Steelers AFC South: Texans | Colts | Jaguars | Titans AFC West: Broncos | Chiefs | Raiders | Chargers

The Cleveland Browns have been through this type of season before. It has just been a long time.

In 1979, the Browns had seven fourth-quarter drives to win games. In the 1980 season they were 11-5 and nine of the wins came by one score. That group got the nickname of the Kardiac Kids. Brian Sipe became a Cleveland legend, and won the 1980 NFL MVP award.

The 2026 Browns aren't at that level yet, but they're getting close. After rallying to beat the Houston Texans on a touchdown with 53 seconds left, the Browns are the first team in NFL history to win each of its first six games by four or fewer points, according to ESPN Insights. Every one of those wins has included a game-winning drive in the fourth quarter. It has been a magical, improbable run.

"We're going to figure something out about starting a hell of a lot faster," Browns head coach Todd Monken said, according to the team's transcripts. "But our guys continue to fight, and they battle, and they don't, they don't blink. Which is probably more important than starting fast, is when you have guys that continue to fight."

Whether it's sustainable might be irrelevant. Suddenly, the 6-4 Browns are in great shape to make the playoffs.

With the New England Patriots likely in for a big drop after Drake Maye's ankle injury and the Browns knocking the Texans back while also clinching the head-to-head tiebreaker, Cleveland's playoff hopes got a lot brighter Sunday. There was another assist on Monday night, when the previously winless Chargers beat the Ravens, knocking Baltimore back to 4-6. The Browns would be the seventh seed if the season ended today and they're a game up on any other team in the running. It's hard to find a team behind them — Patriots, Ravens, Raiders, Texans — that's going to make a big push. Three of those teams' quarterbacks are either injured or traded and 3-6 Houston is miles behind now because of the tiebreaker.

In Week 1, the Browns were blown out 34-10 by the Jaguars and there were plenty of jokes about how they were already tanking for a quarterback. Now Cleveland is 6-4 heading into a well deserved bye week.

"Isn't that cool? Isn't that awesome?" Monken said. "I mean, holy s***. There's no other way to put it, man."

There are still seven games to go and like Monken said, the Browns have to figure out how to play better in the first three quarters of games to win enough to get a playoff berth. But they have already stacked six wins and are in the clear lead for a wild-card spot. Cleveland fans couldn't have expected a playoff berth, but a fun start to the season has the Browns well within range.

Here are the NFL power rankings after Week 10:

One of the signs of a good team is the ability to blow out bad teams. The Jaguars' 40-23 win over the Titans was their fifth win this season by 17 or more points, and it came with Jacksonville gambling that it could give trade additions defensive tackle Jordan Davis and safety Jessie Bates III the week off. In more than half of Jacksonville's games, it has throttled its opponent. The Jags' point differential of +125 is 37 points better than the Bills, who are second.

The Jaguars haven't played a tough schedule, but it actually gets easier. Their remaining games against teams with a winning record come against the Giants, Bears and Steelers. The Jaguars are going to put up a very good record and are just hoping the other top teams in the AFC, most notably Buffalo, lose some.

The 49ers cooked the Cowboys' defense, putting up 46 points and 584 yards. Dallas' defense is dreadful but it's still hard to pile up close to 600 yards in any NFL game.

The way the 49ers have climbed to the top of the NFL in yards per game is worth studying. Brock Purdy was famously Mr. Irrelevant in the draft and now he's an MVP candidate. Christian McCaffrey was at a point in which age and touches should have started to chip away at his effectiveness, but he is tied for the NFL lead with 13 total touchdowns. Draft experts laughed at the Kaelon Black pick in the third round; he's one of the best backup running backs in the league. Deebo Samuel Sr.'s career looked like it might be over after last season, but he has five touchdowns in six games. The 49ers had to give up only a sixth-round draft pick for Keenan Allen in a trade and he had five catches for 61 yards on Sunday.

The conclusion: Kyle Shanahan is your NFL Coach of the Year.

Beating the Jets doesn't seem to be that impressive, but it was to Bills head coach Joe Brady. His explanation made sense once he laid out that Buffalo had short rest before a road game after beating a great Vikings defense in Minneapolis last Monday night.

"This is the best win we've had this season," Brady said, via the team's site.

"We're getting back real early Tuesday morning [from Minnesota] to be able to reset and get their bodies ready to go to go back on the road."

It absolutely was impressive for the Bills to win 31-12 in a letdown spot, no matter the competition. The Bills host the Dolphins this weekend before a big showdown against the Chiefs on Thanksgiving.

The Bengals' performance Sunday night might have been the most impressive of the NFL season. Critics doubted the Steelers' 6-2 start, but they ranked sixth in DVOA coming into Sunday night's game. And the Bengals made them look like a bottom-feeding Conference USA opponent in town for homecoming. The Bengals outgained the Steelers 337-40 in the first half, which shouldn't happen against any competent NFL team. Cincinnati looked like a legitimate Super Bowl contender. It now ranks No. 1 in DVOA this season.

Joe Burrow had 302 passing yards and three touchdowns by halftime. If the Steelers had kept it competitive, Burrow might have challenged Norm Van Brocklin's longstanding NFL record of 554 passing yards in a game. Burrow finished with 409 passing yards. When asked what kind of quarterback he was on Sunday night, Burrow said, "A great one." The Bengals are feeling good, and rightfully so. The biggest key might be the offensive line, which allowed one sack on 46 dropbacks from Burrow.

"They've been unbelievable," Burrow said, via the team's transcripts.

The Bears didn't do anything wrong on their bye week. They didn't "move down." The Bengals just moved up after one of the most impressive games of the season.

Lingering for the Bears is their home defeat to the Vikings in Week 2. They lost 9-3, and based on how the Vikings looked with Kirk Cousins in Week 10, that could be a problem. The Bears play at Minnesota in Week 18 and that's looking like an early candidate to be flexed to the final Sunday night slot.

Chicago is 0-2 in the division (Minnesota is 3-1) and that could also be bad for tiebreakers. The Lions suffered a massive injury to Aidan Hutchinson on Sunday and the Packers lost ground with a bad loss, so the NFC North could evolve into a two-team race. And the Bears might need to win in Minneapolis in Week 18 to take the division.

The Vikings blew out the Packers without three key players: linebacker Blake Cashman, receiver Jordan Addison and tight end T.J. Hockenson. Kirk Cousins is 38 and there should be skepticism whether he can turn the Vikings into a Super Bowl threat, but everything looked much better in Week 10. And receiver Justin Jefferson had a surprisingly self-aware comment on where the Vikings stand.

"All we as an offense have to do is be mediocre at this point," Jefferson said, via The Athletic. "The defense is creating so much pressure."

He's not wrong. The Vikings were fierce on Sunday, pressuring Jordan Love 22 times on 35 dropbacks, according to Pro Football Focus. Love was sacked eight times. The Vikings weren't tremendous on offense with 312 yards, but as Jefferson pointed out, that might be enough.

The Chiefs also didn't do anything to "move down" in a thorough 35-13 win over the Falcons. The Vikings needed to be adjusted after seeing them play well with Kirk Cousins. At this point it's fair to wonder if the Chiefs are being underrated, which is strange for a team that has been in the spotlight so much over the Patrick Mahomes era. There isn't much evidence to suggest they should be higher. They're eighth in DVOA. They're sixth in Super Bowl odds. They're sixth in offensive and defensive EPA. They're clearly good, but are they Super Bowl contender good?

A big piece of that answer could be Josh Downs. He had four catches for 53 yards and a touchdown just five days after he was traded to the Chiefs. Xavier Worthy was shut out, and we can finally close the book on him making any meaningful impact. It's just not going to happen. The Chiefs need something extra in the offense, and perhaps it's Downs, who always seemed to have more potential and the Colts couldn't unlock it. Mahomes had 348 passing yards and five touchdowns on Sunday and perhaps that's a sign of a hot streak coming.

It's hard to find an advanced metric that indicates the Broncos should be higher than this. They're 14th in point differential, ninth in DVOA, 15th in net EPA as of last week and also a stunning 30th in yards per game on offense and 27th in yards allowed per game on defense. Their Super Bowl odds are tied for ninth best, for what that's worth. But here's why that doesn't matter: Denver is 7-2 after stacking (close) wins and has the easiest remaining schedule in the NFL, via DVOA, after playing the hardest schedule to this point in the season.

Here's what Denver has coming up: vs. Raiders, at Steelers, vs. Dolphins, at Jets, at Raiders, vs. Bills, at Patriots, vs. Chargers. That's two playoff contenders (assuming Drake Maye's injury sinks the Patriots) of eight remaining games. Like last season, the Broncos could end up with 13 or 14 wins and an army of naysayers wondering how good they really are.

The Sam Darnold/Drew Lock debate is flawed. Lock's three starts came twice against the Cardinals and vs. the Raiders. Arizona and Las Vegas have two of the worst defenses in the NFL. Still, Lock has played well and Darnold has been prone to turnovers. Lock hasn't thrown an interception and Darnold has nine despite missing three games and almost all of a fourth. But there isn't any indication from Seahawks head coach Mike Macdonald that he's considering a change.

"Drew's been fantastic," Macdonald said Monday on Seattle Sports 710, via the Seattle Times. "Drew's been phenomenal. But Sam's our quarterback."

That's the correct answer. Lock has played well and if Darnold continues to struggle, the Seahawks can turn to him later in the season. It would be premature to make a switch based on three games against bad opponents.

The Rams have won four in a row. They're still more likely than not to miss the playoffs — 44.2% chance to make it, via DVOA — because they have by far the NFL's toughest remaining schedule. But the Rams played well, albeit against a bad Cardinals team using rookie quarterback Carson Beck in his first career start. The Rams won 34-6 by running it 46 times for 202 yards. Konata Mumpfield provided some hope at receiver with Puka Nacua and Davante Adams out, with three catches for 81 yards. L.A.'s defense limited Arizona to six first downs.

It's still a hard path to the playoffs because of the schedule and injuries, but the Rams are hanging around.

"I think it's really tested our team," head coach Sean McVay said of having to find injury replacements. "But it's also shown there's a lot of guys that are capable of, hey let's instill belief and then let's go have that confidence earned through evidence."

The Lions' season changed with the reported ACL injury to defensive end Aidan Hutchinson. They were already without linebacker Jack Campbell, arguably the second-best player on the defense, and Detroit lost its unquestioned star because of a sack celebration. That's brutal. Derrick Moore and DJ Wonnum are capable vets and will fill in, but they're nowhere near Hutchinson's level.

"Any time you lose a player of his caliber, the type of teammate he is — he's a captain of ours — that doesn't make things easy," Lions head coach Dan Campbell said, according to NFL.com.

The Lions won on Sunday without Hutchinson for most of the game to improve to 7-2, and perhaps they have enough wins banked to make the playoffs in what will be a difficult wild-card race. But their hopes of being a Super Bowl contender are likely dashed without their two best defensive players.

The Packers' bye comes at a good time. They were playing very well before Sunday, when they were crushed at home by the Vikings. The break gives the Packers a chance to regroup, and also perhaps get Micah Parsons back for a game against the Rams the day before Thanksgiving. The Packers should rightly believe that Parsons will transform their team upon his return.

But there are concerns after Sunday. The offensive line was in shambles against the Vikings after showing improvement over a few weeks. The Vikings are a tough matchup, but Jordan Love can't get hit 12 times. The running game also disappeared without Josh Jacobs as Green Bay had just 56 yards on 17 carries. There are some things to clean up before a massive game in the wild-card race against the Rams.

Was Sunday's outing just a bad night against a hot Bengals team or a bad sign for the Steelers going forward? It's OK to excuse it as a one-off. Before the 38-18 blowout loss, the Steelers were 6-2 and showing improvement. It's also OK to worry that Pittsburgh was exposed, though Aaron Rodgers didn't think so when asked if the loss changes the season's trajectory.

"That's very dramatic," Rodgers said, via the team's site. "I've been a part of this league for a long time. There's big wins, there's tough losses, so we'll deal with this properly."

One thing that is an issue for the Steelers is their receiver room. DK Metcalf continued to have minimal impact, as he was held without a catch before garbage time in the fourth quarter. Germie Bernard was blanked and has just 57 yards in Pittsburgh's last three games. No other healthy receiver on the roster has more than 39 yards this season. Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. could both return in the next couple weeks, and that is badly needed.

One developing story is what to do with Deshaun Watson. Those who log quarterback wins will say he is 6-3 this season. That's misguided, but many people follow that logic. Watson is 20th in adjusted EPA per play and he has a middling 89.1 passer rating. That's 20th among quarterbacks with at least 100 attempts. The Browns have had some fun comeback wins and it will be interesting to see if Cleveland overreacts to that and extends Watson, whose contract has been one of the worst in the NFL.

Certainly, the Browns would rather not have to come back and win every game, as they've been doing.

"I wish collectively him and I together would play better early and coach better early and we didn't have to get to this," Browns head coach Todd Monken said, via the team's transcripts. "And it's not just been them. It's been our team, last week the same way."

The Buccaneers looked to have a tough four-game stretch after the bye when the schedule came out. It has gotten much easier. Their next four opponents are the Lions (lost Aidan Hutchinson and Jack Campbell to injuries between Weeks 8 and 10), Panthers (lost three in a row before a win Sunday), Chargers (a stunningly bad start to the season) and Ravens (lost quarterbacks Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley to injuries). That's why forecasting schedule strength can be difficult.

Now the Buccaneers have to take advantage of their good fortune. They're up to a 60.9% chance to win the division, via DVOA, in part because their defense ranks first in the NFL in that metric and they have the third-easiest remaining schedule.

The Giants are still in fine shape to win the NFC East because the Cowboys seem incapable of getting a stop on defense and the Commanders are flawed too. But blowing Thursday night's game against Washington keeps the pressure on them. The Giants had two chances to put the game away with the lead but went three-and-out each time. Then J.J. McCarthy threw an interception to end the game.

Otherwise, McCarthy played pretty well. He was 23-of-36 passing for 293 yards. Since Week 6, which was his first significant playing time with the Giants, he's 10th in adjusted EPA per play among quarterbacks with at least 100 plays. He hasn't been perfect but he's still young, and joining a team in-season and becoming a starter is a tough assignment.

"I thought he played very well," Giants head coach John Harbaugh said after the loss to the Commanders, according to the team's transcripts. "I mean, there were some plays made, he made some throws, scrambled. I thought J.J. played very well."

The Panthers paid edge rusher Jaelan Phillips $120 million over four years, and at that price point Phillips should be one of the biggest impact players in the NFL. On Sunday, he finally was. Phillips had three tackles for loss, including a sack, and the Panthers tied their season best by allowing 222 yards in a win over the Saints.

The Panthers' defense, along with a huge game from running back Chuba Hubbard, was the difference in a major victory in the NFC South race. It was Phillips' first game back after missing three due to a hamstring injury.

"Obviously there's been a lot of adversity, a lot of change, things happening, and we deserve some good things happening to us, so it was really good to get out there and play well." Phillips said, via the team's site.

Sunday was the fifth time this season that the Texans have given up a drive in the final minutes of regulation or overtime to lose a game. Houston was supposed to be one of the best defenses in the NFL. The Texans were second in EPA per play allowed last season. This season they're 20th, despite having mostly the same personnel and coaches.

While defensive success can fluctuate from season to season more than offensive success, the Texans' decline is perplexing. If Houston finished just two of those five games, it would be above .500.

"We have to play better — run defense, have to tackle better," head coach DeMeco Ryans said, via Texans Wire.

Fernando Mendoza made plenty of mistakes in his first NFL start, but he faced a Seattle defense that is among the top three in the NFL. Mendoza was 17-of-29 passing for 143 yards, and the biggest issue was that he took six sacks.

Mendoza will get better, though his next two matchups are also tough at Denver and at Cleveland. The schedule will get easier, but one of the concerns of the Kirk Cousins trade was shoving Mendoza into the lineup during a very difficult stretch. That was evident during a tough matchup against Seattle.

"It's good to get punched in the face, because now it's a great opportunity to grow and learn and to really unveil all the deficiencies that I have in my game, as a future hopeful leader of this offense," Mendoza said, via the Raiders' site.

Linebacker Sonny Styles is going to make a case for Defensive Rookie of the Year. Against the Giants, the seventh overall pick of the draft had 13 tackles. His biggest tackle came on a third-and-3 right after the two-minute warning when he shot through for a tackle for loss on a J.J. McCarthy designed quarterback run. Styles read that play because the Giants had run it in Week 5, which is high-level awareness from a rookie.

That tackle allowed Washington to get the ball back, and Jayden Daniels led a game-winning drive that kept the Commanders' season alive. Styles is up to 80 tackles on the season and he is used often as a blitzer. He has one interception and one sack. He is looking like a great first-round pick.

The Patriots took a big drop in the rankings and it might not be far enough. Drake Maye suffered what the Patriots fear is a fractured ankle, per ESPN. At the very least he'll be out a while and the most likely outcome seems to be that his season is over.

That turns the offense over to Tommy DeVito. It has been a long time since he had a few decent games as a rookie for the Giants in 2023 and became a fleeting cult figure. He looked entirely overmatched Sunday after Maye left the game. He had 81 yards on 24 attempts against a bad Lions defense. It's hard to see how the Patriots keep their season afloat without Maye, unless DeVito somehow plays way better than he did Sunday or the Patriots find some very unexpected help off the street.

There's not much more that can be said about the Cowboys' defense. Even noted Cowboys fan Post Malone was absolutely fed up. After allowing 584 yards in a 46-40 loss to the 49ers, Brian Schottenheimer made perhaps his harshest comments on the team since becoming head coach.

"I thought the second half was unacceptable," Schottenheimer said, according to the team's site. "Tackling was atrocious. We're going to do a deep dive and see why."

It's not like the Cowboys' offense has been the 1999 Rams or anything: fifth in points scored, eighth in yards gained, fifth in EPA per play and sixth in DVOA. That still should be enough to win a poor division. The defense is ruining the Cowboys' season, though DVOA still gives Dallas a 32.4% chance to win the NFC East.

The Ravens lost but can't put all the blame on quarterback injuries. Jacoby Brissett had 340 yards and mostly played well. But the running game was invisible on Monday night against the Chargers. Derrick Henry had just 10 yards on 10 carries. Baltimore's defense wasn't good enough, allowing Los Angeles to rush for a season-best 160 yards. The Ravens have lost five in a row and are in a bad place the rest of the season.

The AFC wild-card race is soft, but it's not like the Ravens are looking like much of a threat. They just lost a home game to a Chargers team that was 0-8 coming in. Even if Lamar Jackson heals quickly, it seems like there won't be any reason for him to return late in the season.

The Saints' next three games are at Chicago, at Cincinnati and vs. Green Bay. This could get bad for New Orleans, which is showing that last season's strong second half was a fluke fueled by a weak schedule. The Panthers have allowed less than 300 yards this season four times: vs. the Cooper Rush-led Falcons, vs. the Browns, vs. the Tanner McKee-led Eagles and on Sunday against the Saints. New Orleans had just 222 yards of offense. There were offensive line injuries but it was still a poor performance and a bad home loss.

"Yeah, [coach Kellen Moore] was upset, and rightfully so," Saints quarterback Tyler Shough said via the team's transcripts. "As players, I think we failed him. As coach, I think we had those moments where we had a couple third downs, and I could have found Chris [Olave] or made a play there. We had those opportunities, and he's obviously frustrated. We have to find a way to win that game."

If there was a positive it was seeing a healthy Jordyn Tyson, the Saints' first-round draft pick, grab 11 passes for 80 yards as Carolina focused its coverage on Olave.

In Weeks 3 and 4, with Michael Penix Jr. back in the lineup, the Falcons blew out the Packers and Saints and looked like a potential division winner. Other than those two games, the Falcons are 1-7. Whatever happened in those games was a complete outlier. Atlanta averaged 459 yards in those two games and hasn't been above 370 yards in any other game this season. Bijan Robinson hasn't rushed for more than 100 yards in a game since that Week 4 win at New Orleans.

On Sunday the defense completely let down, days after the team traded safety Jessie Bates III to the Jaguars. Patrick Mahomes had 348 yards and five touchdowns and Kansas City scored 35 points without running back Kenneth Walker III.

"All of this falls on me," Falcons head coach Kevin Stefanski said, via the team's site. "So, I'll take responsibility for everything that goes on in this football team. We all have to be better; that's the truth."

Everyone got way too excited for rookie quarterback Carson Beck because of one solid performance in the Hall of Fame Game. The overreaction to that was fierce. Beck, in his first game since then (he missed the rest of the preseason with a rib injury), had 108 yards and a 38.4 passer rating. Arizona had just six first downs and didn't score a touchdown. Beck had a tough matchup against the Rams and the Cardinals had many injuries on offense. Sunday was still a reality check. Beck could be a good player, but it's going to take time.

"It was a rough day, and that's probably a nice way of saying it, for the offense," Cardinals head coach Mike LaFleur said, via the team's site, though he also said he "was impressed" with Beck.

The good news for Beck is the Cardinals aren't going anywhere and have no other option at quarterback the rest of the way. They'll get seven more games to evaluate Beck and he'll have some easier matchups. Arizona will know whether it has its quarterback for 2027 or needs to draft another one.

The Colts can win without their fans being conflicted because the team doesn't have its own 2027 first-round pick. Something that made no sense at the trade deadline and made less sense Sunday is why Indianapolis didn't deal away running back Jonathan Taylor. The Colts had reported offers of a second- and fifth-round pick for Taylor and they passed. Yet, the Colts split the backfield between Taylor and Seth McGowan again Sunday, with Taylor getting 16 carries and McGowan 12. The Colts could get a committee back for much cheaper than a second-round pick. As for the idea of not wearing out Taylor in a lost season, do they think they'll be a playoff contender next season? Seems unlikely.

The other part of the Colts' season that is hard to believe is how bad Daniel Jones has been. Jones had 108 yards and a 41.5 passer rating against a bad Dolphins defense. Jones has the lowest passer rating of any regular starting quarterback in the NFL this season, at 67. That's a 33-point drop from last season, which is stunning even accounting for injuries and trades.

Kyle McCord wasn't all that bad against the Colts in his first NFL start. He easily outplayed his counterpart, Daniel Jones. He was 17-of-32 passing for 209 yards and he ran for a touchdown. McCord also lost a fumble that was recovered in the end zone for a touchdown. Dolphins head coach Jeff Hafley said he was impressed with how McCord bounced back afterward.

"He didn't blink. He just stayed composed," Hafley said, according to the team's transcripts. "Coming out of the half, I mean, that thing could have gotten ugly and the game could have gotten away from us when he fumbled the ball and they scored, and he just kept responding the whole entire time."

McCord has at least a few more weeks to show what he can do in the NFL as Malik Willis is on injured reserve. With a little more improvement he can perhaps carve out a role as a reliable backup.

Cam Ward has improved, and Titans head coach Robert Saleh revealed Monday that the team gave Ward more responsibility at the line of scrimmage before Week 7. In the three games since, the Titans' scoring average has spiked from 14 points per game to 27.7, and that came against three good defenses (Browns, Bengals, Jaguars). Ward has three games this season with a passer rating above 83 and two of them came since the philosophical change.

The Titans are in the middle of a lost season, but a strong second half from Ward can offer renewed hope for the future.

"To be fair to Cam, we've said from the beginning we were going to give him every opportunity to prove that he is a franchise quarterback of the Tennessee Titans," Saleh said, via the team's site. "So, philosophically, the change was: How are we going to evaluate whether or not he is capable of being our franchise quarterback if we don't give him the opportunity to prove it? And I think over the last three weeks he has done that. Or, at least is doing it."

Not much has gone right for the Eagles this season, but the A.J. Brown trade looks to be a win for them. Brown has been stunningly bad for the Patriots. A high ankle sprain suffered in Week 1 has contributed to his quiet season. Overall, New England can't be happy to have just 11 catches and 128 yards from a receiver who will be 30 years old next season and cost the Patriots a 2028 first-round draft pick.

The Eagles are positioned well in the next two drafts, with extra first-round picks in 2027 and 2028 and extra second-round picks in 2028 and 2029, because of the trades of Jordan Davis, Cam Jurgens and Brown. That might not make the rest of this season any more palatable but Philadelphia should be able to reload quickly.

When Aaron Glenn said he was taking over defensive play-calling this season, he said calling plays was his "superpower." On Sunday, he gave up the "lead" play-calling duties to defensive coordinator Brian Duker, which practically means that Glenn is no longer the play-caller. Not that Jets fans needed another sign that Glenn won't be back in 2027 as New York's head coach, but that adds to the list.

The next big question will be when to flip the QB1 job to rookie Cade Klubnik from veteran Geno Smith. Klubnik got some playing time after Smith injured his ankle Sunday, and a 2-8 team should be thinking about getting a look at a rookie and benching a 36-year-old who will be a free agent after the season. Glenn said that's not a consideration yet.

"Geno's our starter," Glenn said Monday, via ESPN. "Come on now, let's not do that.

"I think Geno's had a really good year and it would be disrespectful to him to just [replace him]. I think we all understand that. I think we all know that he's our starter."

The Chargers got the win they needed to avoid a lot of annoying 0-17 questions. Let's not get too far ahead of ourselves; it was just one win, and on the whole, they've still been the NFL's worst team this season. But give the Chargers credit for a good effort. They figured out how to make the Ravens one-dimensional. Baltimore had 16 yards rushing on 15 carries. That forced Baltimore to lean too much on quarterback Jacoby Brissett, who was making his first Ravens start after being traded less than two weeks ago.

It was also encouraging to see Omarion Hampton emerge with a 105-yard rushing day. Justin Herbert completed only 12 passes but he didn't need to do much more because the running game was effective. Maybe the Chargers can build on their first win and have a respectable second half of the season.
