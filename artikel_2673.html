Can the Raptors avoid 15 losses in a row to the Knicks?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

On Sunday, the New York Knicks extended their winning streak over the Toronto Raptors with a dramatic 112-108 overtime win. Scottie Barnes returned to the lineup for Toronto and poured in 30 points but it wasn't enough to stop Toronto's recent slide, as they now sit at 5-8 and 9th in the Eastern Conference. Can the Raptors put an end to their four-game losing streak (and their much longer streak across multiple seasons) against New York?

There's not a lot of new stuff to add since the last preview, since the two teams faced off mere days ago, but there are a few trends worth noting on New York's side. Josh Hart and Mikal Bridges, two-thirds of the Knicks' vaunted "Wingstop" trio have seen their minutes cut as of late, and neither has looked comfortable on the court, particularly on the offensive end. The team's been stuck in some old habits this season, with its starting line digging itself into holes, only for key bench players like Landry Shamet, Deuce McBride, and Jordan Clarkson (suddenly a deadly offensive rebounder and energetic defender well into his thirties) to provide the jolt they need to shake out of their slump.

It's a troubling sign for the defending champions, one that worked astonishingly well in the playoffs last season, but that has been significantly less successful this year, thanks to some ice-cold shooting from key players. Even Jalen Brunson hasn't been immune to shooting slumps this year, with some of his signature shots inside the arc clanking off the rim more often than usual.

Those same warts reared their ugly heads on Sunday for the Knicks, but, against a Raptors team lacking in firepower and offensive dynamism, it was enough to secure the win, even with Barnes hitting some very difficult jumpers in key moments. Ja'Kobe Walter gave Toronto very little that night, with just seven points on 2-for-8 shooting, Immanuel Quickley's shot dried up after boldly proclaiming "I'm here" following a big bucket, and Collin Murray-Boyles was held to single digits despite snagging four offensive boards.

Even though Brunson led the charge late, Karl-Anthony Towns was the protagonist for New York. He's a major matchup problem for the Raps, too skilled on the perimeter for Jakob Poeltl to deal with comfortably, and considerably bigger than CMB. When the Knicks can space the floor better (i.e. when Hart is on the bench), he can put up buckets in bunches, and he did just that, to the tune of a co-game-high of 28, matching Brunson's total. He can also be a terror on the offensive glass when the Raptors go small, and his five offensive boards were more than even the energetic CMB managed.

The Raptors appeared to have seized control late but the Shamet-Bridges wing duo fared far better than the starting pair, with Shamet's off-ball movement in particular flummoxing a Raptors defence that wasn't always connected for entire possessions late. The former journeyman-turned-semi-starter dropped 23 points on just 12 shots. Meanwhile, they got a few useful minutes from James Wiseman, the former lottery pick-turned-journeyman, in place of Andre Drummond, who, for all of his size and rebounding prowess, seems to be losing his grip on the backup centre spot.

Scottie Barnes put up 30 last game, but it was telling that he had to settle for tough jumpers when matched up against OG Anunoby. Getting Barnes the ball on the move in transition is vital, as is finding ways to get him matched up against smaller players he can bully in halfcourt scenarios. With a lineup a bit light on ballhandling and creativity, the offence goes as Scottie goes and it's vital that they scheme up as many opportunities for him to run free as possible.

Last time around, I said the Knicks are as vulnerable to an upset as they have been in quite some time, and that still stands true. All season, there have been moments where the team has looked like a group of strangers, despite most of them entering their third season together. But, when they turn it on, they can be lethal on both ends. If the Raptors are going to snap their years-long losing streak against them, they'll need to do a much better job taking care of the ball and the defensive glass. Otherwise, they could be in for another disappointing night.
