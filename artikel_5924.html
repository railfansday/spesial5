FBI probes second cyberattack in months as hackers breach key crime database, sources say

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Fox News' Madeline Rivera provides details on a reported cyberattack after a string of outages with the FBI at the center of this attack. Rivera says this is the second breach in recent months.

Hackers breached the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC), three sources familiar with the matter told Fox News, marking the second cyberattack against the bureau in months.

The FBI is investigating the hack to determine the unauthorized entry point, but, for now, it is unclear what information was compromised, sources said.

The NCIC database is a critical information-sharing tool for federal, state and local authorities, helping them track fugitives, monitor registered sex offenders, check whether someone has failed a gun background check, among other information.

In an interview with Fox News' Kayleigh McEnany Saturday morning, President Donald Trump said he had "just heard" about the hack.

"I will say that it’s not good. I don’t like that," Trump added.

The FBI did not immediately respond to a request for comment. CNN first reported the hack.

In September, a group called ShinyHunters hacked into the FBI's job portal website intending to steal sensitive employment records.

The mysterious group, known for extortion plots, claimed it stole sensitive information belonging to almost every FBI agent, including names, home addresses, Social Security numbers, assignments and, in some cases, the names of family members.

FBI STRIKES BACK AT HACKING GROUP WITH OVERSEAS ARREST OF ALLEGED LEADER

Canadian cybersecurity executive Edward Dubrovsky was arrested Oct. 8 and faces charges in the FBI's investigation into ShinyHunters, according to court records and law enforcement sources who spoke to Reuters.

The details of Dubrovsky's alleged crimes are unknown because the court records in relation to his case remain sealed.

FBI Director Kash Patel announced the arrest of ShinyHunters "suspected co-conspirator" on Oct. 9, a day after Dubrovsky was taken into custody.

FBI Director Kash Patel speaks during a news conference at the Department of Justice in Washington, D.C., Dec. 4, 2025. (Daniel Heuer/AFP via Getty Images)

"This is the latest arrest this FBI has made in a matter of days involving this network, as we work non-stop to dismantle the group, pursue new leads and evidence and act quickly," Patel said in a statement at the time.

"We will continue to work closely with our partners to disrupt what’s left of the ShinyHunters group and their associates, no matter where they operate."

In 2023, hackers infiltrated a computer network at the FBI's New York field office used in child sexual exploitation cases, a system that also stored images tied to the Jeffrey Epstein probe, CNN previously reported.
