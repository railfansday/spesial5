Talking Point: What's Your Favourite Tiny Tweak In Zelda: Ocarina Of Time?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

While the Ocarina of Time we see on Switch 2 is undoubtedly the same game we all know from the N64, there are enough tweaks in there that have had the biggest fans amongst us clicking and pointing at the screen like that Leonardo DiCaprio meme.

We're not talking about the big changes, either. You can keep your new visuals, fancy jump buttons and modern camera controls; we're getting excited over new floor textures and wall decals — stuff that wouldn't stand out unless you had played the original more times than you care to admit.

Below, we have shared our favourite tiny tweaks that have stood out so far. Some are more niche than others, but these are the little changes that had us beaming in our first few hours of rediscovering Hyrule and finding it not quite as we remember it.

After you've read through our picks, why not head to the comments and share your top tweaks?

Knowing the original game like the back of my hand, I'm yet to walk into a room without going, "Oooh, look how they changed that bit!" But the one that has stood out to me the most so far is being able to ride Epona right up to the entrance to Kakariko Village without getting chucked off at the bottom of the stairs.

Does it change the game in any way at all? Absolutely not. But praise be to whichever innovative dev at Nintendo thought, 'What if Jim didn't have to sit through a backtracking animation every time he forgot to dismount quickly enough?'

The ragdoll may not be a substantial inclusion, but if I can pick between a stiff, canned animation of Link collapsing to the floor and seeing his lifeless body tumble into the rapids that lead up to Zora's Domain, you know what I'm going to choose.

The option to remove one of the few tweaks I haven’t enjoyed so far — Navi’s incessant dialogue boxes commenting on everything — is a godsend. I was concerned she’d spend the entire game puncturing the mood with “Oooh, big mountain!” and “Wow, great work!” and “OMG, that was a lucky escape!” (I’m paraphrasing, but that’s the gist), and she does.

Turning on the ‘Master’ display option, though, reduces the UI to the bare minimum, removing almost everything, including Navi’s dialogue boxes. So when I emerge onto Hyrule Field, the landscape speaks for itself (with a little help from the music). Bliss. Other people might enjoy her interjections, and yes, I will miss a cute line or two, but thanks for letting me turn off the running commentary, Nintendo.

Turning Grouch Mode off, the slingshot easter egg in the courtyard window is ace. And Kakariko feels more like a real place now. And those little rivulets on Hyrule Field add so much. And that part of Lon Lon... *babbling trails off...

Without heading into spoiler territory, I suppose my niche tweak (if it’s considered niche) would be the addition of Threads of Time, but more specifically the Tapestry.

I’ve filled out my tapestry now, but throughout each stage of Link’s journey it was nice to see the progress I was making. I jumped into the remake with the hope of experiencing it with fresh eyes (although I’d played the original), and it really felt like it at times. It was also a really good way for me to gauge what I could do next should I forget, collectables I likely still needed, or even secrets I still had to unlock.

It’s a very simple addition common in modern gaming, but a tweak which was needed.

I’m enjoying all the revamped music, but one arrangement that took me by surprise is when you encounter common or garden foes. There’s more urgency and danger now: notes are sharply cutting in, there’s a subtle, unnerving rise and fall in volume, and if the battle lasts long enough, it goes up a notch when a horn (I’m guessing!) is eventually layered on top.

For a track I hadn’t given a second’s thought to in the original, I’m now delighted every time an enemy nears.

Note. Very minor pre-Hyrule Field spoilers ahead...

Some of the subtle tweaks to Ocarina of Time’s narrative landed so well with me. I won’t go into the later examples, but I absolutely adored the moment when Link runs into both Mido and Saria after leaving the Deku Tree.

Mido, who feels much more fleshed out in the remake, expresses shock and immediate concern for the Deku Tree, while Saria is devastated at the thought of Link leaving Kokiri Forest. She leaves, heartbroken at Link’s decision, and it’s only when we exit the forest that she catches up to make amends and deliver the Fairy Ocarina.

This extra scene isn’t much in the grand scheme of things, but it really adds weight to Link’s departure, emphasising the home and friends that he’ll be leaving behind. I love it.

The remake is a godsend for someone like me eager to experience it again without suffering the many archaic elements that present themselves today.

I mostly notice that everything feels nicer. There's none of the clunkiness whether that be in the controls, obviously a vastly improved frame rate, and general design tweaks. One that I did note is the narrow path in the Fire Temple where a fall meant a long walk all the way back to trying it again. Boring! Now, Link will cling to the ledge and recover, or will fall into a lava pit, lose health, and respawn.

I bet this is one of the petty examples that IGN used to say this version of Ocarina is "easier" in its obvious agenda to not score it a 10. Whereas to sane people, the punishment from that fall is purely cheap game design. The path is easy to traverse as long as it's not rushed, so clinging to the ledge or losing health is an excellent two-stage warning to be careful compared to the previous brutal method after some simple carelessness. That is much more in sync with the ethos of Ocarina whereas in something like Ghosts N Goblins, that sort of cheap punishment is to be expected!
