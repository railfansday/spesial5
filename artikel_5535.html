The XRP price is struggling to take advantage of institutional credibility for the ledger. Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse has another explanation: Wall Street is pulling liquidity toward blockbuster IPOs.

The XRP Ledger Foundation assessed the network against all ten principles in the Global Layer One (GL1) Market Infrastructure Toolkit. CertiK independently verified the network’s evidence under the Architecture and Resiliency principles.

Garlinghouse said investors have been moving money out of crypto, including XRP, to fund major listings. He expects the market to recover after Anthropic’s debut, just as it did following SpaceX’s IPO.

This makes the assessment relevant to banks exploring tokenized markets. Institutions need to understand how networks handle operational risk, governance, and settlement before building financial products on them.

XRPL’s case is notable because it is public and permissionless. Traditional financial networks often operate behind controlled access. Public blockchains must show how risks are handled across distributed governance, protocol upgrades, and open participation.

The Foundation assessed XRPL against all ten principles. It also supplied supporting evidence for each control. CertiK then examined the Architecture and Resiliency sections, using code, configurations, logs, and test results.

CertiK said it reviewed all 112 controls in the toolkit. Its technical verification focused on those suitable for independent assessment. The firm also verified Partior’s self-attestation under the same two principles.

The XRPL Foundation called it the first such verification for a public, permissionless Layer 1. The milestone gives institutions a structured reference when evaluating networks for tokenized deposits, stablecoins, funds, and other assets.

That is useful progress for XRPL’s institutional pitch. Risk and compliance teams can now examine evidence against familiar market-infrastructure standards. They have more than a blockchain’s own description of its capabilities.

In the interview, Garlinghouse addressed the gap between Ripple’s expansion and XRP price performance. He said the SpaceX IPO triggered a similar pullback as investors shifted liquidity out of crypto. He specifically named Bitcoin and XRP among the assets affected.

Investors moved capital from liquid crypto markets into the highly anticipated SpaceX listing, he said. Garlinghouse sees the same pattern ahead of Anthropic’s debut.

Anthropic priced its shares at $83 and began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday. The offering covers 980 million shares, and the deal is expected to close Friday, November 20.

“What we saw after the SpaceX IPO was a rally in the crypto markets,” Garlinghouse said. “I think you’ll see that happen here as well.”

He also expects digital assets to stop moving in lockstep. In his view, real utility and demand will increasingly separate stronger projects from speculative tokens. That puts the XRP price debate back on XRPL’s ability to attract activity.
