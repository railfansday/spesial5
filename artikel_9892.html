Big Tech's compliance with EU market rules is performative, consumer group says

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

BEUC urges Brussels to impose tougher penalties over alleged barriers to switching services and controlling personal data

Big Tech has earned a finger-wagging from the European Consumer Organisation (BEUC), which says the industry's compliance with the EU's Digital Markets Act (DMA) still leaves consumers short of meaningful choice.

BEUC said: "Big Tech companies Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, ByteDance, Meta and Microsoft (so-called 'gatekeepers') still likely breach the EU's Digital Markets Act (DMA) far too often for consumers to reap the full benefits of real choice over their digital services and products."

The European Commission designated the six companies as gatekeepers in 2023, imposing obligations on specified core platform services such as search engines, messaging services, and app stores. The DMA aims to make digital markets fairer and more open to competition.

The devil is in the details. In the years since, the EC has lined up Amazon and Microsoft's cloud businesses for gatekeeper status. In March 2025, the Commission issued preliminary findings against Alphabet over Search and Google Play, while separately specifying measures Apple had to take to meet its interoperability obligations. In 2024, Microsoft's browser rivals argued that Edge should also be covered by the DMA's gatekeeper obligations, and so on.

BEUC is a fan of the act – it published a report on its consumer benefits in 2025 – but is less impressed with industry's implementation. Its latest analysis draws together evidence of potential breaches submitted to the Commission between June 2025 and June 2026.

In the case of Microsoft, BEUC stated that the company used "behavioural techniques" and "non-neutral interfaces" to elicit consent from users for data sharing across services – emphasizing benefits, while not explaining why a user might want to decline. The Windows behemoth is also accused of making it tricky to change defaults and stop behavior such as apps opening links in Edge or using Bing as a search engine.

"Microsoft also regularly prompts users who have selected an alternative browser or search engine to switch to Edge or Bing using increasingly subliminal techniques, such as prompts coming from the device manufacturer, which undermine effective compliance with the DMA," BEUC said.

Then there's Google, which was fined €890 million in July for favoring its own services in Search and restricting developers' ability to direct Play Store users to alternative purchasing channels. As with Microsoft, BEUC expressed concerns about the non-neutral language Google used to obtain user consent, as well as how it allegedly made it difficult to change default settings or uninstall gatekeeper applications.

The alleged behavior of Apple, ByteDance, Amazon, and Meta was also highlighted, with examples including Apple's restrictions on alternative app distribution and Amazon using interface design techniques to nudge users into handing over their data.

Apple announced new terms for developers earlier this year in response to its App Store dispute with the European Commission, but BEUC said it was "concerned that the new terms do not resolve fundamental non-compliance issues."

Google did not respond to requests for comment from The Register. A Microsoft spokesperson told us: "We are continuing to engage with the Commission and industry at large to ensure Microsoft's designated platforms comply fully with the DMA."

An Amazon spokesperson told The Register “Our consent experience is clear and gives customers informed choice about how their personal information is used across Amazon services. We see that in practice.

“A seller that uses Amazon Stores in the EU can offer the same products on any third-party online marketplace or its own direct to customer website at any price, without affecting their eligibility to be highlighted as the Featured Offer. This is set out clearly in our DMA Compliance Report.”

BEUC director general Agustín Reyna said: "The era of cosmetic compliance must end. It is high time the Commission holds gatekeepers to account and imposes dissuasive penalties in all cases where they bypass the DMA's rules."

The report has been shared with the Commission. Whether BEUC's allegations lead to further findings of non-compliance or penalties is now a matter for the regulator. ®
