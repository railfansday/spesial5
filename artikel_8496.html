Earned Income Tax Credit – Check Every Year, Even If You Didn’t Qualify Before

If you work and earn a low or moderate income, check whether you can claim the earned income tax credit (EITC) every year before you file. A pay cut, a new baby, or a divorce can change the answer. The credit is refundable, so it pays you even if you owe no tax.

About one in five workers who qualify for the EITC don’t claim it, by the IRS’s estimate. The credit is worth up to $8,231 to a family with three or more children and up to $664 to a worker with none.

Your earned income from work and your adjusted gross income (AGI), which counts your other income too, must both fall under the IRS’s income limit for your filing status and how many qualifying children you claim. The IRS adjusts the limits for inflation each year. You also need investment income under a yearly cap and a valid Social Security number for you, a spouse on a joint return, and each child you claim. Without a qualifying child, you or a joint-filing spouse must be 25 to 64 at year’s end.

Take a single parent with two qualifying children and no income but her pay, which came to $28,000 in 2026. By our math from the IRS’s 2026 figures, her credit is about $6,450.

Start with the IRS’s EITC Assistant, which says whether you qualify and estimates your credit. To claim it, file Form 1040, adding Schedule EIC if you claim a qualifying child. Even if your pay was too low to require a return, file anyway to collect it. Your 2026 return may also need the IRS’s new Schedule 3-A, which asks whether you want your “federal public benefit,” the refunded part of your credits. Answer yes.

IRS-certified volunteers at Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA) sites, which mainly serve workers who meet the EITC’s income guidelines, prepare basic returns free. The IRS’s VITA Locator finds sites near you.

By law, the IRS holds the entire refund on any return claiming the EITC until mid-February. The parent above, filing early online with direct deposit and no issues on her return, can expect hers by early March, under the IRS’s schedule.

Use Form 1040-X to claim the credit on any earlier return you filed without it, within three years of that return’s due date.
