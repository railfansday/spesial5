The difference is in doing this, OP has also transferred $100k of assets from taxable to tax-sheltered. That's the issue. OP will have to now pay ordinary income tax on that $100k when withdrawn, and if withdrawn early, a withdraw penalty. That is substantially less tax-efficient than keeping bonds in taxable. Traditional 401ks are tax-efficient in the sense that they allow tax arbitrage when one is working, but in retirement, moving assets from taxable to tax-deferred is a horrible move.
Sounds impressive, but that $100K comes from growth not an actual transfer. Had the balanced allocation been done/kept in both accounts, that gain would have been spread across both accounts. 1/3 would still have been in pre-tax and still need tax paid on it. For the $66K left in taxable it would still need tax paid on it. There is a difference in LTCG and ordinary income, so it will incur a higher tax rate coming from pre-tax. The difference in the tax rates is probably 10 percentage points or so. That makes it $6.6K more expensive in this particular scenario.

33% bonds in taxable is $330K of bonds is about $3.3K of taxes every year (5% interest 22% ordinary marginal rate). With just that number, it sounds like this way is actually more expensive.

But that isn't quite fair, since the pre-tax will get taxed, just later.

Bottom line, it is complicated and it will depend on the sequence of returns and there is no one right answer.
