Manchester City star Enzo Fernandez led Argentina as they lost 2-1 to Uruguay on Friday.

The midfielder was one of few player to come out of the game with any credit after what was a disappointing result.

While just a friendly, the clash carried significance as it marked the first game of the post-Lionel Messi era.

The Argentina legend and captain, widely regarded as the great player of all time, made his final international appearance in October and has left a leadership vacuum to be filled.

Fernandez, who joined Man City for £125m in the summer transfer window, will be among the players looking to fill that role.

The 25-year-old staked his case for the first time on Friday, but will have been disappointed by the result.

🚨🇦🇷 Enzo Fernández: “We did not deserve to lose. Uruguay scored two random goals out of nowhere; we were the better team”. pic.twitter.com/7pzGp4uH5f

Despite Argentina's loss, Fernandez can be happy with what was a strong individual display.

The midfielder has taken criticism of late for his performances at Man City, which have come in Enzo Maresca's system.

But given a slightly different role by Lionel Scaloni for Argentina, the 25-year-old looked to be more at home.

Fernandez had more touches of the ball than anyone, completing a massive 128 passes throughout the match.

He created two chances and had a shot on target as he attempted to pull Argentina back into the game, and that was not all.

The midfielder completed a staggering 17 passes into the final third - at least eight more than anybody else on the pitch.

Fernandez was also fouled twice, made two tackles, a clearance, three recoveries and won five duels.

"We saw him in two different roles: in the first half, playing as he has been lately, also for his club, relaxed, without so much defensive responsibility, focused on making accurate passes," wrote Tyc Sports, awarding him a six out of 10.

"With [Exequiel] Palacios' substitution, we saw him in his other role, the one he hardly uses these days: the playmaker, the midfielder, the one who tries to direct the team with purposeful lateral passes and through balls, like the one that started Argentina's goal.

"It wasn't a spectacular match by any means, but even in adversity, he was clearly the team's leader today."

Juegos Ole rated Fernandez with a six out of 10 also, and wrote: "The most advanced of the midfielders. He pressed hard alongside Julián and took shots from distance, but his finishing wasn't sharp. On Uruguay's second goal, he lost track of Maxi Araújo on the corner kick"

Should he be able to take on the responsibility of being Argentina's main man after the exit of Messi, Fernandez could transform.

The midfielder has enjoyed a superb career so far, but such pressure on the shoulders could create a diamond.

Man City and Maresca will hope to see exactly that take place in the coming months.

There is a reason that the Citizens spent so much money on Fernandez - they believe there is much more to come.
