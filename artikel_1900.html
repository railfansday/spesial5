This West Ham Side Has A Worrying, Dreadfully Familiar Look To It..

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

With only two points from his first four games, West Ham manager Scott Parker is already coming under pressure, with some even questioning whether he is the right man to take the club forward following last night’s defeat at Portsmouth.

For me, it’s a little premature to be discussing the new manager’s job security. But make no mistake about it, West Ham are a club in crisis, and there is a lot more wrong than just the manager.

However, the one thing Parker can control is how he utilises the players at his disposal. And from my seat in Row B at Fratton Park, it was evident that Parker appears no closer to knowing what his best starting XI is.

Issues in both full-back positions, a lack of creativity through the middle, genuine issues on the left wing and three strikers who can’t score goals.

West Ham huffed and puffed against Pompey, and no doubt the statistics will have been boosted by a late flurry towards the end of the game when we battered the Portsmouth goal for five minutes. But that could not disguise the fact that we were watching a very, very poor West Ham team.

I had a close-up view of West Ham hams problems from row B at Fratton Park

Possibly the most disturbing aspect is that neither Preston North End nor Portsmouth, in our last two games, have looked like particularly good teams. The fact of the matter is that you don’t have to be a decent side to beat West Ham.

We now find ourselves closer to the relegation places than automatic promotion, and I’ve seen little to suggest that anyone knows how to turn us around and get us pointing in the right direction.

There’s a big game coming up at the London Stadium against Stoke City, but make no mistake about it, the Potters will be licking their lips at the prospect of turning over West Ham.

The Hammers are now starting to look less like promotion certainties and more like Leicester City.
