Floor & Decor: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Strong liquidity and a loyalty program expansion give Floor & Decor tools to compete while housing demand recovers.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:07AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, Floor & Decor (FND +3.10%) announced it's slowing new store openings next year while preserving its long-term expansion targets. Higher mortgage rates and sluggish housing turnover continue to weigh on big-ticket home renovation spending across the industry. Adjusting capital deployment gives the business flexibility to protect cash flow and return money to shareholders until housing activity recovers.

Three key takeaways from management's comments highlight how the company plans to navigate this cycle.

Management is slowing warehouse openings to 10 to 15 locations in 2027, down from 20 locations in 2026, to align growth with depressed home sales. Rather than chasing rigid store count targets during a housing slump, the retailer is prioritizing capital returns and preserving high-quality real estate opportunities for when demand rebounds.

Sales to professional contractors, or Pros, accounted for 55% of third-quarter revenue as management expanded its supply house approach in stores. To deepen these trade relationships, the company piloted a new loyalty program that rolls out nationwide in the first quarter of 2027.

The retailer ended the quarter with $1.0 billion in unrestricted liquidity, which includes cash and credit capacity under its asset-based lending facility, or ABL facility, a line of credit secured by inventory and accounts receivable. Operating cash flow reached $485.3 million through nine months. That cash flow gives management room to fund internal initiatives and buy back $157.1 million of common stock.

High mortgage rates pressure home improvement demand, yet store teams are focusing on expense control and supply chain efficiency. Direct sourcing and broad tile assortments support long-term market share growth.

Executives are watching fuel costs, gross margins, and contractor activity into 2027.
