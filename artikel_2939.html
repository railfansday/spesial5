Booking Holdings vs. MercadoLibre: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Booking's 25.5% net margin meets MercadoLibre's 47% revenue surge, but their valuation gap tells a sharper story than growth rates alone.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 9:50AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Booking Holdings ›

Investors often choose between proven global leaders and high-growth regional powerhouses. Deciding between Booking Holdings Inc. (BKNG -0.24%) and MercadoLibre Inc. (MELI +0.22%) requires weighing stable profitability against an expanding digital ecosystem in Latin America.

Booking serves the global travel market through its massive network of accommodations and flight options. MercadoLibre dominates Latin American e-commerce and digital payments, functioning as both a retailer and a bank. While both companies operate in the consumer discretionary space, they offer very different risk-reward profiles for long-term investors looking toward 2027.

Booking operates a massive network of consumer-facing brands, including Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, and OpenTable. The company focuses on its "connected trip" strategy, which aims to integrate accommodations, flights, and rental cars into a single, seamless itinerary for travelers. By managing nearly 4.7 million accommodation properties, it relies on a broad network of travel service providers that typically operate on non-exclusive terms.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $28.1 billion, representing roughly 8% growth compared to the prior year. The company reported net income of approximately $7.2 billion, reflecting a net margin of about 25.5%. This performance demonstrates the company's ability to generate significant profit while expanding its presence in the international travel market.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carries a negative debt-to-equity ratio of roughly -3.5x, indicating that its total liabilities exceed shareholder equity. The debt-to-equity ratio, which compares total debt to the equity held by shareholders, measures how a business balances its obligations against its assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow, which is cash flow from operations minus capital expenditures, was about $9.1 billion.

MercadoLibre operates the leading commerce and fintech ecosystem in Latin America, driven by the Mercado Libre marketplace and its Mercado Pago payment platform. As a leader among consumer discretionary stocks in its region, it relies on a vast network of third-party sellers and logistics partners. Its integrated strategy combines retail, lending, and asset management to serve over 136 million unique buyers.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $38.5 billion, marking about 47% growth over the previous period. The company reported net income of approximately $1.9 billion, resulting in a net margin of roughly 4.9%. This rapid top-line expansion highlights the scaling nature of its integrated digital services across major markets like Brazil and Mexico.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 1.7x, while the current ratio is roughly 1.2x. The current ratio measures the ability of a company to cover its short-term debts with its short-term assets, providing a view of its immediate financial health. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached nearly $10.8 billion, reflecting the strong cash generation of its marketplace and fintech platforms.

Booking faces intense global competition from other online travel agencies like Airbnb Inc. (ABNB -0.56%) and technology giants like Alphabet Inc. (GOOG +0.69%). The company depends heavily on third-party traffic sources such as search engines and faces the risk of disintermediation by artificial intelligence assistants. Furthermore, it must navigate complex legal and regulatory landscapes, particularly in the European Union where its status as a gatekeeper imposes restrictive obligations.

MercadoLibre operates in highly competitive markets where it faces pressure from global retailers like Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +1.61%) and low-price regional entrants. The business is exposed to significant macroeconomic volatility, including high inflation and currency devaluation in core markets such as Argentina and Brazil. Additionally, the company manages credit risk associated with its lending operations and faces potential cybersecurity threats across its integrated fintech and commerce services.

Booking carries a lower Forward P/E based on future earnings estimates, while MercadoLibre has a lower P/S ratio which compares stock price to sales over the past twelve months.

MercadoLibre has evolved from the so-called eBay of Latin America to something more akin to a combination of Amazon and PayPal Holdings Inc. (PYPL -0.33%). In 2025, the company made large investments in its Brazilian logistics network, cut down the free-shipping threshold in Brazil in June, and nearly doubled its consumer loan book. This drove impressive growth for the company and investors, with an increasing focus on financial services over delivering goods.

Yet the immediate concern with left-of-center Luis Lula winning the Brazilian presidential election last month is that the new administration will start addressing Brazil's high government and consumer debt levels, which will keep interest rates there around their current very high levels. That's bad for a consumer-loan-focused business like MercadoLibre, and shares have been hammered this week as a result.

Long-term, however, such fears are probably overblown. In fact, in the current fiscal year, revenue is expected to jump 45% to $41.9 billion, with slightly higher free cash flow and slightly lower net income. Overall, it's a good picture for MercadoLibre.

Booking Holdings is successfully shifting from an agency model to a primarily merchant model. That means it used to make its money as an agent--connecting travelers to a hotel and collecting a commission while the hotel manages collecting payment from the traveler. In the merchant model, Booking buys capacity from hotels and other providers on its platform and collects payment directly from the traveler. This tends to lead to fatter margins.

The company is also in excellent financial shape. It has $16 billion in cash, which makes its balance sheet investment grade. Another plus for shareholders is that the company has a $20 billion share buyback program. The brand is increasingly a destination for travelers, which means it is paying less to companies like Google for search engine visibility. Today, two-thirds of Booking.com's visitors are organic, meaning they are direct visitors rather than from paid ad traffic.

Revenue will grow about 9% this year to $29.3 billion. And net income takes a healthier 50% leap to about $8.1 billion.

Booking's shares are priced at a discount to the average S&P 500 stock and well below travel rival Airbnb. Yet the company provides more total sales and has good growth, expanding margins, and a pile of cash. It's too good a bargain to pass up at these prices. For 027, go with Booking Holdings
