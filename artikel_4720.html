The Many Different Flavors of Narcissism

In my consultation office and outside of my office, I frequently hear individuals describe the aversive and difficult people in their lives as narcissists. Not surprisingly, ex-partners and problematic bosses are often described with this label. The assumption is that all narcissists are similar; they are self-centered and care little, if at all, about those around them. Others are in their lives to stroke their egos and praise them endlessly. A meta-analysis of several studies conducted by Orth, Krauss, and Back (2024) examined the longitudinal stability of narcissism over the course of lives. The most interesting aspect of this review, based on data from 51 samples, was that three types of narcissism were studied. All three of these types of narcissism seemed to decline modestly over time yet remained stable.

Think about the narcissists in your life when considering the three factors of narcissism.

I am sure that your next question is whether or not these types of narcissism can coexist. They certainly can and often do. Grandiose narcissism is the label used when agentic and antagonistic narcissism are both present. Vulnerable narcissism is believed to be the appropriate description when neurotic and antagonistic narcissism are both present.

Important takeaways from Orth, Krauss, and Back (2024):

Despite being a stable aspect of personality, narcissism can lessen over the course of time. In this meta-analysis, narcissism was studied longitudinally over the course of decades. Specifically, narcissism was found to decrease somewhat from the ages of 8 to 77. This is an important finding in that future studies can seek to define the factors that lead to the attenuation of narcissism. As we are all painfully aware, narcissism can harm both the lives of individuals with this trait and the lives of the individuals.

We must also keep in mind that narcissists can learn new behaviors. Parents who have identified narcissism as a problematic behavior in a child can and should seek to help these children understand that the world will respond more positively to them if they consider not only their own needs but also the feelings of others. This can help them learn what compassion is and develop skills, including putting themselves in the shoes of others. Assuming these children already possess these skills may be problematic; the children then go through life without this skill set and, as a result, lose friends and relationships and perhaps even hurt people unknowingly. Perhaps you see some of these traits affecting your relationships. If so, therapy might be very helpful as you challenge yourself to adjust some of your problematic behaviors.

Orth,U.,Kraus,S.,&Back, M.D. (2024) Development of narcissism across the life span: A meta-analytic review of longitudinal studies.150(6) 643-665.
