History Says the Nasdaq's Wild Ride Probably Isn't Over. Here's the Case for QQQI and Its 14% Yield.

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

A roughly 10% weekly drop followed by a sharp bounce is the kind of whiplash that tends to linger. One options-based ETF is designed for it.

By David Dierking – Nov 27, 2026 at 5:48PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Neos ETF Trust - Neos Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF ›

Last week, the Nasdaq-100 fell more than 10% on the week, and briefly touched bear market territory, falling 20% below its October high.

On Monday, it rebounded roughly 7% after Nvidia's (NVDA +0.80%) board brought back co-founder Jensen Huang as CEO.

A bounce that large may feel like the bottom is in. But right now, volatility is still too high to comfortably say which direction the market is headed next.

One alternative to owning the Nasdaq-100 here is the NEOS Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF (QQQI +2.60%). Its covered call strategy and 14% distribution yield could be a compelling option for taking some tech-sector risk off the table.

The week ending last Friday was only the 13th weekly loss of 10% or more in the Nasdaq-100's history. The VIX, which is a measure of expected market volatility, briefly touched 40 for the first time since April 2025.

But the CBOE's own research shows that when the equity market experiences a volatility shock, the residual volatility "may remain for weeks, months, and even years going forward." Since higher volatility is usually correlated with falling stock prices, the implication is that the downside may not be finished.

The NEOS Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF owns the stocks in the Nasdaq-100 index and then sells call options on the index, collecting premiums that usually increase when volatility rises. Unlike more traditional covered call strategies, the fund also buys some higher-priced calls. It does this to maintain some share price upside, which many covered call ETFs largely give up.

The Nasdaq-100 is usually more volatile, so the yields generated by this fund also tend to run higher. As of Nov. 23, the distribution yield is 14.1%. Using the phrase "distribution yield" instead of dividend yield isn't an accident. The income comes from options premiums received, not dividends paid by corporations. Therefore, the tax treatment may differ.

When investing in a covered call ETF, the trade-off is that you give up share price appreciation potential in exchange for the high yield. If you believe stocks will move sideways or lower, a fund like the NEOS Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF can outperform the Nasdaq-100. In a steady bull market, however, it's likely to lag.

Given how stocks have already turned volatile, significant questions remain about AI capital expenditures, inflation is high, and the Federal Reserve may hike rates multiple times over the next several months, there's a clear case for why the high-yield strategy makes more sense right now.

Don't think of QQQI as getting a free 14% yield on top of investing in the Nasdaq-100. There's a clear trade-off, and one you need to consider first.

Covered call strategies usually work best in sideways-trending, lower-volatility markets. They usually offer the best opportunity to beat the index while still generating positive total returns.

The second best is downtrending markets. In this case, QQQI would capture most, if not all, of any loss in the Nasdaq-100, while the high yield would help offset some of it. In other words, you're probably losing less than the Nasdaq-100 but still outperforming.

Given the market's history during sharp corrections and current macro conditions, I believe we're more likely in a longer-lasting, higher-volatility environment. That's not necessarily the best environment for total returns, but a covered call ETF could increase the chances of outperformance. Plus, option premiums tend to rise during periods of higher volatility, which improves overall yield.

Overall, the NEOS Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF offers one of the best options in the high-yield covered call ETF space. It uses a fairly straightforward strategy, and the 14% yield is quite attractive. If volatility continues, I like this as a potential alternative to owning tech stocks outright.
