IndiGo hired Air India Express' former chief as its strategy officer in March. Now Air India is creating a strategy role of its own. It looks like both airlines agree the job is worth having.

Air India is creating a new group strategy officer position as part of its efforts to get full-service carrier Air India and its low-cost arm Air India Express working together more closely, sources said.

Rival IndiGo made a similar move earlier this year, naming former Air India Express managing director and CEO Aloke Singh as its chief strategy officer. Singh ran Air India Express for five years before joining IndiGo.

The new strategy job is one of two group-level roles being added. The other is a chief of the training academy, who will be responsible for bringing the group’s flying academy, flight training organizations and technical training under one roof.

Air India CEO and managing director Tewolde Gebremariam outlined the changes at an employee town hall on Wednesday. He also spoke about operational improvements, cost cutting, network changes and the airline's longer-term plans.

Air India is adding two group-level roles — a group strategy officer and a head of its training academy — as part of a push to run Air India and its low-cost arm Air India Express as "One Air India Group," sources said. CEO and Managing Director Tewolde Gebremariam outlined the changes at an employee town hall on Wednesday. Both carriers keep their own air operator certificates and customer-facing, operational, and regulatory work, while finance, HR, IT, and centers of excellence move to group level to cut duplication and bring costs closer to rivals. The airline is also reworking its hubs in response to regional airspace restrictions: more westbound connecting traffic through Mumbai, more eastbound connections through Delhi, with Bengaluru under consideration as a third hub. Air India has estimated the Pakistan airspace closure takes about $455 million a year off profit before tax, Skift reported. Gebremariam said on-time performance has improved over the past three months and that October and November were encouraging, with revenue per available seat kilometer increasing, but he described profitability as the harder problem with fuel costs high. A cost transformation program led by senior executives has collected more than 1,500 employee suggestions for saving money.
