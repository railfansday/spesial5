An airport lounge membership, hotel perks and a handful of statement credits used to be enough to set a premium travel rewards card apart. These days, that sounds like the starting point.

The faces competition from the (see rates and fees), Capital One Venture X Rewards Credit Card and (see rates and fees). All four offer transferable rewards and airport lounge access, along with benefits designed to offset their high annual fees.

Still, the Platinum makes a compelling case for a place in a frequent traveler's wallet. Its strengths include extensive lounge access, generous earnings on flights booked directly with airlines and hotel benefits that can make a stay considerably more comfortable.

At $895 a year (see rates and fees), it's also the most expensive of these four cards.

Here's where the Platinum stands out — and where another premium card might be a better fit.

Complimentary Priority Pass lounge access is a shared benefit across these cards (enrollment required). What makes the stand out is the additional lounges available through the American Express Global Lounge Collection.

The American Express Global Lounge Collection includes Centurion Lounges, eligible Delta Sky Clubs and other participating partner lounge networks.

The Centurion network has a larger footprint than either Capital One’s or Chase’s own branded lounge network. Citi doesn't operate its own network, although cardholders receive four Admirals Club passes per calendar year.

For me, the Centurion Lounges at Dallas-Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) and George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) in Houston are particularly useful when connecting to and from Austin. I enjoy the food at the lounges, but after an international flight, the opportunity to shower before my final flight of the day is what I appreciate most.

Delta Sky Club access is another reason to consider the Platinum. Cardmembers receive 10 visits per benefit year when flying on eligible itineraries. (Basic economy tickets don't qualify, and unlimited visits require $75,000 in eligible annual spending.)

There are other limits to consider. Centurion guest access costs extra unless you meet a spending requirement, and the biggest network won't necessarily be the most useful at your home airport.

Check the lounges along your regular routes before choosing a card based on access alone.

The offers up to $600 in annual lodging statement credits, split into two allowances of up to $300: one from January through June and another from July through December.

Eligible purchases include prepaid Fine Hotels + Resorts and The Hotel Collection bookings through American Express Travel®, plus qualifying prepaid Airbnb Luxe stays booked through AmexTravel.com or the Amex Travel App™.

THC and eligible Airbnb Luxe bookings require stays of at least two nights. Starting Jan. 1, 2027, FHR bookings will also require a two-night stay to qualify for the lodging credit.

I tend to use my credit for THC stays because I often find lower prices than at FHR properties. For January, I've booked two nights at The Standard, Bangkok Mahanakhon for $362. After applying the $300 credit, the stay will cost me $62 out of pocket.

My stay includes daily breakfast for two and a $100 credit toward eligible on-property charges, which I plan to use at an on-site restaurant or for room service. I'll also be eligible for early check-in, late checkout and a room upgrade (subject to availability). Breakfast is an additional benefit at this particular property; it isn't standard across THC.

FHR offers a more consistent package, including daily breakfast for two and guaranteed 4 p.m. checkout. That last benefit can be especially useful before an evening flight.

Competing premium cards have their own hotel programs. The Platinum's appeal is the combination of its credit and booking benefits. Just make sure to compare the total price with other available rates before deciding how much you're saving.

The earns 5 Membership Rewards points per dollar spent on flights booked directly with airlines or through Amex Travel (on up to $500,000 of airfare purchases per calendar year, then 1 point per dollar spent).

The ability to book directly is a meaningful advantage. You can earn generously while dealing with the airline directly if you need to change your reservation or something goes wrong.

The earns 4 points per dollar spent on flights booked directly. It earns a higher 8 points per dollar through Chase Travel℠, while the earns 6 points per dollar spent on flights through Citi Travel.

The Platinum also earns 5 points per dollar spent on eligible prepaid hotels through Amex Travel and prepaid Airbnb bookings through AmexTravel.com or the Amex Travel app. The Airbnb earning rate applies beyond the Luxe properties eligible for the lodging credit.

Those points offer plenty of redemption options.

Membership Rewards airline partners provide access to Oneworld, SkyTeam and Star Alliance flights through programs such as British Airways Club, Air France-KLM Flying Blue and Air Canada Aeroplan. Hotel partners include Hilton Honors and Marriott Bonvoy.

All four cards have useful transfer partners, so consider which programs you actually use. A strong earning rate matters more when it earns points you know how to redeem.

Eligible applicants can find out their offer and see if they are eligible for as high as 175,000 bonus points after spending $12,000 on purchases in the first six months of card membership. (Welcome offers vary, and you may not be eligible for an offer.)

Based on our valuations, the maximum 175,000-point offer is worth up to $3,500 — the highest value among the current publicly advertised offers on these four cards.

The high spending requirement deserves attention. Make sure you can meet it through purchases you already planned to make, and assess the card's ongoing value separately from its first-year bonus.

The 's long list of credits makes more sense when you focus on the ones that fit your existing spending.

Its up to $300 annual digital entertainment credit is one I particularly like. It provides up to $25 monthly toward eligible purchases with participating providers.† I use it for YouTube Premium and Disney+/Hulu; the monthly allowance is shared across eligible subscriptions.

Uber users have two benefits to consider. The card provides up to $200 in annual Uber Cash for eligible U.S. rides and orders (up to $15 monthly, plus an extra $20 in December; you must add the Platinum to your Uber account and use an Amex card to pay). A separate credit provides up to $120 annually toward an eligible auto-renewing Uber One membership.

The up to $400 annual Resy statement credit (available in quarterly allowances of up to $100) can also be straightforward to use if you already dine at qualifying U.S. restaurants.†

There’s also an up to $300 annual Equinox statement credit for eligible Equinox memberships or an Equinox+ digital subscription (subject to auto-renewal).† I use mine for the digital subscription.

These benefits can help cover the annual fee, but only assign them the value they have to you. Signing up for a service you won't use doesn't save money. Monthly expiration dates and U.S. restrictions also make some credits less useful for certain cardmembers.

Perks with Avis, Hertz and National† can also improve the experience of renting a car.

The card provides up to $219 in statement credits per calendar year toward a Clear+ membership (subject to auto-renewal),† plus either a $120 Global Entry application fee credit or an up to $85 TSA PreCheck application fee credit (every four years) when you pay with your Platinum.

Clear+ handles identity verification at participating airport checkpoints, while TSA PreCheck provides expedited security screening. Global Entry expedites eligible arrivals into the U.S. and includes TSA PreCheck.

You can also select one qualifying airline to receive up to $200 in statement credits for eligible incidental fees† charged to your Platinum card each calendar year. (Note that it isn't a general airfare credit.)

Outside its limited bonus categories, the earns just 1 point per dollar spent. Dining and everyday purchases are usually better placed on another card.

The Venture X offers a simpler alternative: a $395 annual fee, at least 2 miles per dollar spent on purchases, a $300 annual Capital One Travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles. There are fewer credits to track to make the annual fee worthwhile.

The has advantages in travel protections. Its included rental car coverage is primary, while Platinum's included coverage is secondary**.

The Sapphire Reserve also offers baggage delay insurance, reimbursing eligible essentials up to $100 daily for five days after a qualifying delay exceeding six hours. Platinum's baggage insurance*** covers eligible lost, damaged or stolen baggage, but it doesn't offer a comparable standalone baggage delay benefit.

Finally, the may be a better fit if you spend heavily on dining and prefer Citi's transfer partners. It earns 3 ThankYou points per dollar spent at restaurants, increasing to 6 points on Friday and Saturday evenings (between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m. Eastern time). It also offers four Admirals Club passes per calendar year, making it particularly appealing if you occasionally fly American Airlines.

The is most compelling when its benefits fit how you already travel and spend. Lounge access, a well-priced hotel stay and regularly used credits can make its annual fee easier to justify.

For me, airport showers and elevated hotel stays and credits I already use make the Platinum worth keeping.

If you'd get more use from broad spending bonuses or a simpler set of benefits, one of its competitors could be a better fit.
