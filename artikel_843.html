Football Daily | Taking a brief minute to remember Pierre Sage’s impact at Crystal Palace

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

So farewell then, Pierre Sage. Many coaches leave under a cloud, not many have their departures completely obscured by the foul, retch-inducing one emanating from another team’s managerial circus, but such was Sage’s fate. The news section of Crystal Palace’s website helpfully tells you the amount of time it might take you to read each story it links through to. For example it should take four minutes or so to get through Tuesday’s “On this day: five big Crystal Palace birthdays”. “England: I love a knee slide!” will take you five. “Prize draw: be a mascot for our next [Bigger Vase] tie!” is a two-minute job. The statement informing the world of Sage’s exit? One minute. Eighty-three words. By way of illustration, the word “words” was the 84th of this paragraph. Just because the man’s French doesn’t mean his exit should be marked with a shrug.

His supporters, such as they are, would argue he was hobbled by knack, suspensions and poor transfer dealings, and approximately nothing went to plan for the man who just six months ago was revelling in being named Ligue 1 coach of the year. His philosophy just never quite translated. “There are two types of teams: one with the ball, and the other chasing it,” he said last year. “I prefer to be the first of those.” And it is true that in Ligue 1 last season only Paris St-Germain and Marseille had, on average, more of the ball than his Lens side. But it turned out that Crystal Palace players were quite happy chasing it thank you, whichever of them he picked – and he picked most of them, fielding more players than any other club this season.

They are averaging 43.2% possession, more than only Coventry and Hull. Against Tottenham at the end of October – when, to be fair, they were hobbled by a 13th-minute red card – they had the ball just 20.8% of the time – which, at least since 2013-14, they’ve never come close to equalling – and ended with an xG of 0.14 – not as bad as the 0.12 they registered against Manchester City in August 2022 (a game which, somehow, they ended with two goals), but worse than all their other ones. “It is,” Sage sighed after the Spurs match, “a disaster.” In that interview Sage said his “main principles” included “to look to get behind the opposition defence non-stop; and to press your opponents hard so you can win the ball back quickly,” Again, lovely ideas.

But Sage’s Palace were absolutely rubbish at pressing – on Opta’s pressed sequences metric, measuring “the number of open-play sequences starting in their defensive third where the opposition has three or fewer passes and the sequence ends in their own half”, they are currently bottom of the league, and what high turnovers they have managed have led to just eight shots (second-bottom) and no goals (bottom). As for getting behind the opposition defence non-stop, Opta has recorded 24 through-balls this season, which actually is the fifth-highest total in the league. They have also recorded 23 offsides. Still, we should take a minute (but no more) to mark another coach’s departure. Farewell, Pierre, and good luck. You certainly had none of it over here.
