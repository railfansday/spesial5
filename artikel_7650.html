Tajik National Convicted of Providing Material Support to ISIS and Firearms Offenses

A federal jury in Brooklyn today convicted Mansuri Manuchekhri, also known as “Mansuri Manchekhri” and “Manysher,” on four counts of an indictment charging him with conspiring to provide material support to the Islamic State of Iraq and al-Sham (ISIS) and the Islamic State-Khorasan Province (ISIS-K), conspiring to possess and possessing firearms and ammunition while unlawfully in the United States, and making false statements to federal agents. On October 30, 2026, Manuchekhri pleaded guilty to a fifth count charging immigration fraud relating to a sham marriage between the defendant and a U.S. citizen. The verdict was returned following a two-week trial. When sentenced, Manuchekhri faces up to 55 years in prison.

“Manuchekhri is a dangerous illegal alien who glorified ISIS’s violence, facilitated payments to ISIS-linked operatives, and unlawfully possessed firearms while living in the United States,” said Acting Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General M. Scott Proctor. “His support for a terrorist organization responsible for murder and atrocities around the world, disregard for our nation’s laws, and obvious hatred of the United States made him a grave danger to the public. Today’s verdict demonstrates that those who aid terrorists will be held fully accountable.”

“The defendant sent money to ISIS, a bloodthirsty terrorist organization, while training with assault rifles and proclaiming to his associates: ‘I am ready,’” said U.S. Attorney Joseph Nocella Jr. for the Eastern District of New York. “But it is we who were ready, along with our law enforcement partners. The defendant’s conviction has neutralized a serious threat and shown the world that we will be unrelenting in dismantling terrorist funding networks.”

“Mansuri Manuchekhri's alleged extremist plans were disrupted by the outstanding work of the New York Joint Terrorism Force,” said Assistant Director in Charge James C. Barnacle Jr. of the FBI New York Field Office. “He spent years living in New York while supporting ISIS and declaring his allegiance to the group. Today, he is off the streets. Anyone who seeks to support or commit terrorism should know that justice will find you.”

As proven at trial, in June 2016, Manuchekhri traveled to the United States from Tajikistan on a non-immigrant tourist visa and unlawfully remained in the United States after his visa expired in December 2016. While in the United States, Manuchekhri collected jihadist propaganda videos that promoted violence and martyrdom and expressed his support for ISIS, praising past ISIS attacks in the United States such as the October 2017 West Side Highway truck attack that killed eight people and seriously injured many others. Manuchekhri’s ex-wife testified that he told her about the terrorist who carried out that deadly attack: “That guy is in heaven.”

From approximately December 2021 through June 2023, while residing in Brooklyn, Manuchekhri facilitated approximately $70,000 in payments to ISIS-affiliated co-conspirators in Turkey and Syria, including to an individual who was later arrested by Turkish authorities for his alleged involvement in a January 2024 terrorist attack on a church in Istanbul for which ISIS-K publicly claimed responsibility. In March 2017, Manuchekhri paid an American citizen to enter into a sham marriage with him so that he could obtain legal status in the United States. However, he failed to provide certain supporting documentation that was requested by the government, and his petition was never granted.

Within the same time frame as the money transfers, Manuchekhri possessed and used firearms and made frequent visits to shooting ranges even though he was prohibited from doing so as an alien unlawfully in the United States. In February 2022, he recorded himself firing an assault rifle at a shooting range in New Jersey and sent the video to one of the ISIS-affiliated individuals in Turkey with the message, “Thank God, I am ready, brother.” An invoice from the shooting range reflects that on Feb. 27, 2022, Manuchekhri and his friend rented a rifle port, an AK-47, and a Smith & Wesson M&P 15 tactical rifle, and purchased ammunition.

Manuchekhri also possessed firearms elsewhere. On Aug. 24, 2024, Manuchekhri sent a different roommate a video recorded from inside a moving vehicle showing his outstretched arm firing a handgun out the window along a highway.

Assistant U.S. Attorneys Andrew D. Reich and Lindsey R. Oken for the Eastern District of New York are prosecuting the case, with valuable assistance from Assistant U.S. Attorney Robert Pollack; Trial Attorney John Cella and Ryan White of the National Security Division’s Counterterrorism Section; and Paralegal Specialist Wayne Colon.
