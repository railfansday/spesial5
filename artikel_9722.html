Why Naive RAG fails in production (and how to fix it)

While Naive RAG works well for proof of concept, it fails completely in a real world enterprise environment. Below are reasons for this and how you can professionally upgrade your system.

Issue: Basic RAG will take all corporate documents and put them into one large index. This can create a massive security issue. An entry level employee can ask the AI about company salaries or executive emails and it will return the data.

Solution: Implement Document Level Access Control (ACLs). The retrieval system will need to check what the user has permission to view in the corporate environment.

Ingestion – Garbage In = Hallucinations Out

A lot of this happens pre-embedding. Ensure you have identified authoritative sources and owners. Remove duplicates, obsolete and low quality documents. Ensure you retain metadata – source, title, sub heading, page number, date, access control list and version number. Support multiple document types (PDF, DOCX, HTML, tables and images) using structured parsing and OCR. Ensure ingesting is incremental, there is de-dupe in place and there is verifiable deletion. Treating your knowledge base as a product, version your documents and have a plan in place to regularly re-sync.

This often has the biggest impact on the accuracy of your system than your embedding or LLM. Best practice recommendations for production are: Use recursive or structured chunking. Use chunk sizes of 300-512 tokens (with 50-100 tokens or 10-20% overlap) for many enterprise corpora. Ensure you retain structure in your data using parent/child chunking. For technical documents ensure you don’t split code and explanations. For legal/policy ensure clauses aren’t split. Only try semantic/LLM chunking once you have measured baseline performance. Ensure you are trying chunking strategies based on real question sets.

We’ve moved beyond vector search. Using hybrid retrieval (dense + sparse/BM25) will give you gains in your recall and precision. Especially if you have queries with specific IDs, rare terms or jargon. Use hybrid retrieval to gain a larger candidate set. Use metadata filtering for access & freshness. Use cross encoder to rerank your top candidates. You can often get good ROI from adding reranking. This will provide you with meaningful gains to your accuracy with minimal gains to your latency.

Not all queries should be treated the same. Production systems will use categorisation/routing, expansion (HyDE style or multi query), multiple indexes/tools and ‘I don’t know’ scenarios. For complex multi-hop or comparison queries you can use light agent patterns. However this should be done once your retrieval is performing well. Agent patterns can be costly so ensure you’re getting good ROI.

Ensure you use grounded prompts to ensure the LLM only uses information from what is provided in the context. Ask the LLM to cite sources. Ensure citations are structured and check that what is being claimed can be traced back to your retrieved chunks. Ensure you have input/output guardrails, PII and out-of-scope detection. If possible use multiple models. Use cheaper/fast models for simple queries and powerful models for complex synthesis. Ensure you can audit your generation.

You can’t improve what you can’t measure. Use RAGAS or other tools to measure: Context precision/recall Faithfulness Relevance Latency (P95) Citations and user signals. Automate your evaluation using CI/CD. Ensure you have a golden set of real questions. Monitor samples in production using LLM judge and manual checks. Ensure your whole pipeline is instrumented with distributed tracing to help identify where your bad answer is coming from. Often your aims will be to have a faithfulness score above 0.85-0.90, context precision above 0.75-0.80 and latency within acceptable levels. Security/Governance/Multi-tenancy Your RAG system will succeed or fail based on trust. Ensure access to documents/chunks are restricted during retrieval. Multi-tenant, encryption and auditing. Ensure PII is handled carefully. Support deletion. Ensure your knowledge is traceable.

Any existing data vulnerabilities will be amplified in production. So it's essential to get your access controls right early: Implement filtering based on metadata. Ensure users can only see documents they have access to. Scale Often you'll find that having the best infrastructure and caching mechanisms in place will reduce latencies better than using the latest model. Consider: Sharing and replicating your vector indexes. Be mindful of how you shard and replicate your vectors to avoid any latency issues caused by your growing corpus of data. Reduce token costs by implementing semantic caching. Cache popular queries. Use a Multi-model architecture. Consider the cost of having up-to-date data. You'll need to strike the right balance between having to re-index frequently and the latency of your data.

Ensure your solution is built with observability in mind. From day one ensure your solution can handle high volumes of concurrent requests and cold starts. Build your solution RAG is an iterative process. Build your solution in stages.

Stage 1 – Get your basics right : Get your use case specific. Start with one use case and clean data. Build your test set. Build a set of 50 to 100+ questions and answers. Ensure your data is optimised. Ensure your chunking strategy is optimal and your metadata is well tagged.

Stage 2 – Get your solution ready for production. Improve retrieval. Ensure you utilise hybrid search and re-ranking in your solution. Ensure there are security barriers. Ensure you have access restrictions, citations and LLM protections. Ensure your solution has monitoring.

Stage 3 – Optimise Once your basics are in place, add extra complexity. Add query rewriting, hierarchical retrieval, multi-modal capabilities and limited agentic solutions. Business Benefits Having a solid RAG solution in production will give compound benefits: Faster response times from experts. Reduced support tickets. Improved compliance. Ensure knowledge stays within the company should staff leave. A springboard for creating advanced AI agents.
