Selar is pairing a lower-emissions ship with small groups, local expertise, and a different idea of expedition luxury. Its opportunity is to make a lighter footprint part of a better experience, rather than simply another sustainability claim.

Sophie Galvagnon’s case for a smaller expedition ship starts on shore.

When hundreds of visitors land in a small Arctic community, she argues, the encounter changes from the start. The chance to understand a place is overtaken by the logistics of moving a large group through it.

“You cannot have real exchange and real contact if you suddenly come with a crowd,” she told me.

Galvagnon is the French-Swedish captain, co-founder and CEO of Selar, whose first vessel, Captain Arctic, is designed for 36 passengers and 24 crew members. Its first expeditions are scheduled for early 2027.

Selar is attempting to offer something different from conventional Arctic cruising, both in the size of its vessel and how it is powered.

Selar, a French-Swedish expedition company led by captain and CEO Sophie Galvagnon, will launch its first ship, Captain Arctic, in early 2027 with 36 passengers and 24 crew. The vessel carries five rigid, retractable sails covered with roughly 2,000 square meters of solar panels, paired with batteries, electric propulsion, and biofuel generators, plus insulation, heat recovery, and wood-pellet heating. Selar says the combination is designed to produce up to 90% lower carbon dioxide emissions than conventional expedition ships — a design target, not a result from a completed season. The commercial model matches the scale: all 36 passengers can go ashore without a rotation system, itineraries follow weather and wildlife rather than a fixed schedule, and the all-inclusive fare covers meals, an open bar, and activities so guests never see a credit card onboard. Shared meals without menus and no room service are deliberate choices. Galvagnon said the first operating year is approximately 80% booked, against an intended annual schedule of about 40 departures, roughly half allocated to charters or other tour operators. Inaugural pricing is intended to stay broadly competitive with the market, with potential for a premium of 15% or more over time based on the complete offering rather than sustainability alone.
