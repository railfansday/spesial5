Dubai International can’t recover on its home airlines alone. Every month foreign carriers stay away, rival hubs keep more of the transfer traffic DXB used to own.

Dubai International is heading for about 70 million passengers this year, down 26% from 95.2 million in 2025, with almost half its airlines yet to return after the U.S.-Iran war.

DXB served 50.4 million passengers in the first nine months, down 28.1%, according to figures released Tuesday. It is now served by 57 airlines, against 108 last year. Third-quarter traffic fell 22.1% to 18.8 million from 24.2 million, with August the busiest month at 6.6 million.

British Airways resumed services this month, joining Royal Air Maroc, SunExpress and Cebu Pacific, with more airlines due back in the coming weeks.

“The return of British Airways is a confidence signal, but airlines are rebuilding exposure cautiously,” said Linus Benjamin Bauer, founder and managing director of Dubai-based aviation consultancy BAA & Partners. Dubai International is on track for about 70 million passengers this year, down 26% from 95.2 million in 2025, after the U.S.-Iran war drove foreign carriers out of the region. DXB handled 50.4 million passengers in the first nine months, down 28.1%, and is now served by 57 airlines against 108 last year. British Airways resumed service this month, joining Royal Air Maroc, SunExpress, and Cebu Pacific, but many carriers are not coming back until spring 2027: Cathay Pacific has extended its suspension to January 31, Singapore Airlines returns February 28, and KLM and United in March. EASA's Conflict Zone Information Bulletin for the region, which European airlines generally follow, runs until January 31, 2027. Underlying demand has held up better than capacity — load factor averaged 74%, down three points — but aircraft movements fell 27.6% to 243,000. Dubai Airports CEO Paul Griffiths told Skift in September that DXB may reach 100 million passengers by the end of next year, and that traffic will begin migrating in phases to Dubai World Central, with Emirates and flydubai expected to use DWC capacity first.
