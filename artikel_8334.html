Officials Blame Climate Change for Glacier Collapse That Killed 17

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Russian authorities are now attributing the September 6 disaster on 5,047m Mount Mizhirgi in the northern Caucasus to climate-related destabilization of a hanging glacier.

A half-million cubic meter avalanche struck a military group undergoing mountain training, killing 17 and injuring 10. Another 10 were unharmed and made it down the mountain on their own.

The impact generated a powerful blast of displaced air that reportedly threw some victims 200m to 300m down the slope before burying them. As we reported at the time, helicopter pilot Alexander Davydov caught the avalanche on video.

Eldar Khashkhozhev of the Russian Ministry of Emergency Situations explained that warmer temperatures melted the protective blanket of snow, exposing parts of the glacier. This weakened the ice support that had glued enormous masses of snow and ice in place for decades.

A helicopter inspection of the region’s snow conditions the month before found no cause for concern, and some officials insist the catastrophe was unpredictable. However, climber Dmitry Polyakov, who was in the region at the time, disputed the idea that the glacier posed no danger. He said climbers had long recognized its instability and generally avoided approaching it in large groups.

The Mizhirgi Glacier against Mizhirgi East and West peaks, site of September’s catastrophic glacial collapse. Photo: Shutterstock

The key points of Khashkhozhev’s explanation were:

Rising temperatures have raised the altitude of permanent snow cover.

Previously protected sections of the glacier became exposed, weakening the supporting ice beneath the hanging masses.

Eventually, gravity overcame that support and triggered a collapse that struck the training group.

This was the largest climbing disaster in the country since 2002, when another glacier collapse in North Ossetia, located between the Black and Caspian Seas, killed 125 people, including local residents.

With major landslides in summer and the possibility of glacial collapse at other times of the year throughout the world, mountaineers will have to adjust their behavior, as previously safe areas become potentially deadly.
