Holiday TV ads from retailers arrive earlier, more frequently in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

While retail historically commands the season, it accounted for 63.7% of holiday ads in October, pacing ahead of recent years.

The holidays are always a busy time for advertising, and Nielsen’s data show activity moving further up the calendar as brands try to make the most of a packed Q4. More campaigns are starting as early as September, with holiday creative this year accounting for 1% of all linear TV ads during the month. Even as ad-supported streaming expands, linear remains on a growth trajectory after surpassing $1 billion in spend in 2023, according to Nielsen.

Some elements of the holidays remain constant, including retail’s dominance, which is particularly pronounced so far this season. The category has commanded an average of 45% of linear TV ad spend over the past five years, and only a slightly smaller share of the burgeoning streaming environment. In October, 63.7% of holiday ads came from retailers.

Promotional messaging is a major theme, with over one-third of retail ads assessed by Nielsen over the past five years carrying mentions of Black Friday or Cyber Monday deals events. Retailers are currently under pressure to reach wary shoppers during a persistent run of inflation. Several campaigns have been themed around more thoughtful, personalized gifting instead of low prices alone.

Effective holiday strategies can boost performance, per Nielsen connected TV benchmark studies for 2025. Cosmetics brands recorded a 3.2 times sales lift compared to non-holiday campaigns while department stores experienced a 2.4 times sales lift, the measurement firm found. Restaurants and fast-food brands recorded sales lifts of 1.5 times and 1 times, respectively, versus non-holiday ads.

Outside of retail, category spending trends have fluctuated more noticeably over time. Automotive shifted from representing 16% of holiday linear TV ad spend in 2023 to just 7.7% in 2025. Nielsen attributed the retreat to a bigger focus on streaming, where auto makes up 18.4% of the market. A dearth of car commercials, once a fixture of prime time programming, has gained media attention as the industry navigates economic headwinds and the failure of electric vehicle bets to take off in the U.S.

Food and beverage advertising also dipped between 2024 and 2025, which Nielsen attributed to big brands tightening their budgets and challenger brands only running smaller experiments for the holidays. Large food and beverage marketers including Pepsi and Oreo have rolled out holiday efforts in the past few weeks, with the latter introducing its first new seasonal creative in five years.
