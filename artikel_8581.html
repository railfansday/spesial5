Investing can feel exhausting when your work is already packed with tasks. Amid meetings, deadlines, family responsibilities, and day-to-day duties, it is unrealistic to allocate several hours of your day to market research.

The good part is that you don’t need to keep investing in research all the time to be a successful investor. All it takes is a small routine that keeps your knowledge current and gives you a sense of market developments without enslaving you to research.

The very beginning of it all is to set up a system that allows you to monitor major market events efficiently without drowning in too many online financial sites each morning.

Professionals use AlphaWire and similar platforms to access the latest news and details on individual companies quickly.

Using one main source of financial updates makes it much easier to see what is happening, and it significantly reduces the time you waste digging through different platforms.

This organized method of gathering stock market information can help you build and maintain your research routine over time.

One of the first things you can do is, for example, identify the companies that have been gaining traction, significant corporate news, movements in the stock markets, etc. You can then decide which of these topics merit further in-depth analysis.

It’s not about knowing everything or reading all the content out there. Set up a system that brings only the essential stuff to your attention so you can use your time effectively.

For many busy people, the 10 minutes or so they spend at the end of the workday isn’t enough to stay familiar with market developments.

Following the entire stock market at once is an easy way to waste time. In fact, one might think that a watchlist would be too limiting, but in reality, the opposite is true.

Your watchlist can include companies that you already own as well as companies that you are considering buying, a couple of their major rivals, and some key market indices related to the style you are using in your investment portfolio.

The research boundaries have been limited in this way. When you ask yourself questions like, ‘What did the market do today?’ you will find answers that matter to the portfolio you follow by switching your question around.

It is a mistake to think you should complete all your daily research at once. You can divide the tasks across the week.

For example, Monday and Wednesday could be update days, while the other days could be for watchlist reviews and company reading sessions.

Differentiating the routine this way will help you stick with it. Dedicate a 30-minute session or a full-day activity during the weekend for conducting thorough research whenever needed.

Eventually, those short, regular sessions will give you such a solid grounding in your research that you will find it much easier to interpret and analyze any development.

Writing a journal of what you discover in the process of investing research is a good idea because it will be a great tool for your further study.

In fact, the simplest possible tools are often the most effective. You can use any plain text file or even a plain word processor file.

The information one could write about a company would include the company’s background, its revenue trends, the types of products or services it deals with, a description of its business model, its main products or services, its latest announcement, as well as industry trends it is involved in and any issue or question that you wish to get clarification on.

Your journals serve you as your personal library where you keep track of all your findings and research.

Later on, for instance, when you want to start anew on a topic, there should be no need for you to go back and read everything. You can remind yourself what you were thinking about and what you have learned before, and match it with what is going on now.

In general, it is good to know the difference so you don’t think a piece of news always leads to a buying or selling decision.

The idea behind investment research is to learn about and become familiar with the company or investment idea.

It may be a good practice to read about one stock repeatedly over time, but unless its changes are significant enough, you won’t make up your mind. That’s how it should be, because the more information you collect, the better your base understanding of the business, industry, and the company’s performance.

When you finally get a major piece of information about the company, you will immediately know the background without having to refer back to all your previous notes.
