ESPN urged to dump Troy Aikman before the Super Bowl

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

After years of promoting the fact that Joe Buck and Troy Aikman will call the first Super Bowl on ESPN, they're struggling to get across the finish line.

For a couple of weeks, it seemed like the tension between Buck and Aikman was beginning to subside. The headlines around their well-documented blowup over differing contract situations had faded, and the Monday Night Football duo continued to produce high-quality broadcasts. ESPN president Jimmy Pitaro even acknowledged he was disappointed in the coverage around Buck and Aikman ahead of this season's Super Bowl, but was confident in their ability to remain professional.

Everything changed Thursday morning, however, when Aikman made arguably the biggest headline by flippantly accusing Buck of "always wanting a one-man booth" during his weekly radio appearance with The Musers on 96.7 The Ticket in Dallas. And while everyone was already coming around to the fact that the relationship between Buck and Aikman would end after this season, Meadowlark Media's David Samson believes it needs to end now.

"Troy Aikman has gotten to the 'I don't give an eff stage' of his career with ESPN because he keeps doing these things. And here's the problem, Jimmy Pitaro has to end this," Samson insisted. "I don't know whether he ends it today, tomorrow, next week or next month. But ESPN cannot have Joe Buck and Troy Aikman call the Super Bowl. They have invested too much money to have any distraction that they know about entering into the lead-up to the Super Bowl.

"There's always going to be something that happens. But if I am Jimmy Pitaro, I am making sure that I find someone to call the Super Bowl right now, and that I start the process. So that the audience, the media, the gossipers, that we get it all out of our system. We deal with the fact that Aikman is gone from Monday Night Football, and we deal with it now. So by the time the playoffs and the Super Bowl comes, it's no longer part of the conversation."

Boomer Esiason floated the possibility that Aikman might not call the Super Bowl when initial news broke around the current turmoil inside the Monday Night Football booth. And it seemed far-fetched at the time, but Esiason was speaking from experience. One month later, the idea of Aikman not making it to the Super Bowl is seeming a little more real.

Chances are Aikman calls Super Bowl LXI alongside Buck. No one will look good if this 25-year partnership goes up in flames to the point where they have to break up during the season. But two or three months ago, no one would have predicted the Buck-Aikman relationship would look as damaged as it does today.

If ESPN wants the most attention on their Super Bowl broadcast and the best chance at having a great call, then they need to keep Buck and Aikman together for the next few months. If ESPN wants to regain control of the situation, then they should boot Aikman from the booth now. But are they really concerned this will become something worse than tabloid headlines? Aikman took a pointed shot at Buck this week. But it wasn't necessarily a sign that Aikman is preparing to turn the Super Bowl stage into open season on ESPN.
