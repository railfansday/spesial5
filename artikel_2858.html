Thoughts Fernando Mendoza’s first game

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Las Vegas Raiders finally unleashed Fernando Mendoza, the 2026 No. 1 overall pick, against the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks. A game that the Raiders would lose 21-13. Without rushing to any judgment, good, bad, or indifferent, lots of good information on Mendoza was gathered. Let's break it down.

For as long as football has been football, the most important test a player has got to pass is the eye test. Mendoza passed the eye test in the first half of the game when he was 11/13 for 98 yards and scored his first touchdown by running a quarterback sneak 10 yards to pay dirt. A simplistic overview, but let's get more in-depth.

Against the champs, Mendoza came out swinging; from the outset, it was clear the moment was not too big for him. He was never rattled and did not make any egregious rookie mistakes. The hits weren't too much for him to handle and the speed of the game did not overwhelm him.

Throughout the game, he played with poise and confidence. Mendoza stayed within himself; once Brock Bowers went down, he remained playing as though he were 1/11 of the offense.

Throughout his collegiate career, particularly at Indiana University, Mendoza always took care of the football. Through the process of getting sacked, tucking and running and taking hits from free rushers, he always secured the football.

By his own account, when running the scout team in practice, Mendoza refused to practice putting the ball in harm's way. Meaning he didn't even want to practice placing the ball in positions where it could be intercepted. Fast forward to Sunday's game and it showed.

The only pass he threw that was moderately questionably placed was when his receiver was assisted in falling down out of his break. Accuracy, ball placement and decision-making were all traits Mendoza was exceptional at in college and so far it appears to have translated to the NFL.

Mendoza's ability to tuck it and run provided a wrinkle the Seattle Seahawks had trouble defending. Using his legs to get what was available on the ground was a missing element from Klint Kubiak's offense. On more than one occasion, it kept the chains moving and both the crowd and offense engaged. Being able to execute bootlegs and move the launching point allowed Mendoza to have early success.

Moving around in the pocket and evading the rush was clearly upgraded by inserting Mendoza into the lineup. Not just sliding in the pocket, but being able to muscle out of a collapsed pocket and still be able to keep his eyes downfield. He made one play, spinning out of trouble, rolling out and then hitting Cody White on a nice sideline pass to keep the chains moving

Mendoza's best trait in the passing game was going through his progressions and getting to his checkdowns quickly. We have seen veteran and even playoff quarterbacks be one-to-two read quarterbacks, then play hero ball. Mendoza wasn't one of them. All week long, the message was that he was 1/11th of the offense and that's what they stuck to.

He hit his checkdowns after establishing that his other reads were covered in an efficient manner. Never committing the cardinal sin of staring down receivers or leading defenders to the ball. Another of the many quality things done was no hospital balls thrown against a head-hunting defense.

The Seahawks defense finished the day with six sacks and got to the quarterback in completely inexcusable ways. Seahawks cornerback Devon Witherspoon, known for his slot/nickel blitzes, tallied three sacks on the day. It wasn't just that he got the sacks; no offensive member laid a finger on him; he came free and clear.

Mendoza, moving forward, is going to need to be more cognizant of his own pass protection. The entire line was struggling mightily, and the overloads/unaccounted for rushers are most certainly something he's going to see repeatedly. Which is something he acknowledged he needed to get better at speaking to the media after the game.

Without Bowers, the offense was brought to a halt. Having four of six second-half drives end in negative yards and 27 yards of total offense in the second half isn't going to cut it. Now, to be fair, blame isn't being placed on Mendoza. While he most certainly has his part to play, the atrocious blocking, running and route running did him no favors either.

An impressive early showing gave way to positive signs for the future. No one should have expected a rookie making his first start to topple the defending champions by himself. The least impressive yet most effective thing was Mendoza not digging a deeper and deeper hole trying to do too much.

There were plenty of wow plays; not many went for huge gains or points. But they were impactful and kept the chains moving in situations where the offense needed a spark. Getting this experience against one of the best defenses the NFL has to offer should be priceless to a film junkie such as Mendoza.

Following it up with a couple more tough defenses in Denver and Cleveland will make him better, as long as it doesn't break him. Raider Nation, be patient. Mendoza is the future and with a properly built team around him, the future is bright.
