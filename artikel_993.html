Hatton storms to big win as McIlroy snatches Race to Dubai title

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Rory McIlroy matched Colin Montgomerie's record of eight Race to Dubai titles with a runner-up finish in the DP World Tour Championship; Tyrrell Hatton cruised to a big victory and ninth DP World Tour title

Tyrrell Hatton stormed to a five-shot win at the DP World Tour Championship, where Rory McIlroy snatched Race to Dubai victory from Eugenio Chacarra.

Hatton took a one-shot lead into the final day at Jumeirah Golf Estates and never relinquished top spot, pulling clear of the field with four birdies in a six-hole stretch on the back nine.

The Englishman posted a six-under 66 to end on 15 under and claim a ninth DP World Tour title, as McIlroy leapfrogged Chacarra in the season-long standings with a runner-up finish after a thrilling Sunday tussle.

McIlroy carded a bogey-free 67 to win the Race to Dubai for a fifth successive season and match Colin Montgomerie's record of eight Order of Merit titles, with Chacarra narrowly missing out despite sharing third with a three-under 69.

Chacarra ended the week on eight under alongside Oliver Lindell, both among the 10 players to have secured dual membership on the PGA Tour via their Race to Dubai ranking, with Jacob Skov Olesen, Kristoffer Reitan, Francesco Laporta, Mikael Lindberg and Victor Perez all a further shot back in tied-fifth.

Hatton scrambled an unlikely par at the first after having to punch out from the trees, which double his lead when Perez made bogey, then quickly grabbed control with back-to-back birdies from the second.

McIlroy - starting four back - opened with a birdie but missed further chances over the front nine, only to race within one of Hatton after three birdies in a four-hole burst from the sixth.

Chacarra made a three-birdie run from the sixth to sit one back alongside Race to Dubai rival McIlroy and Lindberg, who briefly pulled level with Hatton after four birdies in his opening eight holes.

McIlroy reclaimed projected top spot in the season-long standings when Chacarra posted back-to-back bogeys around the turn, with the pair flip-flopping between the two spots during an eventful back nine.

Chacarra responded to another bogey at the 12th by birdieing his next two holes, briefly edging ahead by 0.1 projected points, with McIlroy visibly frustrated after starting his back nine with pars and failing to take advantage of the par-five 14th.

The battle for the Race to Dubai was far closer than the title race, as Hatton posted birdies at the 10th and 12th before extending his buffer with back-to-back gains from the 14th to jump five ahead.

McIlroy reclaimed the lead over Chacarra in the rankings with a stunning birdie at the 16th and pars over his last two holes, with another Harry Vardon Trophy confirmed when nobody could move alongside him on 10 under.

Hatton got up and down to save par at the 17th and closed out victory with a par at the last, seeing him go one better than last week's runner-up finish at the Abu Dhabi Aldar Championship and claim a sixth Rolex Series title.
