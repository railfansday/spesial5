Paul McGinley explains why LIV has hurt the chances of more golf being played outside America

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Paul McGinley has been a well-known critic of LIV Golf over the past few years.

With LIV Golf now on the verge of collapse, it's no surprise at all that McGinley has been revelling in the league's demise.

After Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced that they would be pulling the plug on their investment in LIV Golf back in April, it has been a case of death by a thousand cuts for the league.

Now LIV Golf will be no more, at least in its most recent iteration.

Over the past few years, Paul McGinley has always remained steadfast in his belief that LIV has done more harm than good

McGinley believes that golf fans have been the biggest losers from LIV Golf, with most events outside of the majors often missing the best golfers in the world battling it out against each other.

And now McGinley has highlighted yet another issue which he believes LIV Golf have caused.

McGinley took to his official X account on Monday and offered his take on how LIV Golf has affected the professional golfing landscape since its inception in 2022.

"I'd love to see more international golf played by the world's top players, but LIV's idea & unsustainable business model has given all the players in the game more money, making them less likely to travel – it has worked away from having top players play more international golf," the Irishman said.

"By (certain fans and journalists) supporting LIV so strongly, power now firmly sits with top players. With them being so much better off financially, there's even less chance they will travel and experience some great events – this increased player power is not good for the game internationally."

I'm just not sure McGinley has a valid point here.

There is a lot of damage that LIV Golf has done to the professional golfing landscape. However, I don't get the correlation between golfers being paid huge sums of money and then not traveling to play in tournaments all over the globe.

Had the likes of Jon Rahm and Bryson DeChambeau not joined LIV Golf, they would still be extremely wealthy.

The same goes for most of the players who jumped ship to join LIV.

I would be hugely surprised if golfers remained more in America now that LIV Golf is dead. If anything, more American golfers have been playing on the DP World Tour more frequently over the past few years, and I expect that trend to continue.
