Knicks Bulletin: ‘We haven’t arrived’

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

In the least surprising development in the NBA on Wednesday, the Knicks beat the Cavaliers.

Here are very few quotes from yesterday's affair and elsewhere. Enjoy Thanksgiving today, and make sure to save the leftovers!

On the benefits of potentially missing the NBA Cup knockout rounds:

"It is important (to win and potentially advance), but the reality of it is, when you look at the schedule, and you see that our percentage [to advance] is not that high, and so they kind of shared with me what our schedule would look like if we don't go and if we do go. And you look at it, you're like, 'Whoa, that's a lot of days off if you don't go.' And so again, you want to compete; you want to win. But if you don't, in my opinion, when you look at the schedule… you're like, there's an opportunity if you got some guys banged up to get rest and/or to work on some things in between the games that you have if you're not involved in the Cup."

On the physical and mental toll of competing in the NBA Cup:

"I didn't really break it down to an 83rd game, but when I talk about the Cup, it adds a level of stress maybe on your mind and your body because the games are intense as you go along and you're playing more games in a short amount of time.

"I remember it was like, game, game, game, and then you win the Cup, and you celebrate it that night, and you hop on a plane and go play in Indiana the next night. And so it's great. You like the competition and the setup, but it adds a lot of stress to you physically and mentally. I'm saying this because especially with the extended season we had last year and how taxing the games were for our playoff run mentally and physically, I'm trying to be smart, so if — knock on wood — we end up advancing, I'll try to be smart about it."

On refusing to chase regular-season wins at all costs and learning from Golden State's 73-win season:

"I try not to chase wins, especially early in the season. You want to win, but I'm not going to win every game at all costs.

"I wasn't part of the staff [for the first Golden State title] [Golden State] won Steve [Kerr's] first year. Then they lost in the second year, but they set a regular season record of 73 wins, and one of the things that he used to tell me, they gassed themselves trying to get this record and come playoff time they just didn't have enough in the tank to close it. And so that always sits right here for me."

On calling Pacôme Dadiet into his office and threatening to remove him from the rotation over his lack of effort:

"I didn't think he played hard in the game before. And I actually called him into my office and showed him some film. I told him, 'As a young guy, if you don't play hard, I can't play you.'

"And I said, 'Believe me, I got a lot of guys I can go to. I'm not afraid to go to somebody else. So the least you could do is play hard.' And I showed him how he wasn't. And I thought [Wednesday], he played hard."

On leaving OG Anunoby's injury management to the medical staff:

"There are a lot of medical terms that are above my head. They tell me and I don't really pick up on the injury. If he's out, I change my minutes sheet. And then I'll just wait for them to tell me when he's back. [Wednesday], OG's individual work happened before shootaround and I didn't even know he had individual work until his player development guy told me later on. You never want a guy to hurt himself more. But I don't think about it."

On the Knicks still having plenty of room for improvement despite winning 12 of their last 15 games:

"We're 12-3 since the start, but our s–t still does stink. We haven't arrived, and we still have to embrace the little things."

On Brown experimenting with an unusually deep rotation against Cleveland:

"I looked at the bench in the first quarter and realized, 'Wow, we played like 10 people in that first quarter.'"

On endorsing Tom Thibodeau as a potential head coaching hire for struggling NBA franchises:

"I made three playoffs with Thibodeau as my coach so his track record is one of success. So of course if I was a team that wanted to get back on track, I'd look at Tom Thibodeau to be my coach."

On Thibodeau's tactical knowledge, dedication and ability to prepare his teams to win:

"It's the X's and O's and the amount of care he puts into his craft, his team. He steps into that gym every day and, you know, you can say anything you want about the way he coaches, but one thing I know is his team is prepared to win a game. So that's a testament to who he is as a coach, the preparation he puts his team through and also his ability to have his team ready for the moment."
