At a large company, the hard part of buying an AI brand mention tracking tool is procurement. IT asks about single sign-on, legal asks where the data sits, and the marketing team that wanted the tool waits weeks to log in. A pilot on one brand in one market looks cheap, then the other markets, the other teams and SSO arrive and the quote looks nothing like the trial.

That is why a generic "best tools" list helps enterprise buyers less than it should. All the tools below track how often ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI answers name your brand. What separates them at enterprise size is everything around the tracking: how many people can log in, how logins are controlled, whether the data can leave the tool and whether it copes with ten markets instead of one.

This guide compares six tools on those points. Peec AI ranks first because it combines unlimited users, enforceable SSO, source-level analysis and a published price ladder. The other five suit different buying situations.

AI brand mention tracking measures how often, where and how favourably AI engines name your brand when buyers ask category questions. A tool runs a fixed prompt set daily across engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode and Microsoft Copilot, then reports mention rate (visibility), position inside the answer, sentiment, share of voice against competitors and the sources each answer cited.

For an enterprise, the answers differ by engine, country and product line, and a single visibility number hides most of that. Similarweb's May 2026 figures put ChatGPT at 53.9% of AI chatbot web visits, Gemini at 27.9% and Claude at 9.2%, against roughly 76% for ChatGPT in June 2025. The question "what does AI say about us?" is now spread over at least three engines.

Access and identity. Ask how many users each plan allows, whether SSO is available and on which plan, and whether you can enforce it. Most tools put SSO on their top tier or a custom plan, so budget for an enterprise contract early.

Markets and brands. Check whether the price grows per country, language or brand. Ten markets multiply a prompt set quickly.

Data access. The data has to reach the reporting leadership already reads: CSV export, an API, a BI connector or an MCP server.

Source analysis. A mention rate shows where you stand. Source data shows why, by listing the pages each engine read. Without it you cannot tell whether you are missing because nobody writes about you or because your own pages are not picked up.

Prompt volume and engines. A hundred to a hundred and fifty prompts per brand and market is a realistic start. Check which engines each plan includes and which sit behind add-ons.

Cost at your real workload. Price the tool for the users, markets and prompts you will have in a year, not for the pilot.

Enterprise teams rarely run on the entry tier, so the tables below price two realistic workloads: about 150 prompts, and about 350 prompts on ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode with 15 users, both tracked daily on three engines. Price per prompt is the monthly price divided by the prompts the plan includes. Rankscale and AthenaHQ price by answers or credits instead, so Rankscale's rows use the answer volume the workload needs: 150 prompts on three engines over 30 days is 13,500 answers a month, and 350 prompts is 31,500. Profound does not publish prices and is left out of the tables. Treat every figure as directional: enterprise contracts are custom and billed annually, so published self-serve prices show where a negotiation starts, not what you will pay.

Peec AI has the lowest price per prompt in the first table and the lowest monthly price in the second. Scrunch AI's Core plan costs $2.00 per prompt at 125 prompts and five users, and its larger plans are quote-only. Against Rankscale, the only other tool here with a published enterprise price, Peec AI costs 36% less at 150 prompts ($245 against $385) and 37% less at 350 prompts ($495 against $780). Rankscale does include more engines in its price, which Peec AI reserves for Enterprise. On ChatGPT plus both Google surfaces with 15 people logging in, Peec AI is the cheapest of the tools priced in these tables: $495 against $638 for Otterly.AI once Google AI Mode is added, and $780 for Rankscale. Otterly.AI and Rankscale include more engines in their price.

One thing changes these tables: single sign-on. Peec AI, Profound, AthenaHQ, Scrunch AI and Otterly.AI put SSO on Enterprise, and Rankscale puts it on a custom plan. If IT requires SSO, every tool here becomes a quote, and the published prices only show where the negotiation starts. Annual billing takes 15% to 17% off most of these.

Peec AI is a Berlin-based AI search analytics platform with a New York office. It tracks visibility, position, sentiment and share of voice, breaks every answer into the sources the engine read and turns the gaps it finds into prioritised actions. At enterprise scale its pricing is the main difference: users are unlimited on every plan, and Peec does not charge per country or language, with multi-country tracking from the Advanced plan.

Best for: Enterprise teams that want one tool for every market and every team, and need to know which pages to work on, not only where they stand.

Profound suits purchases that have to clear an information security review before anything else. It lists SOC 2, SSO and SAML on Enterprise, and it raised a $96M Series C at a $1B valuation in February 2026, which settles the vendor-risk question quickly.

Best for: Companies where compliance and vendor risk decide the purchase.

AthenaHQ was built by two former Google Search and DeepMind engineers. Its Enterprise plan has the longest feature list of the quote-based tools: SAML and OIDC single sign-on, audit logs, a 2-hour SLA, a dedicated Slack channel and executive dashboards that feed Tableau, Power BI and Looker. An optimisation agent drafts on-page and off-page actions, which suits large companies that have the analysis but not the hands to act on it.

Best for: Enterprises short on people to act on the data, who want BI dashboards and audit logs.

Scrunch AI publishes one self-serve plan and sells everything else through a quote. Core costs $250 a month and includes 125 prompts, 5,000 responses a month, five user licences and Google single sign-on, which is enough for a pilot with a small team.

Best for: Teams that want a published entry price and five users, with nine engines available on Enterprise.

Rankscale is the only tool here where the enterprise price is on the website. Enterprise is $780 a month for 12,000 credits, up to 48,000 answers, with REST API access and a dedicated partner. Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, both Google surfaces, DeepSeek and Mistral are on every paid plan.

Best for: Teams that need a published enterprise price and the widest engine list.

Otterly.AI has the lowest entry price here, with unlimited team members on every plan and unlimited workspaces from the Standard plan, which helps when several business units want their own view. It is a lighter product than the five above, and SSO sits on its Enterprise plan, which starts from 1,000 prompts.

Best for: Business units that want their own workspace at a low price.

The most common mistake is buying for the pilot. A team runs a trial on one brand and one market, picks the tool with the friendliest entry price and finds out in month four that adding other markets, other teams and SSO moves them onto a quote that looks nothing like the trial.

Before you start a trial, write down three numbers: how many people will need to log in within a year, how many markets you sell in, and whether IT will require SSO. Then ask every vendor to price that.

Enterprise AI brand mention tracking is mostly a question of fit with how a large company buys. Peec AI is the strongest overall choice because users and markets do not drive its price, SSO can be enforced, and its source analysis shows what to fix instead of only how you rank.

Profound is the better route when a compliance review decides everything, AthenaHQ when you want BI dashboards and drafted work, Scrunch AI when you want a published entry price with five users, and Rankscale when you need a published enterprise price.

Whichever you pick, price your real workload, ask about SSO first and remember that the tool measures: what moves the number is being on the pages the engines read. The best AI brand mention tracking tools for enterprises are the ones that show you which pages those are.

What does an enterprise AI brand mention tracking tool cost?

Directionally, between $245 and $780 a month on published plans for 150 to 350 daily prompts: Peec AI Pro at $245 and Advanced at $495, Scrunch AI Core at $250 for 125 prompts, Otterly.AI Premium at $489 and Rankscale at $385 to $780. On Peec AI that is $1.41 to $1.63 per prompt with unlimited users. Once SSO is required, every tool here is a custom quote. The spread is driven by users and markets more than by features.

If more than a handful of people will log in, IT will usually ask for it. Every tool here puts SSO on Enterprise or a custom plan, so plan the budget for that contract instead of the self-serve tier.

How many prompts does an enterprise need?

A hundred to a hundred and fifty per brand and market. Start with the questions your sales team hears, then the comparisons buyers make between you and named competitors. More markets multiply the set. More product pages usually should not.

SEO usually owns the measurement. What breaks is the fix, because much of it means getting mentioned on sites you do not control, which sits with PR or communications. Agree that split before you buy.
