New Zealand election coalition talks will likely take weeks – as it happened

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

This blog is closed, you can read our report on the New Zealand election here

Thanks for following the blog tonight. The election has ended without a clear winner. Here are the top news lines from what will go down as one of New Zealand’s closest elections in many decades.

Christopher Luxon’s National party and its right-leaning coalition partners are on the brink of a wafer-thin majority that could allow it to eke out a return to power.

The left-wing bloc, led by Labour’s Chris Hipkins, fell short of a majority.

Neither Luxon or Hipkins claimed victory or conceded defeat. Both acknowledged an expected wait before a governing coalition is negotiated.

A centrist newcomer, the Opportunity party, has entered parliament, polling above the 5% threshold. They could potentially be a kingmaker in a coalition.

Final results are expected on 27 November. Coalition talks will likely take weeks.

Hundreds of thousands of special votes are yet to be counted and could change the shape of parliament.

Saturday figures showed there could be a 130-seat parliament, with 66 seats needed for a majority.

Results suggested a coalition of National, Act and NZ First would hold 67 seats.

The 67 included 42 for National, 13 for Act and 12 for NZ First.

The left-bloc totalled 56 seats: Labour 35, Greens 16 and 5 for Te Pāti Māori.

Opportunity is projected to gain 7 seats.

Over the coming days and weeks we will get a sense of the magnitude of Qiulae Wong and the Opportunity party’s success tonight. Opportunity are currently projected to claim seven seats in parliament.

Wong has said that if she held the tiebreaker power, she was open to negotiating with either a conservative or a left-wing government and would deal first with whichever major party won the most votes.

The right-wing bloc might not need her, and Luxon has ruled out governing with Opportunity. Analysts say, however, that both major party leaders would likely answer the phone if Wong held the only path to power.

You can read more about the rise of Opportunity here, in this piece from Michelle Duff.

A couple of pics of Christopher Luxon as he arrived to speak to supporters tonight.

On the current count, the Greens are celebrating a best-ever result of what looks to be 16 seats in parliament.

With 0.56% support, Te Pāti Māori will be stoked with its result tonight, writes Jamie Tahana.

It seems odd, but the Māori party has spent the election deliberately asking people not to give them their party vote. Instead, they asked for electorate votes only.

Just a reminder, seven of the seats in New Zealand’s parliament are reserved for representatives of Māori voters – you can read more about them here.

The party’s co-leaders, Rawiri Waititi and Debbie Ngarewa-Packer, have comfortably won their respective seats of Waiariki and Te Tai Hauauru; Oriini Kaipara swept the Auckland seat of Tamaki Makaurau; Hana Rawhiti Maipi-Clarke, who hit international stardom for her haka in parliament, has cruised across the line in Hauraki-Waikato; while Aperahama Edwards holds a narrow lead over the Greens’ Hūhana Lydon in the far North seat of Te Tai Tokerau.

That’s five seats. But with a party vote so low, they will be five overhang seats.

Still, after a term that has seen plenty of in-fighting in Te Pāti Māori, there are signs of frustration in the Māori electorate, with Waititi and Ngarewa-Packer seeing significantly reduced majorities.

The Greens have fallen short of gaining their first ever Māori electorate, but across the seats party vote has risen to about 40%, making the party a significant force in Māori politics.

Labour, having been punished by the Māori electorate in 2023, has improved, particularly in terms of party vote, and has claimed a new MP in Mananui Ramsden, who won the country’s largest electorate, Te Tai Tonga.

So, hundreds of thousands of special votes are going to potentially change where things stand right now.

In his speech tonight, Luxon said 300,000 special votes were yet to be counted. Those votes, which will be counted and known by 27 November, typically sway left.

In the 2023 election there 600,000 special votes, and National dropped from 50 seats in the preliminary count to 48 seats.

In a 130-seat parliament, in which 66 seats are needed to govern, this is where things stand with 97% of the vote counted. However, special votes are still to be counted and the lay of the land will not be clear until 27 November.

Labour 35, Greens 16, Te Pāti Māori 5 = 56

National 29.3%, Labour 29.2%, Greens 13.3%, Act 11%, NZ First 9.8%, Opportunity 6%, Te Pāti Māori 0.6%.

Jamie Tahana has this hot-take on the Luxon speech:

It was a tough line to tread for a speech: National is the biggest party on current results – 29.30% – but Luxon has also led the party to one of its most unpopular points, only 0.1% ahead of Labour. Luxon has also lost some of his most senior and experienced MPs, which he conceded required “some tough phone calls”.

Luxon said that because of the closeness of the result, and with special votes still to be counted, caretaker conventions will apply.

“For now, I want to tell New Zealanders that well established caretaker conventions apply … The daily business of government will continue.”

Caretaker convention basically means that while a new government is being formed, the incumbent government is in “caretaker” mode, which means it will carry on the normal business of governing.

Jamie Tahana notes that for Luxon to continue as prime minister, he will have to cobble together a three- or four-party coalition, from a weakened position compared to 2023.

In his speech, Luxon said: “Negotiations and compromises are going to be required … A number of races are still too close to call.”

Jamie Tahana has some more from the Luxon speech:

Luxon called the election “too close to call”, and said “meaningful negotiations” for a new coalition government will start after the final results are known on 27 November.

“It looks like an MMP special,” he said. “It’s heartening to see that so far National is once again the largest party in parliament and we have a path to forming the next government.”

Luxon concedes result unlikely tonight, saying that meaningful negotiations will begin after final results on 27 November.

Luxon arrived to a crowd of about 700 raucous supporters chanting “three more years”. He was flanked by his wife Amanda and two children, waved and grinned as he descended a stairwell flanked in blue light.

A heavy music track - which could not be immediately identified through Shazam - thrummed as he walked down a long aisle, hugging supporters.

When he took the podium, Luxon conceded the election was “too close to call” but the crowd cheered as he announced National had secured the highest party count.

He went on to acknowledge Labour leader Chris Hipkins, and admonished supporters who booed Quilae Wong and Opportunity, saying it was “not the kiwi way”.

Luxon is also paying tribute to those high-ranking National MPs who find themselves out of a job tonight: Nicola Willis, Paul Goldsmith and Gerry Brownlee. “I want to thank you … for your years of service to our party”.

Luxon has called tonight’s result “an MMP special” and says “on the numbers so far, National is once again the largest party in parliament”.

It will be interesting to see what Luxon has to say. How will he frame the vote count and National’s performance?
