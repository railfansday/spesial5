Airline Norse Atlantic files for bankruptcy, succumbing to aviation fuel costs

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The low-cost airline was only founded a few years ago and had been struggling to turn a profit. The spike in aviation fuel prices caused by the war in Iran has now driven Norway's Norse Atlantic into bankruptcy.

Norwegian low-cost, long-haul airline Norse Atlantic's operating unit has declared bankruptcy, the carrier said on Friday.

The airline had warned on Wednesday that its financial situation was "critical," and suspended all flights the following day, saying it was urgently seeking emergency funding to fend off bankruptcy.

Like many commercial airlines, Norse Atlantic is highly exposed to the vastly increased jet fuel prices of 2026, attributable in very large part to the war in Iran and resultant tension throughout the wider Middle East.

Norse had said slightly earlier on Friday that its lessors had terminated their leasing contracts for all 12 aircraft in the carrier's fleet, demanding that the planes be handed back. Initially the company said it planned to contest this claim, which would have rendered it effectively unable to operate.

But the announcement of plans to file for bankruptcy followed fairly soon after.

"The Board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS has made the difficult decision to file for bankruptcy despite extensive efforts from the company and the group to find a solution," Norse CEO Eivind Roald said in a statement.

Other companies in the group like the stock market-listed Norse Atlantic ASA parent company and the Norse Atlantic UK subsidiary are not part of the filing and are still attempting to stay in business, the company said.

"The board, management and advisers of the listed entity Norse Atlantic ASA and the UK subsidiary are working to find a solution for the remaining part of the business to continue operations," Roald said.

The company said on Thursday that it planned to file for bankruptcy unless financing could be arranged and had warned: "There can be no assurance that a viable solution will be found."

Founded just five years ago and based in Arendal in Norway, Norse Atlantic had a fleet of 12 planes and serviced a total of 14 destinations. It had recorded a net loss of $61.9 million (€56.5 million) in 2025, even before the additional economic strains caused by the war in Iran.

Small and lower-cost airlines are struggling in particular, but the aviation industry in general is facing severe problems amid the strains on global fuel deliveries caused by the conflict in the Gulf.

In early 2026, as the US launched attacks on Iran — soon leading to reduced traffic through the Strait of Hormuz — aviation fuel rapidly spiked, more than doubling in price at its peak.

It rose higher even than in the immediate aftermath of Russia's full scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Although it dipped in mid-summer amid hopes of a resolution to the conflict, it rose once again as these faded and a stalemate developed.

This has posed the worst crisis for the industry since the COVID pandemic drastically reduced the volume of air traffic in 2020 and beyond, a much larger issue that many carriers were still struggling to recover from.
