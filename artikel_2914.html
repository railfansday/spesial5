LeBron Log No. 5: Popcorn in hand, a viral chorus and ‘basketball AI'

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

LeBron Log No. 5: Popcorn in hand, a viral chorus and 'basketball AI' originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

LeBron James spent Monday night in street clothes.

He still managed to argue a call with popcorn in his hand, practice his golf swing during a timeout and reject a report about himself before the Sixers left Brooklyn with a win.

That followed a Sunday afternoon in which James passed Jason Kidd on the NBA's career assists list and received an unusual description from Pacers head coach Rick Carlisle.

Welcome to the fifth edition of the LeBron Log.

James sat out the Sixers' 135-123 win over the Nets because of low back and sciatica management.

It was his fourth absence through 15 games. James has yet to play both ends of a back-to-back this season after logging nearly 33 minutes Sunday against Indiana.

He watched Monday's game from the end of the Sixers' bench in a yellow hat and an "Ohio Baby" sweatshirt. James snacked from a small bag before switching to popcorn.

When Joel Embiid believed he was fouled on a first-half drive, James stood and pleaded his case to official Jacyn Goble without putting down the box. He also joined conversations between teammates and officials during several timeouts.

James found another way to pass a third-quarter stoppage. As "Hot To Go" played inside Barclays Center, he practiced his golf swing near the bench.

His most pointed contribution came on Instagram.

An ESPN story by Dave McMenamin cited a Sixers source who said James had asked the coaching staff to remain patient while he worked toward finding his rhythm and conditioning by December.

James reposted ESPN's graphic and rejected the account.

"🧢! Those words never came out of my mouth!" he wrote.

The Sixers improved to 4-0 without James. They have beaten the Bucks, Hornets, Raptors and Nets in the games he has missed.

Brooklyn pulled within five points during the final five minutes Monday, putting the Sixers in another clutch game.

Philadelphia has reached 10 clutch wins more quickly than almost any team since the statistic began being tracked in 1996-97. The 2026-27 Sixers are the second team in that span with at least 10 such victories through their first 15 games.

The other was the 1997-98 Atlanta Hawks, who had 12.

James was also on the last team before Philadelphia to open a season with at least 10 consecutive clutch wins. The Lakers started 13-0 in those games last season.

James did not play in the Sixers' latest close finish, but he has now been part of the last two teams to start at least 10-0 under those circumstances.

Before Sunday's game, Carlisle explained why he tries to limit his play calls against James.

"LeBron James is one of the reasons we try not to have play calls, 'cause he knows all of them," Carlisle said. "He's a walking NBA basketball AI machine. That's what he is."

James followed with 15 points, 11 assists and seven rebounds in the Sixers' 131-124 win.

His pass to VJ Edgecombe for a layup with 1:19 remaining gave him 12,092 career assists and moved him past Kidd for third in NBA history. Only Chris Paul and John Stockton remain ahead of him.

Noah Levick wrote more about James' milestone and the anticipation behind his passes.

When told what Carlisle had called him, James appreciated the description.

"So, yeah, Rick is great," James said. "He's one of the greatest coaches of all time, and I've had my battles with him over the years. That's super humbling coming from a guy like that."

Philadelphia has found its soundtrack for James' biggest home moments.

The Sixers have played the viral "LeBron, LeBron, LeBron" song after several of his baskets, including his late putback against the Knicks on Oct. 30.

The refrain comes from a version of Steve Lacy's "Oh Yeah?" that replaces "Come on" with James' name. The Baylor University Men's Choir performed its own rendition, helping the song spread across social media.

Lacy later joined in. During an Oct. 15 concert at Freedom Mortgage Pavilion in Camden, he performed the LeBron version as the crowd sang along.

James has noticed Philadelphia's use of it.

"I've seen it. I've heard it. It's pretty hilarious, to be honest," James said. "I heard it towards the end of a game when we were winning. I made a play, and I heard that song. I was just chuckling to myself on the inside."
