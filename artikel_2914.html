Jensen Huang Stepped Away From Nvidia. The Stock Has Crashed. Instead of Rushing to Buy the Dip, Here Are 2 AI Stocks Down More Than 15% to Buy Instead.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

You don't have to buy Nvidia to get exposure to AI growth.

By Brett Schafer – Nov 19, 2026 at 10:07AM EST

Nvidia (NVDA +3.12%) investors have learned a quick lesson in downside risks. Investors couldn't have predicted that the founder and CEO would abruptly leave because of a scandal involving marital infidelity and an alleged Chinese spy. But that’s the story that has evolved over the last few weeks, and Nvidia shares have crashed 36% from their highs.

With its cheap-looking forward price-to-earnings ratio (P/E), investors may be drawn to Nvidia right now. However, with the potential risks from possibly selling its prized computer chips and bypassing sanctions in the process, there is massive uncertainty about buying Nvidia stock today.

Here are two other AI-related stocks getting caught up in the Nvidia carnage that you can buy for your portfolio instead.

When talking about prized technologies, there may be none more important than those of Dutch company ASML (ASML +2.94%). The stock is down 26% over the past three months.

It sells highly complex laser machines that are key to building advanced computer chips like those made by Nvidia, Alphabet, and AMD, and no other company in the world can replicate its capabilities. It also has services associated with these machines, as chip manufacturers perform maintenance on them.

Last quarter, ASML's revenue was 11.66 billion euros, up 25% year over year. It has nice backlog growth, with demand from customers in 2027 and 2028 not meeting its current supply capabilities, even when already pricing in growth. This should lead to stronger revenue growth in the next few years and beyond.

The key manufacturer of Nvidia's computer chips is Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM +2.88%). It has the leading market share in building advanced computer chips used in AI, and has factories centered in Taiwan, with new hubs being built in Japan and the United States. The stock is down 16% over the past three months.

Revenue growth has been phenomenal because of AI. In October, revenue grew 60% year over year. Margins are also fantastic, at 59% last quarter, showing the immense pricing power TSMC has. If it continues this level of revenue growth in the next few years, TSMC may be one of the largest companies in the world by sales and earnings.

What happens if Nvidia's demand for TSMC chips goes away? Well, TSMC should be able to pick up the slack with orders from competitors. No matter how Nvidia does, TSMC has a diversified revenue base that will benefit from demand as long as AI services continue to grow.

Unlike Nvidia, TSMC and ASML have no legitimate competitors. No matter what happens with the AI trade, these companies should see soaring demand in the years ahead, which should lead to growing earnings power.

Their shares are not as cheap as Nvidia’s, with forward P/E ratios of 16.4 at Nvidia, 20 at TSMC, and 29 at ASML as I write this. However, given that there are fewer risks beyond the general cyclicality of semiconductor demand for these other two stocks, long-term shareholders would do better by betting on these more durable AI winners and buying and holding for the next decade.

Avoid buying the dip on Nvidia. Add ASML and Taiwan Semiconductor instead.
