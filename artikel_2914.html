The Midterms Put Data Center Permitting Back in Play. Here Are 2 Industrial Stocks That Win if Power Moves Behind the Meter.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Bloom Energy and Caterpillar have sold off sharply along with the AI sector, but shifting electricity trends could support long-term business success.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 23, 2026 at 7:11PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bloom Energy ›

Artificial intelligence (AI) is likely to be a world-changing technology, but it uses a huge amount of electricity. AI power demand has been so high that it has driven up electricity costs for everyone, hitting the pocketbooks of everyday people even as inflation is running hot. That's resulted in pushback, with data centers playing a prominent role in the recent midterm elections. That could actually be a net positive for companies like Bloom Energy (BE +18.09%) and Caterpillar (CAT +7.17%). Here's why.

The current administration in Washington has been very supportive of artificial intelligence. With the House and Senate changing hands next year following big wins in November's election, support for AI could waver. There are already states that have put up roadblocks to further data center construction. One of the biggest problems is electricity, with a push for data centers to supply their own power to avoid straining the grid.

That, however, could be a huge win for companies like Bloom Energy and Caterpillar. Bloom Energy makes factory-built hydrogen fuel cells that can be easily deployed where they are needed to provide off-grid power. Caterpillar is best known for making the heavy construction equipment used to build data centers, but it also makes generators used to provide off-grid power.

If data centers have to bring their own power, Bloom Energy and Cat are primed to help. Notably, Bloom Energy entered 2026 with a $6 billion product backlog, up 140% year over year. That backlog has likely grown throughout the year (it only reports its backlog annually) and could get even bigger in the future. The company, which has lost money for years, is likely to turn profitable in 2026.

Caterpillar ended the third quarter with a record $78 billion backlog, up 96% year over year. While not all of that backlog is tied to its power systems, the bigger takeaway is that demand remains very strong across all its products. That said, management noted that bringing up manufacturing capacity in its power division remains a primary goal because demand is so strong.

The shifting political winds in Washington could increase the already strong demand that Bloom Energy and Cat are seeing. But investors need to distinguish between the stocks and the businesses here. AI-related stocks, including both of these companies, have been volatile of late. If you own them or are considering buying them, you need to approach them with a long-term mindset. Another option is to add them to your wish list and be ready to pounce if there is a deep AI-driven bear market.
