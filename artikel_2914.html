NFL predictions: 3 keys to victory for Bills over Vikings in Week 9

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Week 9 of Monday Night Football is setting up to be a great game between two of the NFL's hottest teams in 2026. The 6-1 Buffalo Bills are in Minneapolis, MN this evening looking to take down the 6-1 Minnesota Vikings on their home turf in front of a national audience.

It's a matchup featuring Super Bowl-starved franchises with championship DNA all over their rosters. The Bills boast a top-ranked scoring offense (33.3 ppg) led by an all-world quarterback in Josh Allen. However, their defense is among the worst in the NFL when it comes to holding opponents out of the end zone.

Buffalo heads into U.S. Bank Stadium as a slight favorite (-3) with FanDuel Sportsbook, but the Vikings and their top-ranked scoring defense (13.3 ppg) will provide the toughest test yet for Allen and the Bills. Quarterback Carson Wentz is set to reprise his role as backup-turned-starter due to a hamstring injury that has quarterback Kyler Murray listed as week-to-week.

That roster change may actually benefit Minnesota. Despite what Murray brings to the field as a dual-threat quarterback, the Vikings' offense has found more success this season with Wentz at the helm. Wide receivers Justin Jefferson and Jordan Addison could feast this evening if Buffalo's defensive backs let their guard down on even one play.

It's strength versus strength between the Bills' offense and the Vikings' defense, but something has to give. Will that marquee matchup decide who wins, or will it instead be the game within the game featuring Buffalo's defense against Minnesota's offense?

Beyond the obvious need for ball security and points, here's what I predict happens for the Bills on offense, defense, and special teams if they improve to 7-1. There's a segment of analysis that believes the best way to attack the Vikings' defense and the exotic blitz packages is by leveraging running backs in the screen game. That, and getting wide receivers involved in quick bubble screens could help Buffalo take down Minnesota.

The Bills have one of the game's best third-down running backs in Ty Johnson, who is a true weapon as a receiver out of the backfield. Buffalo also features wide receiver Khalil Shakir, a bona fide pinball wizard after the catch and between the down and distance.

If the Bills want to stress defensive coordinator Brian Flores' blitz-heavy scheme, they have plenty of options to send Vikings defenders scrambling back from where they came. Since his return from Injured Reserve to begin the season, Johnson has been lethal catching passes from Allen.

In five games played, Johnson has 15 catches (15 targets) for 174 yards — averaging 34.8 yards per game. I expect a heavier workload for Johnson in Week 9, perhaps doubling his per-game averages in catches and yards. If true, that looks like a six-catch, 70-yard day.

Shakir is the Bills' second-leading wide receiver, having caught 27 passes (37 targets) for 354 yards and one touchdown. Look for Allen and Shakir to cook up a modern throwback that see No. 10 darting through traffic early and often.

Head coach Joe Brady and Allen would be wise to throw more than a cursory pass or two to running back James Cook III. We've yet to see Cook really highlighted as a receiver, which is interesting given his pre-draft status as that class' best dual-threat back. Cook has just 12 catches (18 targets) for 109 yards in seven games. I say he gets at least three receptions tonight, and he takes one of them to the house.

Carson Wentz is a grizzled vet whose scrambling game scares no one. He much prefers passing from a clean pocket, allowing his receivers to do the leg work. Lucky for Wentz, he has two of the best in Justin Jefferson and Jordan Addison.

The Bills can't allow Wentz to sit back and get into rhythm. Bringing disguised blitzes could be the move to speed up and fluster Wentz, forcing him to try and escape the pocket. Even better, confusing Wentz with blitzes might just stop him in his tracks, or send the football the other way with Buffalo.

In his first season as a 3-4 outside linebacker, Greg Rousseau has played the most dominating football of his career. Rousseau has seven sacks, nine tackles for loss, seven pass defenses, three forced fumbles, and one epic blocked field goal.

Minnesota will have to figure out how to handle Rousseau, but it may cost them a couple sacks in the process. That's because the Vikings are likely going to do all they can to try and limit the damage done by defensive tackle Deone Walker. The second-year interior D-lineman has been a wrecking ball each week, and teams have begun focusing on stopping him on every down.

Remember, Ed Oliver is back now, and he's likely to get in on the sack party. Then there's also Bradley Chubb at outside linebacker. Can Minnesota stop Rousseau, Walker, Oliver, and Chubb the entire game? Don't bet on it.

All the focus on Buffalo's primary pass rushers could lead to good things for the interior linebackers and safeties. We saw safety Cole Bishop used as a blitzer against the Baltimore Ravens, and it's likely his number is called when Wentz least expects it. With safety Damar Hamlin now installed as a field general in the defensive backfield, Bishop appears to be playing more free, and instinctual in key moments.

The goal on defense: Make life miserable for Wentz and Minnesota's offensive line. Force him into mistakes while locked-in on Jefferson and Addison, sending the Vikings' offense back to the sideline without points.

Bass-o-matic is back! Through seven games, Bass is a perfect 16-of-16 on field goals in 2026. He's 4-of-4 between 40 and 49 yards, and 2-of-2 from 50 yards and beyond. For a team that struggles holding opponents out of the end zone, having a reliable kicker in crunch time is a must. That's been Bass to a T so far this season.

While it's unlikely he remains perfect all season, Bass has rebounded better than anyone could have predicted. His play is one of the key reasons Buffalo is 6-1, and I have a feeling his number's called late tonight to try and send the Bills home winners.
