Ugly wins count, too. And the Suns will take every one.

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

You know what I like? When the Phoenix Suns win basketball games. Sometimes it's not pretty, and sometimes the journey to the final buzzer is far more stressful than it needs to be. But as long as the Suns walk away with a victory, there's reason to feel positive. Following Thursday night's win over the Charlotte Hornets, that's exactly how I'm feeling.

Phoenix has now won two consecutive games and sits one victory shy of the .500 mark. Once again, the bench unit provided the spark, serving as the catalyst that helped propel the Suns to a 17-point advantage. For a stretch, it appeared Phoenix was on its way to a relatively comfortable victory, the kind where you could sit back, relax, and enjoy the basketball without wondering how the team might find a way to complicate things.

But this is the Phoenix Suns we're talking about. That 17-point lead? Evaporated.

The Suns were outrebounded 52-31, continuing a troubling trend that has plagued them throughout the early portion of the season. And quite honestly, had Charlotte been even remotely competent at taking care of the basketball, Phoenix might have messed around and lost this one. The Hornets committed a staggering 27 turnovers, and while it's tempting to look at that number and credit the Suns' defensive pressure, the reality is a little more complicated. Phoenix finished with 11 steals, certainly demonstrating its ability to disrupt passing lanes and create opportunities in transition. But a significant portion of Charlotte's turnovers came from careless passes and poor decision-making, the kind of mistakes that reveal a team struggling with connectivity and offensive cohesion.

Still, Phoenix deserves credit for capitalizing on those mistakes. The Suns generated 38 points off turnovers, a massive contribution that ultimately played a significant role in securing the victory. They also took care of the basketball themselves, committing only eight turnovers, which provided a welcome example of offensive discipline.

There were other positives as well. Despite being thoroughly dominated on the glass, Phoenix matched Charlotte with 38 points in the paint. And while the Hornets held a 16-7 advantage in second-chance points, the damage could have been considerably worse given the disparity in rebounding. That doesn't excuse the ongoing problems on the boards, but it does provide some perspective on how the Suns managed to survive another evening in which securing missed shots appeared to be an optional activity.

The rebounding remains a major concern, and there are certainly other issues worth examining over the next couple of days before the Suns welcome the Los Angeles Lakers to town on Sunday. We'll have plenty of time to dissect what went wrong, what needs to improve, and why this team continues to make certain aspects of the game far more difficult than necessary.

But there were encouraging moments, too. The bench continues to produce, the Suns are generating points off turnovers, and they're beginning to string together victories despite their imperfections. And ultimately, the most important statistic is the one that matters more than anything else. The Phoenix Suns won the basketball game. I'll take it.
