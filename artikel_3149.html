American Express vs. S&P Global: Steady Revenue vs. Flat Trends

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

American Express kept climbing quarter after quarter while S&P Global's revenue stalled and then dipped in September 2026, a gap worth watching.

By Brendan Coffey – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:10PM EST

American Express (AXP -0.12%) primarily generates its revenue by delivering a comprehensive global suite of charge and credit payment card solutions, managing robust merchant network infrastructures, and offering specialized travel support and fraud mitigation services to individual consumers, small and mid-sized enterprises, and large corporate clients.

It recently launched a new high-yield business savings account alongside a refreshed corporate card suite, while it concurrently entered a strategic agreement to integrate over one million short-term rental properties directly into its consumer travel booking platform.

S&P Global (SPGI -1.32%) primarily earns its revenue by delivering independent credit ratings, extensive market data, advanced analytics, and widely used valuation benchmarks to institutional investors, financial advisors, wealth managers, and corporate professionals operating across the global capital, automotive, and energy markets.

It recently completed the acquisition of the blockchain security firm OpenZeppelin, and it finalized the divestiture of its upstream energy software portfolio.

Revenue here refers to the data provider's standardized income-statement revenue line item, which for banks in this dataset is defined as interest income plus non-interest income and is not net of interest expense, serving as a fundamental metric that helps investors evaluate a company's overall sales generation capacity before accounting for operating expenses. American Express revenue here is the reported amount before net interest expense.

American Express vs. S&P Global Revenue over the last 8 quarters.

The first thing to check for when examining a company's revenue trend is seasonality: some times of the year have stronger buying patterns than others. There usually is some seasonality for every business.

We see this seasonality in American Express's business, which, like other financial businesses, sees an uptick in the fourth quarter from consumer spending, in this case, on its credit cards, and a dip in the first quarter. American Express also sees an uptick in revenue during the summer months.

There is no seasonality in S&P Global's business, but there is a lot of variability, based on the frequency of debt ratings it is commissioned for, which is influenced by interest rate levels and the general financial health of the business world.

The revenue trend is the most obvious metric for investors to examine when considering a stock. In thiscase, the chart shows two relatively stable businesses gorwing slowly over time.
