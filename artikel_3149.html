e.l.f. Beauty vs. Monster Beverage: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Monster's 22.7% net margin dwarfs e.l.f.'s 4.5%, but e.l.f. trades at a cheaper valuation on both forward earnings and sales.

By Seena Hassouna – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 2:23PM EST

10 stocks we like better than e.l.f. Beauty ›

Investors choosing between growth and stability often weigh the explosive potential of cosmetics against the steady demand for energy drinks. This comparison evaluates whether e.l.f. Beauty (ELF -5.01%) or Monster Beverage (MNST -0.57%) is the better buy.

e.l.f. Beauty disrupts the traditional makeup industry by offering high-speed product innovation and prestige-quality items at accessible prices. Monster Beverage dominates the beverage coolers with its diverse energy portfolio and global scale. Both companies have delivered impressive long-term results, but they offer distinct risk and reward profiles for retail investors.

e.l.f. Beauty operates in the competitive segment of consumer staples stocks, focusing on high-quality cosmetics at accessible price points. The company sells its products through mass, drug, and specialty retail channels, including key partners like Target (TGT -0.97%), Walmart (WMT +1.51%), and Amazon (AMZN -4.10%). These retail relationships account for nearly 76% of net sales, and such customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $1.9 billion. This represented revenue growth of roughly 39.9% compared to the prior twelve-month period. Net income for those 12 months was approximately $86.6 million, supported by a net margin of close to 4.5%.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.8x. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity to show how a company funds its operations. The current ratio, which measures the ability to pay short-term debts with short-term assets, is close to 2.3x. In the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $190.1 million. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 40.9% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Monster Beverage functions as a holding company for major energy brands like Reign, NOS, and Bang. It primarily distributes its beverages through an extensive network of bottlers, relying on Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%) as its primary global distribution partner. The company currently serves a growing number of international markets across 80 countries, leveraging this massive network to maintain its market lead.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached close to $9.6 billion. This reflects revenue growth of approximately 20.2% over the previous year. Net income for the period was nearly $2.2 billion, resulting in a healthy net margin of approximately 22.7%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carries a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0x. This indicates that shareholder equity covers all liabilities, leaving the company with no debt relative to equity. The current ratio is nearly 3.7x, indicating the business has sufficient liquidity to cover its short-term obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $2.0 billion.

e.l.f. Beauty faces intense competition in its industry from multinational firms such as Coty (COTY -2.47%) and L'Oréal (LRLCF -1.17%). Because it sources the majority of its products from third-party manufacturers in China, the company is exposed to trade policies and tariff-related risks. It must also manage rapid growth and system integrations while remaining dependent on a limited number of major retail customers, such as Target and Walmart.

Monster Beverage relies heavily on its distribution relationship with Coca-Cola, which is vital to its international performance. The company faces significant competition in the energy drink category from PepsiCo (PEP +1.11%) and other global beverage giants. Additionally, it is subject to risks related to ingredient pricing, supply chain disruptions, and evolving government regulations regarding energy drink labeling and health standards.

e.l.f. Beauty currently appears more attractively valued than Monster Beverage based on both its lower Forward P/E and its lower P/S ratio.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

The Forward P/E compares current share price to future earnings estimates, while the P/S ratio measures market value against sales over the past twelve months.

What sets e.l.f. apart is selling prestige-quality makeup and skincare at mass-market prices, and it launches new products faster than the bigger beauty companies can respond. That's why I'd buy it for 2027. Growth is strong, and rhode, the skincare brand founded by Hailey Bieber that e.l.f. bought in August 2025, gives it a bigger foothold in skin care. Shoppers who keep buying small treats when budgets tighten also help the case. I'd treat this as a starter position to watch through 2027, not a stock to hold blindly. The December quarter is the first fully organic comparison, so it will show how fast e.l.f. grows once rhode sits in both periods. Beauty runs on trends, and rhode depends heavily on one founder, so a fast brand can cool quickly. The debt from its acquisitions is manageable, but it limits e.l.f.'s room if growth slows. Keep the position smaller than you'd give a steadier name. Then watch whether growth holds without new deals, whether margins stay healthy, and whether new launches keep landing. If all three hold, build the position over time. If they don't, you've risked a small stake and not your conviction.
