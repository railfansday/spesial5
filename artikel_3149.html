42 attorneys general fight Character.AI’s plan to auction users’ chats

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The states say the 1 December sale would hand a buyer intimate chats, including children’s, with no limits on their use. The only disclosed bidder was set up a day before it was picked.

A bipartisan coalition of 42 attorneys general wants a bankruptcy court to stop Character.AI from auctioning users’ private chats. The sale covers more than 21 million US accounts, California Attorney General Rob Bonta said on Tuesday. Of those, more than 2.1 million belong to minors.

The chats would be sold together with users’ IP addresses and other metadata. The auction is set for 1 December, according to the company’s own sale notice.

“This kind of behavior is illegal under federal and California law and bankruptcy should not be used to sidestep these consumer protections,” Bonta said.

The chats include conversations in which AI characters drew children and adults into explicit sexual exchanges, the attorneys general say. They also include chats with “therapist” and “physician” characters, in which users discussed self-harm, eating disorders and suicidal thoughts.

Each chat comes with the user’s profile and IP address. Flagged users’ risk scores, which Character.AI assigned, come with them too.

The only bidder disclosed so far is Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC. It was set up in Delaware on 13 November. A day later, Character.AI picked it as the base bid for the auction. The contract names no parent company or investors, the states say.

Character.AI then disclosed that New York investment firm Octave Advisors backs Smirks. A bankruptcy judge approved it as lead bidder on Tuesday, The Wall Street Journal reported.

They want the court to reject Smirks as lead bidder and refuse its $350,000 break-up fee. At a minimum, any sale must leave out children’s chats and other sensitive data, they say.

“Character.AI promised users they would never sell their personal information,” Connecticut Attorney General William Tong said.

The coalition also opposed the company’s separate licensing deal with Disney, which is hiring most of its staff. Disney named Character.AI’s chief executive, Karandeep Anand, as its first CTO. The court will hear the sale on 3 and 4 December.
