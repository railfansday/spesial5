Stock Market Today, Nov. 19: Dow Plunges 1,100 Points as the S&P 500 Suffers Its 6th Straight Day of Losses

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Selling pressure is spreading well beyond technology stocks, raising concerns that Wall Street's recent pullback could develop into a more pronounced market correction.

By RJ Fulton – Nov 19, 2026 at 4:50PM EST

Wall Street's sell-off intensified on Thursday, November 19, as selling pressure spread across nearly every sector of the market. The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI -2.19%) plunged more than 1,100 points, falling 2.19% to 49,097 and moving closer to correction territory. Meanwhile, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.02%) fell 2.00% to 7,177, marking its sixth consecutive session of losses, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -2.25%) dropped 2.23% to 23,886 as investors continued to retreat from growth stocks.

Selling pressure extended beyond equities, with gold falling 2.10% to $3,663.50. The 10-year Treasury yield eased three basis points to 5.07%, offering little relief to equity markets. Energy and consumer staples were among the few sectors to finish in positive territory, gaining 0.14% and 0.04%, respectively, while industrials plunged 2.61%.

Despite the broader market sell-off, Insmed (INSM +20.58%) surged 20.58% following reports that French pharmaceutical giant Sanofi (SNY -2.25%) is considering an acquisition of the biotechnology company. Palo Alto Networks (PANW +6.42%) also bucked the broader trend, gaining 6.42% after reporting stronger-than-expected quarterly earnings and announcing a $4.5 billion acquisition.

Conversely, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -7.72%) plunged 7.72% following a media report alleging ties to a blacklisted Chinese server manufacturer. The report adds another layer of regulatory uncertainty to a semiconductor industry already grappling with tightening U.S. export restrictions and deteriorating investor sentiment.

Perhaps the most concerning development on Wall Street is how broadly selling pressure has spread. What initially appeared to be a pullback driven by elevated technology valuations is increasingly developing into a broader market downturn. Even Nvidia's (NVDA -3.30%) exceptional quarterly earnings have failed to reverse sentiment, suggesting that concerns surrounding inflation, interest rates, and stock valuations are taking precedence over strong corporate results.

Looking ahead, investors should pay particularly close attention to Treasury yields and the U.S. dollar. With the Federal Reserve signaling that further interest rate hikes may be necessary, persistently restrictive financial conditions could put additional pressure on corporate earnings and stock valuations heading into 2027.

Walmart's (WMT -1.04%) upcoming quarterly earnings report will also provide an important assessment of consumer spending heading into the holiday shopping season. While recent results from Target (TGT +0.92%) and TJX Companies, Inc. (TJX -0.63%) have been encouraging, any signs of weakening consumer demand could introduce another headwind for equities.

For a meaningful recovery to materialize, investors should look for improving market breadth, stabilizing Treasury yields, and the S&P 500 reclaiming its 200-day moving average. Until then, investors should be cautious about interpreting isolated rallies as signs of a broader market recovery.
