NexPoint RE Finance (NREF) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Shrinking multifamily supply and rising life science leases shape next moves.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

NexPoint Real Estate Finance, Inc. (NREF -7.17%) reported third quarter results characterized by stable dividend coverage and the tactical deployment of capital into high-yielding residential and life science credit. Management reported that the company is actively developing a supplemental capital program to address liquidity needs for sponsors of agency-underwritten multifamily debt facing extension tests. The company stated it maintains a clean capital structure with significant dry powder following a preferred stock offering and an upsized credit facility. Management indicated that the current supply-demand dynamics in multifamily, marked by a significant drop in new construction starts, are expected to restore pricing power as the pipeline bottoms in 2027.

Matt McGraner: Thank you, Paul. This quarter was a solid, consistent execution. Both EAD and CAD came in ahead of our guidance. Given our positive credit profile and the improving but still challenging macro backdrop, we continue to play offense in this environment and expect opportunities for our solution-oriented capital to grow over the near-term. The Fed held its target rate at 3.75%-4% in late October, and the 10-year Treasury moved back above 5%. Higher for longer pressures borrowers who need to refinance or meet extension tests, and that is exactly where disciplined solution capital earns its pricing power. Our new investments are priced at double-digit coupons at reset basis, and therefore, we believe we are well-positioned for this type of environment.

Turning to our verticals. Residential, between multi and single-family rental, remains our largest exposure. Our multifamily credit sits predominantly behind agency-underwritten senior debt, which has historically been some of the most resilient collateral in real estate. Freddie Mac's aggregate losses have averaged about 7 basis points per year since 2009, with $432.6 million of cumulative losses on $645.8 billion of issuance through August of 2026. That is not to say there is not stress in the system, though. Trepp has Freddie's multifamily delinquency at 80 basis points of UPB in August, up from 48 basis points in August of 2024. We see this as an opportunity, however, which I will elaborate on in a minute.

On the supply side, multifamily construction starts fell more than 40% between 2023 and 2025, and new deliveries are forecasted to bottom near 327,000 units in 2027, well below the roughly 600,000-unit peak in 2024. Supply is what broke pricing power over the last two years, and the falling supply pipeline is what we expect to restore it. That said, the recovery is not linear, and in the third quarter, blended lease rates across our portfolios remained flat to slightly positive, and concessions, while abating, are still prevalent in the market for Class A and Class B multifamily in our geographical footprint.

The good news is we are underwriting and deploying our capital into new residential investments on today's rents, again, on reset basis and not on a recovery pro forma. Our single-family rental book continues to perform really well, with significant equity cushion. Overall, we view the sector's credit profile as slightly more favorable than multifamily, with rents already in positive territory and less pockets of distress. The number of households renting single-family homes continue to rise, and higher for longer should keep demand high for our affordable SFR in our markets. Now on to life science.

At LYFE, the sponsor's recap process is underway, and we continue to expect a substantial, if not all, of our return of capital in the next quarter or two. When the capital comes back, our plan is to redeploy most of it into residential collateral and to move life science back toward roughly one-third of the portfolio over time. Across the rest of our life science exposure, fundamentals continue to improve sequentially, particularly in Boston and San Francisco, with 220,000 sq ft of new leases signed during the year-to-date. The remaining industry-wide speculative pipeline is expected to deliver through 2026, after which new supply will be limited largely to fully pre-leased build-to-suit projects.

Demand is broadening beyond traditional lab users to biomanufacturing, onshoring, and AI-driven users that need power density, floor loads, and lab-grade infrastructure. Importantly, and again, most of this book was originated in 2025 through 2026 at a reset basis. In self-storage, fundamentals troughed in 2025, and with new construction pipelines well below historical averages, we expect conditions to keep strengthening. During the quarter, we continued to add small mezzanine positions on newly stabilized storage assets at double-digit coupons. The sector has already largely reported better-than-expected results during the quarter, and our NSP-owned portfolio continues to perform very well also, with occupancy as of 9/30 at 93.5% and rents up 600 basis points year-over-year.

On the pipeline in April, we outlined more than $190 million of NREF investments and more than $225 million of structured product opportunities. Year-to-date, I'm pleased to say we have closed $280 million of that $415 million pipeline and have another $160 million committed over the next 12 months with an active pipeline backlog building. We were deliberately selective in the third quarter, and with the additional Mizuho capacity and expected repayments, we have the dry powder to lean in at attractive spreads in the fourth quarter and into 2027. I'm glad we waited on some of the resi opportunities because more will appear.

One critical need for the market will be to provide solution and special situation capital for the residential sector, and this is a great opportunity for NREF over the next couple of years, with the caveat that it might not fully materialize if the capital markets rapidly improve, which we wouldn't hate either. The stress in agency multifamily is concentrated in floating rate loans originated in 2020 through 2023, when SOFR was near zero. Those loans need replacement rate caps, cap escrows, and in many cases, a paydown to meet their refi and extension test, and a meaningful number of sponsors do not have the equity to do it.

In agency floating rate securitizations alone, that vintage is more than $32 billion across roughly 1,000 loans, and about a quarter of that balance is already on special servicer watch lists. We have demonstrated time-and time again that we can be a creative partner with the agencies, particularly Freddie, to provide solution-oriented capital on a bespoke basis. We're already developing a program where NREF would provide co-terminous supplemental capital behind the existing first lien so that sponsors can buy the cap, fund reserves, and pass their tests rather than hand back the keys. This is exactly the kind of solution capital we have built the platform around.

It sits behind conservatively underwritten agency senior debt in an asset class and in markets we know as operators, and it is priced like rescue capital. We would also expect it to be financed on a matched term basis, similar to how our original SFR portfolio was financed. The program is not final, but we do believe there is a strong need for it, and we are excited about it. To close and summarize, we are pleased with where we are, particularly in this cycle. Earnings came in ahead of guidance, the dividend is covered, credit continues to hold well, and our balance sheet has more capacity than it did a year ago.
