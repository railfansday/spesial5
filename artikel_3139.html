On Oct. 6, the S&P 500 index (^GSPC -3.70%) and the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index reached record highs. But ever since then, U.S. tech stocks have been going through a sell-off. The Nasdaq-100 has dropped by about 10.5% since Oct. 6, and the S&P 500 has lost about 3.3%.

It's often said that the U.S. stock market has become so top-heavy with mega-cap growth stocks that there is nowhere for investors to hide in case of a crash. If the S&P 500 has become a concentrated bet on artificial intelligence (AI) stocks, the entire S&P 500 might go down along with that industry in case of a tech stock bear market.

But there might be a safe haven for stock investors: international dividend stocks beyond the U.S. market.

The Vanguard International High Dividend Yield ETF (VYMI -1.92%) is an underrated exchange-traded fund that holds a well-diversified portfolio of global stocks likely to pay higher-than-average dividends. This international ETF has delivered a 17% total return in 2026 (as of Nov. 13) and has outperformed the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 over the past year and the past five years.

But is this positive run of outperformance likely to stop soon, or is VYMI still a good choice to invest in for the future? Based on past historical returns, let's look at how fast VYMI could double your money.

The Vanguard International High Dividend Yield ETF (VYMI) holds a well-diversified portfolio of 1,502 international stocks representing 45 countries. But all the stocks have one thing in common: They're forecasted to pay higher-than-average dividend yields. This fund's dividends are exceptional. As of Oct. 31, it was paying a dividend yield of 3.82%. That's better than most of the best dividend index funds.

Because of this focus on dividends and the international makeup of its holdings, VYMI doesn't include the mega-cap tech names, prominent semiconductor stocks, and other AI stocks that have dominated the S&P 500 and some other countries' stock markets in recent years. If you invest in VYMI today, how fast could this international ETF double your money? As always, it's important to remember that past returns do not guarantee future performance. But just as a baseline, let's assume that this fund can keep delivering the same average annual returns it has since the ETF's inception in February 2016. Over the past nearly 11 years, VYMI has delivered annualized returns of 11.27%.

Based on the Rule of 72, if you divide the number 72 by an investment's average annual rate of return, you get the number of years that it takes for the investment to double in size.
