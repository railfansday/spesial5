Futures traders often keep spare stablecoins in their accounts for margin and new entries, and those funds may not earn interest. Futures Earn may allow eligible balances to accrue interest without leaving the account, while those funds remain available for trading.

Quick answer: Futures Earn is an exchange feature that pays interest on eligible balances in a futures account while the funds stay available for trading. Actual earnings depend on the APR, eligible principal, minimum balance requirements, principal caps, and platform-specific conditions.

Futures Earn is an interest product built into the futures account. Once switched on, supported assets in that account, usually stablecoins such as USDT, earn interest automatically. Depending on the platform, qualifying funds may stay in the futures account rather than being locked in a separate savings product, allowing them to remain available for trading.

Keep the two income streams apart: interest comes from holding an eligible balance, while trading profit or loss comes from your positions. You can earn interest while your trades lose money, and it will not cover those losses.

Supported assets, rates, and eligibility rules vary by exchange.

The amount that earns interest is the interest-bearing principal, which is not always your full balance. Some platforms determine eligible principal using balance snapshots, so it can differ from your real-time balance.

Some programs require a minimum balance and set principal caps above which additional funds earn nothing. Base interest and bonus interest may also have different caps.

APR (annual percentage rate) is an annualized rate; the examples below use simple interest without assuming compounding. Unlike APR, annual percentage yield (APY) accounts for compounding.

The base APR may apply without an open position, while qualifying positions can unlock a bonus APR.

For example, MEXC's futures earn program combines interest on eligible futures balances with potential position-based bonuses. The applicable rate depends on the account's eligibility and product conditions.

A higher advertised APR does not necessarily mean higher earnings. Position requirements, principal caps, and the length of time funds remain eligible can all affect the actual payout.

Some exchanges take balance and position snapshots, check eligibility, then calculate interest.

The calculation frequency and distribution schedule depend on the platform. Some products calculate interest hourly and credit it daily, while others follow different settlement rules.

Withdrawals, smaller positions, or lower eligible balances can reduce future earnings.

A simple estimate uses this formula when the same principal qualifies for the full APR:

Estimated Daily Interest = Eligible Principal × APR ÷ 365

However, base and bonus interest may apply to different principal amounts. A more precise estimate is:

Estimated Daily Interest = [(Base Principal × Base APR) + (Bonus Principal × Bonus APR)] ÷ 365

Here, bonus APR means the additional rate, not the combined total.

Suppose 10,000 USDT qualifies for 5% base APR, but only 2,000 USDT qualifies for an additional 15% APR. Estimated daily interest would be:

[(10,000 × 5%) + (2,000 × 15%)] ÷ 365 ≈ 2.19 USDT

These are illustrative calculations, not quotes for a specific account. They assume unchanged principal and rates throughout the calculation period. Actual payouts depend on applicable caps, qualifying periods, balance snapshots, and settlement rules.

What Should Traders Compare Before Choosing?

●       Effective APR: The rate you realistically qualify for, not the highest advertised rate.

●       Eligibility and earning caps: VIP levels, supported assets, minimum balances, and principal limits.

●       Trading flexibility and settlement: Whether funds remain usable as margin and when interest is credited.

The highest advertised APR does not always produce the highest earnings, especially when strict position requirements or principal caps apply.

Futures Earn is often presented as crypto futures passive income, but weigh both sides first.

●       Extra interest on eligible balances that would otherwise sit idle

●       Better capital efficiency, since qualifying funds remain available for trading

●       Automatic calculations and payouts after activation

●       Potential access to higher APR tiers through qualifying positions

●       Variable APRs, principal caps, and eligibility restrictions

●       Volatility, leverage, and liquidation losses exceeding interest earnings

●       Trading fees and perpetual funding payments reducing overall returns

●       Platform custody, operational, and compliance risks

Futures Earn interest does not offset or prevent trading losses. Opening a position just to unlock a bonus tier can cost more than it pays. Reviewing funding rates, open interest, and liquidation data can provide additional context about leveraged market conditions.
