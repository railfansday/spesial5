Echoes of Azteca but not like Argentina: fearless England start afresh in Madrid

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

‘We will not go back on this way of playing,’ says Thomas Tuchel after he delivered response to World Cup critics

Thomas Tuchel made defensive substitutions as England protected a lead. He finished with three centre-backs on the pitch. His team could not get out of their half in the final part of the game. So close your eyes and imagine an alternate universe in which James Trafford did not make his stunning double save in stoppage time on Sunday and England had drawn 3-3 with Spain at the Metropolitano Stadium rather than won 3-2. The reaction would not have been kind because Tuchel has been here before.

The head coach wanted to push one particular comparison from the World Cup over the summer. “It was Mexico reloaded,” he said, with a nod towards the 3-2 last-16 win at the Azteca Stadium and it fitted like a glove – down to the detail of Jarell Quansah and red cards.

Against Mexico, the defender was sent off for a late tackle in the 54th minute with England 2-1 up. Against Spain, he contributed to Elliot Anderson being sent off in the 63rd minute with England 3-0 up. Quansah had his pocket picked by Lamine Yamal and Anderson’s attempt to chase back and rectify the situation ended with him fouling the winger.

It was not only a red card but a penalty; the prompt for Spain to launch a furious and hitherto unadvertised fightback. It had the stand that housed the press box shaking. Structural integrity was very much a theme.

As in Mexico, Tuchel made defensive changes. This time, it was Myles Lewis-Skelly for Harry Kane; Djed Spence for Anthony Gordon. Tuchel also withdrew Jude Bellingham, who went off shaking his head. It was easy to understand his frustration. He was excellent. Tuchel had removed his two prime attackers. He also put Dan Burn on towards the end, albeit this time not as part of a back five. England stayed in a 4-4-1 formation, with Quansah moving from central defence to right-back. As in Mexico, the 10-man low block resisted.

As much as Tuchel disagreed, this felt like Argentina reloaded as well. “No, not at all,” he said, when the World Cup semi-final defeat was raised. “Because of one man down. We would not have been in this situation if it was 11 against 11.”

The parallels, nevertheless, were there. Tuchel made defensive substitutions at 1-0 up against Argentina; it was a back five on that occasion. As against Spain, his team could not get out and they faced a flurry of chances. There were other factors in play at the World Cup, chief among them the high levels of fatigue within the squad. England could not hold out, conceding two late goals to exit and bring down a wave of criticism on Tuchel.

The score is the ultimate takeaway. It can skew analysis. Had Trafford not got back up after parrying a shot from the Spain substitute Fermín López to brilliantly repel Aymeric Laporte’s follow‑up, England would have gone from enjoying a fabulous result to stewing over an opportunity missed. The stakes were nowhere near as high as at the World Cup, but they would have gone from finishing top of their Nations League group to advancing into the quarter-finals as an unseeded second-place team.

In terms of Tuchel’s approach to the closing stages of a big game, what changed?

The truth for England lay in the opening 63 minutes. This was where the evidence of a decisive shift could be found, the reasons for the swell of optimism. England have never played better under Tuchel – and this was against the team to beat; the world and European champions. On their turf.

Spain were without the injured Rodri and Dani Olmo, and Luis de la Fuente strangely decided to use Pedri only as a substitute, but their lineup still shimmered with menace; Lamine Yamal was frightening. Yet before their penalty, converted by Mikel Oyarzabal, they did not have a shot on target. Or a real chance.

What stood out from the first whistle was England’s front‑foot intent; the bravery on the ball. They believed they would win and there was a fearlessness about them. They made forward moves on the half turn. There was a slickness about their combinations.

Spain have never suffered like this under De la Fuente and it was everything Tuchel had dreamed of when he started to plot a new version of his team after the World Cup; a more fluid game model, a response to the Argentina defeat.

Tuchel loved England’s buildup play, which included some of Trafford’s passing. He loved the high press; the aggression in the duels; the threat on set pieces. He said he loved the low-block defending too. Ben White was excellent at right-back and there was a coming-of-age performance from Lewis Hall at left-back, but it felt wrong to single out individuals.

“We didn’t stop and I think we are ready to go [like this] for 90 minutes,” Tuchel said. “It was a complete performance and we will not look back now. Go forward and stay hungry. We will not go back on this way of playing that we find ourselves since the World Cup.”

Ezri Konsa, who headed in the second goal from a Declan Rice corner, said England “would have scored two more” if they had kept 11 men on the pitch. “You saw until the red card how much quality we had,” he said.

“We dominated the game. The instruction was to keep going at them. Don’t stop. To keep our foot on the pedal. Argentina was different; different circumstances.”

Tuchel was asked about the journey from the heartbreak of Argentina to this point; the end of a stirring Nations League group campaign, including the 7-0 win against Croatia in Rijeka. It has not all been super exciting; the 2-1 Wembley win against Croatia last Thursday, for example, was a grind. Was he always confident of a successful reboot?

“It took me a while to digest [the World Cup] and see the way forward,” he said. “How we can help and adjust and improve? We had reviews and so on but the clarity came in the first Nations League camp [in September and October] with the players.

“The first week was not so easy. Some players were still upset from the World Cup. Some were new in the team. Some had digested it, some had not. But it was important to have this process together and everyone committed to wanting to improve in certain aspects of the game.

“We had the energy right to take the next steps and from there we went. Full credit to the players, how they buy in. It’s amazing that it came together like this now.”
