Pochettino: I’d tell Guardiola to ‘take it’ if he’s offered US coaching job

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

As he prepares to depart for Manchester United, the outgoing US coach held court on a number of topics, including what he’d change about soccer in America

Outgoing US head coach Mauricio Pochettino and former Manchester City head coach Pep Guardiola have a long, storied past. The two faced each other as players, when Guardiola was at Barcelona and Pochettino was making his name across town at Espanyol. They faced each other 22 times as managers in the Premier League and the two share a mutual respect – and intensity – when it comes to their approach to playing and coaching alike.

So when Guardiola visited Yankee Stadium in September to serve as New York City FC’s pre-match hype man before their rivalry match with Red Bull New York, Pochettino – in town anyways to scout the match – made it a point to sit with him during the game. He brought along his assistant coach and confidant Jesús Pérez, and US Soccer CEO and secretary general JT Batson.

“You know what I noticed?” Pochettino asked, smiling as he recounted the story during a meeting with reporters on Monday. “[Guardiola had a] good relationship with JT.”

Back then, Pochettino didn’t seem to be going anywhere. Only a month earlier, he’d signed a four-year contract to guide the US through the 2030 World Cup. Guardiola was, and still is, in the middle of a year-long sabbatical from coaching. He remains the federation’s white whale, often mentioned in the same breath as the US job over the past decade-plus but just as quickly dismissed as a pipe dream.

Just three months after taking in that game at Yankee Stadium, Pochettino is on his way to Manchester United and Guardiola has once again found himself at the center of the rumor mill, with reports suggesting that he and US Soccer are set for exploratory talks.

On Monday, Pochettino was asked what he’d tell the Barcelona and Manchester City legend if he was asked for his opinion on the US job.

“Take it,” Pochettino said, flatly. “Why? Because it’s a perfect moment to arrive at the federation. Everything is on the right path, in the right direction. Of course, take it. Of course, you are going to find really good players, good people – the players can fit his style.”

Guardiola was one of many topics the Argentinian manager covered during the roundtable, which lasted about an hour. At times defiant, at times emotional and cycling through Spanish and English, Pochettino spoke of his potential legacy at US Soccer, what lies ahead for him at United and how he views soccer in America as a whole.

Quotes have been lightly edited and condensed for clarity.

However you assess his results, one of the inarguable successes of the Pochettino era are the relatively unheralded players whose status has risen over his tenure. The Argentine cited defender Alex Freeman and midfielder Sebastian Berhalter as two such examples, both of whom were playing in MLS when making their national team debuts under Pochettino and both now playing in Europe. Asked how he assesses players to add to the national team, and how he compares players across different leagues and playing styles, Pochettino insisted that the level of competition matters less than individual form.

“You need to project the individual player,” Pochettino said. “Because he’s playing well, the level is no issue. That player can be good, like Freeman, like Sebastian. That is the example, and I think that is so important. There is talent everywhere … This one is one of the things that we learn – all [of us], we were young and then we start to be a coach, no? That we cannot make a mistake [and] measure the level of the player because of the level of the league.”

With the US being his first international job, there were some doubts going in about how Pochettino would adapt to coaching without daily contact with his players and staff.

“I am ready [to return]. This is what I miss a little bit. I miss to be, every day, with something here,” Pochettino said. “Guardiola asked me when I was at the game, he said, ‘Oh, you don’t want to come back to the Premier League?’ I said, ‘No, I’m happy here.’ [He said] ‘Yeah, but you don’t miss, every day, to…’ I say, ‘A little bit, yes, because you think now today, and then we have December camp against Jamaica for the MLS [players in the pool]. But after, you need to wait [until] March. What do you do? You need to watch games, you need to travel – administrative things. That is nice, it’s good. But every day, to be involved: press conference, training, everything … this type of possibility, like with United, you say yes. I want that. I want to prove myself again.”

But what did Pochettino think of his maiden international job?

“I like and not like,” he said. “I like because it provides you the possibility to manage your time, to be busy, but at the same time, be free and to have some time with your family. Now, I have a grandchild, and [number] two is coming. You can share the life with your people. But now, ciao, because now is [gestures] tra-tra-tra-tra-tra. It’s impossible to spend time with the people you want.”

In the grand scheme of international football, the US national team coaching job has never been close to the top of the list of most desirable destinations. Pochettino’s hire was seen as a possible sign that could be changing. Now, after nearly two years in the position, Pochettino believes it has.

“The vision from Europe, I promise it is completely different after the World Cup,” Pochettino said. “Now, being head coach of the USA is more prestigious. If Manchester United, or Real Madrid, or Barcelona is coming for the US coach, if you start to think about that, if you are another coach – maybe [Antonio] Conte, someone else, you say ‘oh, this is a great opportunity.’ [In my case], ‘Oh, Pochettino, from Chelsea he goes to the US? And now to Manchester United.’

Pochettino has coached some of the most famous players and clubs in the world, giving him a unique perspective on how soccer has developed in the US. Pochettino stopped short of prescribing neat solutions to improve the sport, though.

“I think that business [and] the sport, is [done] in a holistic way. Everything is important, and you cannot pick one. You cannot change one [thing], and that can change everything,” he said. “I think it’s a little bit of everything that you need to change. If you adjust this, you need to adjust this. The balance of the thing changes if you touch here, that will force you to touch all the areas in soccer here, or in the world To find the good balance, no? A process of little by little, is all that we made in these last two years. I think it’s the beginning of [a process]. If that has continuity, it’s the beginning of the new soccer in the USA that can be.

“I think that is, for me, the beginning of the change for the future: that idea that the American player can be very good, can be very competitive. The talent is here; it’s so powerful in this country. I think that is why I feel proud. Maybe that was our job, because I don’t believe in coincidence. That has happened because something needed to happen. Now the tough job is done. Maybe now [the United job] is my reward because of this tough job.”
