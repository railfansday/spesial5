Jose Mourinho reveals unusual half-time team talk that sparked Real Madrid’s Champions League comeback

Following Real Madrid's turnaround victory against PSV Eindhoven in the Champions League, Jose Mourinho revealed his unusual half-time team talk that sparked his team’s second-half onslaught.

Asked how he inspired his side after trailing 1-0 at the Santiago Bernabeu Stadium on Tuesday, Mourinho initially wanted to keep his methods under wraps as he joked: "The locker room is like (Las) Vegas, what happens in Vegas stays in Vegas,"

Jude Bellingham, introduced as a substitute to spark the Madrid attack, later admitted the manager was understandably "a bit angry" following a disappointing opening period.

Rather than relying solely on a dressing-room row, Mourinho selected images to highlight his side’s positional mistakes. The tactical correction paid immediate dividends, with Madrid scoring five unanswered goals after the break to secure a 5-1 win, maintaining a perfect record with five consecutive European victories this term.

"I saw a terrible first half and tried with the analysts to find three or four images that could show the players exactly what we were doing wrong," Mourinho explained. "It was a half time with few words because a picture is worth a thousand words. We looked at three different images, one from a defensive perspective and two from an offensive perspective. I think with visualisation, it’s much easier for the players to understand."

The manager noted that the visual breakdown prompted an immediate "mental click" across the squad. Goalkeeper Thibaut Courtois agreed, adding: "On the screen you could see clearly that we weren’t doing things right and they were,"

Mourinho reshuffled his line-up drastically at the interval, making three changes including a double swap in central defence and introducing Bellingham, who had been rested from the start. The England midfielder’s arrival "proved key" to the outcome as he scored once and assisted another, with his manager noting he "gave the team a completely different dynamic."

Federico Valverde levelled the score five minutes after the restart, before Bellingham produced a striking effort 10 minutes later to put Madrid ahead. Kylian Mbappé then struck twice before Vinícius Júnior wrapped up the scoring late on, assisted by Bellingham.

Before the interval, PSV had exploited Madrid's defensive gaps through swift counter-attacks, alarming the home fans - including newly appointed board member Rafael Nadal and his son - as the Dutch visitors created repeated openings.

To fix the defensive frailties, Mourinho withdrew Éder Militão and Antonio Rüdiger for Dean Huijsen and Ibrahima Konaté, while Bellingham replaced Aurélien Tchouaméni. He also adjusted the tactical roles of Arda Güler and Bernardo Silva.

"The players at the back also felt much more comfortable pressing high," Mourinho said. "PSV gave us a hard time in the first half, and we struggled to win the ball back. In the second half, we had absolute control, and when the players up front wake up and start playing with joy, what happened can happen."

Mbappé’s double made him the second-youngest player to reach 75 Champions League goals after Lionel Messi, reaching the mark in 103 games - the third-fastest behind Messi and Robert Lewandowski, according to Uefa statistics.

Meanwhile, Vinícius scored his 87th-minute strike to record just his second goal of the campaign for Madrid, and his first since August.
