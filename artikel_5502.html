Token sticker shock pushes enterprises from frontier labs toward open-weight AI factories

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Enterprises have spent the AI boom racing to secure graphics processing units, or GPUs, but the harder decisions now sit above the hardware. Token bills, data sovereignty and model choice increasingly decide which AI pilots reach production, a shift on display at the SC26 supercomputing conference.

Dell Technologies Inc. has spent the past year pushing its AI Factory beyond servers and storage. Customers now want a full-stack outcome that covers software, open-source tools, models and operations, according to Brad Maltz (pictured), senior director of AI solutions at Dell.

“The hardware’s key because that drives the tokens,” he said. “You really, truly need a full-stack AI factory view above and beyond just the hardware stack.”

Maltz spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Gemma Allen at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed how token bills and data sovereignty are steering enterprises toward open-weight models and a full-stack AI Factory. The interview came as Dell rolled out AI Factory updates at the conference. (* Disclosure below.)

Many enterprises are still stuck in pilot mode, and coding assistants are often the easiest place to start. Employees have already tried tools from Anthropic PBC and OpenAI Group PBC, so teams want to apply that experience to business use cases. Once those pilots grow, the bill arrives, Maltz explained.

“The token spend right now in the frontier labs and the hyperscalers — that’s a little more than we expected,” he said. “We didn’t expect to spend $10,000, $20,000 per person per month.”

Cost is only one trigger. Data sovereignty, intellectual property leakage, security and governance all have to be addressed before a pilot goes live. Those requirements are steering many enterprises toward open-weight models they can fine-tune and run on-premises, including U.S. options such as Nvidia Corp.’s Nemotron, according to Maltz.

“There will always be frontier labs. There will always be open-weight models,” he said. “The question is going to be: do we have enough diversity with the open weights to meet the enterprise use cases?”

To keep pace, Dell launched its AI Ecosystem Program to validate software partners on its AI Factory. About 60 more partners have applied or are under evaluation, and each addition lets Dell solve 10 to 30 more customer problems, Maltz noted. For sovereign deployments, that often means disconnected, on-premises systems that keep models next to the GPUs and data enterprises already control.

“People understand data is the lifeblood of AI,” he said. “It’s the thing that trains the models. It’s the thing the models operate against. It comes from your enterprise business applications.”
