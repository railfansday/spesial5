Eli Lilly's obesity pill showed it can ease sleep apnea in a late-stage trial

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Lilly plans to submit Foundayo to the FDA for moderate-to-severe sleep apnea in adults with obesity or overweight by the end of 2026

Eli Lilly $LLY announced positive results on Monday from two late-stage trials of its obesity pill Foundayo in adults with obstructive sleep apnea, and the company plans to submit the drug to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration by the end of 2026.

Foundayo, also known as orforglipron, met the primary endpoint of the 52-week ATTAIN-OSA program by reducing the apnea-hypopnea index, the standard measure of sleep apnea severity, the company said. The results were presented at the American Heart Association Scientific Sessions 2026 and published in JAMA.

Participants had moderate-to-severe sleep apnea and obesity or overweight. In the study of 363 participants who did not use positive airway pressure (PAP) therapy, Foundayo reduced breathing interruptions by 21.1 events per hour, or 43.8%, from an average baseline of 50.7. Placebo reduced them by 4.3 events per hour. Of those on Foundayo, 31.5% reached remission or mild disease.

In the study of 349 participants who used PAP therapy, Foundayo reduced events by 18.3 per hour, or 27.5%, from an average baseline of 57.6. Of those participants, 19.6% reached remission or mild disease.

Foundayo cut sleep apnea-specific hypoxic burden, which gauges the depth and duration of oxygen drops during sleep, by 55.9% in participants not using PAP therapy and by 45.8% in those using it. Participants lost an average of 11.5% and 11.2% of their body weight, respectively, and systolic blood pressure fell by an average of 8.6 mmHg and 7.3 mmHg.

Lilly said all figures in its release represent the efficacy estimand, which reflects results had all randomized participants stayed on study treatment for 52 weeks. Figures reported by STAT for the group without PAP therapy were lower: a reduction of 17.8 events per hour on Foundayo versus 4.5 on placebo, with remission or mild disease in 28% of treated patients versus 13% on placebo.

The safety profile was consistent with earlier trials in the ACHIEVE and ATTAIN programs, Lilly said. The most common adverse events involved the digestive system, such as nausea, diarrhea, constipation, dyspepsia and vomiting, and ranged from mild to moderate. Discontinuation due to adverse events was 12.2% on Foundayo versus 3.8% on placebo without PAP therapy, and 6.8% versus 0.6% with PAP therapy.

"Foundayo is already making a difference for people living with obesity, and with plans to submit these results to the FDA by the end of the year, we could have the opportunity to help people living with OSA," said Thomas Seck, Lilly Cardiometabolic Health's senior vice president of product development, in a statement, according to CNBC.

Foundayo is approved in the U.S. for adults with obesity. Lilly had received a recommendation from a European Medicines Agency committee in October to approve the pill for obesity and type 2 diabetes. Lilly's injectable Zepbound is approved in the U.S. for moderate-to-severe sleep apnea in adults with obesity.

Sleep apnea affects an estimated 936 million adults worldwide, and most have never been diagnosed, according to Lilly.
