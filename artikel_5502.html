Nationwide Equities’ Philip Parziale on getting ReverseReach in the hands of more LOs

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Nationwide Equities President Philip Parziale discusses his move from general counsel to president and the launch of ReverseReach. He says the platform is meant to help forward loan officers identify reverse mortgage fits and support wholesale expansion.  AI Summary

Philip Parziale, president of Nationwide Equities Corp., is looking to expand the reverse mortgage lender’s wholesale business and bring more forward mortgage loan officers into the reverse market through the company’s newly launched ReverseReach platform.

In an interview with HousingWire‘s Reverse Mortgage Daily, Parziale discussed his transition from general counsel to president while retaining his legal responsibilities, the early industry response to ReverseReach and plans to expand the platform.

Editor’s Note: This interview has been lightly edited for length and clarity.

Sarah Wolak: You recently became president of Nationwide Equities following Glenn Wallace’s retirement. Can you tell us how that decision transpired, what the transition means for Nationwide Equities going forward and how you’re balancing two roles?

Philip Parziale: I’ve known Glenn and [CEO] Paul Lamparillo for probably 30 years. They were the owners of Nationwide Equities, and I started at the company probably 15 years ago as an attorney. From there, I worked my way up to general counsel, and they basically saw in me that there was more than just an attorney there. I paid attention to sales and every part of the business, and I actually got promoted to chief operating officer.

From COO, I think they really started grooming me for a leadership role back then. If anything ever happened to either one of them, if anybody wanted to retire, I would be there waiting in the wings. So when Glenn was ready to retire, it was just a natural progression. By then, working under them for 15 years, I got to know almost everything about the company. I was exposed to every part of the company, and it really made for more of a seamless transition.

I know there’s been some articles I’ve seen online about holding the president and general counsel positions and how sometimes they could be at odds. Actually, I think it helps. In the old days, it used to be if management came up with an idea, they’d have to go to legal, and legal would say, “Well, what about this? And what about compliance?”

Now, if we have an idea in a meeting, a lot of times, I’ll know the answer right off the bat. And then if we need to, we can go to the other attorneys in the legal department and say, “Can you give an opinion on this and see if you agree with where we stand?”

Wolak: Going back to your law school days, did you anticipate you would ever have anything to do with the reverse mortgage space? How did you end up learning the business?

Parziale: I had no intention of having anything to do with real estate law at all. I thought I was going to be a litigator. All the classes I took at Fordham School of Law were corporate litigation, criminal litigation, and then when I got out of law school, the job market was kind of tight, and I got offered a job at a real estate firm.

They told me basically, “Listen, we’re mostly real estate. We get some litigation, and you could have all the litigation you want, but we’re mostly real estate.” I said, “Fine,” and it turned out there was no litigation. But the owner of the firm, his uncle owned a mortgage company. It was Parkway Mortgage. They were a big subprime business back in the late ’90s, and so I started doing real estate closings, learning about the mortgage business there.

To make my closings go easier, I would actually learn some of the underwriting, how to process loans and stuff like that. So when a borrower had a question, I knew what the answer was.

From there, I just got invested in the mortgage companies. All my clients, when I put out my own shingle, were mortgage companies, and I basically did compliance work for probably 10 different companies. The first company that actually ever hired me to do title review work was Glenn and Paul’s company.

Wolak: Did you ever think about stepping outside of real estate? What has kept you passionate all these years?

Parziale: I actually like the space. It’s different every day. There’s new challenges every day. It’s not like in the legal field, where you’re really doing the same thing a lot of the times, especially contract reviews and stuff like that. You see different clients every day. You have different problems every day, and it makes the day go a lot faster.

It’s one of those businesses where, especially as an attorney, you’re balancing a lot of different hats. I feel like we have a responsibility to the borrower. We have to make sure this loan is truly a benefit to them. Then you have the responsibility to the investor. Safety and soundness are absolutely paramount when doing a loan. We wouldn’t do any loan that we wouldn’t hold ourselves.

But you also want to help out people that need help. Is there a way to help out that person who may be staring at a foreclosure because a spouse died or somebody got COVID and was out of work for three years? It’s just a series of unfortunate incidents. They’re not bad borrowers, but they just had some bad luck.

That’s literally what led to the idea of ReverseReach. There are so many borrowers seeking conventional and FHA loans that didn’t qualify because they had lost income or they had bad credit, but they were actually good borrowers who just came across hard times. With the reverse mortgage, you could actually help them out, make sure their taxes and insurance get paid, and bail them out of a bad situation.

Wolak: Can you explain the ReverseReach product and who it’s geared for?

Parziale: It’s really geared to mortgage loan officers and companies that do primarily forward loans. What we came across was a lot of these forward LOs would just be having a conversation, and they would have a borrower they couldn’t help. They just didn’t qualify. They didn’t have the income because they couldn’t meet the DTI requirements. And we’re like, “Well, how old are they?”

They’d have a 50% LTV and they’d be 75 years old, and we’d say, “Listen, what about a reverse mortgage?” They hadn’t even considered it because it wasn’t on their plate. It wasn’t something their company offered and they didn’t understand it.

So we’ve learned just by talking to them, these guys had tons of leads, and it was a simple sell for them because they’re just reengaging their old borrowers and all their contacts. They were able to go back to borrowers who may not have qualified because they had to make a mortgage payment. But now, without that mortgage payment, they qualify.

It’s funny in this industry, though. I’ve talked to financial planners who are really respected, but because their company didn’t offer reverse mortgages, it wasn’t even on their plate of financial planning.

Wolak: I’ve heard that experience from others in the space, that it’s a little-known tool or was even taboo at one point.

Parziale: Yes, it absolutely was. There was a negative stigma with the loan, especially back in the day when I first started. It was considered a loan of last resort. It was what people did when they were desperate.

In the old days, the underwriting was really lax. It was, what’s the LTV and how old is somebody? We didn’t have financial assessment. We didn’t have any of that stuff. But now it’s very different, especially with the proprietary product.

When we first got into the proprietary product, our average loan size was hovering around $1 million, and borrowers were using the money for home improvements or buying second homes. It wasn’t, “I’m in trouble. I’m desperate.” It was, “Yeah, I want to. I’ve saved my entire life and I want to enjoy my money.”

Wolak: ReverseReach is new, but what kinds of results and feedback have you gotten so far from users?

Parziale: I was actually very pleasantly surprised. We got a contact maybe eight hours after our first emails went out, and we signed up a broker immediately who had a loan that was ready to go. Whenever you roll out a new product, you expect it to take a while to get some traction, and we were trying to roll it out in waves. But we’ve gotten a good enough reaction just from the first week that we’re already looking at scaling.

We’ve had the conversation of hiring new account executives already because the response has been that good. I was really pleasantly surprised. I always think a new product takes time to get some traction.

I think the thing that helps right now is, rates are so high that forward loan officers are having a hard time. I heard some companies were doing furloughs, so these guys are really looking for new leads, and I don’t think there’s as many leads out there as there once was. So us going out there and saying, “Listen, you may already have your next loan in your pipeline already. Go through your LOS and see what you have,” it’s an easy sell. It makes people really say, “Oh, I could do this,” and we’re happy to look at the scenarios.

We’re actually rolling out a ReverseReach University that’s going to have commonly asked questions. We have an FAQ, but we really want to do it with a whole list of classes that these loan officers can access anytime they want. They can go online and say, “I don’t understand what a LESA is,” and they can see a quick two-minute video explaining what it is.

I think we really have to educate. And I want to take everybody from a forward loan officer who may never have heard of a reverse mortgage to somebody who’s experienced and done them before but doesn’t do them regularly. I want to really appeal to all those people. We’re hoping to roll out an AI chatbot and that university in a couple of weeks.

Wolak: Can you talk about some of the other things you plan to do as company president?

Parziale: Honestly, the one thing I’m looking forward to is scaling. With ReverseReach, we try — and I believe that even a small improvement helps. If I can get 10 small improvements, it’s just as good as coming up with one major improvement.

I look at ReverseReach as a large step forward. But if I can find even one little thing that helps the industry or the borrower in a day, I want to try and do it.

In terms of my next goal as president, it’s definitely scaling. With some of the furloughs going on and layoffs, I think there’s a lot of good people out there who are going to be available, and I’d like to hire some of them. I’d like to increase our wholesale presence and grow that way.
