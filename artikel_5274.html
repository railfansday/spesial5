The global space economy is entering a new era and Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (NASDAQ: SPCX) is aiming to be at its center. Despite retreating nearly 50% from its 52-week high of $225.64, the company’s long-term growth narrative remains intact, driven by reusable launch technology, expanding satellite infrastructure, and next-generation space innovation.

As governments and private capital accelerate investments beyond Earth, investors are asking one question: Could this correction be the opportunity before SPCXs next major rally? Here’s our SPCX stock price prediction for 2026-2040.

SPCX stock is building one of the world’s most integrated space infrastructure businesses, combining commercial launch services, satellite connectivity, national security contracts, and next-generation space technologies. As demand for global connectivity and orbital infrastructure accelerates, the company is positioning itself at the forefront of the expanding space economy.

SPCX is building far more than a launch company—it is establishing a vertically integrated space infrastructure ecosystem with multiple long-term revenue engines. From commercial launch services and satellite communications to government missions and next-generation space technologies, the company is positioned to benefit from the accelerating commercialization of space. This diversified business model reduces reliance on any single segment while creating multiple pathways for sustained earnings growth.

The long-term investment thesis now rests on execution. Continued growth in launch cadence, expansion of satellite connectivity services, deeper government and defense partnerships, and disciplined capital deployment could significantly strengthen SpaceX’s competitive position over the coming decade. As global investment in the space economy continues to accelerate, the company’s technology leadership and expanding infrastructure platform have the potential to support a higher long-term valuation than traditional aerospace peers.

SpaceX (SPCX) stock has gained a fresh potential catalyst as merger discussions with Tesla reportedly move closer to a possible agreement. According to a November 12 report, the two companies could announce a deal as early as December, although negotiations may extend into early 2027. The potential merger could bring SpaceX’s Starlink, rocket and AI infrastructure businesses together with Tesla’s electric vehicles, robotics and energy operations under one corporate structure. A combined valuation of approximately $3.5 trillion would make it one of the largest corporate transactions ever considered.
