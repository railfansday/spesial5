BTCPay Server Tells Merchants to Shut Down After Attackers Sweep Hot Wallets: Are Payments Still Safe?

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Bitcoin news: BTCPay Server told merchants to go offline after attackers swept hot wallets on 2.4.1 or older. Here is who is exposed.

In today’s Bitcoin news, BTCPay Server, the free open-source payment processor many Bitcoin merchants run themselves, told every operator to shut down their instance after reports that attackers were sweeping the hot wallets on merchant servers. Less than six hours later, the culprit was old software.

At 04:07 UTC on Monday, the project said its investigation had found an attack vector aimed at servers running version 2.4.1, released on July 23, or older. It has “no credible reports” of drains on the current 2.4.7. BTCPay has not said how much was stolen or how many stores were hit.

That makes this a different kind of Bitcoin news from last week’s Binance hack. No exchange was breached. Merchants who run their own payment stack, the people self-custody is meant to protect, were the target.

So who was exposed, and what should store owners do now?

An attacker logged in as admin on older servers and emptied the hot wallets. BTCPay Server raised the alarm at 22:25 UTC on Sunday and asked operators to run its shutdown script “until we complete the investigation.”

Two hours later, founder Nicolas Dorier said the project’s own public servers on 2.4.7 were not attacked. “The attacker managed to log in as admin on those instance, we are still trying to figure out how,” he wrote.

A hot wallet keeps its signing keys on the server itself, so it can pay out refunds and payouts automatically. Anyone with admin rights on that server can send the coins too.

Hosting providers moved fast. BTCPay thanked LunaNode and others for shutting down older hosted instances on their own, so some merchants may find their store offline on Monday.

The version history is the story. 2.4.1 shipped on July 23. Two weeks later, 2.4.2 fixed a critical flaw the team said was “being actively exploited,” and five more releases followed, ending with Thursday’s 2.4.7, which fixed 85 findings from an AI security scan Anthropic ran for the project.

Whether this is the same hole as August’s is still open. Dorier said a blog post with more detail is coming.

Only if it still runs 2.4.1 or older, on what the project knows so far. “For those who already updated at least once since the release of 2.4.2, there is no reason to panic,” Dorier wrote at 04:10 UTC.

The reported drains all hit hot wallets. Stores that receive into a watch-only wallet keep their signing keys off the server altogether.
