Ledger Confirms Tampered Bitcoin Wallets in Berlin as Spy Implant Probe Spreads Beyond Asia

The tampered Ledger wallets aren’t just a Southeast Asia problem anymore.

Ledger said Wednesday it has confirmed tampered hardware wallets in Berlin and is investigating reports of two more in Dubai, expanding a probe it had previously described as a localized incident in Southeast Asia.

“Our findings since then show tampered devices outside Southeast Asia. It is the same incident,” the company said in an update on its support site.

The investigation began last month after customers who bought devices from reseller CryptoBilis reported losses. Onchain investigators traced a suspected drain of about $87 million from hundreds of victim wallets across Ethereum, Tron, and Bitcoin.

Ledger said at the time that all confirmed cases were tied to CryptoBilis, and it later asked resellers in seven Asian markets to pull devices from sale.

Every device Ledger has confirmed shows the same kind of physical modification, designed to capture a user’s recovery phrase during setup. A recovery phrase, typically a string of 24 words, is the master key to a crypto wallet: anyone who has it can drain the funds.

Ledger maintains there’s no flaw in its secure chip or operating system and no confirmed case involving a device bought on ledger.com.

In Germany, Ledger said a few tampered units were localized to Berlin, and it urged anyone who bought a Ledger device there since January 2026 to contact support.

In Dubai, it’s working with distribution partners to learn how two suspected units entered circulation. Those devices were bought at a Virgin Megastore location, according to multiple reports. Ledger also found one affected Nano Gen5 among the Southeast Asian resellers it previously paused.

The company expects more tampered devices to surface, though it says the number remains small overall. Its Donjon security team is examining suspected devices, and Ledger has asked resellers to pause sales wherever inspections are ongoing.

Customers worried about a device should stop using it, Ledger said. It advises moving funds to a new wallet created on a device bought directly from ledger.com, without reusing the old recovery phrase or passphrase, since a tampered device can capture both. It also recommends filing a report with local authorities.

For a limited time, Ledger is offering physical inspections, including X-rays, at its factory in Vierzon, France.

“We will never ask for your recovery phrase,” the company said on X.

The last few months have been a rough stretch for hardware wallet markers overall. Rival manufacturer Trezor said this week that hackers who breached a third-party shipping partner likely took information tied to about 27,000 orders. That’s the company’s third such breach in as many months.
