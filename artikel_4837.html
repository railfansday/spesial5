When Couples Grow Together

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

One of the most fulfilling experiences in any committed relationship is a couple growing together. However, growing together can feel vague and ambiguous, and at times even reduced to an achievement. Hopefully, this blog will offer concrete suggestions for growing together in ways that feel fulfilling and enjoyable. The idea that people come together and change along the way can feel intimidating. When couples grow together, change loses some of its uneasiness as they talk about it and encourage each other.

Couples can certainly grow together as they learn more about each other through shared activities. Playing tennis, painting the deck, ballroom dancing, scuba diving, and learning to play cribbage can add joy and connection. Shared hobbies do bring couples together. However, the depth of growing together mostly happens when couples focus their learning on one of three areas: Healing, Love, and Service.

Let’s look at each of these topics where deep learning can take place.

When each person’s healing becomes a learning priority, growing together can become the hallmark of the relationship, and the relationship can become an active container for personal growth.

When you want to learn more about healing, it’s important to remain compassionate as you self-examine. Another key is to remain curious without obsessing about every psychological nook and cranny.

Louise and John, a couple talking about growing together as they learn about healing. “John and I have overcome the challenges we faced as children and how those patterns can impact our marriage if we let them,” explained Louise.

“What a relief it has been to join Louise in her healing and getting over the idea that learning about healing is only for women. Also, I’m glad to have discovered a legacy of spousal betrayal, which I don’t want to reproduce and pass on to our children,” added John.

A deeply fulfilling place for couples to grow together is taking on an apprenticeship of love. That mostly means learning how to be more relational and emotionally intimate. It also means accepting that no one arrives able to give and receive love perfectly. We’re either devotionally participating in a love apprenticeship or not. Here are some concrete ways to participate.

Abigail and Cynthia talk about their apprenticeship to love as a couple.

“I’m noticing Abigail’s willingness to take more responsibility to love herself. What a gift that is for me, as I’m not primarily responsible for how she feels about herself. She tells me when she’s not feeling good about herself, and I ask what I can do. She says, 'Just listen.'"

“Blame seems to be on its way out of our relationship. Cynthia is committed to being more accountable. I decided to do it too. It is quite a joy to witness blame receding, with our individual maturation and the maturity of our relationship taking its place,” explained Abigail.

Growing together can involve quite a bit of psychology. Rather than getting hung up on stereotypical understandings of the word psychology, let the old original definition of the word guide you. In ancient Greek, psychology means “care of the soul.” Why not consider caring for the soul of your relationship?
