AMD stock climbed 5.27% to $582.05 on November 12 as investors shifted attention toward Nvidia's semiconductor rivals.

Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia has intensified interest in alternative AI chipmakers, including AMD and Broadcom.

AMD reported 97% annual growth in data-center revenue and forecast fourth-quarter revenue of approximately $17 billion.

Nvidia’s leadership crisis is giving AMD a fresh opportunity to attract Wall Street’s attention. AMD shares jumped 5.27% to $582.05 on November 12, extending their recovery as investors reassessed the competitive landscape following Jensen Huang’s departure from Nvidia. The shift comes at a critical time for AMD, which is already benefiting from accelerating demand for AI processors and server CPUs.

Can AMD stock turn Nvidia’s leadership setback into lasting market-share gains and push its stock above $650?

Nvidia’s sudden leadership change has created a new opening for its competitors, with AMD emerging as one of the immediate beneficiaries.

Huang’s departure has raised questions about Nvidia’s ability to maintain its technology roadmap and customer relationships during the transition. CFO Colette Kress has taken over as interim CEO, but investors are still assessing the longer-term implications.

AMD and Broadcom shares have attracted buying interest as investors look beyond Nvidia for exposure to AI infrastructure growth. William Blair analyst Sebastien Naji also upgraded AMD to Outperform, citing Nvidia’s leadership uncertainty and Intel’s supply constraints as potential opportunities for AMD.

AMD’s investment case extends beyond Nvidia’s current difficulties. Its latest results show that demand for its data-center products is already accelerating. The company reported third-quarter revenue of $13.38 billion, up 45% year over year. Non-GAAP earnings per share increased 74% to $2.09, while data-center revenue grew 97%, driven by demand for Instinct GPUs and EPYC server processors.

Management expects fourth-quarter revenue of approximately $17 billion, plus or minus $300 million. CEO Lisa Su also expects overall revenue growth to exceed 80% in 2027. These figures strengthen AMD’s position in the AI infrastructure market. The next challenge is execution: converting rising demand into larger market share and sustained profitability.

AMD stock trades at $582.05 on November 12, gaining 5.27% during the session. The price chart shows a strong rebound after the stock briefly dropped toward $420–$450 earlier in November. Buyers have pushed shares back above the short- and medium-term moving averages near $510–$534, improving the near-term technical structure.

The immediate resistance zone sits between $650 and $680, where previous rallies encountered selling pressure. A sustained breakout above $680 could open the way toward $700. On the downside, $550 is the first support area, followed by $534 and $510. A decline below $510 would weaken the recovery and bring the longer-term support near $442 into focus.

Nvidia’s leadership transition has created an opportunity for AMD, but the semiconductor market will not change overnight. Customers must qualify alternative processors, adapt their software infrastructure and establish new supply arrangements before shifting large workloads.

AMD already has a growing data-center business, an expanding Instinct GPU portfolio and strong demand for EPYC processors. These fundamentals give the company a credible opportunity to compete for a larger share of AI infrastructure spending.

The real test for AMD is whether it can turn the current investor interest into lasting customer demand. A move above $650 would strengthen the bullish chart, but sustained revenue and market-share growth will ultimately determine the stock’s next leg.
