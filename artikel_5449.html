You Don’t Need a Full-Time Executive to Get C-Suite Expertise. Here’s Why.

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

For entrepreneurs, there are lots of good reasons to hire on a fractional basis — but it might not be right for everyone. Here’s my take.

Opinions expressed by Entrepreneur contributors are their own.

Hiring full-time workers is a commitment, especially at the C-suite level. As a solopreneur myself, it took me a long time before I had the resources to bring anyone on to help shoulder the responsibilities of running my business.

But a lot has changed since I launched my company back in 2006. Now, fractional executives—leaders who share their expertise with organizations on a part-time basis—are quickly becoming the norm. In fact, research has found that the number of workers describing themselves as “fractional” has tripled since 2018; among executives, the most common limited roles are CFOs, followed by CMOs.

For entrepreneurs, there are lots of good reasons to hire on a fractional basis. The right fractional executive can bring valuable expertise, fresh perspective and strategic guidance without requiring the long-term investment of a full-time hire. But it might not be right for everyone. Here’s my take.

Several factors have conspired to create the recent boom in fractional work. Post-Covid-19, more people are seeking out flexible roles and the ability to work multiple part-time jobs. At the same time, a tough job market has produced a surplus of highly skilled workers, while many companies don’t have the funds to fill those roles full-time.

Leadership roles are also simply growing more complex. Between the proliferation of hybrid work and AI adoption, it stands to reason that expertise is also becoming more varied. In general, high level roles are changing, with AI automating several functions previously handled by executives, like operations and reporting. On the other hand, demand for a la carte, specialized skills are on the rise — maybe your organization needs someone fluent in transitioning to an AI-powered workforce, but only on a limited-time basis.

You’d be forgiven for thinking fractional executives are just consultants by another, buzzier name. But the truth is they’re quite different.

The main distinction is that, while they do work on a part-time basis, fractional executives are embedded in your team. They make decisions that drive outcomes and are responsible for what happens as a result.

Consultants, on the other hand, operate outside the organization. They make recommendations, but are not ultimately accountable for how things turn out. For example, a consultant can analyze your pricing strategy and show you a presentation on how they think it can be improved. A fractional CFO, on the other hand, will rebuild the pricing model themselves, and answer for the numbers at the quarter’s end.

Before you hire someone touting themselves as a fractional executive, dig into the details of what they’re offering and make sure they — and you — understand the difference.

Fractional work may be a product of our current moment, but it’s also unlikely to go away any time soon.

For entrepreneurs, the perks of such roles are many. Benefits packages, equity and long-term salary commitments are not part of the equation. You get the expertise without the long-term overhead, and you can scale the relationship up or down as your needs change.

It’s entirely possible that a fractional hire can turn into a full-time offer once the time is right. But understanding the role, its limitations and whether it’s the appropriate fit for your business is key. A fractional hire doesn’t need to feel like a compromise, because it shouldn’t be. Handled correctly, it’s a deliberate way to get the right expertise at the right time, without pretending your company needs something it doesn’t.
