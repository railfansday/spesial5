Future Broncos: 3 Prospects to Watch in Week 11 of NCAA Football Season

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Welcome back to another installment of Future Broncos. Last week, I focused on a trio of defenders for Broncos Country to keep their eye on as the rest of the collegiate football season transpires. This week, I'll switch gears and offer three players on offense since that side of the ball has been Denver's biggest issue in 2026.

So far this season, I've offered my thoughts on a handful of offensive line prospects. Mizzou's Cayden Green, Indiana's Carter Smith, Minnesota's Greg Johnson, and USC's Elijah Paige. Based on the comments section, identifying more big guys in the offensive trenches is something you are all interested in.

Georgia fifth-year senior Earnest Green III has been on my radar for a while. Back in 2023, he was a fourteen-game starter for the Bulldogs at left tackle protecting future top pick Carson Beck. He continued to man down that position for nine games in 2024, but a lingering shoulder issue cost him a few games and slowed the promise he showed as a freshman.

Last season, Greene (6'4″, 320 pounds) flipped to right tackle while Monroe Freeling was manning the blindside. However, he started just nine games due to lingering back issues. Though he didn't give up a sack in the over 800 offensive snaps he played in 2005.

Now healthy, Greene is back home where it all started on the left side of Georgia's stalwart offensive line. The redshirt senior is poised to finish his collegiate career with over 40 starts and over 3,000 snaps between both tackle positions and having three different line coaches. That experience will pay off and mean a lot to NFL scouts and general managers who value experience and top-notch SEC pedigrees.

I'm a big fan of the depth he gets in his pass sets. I also like his ability to anchor and hold his own against strong power rushers. Moreover, I am impressed with his ability to get up field and take on blockers at the second level. It'll be interesting to see how he measures [arm length] during the pre-draft circuit. There are some who believe his best football is inside at guard, but I think he can stick at tackle.

Last week's performance versus Ole Miss earned him the Outland Trophy Player of the Week honors. To date, Greene has only given up four quarterback pressures on the season. The Broncos could use a tough and battle-tested player in their trenches next April, and I believe Greene (if medicals check out) has a shot at being a Day 2 selection. You can catch him in action this afternoon on ABC when the top-ranked Bulldogs host Mizzou.

For most of my Future Broncos installments, I've tried to focus on seniors whether it be fourth or fifth-year players. One particular reason is that NIL money has changed the NFL Draft landscape. Early declarations are basically half of what they used to be. In turn, that makes it a lot harder to find quality players to address needs.

However, a potential early round pick that may wind up declaring early if he keeps playing well is Notre Dame offensive lineman Anthonie Knapp. He manned down the left tackle position for the Fighting Irish for the first 27 games of his collegiate career. For his third season, the junior has kicked inside to guard.

That's where many analysts have stated his best NFL potential might be. However, there are a handful of others who think a further kick inside to the pivot may be where he blossoms. Regardless, his versatility will be a big enhancer for his draft stock if he chooses to declare.

We all know Head Coach Sean Payton values experience and versatility when it comes to offensive lineman. This Golden Domer certainly fits the bill. By the end of this season, Knapp (6'5", 305 pounds) will have logged well over 2,000 snaps and have three full seasons of starting experience.

While he could stand to put on some weight and muscle to handle the rigors of the pro game, his anchor is incredibly strong at this stage. In my opinion, he plays with NFL ready technique at the line of scrimmage. He has been an enforcer in the run game and a key reason for Notre Dame's success on the ground this season. As a pass protector, Knapp has been credited with giving up just one sack in nearly three dozen starts.

Whether or not Knapp chooses to declare remains to be seen but given Denver's long-term needs at multiple positions on the offensive line, he would be a quality prospect for them to consider. I believe he has a legitimate shot to be a Top 50 player easily if he decides to take his talents to the NFL. You can watch him in action when the third-ranked Fighting Irish host Boston College this afternoon.

Thus far in our Future Broncos installments, we have highlighted just two tight ends in the 2027 NFL Draft. Underclassmen Oregon's Jamari Johnson and Virginia Tech's Luke Reynolds. I have a strong feeling both of them will declare and will merit early consideration by NFL teams looking to upgrade the position.

This collegiate season, another underclassman from Iowa is making quite the statement this season—DJ Vonnahme. Over the years, the Hawkeyes have been a tight end producing factory under Kirk Ferentz. Players like George Kittle, Noah Fant, T.J. Hockensen, and Sam LaPorta all come to mind.

Vonnahme (6'4", 241 pounds) appears to be next to follow in their footsteps. Appearing in twelve games as a redshirt freshman in 2025, Vonnahme logged five starts and was the Hawkeyes' leading receiver with 29 catches, 434 yards, and 3 touchdowns. He popped onto the map of scouts last season with a dominant seven catch, 146-yard performance against Vanderbilt in last year's ReliaQuest Bowl.

In eight games in 2026, Vonnahme sports 32 receptions for 314 yards and 3 touchdowns as well as one rushing touchdown. His yards per route ran (2.59) is amongst the highest in the nation. He has one of the best contested catch rates in the nation and can work multiple levels of the field.

While there is no doubting his upside and talent as a receiving threat at the next level, Vonnahme's work as a blocker leaves a lot to be desired. It's important to note he is only 20 years old and is a former quarterback that is still learning the nuances of the position. Getting better at blocking is something he can certainly get better at with more time.

Vonnahme is just a redshirt sophomore. With that in mind, a return back to Iowa seems quite likely. Though with this year's class not having a clear-cut number one just yet, perhaps he goes pro if he gets good enough feedback in the pre-draft process. You can catch him in tonight when the sixteenth ranked Hawkeyes host Purdue tomorrow evening on NBC.
