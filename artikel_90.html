Ice hockey prospect Max Calce follows Leon Draisaitl's path, leaving Mannheim for Canada

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The player widely seen as Germany's biggest ice hockey talent to come along in years is leaving the country's top professional league for Canada – changing tracks in his bid to make it to the NHL.

Max Calce made the surprise announcement that he would be moving to the Brantford Bulldogs of the Ontario Hockey League (OHL) in a statement issued on the website of his German Ice Hockey League (DEL) team, Adler Mannheim late on Tuesday.

"I have long had three goals," he said. "To play for the Adler, in the Canadian Hockey League (CHL), and in the NHL. Today is a difficult and emotional day for me, as I say goodbye to my home."

Having started the season in Mannheim, many thought the 17-year-old would finish the season in the DEL.

The announcement came after Calce, who had been a regular in Mannheim's lineup this season, had been left off the team sheet for Tuesday night's game in Straubing.

This comes less than a month after he became the youngest-ever player to score a goal for the German men's national team – against Slovakia in Germany's final game of the annual Deutschland Cup invitational.

"It was only a matter of time; he's an incredible player," said former NHL player Tobias Rieder, who frequently played on a line with Calce during the tournament.

"The most impressive thing is that, at 17, he plays a very mature style of hockey—he almost always makes the right decision."

Calce is also the youngest goal scorer in DEL history, having found the net for Mannheim in September 2025 at just 16 years and 100 days old.

In his statement, the centerman thanked Mannheim, which has produced a number of NHL players over the years, including Leon Draisaitl, Moritz Seider and Tim Stützle.

"The trust, support, and experiences I gained through my coaches, teammates, and everyone within the organization have played a major role in my development—both as a player and as a person."

This season, his second in the DEL, Calce has six goals and two assists in Mannheim's first 19 games.

"We believe in preparing young players to take opportunities at the highest level of the sport," Dallas Eakins, Mannheim's head coach and sporting director said.

"As much as we would have loved to continue to have him play for us, we respect and welcome his ambition."

On the surface, the notion of moving from a men's professional league to a junior league may seem like a step backwards, but Calce's move to the OHL will give Calce increased exposure to NHL scouts. The OHL is one of the top three junior leagues in Canada and according to figures released by the CHL last season, they produce more NHL players than any other developmental system.

In doing so, Calce is now following the path of Edmonton Oilers superstar Draisaitl, who left Mannheim's youth program at the age of 16 to pursue his NHL dream, joining the Western Hockey League's Prince Albert Raiders.

This, of course is not the only path to the NHL; Moritz Seider of the Detroit Red Wings and the Ottawa Senators' Tim Stützle were both first-round draft choices plucked directly from Mannheim. Calce is hoping to be taken in the first round of next summer's NHL Entry Draft.

Born to Russian parents in Mannheim, Calce changed his birth surname, Penkin, to that of his mother and stepfather last summer.
