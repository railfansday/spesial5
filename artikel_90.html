British hacker who stole $11m through text message scheme jailed for six years in US

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Spanish police arrested Buchanan in Madrid

A British hacker has been sentenced to six years in a federal prison in the US for conspiring to hack into at least a dozen companies via text message phishing, and stealing more than $11.6m (Â£8.8m) in cryptocurrency from individual victims.

Tyler Buchanan, 24, from Dundee, started hacking as a teenager and was part of the collective of young hackers known as Scattered Spider, which has been blamed for a string of high profile cyber-crimes.

US prosecutors said the thefts had "severe, life-altering consequences" for victims and showed the growing threat posed by online crime groups.

The sentence brings to an end a years-long international investigation involving authorities in the UK, US, Spain and Canada.

Buchanan admitted taking part in a hacking campaign that began in 2021, when he was 19.

Working from his parents' home, he and other English-speaking hackers in the US and UK targeted employees at major technology and telecommunications companies.

It made them $8m in less than two years, according to US prosecutors.

Buchanan and his hacker gang sent out hundreds of SMS messages to the mobile phones of people who worked for large tech or telecoms companies.

The SMS phishing messages contained links to spoof websites designed to look like legitimate sites of victim companies.

The group used messages like "Hi, your account is due to expire - you need to log in to your work account here immediately or you will lose access to work apps".

The websites then lured the recipient into providing confidential information, including personal identifying information (PII), and account usernames and passwords.

The boys and young men did it on an industrial scale with Buchanan setting up a Telegram bot to collect all the victims' credentials as the duped workers unwittingly handed over their login details.

For some victim companies, subsequently stolen information included confidential work information, intellectual property, plus account access credentials, names, email addresses, and telephone numbers.

The group then used stolen information and SIM-swapping techniques to target cryptocurrency investors in the United States.

In the largest single theft, prosecutors said one victim lost $1.67m in December 2022.

Overall, authorities calculated that Buchanan and his co-conspirators stole more than $11.6m from victims.

Buchanan was also a victim of cyber-criminals.

In early 2023 his online identity and home address were leaked by rival hackers seeking access to his cryptocurrency.

Cyber security researchers and journalists reported that he was assaulted at his home and threatened with a blowtorch.

"TylerB and his group were highly sophisticated and organised but the violence he and others experienced was a sign of the troubling direction of these online communities of young hackers" says cyber security researcher Allison Nixon from Unit221B.

Buchanan then reportedly fled to Canada to escape further threats before returning home.

In April 2023, officers from Police Scotland searched Buchanan's bedroom after receiving a request from the FBI.

Investigators recovered a device containing the names and addresses of hacking victims, along with cryptocurrency seed phrases and account credentials belonging to at least one victim.

The following month, Buchanan left the UK again.

He was arrested on 31 May 2024 at Palma de Mallorca Airport in Spain while attempting to board a private flight to Naples.

After being extradited to the United States, he remained in custody before being sentenced this week.

In his November 2023 charge, Buchanan was named along with four American men and boys in their twenties and teens for alleged activity linked to the notorious cyber-criminal collective Scattered Spider.

They have all pleaded guilty too and are now serving time in jail.

Two other members of the Scattered Spider group were jailed in the UK in July for various cyber attacks including a disruptive one against Transport for London that caused Â£39m of damage.
