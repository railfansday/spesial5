Asking Mom and Dad for Down Payment Help This Thanksgiving? Here’s How Much To Ask For

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Thanksgiving. A time for turkey, football, and the gathering of family.

It can be a stressful day for many, from the demands of traveling to the obligations of hosting at home. But hopefully, for most, it’s a day to be spent being thankful for having the loved ones gathered around the table.

And what better time to ask those loved ones for a bit of help to achieve the American dream than on a quintessential American holiday?

Jokes aside, if it’s on your holiday agenda to hit up Mom, Dad, Grandma, Grandpa, or even cool Aunt Janet for some money to buy a home next year, there’s a few things you should know before making the trip.

Like how much you’re actually going to need for that dream home.

The reality is, young people really do need help with down payments these days—and many are getting it.

In the past year, 25% of first-time homebuyers used gifts or loans from family and friends to help fund their down payment, according to the National Association of Realtors® 2026 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers.

Furthermore, whether they’re going it alone or with help, all buyers are strapped for the cash for down payments as overall payments are on the decline.

The typical down payment share dropped for four straight quarters, softening to 12.8% in the first quarter of 2026, down 1.1 percentage points from a year earlier, according to the most recent Realtor.com® down payment report.

In dollar terms, down payments sank 19% year over year to their lowest point since the third quarter of 2021.

Before sitting down with your parents to talk numbers, it’s important to know just how much money you’ll actually need.

For context, the typical U.S. down payment in April 2026 reached $25,000, or an average of 13.2% of the purchase price, down from $27,500 and 13.8% in April 2025.

But calculating the right amount you’ll need starts with being honest about how much money you already have—and where you plan to buy.

"Every buyer’s situation and every market is different, so there’s no single number," Realtor.com senior economist Hannah Jones says. Still, there are benchmarks to keep in mind.

"The most useful targets are the 10% and 20% thresholds, and the right ask depends on how far a buyer is from the next one. In lower-cost metros, help reaching 10% down could be just a few thousand dollars rather than tens of thousands. In pricey California metros, the typical down payment is well into six figures, which is very hard to save without a windfall (see the AI boom) or parental help.”

Framing the request around those specific benchmarks should make it easier to explain to your parents why a certain dollar amount matters. Hitting each tier unlocks tangible savings on the loan itself, reducing ongoing housing costs long after closing day.

"Getting to 10% down can pay off significantly in the form of a lower mortgage rate, and getting to 20% eliminates the need for private mortgage insurance," Jones explains.

Still, asking for help is tricky, and if you’re concerned your parents will be hesitant, remember that any help they can offer will go a long way.

“A smaller loan still lowers the monthly payment. More is better if the goal is minimizing the payment, but reaching one of these thresholds is a great place to start the conversation."

Finally, if there is some hesitation from your family about offering a helping hand due to the market conditions and mortgage rates, there’s some hope on the horizon that should sway them.

"Nationally, the market has shifted in a more buyer-friendly direction," Jones says. But location truly makes all the difference.

"Supply is still catching up in many Northeast and Midwest metros, so sellers hold more of the cards there and buyers should put their best foot forward. In the South and parts of the West, buyers are in the driver’s seat.”
