16 MLSs Move to Consolidate Compass Lawsuit

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

More than a dozen MLSs involved in a lawsuit against Compass over broker cooperation rules have joined together to file a motion to consolidate pretrial proceedings.

Should the November 23 motion succeed, the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (JPML) would “transfer and centralize 18 separate actions pending in 12 courts across the country,” the filing states. Essentially, the JPML would combine each of the lawsuits into one under the Southern District of New York (SDNY), to be handled by judge Mary Kay Vyskocil.

In response to the MLSs’ motion, a Compass spokesperson stated that the “filing is a procedural request that does not change the substance of our claims.”

“Each MLS applies its own rules, and the impact of those rules on agents, home sellers and buyers is specific to its local jurisdiction,” the spokesperson said.

The MLSs’ filing does address the locality of the lawsuits, stating that “although Compass may argue that the MLSs’ rules and enforcement differ, any differences are minor and do not affect the core issue in dispute.”

Each of the MLSs agreed that the SDNY was the best place for court proceedings to take place, as the “same motions, witnesses, documents, and experts will recur in every case,” according to the filing. Alternatively—should New York not suffice—the MLSs offer the Western District of North Carolina or the Middle District of Florida as venues for court proceedings.

At the root of the back-and-forth between the mega-brokerage and the MLSs are broker cooperation rules that prevent pre-marketed or private listings from being distributed via the MLS, with the dispute originating between Compass and the California Regional MLS (CRMLS).

The rules in question refer to each MLSs’ implementation of the National Association of Realtors’® (NAR) Clear Cooperation Policy, in which a listing broker must submit the listing to the MLS for cooperation with other MLS participants within one business day of marketing a property to the public.

On Sept. 8 of this year, Compass sent a letter to CRMLS “demanding” that it alter its rules around public marketing, as the brokerage has very vocally pursued its three-phased marketing approach, which allows sellers to list their homes as private exclusives within the Compass’ network, followed by “Coming Soon” public listings on Compass platforms, prior to full MLS distribution.

CRMLS rejected Compass’ demand and lawsuit threat, instead suing the brokerage ahead of the Oct. 6 deadline Compass had set for the MLS to comply.

After the initial lawsuit, Compass filed a countersuit against CRMLS. But several MLSs joined the CRMLS suit against the mega brokerage, arguing that Compass was “waging war against the Cooperation Rules,” according to the latest filing.

In what appears to be an attempt to force the MLSs into a war of attrition, Compass sued 15 of the MLSs in separate courts, dividing the organizations and creating “massive inefficiencies and duplication of effort,” as stated by ARMLS counsel Gregory S. Skidmore.

Moving for consolidation would spare the MLSs from maintaining separate lawsuits, according to the latest filing.

“Cases that share common factual allegations are prime candidates for centralization,” the motion states. “Here, all 18 actions arise from the same conduct and present the exact same legal question: whether the MLSs’ Cooperation Rules requiring that a publicly marketed listing be submitted to the MLSs violates Section 1 of the Sherman Act or related state antitrust laws.”
