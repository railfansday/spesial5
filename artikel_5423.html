$3 billion Remote CEO told every employee to take a five-hour AI course or miss expectations in their performance review: ‘Get with the times’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

With AI coming for jobs, the $3 billion HR platform Remote is forcing its workers to get ahead of it. Employees get five hours of paid company time to take the course. But there’s a catch: skip it and you can forget about a passing performance review.

It’s all part of CEO Job van der Voort’s push to get Remote’s roughly 2,000 employees up to speed on AI.

“AI will run the world so it’s about getting with the times,” van der Voort told Fortune. In his view, employers should get their workers up to scratch—so he launched the free AI crash course, aiforactualwork.com, and made the first course mandatory for his entire workforce.

“It’s very important for companies to take responsibility. They are our people. We hired them. We decided, and then we said to them, ‘the bar now is that you start using AI,’ so yes, we should totally take responsibility for the people that work for us,” he added.

“If you are a company that employs a whole bunch of people, you should take responsibility for the people that work for you. You’re not going to be able to fire all the people that work for you and find new people that are good with this.”

But responsibility goes both ways. Staff who didn’t take him up on the offer would face consequences. “We became really clear. What we said is you have to do it, and if you don’t, you’re not going to get ‘meeting expectations’ in your performance review,” van der Voort said. “So everybody did it.”

Luckily, the threat alone worked, and now everyone in his company is up to speed.

“It just raises the foundational level that we can expect everybody to work at,” he added. “They’re not going to make basic mistakes. When we ask somebody to solve a problem, we don’t have to think to ourselves, oh, but do they know this is super easy with AI?”

Not everyone has a boss who’ll give them hours of paid time to learn AI. But van der Voort says that’s no excuse to avoid learning it.

“If you are an individual and you feel like your employer is not doing this for you, do the course we created because it’s only going to help you—if not with your current job, with a future job.”

He said people have always had to adapt to the latest technology, or get left behind. “People had to learn how to use cell phones. People had to learn how to use computers, right? And this is just another thing.”

And in his eyes, it’s not that hard. “It’s really easy. Just ask the AI, how do I do X, and it will tell you,” he said.

He’s not the only CEO saying you don’t need to be an expert to get started.

Glean CEO Arvind Jain previously told Fortune that “AI is not a difficult thing. You don’t have to sit through 10 hours of a course. Just go into one of these AI tools—whether you want to use Gemini or ChatGPT or whatever—and talk to them like a colleague.”

Orianna Rosa Royle is the Success associate editor at Fortune, overseeing careers, leadership, and company culture coverage. She was previously the senior reporter at Management Today, Britain's longest-running publication for CEOs.
