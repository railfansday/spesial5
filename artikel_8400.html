Zero In Newton, NJ

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

I satisfy my cravings for town food and financial planning. I witness sports history.

What does a Trek blogger do with a free morning? The answer is…tax planning. Seriously. Personal finance is something of a hobby of mine. I had an idea while hiking during the week and wanted to model it and see the impact on my future tax liability. Riveting stuff, lemme tell ya.

While I had my nose buried in a spreadsheet I had one eye on the replay of yesterday’s F1 qualifying session for the Mexico City Grand Prix. Later in the day I could focus on the race itself which proved eventful. When the dust had settled, Formula One had its 2026 champion in Kimi Antonelli. Kimi stepped up in just his second season with a quixotic campaign to become the youngest ever Formula One champion. He is Italy’s first champion in 73 years. His championship is unquestionably well-deserved as he accumulated his large lead while dueling with 4 other F1 world champions, and shared this weekend’s podium with 2 of them in an incredible moment of serendipity. I won’t soon forget this season both for the unlikely champion and for having watched it while hiking the Appalachian Trail (and while writing this blog).

I celebrated Kimi’s title with unlimited soda refills, pizza, and ice cream, as is my usual M.O. The cafe gave me a peppermint mocha free of charge! I can’t say its something I’d order, I tend to stick to the basics with everything, including coffee, but I’ll admit it was pretty tasty.

I spent some time putting together a viable itinerary for the remainder of my hike. The plan gives me one day of buffer and an end date of November 22nd, assuming I take it easy over 3 days to complete the 4 state challenge during the southbound leg to be well rested to attempt to make the return trip northbound in a single day. Lately I’ve been wondering if I should record some video to document the attempt. It isn’t something I’ve done before, but this is certainly a year of firsts!

The Peppermint Mocha, tastes like the holidays.

Incredible. I now have less than 500 miles of hiking left to complete my journey. When I started out, I wasn’t sure it was possible. I had countless ideas to hike most of the trail by skipping sections if I didn’t feel up to the task. It doesn’t look like those will be necessary now.

It was a little bit of an up and down week. Tuesday felt like a new low after twisting my increasingly sensitive ankle several times. No worries though – its probably either a bruise or a slight stretch of the tendon. My ankles have survived years of hiking without feeling so much as a pinch. They are as strong as ever, just meeting their match on the slippery rocks this time of year.

Depending on where you draw the line, I officially completed New York either on Thursday or Saturday. The NY/NJ line I documented on Thursday is not the first time the trail enters New York heading northbound, it first enters New York shortly after the New Jersey high point, but soon crosses back into New Jersey and back again several times. I don’t think it is a coincidence that the AT follows the state line closely, it is likely the result of that stretch of land being public.

I also semi-retired my first pair of Altra Olympus 6 shoes that I put on at the start of the White Mountains since they have now accumulated 1,000 miles. They seem to have plenty of life left, they will accrue more miles on future hikes for sure.

Welcome to November, its finishing month! The time change will be welcome, I feel lazy waiting for the sun to come up to start hiking only to realize that it is already 8:00AM by the time I actually make it onto the trail. The time change won’t bring any more daylight, it will be startling to see daylight running out before 5:00PM.

My first 2-day stint will see me finish all but about 1 mile of New Jersey. I’ll be crossing the Delaware Water Gap on Wednesday and setting foot in Pennsylvania for the first time since mid-July. There are warmer days ahead, but also a chance of rain later in the week. Rocks are guaranteed, northern Pennsylvania is infamous for them. We’ll see if their reputation is warranted.
