Fantasy Football Rankings: Hayden Winks' WR Blueprint for Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Blueprint is my weekly column and basically everything that I use to build out the most accurate rankings I can. There will be an RB column, this WR column and a combination column on the onesie positions. You will find film notes and endless charts. Maybe enough charts to stress test the Yahoo website. I kid.

It's impossible to get every ranking thoroughly early in the week, so be sure to keep checking in on these columns through Sunday morning. As always, my full rankings at every position are available.

1. Ja'Marr Chase (at WAS) - 94.5 proj. receiving yards

Chase is back to averaging 98 YPG this year after a WR1 overall stretch over the past month. The Commanders are a pass-funnel defense without a strong secondary. They've allowed the fifth-most passing YPG, and Chase hung 118-2 against them in his only game against Washington back in 2024.

2. CeeDee Lamb (vs. TEN) - 90.5 proj. receiving yards

Lamb has been pretty up-and-down this year, but on average, he's the WR3 on WR2 overall usage as a no-doubt Round 1 value. He's leading the position in target share (39%) and air yards (145) over the past month, as the Cowboys defense has continued to allow anyone through the front door.

Now he gets a dream matchup against a Titans defense allowing 35.0 half PPR points per game to WRs (first). They've allowed the most points under 20 air yards, specifically, where Lamb does almost all of his damage. They've been even worse of late without DT Jeffery Simmons and S Amani Hooker. Dallas enters as 8.5-point home favorites with a slate-leading 31.5-point team total (first).

3. Justin Jefferson (at SF) - 84.5 proj. receiving yards

The Vikings passed more in neutral situations last week with Kirk Cousins compared to over the past year and a half with underwhelming QBs. That'll help Jefferson find his mid-range WR1 groove, on top of those throws being far more accurate. Jefferson had three screens last week, plus other underneath targets, allowing him to post a season-high 19.3 expected half PPR points (WR5). They didn't rip the explosive play, but those will be coming. In the short term, Jordan Addison won't be out there either.

This week is a great spot for Jefferson. The Vikings are slight dogs, yet still have a 21.5-point team total in what should be a down week across the NFL. The 49ers were shredded by the Cowboys' pass game last week and have been given up 27.7 half PPR points to WR rooms on average this entire season. Most of those will go to Jefferson here.

4. Amon-Ra St. Brown (vs. TB) - 81.5 proj. receiving yards

St. Brown was on a five-game streak without a touchdown before finishing last week with 23.8 half PPR points in Germany with a couple of long scores. He's been a target monster all year long and remains a positive regression candidate. The slot star is the WR5 on WR4 usage and WR4 targets per game this year.

The Buccaneers have been stout against WRs overall, ranking 24th in half PPR points allowed (24.1 per game). However, they rank seventh in points allowed to receiver targets under 10 air yards, fitting St. Brown's underneath profile. Big slots like Luther Burden III (WR9) and Germie Bernard (WR6) have had spike weeks against them recently. Detroit enters as seven-point home favorites with a 25.25-point team total (ninth).

5. Tee Higgins (at WAS) - 74.5 proj. receiving yards

It'll be hard to run as pure as he has so far. Higgins is the WR6 on WR16 usage and WR14 targets per game. The good news is Higgins has the best film of his career, and Joe Burrow is firmly in his absolute prime. This is a pairing that should be dunking all over models. On top of that, the Bengals have the highest neutral pass rate (67%) in the NFL. When games are close, Higgins will see eight+ targets per game. The Commanders are an eruption spot.

6. Nico Collins (vs. IND) - 71.5 proj. receiving yards

Collins ran more routes last week — thank you!!! — and it worked. He turned 15 targets into a WR7 week. The Browns held their own in the second half by blitzing and playing tight man coverage behind it. Collins will have more space to operate in future matchups, and the Texans offense is trending towards being more and more pass-heavy. They're now fifth in neutral pass rate after usually being bottom-10 under the DeMeco Ryans regime. Collins is a fringe WR1/2 with plenty of weekly upside.

Collins is looking to extend his four-game streak of 100+ yards against Indy. The Colts won't have CB1 Sauce Gardner, nor CB2 Charvarius Ward. This should be BBQ chicken. Houston enters as 12.5-point home favorites with an implied team total of 28.5 points (fifth).

7. George Pickens (vs. TEN) - 69.5 proj. receiving yards

We're so back. Pickens is the WR12 on WR10 usage this season, on WR11 targets per game. He's not even running hot either. He finally had one of his signature blowup games last week, thanks to the Cowboys' unwatchable defense. Pickens is on the WR1/2 border depending on the matchup. He is best when facing single high, as Dak Prescott can take perimeter shots to him with more success.

The Titans don't have anyone in the secondary to worry about. They've allowed the most fantasy points to WRs, including the third-most points on targets with 15+ air yards. They won't have FS Amani Hooker roaming deep now either. Dallas carries a 31.5-point implied team total (first) as 8.5-point home favorites.

8. Zay Flowers (at CAR) - 68.5 proj. receiving yards

It would've been best if Lamar Jackson was playing like the MVP, but PPR scam artist Jacoby Brissett is a great backup plan for Flowers' fantasy appeal. We instantly felt that last week when they trailed the Chargers. They had a season-high neutral pass rate (73%) in an offense that's been last in dropbacks per game since 2022 when Flowers entered the NFL. We'll trade some downfield completions for more volume moving forward. I'm throwing out the Week 8-9 sample and putting more faith into last week's 6-74-0 line on seven targets.

We'll see if the Panthers are still going to be without their top-2 CBs later in the week. They've been middle of the pack against fantasy WRs this year, with Jordyn Tyson turning 13 targets into 80 scoreless yards last week as the most recent example.

9. Parker Washington (at NYG) - 67.5 proj. receiving yards

Washington can get game-scripted out of production in second halves, but that is a good problem to have, as he can either go crazy early or walk into full-game production when games are tighter. He's inside the top 15 in every per-route metric essentially, and Washington is still the WR13 despite being the WR25 in targets per game. It's clear that he's both Trevor Lawrence and Liam Coen's favorite target. How could he not be? He's smart, physical, explosive and can be used inside and out. Star.

The Giants do have a strong secondary with a great CB1 in Deonte Banks. Washington gets moved around into favorable matchups, and I trust Coen to find spots for him. But the Giants are 30th in passing yards per game allowed (196). They've shut down deep targets in particular, so it's possible Washington racks up more underneath and intermediate targets this time around. Jacksonville travels as 6-point road favorites with a 26.25-point team total (seventh).

10. Malik Nabers (vs. JAX) - 69.5 proj. receiving yards

Nabers is the WR12 on WR4 overall usage since Week 5 after knocking off the rust early. He's still not 100% himself and does seem to be mad offline, but the spike-week potential is fully on the menu when the ball is going his way. Over that span, only JSN, Puka Nacua, Ja'Marr Chase and Justin Jefferson are ahead of him in target share, so when the Giants are forced into dropbacks, expect huge games down the stretch. J.J. McCarthy is pushing the ball on these deeper out routes relentlessly and looks generally improved, even if he's volatile.

The Jaguars will be a real test. Travis Hunter is in the mix for the second-best CB tape right now, and he'll be joined by FS Jessie Bates III now. The only good news is the Giants are expected to be trailing, and offenses facing the Jaguars have the second-highest neutral pass rate (59%). For those reasons, the Jaguars are 15th in fantasy points allowed to WRs. The Giants enter as 6-point home underdogs carrying an implied team total of 20.25 points (18th).

11. Luther Burden III (vs. NO) - 59.5 proj. receiving yards

12. Garrett Wilson (at LAC) - 65.5 proj. receiving yards

Update: Omar Cooper Jr. and Isaiah Williams are both out, so the Jets' starting and backup slot receivers are out. Perhaps the Jets use Wilson in more creative ways this week and at the very least there is slightly easier target competition, though one pivot would be to increase first-round TE Kenyon Sadiq's snaps. Wilson is the WR20 on WR14 usage this season.

The Chargers defense has been one of the friendliest environments for WRs, ranking 5th in half PPR points allowed to the position. Opposing passing attacks facing Los Angeles have passed at a 57% neutral clip (5th-highest in the NFL), funneling volume into a secondary that just surrendered 26.5 points to Kayshon Boutte and 18.5 points to Tank Dell in Week 9. The Jets will look to exploit that vulnerability as 3.5-point road underdogs with an implied total of 19 points (19th).

13. Carnell Tate (at DAL) - 65.5 proj. receiving yards

Tate (shoulder) has been limited in practice after missing the last handful of games. This assumes he's ready to role, while Wan'Dale Robinson was placed on IR and Calvin Ridley was demoted. It'll be Tate, Elic Ayomanor and Chimere Dike in 3-WR sets moving forward. That's not much of a competition for Tate, who has lived up to his NFL draft status to me. He can win on breaking routes and has great hands. His skill set stands to benefit with the changes the Titans have made offensively with Tate banged up:

Over his past 4 games, Tate is the WR25 on WR22 usage and WR20 targets per game. In good matchups, he can be viewed as a boom-bust WR2 now. The Cowboys are a great matchup. They've allowed the 6th-most fantasy points to WRs, while struggling to defend Keenan Allen and Deebo Samuel last week as the latest example. Michael Wilson is the closest real life comp to Tate, and he hung 128-1 on them as the WR4 three games ago. Tennessee owns a 23.0-point team total this week, easily their highest of the season.

14. DJ Moore (vs. MIA) - 47.5 proj. receiving yards

I wrote about DJ Moore in my weekly film review series.

This week, the Bills catch an injured, inexperienced Dolphins defense at home with a monster 31.0-point team total. The upside is obvious. He just has to come down with one of his downfield shots.

15. Chris Olave (at CHI) - 68.5 proj. receiving yards

I wrote about Olave's potential role change in last week's film review column, but long story short is Tyson is an underneath and intermediate player who is taking away snaps from vertical-threat Bryce Lance. Olave inherited some of the Lance clear out route role last week, to the point that he had season lows in all metrics. The Saints would be in a worse spot if that continued, but that is the risk in the short term as we learn more. Olave is simply too good to go away in an offense that's first in neutral pace with a struggling defense. The more likely scenario that emerges is he takes a volume haircut and settles in as a more volatile WR2, while Tyson dances near the line of scrimmage.

The good news is the Bears have struggled specifically against the Olave skill set. They've allowed the sixth-most fantasy points on 15+ air yard throws to WRs, while nickel CB Kyler Gordon has helped sure up throws under 10 air yards where the Bears have allowed the fewest points per game. Getting over the top is the only hope the Saints have of making this a game. New Orleans heads to Chicago as 11.5-point road underdogs with an implied total of 18.5 points (20th).

16. Jordyn Tyson (at CHI) - 56.5 proj. receiving yards

I wrote about Tyson in the film review once again. Check that out.

This week's matchup against the Bears has some positives and negatives. The negatives are the Saints only project for 18.5 points and may struggle getting onto the field against a clock-killing offense. Their best defensive back is also slot CB Kyler Gordon, who has the exact skill set that could give an in-development rookie some issues. That said, the Saints can get into crazy dropback environments as the fastest-paced offense when trailing. They're 11.5-point dogs this time around, so it's not hard to see both Saints WRs with 10ish targets here. In a closer game last week, Tyson had 13 of them. When in doubt, lean into the volume.

17. Tetairoa McMillan (vs. BAL) - 63.5 proj. receiving yards

McMillan is averaging 8.2 half PPR points on 11.7 expected points in his eight games with Jalen Coker this year. Over the past month against a more difficult schedule, he's the WR53 on WR25 usage. He hasn't been getting as open of late, and then Bryce Young has missed him up the sideline a bit too often. He's still handling 24% targets in an offense that leans pass first in neutral situations, but the alpha WR1 traits haven't fully blossomed in his second season. He's a very good, not elite, WR to me.

The Ravens sent home podcaster Marlon Humphrey, not that he was very good in coverage this year. He'll battle with CB1 Nate Wiggins, who is much lighter but much faster than McMillan. There will be some 50/50 balls that he needs to come down with to win this individual matchup. Tre' Harris (WR24), Parker Washington (WR19), D.J. Moore (WR13) and Tee Higgins (WR2) were able to do just that this past month against them. The Panthers are 2.5-point home favorites with an implied team total of 23.5 points (12th).

18. Rashee Rice (vs. ARI) - 50.5 proj. receiving yards

Rice is the WR25 on WR43 usage this season, removing his injured game in Week 4. He hasn't had a better finish than WR12 in a week, as the offense has become more balanced in all phases including the red zone. They've also split up the underneath WR role between he and Xavier Worthy, and now Josh Downs is in the intermediate mix. The role tweaks have been a lid on his fantasy value, but the Chiefs are also back to being one of the best offenses in the NFL. There will be spike weeks coming as a boom-bust WR2.

The Cardinals defense enters Week 11 ranked 12th in half PPR points allowed to opposing WR rooms. Arizona's secondary has allowed production to the Rice zone, sitting seventh in points allowed to WRs on throws between 5-20 yards downfield. The Chiefs are 15.5-point home favorites with an implied team total of 28.5 points (sixth).

19. Stefon Diggs (vs. CIN) - 46.5 proj. receiving yards

Diggs is quietly the WR21 on WR24 usage this season and could hold the line with Terry McLaurin out for the season. He's not a full-time player, nor as quick as he once was, but his game ages gracefully as a crafty player with excellent hands and toughness. Diggs has been manufactured some targets on top of being their most relied upon underneath and intermediate target.

This is an excellent spot for him with the Bengals expected to put a beating on the Commanders' defense. Game script should be on his side, and he needs these 3-WR sets to get onto the field. On top of that, the Bengals have a fantastic CB1 in D.J. Turner on the perimeter, but Diggs will avoid him and settle into where Cincy has been the worst at defending: the middle. Similar WRs like Chimere Dike (WR8) and Zay Flowers (WR3) have had great games against them recently, while traditional WR1s have struggled. The Commanders project for 23.75 points (11th), too.

20. Jaylen Waddle (vs. LV) - 42.5 proj. receiving yards

Waddle (concussion) has been a full participant and hopefully will be able to finish this next game after injuries have piled up. Waddle only has one game above 13.5 half PPR points this season, and his 5.8 targets per game sit far lower than most, including myself, expected. They had the bye week to figure out how to feature him more, and it's possible he's one of the few veteran WRs who get better down the stretch. That's at least the hope with Bo Nix playing better later in the season than early on historically.

The real hope sits with the matchup. The Raiders have allowed the most yards to WRs in the slot this season on the highest QBR. Against all fantasy WRs, the Raiders have allowed the seventh-most fantasy points, including a blow up game against JSN (WR3). The Broncos have a 25.5-point team total (ninth) at home. Please come through.

21. Emeka Egbuka (at DET) - 45.5 proj. receiving yards

Egbuka has been more good than great on tape this year. He battled a toe injury in training camp and then has picked up new injuries along the way. Baker Mayfield said he "looks great right now" after hinting that the injuries have prevented him from taking off earlier this year. For what it's worth, Egbuka is not on the injury report at all right now and that was not the case earlier this year.

Across the entire season, Egbuka is the WR33 on WR19 usage. Those are probably closer to his floor. We'll see if the Bucs going to more Tez Johnson forced Eguka to play outside more, but to put things simply, the Buccaneers are desperate for Egbuka to turn into an alpha. Everyone else doesn't have the skills to do that, and the pass game has been unwatchable. I can argue he is still frequently the designed first read on a bulk of the Bucs' dropbacks. Everyone has to just play better.

The Lions are too injured at all three levels to be nervous about the matchup. The Bucs are unlikely to be in full run the clock out mode like they've been in the past, and we'll see if CB1 D.J. Reed (hip) can suit up. There should be time for whoever is starting at QB to deliver the ball with Aidan Hutchinson out. A lowly 18.25-point team total holds expectations in check. We're chasing volume more than anything.

22. DeVonta Smith (vs. PIT) - 53.5 proj. receiving yards

Smith is expected to return after a pair of injuries have derailed his 2026 season. Since 2025, Smith is the WR21 on WR25 usage. While he doesn't have A.J. Brown to compete with, he also plays in a bottom-5 offense now. It's hard to calibrate all of the moving pieces, so Smith is a boom-bust WR3 until we have a better clue for how Joe Milton is going to fit in with Smith.

On top of that, the Steelers have an excellent DL that can spoil the entire game. Philly only has a 17.5-point team total, and I totally get it.

23. Rome Odunze (vs. NO) - 45.5 proj. receiving yards

Odunze is the WR33 on WR41 usage since Caleb Williams returned in Week 6. He's not going to get the manufactured touches like others in the offense, and there are games that are taken over by the ground attack. Odunze will have to make good on his red zone and downfield targets to pay off as an upside WR3 in fantasy. That has been an issue for him for too long, but he's very much due for more long scores as we've outlined in this column before he finished as the WR13 (4-60-1) in Week 9.

The matchup suits Odunze well. The Saints have allowed the fifth-most points to WRs on throws over 15 air yards, while being quite good on intermediate and underneath throws. On top of that, S Justin Reid just asked for his release. A 30.0-point team total for Chicago is the invitation for WR2/3 hope.

24. Mike Evans (vs. MIN) - 47.5 proj. receiving yards

Evans (achilles tendinitis) went DNP-limited-limited in practice but is on track to play per his agent and the national insiders. It's a workload-based injury, and this is an important game for seeding in the NFC picture, so it makes sense for them to push his return. It'd also make sense to not push him to anywhere near 100% snaps. I'm projecting him for a part-time yet effective role against the league's best (and most unique) defense. Evans has a 66-1 game against Brian Flores back in 2023, but that defense wasn't nearly as polished as this one.

The 49ers also have the altitude to worry about in Mexico City, so there are far more unusual things to factor in, none of them working in his favor. While playing through various ailments this year, Evans is the WR17 on WR21 usage. Knocking him a tier below that feels fair to me. He's a boom-bust WR3 with six teams on bye.

25. Jalen Coker (vs. BAL) - 42.5 proj. receiving yards

Coker's output has dried up over the past month. Across his last four games, he is the WR87 in half PPR points per game on WR59 expected-points usage, averaging just 1.8 receptions on 4.0 targets. His target share has sat right around 13% across that stretch, bottoming out in Week 10 with 0 catches on four targets against New Orleans. That is a noticeable step back from his full-season baseline, where he sits as the WR32 on WR30 usage while logging 6.3 targets per contest.

The Ravens provide a middle-of-the-road matchup, ranking 16th in half PPR points allowed per game to opposing wide receivers. Baltimore has been much more vulnerable to intermediate targets, where they rank 10th in points allowed, than underneath throws where they rank 26th. While Tee Higgins posted 148 yards and a touchdown against them in Week 7, Quentin Johnston was held to just six yards on one target in Week 10. Carolina enters as 2.5-point home favorites with an implied team total of 23.5 points (12th).

26. Jameson Williams (vs. TB) - 50.5 proj. receiving yards

Never get too high or too low on Jamo. He's a boom-bust WR22 just about every season now. This year, Williams is the WR25 on WR31 usage with stretches of WR1 and WR4 play.

This is a matchup to be more concerned with. The Bucs have two great CBs, and Zyon McCollum is just as good of an athlete as Williams is. That's partially why the Bucs have allowed the fourth-fewest fantasy points to WRs on 15+ air yard throws this year. Tampa Bay is a pass funnel defense, but a lot of that is in the Amon-Ra St. Brown and Sam LaPorta zone. It doesn't mean that Williams can't take it to the crib on low volume, but there have been better matchups per-game than this one. The Lions still have a 25.25-point team total (ninth).

27. DK Metcalf (at PHI) - 42.5 proj. receiving yards

Update: Michael Pittman Jr. (foot) won't play, but Roman Wilson will enter into the lineup here. At the same time, the Eagles placed LB Zack Baun on IR and aren't the scary defense we're used to. The Steelers should win this game here, but Metcalf will go to work against star CB1 Quinyon Mitchell, while the offense has turned away from the pass game in recent weeks with both running backs healthy. He's a positive regression candidate but the time of him being a true No. 1 receiver has came and gone. He's the WR38 on WR20 usage this year. Splitting the gap is fair.

28. Caleb Douglas (at BUF) - 48.5 proj. receiving yards

Chrisitan Benford (groin) hasn't practiced, but the Bills added S Amani Hooker at the deadline and have a more menacing DL in 2026. It'll be almost entirely zone defense for Douglas to sort through with an inexperienced QB. It'll be a tough spot. To his credit, Douglas has been the WR22 on WR50 usage since returning from injury. He's a boom-bust WR3/4 with better odds of flaming out here.

29. Ladd McConkey (vs. NYJ) - 40.5 proj. receiving yards

McConkey rug pulled in Week 9 and then missed Week 10 with back spasms. He's back to limited practices this week. We'll learn more later. There have been far too many half games to calibrate just how effective McConkey can be if he's lucky enough to play more than half of the snaps. The Chargers have been extremely run heavy in every game under OC Mike McDaniel, so McConkey is floorless. His best games of the year have been WR11 against the Cardinals and WR27 against the Rams.

33. Xavier Hutchinson (vs. IND) - 36.5 proj. receiving yards

With Marvin Harrison Jr. (hamstring) still sidelined, Hutchinson is a worthwhile dart throw on Thursday Night Football in a heavy bye week. Hutchinson can score in the red zone given his size.

The Colts are really bad. They also won't have their top-two CBs, which is more important for him as he's more schedule-sensitive. Houston sits as 12.5-point home favorites with an implied total of 28.5 points (fifth).
