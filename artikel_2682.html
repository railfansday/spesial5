Jaguars Winners & Losers From Week 10: Parker Washington Explodes In Rivalry Win

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

It certainly wasn't a blowout, but good teams win and better teams cover. The Jacksonville Jaguars did just that in a 40-23 victory over the Tennessee Titans on Sunday.

Despite their record and their roster, the Titans aren't a completely awful team and showed that a bit on Sunday. The Jags had their hands full at times but made the plays when it mattered most to improve to 7-2 for the first time since 1998. Jacksonville is rattling off wins towards a potential play at the AFC's top overall seed and with one of the league's easiest schedules for the rest of the year, things are looking good in Duval County.

There is only one place to start when talking about Jacksonville's third straight win. It's with Parker Washington.

Jacksonville chopped plenty of wood and carried buckets of water on the way to victory. It was the Washington show in Nashville as the former Nittany Lion rattled off a career-high 152 receiving yards. Washington was quiet in the second half but it didn't matter because he did more than enough work in the first half, plucking ball after ball off the heads of hapless Titans defenders.

Washington's big day was part of an even bigger day by quarterback Trevor Lawrence who finished with 352 yards and three touchdowns and eclipsed 20,000 yards passing in Jacksonville. One of those touchdowns came on the first drive of the game where the QB found Washington at the back of the end zone. The only downside of Washington continuing to play so well is that Jacksonville still needs to pay him. As it has after almost every game this season, the price for the third-year receiver has gone up again.

Back in 2023, Washington was selected in the sixth round, making his way up to wide receiver one status in Jacksonville. If Josh Cameron keeps it up, a sixth-rounder himself, he could be well on his way to Washington levels of status.

The rookie Cameron was second on the Jags in receiving with three catches for 67 yards while catching his third touchdown of the year. Cameron also did decently in the return game once more, collecting 142 total return yards with a long of 32. He did get crushed by Tennessee kicker Joey Slye which resulted in a fumble, but those types of plays are few and far between anyway.

There's no question Jacksonville has a loaded wide receiver room. However, Cameron keeps proving he deserves to be on the field. If Brian Thomas Jr. continues to be up and down, Cameron will continue to reap the benefits.

For another Washington comparison: It should really come as no surprise that Bhayshul Tuten had another solid day.

The running back had 113 total yards with 88 yards rushing and his fifth score of the year on the ground. Tuten now has touchdowns on the ground in three straight games and has gone over 100 total yards in two of his last three outings.

Jacksonville made a great call when it turned over the keys to Tuten. His hard-nosed presence unlocks a nastiness in this offense that gives the Jags an edge anytime the ball is in his hands. In the same mold as Washington, Tuten is trusted to make the plays needed to propel the team to victory. He did so on Broadway.

Here is your obligatory Cam Little Rocks section. Little went 4-for-4 on field goals and 4-for-4 on extra points. It's almost indescribable how important it is to have someone as automatic as Little is. The Jags got points on all but two drives (8-10 overall), largely because once the team crosses the 50, they know they can rely on Little to convert.

Much was said about last year's dustup between then 49ers defensive coordinator Rob Salah and Liam Coen. How sweet must it be for Coen to beat Salah last year, only to turn around and drop 40 in Salah's home stadium, now the head coach with the Titans? Running Tuten into the end zone with under two minutes to play had to feel damn good.

Coen has done a tremendous job with the Jags since taking over. It doesn't take this column to tell you that. Jacksonville has cut down on penalties (just two Sunday) and overall boneheaded mistakes. That game was one Lawrence batted-ball interception away from being about as good as you could get, at least offensively. Coen has won 20 of his first 26 games, and is just the third coach to do so in the last 15 years. He is tops in terms of winning percentage over those games in the history of the sport.

Jacksonville is rolling and the head coach is a huge reason why. Keeping his coordinators in place after last year's hiring cycle cannot go unnoticed too. This is the year to make a run. There's no reason to believe right now that Coen isn't the guy to lead it.

Yeah, so that trade for Jessie Bates was a huge one because the safety play in Nashville was subpar. More will be said on the defense below, but Bates proved his worth before ever even taking a snap defensively for Jacksonville. This team needs help on the back end. Luckily, help is ready to play and maybe even sleeping in the facility.

Does anyone want to talk about that Tony Pollard touchdown? No?

Yes, the Titans aren't a great offense. Yes, the Jags have a smothering defense typically. But also yes that the Titans are playing well offensively as of late. The Jags got exposed at times by that offense and especially on Pollard's 32-yard receiving touchdown where he broke through just about the entire Jacksonville defense. Cam Ward and company didn't light Jacksonville up, but that game wasn't the pure defensive domination many might have thought was coming.

There were some solid individual performances though on defense. Travis Hunter had an incredible leaping interception. Ventrell Miller was crushing guys like usual. Dennis Gardeck even looked pretty solid. But this defense needs the help it acquired at the deadline. Overall, no worries probably. But that was not this unit's best performance of the year at all.

There were no DaVon Hamilton-level injuries following Sunday's game, but that doesn't mean the Jags got out of Nashville unscathed. The big injury question mark after that game was with a knee injury to Robert Hainsey. You can make the case that Hainsey maybe shouldn't even be out there, but it's a blow to the team's overall depth if he is down for a while. This specific injury sort of amplifies a questionable lack of offensive line talent acquired at the trade deadline from the front office. Can't complain about that defensive haul, but still.

In a much less catastrophic way, defensive end Danny Striggow left with a concussion. He looked to be getting some solid reps out there before he was ruled out. Again, depth is always helpful especially if the team is going to keep adding games in the playoffs.

Nothing world-ending, but something to monitor as we keep this thing chugging along.
