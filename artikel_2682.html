3 thoughts as the Mavericks look to get right against the Golden State Warriors

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

If anything can get Cooper Flagg right after a recent three-game lull in production, it just might be a good old-fashioned bobblehead night.

The Dallas Mavericks (7-7), on the heels of three straight losses, welcome Yaxel Lendeborg and the Fightin' Golden State Warriors (8-6) into the friendly confines of American Airlines Center on Wednesday. Tip-off was recently moved from 8:30 p.m. to a more Mavs Moneyball-friendly 7 p.m., as God intended.

The Mavericks, of course, could have selected Lendeborg with the ninth overall pick of June's NBA Draft, but instead went with his college teammate at Michigan, Morez Johnson Jr., if you've been living under a rock and have not yet been bullied with that talking point. First-year head coach Dusty May had this to say about the start of Johnson's rookie season:

"I don't think Rez is happy with the way it's going. … The ball's not going in. But, you know, success is never linear, and it never happens the way we want it to. And there's no one that wants it more or has put in more to have it go well."

Meanwhile, Lendeborg, 24, is averaging 21.7 points per game for the Warriors playing a similar position.

And that was a freebie, so buckle up for three more things to watch out for when the Warriors come out to play in Dallas.

The bobblehead bump is more known and followed in baseball circles, but suffice to say we're grasping at anything in hopes that Flagg can bounce back from his current three-game funk. Perhaps the giveaway of a plasticine figurine with a spring-driven head to commemorate Flagg's Rookie of the Year award last year can do the trick. Or, perhaps just playing a team that ranks in the middle of the NBA pack in defensive rating (the Warriors are 13th through 14 games at 112.3) will provide Flagg a bit more room to maneuver than he saw against a ball-hawking Celtics defense on Sunday, when he shot just 5-of-15 from the field for 17 points in the Mavs' 131-94 loss.

Flagg is averaging just 15.7 points across his last three games and has shot a combined 16-of-48 (33.3%) from the floor. He sounded a little hoarse in his postgame press conference after the loss at the Celtics and admitted he had been under the weather for "a couple of days."

It will be interesting to see how much Lendeborg is called upon to defend Flagg — or how many times he gets switched onto Flagg — and what Flagg can do against the rookie.

Both of these teams come into Wednesday's game riding three-game losing streaks following impressive starts to the year. The Mavericks have one additional rest day in between games. Golden State has dropped its last three to the Houston Rockets, the San Antonio Spurs and the New Orleans Pelicans, while the Mavs come in on the heels of losses to the Minnesota Timberwolves and the Celtics, with a head-scratcher at the injury-riddled Cleveland Cavaliers sandwiched between them.

Both offenses have been held to fewer than 100 points in two of the three of those respective losses. Either one of these offenses is going to pull their team out of an early-season corner, or we've got a pile of unwatchable slop on our hands on Wednesday, it appears.

And speaking of corners, this seems an apt spot to call out that Wednesday's matchup features two of the top nine offenses in terms of shooting the corner 3-pointer. The Warriors come in sixth in the NBA, connecting on 39.5% of their corner 3-point attempts, while Dallas comes in ninth, at 38.3%.

Steph Curry will not be available for this one, mind you, as the 38-year-old star guard is set to be re-evaluated around Nov. 26 after a recent oblique tear. Neither team had submitted an initial injury report ahead of the game as of this writing late Tuesday afternoon.

Much was made of the Mavericks' intensity and effort after Sunday's brutal loss to the Celtics. "Compete" was the operative word in the postgame presser.

A fourth consecutive loss would not be a good look after the team has squandered some of the goodwill the Mavericks gathered during its 7-4 start to the year.

Plainly put, Wednesday's game against the Warriors is an early episode of nut-cuttin' time for this young team. Expect Flagg & Co. to respond accordingly.

The Mavericks and the Warriors tip off from American Airlines Center for Cooper Flagg Rookie of the Year Bobblehead night just after 7 p.m. on Wednesday. The local broadcast will be handled by KFAA Channel 29, with the game also airing on sister stations throughout the Mavs' regional viewership area. You can catch the stream on MavsTV or on NBA League Pass where available. The radio broadcast is on KRLD 105.3 The Fan.
