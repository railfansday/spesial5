Vance Joseph says Riley Moss has the ‘perfect personality’ to play opposite Pat Surtain II

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Now that Riley Moss is locked down long-term he will be Robin to Pat Surtain being Batman for years to come. That he has been able to hold up opposite of the best cornerback in the NFL is a testament to him. It's not easy being the guy every offense looks to exploit because the guy on the other side is just too good.

Defensive coordinator Vance Joseph touched on the extension and why Moss was so deserving of it.

"I'll start with this, he's always been very athletic," Joseph said on Thursday. "He's competitive. To play opposite of Pat Surtain, that's a tough job. Outside of Bo Nix on gameday, it's probably Riley. So that part he showed from Day One. Obviously, year-by-year as he played, he's playing cleaner football. He's played well this year. He's been nicked a little bit as far as missing some games, but he's playing good ball."

He went on to dig into what makes Moss such a vital long-term piece for Denver's defense.

"No one understands his gameday because Pat's out there at left corner, and they never look at Pat," Joseph explained. "They do sometimes, but mostly they don't. They're trying to find Riley. So that's a tough job. He's the perfect personality for that job. Most corners wouldn't have the resolve to kind of over, and over again just take 12 targets a game, and two penalties here and there. He's competing. As a player, if something bad happens, he can come back in five seconds. I'm still shook by it, but Riley [says], 'Coach, I'm good.' I'm like, 'Really?' That's a superpower. He is resilient, and he's one of us."

Those words 'perfect personality' stuck out to me in every way. And like VJ said, none of us can understand what his gameday is like. To be targeted over and over again and have to battle through the mistakes that any human would make if given enough tries to make one. Just heading VJ say those things has actually made my appreciation for what Moss goes through week in and week out grow a bit more.

More importantly, he has cleaned up aspects of his game. He still has a few penalties against him, but most of them are garbage calls anyway. What Riley Moss does for this defense cannot be understated. I'm glad they've got him locked in long-term.

Defensive Coordinator Vance Joseph praises Riley Moss for his resilience and toughness

On the day Riley Moss was extended, Defensive Coordinator Vance Joseph had glowing praise for the fourth-year cornerback.

Broncos aim to hit their stride at just the right time

The Broncos look to carry momentum from the bye and look to build on their success as the season enters the stretch run.

On CB Riley Moss's contract extension and the significance of keeping him in Denver long-term

"It's special. I think it speaks to the [Walton-Penner Family] Ownership [Group], the GM [General Manager George Paton] and the Head Coach [Sean Payton]. That's the program. That's the plan here to draft, develop and retain. So it's working to obviously maintain and get better each year. That's the key to this league. Keep your own, and find them early and pay them."

On maintaining a dominant defense year-to-year

"It's a standard. It's a standard of how you work, and how you gameplan and how you play on gameday. Having great depth obviously. We've had some injuries, and guys stepped up and played well for us. It's tough in this league to play really, really good year-to-year because teams obviously change. Once they get you on tape for three years, they kind of adjust to you. So making the right changes and keeping the core guys intact is key. We've lost some guys, but we're finding new guys and that's key also. For us this year, it's been more about the explosive plays. That's disappointing for me right now, but we're going to fix that. I'm looking forward to us playing better football because it hasn't been to my standard yet or our standard yet."

On S Devon Key making big plays on both defense and special teams against Carolina

"The first thing I'll say is I don't know if I've been a part now, this is 18 years from the NFL… Very few times I've seen a guy get a game ball in two areas in one game. So No. 1, that's a huge accomplishment and kudos to him for that. The thing that I want to say about Devon, probably the thing that's most impressive for me is that no matter how big his role is on defense, he does not want a diminished role on special teams. He is adamant about that with me. I know my role stepped up here, but I'm still doing 'X', 'Y' and 'Z'. I'm still doing these jobs. We do a good job of keeping the rep count, making sure it doesn't get too high or out of control during the game. I have a ton of respect for him. I'm not surprised at his success. I just watch his work ethic, his preparation. So to get an opportunity to make a play, again, not surprised just because I know the player, the football I.Q. and the work ethic. So really, really happy for him. An incredible accomplishment to get a game ball. I think officially he got credited for two, we had him for three tackles on special teams and plus the interception. Just a really, really great job by him."

On what the Raiders special teams unit is doing well

"They've really done a nice job. They got obviously a whole new staff. [Special Teams Coordinator] Joe DeCamillis, who's a name that's recognized around here. Obviously he's been a long time NFL special teams coordinator. I have a ton of respect for Joe. I've known Joe for a long time. Ironically, he was actually at Texas with my son a few years ago. So I think it starts with coaching. When you watch them on film, a huge emphasis in the phases. Then obviously the players, [Raiders RB] Dylan Laube's having a phenomenal year as a kick returner, kind of leads the league. It's not just the 95-yard touchdown. I feel like every time we watch the plays of the week, 40-yard return, 50-yard return, he's got these chunk plays. Downhill runner, really tough, breaks tackles. So he's done a really good job there. [P] AJ Cole's been one of the best punters in our league for the last five years or so. Not only is he punting the ball well, they're covering well. So Joe's done a really phenomenal job. I think [Head Coach] Klint [Kubiak], obviously I was with Klint in New Orleans. I think Klint has given Joe time to prepare, giving them the right pieces. So I think it comes down to a combination, but they're doing a couple things really, really well. They do some unique things as well, so we have to be prepared. Some things we haven't seen yet this year. So this is going to be a huge challenge for us. So kudos to them. I think this will be one of our toughest tests yet this year in this phase."

On Seahawks WR Tory Horton's kickoff return play last week against Las Vegas and when they review situational plays like that

"First of all, phenomenal heads-up play by them. Great coaching by the Seattle staff. It's actually that particular situation we had just covered two weeks earlier. It wasn't the tip back, but it was the time scenario. Obviously they're trying to keep one of the timeouts, trying to keep their opposition from getting the timeout there. So if you guys remember back last year, there was a play in the Chicago game. I think it was Week 1, a very similar play where the ball came out of the end zone and it kind of stole a timeout. So we keep hitting those. Now that we're seeing the ball in play 80 percent of the time, all these things are coming up. There was also a great play in the Washington game. Was it [Commanders WR Luke] McCaffrey, he made a really good play on the sideline where he got himself out of bounds, touched the ball. Those are huge plays. They're chunk plays because now the ball is at the 40 [-yard line] or you burn a timeout. So yes, situation. Our big day for that is Saturday. We kind of take about a half hour or so on Saturdays and review all those situations. So we'll certainly show those couple plays, but just constantly going through the game management part of it. I had a similar play a few years ago on a long field goal. [Former Dolphins WR] Jarvis Landry in Miami saved the field goal from going out of bounds and got a return. Now he didn't score. He got about a 30, 40 yarder. So I've seen that happen before. Then [former NFL RB] Cordarrelle Patterson, he was kind of famous for trying to keep the ball in play. A lot of people didn't realize you could bat the ball back forward and kind of keep it alive from going out of bounds. So now phenomenal play by Horton, phenomenal play by Seattle. We have our situational day on Saturdays where we kind of review all the things that could come up maybe once a season or once every five seasons, if you will."

On finding more efficiency in the run game

"It's a combination of a couple things. I think we can hit them with the variables and run with more conviction. We can do a better job of handling the fronts that we have, and dominating the five- and six-man boxes, and getting downhill, and being satisfied with five-, six-yard gains and getting a little bit more explosive after that. We've done a good job this year of not running too many negative runs. We haven't had negative one, twos. I know there were a couple last week, but for the most part of the last nine games, there haven't been many of those. We've been on track, but we can definitely do a better job of getting to second-and-5 as opposed to second-and-8 and second-and-9 and be a in a better position on second down."

On the offense putting up more points and the next step

"I think it's a combination of a few things. Our defense is doing a good job getting us the ball in short-field positions and us capitalizing. I think we've done a good job. Other than last week, I think there's a three week stretch where we were close to, if not 100 percent in red zone. So dominating that and getting touchdowns as opposed to three points. Those two combinations have been really good. I think we're playing complementary right now. Again the schedule's going to get a little tougher here pretty soon, and we have to continue to get going."

On how they can improve on third-and-long

"First of all, first and second down. Easy answer. When we are in those situations, I think it's important to get better in a hurry. That can be protection, that can be route depth, that can be trusting your eyes in the pocket. I just named three things. It's a combination. It really is. I think we're getting the plays in good enough, and we're doing a good job identifying pressures. However just kind of being 11 [players] as one a little bit better on seven-plus [yards] is important because we haven't been good enough there."

"I'll start by saying I'm just very thankful. I'm very thankful to be in Denver. I'm thankful for [Owner] Carrie [Walton Penner], and for [Owner & CEO] Greg [Penner], for [General Manager] George [Paton] and [Head Coach] Sean [Payton] believing in me. What they're building is really special, and I wanted to be a part of it, and I want to be a part of it as long as I can. It's filled with great people here in the facility. That's the biggest thing. They're always going after good people, and it's easy to build a good team around really good people. I want to thank them. I want to thank my parents because I wouldn't be where I'm at without them. They believed even when it got dark and sometimes I wouldn't believe. There are a lot of people I want to thank. My teammates especially, I wouldn't be able to do my job without them. I'll open it up to questions."

On why signing a contract extension now was the right move

"The biggest thing for me is fit, the people around me. I let my agent handle that stuff, and what he thought is best fit and best for me. I trust him. He's amazing. I'm glad we were able to get this done, but the biggest thing is not being satisfied. I was just talking to 'Court' (WR Courtland Sutton) in the weight room. Just talking about that feeling of it's really cool to get this money and stuff, but there's also work to be done. How do you manage feeling happy for yourself, but also knowing you have to go out and play well against the Raiders on Sunday? It's the 24-hour rule just like after a win. I'm going to enjoy it, but there's still a lot of work to do."

On the mentality that has allowed him to embrace the challenge of playing opposite of CB Pat Surtain II

"I think the biggest thing is it's how I was raised with my parents. The biggest thing was they let me fail so I would be able to figure out how to become successful through that, through that adversity. It's the reason I am who I am today. It's a hard job, but it's a rewarding job and I love it."

On the support of the Walton-Penner Family Ownership Group

"It's crazy because this is all I've ever known. I hear stories from some of my friends who are on other teams, and they say their training room isn't even close to what they had in college. That's crazy because here it's, 'Is it going to help us win?' Then they're going to try to invest and help us with that. That's why we've been able to grow so fast as a team and as a club is [Owner & CEO] Greg [Penner] and [Owner] Carrie [Walton Penner] really caring about us as players and creating that culture, that family culture."
