More Americans pay this Obama-era investment tax every year — how to avoid it

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Every year, more Americans pay an extra tax levy on their investment gains — but there's still time to reduce or avoid that bill for 2026, experts say.

Net investment income tax, or NIIT, is a 3.8% levy on investment income once earnings exceed $200,000 for single filers or $250,000 for married couples filing jointly. The tax was enacted in 2010 under President Barack Obama to help fund healthcare via the Affordable Care Act.

Unlike the federal income tax or capital gains brackets, the NIIT earnings limits don't adjust annually for inflation.

As a result, "more clients get hit every year," said certified financial planner Michael Walstedt, founding advisor of Reliant Wealth Advisory in Hoboken, New Jersey.

Roughly 5.6% of tax returns included NIIT for 2025, according to IRS data released in November for returns processed through mid-July 2026. That's up from about 3.7% for the same time frame in 2021.

As more investors pay NIIT, here are some key things to know.

NIIT applies when you have investment income and your earnings exceed the modified adjusted gross income, or MAGI, limits, according to certified financial planner Marianela Collado, CEO of Tobias Financial Advisors in Plantation, Florida.

"The trick is to keep an eye on both of these numbers," said Collado, who is also a certified public accountant and member of CNBC's Financial Advisor Council.

For the NIIT earnings limit, MAGI is typically the same as adjusted gross income, or AGI. One exception would be if you have foreign income, which changes the calculation. You can find last year's AGI on line 11a of your 2025 tax return.

Head of household (with qualifying person): $200,000

Qualifying widow(er) with dependent child: $250,000

NIIT applies to investment income, which includes interest, dividends, annuities, royalties, certain rents, and more. It can also include capital gains from a primary home sale, for profits that exceed the IRS exclusions. The exclusion is $250,000 for single filers or $500,000 for married couples.

The levy stacks on top of regular income tax rates, or capital gains tax — which can be 0%, 15% or 20%, depending on earnings. That means higher earners can pay up to 23.8% on investment income, including long-term capital gains and NIIT.

For example, if your MAGI is $30,000 above the NIIT earnings limit and you have $100,000 of investment income, you would pay 3.8% of $30,000, or $1,140.

Alternatively, if your MAGI is $120,000 above the threshold and you have $100,000 of investment income, you would owe 3.8% of $100,000, or $3,800.

As year-end approaches, investors should run projections to estimate their adjusted gross income, which can impact NIIT, among other tax consequences, experts say.

If you're above the NIIT limits, you could reduce AGI by boosting pretax 401(k) deferrals or health savings account contributions, according to Collado with Tobias Financial Advisors.

While employee 401(k) deferrals must happen by Dec. 31, you have until next year's tax deadline for health savings account contributions for 2026.

You might also try to avoid selling assets before year-end, which could further boost AGI, experts say. Of course, any current-year tax moves should consider your long-term financial goals.

Amid recent stock market volatility, investors may also consider so-called tax-loss harvesting, which uses losses to offset other portfolio gains, Walstedt with Reliant Wealth Advisory said.

If your losses exceed profits, you can subtract up to $3,000 from regular income. After that, you can carry remaining losses into future tax years.

However, if you're planning to reinvest the sale proceeds, you need to know the "wash sale rule," which blocks the tax-loss harvesting tax break for buying a "substantially identical" asset within 30 days before or after the sale.
