Gabriel Moreno wins Silver Slugger

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Well, it probably comes as not much of a surprise. But Gabriel Moreno won the National League Silver Slugger award for catcher, becoming the first Diamondback ever to win the award at that position. Moreno won the NL batting title, hitting .311, but also hit sixteen home-runs, driving in 83, for an overall line of .311/.388/.486. That's an OPS of .874, an improvement of eighty-eight points over last season – which was already a career high. The resulting OPS+ of 143 was easily the best by any regular catcher – not just in the NL, but the major leagues as a whole. It left Moreno sixteen points above anyone else.

In Diamondbacks' history, it was also the best offensive season by a catcher, by an even wider margin. The previous mark was held by Miguel Montero, who posted an OPS+ of 123, fourteen years ago. Of course, Gaby's season all-round was almost impeccable, with Moreno already having won a Gold Glove. That actually happens more often than you might think though. Ivan Rodriguez managed that double-bill of Silver Slugger and Gold Glove seven times in his career, including a remarkable six times in a row, from 1994 through 1999. Though if I'm not mistaken, this year is the first time each league has had the same guy win both, with Dillon Dingler of the Tigers doing it in the AL.

This was definitely the year that Moreno put it all together. He not only contributed massively on both sides of the ball, he was also able to remain relatively healthy, appearing in a total of 130 games. As we discussed previously, his 2026 performance is going to make it extremely difficult for the D-backs to extend the catcher, as he heads towards free-agency. It's more likely he'll reach free agency and cash in, so I strongly recommend that you enjoy watching Gabby over the next couple of seasons, as he looks to cement his place as, arguably, the best catcher in Diamondbacks history. Thanks, Toronto!

As an aside, Corbin Carroll was also one of the NL finalists, in the outfield, but did not win. The three awards there went to MVP Pete Crow-Armstrong of the Cubs, Jackson Chourio from Milwaukee, and the Nationals' James Wood.
