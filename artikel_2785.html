Mikal Bridges is finding his groove at the right time

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Remember when we all said we wouldn't care about the regular season that much after the championship? That lasted long…

As the Knicks stumbled out to a 0-3 start that didn't seem to stabilize that much until this four-game winning streak that's brought them to 10-6 ahead of tonight's game against Indiana, fans didn't seem to have the patience they promised after this same group showed they can be trusted to step up when it counts.

At the center of that impatience was Mikal Bridges, who, despite putting up a second straight awesome playoff run and fully validating the trade by helping hoist a Larry O'Brien Trophy, was the common target early on as he struggled to find his groove.

Through 11 games, he was averaging just 5.9 points a game on a rough 32.9% from the field and 26.5% from deep. He'd be benched early in games where he didn't have it for Landry Shamet, he looked even more apprehensive than usual, and his defense wasn't as impactful as usual.

We've seen bad stretches from Bridges plenty over the last two years, but this was just maddening. The ring was supposed to free him; it was supposed to allow him to tap into the best of his game for extended stretches. The weight was off his shoulders.

Instead, it just seemed to get heavier? At times, it looked like the yips, basketball edition.

But this is why you exercise patience. These guys are champions; they know how to manage the highs and lows. When they look lost, they'll figure it out.

Body language is a big deal sometimes. You started to see him break out of his shell against the Pacers, where he looked for his shot early and often. When his jumper failed him, he got to the rim. He wasn't getting to the line, but he was attacking gaps and making good reads. The activity was back defensively.

He didn't seem to carry this over into Toronto a few days later, but a pep talk from Jose Alvarado might've done the trick:

Suddenly, he was everywhere defensively. A switch seemed to flip, and it looked like he was back in that Boston series. Needing a big late comeback, he set the tone defensively. Everything he did was purposeful, as he was a +15 in the overtime victory.

The offense came back around two nights later when the Knicks really needed it. Despite being -14 in 30 minutes, he put up an efficient 17 points.

The confidence to take shots like these just wasn't there a few weeks ago. He'd miss one or two threes and just stop shooting. He'd subconsciously pass up shots that had been a part of his game for years. It's pivotal that even when the ball isn't going down that a shooter keeps shooting, and we just didn't see that.

This article comes at a great time after he called game against the Blazers on Saturday night, but I think it shows that he's got his confidence back more than anything. Watch back the tape and see if you notice anything:

First, it's the fact that he consciously looks for the ball off the inbound. A big problem with the Knicks' final-possession offense is that they have one look and no backup plan. We saw that against Indiana with KAT and we see it all the time where they just screen for Jalen Brunson to get the ball and get out of the way.

Here, Bridges goes from a screener to releasing to pop up as a backup option. Then, look at this screenshot:

There are three whole seconds left, and Toumani Camara is on a hard closeout. There is nobody near Andre Drummond underneath the basket. If Bridges immediately hits him, it's an easy dunk (or is it, with how bad his finishing has been…)

In some ways, that's the correct read. A broken inbound has left the opponent scrambling and you have an easy, game-tying bucket under the basket. Maybe if he didn't make the shot, this would be discussed more.

Regardless, the confidence of Bridges matters a whole helluva lot more than winning a regular season game. This is a guy you need in April, May, and June, not November. A 26-point blown lead sucks, blowing the first 15+-point second half lead in the regular season since April 2023 sucks, but you want him taking this shot.

Maybe he just doesn't like the Blazers. That's two game-winners against them in his three years as a Knick.

Now, if only Josh Hart can figure it out too…
