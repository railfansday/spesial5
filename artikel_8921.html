Kelly Wearstler Studio and ESG Architecture Lead Transformation of Minoru Yamasaki's Minneapolis Building

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The proposed conversion of Minoru Yamasaki's former Northwestern National Life Insurance Company headquarters in Minneapolis has advanced with the announcement of its design team, hotel operator, and expanded program. First presented in April 2026, the adaptive reuse project will transform the 1965 building at 20 Washington Avenue South into Minneapolis Proper, a 165-room hotel operated by Proper Hospitality. The project is being developed by Minneapolis-based CDT Realty Corp, with ESG Architecture & Design serving as architect and Kelly Wearstler Studio leading the creative direction. Pending historic approvals, construction is expected to begin in fall 2027, with completion targeted for summer 2029.

Pending historic approvals, construction is expected to begin in fall 2027, with completion targeted for summer 2029.

The conversion will introduce a hospitality program across more than 200,000 square feet and nine levels, incorporating guest rooms, restaurants, bars, wellness facilities, and event spaces. The plans include a rooftop restaurant and lounge, a ballroom with an outdoor terrace, and a 15,000-square-foot subterranean spa and indoor pool. At ground level, a restaurant and fitness facilities will be introduced, while an underground bar will have a separate entrance connected to the Minneapolis Skyway, the city's elevated pedestrian network. The program also includes retail space intended to feature products made in Minnesota.

According to the development team, the existing structure and layout are considered suitable for the conversion, allowing much of the original building fabric to be retained while accommodating the new program. The proposal identifies the preservation of the building's defining architectural features as a central consideration, although detailed information about the proposed interventions and the reorganization of its interior spaces has not yet been released.

The building was completed in 1965 as the headquarters of Northwestern National Life Insurance Company and remained in use under different corporate identities before becoming vacant in 2023. Designed by Minoru Yamasaki, the architect behind the original World Trade Center in New York, it is among his notable works from the 1960s. Its architectural composition, marked by an expansive colonnade, reflecting pools, and landscaped grounds, reflects Yamasaki's approach to combining monumental forms with carefully articulated public spaces. Despite its visual openness and relationship with the surrounding landscape, the building primarily functioned as a private corporate environment.
