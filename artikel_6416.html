Something Enormous Is Buried Beneath Egypt’s Mound of the Pharaohs. We Could Only See It From Space.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Radar from orbit and electric currents in the ground revealed a 2,600-year-old mystery.

The Egyptian archaeological site of Buto (a Greek translation of the cobra goddess Wadjet), now called Tell el-Fara’in or Mound of the Pharaohs, is already known for giving up ancient secrets. An astronomical observatory was previously unearthed at the site of what was once the Temple of Buto. Now, instead of discovering something meant for looking up at the stars, structures at Buto were recently found by satellite technology looking down from space.

Satellites detected hidden structures beneath the existing ruins of Buto, at the Kom C mound. Radar imagery from above was merged with electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) to reveal sprawling architecture dating back to Egypt’s 26th Dynasty. ERT is ideal for investigating suspected archaeological ruins without disturbing the ground until researchers have absolute certainty there’s something worth excavating. Because different subsurface materials—rock, soil, water, and brick—conduct electricity differently, pushing a low-voltage current through the ground and measuring the resistance reveals what lies beneath. Levels of resistivity show what kind of material is located at a certain level underground. That’s how an interdisciplinary team of researchers made the discovery without picking up a chisel.

“Overall, this study focuses on applying—rather than developing—methods using Sentinel-1 SAR imagery in combination with ERT measurements and archaeological excavation to delineate buried remains,” they said in a study recently published in Acta Geophysica. “The integrated approach aims to reconstruct settlement phases and identify subsurface architectural features across the site.”

The history of the Buto site reaches back 5,000 years to the Predynastic period, evolving all the way through the Early Islamic era. Thick layers of clay and silt obscured some of the early era’s remains, which made it uncertain whether it was worth excavating those areas. Some layers were even below groundwater level and would have complicated the excavation project. So with ESA’s Sentinel-1 satellite as their eyes, the researchers searched for patterns deep in the ground that could indicate the existence of buried structures. The Sentinel-1 mission consists of two satellites that can observe shallow layers of Earth, and their imagery confirmed it was worth investigating further. What the researchers found was an oval-shaped anomaly around 420 feet long (128 meters) by 203 feet wide (62 meters).

They employed the ERT to investigate further, using a geophysical survey that involved laying down parallel survey lines across more than 58,100 square feet (5,400 square meters) along which hundreds of metal electrodes were pushed into the ground and connected to an electrode measuring system. Voltage differences from electrical currents hitting different materials slowly started to put together the image of a site that had been underground for thousands of years. Fragments of pottery, mudbricks, and other artifacts in the upper layers were left behind during the Ptolemaic period and Roman occupation. The most significant find was between three and six meters (from 10 to nearly 20 feet) deep. It was an entire mudbrick structure that survived from the Saite Period.

After the invading Assyrians withdrew around 664 B.C.E., the 26th Dynasty dawned when Saite kings, beginning with Psamtik I, reclaimed the Egyptian throne. Egypt was no longer a vassal, but it was struggling to avoid another takeover. While it had lost most of its former power and glory, Egypt had kept its undying cultural heritage, which became visible in the art and architecture of the period. Egyptian builders were aware of how the flooding of the Nile could affect their structures, which is why a layer of sand was packed beneath stone remains as a foundation. The researchers are still unsure of the purpose of the structure they discovered. It may have been a temple, priestly house, or funerary complex. Religious artifacts and amulets suggest it was a secondary temple.

When archaeologists actually excavated, they found an amulet of a chimera with features of a baboon, falcon, and Pataikos-dwarf, a minor deity whose amulets were usually carved out of wood and gilded. A limestone amulet of the goddess Wadjet also emerged, along with an offering basin. Amulets depicting many different deities were left in this basin, including wedjat eyes that represent the healed Eye of Horus, Anubis, Isis nursing Horus, a bronze amulet of Horus as a child, and Bes, a god of war and revelry with humanoid and feline features. A faience plaque depicted the goddess Hathor on both of its sides. She was a deity who ruled over love and beauty much like the Greek Aphrodite, and also music, dancing, pleasure, and fertility. This might explain the fragmented limestone statues found nearby, which appear to be of figures in sexual positions. Also found was a soapstone scarab of King Thutmose III.

“Future research at Buto should expand ERT surveys to other areas with larger electrode spacing, aiming to better understand the site’s deeper occupation phases and environmental context,” said the researchers. “Our findings suggest that, in addition to the known temple, another temple may remain buried under a thick clay layer, which will be investigated through extensive measurements in forthcoming studies.”
