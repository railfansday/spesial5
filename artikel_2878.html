Fantasy Football Rankings: Hayden Winks' WR Blueprint for Week 9

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The Blueprint is my weekly column and basically everything that I use to build out the most accurate rankings I can. There will be an RB column, this WR column and a combination column on the onesie positions. You will find film notes and endless charts. Maybe enough charts to stress test the Yahoo website. I kid.

It's impossible to get every ranking thoroughly early in the week, so be sure to keep checking in on these columns through Sunday morning. As always, my full rankings at every position are available.

1. Puka Nacua (at WAS) - 92.5 proj. receiving yards

The Rams offense has really changed recently. In two games without Davante Adams, Nacua's average depth of target is just 3.4 yards downfield. Everything is shallow. It's fine in fantasy because he will rack up catches with ease, and he can power through tackles for first downs. But it does make it a bit harder for the offense to be a threat for 35+ points like they were in previous versions. Nacua is the WR3 on WR1 usage and has a 34% target share since returning to the lineup in Week 4. He's up to 41% targets (!!!) without Adams.

The Commanders have allowed the seventh-most passing yards per game with one of the most underwhelming CB rooms. They'll muck things up in a Diet Coke version of the Brian Flores defense, but that fits with the Rams' quick-hitting approach on offense right now. Los Angeles enters as 5.5-point road favorites with a 26.5-point team total (eighth).

2. Jaxon Smith-Njigba (vs. ARI) - 94.5 proj. receiving yards

JSN has 19.3 half PPR points or more in all but one game, as the clear-cut WR1 overall in fantasy. He's had big games with Drew Lock before, including in Week 2 against these Cardinals, when he had 38.0 half PPR points on 155 yards and three scores.

3. CeeDee Lamb (at IND) - 83.5 proj. receiving yards

Lamb is the WR3 on WR4 usage this season, and the Colts are giving up on defense. Dallas heads into the matchup as 6-point road favorites with a 28.75-point team total (2nd).

4. Ja'Marr Chase (at ATL) - 76.5 proj. receiving yards

Chase is the WR4 on WR9 usage if we remove his Week 4 injury game. Nothing has changed with this offense, aside from now having one of the best RTs in the NFL, Amarius Mims.

5. Tee Higgins (at ATL) - 77.5 proj. receiving yards

Higgins is the WR4 on WR13 usage this season. He's been the WR1, WR2, WR71, WR7, WR8, WR19 and WR39 this season. The Bengals abandoned their under-center pass game officially and will let Joe Burrow cook as he's done since LSU.

6. Drake London (vs. CIN) - 78.5 proj. receiving yards

Last week's Michael Penix Jr. outing was concerning because of his poor decision-making, but the conditions won't ever be that bad again. It's not a complete throw-out-the-window game, but it's close to it. Before that, London had been averaging fantasy WR1 numbers with Penix dating back to late 2024.

The matchup with the Bengals is pass-first, especially with star CB1 DJ Turner (ankle) not practicing. Cincy's LB play has been amongst the worst in the NFL, and London can hammer teams on quick in cuts, something the Falcons have leaned on more recently. It's a great matchup for London, with Atlanta projected to lose while still owning a quality 24.75-point team total (eighth).

7. Amon-Ra St. Brown (at MIA) - 76.5 proj. receiving yards

St. Brown ranks as the WR16 in half PPR per-game output on WR6 expected-points usage across his last four appearances. He has remained steady with 10.3 targets per game and a 32% target share in that frame. There's no reason to be worried about his recent duds.

Miami ranks 30th in half PPR points allowed to receivers, but Detroit enters as seven-point road favorites sporting a 28.75-point team total (third) and some of their pass-game production allowed has been to TEs in similar areas of the field the Sun God orbits.

8. Malik Nabers (at PHI) - 72.5 proj. receiving yards

Similar receivers to Nabers have been balling out against the Eagles; Parker Washington (WR3), Jalen Coker (97 yards), CeeDee Lamb (WR5) and Stefon Diggs (WR23).

9. Justin Jefferson (vs. BUF) - 77.5 proj. receiving yards

I broke down the Vikings' pass game in detail in another column, but it really comes down to Kyler Murray, Kevin O'Connell and Jordan Addison splitting a lot of the blame for Jefferson's falloff. Over his past 17 games, he's averaging just 50 YPG. It was at 96 YPG before them. This year, Jefferson is the WR18 on WR31 usage, while seeing a lot of bracket coverage. The Lions played outside leverage at CB with a safety on the inside for a lot of the game, and Jefferson was left unprotected from it by the staff. A few inaccuracies and turn-downs from Kyler played into a mediocre game in a great matchup. That said, Jefferson still had a huge red-zone catch, was thrown the ball underneath and Murray's stats are at a career low point. They would likely to get better with more time on task, but now Murray is week-to-week with a hamstring injury. Carson Wentz will start on MNF. I don't know if the Vikings' No. 25 neutral pass rate will change, however. Jefferson is clearly more of a fantasy WR2 than WR1.

He draws an exploitable matchup against a Bills defense that ranks fourth in half PPR points allowed per game to opposing WR rooms (31.1). Buffalo allowed 29.2 points to Puka Nacua and 24.2 points to Tre Tucker in recent weeks. The Vikings are 3.5-point home underdogs carrying an implied team total of 21.5 points (19th).

10. Chris Olave (vs. CLE) - 71.5 proj. receiving yards

Update: Jordyn Tyson (hamstring) is making his season debut and has looked good at practice according to reports. More surprising and arguably more impactful is the return of LT Kelvin Banks Jr. (knee). The Saints are up to a 22.75-point team total against the man-coverage Browns. Hopefully Tyler Shough's hand is feeling better out of the bye. Olave had dropped to the WR22 on WR24 usage over the past month, despite still seeing a 24% target share and 91 air yards per game. Olave will see plenty of CB1 Denzel Ward.

11. Christian Watson (at NE) - 73.5 proj. receiving yards

A quick trip to the hospital couldn't keep Watson off the field for long last week. He's been the WR6 on WR11 usage this season, and nothing about the offense is set to change. Even the Packers' increased neutral pass rate has held sticky with the return of Josh Jacobs. Watson may not run as hot as he did in September moving forward, but the spike week potential is very obvious.

Unfortunately, Watson will see the tandem of CBs Christian Gonzalez and Carlton Davis. They have the size and speed to match Watson, though Luther Burdan and Omar Cooper Jr. just topped 100 yards in recent games. The Packers only have a 20.5-point team total (23rd), so Watson looks like more of a boom-bust fantasy WR2 this time out.

12. Rashee Rice (vs. NYJ) - 59.5 proj. receiving yards

Rice is the WR21 on WR39 usage, a 19% target share and 6.3 targets per game in his four healthy games. He's finished between WR12 and WR32 in all of them, though he's running pretty pure in terms of efficiency with a sprinkling of broken plays. The good news for him is the Xavier Worthy threat has closed based on his tape and Travis Kelce's YAC greatness has stalled. Rice is back to being needed, even if Kenneth Walker III's presence is going to knock Rice down from his pre-2026 peak.

The matchup is a great one. The Jets have been particularly weak against shallow WRs, allowing the eighth-most production on throws within 10 air yards. Jalen Nailor snuck by with a WR20 finish last week, while role players Caleb Douglas (WR8) and Pop Douglas (WR10) balled out in the games prior. A monster 28.75-point team total has Rice's projection looking the best it has all year.

13. Nico Collins (at LAC) - 68.5 proj. receiving yards

Collins ranks as the WR19 in per-game half PPR scoring on WR30 expected-points usage over his past four appearances. His recent stretch was keyed by a Week 4 masterclass against Dallas (WR3 overall), but it's tailed off since then while the Texans have limited his snap counts with more help in the offense. Over this four-game sample, he has averaged 6.3 targets and 79.0 air yards on a 21% target share. It boggles the mind that this isn't higher. Hopefully the bye week was enough time for them to figure some things out — like spamming targets to him.

The Chargers rank 17th in half PPR points allowed to wide receivers at 25.3 per game. While they have been vulnerable overall, they have yielded just 5.5 points per game on targets beyond 15 air yards (31st), which poses a test for Collins' deep-target role. Houston enters as 4.5-point road favorites with an implied team total of 22.5 points (16th). Los Angeles hasn't had SCB Derwin James.

14. Matthew Golden (at NE) - 67.5 proj. receiving yards

The downfield shot variance will be a sight to see with Golden, especially given the degree of difficulty on some of these big shots. Over the past month, Golden is the WR8 on WR3 usage. He has seen exactly 12 targets in three-straight outings, averaging 158 air yards per game and a 29% target share across this recent four-game window. That's incredible stuff for someone who can also take an underneath pass for a long touchdown, too.

The matchup is a tough one. He'll battle with bigger CBs Christian Gonzalez and Carlton Davis this week, and the Packers are equipped with a 20.5-point team total on the road. That's lower than normal.

15. Luther Burden III (vs. TB) - 65.5 proj. receiving yards

Burden played three of 21 snaps in heavy personnel. When the Bears established a lead, they went away from 3-WR sets, which is partially explaining the massive splits between halves for Burden this season. He's the WR3 in yards per route in the first half and the WR61 in the second half. When Johnson hasn't isolated him on a first read, the results are worse. That's the reality of a physically dominant yet in-development player. Burden is the WR24 on WR7 usage in games with Caleb this year, so it's no reason to really panic. He's an upside WR2/3 despite the obvious inconsistency.

16. Parker Washington (at BAL) - 60.5 proj. receiving yards

Washington is extremely volatile. On average, he's the WR20 on WR23 usage. The last time he bottomed out, Washington was the WR3 overall the following week. He's not doing anything wrong. In fact, he's the WR15 in yards per route.

This past week, the Jaguars had five explosives through the air. All of them were to TEs and RBs. That's the magic of Liam Coen's scheme, but when those plays are working and the Colts couldn't move the ball on the other side, then there aren't enough dropbacks to go around. Washington is arguably better in more difficult matchups.

The matchup against Baltimore offers a bounce-back opportunity, as the Ravens rank ninth in half PPR points allowed to WRs at 28.6 per game. Baltimore has struggled against intermediate passing in particular, ranking 10th in points allowed on targets between five and 20 air yards, where Washington has logged 30 targets this season. Jacksonville enters as 8.5-point road favorites with an implied team total of 25.5 points (ninth).

17. George Pickens (at IND) - 62.5 proj. receiving yards

Pickens had his second-lowest usage of the season last week, despite an easy matchup with the Cardinals that even went to overtime. Pickens only played 10 of 28 snaps with more than one TE on the field, allowing Ryan Flournoy to score a red-zone touchdown in a 3-TE set. These are things on the margins that will prevent Pickens from reaching top-15 status while CeeDee Lamb is healthy. Pickens will need to win his difficult assignments as the static X receiver in 3-WR sets. Dak Prescott is talented enough for spike weeks, but the consistency will be painful. Pickens is the WR28 on WR18 usage this season. He's firmly a boom-bust WR2, and I'd understand the Cowboys listening to trade offers for his expiring contract now that they trust Flournoy.

Pickens will battle with Sauce Gardner on the boundary most likely, but the rest of the defense is so overmatched here. The Jaguars moved the ball at will last week with DT DeForest Buckner gone. A monster 28.75-point team total (second) keeps the Pickens' ceiling intact.

18. Michael Wilson (at SEA) - 48.5 proj. receiving yards

I guess I appreciate Wilson's game way more than everyone else. His style is similar to Puka Nacua, although Wilson tends to be a little more out of control compared to the All Pro. How they're used and how physical they are is nearly identical. There have been too many misses by the QBs and too many DPIs/drops to treat him as a set-and-forget WR1, but Wilson is the WR5 in expected half PPR points this season.

He's no longer competing with WRs Marvin Harrison Jr., Kendrick Bourne, Jalen Brooks or Reggie Virgil for targets, and both Jeremiyah Love and Bam Knight also went down with injuries in Week 8. Wilson ended that contest as the WR4 on WR1 overall usage (30.2 expected half PPR points). Have we reached too big to fail? I think so. I'm buying, assuming his two broken ribs aren't going to derail his own season.

The matchup is obviously daunting. The Seahawks have allowed the fewest fantasy points per game to WRs, and they've been elite at stopping big plays over the middle and down the field, where Wilson has done a chunk of his damage. The Cardinals only have a 14.5-point team total with Gardner Minshew walking into an impossible task. Spamming underneath targets to Wilson has to be a big part of the plan.

19. Jameson Williams (at MIA) - 52.5 proj. receiving yards

The key with Jamo is to consistently rank him between WR15 and WR25 whether he's been producing in fantasy recently or not. The matchup is obviously clean, with a 28.0-point team total against the Dolphins. Miami has the youngest defense in the NFL.

20. Tetairoa McMillan (vs. DEN) - 62.5 proj. receiving yards

The on/off splits with Jalen Coker from 2025 on are pretty glaring.

Beyond that, Bryce Young is particularly sensitive to his schedule and OL and both are getting worse moving forward. Since Coker returned in Week 6, McMillan is the WR38 on WR17 usage. He'll play better than he has over that stretch, but gone are the days of top-15 status.

Carolina brings an extreme passing approach, ranking third in neutral pass rate at 62% across 203 neutral plays. They will test a Broncos defense that has been stingy against wideouts, ranking 28th in half PPR points allowed to WRs (22.1 per game). Denver has yielded 21.5 or fewer points to the position in three of their last four games, with only Seattle cracking them for 38.0 points in Week 6. The Panthers enter as 3-point home underdogs with an implied team total of 20.25 points (25th).

21. Zay Flowers (vs. JAX) - 48.5 proj. receiving yards

Over the past two years, Flowers has averaged 11.6 half PPR points without Lamar Jackson. That does not include last week's goose egg, nor does it adjust for Tyler Huntley (turf toe) out for the season. Over the past month with backups, Flowers is the WR22 on WR23 usage and that's probably closer to his ceiling than his baseline with Joe Fagnano under center on a short week.

Fagnano is a big statue QB who made more plays than I thought he did on tape. There was a ton of bad luck involved on his throws to Flowers, who had a drop on a potential YAC opportunity over the middle. Another pass to Flowers was knocked down by an EDGE and another third-down rep by Flowers resulted in him falling down instead of being a first-read target.

The challenge with the matchup is the Jaguars have been very good at stopping the short pass, limiting production on throws to WRs under 10 air yards to a No. 29 ranking. Some of that is having Travis Hunter on these quick in-breakers, and that size matchup wouldn't make me feel great about Flowers here. Where they've been weaker is on deep throws, and we'll see if Fagnano can push the ball against these dynamic edge rushers. It's certainly asking for a lot. The Ravens have a sub-15.0 team total here. Flowers is a desperation WR2/3.

22. DJ Moore (at MIN) - 47.5 proj. receiving yards

Week 8 was extremely encouraging for Moore. He was the WR13 on WR9 usage, and the scheme choices were great. His TD came from the slot in the high red zone on a run-away route, which suits his game best down there. He had another slot crosser, too. They connected on a deep play-action post against Marlon Humphrey, and then tested the veteran safety on a slant from the five-yard line. Moore is getting used like the most important player in the pass game, one that sits here over the past three years:

The matchup is obviously horrific with the Vikings shutting down everyone, including the Lions' pass game last week. They do really shut down TEs, however, so the Bills could lean on Moore and James Cook III a bit more than others have. Allen is also better equipped to deal with the pressure they bring than other statue quarterbacks. It's still a bad matchup, one worth knocking Moore down some, but I'm less sure Moore will be the one specifically hurt most by this.

23. Stefon Diggs (vs. LAR) - 41.5 proj. receiving yards

In three games without Terry McLaurin (IR), Diggs is the WR30 on WR24 usage. His role isn't going to change in terms of snaps or alignment, but the Commanders may pass more without McLaurin helping to establish a lead and first-read targets are easier to justify to Diggs without their top target.

On tape, Diggs is playing this crafty slot/underneath role in ways as Travis Kelce plays it. He'll find soft spots in zone and out of structure. He's a clear red-zone threat with space getting tight. He's also not creating a ton of separation at the top of his routes in man coverage. That's okay. Diggs had 9.8 expected half PPR points (WR24) last week in a decisive win. He's a fantasy WR3 right now, while Antonio Williams is starting to break out in a full-time role.

The Rams rank 26th in half PPR points allowed to WRs at 22.4 per game, and teams routinely tilt away from passing against them, as their 47% neutral pass rate allowed ranks 30th (third-lowest). Diggs operates primarily in the short and intermediate areas, with 70% of his targets coming under 10 air yards and 57% between five and 20 yards, while the Rams sit 14th against intermediate WRs and 24th against short WRs. Washington enters as 5.5-point home underdogs with an implied team total of 21 points (23rd).

24. Jalen Coker (vs. DEN) - 53.5 proj. receiving yards

25. Jaylen Waddle (at CAR) - 47.5 proj. receiving yards

26. Emeka Egbuka (at CHI) - 52.5 proj. receiving yards

27. Antonio Williams (vs. LAR) - 41.5 proj. receiving yards

Williams had his second-best game in terms of fantasy points (WR38) and expected points (WR32) in his first full game without Terry McLaurin, who is out for the year. He played on 31 of 35 dropbacks and is firmly in the WR4 mix. He had an awesome slot go ball, a signature route for Jayden Daniels. Williams beat LBs to the sticks on an out route and a zone sit route, then caught a shallow crosser and an in-motion quick out to round out his day. A 26% target share in this competent offense is a strong signal to start his post-bye rookie bump.

28. Adonai Mitchell (at KC) - 48.5 proj. receiving yards

Update: Garrett Wilson (concussion) is out. That's a huge deal.

Geno Smith and Mitchell were absolutely balling in Week 8. There were three incredible timing, deep out routes that were on the money in addition to his red-zone fade ball TD and his multiple post targets. One of those posts was caught, while a 46-yard one really should've been caught by Mitchell. It was the best Geno Smith game in a very long time. That is a good sign.

Here are target shares in Weeks 1, 7 and 8 when all of the top-3 WRs were active: Wilson (29%), Mitchell (16%) and Omar Cooper Jr. (6%). I can guarantee the first-rounder's target share won't stay that low, so we're looking at a real consolidation up top. Mitchell would do well to hold onto this WR31 on WR34 per-game usage start to his season, but it's clear that his downfield role will lead to big games.

The Chiefs present a difficult draw, ranking 31st in half PPR points per game allowed to wideouts. Kansas City is stingy across all depths, sitting 27th against deep targets where Mitchell has logged a 44% target share with a 17.6-yard average depth of target. The Jets enter as 9.5-point road underdogs with an implied team total of 18.5 points (26th).

29. Omar Cooper Jr. (at KC) - 49.5 proj. receiving yards

Update: Garrett Wilson (concussion) is out. That's a huge deal.

Cooper has a 13% target share and WR60 usage since returning in Week 6. That's the reality when there are this many quality skill guys on one team. Cooper is capable of random spike weeks because of his speed, but there is literally no floor. On tape last week, Cooper ran a lot of vertical routes out of the slot. He did not look bad at all, nor did he face that much man coverage. The ball was just out earlier to their outside targets. There were some crossing routes that could've been explosives had he been targeted, but Geno Smith was balling on sideline throws and posts. It is what it is. I'd brace for outside the top-50 finishes more times than not.

Week 9 presents a stiff test against Kansas City, which ranks 31st in half PPR points allowed to wide receivers. The Chiefs have surrendered very little through the air and allow offenses to pass against them at just a 47% neutral clip (29th). Opposing receiver rooms have regularly been bottled up, with only Jaxon Smith-Njigba's Week 7 outing clearing 60 yards against them across their past four games. The Jets head to Arrowhead as 9.5-point road underdogs with an implied total of 18.5 points (26th).

31. Rome Odunze (vs. TB) - 50.5 proj. receiving yards

Odunze is WR3 in catches of 15+ yards (19). He's also the only WR with 11+ explosive catches to not have a TD on them. That'll change soon. Odunze is the WR46 on WR51 usage since Williams has returned, and the signs are there for a bigger end of the season as a boom-bust WR3.

The matchup will be a battle. The Buccaneers have been great at limiting downfield shots against them, and they have size at CB1 with Zyon McCollum. In total, Tampa is allowing the 27th-most points to the position per game, recently shutting down the Panthers WR duo and Drake London. George Pickens did hit them for a WR5 finish in Week 5, but they've tightened things up since then. The saving grace is the Bears still project for 26.75 points, and the Bucs are allowing the second-fewest rushing yards per game.

32. Tre Tucker (at SF) - 44.5 proj. receiving yards

Bombs away when projected to lose by 9.5 points. Hopefully he comes down with his downfield shots this time around.

33. Ladd McConkey (vs. HOU) - 48.5 proj. receiving yards

McConkey survived in Week 8 because of a fluky 54-yard Hail Mary reception going into halftime that, of course, did not actually score a touchdown. Aside from that, McConkey turned 35 other routes into just four targets (11%), and 3-of-4 were screen variants behind the line of scrimmage. He dropped one of them. His other target wasn't completely out of structure, but it was a chore to complete the ball to him and he slipped after the catch. McConkey hasn't looked like the lightning-quick receiver he was as a rookie so far, and the Chargers were once again the worst offense in success rate (28%) in Week 8 off the bye. McConkey has been the WR39, WR53 and WR9 in expected half PPR points in his three healthiest games. There's a volatile WR3 case for him after 92% routes last week.

34. Denzel Boston (at NO) - 43.5 proj. receiving yards

The swings from Boston have been crazy, but they're to be expected in a crowded offense with low-end QB play. It'll be Shedeur Sanders this time around, and that is likely a downgrade. To Sanders' credit, he is willing to throw it downfield and he might be a better stylistic fit for Boston than others in this offense. I tried to nail the trends in the pecking order this week:

The Saints haven't landed on CBs and Ss they fully trust, and they've been hit by big-bodied deep threats throughout the year, including Brock Bowers if we count that. In total, the Saints are allowing 250 passing YPG (ninth) while also being run on. New Orleans should be nearing full strength again on offense, so Boston's second-half numbers could come in handy here as a boom-bust WR3/4.

35. Keenan Allen (vs. DAL) - 47.5 proj. receiving yards

With Tyler Warren back in the mix, Allen's floor and ceiling take a hit. He's been the WR52 on WR33 usage this month with Daniel Jones now in the "getting benched soon" discussion. The only positive here is Josh Downs and Jonathan Taylor may be getting traded, and the matchup is pristine against the Cowboys. Game script and coverage busts will be on his side.

35. Caleb Douglas (vs. DET) - 39.5 proj. receiving yards

Douglas is the WR30 on WR66 usage this year, if we include his Week 2 injury game. Since returning, he's been the WR8 on a multi-score game and the WR45 in a difficult spot against Christian Gonzalez. He certainly has the size/speed of a WR3/4 with some upside if we can find the right matchups for him ...

The Lions have been one of the most generous matchups for wideouts, allowing the fifth-most half PPR points per game to the position (30.5). Teams routinely attack Detroit through the air, with opponents generating a 59% neutral pass rate against them (third highest in the league). We'll see if DJ Reed can suit up, but if he can't, then it's a big deal for Douglas' downfield role. The Dolphins are 7-point home underdogs with a 21.75-point implied total (18th).

38. Malik Washington (vs. DET) - 41.5 proj. receiving yards

Lemon's usage without DeVonta Smith (and Dontayvion Wicks) is obviously way up. He's been the WR17 and WR15 in usage, respectively, with Tanner McKee over the past two games and had a garbage-time-filled nine targets on Sunday Night Football. There is no ceiling to chase in this offense with his forgettable long speed and short-area role, but if Smith is out, then Lemon can role-play as a fantasy WR3.

On tape last week, Lemon was used in ways that play to his skill set. His 20-yard catch was a deeper zone out route (his best work at USC). He also sat in zone coverage near the sticks countless times, breaking a LB's tackle once and making a shoestring catch on another play. Unfortunately, he didn't get his eyes around in time for a ball to the face and then couldn't come down with an outside go ball. His catch radius and forgettable speed are not those of a No. 1 WR.

45. Ryan Flournoy (at IND) - 35.5 proj. receiving yards

He should be added ahead of the trade deadline, just in case George Pickens' offers for his expiring contract are too enticing to ignore. Flournoy is under contract for 2027, too. Very strong dynasty buy if you can swing it. He is the WR20 on WR49 usage this month.
