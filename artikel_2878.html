Jerry Jones calls Cowboys historic defensive struggle vs. 49ers 'glaring'

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Dallas Cowboys' defense reached a new low in its 46-40 loss to the San Francisco 49ers in Week 10 of the 2026 NFL season.

Dallas' defense surrendered 584 total yards during its loss to San Francisco, marking the most the Cowboys have surrendered at home in franchise history.

Before the Cowboys' showing against the 49ers, their record for yards allowed at home came in a 34-31 overtime loss to the New Orleans Saints during the 2012 NFL season. The Jason Garrett and Rob Ryan-coached unit surrendered 562 total yards in that contest, including a whopping 446 passing yards to future Hall of Fame quarterback Drew Brees.

But during the team's new, record-setting performance, Christian Parker's stop unit struggled in all facets of the game. They surrendered three touchdowns of at least 66 yards, including a 74-yard passing touchdown to Christian McCaffrey that was the longest score of the star running back's career.

And Dallas' three touchdowns of at least 60-plus scrimmage yards were tied for the most surrendered by a team in a single game over the last 50 seasons, per the Elias Sports Bureau, via ESPN.

It's still worth noting the 584 yards weren't the most ever surrendered by the Cowboys. They twice surrendered more than 600 yards in a single game under Monte Kiffin's leadership in 2013, giving up 625 yards to the Saints and 623 yards to the Lions across a three-week span, per Stathead.

Nonetheless, the performance was yet another lowlight in what has been a disastrous season for Dallas' defense. And Jerry Jones acknowledged the shortcomings during his customary postgame media availability.

"It's glaring that they made those kinds of yards," Jones told reporters. "The way we were tackling in the first half, we didn't continue that for the second half. That bit us. Then, they caught us in that blitz on that last big play. All that was there."

"They're a good team," he added of the 49ers. "We were gonna have to play better than we certainly played in the second half to beat them."

Jones also discussed Parker's performance in his new role, largely highlighting the 34-year-old's inexperience as part of his acclimation process.

"He's a young coach that's in his first year coordinating, and basically calling the plays," Jones said of Parker. "You would have to say that you would hope you can win games like the (games) that we've lost. I'll weigh his early times. I've been with teams that had some pretty good coordinators, on defense, that ended up winning Super Bowls."

While Dallas' defense struggled, the Cowboys' offense largely performed better. Dak Prescott enjoyed one of the most productive days of his season, tying a career high with five passing touchdowns and getting the Cowboys within one score after trailing by as many as 19 points in the fourth quarter.

Even so, the Cowboys were not without their warts. Notably, George Pickens endured a lowlight after committing a 15-yard taunting penalty following a 9-yard catch on Dallas' final touchdown drive, when time was at a premium.

Fox's color commentator for the game, Tom Brady, was flabbergasted by Pickens' infraction, which he referred to as "brutal."

"It's a brutal penalty," Brady said on the Fox broadcast. "They had one last week when [linebacker DeMarvion] Overshown ran on the field, too."

"It's just, I don't know man," Brady continued. "Good teams like the Niners just don't see that type of lack of discipline."

The Cowboys have certainly lacked discipline throughout the second season of Brian Schottenheimer's tenure. They entered Week 10 tied for seventh in penalties committed (74) and second in penalty yards for the season (667), per NFLPenalties.com.

Because of that, their struggling defense and their inability to close games, the Cowboys find themselves sitting at 4-6 after 10 weeks in what was supposed to be a season of improvement.
