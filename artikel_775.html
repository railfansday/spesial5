'Most frustrating of career' - Martin on Foxes draw at Blackpool

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

'We should win. You know that, I know that'

A squandered victory at the seaside has redefined Russell Martin's definition of frustration.

The Leicester City manager said he was "hurt" by the solitary point his side picked up in Tuesday's 1-1 draw at lowly Blackpool in League One after dominating just about every facet of the game â controlling 77% possession and amassing 30 shots, of which nine were on target.

Asked if the draw was the most frustrating moment of the season, former Rangers and Southampton boss Martin replied by saying: "This season? Of my short but feels like very long managerial career."

That says a lot coming from a manager who was smuggled out of the stadium under police escort then sacked at Rangers after a draw with Falkirk last season, and whose Southampton side was once undone in the Premier League by two second-half Mohamed Salah goals after leading 2-1.

Those were tenure-defining defeats for a manager whose reputation has taken a battering in recent seasons, with his style of possession-heavy, passing football â an approach he once said was to "dominate the ball and be aggressive with it", external â coming in for particular ridicule when results have gone against his side.

It has been the same at one-time Premier League title winners Leicester this season in the third tier, with Martin's method called into question in the early months of his tenure after the Foxes got off to a slow start.

Results have improved in recent weeks - they are unbeaten in six matches across all competitions and are up to seventh in the table.

But victory would have had them within a point of the play-off places, and Martin said the result will leave him "eternally frustrated".

"We should win, I know that, you know that and I think everyone in the stadium knows that. But we don't," he told BBC Radio Leicester after the match at Bloomfield Road.

"How we only score one goal, I'm really struggling to come to terms with.

"We have brilliant chances in the middle of their goal, not like half chances. And we actually score a goal that is really brilliant, but we shouldn't need a goal like that to win the game.

"I'm not frustrated, I'm hurt that we don't win the game and we concede a rubbish goal."

It took more than an hour for the Foxes to break Blackpool's resistance, with a deluge of chances missed before Will Alves cut in from the left to beat home goalkeeper Bailey Peacock-Farrell with a shot that took a slight deflection.

It was six minutes from the end when Niall Ennis scrambled home the leveller for the Seasiders.

"I'm really proud of the players and how much they have grown in terms of what the performances look like," said Martin.

"But it's my job to be demanding of them, and there is no way we should be drawing that game. No way."
