The Votes Are In: Americans Don’t Want Data Centers

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Democrats won big in Tuesday’s midterms, not only taking back the House of Representatives and Senate by overwhelming margins, but rebuking Republican candidates down the entire ticket in deep-red states like Texas, Mississippi, and Tennessee. Pundits and politicians are offering plenty of explanations for it — the exorbitant cost of living, the war in Iran, Republican candidates simply not being good enough — but there may be another thing that drove voters to the polls: the public’s deep distrust of AI data centers going up across the nation.

According to the report Data Centers on the 2026 Ballot, published by the firm Redbud Consulting, which has been tracking the initiatives, the election showed a decisive loss for data centers. Since yes and no could mean different things depending on the ballot, Redbud broke it down by votes to “restrict” or “allow” data centers in their analysis. Of the 39 nationwide referendums that have been fully counted, 38 of them enacted new restrictions for potential new data centers.

“For as much as Republicans did poorly last night, the one thing that did even worse were data centers,” says Alex Lundry, founder and CEO of Redbud Consulting. “For the most part, the restrict side has gotten about 77 percent of the vote in each of these races.” This lines up with recent polling: On Monday, Gallup published a survey that showed 70 percent of Americans don’t want data centers near where they live.

Aside from the ballot initiatives specifically targeting the construction of data centers, candidates who campaigned against them also saw huge wins in yesterday’s elections. As journalist Jael Holzman noted in the climate news site Heatmap, Keisha Lance Bottoms and Amy Acton won the governorships in Georgia and Ohio, respectively, thanks in part to their hard-lines against data centers. In New Jersey, Zack Mullock beat Republican representative Jeff Van Drew to win a long-red seat in Cape May, thanks to his repudiation of data centers, while in Virginia, Shannon Taylor did the same to Rep. Rob Wittman.

Elon Musk and Trump’s other tech cronies may have poured hundreds of millions of dollars into the midterms this year, but for now, at least, the democratic process is still strong — and Americans are making it clear where they stand on the issues.
