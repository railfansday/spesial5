Advent Calendar Books Are Taking Over Christmas 2026: Romance Authors Explain The Cozy New Reading Trend

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

EXCLUSIVE: The latest trend in Christmas books has been building for a few years, but the 2026 holiday season is when it is truly taking over shelves in the run-up to December. Holiday-themed reads (especially within the romance genre) have been huge for decades, of course, with Christmas novels competing with Christmas movies for snuggly seasonal entertainment, and many readers blowing through a dozen seasonal reads at the end of the year.

However, one of the emerging trends in publishing leans into slowing readers down, pacing them through December in the run-up to Christmas, and requiring a moment of pause that doesn't allow for multitasking with audiobooks or zooming through a digital copy. Instead, Advent Calendar Books split a novel into sealed chapters, designed to be read a little each day - and it's an adorable way to really get into the spirit of the season.

Not to be confused with bookish advent calendars (stuffed with book-themed items), or book advent calendars (where speed-readers whip through an entire book each day from December 1-24), advent books provide a reason to sit down for a little bit each day, to let the story unspool slowly, to enjoy ripping open a new section day by day... or of course, to ignore all the rules and blow through it in one!

With so many new advent releases, ScreenRant spoke to several authors featured in this year's biggest advent offerings about what makes this trend special:

Mistletoe All The Way is particularly interesting, because this holiday romance is available as both a special Advent edition (at Target), and as a non-advent, sprayed-edge edition. The romance novel leans hard into some of the best holiday tropes - small town, grumpy/sunshine (or grinch/jolly!), having to save the family farm over the holidays, forced to work together... even the little red truck, hauling a Christmas Tree on the cover. It's sweet and spicy, and ScreenRant got to chat to author Lauren Blakely about making it her first Advent holiday read:

ScreenRant: Mistletoe All The Way is available both as an Advent book and as a regular read - did you know that this was the plan from the start, and if so, how did it change the writing process?

I learned that Mistletoe All The Way was going to be packaged as an Advent book right after I had finished it at around 41 chapters. But my editor and I loved the idea of 48 chapters, with two chapters per section, to correspond to the 24 days of Christmas in each sticker section. I was able to break up some of the longer chapters into shorter ones so that we could get from 41 to 48 chapters. I didn't have to cut anything from the story at that point, but it was just a matter of rearranging some scenes so that it would fit that way. It was sort of a fun puzzle, and I enjoyed putting it together that way

SR: What about an Advent book appealed to you most - and have you read any other Advent editions?

I had not read any at all when I was doing the edits on Mistletoe All The Way. But I’m currently reading The Twelve Loves of Christmas and having a blast with it. And I love the perforated edges on the Naughty List Agreement.

SR: What are your favorite Christmas-specific tropes to write - and to read?

Some of my favorite Christmas romances have Christmas competitions, and I love the structure they provide in a story, like in MISTLETOE ALL THE WAY. But I also love them for grumpy heroes, which brings me to a Christmas trope I love – grinch/jolly! With a grumpy hero, part of your task as the writer is transforming him. We want to see him change, and a Christmas competition where he is forced to swallow a whole bottle of Christmas is a great way to torture him and make him grow. I also always, deeply and forever love fake dating during the holidays.

The 12 Loves of Christmas leans fully into the Advent style, with a collection of 12 short stories, rather than a single novel broken into sections. This choice definitely makes it a little easier to read in the intended one-a-day format, of course, as there's no burning desire to see what happens next, and has a secondary benefit of being able to fit a whole range of holiday tropes and characters into the mix. Think Love Actually, in book form, with stickers keeping each section separate (pro tip, cut the sticker open, rather than trying to unpeel it, to avoid damaging the pages!).

ScreenRant spoke to Jessica Joyce (author of the first story, A Merry Little Christmas) and Rebecca Jenshak (Twelve Seconds To Forever, day 6):

ScreenRant: What appeals most about an Advent book, especially in an age of speed reading, speeding up audiobooks, and setting huge reading goals?

JJ: I definitely think there's an appeal to advent books being quick reads, but I also think there's a real draw to them in a great bang-for-your-buck way. You have multiple authors and thus multiple stories, and it's a really fun sampler plate, a way for readers to get more of authors they love and also get introduced to authors they may not have read before. The discovery aspect of this type of book is super compelling!

RJ: First off, I'm a huge fan of advent calendars so to see advent books popping up in the last couple of years has been such a treat! As hectic as things can get with the holidays, short stories are a great way to get in a bit of cheer, discover new authors, and also hit that reading goal!

SR: Jessica, As the first story in the book, yours sets the tone - did you know that yours would be for day one at the start? I loved the reversal of the fake dating trope, especially because this is so common in 'home for the holidays' setups - did you start with this trope, or start with a different setup, and just see the perfect opportunity to add it in as a nod to the classic rom-com Christmas movies?

JJ: I had no idea it would be the first story! That was a fun surprise. As far as the setup goes, all I knew when I first started brainstorming was that the main character, Eleanor, was going to tag along to her friend's family's Christmas festivities and find her ex, Max, there. I needed some conflict to keep them apart, but not for long because the entire story was supposed to be 8,000 words. To your point, the fake date is a pretty common trope in holiday rom coms, so from there sprung the idea that Max had brought along his "girlfriend." I loved the idea of surprising both Eleanor and the reader, and I knew I could reveal the twist and get the two of them back together fairly quickly with it. I always really like tackling spins on well-loved tropes, and fake dating is one of my all-time favorites, so it was really fun to weave it into this story!

SR: Rebecca, you chose to write a New Year wedding story, really, a winter story, over an explicitly Christmas one - what was the reasoning behind that decision?

RJ: For me the real joy of the holiday season was always the lazy days between Christmas and New Years when all the holiday parties and obligations were done. My parents would be home from work, my sister and I were off from school, and we'd all laze around together. I also think there's something romantic about NYE - the countdown, the kissing, the hope of the new year! I thought it was the perfect backdrop for this missed opportunity/second chance story!

Ruby Winter's 12 Dates Of Christmas is a genuinely beautiful example of an Advent novel, hardcover, with sections that have perforated edges, and a little bookmark ribbon just to make it truly feel like a gift. It also understands that more is more when it comes to Christmas romance tropes and in-jokes, from a main character named Jake Frost to a burnt-out journalist in London for the holiday season. The book naturally splits into day sections, as the two go on twelve dates for Jake to convince Evie to fall in love with the season, and ScreenRant spoke to the author about how she put together the project.

ScreenRant: Was this conceived of as an advent project from the start, or did that come later? In either case - how did this change your writing or editing process, to fit a whole book into 24 chapters exactly?

RW: This was originally a collaboration with a few editors at evermore, Penguin Random House UK’s romance imprint that launched in 2025, so it was advent-style from the beginning. I think having the format in mind from the time I started drafting was super beneficial. I tend to write long and meandery, so it would have been tough to reverse-engineer one of my typical drafts into the more rigid structure of an advent-style book. Even knowing the formatting requirements, I still had to cut some chapters by half in order for them to fit into exactly 24 14-page long chapters!

SR: The book starts with 16 days till Christmas - were you tempted to make it 24 (or 23, for the epilogue) and make it a day per chapter, at any point?

A big focus of the book is on the setpieces where Evie and Jake have their dates, and there were twelve dates, so that number is what dictated the timeline. I didn’t want Evie lollygagging around London for fourteen pages in between her dates with Jake simply for the sake of having the days in the chapters align with the days in the readers’ December. I wanted Evie and Jake together in every chapter, and despite the tight timeline I wanted their growing relationship to have some time to breathe on the page. I also don’t think fitting a single day into each chapter’s fourteen pages would have been enough room for me to build an atmosphere that felt cinematic and authentically lived in. And from a purely mathematical standpoint, the timeline spills over into New Years’ Eve, so there wouldn’t have been a way for the 24 chapters of the book to align exactly with the first 24 days of December anyway.

SR: Did you intentionally choose a shorter time frame to try and pace it to the reader, who would be reading one chapter a day, or was this story just one that didn’t need a longer time frame?

RW: Definitely intentional, but it wasn’t easy! I usually write slow burns, and I also tend to write stories that take place over long time frames. Writing as Jen Devon, my debut novel Bend Toward the Sun follows the leads’ relationship across a full year, and the follow-up in Right Where We Left Us is a second-chance romance between two leads that have over a decade of history between them. Jake and Evie have less than a month, so the pacing in 12 Dates was a huge shift for me. Their initial attraction to each other isn’t that deep—it’s mostly physical. That’s relatively straightforward to write, but convincing the reader that these two people could fall in love in a matter of weeks was the real goal. Without too much context because of spoilers, I will say that the timing of when Chapter Twenty-Four takes place is very intentional.

SR: Was there something that you find particularly appealing about reading a book chapter by chapter, as opposed to devouring it in one go (especially with the tendency toward big reading goals and fast reads)?

RW: Holidays are all about coziness and ritual, so it makes sense for book lovers to incorporate that same vibe into how they read during this season. I think the packaged chapters of these advent-style books add to the anticipation of the next day’s reading session—it’s like literary edging, you know? I think a slower pace during this time of year is important, too. I hope readers set aside some quiet time in their day to spend with Evie and Jake. I’d be lying if I said I didn’t hope that readers find it hard to resist tearing into more than a chapter a day, though. Nobody has to know!
