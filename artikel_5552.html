Little Giant is recalling 3.6 million ladders over a fall hazard that caused head injuries

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The recall covers 11 models sold from December 2015 through October 2026, with 19 injuries reported to the company

Little Giant Ladder Systems is recalling about 3.7 million ladders that can retract if their Rapid Locks are not engaged, a fall hazard tied to reports of head injuries, according to the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission. The agency’s notice says the ladders “can suddenly retract if the Rapid Locks are not fully engaged.”

Little Giant, based in Springville, Utah, said it has been notified of 25 incidents in which the ladders retracted, and 19 of them involved injuries. Three involved harm to the head, from skull fractures and concussions to traumatic brain injury, along with lost vision, lost hearing and cognitive impairment. The other 16 ranged from bruises and cuts to broken bones and fractured joints. The company has also logged 39 complaints that the Rapid Locks malfunctioned.

Rapid Locks insert into ladder rungs to adjust the ladder's height. They are black or orange and sit on each ladder leg.

The recall covers these models: Adjustable Fortress 2.0, Conquest 2.0, Dark Horse 2.0, Epic, King Kombo XT, Leveler, MegaLite, MegaLite+, Multi, Quantum and Vector. Only ladders without an orange rectangular "QC" sticker on the rail above one of the Rapid Locks are included.

The ladders were sold at Lowe's $LOW, Costco $COST and other stores, and online at Lowes.com, Amazon $AMZN.com, Costco.com, LittleGiantLadders.com and other websites, from December 2015 through October 2026. Prices ranged from about $100 to $1,000. The ladders were made in the United States, China, Mexico and Malaysia.

Consumers should stop using the recalled ladders until they review the information at littlegiantladders.com/pages/recalls or through the QR code on the ladder. They should then contact Little Giant for a repair kit with warning labels and instructions. The labels advise users to verify that the Rapid Locks are engaged before every use and to maintain the locks with lubrication.

Consumers whose Rapid Lock does not function after lubrication should contact Little Giant for evaluation. If a lock still fails after that evaluation, the company will provide either a replacement ladder matching the original model or a comparable one, or a refund. Refunds are prorated for depreciation according to when the ladder was bought, but they cover the full price for purchases made in the last year.

Little Giant posted the CPSC notice, released Nov. 5, on its website. The company is also providing repair kits to every known user who reported Rapid Lock malfunctions, according to Fox Business. Consumers can reach Little Giant toll-free at 866-201-9069.
