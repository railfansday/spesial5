Backdoor References: 37% Say Potential Employers Contacted People They Never Listed

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

New report examines the realities of professional references, as nearly 1 in 3 workers say they've struggled to find enough when applying for a job.

GUAYNABO, Puerto Rico, Nov. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- A job reference may seem like a straightforward endorsement, but what happens behind the scenes can be more complicated.

New research from ZetyÂ®, a leading resume builder, shows some references are recommending people despite their own reservations (40%), some candidates are coaching or even faking references (19%), and employers aren't always sticking to the names they're given (37%).

Surveying over 1,000 U.S. workers, the Professional References Report found that 42% believe references should have little to no influence on an employer's final hiring decision, with the results raising broader questions about the role references play in hiring.

How Far Job Seekers Go to Manage References

Rather than leaving references to chance, many candidates take steps to steer the process in their favor.

Candidates may carefully curate their references, but 37% of workers say a potential employer reached out to a former manager or coworker they never listed.

Meanwhile, the references they do provide aren't necessarily contacted: just 15% say their references were contacted every time they were hired, while 47% say sometimes and 38% say rarely or never.

"Rogue reference checks introduce a wild card into the hiring process because candidates have no control over who is contacted or what version of their work history that person may share," said Dr. Jasmine Escalera, career expert at Zety. "Someone outside a candidate's reference list may have an incomplete view of their performance or a perspective colored by office politics, past conflicts, or personal history. Even more concerning, the candidate may never know the conversation took place, much less have an opportunity to provide context."

The findings presented are based on a nationally representative survey conducted by Zety using Pollfish on August 24, 2026. The survey collected responses from 1,007 U.S. employees and examined their experiences, perceptions, and attitudes regarding professional references, including how references are selected, provided, and contacted, as well as their perceived accuracy and influence on hiring decisions.

Respondents answered different types of questions, including yes/no; scale-based questions, where they indicated their level of agreement with statements; and multiple-choice questions, where they could select from a list of provided options.

The sample consisted of 56% female, 43% male, and 1% nonbinary respondents, with 21% Gen Z, 27% millennials, 26% Gen X, and 26% baby boomers.
