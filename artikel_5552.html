Consumer Sentiment Jumps After Election Results

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

While the economy and housing still face hurdles, consumers are feeling more confident following November’s election results, according to the latest Index of Consumer Sentiment from the University of Michigan.

Consumer sentiment took a leap upward, climbing 7.3% to 51.5 in November due to “substantial post-election gains in both short- and long-run expected business conditions,” as noted by Surveys of Consumers Director Joanne Hsu. Responses were collected from Oct. 20 through Nov. 16.

“Consumers appear to anticipate potential changes to public policy under a divided government to come,” continued Hsu. “Accordingly, the expectations index surged for independents, who swung to the left in the midterms, and increased more moderately for Democrats. Expectations fell for Republicans, who may not favor a slowdown of the White House policy agenda.”

Voters have expressed expectations for the “blue wave” that swept the election to make meaningful changes when it comes to inflation and housing affordability, an undertaking that many Democrats who ran (and won) included in their campaign promises.

Sentiment is also slightly higher (1%) from one year ago, when it was at 51. Hsu noted, though, that sentiment is still “subdued” by 9% from where it was in February, prior to the start of the conflict in Iran.

The index of Current Economic Conditions also saw a monthly boost of 5.8% to 49.4, but was still down 3.3% year-over-year.

Hsu noted that some respite in gas prices during the survey period improved finances for low- and middle-wealth consumers, but “those with the largest stockholdings reported a decline in personal finances, consistent with continued AI-related agitation in stock markets.”

The forward-looking Index of Consumer Expectations saw the largest monthly leap, shooting up 8.4% to 52.9. Year-over-year, the index is up 3.7%.

Median year-ahead inflation expectations shifted downward from 4.7% last month to 3.7% this month, but remained above the 3.4% recorded in February, pre-Iranian conflict. Long-run inflation expectations also decreased slightly, from 3.5% in October to 3.3%. These expectations, however, remained above the 2.8% to 3.2% range seen back in 2024.

Hsu noted that despite consumer expectations “softening,” they “continue to report overwhelmingly that inflation—rather than unemployment—will cause greater hardship for consumers in the next one or two years.”
