‘A source of inspiration’: California celebrates the late civil rights icon Dolores Huerta

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

A public vigil and celebration of life honor the civil rights activist and co-founder of United Farm Workers union

Hundreds of people came out to honor and celebrate the life and legacy of the California civil rights activist Dolores Huerta at a celebration-of-life event Friday in Bakersfield.

Huerta’s daughter Lori de Leon thanked attendees for their support during her mother’s passing, highlighting the widespread tributes and communities gathering in her honor.

Huerta’s casket was escorted inside the Dignity Health arena for the interfaith memorial service as Mariachi Lindas Mexicanas played next to it.

Huerta, a feminist icon who co-founded the United Farm Workers (UFW) in 1962, died on 28 October aged 96.

Attenders came from all over California to pay their respects. Rosa de la Cruz, a 44-year-old farm worker who lives in Tulare, about an hour north of Bakersfield, said she wanted to pay her respects in person.

The world-renowned labor rights leader and activist means “a great deal”, de la Cruz said, “because if it hadn’t been for her, there wouldn’t be many benefits for the people who work in the fields.”

The week was filled with events dedicated to Huerta’s memory, her courage, resilience and her impact on the community.

On Monday, Huerta was honored in Sacramento by state government officials including Monique Limón, the senate president pro tempore, Robert Rivas, the assembly speaker, Xavier Becerra, the California governor-elect, and community leaders in a historic ceremony.

Huerta became the first female, first Latino and first non-elected leader to lie in honor at the state capitol. Her casket was escorted in after a march organized by local unions.

“Our mother devoted her entire life to fighting injustice and lifting up communities too often left out and treated unfairly,” said Huerta’s family. “As a lifelong organizer, her activism has been a source of inspiration and empowerment for people around the world.”

For more than six decades, Huerta spearheaded historic boycotts, fought tirelessly for farm workers’ rights, women’s equality and environmental justice, and coined the enduring national rally cry, “¡Sí, se puede!”

Huerta has received numerous honors including the Eleanor Roosevelt Human Rights Awards. In 2012, Barack Obama awarded Huerta the Presidential Medal of Freedom, the nation’s highest civilian honor.

The two-day tribute started on Thursday evening with a public rosary and vigil in Bakersfield, where she had lived since 1990.

Friday’s interfaith memorial service and celebration of life began with an opening blessing by Aztec dancers. There were performances from music groups Los Lobos, Ozomatli, Las Cafeteras, Nancy Sanchez and Trio los Dados. Rob Bonta, the California attorney general, was among the speakers on Friday.

“Dolores Huerta is irreplaceable and her loss is something I think people are still, including me, getting our heads around,” Bonta said. “To the world, she is an icon and a giant and a legend. For me, she was also a friend and a mentor and a counselor. Someone I texted with and called and sought advice from and leaned on as I launched my career and had my career in elected public office.”

“Her loss is immeasurable. She is irreplaceable but she leaves a legacy of fighting for justice,” Bonta said.

Ten of Huerta’s adult children took the stage to share personal anecdotes about Huerta’s life, emphasizing her skills as an organizer, political strategist and motivational speaker. Her children recounted her dedication to social justice, her love for jazz, and her humility despite her significant contributions.

“While there is much to celebrate in our recent victories, there is still so much work to be done,” said Huerta’s daughter Camila Chavez. “People may expect us to fill her shoes, but she is going to be buried with her shoes. Like Zapata said: ‘I’d rather live on my feet than die on my knees.’ It’s going to take all of us here to fill those shoes.”

Her legacy will continue through the work and empowering mission of the Dolores Huerta Foundation. In 2003, Huerta founded the grassroots community organization to create leadership opportunities for community members, while organizing underserved communities, and pursuing social justice.

Huerta kept on working even as a nonagenarian, dedicating her life as an organizer.

In the Central valley, Huerta supported minorities to run for office to make a difference at the local, state and federal level. In a 2022 speech, Huerta encouraged new US citizens to fulfill their civic responsibilities, especially voting.

For her 94th birthday, Huerta asked people to help her celebrate by getting others involved in elections either by talking to somebody about voting and about registering to vote.

Huerta urged uninsured Latinos and Californians to enroll in health coverage with the passage of the Affordable Care Act partnering with Covered California to spread the message. Huerta also had several schools named after her throughout the state. Last year, Huerta joined Central valley educators to demand David Valadao, a US representative from California, defend funding for schools.
