'Frustrated' - Democrats remain a divided party despite winning big in midterms

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

More than 2,000 people gathered in Denver for a What's Next? Democrat event

When James Talarico - who recently became the first Democrat to win election to the US Senate for Texas since 1988 - appeared onstage at a Democratic political conference, he was greeted by a chorus of cheers and a standing ovation.

But the audience's enthusiasm dimmed when he suggested working in tandem with the Republican Trump administration.

"We should make the final two years of his presidency the most productive of any presidency in American history," said Talarico, who at 37 will become the youngest member of the Senate when he takes his oath of office in January. "Because this moment is so much bigger and so much more important than the red team or the blue team."

He was just one of many rising stars speaking last week during a political conference in Denver, titled "What's Next: investigating strategies and priorities for the future of the Democratic Party".

And the answer to what's next? No one's sure.

Texas Democrat James Talarico got a mixed reaction in the room when he pushed the party to work with President Trump

Those present, who included activists and political organisers, were invigorated and celebratory the week after a "blue tsunami" saw Democrats win in Republican heartlands in the midterm elections.

The party took control of both chambers of Congress, winning a majority in the House by over 80 seats and gaining nine seats in the Senate.

But the atmosphere in Denver was also introspective and forward-looking, as discussions focused on how those victories were won, which issues did not resonate, and what would be needed to continue the party's seat-winning momentum.

Some, like Talarico, suggested working alongside Republicans to push forward an economic agenda that would resonate with Americans, such as extending health coverage and reducing inflation.

Alabama Congressman Shomari Figures - who surprised many pollsters when he held his seat despite Republicans redrawing his district months before the election - cautioned against going after Trump, when there were more pressing concerns for Americans.

Previous Democratic-led efforts to impeach Trump both ended in Senate acquittals.

"We impeached him twice and got him back - with a Republican majority," Figures said.

Yet others, like Maryland Congresswoman Angela Alsobrooks, insisted that voters wanted to see accountability.

Voters "realised the smash-and-grab that occurred, and they want people to account for it", she said, pushing for transparent investigations into the Trump administration.

The two-day event was staged by Majority Democrats, a political action committee launched last year to address tensions between the left and centrist factions of the party, with some of the biggest divisions forming over how to deliver health care and the war in Gaza.

Prominent left-wing members of the party - such as Congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Senator-elect Abdul El Sayed - were not in attendance.

Protesters Vicki Saxe and Tami Butters were demanding Democratic Party change

In Denver, it was clear those tensions weren't going away, as protesters waving Palestinian flags shouted as people walked past and armed police looked on.

Some in attendance made a point to criticise Chuck Schumer, the long-time Democratic Senate minority leader who is seen as a part of the party's centrist establishment.

He has been challenged for the role of Senate majority leader by Chris Van Hollen, a senator from Maryland who is considered a left-wing populist.

Democrats have not been "strong enough in their displeasure with what's been happening with Trump, and I think that's one of the reasons that people are asking for new leadership," said Vicki Saxe, holding a sign criticising Schumer.

"I'm frustrated with the lack of urgency that I feel coming from the party leadership," said Tami Butters, 65, standing beside her with a "Vote Yes for Chris Van Hollen" sign. She cited the absence of 11 Democrats last week, when the House voted on whether to consider impeaching US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The effort failed.

Hakeem Jeffries, a centrist Democrat who is poised to become the country's first black speaker of the House, faced similar pushback, as protesters heckled his speech.

One of the biggest issues of division is the war in Gaza, with both Schumer and Jeffries backing continued military assistance for Israel, while some new members of Congress, like El-Sayed, support ending unconditional US military aid to Israel.

Non-profit founder Shiva Saram, who also chairs the Iranian American Legal Defense Fund, travelled from Connecticut to the conference despite having "almost left my party over Gaza and the Iran War".

Protesters interrupted New York Congressman Hakeem Jeffries at the event

Saram has been newly encouraged, however, by the "real depth in our bench of new and young leaders".

"What my party has done wrong for too long is we did not listen," she said.

Many speakers and guests stressed that the Democrats won because they focused on real policies that would impact their constituents' daily lives - and that failing to do so in previous years might have been why they lost on those occasions.

Delaware Senator Chris Coons told BBC News that he had advised newly elected members to "under-promise and over-deliver".

"We got hired because the average American wants us to help make their lives better - lower their costs, end the cruelty and the corruption and give them more reason for hope," he said. "That's going to require hard work, and it isn't going to be easy to get done."

Michigan Representative Kristen McDonald Rivet agreed, saying she did not care "if we have to put Donald Trump's name" on legislation to gain the president's support for efforts to help her district.

Texan Carlos Repetto-Ayala (L) wants Democrats to be "laser-focused" on issues that voters truly care about

Tahra Hoops, the 28-year-old senior director of economic analysis at the Chamber of Progress, a "left-of-centre" tech think tank, suggested that the party's divisions be embraced, not paved over.

"I think Democrats can look different wherever they are," she said.

While "very progressive" candidates can win in her home town of New York City, she said it made sense that Talarico held more centrist views because running in Texas "is not going to be the same".

"America is a very diverse country; our politicians and their views should also be diverse," she added.

Carlos Repetto-Ayala, a campaign consultant, made a similar point. The 25-year-old Texan travelled with two friends to the What's Next? event, wearing a cowboy hat and a red-white-and-blue pin on his lapel in the shape of the Lone Star State.

"It is a big tent party, but I think, as long as everyone that's in the party is laser-focused on addressing the issues that people would like for them to fix, I think Democrats will be successful," he said.
