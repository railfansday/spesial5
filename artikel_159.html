States sue SpaceX to stop Grok from generating child sex images, revenge porn

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

X says every Grok output is screened, but state AGs say Grok is still making CSAM.

On Monday, Virginia and Oregon became the first states to sue SpaceX, alleging that Grok continues to sexualize, unchecked, real photos of mostly women and children.

SpaceX acquired xAI in February and now must handle the ongoing reckoning over the controversial rollout of Grok’s “spicy mode”—a model feature Musk hyped but was soon found to generate a flood of non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) and child sex abuse images (CSAM).

Initial safeguards were so lax that Grok sometimes generated nude images without being asked. Although X Safety and xAI (now known as SpaceXSI) were silent at first, Musk’s companies eventually apologized and claimed that additional safeguards were implemented and policies redesigned to block harmful outputs. However, state attorneys general alleged that those statements were “misleading” and that SpaceXSI instead quietly “embraced” sexualized content to seize more AI market share, as Grok continues to generate NCII and CSAM today.

Without intervention, kids in Virginia and Oregon risk a lifetime of distress, as Grok images will likely be impossible to remove online.

“Once created and shared,” Grok-generated CSAM and NCII “can never be fully erased, and it becomes a permanent record of the child’s abuse that may resurface for the rest of the child’s life,” Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones’ complaint said. “Virginia has a profound interest in protecting its children from this harm.”

Millions of residents were allegedly harmed, as the attorneys general seek to protect every Grok or X user in their state. Many Grok victims will likely suffer from chronic anxiety, depression, and PTSD, as well as reputational harm, as some viewers may not realize the images are AI-generated, the states alleged. Further—due to Grok’s integration with the social media platform X—users as young as 13 may have been exposed to the AI content and possibly re-traumatized if they, too, are victims of sexual abuse, the states alleged.

“xAI not only knowingly endangered kids and our communities, but they also lied to our faces about non-existent safety measures,” Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield said in a press release.

States alleged that xAI could have conducted rigorous red-teaming to block outputs before the new feature launched. But instead, the X Safety team responsible for Grok red-teaming was reduced to two or three team members, states alleged.

Overall, rather than use industry-standard safeguards, Musk’s firms allegedly put profits over women’s and children’s safety, states alleged, while complaining that SpaceXSI won’t even publicly identify “the officers responsible for safety governance.”

“Industry standard requires not only building guardrails but also vigilantly monitoring whether they hold,” states alleged. SpaceXSI is doing neither effectively, states alleged.

Asked for comment, SpaceXSI shared an X Safety post on X, which repeated claims that the company has a “zero-tolerance” policy for “any forms of child sexual exploitation.” X Safety said that the company “acted promptly to tighten” Grok protections, which are supposedly “designed to refuse any request to generate explicit images of minors or real people.” That statement also claimed that X Safety continually tests and adjusts protections when bad actors find ways to avoid filters, while relying on “sophisticated classifiers that screen every prompt” to catch harmful outputs “before an image is generated and check every output after.” Although the statement did not mention whether xAI sues users who break Grok rules to generate CSAM and NCII, it emphasized that xAI reports harmful content generated to police.

“When someone tries to break our safeguards and generate this content, we take appropriate measures, including actioning accounts and notifying law enforcement,” X Safety said. “Attempting to create this material is a serious crime, and the individuals who do it should be prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law. We will continue to work with the authorities via all available means to pursue these bad actors.”

However, state attorneys general alleged that reports from independent researchers and news outlets this year have shown that Grok is still generating NCII and CSAM. They noted that the chatbot “even advises users how to circumvent its own limited moderation measures to generate those images.” In some cases, a Virginia state probe found that Grok complied “with a user request to draft a prompt designed to avoid moderation.” So, even when filters work, the chatbot helps determined users immediately get around them, states alleged.

Nothing xAI did put a dent in the harmful outputs, least of all putting the feature behind a paywall, states alleged, which only ensured maximum profits. A troubled New York City Council speaker, Julie Menin, confronted SpaceX this October with proof that attempts to digitally undress her own photo still worked for free Grok users, states alleged.

Concluding its probe, Virginia found that moderation remains “inconsistent and ineffective,” with Grok sometimes carrying out prompts “that it later blocked.”

In its complaint, Virginia alleged that Grok protections “were weak, ineffective, sparsely enforced, and easily circumvented.” Oregon agreed that rather than act to protect users, SpaceX entities repeatedly made statements “with the intent to deceive consumers” that Grok was safe. Most egregiously to state attorneys general, in January, Elon Musk denied any harm to real people, claiming that Grok is only “supposed [to] allow upper body nudity of imaginary adult humans (not real ones) and only generated nudity of fake people (not real ones).”

“Contrary to Musk’s representation,” Grok “makes the same sexualizing edits to photographs of real, identifiable people,” states warned. “Defendants intentionally released a product that inundated their platforms with CSAM, NCII, and non-consensual sexualized images,” they alleged, and other “contemporaneous statements by Musk make clear that Grok’s ability to create sexualized material was part of xAI’s business strategy.”

It’s also troubling to states that the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC) reported to Congress that more than 90 percent of the Grok-generated CSAM that xAI submitted to its CyberTipline was considered a “dead end.”

As NCMEC explained, xAI initially refused to provide NCMEC and police with “basic user information, including location information, that law enforcement needs to act.” To fix the problem, NCMEC had to set up a meeting with xAI and get the reports resubmitted. This, states alleged, confirmed that “the information existed and that xAI had not provided it.” The concerning omission allegedly slowed down police response, just as the average number of Grok CSAM reports jumped by 88 percent from December 2025 to January 2026, then increased by another 66 percent from January to June 2026.

“X users were subjected to the risk that any person, at any time, could use their likeness to create CSAM, NCII, or other non-consensual sexualized images,” states alleged.

Lawmakers have asked state courts for an injunction blocking SpaceX entities from violating consumer protection laws by allegedly operating Grok without industry standard safeguards. If they win, they want SpaceX to pay thousands in civil penalties per violation, as well as disgorge any ill-gotten gains from marketing the chatbot as safe to users.

“Most people who use social media do not want to contribute to the harm caused to victims of CSAM, NCII, and other non-consensual sexualized images by being exposed to those images online,” states alleged.

Although Oregon’s complaint only vaguely estimates the number of users Grok attracted with Musk’s allegedly deceitful marketing strategy as at least “hundreds of thousands,” Virginia’s complaint included more detailed estimates. It said that approximately 6.4 million X accounts were linked to geolocations in the state, while the Grok application was downloaded 986,624 times by users with Virginia-based IP addresses.

“When xAI allowed Grok’s creators to sideline consent to turn a profit, they prioritized growth and engagement over safety, and they deceived the public about it,” Jones said. “Today’s filings are the first step towards holding this company accountable.”
