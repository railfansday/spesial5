With no discounts on the official Nintendo Switch 2 Pro Controller in sight, it's worth weighing up whether that $89 price tag is worth it after all.

Here's what the Nintendo does that others... Nintendon't.

The Switch 2 Pro Controller is unmatched in its ability to wake the Switch 2. Others can, and will, bring that screen to life in docked mode, but setup is finnicky and dropouts are regular. The Switch 2 talks to its own gamepad best.

You also won't find Nintendo-specific HD Rumble 2 haptics in third party alternatives. That's the more nuanced level of vibration that makes games like Donkey Kong Bananza sing just that little bit brighter. Haptics are available in third parties, but without that Ninty spark.

Finally, onscreen control over your back button assignments is exclusive to Nintendo's gamepad. Being able to quickly pause your game and configure each of the two additional clickers is super handy - others will have you connecting to a PC or mobile app, or working out individual button presses on the gamepad itself.

If any of those are deal breakers, the Switch 2 Pro Controller is worth it. If you can live without those luxuries, though, third parties have plenty to bring to the table. GameSir and 8BitDo add trigger stops, Hall effect thumbsticks, charging docks, and extra configurable buttons to the party, for example.

The GameSir Super Nova is back on sale for $29.99 at Best Buy (was $49.99) after dropping out of stock earlier in the week. That's a record-low position it frequently returns to during major sales events. It's also a discount that stands in the way of a number of slightly more expensive gamepads, so I knew I had to get it in my own hands before Black Friday.

What do you get for your money? Quite a lot.

The Super Nova feels far more premium than that $30 price tag would suggest. While alternatives like the PowerA Advantage Wired Controller can undercut it by around $10 when the discounts are flowing, they also feel light, hollow, and creaky in the hands. This is as solid as it gets, with nicely rubberized grips, satisfying buttons, and a premium heft.

Customization is your main point of value in this price. Back buttons are par for the course these days, and trigger-stops are creeping down the price scale as well, but motion controls can also be mapped to mouse inputs on PC, GameSir's Connect software can change everything from stick deadzones to trajectory settings, and - of course - that RGB is configurable as well.

Is it perfect? No. I scored the GameSir Super Nova 4/5 stars in my review, dropping points for its lack of presets, rigid-feeling d-pad, and finnicky wake functionality. The latter is a particularly sore point. It was one of the biggest reasons I was so excited to wrap my hands around this gamepad, but in all my testing I just never got it to work. It's a fairly convoluted setup process anyway, but others have been able to get everything running smoothly.

Ultimately, paying more for the GameSir Super Nova over budget gamepads from PowerA and PDP is a no-brainer in my experience.
