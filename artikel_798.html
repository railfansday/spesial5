How Beyoncé, Taylor Swift & More Already Made Grammys 2027 History

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Grammys 2027 is already set to break records, with Beyoncé, Taylor Swift and more making history with their nominations. The award show will air across ABC, Disney+ and Hulu on Feb. 7, 2027.

Let's make some noise for these 2027 Grammy nominees.

Though music's biggest night is still three months out, a handful of performers are already making history with their nominations, which were announced Nov. 16 by a star-studded lineup comprised of Pharrell Williams, Carly Pearce, Olivia Dean, Yungblud and more.

Take Beyoncé, for example. You can't break her soul, but she is always breaking records. With her Grammy nod for “Morning Dew (Donk),” Beyoncé has made history by becoming the first ever artist to reach 100 nominations. And Raye is no longer wondering what is taking so long, as she the 2027 nominees with 11 nods, tying a record first set by none other than the “Break My Soul” singer.

But that's not all as Taylor Swift had another wildest dream come true.

With The Life of a Showgirl up for an Album of the Year award at the upcoming ceremony—airing across ABC, Disney+ and Hulu on Feb. 7, 2027—the "Opalite" singer is dancing through the lightning strikes as the first ever artist with most solo album nods in the category. (Paul McCartney still holds the record for overall nominations, which includes five with The Beatles.)

Meanwhile, there were also many firsts with the introduction of five new categories for the 69th annual Grammy Awards.

Chris Brown, Leon Thomas, Lalah Hathaway, J. Ivy, Kehlani, PJ Morton, Keyon Harrold, Jill Scott and J.I.D all became the first performers to be nominated for Best R&B Collaboration or Duo/Group Performance. The award was created after the Best R&B Performance category was renamed to Best R&B Solo Performance.

Likewise, BABYMETAL, Polyphia, BINI, IVE, KiiiKiii, LISA and SB19 received nods in the inaugural Best Asian Pop Music Performance category, which honors work "originating from or widely recognized within Asian markets, including but not limited to K-pop, J-pop and C-pop, with meaningful use of one or more Asian languages," according to the Grammys website.

But while K-pop giant BTS dropped Arirang—their first studio album since their 2022 hiatus—earlier this year, the boy band consisting of Jimin, Jung Kook, Jin, Suga, J-Hope, RM and V did not land a nod in the new category after opting not to submit it for Grammy consideration.

Their dynamite reason behind the unconventional move?

“We hope our music can be heard and loved for what it is, rather than being divided by region or language,” BTS shared in a July statement. “We thank [BTS] Army and everyone who has always stood by us.”
