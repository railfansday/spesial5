Xiaomi’s Eye-Catching Pro Series Phones Go Global for the First Time

Xiaomi officially released its 18 Pro Series phones, the 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max, at a launch event in Paris, marking the first time it’s pushing its Pro models outside of China. That also makes them outliers in another respect: they are rare slab smartphones with two displays.

Both will hit select markets — not North America — in the coming weeks and months and carry on Xiaomi’s existing partnership with Leica as camera-centric devices. They also feature a rear LTPO AMOLED screen positioned as a creative tool for those who like selfies but want more variety.

Xiaomi already foreshadowed this launch when unveiling the devices at Qualcomm’s Snapdragon Summit in September 2026, so specs, especially on the camera side, weren’t entirely a mystery. Once they launched in China shortly afterward, the cat was pretty much out of the bag.

We know the main differences between the two phones have to do with screen size, image sensors, the Snapdragon chip, and battery capacity. The 18 Pro Max gets a larger 6.9-inch LTPO AMOLED display (2624 x 1208) with Xiaomi’s own Shield Glass protection, a 1-120Hz variable refresh rate, 4,000 nits of peak brightness, and BT.2020 support. It also runs on the more powerful Snapdragon 8 Gen 6 Elite Extreme chipset and packs a 7,050mAh battery.

Otherwise, the Pro has a smaller 6.4-inch panel (2436 x 1120) with the same display specs as its Max sibling. It also steps down a bit, running on the Snapdragon 8 Gen 6 Elite chip, and, given its smaller size, has a 6,000mAh battery.

There are slight differences in the rear display size, as the 18 Pro Max features a larger 2.9-inch panel compared to the 2.7-inch screen on the 18 Pro. Both will come with 12GB of RAM and 256GB or 512GB storage configurations, though the Pro Max also offers a 16GB RAM and 1TB storage variant.

Interestingly, Xiaomi equips both phones with almost the exact same camera specs. The three Leica-branded rear cameras are largely identical on both models, with one key exception: the 18 Pro Max uses the LOFIC-equipped Light Fusion 960L sensor, whereas the 18 Pro uses the standard Light Fusion 960.

The 200-megapixel main camera (23mm equivalent) uses a Type 1/1.28 Light Fusion 960L image sensor and an f/1.67 aperture. Thanks to LOFIC technology, it delivers better dynamic range than both a standard sensor and the 18 Pro’s main camera. You can tell just by pointing both phones at any subject before even snapping the shutter. There’s also optical image stabilization with a CIPA-rated seven stops of shake correction. The Leica Summilux-designed lenses combine precision glass and plastic elements with what appears to be a glare-resistant coating.

The 200-megapixel periscope telephoto camera (75mm equivalent) uses a Type 1/1.56 image sensor, an f/2.4 aperture, and 3.2x optical zoom. Finally, there’s the 50-megapixel ultra-wide camera, whose sensor size remains a mystery. It is somewhat of an outlier in that it’s a 17mm equivalent with a 102° field of view, which is noticeably narrower than most ultra-wide cameras on other phones.

On the software side, Xiaomi adds a new mode called Essential Moment that marries Leica color science with AI-driven image processing to emulate classic film and camera looks. Names like “B&W Film,” “Modern Vintage,” and “Iconic CCD” don’t specify exactly what stock they’re based on, but the new styles add a distinctly Xiaomi-infused element to the camera interface.

This is how Xiaomi frames it: “B&W Film captures the depth and tonal range of classic monochrome photography, Iconic CCD recreates the vivid, saturated colors of CCD-era digital cameras, and Modern Vintage offers refined tones with natural detail for a timeless look. Users can also replicate the optical characteristics of two classic lenses — a 35mm Documentary lens and a 75mm Portrait lens with soft bokeh.”

There are some unusual limitations, however. Pro mode doesn’t allow users to shoot in RAW at 50 megapixels, and there is still no 24fps option for video, including in Pro Video. None of the Leica styles and filters introduced in the Leitzphone by Xiaomi have migrated over to these devices, so you retain the same choices offered in other Xiaomi models.

The Back Display offers a fairly high degree of access to the overall camera interface, including modes, settings, customizations, and more. Xiaomi limits zoom to 0.7x and 1x, meaning there is no way to activate the telephoto camera, though the settings still let you hold up a palm to trigger the shutter hands-free.

While the live preview occupies a sizable portion of the display, Xiaomi configured it to light up in bright white elsewhere to help illuminate subjects in dimmer conditions. It limits available modes to Photo, Video, Portrait, and a retro-style option called Photo Booth, and strangely omits Leica filters from the equation (though you can still choose between Leica Authentic and Vibrant as the overarching style).

You also get the choice to shoot pixel-binned 12.5-megapixel or 50-megapixel stills, with video resolution options ranging from 720p all the way to 8K at either 30 or 60fps. Dolby Vision and a Teleprompter are also available as optional features.

It’s a fully functional display in that it works with swipes and gestures just as the main display does. Tap-to-focus works, as does focus tracking when double-tapping on yourself or another subject in the frame. You can preview shots you’ve taken and either press the onscreen shutter or use the volume buttons to capture a photo or video.

Since the screen uses the rear cameras, the dynamic range advantages apply equally; Xiaomi doesn’t appear to neuter the experience by dialing things back, particularly with the LOFIC sensor on the 18 Pro Max.

An added benefit of the Back Display is that it also works with notifications and real-time information, like calls, maps, ride-hailing, food delivery, audio recording, and more. Pin to Back Display allows you to pin text and images to it for quick reference, such as a boarding pass or QR code. There’s also the option to apply a different wallpaper to it, if you want.

Neither Xiaomi nor Leica made any mention of a photography or teleconverter lens kit for either phone, even as Xiaomi’s Chinese competitors continue to release more of them. There are possibilities there, but you would need a third-party setup to make it work. Thus far, Xiaomi has only made photography kits for its Ultra line, which it maintains is still the company’s flagship with a more specialized photography slant.

You won’t find the Xiaomi 18 Pro or 18 Pro Max in Canada or the U.S. unless you import them yourself. They’re both compatible with North American cellular networks, so there isn’t a hardware limitation in that regard. Warranty challenges aside, Xiaomi is offering five years of Android updates and six years of security updates.

Both models will come in the same four color variants: Black, White, Velvet Red, and Cosmic Blue. As for pricing, the Xiaomi 18 Pro starts at €1,399, while the 18 Pro Max starts at €1,599.
