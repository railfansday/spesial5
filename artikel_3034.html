Walmart Has Raised Its Dividend 53 Straight Years. Why I'd Buy It Before December and Never Sell.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Walmart's 53-year dividend streak points to a durable business. Advertising, memberships, and global growth could keep it expanding for decades.

Some stocks stand out because they've proven they can reward shareholders for decades, and Walmart (WMT +0.06%) is one of those stocks, having raised its dividend for 53 consecutive years. Looking ahead, with new growth opportunities emerging across advertising and e-commerce, here's why investors might consider buying this retail giant before the new year and holding it forever.

The latest increase brought the company's annual dividend to $0.99 per share, up from $0.94 in the previous fiscal year, just as it's done for the past 53 years.

But Walmart's dividend history is about more than returning cash to shareholders. It's proof of something more important: the durability of its business, as Walmart is built around supplying necessities at competitive prices, regardless of whether the economy is booming or slowing.

On top of that, its enormous scale, established customer relationships, and extensive distribution network make it difficult for competitors to replicate what it has built. Instead of relying on its traditional retail business, Walmart has spent years expanding into faster-growing areas that could support decades of future growth.

Retail has traditionally been a relatively low-margin business. Walmart moves enormous volumes of products, but selling groceries and household essentials leaves limited room for profit growth. That's why growing advertising and membership businesses have become increasingly important.

From there, it's important to consider Walmart Connect, the company's advertising platform. Since Walmart has direct relationships with millions of shoppers and access to valuable shopping data, it can offer brands opportunities to reach consumers much closer to the point of purchase.

In fact, that strategy is already driving growth.

Walmart's global advertising business grew 38% in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, while its U.S. advertising business grew 43%, excluding VIZIO. Plus, the acquisition of VIZIO gives Walmart an opportunity to expand its advertising reach into connected television, creating another channel where it can monetize customer relationships.

Meanwhile, memberships offer another opportunity. For example, Walmart+ and Sam's Club memberships encourage repeat purchases while creating recurring fee revenue.

Plus, they give customers more reasons to consolidate their shopping with Walmart rather than spending across several retailers. With that, global membership fee revenue rose 17% in the second quarter of fiscal 2027.

Meanwhile, beyond advertising and memberships, Walmart is expanding globally to drive the next phase of its growth.

These days, Walmart's growth isn't limited to the U.S. In fact, the company operates across multiple international markets, including Mexico, Canada, China, India, and several countries in Central America and Africa. These businesses provide exposure to different markets and give Walmart more opportunities to expand.

On top of that, its international strategy opens up opportunities in digital markets. In India, for instance, Walmart has a significant stake in Flipkart, one of the country's major e-commerce platforms. As India's digital economy and consumer markets expand, it offers a long-term opportunity that's difficult to replicate through traditional U.S. retail operations.

Likewise, Sam's Club is another important part of this opportunity, as its membership-based warehouse model generates recurring revenue and encourages customer loyalty and repeat spending. In particular, the format is seeing significant potential in markets such as China and Mexico, where membership growth and digital adoption can support expansion.

Still, investors should weigh that growth potential against the stock's valuation and risks.

The vast majority of Wall Street analysts covering the stock rate it a buy or strong buy, with an average price target of $131.45. These days, the stock trades at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of about 38, so many investors are already expecting meaningful growth. Still, Walmart's strong competitive position and growth opportunities help justify that premium.

That said, owning Walmart isn't without risks. For one, its enormous size makes growth challenging, while competition from other retailers could pressure margins and market share. Despite that, Walmart's staying power comes from sticking to the business model that has sustained it for decades, while finding new ways to grow and adapt.

Ultimately, Walmart has spent 53 consecutive years proving it can continue to reward shareholders. Looking ahead, with e-commerce, advertising, and memberships creating new opportunities, its growth runway is far from over. And that's why investors might consider buying shares before the new year and holding them for life.
