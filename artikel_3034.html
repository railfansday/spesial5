Tech Stock Sell-Off: Here's Why Apple Has Been Immune

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

While rivals pour billions into data centers, Apple is putting AI into the devices people already own, and its cash flow shows the difference.

By Patrick Sanders – Nov 25, 2026 at 3:00PM EST

Artificial intelligence (AI) stocks have taken a beating in November. The tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index is down 15% this month on fears that AI spending has gotten out of hand. But the world's largest company seems immune to the problem.

Smartphone maker Apple (AAPL +0.43%) is bucking the trend. Its shares are virtually unchanged for the month, and in 2026 it's posting generous gains, up more than 25%, while many of its AI peers slump.

And there's a good reason why. As other tech companies spend heavily on AI infrastructure, Apple is focused on bringing AI capabilities directly to its 2.5 billion devices currently in service.

That strategy is looking pretty smart right now.

Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon are investing heavily in enormous cloud computing networks. Meta Platforms is working on "personal superintelligence," and Tesla is focused on robotics and Cybercabs.

Apple's strategy, meanwhile, is focused on improving the experience for people who already own its smartphones, personal computers, tablets, and smart-home devices. Part of that strategy is integrating its devices with Siri AI, its improved personal assistant that launched this year. "Siri AI marks a new chapter in AI at Apple. It is a completely reimagined assistant that is powerful, personal, private, and deeply relevant to our users' daily lives," CEO John Ternus said.

That's why it doesn't need to dominate the AI infrastructure business to benefit from artificial intelligence. It needs to give customers compelling reasons to buy another iPhone, Mac, or iPad.

Apple's financial results suggest that its core business remains exceptionally healthy.

It generated approximately $136 billion in free cash flow during fiscal 2026 (ended Sept. 26), up nearly 38% from the previous year. That's money remaining after capital expenditures, giving Apple tremendous flexibility to return cash to shareholders, invest in new products, and strengthen its business. Meanwhile, revenue jumped 15% to $478.8 billion for the fiscal year.

Apple's balance sheet has allowed its stock to hold steady even as rising memory chip prices squeeze hardware margins. Management expects gross margins of 45% to 46% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, down from 47.9% in Q4 2026. But its services business, which carries gross margins of around 76%, provides an important financial cushion.

Apple has something that many AI infrastructure investors are still waiting to see from their investments: enormous cash generation without the same requirement to spend billions building the infrastructure that makes it possible.
