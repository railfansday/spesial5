There's a 99% Chance That Democrats Retake the House, and History Says This Shift Will Have a Huge Impact on Stocks

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Nearly a century of S&P 500 return data shows that a worst-case scenario is likely to unfold -- but this isn’t the complete story.

Throughout the day, Americans from across our great nation have headed to the polls or mailed in their ballots to make their voices heard. According to popular prediction market Kalshi, as of 8:50 p.m. ET, the odds have skyrocketed to better than 99% that Democrats will retake control of the House of Representatives. Regardless of what happens in the Senate, President Trump would be working with a divided Congress come January 2027.

Based on what history tells us, a split Congress can significantly alter annualized return expectations for the Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI -0.38%), S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.36%), and Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +0.83%).

President Trump will likely be working with a divided Congress in the new year. Image source: Official White House Photo by Joyce N. Boghosian.

If prediction markets prove accurate and Democrats do retake one or both houses of Congress, President Donald Trump's second nonconsecutive term will mirror his first. He'll have had two years of full Republican congressional control in the first half of each term, and a divided Congress in the second half of both.

To some extent, congressional division is welcome by Wall Street. Political gridlock makes it virtually impossible to pass major tax and spending legislation. In other words, personal and corporate income tax law should remain unchanged for the foreseeable future.

DEMOCRATIC SWEEP? UBS SEES 50% ODDS. UBS assigns a 50% probability to Democrats winning both chambers, while prediction markets put Democratic control of the House at 94% and Senate at 77%. UBS warns a sweep could complicate Trump's fiscal agenda and 2027 debt-ceiling negotiations, while tariffs and AI investment remain largely insulated.

But a congressional shake-up does have its sticking points. For instance, debt-ceiling negotiations would be far more challenging. Additionally, Trump's desire to minimize the regulation of artificial intelligence would clash with Democratic lawmakers who favor much tougher oversight of the stock market's top catalyst.

However, the biggest worry for investors comes from nearly a century of historical return data. A few years ago, online retirement planning site Retirement Researcher examined the average annual return of the S&P 500 from 1926 through 2023 under four political scenarios, yielding one outlier:

Statistically, no political scenario has produced less desirable returns for stock investors than what's expected to unfold on Election Night. But this also isn't the complete story.

While nearly a century of data points to lower average annual returns for the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq Composite during President Trump's final two years in the White House, investors are in better shape than this data suggests.

For starters, a 7.33% average annual return still (pardon the apropos pun) trumps the long-term average annual return of other major asset classes, such as commodities, real estate, and Treasury bonds.

These next six months are historically the best of the year, but they are even better in a midterm year. In fact, 19 for 19 times we've seen November - April higher when starting in a midterm year. Will this time be different? https://t.co/kebp03JxpR

Furthermore, Carson Group's Chief Market Strategist, Ryan Detrick, notes that the six months following midterm elections have historically been exceptionally generous to investors since 1950, with November higher 19 of 19 times. In fact, the S&P 500 hasn't been down in the six- or 12-month period following a midterm election over the last 75 years.

In other words, even though history shows that near-term returns may be tempered a bit as President Trump and a divided Congress learn to work together, the long-term outlook for the Dow Jones Industrial Average, S&P 500, and Nasdaq Composite remains as bright as ever.
