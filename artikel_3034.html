Stock Market Today, Nov. 19: CoreWeave Falls as Profit-Taking Follows Anthropic IPO Pricing Above Range

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Heavy trading in CoreWeave shares came as investors weighed financing costs and demand for AI cloud capacity, today, Nov. 19, 2026.

By Robert Izquierdo – Nov 19, 2026 at 5:33PM EST

CoreWeave (CRWV -6.11%), a specialized cloud infrastructure provider, closed at $44.91, down 6.10%. The stock moved after Anthropic priced its IPO above range, which initially lifted AI infrastructure sentiment, resulting in profit taking before concerns over AI costs and rising interest rates ultimately dragged down shares. Investors are watching demand for neocloud capacity and financing costs next.

Trading volume reached 76.1 million shares, coming in about 63% above its three-month average of 46.5 million shares. CoreWeave IPO'd in 2025 and has grown 12% since going public.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.02%) closed at 7,176, down 2.02%, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -2.25%) finished at 23,879, down 2.25%. In AI infrastructure and data center services, Nebius Group (NBIS -9.39%) fell 9.39% to $96.92, and IREN (IREN -5.45%) dropped 5.45% to $14.41 as investors weighed neocloud growth and capital needs.

On Thursday, Nov. 19, Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above the marketed range, boosting appetite for AI infrastructure names. This helped CoreWeave shares early in the day before profit-taking set in across neocloud stocks.

CoreWeave initially surged over 9% in morning trading to an intraday high of $52.86, helped by co-founder and Chief Strategy Officer Brian Venturo's announcement that he personally purchased $30 million of CoreWeave's 2031 unsecured bonds.

The company posted impressive results for the third quarter. CoreWeave beat Q3 revenue estimates as sales soared by more than $1 billion from Q2, and 168% year over year to $3.7 billion. It also raised 2026 guidance, which remains the clearest fundamental backdrop for the stock's valuation. Earlier this month, CoreWeave said it targets adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028.

However, these positives were offset by the company's ongoing reliance on debt to fuel its AI infrastructure expansion. With inflation remaining persistently high, interest rates are likely to continue moving up. This adds to the cost of CoreWeave's debt payments, with a debt burden exceeding $40 billion at the end of Q3.

If the artificial intelligence boom begins to cool, CoreWeave's business could face trouble. That said, the company signed a multi-year agreement with Anthropic earlier this year to provide AI computing power, and with Anthropic's successful IPO, CoreWeave's prospects may not be as precarious as they seem to some on Wall Street.
