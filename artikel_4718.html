As a therapist, I hear every year about my clients' anxieties around holiday gatherings. Sometimes, the worry is rooted in knowing they will have to field awkward and inappropriate questions from family members and friends. So, I put together this little cheat sheet of questions to avoid asking this holiday season, what you can ask instead, and how you can respond if someone asks you one of these questions.

Regardless of how close you think you are to someone, do not ask questions about their reproductive plans unprompted. Do not ask people about their plans for marriage. Again, if it's relevant, they will offer the information up voluntarily. (When I got engaged, I opened with it in pretty much all non-work conversations for several months.) Some people choose not to get married, and your question comes across as judgmental. Others want to get married but have not met the "right person," and bringing this up could be hurtful. Just don't ask.

Can we as a society please agree that commenting on someone else's body is just not appropriate? It is not helpful, and even if you are trying to give a compliment, your words could still be hurtful or harmful. Maybe they lost weight because of an illness, or maybe you are complimenting the results of disordered eating. Just don't comment on people's bodies, okay?

People choose not to drink alcohol for a number of reasons, which they may or may not feel comfortable sharing with you or with others in the room. While it is appropriate to offer someone who is of age a drink, if they decline, let it go. There is no need for follow-up questions, and it is absolutely inappropriate to pressure someone to have a drink. Remember those peer pressure talks from elementary school?
