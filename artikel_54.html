Labor policy backflip will allow more backpackers to work in regional Australia

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Number of people allowed to stay for third year on holidaymaker visa to be increased from 5,000 to 25,000 under revised plan

The federal government has backpedalled on its plan to cut backpacker numbers, arguing the change is the “best possible outcome” for regional industries who had criticised the crackdown because they rely on travellers for labour.

On Tuesday, the Albanese government announced it would lift the proposed cap on third-year working holiday visas, boost regional work requirements and delay the start of a new ballot system.

The home affairs minister, Tony Burke, had announced in a major speech in September plans to cap the number of third-year backpackers to 5,000 from the current level of about 31,000.

Burke said on Tuesday that the cap would now instead be set at 25,000 places. The home affairs minister claimed despite the increase, the net overseas migration (Nom) target would not change, due to a go-slow on approving UK backpacker visas.

“From the day after the [September] speech, myself, the assistant ministers and the department started consultation with industry to work through what’s the best landing place to deliver the total numbers we’re talking about, but to do it in a way that works best for industry,” Burke told reporters.

“There was some slower processing generally happening before the announcement I made, and we have continued the slower processing with UK only since then. That’s provided the space that allowed me to go to the 5,000 to 25,000.”

The major reforms to working holiday visas unveiled in September included a ballot for backpackers seeking to stay a second or third year which would require them to work in the regions.

Under the original proposal, 45,000 backpackers would be allowed to remain for a second year and would be required to work at least three months in a regional area, and just 5,000 places would be allocated for workers for a third year.

The ballot, due to come into effect in November will now be delayed until early next year. From 1 March 2027, backpackers will be required to work in regional areas for four months, instead of three. (The changes won’t apply to travellers who finish their current regional work by the end of February.)

Burke said the government would introduce a new “guarantee” for backpackers granted second- and third-year visas as part of the ballot process.

“From December, people will be able to apply for the guarantee. And from January, every month we’ll be issuing these guarantees. If you receive the guarantee and you do the regional work … then you’ve got the guarantee for the next year,” Burke said. “We’ll be issuing those every month.”

The minister said the extra requirement was “the best possible outcome” for regional areas.

Making backpackers do 120 days of regional work instead of 88 meant that even with the reduction in visa numbers there was a boost in the overall workforce, Burke said.

The changes followed months of consultation with industry, regional and agricultural groups who warned of worker shortages.

The Nationals leader, Matt Canavan, said the initial policy announcement had done “massive damage” to small businesses and farmers in regional areas.

“The damage has been done to confidence in the sector and we still haven’t seen a cut to migration,” Canavan told reporters on Tuesday.

“Having slightly increased these backpacker number caps in this announcement today, the question has got to go to the government, surely the [net] overseas migration figure is now going up further.”

The government has set a net overseas migration target for 2026-27 of 245,000, and plabs to reduce that to 225,000 in 2027-28.

Workers from the UK are exempt from the caps under a free trade agreement signed in 2021. Sources say the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, is in discussions with his UK counterpart, Andy Burnham, to force British backpackers to also do regional work going forward.

Burke said he was optimistic about negotiations.

“The conversations … have been going very well. But until every detail is landed on, I’m not going to get ahead of an announcement,” he said.

“The United Kingdom have a good understanding of the issues that we need to resolve, so I’m not going to put a timeline on it. But I will say I’m very optimistic.”

The Accommodation Australia chief executive, James Goodwin, said Tuesday’s backflip showed the government had listened to concerns from the sector.

“These are sensible and welcome changes that recognise the important contribution working holidaymakers make,” Goodwin said. “Minister Burke has listened to industry concerns and responded with changes that will make a meaningful difference.”

The government has felt pressure to reduce Nom which has become a contested debate.

In September, ahead of the government’s original announcement, One Nation proposed cutting migration numbers by 750,000 over three years by drastically reducing international student and family visas. That policy has since disappeared from One Nation’s website.

The Coalition has also flagged a reduction in net overseas migration.
