Martin O'Neill 'leapt out of his seat in utter disbelief' after what he saw in Celtic win vs Kilmarnock

Martin O'Neill was incensed when Kasper Hogh's opener for Celtic against Kilmarnock on Sunday was disallowed.

The Hoops ultimately won 1-0, with the breakthrough provided midway through the first half by Liam Scales.

The pressure was on at Parkhead, with the champions responding to failure to win any of their previous two Scottish Premiership clashes.

But had Celtic not found the breakthrough, a serious inquest into Hogh's disallowed goal would have undoubtedly followed.

The 25-year-old's strike had initially been allowed, before referee John Beaton was sent to the monitor for another look.

He did not take a long time to come to a decision, disallowing the goal and awarding a free-kick for a challenge by Mika Baur earlier in the move.

Celtic ultimately should have extended their lead in the second half, but the game remained close.

Benjamin Nygren saw his penalty saved, while others missed other great chances to put the game to bed.

And it almost came back to bite them, with Ieuan Owen going extremely close to finding the net in the final minutes.

It could well have been a very different game if Hogh's goal had been allowed to stand, however.

The summer signing looked to have finally broken his goal duck, which has plagued him since his hat-trick against Kilmarnock at the start of August.

It was a good finish, too, but it would be chalked off just minutes later when Beaton was advised to go to the screen.

Mika Baur was adjudged to have fouled Greg Kiltie in the build-up, seemingly going through the man to get to the ball.

The decision was a soft one, and O'Neill was far from impressed. "Martin O’Neill leapt out of his seat in utter disbelief, there," Amy Canavan told BBC Sport Scotland.

"On first viewing, Mika Baur’s nudge looked minimal, but John Beaton didn’t spend much time at the monitor."

Ultimately, O'Neill will have been pleased to see his way come away with the three points, ending a difficult run of form.

Celtic have opened a four-point gap at the top of the table for the time being, with Rangers holding a game in hand.

"After Thursday night's exertions, I thought we were not at our very, very best today," O'Neill told Celtic TV.

"Having said that, we created a number of good chances in the game. Sam Johnstone makes a big, big save - a double save for us in the first 90 seconds - and we're just really delighted to have won.

"Big, big effort Thursday night, coming back on Sunday again, and we got the breakthrough and we won."
