Senators balk at Duffy’s $50bn air traffic ask as FAA pauses routers

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ted Cruz doubts Congress will fund the full air traffic control plan, and Democrats want answers by Monday. An FAA official signed off the Arkansas work, The Washington Post reported.

US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy speaks at NASA Headquarters in Washington in July 2025

Senate Republicans pushed back on Tuesday against Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy’s $50bn request to overhaul US air travel. On the same day, the FAA said it had paused installing the routers linked to Friday’s nationwide ground stop.

Duffy and FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford pitched the plan to Republican senators at their weekly lunch, Axios reported. The administration sent the formal request in a letter that morning. The $50.1bn plan has five roughly equal parts, including $10bn to modernise air traffic control equipment, according to the Associated Press.

“I don’t think the full $50 billion will be funded. Some of that is not safety related,” said Senator Ted Cruz, who chairs the Senate Commerce Committee.

Cruz said the administration should first show it can spend the $12.5bn Congress approved last year. Senator John Kennedy said Congress would not hand over $50bn, but could pay to replace copper wires with fibre optics. He also faulted Duffy for raising the ask from $30bn after the ground stop. Senators Rick Scott and Jon Husted said airlines and other users should pay more of the bill.

Democrats are wary too. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said the administration does not know how to use the money it already has. Duffy said he hoped Congress would not “piecemeal” the request. He is still open to splitting it up, he said.

The FAA switched off the function behind the trouble on the Juniper routers where it began. It did the same on more than 60 other Juniper routers and paused new installations, it said in a statement. The Wall Street Journal first reported the pause, according to CNBC. Older Cisco routers, in place for 25 years, are running the network for now, AP reported.

Duffy said the FAA had recreated what happened at the El Dorado, Arkansas, site. He said 68 earlier installations of the same routers had worked well.

“But the root cause of what happened, we don’t have clarity yet,” Duffy said.

He called claims that AI played a part “completely unfounded”, CNBC reported. The FBI is helping with the investigation.

Democrats on the Senate Commerce Committee asked the FAA and the Transportation Department to explain the failure in a letter. They want answers by 23 November. The FAA plans to switch more than 3,600 routers to Juniper devices, the letter says. About 400 sit in place so far, and 69 of them are operational.

“Identifying the device that failed does not establish why it failed,” the senators wrote.

Duffy has questioned the work of the contractor, L3Harris. But an FAA official oversaw, tested and signed off the Arkansas work eight minutes before the ground stop, The Washington Post reported. Asked for comment, the FAA pointed to Duffy’s remarks, in which he said L3Harris should “hire better minds”.

The ground stop and the weekend recovery cost the four largest US airlines $345m, Bernstein analysts estimated, according to AP. The ground stop came a day after the Microsoft 365 outage and three days before a software bug disrupted US banks.
