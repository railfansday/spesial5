On Sunday, Nvidia’s Board of Directors announced an independent investigation into founder and CEO Jensen Huang. The inquiry follows multiple intelligence and media reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort involving a romantic relationship with a suspected Chinese spy — along with the admission of an affair.

The investigation occurs against the backdrop of a severe tech stock sell-off, with nearly $2 trillion erased because of fears of an AI bubble. Nvidia, which has served as the cornerstone of this gold rush, dropped 4% in overnight trading value, as it’s now under a microscope of geopolitical and governance concerns.

Jensen Huang’s internal email to Nvidia employees has now been leaked. In it, he wrote: “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did.”

Shortly after, Nvidia’s Board of Directors officially announced an independent investigation into the matter, overseen by outside counsel.

The board states it “prioritizes U.S. national and economic security,” and while it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, it will examine all available information. The board also notes that Huang is fully cooperating with the review.

Lawmakers have already expressed alarm over the national security implications of the relationship, as members of the House Foreign Affairs and Intelligence Committees already requested Huang provide a closed-door briefing. The ball was already rolling on this part.

Meanwhile, speaking to reporters last night, President Trump downplayed the severity of the allegations against his AI adviser. Trump states, “I think he’s an honorable guy. I think he probably has an excuse for it,” adding that he still intends to bring Huang along on an upcoming trip to Asia.
