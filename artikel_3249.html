Ariana Grande’s Best On-Screen Roles

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Ariana Grande may be one of the biggest pop stars in the world, but she’s also a true multi-hyphenate, known for her uncanny impressions and acting chops. Grande can hit a killer vocal run, produce a sleek beat, and steal a scene with terrific comedic timing. (It’s called range.) Her Hollywood career has grown with her pop star status — she started as a Broadway baby, singing her heart out in the musical 13, and eventually became one of Nickelodeon’s most beloved airheads as she played Cat Valentine on Victorious.

Now, she’s starring alongside Robert De Niro in the new movie Focker-In-Law, which is out this week. So what better time to look back at the roles that have helped the megastar get to this point? Here are some of her most impressive moments both on the big and small screens.

Part of what makes Grande’s acting career so much fun to watch is that she never does what anyone is expecting her to do — and she’s not above extreme silliness. That makes her Zoolander 2 performance particularly entertaining. It’s extremely brief, but she pops on-screen just in time to have a wild orgy with Hansel (Owen Wilson). The scene marked her first time meeting Ben Stiller, who she reunites with in Focker-In-Law. She looked back at their meet-cute fondly during a recent appearance on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon: “He was directing me in an orgy scene in Zoolander 2 with a hippo and Susan Sarandon,” she shared. “So right off the bat, we had a comfortability.” —Julyssa Lopez

Grande is one of the best parts of Adam McKay’s 2021 satire Don’t Look Up. She plays Riley Bina, a major pop star who is out-of-touch with the world — and the fact that a humanity-ending comet is headed Earth’s way. Once people start believing PhD student Kate Dibiasky (Jennifer Lawrence) and her advisor Dr. Randall Mindy (Leonardo DiCaprio) about their chilling prediction, Bina performs her new song “Just Look Up” with her cheating boyfriend, DJ Chello (Kid Cudi). The scene finds Grande delivering killer one-liners like, “Listen to the goddamn qualified scientists, we really fucked it up this time” while suspended onstage and sporting a ridiculous get-up of white feathers. According to McKay, many of the song’s lyrics were improvised by Grande. —Maya Georgi

In 2013, Nickelodeon adapted Gordon Korman’s 2008 novel Swindle, shaping it into a made-for-television heist movie revolving around two friends who set out to recover a $1.2 million baseball card. They form an unlikely group with other kids from their school harboring secret skills, and Grande steps in as a cheerleader with an undercover geeky side. The film came during her Sam & Cat days, and teamed her up with co-star Jennette McCurdy, while also letting her go beyond her flaky Cat persona. —J.L.

When Grande reprises the role of Glinda for the second half of the Wicked tale, she’s planning her wedding to Prince Fiyero, all of Emerald City idolizes her, and everything is as she always wished — or is it? As Glinda reevaluates her life and decides to change paths to help her friend Elphaba (Cynthia Erivo), there are fewer of the rollicking comedy moments that made Grande’s performance in the first film so fun to watch. But with big songs like “I Couldn’t Be Happier,” “The Girl in the Bubble,” and the booming duet “For Good,” she makes clear why she was a natural fit for the iconic role. —Kate Storey

Guest-starring on the campy horror-comedy television show Scream Queens as Chanel #2 was Ariana Grande’s first adult role after leaving Nickelodeon’s Sam & Cat to focus on her music. Creator Ryan Murphy said in 2015 that initially, Grande “was just going to do one episode, but she loved the process so much that she wanted to do more.” Even though she played a corpse in a few of those episodes, Ari’s comedic skills shine through, especially in a scene where her character’s ghost visits Emma Roberts’ Chanel #1. Grande is hilarious when speaking about her afterlife in hell. Whether her character is that she’s in a bad mood because Adolf Hitler was motorboating her boobs or that Jesus broke into heaven and stole the dinosaurs, she steals every scene. —Alison Weinflash

Let’s look beyond the fact that Cat Valentine seemingly has a legitimate lobotomy in the middle of the Victorious run. There would not be an Ariana Grande without the exposure, preparation, and genuine war stories that the cast ofVictorious went through. While the will-they-won’t-they nature of Cat’s relationship with Robbie (Matt Bennett) was finally resolved in the apocalyptic music video for “One Last Time,” the most memorable musical moments of the series all involved Grande: from the karaoke bar rendition of “Give it Up” to the “They didn’t say I couldn’t sing” riff on the Victorious iCarly collab “Leave It All to Shine.” As Cat, Grande earned her place in the Hollywood Arts High School hall of fame and started her path toward becoming a household name. —C.T. Jones

Ariana Grande has hosted SNL three times now, and she always makes her appearances memorable by leveraging her exceptional impersonation skills, showcasing her comedy agility, and refusing to shy away from being wildly silly. We’ve seen her play Jennifer Lawrence during a Family Feud recreation and playing a shy intern who saves her radio station with her ability to sound like everyone from Shakira to Celine Dion. But her most beloved contributions might be in an off-key bridesmaid in the famed Domingo skit and giving us a hilarious take on Home Alone. —J.L.

Grande’s character in Focker In Law, Olivia Jones, is a multi-hyphenate not unlike the actress who portrays her. Just swap the gold records for FBI credentials, throw in vegan cooking chops, and you have a future in-law (to grandpa Jack Byrnes, reprised brilliantly by Robert De Niro) hell-bent on entering the “circle of trust” in record time. Grande plays the ultra-competitive Liv with exacting comedic timing, offering the perfect foil for Ben Stiller’s goofball insecurity. —Shirley Halperin

It takes self-determination, drive, and a lot of pink to make the world’s most coveted role your own. Ariana Grande wasn’t the frontrunner when Universal first announced it would be making a Wicked film. But the Petal singer fought to claim Glinda’s glitter wand. “Oz has been my safe space for as long as I can remember,” she wrote in an open letter to fans before the film’s premiere. It’s hard to imagine a world where the gleeful shrieks of Grande’s “Popular” outro didn’t exist — and her participation in the John M. Chu duology is a career-defining moment, proof the former Nick star is never far from her musical theater roots. —C.T.J.
