Netflix has confirmed its slate of new movies and shows for this week, and it’s packed with new originals and some older favorites set to boost its back catalog. Over the next seven days, the streaming service will add 31 movies and shows; that’s a lot to consider, so I’m picking the highlights.

First up, there’s “Power.” Okay, so it’s not a new series, but on the flip side, Netflix is adding all six seasons of this 2010s Starz show, giving you dozens of episodes of NYC-based drama to binge. But if only brand new will do, check out “The Body,” a fresh original that sees a group of schoolgirls cause a serious stir in their town after an initiation goes wrong.

If you’re feeling the festive spirit already, Tyler Perry’s latest is “The Gospel of Christmas,” a faith-focused movie that combines feel-good holiday vibes with seriously powerful singing. Or if you’re not quite ready for a Christmas movie (I feel you. It’s too early for me, too), “Heartland” is a new mystery thriller that explores the darker side of Music City.

Finally, Netflix is clearly still in a Halloween mood as it’s adding two of the 21st century’s most buzzy horrors. You could watch “Insidious,” but personally I’d recommend streaming “Smile" instead. This chilling movie went viral due to its use of ridiculously creepy smiling faces.

These are just some of the movies and shows heading to Netflix this week. Alongside my top picks, you’ll find a full schedule of everything new on Netflix over the next seven days. And for a complete overview, here’s a guide to everything new on Netflix in November 2026.

What it's about: “Power” introduces James “Ghost” St. Patrick (Omari Hardwick), a man who has it all. He own Manhattan’s most exclusive nightclub, Truth. But behind the scenes, he’s also running a drug empire that caters exclusively to the rich and influential. Hoping to use the opening of Truth to go “legitimate” and get out of the criminal game, when everything he loves and has worked to build is threatened, the situation becomes increasingly dramatic.

Why you should watch it: “Power” might have premiered on Starz in 2014, wrapping up in 2020, but it’s very much in the mold of 2000s television, aka it’s heavy on the cliffhangers. That makes it an excellent binge-watch, as each episode's conclusion will have you eager to watch the next immediately. So, it’s good news that you’ve got 63 episodes to binge.

Watch "Power" seasons 1-6 on Netflix starting November 9

What it's about: Halloween might be in the rearview mirror, but if you’re looking for dark stories, “The Body” will deliver. In this eight-episode series, a group of misbehaving Catholic school girls triggers mass hysteria in their town after a dance-team initiation goes wrong. Showrunner Quinn Shephard describes it as “a love letter to all the ’90s and Y2K teen movies and erotic thrillers” she loved growing up in an interview with Netflix's Tudum.

Why you should watch it: “The Body” appears to be another Netflix offering this week that prides itself on cliffhangers. Emmy-nominated writer-director Shepherd has also called it her “most personal” story yet. From the trailer, it looks like a Netflix Original thick with dark atmosphere that will build up plenty of tension, likely before a release in the big finale.

Watch "The Body" on Netflix starting November 11

What it's about: Tyler Perry’s fourth Netflix project of the year is “The Gospel of Christmas,” the streamer’s second original holiday movie of 2026. The Christmas-themed flick sees a struggling pastor (Tank) inherit his family’s church in a fractured New York neighbourhood. With the threat of foreclosure and temptations around every corner, this lapsed man of faith needs the support of the community, and turns out, they need him, too.

Watch "The Gospel of Christmas" on Netflix starting November 11

What it's about: “Heartland” is a movie set in Nashville, so naturally country music plays a big role, but it’s not a musical drama about a young starlet trying to make it big in Music City. It follows Misty Jones (Jessica Chastain), a reclusive former country star, who comes out of seclusion to investigate the disappearance of her niece (Carter Faith), forcing her to reckon with Nashville’s hidden underbelly and also her own past.

Why you should watch it: Usually when a movie involves a character delving into a city’s underbelly, LA or New York is the main setting, so I’m quite intrigued to see what Nashville is like behind the curtain. Plus, the Oscar-winning Jessica Chastain in the lead role is a draw; she’s an excellent actor. Consider this a new mystery thriller that's definitely on my watchlist.

Watch "Heartland" on Netflix starting November 13

What it's about: After witnessing the brutal death of a patient, Dr. Rose Cotter (Sosie Bacon) begins to experience terrifying visions and disturbing occurrences. Turns out she’s been cursed by a sinister, supernatural entity with a fondness for unnerving smiles. To break the curse and save herself from a bloody fate, she turns to an old flame (Kyle Gallner) for help.

Why you should watch it: I guess Netflix really didn’t get the memo about Halloween season ending, as it’s adding both “Smile” and “Insidious” this week. If you’ve not quite had your fill of scary movies, then “Smile” is definitely my top pick of the two. It’s an excellent chiller, with a particularly haunting quality. The sequel is even better, but sadly not on Netflix.
