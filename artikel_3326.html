Man Found Guilty of Igniting Blaze That Became the Devastating Palisades Fire

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

A jury found a former Los Angeles resident guilty of starting what became the deadly Palisades Fire of January 2025, the city’s most destructive wildfire in its history.

Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, was convicted of one count of destruction of property by means of fire and one count of arson affecting property used in interstate commerce. Federal prosecutors accused Rinderknecht of intentionally starting the original blaze, known as the Lachman fire. While the initial fire was quickly suppressed, unbeknownst to firefighters and other authorities at the time, embers of it continued to burn underground. On Jan. 7, heavy winds reignited the fire, causing it to surface and spread. The ensuing Palisades fire was one of the most destructive wildfires in Los Angeles history, killing 12 people and decimating 23,448 acres of land and nearly 7,000 structures across the Pacific Palisades region.

“Twelve people lost their lives and thousands lost their homes because of Jonathan Rinderknecht’s malicious conduct,” said Attorney General Todd Blanche in a statement. “Thanks to the persistence and hard work of our prosecutors in the Central District of California, he will be held accountable.”

“Jonathan Rinderknecht deliberately ignited a fire that killed 12 people and devastated an entire community,” said First Assistant United States Attorney Bill Essayli. “We were determined to hold him accountable, and today’s guilty verdict delivers a measure of justice for the families who lost loved ones and the thousands whose lives were upended.”

After a federal jury in June deadlocked in favor of acquittal in Rinderknecht’s first trial in June, federal prosecutors narrowed their case to charge him with the two arson-related felonies rather than the three charges in the mistrial.

The prosecution argued that Rinderknecht, who was working on New Year’s Eve as an Uber driver on Dec. 31, 2024, was angry and frustrated because he was unable to find companionship on New Year’s. According to an affidavit from an investigator for the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, two of his passengers described him as “agitated and angry” that night. He allegedly drove to a Pacific Palisades trailhead, where he repeatedly listened to and watched a music video for the rap song, “Un Zder, Un Thé,” about despair, and tried calling an old friend, but was unable to connect with them.Prosecutors said that Rinderknecht started the Lachman Fire just after midnight, and that around 12:12 a.m. on Jan. 1, 2025, environmental sensing platforms indicated the Lachman fire had begun. They said that Rinderknecht called 911 several times, but didn’t get through because his iPhone was out of range. By the time he finally connected with the police, the fire had already been reported by others in the neighborhood.
