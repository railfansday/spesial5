Delek Logistics (DKL) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Libby complex commissioning and a $220 million equity raise defined the quarter.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Delek Logistics Partners, LP (DKL +1.03%) reported third-quarter performance driven by the build-out of its Permian Basin infrastructure and a significant equity offering that strengthened its balance sheet. Management reported that the company completed construction of its first sour gas compressor station and acid gas injection well at the Libby complex, which is now in the commissioning phase. The company stated that higher commodity prices and positive Waha gas pricing are supporting sustained upstream activity, driving demand for its three-stream service platform encompassing crude, gas, and water. Financial operations focused on extending debt maturities and reducing leverage to 3.85x, while the Board approved the 55th consecutive quarterly distribution increase. Management indicated that future growth will rely on increasing utilization at the Libby plants and pursuing disciplined organic expansion projects.

The system is currently in the commissioning phase, and we are expected to start up the system in the next few weeks. With increasing flexibility, we are well positioned to serve our customer objective of long-term oil and gas production growth in the Delaware Basin. From DKL's perspective, this will bring additional gas volume to our plant, increase overall system utilization and likely result in a need for overall expansion of our processing capacity. Moving to crude, both DPG and DGG continue to see strong performance, reflecting sustained production across our operating footprint.

We look forward to further optimizing and growing the system. Our water business is also performing well, and we are continuing to explore growth opportunities in this space. Our combined gas, crude and water offering in the Permian Basin has improved our competitive position and established a platform for future growth. We will continue to pursue these growth opportunities in a disciplined manner while maintaining a focus on leverage and coverage ratios. We also intend to remain good stewards of our stakeholder capital. During the quarter, we extended our debt maturities and completed our equity offering to support growth investment while maintaining balance sheet strength.

Our Board of Directors has approved our 55th consecutive quarterly distribution increase, raising the distribution to $1.14 per unit. This is an extraordinary achievement of distribution growth and it reflects the exceptional work of our team. This financial discipline is a cornerstone of our company philosophy. With the foundation we have built and the opportunities ahead, we are confident in our ability to continue delivering sustainable growth and long-term value for our unitholders. Mark Hobbs: Thanks, Avigal. Since joining Delek Logistics in the lead role in July, I have been energized by our exceptional team and the amazing opportunities that we have in front of us at DKL. As Avigal mentioned, we see tremendous growth potential for Delek Logistics as we are uniquely positioned to meet our customers' increasing needs for midstream services across crude, gas and water in the Permian Basin. We continue to see heightened activity by producers in securing acreage and future drilling locations in the Northern Delaware Basin and Permian rig activity is near its highest level of the year, with production continuing to set new records.

Crude and NGL prices remain elevated as a result of the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. And in gas, Waha prices have turned firmly positive, a significant improvement from the negative pricing that we saw in the first half of the year as new takeaway capacity continues to come online, which has provided much needed stabilization for local gas markets in the Permian Basin. Together, these higher commodity prices support sustained upstream activity, driving increased demand for our 3-stream service platform. Turning to our business. We continued to operate well in the third quarter, delivering safe and reliable midstream services for our customers.

During the third quarter, we achieved higher volumes in our gas business versus the second quarter and made significant progress advancing our sour gas solution in the Delaware Basin. We completed construction of our first sour gas compressor station and are in the commissioning phase of our AGI well located at our Libby complex. We are also executing on projects to further optimize our gas system that will not only increase our gas volumes and the utilization of our Libby processing plants, but also improve reliability and redundancy for our customers.

Looking forward, we continue to see the need for sour gas services from our customers and the sour solution we have developed will unlock future growth for producers and demand for our services. As a result, we are evaluating options for future investment that will further expand our sour gas processing capacity at the Libby complex, providing critical support for our customers' production growth in the region. Moving to crude, our crude gathering business in both the Delaware and the Midland operated well and maintained high reliability during the third quarter. We experienced a slight decrease in crude volumes in the third quarter versus the second quarter, primarily due to timing of well connections.

However, we remain well positioned for future growth, especially in the Delaware Basin, where we continue to see positive results from our 3-stream offering. Our decline in water volumes in the third quarter versus the second quarter was driven by a combination of production decline and timing of wells coming online. We expect that to reverse as we move through the end of the year and continue to see tremendous opportunities to increase our produced water volumes across both the Midland and the Delaware Basins. Produced water handling and disposal continues to be a critical and increasing need of our customers.

Our scale and strategic position across the Permian Basin create unique opportunities for us to service our customers and continue to grow our water business. We continue to believe that our 3-stream offering in the Delaware Basin is a critical differentiator from our peers, and we are leveraging these services to develop creative solutions for our customers. Robert Wright: Thank you, Mark. As Avigal and Mark have highlighted, the partnership delivered another strong quarter with adjusted EBITDA of $143 million. Importantly, we achieved these results while staying disciplined on our long-term leverage and coverage targets. During the quarter, we raised approximately $220 million in net proceeds through the largest primary follow-on equity offering by a midstream MLP in the last decade. Together with our second quarter refinancing of the 2028 and 2029 senior notes, these actions have strengthened our capital structure, extended our maturity profile and positioned DKL to fund future growth while maintaining strong liquidity, which ended the quarter at approximately $1.3 billion.

We ended the quarter with leverage of 3.85x, our lowest level of the year, which gives us more capacity to fund attractive growth opportunities. As we've noted before, our 2026 growth capital program of $180 million to $190 million is expected to generate up to $15 million of run rate EBITDA in 2026 with an additional $60 million of EBITDA growth in 2027. Following our August equity offering, our sponsor, Delek ownership of Delek's Logistics common units decreased to approximately 58%. Additionally, our strong and growing third-party business continues to increase our economic separation from Delek. In 2026, on a pro forma basis, we continue to expect approximately 80% of our run rate EBITDA will come from third parties.

Turning to our results. As mentioned already, DKL posted another strong quarter with adjusted EBITDA of approximately $143 million, in line with the same period last year. Distributable cash flow as adjusted was approximately $73 million, and our DCF coverage ratio held steady at approximately 1.11x. We are also proud to announce our 55th consecutive distribution increase, which brings the quarterly distribution to $1.14 per unit. As to our segment results, starting with Gathering and Processing, adjusted EBITDA for the third quarter was $88 million, up from $83 million in the third quarter of 2025. This improvement was driven primarily by higher utilization at the Libby Gas Complex and strong realized margins in our crude business.

In Wholesale Marketing and Terminalling, adjusted EBITDA was approximately $21 million, in line with the prior year period. Storage and Transportation delivered adjusted EBITDA of $20 million compared with $19 million in the prior year period. Moving now to capital expenditures, third quarter capital spending was approximately $55 million, including $48 million of growth capital focused primarily on the continued build-out of our new sour gas gathering infrastructure. Importantly, these investments enabled the completion of our first sour gas compressor station and AGI well at our Libby Gas Complex during the quarter, further expanding our capabilities in the Delaware Basin. The remaining capital went to planned reliability and maintenance activities.

Looking ahead, as Avigal noted, we remain confident in the partnership's earnings trajectory, and we are raising our full year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to a range of $530 million to $560 million. With that, we will now open the call for questions.
