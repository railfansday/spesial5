Why Is the ‘Avengers: Doomsday’ Trailer So… Gloomy? Internet Erupts Over Latest Look at Marvel Movie

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

There’s a storm brewing, chere, and it has nothing to do with the weird Gambit (Channing Tatum) and Herbie the Robot Dunkin Donuts ads that played like once a quarter during all the football games on Sunday.

The final trailer was released ahead of Marvel’s “Avengers: Doomsday,” the mega-Sentinel, crossover-tastic 165-minute Russo Brothers opus out on December 18. And the response hasn’t been great — in part because Cyclops says, “To me, my X-Men!” and there isn’t actually a Storm.

Way back in the salad days of 2012, Joss Whedon first did an “Assemble” shot that roved in a circle all of the original “Avengers” film, while an Alan Silvestri score bwaaamped the brassy “Avengers” theme. Say whatever you like about Whedon’s strengths and weaknesses when it comes to visual style, but the first time this happened, the shot’s continuous nature served a storytelling purpose. It was the cinematic way to finally bind these crazy kids together in superhero fellowship — through the timeless crucible of New York City getting shredded into CGI bits by alien-monster things. But over the many, many Phases of the MCU, the Avengers Assemble shots have kept happening. And happening. And sure enough, the “Doomsday” trailer crescendos with a bunch of our heroes stuck in a gray-black wasteland void that looks like a glitched “Expedition 33” loading screen, with the camera straining to create that same sense of unity.

It doesn’t. At least not in the trailer. Which is why so much of the response to the finale “Doomsday” trailer has been focused on it.

As folks online have pointed out, Steve Rogers (Chris Evans) looks like he’s standing in entirely different lighting from the people closest to him. There’s no sense of texture or proximity that suggests anybody was placed against the same green screen together, let alone acting together (a problem that has been pointed out about the filmmaking process for “Doomsday” already).

No amount of money can make up for shooting for maximum flexibility — which so often can devolve into being indecisive — or for pursuing relentless revisions throughout the VFX pipeline. That is exactly what leads to shadowy, sloppily-framed environments like wherever the hell the Avengers are fighting Doctor Doom (Robert Downey Jr.) and his creations. And because the environment is so flattened and indistinct, only the barest hints of color survive. And really, only because Reed Richards (Pedro Pascal) is still wearing a coat from a different movie.

Now, what comes most naturally to Cyclops is shooting an optic blast from his eyes, and what comes most naturally to us is posting negative takes online. The trailer takes that shot out of context for nostalgia’s sake; its dramatic purpose within the film itself might be more complicated, or the setup for a twist in three different timelines’ worth of Avengers’ fortunes. But when it comes to all the cinematic crimes of artless visuals, repetitive plots, and overbearing lore that have contributed to superhero fatigue? The “Avengers: Doomsday” trailer doesn’t seem to be beating the charges.
