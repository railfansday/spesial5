WW1 Shooter Marks Remembrance Day by Making You Kill More Reliably

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

On a grim autumn morning in 1918, at 11:00 in what we call Central European Time today, the guns fell silent for the last time in the Great War. The start of a peace that would last for about two decades came on a rather heavy day all the same, with 2738 lives lost before the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month.

BlackMill Games, makers of the WW1 Game Series, have developed a reputation for always marking this special occasion, and this year is no different. Upon launching any title in the series (Verdun, Tannenberg, Isonzo and Gallipoli), you’ll be invited to take part in two minutes of silence. On top of some reflection, participating earns you a special medal in the game.

In what you could call a paradoxical move, Gallipoli has also received a special patch that plagues its biggest problem post-launch: bullets were not actually killing soldiers reliably, and BlackMill Games decided that (mostly) ends today.

Gallipoli is the most recent release in the WW1 Game Series, and while it holds the highest critical acclaim among its peers, a quantum tunneling issue has been making the battles on the Ottoman shores very unpleasant for everyone involved.

In a classic issue for multiplayer shooters, hit registration hasn’t really been working as advertised. You can imagine why this is frustrating: an Ottoman soldier jumps into your trench during a bayonet charge, you point your Lee-Enfield squarely at his chest at point-blank range, pull the trigger, and… well, nothing happens! The bullet passes straight through its target, and all you get for your troubles is a bayonet to the heart.

A quantum tunneling issue has been making the battles on the Ottoman shores very unpleasant for everyone involved.

From personal experience, the issue has been especially felt at longer ranges, which has made the sniper class, once upon a time one of the deadliest in the game, feel like spitting BBs at the enemy. At least according to an update posted by the Gallipoli development team today, it is the beginning of the end for this annoying cycle.

Gallipoli patch 2.1.2 “fixes a major issue behind the ‘hit reg’ woes,” though BlackMill is cautious in its wording. Today’s release is “a soft launch of the improvements” in the works, and due to its experimental nature, it is only available on Steam for now. The goal of this update is to “properly test the impact it has, while also getting it to a large portion of the community as quickly as possible.”

It is unclear whether this will affect cross-play compatibility with PlayStation 5 and XBOX Series X/S, and how long it will take for hit-reg improvements to cross no-man’s-land into the console trenches. Whatever the case, it’s a timely fix that PC players have been anxiously waiting for.

To commemorate Remembrance Day, BlackMill Games and partner studios are holding a sale on World War I-themed games, which you can check out here.

Not all titles in this curated collection are discounted, but you can pick up hits Verdun at a 75% discount, or have your conscience weigh heavy on you through indie command simulator Acceptable Losses.

The Great War, although not quite as forgotten as the Korean War, is still not nearly as well-depicted in videogames as World War 2. Titles like Gallipoli, Over the Top, and The Caribou Trail help show a new generation of players what these horrors were like, in an era where the witnesses are no longer around to tell their story

The least we can do is listen to their echoes through these works of art, thinking of Flanders fields where poppies grow.
