Cancer-selective gene circuits via intrinsic ribosomal frameshifting

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Ribosomal frameshifting, an uncommon translational recoding event, is best known for its regulatory roles in viral gene expression1,2, whereas its physiological relevance in mammals remains poorly understood. Here we show that specific codon pairs trigger efficient −1 frameshifting preferentially in cancer cells. Mechanistically, this recoding arises from ribosomal pausing associated with tRNA and amino acid availability, and is amplified by impaired ribosome quality control and sustained phosphorylation of ribosomal protein S6. We found that this codon-pair-specific frameshifting represents an intrinsic state of translational dysregulation, which is prevalent across cancer cells and reshapes protein synthesis. Taking advantage of this frameshifting-permissive state in cancer cells, we engineered a frameshifting-activated synthetic therapeutic circuit (FAST) that conditionally expresses the pyroptotic effector gasdermin D. FAST is activated across diverse cancer cell types, inducing potent and selective pyroptosis, while sparing normal tissues from detectable toxicity. As a proof of concept, systemic AAV9-mediated delivery of FAST in mouse models of colorectal and pancreatic cancer elicited pronounced tumour regression, robust immune activation and synergistic effects with immune checkpoint blockade. Collectively, these findings reveal codon-pair-dependent ribosomal frameshifting as a feature of translational dysregulation in cancer cells and establish translation-coupled gene circuits as a strategy for cancer-selective therapy.

Although a single codon pair is sufficient to induce significant frameshifting signals in HCT116 cells, to increase the detectable frameshifting signal in other cells and thereby facilitate analysis of both regulatory mechanisms and anti-tumour effects, we used 5× tandem repeats of the frameshifting motif in this study. c, Comparison of frameshifting activity using different repeat numbers of the frameshifting motif. For the 3×, 5× and 10× constructs, adjacent motifs were separated by a CCC codon. CCC was selected because cytosine-rich spacer sequences are less likely than A-, T- or G-rich spacers to generate premature stop codons (TAA, TAG or TGA) with adjacent sequences, thereby minimizing unintended effects on reporter expression. Numbers on the bars refer to average values (%) of the three replicates. d, Comparison of frameshifting activity when repeated frameshifting motifs were separated by different synonymous proline codons. CCC produced the lowest frameshifting activity, whereas CCG produced the highest. Because CCG is a relatively rare proline codon (RSCU = 0.46) compared with CCC (RSCU = 1.3), these results suggest that spacer codon usage can influence motif activity. In the AGAACG construct separated by CCT, the spacer introduces a − 1 frame stop codon (TAG), thereby terminating −1 frame translation. e, Frameshifting activity in HEK293T and HCT116 cells stably expressing the indicated 5× motif reporter constructs. f, Box plot comparing out-of-frame translation in the +1 and −1 frames between codon pairs with high in-frame rates (top 10%, n = 372 pairs) and low in-frame rates (bottom 10%, n = 372 pairs).
