How long-term thinking shapes the approach of Mars Snacking

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Hello and welcome to Modern CEO! I’m Stephanie Mehta, CEO and chief content officer of Mansueto Ventures. Each week this newsletter explores inclusive approaches to leadership drawn from conversations with executives and entrepreneurs, and from the pages of Inc. and Fast Company. If you received this newsletter from a friend, you can sign up to get it yourself every Monday morning.

“This may be the most challenging thing I think I’ve done in my career,” says Anton Vincent, president of Mars Snacking North America and Global Ice Cream, part of packaged goods giant Mars Inc. Vincent is reflecting on the work of integrating the assets and employees of Kellanova, maker of Pringles and Cheez-It, which Mars acquired in December 2025 for $36 billion.

Vincent, who joined Mars in 2019 after a stint as CEO of U.K. food manufacturer Greencore and a 15-year run at General Mills, says the merger is operationally complex. Mars is absorbing new manufacturing facilities and dramatically expanding beyond candy, which is often an impulse buy at checkout or a special-occasion treat. Now, it’s growing its roster of savory foods, which are more likely to be on a consumer’s shopping list and come in different packaging formats.

A recipe for success

Mars leadership is also trying to create a welcoming environment for former Kellanova associates who joined the combined company, which now boasts 12,000 snacking employees in North America and 50,000 worldwide. “We’re trying to help people to understand how to imagine a future that we’re building together,” he says. “We spend a lot of time being consistent with what we think makes Mars special but also making sure that [Kellanova associates] see themselves in it.” Vincent has sought to be inclusive, creating a leadership team that includes top Kellanova executives in the combined company’s legal, sustainability, and commercial functions, for example.

There is also another tool for building culture at the new Mars Snacking: its ownership. Mars Inc., founded in 1911, remains a family-owned business. Being privately held allows the company to make investments and decisions for the long term, Vincent says. “We always like to say we don’t think in quarters; we think in generations,” he adds. “We’re in this business to keep this business, to grow this business in all the right ways over time.”

An example of that long-term approach is the company’s sustainability plan. Mars Snacking has goals for reducing greenhouse gas emissions and using more recycled packaging, enduring commitments that executives say they must consistently consider even as they strive to meet financial goals.

Changing tastes in the food sector

Indeed, family-owned businesses are playing a major role in reshaping the food sector, which has seen robust merger and acquisition activity in recent years. Consumer tastes continue to evolve, with the rise of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs shrinking appetites and shifting preferences toward better-for-you options. These market changes are putting pressure on some food conglomerates to remake their portfolios, and family-controlled companies have swooped in. In addition to Mars, closely held Ferrero Group acquired cereal maker WK Kellogg Co for $3.1 billion in September 2025. Earlier this year, family-founded Intersnack said it would acquire snack maker Utz in a deal valued at about $3 billion. In all these cases, the acquiring companies paid a healthy premium.

“Family-controlled acquirers can deploy patient, low-cost internal capital and price deals on a combined rather than stand-alone basis, letting them justify premiums public investors won’t match,” writes Food Navigator, an industry news site. 

Vincent says Mars views the Kellanova acquisition as an opportunity to grow its snacking business and expand its presence in emerging markets. He also sees a chance to increase market share in savory snacks, where Mars is a distant second to PepsiCo’s Frito-Lay division.

Leading as a humility builder

I asked Vincent how he’s changed as a leader while navigating the challenges of the merger, and he didn’t hesitate: “I think I’ve become a lot more humble,” he says. “You have to make really big decisions. And a lot of those decisions are about people.” Earlier this year, the company announced plans to relocate its North America Snacking headquarters to Chicago.

He says he is also, in many ways, reinvigorated. The merger has afforded him an opportunity to think about his impact as a leader. “Is that leadership uplifting and moving people forward?” he asks. “Even at this late stage in my career, I’m growing.”
