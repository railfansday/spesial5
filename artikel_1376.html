Bayern Munich chief Karl-Heinz Rummenigge has sparked outrage for his comments about Jamal Musiala amid his ongoing health issues.

During pre-season, Musiala sparked concerns when he collapsed twice in the space of four days. Following the second collapse, he took to Instagram to reveal that he was being treated for a 'neurological dysfunction'.

Earlier this month, Bayern Munich's honorary president Uli Hoeness revealed that Musiala was suffering from an illness that 'isn't easily curable.'

“With him, we’re in a dilemma: he has an illness that isn’t easily curable, but which can be managed reasonably well with medication," he said.

“But whether he’ll ever regain the performance level he once had… For now, he’s still getting paid. But imagine if FC Bayern were to question his contract now and say: ‘What he’s capable of now isn’t what’s stipulated in the contract’.”

Musiala has struggled for form and fitness this season amid his health problems. He has made just eight appearances across all competitions, scoring twice and recording one assist.

Bayern board member Rummenigge has now given his thoughts on Musiala's condition. He said that the club needs to talk to the 23-year-old as he isn't justifying his lofty salary, believed to be £370,000-per-week.

“(Musiala) has a health-related issue. It's also well known that he has a contract involving very substantial sums of money. As a club, we have to consider how to deal with that situation. I think that's responsible and perfectly normal.

“What we know unfortunately suggests it will be difficult for him to get back to the level he has often shown here in this stadium.

“[We need to] talk to the player, to the family and, above all, to the agency, because, with all due respect, those sums are then no longer feasible.

“I think we’ve handled the player’s situation very carefully, and we all want him to return, ideally in the best possible shape."

He added: "I can’t imagine another club would buy him under these circumstances. We have to think about the financial implications. Uli’s wording was correct. Very serious and fair. We have an interest in seeing him return and play well. If that doesn’t happen, then we will have to think about the situation.”

Rummenigge is now being blasted online by angry football fans. One fan said: "Doesn’t sound to me like they care about his health. All they care about is the money they are paying him."

A second commented: "This is a disgrace. You don’t treat one of your greatest talents with such disrespect, especially when he needs support the most."

A third wrote: "What a terrible and disgusting statement to put out," while a fourth stated: "'What a vile way to treat a player."

Bayern manager Kompany has defended Musiala amid criticism Hoeness and Rummenigge's comments. He said, per Goal:

“He has a very clear role at this club. A coach has to be the one who believes in the players. If we manage that, everyone wins.

“If Jamal rediscovers his form and his level, then Bayern Munich win. So do the fans and, I think, German football too. Because he’s a special player. That’s my role. If a coach says or does something else, then that’s a problem, in my personal view.

“You can’t expect our star players to make the difference immediately. If Lucho Diaz plays 20 minutes next week, we can’t expect him to decide the game straight away. That’s not realistic. Jamal Musiala is in the process of being reintegrated, and Lucho will soon begin his reintegration too.

"Right now, it’s about other players. At some point, those players will be 100 per cent fit to play again, and then we’ll enjoy that again too.”
