Magic: The Gathering Designer Says Commander’s Most Divisive Staple Isn’t Going Anywhere

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Commander’s “foundational, iconic heart” is here to stay

The two best T-shirts I saw at MagicCon: Atlanta 2026 were easy to choose in a sea of delightfully nerdy references and imagery. One was black with a wall of white text spelling out the lengthy definition of Banding, a notoriously confusing Magic: The Gathering mechanic that’s now 33 years old. The other was a white shirt featuring a picture of a Sol Ring card crossed out by a red circle and slash. “BAN SOL RING,” it read.

The first shirt provides a public service and a punchline about ancient Magic history. Based on an interview conducted at MagicCon, the second is a bold demand that Magic principal designer Gavin Verhey has no interest in entertaining.

“Sol Ring is the foundational, iconic heart of the format,” Verhey tells Polygon.

Commander’s most ubiquitous artifact, Sol Ring, costs one colorless mana and taps to produce two colorless mana. There are few Commander experiences more satisfying than drawing one to start the game, allowing you to drop it on the first turn (and maybe even follow that up with an Arcane Signet). Suddenly, your deck’s strategy accelerates. If you have a four-cost Commander, they’re out on turn two. But no matter the cost of your Commander or what your deck wants to do, Sol Ring is going to help it all happen sooner.

I never want to see another turn-one Sol Ring again

That ruthless efficiency is also a reason to ban it. An early Sol Ring can give one player a major advantage, forcing everyone else to either catch up or collectively deal with the person who drew it. Its near-universal usefulness also eats up a slot that could otherwise go toward something more directly tied to a distinctive strategy. Verhey recognizes the card’s power. He just sees its place in Commander differently.

“It’s been in basically every single Commander precon we’ve ever made,” he says. “It’s in almost every Commander deck players build. People love it, and they love their unique variations of Sol Ring.”

There are 146 individual printings of the card, with 75 unique pieces of artwork. One features Garfield the cat dancing with his beloved Arlene. Another depicts Dogmeat from the Fallout franchise staring at his reflection in a bowl of water. Many, including the oldest, show a bright ring of light in the midst of a blazing inferno.

In a casual, four-player format, Verhey argues, the possibility of someone getting an explosive start thanks to a Sol Ring can help make individual games feel different. In a 100-card singleton format like Commander, you have a 7.46% chance of drawing one, so that is actually quite rare. Everyone has access to the same card, but who draws it — and what the rest of the table does about it — changes from game to game.

“I think it actually creates dynamic Commander games a lot of the time,” Verhey says. “You played Sol Ring on turn one. We all see that threat, right? We all know who the enemy is.”

For Sol Ring’s critics, the early advantage and ensuing dogpile are part of the problem. For Verhey, they can be part of the fun: an immediate complication that gives three other players something to respond to.

“Is it incredibly strong? Absolutely,” he says. “Is it necessarily the poster child I would have chosen if I was creating Commander from scratch? Probably not.”

But Commander already exists, and Sol Ring is deeply embedded in it. Verhey doesn’t foresee challenging that status “at any point.”

Still, it has at least been considered in the past. When the former Commander Rules Committee banned Mana Crypt, Jeweled Lotus, and Dockside Extortionist in September 2024, it explicitly acknowledged that Sol Ring met its criteria for a ban. The committee nevertheless made an exception because removing the card would fundamentally change the format’s identity.

There’s room to disagree about whether that distinction makes a turn-one Sol Ring an enjoyable gameplay experience. But preserving variety in Commander doesn’t mean eliminating every staple just because everybody uses it. For now, and probably forever, if you’re the type of person who’s going to show up at MagicCon with a “Ban Sol Ring” shirt, then you’re just going to have to ban it within your pod.
