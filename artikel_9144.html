British Museums Are Experiencing a ‘Bayeux Bounce’ in Attendance, Thanks to Replicas of the Tapestry

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Bayeux Tapestry is the gift that keeps on giving. UK museums such as Reading Museum and the Great North Museum: Hancock are experiencing a “Bayeux bounce,” according to the Museums Association.

Replicas of the scroll-like panel of linen cloth, which measures at 230 feet long and is populated with 58 scenes, are driving visitors to museums other than the British Museum, where the tapestry is on loan until July next year. Tickets to the British Museum’s presentation have been sold out numerous times. Reading Museum’s museum manager, Matthew Williams, said that the museum’s Victorian replica of the Bayeux Tapestry has driven a 74 percent increase in footfall since October, with 16,693 visitors. The tapestry at Reading Museum is free to visit, and photography is allowed, unlike at the British Museum, where the tapestry is ticketed at £33 and photography is banned in order to control the long queues.

“We have trained more staff to deliver our public tours and recruited more welcome volunteers to help us manage the crowds, especially at weekends and school holidays,” said Williams. “We have made sure to keep our website updated to manage expectations and to clearly show when the [replica] Bayeux Tapestry is closed for booked tours or when it will be busy, to avoid disappointment.”

The Bayeux Tapestry replica has resulted in optimistic numbers for the museum, with tours booked until June 2027, the cafe’s income up by 46 percent, donations increased by 83 percent, and shop sales soaring by 93 percent.

The copy of the Bayeux Tapestry at the Great North Museum: Hancock is on display until January 17, 2027, and is made out of paper, stretching approximately 16 feet longer than the original. The museum has experienced a 23 percent increase in visitors this October, with 3,107 more visitors than last year, while donations have climbed 30 percent, retail up 13 percent and the cafe hopping by 11 percent.

Other museums are taking advantage of the Bayeux Tapestry replica. The Nicholson Institute in Staffordshire will display its own copy from January 12 to March 31, 2027.
