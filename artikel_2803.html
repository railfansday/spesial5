Cowboys offensive line versus the Titans defensive line

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Dallas Cowboys will be back in action on Sunday against the Tennessee Titans. They need to win in the trenches on offense to carry the day, as they can't rely on the struggling defense. Here's how it looks.

Here are the Cowboys offensive line metrics for the 2026 season so far.

Last three-week trend: Steady protection, not elite

Every opponent will look at the Cowboys offensive line and ask, where can we break it? Here's what to watch for.

PRESSURE POINT: Jacob Martin has become Tennessee's main problem off the edge

The Titans no longer have Jeffery Simmons after they traded the four-time Pro Bowl defensive tackle to Chicago at the end of October. The player Dallas now has to locate is Jacob Martin.

Martin leads Tennessee with 7.5 sacks and has 31 total pressures, 17th among qualifying edge defenders. He added another sack and three quarterback pressures against Jacksonville last week. That is impressive production for a Titans defense that has only 21 sacks as a team.

Martin wins largely with speed. At 242 pounds, he wants to get an offensive tackle moving backwards and then attack the quarterback before the blocker can establish his position.

Dallas handled a much more intimidating group of individual pass rushers against San Francisco, so there is no reason to panic. But Martin cannot be the player who turns a favorable matchup into a difficult afternoon.

PRESSURE POINT: How quickly does T.J. Bass settle in?

This is probably Tennessee's first place to attack. Tyler Booker had become one of the Cowboys' most reliable offensive linemen. Before the 49ers game, his pressure rate allowed was just 4.6%, fifth-lowest among NFL right guards, now due to a high ankle sprain, T.J. Bass steps into his position.

Bass has already started this season when Tyler Smith was injured, so Dallas is not putting an inexperienced player onto the field. Schottenheimer specifically praised his versatility after Booker went down, but Tennessee should test him immediately.

John Franklin-Myers is the Titans' best remaining interior defensive lineman and led their defensive front with three pressures against Jacksonville. Expect Tennessee to move Franklin-Myers around and see whether it can create confusion between Bass, Cooper Beebe and Steele.

Every defensive line has a weakness, here are the Titans soft spots and where the Cowboys offensive line should attack.

GREEN LIGHT: Run directly at Jacob Martin

Martin is Tennessee's best pass-rush threat, so make him do something else and take him out the game. PFF gives Martin a 47.1 run-defense grade, ranking 105th among 111 qualifying edge defenders.That creates an obvious opportunity.

Whenever Martin is on the field, Dallas should make him defend Javonte Williams rather than allowing him to simply chase Dak Prescott.

If Martin has spent two quarters fighting through run blocks, he cannot attack every passing snap with the same freedom. The best way of slowing down Tennessee's leading pass rusher might be making him tackle Williams.

The Titans do not rely heavily on blitzing. As it stands, Tennessee's blitz rate sits at 26%, that places them among the least aggressive blitzing teams in football and that means Dallas should see plenty of four-man rushes.

The offense should therefore trust the offensive line and not leave an extra blocker beside the tackles unless Martin begins consistently winning. If Guyton, Smith, Beebe, Bass and Steele can handle Tennessee's four rushers, Dallas can put more receivers into routes. That should be particularly valuable against a Titans defense allowing 267 passing yards per game, worst in the NFL.

Here's the Tennessee Titans defensive trends for this season at the defensive line.

Last three-week trend: Front is struggling

Tyler Booker's injury makes this the matchup to watch. Bass enters the starting lineup at right guard and immediately faces Tennessee's most dependable interior defensive lineman.

Franklin-Myers can line up in several positions across the front, but expect Robert Saleh to find opportunities to place him across from Bass.

The Titans run defense has allowed 137 yards per game over the last four weeks, their most productive pass rusher is one of PFF's lowest-graded edge defenders against the run. Dallas should know exactly what to do, run Javonte Williams like a madman.

Use play-action after the linebackers start creeping toward the line, then trust the offensive line when Tennessee rushes four.

San Francisco presented Dallas with one of the most talented defensive fronts in football and Prescott still went down only once on more than 50 pass attempts. Tennessee should be a much easier challenge.

Tyler Booker suffered a high ankle sprain against San Francisco and is expected to miss time.

John Franklin-Myers played last week after dealing with a back issue and had been a full participant in practice before the Jacksonville game. Other than that their defensive line has no new injuries, their issues, however, show up a lot in their linebacker corps. So keep watch on that this week in practice reports.
