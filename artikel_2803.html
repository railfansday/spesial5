Ravens Report Card, Week 11: Mistakes and miscues mount in sixth straight loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Baltimore Ravens dropped their fifth consecutive game on Monday night, losing to the previously winless Los Angeles Chargers by three points (23-20). Here are report card grades for the team's position groups from another disheartening defeat in Week 10.

Jacoby Brissett made a number of good throws in this game and orchestrated five scoring drives. It was not his fault that the Ravens fumbled the ball multiple times and his pass-catchers dropped more than five passes. The veteran finished with a 24-of-37 passing line with 276 yards through the air. His touchdown pass came via a trick play to a wide-open Rashod Bateman for a 25-yard strike in the fourth quarter. Brissett was not perfect, however. He was inaccurate on some pass attempts and threw a game-sealing interception on the team's final drive, which was an inexcusable mistake where he should have thrown the ball away. The Ravens may not have ultimately gotten down the field into position for a game-winning field goal, but there was time left to do so and Brissett's interception cost them that opportunity.

It's difficult to give a poor grade to the running backs when Derrick Henry and Justice Hill combined for 163 rushing yards, averaged over nine yards per carry and scored two combined touchdowns. However, while both were effective churning out yards on the ground, they also each lost a fumble. Hill's fumble at the goal line negated a would-be long touchdown run in the first half that instead resulted in a touchback. Then, in the fourth quarter with the Ravens trailing by six points, Henry had the ball poked out after making a screen catch. It was a controversial call that maybe should have been ruled an incompletion, but nevertheless Henry's ball security needs to be better. Fortunately, the Panthers did not score points off either of these turnovers — but they still costed the Ravens valuable points and possessions.

Zay Flowers had his most productive performance in several weeks, reeling in a team-high eight receptions on 11 targets for 97 yards. He was open a lot and created yards in space on crossing routes, which has been Flowers' bread and butter this season. He did have one drop just before halftime that would have been a long gain. Rashod Bateman started hot with 67 yards and a long touchdown catch early in the game, but only finished with two catches on six targets. Devontez Walker had a nice 34-yard grab on his only target. It was a quiet outing for Ja'Kobi Lane who was not credited with a target after receiving the most throws his way of anyone last week. Lane did draw an offensive pass interference in the first quarter but didn't see another look after that.

Mark Andrews was not able to replicate his season-best performance from last week, as he caught just two passes for 14 yards in this game. Matthew Hibner had an almost identical stat line while Josh Cuevas caught only one reception for eight yards. The tight ends collectively seemed to have a much more positive impact in the run game but failed to do anything more than minimal complimentary work as pass-catchers.

This was definitely one of the offensive line's better games this season — if not the best. They finally had a breakthrough showing in the run game, where they consistently won at the line of scrimmage and opened up big rushing lanes. The Ravens ran for 157 yards as a team and averaged 7.1 yards per carry, which was negatively skewed by one -13 yard attempt from Flowers that was blown up. In pass protection, the Panthers did have two sacks and six quarterback hits, so it was far from perfect. Brissett bought some extra time in the pocket, but also had a decent amount of time to throw on most of his dropbacks it seemed.

For the second straight week, the Ravens didn't do a great job stopping the run. The Panthers were able to rack up nearly 150 rushing yards and averaged 4.2 yards per carry. Chuba Hubbard scored twice and had a long 26-yard run. Travis Jones made some more impact plays with eight total tackles, one sack and one tackle for loss, while Calais Campbell had a quarterback hit and key third-down pass knockdown in the fourth quarter. The Ravens still do not generate enough consistent quarterback pressure through the interior, which is even more true without Nnamdi Madubuike in the lineup.

It was another standout outing from Mike Green, who continues to ascend in his sophomore season. Green had four solo tackles with two sacks, two tackles for loss and two quarterback hits. On the other side of the defensive front, Trey Hendrickso came close to recording his third sack of the season but came up short. Hendrickson did have a quarterback hit and tackle for loss, but he was also flagged for a clear roughing the passer penalty. The Ravens have unfortunately not been getting many splash plays from rookie Zion Young.

The Ravens' starting linebacker duo put forth another middling overall game. Trenton Simpson had a team-high 10 tackles and Roquan Smith had nine tackles with one quarterback hit. While the counting stats are fine, their impact defending the run was evidently not there consistently. Smith was notably beaten a couple times in man coverage, including on a third-down conversion late in the fourth quarter against Tommy Tremble.

Nate Wiggins seemed to have a solid game without his name being called much. Starting in place of Marlon Humphrey was Chidobe Awuzie, who gave up a few catches in the first half alone including a touchdown in the red zone. T.J. Tampa saw expanded snaps as an outside corner and made two impressive plays on back-to-back reps in the second half. He broke up a short pass attempt in the red zone and then intercepted Bryce Young's third-down throw that could have been a touchdown. Tampa also was beaten on a trick-play touchdown, but it was nice to see him come up with a turnover. Nickel corner Keyon Martin made a big open-field stop against Tet McMillan late in the game.

Kyle Hamilton has uncharacteristically been on the receiving end of some long completions in coverage the past two weeks. He was beaten by Jalen Coker for a 35-yard grab in the first quarter and the Panthers quickly scored a touchdown afterwards. He was also stiff-armed by Hubbard on a tackle attempt — also uncharacteristic. He did have eight tackles and one tackle for loss. Malaki Starks also had a tackle for loss but appeared to be somewhat at fault for coverage issues as well on a few plays. Backup K'Von Wallace had a career-high seven tackles.

Devontez Walker made the biggest play of the game for the Ravens, returning a kickoff 86 yards to the house for a touchdown. That second-quarter splash gave the Ravens a temporary five-point lead and big momentum lift. Another positive was Riley Patterson making his lone field goal and four extra point tries. This grade is low because of LaJohntay Wester, who muffed two punts and lost one of them. The Panthers scored a go-ahead touchdown directly off of the fumbled punt that they recovered. He was fortunate to not have lost both, although the second was recovered by the Ravens at the one yard line and created terrible field position.
