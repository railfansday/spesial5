Airbnb vs. Uber Technologies: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Both platforms have moved past their loss-making years, but their valuations and risk profiles diverge sharply for investors weighing 2027 plans.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 24, 2026 at 11:15AM EST

The rise of the gig economy has produced two dominant platforms that changed how people travel and commute. Deciding between Airbnb (ABNB +0.42%) and Uber Technologies (UBER +1.73%) requires looking at their different paths to growth.

Airbnb operates a high-margin marketplace for lodging and experiences, while Uber Technologies focuses on high-frequency mobility and delivery services. Both companies have moved past their early loss-making stages to become profitable enterprises. This comparison examines their business models, financial health, and current valuations to determine which stock is better positioned for investors through 2027.

Airbnb operates a massive global marketplace for stays and unique experiences, supporting more than 9 million active listings across 220 countries and regions. The company maintains a specialized Co-Host Network that pairs property owners with support personnel and utilizes Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -1.65%) for its critical cloud infrastructure. This strategy contributes to the growth of consumer discretionary stocks within the global travel market.

During the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, the company reported that revenue reached about $13.9 billion, representing a growth rate of roughly 16.7% compared to the previous year. Net income for this period was around $3.2 billion, reflecting a consistent demand for travel across major markets like the United States and the United Kingdom. This financial performance shows the ability of the platform to generate significant profits while expanding into newer geographic territories.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.3x, which measures total debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio is roughly 1.4x, which compares current assets to current liabilities to show how easily the company can cover short-term financial obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was about $4.6 billion, though note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 34% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Uber Technologies connects riders, drivers, and merchants through a platform that spans mobility, delivery, and freight across more than 15,000 cities. The company has established major partnerships with retailers like Best Buy (BBY +2.90%) and Tractor Supply Company (TSCO +4.11%) to provide on-demand delivery services to their customers. It also maintains a strategic relationship with Amazon, allowing Prime members to access fee-free orders through its delivery app.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $56.5 billion, which represents a growth rate of approximately 13.9% over the previous period. The company generated net income of about $3.5 billion during this period, supported by its presence in more than 75 global markets. This growth reflects the continued adoption of its mobility and delivery services by more than 215 million monthly active platform consumers.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.4x, measuring the total debt of the company against its shareholder equity. The current ratio is approximately 1.1x, which shows that current assets are sufficient to cover current liabilities as they come due. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached nearly $9.8 billion, providing the company with significant liquidity to fund its operations and expansion plans.

Airbnb faces significant risks from evolving regulatory environments, such as the European Union short-term rentals regulation and local bans in major cities like New York. The company also manages ongoing tax audits and disputes in several jurisdictions, including Italy and the United States, which could impact financial results. Additionally, competition from traditional hotel chains and search engines like Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -1.47%) remains a constant pressure on its marketplace dominance.

Uber Technologies faces ongoing legal challenges over the classification of its drivers as independent contractors, a situation that could fundamentally alter its business model. The company competes in a crowded market against well capitalized rivals like DoorDash (DASH +0.45%) and Lyft (LYFT +1.60%) for both drivers and consumers. It also faces operational risks related to safety incidents and the complex technical hurdles of scaling autonomous vehicle technology across its global network.

Based on their current Forward P/E and P/S ratio, Uber Technologies carries a lower valuation relative to its future earnings estimates and sales than Airbnb.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Airbnb reported an excellent third quarter of fiscal 2026, typically its strongest period of the year, beating consensus revenue estimates by some $120 million, reporting $4.87 billion in sales, up 19% from the same period in 2025, driven by a strong increase in booked nights. North America bookings grew at their fastest rate in four years, while growth accelerated in its other major markets, the U.K., France, and Australia.

The business is finding ways to expand its popular travel business, embracing features like buy now, pay later (BNPL) on reservations, a form of short-term lending. Management has also seen great success getting hotels onto its platform, and says 35% of new Airbnb customers are booking hotels through it. The network advantage of Airbnb's history, over 2 billion guest arrivals since 2008, and the lack of host presence on AI (meaning a competitor using AI has little data to scrape to form a competing product) give it a competitive moat.

For the full year, Wall Street expects sales of $14.4 billion with net income of $3.3 billion.

Uber revolutionized the taxi industry when it rolled out its ride-sharing network, allowing anyone with a car to earn a side gig. Both the industry and the company have come a long way.

Uber Technologies is by far the largest player in the developing ride-share market by revenue. Wall Street expects Uber's sales to increase by $6 billion from last year to $58 billion this year, accompanied by net income of $6.4 billion. But as a sign of the expenses Uber faces in expanding its business model, that would be roughly $3.6 billion less income than in 2025. In the long run, its global ride-sharing, delivery, and autonomous solutions business will be strong, according to analysts.

So which is the better buy for 2027? Uber trades at just about the average S&P 500 stock in terms of forward P/E, and its P/S ratio is also quite reasonable. That doesn't mean Airbnb is bad, although its premium valuation to its main competitor, Booking Holdings (BKNG +1.59%), causes concern over how Airbnb holds onto its valuation in the long term. That gives the nod to Uber.
