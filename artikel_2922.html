Mario Cristobal Says Malachi Toney Is Running At Game Speed

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Miami coach Mario Cristobal says star receiver Malachi Toney is running at 20 miles per hour and could return to the field within a week and a half. If the Hurricanes make the cut, this timetable allows Toney a chance to play in the Atlantic Coast Conference title match on December 5. The update comes after Toney suffered a high ankle sprain during practice on November 4, requiring tightrope surgery just one day later. Per A to Z Sports, the initial diagnosis suggested a recovery window of four to six weeks, but the coach's latest comments show a much faster path back.

Cristobal offered clear details on Toney's physical status during a Friday appearance on ESPN. "As of yesterday, he was running 19, 20 mph, probably a little bit more," Cristobal said in the televised appearance. He added that the receiver is "really close" and "on course" to get back to full speed. With 1,027 receiving yards on 73 catches and 13 touchdowns prior to the injury, Toney was a primary target when the season began, making this progress vital for the team. A to Z Sports noted that Toney was second nationally in catches and tied for second in touchdown receptions when he got hurt.

The coach indicated that Toney would likely miss the final regular-season game against Boston College on November 28. "He knows he's not playing right now, and he's got to coach and encourage and push his teammates to be their very best while he gets prepared to play, but he's probably a week and a half out," Cristobal said. This schedule means the receiver would miss the game against the Eagles next weekend based on the timetable revealed Friday. However, the window opens up for the ACC title game if Miami wins its next matchup against Virginia Tech. The Associated Press reported that Miami could clinch a berth in that championship game with a win on Saturday.

If Toney cannot make the conference title game, the earliest he would return is December 18 or 19, provided the Hurricanes make the College Football Playoff and do not receive a first-round bye. The coach emphasized that Toney's attitude has been a key factor in this rapid recovery. On Friday, "I think the best part about it is that he is being very proactive about not being a distraction," Cristobal stated on ESPN, which was present on campus before the Hurricanes hosted "College GameDay" on Saturday. The receiver understands his role is to support the team while his body heals. A to Z Sports reports that Cristobal verified Toney underwent procedures roughly two weeks ago and now runs at speeds consistent with game conditions.

The stats show why getting Toney back matters so much for Miami's offense. He had 73 catches and 13 touchdowns before the injury, making him a central part of the attack. The coach's confidence in the recovery speed suggests the team might not need to adjust their game plan as much as feared. Reporters heard from Cristobal that Toney is "probably about a week and a half out" and currently running at excellent speeds during his recovery. This news gives the Hurricanes a realistic chance to have their top playmaker ready for the biggest game of the year.

Boston College visits Miami for the home finale of the regular season on November 28.
