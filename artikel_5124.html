Bitmine Stock: Tom Lee Calls ETH ‘Hard Money’ – But BMNR Share Count Jumps 164%

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Tom Lee calls Ethereum hard money as ETH supply growth stays near Bitcoin’s, but Bitmine’s 164% share dilution raises questions for BMNR investors.

Tom Lee made his “hard money” case for Ethereum (ETH) again this Week,  as Bitmine Immersion (BMNR) reported buying another 7,819 ETH last week. Bitmine stock rose 11% the same day, to $14.42, but is still down 44% in a month.

Lee’s argument is about supply. He said ETH’s supply has grown 0.90% a year since Bitmine started buying in June 2025, against 0.82% for Bitcoin (BTC). “In other words, ETH and BTC have essentially the same inflation rate, and both are ‘hard money’ measured in supply growth,” Lee said.

We checked the numbers, and he is right about the coins, but the same test applied to Bitmine’s own shares tells a different story.

Lee’s numbers make a case for ETH as a coin that holds its value. But Bitmine shareholders do not own ETH directly. They own a slice of a company that keeps issuing new shares to buy more of it, and each new share dilutes that slice.

So does the hard-money case for ETH still hold for the people who own Bitmine stock, or is their share of Bitmine’s ETH being watered down faster than ETH’s supply grows?

Lee compared ETH with other big coins in Bitmine’s weekly update. Solana’s supply has grown 7.6% a year over the same period, XRP’s 5.4% and Cardano’s 2.9%, he said. “These others have been far more inflationary.”

Ethereum’s supply did grow about as slowly as Bitcoin’s over the past year. Most new ETH paid to validators is offset by the fees the network burns.

Bitmine paid for its ETH by printing shares. It had 232.4 million shares outstanding on August 31, 2025, and 613.3 million a year later, its annual report shows. By November 4, the count reached 626.5 million.

That is a 164% increase in one fiscal year, about 200 times ETH’s supply growth. Bitmine sold 395 million new shares through its at-the-market program in the Year to August, raising $12.9 billion.

Selling shares to buy ETH works for existing holders when the stock trades above the value of the coins behind it. It hurts when it trades below.

Right now, it trades below. Bitmine says its crypto, cash, and stakes are worth $11.1 billion, about $17.70 per share. At Monday’s close of $14.42, the market values Bitmine stock at roughly 0.81 times its assets, our estimate shows.

Bitmine has pushed back by buying its own shares. It repurchased 20.8 million shares at an average of $17 in July and August, and it has $3.6 billion left under its buyback plan.

Bitmine now holds 6,085,564 ETH, or 4.98% of the 122.2 million in existence. Its stated goal is 5%, roughly 6.11 million ETH.

That leaves about 24,400 ETH to buy. At last week’s pace of 7,819 ETH, Bitmine would reach its target in about three weeks.

What happens after that matters for Bitmine stock. The weekly purchases have steadily driven demand for ETH, and Bitmine has said it would stop buying at 5%. With the stock below the value of its holdings, buybacks may use new money better than buying more ETH.

Most of the coins are already working. Bitmine stakes about 5.23 million ETH, 86% of its holdings, which it says earns about $245 million a year at current prices.

ETH trades at $1,698, below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $2,113, and its 200-day EMA at $2,242.

Lee has made a fair case that ETH is as scarce as Bitcoin. For Bitmine stock, though, the share count matters as much as the coin count, and that has grown far faster.
