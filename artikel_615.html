More than a billion years of Earth's history vanished. Scientists may finally know why

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

More than a billion years of Earth's geological history are missing from some of the planet's rock formations. Scientists have debated the cause of this enormous gap for decades, but new research suggests that the answer may lie in the slow movement of entire continents, driven by powerful forces deep inside Earth.

A study led by a PhD candidate at the University of Wollongong (UOW) offers a new explanation for the Great Unconformity, one of geology's most enduring mysteries. Published in the journal Nature, the findings suggest that ancient continents gradually rose and sank over hundreds of millions of years as hot rock moved through Earth's interior. These changes in elevation may have allowed erosion to remove enormous amounts of rock, erasing vast stretches of the planet's geological record.

Lead author Santosh Dhungana, a UOW PhD candidate, explained that although researchers have proposed numerous explanations for the Great Unconformity, few have been rigorously tested against the long-term behavior of Earth's interior.

"Imagine a book where a huge section in the middle has been ripped out. The Great Unconformity is that gap in Earth's story, with the narrative jumping from the early chapters straight to a much later part of the story. Our research suggests these pages weren't lost by chance," he said.

Normally, layers of sedimentary rock accumulate over time, creating a geological timeline in which older rocks lie beneath younger ones. Scientists can study these layers to reconstruct ancient environments and understand how Earth has changed over billions of years.

Yet this timeline contains a remarkable interruption. At numerous locations worldwide, sedimentary rocks dating to the Cambrian period, around 540 million years ago, rest directly on much older crystalline rocks, including granite that can be more than a billion years old. The intervening geological layers are absent, leaving an enormous break in the record known as the Great Unconformity.

One of the clearest examples is visible in the Grand Canyon in the United States. This region was once part of Laurentia (modern North America), an ancient continental landmass. Similar gaps have been identified in Europe, Australia, Africa and Asia.

The amount of missing time varies considerably between locations. In some places, approximately 200 million years of geological history are absent, while elsewhere the gap exceeds a billion years.

How Forces Deep Inside Earth May Have Erased the Rock Record

To investigate what caused these missing chapters, Dhungana worked with his supervisor and co-author, Associate Professor Nicolas Flament, to simulate the movement of Earth's mantle over hundreds of millions of years.

The mantle is an immense layer of hot, mostly solid rock beneath Earth's crust. Although this material appears rigid on human timescales, it can deform and flow extremely slowly over geological periods. Heat-driven circulation within the mantle, a process called mantle convection, can gradually raise enormous regions of Earth's surface or cause them to subside.

These vertical movements can influence whether ancient rocks are exposed to erosion or buried beneath new deposits. When a continent rises, wind, water and other natural processes can gradually wear away its surface. If the land later sinks, oceans can advance over it, allowing fresh sediment to accumulate on top of the eroded rock.

The researchers found that this process may explain both the disappearance of ancient rock layers and the preservation of the geological boundary that remains visible today.

Ancient Continents Rose and Sank Over Millions of Years

The simulations revealed a striking pattern across three major ancient continental regions: Laurentia, Baltica (northern Europe) and North China.

According to the models, mantle convection caused the ancient cores of these continents to rise during the Neoproterozoic Era, which extended from roughly one billion to 540 million years ago. This long interval preceded the Cambrian period, when animal life underwent a remarkable expansion in diversity, commonly known as the Cambrian explosion.

As the continents remained elevated, prolonged erosion could have stripped away extensive layers of older rock, gradually removing hundreds of millions of years of geological history.

The researchers found that this period of uplift was followed by widespread subsidence during the Phanerozoic eon, which encompasses approximately the past 540 million years. As these ancient continental regions slowly sank, advancing seas covered their eroded surfaces. New sediments accumulated above the exposed older rocks, preserving the boundary between the two.

Over immense spans of time, this sequence of uplift, erosion, subsidence and sediment deposition could have produced the Great Unconformity seen in rock formations today.

A New Explanation for One of Earth's Greatest Geological Mysteries

A particularly important finding was how closely the computer simulations matched the geographical distribution of the Great Unconformity.

The models predicted long-term uplift followed by subsidence in North America, northern Europe and North China, precisely the regions where the geological gap is especially well documented and preserved.

This agreement provides support for the idea that deep mantle movements played a major role in shaping the missing rock record. Rather than requiring a separate explanation for each location, the findings suggest that a common process operating deep within Earth may account for the widespread pattern.

"Sometimes what is missing can tell us more about the story of Earth than what we can see. A single, long period of uplift followed by a long period of sinking would have been the ideal recipe for removing the rock and preserving the worn surface," Associate Professor Flament said.

The research offers a way to connect the geological evidence visible at Earth's surface with the slow processes occurring far below it. Although the findings do not establish that mantle convection was the only factor responsible for the Great Unconformity, they provide a testable explanation for why so much ancient geological history disappeared and how the remaining evidence survived.

"Many explanations for the Great Unconformity had been proposed but few had been tested against how the Earth actually behaves over time. It is a thrill to be able to propose a solution to this geological puzzle."
