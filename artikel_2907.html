'Big Short' Investor Michael Burry Just Closed His Bet Against the Nasdaq. Here's What Investors Should Do Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The investor famous for calling the 2008 housing crash says he's now "temporarily almost entirely long." Here's how to read the move without copying it.

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 25, 2026 at 6:00PM EST

Michael Burry made his name betting against the housing market before the 2008 financial crisis. This week, with tech stocks in a steep slide, he did something somewhat unusual for him: He stopped betting against the market.

In a post on his Substack, Cassandra Unchained, Burry said he had sold the rest of his put options on the Nasdaq-100, bearish bets that pay off when the index falls. "Globally, my portfolio is more long, and less short, than it has been in some time," he wrote. Apart from puts on Palantir Technologies (PLTR +1.50%) and MetLife (MET +0.57%), he added, "I am temporarily almost entirely long."

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images.

Burry is turning more bullish after a sharp drop. The Nasdaq Composite closed Tuesday at 23,733.54, down 14.5% from its Oct. 6 close, and the Nasdaq-100 fell 16% over the same stretch. The Composite is down about 7% in November alone, as doubts about whether the AI spending boom will pay off, new tariff proposals, and the turmoil around Nvidia's leadership have weighed on AI stocks.

Puts gain value as an index falls, so selling them after a slide is a way to lock in profits. Burry said much of his cash, about 25% of the portfolio, came from profitable put options plus money added to the account.

The most important word in his post may be "temporarily." Burry said he is "continuing to assess short opportunities," and he hasn't said the market has bottomed. He also kept his Palantir puts, even though Palantir shares, at about $164 on Wednesday, already sit roughly 23% below their 52-week high.

Burry disclosed large put positions on Nvidia and Palantir in late 2025 after warning about an AI bubble. Shifting from that stance to mostly long tells you he thinks a good part of the downside he expected has now played out, at least for the broad index.

For most investors, the answer is not to copy his trades. Burry can reverse a position in a day and write about it afterward, so by the time a move becomes news, he may have changed it again. Short-selling and options are tools most individual investors shouldn't rely on.

The useful lesson is in how he's behaving. He took profits on a bet that worked, he's holding a big cash cushion, and he's staying selective about what he owns. You can do a version of that: Make a list of quality tech stocks you'd be glad to own for five years, and buy them in stages if the sell-off continues, rather than all at once. Keep enough cash on the side that you're never forced to sell in a downturn.

Markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday, so expect thin trading and bigger swings to end the week. If Burry's right that the worst of the Nasdaq's slide is behind it, patient buyers will be rewarded. If he's wrong, he's already told you he can switch back, and investors buying in stages will have room to do the same.
