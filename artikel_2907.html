Apple Hit an All-Time High While AI Stocks Sold Off. Here’s the Big Tech Stock I’d Buy.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

By Keithen Drury – Nov 23, 2026 at 11:12AM EST

It has been a wild few weeks on Wall Street for big tech. Many of the leading stocks in the sector are down significantly from their all-time highs -- but not Apple (AAPL +1.55%). The iPhone maker has been resilient amid the sell-off, and it's not hard to understand why: Its lack of artificial intelligence exposure.

This isn't necessarily a bad thing, and Apple isn't ignoring AI; it's just approaching the trend in a different way from its megacap peers. While most of Apple's peers are sinking hundreds of billions of dollars annually into building AI data centers, Apple has chosen to stay on the sidelines and instead deploy an AI model from one of these companies. This may cost it an incremental amount of money over the long term for each Apple device on which AI is deployed, but it spares it from having to spend a lot now.

This discipline has earned Apple a ton of respect on the market, and it's why the stock remains near its all-time high. But is it still a buy?

Apple has long been known as an excellent steward of its resources, and its actions over the past few years have been a display of that. It appears to have made a smart decision regarding AI, and the market has rewarded its stock for that.

But the problem is the market may have gone too far in its appreciation. Apple is now one of the more expensive tech stocks on the market. When we use next year's expected earnings to value the stock, it has a huge premium over its peers.

With companies like Nvidia (NVDA +32.52%) trading at 10 times next year's earnings or Alphabet (GOOG +6.58%) (GOOGL +6.92%) at 18 times, there is a clear premium being placed on Apple's stock.

The question investors must ask themselves is whether Apple is worth paying a forward P/E that's more than three times Nvidia's or double the valuation of Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM +15.60%). Even Amazon (AMZN +4.00%) is discounted compared to Apple.

Of those, I'd pick Nvidia for the most upside, but there are also some other stories driving its price. Instead, I think Taiwan Semiconductor is the best pick of this bunch, as it will thrive regardless of which company is designing the chips it manufactures or which clients are buying them.

For me, 33 times next year's earnings is just too high of a premium to pay for Apple, especially when its peers trade for fractions of that. While I understand investors' hesitation about investing in Nvidia amid the tumultuous period it just experienced (and perhaps even now, with CEO Jensen Huang reinstated), companies like Taiwan Semiconductor and Alphabet are still full steam ahead with strong growth prospects. With market sentiment currently bearish on AI, now is the time to scoop up these AI companies, as that bearish sentiment likely won't last forever, especially if they continue reporting excellent results quarter after quarter.

Apple stock has remained strong by staying out of the AI arena. But I think its valuation is just too high right now to invest in it.
