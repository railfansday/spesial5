Ina Garten Reveals Reason She Turned Down Dancing With the Stars Offer

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Food Network legend Ina Garten said she was once asked to join the Dancing With the Stars cast, but explained she declined the offer because “nobody needs to see that.”

Ina Garten will be sticking to the edible form of salsa.

While the Barefoot Contessa chef may be an expert at slicing and dicing in the kitchen, she is adamant her skills wouldn’t transfer to the ballroom.

“Somebody asked me to do Dancing with the Stars,” Garten said on the Nov. 18 episode of her Happy Hour podcast. “I thought, ‘Nobody needs to see that. That’s not happening.’”

Plus, the 78-year-old anticipated that her nerves would get the best of her. As she suggested, “I thought, ‘The closer I got to the day when I had to do it, the more I’d have nightmares.’”

(FWIW, her fellow Food Network star Giada De Laurentiis competed this season on Dancing With the Stars alongside pro partner Alan Bersten, though the pair were eliminated in week two of the competition.)

Of course, Garten’s aversion to a Viennese waltz isn’t the only honest opinion she has served up on a silver platter over the years. In fact, she has been candid about her decision to not have kids during her nearly 60-year marriage to husband Jeffrey Garten.

“It’s amazing to me that I made that decision so young, and that thank God Jeffrey was OK with it,” she recalled on a November 2025 episode of the Good Hang with Amy Poehler podcast. “I just can’t imagine my life any other way I’ve done it. I’ve done what I wanted to do. I’ve had just a wonderful time.”

Not having kids has also allowed Garten and her husband to prioritize their marriage, which she revealed in her 2024 memoir Be Ready When the Luck Happens led them to couples counseling after her burgeoning career put their relationship on the backburner.

"While I was still cooking, cleaning, shopping, managing at the store, I was doing it as a businesswoman, not a wife," she wrote in the book. "My responsibilities made it impossible for me to even think about anything else.”

However, after kneading the lumps out of their issues with hard work and therapy, Garten noted they “heard each other” better.

“Moving forward, we could be equals who took care of each other,” she added. “It wouldn’t happen overnight, but if we worked toward the same goal, we could change things together."
