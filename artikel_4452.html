The Home Depot’s 2026 Black Friday sale has 65 power tool and accessory deals worth a look, led by $99 brushless kits and battery starter packs that include a free bare tool. You can browse the full Black Friday tool sale, or jump straight to the lists below, grouped by free-tool promo, brand and category.

Two rules make this sale easier to navigate. First, buying a $99 to $199 battery starter kit with a free tool often costs the same as—or less than—buying that bare tool on its own. Second, watch out for decoy discounts on older brushed drills and mixed bundles that sit right next to newer brushless kits (brushless motors run cooler, last longer and stretch battery runtime). We checked prices and stock at The Home Depot on Wednesday, Nov. 25, ahead of Thanksgiving and Black Friday; pricing and free-tool options can vary by store.

A drill, an impact driver and two batteries for $169

A drill/driver and an impact driver cover most of what a homeowner asks of cordless tools, and the DeWalt Atomic 20V Max 2-tool combo kit cuts $80 off a compact entry into DeWalt’s battery system. The bag packs the DCD794 drill/driver, the DCF809 impact driver, two 2.0Ah batteries and a charger—a practical baseline if you’re comparing the best tool combo kits. The brushless impact driver is rated at 1,700 inch-pounds of torque, while the drill’s 5.88-inch head slips easily inside cabinets. Both batteries fit the rest of DeWalt’s 20V Max lineup.

Half off a brushless Milwaukee drill with a battery

At $99, the Milwaukee M18 brushless 1/2-inch compact drill/driver kit is the cheapest clean on-ramp into Milwaukee’s M18 battery platform. Measuring 5.7 inches front to back, it fits inside cabinets and between joists without forcing awkward angles. Milwaukee rates the motor at 550 inch-pounds of torque and up to 1,700 rpm, and the box includes one 2.0Ah battery, a charger and a soft bag. If you drive more lag bolts and deck screws than you drill holes, grab the matching M18 compact brushless impact driver kit for the same $99 (or check our picks for the best impact drivers).

Two 4.0Ah batteries, a charger and a free tool

Buy the batteries and the tool comes free. The Ridgid 18V Max Output starter kit pairs two 4.0Ah packs and a charger for $149, and The Home Depot discounts one eligible item in your cart to $0. Standouts on the eight-item menu include a brushless 6-1/2-inch circular saw, a mid-torque impact wrench, an angle grinder, a jigsaw and a 6.0Ah battery. Don’t skip the paperwork after delivery: Ridgid’s Lifetime Service Agreement covers free parts and service on eligible tools bought at The Home Depot, but only if you register them and upload proof of purchase within 90 days.

Hanging a gallery wall or a run of cabinets goes faster with a plumb line already projected across the room. Down to $49.97 from $95, the Bosch GLL50-20 self-leveling cross-line laser casts red horizontal and vertical lines rated out to 50 feet, with an IP55 dust- and water-resistance rating. It runs on included AA batteries (Bosch sells a 3.7V rechargeable pack separately) and mounts via magnets or a 1/4-inch-20 thread (standard camera-tripod mount). Like most red-beam lasers, this model is built strictly for indoor work; step up to a pricier green-beam pick from our guide to the best laser levels if you work in bright, sunlit rooms.

Four other brands mirror the Ridgid starter kit above: add the qualifying battery or combo kit to your cart and select a free tool at checkout. The Ryobi ONE+ starter kit is the lowest buy-in at $99, while the $199 DeWalt 20V Max 6.0Ah and 4.0Ah starter kit unlocks 14 free options (check your ZIP code at checkout, as high-value bare tools sell out unevenly by store). A higher amp-hour (Ah) rating means longer runtime—2.0Ah packs keep drills light overhead, while 4.0Ah to 6.0Ah packs keep circular saws and grinders from bogging down.

Milwaukee’s 12V (M12) and 18V (M18) batteries don’t cross over, and anything labeled “tool only” ships without a battery or charger. Fuel marks Milwaukee’s flagship brushless tier, while Packout refers to its interlocking modular toolbox system. Note that the half-off M18 5-tool combo kit ($349) and 9-tool combo kit ($699) are not Fuel sets and mix in older brushed tools—fine for weekend projects, but check the box list if you want all-brushless gear. The M12 4.0Ah battery two-pack also drops to $79, and the $99 M18 drill kit sits near the top of this page.

Don’t scroll past the 20V Max brushless 3-speed oscillating multi-tool kit at $99—getting the tool, a charger and a 4.0Ah battery at half price is one of the quietest steals in this sale. Meanwhile, watch out for the two standalone drills: the $129 20V Max drill/driver kit throws in two tiny 1.3Ah packs, but its DCD771 uses an older brushed motor. Most shoppers are better off spending $30 less on the $99 Atomic compact drill/driver kit, which trades the spare battery for a shorter brushless tool and a higher-capacity 2.0Ah pack (or stepping up to the $169 Atomic 2-tool kit at the top of the page). For decoding DeWalt’s labels below: Atomic is its compact tier, XR is its pro-grade line and FlexVolt packs power both 20V and 60V tools.

Ryobi delivers the lowest kit prices in this roundup, though the cheapest drill deserves a caveat. The $49.97 ONE+ 3/8-inch drill/driver kit tops out at 600 rpm and lacks an adjustable clutch, making it easy to strip screws or overdrive fasteners. Spend $9 more on the ONE+ 1/2-inch drill/driver kit for a two-speed gearbox and real torque control. HP designates Ryobi’s brushless lineup, and if you already own a charger, the $99 4.0Ah battery two-pack is worth weighing against the $99 starter kit plus free tool listed earlier.

Skip the $149 two-battery starter kit if you only need one tool and a single pack: Ridgid’s $99 shelf includes a 4.0Ah battery with the 1/2-inch impact wrench kit, the 6-1/2-inch circular saw kit and the angle grinder kit.

The headline benchtop cut is $200 off the DeWalt DWS780 12-inch sliding miter saw ($449), which uses an LED shadow-line cut indicator instead of a laser you have to recalibrate and tilts both left and right (double-bevel) so you don’t have to flip long trim boards. Note that the two pneumatic nailers below—the $99 DeWalt brad nailer and the $199 Makita AN924 framing nailer (which takes 21-degree plastic-collated nail strips)—require an air compressor and hose sold separately.

If you’re rounding out a new drill or saw—or grabbing stocking stuffers—consumables start under $10 with the Ryobi 40-piece drill and impact drive kit at $9.97, and the Diablo framing blade two-pack is $12.88.
