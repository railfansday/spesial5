Blizzard Posts Hotfix After WoW: Forever Players Mourn Lost Gold

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

The release of World of Warcraft: Forever has, for the most part, been a very successful and welcome endeavor. Many players who got into the original game in its heyday have appreciated the return to form, and newer players enjoy its relatively simpler, smaller framework compared to the much more sprawling modern WoW experience. That said, the transition to the new face of the past has not been without its hiccups.

One particular element that many higher-level players have noticed is that, unlike in vanilla WoW, Forever doesn’t automatically convert earned experience to gold when you’re at max level. This has proven to be frustrating for players used to the conversion feature, as it essentially robbed them of one of their major in-game income streams. In a thread on the official WoW: Forever forums, players confirmed the change to one another, with some expressing intent to quit the game if it wasn’t remedied.

Luckily, it seems the devs were listening in and took the complaints to heart. One of the forum’s community managers, Kaivax, chimed into the thread to announce that the game would be receiving a hotfix in short order in order to remedy the income disparity while still preserving the quest-focused spirit of classic WoW.

As Kaivax explained in their post, the XP-to-gold conversion feature was added back in vanilla WoW ahead of the lead-up to its first major expansion pack, The Burning Crusade. The idea was to give the game’s economy a little jolt and get some extra gold in everyone’s pockets so that, once the expansion dropped, everyone would have some extra cheddar to play with in the new content.

The XP-to-gold conversion feature was added back in vanilla WoW ahead of the lead-up to its first major expansion pack.

However, the conversion was never taken back out of the game even after Burning Crusade dropped, and became a standard part of the WoW formula. This resulted in players largely ignoring quests in favor of just farming enemies and reaping the cash that came from their XP. The devs of WoW: Forever thought that went against the game’s spirit, and so removed the conversion. However, there was an oversight here: the vast majority of the game’s quests don’t actually reward gold, and so without the conversion, they inadvertently took a huge chunk of players’ income out with nothing to replace it.

RP PVP servers are back on the menu for World of Warcraft: Forever!

This brings us to the hotfix, which has likely already been implemented into the game at the time of writing. As Kaivax explains, the hotfix will “add additional gold rewards to most quests with a quest level of level 48 or higher, including adding gold to quests above level 48 that never previously rewarded gold at all.”

Kaivax said that the point of this change is to help players “feel good about doing quests at the appropriate level to progress your character, and you should feel adequately rewarded with gold for doing so.”

This is a good showing from Blizzard in the ongoing course of WoW: Forever’s development, and hopefully, a positive sign of things to come as the game releases more content and picks up more and higher-leveled players. For as beloved as the original, vanilla release of World of Warcraft was and is, it pays to remember that game launched over 20 years ago in 2004. Gaming standards, particularly in regard to progression and quality of life, have changed exponentially in that time, and if the devs just ported over the game 1-to-1 with no changes at all, we’d all feel the growing pains something fierce.

Making these kinds of little tweaks while still preserving the overall framework of the base game should be the optimal goal, something that feels true to that classic experience while carefully pruning out all the little sticking points of early-2000s MMO game design. Responses in that forum thread certainly seemed positive, or at least less overtly hostile, so that’s a good sign.
