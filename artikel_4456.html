Every EV from Every Brand in America

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

We've created this handy guide to let you know all the brands that sell electric vehicles in the United States.

While gas-powered vehicles still make up an overwhelming majority of cars sold in the United States, their market share is slowly getting chipped away at by battery-electric ones. Since the Nissan Leaf and Tesla Roadster brought electric vehicles back to the national stage in the late 2000s, automakers have worked overtime to noticeably improve the charging speeds and driving ranges of their battery-powered products. No longer limited to small cars, EVs now take up space in just about every automotive segment.

We've compiled an exhaustive list of today's options to make it easy to determine what electric vehicles are available from which automotive brands in the United States. Don't go looking for hydrogen-powered electric vehicles here, though, as we've excluded those from this list.

Audi sells an array of electric SUVs and cars, from the compact Q4 e-tron to the range-topping 912-hp RS e-tron GT. Both of Audi's electric SUVs are available in either wagon or fastback body styles.

BMW's EV sedans look similar to their gas-powered cars, but the iX3 and incoming i3 promise next-generation looks and technology.

Cadillac's lineup of electric vehicles consists of an SUV in nearly every size, including the Escalade IQ, which looks just as ostentatious as the gas-powered Escalade despite its silent powertrain.

Chevrolet's electric vehicle arsenal is centered around SUVs, but the American automaker also caters to business owners with the WT version of the Silverado EV pickup truck.

Dodge currently sells just one EV, the Charger Daytona, which is available as either a coupe or sedan. It assumes the muscle car role vacated by the previous Scat Pack and Hellcat versions of the Charger and Challenger.

The only Fiat sold in the United States right now is an electric version of the company's iconic 500 city car. Fiat also offers the Topolino, which is technically classified as a "Low Speed Vehicle" and is limited to 25 mph.

Ford sells two electric models, the Mustang Mach-E SUV and the E-Transit commercial van.

The Genesis Electrified GV70 is based on the Korean automaker's popular compact SUV, while the GV60 utilizes the same platform found under the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Kia EV6.

GMC's lineup of electric vehicles is just as big and brash as its gas-powered offerings. Two pickups and an SUV are available.

Hyundai has won Car and Driver's EV of the Year award three times in a row, first with the Ioniq 5 SUV in 2022 and then with the Ioniq 6 sedan in 2023. The high-powered Ioniq 5 N, which entertains with faux gearshifts and engine noises, won the award in 2024.

Jeep currently sells two EVs in the U.S. Although the Wagoneer S shares the first part of its name with the gas-powered Wagoneer, the EV is completely unrelated to that behemoth. The Recon is an electric alternative to the off-road-ready Wrangler.

Kia's lineup of EVs currently consists of a trio of SUVs.

Lexus currently sells two EVs in the United States: the RZ SUV, which is related to the Toyota bZ, and an electric version of the ES sedan.

California-based startup Lucid Motors exclusively builds EVs and recently launched its second model, the Gravity SUV.

The Italian luxury brand currently offers three EVs in the U.S., all of which are related to the brand's gas-powered models and carry the Folgore suffix to distinguish them as battery-powered.

Mercedes offers a vast range of EVs, from sedans to SUVs and even vans. The automaker has begun shifting away from its "EQ" naming structure for EVs, starting with the electric G-class, which dons the name G580 with EQ Technology. It's a wordy solution that'll also appear on the upcoming battery-electric 2026 CLA-class.

While Mini previously sold an electric variation of its iconic Cooper hatchback, the latest version has been delayed indefinitely, meaning Mini currently offers just one EV in the U.S., a battery-powered variant of its Countryman SUV

Nissan sells one EV in the United States, the Leaf, which has transformed from a hatchback to an SUV for its third generation.

There are currently three Porsche EVs on sale in the U.S., with an electric successor to its 718 sports car on the way.

Ram, the truck division spun off from Dodge in 2009, currently sells one EV. Ram had originally aimed to start building the 1500 REV in late 2024 for deliveries in early 2025, but the electric pickup truck is now delayed to late 2026.
