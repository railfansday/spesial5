Georgia O'Keeffe Painting Found in NYC Closet Sells for $4.9M

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Calls to exclude Russia from Venice Architecture Biennale, Public Art Fund announces 2027 program, and more in today's art news.

Welcome to our roundup of daily art news, featuring top stories and timely updates handpicked by Hyperallergic’s writers and editors. Have a news tip? Get in touch with us.

Georgia O’Keeffe’s “Near Abiquiu, New Mexico I” (1930), which was rediscovered in the back of an East Village closet where it was stored for four decades, fetched $4.9 million at Christie’s 20th-Century Evening Sale last night, November 18, surpassing its $2-3 estimate. Read Hyperallergic Staff Writer Rhea Nayyar’s report detailing the fascinating backstory behind the painting, created just one year after the artist's first visit to New Mexico.

Days before the close of this year’s Venice Biennale, 10 European Union countries are demanding that the European Commission exclude Russia from the 2027 architecture biennale. “In light of the current geopolitical realities … there should be no room for ‘normalisation’ of Russia’s participation in international sporting and cultural events, as long as there is no just and lasting peace in Ukraine,” reads a letter signed by representatives in Poland, Austria, Croatia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Latvia, Lithuania, and Sweden, according to Rapporteur.

Ahead of its 50th anniversary next year, Public Art Fund has announced its 2027 exhibition program. The organization will continue its longstanding bus shelter series with I Happen to Like New York, which will transform 700 JCDecaux shelters across New York, Chicago, and Boston with the works of 19 artists, including Nicole Eisenman, Louis Fratino, and Toyin Ojih Odutola. New outdoor public sculptures from Ugo Rondinone, Lotus Kang, and Firelei Báez, a photo series from Catherine Opie, and a first-of-its-kind participatory performance by Jeremy Dweller round out Public Art Fund's 2027 offerings.
