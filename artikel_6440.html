Best Kitchen Scales, Tested Over a Decade of Baking (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

I’ve been an avid home baker for well over a decade (my first stint in “food media” was a baking blog I started in middle school!), and I can confirm that there is no single tool quite as important to the outcome of your baked goods as a simple kitchen scale. Bon Appétit’s Test Kitchen team agrees, which is why you’ll see gram measurements in the baking recipes on our site.

Another bonus of a kitchen scale? In most cases, you can kiss measuring cups and spoons goodbye, which means far fewer utensils to clean once your bakes are in the oven.

Scales aren’t just for bakers, either. They make portioning burger patties and meatballs easier, streamline meal prep, and keep your Sunday morning pancake routine cleaner.

I’ve tested 16 models over several years and consulted Shilpa Uskokovic, former BA senior Test Kitchen editor and co-owner of Hani’s Bakery, to find the best kitchen scale for home cooks. The winner is just $27.

New in this update: I tested the new version of the Oxo Good Grips 11-Pound Digital Kitchen Food Scale. While it was easy to use and I appreciated the pull-out display, it was less accurate than the Escali—and over double the price. You can read my full review below.

This Escali Primo has held the top spot in our testing for over six years and has been a favorite in the Bon Appétit Test Kitchen for far longer than that. “This scale has been with me for more than a decade of my career, staying faithfully by my side through 12-hour shifts in pastry kitchens, bouts of religious recipe testing, and weekly sessions of casual baking,” says senior Test Kitchen editor and resident baking whiz Shilpa Uskokovic.

It’s durable, accurate, intuitive to use, and affordable—pretty much everything we could ask of a kitchen scale.

What we love: The Escali Primo is lightweight, comes in a variety of fun colors, and has a simple two-button interface to turn it on, tare it, and toggle between ounces, pounds, and grams. In testing, it was responsive and precise, quick to tare, and delivered accurate readings every time. Its 4.5-minute auto shut-off is among the longest of any model we tested, so you won’t lose your measurements mid-bake. Plus, its 11-pound capacity is large enough to accommodate any task a home cook might ask of it.

Performance aside, the Escali Primo has several design features that helped it stand out. For one, the fact that the weighing platform is elevated and offset from the base means you can easily read the display or use the buttons even when measuring into a large, wide mixing bowl. The plastic body and sealed control panel make it a breeze to wipe down after a floury baking session and also keep stray crumbs from getting stuck in any crevices. Another detail we like? The four rubberized feet keep it stable on your kitchen counter.

The Escali Primo has proven quite durable as we’ve continued to test it long-term, another reason why it’s a perennial favorite among our Test Kitchen editors. Shilpa again: “It’s compact and easy to store, it’s easy to wipe clean, it toggles between grams and ounces seamlessly, and it’s reliably accurate."

What we’d leave: The Escali can only measure in 1-gram increments. While this was typical of most scales we tested, those looking to use their kitchen scale for dialing in their coffee routine will want something more precise (though coffee scales are kind of their own thing). Unlike some pricier models, the Escali’s display is not backlit, but we didn’t find this to be an issue. It’s also worth noting that the Escali is not a touchscreen device, so you’ll need to use a bit of pressure on the buttons.

Amazon Basics products are not consistent across the board, but this scale is a certifiable hit. At about half the price of the Escali, it’s a good option if you’re not positive about this scale thing and want to spend as little as humanly possible to do so.

What we love: There are a lot of similarities between the Amazon Basics scale and the overall winner. The Amazon Basics scale has the same two-button setup, a similar elevated weighing platform that makes it easy to see the display, and the same 11-pound maximum capacity. It’s made of plastic with a stainless-steel platform that’s easy to wipe down and is just a touch larger than the Escali at 7 inches across.

In testing, I was impressed by the Amazon scale’s accuracy, considering its price point. It responded to weight addition or removal almost as quickly as the Escali and provided an accurate readout about 99% of the time. That 1% weight change inaccuracy happened when someone repeatedly added weight or switched back and forth between units of measurement, and when it did happen, the reading was off by a minor amount. The problem could be fixed by removing what was on the scale, taring it, and starting over. For $10 (at the time of writing), you’d be hard-pressed to find a better model.

What we’d leave: Unlike the Escali, the Amazon scale’s buttons lack any kind of laminated sealing to prevent crumbs or liquid from entering the crevices around them. As mentioned, this scale isn’t quite as accurate as the winner and it tended to overshoot an item’s actual weight at first (before correcting itself relatively quickly). And last, the stainless-steel platform is more prone to scratching or smudging than Escali’s plastic one.

You’ll notice there aren’t any other scales marketed as coffee scales on this list, and that’s because the Fellow Tally was the only coffee scale we thought could plausibly work as an all-purpose model too.

What we love: The Fellow Tally is one of our favorite coffee scales (especially for pour-over) thanks to features like a built-in timer and a “brew assist” that directs you on the proper ratio of coffee to water—but its 5.5-pound maximum capacity means it works for most home baking projects too. If you’re the kind of person who likes to nerd out over your brewing and baking techniques, this is your best bet unless you want to invest in two separate scales.

In testing, we quite liked the Tally’s design for its tactile controls and easy-to-read OLED display, and appreciated its ability to measure grams, ounces, pounds, and milliliters (that milliliter measurement is calibrated for water, so one milliliter equals one gram; if you're measuring a heavier liquid like milk it will be slightly off). The elevated 6-inch platform is large enough to comfortably hold a mixing bowl while allowing easy visibility of the display.

To find out how accurate each scale was, I used a set of calibration weights. I tested each scale’s ability to give an accurate reading for the minimum amount of weight it claimed to be able to accommodate (usually 1 or 2 grams), and its ability to detect small changes when there was already weight on the platform. I also placed weights in various places on each scale’s platform to determine whether they could give an accurate reading regardless of whether the weight was in the center or at the edge.

I coated each scale in a mixture of flour, water, and oil and let them sit overnight. I cleaned the residue off and noted which scales were easy to clean and resistant to any scratching or staining.

I distributed winning scales to staffers to see how they fared after long-term use in our homes and in the Test Kitchen and added any long-term testing notes to reviews as they came in.

I looked for consistently accurate measurements and a short response time when adding and removing items of varying sizes and materials from each scale. I took into account how weight distribution affected the readings, looking for scales that delivered spot-on results regardless of where on the platform we placed the weights. I also took note of how precise each scale could be in its measurements, but found our favorite all-around options tended to measure in 1-gram increments.

I looked for scales that were intuitive to use without picking up a manual.

I tested scales with weight capacities ranging from 1.65 to 16 pounds, but found that a maximum capacity of 11 pounds was more than enough for the tasks one might try to accomplish in a home kitchen.

I wanted a scale that felt durable, well-made, and stable on the counter and was also designed to be easy to clean.

Unlike a stand mixer, a scale isn’t the kind of kitchen gadget most home cooks are looking to keep permanently on their counter. Lightweight, compact models that could be easily stashed in a cabinet were better.

I took into account the relative price of each scale. After years of testing, we’ve found that you really don’t need to spend much to get a high-quality, highly accurate kitchen scale, so any models that were pricey needed to wow us with their design or extra features.

I was very excited to test this scale from the brand behind our favorite cooking thermometers, but unfortunately it failed to impress me enough to merit a recommendation. The scale boasts the capability to measure down to the tenth of a gram, but according to ThermoWorks' product description, it does so with ±0.7 g accuracy. In testing, I found this to be true—readings were rarely accurate to the exact tenth. After testing the scale in his home kitchen for several weeks, senior commerce editor Noah Kaufman also noted that readings came in on the slow side, which made measuring ingredients that pour out quickly (like flour or liquids) tricky. I did appreciate the detachable display (helpful when using a large, wide bowl that might otherwise obscure the screen), but it wasn’t enough to outweigh the negatives—and besides, I’m of the opinion that there’s no reason for the average home cook to pay $120 for a scale when a $25 one performs incredibly well.

We also tested the Ozeri Pronto, Etekcity Multifunction Food Kitchen Scale, and GDealer Tempered Glass Kitchen Scale, all of which have been discontinued.

To use a kitchen scale, you’ll have to familiarize yourself with the concept of “tare.” Taring a scale allows you to reset the weight back to zero when you already have something on the weighing platform. This is particularly useful if you want to measure ingredients into a bowl (just place the bowl on the platform, click the tare button, and go on adding your ingredients), or if you want to measure multiple ingredients into that bowl without needing to add or subtract the existing weight in your head.

Baking is the obvious answer here, but the use cases for kitchen scales go far beyond the world of flour and butter. Use a scale to perfectly portion out meat for meatballs or burgers, aid in your meal prep for the week, or for any recipe where precise quantities are of the utmost importance.
