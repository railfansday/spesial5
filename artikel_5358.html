Nvidia’s earnings boom meets a governance test

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Good morning. Nvidia’s third-quarter earnings call, the first for CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO, came less than a week after Jensen Huang resigned. The company delivered stronger-than-expected results, but the leadership transition and a new emphasis on board oversight put governance and regulatory compliance front and center.

“Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing,” Kress said Tuesday on the call.

Board Chair Harvey Jones ended speculation about Huang’s return. “The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” Jones said. The board is conducting an independent investigation after Huang disclosed personal behavior inconsistent with company standards.

Heidrick & Struggles has been retained to conduct an internal and external search for Huang’s permanent replacement. Jones said the board is “enhancing our oversight capacity going forward.” He noted the formation of the National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee to oversee national security policies, export-control compliance and global trade risks. Nvidia’s national security and trade compliance teams will report directly to the committee and to Kress for day-to-day operations.

The move underscores the growing importance of compliance for Nvidia and its new leadership amid questions about the potential diversion of advanced AI chips to China.

The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security announced Monday an interim final rule addressing remote access to advanced computing technology through overseas data centers. Developed at the direction of the Treasury Department and National Security Council, the rule is intended to prevent companies in restricted countries from bypassing export controls through remote computing.

At the center of the issue are questions about whether advanced Nvidia chips supplied through Megaspeed were diverted to China. U.S. export rules have allowed certain overseas data centers to provide remote computing services to Chinese customers, a distinction from unauthorized transfers of restricted physical chips, Fortune reported.

Kress said third-quarter sales of licensed Hopper data center products to China totaled $2.4 billion. However, she added: “Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China.”

For CFOs and corporate directors, Nvidia’s predicament illustrates a broader challenge: As companies expand into strategically sensitive markets, financial performance increasingly depends on the strength of their compliance systems, third-party oversight and internal controls.

For the third quarter ended Oct. 25, Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year, and data center revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110%.

Nvidia also raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to approximately 75%, up from its previous projection of 70% and based on a higher fiscal 2027 revenue base, Kress said. Shares rose about 4% in overnight trading.

Dan Ives, partner and senior managing director of Yorkville Ives & Co., wrote in a note Wednesday morning that the board had drawn a line on Huang’s reinstatement, Kress had raised the number that matters most, and the financing discipline investors wanted appeared unprompted. The firm maintains its outperform rating and $225 price target.

“The CEO search, the investigation, and the 15% special meeting threshold are the open questions we are still waiting on,” he added.

With a growth engine that remains powerful, the more difficult test for Nvidia’s new leadership will be showing that the company’s governance and compliance structures are equally capable of managing the risks.

From CFO to CEO:Richard McPhail, EVP and CFO of The Home Depot (No. 25), was promoted to CEO, effective Nov. 23. McPhail will succeed Ted Decker, who has returned from a temporary medical leave of absence, but will step down as president and CEO, and he will continue to serve as chair of the board of directors.

McPhail joined the company in 2005 and was named EVP and CFO in September 2019, In this role, he oversaw several transformative acquisitions including HD Supply, SRS Distribution, GMS, Construction Resources and Mingledorff's, which have "helped position The Home Depot as a premier distributor and full-service partner for Pros of all sizes," the company stated.

Jordan Broggi was promoted to EVP and CFO at The Home Depot. Broggi is a 13-year veteran of the company, most recently serving as EVP of interconnected retail. In this role, he led the online business and customer experience for the company's digital properties, as well as loyalty, credit services and payments. Broggi has held several leadership positions during his tenure, including senior vice president of finance.

Consumers are increasingly trading down to lower-cost retailers and restaurants as they look for ways to offset rising prices, according to a November report from Bank of America Institute.

Drawing on the bank’s deposit account and credit and debit card spending data, the report found that its Spending Tier Composite, a proxy for trading-down activity, reached its lowest level since 2021, suggesting a greater shift toward value-oriented merchants. Millennials are leading the trend, with their spending tier falling faster than that of Gen X or baby boomers since 2022, particularly in apparel and groceries.

While consumers are choosing less-expensive options for clothing and dining, some households may be forgoing travel altogether rather than switching to cheaper alternatives, the report noted.
