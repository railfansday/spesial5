Jahmyr or JSN: Who’s the Offensive Player of the Year in the NFL this season?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Imagine you're an offensive coordinator in the NFL. And, as long as we're in the world of make-believe, imagine you have both Jahmyr Gibbs and Jaxon Smith-Njigba on your roster. And imagine you have exactly one play to get into the end zone.

Do you go with the running back who can stomp upstream through rush-hour traffic? Or do you go with the receiver who can create space in a mosh pit? Ground game or air game? Who's your favorite for NFL Offensive Player of the Year?

Sunday served as center stage for both Gibbs and Smith-Njigba, half a world apart. Before half the United States had even woken up, Gibbs began a methodical demolition of New England in Munich, rushing for 153 yards and two touchdowns and receiving another 99 through the air as Detroit won 35-10. A few hours later, Smith-Njigba caught eight passes for 171 yards, including a 71-yard beast of a touchdown, as Seattle outlasted Las Vegas 21-13.

Through Week 10, both teams remain in the hunt for playoff berths, in large part because of the outsized, generational performances of their stars.

After three seasons in the backfield alongside David Montgomery as a Sonic-and-Knuckles duo, Gibbs is now a solo act; Detroit thought enough of his potential that the Lions dealt Montgomery to the Texans. Gibbs validated the Lions' trust in him; this season, he leads the NFL in (deep breath) attempts, yards, touchdowns, first downs, rushing yards per game, touches from scrimmage, yards from scrimmage and combined receiving and rushing touchdowns.

Just three-plus seasons into his career, Gibbs is already starting to carve his way into the NFL record books. He just passed Terrell Davis for the most touchdowns through the first four years of a career, with 62. And he joined a couple guys named Emmitt Smith and Barry Sanders as the only players to rush for 50 touchdowns before turning 25.

"I think we have to pinch ourselves sometimes and not take for granted how special he is," Lions quarterback Jared Goff said after Sunday's victory. "He's just so explosive with the ball in his hands. I throw a check-down to him, and he goes for 50 yards. That's so rare for a guy to be able to do all the things he does."

Here, from Sunday, is a perfect encapsulation of everything Gibbs brings to the field: speed, power and a relentless drive into the end zone.

Also in his fourth year, Smith-Njigba is both a defending Super Bowl champion and the defending Offensive Player of the Year. While Seattle is struggling to defend its title, Smith-Njigba is authoring a brilliant sequel to his 2025. He currently leads the league in (another deep breath) receptions, receiving yards, receiving touchdowns, receiving first downs, longest reception, receiving yards per game, and yards per touch from scrimmage.

His performance Sunday against the Raiders was nothing short of exceptional. He totaled 144 yards in a decisive third quarter, including that magnificent 71-yard touchdown … that came after he lost his footing starting his route.

"The thing that stuck out today was he's making (plays) in all phases of the game," Seahawks coach Mike Macdonald said after Sunday's game. "On the extended plays, he's able to uncover and go become a target for our quarterback as well. So it's really in every stage of how we're playing offense. So it's pretty remarkable. He's a beast."

The most impressive fact about Smith-Njigba's dominance is that everyone on the other side of the ball knows it's coming … and they still can't stop it. Seattle doesn't have any other true receiving threats on the roster; Cooper Kupp, the Seahawks' second-leading receiver, is nearly a thousand yards behind JSN's current total of 1,207.

The Offensive Player of the Year award now tends to reward non-quarterback skill players; the last QB to win was Patrick Mahomes in 2018. It's also an award with a wide pool of candidates; the last repeat winner was Marshall Faulk, who won the award three straight times from 1999 to 2001.

Back to the question at the top: You're that offensive coordinator blessed with more talent than any team should have. So who would you choose to score that one-play touchdown, Jahmyr Gibbs or Jaxon Smith-Njigba?
