The Nets have lost their way… Now what?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

"I fully anticipated the Nets to be so fired up having us here, they would blow Chicago out. I'm looking at 62-39, so I was wrong," Nets legend Julius Erving said on YES.

"Been in the league 13 years and this… one of the worst games I've been a part of," Julius Randle said after the game. That is the veteran who once talked about shocking the world with the Nets, now stuck on a disconnected young team and wearing a Chicago White Sox hat right after losing 137-107 to the Chicago Bulls.

"I saw selfishness. I saw pointing fingers, and I saw guys that didn't always play hard," head coach Jordi Fernandez said after the loss.

This is who the Brooklyn Nets are: a 3-11 team with no identity. Nothing more, perhaps something less.

Of course, those who have been here long enough have been through agonizing times like a 12-70 season, MeloDrama, when the Nets pursuit of Melo fell flat, and Dwightmare, when the Nets pursuit of Dwight Howard also came short.

There were absolutely miserable days in the post-Deron Williams era, as they watched Boston pile up lottery draft picks, more specifically, Jayson Tatum and Jaylen Brown. They had none of their own picks, which is what made Sean Marks's early work stand out so much, because he had so little to work with. He was throwing restricted free agent offer sheets at guys like Allen Crabbe (four years, $75 million), Tyler Johnson (four years, $50 million), Donatas Motiejunas (four years, $37 million), and Otto Porter Jr. (four years, $106.5 million).

We know what happened with the Big 3 and we don't need to go back down that road, but ultimately it failed. Marks made two massive trades to get out of the trenches after that predicament, dealing Kevin Durant and Mikal Bridges for picks that might as well be stashed in a Doomsday vault at this rate.

Now? He has too many assets to work with, and it isn't leading to wins, development, or a gritty identity. Fans are waiting for the product to be good, for the Nets to strike, and instead they are staring down another losing season.

Talk about an improbable fall from grace.

The Nets entered Wednesday with very few things to feel good about, but ABA Night was meant to be a night set for those who won two championships and laid the foundation. The reality from those 1970s teams and this one now are night and day, even if times have changed. Dr. J said what everyone was feeling, even if he meant it innocently.

The Brooklyn Nets have dropped to 3-11 after getting blown out by 30 on their own floor, surrendering 120+ points in eight games total and allowing 130+ points for the fourth time this season. It is, in many ways, as miserable a stretch as any of those above.

"I can try to say it in Spanish, because in English they don't understand. So I'll keep trying and find whatever language works. But yeah, our effort was not good."

At this point, a translator won't help. The scoreboard speaks every language fluently, and right now it's shouting that effort has been lost in translation. With Fernandez in Year Three and Sean Marks in his 11th season running basketball operations, the blame can point in plenty of directions.

So where should it go? Frustrations didn't start on Wednesday night.

There is a growing disconnect and stubbornness that shows up in clunky rotations and cautious minutes restrictions. If they are going to be cautious with Mikel Brown Jr., at least distinguish caution from coddling. Seven players got more playing time than him on Monday, and it was only his second game in eight days.

So, if they aren't going to be good (hint: they aren't), it's worth living with the growing pains of developing cornerstones like MBJ at point guard.

The team was otherwise built with positional size, yet as rival scouts told Brian Lewis of the New York Post, "The lineups don't really fit." One Eastern Conference scout put it plainly: "It's like super jumbo right now. Sometimes it's super small, you have like three guards out there. The roster just isn't very clean. … There's small guards or some big guards, but they're guards who need to ball. The lack of shooting on the roster, that can be an issue."

Are the Nets stretching this rebuild out as far as possible? Even scouts looking at the 2025 draft haul see the mess, with one veteran scout saying, "The back end is what the back end is. But yeah, it's tough when you have five firsts and it looks like two sure misses and probably three, more than likely three misses."

Another scout added, "And the other ones, the other first rounders of that five, you wonder are they NBA players. Saraf? Powell? And then Wolf. They probably should have consolidated that and tried to move up."

Rebuilding is one thing; compounding it with avoidable, self-inflicted wounds is another.

What makes this all even harder to stomach is that the Nets are outright awful, and for some reason they carry themselves with an unearned arrogance. Why do they act entitled to patience when this franchise has made the playoffs just eight times since moving to Brooklyn and won only a single playoff series across Marks's entire decade-long tenure?

Flash back to the Milwaukee game when Brooklyn pulled starters in a winnable contest and earned the NBA's first Player Participation fine. After dropping 118 games over the prior two years with Houston holding their pick, there should be a real sense of urgency instead of questionable rotations and questions about effort.

The so-called culture has contradictions piling up even higher. For years, this franchise leaned into progressive milestones, Yet the Nets keep getting pulled into jarring off-court noise around Michael Porter Jr. and his habit of testing dates with Andrew Tate clips. Pair that negativity with something so specific like his anti-vax posture, and it looks glaringly hypocritical next to how the Kyrie Irving situation played out.

On-court merit hasn't come close to justifying the leash leadership continues to get, yet every sign points to Joe Tsai and Marks staying locked in on this slow-drip rebuild at least for the time being. Tsai is a businessman, but his product is spoiled, the people paying are fed up and he hasn't changed anything.
