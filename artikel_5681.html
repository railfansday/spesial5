EXCLUSIVE: Ryan Serhant Warns Homebuyers Against Waiting for 2027: ‘You Don’t Want to Wait Until It’s Too Late’

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Ryan Serhant is warning would-be homebuyers against assuming that lower mortgage rates will deliver an easier path to homeownership in 2027—and says the housing market has a problem that cheaper borrowing alone cannot solve.

Speaking exclusively to Realtor.com®, Serhant shared his outlook for the year ahead, identified markets he believes could surprise buyers, and weighed in on the debate over private listings.

He also offered a glimpse inside his own townhouse and revealed a buyer request that has him searching for an enormous stretch of land with its own airport.

At the center of his housing outlook is a concern about people at opposite ends of the property ladder being unable to move.

“We have entered an environment where people can't afford to buy their first home, and their parents can't afford to leave their last one, and it's a problem we need to solve," he said.

“Lower mortgage rates will help, but rates alone are not going to fix the cost of housing or the cost of moving. We need to create more supply and fewer obstacles," Serhant added.

Serhant argued that the system has become an obstacle to moving, with consequences that have developed over many years.

“I think that what we've created is a environment, that is punishing mobility. Instead of rewarding mobility. And this was set up a long, long time ago. We didn't get here overnight.”

“Like, the housing market and the system we operate in rewards stability, because what's… it makes everyone feel comfortable. But then when that stability does not move, because stability is stability, mobility is mobility. Then everyone gets angry, and that's the environment that we're currently in.”

For his outlook, he is watching whether households have both the confidence and the resources to act.

“I think I'm watching employment, and I'm watching household confidence right now. I think pent-up demand only becomes a transaction when people have the confidence and the financial ability to act, and we'll see if that happens anytime soon.”

For people who can afford to buy but are considering waiting for a better deal in 2027, Serhant pointed to opportunities he believes already exist.

“I think in a lot of markets, we've entered a buyer's market now. You have the ability to negotiate in markets that you haven't been able to negotiate in in the past couple years.”

“I think there's a great opportunity to use the rate pressure that we have to negotiate closing costs, to negotiate rate buy-downs, to negotiate lower purchase prices, to get a house that really, really, really works for you. Remember, it is such an old and annoying saying, but you do marry the house, and you do date through the rates.”

His argument rests on the potential trade-off between borrowing costs and competition, although his comments about future rates and inventory are predictions.

“The market does become very stagnant. Interest rates should come down, and remember, you can refinance. And you should use the opportunity right now, if you can afford it, to purchase in a high-interest rate environment, because there's less competition.”

“Everyone wants low rates, they never want what comes with it. You know what'll happen if rates come down? There will be no houses for sale.”

“You won't even have the opportunity to complain about a high monthly payment. You won't even be able to talk about—the lack of affordability. You won't be able to complain, because there will be nothing for sale, because the demand is so great. There is not nearly enough houses for the demand we have in this country. Every 40-year-old who still lives at home will then move.”

Serhant also argued that the circumstances accompanying lower rates can make buyers reluctant to purchase.

“And what also brings down low interest rates is job insecurity. You know, is unemployment, is war. Are things that happen in the global environment that will make people, not confident to go and make a massive purchase. And so, you might miss that opportunity, too.”

He used the early months of the pandemic to illustrate his point about confidence and timing.

“In the spring of 2020, that was a great time to buy. But no, we had COVID, 'You can't buy. There's a death ticker on the bottom of every TV screen. You might not live.' You didn't take advantage of this environment, and then when the market came back, it was too late.”

“And so, I would use this moment to move quickly, because when things do even out, and they always do, markets go up and markets go down. Always. And forever. You don't want to wait until it's too late.”

Serhant highlighted returning city residents and investment in jobs as themes behind his market picks.

“I think people are returning to cities in a way that we haven't seen before. It's one of the reasons we just were launching, officially Chicago. This coming Wednesday. I'm incredibly excited about that.”

“The demand and the volume we've seen from all of our other Serhant markets wanting to move back to Chicago, who moved out over the past couple years. Taking advantage of good value, like we just talked about, is really exciting.”

“And I think places where you're seeing incredible job growth. Areas like Ohio, where there's major, major tech investment, not just in data centers. In office. And in manufacturing.”

“We have a lot of investment that's going into Western Pennsylvania, and, you're also seeing a lot of movement into Alabama.”

“You know, I think there are markets that will really, really surprise you because of great quality of life for the best cost structure the country has to offer.”

Away from the business of buying and selling, Serhant shared the first thing he does when he walks into his townhouse.

“I yell out my daughter's name. I wanted to see where she is in my house. We have a townhouse, so I'm trying to figure out which floor she's on, so I can, you know, prepare.”

His favorite spaces include a gym on the lower level, although his affection extends to the whole property.

“I have a pretty awesome gym. In the lower level that I built out, that's really, really cool, and I think that's pretty awesome, but I mean, I built it with my bare hand. It was a TV show that we did for Bravo about our renovation, and I love that house. Whole thing is great.”

He also described the practical details that help make his kitchen work, starting with a concealed ladder for reaching high cabinets.

“We have incredibly high ceilings. So we have a hidden ladder in our kitchen that looks like the baseboard under the kitchen. Island, and it pops out with the touch of your toe.”

“And, the ladder shows up, and so for anyone, you know, you know, who's vertically challenged to be able to reach upper cabinetry without having a ladder in front of your face at all times, so that you can still enjoy all that light and sunshine and high ceilings, I think is key.”

“Probably the greatest things that we have also in that kitchen are touchless, so, like, the sink, you know, you're cooking, and your hands are messy, and you're always hitting the thing. We have the kitchen sink, where all you have to do is tap it, and it turns on.”

“And so you just hit it with the back of your hand, and it turns on. So you're not, like, also getting the sink and the faucet dirty.”

“And then the same thing for the kitchen refrigerator and freezer. All you do is you slowly kind of, like, tap it with your elbow, or with the side, so you're not, like, yanking on anything, and it automatically just reveals itself to you. I think those little touches are overlooked.”

Among the unusual requests Serhant receives, one current search involves a vast amount of land—and a private airport.

“They're all wild. I'm dealing with somebody right now that wants 100,000 acres of land, which is impossible, for the most part."

For all the features a buyer might want, Serhant has one clear priority when choosing a home.

“You can change everything about the house you want over the years, you can grow into it, you can grow out of it, you can update it, you can paint it, you can flip it upside down. Unless you are Ken Griffin, and you can pick up a house, and you can move it, for the most part. Change the location.”

“And so, you always buy location first. Whatever that is, wherever that place is you want to be for the next 5-10 years, and most times you're paying more. But no one ever paid too much. And regretted it years later. Very, very rarely does that happen.”

“Remember, if you buy a trophy, you get to sell a trophy. If you buy a discount, because you're cheap, you will have to sell a discount. And you will feel cheap.”
