Lewis Hall jibes, Jacob Murphy's message: Four things you may have missed from Newcastle draw at Fulham

Lewis Hall felt the heat from fans as Newcastle United were held to a 1-1 draw by Fulham at Craven Cottage on Saturday.

The first half was marred by controversy as Dan Burn thought he had put Newcastle ahead before his strike was ruled out for foul by Yoane Wissa.

Harvey Barnes went close for the visitors on several occasions, while Sander Berge nearly put Fulham ahead just before the hour mark with a low driven shot.

Jacob Murphy's fine strike then put Newcastle ahead with 10 minutes to play, but Rodrigo Muniz's stunning flick shortly after gave Fulham a share of the spoils.

Fans of both clubs may have missed several key moments that shaped how the match ended.

Football Insider have looked at four flashpoints from the encounter that may be of interest to Fulham and Newcastle supporters.

Osula had only taken six touches of the ball after 21 minutes, which was still an improvement on his 13 in 90 against Everton last Monday.

The Magpies forward still struggled to get involved in the early stages of the game, and it was after 21 minutes that he gestured to teammate Thiaw in a clear change of plans.

Thiaw had been hitting the ball long to Osula's head or feet, but the Dane used his arm to tell the German defender to start playing the ball into space for him to chase instead.

It was a moment that many fans may have missed, and one that shows just how things have to change in an instant when players know things aren't going their way.

Hall has been a permanent Newcastle player for over two years now, and was on loan with the club for the season prior.

The England international did once call West London his home, though, after coming through the ranks at Fulham's local rivals, Chelsea.

Those in the stands at Craven Cottage did not hold back on their thoughts towards Hall, as a miscontrol of the ball in the first half offered them the chance to sing: "Chelsea reject, Chelsea reject" towards the Newcastle man.

Rob Jones and the VAR officials were busy throughout as several debatable calls forced them into action.

One of those went under the radar as Fulham midfielder Berge avoided a red card for his 51st-minute challenge on Steur.

The Norwegian was jumping for the ball when he landed on the teenager's achilles with his studs up, and while nothing was given, it was another controversial moment that could have resulted in a decision to change the game.

Murphy has been under fire among Newcastle supporters for his sub-par performances over the last 18 months.

His first goal since 28 February was a stunner that Bernd Leno had no chance of stopping, and the Magpies winger was understandably delighted as he sprinted off towards the celebrating Geordies.

He sent a clear message with his own celebration, cupping his hand to his ear in an obvious response to their criticism of his recent showings.
