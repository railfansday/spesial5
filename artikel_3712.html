Purdue Professor, Family Found Dead After Daughter Reported Dad Had a Gun

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

A professor at Purdue University, her husband and their two daughters were found dead inside of their Indiana home after one of her daughters called 911 to report that her father had a gun.

The Tippecanoe County Coroner’s Office said in a Friday, November 20 press release that Dr. Julie Tkach, her husband Thomas Tkach, and their teenage two daughters, Katharyn Tkach and Lauren Tkach, were found dead in the middle of the night on Thursday, November 19, at their home in West Lafayette, Indiana.

The investigation into the killings began around 1:50 a.m. on Thursday when the coroner’s office was notified that four people were found dead inside of the residence.

Despite confirming the deaths, police have not commented on a possible motive. The causes and manners of death have not yet been revealed.

However, emergency dispatch audio obtained by Star City TV implied that Thomas, 51, killed his family members before he died by suicide. In the recording, the dispatcher said they received an upsetting call from one of the daughters before the bodies were discovered.

“Thirty-two, was a shooter at home? It was going to be the caller’s father,” the dispatcher said. “She advised that her father had a gun and was threatening to shoot the family. I heard multiple gunshots go off and the line is now silent.”

Tippecanoe County Sheriff Robert Goldsmith said that one of his employees lived at the home.

“Once the scene was secured, I, Sheriff Robert Goldsmith, made the decision to contact the Indiana State Police,” Goldsmith said in a statement, according to WANE.

Officials with Purdue University confirmed that Julie, 53, worked as a clinical assistant professor at Purdue in hospitality and tourism management. She was first employed by the college in 2024.

“We are saddened by this tragedy and the death of Julie and her family,” the university said in a statement. “We offer our condolences and support to her colleagues, peers, students and all who are affected.”

Norah Ashcraft, a victim services coordinator at the Lafayette Police Department, also issued a statement about the deaths via Facebook on Friday.

“Please allow me to thank every person who tried to help the Tkach family. Always remember YOU DID SOMETHING. If you provided a safe place for the girls to be for a few moments of their life, talked to them after class, gave them a hug on the days they needed it, listened to them on a day they seemed a little off and not quite themself, then remember you did something to help,” she wrote. “If you reached out to the parents in any way, made them laugh on a day they were struggling, YOU DID SOMETHING.”

Ashcraft went on to say that the “awful thing about domestic violence” is that “we are rarely given the chance to reach out to those suffering from abuse before tragedy happens.”

“We were not meant to know what was simmering beneath the surface. To the 1st responders, dispatchers, friends and family members, thank you,” she concluded. “Please find a friend who is a good listener, share memories with them and know that this community loved this family very much.”

The Tippecanoe County Sheriff’s Office did not immediately respond to Us’ request for comment regarding updates in the case. The investigation into the case remains ongoing.
