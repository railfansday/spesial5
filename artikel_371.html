Zelda Switch 2 Pro controller restock: Live restock updates at Amazon, Target, and Best Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

We have been tracking live inventory updates for the new Zelda Switch 2 console, but the matching special-edition Pro Controller gamepad has become just as elusive. Instead of sitting on store shelves, the standalone controller is vanishing within seconds of going live online, turning into one of the most frustrating gear hunts of the entire holiday season.

With preorders completely cleared out and launch day behind us, landing one now means playing the restock waiting game. Digital storefronts might look completely barren, but stock is still quietly trickling through distribution pipelines in surprise, unannounced micro-waves.

If you want to get your hands on it the next time it is in stock, here's what's going on across different major retailers.

Before you start jumping between tabs, bookmark these retailer pages to catch surprise drops:

Best Buy — Online drops occur in waves via a digital waiting line, with scattered walk-in stock available at select physical stores.

GameStop — Web orders are off, so your best bet is to walk in and check directly with your neighborhood store.

Nintendo Store — Sold out online. You can add it to your wishlist to get notified if Nintendo opens another batch.

Target — Sold out online, but early-morning in-store pickup options occasionally go live depending on local truck deliveries.

You will notice Walmart is missing from our quick-buy links above. While the controller has a product listing on the site, stock sold directly by Walmart for the official $99.99 list price is completely gone.

Every buy button currently active is run by third-party marketplace resellers charging inflated, price-gouging sums. Do not pay those prices. If you search Walmart on your own, always filter the seller option to "Walmart" so you only see genuine retail inventory.

The feeding frenzy is directly tied to the arrival of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time on Nintendo Switch 2. The ground-up remake reworks the 1998 classic with upgraded visuals, modern camera controls, rearranged dungeon puzzles, and a full orchestral score. Long play sessions in sprawling dungeons feel more natural on a traditional, ergonomic gamepad than on standard Joy-Con grips, making this matching controller the first thing players want on their desks.

Supply is also tight across the board. In an interview about launch shortages, Bill van Zyll, Vice President and General Manager of Nintendo Latin America, admitted that the company had underestimated worldwide demand for the Zelda 40th-anniversary collection. While he confirmed that replenishment plans are underway for the hardware, software, and special controller, Nintendo has not committed to specific restock dates or shipment quantities. Until the production lines catch up, expect store inventory to pop up in quick, random bursts rather than steady, predictable drops.

The special-edition Pro Controller gamepad features decorative illustrations and subtle gold patterns inspired by the wider Zelda universe. It carries every modern upgrade found on the standard generation-two pad. You get built-in motion sensors for precision bow aiming, HD Rumble 2 for detailed vibration feedback, an integrated amiibo scanner, and a capture button for grabbing screenshots on the fly.

Nintendo also added a dedicated C button to open the Switch 2 GameChat hub, remappable GL and GR shortcut buttons on the back grips, a standard 3.5mm headphone jack, and up to 40 hours of battery life on a single charge. Best of all, it works across the entire Switch family, including the original Nintendo Switch, Switch OLED, Switch Lite, and the new Nintendo Switch 2.

Landing a surprise drop comes down to speed and a little bit of prep:

Dodge third-party marketplace sellers: Sites like Walmart and Amazon let outside merchants charge whatever they want. Always check the "Sold and shipped by" label to ensure you are buying from the actual retailer at the $99.99 list price.

Save your checkout information beforehand: Taking the time to type in a security code or shipping address during a drop will cost you your cart. Keep your profiles logged in with default payment methods saved.

Do not refresh Best Buy's waiting line: When Best Buy runs a drop, it puts shoppers into an automated queue. Hitting refresh resets your spot and sends you to the very back of the line.

Check physical stores in the morning: Online checkout bots can wipe out digital inventory in seconds. Visiting Target, Best Buy, or GameStop right when doors open often turns up fresh shipment boxes that never made it onto website listings.

Retailers love using limited-edition hardware and themed accessories as digital bait during Black Friday and Cyber Monday. It is very likely that stores are sitting on a handful of units reserved specifically for holiday sales events.

The catch is that Black Friday restocks are pure chaos. You are up against overloaded checkout servers, holiday gift shoppers, and scalper scripts all hitting the site at once. If an "Add to Cart" button flickers live on Amazon or Best Buy before the holiday rush officially starts, take the shot immediately so you don't have to fight the crowds later.

Finding one right now is definitely an endurance test, but holding out for that $99.99 list price beats paying double to a reseller every time.
