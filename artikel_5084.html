Australian Crypto Investors Turn to Loans Ahead of 2027 Tax Reform

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Home » Crypto Market News » Australian Crypto Investors Turn to Loans Ahead of 2027 Tax Reform

Upcoming capital gains tax reforms are prompting crypto holders to reconsider how they access cash without selling their digital assets

One in five Australian cryptocurrency investors would consider borrowing against their digital assets rather than selling them, as upcoming tax changes push holders to rethink their financial strategies.

A survey commissioned by digital asset wealth platform Nexo found that 22.7% of Australian crypto holders would consider borrowing against their holdings instead of selling, compared with 10% of other investors.

The findings come as Australia prepares to overhaul its capital gains tax (CGT) system from July 1, 2027.

Nexo surveyed 1,000 Australian investors this October to examine how major expenses and rising living costs affect investment decisions.

The results suggest that crypto holders are more inclined to avoid selling their investments when they need additional funds.

Beyond the 22.7% considering borrowing, another 19.7% said they had delayed or decided against selling their crypto holdings.

Crypto investors can borrow against their existing holdings instead of selling them for cash. This allows them to access money while keeping their Bitcoin or other digital assets, and potentially benefit if their value increases.

The trend comes against a broader backdrop of financial pressure. According to the survey, 29.4% of all investors had delayed, reduced or cancelled a major purchase or financial plan over the previous 12 months.

The upcoming CGT reform is another factor investors may need to consider.

Under Australia’s current system, eligible individuals, trusts and partnerships can receive a 50% capital gains tax discount on assets held for more than 12 months.

From July 1, 2027, this discount will be replaced by cost base indexation and a 30% minimum tax on eligible gains.

Under transitional arrangements, gains accrued before that date will remain eligible for the existing 50% discount, while gains accumulated afterward will be subject to the new rules.

Crypto ownership in Australia is at a record high, with 2.79 million Australians holding or trading cryptocurrency, according to the figures cited in an Australian investor Study.

That equals about 24% of the country’s 11.5 million investors, up from roughly 15% in 2023, making crypto as widely held as exchange-traded funds (ETFs).

As ownership expands, the demand for financial products that allow investors to access liquidity without selling their holdings could also increase.

Consequently, as their holdings rise in value, more investors may look for ways to cover major expenses without selling their crypto.

The new CGT rules could make some investors think twice before selling crypto that has increased in value. As a result, more holders may consider borrowing against their holdings to cover major expenses without having to sell their assets and potentially trigger a capital gains tax bill.
