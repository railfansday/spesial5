Invesco moves stablecoin reserves on-chain, but hides a liquidity gap

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Invesco's new Ethereum-based stablecoin reserve fund requires institutions to follow business-day redemption schedules.

Invesco announced a government money market fund on Nov. 17 that gives eligible institutions a way to hold part of their stablecoin reserves in fund shares recorded on Ethereum.

If a stablecoin issuer needs dollars from the fund to pay customers, it has to redeem those shares on the fund’s business-day schedule.

The Invesco Stablecoin Reserves Onchain Fund (IROXX) is initially aimed at institutions such as stablecoin issuers and digital asset infrastructure providers. Its shares represent an investment in short-duration US Treasury securities and other eligible reserve assets. Holding a stablecoin alone gives a customer no right to redeem shares in the fund.

Investors in supported jurisdictions need an approved Superstate account and a verified wallet on the allowlist to hold IROXX on the Ethereum network.

The standard initial minimum is $1 million per fund account, and Invesco or an intermediary can combine master accounts and subaccounts to meet it.

To redeem, shareholders submit a request through the Investor Portal run by Superstate, the fund’s sub-transfer agent.

Under the fund’s redemption terms, Superstate normally sends a dollar wire on the next business day if it receives a valid request before 5 p.m. Eastern. For a request received after the cutoff on a business day, the fund normally prices the redemption and sends the wire on the next business day.

The price is the next net asset value per share calculated after Superstate receives the request in good order. The ordinary payment terms allow up to seven days after Superstate receives a request in good order.

Early closes can affect same-day payment, and the fund can also withhold same-day payment in the 30 minutes before it calculates the share price.

A fund business day depends on the New York Fed, the fund’s custodian, and its main portfolio markets being open. Management also has to judge that the market can handle purchase and redemption requests.

Shareholders pay blockchain transaction fees, known as gas, on redemptions. Congestion or insufficient gas can delay a request past the cutoff even if the shareholder starts the transaction before it.

Investors can also elect USDC proceeds through a third-party intermediary, with the service setting its own onboarding and conversion terms, including when it delivers the tokens. The fund’s normal wire schedule gives investors a timetable for when dollars are sent, while USDC delivery depends on the intermediary.
