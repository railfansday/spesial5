Bitcoin hashrate falls 8% with 11M BTC in loss – 2 reasons bears are watching

Q4 alone has pushed Bitcoin several years back across multiple market metrics.

Bitcoin’s breakdown to $55k last week took its price back to pre-2024 U.S. elections. So, almost every increase in Bitcoin’s value since the election, even the surge to $126k, has therefore been reversed. The selloff has pushed 11.6 million BTC underwater, the highest on record.

And now, this is starting to affect the Bitcoin options market too.

From the data shown below, BTC options traders are turning increasingly bearish. Put option buying has accelerated to levels last witnessed during BTC’s 2022 capitulation.

Likewise, the put-to-call ratio has reached its highest value since 2021. These extreme readings are rare and suggest that traders are increasingly preparing for more downside.

Naturally, this raises the question: What is driving this shift in sentiment?

From a market psychology perspective, Bitcoin’s [BTC] drop to multi-year lows has put growing pressure on investor profitability. With 11.6 million BTC now underwater, holding through the downturn is becoming a tougher bet, especially in the current macro environment.

Notably, nothing illustrates this better than a recent Glassnode report.

Bitcoin’s falling hashrate has put miner profitability back under pressure.

To date in 2026, MARA Holdings has sold 40,581 BTC at an average price of $74,446, amassing around $3.03 billion. Much of this sale is related to the switch by Bitcoin miners to AI. Yet, the latest figures from Glassnode indicate that there may be further strains on mining.

Bitcoin’s hashrate is currently 8% down on where it was last year, the first time there has been a yearly reduction since China banned mining in 2021. However, whereas the 2021 fall was due to regulatory factors, today’s decline is mainly due to price. The bigger concern is the timing. Just 15 months before the next BTC halving occurs, miners are already contending with reduced profit margins.

In short, Bitcoin’s continued hashrate decline reflects the growing stress across the mining sector.

Miners are already seeing reduced profitability. The upcoming halving will reduce miners’ block reward and so may exacerbate this issue. There is therefore a greater risk that miners may “continue” to switch off hardware and sell Bitcoin to maintain cashflow.

So, for now, the falling hashrate, rising underwater supply, and bearish options positioning all suggest that the market is already preparing for a tougher phase.

Bitcoin’s falling hashrate and tighter mining margins raise fresh fears of miner capitulation.

Bearish options positioning and record-high underwater supply suggest investors are bracing for further downside.
