You don’t need a secret to build wealth on an ordinary paycheck, just a few habits kept for decades. Hold your spending well below your income as it rises, and invest the difference automatically in low-cost index funds. Keep buying when the market falls, and treat each mistake as a lesson rather than a reason to quit.

Warren Buffett has lived in the Omaha house he bought for $31,500 since 1958. You can copy that choice on an ordinary paycheck by letting your spending rise more slowly than your income.

What you don’t spend becomes wealth only once it’s invested and left alone for decades. At an assumed 7% average annual return, a couple who invest $1,500 a month from age 30 pass $1 million at about 53, without a single stock tip. By then they’ve put in about $410,000, so more than half of the total is investment growth.

The $1,500 is there every month because they keep their 10-year-old cars and modest house as their incomes rise.

Expect those drops. In a 1996 interview with PBS’s Frontline, Peter Lynch, who ran Fidelity’s Magellan Fund from 1977 to 1990, counted 53 stock market drops of 10% or more in the previous 95 years, about one every two years. Fifteen of them were 25% or more. His advice was to buy, hold, and add more when prices drop.

Schedule the first purchase for the day after payday, so the money is invested before you can spend it.
