Sell Your House Without an Agent – Buy the MLS Listing, Then Run the Sale

Selling your house without an agent means doing a listing agent’s jobs yourself, in order. Price it from recent nearby sales, pay a flat-fee broker to put it on the multiple listing service (MLS), and decide early what you’ll pay a buyer’s agent. Then show it, negotiate, and close through a title company or attorney.

Of those jobs, the one you can’t do yourself is the MLS listing, since only a licensed broker can post there. It also reaches the most buyers, because their agents search the MLS, which passes its listings to the big home search sites.

A flat-fee broker charges one set price to enter your listing and leaves the rest to you. Check what the plan charges at closing, since some add a percentage of the sale price.

Decide before you list what you’ll pay toward a buyer’s agent. Since August 2024, National Association of Realtors rules keep that offer off the MLS. Buyers who work with an agent sign an agreement setting the agent’s pay, and they can ask you to cover it in their offer. Tell agents who call your answer.

Say you pay $400 for the MLS listing, $300 for photos, and $900 for an attorney to draft and review the contract, then agree to pay 2% of a $350,000 price toward the buyer’s agent’s fee. That’s $8,600 before closing costs, $7,000 of it to the buyer’s agent, with no listing commission.

Before you take the house off the market, call the lender on the buyer’s preapproval letter to confirm it rests on verified income and assets, since lenders don’t all mean the same thing by preapproval.
