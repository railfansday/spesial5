Wait for Every W-2, 1099 & 1098 Before Filing – The IRS Matches Each One

Before you file your tax return, wait until every W-2, 1099, 1098, and 1095-A due to you has arrived, which can take until mid-February. The IRS gets a copy of each form, and when your return leaves one out, it can send a notice proposing more tax, plus interest.

Most of those forms don’t have to go out until January 31, and a few not until mid-February, so a return filed in January can reach the IRS before they reach you.

The IRS checks each return against the copies it received, and income missing from yours can bring a CP2000 notice proposing the tax on it, plus interest from the April due date.

Say you’re in the 22% tax bracket and file on January 25. A week later, a Form 1099-INT arrives showing $600 of interest from your high-yield savings account. At 22%, that’s $132 of tax your return left out. Unless you amend your return first, expect a CP2000 for that $132, plus interest.

If one of these hasn’t arrived by mid-February, ask the sender for a copy, and ask for a corrected copy of any that’s wrong. When a missing form won’t come in time to file, report the income from your own statements, since the IRS requires you to report interest even when no 1099-INT arrives.

The IRS gives brokers until mid-February to send consolidated 1099s, which combine investment sales, dividends, and interest. HealthCare.gov says Form 1095-A, for Marketplace health coverage, should reach you by then, and not to file until you have an accurate one. If you have a brokerage account that isn’t an IRA, wait until mid-March. Companies and funds sometimes change how a dividend they already reported is taxed, which means a corrected 1099, and Ally says those changes most likely come before March 15.

Build a checklist of forms from last year’s return, then add one for each new job, account, and loan.
