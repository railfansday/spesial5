We Tested the Shimano Sustain FK Spinning Reel in Fresh and Salt Water

The market is cluttered with spinning reels, and sorting through them to find one that meets specific needs can be a headache. That is especially true in light-tackle and inshore fishing, easily the most popular market for spinning reel manufacturers. At first glance, many reels in the 2500-size class, the standard size for this category, look identical. Appearances can be deceiving, though, and knowing how and where you plan to use a reel matters more in this class than in most others.

After all, why spend almost $400 on a 2500-size spinning reel that looks and feels about the same in hand as one that costs a quarter of the price? It turns out there are plenty of reasons, and Shimano makes the case for most of them with its updated Sustain FK series.

The Sustain FK gets its strength and light weight from Hagane metal body technology, a process of cold-forging rigid metals such as aluminum and magnesium. As part of Shimano’s MagnumLite series, the metal frame is also paired with an ultralight CI4+ carbon rotor. The result is a reel that feels solid and strong where it should, while staying smooth and light in use.

The Sustain FK uses DuraCross, a relatively new drag system from Shimano. The idea is that precisely layered materials give the reel a smoother, longer-lasting drag than older materials such as felt. Inside, the reel uses S A-RB bearings, which are shielded and rust-resistant to keep debris out. That adds a layer of protection against sand and salt, which helps keep the reel working in the saltwater conditions it was built for. Shimano also uses what it calls X-Protect, essentially a labyrinth of barriers that makes it far more difficult for salt water or grit to reach the interior of the reel.

No reel is invincible to the hazards of extended saltwater use, but the Sustain FK has a number of features that make it well suited to the task. Corrosion-resistant materials in the frame and gearing, S A-RB sealed bearings and Shimano’s X-Protect design give this reel several strong defenses against the wear and tear of frequent saltwater exposure. Shimano also pairs the DuraCross drag with its Rigid Support Drag to give this small reel a surprisingly strong backbone. The DuraCross drag in particular is a serious upgrade from the standard felt or carbon washers of previous models.

Shimano offers a one-year service warranty on its reels, including the Sustain FK. After that year, you can still send the reel in for service, and Shimano will usually inspect it and tell you the cost before doing the work. Experiences vary, but I’ve sent reels to Shimano countless times over the years and have found its warranty and service system easy to deal with and accommodating.

Shimano touts the Sustain FK as a crossover reel for fresh water and inshore salt water, with a combination of light finesse and rigid durability suited to both. With that in mind, I fished it in a wide range of environments over the course of a year, from bass fishing on large lakes and rivers with a variety of techniques to inshore saltwater fishing that ranged from shallow flats to deep near-shore jigging. The goal was to see whether it could stand up to the all-purpose abuse dished out by hardcore anglers nationwide while still delivering the refinement and finesse expected of a top-end spinning reel in the age of forward-facing sonar. Fishing from both boats and kayaks, I caught largemouth and smallmouth bass, redfish, striped bass and speckled trout.

Corrosion will always be the No. 1 concern when using any reel in salt water, especially one as small and lightweight as the Sustain FK 2500. To get a proper idea of how this reel holds up, I spent the better part of a year fishing with it from my kayak in places like Chesapeake Bay and Laguna Madre in Texas.

A kayak is a great place to test how a reel holds up to the salt because the reel gets so much more direct exposure. Spray tends to reach reels sitting in kayak rod holders, and splashing water is much closer to your gear during high-intensity moments like fighting fish or navigating heavy current. Then there are the times anglers (myself included) accidentally submerge their gear when they aren’t paying attention.

So, how did the Sustain FK hold up? Remarkably well, I’m pleased to say. After roughly eight months of consistent, heavy saltwater use, the reel is still about as smooth as the day I opened it. It needs some basic maintenance, such as a freshwater rinse and a coat of reel oil now and then, but overall, I’m very impressed.

Smoothness matters, but a viable saltwater spinning reel needs a lot more than that. Saltwater game fish are bigger, stronger and live in harsher environments than most freshwater species, and a light spinning reel needs the backbone to stand up to that kind of abuse regularly without losing function. This is where the Sustain FK impressed me most. I intentionally used it in situations where the odds of hooking fish well outside its class were high, and I was able to fight and land everything from bull redfish to large striped bass around heavy cover without the reel bending or warping. The drag may be the reel’s best feature. It stayed smooth and clean during long runs that would have fried the drag on many reels in its class. The Rigid Support system was just as impressive. A reel this light will typically flex under that much strain, but the Sustain FK stayed rigid and smooth.

Ultimately, the Sustain FK passed every saltwater test with flying colors.

Being able to land saltwater fish well outside its intended class is great, but a high-end spinning reel should perform best at what it is intended for. That means no hiccups with anti-reverse or drag slippage, and handling line twist and line lay well. Today, it also means having the fine-tuning required for the extremely finesse-oriented game of fishing with forward-facing sonar. Love it or hate it, forward-facing sonar has become a standard part of professional angling, especially in the bass world.

With all that in mind, I used the Sustain FK for a wide range of freshwater applications. It performed well in traditional settings like fishing light jigs for river smallmouth bass and crappie, but that’s admittedly not a demanding test. Where this reel really shone was targeting bass on large, deep lakes where finesse and control at depth were key. Fishing with and without forward-facing sonar, I was impressed with the amount of control I could keep on the lure. From jerkbaits to small soft plastics, it never faltered with line management or fine-tuned movements.

The anti-twist fin that Shimano builds into the line roller on many of its high-end reels may sound like a gimmick, but trust me, it makes a big difference. There’s not much worse than light braid twisting while you try to make long casts or present a bait at depth. It can cause wind knots and a host of other issues. Throughout testing, the line on this reel never kinked, twisted or laid poorly on the spool.

Finally, while the smooth, strong drag is great in salt water, the ability to fine-tune it is even more impressive for finesse fishing in fresh water. Getting a solid hook set in deep water on leader as light as 6-pound test can be critical for serious anglers, and that requires a drag that doesn’t slip or freeze up. The Sustain FK drag can be adjusted to the most minute settings, allowing for just the right resistance when setting the hook or playing fish on light line.

I can’t remember using a reel that was as much at home fishing ultralight for crappie and perch as it was fishing a marsh for drag-ripping bull redfish and striped bass. I may be in the minority, but I love having a lightweight 2500-size spinning reel that pulls double duty as both my favorite freshwater finesse setup and my favorite inshore spinning rig. The crossover ability of the Sustain FK is fantastic. It’s not just a good freshwater reel that’s decent in the salt, or a saltwater reel that’s light enough to use in fresh water. It’s at the top of its game in both.

The Sustain FK is an exceptional reel, so finding areas for improvement is difficult. The most obvious is the price. The engineering and performance can justify it, but there’s no denying that nearly $400 for a light spinning reel is steep for the average angler. There are plenty of equally light and smooth spinning reels on the market for far less. Those reels may not match the Sustain FK’s quality and durability across the board, but those features probably don’t matter as much to many freshwater or casual inshore anglers.

If you’re looking for a light-tackle spinning reel that’s durable and strong while still being incredibly smooth and light, the Sustain FK is the ideal choice. It’s comfortable to fish in both fresh and salt water, with the refinement needed for the most finesse-oriented applications.

This review focused on the 2500, but the Sustain FK currently comes in sizes from 2500 through 5000. The limited size range is a downside, but given that this reel is designed for such a specific level of performance, it makes sense to offer it only in sizes that don’t compromise that.

Although the price tag isn’t cheap, this reel is the complete package for serious anglers who want professional-grade performance from their gear. Tournament bass anglers and hardcore inshore anglers alike will appreciate the refinement the Sustain FK delivers over similar-size, budget-friendly options.
