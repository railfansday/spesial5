‘Heads should roll’: owner of Melbourne Cup horse found dumped in skip bin demands investigation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Stuart Ramsey says it is a ‘bloody disgrace’ the gelding was left in the skip bin while awaiting burial

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

So You Are’s owner-breeder has demanded a full investigation after the Melbourne Cup runner’s body was found in a skip at a Sydney tip, insisting “heads have got to roll”.

Stuart Ramsey says it is a “bloody disgrace” the gelding was left in the skip bin while awaiting burial at Sydney’s Cleanaway Lucas Heights Resource Recovery Centre.

The grisly sight was uncovered by a Sydney man, with online footage outraging industry participants and anti-horse racing activists alike.

Bjorn Baker’s promising stayer took ill at a spelling farm after finishing eighth in Australia’s most famous race on 3 November.

He was rushed to the REC Equine Specialists veterinary clinic at Horsley Park in Sydney’s west after showing signs of colic on Monday morning.

Surgery could not save the horse and he was euthanised under anaesthesia.

Racing NSW said its ongoing investigations found So You Are was being held temporarily awaiting burial when the footage was recorded.

While REC has apologised, renowned breeder Ramsey of boutique Hunter Valley farm Turangga Stud, wants answers and accountability over the disposal of the five-year-old, who earned $1.4m from eight career wins.

“There should be a full investigation. Heads have got to roll – simple as that,” Ramsey said.

“He was more than just a racehorse. He brought our family together, and to think of the stress the family are under now because of this – it’s a bloody disgrace.

“We took 30-odd people down to Melbourne for the race. So You Are brought us all together, as only a horse can do.

REC’s managing partner, Jonathan Lumsden, said the centre had spoken with So You Are’s owners and trainers to apologise.

He pointed out the veterinary practice did not carry out the burial but took “full responsibility” for choosing the service provider and arrangements.

“He should have been promptly and respectfully buried,” Lumsden said in a statement.

Ramsey, who said he hadn’t been contacted by REC, isn’t satisfied with their explanation.

“So they should take responsibility. It wasn’t the contractor’s fault; it was their fault. They were in charge of the horse,” he said.

“They didn’t even give us the option of burying him up at Turangga … they should all be out – gone.

“I don’t mean everyone, but the bosses and the people who’ve made the decisions.”

Ramsey declared racehorses among the best-looked-after animals in the world and spoke of his affinity for So You Are.

The treatment of So You Are’s remains has sparked strong criticism from animal welfare groups.

“There is always the option to bury the horse or have the horse cremated,” the Coalition for the Protection of Racehorses campaign director, Elio Celotto, said.

“However, it is more expedient to dump the horse at a local tip. This is business as usual for an industry where profit is always more important than sentiment.”

Victorian Animal Justice party MP Georgie Purcell said the tragic case should force the industry to confront serious questions about duty of care.

“The racing industry breeds these horses and profits from them but fails the most basic obligation to treat them with dignity after death,” she said.

Racing NSW said “appropriate action” would be taken if any wrongdoing was discovered in its ongoing investigation.

“Under NSW Department of Primary Industries guidelines, a licensed landfill is the preferred option for animals who have died via euthanasia due to the chemicals involved in the process,” it said.

“That burial, however, should have occurred in a timely and respectful manner.”

Baker said he was also left deeply distressed by the handling of the much-loved horse’s remains, adding they deserved to be treated with dignity and respect.
