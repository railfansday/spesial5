Iraola fumes at bungling fourth official for costing Liverpool a penalty

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Andoni Iraola wanted to talk about his team and their need to improve but how could he concentrate on something so straightforward when he had seen something so bizarre?

It is important, first and foremost, to explain this is not painting a narrative about Liverpool being unfortunate. Far from it. Iraola hissed his dismay that Nottingham Forest had shown more intensity and been the better team for long periods in an entertaining 2-2 draw.

But the penalty that changed the contest? Iraola, his coaching staff, even the supporters behind the dugout had been left flabbergasted that the malfunctioning number board of fourth official Tim Robinson presented an avenue for Forest to exploit.

Iraola had tried to make a triple change, wanting to introduce Ryan Gravenberch, Kostas Tsimikas and Rio Ngumoha, but as Robinson pressed buttons to no avail and everyone in Red became frustrated, Forest substitute Liam Delap took a quick throw and the Liverpool house almost caved in.

Neco Williams, once of Liverpool, surged into the penalty area, Alisson Becker arrived a fraction too late after the defender got his shot away and took his old team-mate down. Morgan Gibbs-White exacerbated the damage when sending Alisson the wrong way and this was the last thing a manager wanting to make an impression on a big day needed to see but this was reality.

“I cannot explain because I don’t have an explanation,” Iraola said. “I wanted to make those substitutions about three minutes before. I mean, it was two, three, four times the ball had gone out. I still don’t know the reason why we couldn’t make the subs.

“I think [the fourth official] was having some problems with the board or something. It [the penalty] wouldn’t have happened if I had made the sub. I asked. I had complained the previous time. The supporters were shouting and they were aware of the situation. But I don’t know what happened and I don’t know why they have given the penalty.”

“A lack of intensity hurt us,” Iraola said with obvious disdain and it was only down to a show of character in the second period that a slovenly first half did not enable Forest to win at Anfield for the third consecutive year, for all that the travelling fans thought it was “happening again!”

Forest, bruised after two defeats in four days to Leeds, looked like an Oliver Glasner side. They were aggressive and well organised, the only failing was that physically they could not see the job through, but the Austrian insisted that will change in due course.

Once Glasner is settled in and he gets his transfer business done – “definitely one, possibly two and probably three,” was his mischievous reply to how the window will unfold – Forest will be a formidable proposition, especially if the first 45 minutes are a barometer.

They had been knocking on the door before Dan Ndoye eventually broke the deadlock, sweeping a left-footed drive past Alisson after Igor Jesus had won a header from a long clearance. It was a simple goal, marginally onside, but it was absolutely deserved.

Anfield will host its first Premier League match of the 2026-27 season.

Good morning and welcome to our live coverage of this Premier League match between Liverpool and Nottingham Forest at Anfield.

This encounter pits two summer appointments – Andoni Iraola and Oliver Glasner – against each other as they both search for their first top-flight win at their new clubs.

In the opening Premier League game-week, Iraola’s Liverpool needed a last-gasp 99th-minute penalty from Dominik Szoboszlai to rescue an unconvincing 2-2 draw with Newcastle United at St. James’ Park. Meanwhile, Nottingham Forest have suffered back-to-back home defeats in four days against Leeds United without scoring (1-0 Premier League, 2-0 Carabao Cup).

Iraola has been handed the unenviable task of steering Liverpool back on track following a hugely disappointing campaign under Arne Slot, whose side’s defence of their Premier League ended in a meek fifth-place finish despite spending more than £400m in the previous summer.

The former Bournemouth boss must uncover how to get the best out of big-money recruits such as Alexander Isak, Florian Wirtz, Milos Kerkez and Jeremie Frimpong, and also tighten up Liverpool’s vulnerable midfield and defence. Given the pressure of the job and the club’s hectic schedule while balancing domestic and European football, it’s a tough ask. However, Liverpool have agreed a £120m deal for Paris St-Germain winger Bradley Barcola to bolster their attacking reinforcements, but the transfer hasn’t been finalised in time for today’s match.

Last season, Forest endured an equally tumultuous campaign, churning through a staggering four managers, before eventually stumbling to a 16th-place finish. Glasner has been hand-picked by owner Evangelos Marinakis to usher in an era of stability and success, after winning the FA Cup, Community Shield and Conference League in just over two years at Crystal Palace.

Both Marinakis and Glasner are extremely ambitious and demanding figures. You’d imagine a top-half finish would be considered the absolute minimum for Forest, while Glasner’s track record of winning trophies surely indicates that Marinakis is eyeing some silverware at the City Ground.

It’s a meeting of two managers who must deliver. Three points for either side would serve as a much-needed tonic to kickstart their seasons.

Kick-off is at 12:30, with team news coming shortly.
