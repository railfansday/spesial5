The Good, The Bad and The Ugly: LSU vs. Alabama

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

It seems like the bye week set LSU back. The progress the team was making leading up to the Auburn game was all thrown away in one game against Alabama. This was the first game in which LSU faced a ranked opponent, got handled, and suffered a lopsided defeat.

Here are the good, the bad, and the ugly from the Alabama game.

In contrast to what some of the LSU fan base may believe, the LSU defense was actually good in this game as an entire unit.

Alabama came into this game averaging 342 yards per game on the road. On Saturday, LSU held the Tide to just 285 total yards. Alabama normally averages 129 rushing yards and 213 passing yards on the road, and LSU held the Tide below both averages, allowing only 206 yards through the air and 79 yards on the ground. LSU was also able to record two sacks on Keelon Russell.

But even better than the defense was Jackson Harris, who continued to showcase exactly why he is one of the best receivers in the SEC.

Harris finished the game with 166 receiving yards on 11 receptions. He was the most consistent threat LSU had offensively, along with Trey'Dez Green. Green finished with 10 catches for 85 yards on 11 targets.

I would like to see both players get more opportunities in the red zone to potentially help address those issues.

The red zone and offensive line continue to show up as glaring weaknesses for this team.

LSU had a clear objective to beat Alabama: run the football and convert in the red zone.

LSU finished the game with just 77 rushing yards, and Dilin Jones accounted for 57 of them, with 48 coming after first contact. The offensive line wasn't getting any push and, for the first time in several weeks, gave up multiple sacks.

Leavitt did run himself into a couple of them, but there were also missed blocking assignments, unblocked pressures, and pass rushers who disrupted the timing of routes in key situations.

The play at the line of scrimmage also impacts how the offense can perform in the red zone.

If a team gets inside the 20-yard line and cannot run the ball consistently, it allows the defense to dictate how it wants to play because there isn't much field left to defend. The defense can treat the offense as one-dimensional, become more aggressive, and disguise blitz packages and coverages.

These issues seemed like they were getting corrected, and with the return of Trey'Dez Green last week, it appeared LSU was turning the corner and progressing in the right direction offensively at the right time.

The coaching was absolutely abysmal in this game.

It felt like LSU should have had a coaching advantage in nearly every matchup, but that was not the case.

Lane Kiffin's career record against ranked opponents is 15–27, and against top-10 teams, he is only 6–18 and 0–3 this year.

The coaching was a problem in this game, and there's no other way to put it.

There were several decisions that deserved to be questioned.

The game plan and play-calling when the offense entered Alabama territory would have left you feeling like this was a first-year head coach and offensive coordinator calling plays.

It seemed like Alabama led LSU into a trap and forced the Tigers to play a style of offense they simply aren't capable of executing.

It almost seemed as if Bama's plan was to let LSU get inside the 40-yard line and then play the defense it wanted to play. That way, Alabama wouldn't expose its own weaknesses but would instead expose LSU's.

Defensively, the unit was good, but there were two plays that stood out.

The first touchdown came after LSU attempted and missed a 56-yard field goal. When Bama got inside the 5-yard line, it called a perfectly designed end-around play to Ryan Coleman-Williams, which completely fooled the LSU defense.

The other play came on Alabama's first possession after LSU took the lead by scoring its only touchdown and final points of the game.

Bama completed a deep pass for 54 yards down the left sideline. Four plays later, Alabama completed a pass to tight end Kaleb Edwards, who was wide open in the end zone.

The coaching was bad, and the decisions to go for it on fourth down four times were also very head-scratching.

This game felt like the complete inverse of the Kentucky game.

Against Kentucky, Lane Kiffin decided not to go for it on fourth-and-one with a chance to win the game in regulation. Against Alabama, he decided to attempt nearly every fourth down that was worth considering, even when punting probably would have been the better decision.

LSU finished this game with 457 yards of total offense compared to Bama's 280, recorded 27 first downs compared to Bama's 13, and controlled the time of possession, 32:45 to 27:15.

Looking at those numbers, you would think LSU had won this game 31–13.

LSU has a chance to change the narrative back in its favor next Saturday night against Texas. This will be another ranked opponent and another top-10 opponent for Lane Kiffin to face.

Recently, Kiffin has been able to respond very well following a loss.

Can he break the trend of losing to ranked opponents this year, or will this be the first time he has lost back-to-back games since 2022?
