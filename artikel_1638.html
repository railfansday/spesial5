How Xabi Alonso decisions backfired to plunge Chelsea further into crisis

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Your weekly briefing on Chelsea, featuring team news and expert analysis from Dom Smith

Chelsea are embroiled in a crisis. They look like one of the worst teams in the Premier League right now.

That is the reality their highly-regarded manager Xabi Alonso and expensively-assembled squad must accept they find themselves in after Friday night’s grim 3-2 loss to Nottingham Forest, their fourth league defeat in five games.

Alonso and his players must share the responsibility for another incoherent performance from a team that looks so recently cobbled together that watching them makes you feel as though it is August, not three months into the season.

The manager got some selection decisions wrong. Dropping Levi Colwill instead of Wesley Fofana at centre-back was an eyebrow-raiser before kick-off and was punished brutally when Fofana — not exactly having a cracker of a week — was turned inside out by his former team-mate Liam Delap and then fouled him to concede a penalty from Chelsea’s one-goal deficit became two.

Cue a yellow card, Fofana subbed off for Colwill, and Alonso left facing questions.

Similarly alongside Fofana, where Moises Caicedo was shifted back from midfield to centre-back but was error-prone all night and had an off day, much like Chelsea, who are getting used to having those.

Further forward, Danny Welbeck was ineffectual and, if anything, seemed to repel the ball. At half-time he was replaced by Emmanuel Emegha, who scored Chelsea’s only goal at Leeds last Saturday, now went on to net both of their goals at Forest, and will have been left wondering why he had not been picked to start the game even more so than when the teamsheet was first read out to the squad.

His two goals were striker’s finishes from speculative crosses, goals that just sort of seemed to happen rather than being the product of a flowing move here or a well-formed pattern of play there. Chelsea did none of that, again. Against Sunderland, Leeds and now Forest, they have been confused, confusing and toothless.

In this rotten period where they have so missed the injured striker Joao Pedro and centre-back Maxence Lacroix, they have shown an incredible ability to play people into form. They played Sunderland into form before the international break and now came a gift-wrapped brace for goal-shy Morgan Gibbs-White.

Where Chelsea go from here is really anyone’s guess. Hosting the division’s bottom side Crystal Palace on Wednesday is, on the face of it, a kind fixture. But the last time they faced 20th in the table, they lost at Sunderland and failed to even score.

Most concerning of all for Alonso and his coaching staff will have been how effective Forest’s high-intensity approach to the game proved. A committed press against a confidence-shot defence earned them a victory over Chelsea that future opponents will watch back and surely believe to be repeatable.

That is all their free weeks on the training ground with no European football for disruption has gained them.

This crisis of confidence appears to be teaching their opponents more about Chelsea than it is teaching Chelsea about themselves.
