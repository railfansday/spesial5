Five Signs a Seller Has Genuine Negotiating Flexibility

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Redfin’s September analysis estimated that sellers outnumbered buyers by 57.9% nationally in August, the largest gap in its records dating to 2013. Nashville, Miami and Houston had more than twice as many sellers as buyers.

“Buyers hear that sellers are negotiating again and understandably want to know how much they can take off the price,” says Nick Nastos, real estate broker and founder of Chicago’s Property Shop. “Before we talk about a discount, I want to know what else they could buy. If there are only two suitable homes in their budget, they may have less leverage than the headline suggests.”

Nastos recommends checking five signs before deciding how aggressively to negotiate.

Similar homes are selling below their asking prices. Recent closed sales reveal what buyers actually paid. Compare properties with similar condition, size and location, and check whether sellers provided credits.

“A seller asking $500,000 is giving you their opening position,” Nastos says. “If comparable homes are closing at $470,000, you have something concrete to discuss.”

The listing is taking longer than its competitors. A home sitting beyond the typical selling time for comparable properties may offer an opening. However, buyers should investigate why it remains available, including repairs, unusual layouts or financing complications. Check the original listing date and any previous withdrawals or relistings, rather than relying only on the current days-on-market count.

Buyers have several realistic alternatives. More national inventory means little if the additional homes fall outside a buyer’s budget or requirements. Count suitable alternatives in the same neighborhood and price range.

“If you can genuinely walk away and choose another home, that changes the conversation,” Nastos notes. “Ten listings you would never buy do not give you the same leverage.”

The seller is already adjusting the offer. Repeated price reductions or advertised closing-cost assistance can signal flexibility. A reduction alone does not establish a bargain, particularly if the original price was inflated. Buyers should assess the revised price against recent sales and expected repair costs.

Price is not the seller’s only priority. A seller may value a dependable closing date, flexible possession or fewer logistical complications. Buyers can also ask about repair credits or closing-cost contributions, subject to lender rules.

“Ask what the seller needs before assuming every negotiation has to end with a lower price,” Nastos says. “Sometimes you can solve a timing problem for them and preserve cash for yourself.”

His final advice is to set a firm spending limit before negotiating. A competitive neighborhood may justify a stronger offer, but it does not make an unaffordable payment safer.
