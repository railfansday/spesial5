AI startup Numa raises $45 million to help car dealerships leave the paperwork behind

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Artificial intelligence promises to untangle the messy workflows that have long plagued legacy industries. One startup is bringing that pitch to car dealerships. On Monday, Oakland-based company Numa announced a $45 million Series C round to build out an AI operating system designed to handle the daily grind of dealership customer service, from answering calls and booking appointments to keeping customers updated on their cars.

It works like this: Numa’s AI agents connect to a dealership’s software to check inventory down to a car’s trim and features, pull repair updates and log sales leads alongside conversation transcripts. The platform also handles routine warranty paperwork and flags exchanges that may signal an unhappy customer for managers to review.

“Think about things like filling out warranty paperwork. That is a drudgery of a job that nobody wants to do, and people do it poorly. You can apply an AI and do it instantly,” Numa CEO Tasso Roumeliotis told Fortune.

Blue Cloud Ventures led the round, bringing Numa’s total funding to $91 million. Threshold Ventures, Costanoa Ventures, Touring Capital, Gradient Ventures, Mitsui and Tokio Marine also participated. Founded in 2017, Numa previously raised seed and Series A funding from investors including Draper Fisher Jurvetson. The latest round valued Numa at $500 million after the investment, according to a source familiar with the company.

Numa’s pitch comes as more companies put AI agents to work. In a survey of roughly 2,100 senior leaders across 20 countries and industries ranging from automotive to healthcare, accounting and consulting firm KPMG found that 34% reported widespread employee adoption of AI agents in the third quarter of 2026, up from 25% in the first quarter.

For Roumeliotis, Numa began with a familiar team and a new problem to solve.

He had previously built Location Labs, a mobile technology company that tapped into the early spread of GPS-enabled cellphones to help people track one another’s locations. After selling the business in 2015, he joined venture firm Draper Fisher Jurvetson as an entrepreneur-in-residence.

While he was there, former colleagues from Location Labs approached him with a proposition: They wanted to build another company together. The group included engineers, product specialists and executives who had taken on bigger roles under Roumeliotis.

In 2017, they launched Number AI to help small businesses receive and respond to texts through their existing landline numbers, using natural language processing to bring newer technology to businesses still relying on older tools.

But as they worked with car dealerships, they found problems that went beyond missed messages. Employees juggled calls, paperwork and manual follow-ups, leaving customers waiting. The team narrowed its focus to the automotive industry and renamed the company Numa.

Blue Cloud Ventures also saw an opportunity in dealerships still relying on older software.

“I look at the auto dealership… as a little bit like jumbo shrimp. It’s a massive niche,” Mir Arif, the venture capital firm’s managing partner, told Fortune. “All of them [are still] working off legacy software or literally pen and paper.”

Today, the company has about 160 employees across its Bay Area headquarters, a sales and marketing office in Kansas City and an AI development office in Montreal. More than 1,500 dealerships in the U.S. and Canada use its software, including Longo Toyota, Norm Reeves Honda and Gold Coast Cadillac.
