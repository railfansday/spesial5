A clash to savour at Ffos Las on Wednesday

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Having been declared to clash at Cheltenham over the weekend, Old Park Star and Sober Glory both swerved commitments at Prestbury Park in favour of more suitable conditions in South Wales on Wednesday, and we are treated to a fascinating contest with Baron Noir also thrown into the mix.

Cheltenham's loss is Ffos Las' gain on Wednesday as old rivals Old Park Star, Sober Glory and Baron Noir are set to face-off on chase debut and At The Races' Declan Rix previewed the contest.

You always need a bit of luck in this game, no matter what your role or ability. While the elements have unfortunately conspired against those at Exeter so far this season, Ffos Las is the beneficiary of what they are calling The Welsh Arkle on Wednesday, live on Sky Sports Racing.

The race got a huge boost with the somewhat surprise addition of 2026 Supreme Novices' Hurdle winner Old Park Star, a high-profile non-runner on Cheltenham's Saturday card due to the ground being deemed too quick. His inclusion means we have a huge early season clash with an old foe in Sober Glory, last season's unlucky Supreme runner-up.

The inclusion of Old Park Star is no doubt bad luck for those connected to Sober Glory et al; after all, the Henderson-trained horse was meant to run last Saturday, but it's a huge stroke of luck for Ffos Las and indeed, Sky Sports Racing. Exciting early season clashes like this are incredibly rare for novice chasers in Britain - and in general given the monster that the Cheltenham Festival has morphed into - so it's understandable there is a bit of a buzz around the industry ahead of the five-runner Golwg Gwendraeth Beginners' Chase, due off at 13:18pm.

As of 8.37am on Tuesday, on the back of 16mm since 6pm on Monday, the ground is officially Good (good to soft in places), but with significant rain due. According to the BHA site, tomorrow's forecast reads as, 'blustery with persistent rain, heavy at times throughout the day'. Hopefully it leads to beautiful soft ground which the horses will get through nicely.

As the official ratings and betting suggests, Old Park Star (155; 4/6) and Sober Glory (153; 15/8) are the standouts and are set to renew their rivalry having served up a fantastic Cheltenham Festival curtain raiser in March, along with Mydaddypaddy back in third. At the line, the winning margins were 1½ lengths and a nose, with the also reopposing Baron Noir (146; 10/1), Wednesday's third favourite, a further 6 lengths back in fourth.

Wednesday's prices and a glance at the formbook in black and white could lead you down the garden path of just siding the unbeaten hurdler Old Park Star, but replaying the final flight of their Supreme clash is wise, given it had such a significant impact on the race.

Heading to the last in the Supreme, Sober Glory looked to have everything in trouble with a good 1½+ lengths lead before he put in an extra stride, hit the hurdle and landed steeply, losing critical momentum. It was a huge shame for his connections because up to that point he had jumped so well and looked the best horse in the race going to the last.

That mistake cost him dearly, along with many punters having been a huge gamble from 16/1 in the morning to an SP of 9/2. The Philip Hobbs & Johnson White yard clearly felt like they had their charge in great order, produced him beautifully on the day only for that famous last flight on the Old Course at Prestbury Park to scupper a dream start to the 2026 Festival.

His disappointing run 31 days later at Aintree is easily excused on the back of a long season after a huge Cheltenham run and now with his attentions turned to chasing, this former Irish point-to-point winner for Cormac Doyle rates an exciting prospect for the upcoming campaign for owners Brocade Racing.

Standing in his way again is fellow six-year-old Old Park Star, who, to be fair, didn't exactly ping the final flight in the Supreme himself, also putting in an extra stride which led to the son of Well Chosen landing awkwardly but crucially, not losing as much momentum as Sober Glory.

That saw him stay unbeaten in four hurdle starts, after wins at Kempton, Cheltenham and Haydock, showing good versatility in terms of the tracks he could handle along with also displaying tactical adaptability. Having jumped right in places on debut, his hurdling got better and slicker as the campaign progressed and he looked a straight-forward character throughout.

Having been put away after Cheltenham, Old Park Star's attentions now to turn chasing, a game he possesses the physical scope and athleticism for, although unlike Sober Glory he has no point-to-point background. While not ideal, it's possibly a good sign that Nicky Henderson was willing to pitch him into Grade 2 company at Cheltenham on chasing debut before the ground ruled him out.

A horse said to be big and weak last season, there could be much more to come from Old Park Star, his trainer's comments of 'he's still a shell of a horse' last year continually playing on my mind. If he was able to achieve what he did last season when weak, you cannot but be excited about him after another summer on his back.

Baron Noir is certainly no no-hoper against Old Park Star and Sober Glory, with 7½ lengths and 6 lengths, respectively, to find with the leading pair, especially if he has settled down for a summer's break and/or fences curb his keen-going ways.

It's in his breeding to be highly-strung, being by Vadamos out of a Dylan Thomas mare, but he's a horse with plenty of ability and it was a clear sign of the belief his trainer Alan King had in him to run in a Supreme when only rated 130.

While essentially well-held at the line, the six-year-old shaped better than the distance beaten, not getting up the hill as well as his Wednesday rivals. With that in mind, the much flatter nature of Ffos Las should suit.

He too has no point-to-point background, but with the King yard ticking along nicely, he is certainly worth a market check.

It's not often you would get prices of 33/1 about a Grade 2-winning novice hurdler from the Skelton yard who looks all-over a chaser on novice chase debut, but that's the task facing Carlenrig. His odds are not just about the horses he is taking on, but also the trip, mind.

A winner of the Grade 2 Bristol Novices' Hurdle at Cheltenham last December, he showed a good attitude to win over that 3m trip. On the back of that given his unexposed nature, he looked a big player in the River Don Novices' Hurdle at Doncaster 42 days later - despite carrying a penalty - but having been a stinking drifter (4/1 out to 14/1), fell two out when his race looked to be run.

The former winning Irish pointer picked up a small injury that day and hasn't been seen in 298 days so this looks like a starting point to get his campaign up and running.

Stablemate Bobby's Nelson has his first run for the Skeltons and should be more at home over this 2m trip. He also looks a chaser physically and his three-quarter of a length defeat to Baron Noir last December at Uttoxeter doesn't half look bad now; indeed, you could argue he is overpriced at 80/1 on that effort, but is probably best watched.

In truth, this looks a race to watch for the future, but if forced to have a bet, at the prices, it would certainly be SOBER GLORY. I don't think it's far fetched to say he looked the best horse in the Supreme before his final flight mistake, and with his point-to-point background, might have a jumping experience edge over Old Park Star. That said, the Hobbs and White yard are only operating at a 5% strike-rate currently, for all they've had plenty of others run well. Either way, I am very much looking forward to seeing how all these horses take to fences.
