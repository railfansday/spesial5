Block Says Most Bitcoin Payments on Square Are for Coffee and Food

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Most bitcoin payments at Square sellers are small, everyday purchases at coffee shops and restaurants, Block said in a new report that it hopes will inform a debate in Congress over a tax break for small crypto purchases.

The report, “The State of Bitcoin Payments,” covers completed bitcoin payments at U.S. Square sellers from Nov. 1, 2025, to Oct. 15, 2026. The median payment was about $13, and the average was about $29.

Food and drink businesses took 72% of those payments, Block found. That is a bigger share than the category’s 67.5% slice of regular card payments on Square over the same period.

“Most bitcoin payments are small and routine,” the company wrote on X Thursday, calling the report one of the first datasets on how people spend bitcoin in the local economy.

Coffee shops and fast food businesses accounted for 62.6% of the bitcoin payments in the food and drink category, with a median payment of $9.10. Grocery stores made up a smaller share but had the highest average payment in the group, at $60.73.

Small tickets dominated. About 42% of bitcoin payments on Square were under $10, and 65.1% were under $20. Those sub-$20 payments made up just 19.1% of total payment value, though, with a few larger purchases accounting for much of the dollars spent.

Customers also came back. Among Cash App users who paid with bitcoin at least twice at the same merchant, the median was three purchases per customer-merchant pair. About 44% of bitcoin payments from Cash App were repeat payments, and 86% of those came within 30 days.

The activity was not limited to Block’s own app. External Lightning wallets such as Strike, Wallet of Satoshi, Phoenix and Coinbase handled 56.6% of Square bitcoin payments and about 66% of payment value, while Cash App wallets made up the rest.

Square sellers can choose how much of each sale they keep in bitcoin, and Square converts the rest to dollars automatically. Block said zero-fee processing for bitcoin payments on Square has been extended through Dec. 31, 2027. Local governments are testing similar setups, with Miami-Dade County now taking bitcoin for property taxes and converting it to dollars.

Block tied the data to tax policy. Under current U.S. federal rules, paying with bitcoin generally means calculating a capital gain or loss on every purchase, even a $10 coffee, the company said.

A Morning Consult survey that Block commissioned found that 23.6% of businesses that don’t accept bitcoin cite tax and accounting complexity as a reason. That is about the same share that cite regulation, refunds or price swings. Among businesses that have considered taking bitcoin but haven’t, the figure was 27%.

Consumers pointed to friction, too. Morning Consult surveyed 8,669 U.S. adults who are aware of bitcoin in August. Among those who don’t own it, 29.6% said they would be more likely to pay with bitcoin if it were easier, and 15.7% of that group said they were unsure how it is taxed or regulated.

Block said Congress is weighing a crypto tax package and pointed to a de minimis exemption, which would exempt small gains on qualifying purchases, as one option. Industry groups have pressed lawmakers on crypto tax rules for months, with little movement so far.

The report doesn’t give totals. Block said the number of Square bitcoin payments, the number of sellers receiving them and total bitcoin payment volume are proprietary and not disclosed. Merchant lists like the 425 Los Angeles businesses that accept bitcoin offer a rough sense of reach.

“We think tax policy should account for how people spend bitcoin as well as how they hold it,” Block said.
