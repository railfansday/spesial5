Saatva is renowned for making some of the best mattresses on the market for a luxurious sleep experience. And I should know, I've tested several of them. Which is why I was looking forward to trying out the Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Pillow, a pillow the brand recommends to hot sleepers in all sleep positions. Disappointingly, the reality didn't live up to the expectation.

As the name suggests, the Graphite Memory Foam Pillow is made with mostly memory foam with a little bit of shredded natural latex thrown into the mix. It has a different design to any of the best pillows I've tested before — a thin shredded latex core, sandwiched by two slabs of graphite-infused memory foam for, apparently, improved breathability (spoiler: this was not my experience).

I tested a queen-size Graphite Memory Foam Pillow in standard loft, but it's also available in a high loft. The surface is on the firmer side and, thanks to its memory foam construction, offers a little contouring. Although I prefer a plusher feel, the surface isn’t what makes this pillow uncomfortable. It was how the bottom edge of the pillow seemed to squish flat, causing my neck to sit at an unnatural angle, or my head to slide off — depending on which position I was sleeping in.

Another big drawback had to do with the temperature regulation. Despite testing during November when my house was cool at night, I woke up to warm spots on the pillow.

Saatva is a premium mattress brand, and that’s reflected in the price of their pillows too. The Graphite Memory Foam Pillow has an MSRP price of $165 for a standard loft, queen size pillow. Bedding and accessories aren't included in the current Saatva Black Friday sale. However, you do stand to save $40 when you buy as part of a Sateen Sheet Set bundle.

In my opinion, the $165 price tag is steep, especially considering how much of the pillow is comprised of memory foam and there are no certifications for the organic cotton cover or the natural latex.

The Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Pillow is available in two different lofts and costs $165. While regular Saatva mattress sales shave hundreds off of the best Saatva mattresses during major sale events, pillows and bedding aren't included in those discounts as standalone products. This pillow includes 45 night sleep trial, 1-year warranty and free shipping.

Like many of the best pillows we've tested, there’s a lot more going on with the design of this pillow than appears from the outside. It’s sold in a standard (4-5" tall) or high loft (6-7" tall), with the high loft more suitable to broader bodies.

The pillow effectively has three main layers, starting with a removeable knit cotton cover. Below the cotton cover is an 'inner pillow.' Two medium-thick blocks of memory foam infused with graphite are placed on the front and the back sides of the inner pillow. Memory foam is not a breathable foam, so graphite is used to promote a cool night’s sleep.

Between the memory foam blocks is a bag that contains shredded natural latex and memory foam. It’s quite thin and can be removed entirely from the inner pillow if need be. However, you can’t open that core to add or remove the shredded foams.

Lastly, the memory foam has a CertiPUR-US certification. Although Saatva say the cover is made with organic cotton and they include natural latex in the pillow, no other certifications are provided that verify this.

After one week of testing, here’s what I can tell you about the Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Pillow’s comfort and temperature regulation.

The Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Pillow arrived in a plastic bag within a cardboard box. It was already fully expanded, so I was able to take it out of the bag and sleep on it right away. Unfortunately, I noticed off gassing from this pillow right away, and even more so when I slept on the pillow. As someone with a sensitive nose, I tried to sleep on my back, so I wouldn’t have to smell the pillow.

A week before I tested Saatva’s Graphite Memory Foam Pillow, I tested the Saatva Cloud Memory Foam Pillow. I’m not a huge fan of memory foam but I found the Saatva Cloud Memory Foam Pillow to be extremely comfortable. Unfortunately, I couldn’t say the same of the Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Pillow.

I ordered it in the standard loft size which, according to its dimensions, should have been the perfect size to allow me to sleep on my back, stomach, and side. However, it didn’t matter which way I slept, I couldn’t find any position that was particularly comfortable. And there is a single reason why.

When I laid my head down, it didn’t sink into the pillow very much. There was some slight contouring. But at the same time, the bottom length of the pillow (near my shoulders), flattened down creating a significant angle that offered no support to my neck or shoulders. I tried to move the inner shredded latex and memory foam core to prevent this flattening, but nothing seemed to work.

If I slept on my stomach, my head would slide off the pillow. On my side or my back? My neck would feel strained. Because the bottom had flattened out, my neck was not being held up and thus would dip down at an odd angle. If I was lying on my back, my head would be pushed forward more – which wasn’t comfortable. On my side, I was having to hold my head in place or put my hand under the bottom of the pillow to sort of fluff it up.

All in all, I did not sleep well on this pillow during testing.

Bedding brands have long claimed that infusing graphite into memory foam can offer significant cooling properties. I haven’t ever experienced such a thing, not with Saatva’s Graphite Memory Foam Topper and not with this Graphite Memory Foam Pillow.

I didn’t wake up hot and sweaty when sleeping on this pillow. However, I would wake up and notice warm spots around my head. This was odd considering I tested the pillow in November when my home dropped to the 60s at night. I used a breathable Quince linen pillowcase during this time, too.

I think the lack of breathability in this pillow has a lot to do with the design, in particular, those two blocks of memory foam on the front and back of the pillow. Memory foam doesn’t allow for much air to flow through and dissipate heat.

Most pillows should be replaced after two years. However, if you notice the loft breaking down quicker or your pillow having trouble bouncing back if you fold it in half, you should replace it as soon as possible.

I only slept on the Graphite Memory Foam Pillow for one week, and in that time, I haven’t noticed any signs of sagging or breakdown. Saatva include high-quality, premium materials in their products and estimates longer lifespans for their bedding, so I’m sure you don’t have to worry about this pillow breaking down quickly. That said, the one-year warranty is on the low end of warranties for premium pillow brands.

The cotton cover of the Graphite Memory Foam Pillow is removeable and can be thrown in the wash. Machine wash it on cold with a delicate cycle then tumble dry the cover on low or hang it to dry.

Remember, to wash the cover on occasion as it can absorb sweat stains, facial cream, or even saliva. This will help extend the lifespan of your pillow. If you notice that anything has bled through to the inner pillow, you can spot clean it as needed.

Usually when I test a pillow, there’s at least one type of sleeper I can suggest the pillow to – but that’s not the case with the Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Pillow. Not only does it sleep warm at night, but the design of the pillow resulted in a variety of issues for my neck or head no matter if I was sleeping on my side, stomach, or back.

The materials aren’t certified organic or completely non-toxic and you can only wash the cover. At least you get the chance to order it in standard or high loft. Considering all the flaws, the price doesn’t feel worth it, even with a 45-night trial with free returns.

I hate to say this about a Saatva product, but they have plenty of others like the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow or the Cloud Memory Foam Pillow that are far more comfortable and/or worth their price.

✅ You want 45 days to try it out: Most pillows come with a 30-day trial, but Saatva allows you to try this pillow for 45 days and will offer a full refund and free return if it’s not to your liking.

❌ You sleep hot: I experienced warm spots when sleeping on this pillow at night – and that’s coming from someone who doesn’t sleep hot. Notably, testing was done in late fall when the evenings were particularly cool in my house.

❌ You like more of a sink in feel to your pillow: While the Graphite Memory Foam Pillow offers a bit of contouring, your head won’t sink in that much. It’s better described as having a firm surface.

❌ You want more neck support: The bottom edge of the pillow flattens which doesn’t offer as much neck support for side sleepers. It also means that when you’re sleeping on your back, it pushes your head forward and creates tension in your neck.
