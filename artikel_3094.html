Asian data centres look to US buyers as US chip rules bite, Bloomberg says

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Rental prices for Nvidia’s B300 fell 7% in a month, according to Liquid Compute. In Shenzhen, a B300 server now costs up to $3.4mn on the grey market.

Data centres across Asia are trying to sell computing power meant for Chinese customers to buyers in the US. Washington has closed a loophole that let Chinese firms rent Nvidia’s top chips abroad. Newley Purnell reported it for Bloomberg on Wednesday, citing Ronit Jain, chief executive of Liquid Compute.

Liquid Compute is a New York startup building an exchange for AI computing capacity. It says it tracks more than 200 data centre companies.

“All the neoclouds based in Asia-Pacific selling to Tencent and other companies, or in Singapore or Taiwan with Chinese companies as offtakers, have excess capacity. They’re trying to broker it into the US,” Jain told Bloomberg.

He declined to name the sellers or the buyers.

A B300, Nvidia’s top graphics processor, rented for $7.72 an hour at the end of October, Liquid Compute’s index shows. By Tuesday the price had fallen 7% to $7.19. A year ago it was $4.55, and it rose steadily until last month, Jain said. He expects prices to keep falling. Data centres in Asia-Pacific are trying to place their spare capacity in the US and Western Europe, he said.

Not every sign points to a glut. New B300 orders now take 26 to 40 weeks to arrive, the Taiwanese newspaper China Times reported, citing supply chain sources. GB300 orders take 14 to 26 weeks. The delays only hit new orders.

The remote access rule took effect last Thursday, Owen Walker and Arjun Neil Alim reported for the Financial Times. It covers data centres in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil that supply significant computing power to Chinese customers. They now need US licences to buy advanced American chips, and have 90 days to get them.

Malaysia has told 117 approved data centre projects to comply, the FT reported. It also told them to cut ties with seven companies the US added to its blacklist this week. Megaspeed and its subsidiaries are among them. Malaysia has received just under $100bn of data centre funding in the past five years, according to its trade ministry.

“We do not want Singapore to be used and abused as a conduit by companies to circumvent America’s export controls,” Singapore’s prime minister, Lawrence Wong, said on Tuesday.

Lenders may now hold back. Banks will likely withhold unpaid loans to projects aimed at Chinese customers, said Zhan Kai, a partner at Dacheng Law Offices in Shanghai. He spoke to the FT.

In China, the squeeze shows up in the grey market. Joshua Yang visited Shenzhen’s Huaqiangbei electronics market for The Washington Post. Vendors there quoted $3mn to $3.4mn for a server with eight B300s. One vendor said the same server cost $2mn two months ago. An equivalent server sells for about $500,000 to $600,000 in the US, Kyle Chan, a fellow at the Brookings Institution, told the Post. The Post could not verify the chips on offer.

The shift follows weeks of turmoil at Nvidia, which brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive on Monday.
