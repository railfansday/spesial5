How Do You Pick the Cover Frame for a Hit the Road Dude Clip? A Sharpness-and-Exposure Picker With ffmpeg and NumPy

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

When you post a Hit the Road Dude clip, the State Property meme where a driver at a pickup window repeats "State?" and sends someone off, the cover frame decides whether anyone taps it. Most platforms pick a frame for you, and they often pick a mid-blink or a motion-blurred one. This is a small tool that picks a better one automatically.

I build hittheroaddude.app, an independent studio that turns one photo of an adult or a pet into the 15-second, vertical 9:16 pickup-window clip with a newly generated voice. Which frame to use as a thumbnail is a fair question for anyone posting a remake, so here is a rule of thumb turned into a script. I tested it on a synthetic clip, not on generated customer clips, so the numbers below only show that the picker works, not how it scores any real remake.

What this does not measure is whether the face looks right or whether the driver's expression is the funny one. For a meme with a deadpan pause, the best frame is often the "State?" beat, and no sharpness metric knows that. So the script prints the top few timestamps and you make the final call.

It decodes the clip once with ffmpeg to small grayscale frames (135 by 240, which is plenty for scoring), scores each, and then asks ffmpeg for the winning frame at full resolution:

Real clips would make a poor test because I would not know the right answer. So I generate a 10-second 9:16 clip made of three parts: seconds 0 to 3 blurred, 3 to 7 sharp, 7 to 10 dark. A correct picker must choose from the middle.

The top three all sit in the sharp section (3.25 s is just after the blur ends, 6.50 and 6.75 s are just before the dark part starts). Nothing from the blurred or dark parts made the list. The scores are close because the test pattern is uniformly detailed; I have not measured the gap on real clips.

A plain sharpness score can reward a mostly black frame, because hard edges between black and bright areas score high on a Laplacian. Multiplying by an exposure factor (1.0 if the mean brightness is between 45 and 210, otherwise 0.3) and by the share of non-clipped pixels guards against that. It is a hack, and the thresholds are my choice, not a standard. If your clips are intentionally dark, change them.

The studio makes one 15-second clip from one photo. It takes no prompt: the pickup-window scene and lines are one saved preset, and you choose 480p or 720p.
