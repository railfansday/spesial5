Musah lifts lid on Amorim’s faith, early career, preferred role and Scudetto ‘ambition’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Yunus Musah has insisted that AC Milan must believe in the Scudetto, while also assessing his start to the campaign under Ruben Amorim.

Going into the summer, an uncertain fate awaited Musah. Massimiliano Allegri did not see him as a part of the plans for 2025-26 and so he was loaned to Atalanta, where he had a mixed year and ultimately returned as they did not use their option to buy him.

Many sources presumed that it would be a return in passing and that a new destination would be found, but Amorim had other plans. The new coach clearly saw something in the USA international, and he has become a regular starter regardless of the system used.

Such is his importance, there is even talk of a renewal being discussed. Musah was interviewed by SportMediaset and it was published two days before the Serie A campaign resumes with a home game against Frosinone. His comments were transcribed by MilanNews.

Do you feel like a ‘wild card’ for Milan?

“A little less so this year because I haven’t changed positions as much, even though I can do that. I’ve been able to play in midfield and improve in that area.”

“I think he watched my old games, because he already knew what he expected from me and what he could ask of me. He did his homework, he watched everything, and he already knew what and how he could use me.”

We’ve never seen you play left-back at Milan like you did in Genoa: how did this idea come about?

“I don’t know (laughs). This question is for the coach. Bartesaghi and Estupinan are very good, but that day he wanted a right-footed player on the left.”

If it were up to you, where do you want to play most?

“I think playing in midfield. Not fixed, though, being able to go anywhere so I can get the ball, get deep, without being fixed.”

Do you think Milan is improving? Is the Scudetto possible?

“It’s possible. We train every day for it. We all have the dream and ambition of winning it, and we’ll do everything we can to get there.”

Can you tell us what you remember about your time in the States?

“Playing for the United States helped me gain this sense of belonging. Until I was 17, I only had a passport, but now I feel part of the culture.”

“I can go there. I know the restaurants, the chains, the people from different states, the geography. I understand American people so much now.

“I have many passports, I lived and grew up in many countries, like here in Italy, in England, so I feel a bit of everything.”

Have you ever returned to see your childhood homes?

“Yes, in 2021 I returned to New York. I saw the house where I lived that year. The Bronx. It was beautiful to see it. There was a piece missing from my memory, and it was nice to be able to connect it.”

“Yes. I left him with the neighbours in Valencia because I couldn’t take him to Milan.”

“My family was already in Italy, and my mother had to go back and forth to America.”

What was your childhood like in Castelfranco Veneto?

“Beautiful. It’s a place where my parents weren’t afraid to let me play all day outside in the parking lot near the apartment building.”

Is it true that your parents’ years of financial sacrifice were years of financial hardship?

“Of course. Obviously, when I grew up, we weren’t rich, we didn’t have much money, but from the time I left home, even when I was little, my parents never let me go without anything. I hope I’m their pride, because they are mine.”

You have also played for Arsenal and Valencia. What were the transitions like?

“Playing for Arsenal was a great privilege, because I arrived from Italy at 9 years old, played with a small team, and immediately joined Arsenal.

“I was ready to move to the first team when I was 16, and Valencia had a plan for me. I spent four wonderful years in Spain; it’s a city that will always remain in my heart.”

What’s the atmosphere like in the dressing room?

“Last year I watched them a lot; they were a very solid team and difficult to beat. Amorim brought a very positive vibe, we understand each other. He understands the player very well and vice versa.

“It helps when he understands what the players need; he’s good at that, and the players in the team are pretty calm.”

Is he someone who gets angry when necessary?

“Yes. After Ferencvaros he was very angry, like everyone else.”

“My wife is from Zanzibar, my family is Ghanaian. So it’s nice to travel to our home countries in the summer.”
