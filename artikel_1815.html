‘Curious’ Derek McInnes used Jamestown Analytics to rate Rangers signing while Hearts manager

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Derek McInnes has revealed that he used Jamestown Analytics to rate Rangers' signing of Ryan Naderi while manager of Hearts last season.

Hearts gained access to Jamestown's treasure trove of player recruitment data in 2024 after selling a stake in the club to the man who formed the company, Brighton chairman Tony Bloom.

While used primarily to source signings for their own squad, Hearts are also able to access information on signings made by their Premiership rivals.

McInnes admitted that he ran Naderi through the system and was left hoping that Rangers would not sign the Czech striker as he was identified as a good signing for the Ibrox club.

He explained: "Last January, obviously, when we first seen that Rangers were linked with Naderi, like everyone else, I would say to [Hearts sporting director] Graeme Jones, 'How do they rate him?'

"Because you're always curious. It's just the way it is. It's brilliant when you see other teams round about you signing a player that's not rated. That's the way it was. It's just natural.

"But no, he was someone that I think was well known to them. I think Brugge had an interest in Ryan, and I think USG potentially had an interest in him as well. But the kind of money, the fee, it just kind of went away for them, I think.

"But we were told that he was a good player before I'd even set eyes on him. The feeling was that he was somebody that could help Rangers and make them better. So when I'd seen a lot of the comings and goings, it was on, it was off, it was off, on again, I was kind of hoping that it would fall through, being the Hearts manager. But when I'd seen they'd signed him, I knew that Rangers had strengthened. And I think Rangers did strengthen well in January last year.

"So that was the first we kind of looked a wee bit more at him but, again, you just do it as an opposition manager, looking at him. It's only when you get to work with people on a daily basis that you get to see them."

Naderi initially struggled to live up to his £4.75million transfer tag after joining Rangers in January from German third tier side Hansa Rostock.

However, the 23-year-old has emerged as a talismanic figure this season, scoring 10 goals in his last 20 games and providing the focal point in attack following injuries to Youssef Chermiti and Lawrence Shankland.

"I had a good conversation with Ryan when I first came in the door about what we felt this season could look like for him," McInnes said. "Because it's not always easy for a young player settling in January and they're in the middle of a race, but to try and really establish himself this season.

"I think he stood up brilliantly to a couple of the senior injuries, to other injuries throughout, to Youssef and Lawrence. He's had to be the guy and he's led the line brilliantly. I love the goals he scores. I love his work rate anyway. But it's nice that people don't just see him as a grafter now and a worker. He's actually got good intelligence.

"All top strikers have intelligence to be in the right place at the right time and it's symptomatic of some of the goals he scores, following things in, just always believing there might just be something there for me.

"I love having a striker that has the ability to score those types of goals. But he leads the line well, gets us up the pitch and works ever so hard, as you know. He's done well. He should feel pleased. I think the fact that he's got capped as well, he's taken a real step on from that. I think he's taken a lot of confidence from that.

"However he gets his confidence, whether it's scoring goals, whether it's staying fit, being a big part of the team, whether it's getting recognised at international level, we all want it to continue."
