Phibro: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Phibro's push to cut excess inventory will decide how quickly its trailing $22 million in free cash flow can pay down debt.

On its first-quarter fiscal year 2027 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Phibro (PAHC +2.41%) reported net sales of $386.8 million, up 6% year over year despite softness in the poultry market. Long-term investors need to track how the company shifts revenue into higher-margin specialty products while working down excess inventory. Three things management said stand out.

Performance Products sales grew 16%, and Mineral Nutrition rose 17% during the quarter. Management is intentionally pivoting these business units toward plant nutrition, dairy, and pet food to build durable, higher-margin customer relationships.

U.S. chicken supply grew over 2%, outpacing consumer demand and squeezing producer margins. Farm operators responded by trimming product inventories and pausing purchases of nutritional specialties and vaccines.

Free cash flow reached $22 million over the trailing 12 months, but excess inventory continues to tie up capital. Lowering inventory levels will unlock working capital to pay down debt and fund internal investments.

Revenue momentum across specialty segments holds steady, supporting full-year financial guidance. Structural cost savings from facility consolidations and expanded distribution channels strengthen long-term unit economics.

Management expects protein producer margins to recover as crop harvest yields lower livestock feed expenses.
