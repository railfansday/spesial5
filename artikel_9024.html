former sheet-metal workshop in japan becomes residence with carved-out garden terrace

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

in tatsuno’s historic castle town, akio isshiki renovates a former sheet-metal craftsman’s workshop into a contemporary residence.

Akio Isshiki edited by christina vergopoulou November 18, 2026 4 min

Located in the historic castle town of Tatsuno, Japan, House in Tatsuno is the residential renovation of a former sheet-metal craftsman’s home and workshop by Japanese architect Akio Isshiki. Working within the structural, regulatory, and budgetary constraints of the compact site, the design retains physical remnants of the building’s artisanal past while reorganizing the domestic floor plan around semi-outdoor thresholds and tactile materials.

Beyond the screens, the former workshop functions as an unconditioned transitional zone linked to the dining area via an engawa-like timber platform. Inside, locally applied earthen plaster, a kitchen clad in Tunisian tiles, and a custom built-in table-and-workbench unit sit alongside reused workshop artifacts, including old toolboxes fitted into shelving and a found metal ring integrated into the stair landing handrail. On the upper level, the original layout is largely preserved, stripping back the ceiling to expose structural log beams, adding a sunken study and a tokonoma alcove, and reinstalling the building’s original ridge-raising ceremonial offering at the roof peak.
