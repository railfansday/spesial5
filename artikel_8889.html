## The Moment You Recline Into ANA "The Room" The flight attendant slides your suite door closed. You're somewhere over the Pacific, 35,000 feet above the ocean, enclosed in what ANA calls "The Room" — a fully private, floor-to-ceiling enclosed business class suite on a Boeing 777-300ER. The seat converts to a fully flat 78-inch bed. There's direct aisle access. The service is famously meticulous. And you're flying from New York to Tokyo in a product that rivals first class on most other carriers. This is not a fantasy. This is what 90,000 Virgin Atlantic Flying Club miles buys you on ANA's JFK → NRT route. ### The Math That Should Stop You Cold A one-way business class ticket on ANA between New York and Tokyo typically retails for **$6,000 to $12,000 in cash**. That's not a typo. Premium cabin trans-Pacific fares are genuinely that expensive, which is precisely why the ChooseFI community treats travel rewards as a core pillar of the FI lifestyle — you're unlocking experiences that would otherwise require years of extra saving. At 90,000 Virgin Atlantic miles for that same seat, your points are delivering **6.7 to 13.3 cents per point (CPP)** in value. To put that in context: most points are considered "good" redemptions at 1.5–2 CPP. This redemption is 4 to 9 times that benchmark. This is what a true sweet spot looks like. ### Why the Gap Exists Virgin Atlantic prices its ANA partner awards using a **fixed award chart** — a relic of the old loyalty program era when airlines published static pricing tables rather than dynamic, revenue-based pricing. ANA's own Mileage Club program charges 75,000–90,000 miles for the same route. Virgin Atlantic's chart prices it at 90,000, but — and here's the critical part — **with zero fuel surcharges on partner-operated flights**. What you see is what you pay. The result is one of the most compelling premium cabin redemptions in all of loyalty programs. The ChooseFI community's founding principle is that every dollar has a job. When 90,000 transferable points can do the job of $12,000 in cash, that's not just travel rewards — that's an asymmetric opportunity you'd be leaving on the table.

Here's the aha moment: Virgin Atlantic is selling you access to ANA's inventory at a price ANA itself doesn't offer domestically in the same way. That's the inefficiency. And it's hiding in plain sight.

Virgin Atlantic Flying Club maintains a fixed award chart for partner flights. This is increasingly rare in a world where airlines have moved to dynamic, revenue-linked pricing. Because the chart is fixed, Virgin Atlantic charges 90,000 miles for ANA business class between North America and Japan — regardless of whether that seat would cost $6,000 or $12,000 in cash on the day you're flying.

When cash prices spike (holiday season, cherry blossom season, Golden Week), your points cost stays the same. That's not the case when you book directly through ANA Mileage Club, where pricing can vary, or through programs that have adopted dynamic pricing altogether.

This detail alone makes or breaks the redemption. Virgin Atlantic levies high fuel surcharges on its own metal (Virgin-operated flights). Book a Virgin Atlantic-operated long-haul in business class and you might pay $500–$800 in fees on top of your miles.

But when you book partner-operated flights through Virgin Atlantic — specifically ANA — those surcharges drop to zero. You pay government taxes only, typically $50–$100 on a trans-Pacific itinerary. That's it. No fuel surcharge games.

Airlines partner with each other through alliances and bilateral agreements, allowing their frequent flyer programs to book seats on each other's flights. The pricing inefficiency occurs when Partner A prices Partner B's premium cabin at a different rate than Partner B charges through its own program. Virgin Atlantic maintains a fixed partner award chart that prices ANA business class at 90,000 miles regardless of demand or seasonality. ANA's own dynamic pricing can push award costs significantly higher during peak periods. This arbitrage between partner pricing and the operating airline's own program is the foundation of most airline travel rewards sweet spots.

## Why This Redemption Is Rated Moderate This isn't a beginner redemption, but it's absolutely within reach for someone who understands the basics of transferable points programs. It's a three-step process across three platforms. **The fix:** Follow the step-by-step process above methodically. Don't rush.
