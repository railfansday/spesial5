Most places that use volunteers take beginners, so start near home at your library, a food bank, an animal shelter, or a senior center. If your hours are tight, pick a role you can do online. Bring a work skill, like law or a second language, to a group that needs it.

More than 75.7 million Americans age 16 and up, or 28.3%, volunteered with an organization in the year ending September 2023, according to the latest count from AmeriCorps and the Census Bureau. More than 13.4 million of them served at least partly online.

The 13 places below run from shifts that take beginners to roles that ask for training or a professional skill, so pick by the hours you have and what you’re good at. Volunteering can even help your finances, through a poll worker’s pay, a free federal parks pass, or a tax deduction for your driving if you itemize.

A role that builds a skill you use at work, like bookkeeping or a second language, gives you a line for your resume and a supervisor who can serve as a reference. It can also put you in touch with people in the field you want to work in.

Your hours aren’t tax-deductible, but some of what you spend is if you itemize deductions rather than take the standard deduction. Under the rules in IRS Publication 526, out-of-pocket costs a charity doesn’t repay, such as parking, tolls, and driving at 14 cents a mile, count as a charitable contribution. Log each trip’s date, the charity, and the miles as you go.
