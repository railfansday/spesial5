Your Credit Report Shows How You Borrow – Not What You Earn or Save

Your credit report lists your loans and cards with their balances, limits, and payment history, plus any collections, any bankruptcy, and the companies that have checked it. Your income, savings, and credit score aren’t on it. Pull your free reports and read each section for accounts you didn’t open, wrong balances, and on-time payments marked late.

Lenders read that report before they approve you or set your rate, and landlords, insurers, and phone companies can check it too. Seeing it first lets you catch a mistake before it costs you.

The report covers credit only. Paying with a debit card isn’t borrowing, so that spending normally doesn’t show up. Your score is calculated from the report but isn’t part of it. Since April 2023, the three credit bureaus have also stopped listing any medical bill sent to collectors that’s been paid off, was under $500 when first reported, or is less than a year past due.

Negative items don’t stay for good. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says most, like late payments, can be reported for up to seven years, and a bankruptcy for up to 10.

Not every surprise is a problem. A first-time reader might find a store card she forgot, still open at a $0 balance. That card has been helping her, since an open account with no missed payments keeps adding to the length of her credit history.

Dispute any entry that’s wrong, not just forgotten.

Checking your own reports never lowers your score, so read them now, well before a lender does.
