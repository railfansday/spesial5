Milos Kerkez isn't the problem at Liverpool, there's a far bigger one

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Milos Kerkez isn't the problem that many think he is at Liverpool. Instead, there's a far bigger one and Andoni Iraola has got to fix it.

Milos Kerkez has been getting stick this season as he's struggled for form. He's far from alone in that, of course, but the left-back has looked quite shaky at the back while offering little going forward.

He does, in his defence, have to take on an incredibly difficult task as the long-term replacement for Andy Robertson. Robertson is by far the best left-back Liverpool have had in 30 years and one of the greatest to ever play in the Premier League.

Some could argue that it was a bizarre decision to force him out and move on when he's still clearly capable of delivering at the top level. But it's a little late for that - Kerkez is now the first-choice left-back.

A bigger problem for Kerkez is that he's not in an environment built for success. In fact, his job is incredibly difficult and one that Andoni Iraola has to help with. Because as things stand, we'll never see the best of him.

The Reds are unbelievably static going forward, with very little attacking movement. They have a left-winger, a right-winger, a centre-forward and a no.10 - none of whom really interchange. The centre of the pitch doesn’t really offer space for wingers to run into. The effect of that is a clogged wing, too, which essentially always has a winger sticking wide on it. And the effect of that? Kerkez doesn’t have much space to run into. Virtually all his attacking chances come on the overlap in a short space, usually needing to fire a cross into a crowded box.

The Reds are unbelievably static going forward, with very little attacking movement. They have a left-winger, a right-winger, a centre-forward and a no.10 - none of whom really interchange.

The centre of the pitch doesn’t really offer space for wingers to run into. The effect of that is a clogged wing, too, which essentially always has a winger sticking wide on it.

And the effect of that? Kerkez doesn’t have much space to run into. Virtually all his attacking chances come on the overlap in a short space, usually needing to fire a cross into a crowded box.

Above we've got Kerkez's heatmap this season. As you can see, it's almost symmetrical along the halfway line. Kerkez gets lots of his touches within Liverpool's half, with the rest coming from deep in the attacking half.

Things taper out towards the opposition box, with no defined path actually to and around the attacking box. So let's have a look at Robertson's at Tottenham.

Isn't that different? Robertson has actually had more touches than Kerkez but the spread of his touches is far more diverse. His touches in the attacking half are much more present than those in his own, while you can see that clear, consistent path into the opposition box.

Robertson looks like an attacking full-back here. One who has a consistent path down the flank but also has space to move inside. Kerkez has neither.

And there's another factor. As mentioned, Robertson has had more touches - 625 to Kerkez's 565. The kicker here, though, is that the Liverpool full-back has played much more than his former teammate.

Kerkez has 825 Premier League minutes this season. Robertson has just 668. 157 fewer minutes, so we’re coming up to two full matches worth there. Yet the Scot is far more involved.

Offensively, anyway. Liverpool’s setup, leaving Kerkez deeper, has the knock-on effect of asking him to do much, much more defending than someone like Robertson.

Kerkez faces twice as many aerial duels and around three times as many duels overall. That’s despite the fact Spurs haven’t started the season well and aren’t exactly a team that rarely defends.

But Liverpool are not setup to play to Kerkez’s strengths. He’s not allowed room to attack and has essentially been cast as a defensive full-back - something he can’t really do. Until that changes, he will look sub-par.
