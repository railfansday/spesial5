Celtic set for historic European first, why Torreense match is no banker and ex-Hearts man to watch

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Celtic will encounter a European first when they take on Torreense in Portugal on Thursday night.

It will be the only time the Hoops have faced an opponent from the second tier of their domestic league in a Uefa competition as a result of Torreense claiming the first major trophy in the club's 109-year history last season.

Their shock 2-1 victory over Sporting Lisbon in the final of the Portuguese Cup in May to qualify for Europe for the first time in their history was the stuff of fairtytale. Think second tier Raith Rovers beating Celtic in the League Cup final in 1994.

Of course, by the time Raith came to play in the UEFA Cup the following season, they had gone on to earn promotion to the top flight. Not so Torreense. They lost lost 2-0 on aggregate to Casa Pia in the promotion/relegation play-off, meaning they remained in Liga Portugal 2 for the 2026/27 campaign.

They have since parted company with their cup-winning manager Luís Tralhão, who was sacked last month after overseeing a poor start to the domestic season, and replaced him with former Estrela da Amadora boss João Nuno.

Torreense currently occupy a lowly 13th spot in Liga Portugal 2 and will host Celtic in Leiria, 60 miles away from their home in Torres Vedras, as their own stadium only has 2,500 seats and does not meet Uefa requirements.

But park any thoughts of this fixture providing a banker win for Celtic. Torreense have already beaten Lillestrom and Ararat-Armenia in the Europa League and took the lead against Sunderland before the Premier League side netted a stoppage-time winner.

They sit 19th in the Europa League table, a lofty 13 places above Celtic, who belatedly put their first points on the board with a 3-2 win over Besiktas on November 5 after losing to Ferencváros, Benfica and Celta Vigo.

Nonetheless, this is a match Celtic should be looking to win if they are to retain realistic ambitions of becoming the first team to reach the knockout phase of the competition after losing their opening three league phase matches.

This is their most winnable tie on paper and failure to garner three points would leave Martin O'Neill's men with minimal margin for error in their remaining three fixtures - away to Omonoia, home to Marseille, then away to Union Saint-Gilloise.

Ten points, and a reasonably healthy goal difference is expected to be the benchmark for reaching the knockout play-off round. Nine points may be enough, but 11 points is the target to be absolutely sure.

Celtic should be looking at trips to Torreense and Omonoia, currently fifth in the Cypriot league, as an opportunity to reach nine points before facing the French and Belgian big-hitters in their final two matches.

A familiar face awaits them at the Dr. Magalhães Pessoa Stadium. Former Hearts striker Musa Drammeh has been with the Portuguese club since departing Tynecastle in the summer of 2025.

He was part of their cup-winning squad last season and has played 17 times this term, scoring two goals.

The 24-year-old Spaniard has history with Celtic, having made his SPFL debut against them in a 2-0 defeat at Parkhead in September 2024, and then scoring his first Hearts goal in a 4-1 loss at Tynecastle two months later.
