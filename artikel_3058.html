Scholarships are usually restricted to the necessary costs of attending college, but some scholarships let you apply the money to more than tuition.

Scholarships are meant to pay for the cost of attendance to study at a college or university. This often includes required bills and purchases, such as tuition, textbooks and on-campus living expenses.

Some scholarships restrict the funds to specific costs, such as tuition. How you can apply the money may depend on the scholarship’s requirements.

Here are the most common college costs you can pay with scholarship money.

Scholarships commonly pay toward the tuition and fees your college charges.

Tuition usually covers only the cost to enroll in classes. Your college will also charge fees for a variety of reasons, such as attending a lab portion of a class, access to on-campus technology, facility maintenance and student activities. You’ll have to pay many of these fees regardless of if you use those facilities or participate in campus activities. Fees are often charged with tuition on the same bill so that you pay them together.

Tuition and fees cost students at public four-year institutions an average of $32,730 for the 2026-2027 school year, according to College Board’s 2026 report. If you use scholarship money to pay for these costs upfront, it can help you save a good chunk of money.

Scholarship money also pays for textbooks and supplies you need to complete your courses. These can include physical books, e-books and software licenses to access online content.

Depending on the scholarship, the cost of additional supplies that are required for certain courses might be covered as well. For example, nursing students might have to purchase scrubs with their college’s logo or medical supplies such as stethoscopes for clinical shifts.

Some scholarships will pay for the purchase of a laptop because you’ll likely need one to complete your coursework; however, you’ll want to confirm that the money can be used for that purchase before buying one.

Your scholarship might help cover some of the costs of living on campus, including paying for a dorm room and meal plans (often called room and board). To apply the funds, you usually have to live in university housing, which might be in a dormitory or even university apartments.

Many students can purchase meal plans to eat meals in their college and university cafeterias. These plans are usually for a set number of meals each week and might be required for first-year students. Because meal plans are part of college expenses, some scholarships will let you apply the funds to purchase a meal plan.

Some scholarships might also allow money to pay for campus parking fees because those are paid directly to the college or university.

Scholarships are generally intended only for educational expenses such as those explained above, but there are some scholarships that want you to use the money for specific expenses. If your scholarship has this type of restriction, your award letter should outline how the funds must be used, such as paying for tuition and fees only.

If you aren’t certain whether there are restrictions on how you can spend your scholarship money and the scholarship was paid directly to your college, you can call your financial aid office to ask. But you’ll want to contact the organization that awarded you the scholarship to ask about restrictions if the scholarship money was sent to you instead.

Even if a scholarship is open to most educational costs, expenses that are rarely allowed to be covered by a scholarship include:

Some transportation costs such as car payments.

Non-essential supplies such as clothing or entertainment.

Scholarships can be paid directly to your college or to you, depending on the individual scholarship. If the money goes to your college, it’s applied to your account based on the scholarship’s requirements. For example, if the funds are only allowed to pay for tuition, the financial aid office will apply the money to your tuition bill to reduce how much you owe.

Scholarships work the same way for room and board. If your scholarship will pay toward the costs of living on campus, the money will reduce how much you owe or cover the costs entirely so that you don’t owe anything.

In some cases, you might be able to use your scholarship money to pay for books or supplies. If that’s allowed, the funds might be available on your account through the college bookstore. Instead of paying the entire cost of your textbooks with cash or your student loans, you can pay with the scholarship money.

If your scholarship sends you the money instead of giving it to your college, you’ll be responsible for using the funds to pay for eligible costs instead of your university automatically applying it toward your bill.
