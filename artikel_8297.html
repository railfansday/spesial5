Te Araroa day 32: A cruisy start to the CSQ, and LOFI is born

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

20km, Paeroa Rail Trail Motel to Daly’s Clearing hut

We knew we only had a relatively short walk on this first day, and had a fairly relaxed start. We had to walk through Paeroa and grabbed coffee on the way to the famous L&P bottle. For those not in the know, Lemon & Paeroa (L&P) is a lemonade drink ‘world famous in New Zealand,’ as the strapline goes.

Pre CSQ selfie, leaving the motel. By Sinéad, of course!

Me and Sinéad at the L&P bottle. I have funky new socks! Photo by Lindsay

After that, we joined the Hauraki Rail Trail, a cycle path that would take us to the start of the Kaimai mountain range. The bike path was flat and easy walking, and ran next to the road to start with. It was so nice to not have to worry about the cars whizzing past! After a bit the track turned off the road and onto some farmland, and was quite scenic. Annabel was on the phone, but the rest of us walked along mostly together, having some nice chats.

After a bit more farmland, we started following the Ohinemuri River as it headed into Karagahake Gorge. The river was super pretty, but soon we crossed a suspension bridge – the first of several on this trip – and turned onto another path that would lead up a tributary of the main river. I hadn’t realised but this track went through some old mining tunnels, some of which were quite long and dark! Of course we all had head torches though.

Looking up the river towards Karagahake Gorge

Walking through the historic buildings by the river. Photo by Sinéad

On the Windows Walk – view from an old mining tunnel

I guess this is one of the windows they’re talking about

Out the end of the tunnels, the track continued along the river. It was beautiful, easy walking and a great way to ease back into hiking after several days off. I was already glad we came on this side quest! The river was deep and clear and looked amazing to swim in. We knew we would hit a river-side campsite at Dickey’s Flat at around lunchtime so we planned to have a dip there. But at around 11.30 we rounded a corner and saw a beautiful little waterfall with a big pool of water in front of it. It looked too inviting and we decided to swim there and take an early lunch.

Walking along the river. Photo by Annabel

Me and Sinéad getting in the water. Photo by Annabel

The water was freezing! But it was very refreshing. We sat on the river bank to eat lunch and dry off. A large group of people came along to have a tea break there – they looked like a local tramping club and they were doing a loop along a different track back to Karangahake Gorge.

From the waterfall, the track continued along the pretty river to Dickey’s Flat campsite. The river there was also nice, but not as deep or swimmable and we were glad we stopped for an early lunch. At the end of the Windows Walk through the tunnels, the DOC sign had said 4km and 2 hours to the campsite. We scoffed at that at the time, as the path was flat and easy. But after our extended lunch and swim break, it actually did take two hours. No worries, we had plenty of time that day. As we left the campsite, another sign said 3 hours to do the 8km to the hut. Again, we didn’t think it would be that long.

But as we were leaving the car park, the track took us through a field where a just-born calf was trying to stand up for the first time! We stood and watched for quite some time. There was another cow and slightly older calf in the field with them, and they were super cute too! The cows were of a type that I don’t think I’ve seen before. They looked really fluffy and the adults had a very sweet little topknot on top of their heads.

Crossing the river, in classic thru-hiker style with my towel and underwear drying on my pack. Photo by Sinéad

Leaving the cows, the track became a little rougher as it followed the river. I had stuck with the others earlier in the day, but on this slightly more challenging terrain I preferred to move a little faster and pulled ahead a bit. We met up again though when I stopped for water at an accessible bit of river. Shortly afterwards, the track headed up a pretty steep hill which was a shock to the system after 3 days of no hiking and an easy morning!

Clearly the four of us have been hiking together long enough now to have developed a bit of a hive mind. When I caught up to Sinéad and Lindsay after getting water, they said they had been talking about trail names, which I had also been thinking about. Lindsay suggested LOFI for me – last out, first in – for my habit of being the last to leave in the morning and overtaking everyone else partway through the day. I like this, and I think I’ll adopt it. It’s better than Pee Sticks anyway! Annabel we thought should be Gourmet. Her food is always the fanciest trail food you ever did see. Some evidence:

“I’ll be right there, I’m just making a bruschetta right now” – Annabel

“Who bought sourdough? Oh it must be Annabel” – Lindsay

Brought an avocado, pesto, cherry tomato and sundried tomato sandwich on sourdough for lunch this day

Sinéad we agreed should be something to do with swimming. She’s such a water baby and is always the first to suggest a dip! Is there an water nymph in Irish folklore? This is a work in progress.

Lindsay we were struggling with. ‘Is it because I don’t have a distinct personality?’ No Lindsay, you are an individual and we love you very much. But she hasn’t had quite such a recurring theme throughout the hike as the rest of us. Another work in progress!

There was a bit more up and down through some pleasant forest. The bush over here is beautiful, but unfortunately I have seen it quite a lot and I don’t really appreciate it as much as I should. I’m more of a big mountain views girl! Eventually I arrived at the junction for the hut. The sign said 20 minutes but I popped out in the clearing pretty soon after. The hut looked unoccupied, and a quick peek inside confirmed I was the only one around so far. By the time the others were trickling in, I had filtered water and was waiting for my electrolytes to dissolve.

Clearly, I have low standards for hut cleanliness and smell because Lindsay came in and immediately opened all the windows and doors as apparently it smelled damp. I hadn’t noticed up to that point, but she was right. I had been to this hut before, and remembered that there was a fair bit of rubbish around. This was still the case, but overall the hut is in decent condition if a little tired. But it’s always a delight not to have to pitch a tent!

We made dinner, some of us with more success than others… Then Annabel was very keen that we should play a game. Someone had left Snakes and Ladders in the hut, unfortunately without a dice to use. The numbers 1-6 were written inside the lid of the box, and we ‘rolled’ by putting a Connect 4 piece inside and shaking it. After that, we were pretty tired and wanted to go to bed, but I needed to set out the route options for tomorrow. Basically, we could take an easier and more scenic, but longer route, or a shorter but muddier and less nice route. After a bit of discussion, we settled on the longer, scenic route. I thought the two might take around the same time, and sometimes that’s more important than distance. Then we finally called it a night, although twice when I was juuuust falling asleep I was rudely awoken by one of the others hearing scuttling animal sounds. So I put my earplugs in, and went to sleep.

Someone may have overfilled her cooking pot…
