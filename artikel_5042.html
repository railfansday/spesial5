Hong Kong to License Crypto Dealers and Custodians by Year-End

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Hong Kong will introduce a bill before the end of the year to bring crypto dealers, advisers, asset managers and custodians under a licensing regime, Financial Secretary Paul Chan said Monday, even as the city has approved only two of 36 first-round applicants to issue stablecoins.

Chan delivered the remarks in a keynote at the Bloomberg Global Regulatory Forum in Hong Kong. “Before the end of this year, we will introduce a bill to complete the regulatory perimeter, extending licensing to those who deal in, advise on, manage and act as custodians of digital assets,” he said.

The plan would amend the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance, expanding a regime that now covers virtual asset trading platforms, the government told the Legislative Council on Nov. 4.

“On stablecoins, we have moved forward step by step. Of the 36 applicants in the first round, two received licences in April, and both have since launched Hong Kong dollar stablecoins,” Chan said. He said the emphasis is on “genuine use cases and credible business models,” citing cross-border payments as an example.

The Stablecoins Ordinance took effect in August 2025. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority granted the two issuer licenses this April, the government said in that earlier Legislative Council reply. Chan did not name the two issuers or say when a further application round might open.

The low approval rate is the point. Hong Kong is trying to build a regulated digital-asset market without opening the door to every issuer that applies.

“Our principle is simple: same activity, same risks, same regulation,” Chan said. On tokenisation, he said funds offered to the public in Hong Kong have grown about seven-fold in a year, and that since last month two tokenised money market funds have traded around the clock on a licensed platform. “Our approach is consistent: the same requirements as for conventional products, with additional safeguards for the new risks.”

Those figures line up with a fuller count the government gave on Nov. 4. As of the end of September, the Securities and Futures Commission had authorized 17 retail tokenised products, including 13 tokenised money market funds, with about HK$10.3 billion in assets in their tokenised classes. Two of those money market funds began round-the-clock secondary trading on licensed platforms on Oct. 26.

“Above all, we are firm on one point: Decentralisation does not mean less accountability,” Chan said.

Hong Kong already licenses virtual asset trading platforms. The new bill is meant to pull dealing, advisory, management and custody work into the same ordinance, a step the government has framed as streamlining the legal perimeter rather than writing a new one from scratch.

Acting Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury Joseph Chan told lawmakers on Nov. 4 that international platforms may apply for a license if they meet the statutory tests and the securities regulator’s rules, and that the coming bill is intended to let a wider range of service providers operate in the city. The government did not give a date for when the bill would be introduced, beyond “by the end of this year,” and did not say how long lawmakers would have to debate it.

Hong Kong has spent recent years trying to position itself as a regional hub for digital assets, even as other Asian financial centers have rolled out their own licensing regimes. Chan’s speech did not mention bitcoin by name, but the firms that deal in, advise on, manage and hold bitcoin in the city would fall under the expanded perimeter once the bill becomes law.

If the bill reaches the Legislative Council before year-end, dealers and custodians that have so far sat outside the platform-licensing regime will have to decide whether to apply, or to stay out of Hong Kong.
