Nvidia Board Shuts Down Jensen Huang Comeback, Launches Search for New CEO

Traders, fanboys, investors and believers. It’s time to stop coping.

Nvidia's board has publicly shut down hopes of Jensen Huang returning as CEO, delivering its strongest statement yet against reinstating the company's co-founder following his resignation last week.

"The Nvidia board does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," board chair Harvey Jones said during Tuesday's earnings call.

Jones said the board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and would not answer questions about the circumstances surrounding his departure or the ongoing independent investigation.

The company has hired executive recruitment firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent replacement. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress, who took over as interim CEO, received the board's full backing.

"We have every confidence in Colette's leadership, integrity and ability to deliver as interim CEO," Jones said.

The declaration comes just hours after Huang publicly offered to return if directors believed his reinstatement would benefit shareholders and employees. Earlier today, before Huang’s public statement, sources told CNBC the board was not considering bringing Huang back.

Huang resigned after revelations about extramarital relationships, including one involving a businesswoman whose alleged Chinese intelligence connections have attracted scrutiny from U.S. authorities. An independent investigation is examining his conduct and potential national security implications.

His departure prompted an unusual campaign for reinstatement, with President Donald Trump and Elon Musk among those publicly supporting his return.

Jones also defended Nvidia's newly established National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, saying it would improve engagement with U.S. officials as Washington investigates how restricted AI chips have reached China.

The committee, chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, will oversee compliance programs and receive reports directly from Nvidia's national security and trade compliance teams.

Nvidia reported $2.5 billion in quarterly revenue from sales to Chinese business units under the Trump administration's limited export permissions.

Those arrangements face renewed uncertainty following reports that billions of dollars in restricted Nvidia hardware reached mainland China through overseas intermediaries.

The leadership confrontation overshadowed otherwise impressive quarterly results. Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in revenue, up 103% year over year, with data center sales reaching $107.6 billion.

The company forecast $130 billion in revenue for its fourth fiscal quarter, while assuming no data center computing revenue from China.
