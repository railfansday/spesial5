NYSE’s Avalanche Pick Hints At XRP’s Role

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Home » Trendsetter News » NYSE’s Avalanche Pick Hints At XRP’s Role

NYSE’s reported choice of Avalanche for a tokenized-stock platform does not necessarily rule out XRP.

The New York Stock Exchange’s reported choice of Avalanche for a tokenized-stock platform does not necessarily shut out XRP, wealth commentator Kamilah Stevenson argues in her latest video.

Her distinction: settling share ownership and moving money across currencies are separate jobs, and winning one does not automatically secure the other.

According to Dr. Stevenson, Ava Labs announced that Avalanche would provide the core ledger for the clearing firm behind the proposed platform.

She cites Wall Street Journal reporting that Avalanche would be its sole settlement blockchain, with regulatory approval still required.

The proposed venue would support around-the-clock trading and near-instant settlement. Stevenson says NYSE tested Avalanche for roughly a year, prioritizing transaction speed, predictable costs and control over who can participate.

That permissioning capability matters for a regulated exchange. Avalanche (AVAX) can support customized networks with participation restrictions, rather than requiring the exchange to conduct settlement on an unrestricted public network.

AVAX rose as much as roughly 10% on the news, according to the video. Stevenson, who discloses holding the token, calls the selection a win for Avalanche, but cautions against treating infrastructure adoption as proof of investment returns.

“A network being used by a big name and its token being needed” are different things, she says. The announcement, in her account, provided neither revenue figures nor a commitment that NYSE would buy or hold AVAX.

Dr. Stevenson’s XRP thesis centers on overseas investors funding dollar-denominated stock purchases outside conventional banking hours.

A trader holding yen or another currency would need access to dollars or dollar-linked funding before buying U.S. shares.

She argues that this creates an opportunity for cross-border payment infrastructure, including XRP. But it remains a proposed use case, not a disclosed partnership: “I’m not telling you that NYSE is going to pick XRP for that.”

The YouTube video episode does not establish that XRP would be necessary for such funding. Nor does expanded trading access, by itself, demonstrate future demand for the token.

Dr. Kamilah Stevenson also says the platform would support tokenized stocks issued across multiple blockchains, distinguishing asset issuance from the ledger used for settlement.

Those details matter more than a simple Avalanche-versus-XRP contest: the unresolved questions are which services institutions actually adopt, what generates revenue, and whether that activity requires either token.
