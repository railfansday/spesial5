LIVE Crypto News – Bitcoin and Ethereum Bounce Back: Market Finally Turning?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The CryptoNews editorial team is composed of seasoned writers specializing in cryptocurrency and blockchain technology. Their expertise ensures comprehensive, accurate, and insightful content for...

Bitcoin and Ethereum are bouncing back after a brutal selloff, but the recovery is running into a market that still looks far from healthy. Bitcoin climbed back above $60,000 over the weekend after touching a low near $55,250. Ethereum also gained ground, its first breath of air after several days of relentless selling, a good day for crypto despite bad news and uncertainty.

U.S. stocks suffered another heavy selloff on Friday, with the Nasdaq losing more than 5% as people questioned AI valuations and spending. Asian markets followed lower today, while Bitcoin recovered. Crypto buyers are stepping in, though it doesn’t yet signal that risk appetite has returned.

Crypto will face a tougher test as traditional markets reopen. We are watching technology stocks, oil prices, Treasury yields, and Federal Reserve expectations for clues. A lasting turnaround would need more than short-covering and bargain hunting. It needs buyers willing to hold positions while uncertainty remains high.

The bounce followed a sharp liquidation wave that dragged Bitcoin toward $55,000, its lowest level since September 2024. Yet, this kind of flush can create room for a rebound. Forced sellers disappear, short positions begin closing, and investors who waited on the sidelines see more attractive entry prices.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs are still recording outflows, including a $582 million withdrawal at the end of the week. BlackRock’s IBIT alone suffered $541 million in outflows during the session.

Technically, Bitcoin needs to turn $60,000 into support rather than another rejection. Holding above that psychological level would improve its short-term structure. A move toward $62,000 would give bulls a more meaningful foothold, while a drop toward the recent low would put the recovery in jeopardy.

Ethereum faces a similar challenge, but its performance against Bitcoin matters just as much as its dollar price. If ETH begins outperforming BTC, it could signal that traders are becoming comfortable taking on additional risk. If Bitcoin leads while Ethereum remains weak, the market may still be hiding in the safest corner of crypto even if we see news and catalysts from geopolitics.

The bigger threat to the recovery may come from outside the crypto market. Friday’s selloff hammered technology stocks, sending the Nasdaq down 5.3% and pushing the S&P 500 into correction territory. Investors are questioning AI spending and if it can justify enormous valuations, creating pressure across risk assets.

Bitcoin has increasingly behaved like a high-beta technology trade during periods of stress. When people reduce exposure to growth stocks, crypto gets caught in the same wave. A stabilization in Nasdaq trading could provide an important tailwind, especially if investors begin buying beaten-down technology shares again.

Oil offers another important signal as Brent crude hovers around $92 a barrel, and WTI trades at $83. Both benchmarks had fallen during the previous week, and lower oil prices could help ease inflation pressure, potentially giving the Federal Reserve more flexibility and reducing pressure on bond yields. That would improve conditions for assets sensitive to liquidity.

Gold also rebounds after suffering its steepest weekly decline since March. Bullion rose as traders reassessed interest-rate expectations, but its recovery remains fragile. If gold, stocks, and crypto begin stabilizing together, combined with good geopolitical news, it would suggest forced selling is easing across markets. If equities continue falling, temporary rebounds might disappear quickly.

Today, Bitcoin and Ethereum are showing signs of life, not confirmation of a new trend. Bitcoin needs to hold its reclaimed levels, ETF demand must improve, and Wall Street needs to stop bleeding. Until those pieces fall into place, this bounce may be the market catching its breath rather than preparing for another rally.
