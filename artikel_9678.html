Build specialized multimodal models with LFM3

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

LFM3 is our new generation of Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs), built to be the starting point for specialized intelligence. It brings text, vision, audio, omni, and decision models onto one shared backbone, all in the 2B parameter range. This means you can pick the model for your modality, fine-tune it on your own data on a single GPU, and deploy it where your application runs.

LFM3 brings four main changes compared to our previous LFM2 family:

LFM2 showed that a hybrid of short convolutions and attention can run fast on-device without sacrificing quality. For LFM3, we used our hardware-in-the-loop architecture search to redesign that architecture for agentic use cases, which need more capability per parameter and fast inference over long contexts. The search now covers 14 device and backend combinations, from phones and a Raspberry Pi 5 to laptops, cloud CPUs, GPUs, and the Qualcomm Hexagon NPU.

The payoff shows at long context. Per parameter, LFM3-1.7B matches LFM2-1.2B on short prompts across edge devices, and with 8K tokens in context it is about 25% faster. LFM3-1.7B-Base was pre-trained on 22T tokens with the same 128K vocabulary as LFM2.5-8B-A1B. The final 3.5T tokens are a mid-training stage on curated data with a larger share of agentic traces and tool calls, which also extends the context window to 256K tokens.

Every LFM3 model branches from LFM3-1.7B-Base. Instead of running one large general-purpose model, you pick the one that fits your inputs, customize it, and run it on-device.

Reach for LFM3-1.7B when you need local agents, tool calling, structured outputs, or a base for domain-specific fine-tuning. It is post-trained to run in agentic harnesses such as Hermes Agent, Pi, and OpenClaw. It outperforms its predecessor, LFM2.5-2.6B, with 35% fewer parameters, and the biggest gains are on agentic tasks: on WebArena-Verified, it scores 38.42 against 3.02, and it even beats Qwen3.5-9B on long tasks in browsers and business apps. You can find more details in the LFM3-1.7B model card.

Reach for LFM3-VL-2B when you need on-device visual understanding. It is the first LFM vision model that takes video as input; it reasons step by step before it answers, and it finds objects and reasons about how they are placed, a strong foundation for robotics. Compared with LFM2.5-VL-3B, it has fewer parameters (1.9B against 3.1B) yet raises the average across 62 vision benchmarks from 57.6 to 71.4, on par with Gemma 4 12B, a model more than six times larger. Reach for LFM3-Audio-2B when you need voice chat, speech recognition, speech synthesis, or translation across 18 languages on a laptop or edge device. It takes text or audio as input and responds with text, speech, or both, and it can turn spoken requests into tool calls. Compared with LFM2.5-Audio-1.5B, it uses a new in-house audio encoder and speech codec, speaks with higher quality (UTMOSv2 3.83 against 3.22), and adds audio understanding, general-purpose audio reasoning, and tool calling. Reach for LFM3-Omni-2B when you need one on-device model that sees, listens, and talks back, in place of a separate ASR model, vision-language model, LLM, and TTS model. It can answer spoken questions about an image, watch and listen to a video together, and turn what it sees and hears into tool calls. It is our first omni model, with text, vision, and audio input and both text and speech output, and it keeps the core capabilities of the standalone LFM3 models, including visual question answering, speech recognition, speech synthesis, and instruction following. Like LFM3-Audio-2B, it speaks through LiquidCodec, which turns generated tokens into speech faster than real time, so spoken replies stream as the model generates them. On audio-visual video understanding, it beats models up to 10.7B parameters, such as Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, on Daily-Omni, WorldSense, and AV-SpeakerBench. Reach for the d1 models when you need fast, structured decisions instead of generated text. Give them a situation (a support ticket, an invoice, an app screen, a photo, a voice note) and a list of questions, and they answer all of them in a single forward pass, each with a calibrated probability. Beyond yes/no, choices, and scores, they can now point at objects, read values from documents, estimate numbers with a range, and pick every option that applies. LFM3 is built to be specialized. Frontier models are powerful generalists, but they are not necessarily experts in your use case. LFM3 packs extra capacity into a small, fast model, so a short fine-tune can turn it into a reliable expert on your task, with your data, terminology, and tools. Because it is small, you can deploy the fine-tuned model where your application runs, from devices to private infrastructure to the cloud.

Every model can be fine-tuned on a single GPU, and these notebooks walk you through a complete task for the text, vision, audio, and omni models, from data preparation to evaluation.

We adapt LFM3-VL-2B into three complementary robotics models for orchestration, navigation, and manipulation. The orchestrator operates a robot arm through tool calls: it grounds objects by pointing, measures them with depth tools, invokes motion planners, and checks outcomes in subsequent camera images. The navigator follows route descriptions using recent RGB observations, predicting spatial targets and a waypoint trajectory in just five tokens. The manipulation VLA maps images, language, and robot state to eight-step chunks of continuous actions in a single forward pass. Jointly trained across 15 embodiments, the VLA achieves 96.2% average success across the four LIBERO suites and 45.7% on RoboCasa-GR1 tabletop tasks, generating action chunks in under 50 ms on a Jetson AGX Orin with TensorRT FP16. These results highlight LFM3-VL-2B as a compact foundation for robotics, spanning high-level planning to low-level control with real-time action generation on embedded hardware.
