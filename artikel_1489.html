Man Utd & Palace shocking in 2026-27 Premier League wage bill vs. position comparison

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Chelsea, Tottenham Hotspur, Manchester United, Liverpool, Manchester City and Arsenal are all sitting in vastly contrasting places when you compare their wage bill rankings to their league positions after the first four games of the 2026-27 Premier League season.

Of course, it’s still early in the season. Ten matches can tell us only so much about how the season will eventually play out, and the table is still taking shape.

Still, there are already some big, eye-catching differences between wage outlay and performance.

Brighton lead the way. They are second in the Premier League despite having the 15th-highest wage bill, which stands at just £51million. That is a remarkable +13 difference between their league position and wage ranking.

Brentford are second in that regard. They are eighth while ranking 17th for wages, with a £49million annual bill, giving them a +9 difference. Hull City are fourth, sitting 13th despite having the 19th-highest wage bill at just £16million. Their +6 difference is another remarkable return for a newly-promoted side.

Leeds United have also made an impressive start. They occupy 9th place while sitting 14th in the wage rankings. Their annual wage bill is only £53million, giving them a +5 difference.

Ipswich are another side punching well above their wage bill. They sit 14th while ranking 18th for wages, with an annual wage bill of just £20million. That’s a +4 difference.

The traditional powerhouses have produced a mixed bag. Arsenal are top while having the third-highest wage bill in the division, at £154million. That puts them two places ahead of their wage position, while Manchester City sit third in the league and second in the wage rankings.

Chelsea are fifth with the seventh-highest wage bill, £97million, while top spenders Liverpool are fourth. The Reds have the highest wage bill in the Premier League at £177million but sit three places below that ranking.

The other ‘Big Six’ underachievers are not hard to spot. Manchester United are 18th with the fourth-highest wage bill of £152million, while Tottenham are 11th despite having the fifth-highest at £138million. Oof.

Villa are also slightly off the pace. They are 10th despite having the sixth-highest wage bill, with £98million going on salaries. That puts them four places below their wage ranking.

We’ve ranked every Premier League club by the gap between their league position and wage-bill ranking, from the biggest overperformers to the biggest underperformers. Keep an eye on this one, as we’ll be keeping it updated as the season progresses.
