Scotland skipper Andy Robertson insists time is running out for Pocognoli's men after horror show in St Gallen

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:35 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 17:41 EST, 16 November 2026

Andy Robertson admitted Scotland don’t have time to get things right under Sebastien Pocognoli after the team were humbled by Switzerland on the night of the skipper’s record 103rd cap.

John McGinn warned this week it could take the Scots up to 18 months to fully grasp the Belgian’s tactical plans.

But serious questions are now being asked of Pocognoli after his team were swept aside in a miserable 4-0 defeat to a much-changed Swiss side.

The result, combined with Slovenia’s 3-0 win over North Macedonia, ended Scotland’s already scant hopes of a Nations League promotion play-off spot and condemned them to pot three in next month’s Euro 2028 draw.

Robertson was hoping for a night to remember as he overtook Kenny Dalglish as the country’s most capped player but was clearly furious as the Scots crumbled in St Gallen.

Asked if it was going to take time for things to click under Pocognoli, he told the BBC: ‘I don’t think you’ve got time in international football, you have to do it.

Scotland skipper Andy Robertson laments a night to forget on the occasion of his 103rd cap

‘These supporters who travel everywhere, are they going to give us time? No. Are the people inside Hampden going to give us time? No. Are the people outside Hampden, who are going to be critical and rightly so? No.

‘We don’t have time, the players do not have time, we have got to step up.

‘We have a long break now to March, which is not a good thing, and it is going to be a tough one to swallow.

‘We really need to step up again. In international football, you do not have time.’

After an unconvincing start to his reign, Pocognoli needed a statement victory to convince sceptical fans that he is the right man for the job.

Despite a promising start, the rot set in after Lewis Ferguson’s 22nd minute own-goal.

The Swiss, missing a host of key players, went further ahead with goals from Winsley Boteli, Alvyn Sanches and Zeki Amdouni.

Almost 12 months on from the famous night when Steve Clarke’s side beat Denmark to reach the World Cup, the Tartan Army will be looking towards the European qualifiers with trepidation.

Robertson is tackled by Switzerland's Zachary Athekame on a night of few positives

Robertson said: ‘First half, I thought we were in the game. We lost a stupid goal but I thought we had chances and it could have been 2-1 at half-time.

‘By the time you get to 50 minutes and you are 3-0 down, that can’t happen in international football.

‘We were playing a good team but I felt we were in the game, it is a really tough night for us.’

Pocognoli also felt the final scoreline was harsh and rued his side’s inability to make the most of the opportunities they created.

‘The score is heavy with the 4-0, but I don’t think it’s a reflection of the game, if I’m really honest and objective,’ said Pocognoli.

‘I think we had some very good moments, we started like we wanted, aggressive away from home. I think we played a good first half, we created chances, we were dominant in some moments and we faced Switzerland with different shapes and adapted well from the first minute.

Sebastien Pocognoli will now face some searching questions over his suitability for the job

‘I think we conceded the goal from maybe one of the first chances they had. We also created chances but we were not clinical in that moment in the box.

‘In the second half, yeah, we started poorly from the first minute, a poor corner we gave away. Then you feel that, OK, we will get punished.

‘But I think when we made the substitutions, they made an impact, and we should have scored two goals in that period.

‘At the end, it’s a bad feeling, the score, but I think that the players try to do their best and create moments that we want to see.’
