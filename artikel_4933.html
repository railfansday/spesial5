As a dietitian, I don’t hand out supplement recommendations like candy. But when it comes to products that work, creatine is one I confidently stand behind, personally use, and recommend to just about anyone. It’s one of the most well-researched, widely used, and reliable performance enhancers for boosting strength, muscular endurance, and mood, according to a 2021 study by Nutrients.

Creatine supports your body’s production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the fast-acting energy your muscles depend on during high-intensity effort. While you can get small amounts from food, supplementing is the most effective way to saturate your muscles. Whether you’re training consistently, getting older, or simply looking to preserve lean muscle, creatine can also speed up recovery.

Supplements are unregulated by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)—and that includes creatine. As such, experts say it’s important to consider the following factors and complete your due diligence to find a high-quality product when shopping for a creatine supp. (Reading this article counts!)

Simplicity is often best with creatine. Look for products that list creatine as the sole active ingredient, ideally without added sugars, unless you're choosing a flavored version intentionally. Avoid proprietary blends that don’t disclose exact creatine content per serving.

The recommended daily dose of creatine is a minimum of five grams per day, according to the International Society of Sports Nutrition. This amount is shown to be safe and effective for maintaining creatine saturation within the muscles.

There are several types of creatine available, including monohydrate, citrate, and nitrate, but creatine monohydrate remains the most reliable. It’s the most extensively researched, dependable, effective, and cost-efficient option.

The U.S. supplement industry is largely unregulated, which means that the FDA doesn’t verify supplement marketing claims before products hit the shelves. But third-party testing can help. Look for certifications from organizations like Informed Choice or NSF Certified for Sport, which ensure the product has been tested for purity, potency, and contaminants. This is especially critical for competitive athletes concerned about banned substances.

For most people, taking creatine daily is not only okay, but it’s actually recommended to maintain optimal muscle creatine levels. Consistent daily use supports better performance, recovery, and long-term benefits.

The most common side effect of creatine is short-term water retention, which can lead to temporary weight gain. Taking more than 10 grams at once may also cause digestive issues such as bloating or diarrhea. However, for most people, creatine is well-tolerated when used as directed.

If you have pre-existing kidney or liver conditions, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or are a child or adolescent, it’s important to consult your healthcare provider before taking creatine. For most healthy adults, creatine is safe and effective, but checking in with your doctor can provide added peace of mind and guidance.
