This Bissell Carpet Cleaner Is Lightweight, Compact, and Easy to Store—All for Under $125

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

If you live in a smaller space or multilevel home, the TurboClean is worth a look.

Carpet cleaning is one of those chores that feels like a massive undertaking every time—not to mention, no one likes having to lug around a heavy carpet cleaner sloshing with water. The Bissell TurboClean Upright Carpet Cleaner is made for people like us, with a lightweight design and minimal learning curve.

This machine is great for small spaces, and for those who prefer streamlined and simplified household appliances. Although it does have a smaller water tank capacity and lacks handheld attachments, it provides a powerful clean paired with easy maneuverability and fast drying time.

The compact design makes it easy both to use and to store. It’ll be sitting alongside your favorite dry vacuum as a trusted staple of your cleaning routine in no time, as I found to be the case for myself.

I set aside a weekend morning to test the TurboClean upright across a frequently used area of my main living space, spanning the pathway from the door to the garage through to the kitchen. The area includes some carpet that I've vacuumed frequently, but not deep-cleaned in years, as well as a low-pile area rug. I didn’t pretreat the area with dry vacuuming, but I checked for any larger pieces of debris that might clog up the cleaner.

The cleaner was quick and easy to assemble, and filling the water tank was simple too. I followed the manufacturer’s instructions to add some of the included Bissell cleaning solution along with warm water. Afterward, I tackled my designated area of carpeting, followed by a section of the area rug.

Throughout the process, I monitored how well the cleaner performed in a few ways. I kept an eye on how long the water tank lasted before I had to empty the dirty water and refill the clean tank, as well as how many times I had to go over an area for a satisfactory clean. I also maneuvered the machine as close as possible to the baseboards and into corners to test movability and range. Finally, I checked how long it took for the carpet to dry afterward, as well as how easy it was to clean the machine when I wrapped it all up.

The first thing I noticed about the TurboClean Upright was its lightness. Weighing just under 13 pounds, it’s one of the lightest carpet cleaners I’ve tried. That light weight, combined with a compact base and smooth wheels, made it especially easy to move around, whether I was pulling it along straight pathways, turning corners, or navigating as close to the wall as I could get. Even with a full tank, I found it very manageable.

That lightness, though, didn’t mean flimsiness or poor performance. On the contrary: it pulled up considerably more dirt than I expected, providing a deep, visible clean on a high-traffic area of light-colored carpet.

While I didn’t have any serious stains for it to handle, it did do a great job on visible spots of dirt or water-drop stains that had become dried-in. It worked equally well on the area rug, refreshing it without damaging the fibers or dulling the colors.

As a compact machine, the TurboClean Upright does have one of the hallmark downsides to its class: the need for frequent refills. The water tank has a capacity of just over half a gallon. As I worked through my test area, I had to stop, empty the dirty water tank, and refill the cleaner with fresh water and cleaning solution.

If you’re trying to spot-clean a few small patches, you can probably get away without a refill, but if you’re actually doing a whole room or more, you’ll need to do the empty-and-refill routine—probably multiple times. It’s also worth noting that the machine does not heat the water from within. It recommends adding warm water (no warmer than 140°F) to the clean tank with the solution.

Fortunately, the process of refilling and, later, disassembling and cleaning, is very easy. The tank and handle feel a little light to the touch, but they’re sturdier than they seem, and it’s easy to get a grip and handle the empty/refill cycle without making a mess. Disassembling the main parts (the upper tank and the two-part handle) is as easy as the push of a button. There’s really very little in the way of maintenance, which I appreciated.

If you plan on cleaning an area with a lot of debris (like pet hair), I would suggest vacuuming first. Otherwise, you do run the risk of particles getting tangled up around the cleaning brush, which can take a while to get cleared.

For me, the biggest downside to this cleaner is its lack of any hand tools. Where most home carpet cleaners come with at least a handheld tool or a spot-cleaning tool of some kind, the basic model TurboClean Upright has none. While it’s still a light and small enough machine to maneuver around most spaces, the lack of extensions and tools does limit what it can do.

I was able to lower it enough to clean underneath a coffee table, and I could get fairly close to the wall, but not quite as deep into the corners. I wish there had been a stain tool, a pet tool, or even just a simple hand tool extension. Without those, the cleaner is unable to handle smaller crevices or upholstery.

I didn’t have any deep, dark stains that needed removing, but if I did have those, it would have been nice to be able to really focus the cleaning power on those areas. As currently designed, the best I could do was simply run the full cleaner over the same spot repeatedly.

All that being said, the TurboClean Upright does come in a deluxe “Pet” model as well. That model does include a 7-foot extendible hose, a self-cleaning “tough stains” brush, and a spraying crevice tool. Those additions offer more flexibility to tackle small spots and get into crevices, improving the cleaner’s performance and versatility. And, of course, anyone with pets will definitely want to look into this upgrade to gain the extra cleaning power for pet stains and hair.

The TurboClean Upright comes with two trial-size Bissell cleaning formulas: the Pro Max Clean + Protect and the Pet Pro Oxy Urine Eliminator. I used the Pro Max solution for deep cleaning, adding it to the clean tank up to the indicated “formula” line. The instructions also indicate that users can add a dose of the “Oxy Boost” formula for a stronger cleaning reaction, but since I didn’t have that on hand, I skipped that step.

After multiple passes across my testing area, I really appreciated the strong suction power. Although there’s not a specific “quick dry” mode, the manual does suggest running “dry passes” across the wet areas to suction up as much of the residual water as possible. It only took between one and two hours for the test section to feel pretty much dry to the touch.

The Bissell TurboClean Upright is ideal for smaller spaces and intuitive operation, bringing plenty of cleaning power but keeping the machine itself relatively compact and lightweight. I was able to easily clean carpeting and an area rug, and I could move around corners easily.

The smaller size does mean a smaller tank (requiring more frequent stops to refill), and I wish the standard model included some attachment tools for greater versatility. Still, if you’re looking for a reliable cleaner for a smaller area that does the job well (and doesn’t leave your carpet sopping wet), this could be a great pick for you.
