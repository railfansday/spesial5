Would you pay Spotify extra to remix your favorite songs with AI?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Others were already thinking about what it would take to switch. "Please does anyone know good ways to transfer all my Spotify songs to another site?" one user asked her followers. Over on the subreddit r/indieheads, people were comparing Tidal and Qobuz and discussing buying music directly on Bandcamp. One vinyl buyer explained why leaving would still be hard. "Having podcasts and the ability to just look up new bands instantly is something I'd really struggle to part with," they wrote.

That convenience is part of what Spotify has been using AI to build on. As Mashable has reported, its AI DJ can take spoken requests, and its Prompted Playlist feature lets listeners describe what they want to hear in their own words. AI is showing up in the songs themselves, too. The Velvet Sundown, an AI-generated band, drew more than a million Spotify streams in 2025, and AI-powered singer Xania Monet made it onto Billboard's Adult R&B Airplay chart that same year. There's, of course, some irony in Spotify asking listeners to pay to make AI covers while it’s also trying to limit AI spam and help people tell when an artist persona is AI-generated.

For artists weighing whether to opt in, there's the question of what Spotify would do with their music and what they'd earn from it. The Future of Music Coalition put it plainly on X. "What’s it being trained on? Is it opt-in only? How’s the $ work?" the group wrote.

Spotify and Believe, the French music distributor that owns TuneCore, have answered part of the money question. In an October announcement about their licensing deal, the companies said each remix would link back to the original song and artist. "Streams of these remixes will generate royalties for the participating artists and rights holders behind the original works," they said. The FT also reported that those plays would count toward the original song on Spotify's charts. So a fan-made version could earn royalties while giving listeners a way to find the track that inspired it.

Even if Spotify can make the royalties work, there’s still the question of whether anyone really wants more AI-generated music in the first place. As one X user put it, "Why would anyone want this?"
