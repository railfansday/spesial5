Agencies are selling AI efficiency — clients want outcomes

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Join us Dec. 1-3 in Nashville for the Digiday Programmatic Marketing Summit

Amol Waishampayan, co-founder, fullthrottle.ai

Agencies spent the last two years pitching AI to clients as a cost story. Faster work, leaner teams, lower fees. Clients took the meeting, nodded along and then waited for the invoice to shrink. It didn’t. AI strategies now sound interchangeable to clients, who say the savings never showed up in their fees.

That is not an AI problem. It is a positioning problem. Efficiency was the wrong promise. Clients do not hire agencies to be fast. They hire them to grow the business. AI’s real opportunity for agencies is not doing the same work cheaper. It is doing work that actually moves revenue, and proving it.

Agencies are getting operational value from AI. According to the Digiday Media Agency Report 2026, 70% of agencies say AI has cut costs by making tasks more efficient, while 31% say it cost more to implement than expected. But efficiency is a one-time gain.

Once every agency has it, nobody gets paid for it. As one Gartner analyst told Digiday, the honeymoon between agencies and AI is “essentially over.” The agencies that build their AI story on productivity are competing on margin. The ones that build it on performance are competing on value, and value is what clients renew.

Most media plans are still organized by channel. Search budget here, social budget there, streaming over in a separate deck. AI makes that structure obsolete, because it can plan around the customer instead.

Take a regional home services company running paid search and social. Those campaigns are producing something more valuable than clicks: a live stream of households actively researching its services. AI can turn those signals into a defined first-party audience, figure out where else those households spend their time, and extend the plan into streaming TV, digital video and audio to reach them there. One audience, coordinated across channels, built from the client’s own demand rather than a platform’s inventory.

That is a different kind of planning. The question stops being “what should we spend on CTV” and becomes “who are we trying to reach, and where are they?”

The next step is letting AI act on that intelligence. Agentic systems can take audience and performance signals and adjust targeting, channel mix and spend as conditions change, instead of waiting for the monthly optimization meeting. Static plans with quarterly reviews are a relic of how long it used to take humans to read the data.

But here is where agencies should be careful. The advantage is not how much of the buying you automate. Anyone can automate. The advantage is whether the agent is optimizing toward something that matters. An agent chasing cheaper CPMs will find them, and the client will not care. An agent optimizing toward the client’s actual business outcomes, with the agency keeping full transparency and control, is a different product entirely.

This is the part most of the industry still avoids. Clicks and impressions are easy to report and nearly impossible to defend in a budget conversation. The only metrics a CFO cares about are sales, customers and revenue.

Once that loop exists, everything upstream improves. Planning gets smarter because it is fed by real conversions. Agentic buying gets better because it is optimizing toward revenue instead of proxies. And the agency walks into every renewal with proof rather than a deck.

Agencies are already monetizing AI. Digiday’s report found that 59% are launching new AI-powered products, 34% are selling strategic roadmaps or audits and 31% are charging additional fees for AI-powered services. But if the product is “we have AI,” the pricing power will not last. Every competitor has AI. Within a year, every client will have it too.

The agencies that win the next cycle will not be the ones with the most AI. They will be the ones who can show a client exactly what it delivered, in the client’s own numbers.
