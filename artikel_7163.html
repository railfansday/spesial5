Pet Brands Find New Ways to Invite the Family Dog to Thanksgiving Dinner

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

In 2023, pet information site Veterinarians.org polled 1,000 dog owners and discovered that 68% planned on making a Thanksgiving meal just for them.

Think that’s a lot? This year, that number is closer to nearly all of them.

Premium pet-food brand Ollie recently surveyed 1,000 dog owners and found that, as the holidays approach, 96% of dog households plan to prepare Rover “something special to eat.”

Few are more aware of this shift than pet brands, which are rolling out Thanksgiving-specific products and promotions to help consumers include dogs at the dinner table—at times literally.

Ollie—which sits in the fresh, human-grade dog food category—is offering a Holiday Feast meal made with traditional Thanksgiving ingredients like turkey, cranberries, and sweet potatoes. On Nov. 21 and 22, select Petco stores hosted Petsgiving, a cooking lesson on how to cook a dog-appropriate Thanksgiving meal.

“These are the latest in our series of retail-tainment events designed to create opportunities for connection and bonding between pets and pet parents,” Petco’s SVP of brand creative Alison Hiatt told ADWEEK.

In a similar bid to “let dogs join in Thanksgiving,” legacy pet food brand Purina recently announced a limited edition run of its Beggin’ line of treats in flavors like Bacon ’n Turkey and Honey ’n Ham. Just Food for Dogs is selling its limited-run Thanksgiving Feast Recipe online this year for the first time.

And to promote its limited-time Harvest Turkey dinners, premium brand Maev has launched its “Seat at the Table” campaign that presents dogs “as respected guests of honor at the Thanksgiving table.”

“We’re in the midst of a fundamental shift in the role dogs play in our lives, from existing on the periphery of our identity to becoming an extension of it,” said Maev’s senior director of brand Maddy Tank. “We’re increasingly bringing them into the rituals, routines and experiences that make us human.”

Holiday promos are nothing new, of course, but why the concerted effort now to give dogs a place at the Thanksgiving table? It’s a combination of several forces.

First, more people own dogs. In 2025, dog ownership in America rose from 51% to 53%, according to the American Pet Products Association. Most of that growth is coming from zoomers and millennials, and these are the generations most likely to regard a pet as a full-fledged family member—by rates of 68% and 69%, respectively, according to Euromonitor data.

“There are also fewer people having children,” Ollie CMO Allison Stadd told ADWEEK. “So dogs are increasingly filling that role that people might have filled with human children in the past.”

Even among families with children, Purina senior brand manager John Paul Vella added, “dogs have been elevated to child status among consumers.”

Finally, there’s a growing awareness that feeding fido scraps from the Thanksgiving table can actually be harmful, a concern that opens the door to these specialty meals and offerings.

Some of which can be extremely special. Ollie automatically enrolled everyone who bought its Holiday Feast online before Nov. 3 in a drawing to win a $1,700 Baccarat crystal bowl—for the dog, of course.

Robert Klara is the senior editor of brands at Adweek, specializing in the evolution and impact of brands.
