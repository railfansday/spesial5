If You Invest $1,000 in JEPQ Right Now and Never Add Another Dollar, Here’s What It Could Deliver in 10 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

This covered call ETF offers a lucrative monthly income stream.

By Matt DiLallo – Nov 23, 2026 at 4:06PM EST

10 stocks we like better than JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF ›

The JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF (JEPQ +6.28%) has provided investors with an 11.5% income yield over the last 12 months. That income has supplied investors with a tangible return amid a very choppy stock market. However, the fund's total return has been only about 8% this year, trailing the nearly 13% return of the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index.

Here's what $1,000 invested in the JP Morgan Nasdaq Equity Premium ETF today could deliver over the next 10 years if you never add another dollar.

JEPQ holds a portfolio of Nasdaq-100 stocks and sells call options on the index. Those option premiums fund its lucrative monthly distributions, in exchange for capping some of the upside. The fund paid $6.95 per share over the past year, including $0.539 in November.

At about $54 a share, $1,000 buys roughly 18.5 shares. That would produce around $115 of income in the first year at its current yield, though the monthly amount moves with market volatility.

Since its launch in 2022, JEPQ has returned about 15% per year, with distributions reinvested. That run came in a strong tech market. Here's what $1,000 could become over 10 years with distributions reinvested at three different annual return scenarios:

The base case would see a $1,000 investment more than double in value in a decade, while the bull case is even stronger.

The trade-off is that JEPQ's covered-call strategy limits gains when the Nasdaq rallies. We've seen that in the ETF's relative performance compared to that benchmark since its inception. While it has delivered a more than 15% annualized total return with distributions reinvested, that has underperformed the index's 19.7% annualized return.

JEPQ offers a big monthly payout and a smoother ride than the Nasdaq-100, but it won't capture all the index's returns. I also wouldn't bank on another 15%+ annualized return over the next decade, though I think a $1,000 could more than double in value by 2036.
