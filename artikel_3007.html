GTA 6 is the most impressive video game I’ve ever played. I’m not surprised, given Rockstar Games’ history of releasing technically proficient (and insanely profitable) titles, but the storied developers have truly outdone most other games with what they’ve accomplished here. The long wait and anticipation are more than justified.

Larger than any locale in the franchise’s history, the fictional state of Leonida is a character unto itself, evoking real-life Florida with its myriad eccentric characters and diverse locations. You explore this vast landscape alongside the game’s two protagonists, a Bonnie-and-Clyde-like couple who help keep the emotional center in place amid the chaos of the main story and side quests. Together, the narrative and open-world elements deliver an experience that keeps you hooked for hours.

I’ve been a huge fan of the GTA series since GTA 3 arrived in 2001 and completely changed the gaming landscape. I had some trepidation about GTA 6, given how long we’ve waited for it and how much gaming (and the world) has changed since GTA 5was released in 2013. Thankfully, GTA 6 far exceeds my expectations and will more than likely be my favorite game of the generation. Here’s why...

GTA 6 straddles the line between the more grounded tone of GTA 4 and the over-the-top insanity of GTA 5. It’s fair to say this game feels like Red Dead Redemption 2 set in the 21st century, in both tone and theme. Given that RDR 2 is in my top 10 favorite games of all time, this is a major bonus.

That Red Dead inspiration is most evident in the game’s first chapter, where we slowly learn about the two protagonists, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a criminal couple trying to build a new life. The slower-burn intro will turn off anyone who's played the arcade-y GTA Online for the past 13 years—and it’s a valid criticism, even if I personally didn’t mind it. After chapter 1, it’s full-on GTA-style mayhem.

Jason and Lucia have more grounded personalities than some of the wilder protagonists in older GTA titles, but the game generally does a good job of showing what motivates them and making you feel invested in their journey. Jason and Lucia balance each other, with the former more stoic and calculated and the latter more impulsive and instinctive. The characters are at their best together and feel incomplete apart. The leads’ relatively toned-down attitudes also contrast nicely with the absolutely bonkers characters they meet along the way.

In true GTA fashion, this is a rags-to-riches criminal tale that builds with each subsequent mission. You feel the sense of progression as you move up in the world and take on bigger, more dangerous heists. Watching the protagonists evolve over the course of the story is also satisfying.

In the Grand Theft Auto series, driving has often been a point of contention, and that hasn’t changed here. At least initially. During the first few hours, I found the driving mechanics overly sensitive and loose. It felt like I was driving on ice, especially in the beat-up jalopy Jason owns at the beginning.

Then I realized that, at least on PS5, the triggers are pressure-sensitive, and I shouldn’t just hold R2 all the way down. In fact, you can feel a bit of resistance when you depress the button halfway. When I figured this out, I was golden. Though some vehicles naturally control better and differently than others, I now have a good handle (no pun intended) on the pretty intricate driving mechanics.

Pay & Spray shops are not only great for a quick paint job to lose the cops, but also for tuning your car’s performance for better handling. This can be expensive, so it’s best to wait until you have enough money and a car worth investing in, since you don’t want to upgrade a piece of crap. I like tuning cars because it reminds me of Rockstar’s Midnight Club racing series. And yes, you can totally add neon lights and nitrous!

Stealing cars is more involved than ever. While you can easily steal most cheaper or older cars, higher-end vehicles will have alarms, key fobs, and trackers. And even when you successfully jack a car, you need to hotwire it. The game doles out specific tools needed to steal various cars at specific points, so you'll need to be patient to grab the really high-end rides. Even if jacking cars requires more than a button press, the extra steps make stealing them feel more immersive.

As criminals, Jason and Lucia always end up getting into gunfights—either with fellow lawbreakers or law enforcement. Though the shooting and cover mechanics aren’t as precise and responsive as a dedicated third-person shooter like Gears of War: E-Day, they’re the most refined of any previous GTA game.

Auto lock for weapons was essential in GTA 5 and RDR 2 because the free-aiming mechanics were so dodgy and imprecise. That’s not the case here, as I can easily land headshots with auto lock disabled. This game has its own version of Red Dead’s “Dead Eye,” called Focus Mode, which slows the world down so you can target specific body parts. This is crucial if you want to shoot arms or legs to disable rather than kill, especially since it ties into the game’s morality system. Jason and Lucia both have a Focus meter that drains while you’re in Focus Mode.

The cover mechanics are good but not great. I say that because while your character sticks to walls or the sides of cars you’re hiding behind, it’s too easy to snap out of cover when you try to move. Still, the mechanic works well enough most of the time, and you definitely want to stay in cover during intense shootouts with gang members or the police.

You have access to all manner of handguns, shotguns, assault rifles, and more, which you can purchase from Ammu-Nation, pilfer from dispatched enemies, or find in the open world. Like cars, you can modify weapons. Of course, buying upgrades that improve handling, extend magazine capacity, add scopes, and more can get expensive, but it’s well worth it given the number of insane shootouts you’ll encounter during the story or out in Leonidas.

Sticking up pawn shops and robbing banks has always been fun in GTA, but it’s even more enjoyable now with two protagonists. I never get tired of watching how shop owners and customers react when Jason and Lucia bust in with guns drawn. While one makes sure no one tries anything funny, the other threatens the clerk into emptying the register. Sometimes someone tries to be a hero and stop you, so you have to stay on your guard.

That scenario is for small shops, but if you want to break into a bank, you’ll need to prepare. Scoping out a place, either from the inside or outside, to locate exits and where the goods are kept is crucial to pulling off a successful heist. And don’t forget to have a getaway vehicle nearby, as the cops will arrive quickly if alarms are triggered (though you can always try to disable them).

Along the way, you’ll also discover or be told where gangs hang out or where other criminal activity occurs. These locations often have jewelry, drugs, or other contraband that you can then sell to a fence for a big haul. As with bank robberies, you’ll want to scope out a location to identify entry points and other opportunities to exploit.

The wanted system is more intricate than before. Committing crimes in a specific area raises your wanted level by up to six stars, with each star bringing more heat from law enforcement. You can drive to another county to lose the heat, but the cops will remember your vehicle, face, hairstyle, clothing, and that there were two of you. To really lose the cops, you’ll need to change your clothes, repaint your car, or temporarily split up.

For example, I got a 4-star wanted rating after a robbery went wrong. I drove my car deep into the woods and got out, using the trees for cover to avoid being spotted by helicopters. After finding a truck depot, I broke into a truck using a slim jim carjacking tool and stayed in the truck without turning it on. The cops swarmed the area but didn’t find me because I was lying low. After a few minutes, they left, and I drove off to change clothes and switch vehicles. None of this was scripted.

Since this is a crime game, I’m glad Rockstar updated the wanted system to feel more realistic and give you the freedom to evade the fuzz in ways not available in previous titles.

Now, I won’t go into exhaustive detail about all the side activities available. Suffice it to say that if you need a break from your criminal pursuits, there is plenty to do. That said, I want to discuss some of my favorites.

Like in previous games, you can do side missions for some of the zany characters you meet. I enjoy these because they are small stories unto themselves, and also let you experience more of Leonidas’ environment and cultures. No spoilers here, but some of these side missions are completely bonkers, and sometimes quite literally out of this world.

This may sound weird, given how I neglect to do this in real life, but I’m obsessed with taking Jason and Lucia to the gym. And it’s not just because I want them to look buff and hot. For instance, lifting weights boosts your strength, making you a bit harder to kill and letting you punch enemies out more easily. Boosting your cardio lets you run and swim longer, while raising your Focus can extend your Focus Mode. You can also boost these stats by brawling, running, or eating, but I find hitting the gym more efficient and fun.

You’ll also find several events that lead to missions not marked on the map. For example, you might return to your car only to find a random woman in the backseat asking for help retrieving her dog from a biker gang, or an influencer might decide to snap photos of herself with your fancy ride. This is why it pays to explore methodically, as surprises are literally around every corner.

Among other things, you can participate in street or boat races, get drunk and blow all your money at strip clubs, do some mud wrestling, or unwind by watching TV (the in-game TV shows are hilarious) or doomscrolling on your phone with your partner in bed after a long day of chaos. There is no shortage of interesting activities to partake in here.

I say this without a hint of hyperbole, but GTA 6 is the most graphically impressive and detailed game I’ve ever seen. Rockstar’s proprietary RAGE Engine is in full effect, delivering awe-inspiring visuals that bring Leonida to life. Videos online can’t compare to the experience of playing GTA 6 firsthand.

The map is immense. Even at full speed on the highway, it can take five or more minutes to reach a destination. And you won’t even mind, since everything you pass is so wonderfully rendered, from distant bridges to forests beneath thick, darkening clouds. I especially love driving through Vice City at night, as the metropolis’ gleaming buildings and neon-drenched streets are a sight to behold.

Since Leonida is essentially a miniature version of Florida, it has similar biomes. These include muggy swamps, dense woods, and the terrifying marvel of the Caribbean Sea. As in RDR 2, the world is filled with all manner of animals, from small rats to enormous sperm whales. You can easily spend hours exploring each locale, as long as you’re wary of hungry alligators or sharks.

Humans are as intricately detailed as the animals and environments. During my playthrough, I didn’t see a single NPC who resembled another. I’m also impressed that certain character types appear only in specific neighborhoods, which adds to the realism. You can spend all day people-gazing, as the fine folks of Leonidas always have something interesting to say. But as with exploring the wilds, keep a weapon handy for when someone inevitably tries to mug you.

I played on a PS5 Pro, which, like all consoles that GTA 6 is available on, is capped at 30 frames per second. During my time, I never once saw the frame rate dip, even during intense shootouts or fast-moving chase sequences. Naturally, your experience may differ, even if you’re also playing on a PS5 Pro, but I never encountered performance issues. And though I wish we could have gotten at least 40 fps on the PS5 Pro, the locked 30 fps runs as smoothly as possible.

Beyond the gorgeous visuals, the GTA 6 experience isn’t complete without its incredible soundtrack. With over 22 radio stations featuring dozens of songs, there’s no shortage of awesome tracks to play while you commit crimes.

As before, you get a nice mix of stations featuring contemporary and classic tunes. There are also a handful of talk radio stations, which have always been a favorite of mine. Every station has an eclectic DJ or two to keep things lively. The radio stations also round out the world of GTA 6, offering entertaining insights on celebrities, famous establishments, current news, and more.

The original tunes are just as nice as the expansive licensed soundtrack. You mostly hear this music during cinematic scenes, but it also pops up when you’re fleeing the cops. There’s also a particular track from rap duo Bau-Luxe that will have you laughing out loud because of its obscene lyrics that I won’t even hint at here!

This has been one of my longer game reviews, and even it only scratches the surface of the GTA 6 experience. Suffice it to say, you can (and some will) sink countless hours into this world and still not see everything Rockstar managed to cram into it. Like an expensive drink you can buy in Vice City, this is a title you’ll want to savor, not rush.

GTA 6 is everything we could have hoped for and more. Despite a few minor quibbles, nothing here detracts significantly. I’ve never given a game I’ve reviewed at Tom’s Guide a full five stars, but if any game deserves that honor, it’s certainly GTA 6. It’s a once-in-a-generation title that shows what’s possible when you give a talented team the budget and time to craft something exceptional. GTA 6 really is that damn good!
