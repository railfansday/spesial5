Richemont’s Jewelry Sales Rise 14%

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Jewelry sales at Cartier’s parent company decelerated in fiscal Q2, after surging 24% in Q1. Jewelry maisons’ sales were up 14% to €4.4 billion in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, below consensus expectations of 16% growth, while Richemont posted group sales up 13% at constant exchange rates to €6 billion, above consensus expectations of 12%. The Richemont share price was down 6% in morning trading.

Sales at the specialty watchmakers division, including Piaget, IWC Schaffhausen and Jaeger-LeCoultre, rose 12%, smashing consensus expectations of 4% — a “major positive surprise”, as Bernstein analyst Luca Solca put it. Second-quarter sales in the conglomerate’s “other houses” division, which includes Chloé and Alaïa, were up 9%.

By geography, Richemont sales in the Americas were up 19% in Q2, Europe was up 15%, Asia-Pacific increased by 10%, and Japan rose by 23%. The Middle East and Africa were down 7%, significantly missing expectations of 4% growth.

China’s macro environment has been a major drag on luxury’s third-quarter earnings. Richemont was not immune to the challenges in the Mainland China market but is seeing growth in Greater China overall. “The confidence factor in mainland China is still not at its all-time high. We still see some rather soft business, although stable,” Richemont CEO Nicolas Bos said during a media call on Friday. “What’s interesting is that the trends in Greater China, particularly in Hong Kong and Macau, are extremely positive, so the Chinese clientele as a whole is really showing positive trends.”

Richemont chairman Johann Rupert, who was on the call, also noted the group’s difficulties in China. “ If anybody thinks we're less affected by [market conditions in] China, on the contrary. I’ll give you, for instance, Jaeger-LeCoultre is down 46% in China, yet 17% up for the year. So the rest of the world did so well that they could absorb China. Very good management and excellent products.”

Meanwhile, Alaïa has yet to appoint a new creative director following the departure of Pieter Mulier in March. Asked to comment on the timing for a successor appointment, Bos said: “The maison is doing very well. We have very good momentum and dynamics, and we had a beautiful presentation this September that was completely driven by the team in the studio, which was highly appreciated and successful. So we take our time. Alaïa is very much a maison that’s built on the long-term, and we believe there is a timeless style.” The executive declined to comment on speculation surrounding possible creative changes at Chloé.

He also declined to comment on “any rumors that have to do with other companies” when asked to confirm Richemont’s involvement in discussions to join a consortium bidding for De Beers.

As British mining group Anglo American seeks to sell its 85% stake in De Beers, Bloomberg reported on October 15 that Richemont participated in talks.

Johann Rupert’s son,  Anton Rupert, participated in the earnings call for the first time since being appointed Richemont co-deputy chairman in September.  When asked during the media call when he will hand over his post to his son, Johann Rupert replied with a laugh: “Whenever I want to. Do you think I should?” He continued: “ I’m definitely not wedded to this. We’re having fun. I’m leaving a lot of decisions.”

Despite the slowdown, Richemont remains the best-in-class in this season’s earnings calendar. On why Richemont is doing so well, Johann Rupert said: “ That’s really due to already having handed over a lot to Nicolas [Bos], Louis Ferla at Cartier, and Anton [...]. The transitions have already taken place. Cartier, Van Cleef, and Jaeger-LeCoultre are run by different people. And maybe we’re doing better because we have a better system and a culture of collegiality[...].  And we have a pretty good idea where we’re going to be in three years’ time. That gives all of our colleagues a sense of purpose.”

Analysts had anticipated a decline in third-quarter growth, but the reality turned out worse than expected. LVMH reported a sales increase of 1% in Q3; Kering sales were down 1%. Meanwhile, Hermès sales were up 4.3%, the Prada brand reported sales up 1% and Miu Miu down 3%; the Moncler brand was down 1%; Burberry reported a 4% rise in second-quarter comparable store sales and the Zegna brand was up 8.2%.

“Richemont continues to be head and shoulders above the rest of the major luxury peers,” Bernstein’s Solca wrote in a note. “The recovery in profit margins on the back of lower gold prices and abating currency headwinds is particularly reassuring.”
