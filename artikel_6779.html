12 Todd Snyder Gifts That Even Menswear Snobs Will Appreciate

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

There’s a certain kind of guy who’s impossible to shop for. You know the one: he’s had the same pair of resoled boots since 2015, can tell you exactly why his Oxford shirt is better than yours, and has strong opinions about how wide a pant leg should be. Pleasing him might seem hopeless, but there’s a cheat code, and it’s called Todd Snyder. The brand has spent more than a decade quietly perfecting the American wardrobe: the sweaters, cords and jackets that look like they’ve been in his closet forever, only better.

Consider this your shortcut. I went shopping the way I always do and pulled the Todd Snyder gifts I’d be thrilled to unwrap. All that’s left for you to do is pick one and maybe hide the receipt, since he won’t be needing it. Fair warning: there’s a good chance you’ll end up buying something for yourself, too. ‘Tis the season.

A barn jacket gets an upgrade. Flocked denim gives it a velvety, suede-like surface, and the leather collar adds a nice bit of contrast. It’s rugged enough for a weekend upstate and polished enough for a swanky dinner in the city.

Cords are back in a big way, and it’s not just vintage obsessives wearing them: they’ve become the go-to cold-weather pant for guys who want something softer than denim. This straight leg camel pair is an easy way to add some warmth and texture to his wardrobe. He’ll wear them just like his favorite 501’s.

Every guy needs one great flannel, and this blue-and-cream plaid is it. It’s soft enough to wear on its own and sturdy enough to layer as a light jacket on milder days.

A cashmere sweater is the gift that never misses, and this rich red version is perfect for the holidays without screaming “Christmas sweater.” It’s the kind of thing he’ll pull on every December for years.

Colorful flecks of Donegal yarn take a simple black beanie somewhere much more interesting. It’s cashmere too, so he’ll actually want to wear it instead of stashing it away in a coat pocket in case of emergency.

Supple leather on the outside and cashmere on the inside: this is a cold-weather essential done right. It’s a grown-up upgrade over the gloves he’s been losing every winter since college.

An olive wool bomber with a plush shearling collar hits the sweet spot between a flight jacket and a proper winter coat. It works with everything from cords to tailored trousers. Bonus points since it will look better every season after some wear and tear..

For the guy who lives in dark denim, black cords are a smart switch. They’re just as versatile, much cozier, and dress up easier than you’d think.

Brushed cotton turns the camp collar into a fall shirt. The muted plaid and relaxed fit make it a great layering piece over a tee, or something to wear buttoned up on its own when the heat’s cranked indoors.

The waffle knit gives this navy sweater a depth and texture that a flat knit can’t match. It’s understated in the best way: the kind of piece that gets compliments without trying too hard.

Joshua Ellis has been weaving cloth in Yorkshire since the 18th century, and it shows. This bold red tartan is the coat accessory that does the most work, brightening up even the gloomiest January commute.

Pleats, a roomy leg and a flecked silk-Donegal fabric from Italy make this the dress pant for guys who don’t like dress pants. It’s sharp enough for the office party and relaxed enough to wear with a sweater the next morning.
