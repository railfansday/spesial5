7 Most Hopeless War Books of All Time, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Whether they’re on a screen or on a page, great war stories play a key role in educating the wider public about the horrific realities of armed conflict. Particularly in the 20th and 21st centuries, the lived experiences of the World Wars, the Vietnam War, and more have inspired some brilliant and bleak novels, many of which rank among the greatest works of literature ever written.

These novels span many genres, from tragicomedy to drama to science fiction, but what they all share is a grounded emotional and psychological realism that makes for a truly devastating read. They’re not easy, but they are inventive, intelligent, visceral, and above all honest, which is the quality that you want most of all in a story about humanity’s darkest chapters. So, without further ado, here’s our ranked selection of some of the most hopeless war books of all time.

Joseph Heller’s debut novel, which has been adapted multiple times, Catch-22 is a satirical war story set during World War II. The book uses a non-linear third-person narrative, exploring events from 1942 to 1944 from the perspectives of multiple characters. Primarily, it’s the story of Captain John Yossarian, a U.S. Army Air Forces bombardier based on the Mediterranean island of Pianosa who desperately tries to cling to his sanity while dealing with the relentless absurdities of war and military life.

Primarily a satire of military bureaucracy, Catch-22 derives its title from a bureaucratic fallacy: an insane person should not fly missions, but anyone who asks not to fly on grounds of insanity must be sane enough to fly them. The book explores how this rule, and other equally counterintuitive military reasoning, slowly grind away at a soldier’s sanity. It’s easily one of the most important war novels of all time and a decidedly bleak narrative; the only reason it doesn’t rank higher on this list is that the hopelessness is balanced by Heller’s wit and dark sense of humor.

Written by novelist Tim O'Brien and based on his experiences as a soldier in the Vietnam War, The Things They Carried is a collection of linked short stories revolving around a fictional platoon of American soldiers fighting in the war. A semi-autobiographical work of metafiction, the book explores personal stories of love, loss, and trauma as well as the importance of storytelling, especially when it’s the stories of the people you love. Though fictionalized, the story is inspired by real people and events that O’Brien encountered during the war.

The Things They Carried is a powerful book that blurs the lines of fiction and non-fiction but ultimately delivers a deep truth about life and war. Since the time it was first published in 1990, the collection has received near-universal critical acclaim and become an international bestseller. The book has won several accolades as well and was a finalist for the Pulitzer Prize and the National Book Critics Circle Award.

Kurt Vonnegut’s Slaughterhouse-Five, or, The Children's Crusade: A Duty-Dance with Death is a genre-blending anti-war novel partially inspired by Vonnegut’s own experiences and those of other people he knew who served in World War II. The narrative follows Billy Pilgrim, an American soldier who becomes unstuck in time and experiences his own life in a non-linear fashion after an encounter with fourth-dimensional aliens. Over the course of his life, Billy’s experiences, particularly the trauma of surviving the bombing of Dresden, drive him to adopt a fatalistic worldview as death loses all meaning for him.

One of the most acclaimed war novels and sci-fi books of all time, Slaughterhouse-Five is a highly original book that uses its metafictional narrative to process the author’s personal experiences. Like Billy Pilgrim, Vonnegut also survived the Dresden bombing, and the book is largely his attempt to finally put what he experienced into words. The novel was Vonnegut’s first bestseller when it came out in 1969 and has been highly acclaimed ever since. It’s also been banned in multiple places in the United States as recently as 2024.

Ernest Hemingway’s A Farewell to Arms is a novel set during World War I that follows the story of an American medic, Frederic Henry, who serves in the Italian Army. The book primarily explores Frederic’s romance with English nurse Catherine Barkley, which provides the sole light in his life as the war turns bloodier and more brutal than ever. Eventually, he leaves the army and seeks out Catherine, hoping to start a new life in Switzerland.

A Farewell to Arms is a truly tragic war romance, mainly because, unlike in many war novels, there is some hope to be found in the book: the hope of love and family that Frederic feels thanks to Catherine. However, by the end of the novel, that hope is cruelly taken away, not in a high-stakes battle but in a painfully normal, everyday manner that is all the more heartbreaking for its simplicity. A Farewell to Arms has been widely hailed as one of Hemingway’s greatest novels, and it has been adapted as films, a play, and a TV miniseries. In 2025, the novel entered the public domain and is now freely available online.

Written by Joe Haldeman, The Forever War isn’t a war book in the traditional sense: it’s a military science fiction novel that explores an interstellar war between humans and an alien civilization known as the Taurans. The book follows William Mandella, a physics student drafted into an elite task force to fight in the war using advanced weapons and near-lightspeed technology. Due to the relativistic nature of time, every time William returns to Earth, he finds a world that’s drastically different in its culture, language, and even biology, leading to an ever-increasing sense of alienation.

Published in 1974, The Forever War was highly acclaimed by critics and won multiple accolades, including the Nebula, Hugo, and Locus Awards. Despite its science fiction narrative, the book is inspired by Haldeman’s real experiences serving in the Vietnam War and is arguably more realistic than most historical books. It’s also unique in the sense that, while the story has what you could call a happy ending, it also delivers a bleak message about the inherent meaninglessness and alienation of war. The book was followed by three sequels: 1997’s Forever Peace, 1999’s Forever Free, and the 1999 novella, A Separate War.

A German semi-autobiographical novel by Erich Maria Remarque, All Quiet on the Western Front is one of the most famous war books ever written. The novel follows Paul Bäumer, a young man who, along with his classmates, enthusiastically signs up to fight in World War I. Over the course of the war, Bäumer learns firsthand about the horrors of war, the pain of loss, the alienation from civilian life, and the utter meaninglessness of the patriotic fervor that led him to join military service.

Since its publication in the late 1920s, All Quiet on the Western Front has been widely hailed as the greatest war novel of all time. The book is a bleak, painful, and necessary narrative that exposes the true cost of warfare on the individual psyche and soul — a message that got it banned and burned in Nazi Germany. Today, the novel is universally acknowledged as one of the greatest literary treasures of human civilization, and it has received multiple film adaptations, most recently Edward Berger's 2022 German adaptation, which won four Academy Awards.

Written by Dalton Trumbo, Johnny Got His Gun is a highly influential war novel that isn’t nearly as talked about as it ought to be. Set during World War I, the book is centered on Joe Bonham, a young American soldier who wakes up in a hospital bed and slowly realizes that he has lost all his limbs, eyes, ears, mouth, and nose. Trapped in his own body, his only hope is death, but even that is rendered impossible by military “regulations." All he can do is reminisce about his old life and hard-learned lessons about war.

Despite being an anti-war novel published during wartime, Johnny Got His Gun was quite acclaimed in 1939 and became a popular text among the American left of the time. The book has had a significant influence on American literature, shaping political discourse and literary craft with its stream-of-consciousness critique of war. Trumbo wrote and directed a film adaptation, released in 1971, which became a cult film, and the book has also inspired several other works of cinema and theatre, not to mention the Metallica song “One."
