Trump Treasury aims to slam shut banking loopholes that could enable fraud tied to illegal immigration

FIRST ON FOX: The Trump administration is moving to tighten financial safeguards it says could help stop illegal immigrants from exploiting the U.S. banking system, putting banks on notice to scrutinize business ownership more closely when warning signs emerge.

The proposed rule from the Treasury Department’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is part of President Donald Trump’s effort to address what his administration calls national security and public safety risks tied to illicit financial activity and people in the country illegally.

In short, the proposal would give banks more leeway to dig into who owns a business when they see potential red flags.

Trump’s executive order directs Treasury to strengthen customer due diligence. It says financial institutions should be able to seek information about immigration status and work authorization when relevant to assessing fraud or other illicit activity.

Under the proposal, covered institutions could obtain additional information about the beneficial owners of business customers to mitigate risk or address supervisory concerns. The rule would also end the requirement to reverify those owners each time an existing customer opens a new account and spell out what information banks must collect.

The proposal does not require banks to verify every customer’s immigration status. FinCEN says the changes are intended to combat money laundering and terrorist financing while cutting unnecessary compliance burdens.

"Financial institutions are on the front lines of protecting our financial systems from criminal organizations and cartels that target law-abiding businesses and workers," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said.

He said the proposal would give institutions more flexibility to combat risk and strengthen the fight against financial crime.

FinCEN is accepting public comments for 60 days after the proposal is published in the Federal Register.
