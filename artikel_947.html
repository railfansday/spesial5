Football Daily | Jürgen Klopp hard-launches his Germany rebuilding project in style

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Barely six weeks ago, Jürgen Klopp’s big home debut in the Germany dugout ended in a deeply frustrating 1-0 Nations League defeat to Greece – but the manager tasked with returning Die Mannschaft to relevance claimed not to care. “I’m completely unbothered,” Klopp trilled unconvincingly. “I’ve lost far more matches than I would have liked, but as long as you draw the right conclusions, a loss is ultimately a piece of information – no different from a win.” It was a quote straight out of the Pierre Sage playbook for unfussed continental coaches – and probably worried a few German FA suits, given Klopp had already threatened to quit at his own unveiling over potential media intrusion and criticism. “If you say tomorrow, ‘he’s [sh!t]’, tell me to my face and I’m out,” Klopp roared to trembling hacks in July. That imagined confrontation now looks unlikely to take place, because his Germany side are suddenly very good indeed.

If subsequent Nations League results – two wins over Serbia and a 0-0 draw in Greece – didn’t move the needle much, Monday’s 5-0 demolition of the Netherlands was different. This was the perfect night to hard-launch Klopp’s rebuilding project, with young talents pressing and passing relentlessly up front, forcing keeper Bart Verbruggen to gather his defenders for a chat in the Sunday League style. It didn’t help as Germany racked up four goals in 17 second-half minutes, leaving Klopp’s fellow big-name newcomer, Xavi Hernández, facing questions just days after they had smashed seven past Greece.

Klopp has packed his early squads with untested, overlooked talent and the approach is bearing fruit, with the newcomers Brajan Gruda and Derry Scherhant both on the scoresheet in Berlin. As an added bonus, he is also getting the best out of Florian Wirtz – a move that may achieve the unthinkable and make Klopp even more popular on Merseyside. “For at least the next three or four months, no one will be questioning the German national team,” Klopp cheered, showing a solid understanding of how international windows work. Gruda’s goal to make it 5-0 also edged Germany above their opponents on goal difference, meaning they will be seeded for the last eight, avoiding France and England.

Speaking of which, Klopp is not the only German coach riding a wave of vindication towards Euro 2028, flicking Vs at crowds of haters on the way past. Thomas Tuchel’s England side are further along in the process, and look more ready to land a major trophy than Germany – which, all in all, is a very weird sentence to write. While the mutual respect is strong between these two pulsing managerial minds, Tuchel has previously struggled in Klopp’s shadow, following the beloved coach at Mainz and Dortmund in the manner of a surly substitute teacher. Are their paths destined to cross again in a major final before long? It’s too early to say. But here’s one piece of information we can all agree on: Germany are back.
