Celtic defend head of security amid fan group tensions

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Celtic have defended their head of security and the action taken against the Green Brigade last season after the fan group declared that four supporters had been cleared over the incident that sparked a lengthy ban.

A statement from the ultras group stated the four had been “finally cleared of wrongdoing” over the incident, which took place during a game against Falkirk on October 25 last year.

The group accused Celtic of “smearing” them with their statement on November 7 last year, which outlined an indefinite ban on about 250 supporters that eventually lasted nearly six months.

Celtic had written that about 100 Green Brigade members took part in an “orchestrated” attempt to prevent an individual being arrested following an incident with a steward and that the large group “verbally abused and assaulted the steward and police officers”.

A Green Brigade statement read: “Not guilty pleas were accepted by both Procurator Fiscal and sheriff for breach of the peace charges before the trial commenced.

“The four supporters cleared were subjected to dawn raids, damage to homes and harassment of family members including confiscation of personal devices, this included a vulnerable adult who was left alone in the cold with no means to contact anyone after the front door was removed.”

The group called for Celtic to apologise for the collective ban and to lift suspensions on two supporters who remain banned over their interaction with the club’s head of security, Mark Hargreaves, at last year’s abandoned annual general meeting.

“This week’s outcome represents significant vindication for all supporters concerned in these events over the past year,” they added.

“It also calls into question the credibility of Mark Hargreaves’ subsequent allegations against two Green Brigade representatives.

“We maintain that Mark Hargreaves has introduced a poisonous security culture to Celtic Park and does not hold the trust of supporters whose safety he is supposed to be responsible for. His position is untenable.”

Celtic pointed out they play no part in criminal proceedings and added that they were not aware all had been completed. And they stated that the outcome of the four cases did not alter their assessment of the incident.

A statement added: “The conduct of the group witnessed that day was unsafe and completely unacceptable – it has no place at Celtic Park or anywhere in football. The club took reasonable and proportionate steps to deal with this conduct.

“Such was the seriousness of the incident that the club was called to attend a special meeting of the Safety Advisory Group of Glasgow City Council.”

Celtic accused the Green Brigade of continuing a “campaign of personal harassment and abuse” towards former senior police officer Hargreaves.

They added: “Mark is a highly-qualified, dedicated professional who plays a crucial role at the club in ensuring the safety of our supporters.

“Where conduct presents an unacceptable risk to that safety or demonstrates an unwillingness to co-operate with it, the club has to take appropriate and proportionate action.”
