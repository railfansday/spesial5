18 Family Resorts That Go Above and Beyond for Those With Babies and Toddlers

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

I've traveled to some pretty far-flung places, but nothing could have prepared me for the logistical circus of vacationing with a baby. Four years and 30 countries later, my son Julian has taught me a thing or two about what makes a resort genuinely welcoming to its littlest guests. For starters, there’s a massive difference between a hotel that hands your kid a coloring book at check-in and one that has anticipated the thousands of little things that can make or break a family vacation.

The best resorts for infants and toddlers also understand that keeping littles happy isn’t a one-size-fits-all proposition. A baby might need a quiet nursery and a reliable nanny; a three-year-old might be thrilled to feed goats on an Austrian farm, explore a Japanese forest, or make plaster casts of lion tracks in the Kalahari. And parents? Sometimes we just want to eat dinner without having to negotiate with a tiny inquisitor.

That kind of thoughtfulness is what sets the properties on this list apart. I’ve seen it firsthand at more than half a dozen of them, where the greatest luxury wasn’t necessarily the thread count or the size of the swimming pool, but getting to enjoy a five-star vacation instead of merely financing one for your offspring. Read on for the best resorts for infants and toddlers.

Location: Savusavu, FijiPrice: From $1,136 per night

Jet lag aside, this is one of the rare luxury resorts where traveling with a child feels not just doable, but deeply supported. Every family with a child under five is assigned two dedicated nannies—one for daytime adventures, one for evenings—so parents can book a snorkeling trip with the resident marine biologist or linger over a blissfully kid-free dinner. When my son and I arrived in 2024—the first international stop on our 88-day, 14-country trip—it took me all of four minutes to abandon any guilt I had about dropping him off. His nanny was warm, attentive, and completely unfazed by toddlers being toddlers; she whisked him away for glass-bottom boat rides, hermit crab hunts, and splashes in the resort’s “mushroom-cascade” wading pool.

Parents of even younger children will appreciate the made-to-order purées and unlimited milk supply; nurseries stocked with infant cots, toys, and baby baths; and daily laundry service and disposable diapers for sale in the on-site boutique. Following a spring 2026 renovation, the resort now has five two-bedroom bures (a traditional Fijian hut or cabin), giving families more room to spread out. The Bula Club—essentially a resort within a resort—has a sun-safe junior pool with a waterslide, trampoline, and tricycles.

Location: Flamands Beach, St. BartsPrice: From $805 per night

With 61 rooms, suites, and villas, Cheval Blanc is one of the glammest maisons on an island full of glammy maisons. The inn has even partnered with high-end Parisian baby accessories brand Baudou to spoil its style-conscious guests. Cots are dressed in the finest linens, and rooms include a changing table with a diaper basket and baby care products, a rattan high chair with a cushioned seat, baby-friendly crockery, and a hand-stitched bib embroidered with seahorses and stars. Wee beach towels and a selection of beach toys are available for afternoon romps on Flamands, one of the prettiest powder-white beaches in St. Barts. At the new Le Carrousel play space, children ages 3 to 12 can climb aboard an imaginary exploration vessel, tumble into a coconut-themed ball pit, and discover the island’s Arawak history through interactive storytelling. Come mealtime, they can indulge in made-to-order baby purées prepared by the in-house culinary team. Upon request, a professional photographer can be brought to the premises to document your family trip.

Location: West End, AnguillaPrice: From $725 per night

Toddlers are greeted with custom Anguilla-themed stuffies, pint-sized bathrobes, and slippers at this upscale resort on Anguilla’s West End, where accommodations range from contemporary-chic guest rooms to five-bedroom beachfront villas, some with private pools and kitchens. Wherever you rest your head, expect kids to squeal when they catch a glimpse of the iguanas that freely roam the property. The zero-entry Bamboo Pool has mini loungers for infants; other five-star amenities include complimentary cribs, strollers, car seats, bottle sterilizers, baby tubs, Le Petit Prince toiletries, SlumberPods, and organic puréed baby food on the room service menu. A refreshed splash pad offers another place for little ones to cool off, while children five and under eat free from the kids menu throughout the resort. Also notable: American Airlines operates year-round nonstop flights from Miami, while AnguillAir offers seasonal service from Boston, Newark, and Baltimore, so families no longer have to hitch a ride over from St. Maarten.

Location: Karatu, TanzaniaPrice: From $518 per adult per night, including full board

After a long-haul flight with a toddler, Gibb’s Farm feels like an exhale. Set on 80 acres of organic gardens and coffee groves on the forested outer slopes of the Ngorongoro Crater, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the property is an ideal place to begin or end a safari. My son was only 20 months old when we stayed here, but we happily spent hours roaming the gardens, chasing chickens, and learning how to milk cows. The farm’s one-bedroom cottages—many decorated with pieces from Gibb’s unique artist-in-residence program—are ideal for families, offering private terraces, outdoor showers, and enough breathing room for mid-tantrum resets.

There’s no kids club, but toddlers are warmly embraced here. The staff arranged a private game drive for us so we could move at Julian’s pace, stopping for snack breaks and diaper changes as needed. In the crater—home to one of the densest populations of large mammals in Africa—we saw lions, hippos, warthogs, wildebeest, and flamingos, but it was the dazzle of zebras milling around our vehicle that most delighted my son. Back on the farm, little ones can collect eggs, feed the pigs, wander among the coffee shrubs, splash in the pool, or learn about the music and customs of the local Iraqw tribe through hands-on cultural activities.

Location: Palm Beach, FloridaPrice: From $890 per night

Florida is a perennially popular destination for young families, and no resort rolls out the red carpet for babies better than the Breakers Palm Beach. Founded in 1896, the luxury hotel has a private beach and four oceanfront pools, including a zero-entry one designed specifically for youngsters. There’s also a 4,500-square-foot playground with slides, tunnels, and bridges, plus the new Seaside Family Entertainment Center, a beachfront hub with a dedicated Toddler Cove, arts and crafts, and interactive games. Cribs, infant baths, bottle warmers, and strollers are available upon request, as is the childproofing of guest rooms, which involves padding perilously edged furniture, covering electrical sockets, and removing trash bags from cans. Bicycles with baby seats and children’s bikes are available to rent, as are private poolside bungalows with a dedicated concierge standing by, ready to fetch popsicles and pool toys at a moment’s notice. Other big wins for tiny visitors: poolside spin art, Build-A-Bear workshops, and daily children’s programming.

Location: Punta Cana, Dominican RepublicPrice: From $850 per night

For parents seeking a more home-like environment, the multi-bedroom villas at this secluded Relais & Chateaux property offer spacious living rooms, full kitchens, and private pools. Staff arrange scavenger hunts and arts and crafts hours; for an additional $325, parents can reserve the VIBabies package. This comes with two hours of complimentary babysitting plus a host of handy gear—including a portable crib (with turndown service!), a changing table, diapers, a bottle warmer, a baby monitor, an infant tub, pool alarms, a Baby Einstein play table, a 4moms MamaRoo baby swing, and a basket of kid-approved snacks and juices. For fun in the sun, Eden Roc will throw in a pop-up beach playpen, swim nappies, and sun protection gear (a bucket hat, colored sunglasses, Nivea sun lotion, and aloe vera gel) for use at the sandy beach and artificial lagoon.

Location: Nuevo Nayarit, MexicoPrice: From $454 per adult per night

Families vacationing at this AAA Five Diamond resort on the Riviera Nayarit are assigned a baby concierge who coordinates all meals and nanny services. Whatever gear you don’t want to drag from home—crib, stroller, baby tub and toiletries, bottle brush and sterilizer, pacifier, floaties—the team can arrange for on-site, all complimentary. Parents checking in with infants also receive a sleep guide developed with pediatric sleep consultant Rachael Shepard-Ohta of Hey Sleepy Baby, with tips for managing time zone changes and maintaining bedtime routines on the road. The kids club is stocked with books, blocks, and a play kitchen; parents may additionally request a crib mobile, rattle, or teether for use in the room.

Some suites have private plunge pools, but the hotel jewel is the triple-tiered, multi-temperature infinity pool. Mini cabanas await down at the beach, along with hooded baby towels and fresh-squeezed juice. Young spa-goers can opt for a gentle sunrise massage soundtracked by Mozart or a chocolate explosion face mask, then strike a pose with mom or dad during a baby yoga class. Executive chef Guillaume Morancé and his team prepare gourmet purées to challenge young palates (think white fish with celery, leeks, and mushroom) at every meal. The resort even offers a Mi Primer Fiesta package for celebrating a baby's first birthday, complete with piñatas and foam.

Location: Koh Yao Yai, ThailandPrice: From $308 per night

Set on one of the Andaman’s last unspoiled islands—reachable only by boat and blissfully free of traffic—this resort feels worlds away from the party throngs on Phuket and Krabi. When Julian and I visited in 2024, it was during a vintage sidecar tour through rubber plantations and rice fields that I realized how special this island really is: no honking, no rush, just villagers waving as you pass. The accommodations were a slam dunk too: Family suites with bunk beds and tipi play tents come stocked with baby tubs, bottle warmers, sterilizers, baby chairs, and toys. The Grand Family Suite ups the ante with a chocolate treasure box at check-in, while all families with children receive a treasure map. Ground-floor suites open directly onto the resort’s enormous family pool, while infants and early swimmers have dedicated splash zones and shallow areas. As a bonus, the staff truly adores kids—one affable pool attendant peeled off his socks and shoes and joined my son on the slides when he saw how happy it made him.

Pristine beach and pools aside, the resort is home to southern Thailand’s largest kids club, featuring climbing walls, a dance stage, and an epic ball pit. Parents can join kids under six for Junior Explorer activities like kite making, batik painting, and sandcastle building, or arrange a family excursion to go island-hopping by longtail boat. For something even more tailored, little ones can try Muay Thai lessons, aerial yoga, or beach fishing with locals.

Location: Beaver Creek, ColoradoPrice: From $275 per night

Ask anyone who has ever attempted skiing with a toddler—it takes an act of Congress just to get them into a snowsuit. What saved my sanity at Park Hyatt Beaver Creek was that the slopes were, quite literally, outside our front door. This 189-room, chateau-style property is one of only two true ski-in, ski-out hotels at the base of Beaver Creek Mountain Resort, and that convenience is priceless. I could bundle up my kiddo and hand him off to a private instructor at the Ski and Snowboard School (open to ages three and up) and know that he was in eternally patient hands. After his post-lesson hot chocolates, Julian burned off whatever energy he had left in the family game room and the snowy outdoor playground before splashing around in the heated pool and hot tubs—his preferred après.

Location: Palm Desert, CaliforniaPrice: From $348 per night, including a $45 daily resort fee

This 450-acre resort features a 12,000-square-foot arcade—the largest hotel arcade in California. This is great news for kids of all ages who are stoked to play retro video games, test their skills in high-tech simulators, or try their hand at duckpin bowling. Family by JW programming gets the whole gang together with seasonal arts and crafts, fish feedings, movie nights, and more. Tots will also love meeting the property’s flock of Chilean flamingos, practicing their putt-putt on the 18-hole miniature golf course, and ordering off the children’s menus available at all on-site dining outlets. Families can also hop aboard boats departing from the hotel lobby for a cruise along the resort’s waterways.

Location: Chicago, IllinoisPrice: From $129 per night

The Loews Loves Families initiative, available at 27 properties throughout North America, is one of the most baby-friendly hotel offerings around. Guest rooms are equipped with mini fridges and kettles for storing and sanitizing bottles; full-size cribs, baby tubs, and night-lights are complimentary. Housekeeping will cover electrical outlets and supply foam bumpers upon request, and kids are given a cute amenity kit with baby lotion, wipes, shampoo, conditioner, and body wash. Board games, coloring books, pool toys, and iPads preloaded with movies and games are available from the concierge desk, as is a family activity guide highlighting the best local options for museums, zoos, and kid-friendly restaurants. There’s even a Bedtime Book Cart to help parents maintain their routine while on the road.

Location: Sagres, PortugalPrice: From $149 per night

At Martinhal Sagres, a stylish beachfront resort tucked inside a protected natural park in Portugal’s Algarve, the littlest guests get the VIP treatment. A baby concierge stocks rooms with cribs, bottle sterilizers, high chairs, and other essentials before families arrive—sparing parents the frustration of hauling half the nursery overseas. Even better, the resort’s supervised kids clubs welcome infants as young as six months, with separate programs for crawling babies, curious toddlers, and older children. Accommodations range from contemporary hotel suites to spacious villas with kitchens and washing machines; other perks include five swimming pools, sandy playgrounds, and restaurants offering dedicated baby menus. The Baby's First Holiday package sweetens the deal with a couples massage and romantic dinner—babysitting included.

Location: Oklahoma City, OklahomaPrice: From $149 per night, inclusive of water park access for two people

Operated by the Chickasaw Nation, this 404-room property blends big-ticket family fun with a strong sense of place. When Julian and I visited, it was the cultural aspect that surprised us most—especially the Chickasaw-curated gallery and easy access to the Smithsonian-affiliated First Americans Museum next door, which recently debuted the fully immersive FAMily Discovery Center. Outside, the 4.5-acre lagoon complex boasts the fourth-largest pool in the country—complete with zip-line plunges, an inflatable obstacle course, and an adults-only swim-up bar for parents who need a moment of respite. Inside, the 100,000-square-foot water park is kept at a blissful 82 degrees and features a low tide pool with pint-sized splash structures that didn’t overwhelm my budding swimmer.

There’s an arcade, mirror maze, and candy store, plus a slew of specialty eateries serving the four main food groups for toddlers: hot dogs, pizza, ice cream, and tacos. On Friday and Saturday evenings, there’s even a storytelling Pajama Hour led by resort staff at the lobby fireplace, with bedtime attire encouraged. Families will appreciate the value here: Children under two stay free, and spacious suites offer bunk beds and semi-private sleeping areas, with cribs available on request.

Location: Puerto Morelos, MexicoPrice: From $420 per night

As far as resorts for families go, Sensira, an all-inclusive luxury resort on the Riviera Maya, sure knows how to make a kid feel special. The Baby Explorer Club, designed for ages 18 months through 3 years, has indoor slides, bounce houses, and a ball pit, plus supervised sensory activities, music, and storytelling. For families with older kids in tow, there’s a movie theater, miniature golf course, zip line, water park, basketball court, tennis court, climbing wall, gaming stations, indoor trampoline park, and two swimming pools (there's a third pool designated for adults only).

The hotel can prepare fresh purées on request for infants and toddlers and has a kids corner at the buffet with food displayed at little ones’ eye level. It also supplies all the equipment parents need for a hassle-free stay, including bottle warmers and sterilizers, full-size cribs, and car seats. Babysitting services are available from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. daily, and parents are given a beeper to stay in touch. (If an infant is still breastfeeding, the nanny will fetch a parent for mealtime.) Even the spa takes family well-being into consideration, offering a full slate of all-ages wellness programming, including gentle therapies for children and teens that mirror adult rituals, and purpose-built family treatment cabins.

Location: Crete, GreecePrice: From $199 per night

Phāea Cretan Malia is one of those rare design hotels that’s as thoughtfully geared toward families with babies and toddlers as it is toward style-hungry adults. The low-rise, modernist buildings sit in lush, shady gardens that keep the grounds cool even in peak summer heat. When Julian and I stayed here in 2024, we spent hours pinballing from the sandy, shallow beach to the swimming pools to the Tiny Explorers’ Club—a padded, sunlit playroom for ages three and under where parents can spend time with their little ones.

For ages 3–12, the Little Explorers Kids Club has its own kitchen for baking sessions, a mini stage for dance and theater, an environmental play area, an organic garden to tend, and an outdoor playground—a godsend when little ones have energy to burn. Parents can also request the Baby Package, available for an additional charge, which includes babysitting services and toddler gear to cut down on packing. And while the open-air restaurants serve gorgeous Cretan dishes built around local produce, they’re equally prepared for picky palates: think wood-fired pizzas, simple pastas, and fresh fruit. When you’re ready to venture out, Potamos Beach and the Minoan ruins of Malia Palace, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, are an easy walk away.

Location: Southern Kalahari, South AfricaPrice: From around $7,000 per night for two adults, all-inclusive

Taking a toddler on safari sounds like a terrible idea until you discover Tswalu, South Africa’s largest privately protected conservation area, in the malaria-free Kalahari. At Loapi, six stand-alone safari homes with one or two bedrooms have private plunge pools, chefs, house managers, and dedicated guides, trackers, and vehicles—meaning no one cares if your child talks incessantly through a lion sighting. (Mine certainly did.)

When we visited in August 2026, Julian raced our guide and tracker through the red dunes, dictated his pasta preferences to the chef, and played catch with his child minder while I squeezed in some work. The flexibility is extraordinary: Game drives can be tailored around naps and short attention spans while older siblings can learn archery and animal tracking through the Junior Ranger program. Other highlights include close-up encounters with habituated meerkats and some of the best stargazing I've experienced anywhere. We also spotted black-maned lions, cheetahs, wild dogs, and desert black rhinos, but some of Julian’s happiest moments were spent chasing a ball with the staff. For families seeking a (slightly) less extravagant safari, Tswalu’s Motse camp offers one- and two-bedroom suites and shared facilities; its five-bedroom Tarkuni homestead is another option for multigenerational groups of up to 10.

Location: Atami, Shizuoka Prefecture, JapanPrice: From $238 per night

Less than an hour by bullet train from Tokyo, Risonare Atami sits high above a Japanese seaside town famous for its hot springs and fireworks festivals. All 81 rooms overlook Sagami Bay, with some offering front-row views of the town’s fireworks displays. The resort’s Baby-Friendly Stay Plan outfits spacious rooms with cribs and other infant necessities, while the restaurants serve complimentary rice porridge and vegetable purées. Parents can even hand off children as young as six months to an in-house nursery for up to 150 minutes of complimentary childcare while they tuck into a multicourse kaiseki dinner at the hotel’s restaurant, Hanabi.

Little ones have a heated indoor pool with a shallow splash zone and an ocean-view playroom with a ball pit and toddler-friendly climbing wall. Outdoors, Kusu Kusu centers on a tree house built into a 300-year-old camphor tree, with forest treasure hunts for children ages two and up and a treetop obstacle course for older kids. Parents can also steal away to the resort's semi-open-air onsen overlooking the bay.

Location: Grossarl, AustriaPrice: From $669 per night, including full board and childcare

Part luxury Alpine retreat, part working organic farm, Moar Gut is a five-star family resort where children can feed goats, cuddle rabbits, and even “adopt” an animal to care for during their stay. Set on a sprawling car-free estate in Austria’s Grossarl Valley, the 46-suite property takes its back-to-nature philosophy seriously. Its 10,000-square-foot kids club is built from natural materials like wood, linen, and sheep’s wool, with a painting studio, woodworking workshops, and guided forest adventures. Even babies get the full Alpine treatment: The separate nursery, offering supervised care from 30 days old, has Swiss stone pine cribs, organic bedding, and hay-covered walls. At the Lillee Baby Spa, parents can book guided infant massage, yoga, or floating sessions starting at three weeks old. Older children can ride ponies, forage for alpine herbs, and bake traditional Austrian treats, while parents have an adults-only spa and six-course farm-to-table dinners to look forward to. There's even a dedicated organic baby buffet with fresh purées and formula.

What is the best destination to travel to with an infant and/or toddler?

For a first trip with a baby or toddler, destinations with manageable flight times, reliable medical care, and resorts that minimize the logistical headaches of traveling with little ones are ideal. Florida, Mexico, and the Caribbean are excellent starting points, with direct flights from many US cities, and resorts that cater specifically to young children. Four Seasons Resort and Residences Anguilla is one standout, offering complimentary baby equipment, a zero-entry family pool, and a splash pad. For families ready to venture farther, Portugal's Algarve is another excellent choice, especially Martinhal Sagres, where supervised childcare starts at six months. In the Austrian Alps, Moar Gut goes a step further, welcoming infants as young as 30 days into its professionally staffed nursery.

Are there any good all-inclusive resorts for infants and toddlers?

Absolutely. Jean-Michel Cousteau Resort in Fiji is particularly impressive, with meals, activities, and dedicated nanny care included in the rate. Children under six are assigned two nannies who work opposite shifts, providing personalized care throughout the day and evening, while older children are paired with Fijian companions who guide them through age-appropriate activities. Closer to home, Grand Velas Riviera Nayarit in Mexico features gourmet dining, a baby concierge who arranges infant equipment and coordinates meals and childcare, and specialty purées for its youngest guests (babysitting costs extra).

What resort in the US is best for families with infants and toddlers?

The Breakers Palm Beach in Florida is hard to beat. Its private beach and zero-entry family pool are ideal for little swimmers, while a 4,500-square-foot playground and the Seaside Family Entertainment Center—with its dedicated Toddler Cove—give youngsters room to burn off energy. The hotel can also supply cribs, strollers, and bottle warmers, as well as childproof guest rooms upon request. Parents, meanwhile, get the restaurants, spa, and old-school Palm Beach glamour that have made The Breakers a fixture on luxury travel lists for generations.
