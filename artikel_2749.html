Inside the Suns: Ryan Dunn, Koa Peat, a wake up call?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Welcome to Inside the Suns, a deep down analysis of the current Phoenix Suns team in which the Fantable — a round table of Bright Siders — give their takes on the Suns' latest issues and news.

GuarGuar: I've been really impressed with Ryan so far this season. He's showing more control and poise in his game. He's not recklessly driving at 100 mph to the rim every time he touches the ball. The jump shot mechanics look better, and he's been impressive as a roll man this year. He's been a pleasant surprise.

zenzino: I love it! How could I not? As I wrote in a previous Fantable (or thread, I can't remember), Dunn was considered by many (including me) to be the odd man out for rotational minutes this season. However, he became the poster child for the fact that progress is not linear. After a sophomore year where the improvement was slight (especially alongside Oso's improvement), Dunn has made a leap in his third year.

We never questioned his defense. He reportedly put in a lot of work on his shot this off-season. As he said himself, he 'has to trust in the work'. This becomes evident during a game. When he has time to think about it, he'll either pass up a shot or look more like last year's shooting. When he doesn't have time to think, his shot looks really good and goes in more often than not.

The more he works, and the less time he has to think, the shots will go down, and he'll gain enough confidence to think and shoot.

This is also a lesson not to give up on Sheer. He may have a sophomore slump this season, but that doesn't mean he's not working and could come out later in the season or his third year to make a Dunn-like leap.

Ashton: I am a little curious why Dunn's minutes seem to vary by game. He is clearly the next man up at the power forward position. But, thus far, I have not paid enough attention to Dunn on the Fantable and he is impressing me as a future breakout candidate to start. So, my bad on not giving Ryan Dunn more love early.

Also, Brooks said he would be willing to play bench minutes? Jaw hits the floor as he is coming off one of his best games against the Sacramento Kings. He stated that is to work on his playmaking with the better spacing that the bench provides. I applaud his heroic statement, but I am calling BS. The real reason is so he can terrorize opposing bench unit defenses into therapy with his constant chirping. Still, an intriguing thought to consider. Does Ott buy-in? I doubt it.

That would make Ryan Dunn the starter for minutes if that were to come true. Some of the Suns' lineups would be rife for consideration in the comment sections.

OldAz: Personally, I love what Ryan has done this year. He has taken advantage of the lowered expectations, especially compared to last season when he was expected to come in and capture a larger role only to struggle with the new system and get stuck behind a number of veteran guards.

This season, most had written him off coming into the season and were fine throwing him into whatever trade would accomplish moving whatever core player that fan wanted to move. Instead, he ratcheted up his defensive intensity and the speed at which he plays and has made a significant impact.  My only complaint is with the coaching staff that seems to have way too long of a leash for veteran players to play emotionless, effortless basketball and only give a player like Ryan a few minutes despite their obvious positive impact on the overall result on the floor.

Ryan is not going to fill up the stat sheet, but he is going to hustle on defense, compete for rebounds, and is willing to shoot or drive when he is left open.  He is a perfect complement to Booker or any of the other "star" players on the court when Ott chooses to play him.  He is one of those players (along with Goodwin, Gillespie, and Kennard) that you can pair with either center and 2 of the core starters and get a pretty decent lineup because of their hustle and energy. The only difference is he is the only one of those 4 that also brings some length to the court.

I'm not overly worried about the recent little injuries, as I believe he has figured out that his energy and effort mean more than the stats he puts up each night. Hopefully Ott is starting to figure out the same thing.

Rod: I hated to see him get hurt in the Jazz game. Hopefully. he won't be out long because he's become an important part of the Suns' rotation. His offensive impact hasn't been great, but he's been playing more under control on that end and making positive contributions, both inside (5th most dunks on the team in just the 10th most minutes per game) and outside (42.3% from three). His biggest areas of impact have been on defense and in rebounding (per 36, he's tied with Bridges for 2nd at 8.6).

I'll admit that I was one of the people who was almost ready to give up on Ryan this season if he didn't show some improvement. Happily, he's come through for us and is playing very well and under control.

GuarGuar: I really hope it was a wake-up call. This starting lineup has been awful together, and the metrics don't lie. The Jalen Green PG experiment has been an abject failure, and the sooner we move off of it, the better we will be. The bench plays together as a unit and with a ton of energy, the complete opposite of our starting unit.

zenzino: I guess? How many wake-up calls do they need?

They supposedly had one in the first game of the season. The first game!

They definitely looked better against Denver and really came out against the Kings with aggressiveness, energy, and surprisingly good ball movement. It was the Kings, but maybe playing a few easier games in a row (Jazz & Pels next) will allow them to get some confidence/momentum.

Recent reports amidst all the trade rumors/ideas say that the Suns are not shopping Green. They like the roster they have and are content to give these guys time to gel. I don't know if it has anything to do with the benching, but we're definitely seeing some improvement.

It occurred to me the other day that it is the fans' job to (over)react from game to game and the coaches/FO's job NOT to react from game to game.

Ashton: I hate this question. Beat LA, Beat LA!

The fact of the matter is that the Lakers are that good. It was a nice salve to the wounds to see the Suns beat a good Nuggets team the day after. Which is exactly what the Suns need: beating the teams they are supposed to beat, but picking up a few wins against the teams that are not (Houston and Denver). The Lakers were not on the bingo card in the W category.

Still, Ott probably wisely threw in the towel against a very good Lakers squad and allowed his starters to watch real basketball from the sidelines without getting injured. I was fine with it from a strategic point of view, but I am not fine with it from a fan point of view.

OldAz: Maybe. It certainly couldn't have hurt as we have seen a better version of Brooks and Green since then, with more focus on the defensive end and moving the ball on offense instead of always forcing their own shots. I think Booker and Bridges were already playing well, but it really requires all the starters to be on the same page for that group to succeed.  The better play and effort from all has also allowed Ott to play a little more randomly with lineups.

Now the question is if the recent successes are more about this improved play and more diverse lineup combinations, or just a product of playing lesser teams that the Suns should handle like the Kings and Jazz.

Rod: You could call it that, or you could call it a message from Ott, "Play with some heart or you'll sit and watch your teammates show you what that means." Whatever you want to call it, it seems to have worked in the games since then.

You can have all the talent in the world, but without effort, players are just wasting the fans' time (and patience) when they're just going through the motions. I don't want to see the starters playing like that again, and hopefully they got that message from Ott loud and clear…and won't forget it.

GuarGuar: Koa is probably only useful as a small-ball 5 right now until he develops a jump shot, which looks to be a ways away. He has good instincts and can finish through contact pretty well, but he's not close to being ready for a consistent rotation spot in my opinion. Next year will probably be a different discussion.

zenzino: I think it's pretty telling that he has spent zero time with the Valley Suns. My wild speculation is that Ott wants him with the team and ready to play.

After looking great in quality minutes against the Lakers, Ott teased us with a 2nd-quarter appearance of Peat against the Nuggets. That lasted a minute. Literally. The next time we saw him was garbage time against the hapless Kings.

When this question was first sent to us at the beginning of the week, my answer was that Koa should definitely get some Brooks minutes. Or even some Bridges minutes. But mostly Brooks.

Up through the Lakers game, Brooks has been a shell of what we loved about him last season. Maybe, like cutting Sampson's hair (and Dillon's hair), Dillon's effort to not get techs this season has stripped him of his strength.

He was better against the Nuggets (got a tech) and was on fire against the Kings (no tech). Hopefully, he has turned a corner and will continue with the aggressive Villain we know and love. Techs and all.

Bridges is showing progress with every game, and we are beginning to see what the FO envisioned with this addition. 14 games is not even close to enough games for a starter to fully integrate. As I said, the FO has to take the long view and not overreact game to game.

So, barring injuries or a slide by either Brooks or Bridges, we will probably only see Koa in very specific matchup situations or garbage time. That's ok for now. There's a lot to learn from just being with the team. Especially if they continue the winning trend.

Ashton: How to answer this one? Peat's best game came against the Lakers. At least the Arizona guy gets it. But in the end, stats do not lie on small sample sizes. I am stealing a copy and paste from Hoops Nightly:

Peat has appeared in 8 games and averages 4.5 minutes, 3.1 points, 0.9 rebounds and 0.1 assists. He is shooting 64.7 percent from the field and 100.0 percent at the line, with a 0.0 mark from three. His true shooting is 68.2 percent and his usage is 21.1. The plus-minus is 3.8. These numbers come from very few minutes, so they say little yet. His minutes have ranged from 1.3 to 8.2 in a night, and he has not scored in three of the eight games.

Okay, with that out of the way (I hate talking stats – leave that to the board members), the stats still suggest that Peat's minutes should increase if only to increase the sample size. And consistent minutes would probably help rather than 8 minutes here and a throw-in one minute there.

Since the question asked if he "looks" ready for more rotational minutes, I would state an emphatic yes until proven otherwise.

OldAz: In small doses, certainly.  Just like we saw in the Jazz game, he needs to learn to play at this level without fouling, but that isn't going to happen at the G League level or during garbage minutes when the refs also just want to get the game over.

I think he should get about the same 5-6 minutes Dunn was getting earlier in the season, and given an opportunity to play more as he does well. To me, these minutes should be coming from Brooks, Green, Bridges, and even Booker, as up until the last few games the bench minutes have been better anyway, so Ott might as well mix and match more minutes with just 2 of those core 4 on the floor with a center and 2 bench players. Go ahead and compress the starters' minutes while this is working, save more strain on the starters' legs until later in the season and see who actually develops.

I know this will never happen, but the Suns really have to come up with a better way to give minutes to young players at the NBA level and start developing them faster than they have been.

Rod: Peat does look ready for more playing time, but for now, I think most of that time will come when Ott decides to play a small-ball lineup, so I don't think he'll be taking minutes away from anyone specifically. I've seen Ott try various players out in that role, and Koa just seems to be the best fit in that role. And considering the guy's built like a linebacker, I'm not at all certain that we should even call it "small" ball when he's at the 5.

The three quick fouls he got in the Jazz game don't change my mind. He may not be ready for a full-blown rotation spot, but he's ready for spot rotation minutes.

On November 23, 1972, Charlie Scott recorded the Phoenix Suns' first-ever triple-double with 42 points, 10 rebounds, and 15 assists in a 128-122 victory over the Detroit Pistons on Thanksgiving Day.

On November 23, 1994, Wesley Person hit for 19 points to lead 10 double-figure scorers in Phoenix's 140-109 home win over the LA Clippers. The Suns became the third team in NBA history to have 10 different players score in double figures in the same game. No Suns player shot less than 50% from the field that day, and the team FG percentage was 64.7% while also hitting 47.1% of their three-point attempts.

On November 23, 2015, Devin Booker got his first career start for the Phoenix Suns, filling in for an injured Eric Bledsoe. Booker had 4 points, 1 assist, 4 rebounds, and 1 steal in his debut in the Suns' starting lineup as Phoenix fell to the San Antonio Spurs 98-84.

On November 24, 1989, the Suns reserves were outscored 16-0 to start the 4th quarter, so "only" up 28 points, head coach Cotton Fitzsimmons brought back his starters, allowing Jeff Hornacek to record the first triple-double of his career (22pts, 12 rebs, 15assists) in a 121-94 win over the Orlando Magic.

On November 25, 1995, down 110-100 with 90 seconds left, the Suns nailed 4 three-pointers and, after Nick Van Exel inexplicably allowed himself to be tied up with 6.7 seconds left, Michael Finley hit a buzzer-beater to defeat the LA Lakers, 114-113.

On November 27, 1977, Paul Westphal set a new franchise and career-high when he scored 48 points in a 115-97 win over the Denver Nuggets. Westphal was on fire all night, shooting 20-30 from the field and 8-10 FTs. When John MacLeod pulled him with 3:17 left in the game, fans began to chant, "We want Westy."
