Dan Campbell sends Lions clear celebration message after Aidan Hutchinson injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

With the Lions losing star edge rusher Aidan Hutchinson for the season in their 35-10 overseas win over the Patriots, head coach Dan Campbell stood firm on his stance regarding player celebrations.

Hutchinson's season came to an unfortunate end after a sack on Patriots quarterback Drake Maye in the first quarter; the five-year veteran landed awkwardly after jumping in the air in celebration and tore his ACL in the process.

During Tuesday's appearance on Detroit's 97.1 The Ticket, Campbell verified the defensive star's injury status and how it will affect an already depleted defense.

Detroit Lions defensive end Aidan Hutchinson (97) sits on the field after an injury during a game in Munich, Germany, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. AP Photo/Matthias Schrader

"What a gut punch, just because … he's such a phenomenal player," Campbell said. "This guy's an impact player, but more importantly, he's an unbelievable teammate, and he's one of our captains.

"It just stinks, man. It hits you. And I hate it for him, man, because he was on a heater. What a hell of a year he was having. He's just improved every year and to come off the injury he had [in 2024]. But I also know this, man: this guy will overcome this and he will come back better than he was, too. It's just the way he is, it's the way he's wired, and he'll come out better for this."

Regarding whether the celebration caused the injury, Campbell played it off as a freak accident.

"No, I don't believe that," Campbell said. "I think what happened speaks for itself. We're not talking to 8-year-olds here. And I'll give you a story. I remember one time I made a big play and I freaking pumped my arm up and I'm pretty sure I tore my bicep, but I didn't tell anybody. I was just excited. I just pumped my arm. It's not like I — so if you say, 'Hey, don't pump your arm next time, make sure you stay stoic,' man, this game is built off of emotion. And if you have to contain yourself after a play is made … then you're really becoming something you're not."

Detroit Lions head coach Dan Campbell speaks at a press conference after an NFL football game in Munich, Germany, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

The gesture was similar to the typical goal celebration in soccer that fans of FC Bayern Munich would see at Allianz Arena in Munich, Germany, where the latest European NFL matchup was held.

Lions fans remember the outcome vividly, as former linebacker Stephen Tulloch suffered the same fate as Hutchinson back in 2014 against the Green Bay Packers.

Hutchinson had 24 tackles on the season and six sacks before getting hurt.

This marks the defensive end's second season-ending injury in the span of three seasons.

The injury, Campbell said, was "one of those things that happened."

"I hate it, I know he hates it. But I mean, the number of moves that I've seen Hutch make and the number of plyometrics, explosive movements, the spins, the one leg lateral movements, lateral balances, I'm just like, it was one of those things, man," the coach said. "I hate it. I think we all understand what it is, and let's be smart, but no, I'm not gonna say, 'Nobody celebrates anymore.'"

The Lions went into the international matchup without middle linebacker Jack Campbell, who left Week 8's 19-15 win over the Minnesota Vikings on a stretcher.

Campbell is currently on injured reserve with a neck injury.

Celebrations have been a recurring topic in the NFL, and the Lions have been at the center of the conversation, from Amon-Ra St. Brown emulating President Donald Trump's dance to Jameson Williams getting a penalty for dry-humping the turf after a touchdown.
