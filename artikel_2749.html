Takeaways from Sixers-Magic: The clutch kings do it again

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Things weren't looking so hot for your Sixers Wednesday night.

Jaylen Brown tweaked his hamstring late in the win over Brooklyn Monday and had been ruled out Tuesday ahead of Wednesday's game. Dean Wade also had a hamstring issue, popping up on the injury report as questionable in the afternoon before being ruled out in the evening. Then, Tyrese Maxey was listed as questionable after warming up in Orlando. He was also ruled out right before tip-off.

Then, the Sixers found themselves down 17 in the first quarter as the Magic were red-hot to start the game. It felt like maybe it just wasn't the Sixers' night. These things happen over the course of an 82-game season.

But this isn't the same old Sixers. Led by VJ Edgecombe, and with contributions up and down the lineup, they moved to 11-0 in clutch games, securing a 121-116 win. They're 13-3 and sit alone atop the East.

Even with LeBron James and Joel Embiid available, it was likely going to take a great night from Edgecombe for the Sixers to win.

It was another night where he was everywhere and stuffed the stat sheet with 24 points (13 in the fourth), eight rebounds (three offensive), six assists and two steals. He was efficient, going 8-of-15 from the floor and 4-of-9 from three.

But the box score doesn't come close to telling the story. He made clutch shots, timely passes, defensive plays and seemed to be around the ball all night, especially late in the game.

The last five minutes of this one showed you all Edgecombe has to offer. Around the five-minute mark, he got downhill off an Embiid screen and found Jabari Walker in the corner for three, giving the Sixers their first lead since the opening minutes of the game. After the teams were jostling back and forth, Orlando went up by one on a Jalen Suggs' three. On the next possession, James passed off a grenade to Edgecombe, who coolly buried a deep triple with a hand in his face.

On the next possession, Kentavious Caldwell-Pope stood up Franz Wagner, who had his way for most of the night (Edgecombe also had a couple terrific defensive reps on Wagner late). Then, Edgecombe fought for the rebound, knocking it off Desmond Bane out of bounds. The best part is Edgecombe seemed more fired up about the hustle play than the three-ball.

It's to a point where we can't call Edgecombe a "future star" or "star-in-the-making." Screw the qualifiers — this kid is a star right now.

With Maxey, Brown and Edgecombe able to do a lot of heavy lifting, the Sixers aren't as dependent on James and Embiid to be The Guy on a nightly basis — but it's a nice luxury to have two former league MVPs who can scale up roles when needed.

After posting a season-high 37 points against the Nets Monday, Embiid wasn't his most efficient offensively. Still, he had moments where he controlled the game, getting to the line 14 times and throwing off the Magic's rhythm. He finished with a team-high 26 points on 7-of-18 from the field.

But his biggest contributions were on the other end of the floor. The first quarter was ugly. The Sixers' defense was a mess and Orlando was making everything. From the second quarter on, that changed dramatically, with Embiid being a big part of it. He came up with two impressive blocks on Wendell Carter, Jr. to close the first half. With three minutes left and the Sixers clinging to a two-point lead, Embiid came away with an impressive block on Anthony Black, an infamous Sixers killer.

Embiid started the season protecting the rim at a high level. As his plantar fasciitis flared up and he was playing big minutes, that started to wane. Wednesday night was an example of what he can do when he's feeling good and engaged on that end. Of course, he isn't what he once was physically, but this is a guy who was a Defensive Player of the Year finalist. The instincts are still there.

As for LeBron, it's obvious he needs to pick his spots at 41 years old, but the moments that he does are still special. He took the game over the second quarter, posting nine points as the Sixers chipped away at Orlando's huge lead.

Then in the fourth, James came up with multiple big plays. He finished a layup off an Embiid pass — the big fella's seventh assist of the night — from a great Nick Nurse play design(!). After Edgecombe's big three and with the Sixers holding a two-point lead, James was left alone from 20-feet after a screen from Edgecombe. The NBA's all-time leading scorer decided to pull up, burying a jumper to make it a two-possession game. After Suggs split free throws on the other end, James set up Anfernee Simons for the dagger.

Will the Sixers win every clutch game this season? Of course not. Is there a little luck involved? Sure.

But the Sixers' luck feels like it changed in late July when The King chose Philly.

That trio shined, but make no mistake, this was a team effort.

Simons and KCP, starting in place of Maxey and Brown, both played their roles to perfection.

Simons, a Florida native named after Magic legend Anfernee "Penny" Hardaway, dropped 18 points, going 7-of-12 from the field and 3-of-7 from three, including the triple that sealed the game. A positive development in recent games is Simons is starting to make more twos. He's more than capable. He's not just a shooter, he's a bucket-getter.

KCP continues to look like the guy who helped the Lakers and Nuggets win championships. He's probably been the unsung hero for the Sixers' great start. He's been available for every game this season and has made an impact almost every night. He's shooting 44% from three (likely not sustainable, though he's shot over 40% from deep in three different seasons), playing outstanding defense and doing all the little things. He did a great job on Wagner down the stretch and also pulled down eight rebounds on the night. He was a team-high +10.

And let's not forget about Tacko Fall. Nurse let the 7-foot-6 center get two stints in this one and Fall was impactful in both. In a little over 10 minutes, Fall pulled down three rebounds and came away with three blocks. He also got his first bucket off a nice pass from James. The most surprising stat of the night: Fall was a +7 while Embiid was a -1. What a time to be alive.

Walker continues to be a solid contributor, nailing that big three off the Edgecombe pass. Labaron Philon Jr. came off the bench and scored 10 points and recorded two assists in 17 minutes. There's an argument to be made for the rookie to play more minutes to spell Maxey and Edgecombe. Nurse even got Justin Edwards and Caleb Love some run.

They're 11-0 in clutch games. They're 7-1 on the road, their best start away from home since 1982-83. They're 13-3 overall, their best start since 2000-01. What happened in those seasons again?

Again, you don't love saying it so early in the season, but something about this team feels special.
