6 winners and losers from the Patriots’ trade deadline inactivity

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The 2026 NFL trade deadline saw some big moves being made, but the New England Patriots did not join the fun. The team of head coach Mike Vrabel instead decided to let the Tuesday afternoon deadline come and go without either acquiring any players from the outside or moving on from some of their current personnel.

As a consequence, the roster will largely stay intact heading toward a Week 10 matchup with the Detroit Lions in Munich. Who can be seen as the primary benefactors of that inactivity, though? Here are some names that come to mind.

RB Corey Kiner and RB Lan Larison: With Rhamondre Stevenson reportedly set to miss time due to a knee injury, and with TreVeyon Henderson sidelined because of an illness, the Patriots adding some running back depth ahead of the deadline would not have come as too big of a surprise. The Colts' Jonathan Taylor or the Ravens' Derrick Henry were among the names mentioned as potential candidate, but even players with lesser star power would have negatively impacted the current group of backups. However, no trade happening can be seen as a vote of confidence in the likes of Corey Kiner and Lan Larison.

DT Christian Barmore: For starters, Carlton Davis getting traded always felt more like media speculation than anything else, so we are not going to spend much time on him. Christian Barmore, on the hand, could have changed teams had an attractive offer come along. It didn't meaning that he will stay put for the time being. Of course, Barmore's long-term outlook beyond the 2026 is still uncertain: while he can be a disruptive player and is currently ranked fifth on the Patriots with 15 quarterback pressures, he also is primarily a passing-down player that is being paid like a three-down contributor.

ED Harold Landry III, maybe: Pre-deadline rumors mentioned the Patriots as a potential landing spot for an edge rusher to complement a depth chart currently led by Dre'Mont Jones, Elijah Ponder and Gabe Jacas. No extra players being brought aboard means that the trio will continue leading the charge, but also that New England does feel at least good enough about its depth and positional outlook not to invest any extra resources in bolstering the group. One could interpret this as positive news for one player in particular: Harold Landry, who remains on the PUP list but is a candidate to be reactivated for the Patriots' home stretch. If the team felt uncertain about his ability to return at one point, it might have been more aggressive pursuing depth at the spot.

Depth receivers: A.J. Brown is nearing a return, but his latest absence due to a knee issue showed that the Patriots' wide receiver group is thin especially at the perimeter; Kyle Williams, the next man up, did little with the extended opportunity he received over the last two weeks and during Brown's previous stint on IR with a sprained ankle. Still, no outside help being added is good news for him and fellow depth receiver Efton Chism. The same is true for the depth at tight end, where Cameron Latu remains the de facto TE3 now that New England opted against bolstering his position group.

Receiver depth (and more): Of course, while the Patriots forgoing making any moves is good news for the individual players not negatively affected, it also means that the questions coming out of the Week 9 loss to the Packers remain. Kyle Williams is still the next man up at wide receiver, and Cameron Latu possibly one snap away from meaningful offensive playing time. Likewise, the Patriots' best-case scenario at right guard remains their second-stringer being able to return to the 53-man roster off of injured reserve. They also are putting a lot of trust in those aforementioned Corey Kiner and Lan Larison, the three-player edge rotation, and other positions who could on paper have benefitted from some fresh talent.

Next men up: Christian Barmore not getting traded suggests that the Patriots' status quo at defensive tackle will not change: Barmore is still a top-three guy alongside Milton Williams and Cory Durden. Had he been removed from the mix, the depth options — with Leonard Taylor probably the first name worth mentioning — would have been given more high-quality opportunities in the rotation.

The defensive outlook: Had the Vikings decided not to trade for now-former Raiders quarterback Kirk Cousins on Tuesday, the Patriots defense would have looked at its Week 14 matchup with Minnesota as a favorable matchup. Before Cousins joined the mix, Kyler Murray, Carson Wentz and Max Brosmer were the only QBs on the team's roster. Needless to say that Cousins, while not a world-beater himself, is an upgrade over that trio — and more of a challenge for New England's defense when the Vikings come to town in mid-December.
