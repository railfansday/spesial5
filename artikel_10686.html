Viral Claude AI video simulates why human fingers never really touch

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

A developer has used Claude AI to demonstrate a 3D simulation exploring why human fingers never truly touch, even when they’re pressed together.

The animation was shared on November 10 by An Xuan, a developer who creates AI-assisted 3D projects, and shows two fingers moving closer together before zooming in to reveal the microscopic structures beneath the skin.

Xuan said he used Claude to build the animation, which was rendered using 67.js, an open-source 3D engine designed to work with AI agents.

As the simulation zooms in, the distance between the fingers becomes progressively smaller, eventually revealing the atomic structures that make up human skin.

The project was inspired by an earlier interactive app created by Japanese developer @kakouton2bro, who wanted to help his daughter understand Zeno’s paradox.

The video has since gone viral on X, attracting millions of views and spurring debate over whether the simulation actually has any relation to Zeno’s paradox.

Built with the help of Claude, the app allows users to repeatedly halve the distance between two fingers, zooming from fingerprints and cells down to the atomic scale.

Zeno’s paradox is a philosophical puzzle dating back more than 2,400 years, exploring how an object can reach its destination if the remaining distance can always be divided in half.

However, this doesn’t mean two objects can never touch. An infinite series of increasingly small distances can still add up to a finite total, meaning the paradox doesn’t prevent physical contact.

At the atomic level, meanwhile, electromagnetic interactions and quantum effects prevent the atoms in our fingers from simply passing through one another.

While the simulation offers a visual explanation of these concepts, it doesn’t scientifically prove that human fingers can never touch.
