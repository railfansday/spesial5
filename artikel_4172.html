10 Survival Games That Don’t Make Hunger Their Whole Personality

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Contributor at DualShockers, former Esports Insider DACH content lead, former Escapist Magazine Germany content lead, software engineering background.

Survival games have had a hold on me for years now. There’s truly nothing like crash-landing somewhere hostile with nothing but the clothes on your back, punching a tree, and slowly building something that resembles a home. What I could absolutely do without, though, is the little drumstick icon that starts flashing at me roughly ten minutes into every new world.

From Rain World's terrifying sights to Subnautica's oceanic survival, these are the 10 Best Open World Survival Games.

Look, I get it. Hunger is realistic, and in some games, it genuinely adds to the experience. But far too often, it feels like a chore that’s been bolted on because that’s just what survival games do, and I’m not being funny, but I didn’t plan an entire expedition just to turn around because my character fancies a snack.

Thankfully, not every developer feels the same way. Some have turned food into a reward rather than a punishment, some have made hunger completely optional, and a few have decided that your character doesn’t need to eat at all. They’re all still survival games, and some of them are pretty brutal, so let’s take a look at my favourites that found far more interesting ways to try and kill you.

Valheim is pretty much the poster child for survival games that do food differently. There’s no hunger meter, and you will never starve to death, no matter how long you go without eating. Instead, you can have up to three different foods active at once, with each of them boosting your maximum health and stamina before slowly wearing off.

Skipping a meal won’t hurt you, but it will leave you with a measly 25 health and 50 stamina, healing a single hit point every ten seconds. Considering a single troll can flatten you in a matter of seconds, that’s more than enough motivation to get cooking. Food becomes part of your preparation, just like repairing your gear before a big boss fight, rather than something you need to do just to stay alive.

Note: Iron Gate reworked the entire food system with the Hearth & Home update back in 2021, and truth be told, not everyone was thrilled about it at the time.

After more than five years in Early Access and over 17 million copies sold, Valheim finally hit 1.0 on 9 September 2026, together with the Deep North biome. Eating is still optional. Heading into the Mistlands on an empty stomach, however, is a mistake you’ll only make once.

Keen Games, the Frankfurt studio behind Portal Knights, didn’t exactly hide where its inspiration came from. Ahead of Early Access, the team said that Enshrouded would have no hunger meter, no thirst meter and no sleep meter, and that Valheim was the closest comparison for how food would work. And honestly? It shows, in the best possible way.

Food in Enshrouded is all about buffs. You get three food slots, and only one food of each type can be active at a time, which nudges you towards mixing and matching rather than just shovelling roasted meat into your mouth. Some meals boost your health, others your melee or magic damage, and it ends up feeling much more like putting together an RPG build than ticking off a survival chore.

Note: About six months into Early Access, Keen Games added an optional Survival difficulty that brings the hunger bar back. On that setting, it can actually kill you.

I think that’s the perfect compromise. If you want the classic experience, it’s there for you, but if you don’t, you’ll never have to think about it. More than five million players had already wandered into Embervale before the 1.0 release on 15 October 2026, so I’m clearly not the only one who appreciates that.

Terraria has never had a hunger meter. Sure, food exists, but it gives you the Well Fed buff, a lovely little boost to your stats rather than something standing between you and certain death. You can spend hundreds of hours digging, building and taking down bosses without ever having to think about lunch.

That changed, sort of, with the 1.4.3 update. Re-Logic teamed up with Klei Entertainment for a crossover with Don’t Starve Together, and the result was The Constant, a secret world seed that brings in hunger, Don’t Starve-style shading and darkness that actively hurts you. Hunger works in stages, and if you ignore it for long enough, you will start starving.

Note: The whole crossover started with a Reddit AMA for Terraria’s 10th anniversary, where the team said Don’t Starve Together was the franchise they’d most like to collaborate with. Klei just so happened to be reading.

I absolutely love this approach. Nobody is forced into it, but it’s there as an opt-in challenge for anyone who wants it. The fact that you need to type in a secret seed to even get hunger tells you everything you need to know about how essential it is to the Terraria experience.

Players will need to be extra careful when exploring in these open-world and survival games.

Astroneer has a pretty simple take on survival: you need oxygen, and that’s about it. Your shelter provides you with both power and oxygen, so as long as you stay within range, you’re absolutely fine. The moment you wander off, though, your meter starts to drop, which is where tethers come in to carry that oxygen with you.

What I love is how that one mechanic shapes everything else. Your tether lines double as breadcrumbs, so you always have a way back home, and the tension only really kicks in when you’re deep in a cave and very much off the grid. It’s survival with all of the tension and none of the snack breaks, and that’s exactly why I keep going back to it.

System Era Softworks released Astroneer into Early Access in December 2016, before launching the full game in February 2019. Sadly, co-founder and lead artist Paul Pepera passed away in March 2017, before the game was finished. His distinctive low-poly style still defines Astroneer to this day, and by March 2022, the game had been purchased nearly four million times.

No Man’s Sky doesn’t have a hunger meter, a thirst meter or anything that even resembles sleep. What it does have is life support and hazard protection, and both of those need topping up whilst you explore planets that are, more often than not, actively trying to kill you. Some players will argue those are just hunger and thirst with different names, and fair enough, but in practice it feels completely different.

You’re not foraging for berries. You’re mining oxygen and sodium, refining resources and keeping your exosuit running whilst dodging toxic rain or extreme temperatures. Instead of pulling you away from exploring, your survival is tied directly into it.

Food did eventually find its way into the game with a full cooking system, letting you turn ingredients into dishes that restore life support or hazard protection. There’s even a food critic, Cronus, who will rate your culinary creations and reward you with nanites. It’s completely optional, slightly absurd and exactly the kind of thing No Man’s Sky does best.

Subnautica does have hunger and thirst in its default Survival mode, so this one comes with a little asterisk. When you start a new game, though, you can pick Freedom mode instead, which is identical to Survival, just without hunger and thirst. You’ll still have to manage your oxygen, your health and your nerves, and trust me, that is more than enough.

Plenty of players recommend Freedom as the best way to experience Subnautica for the first time, and I’d have to agree. Inventory space is already tight, and every slot you don’t fill with fish is a slot you can use for resources. That way, you can fully focus on exploring the oceans of planet 4546B, which are, quite frankly, terrifying enough on their own.

Note: Freedom mode isn’t a late addition, either. It has been there almost from the start, and PC Gamer’s preview in January 2015, just weeks into Early Access, already listed it alongside Survival.

Unknown Worlds launched the full game on 23 January 2018, celebrating with a livestream from the Monterey Bay Aquarium. By then, nearly two million people already owned it. Considering what lurks in the deeper parts of Subnautica, I personally would’ve picked a slightly less fishy location.

These games are sure to keep fans of the Subnautica series entertained.

Satisfactory is a factory-building game first and a survival game a very distant second, but it still drops you onto an alien planet full of wildlife that would love nothing more than to take a bite out of you. What it doesn’t do is make you eat. Food is purely there to heal you, and the planet’s edible flora are more like a first aid kit than an actual meal.

Paleberries heal 10 HP, Beryl Nuts a little less, and the Bacon Agaric mushroom heals 20. Eventually, you can even process them into inhalers, because of course the answer to everything in Satisfactory is automation. The only real hunger in this game is your factory’s endless appetite for more power, more iron and more conveyor belts.

Note: ADA, your ever-so-helpful AI companion, refers to the Bacon Agaric as Amanita Lardum. Whether it actually tastes like bacon remains a mystery.

Coffee Stain Studios released Satisfactory 1.0 on 10 September 2024, after over five years in Early Access and 5.5 million copies sold. And the big reveal in the release date trailer? A flushable toilet. We all have our priorities.

Starbound, Chucklefish’s sci-fi sandbox, lets you decide on your relationship with food right at character creation. Casual mode removes the need to eat entirely, Survival mode adds a hunger meter, and Hardcore combines hunger with permadeath for anyone who enjoys suffering. There’s no switching later on, so you’d better know what you want before you start.

Interestingly, hunger had actually been taken out during development, and was only brought back for the 1.0 release on 22 July 2016, specifically for the Survival and Hardcore modes. In those, food rots over time, and you will starve if you ignore it. In Casual, food still heals you and grants buffs, so cooking never feels pointless.

I really appreciate a game that respects the fact that some of us just want to build a pretty space station and go planet-hopping in peace. Not every adventure needs a ration plan. Starbound can still be pretty challenging in other ways, but in Casual mode, the biggest threat on a new planet is the local wildlife, not your rumbling stomach.

There are so many great co-op games coming out today, and many allow players to just explore and relax together.

Pacific Drive is a survival game where you don’t need to eat, drink or sleep. Instead, almost all of the survival pressure lands squarely on your station wagon. You patch up panels, swap out tyres, keep the battery charged and the tank full, all whilst driving through the bizarre, anomaly-filled Olympic Exclusion Zone.

Seth Rosen, who led the game’s design, has been pretty open about the fact that a lot of his choices were aimed at smoothing out the things that bugged him about survival games. He even had pages of notes on turning radiation damage into an exposure meter, but ultimately decided against it, since you already have your hands full taking care of the car without another time pressure and distraction. As Rosen put it, the car’s health is generally a better indicator of how a run is going than your own.

Note: Creative director and CEO Cassandra Dracott’s 1989 Buick Estate was the primary reference car during early development. Her parents’ Volvo station wagon, which became the car she drove from the moment she could drive, played a part, too.

Ironwood Studios released Pacific Drive on 22 February 2024, and it sold over a million copies within a year. There’s even a television adaptation in development, and I truly have no idea how they’re planning to make a car the main character. I cannot wait to find out.

LEGO Fortnite launched on 7 December 2023 with a fairly traditional hunger system. You could already switch it off when creating a world, but for anyone who kept it on, it wasn’t exactly a great time. Within two weeks, Epic patched it to make snacks more filling, because food simply wasn’t reducing hunger by enough. In hindsight, it was the first sign that hunger and LEGO minifigures weren’t the most natural pairing.

In mid-2024, Epic added Cozy mode, which turned hunger, temperature and stamina off by default. That should’ve been the end of it, but Epic eventually went one step further and removed hunger from Odyssey altogether. Food and potions still exist and give you buffs, but there’s no meter to keep topped up anymore.

Note: Not everyone was happy about it, with some players saying the change made the game feel a little too arcade-like.

Personally, I think it was the right call for a game that lives inside Fortnite and attracts plenty of younger and newer players. Odyssey is all about building villages, exploring and fighting off the occasional Storm-Wild creature. Nobody needed to stop every few minutes to hunt down yet another LEGO berry.

These survival games are the best to come back to and explore to your heart's content solo or with friends time and time again.
