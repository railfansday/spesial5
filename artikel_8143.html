The Best Men’s Winter Running Jackets (2027)

Since the dawn of time, the base layer/midlayer/shell sandwich has been the go-to for winter adventures. But for runners, a good jacket paired with just a technical T-shirt or long-sleeve is often a better choice. Today’s jackets use the latest advances in insulation and waterproofing to replace two or three traditional layers, and adapt better to changing conditions and activity levels.

The jackets in this review proved sufficiently lightweight, breathable, and weatherproof that we rarely had to remove them to manage heat—and when we did, it was much easier to deal with one item than several. We tested these jackets in conditions ranging from wind-blown fall rain to frigid cold mountain mornings, and they consistently kept us moving forward with minimal fuss, wrapped in warmth and shielded from the elements.

Pros and Cons + Surprisingly warm + Unrestrictive fit + Smart vent panels – No hood

The Thermalrunner proved an excellent choice for a surprisingly broad range of temperatures and weather conditions. On a blustery 40-degree day with light rain, the water-resistant fabric treatment handled the challenge, beading moisture on the exterior while the soft interior lining stayed dry against the skin. On another run when the sun burned through clouds unexpectedly fast, we were impressed by how well the vent panels in the back shed excess heat. One tester called the lightweight PrimalLoft insulation a “Goldilocks” amount of warmth that consistently tamed the chill without overheating him.

This jacket features all the premium touches you’d want, including zip hand pockets and chest pouch, reflective patches, and a slightly extended, insulated back panel that added extra warmth and protection from the elements. Paired with a light, short-sleeved base layer, the Thermalrunner jacket was comfortable for running on mild 50-degree days, and with a hat (it lacks a hood), gloves, and a long-sleeve undergarment, it handled near-freezing weather just as well.

Pros and Cons + Featherweight + Packable + Protective hood – Fragile

Stuffing the Aerolight Wind Jacket into a pack before heading out on an adventurous mountain trek in the Swiss Alps proved to be a good decision for one tester. Late in the afternoon, the summer skies filled with clouds, the temperature dropped, and bursts of rain started soaking the mountainside. The jacket provided immediate warmth, blocked the wind, and, though it’s by no means waterproof, kept enough moisture out to keep moving comfortably—with almost no weight penalty.

The water-resistant treatment is PFAS-free, and small touches like an adjustable hood and elastic wrist cuffs add to the better-than-expected protection. The ultralight fabric is soft and quiet on the move and breathes well at high exertion levels, but offers no stretch. The lone chest pocket doubles as a stuff sack, making this a great item to keep on hand for mild days in the 40s and 50s. The feather-weight construction comes at the price of being somewhat more fragile than other top picks: we’ve heard reports of the fabric blowing out under stress, although we didn’t see any excess wear during testing.

Pros and Cons + Windproof + Highly visible + Distinctive look – Lacks breathability

When was the last time someone on the street complimented your running outfit for looking good? Our testers got compliments on the PYNRS (pronounced like “pioners”) Magnolia Track Jacket all the time. This NYC-based company started as an inclusive urban running club before branching into apparel; the result is functional outerwear that stands out from the crowd.

The iridescent, tightly woven nylon shell features reflective piping for excellent visibility from all sides, and the polyester lining is soft against skin. Cut on the looser side, this jacket easily accommodated insulating layers and proved comfortable for running in near- and sub-freezing weather. It offers roomy, zippered hand pockets and an interior zippered chest pocket, plus elasticized cuffs and a drawstring waist to seal out the weather. The jacket is a little hefty and lacks some of the breathability higher-performance materials offer, but for cool-to-cold weather running (low 40s to mid-20s), we stayed comfortably warm and looked stylish in the Magnolia Track Jacket.

Start ready and stay ready with this do-anything hooded workout layer. Crafted from a soft performance stretch knit, this jacket is both insulating and flexible with a protective woven overlay that gives you added coverage in cool and variable conditions. It wicks moisture and includes a drawcord hood and zip pockets to keep you and your belongings dry and safe in any weather. Pair it with the Vuori Sunday bottoms to make the perfect set.

Pros and Cons + Soft wool fabric + Polartec insulation + Wind-blocking torso – No reflectivity

The Alpha Active’s medium-weight blend of Merino wool and polyester, with a touch of bamboo rayon, proved exceptionally soft and warm, and excelled at wicking moisture away from skin. Baffled Polartec Alpha insulation and a wind-blocking nylon fabric overlay the chest. The overall effect felt like wearing a lightweight vest over a premium base-layer shirt—but integrated into a single piece that moved comfortably and quietly with us.

The breathable Merino-blend sleeves and back, with no wind-blocking material, kept us from overheating at easier paces up to the 50-degree mark. The jacket proved comfortable down to freezing when working hard—adding a lightweight outer shell produced a warm combination that handled all but the most bitter or windy conditions. The sleek fit and black fabric look good, but bring a reflective hat or wear a light for nighttime running.

Pros and Cons + Toasty, 95% recycled fleece front + Functional and stylish fit + Solid warmth – No backside windblock

Our testers agreed that the front of this attractive jacket provided solid insulation for frigid conditions. They were divided, however, on whether it would have benefited from better wind-blocking on the back, where a large fabric panel vented heat, leaving some feeling too exposed to the elements. Still, at an affordable price, most reviewers found the Winter Warm Pro kept them comfortable in temperatures well below freezing (there’s no hood, so bring a hat for frigid days). The body fabric fleece is made with 95 percent recycled materials, feels soft against bare skin, and stretches nicely. The polyester torso insulation is reassuringly thick without feeling bulky. Additional touches include a water-repellent coating, hand pockets, side mesh storage pocket, and a zippered chest pocket.

Pros and Cons + Stormproof + Effective hood and brim + Chest snap – Crinkly

Salomon’s adv.DRY material—a thin, microporous membrane bonded to a nylon shell—proved impressively waterproof during drenching rain showers while staying breathable in dry conditions. The Bonatti Pro features fully taped seams, waterproof zippers, and an excellent, adjustable brimmed hood that kept our hair dry and our heads warm without impeding our field of vision, even on the move. A snap closure at the chest lets you open the zipper and vent heat quickly without removing the jacket, and reflective patches on the front and back provide good visibility in poor weather.

The fit is close yet roomy enough to accommodate insulating underlayers, extending the jacket’s comfortable range below the freezing mark. The fabric is somewhat crinkly, but it still packs easily into a small bundle. There are vents at the waist, but they’re fairly minimal, and we didn’t notice much airflow. Salomon’s “aeroPACK” flap system is meant to accommodate a small hydration backpack without impeding the jacket’s fit; we didn’t test this feature but noted that it didn’t get in the way when running without a pack.

Choosing the best winter running jacket is primarily about the kinds of winter conditions you face where you live, travel, and run. Consider if you need a waterproof layer, or can get by with water resistance—waterproof jackets are more costly, don’t breathe as well, and tend to be somewhat stiffer and noisier, but are worth it when you’re out in a downpour or blizzard.

Then think about whether you want your running jacket to be insulated and provide warmth on its own, or work as a thin, exterior shell layer. Insulated jackets can make heading out in frigid temps less daunting, especially if you tend to run cold, but can get hot during high-intensity exercise or if the temps rise. Shell jackets allow you to layer underneath to adjust for a range of conditions. Also consider whether you want a fitted jacket that stays close to the body for streamlined mobility, or a looser fit that allows flexibility for layering underneath.

The jacket review test crew put these products through their paces in locations as diverse as the Swiss Alps and Nebraska’s High Plains, logging miles on both roads and trails. Testers recorded their notes for each sample piece during easy runs, up-tempo workouts, and everything in between, in good weather and full-on storm conditions. Jackets were evaluated for their effectiveness at keeping testers warm and blocking wind and moisture while breathing and venting excess heat, with bonus points given for the versatility to handle a range of weather conditions and to look good in diverse contexts.
