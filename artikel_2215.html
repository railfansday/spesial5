James Bree incident in West Brom v Southampton match sparks reaction

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Southampton full-back James Bree has sparked a strong reaction on social media following his side's 1-1 draw with West Bromwich Albion on Tuesday night.

With the scores level in B71 after Samuel Edozie equalised early in the second period following Isaac Price's first-half opener, it has been debated whether the Saints defender successfully deceived referee, Josh Smith, who stopped the game for a head injury.

Both West Brom and Southampton have started the Championship season in impressive form, with Tonda Eckert's side still sitting amongst the division's early pacesetters despite being deducted four points due to the Spygate scandal.

Those who are a part of the South Coast club have, this season, been playing the role of pantomime villains, and that was certainly the case at The Hawthorns for those backing the hosts, who are now unbeaten in six successive matches.

In what was a pulsating encounter, it is fair to say that Albion were more than deserving of their half-time lead, with Saints' attack leaving Max O'Leary untroubled, before Edozie's 53rd-minute leveller certainly gave Eckert's side a fresh impetus.

West Brom still showed signs that they could take all three points, and after a spell of attacking promise, Bree was seen on the ground without movement, leaving Smith to stop the game, although the Southampton physios were swiftly waved away by the full-back.

Asked about the incident post-match, Morrison stated: "I think, because the pressure was coming from us, he tried to stop the game.

Asked whether Bree should have face further punishment, the Baggies boss added: "We've asked for consistency, so let's hope it comes."

West Brom, of course, saw Aune Heggebo banned for two matches for the deception of a match official in August after his handballed goal on the opening day against Norwich City, which was the first time a player had been banned for such wrongdoing in English football.

As such, Baggies fans are calling for Bree to face a similar punishment.

"Faking head injuries is becoming all too common and requires stronger action from officials. If staying down holding your head resulted in an automatic 3 minutes off the pitch while checked over by medics, I suspect we’ll suddenly see a lot less of it," was the opinion of one Albion fan.

Another Baggies supporter wrote: "Disgraceful behaviour, so obvious what he was doing before ref blew his whistle & then waved his hands to say he don’t need any medical attention so he don’t go off. Ref was very poor all night tho imo. Standard EFL ref."

"Absolutely appalling behaviour - so clear and obvious - should have been booked and EFL need to take immediate action for the credibility of football," was another strong verdict on the matter.

"It shouldn’t be done, but it literally happens in every game of professional football," another comment read.

There is, ultimately, no proof that the Baggies would have definitely restored their advantage, but given the punishment which Heggebo received two months ago, the stance of those associated with the West Midlands club is hardly a surprise.

Bree's decision to go down isn't the only incident which has been heavily criticised in recent days too, with Liverpool legend, Jamie Carragher, slamming Alexis Mac Allister's decision to 'feign' injury after the Argentinian midfielder brushed heads with Arsenal's Noni Madueke in the Gunners' 1-0 win at Anfield.
