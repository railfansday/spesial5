Top 15 best away fans in the EFL ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

When it comes to topics of discourse throughout the EFL sphere, few subjects promise to spark fiercer debate than arguments over which clubs have the best - and worst - away support.

Supporting your team at home is one thing, but committing to pocket-burning expenses and significant mileage travelling up and down the country in the name of fanfare takes on a new level of loyalty and is worn as a badge of honor by the clubs who make a particular impression in terraces on the road.

Factoring in a blend of noise and numbers while giving special attention to the teams that really clock up the miles, Football League World has decided to run the rule and rank the FIFTEEN best-supported clubs away from home in the EFL right now...

We're heading underway with Leicester City, who have always been historically well-followed despite never being a major-hitter in the realms of EFL fanfare.

Leicester's location within the East Midlands means few destinations are "too far", whereas relegation to League One for only the second time in the club's history makes for many interesting away days that, admittedly, supporters never thought they'd be embarking on.

Nonetheless, the Foxes often travel in numbers, are noisy when they're up for it and have already recorded two of the highest away attendances in the EFL this season after taking in excess of 4,000 fans to both Barnsley and MK Dons.

Southampton's fanbase, for all intents and purposes, is rather comparable to the contingent in support of Leicester, though it's the Hampshire outfit who just about gain an edge here.

The Saints' location doesn't always make away travel easy, yet that rarely stops them from bringing swathes of supporters on their trips with a decent atmosphere to boot.

They posted the sixth-highest average away attendance in the Championship last season, and when those numbers are regularly accompanied by noise despite more arduous mileage than most, it's hard to resist giving Southampton their flowers.

West Bromwich Albion have always retained solid support both home and away amid plenty of local competition within the Black Country and wider West Midlands region, which is why it's only right to recognise their own standing on the road.

Once again, the majority of away trips fail to require lofty travel commitments and the club's West Midlands status makes it easy to reach most destinations by train.

That culminates in consistently respectable numbers with the capacity to make a serious racket when the good times are rolling or a local derby arrives, but whilst only six sides travelled better than the Baggies last term, the fact that multiple fellow Midlands clubs did prevents Albion from featuring higher.

Bolton Wanderers' support has been tested with rocky fortunes over the last decade, though with the tide having now truly turned following Steven Schumacher's appointment in January of last year, it has stood the test of time and we're now seeing a persistently-loyal fanbse rewarded for their efforts.

Only one club in League One recorded a higher average away attendance than Bolton, who averaged just under 2,000 supporters on their travels as Schumacher's side finally returned to the Championship, where their numbers and noise have both strengthened in tandem.

Bolton's home support can be outstanding, and their away standing is not too far behind either.

We're looking at both numbers and noise here, of course, and it's the latter criteria that truly does the lifting for Stockport County.

The Hatters have enjoyed a remarkable ascent up the footballing pyramid in recent seasons and that feel-good factor is palpable and reflected in the way that County fans support their side, proving to be something of a driving force towards aggressive unprecedented promotion pushes in League One.

At face value, the statistics would suggest that Stockport's away following doesn't necessarily pull up any trees, as five clubs recorded a superior average attendance to their 1,342 in 25/26.

However, the noise can be eclipsed by very few at its best, with Stockport fans rarely failing to create an almighty racket with the sort of volume, passion and intensity that speaks far louder than the numbers alone.

Another fanbase to have brought bodies and noise decibels aplenty to League One terraces last season was Cardiff City, who gained promotion at the very first time of asking with a fanbase that retained all of its volume and passion after returning to the third-tier for the first time in more than 20 years.

Cardiff's average away attendance of 1,716 was bettered by just two clubs, which is made all the impressive when you consider the fact that only a handful of sides throughout the EFL have to travel further than the Bluebirds.

Indeed, barring vociferous local derbies against Swansea and Bristol City, the Welsh outfit's closest Championship trip this season will be to Birmingham City - yet they regularly record stronger away averages than most while bringing even better noise and relish the 'us against them' mentality that comes with journeys across the border to England, which always carry an extra edge.

Derby County's home atmosphere is even better than what they conjure up on the road, though that's not to say that the Pride Park faithful fail to represent one of the EFL's stronger away fanbases.

In a season that saw John Eustace's side narrowly miss out on a play-off finish, the Rams' average away attendance comfortably exceeded the 2,000 figure and was among the best in the Championship.

Dismal form in the opening months of the current campaign has, quite naturally, reduced the volume of the Rams' ever-loyal travelling contingent, who are admittedly aided by a handy East Midlands location smack-bang in the centre of the country but have historically provided a level of away support bettered by few.

Bradford City's loud and proud fanbase has offered major fuel behind the club's rise from mid-table mediocrity in League Two to a now-consistent status as League One promotion contenders, having made the play-offs during their first season back at third-tier level last time out.

As the Bantams roared on to upset the odds and finish in fourth before losing to Bolton in the semi-finals, no team averaged a higher away attendance than City's 2,000+ figure.

West Yorkshire isn't exactly the easiest location to travel from for many fixtures down south either, even if Bradford were helped by a high volume of relatively-local northern encounters.

Either way, Bradford's home support gets immense credit and their fanbase, in terms of both noise and numbers, deserves just as much for what they get up to away from Valley Parade.

One of the strongest EFL fanbases full-stop comes in the form of Portsmouth, who, akin to many on this list, have stuck with their side through thick and thin before earning some relative rewards after persevering with no shortage of adversity.

John Mousinho's men are now establishing themselves as a second-tier staple once more, and even if Pompey aren't always making a splash on the pitch, you can be sure that they'll catch the eye - and the eardrums - away from it in the terraces.

Fratton Park is a well-documented cauldron of noise, but the same can be said for just about any away end that Portsmouth fans find themselves in.

Those same fans also have to contend with some of the most challenging travel requirements, with their closest trips after local foes Southampton arriving more than 80 miles away in London, yet that never prevents Pompey from creating serious noise on a consistent basis and travelling well after averaging more than 2,000 supporters on the road last time out.

The pride of travelling with both noise and body volume despite being so inconveniently located for just about any away fixture is a glory belonging to Plymouth Argyle, whose fanbase is one of the loudest, proudest and, in many ways, admirable, in all of the land.

It's remarkable that the Pilgrims feature so high when their closest away fixture in the 2026/27 term will be to Reading some 181 miles away following the relegation of rivals Exeter City to League Two, but the fact they still averaged away attendances comfortably in excess of the 1,000 benchmark while always managing to usurp their numbers with noise is testament to an especially-impressive Green Army contingent.

No team has to trek further on their travels, but Plymouth have always shown that it won't stop them.
