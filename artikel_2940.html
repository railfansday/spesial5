Week 9 fantasy football RB start-sit: Marks, Jones, Hall, Gordon

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Texans running back Woody Marks and Vikings running back Aaron Jones Sr. are Week 9 fantasy football start 'em picks against the Chargers and Bills, two defenses that rank among the 10 most generous to running backs.

Jets running back Breece Hall is a sit 'em pick at Kansas City in his expected return from a quad injury, and Dolphins running back Ollie Gordon II is a sit against Detroit. Jones is my RB23, Marks is RB24, Hall is RB31 and Gordon is RB32.

Buffalo has allowed 24.2 half-PPR points per game to running backs, sixth most in the NFL, and the Chargers have allowed 22.5, 10th most. Kansas City has allowed 17.6 and Detroit 16.2, both among the 10 stingiest defenses against the position.

Gainwell delivered the top result of the week. He ran 25 times for a career-high 140 yards in Tampa Bay's 16-0 win over Atlanta, finishing with 14 half-PPR points, as he and Bucky Irving became the first teammates in NFL history to each log 25 carries and 125 rushing yards in the same game.

Henderson's start missed because he was inactive for New England's 24-19 win at Miami.

The Jones sit landed. The Vikings veteran ran five times for 22 yards and caught two passes for 18 yards in a 19-15 loss at Detroit, good for five half-PPR points.

Dowdle's sit missed, since he ran 15 times for 56 yards and opened the scoring with a 9-yard touchdown in Pittsburgh's 27-6 win over Cleveland.

With limited competition and volume, we're hoping a semi-plus matchup pushes Marks into RB2 territory.

David Montgomery missed Houston's last two games with a groin injury and didn't practice before either one. Texans head coach DeMeco Ryans said Monday he had no significant update on the veteran coming out of the bye, so Marks is lined up for another week as the lead back.

Marks has handled the expanded role well. At least considering the inconsistencies of the Texans' offense. He played 54 of 61 snaps against the Giants, and Ryans has been clear about the plan since Montgomery got hurt. "Dynamic, will carry the load for us," Ryans said of the USC product.

Marks' efficiency is the rub, since he's averaging 3.5 yards per carry. But volume wins at this range of the rankings, and the Chargers have given up eight rushing touchdowns to running backs in seven games. A 19-carry workload against that defense makes Marks a solid flex with touchdown upside.

It hasn't been pretty for Jones since Mason returned. Neither back has accomplished much, in fact.

Jones goes from included in my sits to a start in one week because the matchup flipped. He has played most of the season with a sprained MCL in his left knee, and he has only 13 carries in two games since Jordan Mason returned from a fractured thumb.

Still, Mason didn't take the job at Detroit. He ran eight times for 20 yards and played fewer snaps than DeeJay Dallas, so the backfield remains open for the 31-year-old. Jones carried Minnesota while Mason was out, piling up 105 carries for 436 yards and two touchdowns in six games.

Buffalo is the right opponent for him to get back on track. The Bills have allowed 10 total touchdowns and 42 receptions to backs in seven games, and Jones caught both of his targets against the Lions. The Monday night kickoff also gives his knee an extra day of rest.

Season: 3 games, 51 car. for 163 yds, 1 TD

You've been patient. Kudos. However, this isn't a great spot to dive headfirst back into Hall waters. Consider the following.

Hall hasn't played since Sept. 27 at Detroit, and NFL Network's Ian Rapoport reported he's now good to go and should play against the Chiefs. A five-game layoff is a tough setup, since Hall was averaging 3.2 yards per carry before the injury.

Braelon Allen also made sure Hall won't walk back into a full workload. Allen ran 21 times for 147 yards and a touchdown against the Raiders, and he played 78% of the snaps or more in every game since Hall went down.

Kansas City has held running backs to 3.4 yards per carry this season. A split backfield in that matchup leaves Hall as a risky flex at best in his first game back.

It's tough to walk away from a running back seeing volume. All we can really hope for is the ball getting in a guy's hands. However…

Gordon entered Week 8 with momentum, and Patriots head coach Mike Vrabel noted the Oklahoma State product had four touchdowns in four games. "Ollie's a big back. He's taken advantage of his opportunities," Vrabel said.

New England still shut him down. The Patriots stuffed Gordon five times and held him to a 16-yard long, and his unsportsmanlike conduct penalty turned a third-and-5 into third-and-20 on a stalled drive.

Gordon also has no receiving role to fall back on, after going without a catch on one target last week. Detroit has allowed four rushing touchdowns to backs in seven games, which leaves the touchdown-dependent bruiser outside the flex range at RB32.
