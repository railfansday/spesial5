GdS: Worth the price tag – how Moreira became ‘untouchable’ for Milan and Belgium

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

It has been a positive international break for Diego Moreira, who continued his excellent form from club football.

As La Gazzetta dello Sport write this morning, Ruben Amorim will welcome Moreira back to Milanello soon. The winger has become a regular starter and producing goals and assists, but the Portuguese coach knows the pitfalls of the match against Frosinone all too well.

Therefore, starting Thursday – when the squad will be fully back together after the day off tomorrow – everyone’s minds will be focused solely on the new busy schedule awaiting the Rossoneri until Christmas. More specifically, seven matches from Sunday until December 19th.

Immediately after the last international break, the Rossoneri drew away to Sassuolo, a result that certainly wasn’t greeted with a smile. The goal is to avoid repeating that misstep against Alvini’s team, and Moreira will be key to that.

Despite his 22 years of age and the €50m valuation plus bonuses that could have weighed heavily on his shoulders, Diego hasn’t taken long to win over Milan fans. In his last six league matches (he missed two due to injury), he’s scored four goals and provided three assists.

He has also demonstrated his ability to switch roles with ease, playing as a winger on the right or left, as a wing-back or even as a full-back. He plays with the boldness of someone who targets his opponent and tries to dribble past him, something that fans always appreciate.

Moreira always seeks one-on-one situations, both due to his strengths and the explicit request of Amorim, who asks his wide players to create numerical superiority. Diego is succeeding remarkably, as his assist for Christian Pulisic in the derby shows, as he got past Carlo Augusto and crossed.

Moreira made the difference against Italy on Sunday too: not only did he score the fourth goal, which he celebrated by making a gesture of ‘one, two, three and four’ with his fingers, but he also kept accelerating, hit the woodwork, shot wide once more and had two shots blocked by his opponents.

He set up De Bruyne for a shot, had more touches than anyone else in the opponents’ area (11) and often took on Calafiori and Dimarco, delighting the crowd with a spectacular backheel. He had around 50 touches and 27/31 completed passes, earning the applause of the entire stadium when he came off.

Moreira is currently captivating thanks to his drive and desire. He’s that way at Milan and he’s that way with the national team. That’s why there’s a version of the Rossoneri with him and another without him which, frankly, isn’t as threatening.

Amorim carefully integrated him into the team’s operations at the start of the season, but now he has broken the padlock off and now considers him a key player in the team. This doesn’t mean he won’t need a breather, given the schedule, but the coach will have to think about the right moments to rest.

With four goals in Serie A (one every 121 minutes), he’s already matched his four goals from last season in Ligue 1, and he has no intention of stopping. He feels the confidence in himself and the team.

The leap from Ligue 1 to Serie A didn’t bother him. In fact, he intends to continue dancing on the pitch, just like in the derby or against Italy. On Sunday, he wants to show he has the same energy with which he brought Italy to its knees. Milan and San Siro await him.
