Review: Amazon’s Alexa Tablet 8 is a Fire Tablet with Google Play for $150 – what’s not to love?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Amazon shocked everyone in October when it launched its new Alexa Tablet series with Google Play support built-in, addressing the biggest shortcoming of its older Fire Tablets. For Black Friday 2026, the Alexa Tablet 8 is a mere $150 and, with Google Play on board, this feels like a Fire Tablet that’s all grown up.

The Alexa Tablet 8 is one of three new Amazon tablets that launched in October with Google Play on board, and it’s also the one I was personally the most excited about. I love a good tiny Android tablet, but there just aren’t that many out there – and some of the ones we do have are exorbitantly expensive.

Right up there with Amazon’s long-running reputation in the tablet space, Alexa Tablet 8 is very much an affordable device, and that’s reflected in its fairly meager specs:

Yet, I didn’t find myself put off by the device.

I specifically asked Amazon for a review unit of the absolute base model – 4GB of RAM and lockscreen ads – and I can say with confidence it’s Not Bad at the end of the day.

The display is objectively not good, but it’s not really trying to be flashy either. The panel gets bright enough for most indoor use and, for general browsing or watching videos around the house or even on a flight, it gets the job done. If anything, my biggest complaint on the display was just how smudgey it gets with fingerprints.

Hardware is a strong suit. The Alexa Tablet 8 has an aluminum build that comes in this gorgeous teal color as well as a softer “Rosette” pink (or “Graphite,” if you want to keep it boring). It’s thin and light and slips into a bag easily, and even fit into the back pocket of my jeans. Buttons are clicky and aspects such as the speakers actually do well enough – like the display, the speakers aren’t good, but they’re not bad by any stretch either.

Amazon has a folio cover available that’s simple but nice, with a fabric finish that’s grippy. It’s an extra $40, though, which feels like a bit much for a super-affordable Android slate.

The biggest question was around the software experience, and this is where this new effort really shines.

Alexa Tablet 8 ships with Android 16 out of the box, not FireOS or whatever Amazon was calling its forked experience on Fire Tablets (I genuinely refuse to go double-check because it’s a memory I’m thrilled to never have to revisit). It’s a pretty “stock” experience, with Amazon leaving aspects such as the notification shade/quick settings alone, and even the Settings menu feels pretty clean.

That’s not to say nothing has changed. The homescreen has a fairly standard layout , but with these neat little corner widgets – one a “dynamic” widget with scrolling content (mostly ads) and the other a shortcut for Alexa search. Amazon is pushing Alexa as a voice assistant pretty heavily on these tablets, but I’m happy to report it’s pretty easy to ignore if you choose. These tablets will be great additions to your Alexa ecosystem if you’re invested, but I’m simply not.

The biggest change you’ll find is the lockscreen, where Amazon’s base models all come with lockscreen ads. This saves you about $20 on the hardware, and it’s not that bad of an experience. That said, I’d absolutely spend the extra $20 at purchase to go without them, especially seeing as this blocks the ability to set a lockscreen wallpaper at all. It doesn’t cost anything more to remove them later, though, with a $20 charge through your Amazon account removing ads.

But what’s important here is that, for the fire time, Amazon is admitting defeat and installing the Google Play Store.

Alexa Tablet 8, and the rest of the lineup, has Google Play out of the box and access to any app you want. This means that you can, if you choose, fully ignore Amazon’s own services and do whatever you want. The smallest of examples: While Amazon’s built-in keyboard is fine, it’s wonderful that I don’t have to jump through hoops to switch over to Gboard. Fire Tablets were always an enticing value with a big catch, but Amazon’s change in this regard truly makes Alexa Tablets an attractive option for everyone – not just those willing to put up with the restriction or put in the work to hack Google apps on board.

Performance, despite the meager specs, is not awful. MediaTek’s low-end chip has just enough horsepower for general use, and 4GB of RAM isn’t as detrimental as I would have expected. It’s not fast by any means, but you could do a lot worse. There are 4GB Android tablets I’ve literally decided not to review because I couldn’t use them past the setup process, so the fact that Amazon is delivering a completely usable experience is a win in my book, even more so that I don’t actually mind using it. There’s a caveat to that, of course, and it’s that you shouldn’t expect to do a ton on this base model. For light browsing, reading Kindle books (or whatever other reading app since, you know, Google Play), or watching videos, this is great. For games, you’ll want to look elsewhere (and expect to spend more).

Alexa Tablet 8 isn’t some shining example of a perfect budget tablet, but I think it really nails the fundamentals. At its usual $230~ starting price, it’s a plenty-good deal for those in the market for an affordable Android slate, but the Black Friday pricing of just $150 is all the better – I just wish the model with 8GB of RAM was also on sale.

Amazon’s pivot to Alexa Tablets is great – though I hope the company knows full well that everyone is still going to call these Fire Tablets or Kindles or whatever combination of brands that spring to mind. But that reputation might work in Amazon’s favor at the end of the day. While there’s a huge portion of the audience that’s been left frustrated for years at the lack of Play Store support on Fire Tablets, they’ve still been a market success for the most part, so this shift is just a win for everyone. Users get a better experience, Amazon gets to shuffle its priorities away from a dead app store, and the market as a whole gets an affordable piece of hardware that doesn’t suck.
