Nearly half of Americans are making the same gamble with their holiday travel plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Air travel is getting more and more expensive. And as prices continue to soar, American travelers are becoming desperate for deals on flights. Oftentimes, they’re even delaying bookings or totally altering holiday travel in order to score reasonably priced plane tickets.

The findings come from a new report from the online travel agency and flight consolidator CheapOair, which surveyed 1,000 U.S. consumers to understand how travelers are making decisions around holiday travel promotions. The survey found that modern day fliers are more willing to book flights at the last minute, often waiting for promotions that happen on Black Friday, Cyber Monday, and Travel Tuesday. In fact, nearly half (49%) of the survey’s respondents said they have delayed bookings in order to shop holiday travel deals. Another 60% say they are likely to consider doing so in the future.

Still, while fliers are perhaps more deal-driven than ever before, they’re not consistently convinced that they’ll be able to find true savings. More than a quarter (26%) said they believe holiday deals are mostly marketing hype, whereas 17% believe the savings are totally legit. Another 57% say the deals are a mixed bag of both savings and hype.

It’s not all that surprising that consumer confidence is fading when it comes to shopping for deals, however. As companies increasingly utilize tactics like surveillance pricing—which relies on using consumer data and algorithms to adjust prices on things like groceries, home items, and even flights—many people worry about how transparent modern businesses are being with their marketing tactics. 

Last year, Delta Air Lines announced it would more widely deploy the method of price-setting. “The practice of dynamic pricing is certainly not new in the airline industry,” Kerry Tan, professor of economics at Loyola University Maryland, previously told Fast Company of the announcement. “Certainly, Delta, as with any other company, is profit-driven and stands to gain” from the tactic,” he said. 

Tan added: “As an economist, we assume that companies are profit maximizers, and so in a way I see this as companies really making a push toward that objective of profit maximization.”

It’s also not that surprising that customers are increasingly worried about the cost of air travel. That’s true because prices are undoubtedly reaching staggering new highs. According to federal data, airfare was up 22% this past September when compared with the year before. A September 2026 report from Hopper media also found that round-trip domestic airfare for Thanksgiving travel is up 31% from 2025, while Christmas travel is already up by 23%.

As prices keep climbing, cost is by far the biggest concern consumers have when it comes to holiday travel. Per the CheapOair survey, 39% say it’s their biggest worry compared with other concerns like flight delays and cancellations (18%), crowded airports (15%), and winter weather disrupting their plans (10%).

Still, savvy shoppers aren’t just sitting back and fretting. Some are altering their holiday travel plans altogether. While changing their travel destinations is an unpopular choice, with only 7% saying they’re open to the change, 43% will change their preferred travel dates in order to land a better price.

While it’s not unheard of to book flights on cheaper travel days, holiday travelers who do so risk missing out on family traditions or planned events. However, with soaring energy costs making travel less affordable than ever, missing the family football game or even the whole turkey dinner might be what it takes to go home for the holidays.
