Sandisk’s 8-for-1 Split Is Done. Here’s What Investors Should Do Next.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Sandisk's stock split is finished, so the only question left is whether its AI-fueled flash memory business can hold up as prices cool.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 26, 2026 at 4:00PM EST

When a stock splits, the headlines tend to make it sound like something big just happened, but for Sandisk (SNDK -5.57%) shareholders, the real decision starts now that the paperwork is done and the share price looks a lot friendlier.

Sandisk announced its 8-for-1 forward split alongside its fiscal first-quarter results on Oct. 29, giving every holder of record on Nov. 9 seven extra shares for each one they owned. The change became effective on Nov. 19, and split-adjusted trading began the next morning.

A stock split, which is when a company divides each existing share into several cheaper ones, works like cutting a pizza into more slices without adding any cheese. Sandisk itself said that its overall market capitalization and stockholder ownership percentages would not be affected, and it framed the move as a way to make shares more accessible, including to employees buying through its stock purchase plan.

What the split can't do is change the business, so that's where investors should focus.

Sandisk makes NAND flash, which is the memory chip that keeps your data when the power goes off, and AI has turned that once-sleepy product into a hot commodity.

In the quarter that ended Oct. 2, revenue jumped 378% year over year to $11 billion, data center sales rose 61% from the prior quarter, and gross margin, which is the share of each sales dollar left after the cost of making the product, reached 85%.

CEO David Goeckeler said in the release, "We have started fiscal year 2027 with another quarter of strong performance, with robust and growing demand for our products driving outpaced growth in our data center business."

The AI connection is more specific than it sounds, because AI models need fast storage to hold the context of a conversation. On the earnings call, Goeckeler said, "We believe this is a secular dynamic that is expanding NAND's role across the AI stack."

The part I find most reassuring is Sandisk's long-term customer deals, which it calls New Business Models or NBMs. About half of its fiscal 2027 supply and two-thirds of its fiscal 2028 supply are already committed, 95% of those deals are with investment-grade companies, and Goeckeler said the contracts have "become the predominant way we deal with our customers." CFO Luis Visoso went further on the call, saying, "Our NBM pipeline exceeds our supply throughout the end of the decade."

Those contracts matter because flash prices are cooling. Research firm TrendForce now expects NAND contract prices to fall as much as 5% in the first quarter of 2027, with declines spreading across all product categories after that. Concentration adds risk too, since two customers each made up 12% of revenue last quarter.

The stock has already priced in plenty of that worry. Shares closed at $87.71 on Nov. 23, roughly 70% below their split-adjusted 52-week high, and trade at a price-to-earnings ratio of about 5.6. Management guided for split-adjusted earnings of $6.88 to $7.13 per share in the current quarter, which puts the stock near 3 times annualized earnings.

Visoso also cautioned on the call that the March quarter "is normally our weakest sequential quarter," so results may look less dazzling by spring, but the company still bought back $5 billion of stock last quarter, and Visoso said, "Our repurchases reflect our conviction in the long-term value of the business and our disciplined approach to capital allocation."

I think long-term investors who can stomach big swings should consider a modest position in Sandisk, sized for a flash market that could get rougher before it gets better, and add only if the long-term contracts keep holding up as prices fall. The split changed the price tag, but the contracts will decide the value.
