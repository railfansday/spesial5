AST SpaceMobile: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Its custom ASIC chip pulls a tenfold capacity gain forward to satellite 34, a shift that matters more to long-term holders than the launch timeline alone.

On its third-quarter 2026 business update call, held on Nov. 9, 2026, AST SpaceMobile (ASTS -8.67%) announced plans to build its own reusable rocket alongside securing $1 billion in non-dilutive Japanese government funding. Gaining control over launch schedules and securing non-dilutive capital significantly reduce execution risks for the satellite network build-out. Three things management said stand out.

AST SpaceMobile is developing a 20-ton reusable rocket called Blacklift to lower launch expenses and eliminate reliance on third-party launch schedules. Reusable rockets give the business owner economic benefits and direct control over its orbital deployment schedule starting in late 2028.

The company is pulling forward its next-generation application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) -- a custom microchip designed for high-speed signal processing -- to BlueBird satellite 34. This hardware shift increases data throughput tenfold while keeping monthly factory output on track for six satellites in December 2026.

International governments want national control over satellite communications, creating opportunities for joint ventures and dedicated regional constellations. Japan's $1 billion subsidy demonstrates how sovereign demand provides capital without diluting existing shareholder equity.

Commercial deployment and Space Force contracts are accelerating. Proprietary phased arrays and $3.2 billion in cash provide key advantages as management tracks production throughput.

Long-term growth depends on manufacturing execution and launch control.
