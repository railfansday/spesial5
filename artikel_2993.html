A Look Back at the History Between LSU and Alabama: The Monopoly vs. The Innovator

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

What are you reading this article on? If you are reading this on an Apple device, you are holding ultimate proof that winning one out of three fights can still change history. Early on in the years of the personal computer era, Microsoft dominated the market while Apple was still operating out of the garage of Steve Jobs' parents. Apple did not survive by trying to replicate what Microsoft was doing; it decided to change the game entirely. Apple released the iPod in 2001 and the iPhone in 2007, paving the way to overtake Microsoft in technology sales and define the history of technology. LSU and Alabama are not too different in this dynamic. LSU's .328 win percentage against Alabama is much worse than a coin flip, but it is not full-on submission. Alabama boasts the win percentage, but LSU has the ability to create culture-shifting runs like 2019 that will forever be the measuring stick for any really good team in college football.

LSU and Alabama first played in 1895, with the Tigers taking a 12-6 victory. Since then, Alabama has taken command of the series, with a 58-27-5 record. For LSU, this is always a game that carries a lot of weight since every National Championship LSU has won or participated in has come with a win over Alabama in the regular season. It was already a heated rivalry, but Nick Saban switching sides poured more gas on the fire.Saban, while at LSU, was 4-1 against the Crimson Tide, with the lone loss coming in 2002 when the Tide came into Baton Rouge and shut out the Tigers 31-0. Saban and LSU would get their revenge a year later in Tuscaloosa, winning 27-3. Matt Mauck completed 24 of his 36 passes for 251 yards with two touchdowns and one interception. 130 of those yards went through Michael Clayton, who reeled in 12 passes with one for a score. Justin Vincent and Alley Broussard led the Tigers in rushing with a combined 27 carries for 162 yards and one score. LaRon Landry led the team in tackles with eight, while Marquise Hill was a force on the defensive line with one sack and three tackles for loss. Brodie Croyle was the quarterback for Alabama, completing 12 of his 33 passes for 154 yards with one interception thrown to Chad Lavalais. A dominating performance that led to LSU defeating Oklahoma in the Sugar Bowl for the National Championship.

Saban would then leave LSU after the 2004 season to become the head coach of the Miami Dolphins, something he said was one of the biggest mistakes of his career. Les Miles would take over the reins in Baton Rouge, winning 16-13 in 2005 and 28-14 in 2006. Saban would return to college football in 2007, becoming the head coach at Alabama following the removal of Mike Shula. Saban and Alabama would host LSU in his first year back in college in a wild game. The Tide would score first on a field goal before LSU would go on a 17-0 run capped off by a Jacob Hester rushing touchdown. Alabama would answer with a 24-point run of their own to make it 27-17 in favor of the Tide. The Tigers would answer back with a 61-yard touchdown pass from Matt Flynn to Demetrius Byrd and a 49-yard field goal from Colt David, tying the game up at 27. Alabama would retake the lead on a punt return from Javier Arenas. LSU would tie the game again late with a 32-yard touchdown pass to Early Doucet before Jacob Hester would fight for his second rushing touchdown of the game, securing the 41-34 win. That 2007 matchup marked the last time LSU was the betting favorite for a meeting.

Saban and Alabama would, of course, get their revenge in 2008 in Saban's return to Baton Rouge. It was the first time numerous fans got to act on the betrayal they felt by Saban leaving and returning to coach at one of their biggest rivals. The hatred for Saban was so strong that, for many years later, his wife, Mrs. Terry, would spread holy water on the field before games when they returned to Baton Rouge. The "homecoming" for Saban was a back-and-forth fight that led to overtime, where Jarrett Lee threw an interception, and Alabama answered with a rushing touchdown from John Parker Wilson a few plays later.

Alabama would take the 2009 matchup, with LSU taking the 2010 matchup. The 2011 matchup in Tuscaloosa pitted No.1 LSU vs. No.2 Alabama. Today, if someone told you a game would end 9-6, you would probably be wildly uninterested, but in 2011, this game was one of the best defensive showcases college football had ever seen. The first quarter was highlighted by two missed field goals by the Tide and an interception from Jarrett Lee, for LSU's first interception in five games at that point. The second quarter saw Alabama miss another field goal, which Bennie Logan blocked, but both teams put points on the board before the half ended with one field goal apiece. Coming out of the half, both teams would punt before Jarrett Lee threw another interception, leading to another Tide field goal. Near the end of the third quarter, Alabama would turn the ball over for the first time with Morris Claiborne picking off AJ McCarron and returning it for 33 yards. That set up another LSU field goal to make it 6-6. A bit later in the fourth quarter, Eric Reid would pick off Marquis Maze from a Wildcat setup, where Reid would make a great read on the play and steal the ball in midair from Michael Williams on the goal line. The game would eventually head to overtime, where Alabama would miss their fourth field goal of the game. LSU would take over, and Drew Alleman would kick the game-winner from 25 yards out. Both defenses were so smothering that listing out the offensive stats feels like a waste of time. Defensively, Ryan Baker led LSU in tackles with eight. Sam Montgomery led the Tigers with two sacks and tied for second in tackles with Eric Reid, with six each. Tyrann Mathieu, Brandon Taylor and Bennie Logan all tallied five tackles, while Taylor added one for loss. Kevin Minter and Ron Brooks also both added one tackle for loss. In all, the Tigers held the Tide to 295 yards of total offense. Alabama's defense was just as good, if not better, holding LSU to 239 yards of total offense. To highlight just how good both defenses were, LSU rolled out offensive stars like Michael Ford, Jordan Jefferson, Spencer Ware, Russell Sheppard, Kenny Hilliard, Ruben Randle and Odell Beckham, while Alabama had Trent Richardson and Eddie Lacy. LSU would finish the season undefeated. The Tigers and Crimson Tide would square off once again in the National Championship, where LSU would only cross the 50-yard line one time. Alabama would kick five field goals and score one touchdown but miss the extra point for a dominating 21-0 win.

That would be the first of an eight-game win streak Alabama and Saban would put together against LSU. In 2019, with Ed Orgeron at the helm for the Tigers, LSU would March into Tuscaloosa ranked second in the country to face the third-ranked Crimson Tide. Alabama would start with the ball and drive deep into Tiger territory before Tua Tagovailoa would fumble inside the 10-yard line, and it would be recovered by Ray Thornton. LSU, led by Joe Burrow, would drive down and find Ja'Marr Chase on a 33-yard score. The Tigers would add a field goal on their next offensive series before Jaylen Waddle would return a punt 77 yards for a touchdown to close out the first quarter with LSU leading 10-7. Burrow would answer right back on the first drive of the second quarter with a touchdown pass to Terrace Marshall. The PAT would be blocked, but LSU would hold a 16-7 lead. A few drives later, Alabama would answer with a touchdown and a missed PAT of their own to make it 16-13. LSU added a field goal on the next drive before the next possession, where Thaddeus Moss would make an incredible toe-drag catch on the sideline to set LSU up inside the one-yard line for Clyde Edwards-Helaire to add a rushing touchdown. Patrick Queen would intercept Tua on the next Tide drive, and LSU would score on the first play of the drive with Edwards-Helaire catching a touchdown pass from Burrow. LSU would lead 33-13 at the end of the first half. Alabama would attempt to storm back in the second half but a pair of rushing touchdowns from Clyde Edwards-Helaire would keep the Tide at bay as LSU held on to win 46-41. LSU would go on to complete the greatest run of all time, winning the National Championship.

LSU would only beat Saban one more time in his time at Alabama, and that was in 2022, with Brian Kelly leading the Tigers and Mason Taylor scoring on a two-point conversion in overtime to secure the win we talked about in the Contenders or Pretenders piece. Saban would finish with a 13-5 record against LSU while at Alabama.LSU and Alabama met just a year ago after the firing of Brian Kelly. Frank Wilson was the interim head coach. Garrett Nussmeier and Michael Van Buren would split time at quarterback, completing 23 of their 32 passes for 173 yards. Harlem Berry led LSU in rushing with 12 carries for 66 yards, while Zavion Thomas led receivers with five catches for 49 yards. LSU would not score a touchdown in the game and only scored from three Damian Ramos field goals. Ty Simpson completed 21 of his 35 passes for 277 yards and one touchdown of 33 yards to Ryan Coleman-Williams. Daniel Hill led Tide rushers with seven carries for 21 yards and a touchdown, while Germie Bernard led receivers with three catches for 79 yards. Harold Perkins led the Tigers defensively with nine total tackles and one sack. LSU would lose that one 20-9.

LSU and Alabama will meet for the last time consecutively since before 1964. They will skip the matchup in 2027 under current SEC scheduling procedures and play again in 2028 in Tuscaloosa. They will also skip 2029 and will not play again in Baton Rouge until 2030 at the earliest. Kickoff for this year's matchup is set for 2:30 PM CST in Baton Rouge, with the game set to be broadcast on ABC. The Tigers are 6-point favorites.
