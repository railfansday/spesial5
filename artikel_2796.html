Dodgers will be involved in Tarik Skubal's free agency, but will they sign him?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Dodgers' early offseason to-do list includes a meeting with top free agent Tarik Skubal, the two-time Cy Young winner who they acquired at the trade deadline this year.

Other than the Dodgers' National League Championship Series exit, the three-month fling was as happy of a partnership as either side could have hoped.

Skubal jelled with the group immediately, becoming especially close friends with fellow lefty Blake Snell, who promised that he'd worked to recruit Skubal back this offseason. And after posting a 2.61 ERA in 10 starts, Skubal, the Dodgers' Game 1 starter in both playoff series, limited postseason opponents to a total of three runs.

"I know that he enjoyed his time here, we loved having him, so I think that's certainly helpful as far as these conversations go," Dodgers general manager Brandon Gomes said Tuesday at General Managers meetings. "And outside of that, he's earned the right to be in this position and figure out what's best for him and his family."

That position, while it guarantees him a highly lucrative contract, is complicated by the impending lockout. Skubal's agent, Scott Boras, when asked during his media availability Wednesday whether he'd consider a shorter deal with a higher average annual value, said he wouldn't discuss contract negotiations. But creativity around the structure of a deal could help counter teams' anxieties over a possible salary cap in the next Collective Bargaining Agreement.

It's clear already that CBA uncertainty won't fully freeze the market in November. But executives and agents alike have expressed skepticism that the expected lockout will inspire the same kind of spending flurry that arose in 2021, the end of the last CBA cycle.

Boras, however, argued against the notion that salary cap talk could put a damper on the superstar market before Dec. 1, when the lockout is expected to go into effect.

"Much like 2021, there's a clear intent on a group of players, and we have no reservations by any teams that they're going to operate within their abilities and freedoms because they want they don't want to lose to their competitors," Boras told reporters. "They want the market to be what it is for them, and they're utilizing their rights to compete. I think they're very concerned about losing the opportunity to sign select, key players, and they're pursuing it aggressively."

The Dodgers are among the teams planning to meet with Skubal, a group that reportedly includes the Mets, Padres and others.

The Dodgers certainly have a track record of splashy offseason deals with eye-popping numbers. Look no further than last offseason, when they broke records with their signings of closer Edwin Díaz (three years, $69 million, highest AAV for a relief pitcher) and right fielder Kyle Tucker (four years, $240 million, highest present-day AAV).

Even the Dodgers' trade-deadline acquisition of Skubal appeared to be superfluous at the time. Once two-way phenom Shohei Ohtani's health forced him to give up on pitching for the rest of the season, however, Skubal's addition maintained the Dodgers rotation's status as one of the most talented in MLB history.

They were not, however, among the most aggressive teams before the last lockout. And their approach to Skubal's free agency will hinge, in part, on how they evaluate their starting pitching needs.

"We've talked about the different paths that you can go down, and what does that look like," Gomes said. "Do you need it? I don't know. Do you want it? Maybe. You may not know that you need it until it's too late. So I think it's balancing all of this, as different scenarios play out. … The floor right now is really high. We do have a good amount of depth in our pitching. So now it's just taking into account, what makes sense for us based on different avenues of how things shake out."

As the Dodgers project out the potential of their rotation, they can reasonably expect improvement from some of their younger pitchers. They were pleased with the step forward that right-hander Roki Sasaki took this year.

"Expect him to take another big step forward," Gomes said, "and really become the front-line-type guy that he knows that he can be and we know he can be."

Regardless of lefty Justin Wrobleski's second-half regression, he earned his first All-Star nod with a dominant first half. Though right-hander Emmet Sheehan had an up-and-down season, the Dodgers continue to believe in his upside. And they expect Gavin Stone, who last pitched in the majors in 2024, to be part of the equation, after a long road back from shoulder surgery.

They still have the star power of Yoshinobu Yamamoto, a two-time All-Star and 2025 World Series MVP, Snell, a two-time Cy Young winner, and Tyler Glasnow.

Ohtani's return to the mound, however, raises the most questions. The Dodgers expect him to return to two-way duties, that much is clear. But they have yet to lay out a plan for how to keep him healthy for the season while doing so.

"I think it's just making sure that we are taking all of the information into account and trying to set up a system that allows him to pitch and be successful and be healthy towards the end," Gomes said. "Because we know how dominant he can be on the mound."
