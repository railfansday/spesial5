Jaguars Winners & Losers From Week 11: Miserable Day At MetLife Stadium

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

There have been some historically bad Jaguars games through the years. Relative to expectations and current team trends, that one against the New York Giants has got to be up there.

The Jacksonville Jaguars fell on the road at the Giants, 23-18, in sloppy conditions with even sloppier play. The Jags were overpowered along the offensive line, couldn't throw the ball, and had an even tougher time not committing penalties as the team reverted to a weird form of itself that we saw briefly at the start of this season and even more often at the start of last season.

The refereeing in that game will be the subject of discussion for months to come but the Jags should never have been in a position to be one touchdown away from winning or losing to a team with J.J. McCarthy and Jameis Winston at quarterback. The worst part is this game is largely for nothing. The Jags had far more to lose in the mid-afternoon slot against an NFC team than they ever had to gain. And boy did they ever lose.

Because I mostly feel emotionally obligated to have a winner from Sunday's game, we will go with the defensive line. New York isn't known for stellar offensive line play and the Jags outmatched that line for most of the game. McCarthy had guys in his face and despite just one sack from Josh Hones-Allen, this unit was overall impactful.

Especially in the second half, it almost became hard to count how many times McCarthy was skipping balls to his receivers because he had so much pressure on him. In total, the Jags batted away nine passes. Travon Walker had two of those swats.

The defensive unit had an up-and-down day but they certainly were more consistent than the offense. The defensive line did its part. Not many others can say that.

Now on to the not-so-fun stuff that was the rest of that seemingly endless afternoon game. Jacksonville just never got going and every little thing this team did in New Jersey felt so difficult. Watching that offense try to execute felt like trying to stuff Jordan Davis through the eye of a needle. It was painful.

Trevor Lawrence (and we will talk about him below) was out of sorts the entire day. He had a weird finger injury and then later suffered a seemingly rolled ankle. His offensive line did him no favors as he was harassed all game long. On almost every throw, Lawrence was heaving the ball off his back foot and being taken to the ground. Granted, the offensive line is patchwork at best currently and the Giants have a good defensive line but something has to be done to help this dude when clearly the rhythm is off. That didn't ever materialize.

Jacksonville ran fine at times (just 77 yards on the ground in total) but that part never got going either. You would think in the driving rain that the game plan would be to run the ball, but the Jags couldn't get that aspect of their game going. It was just a brutal effort all around.

Defensively, things were not much better. Ventrell Miller was busting skulls and Arik Armstead had a few good reps but everyone else looked to be asleep at the wheel, too.

Sunday's game reminded me of that Seattle Seahawks game from last year. Yes, the final was just a one-score difference at 20-12 but if you actually watched the game it was so much more lopsided than that. Seattle had seven sacks in that game, the Giants had three. The Jags committed 10 penalties against Seattle and 13 against New York. Neither game featured the Jags running the ball well.

All-around bad day. And it only got worse as injuries piled up.

Whatever the case may be, something was really off about Lawrence.

He had undoubtedly his worst game of the year in MetLife after playing like a real MVP candidate for the earlier part of the season. His offensive line didn't help, but it also didn't seem like Lawrence was doing much to help himself. Yet again, red zone struggles plagued Jacksonville as the team kicked a handful of close field goals and turned the ball over at the New York 11. Despite not counting as an interception, Lawrence even threw a pick on a two-point conversion.

It's a real shame that that game will largely be the NFL's perspective on Lawrence for the foreseeable future. With much of the country watching, the Jags laid an egg. Fair or not, a lot of blame will fall on the head of Lawrence.

Part of the reason the Jags had an awful day offensively was because tackles Walker Little and Cole Van Lanen got their asses handed to them. As previously mentioned, it's no question New York has a solid defensive line but good golly they killed the tackles. Spin move after spin move, Abdul Carter was whipping up on anyone the Jags tried to block him with. It didn't even look like Little and Van Lanen had ever played football before.

To make matters worse, with Anton Harrison already sidelined, Little got hurt. He wasn't carted off like Harrison but an injury to him further cuts into the already thin depth of the offensive line group.

To go along with a handful of penalties committed (or maybe not) but the tackles, this group sucked it up. If there is a flaw on this team, it's the offensive line group.

While the Giants defense picked on Lawrence all day, McCarthy decided to pick on cornerback Montaric Brown on the other end.

McCarthy and the Giants receiver room targeted Brown early and often and the veteran corner did little to help himself. With Jarrian Jones suffering an injury, it was up to Brown and Travis Hunter to man the outside. Who would you throw against in that scenario?

Jones, Little and running back Bhayshul Tuten all got banged up against the Giants. As we saw with the offensive line, things are scary up there with injuries mounting. Now the defensive backfield took another hit. If the Giants can expose the Jags' secondary with McCarthy throwing it, imagine what could happen with a better quarterback.

There really is no use screaming into the ether about the ref show that we all saw Sunday. The Parker Washington no-touchdown call is truly one of the more baffling things anyone will ever see. Sure, some of those penalties committed by the Jags were legit as we do know they are liable to rack those up from time to time. But certainly not all of those calls were legit.

The Jags couldn't have beaten anyone yesterday. They were given an even smaller chance to win with the refs as involved as they were. We could get all conspiratorial about that, but what's the use? I have no idea what was up with that game but something was way off. As bad as Lawrence was, the refs were far worse. What a way for the Jags to show themselves off to the world in that 4:25 slate.

Well, that sucked. On to Tennessee as the Jags look to sweep the lowly Titans on Sunday back at home. Wins in the AFC are what matters now.
