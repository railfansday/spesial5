Vikings' Kirk Cousins deal might doom NFC West: NFL trade deadline winners, losers

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Another eventful NFL trade deadline didn't disappoint on the final day the league's teams could swap players ahead of the 4 p.m. ET deadline on Tuesday, Nov. 10.

The most impactful move of the final week came down with little more than an hour to spare, the Las Vegas Raiders agreeing to send 38-year-old QB Kirk Cousins back to the Minnesota Vikings for a third-round pick in the 2027 NFL draft. The deal materialized not 24 hours after the Vikes were embarrassed in a 23-6 loss to the Buffalo Bills on "Monday Night Football," when Minnesota's offense couldn't find the end zone and struggled to simply get first downs.

But the Vikings weren't the only team that's been busy. Here's a fuller breakdown of this year's trade deadline winners and losers.

He leaves the rebuilding Raiders, his tutelage of No. 1 pick Fernando Mendoza complete, in order to return to Minnesota and pursue the ring that's eluded him throughout his 15-year career − six of those seasons spent in the Twin Cities. Cousins, who had 20 TD passes in games with the Silver and Black, is playing his best football since 2023, when what had been his presumed final season in Minnesota was cut short by an Achilles injury. Pretty cool that Cousins, who wasn't enthused about leaving for Atlanta in 2024, gets to return and maybe complete some unfinished business.

In a stacked division where every other team has bona fide stud at quarterback, the Vikes at least upgrade significantly at the position by bringing Cousins back in light of the failed Kyler Murray experiment. Armed with one of the elite defenses the league has seen in the 21st century, Minnesota at least gives itself a more viable Super Bowl shot than it had 24 hours ago.

"It's the best defense in the National Football League, and they have nothing to show for their work because of the ineptitude on the offensive side of the ball," ESPN analyst and former NFL defensive lineman Chris Canty said prior to the acquisition of Cousins.

Seems that work should now be more appropriately rewarded.

Their acquisiton of All-Pro DT Jeffery Simmons two weeks ago might very well wind up being the most impactful trade made over the past six weeks − particularly given how seamlessly Simmons seems to be integrating. Chicago has won both of its games since his arrival.

It was a painful (additional) reset, notably the decision to part with Simmons and SS Amani Hooker. But GM Mike Borgonzi and first-year coach Robert Saleh now have two first-round picks next year, two second-round picks next year and two first-round picks in 2028.

After trading WR A.J. Brown during the offseason, C/G Cam Jurgens last month and DT Jordan Davis yesterday, the Philadelphia Eagles' renowned personnel guru now owns 10 draft selections (first or second round) spread across the 2027-29 drafts. The Eagles may be down right now, but they won't be out for long.

But McCarthy, who's now 2-0 as the New York Giants' starter, seems as if he landed in the right spot. And given Cousins is back with the Vikings, perhaps this is your classic win-win.

Both Keenan Allen (San Francisco 49ers) and Josh Downs (Kansas City Chiefs) were rescued from a sinking ship and now both get a shot to pursue a championship and, in Downs' case, maybe a sizable second contract as he plays out the remainder of his rookie deal with Patrick Mahomes throwing to him.

It received an unmistakable message Monday, when the team aggressively obtained S Jessie Bates III and DTs Jordan Davis and Davon Godchaux to bolster a defense that recently lost S Eric Murray and DT DaVon Hamilton to season-ending injuries. The Jags, who should be on a glide path to a second straight AFC South crown and, quite possibly, the No. 1 seed in the conference, are undeniably in hot pursuit of the franchise's first Super Bowl berth in a year when the AFC seems fairly wide open.

Bates and Hooker both brought second-round picks to their former teams, a fairly phenomenal return given the position they play − and neither of them routinely man the ever-challenging slot corner role.

The Colts' Jonathan Taylor, Eagles' Saquon Barkley, Titans' Tony Pollard, Ravens' Derrick Henry and Jets' Breece Hall were among the backs whose names were floated as potential trade targets in recent weeks. Given the rebuild Indianapolis has initiated and Pollard's expiring deal, it seemed a veritable foregone conclusion both would change addresses. But it wasn't to be, with tailbacks apparently not as valuable as safeties.

Beginning your tenure as the Raiders' QB1 against the Seattle Seahawks' "Dark Side" defense, which isn't even three weeks removed from forcing three-time Super Bowl MVP Mahomes to endure the worst start of his NFL career? Good luck, pal.

Second-year GM James Gladstone gave up a second-round pick to effectively rent Bates, plus a first- and second-rounder for Davis, who most definitely isn't the second coming of Jeffery Simmons. It's time to win in Duval County. Now.

LB Daiyan Henley was the only Bolt to bolt, sent packing to the AFC West-leading Denver Broncos in a fortuitous twist for him.

With Puka Nacua set to join Davante Adams on injured reserve, a move for a veteran wide receiver seemed almost incumbent here. But Sean McVay and Les Snead will apparently roll with Konata Mumpfield and Tutu Atwell for better or, more likely, worse.

With the Vikings shored up by the Cousins acquisition, the reigning champion Seattle Seahawks and presumed 2026 champion Los Angeles Rams – both are currently 5-4, which is worse than the Green Bay Packers (6-3), last-place residents in the NFC North – can no longer bank on Minnesota's downward trajectory continuing. And, yes, while NFC North teams could attrit themselves to a degree in the latter part of the regular season, so will the Seahawks and Rams, who better get hot fast given all three of the conference's wild-card slots are currently projected to go to the NFC North.
