Average Four Factor Index Power Rankings: 2026-2027 NBA Season Week 6

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

In contrast to the week prior, this last week has seen remarkable stability in the aFFI power rankings, with no team moving more than three spots in any direction. However, this lack of dynamism doesn't mean that we haven't gained valuable information, as I'll detail below. Furthermore, I am introducing a new deep-dive section on the Spurs aFFI performance, so please let me know what you think!

We'll get to the broader rankings (and San Antonio's position within them) shortly, but before that I'd like to provide a little aFFI deep-dive to illustrate how the Silver and Black have been performing in individual games, and how those performances have driven its season-long aFFI-value.

The quickest way to summarize the last week is that San Antonio's overall aFFI (i.e., it's "season aFFI") is little changed, having ticked down slightly from 59.43 to 59.13. The three games they have played since last Friday include two impressive wins against admittedly weak competition, as well as a frustrating loss against Portland in which a brilliant performance by Wemby was undermined by weak play across the rest of the team.

Digging into the "game aFFI" values for each of these contests (which capture the Spurs' four factor performance relative to their opponent in each individual game), we see that these contests were decided in very different ways. For example, San Antonio's win over New Orleans was driven entirely by an exceptional effective field goal percentage differential of +14.5 percentage points, which corresponds to a ∆EFG% percentile value of 94.32 (i.e., 94.32% of all other regular season effective field goal percentage differentials achieved by NBA teams since the 2012-2013 season have been worse). In contrast, the Spurs ∆EFG% percentile in the Sacramento game was just 42.68, but they were excellent with respect to every other factor, especially their FTM/FTA differential. San Antonio's effective field goal percentage differential was also slightly below-median in the Blazers loss, but a dismal ∆TOV% percentile (13.6) pushed their game aFFI down significantly.

Looking at the league as a whole, we see that not much has changed since last week, especially at the top. In fact, six of the top ten teams (including San Antonio) have the same ranking as before, and the four remaining teams have moved up or down by just one spot. Furthermore, the top four teams remain exceptionally close according to their season-long aFFI, with just 1.2 points separating first-ranked Minnesota (60.33) from the fourth-ranked Spurs (59.13). These four teams have been extremely close for most of the young season, and it will be interesting to see if any one of them begins to stand above the rest in the weeks ahead.

Across the entire league, the Miami Heat have achieved the largest rise in aFFI ranking, moving up three spots since last week and getting within a half-point of the top ten. Several teams have moved up two spots, with Dallas being the highest-ranking member of that set at #13.

On the opposite end of the spectrum, Cleveland and OKC have both dropped three positions since last Friday and appear poised to fall further, though from a practical point of view these two team's have divergent outlooks. Specifically, Cleveland could likely continue its poor play and remain in the playoff picture, whereas the injury-plagued Thunder have already fallen out of the play-in set in the West. Even though OKC is expected to get some of its key players back before the end of the season, those reinforcements may simply arrive too late to make any difference.

Although the overall aFFI rankings have remained pretty static this week, Figure 3 reveals that a more detailed set of tiers may be emerging:

Clear Contenders (5 teams) — Not much has changed at the top, except to say that last week's "emerging contenders list" appears to be splitting in two, with five teams (Minnesota, Memphis, Houston, San Antonio, and the Lakers) setting themselves apart at the very top. Movement within the top four teams has been fluid, with teams moving up and down one or two spots depending on the outcome of individual games. Los Angeles sits slightly farther back but may rise or fall dramatically as their schedule gets significantly tougher in the days ahead (starting tonight against the Spurs).

Potential Contenders (4 teams) — Outside of the top five, aFFI values begin spreading out, with a gap of about 3.4 points separating sixth-ranked Boston (57.8) from ninth-ranked New York (54.41). All four of the teams within this range have shown flashes of brilliance and an ability to beat the best (see Portland's victory over San Antonio on Wednesday); however, they have also lacked consistency and each have six losses.

Likely Playoff/Play-in Participants (8 teams) — Within this eclectic group, Philadelphia and Atlanta continue to stand out as having exceptional records relative to their aFFI ranking. This is particularly true of the 76ers, who sit firmly atop the East despite having a middling point differential of +2.2 and an aFFI value of just 51.69. Similarly, the Hawks' aFFI score suggests that they should be a slightly below 0.500 team, yet they are currently 11-6 and seeded fourth in the East. It is still very early in the season, but the true nature of both teams may be revealed soon, as they have difficult schedules over the next week.

Fading Hope (6 teams) — Some of these teams are probably not too surprised to fall into this category; in particular, having marginal playoff hopes but no real chance at success seems to be the default setting in Chicago and Orlando. Fans of the remaining teams in this set probably have more complex emotions. For example, Washington fans are likely torn between joy over not being terrible for the first time in years and the haunting realization that their offseason additions are overpaid and underwhelming. Indiana and Toronto fans are doubtless disappointed thus far, though the Raptors can at least take solace in the fact that Kawhi Leonard's injury has played a large role in their lackluster season to date (shocker). Finally, the Suns are likely feeling pretty good about their chances to move up, as they have played quite well after starting the season terribly.

Was There Ever Really a Chance? (5 teams) — Every member of this group (Brooklyn, the Clippers, Utah, New Orleans, and Charlotte) is effectively eliminated from the playoff picture barring any shocking developments, but I suspect their respective fan bases feel quite differently about that position. For example, it is hard to imagine anyone thinking that New Orleans, Brooklyn, or the Clippers had a realistic chance of success heading into this season. However, there was some buzz surrounding Utah's potential, and quite a few people thought that the Hornets' offseason moves were going to vault them firmly into the East's playoff picture. They may still get there, but only with a dramatic turn of the tide.

Kings' Corner (2 teams) — On the bright side, both the Kings and Bucks have doubled their season win total in the last week, which allows both fanbases to use the plural "wins" for the first time all season. Unfortunately, it seems likely that the NBA will force these clubs to play all of their remaining games, so the fans, players, and staff are doomed to continue suffering until Spring.
