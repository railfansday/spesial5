3 thoughts as the Mavericks face another winnable matchup at home against the Los Angeles Clippers

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Dallas Mavericks (8-7) remain an interesting case study in the topsy-turvy NBA ahead of Saturday's clash with the Los Angeles Clippers (4-10), and their own penchant for wild swings in performance level are only part of that story.

They've got significant injuries holding them back in some ways, but that comes against the backdrop of players like Zaccharie Risacher and Caleb Martin, who are enjoying various levels of resurgence this year. Kyrie Irving is back and generally playing well, but how back is he really, and how committed are the Mavericks to seeing Irving finish the season with the team? Naji Marshall is playing the role in terms of minutes of a guy who just got extended for three years after setting a career-high scoring mark, but he's averaging just 10.9 points and 2.8 rebounds per game in November, so what gives there? Marshall seems to simply be the lesser evil right now in the mid-off between himself and Max Christie.

The stew is pungent and rich, and it should not be overlooked that, despite the mounting question marks, an 8-7 record is at least one step ahead of schedule as we near the season's quarter pole. The best part about that record is the chance to make it 9-7 against another vulnerable opponent at American Airlines Center on Saturday.

The Clip Show this year is a Darius Garland-Brook Lopez-Rui Hachimura production, with a little Keaton Wagler and Derrick Jones Jr. sprinkled in thus far. The 38-year-old Lopez, in particular, averaging 15.4 points on better than 55% shooting from the floor, is an inspiration for all of us who are past our prime. He's scored 23 or more in three of the Clippers' last four games and 20 or more in five of the last seven. That includes games against the San Antonio Spurs (23) and the Oklahoma City Thunder (26). He' shooting lights out from 3-point range — nearly 45% to start the year.

And Lopez isn't the only Clipper doing work from long range this season. LA is second in the NBA in team 3-point percentage, at 39.2%. Hachimura and Jones Jr. are also hitting about two per game from deep on surprisingly solid shooting clips. Jones' 50% mark on 3.7 attempts per game thus far this year will likely regress, but he's been feeling it from out there to say the least. Max Strus, Kriss Dunn, Gradey Dick and Wagler are all shooters as well.

But yet, they're 4-10. They don't rebound well, they turn the ball over the second most (17.6 per game) in the NBA and they rank 25th in defensive rating (117.2). A tale as old as the peach-basket net, really. Irving and Cooper Flagg should be able to have their way with this bunch, as they did on Wednesday against the flailing Golden State Warriors.

Mavericks first-year head coach Dusty May was asked on Friday if the team is close to having center Dereck Lively II back in action. He gave a very Clintonesque answer, questioning the technical definition of the word "close," which is never a good sign. Yes, Bill, er, Dusty, the whole question does center around what the definition of the word "is" is.

"I asked the same question this morning & close is a term that was used by D-Live," May said, according to the Dallas Morning News. "And when you look at it from the magnitude that he's been out for a while. He's doing a lot more now, but close, I'm not sure what close is. I would have to defer to our medical staff on that. He's in great spirits. He's moving well. I don't know what the exact ramp-up and build-up is, but he's progressing."

"I'm not sure what close is," is doing a lot of heavy lifting in that answer. Close is close. Can you put a number on the number of days or weeks away the player is? No? Ok, then it's not close. It's far.

The whole saga serves to weaken the premise that the Mavs' frontcourt was a definite strength coming into the season. The forwards, led by Flagg and a mildly resurgent PJ Washington, are playing well, but the center position remains a revolving door of things thrown against a wall. The ancient Lopez may find himself in a comfortable spot against the Mavs on Saturday as Dallas looks for a stopgap, but the rest of the team can compensate in other matchups.

The Mavericks have done well against 3-point shooters to start the season, with opponents shooting just 33.4% from distance against them through 15 games. That's the fifth-best mark in the league, and before you make the "variance" claim, just know, that includes a game at the Celtics in which Boston made 23-of-55 (42%) and at the Timberwolves in which Minnesota canned 15-of-32 (47%).

There appears to be something to the Mavs' ability to close out on shooters. If Dallas can limit the Clippers' production from deep, the Mavericks have the ability to make quick work of a Los Angeles side that seems to lack identity above all else.

The Mavericks and the Clippers tip off from American Airlines Center shortly after 7 p.m. on Saturday.
