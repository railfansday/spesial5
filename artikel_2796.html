All eyes on not only Fernando Mendoza’s growth, but Klint Kubiak too

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

In terms of accountability, the Las Vegas Raiders have that locked down at the all-important head coach and quarterback spot with Klint Kubiak and Fernando Mendoza, respectively.

After the team's 21-13 defeat at hands of the visiting Seattle Seahawks this past Sunday — dropping the team to 4-6 overall — both Kubiak and Mendoza went up the podium to shoulder the blame.

"I thought overall when we lost Brock (Bowers), you definitely saw a big change in production," Kubiak said after the game when asked about the simple gameplan and stagnant offense against Seattle. "So I look at myself; I have to do a much better job of moving the ball, getting and spreading the ball around."

So I got us in the wrong check, and then they sacked me, and that's why a lot of the sacks today, if not all of them, were on me, not on the offensive line," Mendoza said in particular of Seahawks nickel corner Devon Witherspoon sacking him twice off slot blitzes. "I thought they played great and displayed great leadership today, and I need to be better, especially with those DB blitzes."

Watching the Raiders franchise quarterback getting absolutely walloped by 'Spoon and other Seahawks defenders was certainly not ideal.

But welcoming the brunt of the blame and pointing the finger at themselves, one could even label the Silver & Black's promising head coach and quarterback combo the "I-Twins" as their quick to pin the blame on themselves and note they must improve for the overall good of the team. And that's an endearing trait for two key figures in the organization that are rookies in their respective roles.

While we won't know what's said behind closed doors at Raiders headquarters, the practice field, or in the locker room game days — there have been few instances where I was privy too and within earshot of tirades in post game locker room access — we were given a glimpse of Kubiak's fury when the CBS broadcast captured the moment he was talking to his offensive line on the sidelines.

So while it's admirable Kubiak stands in front of media row and says it's "my fault", it's good to see that Kubiak doesn't absolve his team of their own responsibilities.

But the following day, during his media session on Monday, Kubiak ensured it's also a "we" thing.

"I think overall, as an offensive line, there's a solid first half of run game production followed by a very poor half of protection, and that's not all on them," the Raiders rookie head coach said. "That's on getting open, that's on getting rid of the ball, that's on play calls. It's a team thing. So, we win together and we lose together."

Kubiak was also quick to note that his side of the ball — the offense — didn't play enough complementary football when Rob Leonard's defense helped maintain a 10-3 advantage against Seattle — precarious as that lead may be. Sure, losing Bowers — an elite pass catcher who Kubiak deploys as equal parts wide receiver and tight end — scuttled things and plans went awry against the Seahawks. And once Mike Macdonald recognized that Mendoza can not only throw dimes but also fire lasers on the run and take off to gain yards with his legs, the Seahawks' defensive mastermind dialed up man concepts with blitzes.

Mendoza said getting punched in the face gives him the opportunity to watch his mistakes and grow from them. Kubiak, too. And it's integral that duo take these tough growing pains and apply the lessons going forward.

Because what Seattle put on tape defensively against the rookie quarterback will become a regular occurrence — until — Mendoza shows consistently that he can identify the pressure, adjust protection, let the other 10 players on offense what's up, and burn the defense when they bring the pain. The good old Uno reverse card, if you will.

That noted, the 6-foot-5, 225-pound No. 1 overall pick from the 2026 NFL Draft showed he is NFL ready by not only standing in the pocket and progressing through his read and firing, scrambling for positive yards when he needed to, but also hanging onto the football when he got waylaid. Even when Mendoza was rocked, he didn't suddenly see ghosts and get happy feet. He got up, went back to the huddle, barked out the play and went about his business. The mental and physical toughness are ideal.

The footwork, the arm, the legs, and the mind all show Mendoza can play at the pro level and if protected, can succeed. And the miscues from the 23-year-old Indiana product are fixable.

Now, it's about Kubiak, offensive coordinator Andrew Janocko, quarterback coach Mike Sullivan, offensive line boss Rick Dennison to speak to their players and get the offense aligned. Protect the franchise and good things happen. And franchise, take care of yourself and don't take those hits that can cause fright — for the fanbase and yourself.

Kubiak provided insights on what he wants to see going forward.

"Well, remain a passer. Number one, keep your eyes downfield. Remain a passer like he did to Jack Bech on that left sideline, like he did to Ian (Thomas) early in the game, getting through his progression," the head coach and play caller said. "Remain a passer, and then obviously protect yourself when you start to run down the field."

"Yeah, it wasn't up to our standard for quarterback hits and sacks," Kubiak said about the six sacks and 12 quarterback hits Mendoza absorbed. "That's an entire offensive stat. It's getting open fast, that's protecting better, and that's him getting rid of the ball. There's three things there."

While it'd be grand if Kubiak, Janocko, Dennison and Co. can get the run blocking to be on par with the pass protection to give their young quarterback a ground game he can rely upon, that's been a longstanding issue when heady veteran Kirk Cousins was under center.

But there's signs that Mendoza has the ability to become the leader — not hopeful leader as he said above — of the Raiders offense.

And boy, that'll be a sight to behold, when it does happen.
