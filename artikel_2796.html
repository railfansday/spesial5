Sharks Insight: Why Is San Jose “Boring”?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The San Jose Sharks want to run. They want to be fun.

But the Calgary Flames wouldn't let them, smothering the Sharks 5-2 on Tuesday night.

Per Stathletes, it was actually the Flames that ran: San Jose was outchanced 5-1 in Rush Scoring Chances in All Situations.

That's a personal KPI, when I evaluate these young Sharks this season. They're not a team that grinds, first and foremost.

Their best games this year? The games where the San Jose Sharks looked like the San Jose Sharks?

On Oct. 1, a 4-3 OT victory over the Florida Panthers, San Jose had a 7-2 Rush Scoring Chances advantage. On Nov. 9, a 3-2 shootout win over the New York Islanders, 10-3. On Nov. 15, a 4-2 victory over the Nashville Predators, the closest thing to a signature win for the Sharks this season, 6-2.

The bigger question: Why couldn't San Jose run on Calgary?

I know a significant part of the fanbase is howling for head coach Ryan Warsofsky's head and blaming him for making the Sharks "boring".

And there's some truth in that, in this sense. Warsofsky is trying to instill defensive principles into the team, and clearly, there have been growing pains with applying these principles. For sure, it's not all on the students, it's also on the teachers, too.

Ideally, good defense will lead to good offense, that's the winning formula, but that hasn't happened consistently yet.

The other side of it, there's excellent reason for Warsofsky to place some guard rails on his team. You want to play fast, but if you play too fast, you're out of control.

San Jose had Calgary on its heels for the first five minutes of the contest, highlighted by an Adam Gaudette goal.

But then, Will Smith (2) lost his place as the high defensive forward on the forecheck, doubling up Zach Whitecloud (28) with Macklin Celebrini (71). Whitecloud is able to punch the puck ahead to Joel Farabee (11), who Sam Dickinson (6), inexplicably, chases. And because Smith had doubled on Whitecloud, he lost his man, Matt Coronato (27). Jacob Trouba (65) has to deal with a 3-on-1 that never should've happened in the first place.

This is cheating for offense, at its worst.

The next question is one of belief: Do you think this San Jose Sharks squad, as currently constituted, is equipped to trade chances with other teams, and win more games than they lose?

Personally? I don't think so. They have one elite player in Celebrini, versus say the offense-first Edmonton Oilers, who have Connor McDavid, Leon Draisaitl, Evan Bouchard, and more polished depth. San Jose will have more elite players one day, if you believe Will Smith, Michael Misa, Ivar Stenberg, and company are going to get there, but the youngsters aren't quite there yet, not on a nightly basis.

So instead, you had San Jose banging their heads against the wall against a structured Calgary side last night, over and over.

"We were trying to make plays when there was no play to be made," Alex Nedeljkovic observed.

The Flames backcheck Stenberg (41) hard, but there's a better play to be made than losing it in the neutral zone, even if it's a soft dump forward for Smith to try to claim. At least, you gain some territory and push the Flames back.

Hate it or love it, Warsofsky is trying to coach some of this out of the Sharks, which is, big picture, absolutely what he should be doing, if you're interested in winning a Stanley Cup one day.

"We can still make plays out there," Gaudette said. "We just got to choose the right times to do it."

When you can't make plays, you have to have a B-game, a grind game, which San Jose was sorely lacking last night, especially in the absence of injured winger Kiefer Sherwood.

That's the balance that Cup contenders strike, biding their time with their B-game to set up their A-game. Be boring, until it's time to have fun.

Finally, and here's another question of do you blame the players or the coaches or both for why San Jose couldn't run on Calgary on Tuesday?

This isn't on Celebrini, but simply an example of how relentless the Flames were, on both sides of the puck.

If you want to run the other team out of the building, you have to outwork them, too. The Sharks failed on both counts on Tuesday, hence having to work the next day, an hour-long practice that concluded with a bag skate.
