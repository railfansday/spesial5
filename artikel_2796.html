40-year-old Josh Johnson making historic Cardinals start not seen since Jim Thorpe

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It is rarely a positive development for a team that it must start a fourth different quarterback in four weeks.

For the Cardinals, it is somewhat historic.

The quarterback-desperate Cardinals will turn to the ageless but also aged Josh Johnson on Sunday in Kansas City, where the longtime backup will play a game at 40 years and 191 days old.

Arizona Cardinals quarterback Josh Johnson leaves the field after an NFL football game against the Los Angeles Rams in Glendale, Ariz., Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. AP Photo/Ross D. Franklin

According to Mark Dalton, the organization's senior vice president of media relations, Johnson will become the second-oldest non-specialist to play in a game for the Cardinals. To find the oldest, go all the way to Jim Thorpe in 1928, when the legend was 41 years and 185 days old in a Thanksgiving Day game against the Bears (which did not go well, with the Chicago Cardinals losing 34-0).

Johnson is getting a chance because Jacoby Brissett, who started in Week 8's win in Dallas, was traded to the Ravens; Gardner Minshew II, who started in Week 9's loss in Seattle, required season-ending elbow surgery; and rookie Carson Beck, who started in the Week 10 loss to the Rams, suffered back spasms that will not allow him to play Sunday.

So it is Johnson, who was signed off the Bengals' practice squad earlier this month, getting an opportunity to play a game for an eighth different franchise in a career that began as a fifth-round pick in 2008.

Football player Jim Thorpe poses for a photo between 1910 and 1920. HUM Images/Universal Images Grou

"There is no next step to the playbook, this is going back to the starting table of contents, and that's OK," head coach Mike LaFleur said, according to the team's website. "For a three-and-a-half-hour window, it's my job to figure out as you know whatever we can to get the ball moving forward."

Johnson will not become the oldest active player in the NFL. That title belongs to Aaron Rodgers, who turns 43 on Dec. 2 and will start for the Steelers in Philadelphia on Sunday.
