Ref Watch: Three blunders made by Clement Turpin in Liverpool vs Fenerbahce

Liverpool will have some questions for referee Clement Turpin after their 3-2 defeat against Fenerbahce.

The Reds travelled to Turkyie on Tuesday in a return to Champions League action.

Andoni Iraola utilised Bradley Barcola as a striker, but it was Trey Nyoni who opened the scoring with a deflected effort.

It was a similar story the other way as Mason Greenwood went on to net a from a free-kick that ricocheted of Jeremy Jacquet.

Alexander Isak came off the bench to score before quick-fire strikes from Archie Brown and Vedat Muriqi sealed victory for the hosts.

The result may have gone the way of Fenerbahce, but that won't stop complaints towards Turpin from both sides, as we assess some of his questionable calls.

There was high energy in this encounter from the very first kick.

Things got underway and less than 20 seconds later, Romelu Lukaku was quick to press from the front.

The Belgium international almost immediately collided with Virgil van Dijk, and the Dutchman felt it.

There was no foul awarded by the French match official as the striker got away with one.

It seemed as if there was more to it as the Reds' skipper was seen hobbling at times throughout the first half after the event.

With that potential foul not being given, many would have thought that the referee was setting a high bar.

That wasn't the case as the Fener striker clashed with van Dijk again on 16 minutes.

The ex-Chelsea man was quick to pounce on the Reds' backline before Kostas Tsimikas had played a short back pass.

He had already left the Liverpool skipper on the floor by the time the ball came to N'Golo Kante inside the area.

As he got a shot away, Turpin blew for a free-kick in favour of van Dijk.

That seemed like the right call, but it raises questions as to why the first challenge wasn't met with a free-kick but the second was.

If Liverpool felt hard done by with those decisions, they can have no complaints for this one.

Before Nyoni had opened the scoring, Greenwood had charged through on goal.

A terrific first touch looked set to make way for an effort on goal, but he was stopped in his tracks.

It was clear that Tsimikas had pushed the Englishman in the back with two hands, with replay only backing up that claim.

It realistically should have been awarded as a penalty, but Turpin waved the challenge away and was backed up by VAR.

There is no doubt that the Anfield outfit got away with one in that instance.
