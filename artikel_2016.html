5 players should follow Ernie Weaver out of Sheffield Wednesday in January

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Sheffield Wednesday find themselves in a peculiar position regarding their academy, as a lot of young homegrown talents introduced themselves to the fanbase at Championship level last season due to "circumstances," but now need to find regular game time elsewhere.

That's the view of new Owls boss Dave Challinor, who has hinted that a few loan departures may be on the cards for some of the younger players in the squad who put themselves in the limelight during their difficult campaign last season under administration.

Wednesday's ability to build a squad over the past summer window has meant that, despite some fans looking forward to seeing these young players become the core of a new-look side, they've fallen by the wayside.

Challinor stated that Ernie Weaver, one of the early standouts from last season before being sidelined for the majority of the campaign with a foot injury, could be someone who leaves on a short-term deal in January, following the likes of Charlie McNeill, Cole McGhee and Reece Johnson, who all played some part in the Championship last season.

These five players should also be tipped for a loan move in the near future to get some valuable game time, too.

Dave Challinor namedropped a couple of other players who could leave the club on loan at some point in the near future. One of those was fellow centre-back Gabriel Otegbayo.

The Irish Under-21 international epitomised the chances that young players got last season while the club was in administration. The former Burnley academy man made 35 Championship appearances last season, including a run of 23 starts in the final 25 games.

Otegbayo began this season as one of the first-choice defenders under Henrik Pedersen, too, starting four of the first five in league action and scoring in his one appearance off the bench against Bromley in that 7-2 demolition.

Since that fifth game, though, the 21-year-old hasn't started in the league, and he's to feature in a matchday squad in League One under Challinor. Indeed, his only appearance under the new boss came in the 4-0 EFL Trophy defeat against Accrington Stanley.

Wednesday fans weren't too enamoured with his performances in the early stages of the season, and also feel that he could benefit from a spell elsewhere.

The other player mentioned by Challinor as a potential loan option was midfielder Jarvis Thornton, who signed a new two-year deal with the Owls this past summer.

The 20-year-old has been at Wednesday since he was eight years old and was one of the many academy graduates who made their first-team debuts last season. He was one of the main features of their run to the EFL Cup third round, and he made 23 league appearances, too.

This season, though, Thornton has been one of the main victims of the midfield being strengthened at Hillsborough. The returns of Barry Bannan and Jaiden Heskey, plus the addition of Billy Mitchell, have made it difficult for him to get minutes.

His last league appearance came in that 7-2 win over Bromley nearly three months ago, and he's made a matchday squad just once since then.

The new contract ensures that the decision-makers at Wednesday think he has a bright future, but he may need to begin to realise that elsewhere.

It was a proud moment for Sean Fusire against Accrington Stanley in the EFL Trophy, as he wore the captain's armband for the first time. Unfortunately, the Zimbabwe international hasn't been able to stake a claim for regular league minutes this season, though.

The 21-year-old, unlike the others on this list so far, has actually spent time out on loan from Sheffield Wednesday. He spent the second half of the 2024/25 campaign at Carlisle United in League Two, but was then thrust into Championship action last year.

Only seven players played more league minutes than Fusire last season, but the full-back only has a couple in League One this year, coming on for the final stages of Henrik Pedersen's final game in charge at Doncaster Rovers.

Under Challinor, he has appearances in cup competitions, but hasn't made a league squad yet. You feel that, once the January window opens, he'll be one of the first out the door.

It was a pleasant surprise for Wednesday fans to see George Brown starting against Accrington Stanley. It was the 20-year-old's first appearance in nearly 11 months after a season plagued by injury.

Like Weaver, it was a real frustration to see him sidelined during the campaign in which he'd have gotten exposure at Championship level. He scored twice in just seven second-tier games last year, including one of their two wins all year at Portsmouth, and looked very promising.

He needs game time, but he won't get it at Hillsborough anytime soon. A good loan move away, perhaps at the top of the National League, could be massively beneficial to his chances of making it back in the senior fold in South Yorkshire.

There arguably isn't a more exciting talent on Sheffield Wednesday's books right now than Will Grainger. The Welsh Under-19 international only just turned 18 earlier this month, and broke through towards the end of last season, scoring in a 4-1 defeat against Oxford United.

He also extended his contract with the Owls over the summer, but he, too, has been a victim of the strengthening of the squad, especially across the frontline.
