Former Sotheby’s Vice President Michael Bouhanna Launches Miami-Based Art Advisory

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Michael Bouhanna, the former vice president of contemporary art specialist and head of digital art at Sotheby’s, has launched his own art advisory. Called Bouhanna Art Advisory, it will focus on modern and contemporary art based out of Miami.

Bouhanna was the last man standing at Sotheby’s Metaverse and other NFT divisions when the auction house laid off four staffers in 2024. He joined the company in 2016 as CTP junior cataloger and quickly moved up the ranks in the contemporary art department before being given the joint responsibility of contemporary and digital art in 2021. Bouhanna exited Sotheby’s in January, after just over a decade at the auction house working in its London, Paris, and New York headquarters. Bouhanna’s exit was unrelated to the layoffs, although following his departure Sotheby’s integrated NFTs and digital art into its contemporary art department. In September 2025, Christie’s closed its digital art department as the demand for digital art cooled down. In a wider view of the digital art market, Bouhanna told ARTnews that digital art is still part of contemporary art and it should not be seen as a separate entity. “Digital art still interests collectors who follow technology and AI. Bouhanna’s new chapter in Miami will involve placing modern and contemporary art by blue-chip artists in private collections and hands. In Miami, he has started working with private collectors, family offices and estates across acquisitions, sales, valuation and the management of collections. “Miami and South Florida collectors have built serious collections over the last decade, and a new generation is entering the market at a high level and often, they’re making their first significant acquisition,” Bouhanna said. “The established and emerging collectors need sophisticated advisory services that have long been available in London, New York or Paris, and also because transactions have become more complex with guarantees, private sales, and financing, collectors need experts on their side to evaluate them more than ever.”

Bouhanna moved to Miami earlier this year with his wife, Alexandra Benezra, founder of the public relations agency ASB PR Agency and fashion label Selvi. In the last year, he has been getting a feel for the city, which has absorbed more wealth in 10 years than any other American city and single-family offices are expected to grow by more than 10,000 by 2030. “Founders of companies, financial firms and families from the Northeast, West Coast and Latin America, who have had a strong habit of collecting art now live in South Florida,” Bouhanna said, noting that there’s also an influx of young, self-made entrepreneurs and financiers who are taking up art collection as an investment. Miami’s cultural infrastructure is following the same curve with institutions such as Pérez Art Museum Miami, ICA Miami, Bass Museum of Art and Rubell Museum offering competitive programs all year round as opposed to just one week during Art Basel Miami.
