VENICE BIENNALE TAKES A BOW. The Venice Biennale, curated by the late Koyo Kouoh around the theme “In Minor Keys,” closed on Sunday, reports Finestre dull’Arte. The 61st edition, which opened May 9, was fraught with controversy, primarily over the inclusion of the Russian pavilion, but also the Israeli pavilion, both of which the Biennale jury excluded from the event’s prizes. The often-dubbed “art Olympics” nevertheless attracted 714,015 visitors, about a quarter of whom were under 26. Meanwhile, according to Monopol Magazine, this year’s two Visitor’s Lions awards, chosen by attendees, will be announced tomorrow. The prizes were conceived as a replacement for the Venice Biennale’s traditional awards. However, some national pavilions have said that if they are selected, they would not accept it, in a show of unity with the jury, which resignedjust prior to the Biennale opening. IT’S ALL GREEK TO ME. Athens police have arrested a man and seized 35 forged paintings allegedly passed off as authentic works by Greek and international artists, valued at nearly $2 million, reports Ekathimerini, a local news outlet. Investigators believe the forgeries, which included paintings attributed to Greek artist George Bouzianis, were primarily being sold online. A second suspect in the art trafficking scheme, who is also a cleric, remains at large.

Russian strikes on November 20 have damaged several cultural and historic sites, including Kyiv’s St. Volodymyr’s Cathedral and Monastery, the National Museum of the History of Ukraine in World War II, and 12 heritage sites in the center of Odesa. [United 24 Media]

Boby Dylan’s 1960s handwritten draft lyrics for the songs “Masters of War” and “Subterranean Homesick Blues” will be up for sale at Sotheby’s New York Rock & Pop auction, from December 2 to 15. [The New York Times]

Ireland’s Basic Income for the Arts (BIA) program, providing €325 weekly to artists from 2022 to 2025, gave participants a much-needed, albeit temporary financial cushion, but for now, the country’s art market continues to ‘survive’ on a relatively small pool of collectors. [ARTnews]

Art Basel Hong Kong announced the 232 exhibitors at its 2027 edition, from March 25-27, marking a slight contraction from the 240 galleries that participated in 2026, neither of which includes participants in the Zero 10 curated section dedicated to digital art. [ARTnews]

Art Basel Qatar announced two newly commissioned design spaces by architect Büro Koray Duman, intended for public gathering, performances, screenings, and fair conferences, located at points linking the forthcoming fair’s main venues. [press release]

QATAR QUADRENNIAL. Doha’s Rubaiya Qatar, the country’s first quadrennial, opened to the public on Saturday and runs through April 30, 2027, across three venues. This includes the flagship and largest exhibition titled Unruly Waters at ALRIWAQ Arts + Architecture, curated by Tom Eccles, Ruba Katrib, Shabbir Hussain Mustafa, and Mark Rappolt. ARTnews was on the ground at the event, which offers an opportunity to discover lesser-known artists from the region along with the local cultural scene as it continues to develop with the support of Sheikha Al Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, chairperson of Qatar Museums. Stay tuned to read ARTnews‘ take on the inaugural exhibition. Also, in time for the quadrennial, a permanent pavilion dedicated to Gerhard Richter was unveiled on Friday in Doha’s waterfront park beside the Museum of Islamic Art, a short walk from Unruly Waters. The free-standing pavilion was designed by Selldorf Architects and contains the artist’s large, colorful “Strip” paintings hung horizontally in a hexagon-shaped room. At its center rises a central column of vertically arranged “Strip” paintings, forming an almost dizzying intersection of color and line. On the exterior of the structure, the artist has created patterned tiles from his abstract paintings, which evoke vibrant Islamic tiles, for an experience definitely worth a visit.
