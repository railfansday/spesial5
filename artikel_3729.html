World of Warcraft: Forever's absolutely had me enthralled, as per my review (and about 220+ hours of my actual life), but that's not to say there aren't issues. Over the weekend and going on into this week, Blizzard's had to play whack-a-mole with both exploits and what appears to be a long-standing bug affecting one of the tanking classes' core mechanics.

First, those exploits—players discovered that lockboxes contained a sort of quantum loot, in that the actual roll of the lockboxes seemed tied to the player peeking inside of them, rather than what was inside regardless. If you turned off auto-loot, you can open a lockbox without actually pulling the loot into your bag. Then, if you sent that lockbox to another player via mail, they'd get something different.

Essentially, players could simply play a game of mass pass-the-parcel to ensure favorable lockbox results, further busting up the economy after the Blackmaw Hold drama earlier this month. That's since been hotfixed, simply disabling the mechanic, with a more elegant solution to come further down the road: "Today, we’re working on a proper fix that restores the ability to peek and partially loot lockboxes, and makes them otherwise work as intended."

Meanwhile, the Obsidian Sentinel in Uldaman was briefly rendered lootless. As spotted by WoWHead, players could hop into the dungeon and repeatedly rinse the boss to grab a cheeky 18 or so gold in 15 minutes by vendoring the loot as a max-level character.

While that's not really game-breaking (instance lockouts are limited to five per hour), if you had an organised group of level 60s all funnelling gold to the same guy—like, say, if you were a streamer with a dedicated fanbase that didn't mind wasting a quarter of an hour, or if you were a large-scale RMT operation—there's a bigger problem.

That lootless state turned out to be a band-aid, as Blizzard simply lowered the vendor costs later.

Perhaps the biggest hiccup over the past weekend, though, is the fact that Sunder Armor (and other armor-reducing effects like faerie fire) very well might just not do anything. I hopped in-game to test it on my warrior and saw no noticeable difference between Sunder Armor in both dungeons and the overworld while fighting lower level targets, though my testing capabilities were limited solo, since armor is affected by the level of whoever's attacking the target: And if I rock up to an on-level elite in a dungeon at 60, I tend to die before I can get a good sample.

Fortunately, I don't need to be too thorough. There's a lot more comprehensive math and log-testing on this github post: "Once an armor debuff goes on, players' and pets' hits barely move, same as the report says. About 5% of the armor drop actually shows up in the damage, and more stacks don't help." The evidence is all pretty convincing.

While those other exploits are annoying oopsies, arguably just a result of players—who find edge cases like water finds cracks—stress-testing the system? The Sunder Armor bug is sort of huge, since that's one of the protection warrior's main group utilities (aside from being a tanky meatshield). It still appears to be filling its primary role of threat generation, but: Yikes.

There's no mention of it in the hotfixes, but I'll update this article if one pops up in the coming days.
