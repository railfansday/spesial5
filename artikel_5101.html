XRP’s institutional adoption story has a catch that investors may be overlooking

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Aviva’s manager or platform pays the ledger fees. Ondo investors can deal in RLUSD, while the accounts responsible for fees or reserves need XRP.

The XRP Ledger Foundation said on Nov. 18 it had assessed the network against all ten principles of the Global Layer One Market Infrastructure Toolkit. It dated an independent report from blockchain security firm CertiK Nov. 13. On Nov. 19, CertiK said it independently checked selected technical controls under Architecture and Resiliency, using evidence that included code, configurations, logs and test results.

Legal basis and settlement finality were part of the Foundation’s own assessment. The GL1 toolkit gives institutions a common set of questions for their own due diligence. The Foundation says the exercise confers no regulatory approval or certification. Both fund designs were in place before the November disclosures.

Aviva handles sterling dealing off-chain and records matching tokens on the ledger. Ondo lets eligible investors deal in RLUSD on the ledger.

The asset investors use to deal is separate from the XRP needed for network fees or reserves.

Aviva put £50 million into the tokenized sterling share class it announced on Oct. 26. The approximately £22 billion quoted at launch was the size of the underlying Sterling Liquidity Fund. The smaller figure measures Aviva’s own investment in the class.

Under the fund prospectus, an investor sends a subscription through the administrator, which deals in cash off-chain and records the shares on its register. The platform issues the matching tokens after the subscription money arrives and the shares are issued. On redemption, the administrator processes the order and updates the register before the tokens are burned. The manager reconciles the two records daily. If the share register and the ledger diverge, the prospectus gives the register the final say over legal ownership.

Digital custodian Komainu holds the tokens in wallets for eligible investors. Ordinary investor-to-investor transfers are unavailable under the model. Aviva’s prospectus assigns the ledger transaction fees to the manager or tokenization platform, so investors can use the share class without buying XRP themselves. The fee provision leaves account reserve funding unspecified.

Ondo’s May 2026 bank-payout pilot used a different route. Ripple redeemed some OUSG on the ledger, then Mastercard routed the payout instruction and J.P. Morgan’s Kinexys sent U.S. dollars to Ripple’s bank account in Singapore. Ondo’s disclosures give no figures for routine on-ledger dealing and leave the reserve funder unspecified.

The network destroys XRP used for transaction fees. The documented minimum for a standard transaction is 0.00001 XRP, and it can rise under load. Account reserves work differently. The mainnet base reserve is 1 XRP, and an account typically needs another 0.2 XRP for each eligible ledger object it owns, subject to exceptions. Reserve XRP stays in the balance and can still cover fees. The account responsible for a fee or reserve needs to have the XRP for it.

Evidence of a bigger XRP requirement would come from fund terms that call for XRP settlement or collateral, or from repeated XRP transfers or posted collateral linked to identified fund accounts. Matching those transactions to product terms or settlement records would show what the XRP is doing. A wallet balance could simply keep the account funded for future fees and reserves.
