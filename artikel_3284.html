A low-cost way to start seeds in the colder months

With cold temperatures and frosty soil, winter is hard enough for your existing garden let alone trying to plant any new seeds. And, if like me, you're lacking indoor space and don't have an external greenhouse then this hack might be the game changer you're looking for.

I've been researching about setting up my own mini greenhouses while I wait to plant these 7 winter-hardy vegetables in February. While there were several ideas on what to buy, I stumbled across a TikTok that said I could make them pretty easily yourself – and all I'd need is a milk jug. So, I thought I'd give it a try. Here's what happened.

Sharing DIY home and garden tips on TikTok, @sandra.maria.home has almost 65k followers watching her videos, but it was what she had to say about milk jugs that really caught my attention.

In one video, she explains: "This is a milk jug greenhouse method I have perfected over the years. A mini greenhouse lets you start seeds outdoors way earlier without grow lights or without bathing seedlings inside. For me, it's the start of the growing season."

And all you need is a milk jug (or several, depending on how many seed starters you're planning), a pair of scissors, a kitchen knife and some duct tape. And, of course, some soil and your seeds.

Sandra has some advise from her years of mini greenhouse making when it comes to the soil you choose: "Don't use anything with fertilizer, it's too harsh for the seedlings and will kill them."

And, if you're planning on planting multiple seeds, use a pen or wax pencil to number the jugs so you don't lose track of which ones are which. Once spring comes around, you can gently release the duct tape and let the plants acclimatize for planting in your yard.

If you haven't already got duct tape lying around the house, you can pick it up for just under $4 to secure the edges of your mini greenhouse. It's durable, heavy duty and has excellent adhesion. If your duct tape is struggling to stick in the cold, Sandra recommends heating it up with a hair dryer.

While Sandra says she picks her jugs up at her local coffee shop, which is a seriously clever idea, I live in a busy household where we happen to drink a lot of milk. Or, at least enough for me to collect a few jugs myself.

So, milk jugs in hand, I followed her steps using scissors to cut and make drainage holes as I found this a lot easier. I then lined them up in my yard in a place I knew I could keep an eye on them without having to venture too far.

Another piece of advice that I think is worth mentioning from Sandra's video is that she says to densely pack the seeds in since this small jug of soil won't be their forever home. That means there will be more to move over once spring rolls around and they can grow into their new environment.

Sure, I need to wait and see how effective my mini greenhouses actually are when spring comes around, but I'm happy that I didn't waste any money and hardly any time doing it. So, if some seeds are successful and others aren't, I'll still be happy to try again.

What I loved about this gardening hack is that making the mini greenhouses really took me no time at all – and it also didn't cost me anything. Considering how easy and affordable this hack is, I'll definitely be recommending it to friends. I just might wait until next year, so I can show them proof that it really does work. Not that I don't trust Sandra!
