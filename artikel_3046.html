Defensive Coordinator Vance Joseph praises Riley Moss for his resilience and toughness

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Denver Broncos have become one of the best franchises in the National Football League when it comes to rewarding their players who perform well. Since the Walton-Penner Family Ownership Group took over several seasons ago, the Broncos have doled out nearly a dozen large contracts to assemble a championship caliber roster.

Yesterday, fourth-year cornerback Riley Moss became the most recent player to earn a lucrative multi-year contract extension. While no particulars on guarantees have been provided, the known terms are a four-year deal worth up to $67 million dollars. That's a shade below $17 million annually, and several million less on average than many thought he would garner on the open market.

Yesterday after practice and the news about the extension, Defensive Coordinator Vance Joseph met with the media and was asked about the importance of getting the deal done.

"It's special. I think it speaks to the Walton-Penner Family Ownership, the GM, and the Head Coach. That's the program. That's the plan here to draft, develop and retain. It's working to obviously maintain and get better each year. That's the key to this league. Keep your own and find them early and pay them," stated Joseph.

The Broncos moved up in the third round of the 2023 NFL Draft to select Moss out of Iowa. As a rookie, it was mostly a learning season for the former Hawkeyes standout. He appeared in 14 games, but most of his action was as a core special teams' player. He only had 23 defensive snaps that season.

Over the next two years, Moss would be a 31-game starter for the Broncos opposite of All-Pro cornerback Patrick Surtain II. Between the 2024 and 2025 seasons, he would log over 1,900 defensive snaps and proved himself to be an important piece in Joseph's talented secondary.

"I'll start with this; he's always been very athletic. He's competitive. To play opposite of Pat Surtain II, that's a tough job. Outside of Bo Nix on gameday, it's probably Riley," Joseph remarked stating who likely has the hardest assignment on a week-to-week basis.

In his third season as a starter, Moss has missed some time, but he is performing very well—allowing 223 yards in coverage on 18 catches. Opposing quarterbacks aren't having much success targeting him at all, completing just 51-percent of passes that go in his direction. Those are all career bests for him.

"No one understands his gameday because Pat's out there at left corner, and they never look at Pat. They do sometimes, but mostly they don't. They're trying to find Riley. So that's a tough job. He's the perfect personality for that job. Most corners wouldn't have the resolve to kind of over, and over again just take 12 targets a game, and two penalties here and there," added Joseph.

Speaking of penalties, that's one area Broncos Country has been right to criticize Moss for the past two years. He had fourteen defensive pass interference calls in 24' and '25. However, things are markedly different in 2026. To date, he has just two of those on the season. That's significant progress.

When the time came yesterday afternoon, Moss answered a handful of questions for reporters. He was extremely grateful for the extension and had high praise for his coaches, teammates, and ownership. There is no doubt the talented cornerback has had his fair share of ups and downs throughout his career. Nevertheless, he has proven to be a gritty player who can overcome the obstacles that have come his way. He gave credit to his upbringing for this ability to do such things.

"I think the biggest thing is it's how I was raised with my parents. The biggest thing was they let me fail so I would be able to figure out how to become successful through that, through that adversity. It's the reason I am who I am today. It's a hard job, but it's a rewarding job and I love it."
