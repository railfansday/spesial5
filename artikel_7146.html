Trump taps DEI opponent to lead DOJ’s civil rights division

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Let K-12 Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

While at the U.S. Department of Education, Craig Trainor threatened to strip federal funding from schools over their diversity initiatives.

The selection of Trainor, who the Justice Department said is entering the position in “an acting capacity,” signals that the Trump administration isn’t backing away from its heavy crackdown on schools and their diversity initiatives. Trainor is set to replace Harmeet Dhillon, one of several top DOJ officials who stepped down after Democrats seized control of both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections.

Under Dhillon, the department’s civil rights division aggressively pursued schools and states over LGBTQ+ policies — such as transgender student inclusion on sports teams and parental notification of students' gender identity concerns. Additionally, the division has pursued race-related investigations into programs meant to be inclusive of historically marginalized students, and it lifted a number of decades-old desegregation orders.

The Justice Department also joined a lawsuit in February against the Los Angeles Unified School District over a court-ordered desegregation program.

During Dhillon’s tenure, the agency entered an interagency agreement with the Education Department, taking on some civil rights investigation duties that were previously housed within the Education Department's Office for Civil Rights.

Since then, the two agencies rescinded at least seven documents related to English learner and other minority protections on the grounds that they were "not enforceable." Trainor has likewise spearheaded major efforts to stamp out DEI in K-12. While serving as the Education Department’s acting assistant secretary for civil rights, Trainor threatened in February 2025 guidance to pull federal funding from schools over DEI initiatives that it said amounted to "race-based decisionmaking."

The letter said "a school may not use students’ personal essays, writing samples, participation in extracurriculars, or other cues" as a proxy for race in issues "pertaining to admissions, hiring, promotion, compensation, financial aid, scholarships, prizes, administrative support, discipline, housing, graduation ceremonies, and all other aspects of student, academic, and campus life."

Federal judges have struck down the guidance. However, the DOJ released similar sweeping guidance last year that threatens to strip federal grant funding from schools over what the agency deems as illegal DEI. DOJ officials flagged several commonplace practices as potentially unlawful, ranging from requiring job applicants how their “cultural background informs their teaching,” using recruitment strategies targeting candidates from specific geographic areas or racial backgrounds, and asking job candidates to describe how they overcame obstacles.

Trainor pursued similar anti-DEI policies in his most recent appointment as the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development’s assistant secretary for fair housing and equal opportunity, a position he was confirmed for by the Senate in October 2025. In that role, Trainor issued guidance warning colleges that affinity housing may violate federal law.
