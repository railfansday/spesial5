Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Binance Hack Wallets Move $137 Million in Ethereum After Two Weeks of Silence

Wallets linked to the $6.3 billion Binance hack started moving large amounts of stolen Ethereum on Tuesday, breaking more than two weeks of inactivity as U.S. authorities continue trying to recover the funds.

Blockchain security service MistTrack reported that one address associated with the October 29 theft sent 34,279.66 ETH, worth roughly $63 million at the time, to three newly created wallets. The address had not moved its funds since October 30.

The on-chain record shows three outgoing transactions shortly before 7 a.m. UTC: two transfers of 12,000 ETH ($21.2 million) each and a third of about 10,279.66 ETH ($18.2 million). MistTrack said the receiving wallets had not sent the ether onward when it posted its initial alert.

That $63 million movement was only part of Tuesday’s activity. A second wallet linked to the same hack transferred another 40,600.66 ETH, worth roughly $74 million, across five transactions.

Analytics firm Arkham shows wallets linked to the attack had transferred a combined 74,880 ETH, valued at $136.8 million, over roughly three hours, and funds were beginning to be swapped for Bitcoin through Thorchain, a service that moves and exchanges crypto between blockchains.

The transfers come five days after the Justice Department said courts had authorized it to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion connected to the Binance theft. That sum is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector action, not an additional recovery on top of it.

The FBI has attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, a hacking operation it links to North Korea’s military. The October 29 breach drained roughly $6.43 billion from Binance across 11 blockchains, according to blockchain analytics company Elliptic, making it the largest known crypto theft. North Korea denied involvement.

The attackers rushed to convert stablecoins into assets their issuers could not freeze. Tether and Circle can block stolen tokens on their networks, but ordinary ETH and Bitcoin do not have a central company capable of stopping a particular wallet from sending them.

Elliptic’s November 16 analysis put the frozen or seized portion at $2.85 billion and said the main Bitcoin and ETH holdings were still sitting in attack-linked addresses. That was before Tuesday’s transfers, which show at least part of the previously stationary ETH has begun moving.

The attackers had also spread their activity across almost 27,000 wallets and more than 83,000 transactions. That complicated trail does not mean they have managed to turn most of the stolen assets into cash.

Elliptic said only a negligible amount had likely been cashed out as of its latest assessment. To do that at scale, the holders would typically need an exchange or an over-the-counter broker willing to take the funds, creating another point at which authorities could identify and potentially block them.
