Camino Picnicking Hiker Trash Style

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

In the wee hours of the morning—once the room finally cooled off—I rolled the metal window shutters (persianas) completely down to block out the noise from partygoers in the street. The shutters created pitch darkness: a 100% total blackout where you couldn’t see your hand in front of your face. It made for an amazingly deep sleep.

When I finally woke up, I was shocked to see it was already 8:00 AM. Outside, it was a crisp 50°F morning under bright blue skies. With only about 9 miles to Arzúa, we had another short, relaxed day ahead of us.

Leaving Melide, we made a brief stop at a 12th-century church that was already bustling with fellow walkers. From there, the Camino led us onto a scenic forested path. Although the trail remained crowded with pilgrims, the canopy provided a peaceful backdrop.

Before long, we pulled into a roadside café to grab some breakfast, refuel with caffeine, and collect our first passport stamp of the day.

Resuming our trek, the route alternated between shaded woodland paths and short road stretches connecting quiet villages. In one of these hamlets, we stopped at a small local church for our second passport stamp, fulfilling our daily requirement for the Compostela.

Back under the trees, the trail wound past towering ancient trunks as we tackled our first major climb of the day. It was a short but steep ascent. After powering up a couple more rolling hills, we descended sharply into the picturesque village of Ribadiso.

At the bottom of the valley, we paused at an ancient stone bridge so Knockerz could soak her lower legs in the icy river water. Watching pilgrims cool their feet in the river at Ribadiso is a classic trail tradition.

While relaxing by the water, we reconnected with our friend Manuel from Porto and exchanged phone numbers. He told us about the Festa de São João do Porto—a wild, 700-year-old midsummer festival held in late June where people hit each other on the head with plastic hammers! Unfortunately, we’ll be leaving Porto for Paris on the exact day it starts.

After wandering through medieval Ribadiso, we had one final hill to conquer. Before long, we arrived on the outskirts of Arzúa, a unique small city that felt strangely quiet.

Our first stop was a Carrefour grocery store to stock up on fresh watermelon, sandwiches, and yogurt. With no seating nearby, I repurposed some wooden shipping pallets outside the storefront into a makeshift picnic table. We happily devoured our lunch right there while local shoppers gave us classic “hiker trash” looks.

Arzúa had an unusual, almost abandoned vibe with its wide modern streets and minimal foot traffic. However, our accommodation was a centuries-old stone building that had been completely renovated into a beautiful modern albergue inside. Later that evening, we reconnected with our tramily, Robert and Jill, for drinks before heading back to turn in for the night.
