Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Moving everyday banking away from Fidelity for security. Vanguard Cash Plus or another bank?

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

I’ve consolidated almost all my finances at Fidelity, including my taxable brokerage, traditional IRA, Roth IRA, 401(k), long-term savings, short-term savings, and checking through Fidelity CMA. I still have Bank of America and Ally accounts as backups, along with a Vanguard account I haven’t closed.

I originally consolidated everything at Fidelity because I was concerned about ACATS fraud and unauthorized transfers, and I felt more comfortable with Fidelity’s security features.

However, with increasingly sophisticated AI-enabled scams and account takeover risks, I’m reconsidering whether keeping everything at Fidelity, including everyday banking, is a good idea.

My main concern is having access to almost my entire net worth on my everyday phone. Since I use Fidelity CMA for checking, I frequently need to log in to check transactions or transfer money. I’m increasingly uncomfortable with this, especially considering scenarios where my phone is stolen while unlocked.

Ideally, I’d like to remove Fidelity from my everyday phone and access it primarily from a separate device kept at home.

What I’m considering:

* Fidelity: Keep my existing investments and retirement accounts, but stop using it for everyday banking.
* Vanguard Cash Plus: Use for checking, bill payments, and everyday transactions.
* Vanguard brokerage: Keep around 3–4 months of emergency savings in Treasury money market funds (similar to FDLXX at Fidelity), taking advantage of favorable California state tax treatment.
* New investments: Gradually direct future taxable investments to Vanguard rather than Fidelity. I don’t want to go through the process of transferring my existing shares, as it just makes me nervous.
* Ally / Bank of America: Keep as backup accounts if needed.

One consideration is that I currently prioritize maxing out my 401(k), including mega backdoor Roth contributions, so I don’t expect to invest substantial amounts in taxable accounts for at least the next 2-3 years(until i am in my current job).

Eventually, if my Vanguard investments grow substantially, I could face the same concern about having access to significant assets through an everyday banking app. But that’s unlikely to be an issue for some years.

I considered Ally since I already have Savings and Spending accounts there, but I don’t particularly like the savings transaction limits. I’d also prefer to keep emergency savings in Treasury money market funds for the state tax benefits rather than a traditional savings account.

My questions:

1. Does using Vanguard Cash Plus for everyday banking, alongside a Vanguard brokerage account for emergency savings, seem like a reasonable setup?
2. Is there another bank or brokerage that would be better suited for this, particularly one offering competitive yields, easy transfers, and access to Treasury money market funds?
3. Would you recommend keeping everyday banking completely separate from investment brokerages instead, especially considering that Vanguard could eventually accumulate substantial investments too?

I’m trying to balance security, convenience, and earning a reasonable return on cash without creating too many additional accounts.

I’d appreciate hearing how others have approached this.
