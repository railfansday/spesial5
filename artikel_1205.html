Most expensive goal in Liverpool history gives Iraola era lift-off

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

A quick sprint, a well-placed pass and an instinctive finish. Bradley Barcola and Alexander Isak will have done this dozens of times in training but here, on a squally Sunday afternoon, this combination secured a little piece of history.

Anfield has witnessed plenty of significant goals down the years but never had this crowd seen one that cost £248m to make. Liverpool invested a near quarter-billion pounds on Barcola and Isak for bigger occasions than a tussle with Brighton and Hove Albion, but this rapid interchange felt like a significant moment.

Liverpool are a work in progress under Andoni Iraola but with each minute of each game you can see signs of things knitting together, and if Barcola and Isak are finding harmony, with Florian Wirtz in behind, then, perhaps, this journey will start to accelerate.

This was an important afternoon for Liverpool’s head coach, a difficult assignment against high-flying, well-respected opponents in Brighton, who arrived on Merseyside having won their last four Premier League fixtures with a combined aggregate of 15-0, including a 3-0 thumping of reigning champions Arsenal.

But Liverpool rose impressively to the challenge and there are grounds for saying this was the most significant success to date for Iraola, whose team defended relentlessly to preserve a clean sheet. They are up to fourth now after this 2-0 success and deserve their position.

“I’m very happy,” Iraola said. “We faced a very good opponent, similar to Villarreal [in the Champions League]. It’s difficult to defend them a lot of times and it was key how we started. We needed to be aggressive, recover balls, win our duels, be dangerous in transitions: I think we’ve done it.”

And how. Iraola was able to march towards the Kop at the end, to clap the supporters and acknowledge only Liverpool’s fifth (and his first) Premier League win at Anfield in 2026, and it was evident he had a spring in his step from what he had seen.

Whether the game would have flowed in the direction it did had Brighton taken the lead can only be speculated but what a glorious chance they wasted for us to find out: in the 13th minute, five swift, one-touch passes bamboozled Liverpool’s defence and created space for Yasin Ayari.

With the goal at his mercy, Ayari could have made things exceptionally uncomfortable but he leant back and blazed over. He clasped his face and grimaced, knowing what had gone begging in front of the Kop; four minutes later, his misery was exacerbated.

Liverpool’s lead was secured with an element of good fortune but, equally, the tenacity of Ronald Araújo and Wirtz was instrumental in pressing Brighton back to the point that they were panicking; Wirtz’s shot, deflected off Pascal Gross past Bart Verbruggen, compounded it all.

“I think everyone in the stadium – and you as well – felt there was a missed opportunity for us,” groaned Fabian Hürzeler, the visitors’ head coach. “It’s not only after the goal or the goals they scored. Before they scored, we had two big chances. I think it was just the difference that they scored and we didn’t score.”

It would be wrong to say Wirtz was doing cartwheels – perhaps he knew immediately he would not get the goal – but it was an important moment for him and you could feel the sense of goodwill towards him from team-mates and supporters alike.

Remarkably, this was the first time Liverpool had scored in the first half at Anfield in a Premier League fixture since May 9 and, with that momentum, it led to one of those periods when they started attacking in waves and carried the crowd with them.

What they needed was a second goal to arrive quite quickly and that is what happened when Barcola won possession, scuttled forward and played a perfectly weighted bobbling ball into Isak’s path; the Swede did the rest, calmly finishing from eight yards.

Everything with Isak feels to be going under the radar at the moment, but that is six goals in six Premier League appearances. He is starting to show the consistency and killer instinct that everyone felt he would. Iraola will be hoping it continues for the foreseeable future.

“The performance was really good,” said Isak, the quiet assassin. “We were really solid against a really good team. The first half is what we really want to be about. It was about intensity, winning balls and countering.”

Brighton, as much as Hürzeler protested, could not properly recover, though this is no criticism. They remain committed to attack, they struck the frame of the goal when Ayari tried to punish an Alisson Becker mistake and the desire to keep pushing ensured Liverpool could never relax.

What Liverpool had going for them this time, however, was an intensity to preserve a clean sheet and nobody embodied the indefatigability more than Araújo, the Uruguay international whose loan from Barcelona is looking an increasingly shrewd piece of business.

Two moments stood out: one thunderous tackle on substitute Malick Yalcouyé in front of the Main Stand that had the locals roaring their approval, another in the first half when he was on the floor but still managed to head away to thwart Maxim De Cuyper.

They remember those moments as much as the stardust that Barcola and Isak provide. Put it all together and it might just be a launchpad for Iraola.

Even by his prodigious standards, this has been a different week for Rio Ngumoha. After the coming-of-age performance against Villarreal, so followed the slew of attention on social media.

A clip of his best moments during the chaotic 4-3 dispatching of the Spaniards, posted on Uefa’s official X account, attracted 1.2 million views and was liked more than 24,000 times. Hot on the heels came another video, this time when he was 11, when he nutmegged a dumbfounded cameraman.

Ngumoha has always had feet that move as quickly as the hands of street artists who perform tricks with cups and balls: now you see it, now you don’t. We live in an age where talent is quickly amplified and this young England international has it in bundles.

You saw it in those exhilarating 74 minutes: Ngumoha had his most touches in a game this campaign (51), most touches in the opposition box (12) and attempted the most dribbles (10), as well as creating the most chances (three) and providing two assists.
