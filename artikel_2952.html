Arista Networks vs. Snowflake: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Arista's 40% net margin and Snowflake's stock-based compensation load tell very different profit stories for investors weighing cloud infrastructure exposure.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 27, 2026 at 7:20PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Arista Networks ›

As cloud infrastructure spending surges, investors often choose between the hardware enabling the connection and the software managing the data. Choosing between Arista Networks (ANET +6.55%) and Snowflake (SNOW +3.20%) defines this high-growth cloud infrastructure comparison.

Arista Networks provides the high-speed networking equipment essential for massive data centers, while Snowflake offers a cloud-native platform for analyzing and sharing vast data sets. While both companies ride the wave of artificial intelligence adoption, they offer vastly different financial outcomes and market strategies for your portfolio.

As networking demand evolves for tech stocks, Arista Networks focuses on high-speed Ethernet switching for cloud titans and specialty cloud providers. The company provides the underlying hardware allowing large data centers to function efficiently while supporting campus and routing environments. In its latest annual report, two major end customers accounted for 16% and 26% of revenue, which adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $11.9 billion, representing strong revenue growth of about 40.7% compared to the prior twelve-month period. GAAP net income for this period was about $4.8 billion, which is a significant figure for a company of this scale. This resulted in a net margin of 40%, representing the percentage of revenue remaining after all operating and non-operating expenses.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is about 3.0x, indicating its ability to cover short-term liabilities with assets like cash. The debt-to-equity ratio is 0.0x, which measures total debt relative to shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, the company generated $4.3 billion in free cash flow, which is cash flow from operations minus capital expenditures.

Snowflake operates a consumption-based business model for its AI Data Cloud platform, serving approximately 13,000 organizations globally. The platform enables data engineering and AI collaboration across various sectors while serving approximately 800 of the Forbes Global 2000. The company has established strategic partnerships with companies like Lumen Technologies (LUMN +3.55%) to enhance its data connectivity and AI capabilities.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached around $5.4 billion, which reflects revenue growth of about 32% over the previous year. This growth is driven by enterprises transitioning to cloud-native data solutions to better manage their artificial intelligence workloads. The company reported a GAAP net loss of about $1.1 billion, resulting in a net margin of negative 20.1% for that period.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is about 1.3x and the debt-to-equity ratio is around 1.4x, which measures total debt against shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, Snowflake generated approximately $1.1 billion in free cash flow, representing cash from operations minus capital expenditures. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 130.9% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Arista Networks faces significant revenue concentration from a limited number of large customers, which can cause order volatility. It relies on Broadcom (AVGO +3.82%) for silicon and faces competition from Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE +4.75%) and Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%). The company also contends with geopolitical tensions and trade restrictions that could affect its global supply chains.

Snowflake faces risks from the evolution of artificial intelligence and potential vertical integration by frontier AI model providers. The company relies on major public cloud infrastructure providers, including Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) and Microsoft (MSFT +0.60%), who also compete directly with its services. Additionally, Snowflake manages ongoing legal challenges and cybersecurity risks related to its shared responsibility model.

Snowflake trades at a slightly lower P/S ratio than Arista Networks, but Arista Networks offers a significantly lower Forward P/E based on future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Arista Networks. AI data centers have to link thousands of computer chips so they can work together, and Arista makes the networking gear that does it. What sold me is the growth: Sales jumped 58.5% year over year last quarter, the fastest since the company went public.

Arista does have things to watch, though. It leans on a few giant customers, such as Microsoft and Meta, and parts shortages could slow deliveries. Even so, management expects growth to stay strong in 2027.

To give Snowflake its due, its AI-fueled growth is speeding up, and customers keep spending more with it. But it still loses money once stock pay for employees is counted, and its price is steep: Based on next year's expected profit, its stock costs around 120 times that profit, versus about 40 times for Arista.

Arista, meanwhile, keeps a big chunk of every sales dollar as profit. For long-term investors, an AI networking leader growing this fast at a far lower valuation makes Arista the better buy for 2027.
