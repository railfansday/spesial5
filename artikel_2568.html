Tee Higgins touchdown highlights inconsistency of replay review

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Just when we thought we were out when it comes to not knowing what a catch is, they pull us back in.

The past two days have included three examples of plays that make more confusing the question of whether a player has completed the act of catching the ball — and whether replay review was properly used to correct rulings made on the field.

It started on Sunday afternoon, when an apparent touchdown catch by Jaguars receiver Parker Washington was ruled not a catch on the field, with replay review not overturning the ruling. Even though the visual evidence seemed to provide clear and obvious proof that the ball was caught.

On Sunday night, 49ers receiver Mike Evans bobbled his way to a touchdown catch. He quite possibly didn’t get two feet in bounds after finally securing control of the ball. The play wasn’t even reviewed.

On Monday night, Bengals receiver Tee Higgins made a touchdown catch even though the ball was moving as he landed on the ground and out of bounds. There was no replay review.

The common denominator in these plays (beyond the fact that they involved potential touchdown catches) is the shadowy, uncertain replay process. As one head coach explained it to PFT on Monday (before the Bengals-Commanders game), it’s still not known who is making the decisions in New York as to what will be reviewed and what the outcome of the review will be.

It’s another reason for the NFL to embrace full transparency when it comes to replay review. We need to know who is making the decisions. We need to know their names. We need to hear their voices.

And we also need to know why — as happened in the case of the Evans and Higgins touchdowns — there was no full-blown review.
