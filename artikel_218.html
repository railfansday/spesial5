No more heated seat subscriptions for cars in New York from 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Services with “ongoing costs” are a pretty big loophole in the new law.

Car buyers in New York may find the cost of ownership reduces a little next year. Maybe. Governor Kathy Hochul signed a bill yesterday that bans car companies and dealerships from locking up car features behind a subscription, a law that goes into effect on February 27 next year.

“People spending their hard-earned money on a car shouldn’t have to fork over even more money for features that should automatically come with the car. My message to car companies is clear: this kind of deceptive business model isn’t going to fly in New York State. Keep your hands out of New Yorkers’ wallets,” the governor said.

In the old days, the only options you paid to use after you bought a car were mods or upgrades you installed after the fact. That started to change with services like General Motors’ OnStar, which introduced telematics to the vehicle—for a monthly fee.

We end users might not like having to open our wallets each month, but the capital markets sure do, and car companies love making the capital markets happy. Which is why there has been such a push toward connected vehicles. Remote start and predictive maintenance alerts are nice, but your regular payments to the car company—plus any data of yours it can sell—is the goal.

Some automakers have gone even further. Infamously, BMW and its heated seats. Like the McDonald’s coffee lawsuit, it’s a fact half-remembered; BMW North America never tried such a thing, just in countries like South Korea and the UK, and in 2023 BMW’s board said that subscriptions would only be for software-related features from then on.

New York’s press release mentions heated seats but also calls out a number of other modern tech features: “enhanced navigation, hands-free driving and entertainment options that do not present an ongoing expense for dealers and manufacturers.”

But the way the actual law is written appears to leave a rather large loophole, at least to these eyes.

S.9348A/A.10411A prohibits motor vehicle manufacturers, dealers or third-party service providers from adding subscription services or charging post-purchase fees for any motor vehicle feature that uses pre-installed components or hardware that do not have an ongoing cost. As vehicles become more advanced, the new law aims to establish clear rules preventing exploitative practices and prevent post-purchase fees as a future automotive business model.

A heated seat might not have “an ongoing cost,” but the maps and databases that enable “enhanced navigation” and “hands-free driving” systems definitely do, as these need to be constantly updated. A regular cadence of software updates also generates an ongoing cost to an OEM, arguably. And car companies pay for plenty of lawyers who really love to argue.

And so, while experiments like BMW’s seat heater subscription won’t be allowed in the state, the new rule might still allow for plenty of ongoing fees for services like OnStar or Mercedes’ Drive Assist Pro system we tested recently.
