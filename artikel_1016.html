Leicester City part ways with sponsor after controversial porn actress partnership

Leicester City have parted ways with front-of-shirt sponsor BC Game “with immediate effect” after a row engulfed the company over its partnership with a porn actress.

Gambling firm BC Game, which has sponsored the League One side since the beginning of the 2024-25 season, announced Russian adult film actress Eva Elfie as a brand ambassador earlier this month. BC Game described Elfie as “an established presence in gaming communities”.

The move sparked widespread backlash from Leicester supporters, including the Foxes Trust, which issued a damning response: “From the moment the club decided to take sponsorship from a betting company we felt Leicester City were ditching its values.

“Now throw into the mix that BC Game have entered into a partnership with someone who continues to make their name via adult content.

“Both gambling and pornography can have devastating impacts on people's lives, in particular young people.

“Partnerships like these bring these two worlds to a larger audience and, by association, link them to Leicester City. Is this who we are now?”

On Wednesday Leicester said it and BC Game, which is registered in Belize and not licensed to operate in the UK, had “reached a mutual agreement to conclude our commercial partnership with immediate effect.”

It added: “The decision follows constructive discussions between both parties in recent days, during which we recognised our differing commercial priorities moving forward.

“We would like to thank BC Game for its support throughout its two-and-a-half-year association with Leicester City and for the constructive approach taken in reaching this agreement.”

The deal with BC Game had been due to run until the end of the 2026-27 season.

Leicester added that supporters who had bought a BC Game-branded shirt through official retail channels within the last 28 days could return it for a full refund or exchange until 2 December, including shirts which had been worn or personalised, which would not normally qualify.
