“C’est Bow!”; Lea Seydoux Wore the Night’s Best Dress at the 2026 Governors Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Last night, the 2026 Governors Awards served as the official kickoff to the 2027 awards season—and all of Hollywood’s finest came out for the cinematic occasion. One such well-clad star in attendance was the French actor Léa Seydoux, who hit the red carpet in a red, crêpe jersey off-the-shoulder gown by Louis Vuitton, complete with an oversized satin bow at the back. “It was a night to celebrate the people who make us love cinema,” says Seydoux of attending the ceremony. “It's about the craft—the people behind the films. That means a lot to me.”

The star herself has been garnering buzz for her new film, Marie Kreutzer’s Gentle Monster, which releases this week. In the dark drama, Seydoux portrays a renowned concert pianist whose life is turned upside down after her husband is arrested for possessing child sexual abuse material. “Marie Kreutzer is a fearless director, and she trusted me with something very delicate,” says Seydoux. “Honestly, at first I was afraid of the subject. But I think when you are afraid, it means maybe you have to do it.” The film served up another challenge for Seydoux, too: It marked her first time having to sing in a project. “It was like jumping from a cliff,” says Seydoux. “And to finally work with Catherine Deneuve—it was a dream!”

Last look. We kept the hair and makeup very simple—the bob, a red lip—and we let the dress speak.

Given that she has a buzzy new film everyone is talking about, it only made sense that Seydoux went into full-on movie star mode at the Governors Awards last night. She wanted a look that channeled classic Hollywood glamour—and who better to do it custom than Vuitton, who Seydoux has worked with for a decade now. “It’s always like coming home,” says Seydoux of collaborating with the French label. “I wanted something a bit old Hollywood, but clean and modern. And Red, because it’s a night for the cinema: It's joyful, confident, and not shy.”

Though the powerful color made a statement on its own, Seydoux was also drawn to the striking silhouette of her Vuitton look—courtesy of that big bow. Forget making an entrance, this was a dress that made an exit. “I liked that from the front it's simple, and then when you see the back, there is a surprise,” says Seydoux. “The front of the dress is very pure, just the shape and the satin on the shoulders. And then you turn, and there is this huge bow with the train. It's a bit theatrical, a bit playful—I love it.” She accessorized with the label’s high jewelry pieces.

C'est bow. I think this is my style—quite minimal, but with one strong idea.

It made for one of the night’s most memorable fashion serves—and the good news is Seydoux has plenty more red carpets to hit in the busy awards season ahead. Gentle Monster hits Netflix on Wednesday, but she also has other projects coming up. “I'm very excited for people to see it,” she says. “There is also The Unknown from Arthur Harari, with Neon. And in February, I will be president of the César [Awards], which is a big honor for me.”

In between all of the premieres and red carpets, however, Seydoux will be trying to squeeze in a little “me time” this fall—back in her native City of Light, of course. “In between, I try to stay home in Paris with my family as much as I can,” she says. “For me this balance is very important.” One can’t be serving glamour 24/7—even stars need a little break.
