No Black Friday in Crypto: Bitcoin (BTC), Solana (SOL), XRP Rise as $170 Million in Shorts Get Wiped Out

Crypto traders who bet on a decline lost nearly $170 million to liquidations over the past 24 hours as Bitcoin, Solana and XRP rose on Black Friday. Short positions made up 68.3% of the $248.8 million in total liquidations.

CoinGlass data showed on Friday that liquidations hit 72,023 traders over that period, and Binance accounted for 44.9% of the total. The largest single order was a $9.3 million BTCUSDT short on Binance, which CoinGlass recorded at a price of $62,147.

Bitcoin shorts lost $64.9 million over 24 hours, and Bitcoin longs lost $20.9 million, according to CoinGlass. On the CoinGlass heatmap, shorts made up 75.7% of Bitcoin's $85.8 million in liquidations across all trading pairs. Bitcoin was trading at around $61,400 on Friday, up 0.9% over 24 hours.

CoinGlass recorded $139.6 million of the short liquidations in the past 12 hours, against $29.9 million in long liquidations over the same window.

Ethereum shorts lost $38.1 million, the second-largest amount among all assets on CoinGlass. ETH traded at around $1,721 on Friday, up 1% over 24 hours.

According to CoinGlass, Solana gained 3.3% to around $73, and SOL shorts lost $6 million. XRP rose 1.9% to $0.97, and its shorts lost $2 million.

On Thursday, CryptoQuant CEO Ki Young Ju estimated in a post on X that Bitcoin had about $16 billion in potential short liquidations between $60,000 and $100,000. According to Ju, potential long liquidations below that range totaled less than $5 billion. "The squeeze risk is on the short side," he wrote.

Deribit's monthly Bitcoin options expiry took place at 08:00 UTC on Friday. About 110,800 BTC contracts, worth roughly $6.75 billion, expired with a max pain price of $70,000, according to Deribit data.

BitMEX co-founder Arthur Hayes warned about the weekend in an X post on Friday. "If there is no Fed dovish rhetoric pivot before the pre-FOMC blackout begins EOD Fri US time, this weekend could get spicy," Hayes wrote. He added that liquid assets and crypto could get dumped before Monday's open.

Earlier this week, Hayes said that Bitcoin "will retest $55k" and shared a chart with the price at $58,773. On Nov. 21, Bitcoin fell as low as $55,150, its lowest price since September 2024.

At its October meeting, the Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.75%–4% in a 10–2 vote. Fed Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voted for a quarter-point hike.

The Fed's blackout period starts at midnight ET on Nov. 28, and the FOMC will announce its rate decision on Dec. 9. CME FedWatch put the odds of a 25-basis-point hike at 100% on Nov. 12.
