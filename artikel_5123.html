French Senate Rejects Disclosure Rule for Crypto

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

French senators on Nov. 24 rejected a proposal that would have required taxpayers to disclose self-hosted cryptocurrency holdings exceeding €100,000.

They would have had to report those assets as of Dec. 31.

This would have been the case even if they had held the same coins since January without making any sales.

Two matching amendments to the French 2027 budget were rejected by the Senate during the first reading of the draft.

Senators Pascal Savoldelli and Anne-Sophie Romagny reintroduced the proposal after it briefly passed in the National Assembly earlier in the month.

French taxpayers must disclose their crypto assets held on foreign crypto platforms.

These amendments would have expanded disclosure requirements to wallets that owners directly control. This pertains to a wallet that is not managed by an exchange or another custodian.

The French National Assembly approved the €100,000 disclosure threshold in a 150-44 vote on Nov. 3. There were also eight abstentions.

The next day, the National Assembly voted down the entire revenue portion of the budget.

This vote brought down the wallet disclosure provision along with the rest of the budget.

However, the subsequent effort to revive the provision in the Senate was defeated.

It should be noted that the 2027 budget is still being considered at the time of writing.

The Senate also rejected two other crypto-related measures debated as part of the budget.

One was a provision to tax gains realized when holders exchange cryptocurrencies for certain stablecoins.

The second was a proposal to expand France's exit tax to include unrealized gains on cryptocurrencies held by taxpayers who move their tax residence outside the country.
