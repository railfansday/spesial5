Bitcoin Whales Accumulate $7.4 Billion in BTC, Breaking a Nine Year Pattern

Bitcoin whales have reversed their usual year-end pattern. Between September 30 and November 21, balances in addresses holding 1,000 to 10,000 BTC rose by 121,117 BTC even though Bitcoin lost 29.5% of its value. At a reference price of $61,000, those additional coins are worth roughly $7.4 billion.

The figures come from CryptoQuant’s November 27 research report, which was supported by crypto lender CoinRabbit. The study excludes exchange and mining-pool addresses from its balance cohorts. Its findings are a snapshot through November 21, not a final tally for the quarter.

The timing matters. NewsBTC recently reported more than $2 billion in US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows. Fund withdrawals and rising whale balances point in different directions, but they measure different things. Neither tells us whether the same investors are moving their money from ETFs into direct Bitcoin ownership.

A broader measure from Maketo’s whale holdings data puts the balance of addresses with at least 1,000 BTC at 7.22 million BTC on November 26, up 54,379 BTC over the preceding 30 days. That group includes exchange and custody addresses, so it provides a separate view of where coins are held rather than an independent check of CryptoQuant’s 121,117 BTC figure.

The combined balance of the 1,000 to 10,000 BTC cohort at just under 2.95 million BTC on November 21, up from 2.83 million at the end of September.

Large addresses reduced their balances in seven of the nine preceding fourth quarters. Only 2019 and 2020 escaped that pattern. The 4.29% increase recorded so far this quarter is the largest Q4 gain since 2020, although December could still change the result.

For comparison, the cohort lost nearly 203,000 BTC over Q4 2025. This year’s increase has come during a falling market, rather than a rally.

There is a catch in calling this a $7.4 billion accumulation. The figure values the additional coins at a reference price; it does not measure the dollars spent buying them. Transfers between addresses, including movements across the cohort’s balance thresholds, can change the total without a market purchase.

The increase tells us where Bitcoin is sitting. It does not tell us how much fresh capital is behind it. That distinction matters when liquidity is scarce.

The 121,117 BTC figure makes for a bullish headline. The number I would pay closer attention to is the remaining profit margin.

Among addresses holding between 1,000 and 10,000 BTC, the unrealized profit margin fell from 33.9% at the end of September to 5.7% on November 21, its lowest reading of 2026 in the report.

The deterioration is even clearer in dollar terms. Combined unrealized profits for addresses holding at least 1,000 BTC stood at about $24 billion on November 21, down from a peak of $294 billion in August 2025.

Across the broader group of addresses holding at least 1,000 BTC, net realized losses reached approximately $2.1 billion in the first 52 days of Q4. The on-chain data suggests that some large holders are already moving coins at a loss rather than waiting for a recovery.

The relevant distinction is between the balance held at a given date and the profit or loss on coins that move. A growing balance does not prevent other coins within the cohort from being spent below their estimated cost basis.

Exchange deposits deserve a closer look, too. Inflows from unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs) of at least 1,000 BTC averaged 5,012 BTC per day in November through the report’s cutoff, compared with 3,239 in October. That is a measure of large coin outputs, not a count of individual whales.

November’s pickup should not obscure the bigger picture: the quarter-to-date average of 3,955 BTC per day remained below the 2019–2025 Q4 average of 4,575 BTC. Large deposits are not proof of sales, and a rise in this metric alone does not establish that selling pressure has intensified.

The 2018 and 2022 bear markets offer a useful comparison, though not a roadmap for this one.

Balances in the 1,000 to 10,000 BTC cohort declined in both of those fourth quarters. Their average unrealized profit margins were negative, while the group still had a positive margin at the November 21 reading this year.

Today’s accumulation may be a sign that some large holders are willing to retain exposure through the downturn. The address data cannot establish their motives or identify how much of the increase came from new purchases.

I would not call a market bottom on these numbers alone.

A further price decline could wipe out the cohort’s remaining profit cushion. Whether that produces more selling depends on the holders’ liquidity needs and positioning, neither of which can be read directly from address balances.

The next test is whether large-address balances keep rising while net realized losses and large exchange deposits ease. That combination, alongside evidence of renewed spot demand, would make the recovery case more convincing.

For now, the change in year-end positioning is real within CryptoQuant’s measured cohort. Evidence of a durable recovery is still missing.
