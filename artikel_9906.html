Microsoft are rejecting your mail

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

On Friday morning, we became aware that a significant amount of email to Microsoft-run mail systems was being rejected. This included mail to hotmail.com and outlook.com.

The messages were being rejected with the following message:

550 5.7.1 Message rejected: Message lacks SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth Records

We checked our logs, and found that we’d seen this message over 500 times on Thursday, but not once before that.

The affected messages all had completely valid SPF and DKIM, but did not have a DMARC record. We added a DMARC record with a “none” policy to some of the affected domains, and sure enough, mail was then accepted again. A quick fedi-survey confirmed that we were not alone.

We don’t normally re-use blog photos, but this one seemed too perfect.

Publishing a policy of none is effectively the same as not publishing a policy at all.

Microsoft are now rejecting mail without a DMARC policy, but accepting mail with a policy of none. This is a massive and pointless change in their policy for incoming mail, made with no notice whatsoever.

Last year Microsoft, introduced a policy that required domains sending more than 5,000 messages per day to have a DMARC record. This change was announced in advance. This latest change appears to be completely unannounced, which makes us wonder if it was even intentional. Of course, trying to contact Microsoft in order to resolve mail issues such as this is futile.

Update: apparently it wasn’t intentional, but they haven’t figured out how to roll it back yet.

We’ve just applied p=none DMARC records to a large number of our customers’ domains. We generally try to avoid making unsolicited changes to DNS zones, but in this case we feel that it is the best option.

If you’re in contact with Microsoft users affected by this, please encourage them to complain to Microsoft; it’s very likely that they are losing other legitimate email from other providers because of this unannounced change. Better still, encourage them to move to an email provider that is pro-active in investigating email deliverability issues.
