If your mortgage rate is below today’s rates, keep that mortgage and pull equity out with a home equity loan or line of credit. Either one charges a new rate only on the cash you take out, while a cash-out refinance puts your whole balance at today’s rate.

In the second quarter of 2026, 49.1% of existing mortgages had a rate under 4%, the Federal Housing Finance Agency reports. If yours is one of them, a cash-out refinance would raise the rate on money you’ve already borrowed.

Say you owe $200,000 at 3.5%. Refinance at 7% to take out cash, and that $200,000 costs about $7,000 more in first-year interest than it does at 3.5%. A second loan, either a home equity loan or a home equity line of credit (HELOC), leaves it at 3.5%.

A cash-out refinance costs less than a second loan only when today’s rate is below yours or you need far more cash than you still owe.

Each of the five ways to pull equity out carries its own cost and risk.

All five are secured by your house, so borrowing against your home makes sense only for costs that outlast the debt, like a new roof.

Most lenders cap everything you owe on the house, old loan and new, at 80% to 85% of its value. Multiply your home’s value by 80% and subtract what you owe to see roughly how much you can take out.
