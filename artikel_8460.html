How to Close a Bank Account – Empty It Last, Then Get It in Writing

Before you close a bank account, wait until every check and automatic payment tied to it has cleared. Then move the balance to exactly zero, ask the bank to close it, and get written confirmation. Otherwise, a late payment can overdraw the account or bounce, and other banks can hold that against you for years.

That’s because a bank can close an overdrawn account on its own and report it to ChexSystems, a reporting agency banks use to screen new customers. ChexSystems keeps that record for five years, even after you pay what you owe.

Payments already on their way don’t stop when you empty or close an account. A check you wrote last month can overdraw an empty account, and your bank can make you repay the overdraft before it lets you close it.

On a closed account, the same check can bounce, which the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) warns can cost you fees and make your next checking account harder to open.

Either owner of a joint checking account can usually close it alone, the CFPB says, so make sure the other owner’s deposits and payments have moved too. A custodial account you manage for a child holds the child’s money, so close it by moving the balance to another account for that child, never to yours.

However you close it, get an email or letter confirming the closing date and the $0 balance, and keep it with your final statement.
