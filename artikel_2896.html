Nick Sirianni sticking with Sean Mannion, Joe Milton out of bye

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Nick Sirianni sticking with Sean Mannion, Joe Milton out of bye  originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

The Eagles are sticking with offensive coordinator Sean Mannion and quarterback Joe Milton coming out of the bye week, head coach Nick Sirianni said on Wednesday afternoon.

Before their Week 10 bye week, the Eagles lost seven straight games with an offense that now ranks dead last in yards and 30th in scoring.

But Sirianni is sticking with Mannion as the Eagles 'play-caller going into Week 11's game against the Steelers.

"I think we had a really good bye week process," Sirianni said. "Got to evaluate a lot of things within that. I think I put into pretty good detail of kind of how we go through things and the things that present themselves as you go through it. I think we have a good plan of action moving forward of the things that we want to do to move forward and to get better. Obviously, we haven't been good enough on offense but I like our plan of attack and our process that we went through last week."

There was a lot of excitement when the Eagles hired Mannion this offseason but the offense has struggled all season. Mannion is a first-year OC, so maybe the Eagles expected some growing pains. But this has been extreme. They literally have the worst offense in the NFL. Even when you acknowledge the Eagles lost Jalen Hurts and have been really injured, that's still not acceptable.

Sirianni has previously praised Mannion for his work ethic. But aside from that, what gives the head coach confidence in Mannion?

"I think he's really smart. I think he has a great football mind," Sirianni said. "He thinks through everything. I think one thing I always value about him is anybody can come and give a play and say, 'Hey, I want to do this or I want to do that.' But I think he has a really good monotonous approach to figuring out everything that could potentially happen in that situation and what are the downfalls and what [is] the upside and do we move forward with some of those things? Again, I think that just speaks to the detail of what he has.

"Again, it hasn't went well enough for any of us this year offensively. That's coaches, that's players. We all have things that we need to do better. But I do love that approach that he has with that. As we looked at some of our schemes that he's brought in, some of those have been really, really good. Again, it has to be better as a whole. Without getting too much into the specifics of what those are. We just need to build on that."

The Eagles did recently bring back T.J. Paganetti as a consultant, but Sirianni said he didn't make any other staff changes during the bye week.

The Eagles gave Milton his first-career start before the bye week in the 19-11 loss to the Giants. While Milton did some good things, the offense still struggled, which was probably to be expected after he had been with the team for just three weeks.

Perhaps Milton will be in a better spot against the Steelers on Sunday.

"He works extremely hard with everything," Sirianni said of Milton. "Really love his approach, like his personality. But I really appreciate his approach of how he goes through everything. Coaches have done a nice job, Parks (Frazier) has done a nice job. But his approach, he's a really hard worker and I really value that."

The good news for Mannion, Milton and the offense is that Sirianni said the team is "hopeful" that DeVonta Smith (hamstring) and Dontayvion Wicks (ankle) will be able to play on Sunday. The Eagles' top two receivers missed the three games before the bye week.
