Eli Lilly Is Now Worth 7 Novo Nordisks. Here’s How the Gap Got So Wide.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Eli Lilly's GLP-1 weight-loss drugs continue to grow at a rapid clip.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 26, 2026 at 11:20AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Eli Lilly ›

Eli Lilly's (LLY -1.09%) market cap is roughly $1.2 trillion as of this writing. Novo Nordisk's (NVO +1.64%) market cap is $172.5 billion. Both are very large companies, but Eli Lilly is roughly seven times as large as Novo Nordisk. All the same, they are the number one and two players in the GLP-1 weight-loss niche of the pharmaceutical sector.

Given that Novo Nordisk was the first to market with its GLP-1 shot, how did Eli Lilly get so much bigger? The story is a mix of self-inflicted wounds, intense competition, and short-term investors on Wall Street who may be missing a long-term investment opportunity. Here's what you need to know.

Novo Nordisk brought out its Wegoy/Ozempic GLP-1 weight-loss shot before Eli Lilly introduced Mounjaro/Zepbound. That should have given Novo Nordisk a big head start, but the company underestimated the intensity of demand. It couldn't produce enough of the drugs, which opened the market to generic competition more quickly than usual. When Eli Lilly introduced its own GLP-1 drug, Novo Nordisk was competing with both Mounjaro/Zepbound and generic versions of its own drugs at the same time. Adding to the problem was the fact that Mounjaro/Zepbound was more effective. It isn't hard to see why Eli Lilly quickly took the lead in this emerging drug niche.

Eli Lilly hasn't looked back. Through the first nine months of 2026, Mounjaro revenues grew 89%, while Zepbound revenues increased by 56%. The two drugs now account for 65% of the company's revenues. To be fair, that's a concentration problem, given that the patents protecting the two drugs will eventually expire.

However, investors have taken heart in the fact that Eli Lilly is using the cash influx from its GLP-1 drugs to invest in its pipeline. Notably, management has specifically been broadening the drug maker's portfolio into new treatment areas to create a more diversified business. Given the still-strong growth in the GLP-1 niche and the company's expanding drug portfolio, investors have only gotten more and more upbeat about Eli Lilly's future.

Novo Nordisk is definitely working from behind right now, attempting to catch up to the GLP-1 leader. And given the depressed stock price and lofty 4.5% yield, Wall Street doesn't seem convinced that it can make a comeback. And yet, Novo Nordisk was again first to market with its Wegovy GLP-1 pill, beating out Eli Lilly's Foundayo. Foundayo is not the same drug as Mounjaro/Zepbound, so it hasn't performed quite as well as the Wegovy pill. In fact, the Wegovy pill has seen even stronger demand than Novo Nordisk's GLP-1 shots. This time, however, Novo Nordisk is making sure it can adequately supply the market.

There's just one small problem. Despite the success of the pill, the company's financial results have been weak as it shifts to a low-price, high-volume model. Novo Nordisk appears to be making strong progress, given that it increased both its revenue and earnings guidance in the third quarter of 2026. But Wall Street is clearly in a show-me mood.

If you are a long-term dividend investor, however, the stock could be worth a look. Its lofty 4.5% yield is supported by a very strong 33% payout ratio. (For reference, Eli Lilly's yield is a tiny 0.5%.) And Novo Nordisk is clearly still an innovative drugmaker, given its head start with both a GLP-1 shot and a pill.

If you want to buy an industry leader, Eli Lilly is clearly a company you should look at in the pharmaceutical sector. However, the average pharma stock's price-to-earnings ratio is roughly 27x, and Eli Lilly's is 44x. Meanwhile, Novo Nordisk's P/E is just under 10x. If valuation matters to you, Novo Nordisk may be more attractive, and even if it isn't, Eli Lilly could still be too expensive for your tastes.
