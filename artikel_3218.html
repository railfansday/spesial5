The Apple executive behind the iPhone’s Camera app has left

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

He argued photos should show real moments, as rivals lean into AI image editing.

Jon McCormack, Apple’s head of camera and photo software, retired at the end of October after more than eight years, Bloomberg reported. Apple has not named a replacement, and his deputies are taking over his work under other executives.

Jon McCormack, the Apple executive in charge of the iPhone’s Camera and Photos software, has retired, Bloomberg reported on Thursday. He left at the end of October after more than eight years at the company, according to people familiar with the matter.

Apple has not named a new vice president for his role. His former deputies are taking on his work under other executives, Bloomberg said. An Apple spokesperson declined to comment.

McCormack oversaw the software behind how iPhone users take, view and edit photos and videos. His team worked on the redesigned selfie camera in the iPhone 17 and the new camera controls this year, when the iPhone 18 Pro got Apple’s first variable-aperture camera.

He was a strong opponent of using generative AI to create or alter photos, people inside Apple told Bloomberg. Google and Samsung have leaned into AI tools that can make big changes to images.

“A photograph is of something that actually happened,” McCormack told Wired earlier this year.

Unlike its rivals, Apple added a mode that lets the iPhone 18 Pro prove photos are real. Bloomberg’s sources said his retirement was not linked to any clash over Apple’s AI strategy.

McCormack reported to software chief Craig Federighi, whose team is under pressure to speed up AI work. He follows Apple Pay head Jennifer Bailey out the door this autumn, as new CEO John Ternus cuts management layers to move faster.
