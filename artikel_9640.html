Islo Secures $15M Led by Insight Partners for Enterprise Software Factory

Islo has secured a $15 million investment led by Insight Partners to speed up the development and adoption of its Enterprise Software Factory platform, the company said in a press release. The investment was first reported by Axios. Islo is part of the Incredibuild family of products and is a new entrant in the fast-emerging software factory market, where the focus is on automating full engineering workflows with AI agents.

The Enterprise Software Factory is built for engineering organizations that want to go beyond AI coding assistants and adopt automated, agent-driven software development one workflow at a time. It combines enterprise-grade security, continuous verification and persistent development infrastructure in a single platform, so enterprises can automate engineering work while keeping control over security, code quality, infrastructure and deployment.

Along with the investment, Islo announced an upcoming partnership with Databricks, the Data and AI company, which is expected to provide backend inference for the platform. Through that relationship, Islo customers will get secure access to state-of-the-art proprietary and open AI models at global scale.

The $15 million is dedicated to advancing the Enterprise Software Factory platform. Islo plans to use the capital to speed up product development and to compete in a market that is increasingly focused on automating entire software engineering workflows rather than individual coding tasks.

The funding will also support the development and expansion of the Enterprise Software Factory as a dedicated product line. That setup lets Islo move at the pace of a focused AI software venture while drawing on an established enterprise customer base and go-to-market infrastructure.

Shimon Hason, CEO, said the company saw early on where engineering teams would run into friction. "In this AI era, we recognized early on that writing code at velocity would no longer be the bottleneck for engineering teams," Hason said. "The real bottleneck is the processes around it. The isolated environments, continuous verification, and operational gates required to ship software safely. That's what Islo solves, and Insight's investment gives us the resources to bring autonomous, agentic development to enterprises faster and at scale."

As AI agents get better at writing code, enterprise engineering teams are under a new kind of pressure. The growing volume of AI-generated work has to be tested, verified, secured and deployed without overwhelming the development processes those teams already rely on, and organizations need a way to build confidence in the safety of that work over time.

Islo designed the Enterprise Software Factory from the ground up to meet those needs. "AI can dramatically increase the amount of code an engineering organization produces, but that doesn't automatically translate into more software being delivered safely," said Adam Gold, CPO of Islo. "The challenge is building the infrastructure around AI agents so enterprises can automate more of their engineering work without surrendering control over their data, processes or software quality. That is what we built Islo to solve."

Early Islo customers have turned code refactoring, code reviewing, quality assurance, bug fixing, issue triage and long-tail feature delivery into factory lines that run continuously. Depending on how the engineering team sets them up, these lines either react to events or run on a defined schedule.

Each factory line has its own gates for safety, testing, functionality, security, performance and cost, which keeps every line strictly in line with enterprise policies and engineering processes. Teams also decide exactly where and when human approval is required, allowing them to require sign-off before AI-generated changes reach production.

Unlike AI development tools that require organizations to adopt a prescribed technology stack, Islo is built to operate within existing enterprise environments. Customers can choose their preferred large language models, agents, harnesses and compute infrastructure while keeping complete control over data residency, credentials, permissions and security policies.

Agents inside the Software Factory run in isolated environments that are equipped with an engineering team's full development stack. Credentials, secrets and keys can be protected through gateway-based access rather than being exposed directly to the agents. Customers strictly control and configure access to enterprise systems and data, and all agent actions and sessions are recorded so they can be inspected at will or watched live while they're underway.

"Companies like Islo partner with Databricks, to ensure that their customers can trust the products built on our stack to meet their needs at scale," said Heather Akuiyibo, Global VP of GTM Integration at Databricks. "Starting with inference and expanding to a broader product integration with the Databricks platform, we are excited to include Islo into the Built on Program and become the engine that drives Autonomous Engineering for their customers."

The Enterprise Software Factory builds on Incredibuild's decades of experience shipping production software that sits in the critical path for hundreds of enterprise software engineering teams at companies like Citibank, Bosch and Adobe, according to the company. Incredibuild acquired Kypso, the startup founded by Islo's leadership team, in 2025.

That combined expertise contributes to Islo's enterprise-first approach. That approach includes built-in test, build and continuous integration acceleration based on Incredibuild technology.

Insight Partners sees trust as the main barrier to enterprise adoption of AI-driven software engineering. "The blocker to adoption of AI driven software engineering in the enterprise is trust. Trust that an agent didn't access data that it wasn't supposed to. Trust that changes are tested and verified and won't break in production. Trust that policies and controls are strictly enforced," said David Stark, Vice President at Insight Partners. "Islo is built to serve this exact need and help engineering leaders safely move from copilots and agents to automated software development while preserving total control over data security and software quality."

Taken together, the investment, the Databricks partnership and Islo's enterprise foundations position the company to help organizations move from experimenting with AI coding tools to running controlled, repeatable and increasingly autonomous engineering workflows. For Islo, the next step is putting the new capital to work on the Enterprise Software Factory as a dedicated product line.
