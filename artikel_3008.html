Game Recap: Suns survive late Jazz rally to secure 111-101 NBA Cup victory

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Man, this game was an emotional rollercoaster, especially late, but the Suns pulled this one out in a big way. For a team that started 1-5, they have turned things around quickly, winning three straight and only losing two in their last nine games. This is a good trend for a team still figuring things out on the fly with their starting lineup. It's another big win, as this was an NBA Emirates Cup game, which helped them bounce back after their first loss in Group A to the Denver Nuggets.

This game started hot for Phoenix, who took a commanding lead in the first half and held on to it through the third as a lot of free throws were made. In the fourth, the Jazz tried to climb back, cutting the deficit to four, but it was too little, too late as the Suns took care of business on the road. Even though the Suns allowed 17 OREB, their 19 points on 15 turnovers helped them early and led to this deserved win.

Devin Booker led Phoenix with 23 points, two rebounds, and three assists, while Miles Bridges and Dillon Brooks each had 18. Bench mob beasts Collin Gillespie and Jordan Goodwin sealed the win: Gillespie added 16 points, 4 rebounds, and 5 assists, while Goodwin grabbed 9 rebounds and 4 assists, doing much of the dirty work alongside Bridges.

For the Jazz, Darryn Peterson had 20 points, ten rebounds, and eight assists, which was close to a triple-double, while Jaxson Hayes caused havoc on the glass. He finished with 18 points and 14 rebounds, a big reason Utah stayed in this one. Brice Sensabaugh also got hot late, adding 18 of his own points with five threes to keep the Jazz thriving from behind the arc.

For the Phoenix Suns, coming out of the gate hot was needed to get to a three-game win streak. For the Suns, though, before they could score a basket, they had to win their first challenge call that night because of the refs. The Suns starters did look solid in their first stint, as they got Dillon Brooks going early, with Keyonte George guarding him for an easy four points. Phoenix got off to an early run, but the Jazz matched it and tied it up at 12.

Sadly for the Suns, they would have to use their second challenge just seven minutes into the game when Booker was hit across the face by a driving Ace Bailey. Even though Phoenix won both challenges, it was tough to lose them with 40 minutes of game time left. This allowed the bench to come in and resume where they left off. Jordan Goodwin and Collin Gillespie made it known when they got out there, with Gillespie making the first three of the night for both teams.

With Khaman Maluach and Ryan Dunn checking in, the Suns seemed more aggressive on the boards and limited the Jazz's second-chance points. The Suns gave up 12 OREB in the first half, and that cannot continue into the second half. With the Jazz finding some threes late, they brought the game closer to the end of the first quarter, but Booker made the last shot count, hitting a buzzer-beater off a full-court pass from Miles Bridges. The Suns led 26-23 at the end of the first quarter.

Heading into the second, we saw the bench continue to prove they are the best in the league, with Gillespie making some tough buckets and Luke Kennard getting an easy five points on two quick shots. Unfortunately, the trouble on the glass is an issue, as Jaxson Hayes is dominating and getting multiple tip-ins and one-and-one opportunities.

Though this was the case, Brooks and Bridges provided the Suns with the scoring to take their largest lead so far, 43-30, with a strong run and Bridges getting comfortable. Sadly, the Suns saw an injury when Ryan Dunn caught a knee to the thigh and went to the locker room. He provided solid defense and rebounding, allowing the offense to flow better with ball movement.

The Jazz, though, didn't want to go out quiet as Darryn Peterson started showing off his skills on both ends. Bailey secured a tip-in at the end, but the Suns saw some nice moves from Malauch in his second stint. The big caught a nice block and made some easy passes for putbacks and points. The Suns finished the first half up 61-49. Brooks and Gillepsie lead the way for the Suns with eleven points each, while Bridges. Booker and Maluach have eight. It's truly been a team game offensively, with the ball being shared, especially in the second unit. For the Jazz, Peterson has 12 points, while Hayes has 11, and Sensabaugh has 3 made threes. To win this game, the Suns have to win the rebounding battle and control the possession.

To kick off the second half, the Suns found out that Ryan Dunn would not return, leaving his minutes in limbo. That is where Koa Peat got some burn and, unfortunately, didn't get too much of it, as he picked up some early fouls. The Suns did a better job on the glass, limiting the Jazz's second-chance points, but the Jazz were still getting to the charity stripe.

This is where the game took a toll on me, as it dragged on with both teams finding opportunities to score only at the free-throw line. Brooks and Bridges, though, continued to show their offensive flow, with Bridges driving and delivering some nice paint scoring alongside some boards. The Suns got the lead back to 12, and Booker found a rhythm of his own, scoring 10 points in the quarter to bring his total to 18.

The thirteen Jazz turnovers really cost them in this game, and with the Suns doing a better job of limiting them on the glass, the Suns looked on track for an easy win if they closed out the fourth. Although this is the Phoenix Suns we are talking about, they always make your heart skip a beat, even when they play the worst teams.

The fourth quarter opened with Brice Sensabaugh getting red hot and quickly scoring four points. The Suns answered with tough shots of their own from Gillespie, but the Jazz weren't bowing out of this one. With more rim pressure from Hayes, the Jazz got back on the glass and caused some disruptions. Peterson also showed off his quickness to get to the cup and now had a double-double with some time left.

Bridges once again helped this team, alongside Booker, keep them afloat with some nice baskets. Brooks picked up his fifth, then eventually his sixth foul, and was fouled out of the game, which was not a good look as the Jazz rallied back. With more made threes, the Suns, who had a 16-point lead at one point, were down to just four with under a minute left.

Luckily for this team, Bridges was on tonight and made multiple nice inbound passes to Booker to get a guaranteed bucket, and here was another example of that. Booker then went on to get another three points by hunting his shots, and the Suns pulled off a 111-101 win. This is now the team's longest win streak this season, at three, and they just secured their first NBA Cup win this year. Overall, it wasn't the best win on paper, but it was solid nonetheless.

The Suns continue their road trip, heading to New Orleans to take on the Pelicans.
