‘Squid Game’ Creator Hwang Dong-hyuk Teases Possible Return to Franchise: ‘When I Have a Great Idea, I’ll Make Another One’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Emmy-winning director Hwang Dong-hyuk said he believes he will "someday" return to the hit franchise while speaking about its massive success at the Doha Industry Days.

Hwang Dong-hyuk, the Emmy-winning creator and director of Netflix‘s global phenomenon “Squid Game,” said he plans to “someday” return to the story that launched him into international stardom. As it stands, “Squid Game” officially ended with Season 3 in 2025, with Hwang himself confirming the end of the series. Netflix has already launched a spin-off, “Squid Game: The Challenge,” with the rumor mill swirling around other possible expansions, including a U.S. remake. Speaking on a panel at the Doha Industry Days, the industry program of the Doha Film Festival, Hwang was asked what would be next in the world of “Squid Game.” “Unfortunately, it’s not mine. I don’t own ‘Squid Game.’ Netflix owns the IP, so it’s not my decision how to expand,” he said.

“As far as I’m concerned, someday I’ll get back to ‘Squid Game,'” he added. “When I have a great idea, I’ll make another one.”

For now, Hwang is focused on his next feature film, “K.O. Club.” The project will reunite the director with “Squid Game” star Lee Byung-hun, joined in the cast by fellow franchise alum Park Hae-soo, Oh Jung-se (“It’s Okay to Not Be Okay”), Cho Yeo-jeong (“Parasite”) and Kim So-jin (“The Man Standing Next”). Firstman Studio, the production company Hwang runs with producer Kim Ji-yeon, who also goes by Jenny Kim, confirmed the casting, with filming targeted for the first half of 2027.

“K.O. Club” is a dystopian sci-fi set around 20 years in the future, where the rich can buy youth. In that scenario, an increasingly desolate and disgruntled young generation starts the titular “Killing Old Men Club” to organize assassinations of the wealthy and powerful over 80. “I believe the world is getting worse and worse,” Hwang said of the inspiration behind the project.

“People live longer because of technology and science, so they are not dying,” he added. “Maybe they’ll [get to] live to 150 years old. The rich never go away. They grab the power and money in their hands, which means the younger generation has nothing to do; they have no power, no money, no jobs.”

Hwang said the effect on young people is already visible, pointing to “all those riots in Paris and everywhere.”

Hwang and Kim spoke at length about the surreal experience of seeing their series blow up into one of Netflix’s most-watched shows ever, a global sensation that defined culture in the pandemic years. “After the big reaction from the global audience and the success that followed, as a producer, I faced a new challenge and environment,” said Kim. “Most of the people, all the people except for the main character, died during the game [in the first season], so the biggest challenge was to develop the new story with new people.”

“There was pressure from Netflix,” Kim or Hwang said of Season 2, which arrived three years after Season 1. “We needed to deliver it fast, and the global audience was waiting for the next season. We were always getting questions: ‘Where is Season 2? Season 3?’ It was a nightmare.”

The team kept the production process the same across seasons, Kim said, largely to meet the expectations of “Squid Game” fans all over the world. “What was it that fans liked about ‘Squid Game’? We tried to maintain that.”

Hwang said he never tries to “make something that people like.” “I always try to make what I like to make while at the same time hoping and praying people like what I like to make. If I lose that mindset, I’ll go crazy.”

“When I first wrote the script, the beginning point of my creative process was when I was poor,” he recalled. “I didn’t have much money and imagined: What if it was me? Would I enter this kind of competition and try to win the prize? I was so broke in 2009. I was so broke. I only had like $5 in my account.”

Hwang recalled reading Japanese comics and manga about survival games where the tasks were “mentally, intellectually, and physically difficult.” “A guy like me could not survive [it]. I started thinking like that. What if there was something where a normal guy like me could not only compete but become a winner? That’s how I developed the idea of ‘Squid Game.'”

Because Season 1 was so well received by a global audience, Hwang said, he had the self-belief that Seasons 2 and 3 would also be loved. “I tried to brainwash myself throughout the creative process.”

“I felt more free to do things my own way,” he added. “I believe that the success of the first series, whatever the previous success was, was leverage, an asset, to be able to take bigger risks. When you have big success, everybody believes you. No matter what you say, they say I love it. That’s when you take a big risk in your life. That’s the only chance.”

As for what’s next for Firstman Studio, Kim said the company’s credibility went up after “Squid Game,” but that it remains a very small company. “We’re not making a lot of projects. The reason we want to do this is that, as an independent producer, rather than focusing on profitability, we want to focus on taking the right creative risks to create what we believe in.”
