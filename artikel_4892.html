Weighted blankets are a force in the sleep world, with fans swearing they can do everything from easing anxiety to helping you sleep better. While there are some small studies to support those claims, the science is still catching up.

Still, if you’ve spent any amount of time wrapped in a weighted blanket, you know how cozy it can feel. You’re basically in a cocoon, protected from the outside world. That extra weight also means this blanket won’t just slide off during the night, keeping you nice and snug all night long.

How do they work, you ask? The concept is pretty simple: These types of blankets mimic healing touch—something that reduces cortisol (the stress hormone) and boosts oxytocin (the feel-good hormone), Heidi Hanna, PhD, author of Stressaholic and an advisory board member for the American Institute of Stress, explains.

“While there isn’t much research to verify, the theory is that a weighted blanket works much like a hug,” adds Leisha Cuddihy, PhD, a clinical psychologist in behavioral sleep medicine. Hugging lowers blood pressure and stress hormones, which she says can help you snooze soundly. So, if you’re looking for some much-needed relief in the p.m., a weighted blanket is worth a try. Weighted blankets also make excellent holiday presents—who doesn’t want the gift of sleep?

Not all weighted blankets are created equal, though. Fill, material, and the actual weight of the blanket matter.

It’s no surprise that this is the most important consideration. There’s no one perfect weight. Rather, it’s essential to get a blanket that’s right for your body type. Weighted blankets typically range from 5 to 30 pounds, and many experts suggest picking one that’s around 10 percent of your body weight. This will ensure you don’t experience any discomfort as you sleep. Ideally, you want the right amount of pressure without putting so much weight on your body that you feel “trapped” or suffocated.

You’ll want a blanket that offers uniform pressure to feel the calming effect, says Cuddihy. Look for quality stitching, layers of material, and glass-bead fill. Some lower-quality weighted blankets will have plastic pellets instead of glass beads, so keep an eye out when shopping.

Depending on the time of year and whether you run hot or cold, the material your weighted blanket is made from matters. If you’re a hot sleeper, look for blankets made with breathable fabrics like bamboo or cotton. A removable cover can be helpful, too, for easier machine washing. If you like to be bundled in warmth under your blankets, look for materials like fleece or microfiber.

Weighted blankets need to be washed, too, making it important to find one that won’t make your life harder. Blankets filled with glass microbeads, plastic pellets, steel beads, or pebbles can usually go in the wash, while those that are filled with organic materials often can’t. Your washing machine may also not be able to handle the extra weight. So, read the care instructions before purchasing your blanket to make sure it lines up with your lifestyle.

There’s a lot of variety with weighted blanket sizing, making it important to read the measurements carefully. If you plan to use your weighted blanket on your bed like a comforter, or just want to make sure it’ll cover your body while you snooze, try measuring out the size on your bed to see what it will look like to get a better sense. Consider sizing up if you’re unsure.
