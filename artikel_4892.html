Standard Chartered’s Zodia Custody suffers data breach

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Zodia Custody’s CEO said the breach exposed names and email addresses of some clients and employees and was detected by the company Monday.

Update (Nov. 26, 1 p.m. UTC): This article has been updated with Zodia Custody’s statement clarifying the incident’s timeline, the information accessed and its finding of no unauthorized transactions to date.

Standard Chartered’s cryptocurrency custody unit, Zodia Custody suffered a security breach that exposed some client and employee information.

Zodia detected attempts to probe its platform Monday and temporarily took its business-to-business API offline as a precaution, CEO Richard Clark said in a statement Thursday. The company restored the service after implementing additional security measures.

On Tuesday, monitoring revealed that an unauthorized party had accessed publicly available organization details and business contact names and email addresses for a small number of clients and employees.

Zodia’s investigation found no unauthorized transactions.

Cointelegraph approached Standard Chartered and Zodia Custody for comment but didn’t receive an immediate response.

The breach comes just over a month after Standard Chartered signed definitive agreements to acquire Zodia Custody’s regulated operating entities and fold its crypto custody business into the bank’s existing Securities Services operations.

Standard Chartered reportedly began considering incorporating Zodia’s crypto custody business in April. The UK-based lender also expanded its crypto services in September, when Standard Chartered launched spot Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH) trading for institutional clients in the United Arab Emirates.
