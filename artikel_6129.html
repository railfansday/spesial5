The  is getting a significant overhaul, pairing a higher annual fee with new Atmos Rewards earning rates and a collection of perks for businesses that regularly fly on Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines.

Starting Nov. 18, new applicants can earn 10 points per dollar on qualifying flights booked through Atmos™ for Business, receive four Alaska Lounge passes annually and work toward flight discounts and bonus status points through spending.

The biggest winners may be companies with multiple employee cardholders that regularly book Alaska or Hawaiian flights. They'll pay a flat $195 annual fee regardless of how many employee cards they issue, while benefiting from new travel perks.

However, the refresh also eliminates some valuable benefits, including the annual $99 Companion Fare and the $100 discount on an Alaska Lounge+ membership. Existing cardholders won't automatically transition to the new version and can keep their current benefits.

New applicants can earn a limited-time offer of 90,000 bonus points after spending $6,000 on purchases within the first 90 days of account opening, worth about $1,440 based on TPG's November 2026 valuations. By point total, that's the largest offer in TPG's recorded history for the card, but it's not the richest because the new offer doesn't include Alaska's famous $99 Companion Fare.

Starting Nov. 18, the Atmos Rewards Business card will feature a new flat annual fee regardless of the number of cards, stronger rewards on flights booked through Atmos for Business and several new travel-day perks.

Annual fee: $195 for the company, with no additional fee for employee cards (was previously $70 + $25 per card, including for the primary cardholder)

The refresh also takes a few things away.

New cardholders will no longer earn 2 points per dollar spent on gas, electric vehicle charging, shipping and local transit/ride-hailing. New cardholders will also lose the ability to earn the annual Companion Fare through card spending and the $100 discount on an annual Alaska Lounge+ membership.

Overall, the theme is clear: The refreshed card concentrates its value around Alaska and Hawaiian travel, especially for companies willing to book through Atmos for Business and issue cards to multiple employees.

If you opened your before Nov. 18, 2026, you won't automatically receive the refreshed benefits or move to the new annual-fee structure.

Instead, Bank of America says existing cardholders will keep their current benefits, including the opportunity to earn the annual $99 Companion Fare under the previous card's terms.

If you're interested in switching to the refreshed card, you can call Bank of America at 1-800-892-3219 to explore your options.

Applications submitted before Nov. 18 will also remain under the previous card offering, even if they're still pending.

One of the biggest changes is the new annual-fee structure.

The previously charged $70 per company plus $25 per card, including the owner’s card. Now, it’s a flat $195 regardless of how many cards you have. A solo business owner will now pay more than double what they paid before, a company with five cards pays the same as before, and one with six or more pays less. This is arguably the most significant part of the refresh.

Each cardholder will continue to receive a free checked bag for themselves and up to six guests on the same reservation when the flight is paid for with the card. They also receive preferred boarding on eligible Alaska and Hawaiian flights.

That could make the card particularly appealing to small businesses with several West Coast or Hawaii-based road warriors. The more employees who consistently use the travel benefits, the easier it becomes to justify the companywide fee.

Solo owners who do not need employee cards may find one of the personal Atmos cards better suited to their needs.

In that case, you won't earn points from business spending, but you can enjoy similar perks with the Atmos™ Rewards Ascent Visa Signature® credit card for a lower annual fee or take advantage of more generous benefits (albeit with a higher fee) on the Atmos™ Rewards Summit Visa Infinite® credit card.

However, neither is a direct replacement for a business card, particularly for owners who want to separate business expenses or issue employee cards.

In addition to checked-bag and preferred-boarding perks, the refreshed card will include one Alaska Lounge pass per calendar quarter, for a total of four per account anniversary year. That cadence is best suited to a consultant or salesperson who flies throughout the year, rather than a company whose only major trip is an annual conference.

Meanwhile, the waived fee for same-day confirmed changes ($50 on most Alaska flights) could be particularly useful for employees whose meetings finish early, run late or move unexpectedly. That practical flexibility may save some employees frustration — and save the business itself money on fees.

The card also adds a $50 Instant Travel Delay Credit voucher, which can be used toward a meal or drink at the airport when an eligible flight is canceled or delayed by at least two hours on the day of travel.

Despite the new higher fee, even a solo business owner could potentially offset much of the $195 annual fee through these travel benefits. Someone who checks a bag on two round-trips would save $180; visit a lounge on each of those trips and you'll save another $130 compared to buying day passes.

Earning 10 points per dollar sounds exceptional, but it only applies to flights booked through the Atmos for Business website, the airlines’ self-managed booking platform. That means companies that don't qualify for Atmos for Business — it requires a minimum of $5,000 in annual travel spend — or businesses that book through a corporate travel agency or another platform won't earn this higher return.

Other eligible Alaska and Hawaiian purchases earn 3 points per dollar spent, dining earns 2 points per dollar spent and everything else earns 1 point per dollar. Companies with an eligible Bank of America business account also receive a 10% bonus on points earned from card purchases, effectively turning the 10-point rate into 11 points per dollar.

Lastly, travelers who select the Price Paid earning option and hold Atmos Platinum or Titanium status can earn 20 points or more per dollar on qualifying flights booked through Atmos for Business.

The card's weak general spending rate makes it better suited as a secondary card specifically for Alaska and Hawaiian spending than as a business’s only credit card.

The refreshed no longer includes its annual $99 Companion Fare (plus taxes and fees from $23), which cardholders prior to Nov. 18 earned after spending $6,000 during an account anniversary year.

That is a significant loss for owners who could redeem it for two travelers flying together. Four lounge passes and same-day change flexibility may be more useful for employees traveling alone, but they aren't an automatic replacement for a Companion Fare.

While the new discounts could be valuable for businesses with substantial spending, the $100,000 threshold is significantly higher than the previous $6,000 requirement for earning a Companion Fare.

The welcome offer drops the Companion Fare too, but it includes more points than past offers. Its 90,000 points are enough for three round-trip economy tickets between the West Coast and Hawaii on Alaska or Hawaiian, a one-way ticket on American Airlines in transatlantic business class or a round-trip premium economy ticket from Seattle to Tokyo on an Atmos partner airline.

The refreshed Atmos Rewards Business Card has a higher starting price, but it also has a clearer identity: It's built for companies with multiple cardholders and for recurring Alaska or Hawaiian travel.

Businesses that need employee cards, book substantial airfare through Atmos for Business and have employees who can use the lounge passes and same-day change waiver may come out well ahead.

However, the loss of the Companion Fare and several business spending bonus categories makes the card less appealing for some existing cardholders.

Sole proprietors or companies that don't book through Atmos for Business may still have a case for the card, but they'll need to run the numbers more carefully. And companies that rarely fly either airline or primarily need rewards on everyday operating expenses will likely find the card too specialized.
