Strategy raises its buyback ceiling to $4 billion as MSTR shares sink 21%

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The company has spent nearly $2.9 billion retiring preferred shares, while analysts question the economics of funding those purchases with common-stock issuance.

Strategy raised its STRC buyback authorization for the third time on Monday, bringing the program to $4 billion as its common shares continued to struggle.

The Bitcoin treasury company had exhausted nearly $2.88 billion, or nearly 96% of its previous $3 billion authorization, after spending another $211.8 million repurchasing STRC during the week ended Nov. 22.

The latest expansion comes as Strategy's common shares have fallen 21.1% since it announced its first STRC repurchases on July 27. MSTR closed at $77.80 on Nov. 20, compared with $98.65 nearly four months earlier.

Strategy launched its initial $1 billion digital credit repurchase program in June, then increased it to $2 billion in September and $3 billion in October. Monday's announcement marks the third consecutive $1 billion expansion, bringing cumulative expansion to $4 billion.

The growing commitment has coincided with a shift in how Strategy deploys money raised from common shareholders.

According to its latest SEC filing, the company issued 1.93 million MSTR shares last week, raising $176.7 million.

Of those proceeds, $147 million (83.2%) funded STRC repurchases, while only $29.6 million (16.8%) went toward acquiring 457 Bitcoin.

Strategy contributed another $64.8 million from existing cash to complete the $211.8 million preferred-share repurchase.

The spending has helped reduce its future dividend obligations but has not kept STRC consistently near its $100 stated amount. The security closed at $93.60 on Nov. 20, despite Strategy maintaining a 12% annual dividend rate to support its price.

Meanwhile, Strategy retains a separate $1 billion authorization to repurchase MSTR shares, which remained entirely unused last week.

Bitcoin analyst Derin Olenik criticized the continued issuance of common shares to finance preferred-share repurchases, arguing the transactions weaken shareholders' underlying Bitcoin exposure.

Strategy's published figures support a decline in gross Bitcoin holdings per assumed diluted share during the latest reporting week.

Its outstanding Bitcoin balance increased from 850,849 BTC on Nov. 15 to 851,305 BTC on Nov. 22, while assumed diluted shares outstanding rose from approximately 457.24 million to 459.19 million.

Consequently, gross Bitcoin per share declined from approximately 186,082 satoshis to 185,392 satoshis, a reduction of about 690 satoshis (0.37%).

However, Strategy executive chairman Michael Saylor defended the transactions, arguing that Strategy's approach has consistently increased shareholders' net Bitcoin exposure despite the dilution.

“Our trades accreted Net BPS in each of the last 13 weeks. Cumulatively, we increased Net BPS by 584 sats/share and generated a Net BTC Gain of 2,505 BTC or $199M before preferred dividends, measured at a fixed $BTC price each week.”

Saylor also reported that the latest week's transactions increased Net Bitcoin Per Share by six satoshis and improved Strategy's Net Reserve by $183 million.

The conflicting figures reflect competing assessments of Strategy's capital allocation. Gross Bitcoin per share measures its holdings against diluted shares, while Strategy's Net Bitcoin Per Share accounts for senior claims and dollar assets when calculating the Bitcoin exposure attributable to common shareholders.

Under Saylor's framework, retiring preferred securities can increase net exposure even when common-share issuance reduces gross Bitcoin holdings per share.

Olenik remained critical of the economics, questioning whether the increasingly complex transactions justify the continued dilution of common shareholders.

He also challenged the economics of STRC repurchases. His calculations put the security's average issuance price at $95.81 and its average repurchase price at $97.93, meaning Strategy has spent more buying back preferred shares than it originally received per share issued.

Strategy has defended repurchases below STRC's $100 stated amount because retiring the securities reduces future dividend obligations.

Saylor said Strategy has reduced outstanding credit by $2.9 billion in recent months, lowering annual dividend obligations by approximately $353 million.

The latest transactions alone retired 2.18 million STRC shares, eliminating approximately $26.2 million in annualized dividends at the current 12% rate.

However, the company's latest decision suggests that reducing those obligations will remain a major capital priority.

Alongside the third buyback expansion, Strategy authorized transferring $1 billion from its dedicated USD Reserve into discretionary USD Cash, which management can use for further repurchases or Bitcoin purchases.

The change marks a departure from its original July policy, which explicitly prohibited using the USD Reserve to fund STRC repurchases.

Saylor said the reallocation was possible because the company's remaining reserve could cover its dividend and interest obligations for 3.4 years.

Following the adjustment, Strategy reported approximately $3.7 billion in dedicated reserves and over $1 billion in discretionary cash, leaving total dollar assets at roughly $4.8 billion.

Olenik interpreted the move as evidence that Strategy was preparing to rely more heavily on existing liquidity when issuing MSTR shares becomes less attractive.

That would let Strategy continue retiring preferred securities without relying entirely on new common-share issuance, although the company has not committed the reallocated cash to a specific purchase.

Strategy now has $1.1 billion in remaining preferred-share buyback authority, nearly matching its discretionary cash balance.

Its next capital-allocation decision will determine whether the expanded financial resources support further STRC repurchases or reverse the recent pattern of relatively modest Bitcoin purchases and continued MSTR dilution.
