CleanSpark is selling Bitcoin to fund its AI expansion as annual losses approach $1 billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The miner is turning to its Bitcoin treasury to finance acquisitions without diluting shareholders as mining profitability deteriorates.

CleanSpark has begun selling Bitcoin from its treasury to finance acquisitions as the company accelerates its expansion into artificial intelligence infrastructure.

During the company's Nov. 24 earnings call, President and CFO Gary Vecchiarelli said CleanSpark had sold some of its Bitcoin holdings since the end of September to strengthen its cash position ahead of anticipated acquisitions.

“We've sold some Bitcoin to pad the balance sheet with cash to prepare for some acquisitions that we expect to make in the near future.”

Vecchiarelli added that CleanSpark could sell additional Bitcoin or borrow against its holdings to finance acquisitions without diluting existing shareholders. The company also intends to sell virtually all newly mined Bitcoin to fund daily operations.

CleanSpark ended September with approximately $1.3 billion in reported liquidity, comprising $204.8 million in cash and cash equivalents and approximately $1.13 billion in Bitcoin holdings and related exposure.

The company reported exposure equivalent to 13,530 BTC at fiscal year-end but has not disclosed how much Bitcoin it subsequently sold.

The treasury strategy comes as deteriorating Bitcoin mining economics weigh on CleanSpark's financial performance.

According to its fiscal 2026 results, annual revenue declined 25% to $576.5 million, while the company recorded a net loss of $987.1 million, compared with net income of $364 million a year earlier.

Vecchiarelli attributed approximately two-thirds of the earnings reversal to noncash adjustments in the value of its Bitcoin holdings.

However, profitability also deteriorated across its mining operations. Annual gross margins contracted from 55% to 41% as the average value of mined Bitcoin fell from approximately $97,600 to $79,500.

The decline occurred despite improvements in fleet efficiency and a reduction in electricity costs from $0.057 to $0.055 per kilowatt-hour.

Against this backdrop, CleanSpark is directing more capital toward AI data centers and acquiring land and power infrastructure.

The company secured a 20-year, $6.6 billion lease with an investment-grade tenant at its Sandersville facility in Georgia and raised $2.276 billion through secured notes to fully fund the project's development. It also expanded its Texas infrastructure portfolio through acquisitions supporting up to 885 MW of power capacity.

Vecchiarelli said management sees further acquisition opportunities as a better use of available capital than repurchasing CleanSpark shares, despite believing its stock is undervalued.

While Bitcoin provides CleanSpark with an alternative to equity financing, the cryptocurrency's recent decline has reduced the collateral value supporting its existing borrowing.

CleanSpark's annual filing showed that it had borrowed $90 million through a $100 million revolving credit facility with Two Prime Lending as of Sept. 30.

The company pledged 1,665 BTC worth $139.1 million at the time, giving the loan a loan-to-value ratio of approximately 65%.

Under the agreement, a ratio exceeding 74% triggers a margin call, while liquidation can occur above 80%. Based on the September loan balance and collateral, those thresholds correspond to Bitcoin prices of approximately $73,046 and $67,568, respectively.

With Bitcoin trading near $59,000 on Nov. 25, the same collateral would be worth approximately $98.2 million, pushing the loan-to-value ratio to nearly 92% if the position remained unchanged.

CleanSpark has not disclosed whether it has subsequently increased its collateral or reduced its outstanding debt.

Shareholders will also have less frequent visibility into those decisions after CleanSpark discontinued monthly operational updates in favor of quarterly reporting.

Its next quarterly disclosure could reveal how much of its Bitcoin treasury the company has committed to financing its AI expansion and whether additional acquisitions will require further sales or borrowing.
