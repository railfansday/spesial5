Learn From a Failure – Write a Post-Mortem Within a Week, Then Change One Thing

Within a week of a failure at work or with money, write a short post-mortem while you still remember what you knew then. Note what happened, which parts you controlled, and the warning signs you missed. Then pick one specific change and put it in place before the same kind of decision comes up again.

Wait a month and the details fade. What’s left is an easy story, either bad luck or proof that you’re not cut out for it, and neither one tells you what to change.

Writing it down lets you separate a bad decision from a bad outcome. A careful decision can still go wrong, and a careless one can work out. So judge each choice by what you knew, or could have found out, when you made it, not by how it turned out. That way you stop blaming yourself for luck and find the steps you skipped.

Taking responsibility for your part doesn’t mean beating yourself up, and going easy on yourself can make you try harder. In a UC Berkeley experiment, most students did poorly on a hard vocabulary test. Those who then read a short note telling them they weren’t alone and not to be too hard on themselves studied about 50% longer, on average, for a second test than those who got no note.

Keep the post-mortem to one page, in three parts.

Then turn the step you skipped into one change you’ll make next time. Say an online seller loses $1,200 on a holiday product line. Her post-mortem shows she ordered all the stock before testing whether customers wanted it, so for her next line she takes preorders first and orders only enough to fill them.

Write your change as one sentence that starts with a verb, like “Take preorders before ordering stock,” and put the day you’ll first use it on your calendar.
