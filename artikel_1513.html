Gary Neville compared De Ligt to a man six pints deep. That was him being kind.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

You don’t exactly need to read between the lines to see that Gary Neville isn’t the biggest fan of Manchester United centre-back Mathijs De Ligt.

And that was before the Dutch defender was partly at fault for the major early flashpoint against Liverpool, misjudging a simple long ball and allowing it to run before Leny Yoro committed a last-man challenge on Alexander Isak.

Earlier this week, Neville appeared on the United Stand with Mark Goldbridge and discussed the major squad issues that new boss Mauricio Pochettino will be faced with.

It was a bit of a half-hearted defence, in which he conceded that Diogo Dalot is ultimately a very limited footballer, but he praised the Portuguese full-back for never shirking and always being available.

That’s when attention turned to players he would get rid of.

“I would be getting rid of Mazraoui, well gone, before him. I would be getting rid of Martinez well before him. I would be getting rid of De Ligt gone before him,” Neville said.

Neville’s major issue with De Ligt appears to be his (lack of) availability, having found himself sidelined for most of 2026.

He made a grand total of two appearances under Michael Carrick, one of which was a two-minute cameo against Roma and the other was a rare start in the disasterclass against Roma.

The likes of Cristiano Ronaldo, Jadon Sancho and even Donny van de Beek all somehow had more gametime under the last boss, albeit from his prior interim stint.

“He has got one big problem: he doesn’t play football,” he continued.

“If a player is injury-prone and doesn’t play football, just because he hasn’t played doesn’t mean to say he should go above a player who has played every single week and is throwing his hand up.

“You want someone who’s actually reliable and available to play.

“So, De Ligt has to go. I mean, seriously.”

Sound the narrative klaxon, then, as Pochettino fielded De Ligt for his first Premier League start in over a year.

Unfortunately for him, De Ligt’s fingerprints were all over the match-defining early incident, and he looked shaky – shellshocked, even – as Liverpool had their tails up, swiftly adding a second and looking in the mood to register a historic humiliation on their old foes.

“Well, when I saw Matthijs de Ligt’s name on the teamsheet, I was absolutely stunned, and the reason I was stunned was because of this,” Neville responded to the early red.

“You watched him in Como four weeks ago, and he was very much off it, and that is just a basic ball over the top of him. It’s his problem to start with.

“I mean, Yoro could be further across to start with, but if you look at De Ligt in this channel, he’s miles away from it, and then Yoro should be across; he has to react, and he has to cover, and he doesn’t.

“He’s late, and his anticipation is really poor, and then because he doesn’t play the ball, Yoro, it could be outside the box; it could be in, but it is a red card. It’s so clumsy from Yoro; he makes a lot of these types of mistakes.”

Stood alongside Gary Neville in the Anfield commentary box, there was an audible gasp from Jamie Carragher as Isak then darted through the ragged United backline, nutmegging De Ligt as if it were Soccer Aid and he was up against a washed-up 90s boyband member.

Later on, he completely misjudged a ball to allow Isak (above) with yet another golden opportunity to score.

“It’s almost as if you’ve had six pints,” Neville added later, with more than a hint of sympathy, as he reminisced about his own experience of returning from a long injury lay-off.

De Ligt remained on the pitch, somehow, for over 70 minutes as Pochettino refused to put him out of his misery.

Safe to say it was an early roll of the dice that didn’t pay off for the new manager.
