Why fresh £70m Middlesbrough update will spark interest at Blackburn Rovers

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The end of the calendar year is approaching, which means so is the January transfer window, and Championship clubs will be bracing for interest in some of the better-performing players in the division.

All eyes are on Middlesbrough at the moment, and how much interest they'll face for Will Lankshear, who only just joined the Teessiders this past summer in a deal which could break their transfer record.

As it stands, that potential £20 million fee may not be fully paid, as the 21-year-old has showcased his Premier League quality in the few months he's spent at the Riverside, and his potential is something that elite top-flight sides are keen to invest in.

Middlesbrough will be doing their best to scare off any potential suitors this January with a hefty price tag, but in the wake of their reported asking price, it raises the question of how much other clubs should demand for their big-hitters; more specifically, how much Blackburn Rovers should demand for Andri Gudjohnsen.

There was plenty of expectation surrounding Lankshear ahead of this season. The 21-year-old was still very highly rated out of the Tottenham Hotspur academy, hitting headlines after he scored and then was sent off in a Europa League defeat at Galatasaray in November 2024.

He'd also scored 12 in all competitions last year for an Oxford United side that was relegated from the Championship, with it almost universally agreed that he was a big bright spark in an otherwise disappointing campaign for the Yellows.

However, not many expected Lankshear to be this good, this soon, at his new club. The 21-year-old has 15 goals in 16 games in all competitions for Boro, including 13 in 13 Championship games.

The Middlesbrough frontman has been the clinical focal point that Kim Hellberg needed in his side, and it's evident through their lack of goals after he picked up a small hamstring issue how important he is to this Boro side.

Due to these performances, though, the likes of Arsenal, Manchester United and other Premier League sides are reportedly keen on his services as soon as this January. In response, the Sun reported that Middlesbrough have slapped a £70 million price tag on his head.

He wouldn't be the most expensive Championship departure if that did go through - West Ham United somehow managed to get £85 million for Mateus Fernandes this past summer - but it's an eyebrow-raising demand, and an equal indication that Lankshear is not for sale without blatantly outlining that.

Blackburn may be looking at the news regarding Middlesbrough's price tag for Lankshear and wonder why they can't adopt a similar approach as and when interest forms for their talisman, Andri Gudjohnsen.

Of the players who have played 750 minutes or more in the Championship this season, Lankshear and Gudjohnsen stand atop the goals-per-90 charts. The Boro man averages 1.12, while the Icelandic forward sits at 0.96. There's a sizable drop to Jack Moylan in third with 0.70.

Unlike the 21-year-old, though, Gudjohnsen hasn't needed a plethora of chances to score his goals, as Blackburn don't have the quality behind the striker as Middlesbrough does. Indeed, Lankshear has taken 28 more shots — more than double the Rovers man so far this season.

Gudjohnsen stands out due to his sheer prolific nature infront of goal. He has 10 this season from just 20 shots; a 50% conversion rate, which makes him the most clinical player in Europe.

Lankshear is important to Middlesbrough, but, arguably, Gudjohnsen at this stage is more crucial to Blackburn. If clubs come calling in January, Rovers need to be firm with their demands for the 24-year-old.

Ultimately, Middlesbrough's demands when it comes to a fee for Lankshear are heavily influenced by 'English tax.' English clubs always demand that little bit more, or in this case, a lot more, for home-grown domestic players.

Gudjohnsen isn't that, but instead, he comes with a marketable name that not many in his position have.

The 24-year-old is a third-generation star, and both his grandfather and father were legends in Icelandic football. His Dad, Eidur, made quite a name for himself in England and Spain, too, winning a couple of Premier League titles with Chelsea and being part of the treble-winning Barcelona side under Pep Guardiola in 2008/09.

That kind of heritage is incredibly marketable. It'll make Icelandic fans follow their club and will draw eyes towards them to see how an Icelandic legend's son is performing in a big Premier League side.

Blackburn can use the Gudjohnsen name to their advantage to add a few more millions to the price tag to almost counteract the lack of homegrown status.

Ultimately, that £70 million price tag for Lankshear is a way to scare off suitors, and Blackburn should be doing the same. They aren't under any pressure to sell Gudjohnsen, given he signed a new "long-term" deal just last May.

The 24-year-old has showcased that this club form isn't just a fluke, netting a hat-trick for Iceland in their Nations League win over Bulgaria last Friday, and will return to Ewood Park looking to continue this momentum.

If he maintains this clinical nature in front of goal, Rovers could emerge as late play-off contenders as the campaign goes on. At the very least, it will attract further suitors in the summer, and Gudjohnsen's price tag could be even higher by then.

It may not be as lucrative as a £70 million fee, but Blackburn should be getting a little excited over how much they could earn from a Gudjohnsen sale in the future.
