Don't sleep on the NFL 'uncs' - can they finally win a Super Bowl?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

We preview Sunday in Week 12 of the 2026 NFL season as the San Francisco 49ers face the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks live on Sky Sports from 9.25pm; 49ers quarterback Brock Purdy has been ruled out due to injury with Mac Jones set to start under center

Kyle Shanahan's San Francisco 49ers are the best team in football. Is this finally the season they win a Lombardi Trophy? Their next test is against the champions.

Most offenses in the NFL look and function the way they do because of the Shanahan. His fingerprints, and still that of his father, have long been ingrained in trends, wrinkles and schematic interpretations dominant across the league as fans know it.

He tops the tree that has shaped modern football. And he is beating them all at his own game. He has done so for some time, minus the shiny reward at the end.

In that sense he sits alongside Josh Allen, even Joe Burrow and Lamar Jackson, in likely believing he would have a ring were it not for Patrick Mahomes, Andy Reid and the Kansas City Chiefs' Super Bowl dynasty, which twice have taken down Shanahan's 49ers in the season finale.

But the Niners look good for another Championship run. And few deserve it quite like Shanahan.

It would be a staggering case study were he not to win a Super Bowl at some stage in San Francisco, bearing in mind not only the number of close calls for a team he has led to four NFC Championship Games - two of which he won - in nine seasons, but also the genius football brain that has governed sidelines and film rooms across the nation.

His 49ers sit 9-1 in pole position for the No 1 seed in the NFC heading into Sunday night's matchup with the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks. They do so having just dismantled the Minnesota Vikings and their league-leading scoring defense 35-10 in Mexico City; only twice this season had teams put up more than 20 points against Brian Flores' defense. Shanahan torched them with ease.

It was a message to teams across the league, many of which had been foiled by Flores, his blitz bombardments and camouflaged coverages. It, too, was further evidence of an expertly drilled operation that stormed past the Los Angeles Rams in Melbourne, Australia in kickoff week before making Mexico home in famously tricky high-altitude conditions. Should they win a Super Bowl, the NFL's 'are they really world champions?' debate loses some substance; the Niners have gone global.

Shanahan's offense entered Week 12 ranked second in yards per game, sixth in passing, fourth in rushing and No 1 in scoring while topping all teams in EPA/play and success rate as the most prolific attack in football.

At the heart of it has been Brock Purdy, who Shanahan both identified and cultivated from Mr Irrelevant to undeniable MVP candidate. Non-Purdy believers had deemed him the product of the Shanahan system; to admit that could be partly true would be a deserved nod to what San Francisco's head coach does as one of football's masterminds. Nonetheless, Purdy has proven he is more than a mere puppet surrounded by stars.

Purdy is 206 of 290 passing (71 per cent) for 2,624 yards and 23 touchdowns to just five interceptions with a league-high passer rating of 118.2. Though the Niners will have to fare without him this week after he was sidelined with an ankle injury, paving the way for Mac Jones to make his first start of the season.

He is wildly accurate, underrated as a creator outside of the pocket, clinical in decision-making, aggressive enough to attack downfield and an outstanding diagnostician. The win over Minnesota in Mexico was the newest addition to his MVP resume, behind which Shanahan has been phenomenal.

Purdy was 16 of 18 for 195 yards and two touchdowns when facing the blitz, while finishing eight of nine for 100 yards when pressured during blitzes, according to NextGen Stats. It would contribute to an offensive display that saw the Vikings managed an EPA per dropback of 0.613; no other team had surpassed 0.037 EPA per dropback against Minnesota entering Week 11, per Pro Football Focus. His head coach is putting him in positions for success, and Purdy is executing. Now it is time to spin some wizardry with Jones against the best defense in the league.

Purdy may not win MVP with Joe Burrow around. And Shanahan probably will not win Coach of the Year. That doesn't mean he isn't the best coach in the NFL, and a chance to remind of as much could come with Jones under center.

The former first-round pick was 5-3 as a starter in place of the injured Purdy last season in yet another demonstration of Shanahan's mastery and ability to adapt. In that time Jones completed 69.6 per cent of passes for 2,151 yards and 13 touchdowns to six interceptions to help the 49ers keep their playoff aspirations alive before Purdy's return.

George Kittle has been Purdy's most targeted pass catcher with 47 catches for a team-high 591 and seven touchdowns, while Christian McCaffrey has over 1,000 yards from scrimmage with a second-most 14 total touchdowns. Mike Evans is the big-bodied one-on-one target Purdy and Shanahan have been missing, but has missed time through injury, and Deebo Samuel is channelling his wide back days again, but has missed time through injury.

"I think it's hard not to look at what happened in Mexico City and not feel like this team is unstoppable, even with a litany of injuries that are going on," said Sky Sports NFL's Phoebe Schecter.

The next test comes against the champions, whose defense leads the NFL in yards allowed and passing while ranking fourth in scoring and second in pressure rate.

"This Seattle defense have been so fun to watch all season long, and I think you couldn't ask for someone better in terms of Mike Macdonald," said Schecter. "They are gonna come after Brock Purdy. I know we saw the Vikings come with what felt like 90 per cent blitz rate, but I think this Seahawks team will come with a different attitude because Purdy picked them apart."

"This is a battle where the NFC West is kind of in the hands of Seattle and San Francisco."

Is this finally Shanahan's time? With the Seahawks, Los Angeles Rams and Chiefs coming up over the next month, their Super Bowl credentials are about to be put to the test.

Mike Evans is 33. Keenan Allen is 34. They are two of the NFL's greatest wide receivers, and now find themselves with instrumental roles to play in San Francisco's Super Bowl hunt. Evans arrived in the offseason and Allen more recently ahead of the trade deadline, playing 19 snaps together in the 49ers' 35-10 win over the Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City. Behold the "unc package".

"We mix in so many guys, and I know everybody's name, but when I'm thinking about football and stuff, I don't always just spit out names fast. So sometimes it's just easier for me to say unc," said head coach Shanahan. "Everyone knows who I'm talking about. "I hope they like it. I think it's cool. They're not, like, really uncles. They're in their 30s. They're still young."

The nickname stemmed from George Kittle as he declared the 49ers' veteran-heavy offense "Unc city", the criteria for which is being over the age of 30. Evans is in his 13th season and Allen his 14th, both still one-on-one headaches for defenders across the league and crucial pairs of hands for Purdy come January playoff football. Evans notably starred with six catches for 109 yards and a touchdown in San Francisco's win in Mexico.

Sleep is a sensitive subject in the NFL this week. But don't sleep on those uncs. Kittle, for the record, insisted he is not part of 'Unc city', despite being 33.

The state of things in the NFC East. No, quite literally - the state of things!

The truly dreadful standard of the NFC South is letting the East off the hook a little, but the 'battle' for supremacy among four of the NFL's most storied franchises isn't much better.

The New York Giants (6-3-1) hold the advantage, having secured arguably the best win of their season over the Jacksonville Jaguars last week, but their schedule down the stretch - following a winnable trip to the Indianapolis Colts this Sunday - looks rough, and so they could conceivably still be caught.

Whether the Dallas Cowboys (5-7), Washington Commanders (4-5-1) and Philadelphia Eagles (4-7) are capable of chasing the Giants down, though, is another matter entirely!

The Commanders could gain some ground with wins against the lowly Arizona Cardinals and Tennessee Titans over the next two weeks, plus they hold the head-to-head tiebreaker with New York.

As for the Cowboys, a season already rich with embarrassing lows reached a new nadir when blowing a Thanksgiving-record 21-point lead (with little more than a quarter left) to the Eagles, who are surely already out of contention despite that stunning comeback.

Kirk Cousins doesn't exactly scream somebody who chases revenge, but how he would love to stick it to the franchise that cast him aside as he leads the Minnesota Vikings (7-3) out against the Atlanta Falcons (3-7) this weekend. Cousins signed a bumper four-year $180m deal with Atlanta in 2024 after six seasons in Minnesota, only to see the Falcons go and select another quarterback in Michael Penix Jr with the eighth overall pick in the Draft that same year. Cousins was benched ahead of Week 16 before beginning the 2025 season as backup and starting just eight games until he was released this past offseason. He has since prolonged his days as a starter in the NFL with the Las Vegas Raiders, and recently returned to the Vikings via a trade in a bid to ignite Kevin O'Connell's slumping offense. Once deemed something of a final piece route back to prominence for the Falcons, Cousins is now intent on plunging Kevin Stefanski's side further towards trouble.

Sam Darnold has come a long way from "seeing ghosts" during his early struggles with the woeful New York Jets, returning a superb 28-6 record as a starter over the past two seasons which culminated in a Super Bowl ring with the Seattle Seahawks back in February.

As such, you'd have thought his expected return this week for a key NFC West divisional clash with the San Francisco 49ers would be greeted with great fanfare. Not quite…

Darnold has struggled to replicate his championship-winning form of last season, and that of 2024 with the Minnesota Vikings, with his record 3-4 this year. Meanwhile, backup QB Drew Lock has won all three of his starts, in which he's thrown for 837 yards, seven TDs and zero interceptions, completing 73.1 per cent of his passes.

Seattle's playoff hopes, and Darnold's job might both be on the line this Sunday.

He was the most athletically-gifted quarterback to ever test at the position coming into the NFL, but between persistent accuracy issues and injury setbacks, it has never quite happened for Anthony Richardson since being selected as the fourth pick of the 2023 Draft. He has an arm capable of the otherworldly throws some quarterbacks could only dream of making, and yet an arm prone to botching the most routine plays. At his best there is box office talent, but consistency has tripped him up. Richardson gets a chance to begin rebuilding his career as starter this weekend when he takes the reins for the Indianapolis Colts against the Giants - his first start since the 2024 season. It comes following the release of Daniel Jones amid an outright rebuild in Indianapolis. Awaiting him is a Giants defense ranked seventh in yards and fifth in passing.
