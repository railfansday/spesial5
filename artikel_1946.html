Sheffield Wednesday's Dave Challinor on his summer set-piece education at Brentford

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

During his time out of work at the start of this season, Challinor visited the training grounds of Celtic, West Bromwich Albion and Brentford amongst others to broaden his horizons.

And the effect of his trip to see the Bees was evident on Saturday as the Owls registered their first win under him courtesy of two goals from set pieces to beat Plymouth Argyle.

Billy Mitchell opened the scoring with a shot which took a massive deflection at a 45th-minute corner. It was the midfielder's first goal since 2021.

Davis Keillor-Dunn then scored his sixth goal in nine matches when the Pilgrims failed to deal with a Callum Slattery free-kick.

Centre-back Di'Shon Bernard completed the scoring with a brilliant individual effort.

"I went to Brentford in the summer for a few days and saw how seriously they take it," said Challinor of set-pieces.

"They have set-pieces as part of every single training session for them, whether it's warm-ups or practise, delivery, finishing will be important. It's something that we are really keen to make sure we do practise and we do work at.

"It's not the most exciting thing in the world is it, practising set-piece delivery, but if you get the reinforcement on a Saturday it makes it a whole lot easier.

"We are really conscious and want to make sure the record we have is a positive one and Saturday helps.

"Keith (Andrews, Brentford's coach) was really helpful in me going up there so I spent a few days with them.

"It's an amazing environment, I learnt so much. There will be things from there that we'd love to incorporate here.

"It's a brilliantly run football club that have the success based on consistency right from the very top."

Wednesday's win, their first in seven matches in all competitions, took them above Tom Cleverley's Plymouth and back into the League One play-off places.

The result, it must be said, was better than the performance, and Challinor was not getting carried away at the end of his first league game as Owls manager, after consecutive cup defeats to begin his reign.

"I think we've got loads to do,” he said. “(At half-time) I was really open with the players and asked them what they thought. Sometimes it takes 10 minutes to earn the right (to play), sometimes it takes 60 minutes, sometimes you never earn the right.

"Plymouth are really good at asking questions of you, and in the first half I don't think we dealt with them questions well enough at times.

"We maybe needed to compete better and win first contacts and drop on second balls and track runners. We probably got away with it a little bit at times.

"But they were really honest about that and said that was the case.

"The second half was much, much better. I thought we dealt with their threats and then we were able to make the game look a little bit more like we'd want it to look like."
