Google Adds Ocarina of Time Easter Egg Ahead of Remake Release

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

The original version of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time launched in November of 1998, which was a positively jam-packed year for gaming. Coincidentally, that was around the time that Google first launched, introducing the world to the wonders of online searching and taking its first step to becoming an ever-ubiquitous behemoth.

Nintendo's storefronts in New York City and San Francisco will be holding reservation-only midnight launches, with registration opening later today.

Because of this link, not to mention Google and Ocarina of Time’s respective vital places in the history of pop culture, it’s not unreasonable for one to give some flowers to the other, particularly on the precipice of a major revival.

In case you somehow managed to forget, this Thursday, November 5, the full remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time will be launching for the Nintendo Switch 2, a graphical and gameplay overhaul of one of Nintendo’s most beloved games of all time. In recognition of this event, Google has added a new easter egg to its sizable catalog of hidden tricks and silly sight gags. All you have to do to trigger it is search “Zelda Ocarina of Time,” and click the little glowing sword that appears in the bottom right.

Google’s Ocarina of Time easter egg takes the form of the classic Master Sword pedestal located in the Temple of Time in the game. When you first perform the search, the Master Sword has already been removed from the pedestal, emanating an ethereal blue glow.

However, when you click the Master Sword, Link’s distinctive grunt sounds out, and the screen is engulfed in that blue glow, accompanied by a golden Triforce sigil. When the view clears, Google’s current veneer has completely disappeared, replaced instead by the much more simplistic results list Google would have displayed during its inception way back in 1998. Clicking the sword again will have Link grunt as he removes the sword from the pedestal, returning you to the normal Google search page.

Google’s Ocarina of Time easter egg takes the form of the classic Master Sword pedestal located in the Temple of Time in the game.

Obviously, this is a reference to how, in Ocarina of Time, when Link pulls the Master Sword from its pedestal, he’s placed in suspended animation and whisked into the future as an adult. Inversely, upon returning the Master Sword to its pedestal, he’s returned to the past in his youth. The only difference is that Link was only sealed for seven years, whereas it’s been nearly 30 years since Google’s advent, but y’know, details.

Google easter egg antics are well-known among frequent users (i.e. pretty much everyone), usually being added to the platform in reference to popular movies and shows, or to coincide with major holidays, pop culture phenomena, and so forth. It’s a fun little way to celebrate things that personally interest you, and in the specific case of Nintendo games, there have been a few different ones.

Searching “Pokémon” in Google brings up a little PokéBall that, when clicked, allows you to play a minigame in which you can attempt to “catch” the original 151 by searching their names based on silhouettes and basic descriptions. Searching “Splatoon” shows a small colored blotch that, when clicked, allows you to fire shots of ink all over your results page, then click the convenient button to quickly wash it off.

Google has had a particular affinity for the Mario franchise over the years. Currently, if you search “Super Mario Bros.” on Google, you’ll see a little ? Block that you can click for coins, 100 of which plays the 1-UP sound. Previously, in celebration of Mario Day 2018, Mario appeared in a kart in lieu of the Google Maps navigational arrow, with a similar coin-generating sound player also included.
