The Honda Avancier SUV Hints at the Next-Generation Hybrid CR-V

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Avancier is a three-row SUV that won’t be sold in the U.S. market, but it gives us an idea of what to expect with Honda’s upcoming hybrid product blitz.

Honda has revealed the second-generation Avancier today, and while it won’t be offered in the United States, the new SUV gives us a preview of the upcoming next-generation CR-V and other hybrid models due out over the next few years. Designed for Asian markets such as Thailand, Japan, and China, the three-row Avancier sits above the CR-V and pairs clean, modern styling with a new hybrid powertrain.

The Avancier debuts Honda’s new two-motor hybrid powertrain, called Honda Hybrid. For now, Honda is remaining tight-lipped about many of the details. The automaker promises “excellent fuel economy,” and the Avancier also launches Honda’s new DualPower eAWD system, controlling the front and rear wheels independently via separate electric motors. The S+ Shift function, which simulates gearshifts for the hybrid powertrain and has been seen on the Civic Hybrid and Prelude, is also present.

The Avancier is also the first Honda that is not an EV to feature the brand’s latest “H” logo, which was introduced in 2024 and was originally set to be used exclusively for electric models. With Honda cancelling its trio of EVs that were meant to be built in Ohio—the Acura RSX and Honda 0-Series Saloon and 0-Series SUV—the company has decided to roll out the simplified “H” logo across its entire lineup starting in 2027.

The new badge sits proudly on the nose of the Avancier, just below a thin opening that connects the squinting LED daytime running lights. The main headlights sit below, linked by a series of geometric trim pieces that, along with a vent in the lower bumper, help channel air to the engine.

The side profile of the Avancier is clean, with flush door handles and gloss black trim that frames the wheel arches and the bottom of the SUV. Around back, the taillights consist of blocky, vertical elements connected by a light bar. While we don’t think the next CR-V will look exactly like the Avancier, we expect to see elements such as the thin DRLs to migrate across the Honda lineup in the coming years.

The cabin will also likely inform future U.S.-bound Hondas, and it gives off an airy, tranquil vibe. Unfortunately, the Avancier appears to have skipped out on most physical controls, putting nearly every function into the sizable screen that sits above the air vents in the center of the dashboard. We hope Honda reconsiders this approach in the U.S., given how much we appreciate the intuitive, straightforward physical buttons and knobs found in current Hondas.

The Avancier also introduces a column-mounted shifter, a feature we also tested on a next-generation Civic prototype we drove last year. It frees up space on the center console. The steering wheel has an unusual squared-off shape with a flat bottom, and behind it there is a digital gauge cluster. Glowing ambient lighting and white upholstery create a futuristic, sterile appearance, and a panoramic glass roof extends all the way to the third row.

Honda loaded the SUV with premium features, such as heated and ventilated seats in the second row and a 12-speaker Bose stereo with Dolby Atmos. There’s also a system that uses cameras on the inside and outside of the Avancier to let owners check on the cabin and car’s surroundings remotely via a smartphone app.

While the Avancier won’t be sold on our shores, we expect to see elements of its styling on upcoming Hondas, as well as its new hybrid powertrain. Honda plans to launch 15 new hybrid models globally by 2030, which includes a new CR-V next year, as well as a new Civic and a heavily updated Accord in the near future.
