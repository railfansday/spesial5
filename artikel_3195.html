ExxonMobil (XOM) Just Achieved Something 95% of S&P 500 (GSPC) Members Haven't Done

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

ExxonMobil's relatively small dividend increase was actually a big achievement.

By Selena Maranjian – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:05PM EST

10 stocks we like better than ExxonMobil ›

In late October, the energy giant ExxonMobil (XOM -3.96%) declared a fourth-quarter dividend of $1.07 per share, payable on Dec. 10, 2026, to shareholders who owned their shares at the close of business on Nov. 16, 2026. That represented a 3.9% increase over the previous quarterly dividend of $1.03.

Ho hum, right? That information isn't remarkable -- but this is: The increase was the 44th consecutive annual dividend increase -- a feat that 95% of the companies in the S&P 500 index have not yet managed.

Companies don't usually initiate regular dividend payments until they have grown to a point where they have sufficient reliable income with which to do so. They don't want to have to reduce or suspend their dividends if they fall on hard times, because that will reflect poorly on the company and can cause shareholders and others to lose confidence. Still, even well-known and well-respected companies have had to pull back on their dividends -- such as the 50% cut in 2025 from Dow, a company founded way back in 1897.

Thus, it's impressive that ExxonMobil has kept paying -- and increasing -- its payout for 44 years. That time period included the stock market crash in 1987, the dot-com bubble bursting in 2000, the global financial crisis in 2008, and the COVID-19 economic disruption that began in 2020, among many other events.

If you're wondering whether you should invest in ExxonMobil now, you might certainly consider doing so. Its dividend recently yielded 2.6%, and it's likely to keep growing every year, as a company with a 44-year streak is not going to want to lose that. (Once it hits 50 years, it will attain the elite rank of "Dividend King.")

Despite being near record highs, the shares seem reasonably valued at recent levels, with a forward-looking price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 13.7, a bit above the five-year average of 12.1. If you plan to hang on for many years, buying now could reward you.
