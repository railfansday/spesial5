31 Thanksgiving Desserts to Cap Your Holiday Feast

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

There’s Thanksgiving dinner, and then there are Thanksgiving desserts. These are two separate events, and in between, it’s perfectly fine to take a disco nap. (In fact, we recommend it.)

“This is the magnum opus of Thanksgiving dessert recipes,” writes one reader whose 90-year-old grandfather asked for a repeat performance. “You bet your boots I will—it’s the best apple pie, ever.”

A colossal, self-serve mousse is a baller host move—no tidy slicing, no fussy garnishing, no jockeying for oven space. Set out bowls and spoons and let guests help themselves. Vanilla lovers, try our Colossal Crème Brûlée.

Butter-toasted pecans get the glory, but sour cream does the work, balancing both the gooey pecan layer and the pile of whipped cream. Between them? A thick layer of silky brown sugar custard.

A slice of Thanksgiving pie is nice, but how about a slice of pumpkin flan under a veneer of caramel? This one takes a page from Puerto Rican flan de queso, with cream cheese lending richness, tang, and luxurious texture.

Consider this pie a tangy exclamation point to the holiday meal: cranberry-lime curd in a flaky cream cheese crust, crowned with billows of meringue.

Brown sugar takes some of the fuss out of caramel cake’s famously finicky frosting while browned butter adds toasty depth. Spread it over tender yellow cake layers and finish with flaky salt.

Silky filling, flaky crust, a whisper of spice: This is pumpkin pie done right. Sweetened condensed milk gives the custard its richness; maple syrup and vanilla round out the flavor.

Skip the rolling pin: Buttered phyllo encloses an orange-scented custard, with crumpled sheets forming a dramatically ruffled top. Cinnamon sugar adds crunch to this no-roll pie.

This plush pumpkin spice cake wears a thick swoop of tangy cream cheese frosting. Bake the cake a day ahead, then add the frosting in the lull between dinner and dessert.

If most holiday desserts feel a tad too sweet for you, herbal rosemary and fruity olive oil give this cake just enough savory elements to justify a second helping.

Blanketing quartered apples with rough-puff pastry feels rustic enough, but once baked and inverted, this shimmering, blushing tart dominates the holiday table.

Sweet potato, warming spices, and orange zest get a gloss of dark chocolate in this autumnal loaf cake. Bake it up to three days ahead, leaving the ganache for later.

A deeply browned top and cracked sides are the goal here, not a baking mishap. Underneath: a custardy, pumpkin-spiced cheesecake that needs neither a crust nor a water bath.

Pecan pie meets Hawaiian-style butter mochi in this chewy, coconut-milk-rich cake. Miso-and-maple-coated pecans bring salty-sweet crunch, with some sinking into the batter as the cake bakes.

This date cake blanketed in toffee sauce tastes like cozying up by the fireside after a game of football on the lawn. Bake the unsauced cake a day ahead, then rewarm before finishing.

Cinnamon-spiced pumpkin cake rolled around walnut-studded cream cheese filling needs little embellishment. A dusting of powdered sugar is plenty—each slice supplies its own dramatic swirl.

This glamorous layer cake, with jewel-toned swipes of cranberry jam, white chocolate–cream cheese frosting, and sugared cranberries, looks and tastes like winter is coming (in the best possible way).

A no-bake Oreo crust, a rich chocolate filling, and a tower of whipped cream come together in this shut-the-room-up dessert.

Caramel-glazed apple halves crown this nutty upside-down cake, all baked in the same skillet used to brown the fruit. A scoop of vanilla ice cream on top? We wouldn’t say no.

A floral, earthy saffron soak gives this sponge cake a pudding-like texture and its vibrant yellow color, while pistachios add nutty crunch.

Inspired by the flavors of a pumpkin spice latte, this rendition of old-school tiramisu calls for soaking ladyfingers in bourbon-spiked espresso, then enveloping them in plumes of pumpkin-mascarpone cream.

Cranberries with dinner and again for dessert? Absolutely. Tart filling hides between layers of buttery, crumbly walnut crust in this Linzer tart. Prefer individual portions? Try these cranberry hand pies.

Pumpkin crumb cake is like the brownie of Thanksgiving desserts: easy to tote and slice for sharing. Here, sweet rice flour gives the cake a chewy bounce beneath a buttery, crispy-crumbly pecan streusel.

The ultraflaky crust barely contains the lofty, fudgy almond filling, but have faith: It will puff and dome as it bakes, then settle back into sliceable form once cool.

Alternate layers of pumpkin and chocolate batter create this Bundt cake’s mesmerizing marbling—no knife-swirling necessary. A blanket of chocolate ganache makes it equally appealing to the pumpkin crowd and the chocolate diehards.
