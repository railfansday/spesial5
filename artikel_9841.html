UK government working 'around the clock' on Capita contingency plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Minister says outsourcer should be 'absolutely ashamed' of pensions failures after CEO exits and shares plunge

The UK government is working "around the clock" on contingency plans for Capita contracts after the outsourcer's CEO quit and its shares plunged, a Cabinet Office minister told MPs.

Capita announced plans this week to raise around £190 million through a share issue in the first half of 2027 after warning of sharply lower profits and further costs linked to its Civil Service Pension Scheme (CSPS) contract.

The outsourcer "should be absolutely ashamed of its performance" on the £239 million contract, Sally Jameson, parliamentary secretary in the Cabinet Office, told MPs on Tuesday.

"We have seen evidence that it has stepped up in recent months and begun to rectify the problem, but it is inexcusable that it let the situation get to this point," she said.

Adolfo Hernandez stepped down as CEO with immediate effect on Monday, with finance chief Pablo Andres taking over. Shares fell around 70 percent following the announcement.

In the House of Commons, Jameson was asked about the government's plans if Capita were unable to carry out the remainder of its contract given the outsourcer's performance on the CSPS.

"On contingency planning, it is important that we stabilize the situation and that Capita can continue to deliver the pension scheme. However, the government is working across departments, as we do in relation to all strategic suppliers, to ensure that the relevant contingency plans are in place. People are working around the clock on that," she said.

Jameson said contingency planning for the pension scheme was already underway before Monday's announcement, describing it as standard practice for major government suppliers.

She told the Chamber that the Cabinet Office was "currently arranging to meet the new CEO in the coming days" following Hernandez's departure.

The Register first exposed problems with Capita's CSPS portal last December. The site, serving 1.5 million current and former public servants, rejected login details even for newly created accounts, contained broken and circular links, and displayed dummy text.

In January this year, the Cabinet Office was forced to promise "interim support measures" for the CSPS following Capita's botched takeover, including 150 additional government staff to clear the backlog in cases.

Speaking in Parliament, Conservative MP Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, chair of the Public Accounts Committee, said there had been "some tragic and dreadful cases" since Capita took over administration last December.

"I heard of one poor lady who was living on benefits because she could not get the bereavement payments she needed after her sister's death. I have heard of a member of parliamentary staff, not far from this Chamber, who is having to delay his retirement by a year because he cannot even get a statement from the scheme."

The government has withheld nearly £10 million in contractual payments over Capita's performance. It is currently locked in negotiations over further penalties following the appointment of an auditor, first revealed in July. At the time, Hernandez apologized for Capita's performance but refused to commit to further compensation.

The National Audit Office and Public Accounts Committee have both launched probes into Capita's handling of the CSPS and its appointment as a contractor.

In February, The Register revealed a legal challenge to the government's award of the separate Synergy shared services contract to Capita. The deal covers HR and finance services across four Whitehall departments and associated arm's-length bodies. Rival bidder Sopra Steria, which owns incumbent provider SSCL, alleges Capita's bid was abnormally low and its proposed staffing raised concerns about delivery. Capita and the Department for Work and Pensions, which led the procurement, deny the allegations and say the competition was fair and open. Capita values the contract at £370 million.

The Public and Commercial Services Union is now calling on the government to scrap Capita's shared services contract with the DWP, citing the outsourcer's troubled handling of CSPS. ®
