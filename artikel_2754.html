Dynasty Basketball Stock Watch: Cedric Coward continues to level up

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

We're a month into the 2026-27 NBA season, and we've seen more key developments over the past week. Cameron Boozer is now out for at least a month with an ankle injury, the Timberwolves are surging, and the Thunder are struggling.

As always, we have another two dynasty risers and two dynasty fallers to break down. Check it out, and enjoy your Thanksgiving!

To begin his sophomore campaign, Coward is averaging 15.4 points, 8.1 rebounds, 3.4 assists, 1.6 steals and 0.4 blocks per game. He's shooting 47.4% from the field, 44.7% from three and 80.5% from the free-throw line. His three-point percentage is up 10.9 percentage points from last season, though his two-point percentage is down 6.8 percentage points. There's room for improvement there, but he's been more aggressive at getting rebounds, and overall has been a big reason for the Grizzlies' success early in the year.

In addition, NBA teams are always looking for wings who have great positional size, can hit threes, defend, and be a great supporting piece for their stars. We've seen how Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, and Jaden McDaniels have been helping their respective teams win games in the playoffs. Coward could be the next player in that mold.

In Rotoworld's most recent dynasty rankings update, Coward came in at No. 68. He's only 23 years old and he's aware of his weaknesses. He's gotten a lot better every year of his career dating back to his time at the Division III level of college basketball. At this point, I see him as one of the safest young players in the NBA with more room to grow.

Jonathan Kuminga signed with the Timberwolves this summer on a two-year, $12 million deal which includes a player option. So far, the 24-year-old is averaging 13.4 points, 5.4 rebounds, 2.8 assists, 0.9 steals and 0.8 blocks per game. He's shooting 45.2% from the field, 39.7% from three and 65.6% from the free-throw line.

On a surface level, those numbers aren't that dissimilar from Kuminga's stint with Atlanta last season. What has changed is that he has been willing to play defense and do the dirty work. From when Golden State drafted him, this is what the Warriors and Steve Kerr wanted to see out of him. Minnesota head coach Chris Finch got him to buy into his role, which is one of the key reasons the Timberwolves have gotten off to an impressive 13-5 start.

Kuminga is ranked No. 186 on Rotoworld's most recent dynasty rankings update. At his young age, there's still room for him to grow as a player. He's not like Cedric Coward who can hit threes at a high level, but he's a better slasher. While the fantasy numbers might not ever be amazing from a 9-category perspective, Kuminga has value as a scorer and rebounder.

Signed to a three-year, $47.4 million contract this past offseason, Mitchell Robinson is averaging 5.1 points, 5.4 rebounds, 0.9 assists, 0.7 steals and 0.8 blocks per game. He's shooting 65.3% from the field and a career-low 36.4% from the free-throw line. He's also averaging 3.4 fewer rebounds per game than he did last season.

Here's one striking part of his season: The Celtics outscore opponents by 23.4 points per 100 possessions with Robinson off the court. Opponents outscore Boston by 8.1 points per 100 possessions with him on the court, a 31.5-point swing. Over the past week, the Celtics have started to play rookie Chris Cenac Jr. and Luka Garza instead of Robinson.

So, what's changed? There are a few factors that are worth pointing out. A lot of what made Robinson great in New York was effort, defense, and a simple role. He's adjusting to Celtics offense, which is different from the Knicks' offense. The effort hasn't been the same, and the defense has been bad this season.
