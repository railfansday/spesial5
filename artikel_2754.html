Expect Travis Kelce to continue playing through his ankle injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Most observers expect 2026 to be the final season for Kansas City Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce — who will likely then be a first-ballot Pro Football Hall of Fame selection.

Unfortunately, our possible final months of watching the star appear are impacted by a recurring ankle injury first suffered in Week 6 against the Los Angeles Chargers.

After seeing the ankle repeatedly taped since — and covering Kelce on every published injury report — he seemed to aggravate it badly in the first quarter of Sunday's win over the Atlanta Falcons. Although he went to the locker room and was listed as questionable to return, Kelce made it back into the game and even recorded another catch.

In the team's first practice before Week 11's game against the Arizona Cardinals, Kelce was listed as a full participant with the ankle injury.

Speaking before practice, head coach Andy Reid said Kelce was ready to fight the coaches to reenter.

"He wants to play," remarked Reid. "He wants to practice every play. He wants to play every play. I want to make sure that thing doesn't get worse. Unfortunately, that play — the whole action of twisting probably wasn't really good for it."

Still, how Kelce looked limping off the field in Atlanta should not be confused with how he must be feeling as the Chiefs start another practice week. The coach also noted that Kelce's particular ankle injury lends itself to quick recovery, even when it is aggravated.

"He's doing well now," Reid explained. "He's out running around. With high ankle sprains, he's had this before. They get tweaked, literally, and then they recover fairly quick from there. That's what this was. It just happened early in the game. So, I kind of held back on him. He was very mad. He wanted to play. That's all part of it."

Many have suggested limiting Kelce's practice reps, or even sitting him against the lowly Cardinals, with a Thanksgiving road contest against the Buffalo Bills four days later looming.

Reid showed no desire to fight with Kelce to limit his work, even as he plays through obvious pain.

"That doesn't work," Reid declared. "He thinks he's still 20."

In this week's episode of the New Heights podcast, hosted with his brother Jason, Kelce left no doubt about his intentions for Sunday.

"I'm ready to rock and roll this week," he said. "It's one of those things where once you turn it in at the beginning of the year, you've just got to deal with the high-ankle sprain and the process it takes to heal. When you're battling through week to week trying to get it to 100%, it's going to be a lot slower than just taking time off. But I love playing this game, and if I'm out there on a good ankle or a bad ankle, it doesn't f–king matter to me. I'm out there because I love this game. I signed up for this thing to be out there putting my heart on the line and my body on the line for my teammates."
