Yang believes Celtic can defy the odds with history-making move into the Europa League knock-out phase

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 13:39 EST, 22 November 2026 | Updated: 13:49 EST, 22 November 2026

Yang Hyun-jun is predicting that Celtic can make Europa League history by bouncing back from a dismal start in the group stage to qualify.

Since the competition was revamped by UEFA in 2024, no side have made it through to the knock-out round having lost their first three games.

With defeats to Ferencvaros, Benfica and Celta Vigo arriving in quick succession, Martin O’Neill’s men looked to be heading for an early exit.

After beating Besiktas last time out, however, they are still in with a shout when matchday five takes them to Portugal to face Torreense on Thursday.

Winger Yang, who made his first start since early September in the win over St Mirren on Saturday, feels there’s a growing belief among the players they can yet emulate last season’s feat of reaching the play-off round.

‘Can we make history? Of course,’ he said. ‘We believe we can qualify. We are getting better all the time. You could see that against Besiktas.

‘They are a very good team, and that has given us the belief that we can go to the next round. It’s important to take it game-by-game, though. This is the most important one. If we can get the points we can go on from there, step-by-step.’

Torreense made history last season by becoming the first second-tier team to win the Portuguese Cup and qualify for Europe. Despite currently sitting 13th in that division, they have confounded expectations by taking six points from four Europa League games.

‘We are playing against a Portuguese second division team but we have to be focused and aggressive,’ warned Yang. ‘They are higher than us in the Europa League table. They’ve already won two matches so they will be a good team.

‘We have to approach it the same way as we would playing a team like Benfica or Sporting Lisbon. We have to go there and try to win. If we play like we did against St Mirren, we can do it.’

Meanwhile, Yang is adamant that being made exempt from military service wasn’t even on his mind as South Korea won the Asian Games this year.

Played in Japan in September and October, Yang’s side defeated the hosts 1-0 in the final.

That meant all the South Korean players were no longer obliged to spend time serving in the military before they turn 28.

Yang, 24, who suffered a hamstring injury in the opening victory against Qatar, reflected: ‘It was great. Obviously, it was nice to win it but I also got injured, which was unfortunate.

‘I was trying to be around the team, to help in any way I could.

‘It wasn’t really about the national service. Everyone was playing for the team, the country and the fans.

‘We managed to get the victory. Being exempt from national service was just a bonus, I guess.

‘The celebrations were enjoyable. It’s the first time my national team have won the Asian Games.

‘It was really hard — especially the game against Japan. It was a great achievement.’
