Maitland-Niles’ last-gasp debut goal salvages Everton’s miserable week

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

At the final whistle, the first song the stadium DJ played was Spirit of the Blues and how those relieved Evertonians lapped it up after a week of angst.

Their team had been on the precipice of a defeat that would have exacerbated all the frustrations that bubbled away following the chaotic conclusion to the transfer window. Indeed, it was 10 seconds over the allotted five minutes of added time when the ball fell to Ainsley Maitland-Niles.

It had been three years since the former Arsenal full-back had played in the Premier League – his last goal in England was on October 30, 2019, in an EFL Cup tie with Liverpool at Anfield that ended 5-5 – and he would not have joined Everton if their attempt to sign Kenny Tete from Fulham had not failed.

But here he was 30 yards from goal, with no need to break his stride as Manchester United’s backtracking defenders presented him with an invitation to shoot. So that’s what he did. With one perfectly timed swish of his right foot, an unlikely hero transformed the mood of a club.

"That is something SPECIAL!"Ainsley Maitland-Niles earns a draw for Everton on his debut with an unbelievable 96th minute strike! 🤯 pic.twitter.com/HNdx9PxrUP

“Brilliant technique,” said David Moyes, Everton’s manager, who had only introduced Maitland-Niles in the 77th minute after James Garner had succumbed to cramp and could run no more. “We are still getting to know Ainsley but that was a brilliant goal.”

Only those with the coldest hearts would begrudge Everton this point, which was especially hard-earned given they had conceded an 88th-minute header to Benjamin Sesko. But there is no chance a disbelieving Michael Carrick will feel that way.

Benajmin Sesko puts Manchester United back in front! 👊 pic.twitter.com/MF3A9LyaY8

United should, really, have won this game comfortably. Everton were there to be exploited and when Bryan Mbuemo put them ahead early in the second half, they should have powered away into the distance. Everton’s effort was good but they were in danger of losing hope.

Yet, incredibly, United gave Everton two helping hands. They had not been in a position to assist last Tuesday evening, when phone calls from Merseyside to Old Trafford to take Joshua Zirkzee on loan were met with rejection. But the second half display more than made up for that.

“We’re bitterly disappointed,” said Carrick, who was one step removed from having steam puffing from his ears. “All the work that went into putting us in that position at different stages to then come away like that… it’s not a great feeling. It feels like a defeat. It’s a bit of a cliché to say.”

But clichés are true. First United were passive when Tyrique George crashed in a brilliant right-footed drive in the 83rd minute and then, after Sesko profited from Jordan Pickford’s weak hands, they were passive again in allowing Everton to build the move that ended up with Maitland-Niles’ howitzer.

How had it come to this? It was beautiful bedlam, the kind of joy that only football delivers, but for long periods it felt like it was an opportunity for United. There was anticipation that Everton’s fans would create a furnace but there was also the fear it would be dispiritingly quiet.

Had this fixture been at Goodison Park, with its steep stands, tight confines and acoustics that split your ears, United would have been aware of the siege mentality as soon as their bus rolled up outside the stadium but their new sparkling home is rather more hospitable.

This is no criticism of the home crowd – it is anything but. Why should it always fall to them to bail out the failings of those in charge when those in charge, specifically the executives and recruitment department, have given them scant reason to have hope?

The route to victory for United, then, was relatively straightforward: for all Everton’s initial huff and puff, it always appeared that it would be a case of the away team repeating the simple things, retaining their concentration and waiting for a breakthrough.

It could have come as early as the eighth minute. Luke Shaw’s deep cross to the back post picked out Mbuemo, whose cushioned header invited Bruno Fernandes to take aim but the Portuguese’s volley clipped the crossbar with Pickford stranded.

United dominated and Marcus Rashford was central to it all, a scampering presence down the left who gave Merlin Röhl, playing as an auxiliary right-back, all kinds of problems. The only thing missing from his opening 45 minutes was a goal.

When Mbuemo scored so early after the break, it felt as if this was going to be one puncture too many for Everton to withstand and the anticipation was United would get the job done. In all fairness to Moyes, however, he was not in the mood to accept excuses.

His substitutions were all positive, with Jack Grealish changing the mood and Hayden Hackney providing dynamism. But it was the former Middlesbrough man who drove forward to give George, whose loan move from Chelsea was made permanent in June, the opportunity to smash an 83rd-minute bullet past Senne Lammens.

“I told the players there would be no excuses and the players all agreed,” Moyes said. “They have hugely bought into it. And, you know, sometimes when you have [fewer] players it means everybody has to be ready. Sometimes when you have a small squad you have to find a way of making it work.”

They did just that. Pickford should have saved the header from Sesko that looked as though it had given United the lift, but Everton, with their manager belligerent and their fans sensing all was not lost, had other ideas. The noise they generated as Maitland-Niles’s goal went in was unique to this stadium.

As Moyes celebrated, shaking his fists and revelling in the positive turn of events, Carrick put his hand across his face and shook his head repeatedly. Good afternoon and welcome to live coverage of Everton vs Manchester United from Hill Dickinson Stadium. The hosts have made a creditable start on the field but four points from their opening two games cannot gloss over the discontent fostered by the owners and executives because of the credibility gap between their talk about recruitment and their paltry achievements.

The fact that their comeback against Bournemouth to earn a draw last week was achieved with Beto, Tim Iroegbunum and Iliman Ndiaye on the field and all three have now been sold with only Jack Grealish returning on loan to compensate tells its own story.

Speak to supporters and it is emblematic of ownership, they fear, who are prioritising Roma and view the Hill Dickinson as a terrific multisport and concert venue with a heritage football club attached. The bullishness of Angus Kinnear, the chief executive, rings hollower with each pronouncement and it is little wonder that the fans are so disenchanted.

David Moyes will not walk away but once again he has been sent into the fray against teams with £250m midfields armed with a spud gun. Having said that, we should not underestimate his capacity to rally the old “dogs of war” spirit even if the crowd is still yearning more for a “school of science” revival.

Manchester United won on their first visit to the Hill Dickinson last season when Benjamin Sesko came off the bench to score the only goal of the game and give Michael Carrick a fifth win in six league games as caretaker manager. They were far more fluid in their forward play during the victory over Ipswich last week but it was against an error-stricken defence and they continued to be as worryingly open at the back as they had been against Hull City in the opening round of fixtures with Luke Shaw and Lisandro Martínez having brain fades.

Shaw is a doubt today, along with Mason Mount, while Amad Diallo, Matthijs de Ligt, Carlos Baleba and Manuel Ugarte have all been ruled out. Only Christian Norgaard is absent for Everton but so thin is their squad, they will cast covetous glances at Man Utd’s bench, not least at Joshua Zirkzee who was the subject of a very late rejected bid on deadline day.
