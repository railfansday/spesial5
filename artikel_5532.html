Inside RFK Jr.'s Day-to-Day; 'Not Dying Fast Enough'; Peddling Unproven Cures

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

— A weekly roundup of how public health policy is shaping practice

The release of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s calendar for his time as HHS secretary shows meetings with the CEOs of Eli Lilly, Chipotle, Tylenol-maker Kenvue, and Philip Morris, along with the hours prepping for Joe Rogan's podcast and time to film videos with Kid Rock. (Politico, STAT)

Medicare's cap on hospice payments penalizes patients who are "not dying fast enough," as one nurse described it. (New York Times)

A Harvard professor who studies the link between diet and health -- a favorite topic of HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. -- had his grants cut anyway. (New York Times)

The fight over ultraprocessed food caused some big divisions inside the White House. (The Atlantic)

Kristine Blanche, wife of Attorney General Todd Blanche, runs a website that sells unproven dietary supplements and medical treatments. (The Guardian)

A proposed rule from the Labor Department would end fast-tracking of green cards for nurses and physical therapists. (Fierce Healthcare)

NIH officials are wondering whether Anthony Fauci, MD, former director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, clandestinely wrote a major paper debunking the "lab leak" theory of COVID. (Politico)

The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force had its first meeting in a year and a half, newly stocked with Kennedy appointees. Reporting describes it as "tumultuous." (New York Times)

CVS's decision to break up into two separate companies would "continue to enable the company to drive up prices and crush competition," Sens. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) and Josh Hawley (R-Mo.) wrote in a letter to the company's CEO. (The Hill)

Nearly 60% of Americans think the U.S. could have prevented the loss of its measles elimination status if the federal government had done a better job of encouraging vaccination and responding to outbreak, according to a new Ipsos poll.

A dozen Democratic members of Congress wrote to President Trump to ask how creation of a Women's Health Task Force is not duplicative of the NIH's Office of Research on Women's Health.

Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed, MD (D-Mich.), said he'd be fine with putting President Trump's name on a "Medicare for All" plan, if that's what it would take to get it done. (The Hill)

A group has formed to draft Sen. Rand Paul, MD (R-Ky.), to run for president in 2028. (The Hill)

Federal scientists are increasingly concerned about the health effects of glyphosate. (Washington Post)

HHS and CMS need to better manage the risk of Medicare fraud, according to a report from the Government Accountability Office.

HHS awarded $47 million to 15 organizations to test new approaches to chronic disease prevention and management.

The FDA said it will now include information about accelerated approvals to patient labeling for drugs.
