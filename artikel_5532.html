AI shopping tools are steering consumers toward unfamiliar retailers, Norton warns

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Gen's Norton says 42% of Americans plan to use a tool like ChatGPT for holiday shopping as fake shop activity rises 129%

Norton, a unit of Gen, warned in its 2026 holiday scams report that AI shopping assistants are steering more shoppers to unfamiliar retailers, while scammers use AI to build fake online stores.

The company released the report Nov. 5. It said 63% of Americans surveyed have used an AI shopping assistant or agent, and 83% would use one. Of those who would, 42% are confident the assistant would recognize and avoid a fraudulent website.

Of the respondents with experience using an AI shopping assistant or agent, 56% reported that the tool had suggested or completed a purchase from a retailer they were unfamiliar with, according to CNBC.

Gen Threat Labs has seen AI assistants miss scams, the company said. In one case, a shopper asked an assistant for affordable alternatives to a designer leather jacket. The recommendations included a $279 jacket from a fraudulent online store, listed beside legitimate retailers with no warning. Norton said shoppers may trust a recommendation because it comes from AI, even when the site behind it cannot be trusted.

Gen Threat Labs data shows a 129% increase in fake shop activity compared with the start of last year's holiday season. The Gen family of brands, including Norton, blocked nearly 25 million attempted visits to fraudulent shopping sites in the last month, the company said. The number of fake store domains blocked doubled from 260,000 in the 2024 holiday quarter to 526,000 in 2025.

Leyla Bilge, Norton’s global head of scam research, said AI’s biggest effect on scams has come through coding, which lets scammers put up “thousands and thousands of fake e-shops online,” she told CNBC. That makes any given shopper more likely to land on one.

"Scammers are counting on holiday shoppers being rushed, distracted and afraid of missing out," Bilge said in a statement.

Norton's survey of 1,000 U.S. adults, conducted by Dynata from Sept. 10 to Sept. 21, found that 16% have been scammed while holiday shopping online. Among the victims, 88% lost money, and the average amount lost came to $394. A further 35% said they had made a purchase from a site or seller they suspected could be a scam, tempted by a deal that seemed too good to pass up.

Norton advises shoppers to vet any retailer an AI assistant suggests before confirming a purchase. It also recommends going straight to the retailer's website or app instead of clicking a social media ad, and paying by credit card or a payment service with buyer protection.

Matt Schulz, chief consumer finance analyst at LendingTree, said shoppers cannot assume an AI suggestion has been checked for legitimacy. "You absolutely need to check with the retailer directly," he told CNBC.
