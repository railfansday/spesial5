15 free Harvard online courses you can start at your own pace

A selection of 15 self-paced Harvard courses, from computer science to law, drawn from a catalog of 287 courses.

Plenty of curious people would welcome a Harvard course but cannot pay tuition, relocate to Massachusetts, or bend a workday around a fixed class schedule. Online catalogs strip away much of that friction. Harvard, a private Ivy League research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, dates to 1636 and counts among the oldest institutions of higher education in the U.S. Hundreds of its courses sit online for anyone with an internet connection, and most cost nothing to start. Access, then, is the easy part. The harder question is which course deserves several weeks of a learner's evenings.

One course directory lists 287 Harvard offerings, a volume that turns choosing into a chore. A free tag marks 227 of them. Beginners get most of the attention, with 225 entry-level courses against 50 intermediate and 12 advanced. The subject mix is lopsided. People and society claims 119 courses, technology 65, STEM 42, business 22, health and wellness 21, and creative fields 10. English covers 284 courses, Spanish two, and Arabic one. Credentials add another layer. A total of 252 courses offer a certificate, including 41 career certificates. The free tag and the certificate appear as separate labels. Free access does not guarantee a free credential, and anyone who wants proof of completion should check each course's cost label before committing time to it.

Class Central, a course search site that describes itself as built by learners since 2011, runs the directory. Its Harvard page has 591,500 followers. Each entry shows the number of ratings a course has collected, the platform hosting it, its length and weekly workload, its pacing, and a cost or certificate label. The page displays rating counts without average scores. Its default view orders courses by relevancy, and readers can switch to highest rated, lowest rated, or most recently added. The 15 courses below are the first 15 in that default order, and 272 more remain in the catalog. Relevancy alone sets the sequence. A few entries carry a Best of All Time badge, yet the page explains no other method for choosing or arranging courses. Every course in this selection is self-paced. Fourteen run on edX and one on freeCodeCamp, and each carries a free label whose exact wording varies by course.

Ronald Heifetz, author of Leadership Without Easy Answers, divides leadership problems into two kinds, according to a CDC journal article. The first kind calls for technical solutions. People know both the problem and its answer, and existing skills and experience carry them from one to the other. The piece suggests the Back to Sleep campaign against sudden infant death syndrome could stand as a technical problem that found its solution. Researchers identified a leading risk factor, and a nationwide public education effort followed.
