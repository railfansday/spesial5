8 JRPGs With the Most Nominations at The Game Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Murillo Zerbinatto is a JRPG enthusiast, writer, and editor. He has played over 300 RPGs during his gaming career, with 90% of them being JRPGs.

The gaming industry is no stranger to award shows. Nowadays, we have countless ceremonies, such as the Golden Joystick Awards, the BAFTA Games Awards, the D.I.C.E. Awards, and more. However, it is hard to deny the massive influence of Geoff Keighley's The Game Awards, whether because of its sheer audience reach, the spectacle, the world-premiere announcements, or the acceptance speeches that become industry touchstones for years to come.

From Nioh to Black Myth: Wukong, these are the seven Soulslikes with the most nominations at The Game Awards.

We're nearing The Game Awards season, so I put together a themed list to celebrate. Rather than just discussing the ceremony itself, why not look back at the JRPGs with the most nominations at The Game Awards? True to my personal branding around here, I went ahead and did just that. It is wild to think that in the past, JRPGs would barely even be considered for major awards, yet here we are. Not only do they regularly rack up nominations today, but a certain JRPG-inspired title even swept the TGAs and currently stands as the most awarded game in the event's history.

Octopath Traveler caught plenty of people by surprise, myself included. Just when we thought Square Enix had left pixel art behind for good, the company proved there is still plenty of room for the visual style while elevating it to new heights with its HD-2D graphical engine. Beyond the visuals, it recaptured the nostalgia of classic Square titles through the core gameplay loop as well.

Octopath Traveler drew heavy inspiration from Final Fantasy VI to weave together the tales of its eight protagonists. That achievement earned the JRPG three nominations: Best Art Direction, Best Score and Music, and Best Role Playing Game, all of which felt entirely fitting. It didn't walk away with a trophy, but earning those nods showed how deeply critics appreciated the experience at the time.

It's not every day a JRPG competes for Game of the Year, so when it does, you know the game is an absolute banger. As far as I am concerned, if Xenoblade Chronicles 3 had taken home the top prize, it would have been completely deserved. To be fair, it lost both Best Role-Playing Game and Game of the Year to Elden Ring, which is entirely understandable, even if I would still hand the trophy to Monolith Soft if we were running Murillo's TGAs.

Losing Best Score to God of War Ragnarök, however? Look, I know Ragnarök features a wonderful composition, but anyone who has played Xenoblade Chronicles 3, or any Xenoblade title for that matter, heck, any modern JRPG, knows that musical composition is the beating heart of the genre. You could play a JRPG where every other element falls flat, and I guarantee the soundtrack would still blow you away. But I digress, and that is just the fanboy in me talking. I am simply thrilled that a JRPG received that much recognition and spotlight back in 2022.

Speaking of Best Score, NieR: Automata nailed that category in the year it competed, and honestly, that was the bare minimum. If they hadn't given the score award to Automata, who else could possibly have taken it? I do know who beat it out for Best Role-Playing Game that year, though, and I will get to that entry soon. NieR: Automata also earned a nomination for Best Narrative, and while it didn't win, I can understand why.

The story was insanely good, touching on deep philosophical and existentialist themes that leave you chewing on their meanings for months. However, its narrative delivery and structure can feel a bit complex for some players. You have to play through the game multiple times to get the full picture, and while I had a blast on every playthrough, some folks might drop off after the first ending and miss everything NieR: Automata truly offers. Plus, I haven't played What Remains of Edith Finch, and since the TGAs are ultimately a competition, it may have offered a tighter narrative experience.

Even before Final Fantasy XVI hit store shelves, it was already sparking heated debate. I have never minded the franchise evolving its formula, though certain corners of the fanbase got irked just hearing that Final Fantasy XVI would lean into full action combat. Regardless of the broader discourse, I loved the game. It moved 3 million copies at launch (even if Square Enix wasn't fully satisfied with the numbers) and walked away with 4 nominations at the 2023 TGAs.

Had Final Fantasy XVI not launched in the exact same year as Baldur's Gate 3, it might easily have swept more categories, including Best Role-Playing Game and Best Performance, because let's face it, Ben Starr as Clive was perfection. The JRPG did take home Best Score and Music with Masayoshi Soken at the helm, a victory that was overwhelmingly deserved. To this day, I still blast either the Phoenix or Bahamut theme at the gym and feel like a Dominant hoisting weights.

From Axiom Verge to Hollow Knight: Silksong, these are the ten Metroidvanias with the most nominations at The Game Awards.

Remember how I mentioned that NieR: Automata lost Best Role-Playing Game and that the outcome was pretty justifiable? Well, it lost to Persona 5, and given that it stands as one of my all-time favorite JRPGs and the best-selling Persona entry ever, I completely understand the decision. Furthermore, in TGA history, Persona 5 was the first JRPG to compete for the main Game of the Year prize. It didn't win because it went up against The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, and really, who can complain about that outcome?

It still secured Best Role Playing Game over NieR: Automata, Divinity: Original Sin II, and Final Fantasy XV, which speaks to what a stacked year 2017 was for RPG fans. If the votes were up to me, Persona 5 would have swept practically every category and called it a night. It is a JRPG through and through, featuring a slow burn, extensive social sim segments, and mountains of dialogue, yet it still shattered niche barriers to reach a massive global audience. May Atlus keep on winning.

Apparently my wishes were granted in record time, because Atlus kept on winning. While Persona 5 earned 4 nominations, Metaphor: ReFantazio racked up 6 nominations and took home 3 major trophies: Best Narrative, Best Art Direction, and Best Role-Playing Game, edging out Square Enix's own Final Fantasy VII Rebirth. That is an extraordinary feat, mind you. Despite Persona's massive growth, Final Fantasy remains a far more recognizable mainstream brand, making it all the more impressive that critics favored Metaphor.

I adore Metaphor, by the way. It was the very first JRPG I covered right after joining the team here at DualShockers, and I fell in love with it immediately. It is stylish, gutting, gripping, challenging, and endlessly engaging. It came dangerously close to dethroning Persona 5 as my favorite Atlus JRPG, falling just a hair short, though not quite yet. I don't know whether Studio Zero plans to release DLC or jump straight into a follow-up, but I will definitely be there on day one and hope to see its successor pull in even more nominations.

Eagle-eyed readers might notice that I left Final Fantasy VII Remake off this list. That entry received 6 nominations, though including it felt slightly redundant since both games competed in nearly the same categories, simply swapping Best Art Direction for Best Audio Design, while Final Fantasy VII Rebirth added a Best Performance for Briana White as Aerith. She ultimately lost to Melina Juergens as Senua, which is tough luck.

Even so, if we look specifically at Japanese-developed JRPGs (as awkward as that phrasing sounds), Final Fantasy VII Rebirth holds the record for the most nominations in TGA history so far. The title earned nods for Best Game Direction, Best Narrative, Best Score and Music (which it won), Best Audio Design, Best Performance, Best Role-Playing Game (losing to Metaphor: ReFantazio), and Game of the Year (losing to Astro Bot). No matter how hard certain corners of the internet try to downplay Rebirth's success, critics worldwide universally acclaimed it, and I cannot wait to see Final Fantasy VII Revelation rack up just as many nominations and plenty of wins.

We are well past debating whether Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 counts as a JRPG, so I will skip the preamble. What I want to highlight here is something everyone who follows the TGAs already knows: Expedition 33 holds the record as the most awarded game in TGA history. That milestone is not only deeply symbolic for the current climate of the gaming industry, where publishers constantly chase infinite exponential profits, but it also proves that simply throwing massive budgets at a project is not the secret to making a great game.

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 represents a union of passionate fans, sheer willpower, rich inspiration, and genuine love for art and video games distilled into something tangible, fun, emotional, and lasting. From the very moment I started playing, I could not put the controller down, completely enthralled by the journey of my Expeditioners. As proof of that obsession, I actually earned the world's first platinum trophy for the game, and I am not kidding. Even director Guillaume Broche himself confirmed it to me on LinkedIn. Not trying to gloat, just showing how transcendental my love for this title truly is.

We're not just talking about Grand Theft Auto VI and Ocarina of Time, but up to seven video games that could sweep The Game Awards.
