Dem governor’s tale of mourning fellow serviceman ripped apart after soldier's wife gives brutal fact check

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Fox News Digital spoke to Retired Lt. Col. Jamie Gottschling who pushed back against Moore’s claims that he "led paratroopers in combat" in Afghanistan.

Maryland Democratic Gov. Wes Moore – widely believed to be seriously weighing a presidential run in 2028 – is facing controversy yet again over his characterization of his military record, this time facing allegations he embellished his relationship with a fallen soldier and used an impossible timeline to tell an emotional story in his memoir.

The issue stems from a Baltimore Sun report over the weekend highlighting a passage in Moore's 2015 book, "The Work: Searching for a Life That Matters," where he told the story of breaking into tears at his grandfather's funeral when hearing about the death of his friend Tobias "Toby" Meister.

"Inside that casket, I also saw Toby," Moore wrote in his book, adding that he cried for his grandfather, Toby and other fallen soldiers.

However, Moore's grandfather's funeral was on Dec. 17, 2005. Meister was killed in a roadside bombing 11 days later, on Dec. 28, 2005.

Moore did not learn about Meister's death until January of the next year, when he returned to Afghanistan, the Baltimore Sun reported.

After Meister's death, his wife Alicia told the outlet that many of his friends from the military have reached out to her over the years and kept his memory alive with their son, who was just over a year old when his father was killed.

"I’ve never heard from him," his wife, Alicia, told the outlet. "I’ve never personally heard from him," she added. "I don’t know who [Moore] is. I don’t recall my late husband ever talking about him."

Days after the Baltimore Sun reached out to Moore's office for comment, Alicia Meister said she heard from Moore.

"It was good," she told the outlet. "He paid his respects and apologized for not reaching out sooner."

She added, "I do wish, though, that my late husband would be removed from the situation completely. I just don’t like that he has been brought in the middle of it."

In a statement to Fox News Digital, Moore spokesperson Ammar Moussa pushed back.

"Governor Moore is deeply proud of his military service. He raised his hand to serve his country at 17, left his civilian career, and deployed to one of the most dangerous regions in Afghanistan, where he served with distinction and earned the praise of his superiors and fellow service members," Moussa said.

"A Republican swiftboat campaign orchestrated by Sinclair executive chairman David Smith reached a new, despicable low. Smith—a right-wing billionaire ally of Donald Trump—is now exploiting grieving families and fellow service members for a political hit job. The facts are clear and confirmed by the Governor’s fellow service members: Governor Moore knew and worked with First Sgt. Toby Meister in Afghanistan, and this shameless attempt to drag a grieving Gold Star family into the political mud is a disgrace."

Moussa also accused Sinclair of lying about Moore's response in the article and calling it a "bad-faith approach" that is "typical of an organization run by a right-wing billionaire who is using his media empire to try and intimidate the Governor into silence."

1st Lt. Anthony Del Signore, a Marine who served alongside Moore in Afghanistan, has also defended Moore against recent criticism.

"Questioning his combat record isn't just a political attack on him—it diminishes the very real dangers all of us faced out on those roads. Wes did his job, took the risks, and served honorably," he said. "Turning our deployment into a partisan hit job is an insult to everyone who wore the uniform."

Moore previously acknowledged the conflicting timeline in interviews with the Baltimore Banner in June, attributing the inaccuracy to poor editing and the effects of stress, grief and time on his memory.

The report comes on the heels of a Baltimore Sun story earlier this month in which his former Army supervisor questioned the governor's claim that he led soldiers in combat, a story told in the same book.

In an investigative deep dive into Moore's military service, the Baltimore Sun spoke with retired Army Lt. Col. James A. "Jamie" Gottschling, who served with Moore in Afghanistan in 2005 and 2006. What Gottschling described was Moore's patriotic service and dutiful fulfillment of his role, but exaggerations — and in some cases falsehoods — about incidents that occurred overseas.

Del Signore pushed back on that criticism as well, saying in a statement, "I’ve heard the comments from Major Gottschling claiming this didn't happen and that Wes didn't go outside the wire. I can tell you that this 100% happened because I was there."

"Wes and I were outside the wire an average amount of the time, we traveled a decent amount, and we'd be gone for a week at a time traveling around the region. It is not surprising that Major Gottschling doesn't think this happened because he was not in the chain of command that would have been reported to, and he wasn't in charge of the convoy."

Additionally, a New York Times exposé found in 2024 that Moore for years claimed to be the recipient of a Bronze Star when he had not been awarded one at the time. He included the Bronze Star claim on his resumé when applying for a White House fellowship in 2006. In 2024, he received the award after then-Sen. Laphonza Butler, D-Calif., urged the Army to give him one.
