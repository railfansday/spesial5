Watching the Market: Nov. 16-23 – The stars have aligned

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

While the Cleveland Cavaliers continue to sort through the highs and lows, the players individually have been trending in the right direction.

Mitchell has received some criticism for his start to the season from Cavs fans, but he has responded well recently by stringing together a dominant stretch of games. Since the Cavaliers' win over the Chicago Bulls on November 16, Mitchell has averaged 31 points on 55.1% shooting in five games.

Mitchell has felt like the Cavaliers' counterpunch this past week. Cleveland found them in quite a few back-and-forth contests as of late; just when it looked like the Cavaliers were going to lose the effort, Mitchell would insert himself into the mix to close out the contest.

These types of stretches quell many people's anxiety about Mitchell, and more specifically, the length of his contract. For a mere $273 million over the next four years, you'd like to be reminded why the Cavaliers shelled out the money they did. This stretch proved once again that Mitchell is still the perennial All-NBA guard who's one of the best scorers in the league.

Before the Cavaliers' Friday night win over the Indiana Pacers, the concerns over James Harden's play had really reached an apex. The shooting splits were gnarly. Harden was averaging .385/.375/.857 shooting splits going into that contest. Far from what you are hoping for from the future Hall of Famer.

Then against the Pacers, it was quite evident that Harden was feeling it from the jump. He erupted to the tune of 51 points. This wasn't a game where he had a ridiculous volume to get to 51, this was from shooting 57% from the field and 63% from the perimeter on 11 attempts. The icing on the cake was that Harden was a perfect 14-for-14 from the charity stripe.

Harden has spoken about how he is still ramping up to in-season form. For the first month or so, it looked like he wasn't far from his ideal form. If Friday was any indication, Harden might have been true to his word. While he will certainly not keep up this nuclear form of scoring, it shows that this is a possibility, which it looked like was not in the cards as of last week.

Merrill has become somewhat of a divisive player within the Cavaliers fandom. Some still hold Merrill accountable as the reason Ty Jerome is no longer on this roster, due to both being free agents at the same time. Although, it's completely unfair to the player to hold him to a decision made by the front office. However, any time Merrill has a far-from-optimal game, expect the timeline to look up Jerome's box score.

Merrill has been in and out of the lineup due to injury, allowing for the younger guards like Tyreese Proctor and Meleek Thomas to get some serious run. The duo has constantly flashed in Merrill's absence, making him seem expendable in the eyes of the fans.

When the Cavaliers were going to the wire with the Memphis Grizzlies (ironically enough), it was up to Merrill to give the Cavaliers the lead with two free throws. As you can tell by where he resides in this stock watch, it didn't go great.

Merrill missed both, which sent the socials ablaze. Absence did not grow the heart fonder with Merrill, and moments like these stick in the memories of fans. While they do not control front office decisions, it is an interesting thought: will Merrill's value to the organization remain if his sharp shooting can be replicated by the younger guards on the roster?

Merrill will likely hit his groove again in due time; however, the patience from the fanbase might lag behind.

I am convinced Atkinson will never recover from his "analytically" comments in the Eastern Conference Finals. That meme lives with you till Valhalla, brother. However, he does still find ways to cause ripples in the social media sphere.

His latest doozy is after Harden's 51-point eruption, he decided to soft launch his take on how Harden is a top-ten player of all time.

At some point, you have to give it to Atkinson. This is some great engagement-farming tactics at work here. However, I would like these fun comments to come from more consistent coaching. I guess entertainment at the podium is the price to pay for adaptable coaching. Sigh.
