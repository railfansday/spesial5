One of the biggest turnarounds from the Commanders tie back in Week 5 against the Giants and their win over the Giants on Thursday night was the plan of attack against Cover-0. In Week 5, the Giants spammed Cover-0 blitzes throughout the second half and overtime and the Commanders just couldn't handle it. They continually attempted to take shots down the field to try and hit explosive plays rather than just finding a simple quick solution.

Daniels was seeing those blitzes a lot better and it showed in the most critical moment.

Having thrown two late interceptions, it looked like the Commanders season was over, but the defense bailed Daniels out and gave him another opportunity, which he wasn't going to pass up. On third and four with under a minute remaining in the game, the Giants of course show Daniels another Cover-0 blitz. This time, Daniels doesn't adjust the protection, but instead signals to Stefon Diggs in the slot to change his route. In the first game, when Daniels saw Cover-0, he often checked into a shot play to try and attack the blitz and throw a big touchdown over the top of it. But clearly this time they were looking to play smarter, and it worked out much better for them.

Daniels adjusts Diggs route, having him run a slant against the slot corner. When he snaps the ball, the defense comes on the blitz, but the defensive tackle to the right side of the line pops up and drops out into coverage, trying to take away a hot throw over the middle. Daniels just checked into a hot throw over the middle with Diggs running a slant, so that could potentially be a problem. But Daniels does a great job adjusting his throw to beat that defensive lineman dropping into coverage.

Diggs runs a great route, beating the corner with a nice stutter move before sharply cutting across his face to get inside. The throw from Daniels has to be slightly behind him because of that defensive lineman dropping into coverage, but Diggs does well to adjust to the pass and make the grab. Diggs breaks a diving tackle from the slot corner and gets some help from fellow receiver Van Jefferson, who helps block the other nearby defender. With both defenders out of the picture, Diggs runs uncontested into the end zone for a 32-yard game-winning touchdown.

It goes to show that you can still score a big touchdown against Cover-0 without having to try and take a deep shot constantly. As I said back in Week 5, I don't mind that their philosophy against Cover-0 is to go on the attack, in fact I quite like it. It offers you one-on-one matchups and I like that they feel like they can take shots with those matchups. But they clearly went too far in Week 5 in trying to throw bombs down the field. This game proved there's more ways to go about it. Winning a one-on-one doesn't have to be 20 yards down the field, it can be a quick slant like this when you have Stefon Diggs or Antonio Williams in the slot. With it being Cover-0, if you win that one-on-one quickly, there's every chance the receiver can run freely after the catch and score as there's no safety help over the top.

So credit should go to David Blough and Jayden Daniels for making that adjustment. It's a shame they couldn't have made it back in Week 5 because they would have won that game too if they had, but it paid off for them this time around and it meant that the Commanders have just about kept their season alive.

After catching just 17 passes over his first two-plus NFL seasons behind Zach Ertz and Chig Okonkwo, third-year tight end Ben Sinnott stepped in when Okonkwo exited Thursday night's game with a back injury and delivered four receptions for 42 yards and a touchdown.

Sinnott hurdled a defender to convert a critical first down on the final drive of the first half before hauling in a go-ahead score on third-and-goal, then added a 24-yard catch-and-run early in the third quarter. He has now caught 21 of 24 career targets, making a strong case for offensive coordinator David Blough to feature more two-tight-end sets down the stretch.

In two-tight-end packages, Commanders offensive coordinator David Blough should give Sinnott a few defined passing-game opportunities on early downs and near the goal line. Blough should continue his approach of giving each tight end their own distinct role, but should also look to take advantage of Sinnott's receiving and after-the-catch ability that, until Thursday night, was going to waste.

If Okonkwo is back to his usual workload against Cincinnati, I'll be watching the snaps he shares with Sinnott. When Washington needs a first down and both tight ends are out there, does Daniels go back to Sinnott?
