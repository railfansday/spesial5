Fantasy Football 2026: Week 11 preview — Start/sit and more

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Welcome to Week 12 of the college football season!

Hang on, I got confused because when I looked at this week's betting lines, I saw five double-digit point spreads, with two in excess of 15 points. What??? Apparently, the Chiefs are playing Appalachian State while the Bills square off against Coastal Carolina. And on and on. The talent differential in the NFL isn't that great, and it's rare to see a slate that looks like this. Welcome to Week 11 of the NFL season!

Last week gave us an explosion of scoring and some epic fantasy performances. If you were up against Christian McCaffrey, Jahmyr Gibbs, George Pickens, Emmett Johnson, Patrick Mahomes, or Dak Prescott, among others, it was a long day. We might get more of the same in Week 11. Favorites are 24-4 outright the last two weeks and a lot of those games haven't been close, with the winners running up the score.

The game of the week is Minnesota (7-2) at San Francisco (8-1), which pits the league's #2 scoring offense (31.2 points per game) against the league's top scoring defense (14 points per game). Something's gotta give, right? It's the Sunday night game and it's being played in Mexico City. Did Brock Purdy make my Fades list? Read on to find out.

Six teams are on bye this week, for the only time this season. Throw in all the injuries and the challenge for fantasy managers is clear. I'll try to help you navigate it.

Every team in the NFC North is over .500 and every team in the NFC South is under .500.

NFC South teams are a collective 4-17 at home.

All six of the Browns' wins have been by four points or less. No team in NFL history has done that across their first six wins of a season.

The 49ers are the first team since the 2000 "Greatest Show on Turf" Rams to have three touchdowns of 60+ yards in one game. I don't think Dallas defenders laid a hand on the ball carrier on any of those plays.

Josh Allen has 88 rushing TDs, halfway through his ninth season. Eric Dickerson had 88 rushing TDs through his first nine full seasons.

Jahmyr Gibbs has the most TDs through four seasons (62) of any player in NFL history.

In the Bengals' curb-stomping of the Steelers, Joe Burrow was 26 for 34 for 304 yards. At halftime. Late in the third quarter, net passing yards read as follows: Bengals 356, Steelers (-9).

Fernando Mendoza is the first rookie quarterback in NFL history to make his starting debut against the defending Super Bowl champions.

The Ravens – who have a healthy Derrick Henry – put up 16 rushing yards at home against a previously winless team.

Over their last three games (all wins), the Jaguars have outscored their opponents by 75 points.

Bye Weeks: Falcons, Browns, Packers, Rams, Patriots, Seahawks

For those who are familiar with this column, you know the drill. For everyone else: The Rides, Fades, and Sleepers is an analysis of players that I think are primed for an especially strong or poor performance, often as compared to consensus expectations. This isn't a straight-up Start/Sit exercise. As a general rule, start your studs. I'll rarely list the most obvious names at a position as "Rides" because those players are almost always expected to have strong performances. You don't need me or anyone else telling you to start Jahmyr Gibbs. While this analysis is intended for season-long play, it works for DFS formats too. Half PPR scoring and Expert Consensus Rankings (ECR) are used for the column.

Each week, I'm picking a Ride, Fade, and Sleeper of the Week. The rules: The Ride of the Week can't be a truly elite option, the Fade can't be someone who nobody is starting anyway, and the Sleeper must be an actual sleeper, from down in the rankings.

Last week, my Rides, Fades, and Sleepers did well, with a lot more hits than misses. Ride of the Week James Cook ran for 157 yards and a TD, and Fade of the Week Ashton Jeanty was held to 18 rushing yards and 8.4 fantasy points (Half PPR). You can check my work here.

Ride of the Week: Tony Pollard (@DAL). This selection isn't about a revenge game narrative. The analysis is straightforward: Pollard has been great over the last month, and Dallas's defense is horrific. Christian McCaffrey has had a lot of incredible games in his brilliant career, but perhaps none better than what he did to the Cowboys last week (44 fantasy points in Full PPR). With that game on its resume, Dallas has allowed the most fantasy points per game (FPPG) and total TDs (16) to opposing running backs. As for Pollard, his last three games have produced RB1, RB3, and RB8 weekly finishes yet he's still flying a little under the radar. This is a perfect marriage of red-hot player and excellent matchup. Enjoy the wedding vows and don't overthink it.

Fade of the Week: Tyler Warren (@HOU). Warren has been a major disappointment this season. He was drafted as a Top-5 TE, and through ten weeks, he's the TE12 on the season, at just 8.3 FPPG. He's been especially cold lately, even with the Colts jettisoning receivers at the trade deadline. He's averaging 3 catches for 21 yards over his last three outings, with no trips to the end zone. Houston's defense has been a huge disappointment, but they allow the seventh fewest FPPG to opposing tight ends and have yielded just one touchdown to the position. Throw in how badly Daniel Jones is playing and the short week, and Warren is s sit for me on Thursday Night Football.

Sleeper of the Week: Justin Herbert (vs. NYJ). Yup, I'm going here, after fading Herbert week after week. Cam Ward's weekly ranking is too high (QB15) to put here. More on him later. Herbert and the Chargers have been a punchline all season, but they finally broke through at Baltimore on Monday night. Herbert is ranked as the QB20 this week, even with six teams on bye and multiple starting quarterbacks hurt. Come on! I get that he's been awful, and he wasn't exactly great on Monday night. But there were signs. He didn't turn the ball over for the first time all year, used his legs to make big plays, and put up his best result of the season (16.3 fantasy points). He didn't have Ladd McConkey, who could be back this week. Do I think the Chargers' offense turned a corner? No. But they're hosting the equally hapless Jets and should make it two in a row. New York is a Bottom-10 defense against opposing QBs, and importantly, they've allowed the most rushing TDs to the position (4). The Chargers are finally getting healthier along the line. Lock Herbie in for a finish in the QB 8-12 range.

Elite options this week – Josh Allen, Patrick Mahomes, Dak Prescott, and Joe Burrow;the analysis starts below them.

Three quarterbackss ranked in the Top-10 this week who I think will outperform projections: Caleb Williams (vs. NO), Trevor Lawrence (@NYG), and C.J. Stroud (vs. IND). The last two listed are playing at a very high level and have lots of weapons to choose from, and neither matchup is all that scary. Williams has had two weeks to get past his 4-INT nightmare game against the Bucs (which the Bears still managed to win), and I think he'll play a clean game at home against a defense that can't stop the run and will be easy to exploit with play-action. Play all your Bears this week.

Cam Ward (@DAL) is ranked as the QB15 this week but he's going to be a very popular streamer for those without a QB in Week 11. He'll have high usage in DFS too. He's averaging 23 FPPG over his last two games, and the Cowboys are about as generous as it gets. They've allowed the third most passing yards and fourth most FPPG to opposing QBs, and this game has shootout potential. Wan'Dale Robinson is gone for the season, but Ward should also get Carnell Tate back this week.

Bo Nix (vs. LV) has been extremely hard to trust, but he's got a very nice matchup against the Raiders and might have all of his receiving weapons healthy for the first time in a while. Vegas allows the third most FPPG to opposing QBs and they're tied with Arizona for most TD passes yielded (22).

Other QB2-types to consider if you need it: Jacoby Brissett (@CAR), whoever starts for the Buccaneers (@DET), Geno Smith (@LAC, assuming he plays), and if you're really desperate, Joe Milton (vs. PIT) and Kyle McCord (@BUF) could surprise – Milton with his legs and more weapons to throw to, and McCord with extended garbage time and the Bills possibly just trying to get to their big Thanksgiving night game with the Chiefs in one piece.

Brock Purdy (vs. MIN) is this week's stud quarterback facing Brian Flores's pressure defense. I've seen enough. I can bury you with stats, including that the Vikings have allowed the fewest FPPG and just six total TDs to opposing QBs. But this matters even more: Over the past two weeks Minnesota held Josh Allen and Jordan Love – two QBs having monster seasons – to season lows in fantasy points, while making their lives miserable. Even when the Vikings don't blitz, which is rare, QBs see ghosts and feel rushed. Purdy has a great ability to move in the pocket and extend plays and he has playmakers who can exploit mismatches, so I don't think it's crazy to roll with him. But if you have another decent option, you have to consider it. Love was on this Fades list last week for the same reason.

Jared Goff (vs. Bucs) is another high-end producer who is very tough to sit, but the Bucs are also no joke so I'd at least temper expectations. After picking Caleb Williams's pocket four times in their last game, Tampa leads the NFL in interceptions (16) and allows the fourth fewest FPPG to opposing QBs. Todd Bowles's defense is also well-rested off a bye.

Dropping lower, here are some QB2s who fantasy managers could be considering this week, but that I'm avoiding if I can: Fernando Mendoza (@DEN), J.J. McCarthy (vs. JAC), and Aaron Rodgers (@PHI).

Elite options this week – Jahmyr Gibbs, James Cook, Christian McCaffrey, and Kenneth Walker (if he plays); the analysis starts below them.

If Walker misses another game, Emmett Johnson (vs. ARI) is a no-brainer. Even if Walker starts, Johnson could see an increase in reps as they try to manage Walker's workload and is worthy of consideration as an RB2 or Flex play. This game won't be close.

Chuba Hubbard (vs. BAL) continues to be a set-it-and-forget it starter, and one of the top values this season. He's quietly the RB4 in 2026 on an FPPG basis, ahead of names like Jonathan Taylor and Bijan Robinson. Fire him up against a so-so run defense that just made Omarion Hampton look like Ezekiel Elliott and is playing on a short week.

I think D'Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai (vs. NO) are both startable this week, in a game Chicago should control. Assuming Monangai is good to go, I expect the kind of splits we saw down the stretch last year. New Orleans allows the second most FPPG to opposing backs. They've also given up the most rushing TDs to running backs (13), while Chicago's RBs have scored them at the highest clip (14).

Derrick Henry (@CAR) was stuck in the mud on Monday night and had one of the lowest rushing yardage totals of his Hall of Fame career. Thankfully, he got bailed out with two short TD runs. He's always going to be a threat to get those easy plunges, and I expect easier sledding this week against the Panthers, who've allowed the fourth most FPPG and second most rushing TDs (12) to the position.

Others ranked inside the Top-25 this week who I think have a good shot to outperform their weekly projections: Bhayshul Tuten (@NYG), Jonah Coleman (vs. LV), Chase Brown (@WAS, and it was nice to see him finally break through), Breece Hall (@LAC), Omarion Hampton (vs. NYJ) to keep it going for another week, and Ollie Gordon II (@BUF).

It's once again hard to find sleepers at running back this week. A few lower-end options to consider if you're stuck: David Montgomery (@IND, and this is a fingers-crossed special as he's been awful, or hurt, since Week 1) and Justice Hill (@CAR). If R.J. Harvey (vs. LV) returns, I think he's another dart throw to consider.

I'll keep fading Ashton Jeanty (@DEN), who hasn't topped 27 rushing yards since Week 4 (that's not a misprint).He's getting enough receiving work to keep him at the borderline, but it's a tough way to live.

Bucky Irving (@DET) has been too good to consider sitting, but temper expectations against the Lions, who are tough to run on (third fewest FPPG and rushing yards allowed to the position). I think this game is going to be lower scoring than people think. I expect Tampa to try very hard to run the ball, as long as the game is close. But they have major issues at quarterback and Detroit should be able to load the box.

Cam Skattebo (vs. JAC) is another player who's tough to sit, and especially after his big game last week. But he could have a tough go of it on Sunday. The Jaguars have allowed the second fewest rushing yards and fifth fewest FPPG to opposing backs, and they keep putting their opponents in catch-up mode early.

Two backfields to avoid in their entirety: Washington (vs. CIN, it's a crowd and a tougher matchup), and Arizona (@KC).

Other RB Fades: Jordan Mason (@ SF) and Rico Dowdle (@PHI).

Elite options this week – Ja'Marr Chase AND Tee Higgins, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Justin Jefferson, and CeeDee Lamb; the analysis starts below them.

Here are six Rides this week, all ranked inside the weekly Top-20: George Pickens (vs. TEN, and yes regression is coming but I think he can feast a little in a nice matchup), Nico Collins (vs. IND), Malik Nabers (vs. JAC, for a bounce-back and they'll probably be chasing points), Luther Burden III (vs. TB), Jordyn Tyson (@CHI, and he should again see plenty of volume), and Carnell Tate (@DAL).

Moving down to those ranked between 21 and 30 for the Week — I like these guys as WR2/WR3 or Flex plays: Rome Odunze (vs. NO), Caleb Douglas (@BUF, assuming he plays), Josh Downs (vs. ARI), and (gulp) Jaylen Waddle (vs. LV).

WRs ranked outside the Top-30 this week that I think you can plug into lineups if you're in need include: Pat Bryant (vs. LV), Elic Ayomanor (@DAL), Xavier Hutchinson (vs. IND), Makai Lemon (vs. PIT), Tre' Harris (vs. NYJ), Ja'Kobi Lane (@CAR, 11 targets last week), and if you want a real longshot, Myles Price (@SF).

It's a favorable matchup on paper, but I'm sitting Emeka Egbuka and Chris Godwin (@DET). It's either going to be a gimpy, one-thumbed Baker Mayfield, or Jalon Daniels. I expect a defensive struggle in this one. No thanks.

D.J. Moore (vs. MIA) is tough to sit, but I don't expect a big game. Game script could be an issue, and Miami has been surprisingly tough on opposing WRs (second fewest yards and third fewest FPPG allowed to the position).

Jalen Coker (vs. BAL) and Germie Bernard (@PHI) are too cold to be in lineups at the moment. I'd hold both, for at least another week.

Don't start any Arizona receivers (@KC). They don't have any who you'd want to play and it's a brutal matchup (second fewest FPPG allowed to opposing receivers). Speaking of receivers you couldn't pick out of a police lineup, no Colts WRs (@HOU) should be anywhere near fantasy lineups either.

Other wide receivers ranked inside the Top-30 and/or who should be starting options this season and who I'm lower than consensus on this week include: Stefon Diggs (vs. CIN), Keenan Allen (vs. MIN), Tre Tucker (@DEN), and Jakobi Meyers (@NYG).

Elite options this week – Brock Bowers if he plays), Trey McBride (let's give this Carson Beck thing one more week before we panic), and Isaiah Likely; the analysis starts below them.

I like all of these tight ends who are ranked inside the weekly Top-12 to beat their projections: Colston Loveland (vs. NO, seventh most FPPG allowed to opposing TEs), Dalton Kincaid (vs. MIA, who has been great defending receivers but victimized by tight ends (sixth most FPPG allowed)), Chig Okonkwo (vs. CIN, ninth most FPPG allowed to opposing TEs), and Mike Gesicki (@WAS).

There are several names to consider this week.

Mark Andrews (@CAR) saw a season-high eight catches on eight targets, in his first game with Jacoby Brissett. He's no Trey McBride, but I expect Brissett to keep looking for his big target in a pass-heavy offense.

Michael Mayer (@DEN) is in play, and especially if Brock Bowers is out. The Broncos have been vulnerable to the tight end position.

Others to consider if you want to go deep: Brevyn Spann-Ford (vs. TEN) and in the same game Gunnar Helm (@DAL). Cade Otton (@DET) has the single best tight end matchup on paper, but I don't trust the QB situation.

Notably absent from the elites list this week, and this week only, is George Kittle (vs. MIN). And let me get this out of the way: Don't sit Kittle. But he's listed here because of the brutality of the matchup. Minnesota is one of several teams to only allow one touchdown to tight ends on the season, and they've also yielded the fewest catches and FPPG to the position.

It's gotten very tough to keep rolling with Sam LaPorta (vs. TB), He's gone four straight weeks with a weekly finish of TE24 or lower. I'll keep fading him, although I think he could be more involved this week.

Three others I'm low on this week: Pat Freiermuth (@PHI), Juwan Johnson (@CHI), and Brenton Strange (@NYG).
