13 winners, 4 losers from Detroit Lions’ win over the Tampa Bay Buccaneers

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

There was no jet lag for the Detroit Lions.

A week after their overseas tilt in Munich, Germany, the Lions returned to home turf to host the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. The Buccaneers were projected to be a Minnesota Vikings-esque team—a stout defense offset by a lackluster offense. However, with Baker Mayfield healthy enough to start and Bucky Irving on a tear recently, it would not be an easy task for Detroit on either side of the football.

After a scoreless first quarter, the Lions woke up on offense and stayed strong on defense. The offense dropped 31 points on the NFL's best defense by Defense-adjusted Value Over Average, all while holding their own on defense. The Buccaneers finished with just 274 total yards on offense. All of a sudden, the Lions are clicking on both offense and defense:

Detroit faces a short week with Thanksgiving and the Chicago Bears on deck. Which players emerged from this game as winners or losers?

Coming off a game versus the New England Patriots in which he threw two ugly interceptions, Goff faced a tough task versus the top-ranked Buccaneers defense. In spite of the pressure, Goff delivered arguably his best game of the year.

Stat-wise, Goff has had bigger performances this season, but he was phenomenal against Tampa Bay in ways that don't show up in the numbers. The offensive line was not at its best, largely due to a large Vita Vea up the middle, yet Goff navigated the pressure swimmingly. He survived a collapsing pocket to hit Tyler Conklin for an 18-yard gain. He had a 10-yard scamper for a first down. One of Goff's best throws of the season came on his downfield shot to Jameson Williams with pressure in his grill:

Goff was not afraid to chuck it downfield, also connecting on deep shots to Sam LaPorta (47 yards) and Brock Wright (27 yards), nor did he shy away from Williams or Amon-Ra St. Brown on intermediate routes. If anything, the Lions were going away from their usual short passing attack—Jahmyr Gibbs only caught four passes for 20 yards. Goff finished the game averaging 10.0 yards per attempt, the highest average of his season thus far.

Lesser running backs would have been stonewalled by the Buccaneers defense, but Gibbs is simply too special. He is too fast and explosive to stop for an entire game. Early on, the Buccaneers admittedly had his number. However, Gibbs does the little things that make an impact over the course of a game. He ekes out any extra yard he can get. He can make defenders miss on a dime. Eventually, he can and will get his breakout plays—he is inevitable.

Gibbs turned 23 carries into 98 yards and two touchdowns, and he earned every single one of those stats. We are so lucky to witness a player like him.

On paper, the Lions defense walked away with four sacks, but I think that tally is misleading. The first sack, courtesy of DJ Wonnum and a few assists, was a legitimate pass rush win from the defensive line. The second sack was a key third-down sack from safety Chuck Clark. The third and fourth sacks came on back-to-back plays late in the game, one via a Trevor Nowaske spy and one via a seven-man blitz.

Individually, these are great plays, but it masks the problem for the pass rush: consistency. Consistency was an issue even with Hutchinson in the lineup, but you could at least count on him to generate a half dozen pressures per game—he is too good to stop for an entire game. Without Hutchinson, the Lions are going to have to scheme up their pass rush wins more than rely on individual effort.

I think Sheppard did a great job guiding a Hutchinson-less defense on Sunday. The Lions had timely blitzes. The run defense was solid for all but one drive. The secondary once again eliminated the big plays. The team looks far more cohesive and disciplined than when they started.

However, I am still a bit concerned about the pass rush. Outside of those schemed plays, the Lions were not generating much pressure. The Buccaneers' final drive of the first half exemplified that—Mayfield had too much time and delivered perfect passes despite tight coverage. Against better offenses, slow developing pressure is not enough. The Lions have guys with motor, certainly, but they need someone to consistently collapse the pocket without having to dial up a blitz or a stunt. Wonnum is steady and Derrick Moore is progressing (who added another sack to his resume), but the lack of Hutchinson is quite evident—I would like to see more one-on-one wins.

I am really impressed with Williams' growth this season. The deep ball remains his greatest strength, but he makes the most of every down. He is beginning to thrive on the intermediate routes as well, using his speed to bait defenders into covering deep. His hands have improved of late, and he has now has back-to-back games with toe-tap catches along the sideline. Coupled with his stout blocking for a receiver (especially one as skinny as him), there is a lot to love about how Jamo is playing right now.

LaPorta needed a game like this. Given how well the offense had been playing over the last month, they have not needed LaPorta to carry the offense—a few catches a game and some key blocks were more than enough for what Detroit needed. However, it was important for LaPorta to show that he can still be impactful in the receiving game, not only for his own confidence but as a reminder to the world that he is still a top-five tight end in the league.

LaPorta finished with three catches for 62 yards, the biggest of which was his 47-yarder early in the game. This was his most receiving yards in a game since Week 5 versus the Arizona Cardinals. He also sealed the way for Jahmyr Gibbs' second touchdown run of the game.

Last week, I thought that Barnes quietly had one of his best games of the year. This week, I think he quietly had one of his worst games of the year. Tackling was the biggest issue for Barnes, who struggled on multiple occasions to wrap up the ball carrier. The athleticism that shined last week was absent against Tampa Bay. Barnes has the potential to be a sideline-to-sideline type of linebacker that can also bring pressure, but both of those facets were lackluster. Too often he looked out of place. During the times he was in the right spot, a bad angle or tackling attempt gave Tampa Bay more yards.

Considering that the Lions are getting good linebacker play out of their other linebackers, it might be time to dial back Barnes' role on defense.

Clark earned the captaincy for his presence in the locker room, but has also earned it for his play on the field. Clark was a menace versus the Buccaneers, his first game donning the green dot (a role usually reserved for linebackers). He recorded a sack on a perfectly timed blitz in the second quarter. Shortly after, he blew up a Buccaneers receiver to force an incompletion. His tackling was steady too, as he was one of the few players able to wrap up Bucky Irving. On the day, he finished with eight tackles, a sack, a tackle for loss, and a pass defended—this diverse stat line shows how active Clark was across the field.

With Kerby Joseph back in the mix, Brian Branch capable of playing all over, and Clark playing at a high level, the Lions' best defensive look going forward surely has all three safeties on the field.

Winner: K.J. Britt, LBBritt had two impact plays early in the game: he notched a key fourth-down stop, then followed it up with a big special teams tackle. His role continues to grow and he could be a starter before long.

Winner: Malcolm Rodriguez, LBRodriguez continues to be the Lions' best cover linebacker. The Buccaneers' tight ends accomplished very little thanks to Rodriguez locking them down.

Winner: Trevor Nowaske, LBNowaske is the Lions not-so-secret weapon on defense. His athleticism pops on every play, as shown by his sack of Baker Mayfield in which he spied the quarterback like a heat-seeking missile.

Loser: Juice Scruggs, CIt was far from a bad day for Scruggs, but he was struggling versus Vita Vea. Power isn't Scruggs' A-game, so whenever the Lions were running into Vea, they were met with a brick wall. In the future, the Lions need to scheme up help for Scruggs versus big nose tackles like Vea.

Winner: Brock Wright, TEAnother good game from Wright as a blocker, but he was surprisingly active as a receiver, catching two passes for 42 yards (more yards than Amon-Ra St. Brown). This was a good reminder that he can be a sneakily effective pass catcher.

Loser: Isaac TeSlaa, WRTeSlaa had three targets but failed to corral any of them. Two of them were difficult but catchable, especially for the guy known for acrobatic grabs.

Winners: Rock Ya-Sin and Roger McCreary, CBsAnother week where the opposing receivers accomplished very little. Ya-Sin and McCreary have been solid over the past month. Both were officially credited with allowing touchdowns, but Ya-Sin's was a perfect pass from Mayfield and McCreary's was a garbage-time score (and even then, it was another nice outside shoulder throw for Mayfield).

Winner: Kerby Joseph, SIt was modest outing for Joseph, but simply getting him back on the field is a win.
