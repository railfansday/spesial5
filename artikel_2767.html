Grand Prix Finland: Mao Shimada wins, Isabeau Levito climbs to podium

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Mao Shimada extended her undefeated record in international figure skating competition, while American Isabeau Levito climbed onto the podium at Grand Prix Finland.

Shimada totaled 229.49 points, overcoming a fall on an under-rotated quadruple toe loop in Saturday's free skate to prevail by a comfortable 15.43 points over Niina Petrokina of Estonia.

Levito jumped from sixth after Friday's short program to place third overall, making her ninth career Grand Prix podium in 11 career starts — the highest podium percentage for a U.S. woman since Michelle Kwan.

Grand Prix Finland concludes later Saturday with the men's free skate (10:15 a.m. ET), and free dance (12:25 p.m.), live on Peacock. NBC airs highlights on Sunday from 4-6 p.m.

Shimada gave Japan a fifth win in five Grand Prix women's singles events this season. The last regular season event is next week in Japan, where Japan's Mone Chiba is the favorite. The only time a nation swept one season's Grand Prix titles in one discipline was when Russia did so in women's singles in 2019.

Shimada, 18, is in her first senior season after going undefeated in four junior international seasons, which included a record four world junior titles. She spent extra years at the junior level due to age rule changes that left her four months too young to be eligible for the Milan Cortina Olympics.

Shimada ranks second in the world by best total score this season behind countrywoman and Olympic bronze medalist Ami Nakai, who also won her two Grand Prix regular season starts.

Shimada and Nakai will go head-to-head at December's Grand Prix Final, which takes the top per discipline from the six-event Grand Prix regular season, where the world's top skaters each compete twice.

Petrokina, the two-time European champion, became the first Estonian to qualify for a Grand Prix Final with her two runner-up finishes this season.

Levito has a slim chance to qualify for her third Grand Prix Final, but must wait to see how the last regular season event in Japan plays out next week. She needs Chiba to finish outside the top five and Yuna Aoki to finish outside the top two.

Earlier in pairs, Germans Annika Hocke and Robert Kunkel overtook short program leaders Lia Pereira and Trennt Michaud of Canada by 1.12 points for their second Grand Prix title after 2023 Skate America.

Americans Audrey Shin and Spencer Howe and Katie McBeath and Balazs Nagy were third and fourth.

Shin and Howe posted the best overall score for a U.S. pair this season, supplanting the total from two-time U.S. champions Alisa Efimova and Misha Mitrofanov from Skate Canada.

McBeath and Nagy, who were third at Saatva Skate America, were third in the short in Finland, then dropped behind Shin and Nagy after the free skate. That hurt their chances of qualifying for the Final, which will be decided by other skaters' finishes at next week's NHK Trophy.
