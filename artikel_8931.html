Anawana Haloba Chosen for Tate Modern’s 2027 Infinities Commission

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Anawana Haloba will become the third artist to create a major new work as part of Tate Modern’s Infinities Commission. to be unveiled in June 2027, the commission will be installed in the Tanks, massive underground spaces that once held the oil that fueled the former power station and are now dedicated to experimental forms of performance, installation, and time-based media.

Haloba, who was born in Zambia in 1978, creates video, performance, and sculptural installations using sound, music, oral tradition, and storytelling, including African opera, ritual, and philosophy. In this way, she explores how history is remembered and reimagined, while often reflecting on the continued impact of colonialism on society. She is based between Zambia and Norway, and has exhibited at the Centre Pompidou in Paris the Smithsonian National Museum of African Art in Washington, D.C., as well as the Venice Biennale. “Anawana Haloba is an exceptional artist whose boundary-pushing, interdisciplinary work brings together sound, storytelling, performance and installation in profoundly affecting ways,” Catherine Wood, Tate Modern’s interim director, said in a press release. “We are delighted to support her in creating a visionary new work for the Tanks.”

Past winners of the commission since its launch in 2025 have included Christelle Oyiri and nora chipaumire.

Additionally, Tate’s panel of judges—chaired by Charmaine Toh, acting head of curatorial at Tate Modern-has selected three emerging artists to receive a £10,000 ($13,195) grant for research and development. They are Saudi Arabian artist Sarah Abu Abdallah, Brazilian artist Manauara Clandestina, and Sudanese artist Ola Hassanain.
