Why Rao’s heated up brand building with a request to slow down meals

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Campbell Company sauce brand reimagines the concept of a limited-time offer by keying in on how many meals people have left with loved ones.

Rao’s Homemade today revealed a four-minute short film that reimagines the concept of a limited-time offer by reminding consumers that the most limited time is the time people spend together, per details shared with Marketing Dive. “Limited-Time Only” was directed by Aqsa Altaf and John X. Carey, the duo known as Aqsa + John X. who were behind Dove’s “Real Beauty Sketches” campaign.

“Limited-Time Only” shares the story of three individuals as they are interviewed about how often they have dinner with family. The heart-tugging video culminates with each being presented a jar of Rao’s sauce labeled with an estimate of how many meals they have left with certain family members before being surprised with a dinner with their loved ones.

The short film is part of a larger “Make Time” campaign created with Goodby Silverstein & Partners that spans premium video, social, influencer, public relations, retail media and owned channels.

In a survey of more than 1,000 individuals and in-depth interviews with 200 people, the brand and agency learned that 84% of Americans wish they shared more meals with loved ones, yet only one-third of families actually sit down together. The campaign unites those findings with product truths about the time-intensive way Rao’s sauce is made, from hand-picked tomatoes and hand-peeled onions and garlic that are simmered for hours.

“It's not just about the food; It's about the experience that creating that food enables, and it's an opportunity to really slow down and savor the food and to savor those that you're sitting around the table with, and to make meal time less of a transaction and more of an experience and an opportunity to connect with others,” said Chris Tutor, vice president of advertising and design at Rao’s parent The Campbell Company.

That experience was captured in a series of ads that feature groups of people enjoying a meal and include taglines like “the best part of the day is sharing it” and “keep long stories long.” The spots are set to live orchestral music and end with a scarpetta, the Italian tradition of using bread to mop up the last of the sauce. The creation of those spots led the team to investigate the larger narrative around meals with loved ones that informed “Limited-Time Only.”

“The long-form [video] allowed us to tell more of that story from the impetus for it to the aha moment to then the celebratory moment, and it really allowed us to blow out that visceral moment around the table into the full story,” Tutor said.

While “Limited-Time Only” does not have a traditional ad length, the creative is in line with a trend around longer-form content that marketers have adopted to tell more engaging stories to consumers. Depending on the platform, the brand will either run the long-form asset or teasers that point to the full video.

“There's been a lot of media attention around this idea, and so we’re leveraging media partners to help tell the story,” Tutor said. “Setting up the context before watching the film will also, we think, drive a lot of eyeballs to it. We're trying to do the math and science of making sure the right piece of content shows up on the right channel at the right time.”

The Campbell Company acquired Rao’s parent Sovos Brands for $2.7 billion deal in March 2024. Rao’s is a billion-dollar brand for Campbell’s and continues to grow, with consumption up 11.3% in fiscal year 2026. To fuel this growth, Rao’s increased media spending and will further increase it in fiscal 2027, executives said on a recent earnings call.

While Rao’s has grown household penetration, it still lags behind sister brand Prego. The “Make Time” campaign is key to the brand’s marketing efforts and represents both a brand building story and an awareness play.

“As people become aware of the brand or if they're already aware of the brand, what does the brand stand for beyond just that exceptional food? It's bringing this notion of slow living to every meal, reminding people that life tastes better when it's savored, and establishing what the brand means on an emotional level, versus just a rational level,” Tutor said.
