Northern Ireland promoted to Nations League top tier with draw in Hungary

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Hungary vs Northern Ireland. UEFA Nations League Group B2.

Northern Ireland are in the Nations League top-flight after topping their group after a draw in Hungary; Barnabas Varga ended Northern Ireland's proud record of not having conceded in this campaign but Justin Devenny equalised

Justin Devenny's superb strike saw Northern Ireland earn promotion to the top tier of the Nations League with a hard-fought 1-1 draw away to closest challengers Hungary in Budapest.

Barnabas Varga ended Northern Ireland's proud record of not having conceded in this campaign when he fired the hosts in front after 35 minutes - a goal that also sent Hungary to the top of the group.

But moments after Isaac Price had hit the post for Michael O'Neill's young side, Devenny smashed the ball into the top left-hand corner in the 65th minute to restore Northern Ireland's one-point advantage as group leaders.

The visitors were on the back foot for much of the first half, with Hungary captain Dominik Szoboszlai hitting the post, but O'Neill, serving a touchline ban in the stands as former captain Steven Davis led things from the touchline, orchestrated key substitutions.

Northern Ireland started with Patrick Kelly in place of the suspended Jamie Donley up front but brought on Josh Magennis at half-time before Kieran Morrison and Brodie Spencer soon followed to help the visitors become a much greater threat going forward.

Magennis' physical presence and the extra pace paid dividends as, just two years after earning promotion from League C, Northern Ireland completed an unbeaten campaign in Group B2 to secure their place at the top table of European football.

Without Donley, the lack of a clear outlet told in a first half that saw Hungary come forward time and again.

There was a big panic after five minutes following an uncharacteristic mistake from Pierce Charles, whose pass went straight to Bendeguez Bolla, a late change to the Hungary side, who whipped in a superb low cross from which skipper Szoboszlai struck the post.

Northern Ireland were pegged back but might have snatched the lead from a Ciaron Brown long throw in the 33rd minute. However, Tom Atcheson did not seem to realise how much space he had as he prodded a weak shot towards his Blackburn team-mate Balazs Toth.

Two minutes later Pierce Charles launched a long free-kick into the box. Brown went down under an Andras Schafer challenge, but as Northern Ireland appealed in vain for a penalty, Toth kicked it long. Trai Hume got his head to the ball but could not get it as far as Pierce Charles, allowing Varga to nip in and score.

O'Neill will have felt the frustration of his ban with his side trailing at half-time, but his changes worked regardless.

Terry Devlin, the matchwinner on Saturday, had already fired into the side-netting before Price's low strike from the edge of the box cannoned back off the post.

Four minutes later they were level. Hume's long throw almost fell for Shea Charles but instead sat up for Devenny to take a touch before thumping an unstoppable strike into the top corner.

As time ticked down, Hungary pushed forward in increasing desperation - with Rajmund Toth firing over late on - but Northern Ireland dug in with the defensive resilience which has been the cornerstone of this campaign.

Northern Ireland boss Michael O'Neill speaking at his post-match press conference:

"It's a great feeling for us. We set out when we came into this level of the competition to first of all try and stay and we knew to be in the top two positions would help us.

"We knew that would help us potentially with the draw for Euro 2028 (qualification), but to come away unbeaten, to finish top, and to give the performance we've given against some very good teams, it's very encouraging.

"We had to dig really deep. We had to make substitutions; we had to try and get freshness. I felt in the first half we looked a little bit leggy, which is natural, but I just felt we needed that freshness at the right points in the game.

"But it was the belief we could get back into the game. We did that, and it was the belief that we could see the game out."

On Deveny's equaliser: "I was surrounded by Hungarian supporters, so I had to be slightly restrained! It was a celebration which was muted, shall we say!"
