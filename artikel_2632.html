Captian Connor Bedard injured in the Chicago Blackhawks’ 6-3 win over the Toronto Maple Leafs

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Chicago Blackhawks star center and captain Connor Bedard suffered what appeared to be a right hand, arm or shoulder injury in the third period of Tuesday's 6-3 win over the Toronto Maple Leafs at the United Center. Coach Jeff Blashill said the 21-year-old will undergo testing on Wednesday.

Bedard was seen postgame without a sling or any additional support on his right arm.

"I hate to comment further until I know more, I think everybody saw it," Blashill said. "I don't know what that means, though."

On a faceoff 54 seconds into the third period, forward Nick Paul's swipe hit Bedard in the right hand. The captain was clearly frustrated, slamming his stick as he skated directly to the locker room. He didn't move his right arm on his way to the back and didn't return to the game.

The captain had a similar-looking injury last season in a road game against the St. Louis Blues. On a last-second faceoff, center Brayden Schenn lifted his stick under Bedard's right arm, causing the Hawks' star to leave the ice in visible pain. That right shoulder injury kept him out for 12 games, and in that stretch the Hawks went 5-6-1.

In July, Bedard injured his left shoulder during offseason practice in Vancouver, British Columbia. He slipped and crashed into the boards, and skated off the ice clutching his left arm. He had surgery on the shoulder, causing him to miss four months of action — including all of training camp and the first six games of this season.

Bedard has dealt with long-term injuries in three of his four NHL seasons since being selected No. 1 in the 2023 draft. He fractured his jaw in 2023-24, his Calder Trophy-winning rookie season, and missed 14 games. He played all 82 games in 2024-25.

The center has scored a team-leading 18 points this season (seven goals, 11 assists), including a second-period goal against the Leafs. In 234 career games, he has scored 221 points (82 goals, 139 assists).

Bedard's absence briefly took the air out of what was the Hawks' best offensive performance of the season. Struggling Maple Leafs goaltender Sergei Bobrovsky couldn't stop the high-octane home team, as he saw a first-period shutout turn into "Chelsea Dagger" on repeat.

Frank Nazar and company rallied the troops and didn't lose momentum after their captain left.

"Seeing a guy like him go down, you know he's probably not coming back for the rest of the game and guys need to step up," Nazar said. "That was a huge thing for all of us … we've got to go out and get the win for him."

The Hawks didn't have any offensive rhythm until the end of the first period. Toronto right wing Easton Cowan scored the period's lone goal at the 3 minute, 22 second mark, rebounding Auston Matthews' wrap-around attempt.

A not-so-memorable first period flowed into most of the next. Nazar continued his recent heater, scoring his third power-play goal in as many games at 13:17 to tie the game.

The end of the second period gave the crowd of 19,988 their money's worth. A calm game turned into chaos, and the teams had three back-to-back shifts that made the United Center crowd erupt.

First, Nazar sent a snipe from the right faceoff circle at 17:54 to give the Hawks a 2-1 lead. That was his fifth goal in three games, as well as his seventh in the last six.

In the second shift, Maple Leafs center Teddy Blueger floated a shot from behind the net, one that Spencer Knight accidentally bounced into his own net. No one, aside from Blashill, seemed to notice that, and a Hawks rush ensued, ending in a crowd-popping Oliver Moore sliding wrist shot. A referee stoppage corrected the mistake, confirming Blueger's goal and a 2-2 tie.

"I thought their shot went in," Blashill said. "I thought it ricocheted and went in originally, so I was looking at it the whole time. Then, we come down and score and I was still, 'oh, I don't know.'"

Bedard's lethal shot 35 seconds later gave the Hawks the lead for good as the period closed with a 3-2 lead.

"To me, that response was the word of the night," Blashill said. "After they scored, we responded, after (Bedard) got hurt, we responded. Obviously when (Bedard) went out, that can be a hard thing on a team and I thought our team handled it great. They really dug in and I thought they played extremely hard."

The third period became the Hawks' shooting practice. Maple Leafs enter John Tavares (11:09) scored a goal in the final 20 minutes, but it was sandwiched between two from Tyler Bertuzzi and an official Moore goal.

"(Bedard's) goal was huge before that, like just getting that one back, especially after you know they get that goal there, it was a swing of emotions," Moore said. "The crowd pop, I felt like it was pretty loud. I was fired up … I think the response was the word tonight from our group."

It was an entertaining affair, so it was easy to push aside the centennial game celebrations: 100 years ago to the night, the Hawks played their inaugural game against the Leafs — then the Toronto St. Patricks — in the Chicago Coliseum.

Team ambassadors including Chris Chelios, Eddie Olczyk and Pat Foley sounded the horn as part of the festivities. The team also named the three members of the 2026-27 Blackhawks Hall of Fame class: Brent Seabrook, Jeremy Roenick and Cliff Koroll.

The Hawks, again, might be without their captain for a period of time, depending on Bedard's assessment Wednesday.

"We've been through some tough moments over the course of this early part of the season, whether it's giving up late leads, giving up four-goal leads, late lead in Boston, getting our asses kicked in Carolina," Blashill said. "We talked about mental toughness this morning and it's a huge part of success in anything in life. You have to stay mentally tough in those moments when it doesn't go the right way. They did a good job and they should be proud of themselves for that."
