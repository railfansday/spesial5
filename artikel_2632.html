Utah Jazz lose heartbreaking game against San Antonio Spurs

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Jazz welcomed the red-hot San Antonio Spurs to the Delta Center on Sunday afternoon, hoping to snap an 11-game losing streak. Unfortunately for Utah, the Spurs entered the game riding an 11-game winning streak of their own. Polar opposites. San Antonio pulled away in the final minutes, winning 110-102.

This is about as drastic of a matchup as you'll see in the NBA, but the Jazz came out hot, looking to build on the momentum they established against another one of the league's best teams, the Houston Rockets, who they narrowly lost to just a few days ago.

The Jazz put up 33 points in the first quarter, led by Mo Bamba, who scored 10. Speaking of Bamba, what an incredible performance he put together. While the Jazz ended up losing, this will forever be remembered as the Mo Bamba game.

Bamba finished with 26 points, five rebounds, four assists and three combined steals and blocks, highlighted by shooting a perfect 6-for-6 from 3-point range. The breakout game took a while for Bamba, but it finally happened.

The Jazz held a 10-point lead with just under two minutes remaining in the first quarter. The second quarter was a response from the Spurs, who eventually built a 10-point lead with nine minutes remaining in the third.

It looked as if the red-hot Spurs were going to run away with their 12th straight win.

Peterson went on to score eight straight points, making it look easy. He also could've easily earned a few trips to the free-throw line, yet he finished with zero attempts. As a team, the Jazz attempted just six free throws compared to San Antonio's 20.

It wasn't Peterson's best night, yet he still managed to demonstrate the talent that could lead him to receiving MVP votes someday. If a bad night for Peterson consists of 17 points, five rebounds, four assists and three combined steals and blocks, then goodness, everyone else is in trouble.

His efficiency was actually solid until the fourth quarter, when he simply couldn't get a shot to fall, going 0-for-5 in the final period. Several of his attempts came up short, leading me to wonder whether all the minutes Peterson has played are starting to wear him down just a little.

Peterson will be just fine, and he doesn't even turn 20 until January.

Keyonte George had another rough night, shooting under 30% from the field for the second time since returning from his ankle sprain. His shot selection was questionable at times, and that was the biggest difference between Peterson and George tonight. Neither was particularly efficient, but Peterson was generating good looks while George often found himself forcing difficult shots.

Isaiah Collier and Kyle Filipowski both received DNPs from head coach Will Hardy.

Josh Green and Josh Okogie both made positive contributions, with Green finishing at plus-4 and Okogie at plus-2. Svi Mykhailiuk, who has received plenty of criticism for his play, deserves some credit tonight after going 2-for-3 from beyond the arc and finishing with a team-high plus-15.

The Jazz are now a league-worst 1-13, having lost 12 consecutive games.

I know the injuries have been crazy. George missed the Miami game, and with a good performance from him in that matchup, Utah probably comes away with a win.

We also haven't seen projected starter Jusuf Nurkić yet, although he was going through some warmups before Sunday's game. Getting Nurkić back will help, but it won't solve everything. Don't expect him to shoot 6-for-6 from deep and score 26 points like Bamba did today.

Nobody can argue that missing two All-Star-caliber players isn't having a massive impact on this Jazz team right now. Jaren Jackson Jr. and Lauri Markkanen are clearly missed.

That being said, there's still at least a month before Markkanen returns and probably a month and a half, minimum, before Jackson is back.

This current group has to figure something out. Otherwise, when the cavalry finally arrives, it might not matter.

And don't forget, Jaxson Hayes also missed today's game.

Will Hardy has to figure things out as soon as possible. At this point, it's not even about making the playoffs or the play-in tournament. It's about showing that he's capable of winning basketball games.

Right now, that's literally something the Jazz cannot do.

If one Dejounte Murray shot goes differently, the Jazz are sitting at 0-14.

I'm not sold on Hardy right now. These weird two-man games he runs, which seemingly always result in a contested jump shot during the final seconds of the shot clock, continue to infuriate and confuse me.

On the other side, Dylan Harper dominated the Jazz in the fourth quarter. Stephon Castle mostly had his way as well, using his strength to overpower Peterson on a few occasions.

Victor Wembanyama was mostly slowed down in the second half. After posting 21 points in the first half, he managed just five in the second. For a player of his stature, you'll take that.

The Jazz wasted an incredible performance from Bamba today. They played well enough to win, but for what feels like the hundredth time this season, Utah was unable to close out a game when it mattered most.

The young Jazz will search for answers Tuesday when they take on a Los Angeles Clippers team with just two wins of its own. Tipoff is scheduled for 9 p.m. MST.
