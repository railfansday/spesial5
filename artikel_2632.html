By the Numbers: Ohio State’s 28-19 win over Northwestern

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

While not the prettiest brand of football we've ever seen in Columbus, Ohio State managed to get back in the win column on Saturday with a victory over Northwestern. The Buckeyes remain a very flawed team, but some incremental improves on both sides of the ball were enough to edge out the Wildcats in the final home game for Ryan Day's team before hosting Michigan in less than two weeks.

This is Ohio State's 28-19 win over Northwestern, by the numbers…

After foolishly going two full quarters without a single target for Jeremiah Smith in the loss to Oregon, Arthur Smith decided it would be best to get the Buckeyes' generational receiver involved early and often against Northwestern.

Smith racked up 17 targets in the game, catching 11 of them for 117 yards and a touchdown. It was clearly a point of emphasis to get the junior receiver the ball whenever possible, especially in the red zone. In fact, most of those six incompletions on balls thrown Smith's way were targets in the end zone that were simply out of bounds or out of reach.

Ohio State finally managed to get Smith his touchdown midway through the fourth quarter. Luke Fahey did a nice job on a Cover 0 blitz to hit Smith over the middle, and the Heisman Trophy frontrunner did the rest.

For Smith, it was also his 17th career game with at least 100 yards receiving, with six of those performances coming this season. Smith has only two games in his career with at least 200 yards receiving, with both of those happening this year against Illinois and Iowa.

Speaking of Fahey, while this was statistically his lowest passing output through his first three starts, passing for only 201 yards against the Wildcats, it was also his best outing as the Buckeyes' starting quarterback.

Fahey finished the game completing 21-of-32 pass attempts with three touchdowns and an interception that was very much not his fault, as Chris Henry Jr. appeared both not to know what route he was running or that the ball was coming his way as it careened off his helmet and into the hands of a Northwestern defender.

In fact, had Henry Jr. not also dropped a wide open deep ball — perhaps the best long pass attempt we've seen from Fahey in his limited action as a Buckeye — those passing numbers could have looked even better.

The freshman is still a bit feast or famine at times. While Fahey had quite a few impressive moments, extending plays and either finding an open receiver or picking up yards with his legs, he also made a handful of poor decisions and under-threw a wide open receiver on more than one occasion.

Ohio State's offense remains a bit limited with Fahey at the helm, but it was a step in the right direction.

It was certainly beneficial to Fahey that Ohio State did not immediately fall behind in this game was able to run the football, as Bo Jackson posted his third 100-yard game in the Buckeyes' last five contests.

The Ohio State rushing attack has actually been pretty good overall for the past several weeks, but the Buckeyes were forced to go to the air once getting behind by multiple scores against both USC and Oregon. On Saturday against Northwestern, they were able to feed Jackson with 20 carries, with which he racked up 114 yards and a touchdown.

In all three games this season where Jackson has gotten at least 20 carries, he has rushed for at least 100 yards, with this being his second-best rushing output of the season behind only his 121-yard game against the Trojans.

Jackson is up to 799 yards on 159 carries with seven touchdowns on the year, good for exactly five yards per carry. It hasn't quite been as efficient as his freshman campaign, where he averaged 6.1 yards per carry, but he has already surpassed his touchdown total (6) and is less than 300 yards shy of his 2025 rushing total having played three less games thus far.

One of the biggest issues for the Ohio State defense this season, especially in its previous two losses, has been the inability to get after the quarterback. The Buckeyes registered one total sack on 78 combined dropbacks against USC and Oregon, and that came on a corner blitz.

Larry Johnson's group finally got things going against Northwestern, tallying a season-high five sacks in the victory. Kenyatta Jackson Jr. led the way with two sacks, the first multi-sack game by any Ohio State defender this season. Qua Russaw, Will Smith Jr. and James Smith all added one sack apiece.

Smith Jr.'s sack in the first quarter broke a streak of five consecutive games where the Buckeyes failed to get a single sack from its defensive linemen. Even after this five-sack bonanza, Ohio State is tied for 100th nationally with only 16 sacks on the season,

For an Ohio State program that used to regularly produce first round NFL Draft talent up front, it has been a stunning drop off this season in terms of the pass rush. Hopefully this performance against the Wildcats is a sign of more production to come.

While the pass rush was much improved, the third down defense was not. Ohio State allowed Northwestern to convert on six of its first 11 third down tries, with many of those being third-and-long.

Matt Patricia has struggled to stop opponents from moving this sticks this season, especially over these last few games. The Buckeyes did a decent job holding the Wildcats in check on first and second down, with Northwestern's facing an average third down distance of 8.5 yards, but were regularly incapable of getting off the field.

Of the seven big plays Ohio State allowed (passes of 15+ yards or runs of 10+ yards), four of them came on third or fourth down. The Buckeyes twice allowed passing plays of 20+ yards on third-and-long, and also gave up a 10-yard run on a third-and-2 late in the second quarter.

To their credit, Ohio State held Northwestern to 0-for-4 on third down in the fourth quarter to help with the overall numbers, but that was a bit of desperation time for Chip Kelly's offense.

While the Buckeyes ultimately won the game, it was a continued concerning trend that may have been taken even greater advantage of against a more talented offense — or even in this game had Northwestern had its starting quarterback.

Jeremiah Smith remains the clear Heisman Trophy favorite, and while Ohio State has fallen off a bit of a cliff in the past month, the star receiver has been a beacon of light for Buckeyes fans.

Smith's 2026 campaign is running right in tune with the most recent full-time wide receiver to win the award.

Through each of their first 10 games in their respective seasons, No. 4 trails former Alabama wide receiver and 2020 Heisman Trophy winner DeVonta Smith by only 10 yards on three fewer catches, with the exact same amount of touchdowns and a better yards-per-reception average.

In 10 games during the COVID-shortened 2020 season, DeVonta Smith caught 83 passes for 1,327 yards and 15 touchdowns — an average of 16 yards per catch and 132.7 yards per game.

In 10 games this season, Jeremiah Smith has caught 80 passes for 1,317 yards and 15 touchdowns — an average of 16.5 yards per catch and 131.7 yards per game.

It is actually kind of wild that the two elite pass-catchers with the last name Smith have nearly identical stats through a 10-game stretch, and of course the former Crimson Tide stud walked away with the hardware as a result.

Jeremiah Smith still has at least two more games to add to his legacy, and with no other player in the country having stepped up in the midst of Ohio State's collapse — not that Smith had anything to do with that — the three-year starter just needs to finish strong to add college football's most prestigious award to his trophy case.
