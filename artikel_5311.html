Bitcoin's $90,000 Comeback Now Unlikely, Novogratz Warns

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Galaxy Digital CEO Mike Novogratz said that the odds of Bitcoin hitting $90,000 by the end of 2026 are slim in light of prevailing macroeconomic headwinds and the growing likelihood that the Federal Reserve may hike interest rates.

During a live event with Anthony Scaramucci at the Digital Assets Forum in New York City, Novogratz was asked if Bitcoin could get back to $90,000 by the end of this year.

"Less likely than it was. You need something to happen," Novogratz said.

His gloomy outlook comes as Bitcoin has struggled to rebound after a devastating drop that saw its price dip as low as $62,000 over the weekend. Bitcoin's price stood at around $64,600 on Nov. 17. The largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization has declined for six consecutive weeks.

A shift in the U.S. monetary policy outlook, namely the prospect of more rate hikes from the Federal Reserve, has been largely to blame for Bitcoin's decline, according to Novogratz.

"At the start of the year, everybody thought the Fed was going to be cutting now," he said.

But high inflation and ongoing supply shocks have forced investors to readjust their expectations.

"Inflation's going up. We've got inflation going up," Novogratz said, warning that inflationary pressures could become increasingly entrenched.

"The Fed's going to hike. At least two more, probably three more," he said.

Novogratz predicted that the Fed would raise rates by 25 basis points in December and then hike again at its next meeting.

He said that higher rates, at least in the near term, tend to weigh on Bitcoin. But the higher cost of government borrowing could eventually provide support for the cryptocurrency.

"Yeah, later," he said in response to the suggestion that higher borrowing costs could be a tailwind for Bitcoin.

"First, you're gonna hike rates. Oh, sell gold, right? You're going to go to the dollar. It's so ingrained in peoples' heads," he said.

"Carry costs going up," he said. Bitcoin doesn't offer the same yield as interest-bearing dollar assets.

"You don't make any interest on Bitcoin in dollars," he said.

In particular, Novogratz said that the massive rise in Treasury yields has been particularly detrimental to the crypto market.

"Literally 40 basis points in like four days of trading. So that's why crypto's down," he said.

He also said that the massive crypto hack could still be weighing on investors' minds, adding to market jitters.

"I think in the back of their minds is the hack," he said.
