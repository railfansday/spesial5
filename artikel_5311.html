A 4-Question Test for Deciding Where AI Belongs in Your Business

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

You're viewing the US website. Looking for the Site edition?

Every business is adopting AI. The businesses that utilize it best are the ones that know where to put it and how to test it.

Opinions expressed by Entrepreneur contributors are their own.

In a 2026 Gartner survey, 87% of customers said companies using generative AI for customer service must provide access to a human agent.

That doesn’t mean customers are against AI. It means businesses need to think carefully about where they use it and where human support still matters.

Every business was built on a dream, and that’s a deeply human thing. When we replace real care with automation in the moments that matter most, we risk chipping away at the very thing that made the business worth building.

So how do you know where AI belongs and which moments need protecting? Start by asking four questions, then test your answers.

Before using AI in any area of your business, ask these four questions:

If a behind-the-scenes task is predictable, simple and low-stakes, let AI handle it. If it’s personal, complex or high-stakes, keep a person in the loop.

I recently heard from an AnswerConnect client, Craig Grove, founder of GRT Realty, who learned the hard way where not to put AI. He partnered with an AI answering service, only to realize his leads were hanging up on the robot. After switching to human phone answering, he grew his business from six real estate agents to 70.

Running the four questions tells you where AI could go. Testing tells you whether it’s actually working.

I’ve learned firsthand that even the best ideas benefit from a little testing before they’re rolled out more widely. I’m naturally someone who likes to take action, particularly when I’m confident something has potential. After a few instances where the direction or idea I had in mind wasn’t as great as I thought, I found myself in a place where I’d made mistakes.

In hindsight, I should have started smaller, talked to more people and got feedback first. I’ve never regretted taking a little extra time to test an idea before jumping in. AI deserves the same approach.

Look at it from two angles: your customers and your team.

Then make testing a habit. Things are constantly changing, including your customers’ expectations. What worked in January can fail by June.

AI is one of the most useful tools business owners have ever had. Used in the right place, it gives your people more time for the work that matters. Used in the wrong place, it erodes the trust your business was built on.

So ask the four questions, test the answers, and keep checking.
