I’m so glad I didn’t give up on the Galaxy Z Fold 8

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

For a phone that heavily tested my patience in the first few weeks, the Galaxy Z Fold 8 has become an all-time favorite.

The amount of hype this phone brought with it at launch was unreal. Even then, I ultimately wound up going with the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra of the two models Samsung released earlier this year. It’s a form factor I was used to, but only looking back now do I realize just how much I was missing out on.

I still believe the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra is an excellent device. But the widely accepted blueprint for a consumer foldable might make a lateral move to what the Galaxy Z Fold 8 offers. That, I wouldn’t mind.

By comparison, the Galaxy Z Fold 8 is tiny. Its front display is squared-up and offers less vertical real estate than any other mainstream device coming out of Samsung. But that’s even more true compared to the lengthy profile the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra has. It’s called a “candy-bar foldable” for a reason.

The entire way I used my phone had to change, and the first few times I used the Galaxy Z Fold 8, I was left with a slightly bad taste in my mouth. I genuinely didn’t enjoy using it because of the amount of friction I was running into – not because the phone caused it, but because of my contradicting usage habits.

I always felt like I was missing out on something with the smaller front display, and I stubbornly tried to use the inner display in its wide orientation for most applications. I considered switching back, but I’m glad I stuck with it.

On the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra, or other standard foldables, I find myself using the inner display as the “main” display more often than not. But the Z Fold 8 gave me enough screen space in its closed state to come away with a good experience.

Since release, the majority of apps I use have been optimized in Android 17, or One UI 9. Social apps work well because that little bit of extra width leaves breathing room for videos to expand and fill the screen. Shopping and productivity apps are similar, and everything seems to fall into place.

I hit a wall with the inner display, too. It took some time for me to realize using the Fold 8 in landscape was genuinely the wrong choice 70% of the time. It sounds weird, but a phone that was designed to open up to a wider inner screen offers a better experience when you rotate it. At that point, you’re getting the best part of the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra, without the other cons.

Obviously, the wide inner display is great for videos and multitasking – not as good as the Ultra. But the inner display in its relative portrait orientation is excellent on hand for apps that need a little more room. It essentially boils down to whether you want a big screen for what you’re using the outer display for – a crazy thought, but not always the user experience.

Now, the Galaxy Z Fold 8’s outer display feels like the new normal to me, and using another phone feels foreign. The inner display has its place, and I automatically rotate it for nearly everything but media.

Even if this phone were just a proof of concept, it was successful. These smaller devices can work with some getting used to.

If given the choice between the design of the Galaxy Z Fold 8 and the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra once again, I’d now choose the smaller, wider Fold 8. Sure, it has a less capable camera loadout. The user experience does more to outweigh my ability to snap telephoto pictures, though.

This is a phone that has performed phenomenally in every major area, including how well it runs apps on Android and in battery life. I’m still getting more than a day’s worth of charge several months into using it. The same can’t be said for the Galaxy Z Fold 7.

It’s possible Samsung is working on a next-gen Galaxy Z Fold 9 with the build of the Fold 8 and the camera power of an “Ultra” model, but there’s nothing concrete. Until then, the Galaxy Z Fold 8 is riding the wave it created.
