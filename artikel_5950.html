Dem governor rips College Republicans for hosting ex-Trump Border chief, provokes fiery response

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

New Jersey Democratic Gov. Mikie Sherrill is pledging state coordination with Rutgers University as the school's College Republicans chapter hosts an event with President Donald Trump's former border commander, Gregory Bovino.

Sherrill said in a post shared across her social media accounts Monday afternoon that she was "deeply disappointed" the school's student Republican group would invite Bovino. The governor added that, "if the club moves forward with hosting Bovino," she would ensure the state works with Rutgers "to protect the safety of everyone and the rights enshrined in our Constitution."

Meanwhile, Sherrill also told any students attending the event that they should challenge the former Trump border commander on his record, including "ask[ing] him why he chose cruelty over humanity and put Americans’ lives at risk in their own communities."

New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill, pictured during a May 2, 2025, interview in New York City while she was a congresswoman, pledged state coordination with Rutgers University ahead of former Border Patrol commander Gregory Bovino's campus speech. (Victor J. Blue/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

"I’m deeply disappointed in the Rutgers Republicans Club’s decision to invite Greg Bovino to speak on campus. Giving him a platform is the wrong decision," Sherrill said in a social media post Monday. "I will ensure the state works with Rutgers to protect the safety of everyone and the rights enshrined in our Constitution. I would hope that those in attendance use that opportunity to ask him why he chose cruelty over humanity and put Americans’ lives at risk in their own communities. They should also ask him what he makes of Americans’ overwhelming rejection of his policies in the midterms."

She continued: "In New Jersey, we believe deeply in the values of this country. Those values do not include dehumanizing and cruel policies, and militias that pledge loyalty to the president instead of the Constitution. Maybe the only question to ask of Bovino is what part of his oath to the Constitution did he miss and how did he get so caught up in the group think of this Administration that he could justify ignoring his obligation to the very people he was meant to serve?"

The invitation for Bovino to speak on Rutgers' campus came from the Rutgers College Republicans Club, a student organization that scheduled the former Border Patrol commander to speak Monday evening at the Douglass Student Center. The group's president, Andrew Joseph, acknowledged Bovino’s reputation in comments to NJ.com.

Former U.S. Border Patrol commander Gregory Bovino, pictured driving agents around Charlotte, N.C., during a November 2025 immigration enforcement operation, fired back at New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill ahead of his scheduled Monday speech at Rutgers University. (Ryan Murphy/Getty Images)

"I can see why he’s very inflammatory. I’m willing to take the risk on that. We are the Rutgers Republicans. We believe in the open market of ideas, " Joseph said. He added that the club initially sought James Fishback, who ran unsuccessfully for the Republican nomination in Florida’s 2026 gubernatorial race, before turning to Bovino.

An anti-ICE counterprotest was also planned for Monday evening, according to online posts by organizers. Opponents of Bovino's appearance at Rutgers also started a petition urging university leadership to bar him from campus.

A Rutgers spokesperson told Fox News Digital that the Rutgers College Republicans are a recognized student organization permitted to host guest lectures and other activities. The university said it is committed to protecting free expression, and campus police will be prepared to provide a safe place for attendees and demonstrators.

"The university also protects the right to peaceful protest. A designated area will be available for those who wish to express opposing views. All participants are expected to follow applicable laws and university policies," a statement from the spokesperson added. "As a public research university, Rutgers is committed to protecting freedom of expression and maintaining a secure campus."

A pedestrian walks across Rutgers University's Newark campus in this April 8, 2013, file photo. A university spokesperson said Rutgers is committed to protecting free expression ahead of Monday evening's scheduled speech by former U.S. Border Patrol commander Gregory Bovino. (Reuters)

Bovino fired back on X in response to Sherrill's comments, accusing the New Jersey governor of prioritizing illegal immigrants over state residents.

"Spoken like a true lib who puts illegals aliens before her own citizens," Bovino, who has reportedly explored a 2028 presidential bid, commented on a version of Sherrill's lengthy post shared on the governor's official X account.

Fox News Digital reached out to Sherrill’s office for clarification on her pledge to work with Rutgers on campus safety and constitutional rights and on her references to "militias," but did not receive a response by publication.
