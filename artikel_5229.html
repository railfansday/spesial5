Bitcoin News: Swiss ETF Debut Meets $7B Options Expiry and US Strategic Reserve Push

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Bitcoin is facing a crowded weekend as bearish options positioning collides with a new Swiss spot ETF and news on the US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve. Nearly $7 billion in Bitcoin options expire November 27, while traders have shifted heavily toward downside protection. Meanwhile, Switzerland’s first spot Bitcoin ETF begins trading, and Washington is moving legislation that could lock government-held BTC away for 20 years.

Bitcoin hasn’t found much footing near $60,000, and Friday’s massive options expiry could make matters worse. But the bearish mood contrasts with developments in institutional access and US crypto policy, creating a market that could swing sharply with sentiment changes.

Around $7 billion in Bitcoin options expire Friday on Deribit, with another $600 million in Ethereum contracts expiring alongside them. Bitcoin options positioning has shifted rather sharply since October, when traders were betting more heavily on a recovery toward $90,000 and $100,000.

The put-call open interest ratio has climbed to 0.81 across all expiries, compared with 0.55 ahead of October’s settlement. Friday’s contracts show a ratio of 0.89, reflecting growing demand for downside protection as Bitcoin trades around $59,000.

Deribit Chief Commercial Officer Jean-David Pequignot said positioning had changed considerably since October, with puts now concentrated at $60,000 and lower. He pointed to major defensive clusters around $60,000, $58,000, $50,000 and $40,000.

Implied volatility has also climbed. Deribit’s Bitcoin DVOL index, which measures expected volatility over the next 30 days, rose to around 55 from 38 in October. Near-term options are now commanding a higher premium than longer-dated contracts, suggesting traders expect turbulence in the coming weeks.

History, however, offers no clear bearish signal from expiry alone. Bitcoin finished higher in the week following seven of the past 12 monthly expiries, lower after four, and unchanged after one. The median absolute move was 2.15%, compared with 3.1% for ordinary seven-day periods.

Bitcoin’s institutional access is expanding even as traders brace for another volatile session. The Bitcoin Fund ETF, ticker BBIT, began trading on the SIX Swiss Exchange today, giving Swiss investors direct Bitcoin exposure through a regulated investment fund.

The fund launched with 350 BTC and charges a 0.19% annual expense ratio. Bitcoin is held in cold storage at Swiss bank AMINA, with the fund available in Swiss francs and US dollars through traditional securities accounts.

The House Financial Services Committee advanced the American Reserve Modernization Act, H.R. 8957, which would establish the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve in federal law. The bill would prohibit reserve Bitcoin from being sold, swapped, auctioned, or otherwise disposed of for at least 20 years. It also requires federal agencies to provide the Treasury with an accounting of their Bitcoin and other digital asset holdings.

The legislation has now been formally reported to the House and placed on the Union Calendar, making it eligible for a floor vote. It has not yet passed Congress, but it is good news for Bitcoin holders nevertheless.

Bitcoin now has a near-term derivatives test and two longer-term developments running in its favor. Friday could expose just how pessimistic the market has become.
