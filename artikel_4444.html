As if the Ford Megazilla crate engine wasn’t already crazy enough at 7.3 liters of displacement, the Megazilla 2.0 engine added a 3.0-liter Whipple supercharger to the equation, unlocking over 1,000 horsepower. But now Ford Racing Parts has taken the recipe even further, showing off a new take on Megazilla 2.0 at the 2026 SEMA Show. Crammed under the hood of a Bronco dubbed Frostwarden is “the most powerful crate engine ever created by an automaker,” boasts Ford, producing over 1,200 hp and over 1,000 lb-ft of torque, and it’s taken a lot of extra engineering to ensure the engine can deliver those numbers reliably.

Ford is quick to point out that creating Megazilla 2.0 wasn’t as simple as bolting a supercharger to Megazilla 1.0, noting that the program required developing a stronger block, a camshaft specifically designed to work with a supercharger, beefier pistons, bigger fuel injectors, a different belt drive and damper, and the Gen 6 version of Whipple’s 3.0-liter blower, with a bright blue painted cover. But an engine sitting on a stand in a SEMA hall struggles to stand out, impressive as it may be, and that’s why the Frostwarden Bronco was built. The idea was based around a Bronco used for search and rescue operations in Arctic conditions, and that means the off-roader needed more than just a glow-up under the hood.

In association with Kincer Chassis in Tennessee, the widened Bronco was given portal gear lifts from 74Weld, custom 18-inch Weld bead-lock wheels wrapped in 42-inch BFGoodrich rubber, steel bumpers (with a winch in front), and custom Bilstein dampers. Driveline hardware was upgraded with help from Dana, and various accessories were added to the roof and tailgate, including traction board holders from RTR and extra lighting from Rigid. With a black-and-mustard theme on the exterior, the cabin was given similar treatment, along with additional carbon fiber highlights. Altogether, it looks as good as an OEM vehicle that might be offered on a dealer floor, and we quite like the dual-exit exhaust tips, but the Frostwarden, like other SEMA concepts, is not destined for series production.

The Frostwarden is basically one big, expensive advertisement for Ford Racing Parts and the scope of customization open to the Blue Oval’s products, but the main focus is undoubtedly this wild new version of the Megazilla supercharged V8. While a production vehicle with such an engine may not be feasible, builds like this help enthusiasts envision what is possible in a Ford, encouraging drag racers, drifters, off-roaders, and more to explore buying a one-size-fits-all engine regardless of their specific motoring application. So what does it cost? Ford has not said, but a naturally aspirated Megazilla 1.0 starts well over $20,000, and a 3.0-liter Whipple supercharger costs anything from $7,000 to $15,000, so Megazilla 2.0 will surely be in the $30k bracket.
