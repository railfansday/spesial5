The Premier League is back after the recent international break and the Sky Sports football writers dissect all the major talking points ahead of another busy weekend's action.

Are Man City in crisis heading into Saturday lunchtime's clash against Fulham, with Enzo Maresca's side having lost their previous three Premier League contests to lose top spot to Arsenal?

Those trio of reverses against Aston Villa, Brighton and, just prior to the international break, Nottingham Forest, mean City have now lost as many times as in their previous 36 league outings.

In fact, if Maresca could pick one side to face to get back to winning ways, it would be Fulham, who City have got the better of in each of their last 17 Premier League encounters, scoring at least twice each time.Rich Morgan

The Black Cats have not travelled well this term with just one win on the road, which came in their narrow escape at Portuguese second-tier side Torreense, but Villa Park's crackling atmosphere has been dampened by the closure of the North Stand this season.

Injuries, storylines and ramifications. Plus, if it is anything like their last meeting at Villa Park, a dramatic 4-3 win for Villa courtesy of Tammy Abraham's injury-time winner, then this is one you will be wanting to follow along in the Sky Sports live blog.Pat Rowe

The managerial merry-go-round is spinning again. Crystal Palace have jumped aboard, handing Thomas Frank the keys to Selhurst Park. New hope. But a new manager doesn't necessarily mean new results.

In fact, recent evidence suggests quite the opposite for teams in trouble.

The famous 'new manager bounce' is one of football's most romanticised theories. A fresh face walks through the door, standards supposedly rise overnight and points start rolling in.

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

Of the last six Premier League teams sitting in the bottom eight to change manager between November and March, five actually saw their points-per-game average drop across the following five matches.

Vitor Pereira at Nottingham Forest. Igor Tudor at Tottenham. Rob Edwards at Wolves. Graham Potter at West Ham. Ivan Juric at Southampton.

It's a fascinating trend that should serve as a warning to Palace supporters expecting Frank to arrive waving a magic wand.

The reality is that struggling teams usually have deep-rooted problems that can't be solved by a few tactical tweaks and an inspirational team talk. Confidence, structure and, ultimately, quality of players tend to outweigh a difference voice in the dugout.

Coventry are next up for Palace and Frank will be desperate to buck the trend.

But history suggests the bounce might be more of a bump.Lewis Jones

Bournemouth couldn't buy a Premier League victory in the opening six weeks or so of the season, but over the last month they have looked like an unstoppable force. Now they can't stop winning.

Since a 3-0 win over Sturm Graz in the Europa League on October 15, the Cherries have won four in a row in the Premier League, three in a row in Europe and once in the Carabao Cup, booking a spot in the quarter-finals.

Eight wins in succession in all competitions; a club record equalled.

It's made even more astonishing when you realise Marco Rose has only been in charge for 16 games. And that it's the club's first ever season in Europe. And that those eight wins came within a period of just 25 days.

With the schedule that lies ahead, the run will naturally come under threat sooner rather than later, but where's the harm in trying to squeeze out one more win to make history?Dan Long

Ipswich have a Julio Enciso problem. Not because the Paraguayan isn't delivering the goods. Quite the opposite. He's become so influential to their attacking output that stopping him is the key to stopping Ipswich.

Bournemouth certainly cracked it before the international break.

Their 2-1 victory over Ipswich was a tactical lesson in how to suffocate a team reliant on one creative spark. Cut off the supply lines into Enciso, deny him the space to weave his magic and ask the rest of the Ipswich attack to come up with the answers.

The numbers were quite staggering. Despite trailing for almost the entire game, Ipswich created just 0.30 worth of expected goals. Even more damning was their inability to register a single touch inside the Bournemouth penalty area in the second half until the 73rd minute, when Jack Clarke scored from the penalty spot.

And that should concern O'Neil, who has seen his team win just one of their last five games in the Premier League.

Ipswich's solid start to the season has been built on some encouraging foundations, but the Premier League is a ruthless environment for teams that become predictable. Opposition analysts will have watched that Bournemouth performance with great interest.

Tottenham are next up and the blueprint is there for them to follow.

O'Neil needs other avenues of attack. Otherwise that promising start could unravel rather quickly.Lewis Jones

Chelsea and Leeds will reignite their six-decade-long rivalry on Saturday with both sides at a crossroads in their seasons.

Inconsistency has dogged each in their pursuit of better things in the opening 10 games of the campaign, but Chelsea's looks particularly apparent after their 1-0 defeat at then-bottom club Sunderland before the international break.

That result should have been as much a surprise for failing to score against their previously porous hosts as it was returning empty-handed, given their own free-scoring start to the campaign.

But not only have they managed to back up one win with another just twice in the league since Xabi Alonso's arrival, they have now failed to score in half of their last six games.

Even with the loss of the influential Joao Pedro from their patented 'JP Morgan' front three, that inconsistency is something Alonso must address quickly.

The decision to start Pedro Neto as a False 9 against Sunderland's low block was an odd one, and played out as such with the Portugal forward failing to register a single touch in the Black Cats box before he was hooked for Danny Welbeck shortly after the hour mark.

The Blues could have been three points off second had they won that game and may still be able to end the weekend in the top four should they do so against Leeds. But Alonso must find that sweet spot quickly before the table turns less forgiving.Ron Walker

Premier League titles are not won in November but Arsenal face Newcastle, live on Sky Sports on Saturday, knowing they could join a handful of sides in the history of the competition to hit the 30-point mark after only 11 games of a campaign.

The only sides to have achieved the feat up until now are Manchester City in 2017/18 and 2011/12, Liverpool in 2019/20 and Chelsea 2005/06. It should be noted that each of them went on to win the title, finishing an average of 11 points clear at the top.

Arsenal, who have won every game this season bar their 3-0 reverse at Brighton in September, have not found it easy at St James' Park in recent years. They needed two late goals to win there last term and lost three straight games there before that.

But the Magpies are at a low ebb as Matthias Jaissle struggles to get his ideas across, with only one win in five Premier League home games this term and seemingly vulnerable. Arsenal, five points clear already, will aim to take advantage and continue their charge.Nick Wright

Are Brighton the only team capable of stopping Arsenal from running away with the Premier League title? It's a question that would have sounded rather bonkers a few months ago. Not anymore.

A return of 22 points from their opening 10 games, backed up by excellent underlying numbers. Arsenal have already been beaten. This is a team playing with the swagger, confidence and control of genuine title contenders. This is no ordinary start from Fabian Hurzeler's side.

And at the heart of it all is Pascal Gross. The man has a football brain that operates on an entirely different frequency to most mere mortals.

Only Erling Haaland and Evanilson have registered more Premier League goal involvements than Gross, who has eight to his name, including four assists. But even those numbers don't do justice to his influence.

Nobody in the Premier League has created more chances (29) or big chances (8).

He sees passes before defenders have even realised there's danger. There are quicker players. More explosive players. But very few who think the game quite like Gross.

And Brighton are reaping the rewards.Lewis Jones

What is one sure-fire way to win over Manchester United fans as a new head coach? Beat Liverpool at Anfield.

That is, of course, easier said than done. But what a statement it would be for Mauricio Pochettino if he could pull it off.

The Argentine's appointment feels like it's been years in the making, yet has also come at a time when his stock is lower than it was when he was previously linked with the Old Trafford hot seat. Had United appointed any other manager of the USMNT, they may have been ridiculed.

However, Pochettino comes with undeniable Premier League pedigree. He just needs to show that he still has that magic touch. His players are expected to be put through their paces with his fitness-first approach. Even for a team with some of the worst running stats in the division, putting a shift in on Merseyside should not be an issue. On the contrary, it's a prerequisite. If they don't, then it's a major mark against Pochettino in just his first few days at the helm.

Perhaps his task shouldn't be to try and win the game. He will probably be forgiven for not. But his team have to put in a performance that epitomises what the Pochettino era is supposed to look like. If he needs an example, look no further than January at Michael Carrick's first two games in charge. Arguably his two best.

If there is a level of work ethic on and off the ball that mirror those games against Manchester City and Arsenal, the new regime will earn supporters among the doubters. Add a win to the equation, and the Stretford End may well be signing 'Poch is at the wheel' come the time to face Brentford six days later.Callum Bishop

There is a feeling that Brentford Football Club, sat eighth in the Premier League, might just be underperforming in the early part of this season.

The Bees have just three wins all season, all at home, with too many draws on the road halting their progress under Keith Andrews.

It may be in part due to Igor Thiago. His 22 goals last season so often proved the difference in tight games for Brentford. But he doesn't look the same confident striker he was last season in front of goal, with just two in 10 so far - and neither being decisive in terms of the result.

They will be without him for the clash with Everton after his red card against Brighton before the international break.
