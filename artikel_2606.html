The relationship between the Jets and their best-ever player in Hellebuyck, who was also the primary goalie for gold medal-winning Team USA in the Winter Olympics, had been strained for a while. Then it broke over the summer when he publicly demanded a trade and stayed away from the club until he got it. Hellebuyck is in the third year of a seven-year contract extension with an $8.5 million average annual value and said it was about family and not money.

The 2024-25 Hart Trophy winner preferred Florida, but the sides couldn't agree on that deal. On Oct. 23 the Jets sent him to New Jersey for Dawson Mercer, Alexander Command, Nick Bjugstad, goalie Nico Daws, a 2029 second-round pick and a 2027 seventh-round pick. That date was important, as it was the day Hellebuyck's suspension by Winnipeg was set to end and he was expected to report. He waived his no-movement clause to facilitate the trade.

Things might have gotten ugly without a deal, but Hellebuyck became simply the latest prominent American NHL player to push for a move from a Canadian club, following the likes of Brady Tkachuk and Quinn Hughes.

Hellebuyck is reviled now in Winnipeg, so it's a massive shame the Devils will let him rest tonight, and boy is the team getting ripped for the decision – it almost makes this a bigger deal now. It certainly wasn't Hellebuyck's decision to sit, as GM Sunny Mehta announced the following after Hellebuyck pitched a shutout Sunday in Toronto.

"Connor Hellebuyck was adamant that he felt a duty to play on Tuesday in Winnipeg," Mehta wrote in a statement. "However, in our estimation, playing in this game would potentially give us a reduction in our expected win probability."
