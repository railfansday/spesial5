Apple blames an internal update for its iCloud and Apple Pay outage

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Apple Pay and iCloud reports spiked on Tuesday, and every service was back by early evening. The Apple outage is the latest in a week of US failures that officials say are not linked.

Greg Joswiak and Vanessa Trigub greet customers at Apple Fifth Avenue in New York in October 2026

Apple said an internal update caused Tuesday’s outage, which hit iCloud, Apple Pay, the App Store and other services. The problem was not a cyberattack, the company said, Reuters reported.

“Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning. We’re working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible,” Apple said in a statement.

By early evening, all systems were working normally again, according to Apple, The New York Times reported.

Users reported problems with Apple Pay and iCloud sign-ins on social media and on Downdetector, which counts user reports. Apple Pay drew about 2,300 reports in the US around 12:20pm ET, Reuters reported. iCloud reports peaked at about 1,800.

Apple confirmed full outages at Apple Music, the App Store and AppleCare, the Associated Press reported. Apple’s retail stores worldwide also reportedly had problems that halted sales and in-person support, according to The Register.

The Apple outage came after a run of failures in the US. Microsoft blamed a dormant July defect for its Microsoft 365 outage last Thursday. On Friday, a router upgrade in Arkansas led to a nationwide ground stop of US flights.

On Monday, Chase blamed a software bug for problems at several US banks. United Airlines also had a system outage that held up departing flights for about an hour.

On Tuesday, the NSA’s classified internal network, NSANet, went offline too. The agency described it as a “temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance”, The Register reported.

On Wednesday, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the US was not under systemic attack, The Guardian reported.

“We have zero evidence or any reason to believe that any of these isolated or individual instances have anything to do with nefarious actors, with cyber-hackers, with criminals, with another nation state, with a malign actor of any kind,” Rubio said.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told Fox Business that the failures came from security upgrades that took longer than planned or did not come back as expected. Fixes for the recent PHP flaws were one example, The Guardian reported. Senator Mark Warner, the vice-chair of the Senate intelligence committee, backed that view.

Security experts told The Register that outages like these may now be routine. AI tools are finding old flaws faster, and companies are racing to patch them. Mike Wiacek, Stairwell’s chief technology officer, founded Google’s Threat Analysis Group. The Register asked him if this is the new normal.

“The brutal truth is yes. Especially in the AI world that we live in,” Wiacek said.
