Buying Vacant Land? Check Zoning, Access & Utilities Before You Sign

Before you sign a contract for vacant land, confirm the zoning allows what you plan, the lot has legal access, and you can afford to bring in power, water, and sewer or a septic system. Make the contract contingent on any check you can’t finish first, and expect a land loan to need a large down payment.

Land is worth what you’re allowed and able to do with it, and most of what decides that doesn’t show when you walk the property.

Say you pay $40,000 for five rural acres to build a house on later. After closing, you learn the soil fails a percolation test, or perc test, which measures how fast soil absorbs water, so the health department won’t approve a standard septic system. And bringing power from the nearest line, a quarter mile away, costs about $25,000, more than half the $40,000 you paid. Either problem can put the house you planned out of reach.

Under federal guidelines, a bank should lend no more than 65% of raw land’s value, so expect to put down 35% or more, or ask the seller to finance the sale.

You’ll also owe property taxes every year with no rent coming in, and what the land sells for later depends on growth, such as new roads and homes, moving toward it.

For any check you can’t finish before signing, add a contingency to the contract with a deadline, so a failed perc test or a missing easement lets you cancel and get your deposit back.
