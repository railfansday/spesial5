United Airlines CEO Says “The Lead That We Have Is Going To Grow” In Thanksgiving Message To Employees

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

United Airlines CEO Scott Kirby used his Thanksgiving message to thank employees working over the holiday and repeat his conviction that United is the best airline in the world. But his most revealing comment concerned what comes next: he expects United to widen its lead over every other airline.

In a video filmed at San Francisco International Airport and shared with Live And Let’s Fly, Kirby acknowledged the employees who will spend the holiday taking care of travelers rather than celebrating with their own families.

He also described hearing regularly from customers about employees who made their journeys easier or did something special for them. That was the most predictable and customary part of a Thanksgiving message, though Kirby could not resist adding a competitive prediction.

Kirby again called United the best airline in the world, then told employees:

“I know we’re going to keep that up in the years to come and the lead that we have is going to grow versus all of the other airlines.”

That is quite a statement, particularly the reference to all other airlines rather than just American or Delta.

I increasingly enjoy flying United. I recently wrote about how Starlink influences which flights I book, and my experiences with the crews and operation have generally been very positive too. Even as a free agent, I find myself drawn back to United. That is a good position for an airline to be in: winning my business because I like the flight it offers rather than because I need another qualifying dollar toward elite status.

But calling United the best airline in the world remains a much broader claim than I would make. My recent Air France A350 business class flight, for example, offered a level of comfort and dining that United would struggle to match consistently.

United also still has a wide variation in its onboard product. An A321neo with Starlink and a comfortable cabin presents a very different experience from an older aircraft with unreliable internet.

We can all understand why Kirby speaks this way to employees. He wants them to take pride in the airline and believe that their work is building something better. But the claim becomes more persuasive when passengers can board any aircraft in the fleet and reasonably expect a similarly good experience. That may be coming, but United is still quite a ways off.

What I appreciated most was Kirby’s emphasis on the individual employees who make travel easier.

A new cabin or faster internet can influence which flight I book. The way an employee treats me often determines how I feel about that flight afterward and critically to airlines, whether I would recommend it to others (part of the NPS score)

That becomes especially apparent when something goes wrong. A delay is frustrating, but an empathetic employee can preserve a lot of goodwill. Being ignored or brushed aside can turn an ordinary disruption into a reason to book another airline next time, which is a frequent issue for me on American Airlines.

Kirby says customers tell him about those positive encounters, including on the flight he took that day. It also connects with his recent message after the FAA ground stop, when he highlighted a crew helping a mother traveling with three young children. These are the experiences passengers remember, sometimes long after they have forgotten what they ate or which aircraft operated the flight.

Happy Thanksgiving everyone! At United, we’re about connecting people and uniting the world and that’s never more true than over the Thanksgiving holiday when we’re about to have our two busiest days of the travel period. I know many of you are going be working hard at the holidays and missing time with your friends and family so I want to say thank you for doing that and really taking care of our customer during this important time.

As we enter the Thanksgiving holiday, I’m thankful for an awful lot of things in my personal life but in my professional life, I’m thankful for all that you have done in the last several years to make United Airlines the best airline in the world. I fly a lot, including on my flight today, customers constantly are telling me stories about what a flight attendant or a pilot or a gate agent has done to ease their travel to do something special for them that makes a memory for them. So thank you for all you’ve done in the past several years to vault us to a leadership position.

Thank you for everything you’re doing right now during the holidays to take care of customers and I know we’re going to keep that up in the years to come and the lead that we have is going to grow versus all of the other airlines. Thank you for really doing things to not just make United Airlines the best in the world but to set a new standard for what it means to be a great airline in global aviation. Happy Thanksgiving and thank you.

Kirby is thankful for the employees who he believes have made United the best airline in the world, and he expects its lead over competitors to grow.

I share his enthusiasm for much of what United is doing, though I am not ready to award it that title. The more compelling part of his message was the recognition that employees earn customer loyalty through the way they treat people.

United has given me plenty of reasons to return lately. Continuing to deliver those good experiences will do far more to persuade me than any declaration that it is already the best. To everyone working at the airport or onboard today, thank you. I hope passengers remember that the person helping them get home may be spending the holiday away from their own family.
