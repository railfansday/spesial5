The Highest Paid International Flight Attendants' Ranks & Their Average 2026 Salaries

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Flight attendant pay in 2026 varies widely by rank, seniority, region, and carrier, but the top of the ladder is where the real money is. At major US airlines, entry-level cabin crew typically start around $25–$40 per hour, while senior international flight attendants can push base earnings toward $90,000 and beyond once premiums, per diems, and international layover pay are factored in. Seniority is key at all airlines, which usually give an annual increase in salary and more pay as crew move up the ranks.

Pay structure can also vary considerably depending on region as well as the base salary. New hire flight attendants may earn as little as $2,000 per month, compared to a senior crew member with an international roster and a full schedule, who may earn as much as $10,000 per month. Different regions also offer different benefits, some better than others.

According to IndexBox, flight attendant salaries at American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, and United Airlines vary widely depending on seniority and hours worked. While the most senior cabin crew members can earn a base salary of nearly $80,000 a year, with some making six figures, their pay does not match that of pilots at the same carriers.

This is above average in the rest of the world and probably due to the fact that most US airlines are heavily unionized, the exception being Delta. Flight attendants in the US earn a base hourly rate plus a per diem when away from their base airport, and can earn more for holidays, night flights, or specialized positions such as language of destination speakers.

Most are guaranteed a monthly minimum of paid hours. The average international flight attendant in the US makes $60,016 according to Comparably. They also say that most international flight attendants earn between $37,260 and $45,760 annually.

It is also worth noting that flight attendants in the US start their careers on reserve or standby, so they do not have a planned schedule. Whereas at other airlines worldwide, flight attendants have a scheduled roster every month, produced every four to six weeks. This may also include standby days and available days, and these can change the original schedule.

Cabin crew in Europe typically earn between €1,500 and €3,500 per month or €20,000–€50,000 annually ($22,800 to $57,000). Pay varies heavily by carrier type, base location, and seniority. Total compensation is built from a base salary, hourly flight/sector pay, onboard sales commissions, and tax-free per diems. Low-cost airlines typically pay between €17,000–€25,000 ($19,300 to $28,500) annually.

Legacy carriers pay €26,000–€45,000 ($29,700 to $51,300), but this can be as high as €60,000 ($68,000) in Switzerland, due to the high cost of living. As of July 8, 2026, according to ERI Salary/Expert, the average cabin crew gross salary in the United Kingdom is £42,475 ($56,800) or an equivalent hourly rate of £20,42 ($27,31). In addition, cabin crew earn an average bonus of £1,144 ($1,530).

An entry-level cabin crew member with one to three years of experience earns an average salary of £30,807 ($42,000). At the higher end of the pay range, a senior cabin crew with more than eight years of experience earns an average salary of £48,830 ($65,200). Per diems are only paid on nights spent away from base and can be either a set rate or based on the cost of living in the city visited.

ERI also says that the average flight attendant gross salary in Australia is AU$98,578 ($69,000) or an equivalent hourly rate of AU$47.39 ($33). In addition, flight attendants earn an average bonus of AU$2,655 ($1,858). An entry-level flight attendant with up to three years of experience earns an average salary of AU$71,612 ($50,120).

At the higher end of the pay range, a senior-level flight attendant with over eight years of experience earns an average salary of AU$114,123 ($79,800). According to information on SkillUp, a flight attendant at Singapore Airlines earns S$2,800 – S$3,500 ($2,169 to $2,712) per month as junior cabin crew and up to S$8,000 ($6,200) as a senior purser with over eight years of experience.

Do flight attendants make more money on private jets than on airliners?

The highest-earning rank is the purser, or lead flight attendant or senior cabin crew member. They have the responsibility for the whole cabin or part of it on some widebody aircraft. In the US, they command a premium of roughly $1 to $7.50 extra per hour on top of the top seniority rate. This lifts the total annual compensation at carriers like Delta and United to $120,000–$150,000 for the most senior crew flying full schedules.

A senior crew member on a widebody aircraft operating international flights from the US will usually include the hourly rate, boarding pay, international per diem, incentive pay, and profit share. According to FutureFlightAttendant, a senior flight attendant may earn between $84,000 and $120,000+ per year, achievable through senior lineholders flying high-time international trips, holiday premiums, and profit-sharing bonuses.

In the UK, a senior cabin crew member earns £40,000 to £55,000 annually, including nightly per diem and hourly flying pay. In Europe, senior cabin crew at low-cost carriers such as easyJet or Ryanair would typically earn €28,000 to €45,000 depending on seniority, and salary is supplemented by commission on onboard sales. Total compensation varies significantly around the world because of pay structure and whether the airline has a union representing them.

A landmark change happened in a May 2026 contract, in which United's nearly 30,000 flight attendants ratified a five-year deal averaging a 31% wage increase plus new boarding pay. The starting pay is now $37.10 per flight hour with the opportunity to increase pay annually through to the 13th year, reaching up to $100.13 per flight hour.

There is also an increase every year, paid on the date of joining for each year of seniority. In addition to hourly pay, there is per diem pay for every hour spent away from base. This is currently paid at $2.97 per hour on domestic routes and $3.54 per hour on international routes. Furthermore, flight attendants also receive an additional $2.00 per flight hour reserve override when on reserve duty.

Boarding pay is paid at between 29% and 41% of the hourly rate depending on aircraft type and is based on the scheduled boarding time and per flight sector. The TA2 contract also included retroactive pay and new terms and conditions, after previous contracts were rejected.

By the end of the contract in 2030, the highest hourly rate for crew will be over $100 an hour, overtaking Delta's highest rate of $86.32. This makes United flight attendants some of the highest paid in the aviation industry. No doubt in the future, this will be the benchmark contract that improves pay and terms and conditions.

United flight attendants could soon become the highest-paid in the industry.

Gulf carriers such as Emirates and Qatar Airways generally offer lower gross salaries (roughly $40,000–$70,000 at the top for Emirates), but their tax-free packages, free housing, and transport can narrow the real-world gap. Their salaries include layover allowances and hourly flying pay.

They also receive 30 days' leave per year and an annual leave ticket, generous discounts locally and on travel, as well as on dry cleaning of uniforms. Medical care and life insurance are also included. Elsewhere, One Mile At A Time notes that Emirates' salary breakdown is clear. New-hire flight attendants have a basic salary of 4,835 AED ($1,316) per month and receive flying pay of 66.30 AED ($18) per hour.

This is based on an average of 80-100 hours per month of flying. The airline states that starting flight attendants earn an average of $2,940 per month, which comes out to over $35,000 per year. Cabin service directors or pursers earn between AED 18,000 and AED 20,500 ($4,900 to $5,580) according to Global Aviation Files.

At Qatar Airways, cabin crew can expect to earn between QAR 10,000 and 15,500 per month, including allowances, which equates to between $2,800 and $4,300. Cabin Service Directors earn a basic salary of QAR 5,500 at the base rate and up to QAR 18,500 according to TheseGoldWings.

The difference between flight attendant salaries varies dramatically across regions. This is down to different pay structures and how pay is made up. This can be very variable between crew members, as it depends on routes flown, commission earned (where applicable), and hours operated.

Per diems cover any expenses down-route and are tax-free. In the US, pay is very much more structured and highly regulated. Overall, flight attendant pay is at its highest in the US, mostly due to strong union support. In Asia, flight attendant pay is quite low due to the lower cost of living in general.

In Europe, salaries are quite low, with some of the lowest being with low-cost airlines, which do not have layovers but do receive higher commission for buy-on-board products. Legacy carriers pay more and operate internationally. In the Middle East, the starting salary is considered low, but they have numerous benefits, such as free accommodation and transport.

Flight attendants can earn extra by speaking the language of the destination or moving up ranks to purser or to senior crewmember. International flight attendants or cabin crew earn more as they receive more per diem and are away from home more. Seniority is very important for a number of reasons, including salary increases and requested trips. It is also crucial for moving up ranks or changing bases or fleets, as seniority is given priority.
