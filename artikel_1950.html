Scotland half-centurion issues Pocognoli warning and reveals why misfiring striker should keep starting berth

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Sebastien Pocognoli will need time to fully implement his ideas in Scotland’s new look squad, but has been warned fans will want to see signs of progress in the upcoming Nations League games this week.

Pocognoli, 39, had a mixed start to his tenure as Scotland’s new head coach, losing to both Switzerland and Slovenia at Hampden Park, drawing away to Slovenia and winning just one of their opening Nations League games with a 2-0 away win against North Macedonia.

Handing international debuts to no less than five players in his maiden camp as Scotland head coach, the Belgian boss has shown no fear when it comes to introducing fresh faces to his pack. However, he came under scrutiny for his willingness to adopt a back three formation, with many pundits questioning whether the team has the personnel to adapt.

Curious to see what he has learned from last month’s games, 50-cap former Scotland international Alan Hutton thinks it will take time for the team to adapt to his methods, but would like to see improved performances against North Macedonia and Switzerland over the next week as the team return to action.

“Taking the four games Pocognoli has had, he's just come into the job, so a lot of information has been passed down to the players,” said Hutton. “Even with an extended break, which he would have appreciated, it's very difficult to get concepts across in such a short period of time. He'll be looking to develop that and reinforce his tactical ideas. Hopefully, they digest it better going into these two games.

“They beat North Macedonia away, so you'd hope he can get his first win at home to put that to bed. Switzerland away is going to be difficult, but you want to see progression in how they understand his philosophy on the pitch. It's still very early in his tenure and he's going to be given time to get it right. He's refreshed the squad with younger faces, and they need time to adapt as well, so while it has to improve, it requires patience.”

One of the main points of contention has been Scotland’s inability to finish chances that come their way. With Kieron Bowie the only striker to get on the scoresheet last month, questions have been asked about who should get the nod to lead the forward line in the upcoming games. Hutton believes one man has the number nine shirt - for now, at least.

“I know it's been an issue,” Hutton told OLBG. “Even going back to when I was playing, and that's no slight on anyone who played, but we lacked a guy who guarantees goals. Everything has been workmanlike, working hard and holding the ball up, but you need someone to put the ball in the back of the net.

“I think [Oli} McBurnie is the go-to guy at this moment. I really like Kieron Bowie and Robbie Ure, they're young and they will get better in the leagues they're in, improving technically and in their hold-up play. McBurnie is the main man right now, though the others will be breathing down his neck.”
