Traditions connect generations, carrying the cultural and moral values of ancestors to new generations, giving them a sense of identity and belonging. Some of these traditions are so lovely that they are adopted more widely. The beautiful old Christmas tradition of Christmas wheat is one of them.

The sowing of Christmas wheat is a tradition across Europe from France to Ukraine and the Balkans. Early in December, people “plant” wheatgrass seeds in bowls indoors early in December and let the shoots grow until Christmas. Initially the growing wheat seeds were considered a blessing for the coming harvest. But beyond that, the green shoots have come to symbolize fertility, new life, and eternal renewal.

You don’t have to live in the South of France or other parts of Europe to practice this lovely tradition. We’ll tell you all about the custom of growing wheatgrass indoors and how you can make it your own this Christmas.

All around the globe, cultural heritage shapes how people celebrate, and also how they express beauty. The lovely tradition of planting Christmas wheat is still very much a part of Christmas festivities in Europe, thought to have been inherited from the Romans.

It is practiced in the Province area of France, as well as Croatia, Ukraine, and parts of Italy and Portugal, each region claiming it as their own. In Province, it is called Saint-Barbe wheat (le blé de la Sainte-Barbe) because the day of planting, December 4, is the feast day of Saint Barbara. By Christmas day, the seeds have grown fresh green shoots that become decorations on the Christmas table.

In Province, this age-old tradition involves sprouting grains of wheat in three separate bowls. The ritual is said to ensure prosperity and happiness for the coming year, a promise memorialized in the old Provençal adage, “when wheat goes well, everything goes well.”

In Croatia, the origins of this tradition are still related to religion, but they are different. Growing wheatgrass is related to the story of the holy family fleeing Herod’s soldiers. It is said that the family found a farmer sowing wheat. The family requested that the farmer tell the Herod’s soldiers that the family had already passed through.

The wheat grew remarkably fast that year so by the time the soldiers came, there was a fine crop. Then, the farmer told them that the family passed through while he was sowing his crop. Because the wheat was so tall, the soldiers assumed that the family had passed by long ago and abandoned their quest. As such, wheat seeds are sown in December to represent light found amid the winter's darkness.

Well, if you would like to incorporate this ancient tradition into your own holiday celebrations, we’ll walk you through the steps. There are three: gather what you need, “plant” the seeds, then arrange the centerpiece.

The most important "ingredient" you will require to grow your own Christmas wheat are the wheat grains themselves. Traditionally, you use hard red winter wheat berries (sold as "wheatgrass seeds”) although it is possible to use alternatives like lentils or chickpeas instead.

The presentation also varies from region to region. In Province, for Saint Barbe’s wheat, they use three small bowls or saucers and damp cotton wool. The three containers have religious significance, representing the Holy Trinity. In Croatia, you can use any container, but you still need to line it with either cotton wool or soil as a growing medium. A more practical alternative is to use a growing tray filled with potting soil with a solid tray beneath it to catch water.

Soak the grains overnight in water. After that, place them evenly on the growing medium - like the bed of damp cotton wool or the potting soil. If you are using three bowls, spread it evenly between them. If you are using a tray, spread the seeds there.

Place the seeded container near a bright window at a mild temperature. Spray it with water and cover with plastic paper or a plastic bag. Remove this plastic when the seeds begin to germinate.

Mist the cotton with water daily to keep it moist. This allows the shoots to germinate and grow straight and green.

How you choose to arrange the centerpiece depends entirely on your own preferences and traditions. By Christmas Eve, the little shoots are green and straight,

In Croatia, the wheat sprouts are often decorated with the Croatian tricolour – a red-white-blue ribbon. This adds patriotic symbolism. The Croatians sometimes add a red candle or even a red apple in the centre of the container to add more holiday cheer.

In Province, the French often toe up the sprouted wheat with red and yellow or red and green ribbons. The wheat display becomes the centerpiece for their traditional Christmas Eve feast. After Christmas, the French place the pots in or near the family crèche (aka the nativity scene) alongside handmade clay saint images. Finally, the wheat can be planted in open ground or burned in the fireplace for protection.

Remember, this is a lovely tradition, but while wheatgrass might look like a microgreen it should not be used as one. Yes, it has many incredible health properties (it is said to prevent some types of cancer, fight infection and improve cognitive function), but it is not edible.

That being said, you can juice it and drink its juice, which is full of vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants. Sounds like a great tipple after a big holiday feast, eh?
