Lethal Trent Alexander-Arnold sends Tuchel a statement in England victory

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Thomas Tuchel has never been inclined to place much faith in the most famous right-back at his disposal, Trent Alexander-Arnold, once of Liverpool, now of Real Madrid, and – back in an England shirt – still capable of changing games in the blink of an eye.

England always looked likely to defeat a beleaguered Czech Republic team down to 10 men with just 25 minutes played, but it still required one man to conjure the moment of inspiration in this Nations League tie. This was the first cap in 15 months for the right-back now known simply as “Trent” in an England shirt – as well as his Real Madrid jersey – rather than the six syllables of his surname. With the game level at half-time, two minutes into the new half, he twice won the ball before landing a cross on the head of Anthony Gordon for the first goal.

Will the Alexander-Arnold with England experiment survive? One feels that Tuchel will need a little more to convince him to abandon whatever reservations he has about the Liverpudlian. A week short of his 28th birthday, Alexander-Arnold was winning just his 35th cap. His Real Madrid team-mate Jude Bellingham, a second-half substitute, was earning his 58th cap less than two months after his 23rd birthday. Bellingham was on as captain in place of the man he replaced, Harry Kane, scorer of England’s second. Bellingham’s international career has taken a much less rocky path than that of his Real team-mate.

Having missed the World Cup finals this summer, and had little good fortune around previous tournaments, one wonders if Euro 2028 might be the watershed moment for Alexander-Arnold. Either way, against opponents who sit back, he is lethal when it is time for the big delivery. Alexander-Arnold may not have the breadth of England experience that some of his peers have accumulated, but he does have plenty of experience of big games.

“I make that run time and time again,” Gordon said of his anticipation of the Alexander-Arnold cross, “but only a set number of players have that ability, that technique and the bravery to risk a mistake to play that pass. And that’s why I have been dying to play with him [Alexander-Arnold]. He’s been on his own journey but has trained really well this week. He should have played more. Sorry – he would have loved to have played more.”

A Freudian slip from the Barcelona winger, another Liverpudlian in Spain, who himself has barely put a foot wrong for England of late. He has three goals in three games for England, including the World Cup semi-final against Argentina. This was an England team that Tuchel fine-tuned, first to give a rest to Bellingham and in other quarters, too. Lewis Hall started at left-back. Trevoh Chalobah won his third England cap at centre-back. Alex Scott, of Bournemouth, was given his debut off the bench in the second half and Rio Ngumoha won the second cap of his England career, just one month after his 18th birthday.

As for Kane, his 87th goal for England now puts him within two goals of Robert Lewandowski, among four greats in the seven ahead of Kane on the all-time international goalscorers’ list. By the end of this international break the England captain should have equalled Peter Shilton’s record 125 caps. It has been an astonishing career even if it ends tomorrow. Following the missed penalty against Spain, Kane was again the man in the right place at the right time when Morgan Rogers’s shot was blocked in the second half.

“Strange to get an early red card,” Tuchel said. “Not the best for the team and spectators. It’s an awkward one, but we kept going, got a win and that’s what counts.” Not quite a vintage Czech Republic team, either, and when their best player of the opening stages, the attacking midfielder Pavel Sulc, of Lyon in Ligue 1, was dismissed after 25 minutes, it certainly did not become any easier for the home team. Sulc, the Czech player of the year in 2025, had mistimed a tackle on Elliot Anderson, which on a VAR review, the Turkish referee Umut Meler decided was worthy of an upgrade from yellow to red. It was high on the right calf of the England midfielder.

England had looked once more a little open to the counter-attack in the early stages, with Sulc one notable runner. This was not an opponent of the quality of Spain and Tuchel’s players were able to recover the situation. When they lose the ball in the opposition’s half, England tend to look vulnerable.

Even before the red card Rogers had hit a post. Myles Lewis-Skelly, playing in midfield, was booked for a foul on Lukas Serv and was pursued thereafter by the Czech players hoping to get an upgrade on the yellow card. He was substituted at half-time, with Eberechi Eze on in his place.

“I spoke to Ant [Gordon] at half-time,” Alexander-Arnold said later to ITV, “and I said if I’m getting in those spaces make sure you get to the back post. You don’t expect to get it that fast, but it did and it worked.”

Two minutes after the break, Alexander-Arnold suddenly burst into life. He won the ball back in his own area and then drove forward in search of the return. When that did not come, he raced back to win the ball again and unleashed one of those right-footed outswingers to the back post, where Gordon guided in the header. “Really proud to score that,” Gordon said, “because I can’t tell you the number of hours I’ve spent practising headers.”

Kane scored the second just a minute before he was replaced and Bellingham took the captain’s armband and the role as a No 9. One day someone will have to step into those goalscorer’s boots on a permanent basis and try to replace Kane – although it may be a while yet.

Thomas Tuchel has backed Jude Bellingham as a future England captain after giving him the armband in the victory over the Czech Republic.

In the absence of Declan Rice, who was vice-captain at the World Cup, Bellingham assumed the role when he came on for Harry Kane, who is now in his 10th season as England captain and also scored in the 2-0 win in Prague.

Tuchel, who needs to check on Bellingham ahead of Saturday’s game against Croatia after a “niggle,” has praised the midfielder’s attitude during the extended international break one year after he was back from an injury but left out of the squad.

“If you are the captain at the end of these matches, you can be the permanent captain,” Tuchel said when asked whether Bellingham could eventually lead the team. “He was the captain against New Zealand before the World Cup. It is always a reward and always an honour.

“I think he loves to be with us at the moment. That’s what I feel, and we had this small talk today about his role. We felt in our hearts or our stomachs to say ‘let’s go again, let’s start again’ and also our heads told us maybe 30 minutes is enough for this match because he hasn’t played in this quick turnaround through this season. He has done it before, but not in the season, so we didn’t want to be the first ones to try that.

“But even in this little encounter, you can see that he’s confident and happy to help from the bench. So it’s a reward. It’s good. He had a little niggle at the end of the match, so hopefully it’s nothing serious, and he’s available for Croatia.”

Nico O’Reilly is a doubt for the Croatia match in Rijeka and there are also concerns over Ezri Konsa, who was on the bench but not fit enough to play.

Trent Alexander-Arnold provided an assist for Anthony Gordon in the Real Madrid full-back’s first cap in 15 months. Gordon, meanwhile, has now scored in the first two Nations League games of the campaign and is staking his case to be an indispensable player for Tuchel.

“He’s getting there. He’s defending his shirt, he’s defending his position, an excellent performance again after his performance against Spain,” Tuchel said.

“He was very decisive today and had an excellent goal. It was very, very difficult, but the technique and finishing was excellent. He scored against Spain, so he has decisive actions and he’s defending his shirt and his position. He does it on the highest level and he’s a very important player.”

Man City used ‘sham’ sponsorships in £830m cheating scheme

Manchester City arranged “sham” sponsorships in a near-£1bn plot to flout Premier League rules, an independent commission has found.

It was confirmed on Tuesday that City had been found guilty of all charges related to “serious breaches” of the league’s financial rules, with the commission also upholding all but one in relation to their failure to co-operate with an investigation spanning over three years.

The commission found that, of £949.94m City claimed in commercial income over a nine-year period, only £119.25m was legitimate, with the remaining £830.69m illicitly paid by their Abu Dhabi owners.
