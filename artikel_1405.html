Scotland’s Pocognoli era starts with encouraging signs but familiar issues in front of goal

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Scotland’s ramped-up second-half performance offered some cause for optimism as Sébastien Pocognoli’s tenure as head coach began with a goalless draw with Slovenia in Ljubljana.

The 39-year-old Belgian head coach, appointed in August as Steve Clarke’s successor, refreshed the side for the Nations League Group B1 opener in the Stozice Stadium, but they were fortunate on a couple of occasions at least not to fall behind before rallying before the break.

Scotland had been insipid and uninspiring for most of the first half but after the interval they played with pace and purpose as Slovenia captain and goalkeeper Jan Oblak became a key figure, making saves from Oli McBurnie and Kieron Bowie.

“It took us a bit of getting used to in the first half, the way the gaffer wants to play,” McBurnie told BBC Scotland.

“We created enough chances to win the game. I should have scored. I was asked to play a different position and I enjoyed it. I’d rather be getting in the positions than not getting chances.

“Second half was a lot more of what the manager wanted from us. It was waves of pressure and the only thing missing was a goal or two.”

However, the goal that would have made the former Union Saint-Gilloise and Monaco manager’s debut as an international boss more memorable ultimately failed to arrive.

In this extended international break, the Scots will host Switzerland at Hampden on Tuesday and then travel to North Macedonia before taking on Slovenia in Glasgow, with the Tartan Army hoping to see further improvement – and goals.

On paper, it looks like Sebastien Pocognoli has opted for a five-back formation, with Swansea City defender Stephen Welsh making his Scotland debut as one of the centre backs.

Captain Andy Robertson and Brentford’s Aaron Hickey will operate as the wing-backs, with Billy Gilmour, Lewis Ferguson and John McGinn forming a midfield three.

A new-look attack is spearheaded by the returning Oli McBurnie and 22-year-old Robbie Ure, who like Welsh, will also make his Scotland debut.

Angus Gunn remains his country’s first-choice goalkeeper.

Good afternoon and welcome to our live coverage of this UEFA Nations League B, Group 1 match as Slovenia host Scotland at Stozice Stadium in Ljubljana.

Scotland qualified for their first World Cup in 28 years this summer, but they exited the tournament with a whimper after failing to finish as one of the best third-place group sides. Steve Clarke’s men beat tournament debutants Haiti 1-0 in their opening match, before falling to defeats without scoring against Morocco and Brazil.

Following the World Cup, Clarke stepped down as Scotland’s head coach, ending a seven-year tenure, and was replaced by Belgian manager Sebastien Pocognoli. The former Monaco boss will take charge of his first game in his new role today and will be itching to make an encouraging start in a new era for Scottish football.

Over this extended international break, Pocognoli’s side will also face Switzerland and North Macedonia, as well as a return fixture against Slovenia in Scotland on October 6 to round off a busy period.

While Scotland took part in the World Cup, Slovenia were reflecting on their hugely disappointing qualification performance, where they finished third in their group behind Switzerland and Kosovo without winning and netting just three goals across six matches.

Manchester United striker Benjamin Sesko – Slovenia’s star man and most well-known player – withdrew from Bostjan Cesar’s squad earlier this week after the recurrence of a shin injury caused the 23-year-old to completely miss the 1-1 draw against Fulham last Sunday.

Scotland have a few notable absences, with Scott McTominay, Che Adams and Lawrence Shankland all unavailable, but Billy Gilmour returns to the fold after missing the World Cup through injury, Oli McBurnie has been recalled and Sevilla striker Robbie Ure has received his first senior call-up.

Kick-off is at 14:00, with team news coming shortly.
