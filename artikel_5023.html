Bitcoin faces a liquidity drain as Anthropic IPO unlocks millions

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Anthropic’s IPO unlocks test Arthur Hayes’s $1.245 trillion AI selling scenario and its potential impact on Bitcoin liquidity.

Anthropic’s planned IPO brings a real risk of insider selling, but its share-release schedule does not make a drain on Bitcoin liquidity inevitable.

Arthur Hayes’s Nov. 5 essay puts falling AI share prices at the start of a broader credit squeeze. He argues that insider sales will weaken stocks, make borrowing more expensive or unavailable, curb bank-balance-sheet growth, and reduce the dollar liquidity supporting crypto.

Hayes estimated 83% dilution by June 2027, arguing that roughly $1.245 trillion in new capital would be needed to absorb potential selling at a hypothetical $1.5 trillion IPO valuation.

Anthropic provides a concrete test of the first link, since its offering creates new shares, while subsequent releases also permit existing holders to sell.

Those changes affect supply differently, with different consequences for company fundraising and investors’ ownership.

Anthropic’s Nov. 10 announcement proposes 825 million Class A shares, with 801,351,354 offered by the company and 23,648,646 by existing stockholders, and the AI firm receives no proceeds from the existing holders’ sales.

The preliminary price range is $70 to $78, with an option for underwriters to purchase another 123.75 million shares.

New company shares increase the outstanding share count and raise money for Anthropic. Secondary shares already exist, and selling them transfers ownership and pays the selling holder. A later unlock removes a restriction on selling without issuing another share.

The preliminary resale disclosures allow up to approximately 316 million additional shares to become eligible for sale from the first trading day. Further quantities include 299 million at each of 27 and 48 days after the US prospectus date, and 349 million at each of 62 and 76 days.

Non-founder personnel and certain other holders have restrictions ending at the earlier first-quarter results trigger or 180-day endpoint. Co-founders and certain larger holders follow an earlier third-quarter results trigger or 365-day endpoint, with staged early-release exceptions.

Earnings-linked triggers use public results announcements and subsequent trading-day intervals, and representative waivers and other restrictions add further conditions.

Multiplying $1.5 trillion by 83% yields $1.245 trillion, but that's a theoretical value. That interpretation depends on what holders sell, the prices buyers pay, and demand for the stock

The Federal Reserve’s Nov. 13 H.8 release shows seasonally adjusted bank credit rising from $20.02 trillion on Oct. 28 to $20.08 trillion on Nov. 4, a $58 billion increase.

Published after Hayes’s essay, the release shows aggregate bank credit expanding over the later reported week. Future stress remains possible, and these totals provide no AI-specific lending breakdown or causal evidence about Bitcoin.

Hayes’s warning remains a scenario worth tracking: insider selling, weaker share prices, higher financing costs and reduced lending would support successive links in his argument.

Anthropic’s conditional IPO timetable brings the selling-risk test closer, while the transmission from share supply to Bitcoin liquidity still requires evidence.
