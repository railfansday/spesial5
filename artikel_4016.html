Talking Point: As Xbox Celebrates Its 25th Anniversary, What Are Your Hopes For The Next 25 Years?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Maybe we'll be looking back on this in 2051!

The celebrations for Xbox's official 25th anniversary roll on! We've finally arrived at November 15th, which marks the exact date when the original Xbox launched all the way back in 2001. How time flies!

Of course, this is a great chance to reflect on everything that's happened over the past 25 years, which we've been doing in various articles throughout the month (and indeed the year), but it's also an opportunity to think about the next 25 years and where Xbox as a brand might be headed in the future.

I think the cynical view, and perhaps the logical view, is that Xbox won't exist by the time we get to 2051. Let's not forget that PlayStation was still in its infancy 25 years ago, SEGA had just got out of the console business after being a major player for so long, and Nintendo hadn't even released the GameCube in North America until November 18th of 2001.

In other words, 25 years is a very long time, and we've seen many ups and downs from Xbox throughout its history. The glory days of Halo, Xbox Live and the Xbox 360 were followed up by a horribly botched Xbox One launch, and the Xbox Series era has been summed up as everything from extremely promising to extremely disappointing, now hovering somewhere in the 'cautiously optimistic' territory for some Xbox fans since Asha Sharma took the helm earlier in 2026.

While console sales might be down, and the Xbox CEO keeps talking about a need to "return to growth", there's every reason to believe Xbox might still be a powerhouse in 25 years' time. Microsoft's backing is obviously a big aspect of that — assuming the brand never gets sold off — but so is the mountain of incredible first-party IP (Minecraft, Call of Duty, World of Warcraft, etc.) that should continue to rake in money over the next decade at least, coupled with the ability to innovate in the hardware space and eventually win back some market share with handhelds and consoles.

Realistically, the answer to all of this is that we don't have an answer - it's impossible to predict a future that's still 25 years out, especially when Xbox's mission statement appears to have changed on an almost yearly basis during the 2020s. There's no doubt that Xbox will face many challenges in the 25 years that are ahead and it'll be forced to adapt and innovate along the way, but I wouldn't be surprised if we're still talking about it in 2051!

For what it's worth, here's what Xbox boss Asha Sharma said on this topic a few days ago:

"While modern games demonstrate the heights of human craft in ways unimaginable just decades ago, the cost, resources, and risk required to create them have grown just as dramatically. As a result, too many ideas never become games, and too many games fall short of what their creators imagined. For decades, this has been the unavoidable tradeoff of ambition. We must change this. [..] A single person will soon be able to wield capabilities that once required entire nations. A small team will be able to build worlds that today demand hundreds of developers and hundreds of millions of dollars. And our largest studios will be able to pursue ambitions as far beyond today’s games as Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 is from its first entry in 1982."

"I believe the future of gaming will look more like its beginning, when a small team with a big idea could change how the world plays. Our responsibility is to deliver great games, unmistakable hardware, and give more people the chance to make something the world has never seen."
