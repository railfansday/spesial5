Dear Tunisair: How About A Refund For My Downgrade, Please?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

I’d like to think that I’m pretty understanding of airlines, and how they can fall short. The airline industry is incredibly complex and low margin, and sometimes consumers have unrealistically high expectations, while not acknowledging the real cost and challenges of running an airline. Buying a ticket on a flight is more like buying a ticket on Amtrak than buying a night at a Four Seasons, and you should adjust expectations accordingly.

At the same time, sometimes there are situations where airlines act in a way that feels to me a little like theft. Readers often share these stories with me, and I write about them when I can. Now I’d like to share my own story…

Several weeks ago, I booked a ticket on Tunisair in business class from Tunis (TUN) to Marseille (MRS). A few days before the flight I received an email from Tunisair informing me that I had been downgraded. As it turns out, it was because my flight was swapped to a wet leased plane, in an all-economy layout.

The email informed me of this change without actually acknowledging it was a downgrade. Instead, the email was in French, and it translated to “we are writing to inform you that your flight reservation has undergone changes.” And the only reference to the downgrade was that the fare class only showed as being “Y,” which obviously isn’t business class.

There was zero other proactive communication from the airline about the incident, and I flew the flight in economy. The airline of course acted as if nothing happened, and there was no mention of how to request downgrade compensation, or anything like that.

After the flight, I submitted a request with customer service, asking for a refund for the fare difference. Making that request was a challenge in and of itself, because the customer service form is in French, and is not at all intuitive.

But anyway, it has been weeks, and I haven’t heard back. I’m a bit conflicted here. I try not to abuse credit card disputes, but you typically only have 60 days to file a dispute. So am I at the point where I should just file a dispute, or how would one handle that?

As I said above, I try to be pretty understanding of airlines, and recognize how many different scenarios there are. Just in terms of doing business ethically, I do feel like there should be a proactive refund offer if the airline is informing you in advance that you won’t be getting the product that you paid for. I mean, if you can inform me that I’ve been downgraded, you can also process some sort of a refund.

We’ve become desensitized to this kind of service level with airlines, but I’m trying to come up with a comparison. I guess it would be like buying premium tickets to a sporting event, being informed in advance that you’ll be given worse seats, and then customer service just not responding to you for weeks on end.

If you managed to take my money, surely you can also manage to give it back if you can’t give me the thing I paid for, no?

Tunisair informed me days before a flight that I had been downgraded (as it turns out, due to an aircraft swap), though there was no offer a refund for a fare difference, or anything. Weeks ago I sent an email to customer service asking for a refund for the fare difference, but I haven’t heard back.

It just strikes me as really unethical to take someone’s money for an experience you’re not going to offer, and then not have a way to get a refund in a reasonable timeframe. I know airlines hate when people file credit card disputes, but when you’re approaching the 60-day mark with no response, what is one supposed to do?

Am I the only one who thinks it’s ridiculous to proactively downgrade a customer, but then make getting a refund such a runaround?
