The Blueair 511i Max Includes More Automation Features Than Any Other Air Purifier I’ve Seen

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Smaller and sleeker than most air purifiers, it doesn’t skimp on functionality.

It’s certainly true that good things come in small packages, but with a lot of home appliances, small packages mean accepting a trade-off. In exchange for the convenience associated with a less bulky physical profile, you give up some functionality—cooling rate with air conditioners, capacity with a vacuum, and smart features when it comes to many smaller space heaters and air purifiers.

The Blueair 511i Max, however, is an air purifier that gives you everything you could expect or want in terms of features and functionality but in a relatively small package. While it’s not going to set speed records, especially if you’re running it in a more open space, this is an excellent air purifier for a home office or bedroom. I say this because it runs quietly, looks sharp, and comes with scheduling, air quality monitoring, and more.

Out of the box, the 511i Max is basically ready to run. With WiFi or Bluetooth, you can connect the purifier to the Blueair app. Here, you can turn the purifier on and off, change speeds, and adjust the brightness on the display. This is also where you can utilize the purifier’s bevy of smart features.

First up is the app’s scheduling hub, where you can customize schedules along a number of criteria. You can select the start and stop time, and if you want a particular schedule, you can set it to run daily, on weekdays, or on weekends. You can also select what mode or speed you want, too.

“Welcome Home” mode is one of the features that comes with this purifier that I haven’t seen before. Basically what it does is identify when your phone is within a certain radius of your home and tell the purifier to turn on or off once you reach that threshold. The idea is that it will run roughly 10 to 15 minutes before you get home or after you leave. I wouldn’t say it’s an essential feature, but it’s a thoughtful foray into smart scheduling.

Outside of scheduling, the 511i Max features a filter health report that will alert you when it’s getting close to time to replace it. There are also real-time air quality reports that give you more detailed information than is available on the purifier’s display.

One of my pet peeves with air purifiers is simply giving you a quality reading that’s color-based (for example, blue for excellent or red for poor air). That was my biggest complaint with the Levoit Vital 200S-P, the purifier we named Best for Pets.

That’s what the 511i Max does on the display, but you can get a numeric reading in the app. I find it helpful to know if I’m on the right or wrong side of moderate air, and I appreciate that I can do that with the Blueair, even if I can’t by looking at the unit itself.

When I first looked at the 511i Max, I was struck by how aesthetically pleasing it is. The gray cover around the base of the unit makes it feel less like a home appliance, but it’s actually just another working part of the purifier.

This is the first of four stages of filtration—the pre-filter. It captures larger pieces of debris, which helps extend the life of the filter inside. Over time, you will need to clean the pre-filter regularly, or you run the risk of diminishing its visual appeal. What looks nice unused starts to feel like an eyesore if it’s covered in dust and pet fur.

This also means you need to handle the base more gently than you might other air purifiers—it’s just a fabric cover and the filter. You can feel the ribs of the filter when you handle this portion, so if you’re not gentle with it, you can dent or otherwise damage the filter, and replacements aren’t cheap.

After the pre-filter, there is another filtration layer before you reach the primary filter. It’s proprietary to Blueair, and it uses electrostatic filtering technology to eliminate some particles. This also helps extend the life of the primary filter, and it allows the purifier’s fan to run quieter and more efficiently.

The final stage of filtering is activated carbon, which is meant for odors and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). I ran the 511i Max in my finished basement over the course of several days. My cat’s litter is down there, and there was no noticeable odor while the purifier ran. I also used it upstairs in my house after some kitchen smells moved through the house. The air read as “moderate,” and 20 minutes later, it was back to “excellent.”

Also worth noting is the purifier’s cylindrical design, which, in addition to looking nice, means it pulls in air from 360 degrees. This gives you more options for placement and should allow the purifier to clean more efficiently.

This isn’t the purifier you’ll want if you need to clear air quickly or in a very large room or open space—for that, check out the Coway Airmega 400, our choice for Best for Large Rooms. But for a single room, this is one of the more impressive options out there thanks primarily to its design and collection of smart features.
