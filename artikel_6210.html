Bookings for the Gulf’s peak quarter are on hold until after the U.S. vote. With airlines still staying away, some operators don’t expect a real recovery until late 2027.

Gulf hotels are entering their peak season well behind last year. Leisure travelers are holding off on bookings until after Tuesday’s U.S. midterm elections, and some are canceling outright.

U.S. President Donald Trump has said he would not launch an attack on Iran before the November 3 vote. Iran has not attacked the UAE since May, but the country bore the brunt of Iranian missile and drone strikes in March. Operators say guests now fear a new round of attacks once the election passes.

“Is Q4 as good as what we thought in August? No, it’s not,” said Saurabh Tiwari, vice president for the Middle East, Maldives and Sri Lanka at hotel group IHCL, which operates the Taj brand. “Leisure travelers are waiting and watching until after the U.S. midterm elections. They are thinking twice before booking their tickets, also influenced by the high airfares.”

Gulf hotels are heading into their most important quarter well behind last year, with operators and analysts pointing to travelers delaying or canceling trips ahead of the November 3 U.S. midterm elections and fears of renewed strikes on the region afterward. UAE hotel occupancy fell to 67.6% in September from 79.9% a year earlier, according to CoStar, and Dubai's international visitors dropped 44% to 6.97 million in the first eight months of the year, according to Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism. Short-term rentals have deteriorated fastest: AirDNA data shows November forward bookings down 37.5% year over year as of October 30, with December and January roughly 26% behind, a sharp decline from late September when the gap was 6% to 8%. Airline capacity is a compounding problem, with KLM, Singapore Airlines, Wizz Air, Cathay Pacific, United, Air Canada, Finnair, Luxair, and Virgin Atlantic suspending Dubai flights into 2027. Business travel is also being pulled, with ExxonMobil and Chevron reversing executive travel plans to Adipec in the UAE over security concerns, Reuters reported. Staycations, long-stay guests, and visitors from India, Russia, the UK, and Eastern Europe are cushioning the quarter, but one analyst estimates international tourists generate 70% of Gulf hotel revenue. Dubai has responded with incentives including the new invitation-only Dubai Access Club, which offers a five-year multi-entry visa, a SIM card, and AED 2,000 ($545) in spending credit.
