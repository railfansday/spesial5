How to Spend Christmas in Paris

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Strasbourg may be France’s holiday capital, claiming the country’s oldest Marché de Noël, but there’s no denying Christmas in Paris is a showstopper. The city shines brighter each year with thousands of lights illuminating its famous squares and landmarks like the Champs-Elysées, dubbed the “world’s most beautiful avenue.” Holiday cheer seeps into each of the 20 arrondissements, decorated with larger-than-life garlands, forests of bauble-strewn fir trees, sculptural light displays, and wooden chalets doling out vin chaud (mulled wine) and holiday specialties like foie gras—best spread on subtly sweet pain d’épices (gingerbread).

Oyster stands are as much a fixture of the holidays in Paris as Christmas markets—which happen to be one of the best places to try them. Fête the season in true French fashion with a movable feast dining and drinking at some of the city’s most iconic institutions, from Left Bank cafés where famous writers and artists once gathered to the Palace hotels huddled around Place de la Concorde and Place Vendôme. And when you’re ready to indulge in Paris’s renowned retail therapy, swing by one of the grand magasins (department stores), which rank among the best in the world for their holiday window displays—New York City included—executed with the same style and precision as a couturier. The city may not be blanketed in snow, but the holiday spirit is as strong here as on the slopes in the French Alps—and even has the cuisine and chalets to prove it. If you’re planning to spend Christmas in Paris, you’ll find plenty of ways to celebrate, but these nine are some of the best things to do and see during the holidays in the City of Light.

This winter, Oscar winner Cate Blanchett will lead the lighting ceremony of the 2 million LED lights illuminating the Champs-Elysées, running all the way up to the Arc de Triomphe. Sparkling each evening from 5:00 pm to 1:00 am, the most iconic avenue in Paris and its 400 balloon glass garland-adorned trees are coordinating with another famous landmark—the Eiffel Tower—twinkling in unison on the hour. Every quarter of an hour, the Champs-Elysées will put on a light show set to Vivaldi’s Four Seasons, and on December 24 and 25, the avenue will glow in Christmas-themed green and red. As part of the New Year’s Eve countdown to midnight under the Arc de Triomphe, when a music-themed fireworks show will ring in 2027, the lights will shine in a tricolor blue, white and red display running down the avenue. Making a grand return after a decade-long pause, a Christmas market will run from the Place de la Concorde to the Arc de Triomphe with more than 200 stalls and an ice rink near the Pavillon Ledoyen.

Dining with a view takes on a whole new meaning as you settle in by the panoramic windows on Ducasse sur Seine, the sole electric boat cruising the river. The floating restaurant is as gastronomic an experience as anything on land thanks to Alain Ducasse, the namesake chef behind the modern Parisian cuisine who claims a record-setting number of coveted Michelin stars. With chef Jean-Philippe Berens and the team prepping everything fresh on board, you’ll have prime views of the Seine while dining in style on Christmas Eve during the six-course menu, which features everything from silky foie gras royale to raw scallops topped with gelée and caviar—plus a cheese course with 18-month-matured truffled Comté.

The ambiance is equally as elegant at Ducasse Baccarat, where the chef’s cuisine is presented in an opulent space dripping in chandeliers and tableware designed by the French fine crystal creator. One of the few spots open daily during the holiday season, the restaurant ups the ante on Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve with themed degustation menus.

France’s Capital of Christmas, Strasbourg, is home to the country’s oldest Marché de Noël, but Paris is dotted with more than a dozen markets and Christmas villages—the largest being La Magie de Noël in the Tuileries Garden, stretching from the Louvre to Place de la Concorde, which remains open until early January. There, you’ll discover hundreds of wooden cabins selling small gifts and seasonal treats; a selection of stands run by French artisans, from glassblowers to woodworkers; and attractions like a small ice rink and carnival rides—the Ferris wheel being the highlight, especially during the Eiffel Tower’s hourly light show.

On the Square René Viviani, directly across from Notre Dame, you’ll find an intimate market with some 30 exhibitors selling regional food, hot chocolate, and handmade crafts. On the Left Bank, the Saint-Germain-des-Prés Marché de Noël unfolds along the namesake boulevard, next to the church, with wooden chalets and gourmet stands serving up everything from caviar and foie gras to Savoyard favorite tartiflette (a reblochon cheese and potato gratin) and sweet and savory pretzels. Unfolding in the courtyard of the Gare de l’Est train station, the historic starting point for the first Orient Express, the Marché de Noël des Producteurs Alsaciens is dedicated to Alsatian delicacies, with stands displaying regional specialties like kougelhopf, a fluted brioche cake filled with rum-soaked raisins and almonds.

More than the decorated avenues and elaborate window displays, it is the Bûche de Noël (yule log cakes that may or may not be shaped like logs) that takes the lead as the ultimate holiday essential during Christmas in Paris. While most cakes are prepared and sold for take-away, there are a few spots to linger over tea and an individual slice of the indulgent Christmas cake, including Fouquet’s Paris, where the red, velvety chocolate and roasted corn mousse dessert is designed after the arabesques in the hotel’s historic salons and staircases. At Butterfly Pâtisserie, the Hôtel de Crillon’s boutique and tearoom, pastry chef Matthieu Carlin’s exquisite selection of seasonal pastries, including his Bolivian dark chocolate mousse Bûche de Noël, shaped like a submarine out of Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea—a tribute to the hotel’s Jules Verne-inspired holiday theme—can be enjoyed on-site with a cup of tea or a thick hot chocolate, or taken to-go. On the Place de la Concorde, snack on praline hazelnut and pistachio and orange blossom-flavored French toast from the hotel’s garland-strewn, striped winter chalet, or sip espresso martinis at Terrasse Concorde’s pop-up bar, which has been redecorated to mimic the most exclusive of ski resorts with French cashmere house Kujten’s custom-crafted cushions and blankets.

At Ritz Paris, craft your own yule log during a baking class, or settle in by the fireplace of the opulent, champagne-colored Salon Marie-Louise for a Christmas tea, where you’ll indulge in spruced-up takes on the afternoon tradition, including truffle cream finger sandwiches, lobster focaccia, and Crêpes Suzettes prepped à la minute. Next door, Ritz Paris Le Comptoir’s carousel-shaped Bûche de Noël has been deemed the city’s most stunning this season—and with only 65 available, it’s a gift unlike any other. Nearby at Mondovi Luxury Suites, chefs come to you for in-suite macaron-making sessions and mixology workshops—and with the “world’s best bartender” Colin Field behind the Le 7 Cocktail Bar, you’ll be learning from the best. On the Left Bank, the palace hotel Mandarin Oriental Lutetia Paris is serving a lavish Sunday brunch buffet, winter-themed afternoon tea with hot chocolate, and pastry chef Nicolas Guercio’s wreath-shaped yule log filled with layers of vanilla mousse, pear confit, and housemade pecan praline.

Dating back to the mid-13th century, Sainte-Chapelle was once a royal chapel constructed to house the precious Crown of Thorns relic. Today, it’s admired for a different reason: the 15 stained-glass windows—comprised of 1,113 panels—are some of the most impressive in France, let alone all of Europe. Combine sightseeing with an experience only taking place during the holiday season listening to the music of Antonio Vivaldi or Johann Strauss during a classical concert within the chapel’s Gothic nave.

Paris’s grand magasins (department stores) offer more than retail therapy. Their elaborate holiday window displays executed in couture-like fashion have earned them the title of best in the world—and that even includes New York City. There’s a theme for every taste: The Samaritaine has been designed to feel like you’re taking a Christmas stroll through a snowy Paris, with winter lights and snowflakes crowning the grand magasin’s facade near the Seine. Shop at the indoor Christmas market and rest assured your presents will sit pretty under the tree this year thanks to the help of the pros at the dedicated wrapping studio, who can swath gifts in plush paper and custom-made ribbons. Over at Printemps Haussmann, behind the Opéra Garnier, the department store decorated its windows around the theme of “A Well-Knit Christmas," commissioning artisan-crafted crochet pieces; 160 animated characters (including felt cats and mice) chasing a ball of wool; and costumes by the store’s seamstress, Valérie Cuter.

At Galeries Lafayette Haussmann, holiday cheer doesn't end at the sidewalk, where French fashion designer Jean-Charles de Castelbajac themed the vibrant window displays around tales of angels and Matisse-inspired shapes. The Christmas tree and holiday displays beneath the store's Art Nouveau dome are an annual event, comparable to New York City’s Rockefeller Center tree lighting. Always vibrant and impressive, visitors who venture into the atrium of the shop this season will find a tree towering 16 meters high—but this year, the wing-shaped structure follows the animated storybook theme with an angel hovering overhead and a soundtrack by French DJ Vladimir Cauchemar. From the 30-foot-long Glasswalk footbridge, on the third floor of the store, you can catch an even better view of the “tree”—and snap a few photos. Hotels like SO/ Paris, on the banks of the Seine, are also dressing up for the occasion with paper artist Maud Vantours’ sculptural Nutcracker creation, which you can admire while sipping seasonal coffee or an espresso martini along with a slice of the hotel’s Bûche de Noël.

Nothing warms up locals and visitors like hot chocolate. For the most well-known, head to Angelina on the rue de Rivoli for its thick, decadent Chocolat Chaud Africain that will leave you wondering if they just melted an entire chocolate bar into your cup. If the line snakes around the block, take it à emporter. Near the Opéra Garnier, chocolatier Jade Genin (daughter of legendary Jacques Genin) is famous for her signature Concorde obelisk-shaped chocolate pyramidions, but her modernized take on hot chocolate—entirely plant-based with homemade almond milk—is as much a draw during the holidays. In Saint-Germain-des-Prés, Les Deux Magots’s scarlet banquettes may have been where the literary set like Jean-Paul Sartre once held court, but the cafe is also one of the best on the Left Bank for hot chocolate—where it’s prepared the traditional way, with melted Valrhona blended with warm milk and poured out of a classic carafe.

While you can spike your hot chocolate with Irish whiskey and Chantilly cream, the city’s leading cocktail bars are taking some of the holiday season’s signature flavors and mixing them into creations like Beurre, Little Red Door’s cocktail version of fresh brioche, with crisp apple, butter, vodka, and yes—bread. At The Cambridge Public House, classic Irish coffee is given a French twist with calvados and silky salted maple cream, while Hôtel Lancaster partnered with historic French tea house Damman Frères for a gingerbread punch blended with rooibos de Noël tea and sherry, served alongside a tonka bean and gingerbread pastry.

Moulin Rouge’s lavish costumes adorned with sequins and rhinestones feel all the more festive when you’re dressed up yourself on Christmas night for a gourmet feast followed by the iconic Féerie revue show. At the Musée des Arts Forains (Fairground Museum), its annual Christmas extravaganza, Le Festival du Merveilleux, runs from December 26, 2026, to January 3, 2027. The makeshift Christmas village transforms into a light-strewn theater with magicians, aerial silk dancers, musicians, puppeteers, and tap dancers putting on dreamlike performances around the Belle Époque funfairs and gardens.

On Christmas Eve and Christmas Day, get your culture fix while sightseeing, sipping champagne, and listening to an orchestra perform carols and violin concertos during a classical Christmas show in one of the world’s most famous landmarks: the Eiffel Tower. And for the full winter wonderland experience, Disneyland Park’s characters are adorned in holiday attire during Christmas parades, musical performances by The Arendelle Carollers in the new World of Frozen, and meet-and-greet moments with Santa hat-clad favorites like Stitch.

Embrace the festive feel of slope-side specialties like fondue at Chalet des Îles, a historic wooden Swiss chalet reconstructed by Napoleon III for Eugénie that hovers on an island in the Bois de Boulogne. Top Chef contestant and co-founder of Paris’s Ambos restaurant, Pierre Chomet, is behind this season’s fireside feast of Alsatian-style tartes, flambées, and creamy Mont d’Or cheese, while Park Hyatt Paris-Vendôme’s timber-clad Chalet du Park has popped up for the season in the Palace hotel’s terrace, serving winter menus designed by Michelin-starred chef Jean-François Rouquette.

At Hôtel Le Grand Mazarin, join a chocolate expert for small-group tastings, or take a seat for Christmas Eve dinner at candlelit restaurant Boubalé for a Mediterranean take on traditional plates like toasted challah with foie gras and fig and scallops wrapped in kadaif (shredded phyllo) paired with a holiday-themed DJ set. A dinner worth dressing up for, two Michelin-starred Le Clarence, off of the Champs-Elysées, has designed a feast worthy of royalty complete with the finest of ingredients, from dive-caught scallops to lobster and caviar—plus a collector-worthy wine pairing that fêtes the season in true French fashion.
