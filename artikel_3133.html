Annaly Sold $757 Million of New Stock Last Quarter. It Says the Sales Added Value Instead of Diluting It.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

As long as Annaly Capital sells stock at a price above its book value, it adds value.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Annaly Capital Management ›

Annaly Capital (NLY -0.59%) has a long history of selling shares, just like most real estate investment trusts (REITs). However, its business model is a bit more complicated than a traditional property-owning REIT, since it is a mortgage REIT. It is much easier to assess the benefit when Annaly sells additional stock. And the $757 million of shares it sold in the third quarter were likely, as the company claimed, accretive. Here's why.

It is hard to assign a value to a property-owning REIT because you have to make judgment calls about very large assets, like offices and apartment buildings, that don't trade frequently. It is totally different with an mREIT like Annaly. Annaly owns a portfolio of mortgage securities (created by pooling mortgages together into a bond-like asset). Mortgage securities trade regularly.

In fact, Annaly actually tells investors what it is worth every quarter when it reports its book value per share. At the end of the third quarter, the mortgage REIT reported a book value per share of $17.86. The stock traded above that value through most of the quarter. In fact, it didn't dip sustainably below that value until after the company reported earnings. This is important.

So long as Annaly sells stock at a price above its book value, it is adding value for existing shareholders. Essentially, the investors buying the new shares are paying a premium. Every penny paid above the book value allows the company to buy more mortgage securities than it would if the new investors bought at book value. In other words, given the stock price through the third quarter and the book value at the end of the quarter, there's no reason to doubt that Annaly's stock sales were a benefit to existing shareholders.

To be fair, book value changes daily, along with the value of the mortgage securities the mREIT owns. The stock price is now below the book value at the end of the third quarter. That, however, isn't how you should assess the benefit of the third-quarter stock sale. At the time of the sale, Annaly's stock price appeared to be above its book value. That suggests the sale was accretive to existing shareholders, who should view the move positively. How beneficial it was for the investors who paid a premium to buy Annaly's shares above book value is a different story altogether.
