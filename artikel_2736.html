Wolves 142, Nets 116: Defense Optional, Vibes Exceptional

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Well, that was fun. If beatdowns and revenge against a former coworker are your thing, tonight's matchup was for you.

If they aren't, well, this was still a fun one.

On Monday night, the Minnesota Timberwolves dispatched the Brooklyn Nets 142-116. Minnesota did what it was supposed to do against one of the worst teams in the league as the Wolves look to put the harrowing memories of the Chicago Bulls game to rest.

Defense was clearly optional in this one, as both teams shot greater than 50% from the floor and over 40% from deep. Fortunately for us, what little defensive presence that did exist in this game came from the Timberwolves. Rudy Gobert and Joan Beringer had four steals apiece, and Beringer capped off his defensive showing with a whopping four blocks. All in all, this was one of the most complete showings that the Timberwolves have had all season.

With Thanksgiving around the corner, I'd like to take this opportunity to remind everyone that we, as Wolves fans, should be thankful that this team is good enough to consistently and authoritatively put away bad teams. Being at home for eight straight games surely helps team morale and rest, though this was a game that should have been, and was, definitively won. Not much room to poke holes in this performance. Even Rudy Gobert had a nice game!

While the Nets made a couple of runs in the second and fourth quarters to keep the game mildly interesting, there was never a sense of danger for the Wolves. They handled their business and look forward to the next matchup. Onward!

Here are a few of my takeaways from the game:

I'm not sure Anthony Edwards's prophetic line about how "we got Jaden McDaniels" is wholly accurate anymore. We might just have Kevin Durant. What a scintillating performance.

Already averaging career highs across all but one counting stat (blocks), McDaniels looks like there are no weak points left in his game. His midrange shots feel automatic. His threes are going in at a consistent clip amidst a steady volume of just over four attempts per game. Tonight, he made all four of his shots from distance, shot 12/16 from the floor as a whole, and set a new career high in points with 33 big ones.

Cap his performance off with 10 boards and six assists, and we might be looking at one of the best games of his career.

Most Wolves coverage this year has focused its attention on LaMelo Ball, Jonathan Kuminga, and the zany foibles of Gobert. In a sense, the silence around McDaniels speaks to his consistency and the greater expectations that the fanbase has for him. He's coming to work and doing his job every single night.

To say McDaniels might be the most important member of the team in the absence of Edwards is not an understatement. It certainly doesn't hurt that Jaden looks like Kevin Durant when he's playing, too. Silky. Smooth.

Ball has been in a statistical slump over the last few games – that much can't be argued. Tonight's performance was not much better in that regard, as he shot 6/16 from the floor, 2/10 from three, and only compiled three assists. At face value, this looks like a pedestrian performance.

Yet, I remain convinced that he is one of the most impactful players that the Timberwolves have on their roster. The highlights and deep shots do not necessarily tell the entire story of who he is as a player and how he impacts the game. What I'm saying is: we need to look beyond the box score to measure his effectiveness.

Yeah, I'm going to make a comparison to Rudy Gobert.

In the same vein as how Gobert's defensive impact cannot always be measured by things that show up on the stat sheet, Ball has that same effect on the offensive end. He is a master at manipulating defenses and drawing attention. His court vision is elite enough to make the right decision almost every time, and more often than not, he is putting his teammates in position to either make a play for themselves or to make a play for someone else.

Of course, hockey assists and other things like that are not counted in basketball. This is, however, why the Charlotte Hornets had such a crazy offensive rating last season when Ball stayed healthy. When he's on the court, everybody around him is better. It's not a coincidence. He carries all of the benefits of a heliocentric player with so few of the risks.

The shots will drop eventually. They may not be consistent, but that's okay! You live and die by those moments until Edwards returns. Until then, continue to appreciate everything that Ball has been offering this team in terms of flow, pace, and vision.

The sentiment of "they're all bad defenders" is no longer reserved for the Denver Nuggets. These Nets players have no defensive identity, or any identity at all.

It should come as no surprise that a team that lacks defensive intensity is one that is helmed by Julius Randle. Look, I get it, he did some great things during his time in Minnesota. He was an integral part of a team that made the Western Conference Finals. Randle had some great series, especially some standout ones on defense against the Los Angeles Lakers and Golden State Warriors, but that's about as far as any praise for defensive effort can go in his direction.

Randle had a great offensive showing tonight with 29 points on 11/16 from the floor. He also had his trademark five turnovers and finished with a team-worst -25 across his 35 minutes of play.

To be fair, the Nets are not a franchise that needs to be winning just yet. Still, when you have guys like Moritz Wagner blowing kisses to the away crowd while down by 20+ points, it's indicative of a team culture that does not take the game seriously. That comes from the top.

I do not think there should be any ill will towards Randle, though the resurgent team culture of the Timberwolves under LaMelo's leadership is exactly what the doctor ordered. Randle will get his stats while the Wolves accrue happiness points and wins. Seems like a good deal to me.

The Timberwolves take on the skidding Oklahoma City Thunder on Wednesday night at 6:30 PM. Catch that matchup on DAZN-MN and ESPN.
