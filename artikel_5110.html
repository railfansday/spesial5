Bitcoin tumbles below $69,000 as Nvidia turmoil adds to Fed-driven market jitters

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A total of $798 million in crypto positions has been liquidated over the past four hours, including $718.5 million in long positions.

Bitcoin Bitcoin (BTC) and other major cryptocurrencies continued to fall during Asian morning trading as the fallout from Nvidia cofounder Jensen Huang's exit and expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike weighed on market sentiment.

The world's largest cryptocurrency plunged 7% over the past 24 hours to $68,408 as of 12:10 a.m. ET Friday, its lowest level since Aug. 20, according to The Block's bitcoin price page. Ether Ethereum (ETH) dropped 6% to trade at $1,921. BNB slid 6.3%, while XRP lost 3.5%.

Crypto liquidations totaled $1.47 billion over the past 24 hours, with 175,299 traders liquidated, according to CoinGlass data. Long positions accounted for $1.31 billion of the total. Notably, liquidations totaled $798 million over the past four hours, including $718.5 million in long positions.

"The sharp leg lower in crypto looks like a leverage event as BTC broke key levels," said Rick Maeda, head of markets at Laevitas. "BTC had already lost $72,000 in the U.S. session, and once it broke $70,000 late morning in Asia, the long positions clustered below that level were forced out quickly, with more than $600 million in four hours and over $1.3 billion in the past day."

The market downturn came as traders continued to assess the fallout from Nvidia's leadership shake-up amid expectations of another Fed interest rate hike, analysts said.

On Thursday morning, Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO after the company launched an investigation last weekend into his relationship with a suspected Chinese spy. There had been reports that said Huang engaged in extramarital relationships with an Nvidia executive who reports directly to him and a second Nvidia employee.

"Traders trying to catch a reversal from the Nvidia-driven risk-off, combined with thin bids after this week's CPI, have both played a role in the liquidations," said Maeda.

Meanwhile, BTSE COO Jeff Mei told The Block that bitcoin also fell on assumptions that the Fed would raise rates in December to combat inflation. Mei said traders are watching out for signals of a potential 50-basis-point rate hike, which "hinges on how inflation and unemployment data will trend, as well as oil prices and geopolitical developments in the Middle East."

"If the Fed hikes rates by 50 basis points rather than the expected 25 basis points, bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies could have more room to fall," Mei added.

However, Jeff Ko, chief analyst at ViaBTC, said persistent inflation might actually strengthen bitcoin's appeal as an inflation hedge and bolster the currency debasement trade over the longer term.

"Looking ahead, markets will be closely watching the upcoming PCE in late November, and more importantly, the Dec. 9 FOMC dot plot. As for geopolitics, a de-escalation in Iran could offer one of the quickest paths to bringing inflation down," said Ko.
