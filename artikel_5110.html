Deribit outage hit Bitcoin hedgers on Coinbase

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Global Bitcoin and Ether perpetuals shared a Deribit order book. Coinbase said its separate US derivatives venue was unaffected.

Bitcoin and Ether perpetual futures were among the markets affected by the Nov. 20 Deribit outage, which the exchange said interrupted trading twice. Traders who use those contracts to hedge through the Coinbase app or Deribit’s website rely on the same venue to match their orders, so switching between those interfaces offered no way around the interruptions.

Deribit listed BTC-PERP and ETH-PERP, as well as its direct trading interface, FIX order and market-data connections, drop-copy API, REST API and WebSocket feed. It listed order-entry connections alongside systems that send market data and execution reports. In its 20:46 UTC status update, Coinbase said some customers might be unable to trade global derivatives or use margin trading and might have trouble seeing their balances. It said US derivatives were unaffected.

On Oct. 1, Coinbase moved perpetuals from its International Exchange to Deribit. Under the migration, eligible retail customers kept trading in the Coinbase app or on its website, including Coinbase Advanced. Existing Deribit users kept their accounts. Orders for the migrated contracts went to the same Deribit order book, where buyers and sellers are matched.

Coinbase app and website orders for global perpetuals share Deribit execution with orders placed directly on Deribit. Eligible Prime clients could access a separate US futures exchange.

Eligible, funded Prime customers could use US futures to offset some price exposure. Closing or resizing a Deribit perpetual still required Deribit.

Former International Exchange institutional clients moved their accounts, positions and balances to Deribit and had to switch trading API endpoints. The old exchange’s APIs were kept temporarily for historical data. The migration left those institutions without a parallel order book on the former exchange.

On Friday, Deribit first posted that trading was unavailable at 16:17 UTC and said it had resumed at 16:36. Coinbase warned of possible latency or degraded derivatives trading at 16:30 and moved to monitoring at 16:45. Coinbase posted a second warning at 19:26 that some users might be unable to trade. Deribit reported that trading was unavailable again at 19:28.

Both companies announced a 21:25 UTC pre-open ahead of a planned 21:30 restart. Deribit confirmed trading had resumed at 21:31, and Coinbase confirmed the restart at 21:43. At 22:02, Deribit said Bitcoin and Ether were back to normal limits. Tier 2, Tier 3 and real-world-asset instruments still faced limits of 10 orders and 2.5 mass quotes a second. Deribit said it had lifted the remaining caps at 22:31.

Deribit had marked a scheduled deployment complete at 09:15 UTC that morning. The later notices give no cause linking the disruptions to that deployment. Deribit reported a separate outage on Nov. 16, posting that trading was unavailable at 10:35 UTC and had resumed at 10:47. It marked that incident resolved at 14:37.

Coinbase says eligible institutions using Prime can access two execution venues under the same account. Its Global Derivatives offering sends options, dated futures and perpetuals to Deribit. The incident reports identify the Bitcoin and Ether perpetuals and affected trading infrastructure, with no breakdown by individual option or dated futures contract.

Coinbase says Deribit routes spot orders through Coinbase Exchange. Deribit still listed BTC-USDC and ETH-USDC among the markets affected through its platform on Friday. The listing leaves open whether those traders could reach the pairs directly on Coinbase Exchange during the outage.

Prime’s US futures and perpetual-style futures trade on Coinbase Derivatives Exchange. Coinbase Financial Markets has offered nano Bitcoin and Ether contracts through its US derivatives business. For US futures on Prime, Coinbase requires a funding setup and an activated portfolio. An institution with that access and sufficient funds could take an opposite position in a tradable US contract to offset some price exposure. Closing or resizing the original perpetual would still require trading on Deribit.
