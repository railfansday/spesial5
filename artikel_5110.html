178 Websites Infected in Major Optimizely Hack: Crypto Wallets Targeted, but Bitcoin Barely Reacted

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Optimizely attack infects 178 websites, steals crypto and card data, and exposes users to wallet-address swaps and iPhone exploits.

An Optimizely attack on Monday, November 16, planted crypto-stealing code on the websites of 178 organizations, including Ledger, HSBC, Zoom, the BBC and CNN. The malicious script swapped crypto wallet addresses, skimmed card numbers, and tried to break into iPhones for about 19 hours before it was cleaned up.

Optimizely is a tool companies use to test different versions of their web pages. An attacker slipped code into the scripts it serves, so every site that loaded them ran the malware.

We traced the attacker’s Bitcoin wallet. It received five payments worth about $5,500, and one victim alone sent nearly 90% of it. Bitcoin barely moved while the attack ran.

So what happened, and what is the real impact of hacks like this one?

Security researcher Scott Helme of Report URI found the code and published a full breakdown. The attacker wrote it into a settings field in customers’ Optimizely projects, so it was served from Optimizely’s own servers and looked legitimate.

Optimizely said it “secured affected user accounts and added protections against further unauthorized code delivery.” Report URI found no sign that Ledger hardware wallets themselves were breached. The danger was on the website, where a copied address could be swapped before a user pasted it.

Report URI listed the attacker’s wallets. We checked the Bitcoin one on mempool.space, the public Bitcoin block explorer.

The Optimizely attack’s impact was tiny, and the market ignored it. Bitcoin is near $64,300 after its weekend low of $62,006, and that slide came from wider selling, not this attack.

The money stolen in the Optimizely attack is small, but the method is the year’s most damaging one. We sorted DefiLlama’s 2026 hack list by attack type. Attacks on websites and infrastructure, rather than on smart contracts, are now the costliest category:

These attacks hit users where no wallet setting can protect them. The fix is on the user’s side: check the address on the hardware wallet’s screen before signing, send a small test transfer first, and keep phones updated.

On the daily chart, Bitcoin trades at $64,326. It sits below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $76,208, and its 200-day EMA at $75,864.

The Optimizely attack did not move Bitcoin, and the attacker walked away with about $7,000. Its real cost is trust: when a hardware-wallet maker’s own website can serve malware for hours, checking every address on the device is no longer optional.
