Activision calls Banjo-Kazooie one of its "iconic gaming franchises," raising hopes for long-awaited new game

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Banjo-Kazooie - the iconic Rare platformer starring an absent-minded bear and the sassy red bird he carries in a backpack - has been on hiatus for years, but a new Activision job listing has raised hopes for a new game.

In a LinkedIn post advertising a 'Product Manager, Emergent Franchises' role, the publisher wrote that it's "home to iconic gaming franchises including Call of Duty, Crash Bandicoot, Spyro the Dragon, and now… Halo, Age of Empires, Sea of Thieves, and Banjo Kazooie."

"Become part of the team working on building, growing, and shaping many of these franchises," the listing goes on. "As a Product Manager, you will serve as the publishing product lead for an upcoming premium console & PC title, driving the product from concept to launch."

If you're scratching your brain trying to remember when Activision took control of the beloved platformer, well, it happened during Xbox's last massive internal shakeup. Amid the mass layoffs and studio divestments, Xbox also made some drastic changes to its corporate structure - Halo, World's Edge and Rare were all slotted under Activision's supervision.

Rare's 40-year history includes almost too many notable games to list, but the internal rejig presumably put Activision in control of games like Conker, Viva Pinata and Killer Instinct, alongside the aforementioned pirate hit Sea of Thieves and Banjo-Kazooie.

The job post is by no means confirmation that a new Banjo-Kazooie game is in development, but it is the only series named here that can be considered dormant, perhaps hinting that Activision has plans to revive it down the line. Crash Bandicoot 4 and Spyro: A Realm Beyond lay out a pretty good blueprint for its return, I think.

The last properly new game in the series came 18 years ago with the Xbox 360's Banjo-Kazooie: Nuts and Bolts, a surprisingly good physics-driven vehicle construction sandbox.

Since then, Banjo and Kazooie showed up as a playable fighter in Super Smash Bros Ultimate. More recently, a fan-made PC port and an Evercade Double Pack have kept the series chugging.

Composer Grant Kirkhope was slightly more hopeful about the prospect of a Banjo-Threeie back in September. "I do know of like three companies that have actually physically pitched to Microsoft or to Rare about making another Banjo-Kazooie game, and I don't think anyone was successful," he said at the time.
