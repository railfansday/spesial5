Why Spam’s brand work has felt decidedly uncanned

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

At nearly 90 years old, the Hormel-owned meat brand Spam still knows that the best way to create a new fan is to have someone try it.

The company introduced its legendary product in new venues, including, in August 2025, fresh-sushi counters at Kroger and Ralphs grocery stores nationwide. Spam musubi—Spam and sushi rice wrapped in seaweed—arose in Hawaii and spread in the U.S. and Japan, where it was many consumers’ “first experience with Spam,” says Jason Levine, Hormel’s CMO.

Spam also debuted a new shape this year, at convenience chain Rutter’s. Spam Dogs—which the company suggests pairing with kimchi or pineapple salsa, nodding to two markets where Spam is especially popular, South Korea and Hawaii—quickly sold out. It is now the official dog at the U.S. Bank Stadium, where the Minnesota Vikings play. (Hormel is based in Austin, Minnesota.)

The brand is also courting Gen Z, with a focus on collectibility and co-branded limited-edition releases. In June, it partnered with Hello Kitty to create packaging for its low-sodium product. Hello Kitty herself appeared in a video with an animated can of Spam named “Spammy” promoting Spam-centric global recipes. The campaign targeted Gen Z collectors and lifestyle influencers and generated more than 680 million earned media impressions and nearly 4 million organic social impressions.

The Hello Kitty collaboration borrowed the playbook from a summer 2025 collectors’ pack that celebrated the 65th anniversary of Dr. Seuss’s Green Eggs and Ham: It showed Sam-I-Am holding Spam on a platter. On social media, the cans inspired videos from curious shoppers, and content creators cooked their own green eggs and Spam on TikTok.

The book’s message dovetails with Hormel’s approach to selling the product, Levine says: “You can eat it here. You can eat it there. You can eat it anywhere.”
