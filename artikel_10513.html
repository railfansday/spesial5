That is also good web architecture independent of SEO.

Internal links should not exist merely to move ranking signals around.

They should help users move between related tasks.

A technical guide might lead to a product page. A product page might link back to the configuration guide. A category page should make important child resources discoverable without requiring a user to know the exact URL.

This is why on-page SEO and internal linking are closely connected to site architecture. Good internal links clarify relationships for both visitors and crawlers.

At a broader level, crawlability is one part of technical SEO and search visibility. A page that nobody can reliably discover is difficult to evaluate regardless of how strong its content may be.

If an important URL appears disconnected because navigation is created only after client-side JavaScript runs, the script has surfaced a real architectural fact.

You can then decide whether that is acceptable.

The best output from this tool is not an "SEO score."

It is a concrete question for the development team:

This page exists. The sitemap knows about it. Why can't a normal HTML crawl reach it?
