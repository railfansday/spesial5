Belgian Travel Industry Fury at ‘11th Strike in 11 Months’

How many strikes are too many? With almost all of the country's flights grounded on Monday, many of Belgium’s travel leaders have had enough.

Belgium’s airline and travel sector is demanding stronger safeguards against strike-related disruption after air traffic controllers helped bring the country’s airports to a standstill on Monday.

The Board of Airline Representatives in Belgium (BAR Belgium) described it as the 11th strike affecting Belgian airspace in 11 months and accused public-sector workers of repeatedly undermining the country’s international connectivity.

Air navigation provider Skeyes said it could not safely provide regular air traffic control from 7 a.m. to 9 p.m. local time after some of its staff joined a nationwide demonstration against government austerity measures. Only emergency and other safety-critical flights would be supported, it said.

Brussels Airport said all arrivals and departures scheduled within that 14-hour window would have to be canceled, while Charleroi Airport said disruption would affect its entire Monday schedule.

Belgium's aviation and travel sector is calling for legally guaranteed minimum service at airports after air traffic controllers joined a nationwide protest against government austerity on Monday, shutting Belgian airspace from 7 a.m. to 9 p.m. Skeyes, the air navigation provider, said it could not safely provide regular air traffic control during that 14-hour window and would support only emergency and safety-critical flights. Brussels Airport CEO Arnaud Feist said just 25 of 429 scheduled passenger flights were expected to operate — fewer than 6% — affecting more than 51,000 passengers, while Charleroi Airport said its entire Monday schedule was hit. BAR Belgium, which represents 27 airlines, called it the 11th strike affecting Belgian airspace in 11 months and described the repeated action as "excessive, irresponsible and wholly disproportionate." Brussels Airlines COO Filip Aerts said Skeyes informed partners late on Saturday afternoon, turning close to zero expected impact into a near full-day closure, with about 25,000 customers affected. The Association of Travel Professionals (VVR) in Flanders put the one-day cost to the organized travel sector at at least €4.5 million ($4.9 million), and the cumulative cost of recent strikes at more than €55 million ($60.3 million).
