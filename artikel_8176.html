The Best Ski Gear for Powder Days and Storm Chasing

Chasing powder this winter? These tester-favorite skis, boots, shell, goggles and avalanche gear keep you dry, safe and skiing deep all storm cycle long. (Photo: Ray J Gadd)

Storm chasing is what most skiers dream about. One minute you’re scrolling the forecast from your desk, the next you’re cramming gear into a bag and packing up the car or hopping on a flight to score some freshies. It’s how we’d all love to spend our winters, sniffing out the best forecasts and bouncing between deep days around the country. With a strong, possibly historic El Niño predicted for this winter, there’s no telling exactly where the storm track will decide to unload. Nailing a good storm cycle takes patience, a little planning and a willingness to drop everything and go when the conditions allow. That, and the right gear to stay dry and comfortable all day long on the mountain—nothing kills a day faster than soaking wet mittens or fogged-up goggles.

Here’s the ski setup, storm-ready layers, and safety essentials our testers have long counted on for traveling far and wide to chase the biggest storms—plus a rolling bag to get it all there. Because the more dialed your gear is, the more time you can spend hunting for powder stashes. Pair these gear recs with SKI’s 2027 Resort Guide, the best tool for getting familiar with resorts across North America. That way, you’ll have a shortlist of options ready when the snow starts flying, wherever El Niño sends it.

If you have to choose just one ski for a powder-hunting mission, the Salomon S/Lab QST X is our top choice. With a 116-millimeter waist and freeride rocker profile, these sticks were made to float through champagne powder as well as dense, surfy snow, with a caruba and poplar wood core that’s light and lively. Flax and carbon fibers plus cork in the tip and tail keep things smooth and stable—a ski that can stand up to hard-charging freeriders but remains accessible for intermediates. Its midweight design—2,000 grams per ski at the 184 centimeter length—allows skiers to ride all day inbounds or mount up a hybrid binding for dropping out of bounds.

Even on a resort trip, it’s nice to keep your options open. If your plan is to primarily ski inbounds with a few short tours, the Salomon S/Lab Shift 2 10 MN Bindings are your ticket to ultimate flexibility. Salomon redesigned the Shift bindings this year, beefing up the construction in the heel and toe to help prevent pre-release and boost durability. On snow, most skiers won’t notice a big sacrifice compared with a traditional alpine binding, and you get the added benefit of a tour-capable binding perfect for a quick tour when the terrain across the valley beckons.

No one wants to tote around a second pair of boots on a ski trip. Tecnica’s new Zero G Decoy Pro boots are powerful hybrids, an ideal one-boot solution for deep trips. The Decoy delivers solid inbounds performance thanks to a stiff 130 flex, and the walk-mode lever is easy to flip with gloves on, making it simple to power up a bootpack or skintrack—or just cruise around the base area without killing your feet.

For deep days inbounds and in the backcountry, the 3-layer PowSlayer jacket and pants—available in men’s and women’s—provide ample protection without weighing you down. Powder days are full of temperature swings, and the PowSlayer kit uses a Gore-Tex ePE membrane, which repels moisture while regulating your body temp as you transition from frigid summits to top-to-bottom runs. The jacket sports a cinchable storm hood for blustery lift rides, and the pants feature a sewn-in pocket for stowing a transceiver securely.

Visibility is often limited on the deep days we dream of, and high-performance goggles become more crucial than ever. Smith’s 4D MAG ChromaPop Goggles with an extra lens offer a large field of view and the option of a photochromic lens, which is ideal on days when the light flows in and out as a storm moves through. Lens swapping is easy with these—a magnetic system lets you pop lenses in and out during a quick gondola ride.

For deep days when it feels like fresh snow is sneaking into every little crevice, cinch up Black Diamond’s Mercury Mittens and consider the problem solved. A toasty fleece-lined gauntlet design delivers ample coverage while you explore the white room, and a waterproof BD.dry insert keeps your hands dry all day long. Synthetic insulation is low-profile, and the 4-way stretch shell provides a comfortable fit that lets you grip your poles securely.

An extra neckwarmer is crucial for storm skiing, and having a dry one on hand to swap in midday can easily add a few extra hours to your day. Skida’s Alpine Neckwarmer provides cozy relief from frigid wind, with a stretchy polyester shell that sheds moisture, and a fleece lining that’s like a hug for your face. The Alpine Neckwarmer is ideal for those uber-chilly powder days, while a lighter option such as the Snow Tour can be great for warmer PNW storms.

Low-profile yet spacious, the Dakine Heli Pro 20L Backpack is compact enough to ride chairs all day, with enough space for shorter ski tours and out-of-bounds laps. There’s a dedicated safety pocket to stash your shovel and probe, as well as a fleece-lined goggle pocket to protect your goggles while you hike, or keep an extra lens on hand for fogging emergencies. There are both diagonal and A-frame carry options, so it’s easy to stow your skis for quick bootpacks.

A beacon is a necessity for skiing out of bounds, and it’s never a bad idea to switch yours on for deep days at the resort as well. The industry-leading beacon for professionals and advanced skiers alike, Mammut’s Barryvox S2 Beacon features all the bells and whistles you’d want out of a pro-level device: Group Check modes, an advanced flagging feature and a 70-meter range. Recent updates include a high-contrast display and Rescue Send, which switches itself back into send mode if you’ve been still for four minutes or more.

Hauling your gear for a ski trip can feel like a hassle, and the Backcountry BGA Cottonwood Double Ski and Snowboard Rolling Bag makes the process smoother. With a unique roll-top design, the padded bag can hold skis up to 195 centimeters—but easily rolls smaller if you don’t need the space—and internal and external storage pockets are perfect for stashing dirty socks for the way home. It’s spacious enough to fit two pairs of skis plus ample gear, and the durable 840-denier polyester and nylon construction stands up to years of wear and tear.
