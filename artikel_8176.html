The Best College Ski Towns in the U.S.

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Big Sky and Lone Peak (Photo: Aerial_Views / Getty)

In today’s fast-paced, instant-gratification-obsessed world, it’s harder than ever for colleges to grab a young person’s attention. Don’t get us wrong, they’re trying, turning to drastic measures like on-campus waterparks, steakhouses, and even valet parking to attract a rising crop of future graduates.

But what if colleges had a taller, more natural card to play? For some mountain lovers, the idea of a college experience that combines curriculum with outdoor adventure is a prime way to push the needle. The prospect has made university towns like Burlington, Vermont, and Salt Lake City, Utah, into winter sports juggernauts, but it has also brought smaller towns into the discussion, putting them on the map and putting their universities on the “must apply” list.

So, what’s the best U.S. college town for skiing and snowboarding? We considered a handful of factors (proximity is just the start, after all) to find that answer, region by region. That means more than just the skiing itself, but also the culture, cuisine, and community that come along with it, making these mountain paradises appeal to college students and graduates alike. When it comes to studying hard and shredding harder, here are the best places to earn those turns…err, degrees.

The sheer amount of ski areas and college towns in the Northeast is enough to overwhelm, but let’s keep it simple: there’s only one Burlington, Vermont. Perched on the edge of Lake Champlain, this four-season wonder is also home base for the University of Vermont, St. Michael’s College, and Champlain College—and a whole lot of skiers and boarders between the three. In addition to national championship-winning alpine and Nordic ski programs, UVM has also grown one of the strongest ski and snowboard clubs in the country. Mountains around Burlington are all about showing their student population love, serving up heavily discounted college season passes at resorts like Jay Peak, Sugarbush, Mad River Glen (skiers only), and Smugglers’ Notch. The days of the legendary Triple Major may be over (if you know, you know), but there are still plenty of cheap turns for the higher education crowd.

After a long day on the slopes, take a walk down Church Street for the ultimate cultural contrast. This pedestrian-only brick walkway echoes Europe with a uniquely New England appeal. Stop at the Church Street Tavern for a local brew and then trek to Bove’s for its famous Italian cuisine. Burlington is a music hotbed, so don’t forget to pay your respects at Higher Ground on your way out of town.

Lake Champlain—need we say more? This large body of freshwater is Burlington’s lifeblood, providing public beaches, public sailing centers, unmatched paddling access, and even some fun urban cliff jumping (we suggest Red Rocks for an evening plunge). The Green Mountains earn their name here in the summer, crisscrossed with hiking and biking trails that rival most any in the country. Camel’s Hump might be the crown jewel, but there are plenty of day hikes within a short drive of town.

You might not think of the Southeast as ski country, but don’t tell Boone, North Carolina. This college town is home to Appalachian State University and set at the foot of the Blue Ridge Mountains—one of the premier snow-sliding regions in the South. Boone is an easy drive from three formidable Southeast ski resorts, and Appalachian Ski Mountain is less than 15 minutes from campus. In addition to having one of the best ski schools on the East Coast, the local area has night skiing and four terrain parks. On select nights throughout the season, students can score $16 night skiing tickets as well. For the more advanced, Beech Mountain is just 45 minutes away, opening up a realm of on-hill possibilities.

If you’ve made it out to Beech Mountain, don’t sleep on the 5506’ Skybar—the highest elevation bar on the Eastern Seaboard. For those looking for a lower elevation reward, the town of Boone is known for its home-crafted beer scene and beautiful downtown. Grab a pint at Appalachian Mountain Brewing or size up a mocktail at Fizzed before checking out Blowing Rock Village south of Boone’s downtown.

Boone was a summer escape long before it became a winter destination, once considered the finest vacation retreat between Newport, Rhode Island, and Palm Beach, Florida. Nowadays residents live the best of both worlds, scoring winter turns before hitting the summertime bike trails at Rocky Knob Mountain Bike Park. Fly-fishing aficionados have their pick of honey holes along the Blue Ridge Parkway and local guides are friendly enough to share the goods, or at least some of them.

Set smack dab in the middle of one of the country’s most overlooked snowbelts, Marquette, Michigan, has the distinct pleasure of being both off the tourist map and in the crosshairs of winter’s biggest storms. The largest city on Michigan’s Upper Peninsula, Marquette is also a university town, welcoming students to Northern Michigan University every fall. But when snow starts to fall, this student population converts to bona fide powder hounds. Marquette Mountain sits within city borders and receives over 210 inches a year—making it one of the snowiest places east of the Mississippi. NMU students get a whole season of it for just $361.

If just enjoying the slopes isn’t enough, undergrads can also pursue an associate degree in ski area management. This unique program runs in conjunction with Gogebic Community College, a school that places students into five-month internships at actual ski resorts and has its own ski hill, Mount Zion, right on campus.

Marquette is where college bar and ski bar collide into one heck of an après evening. Grab a $2 mystery beer at the Wooden Nickel Bar or dive into a face-sized cocktail while taking over the jukebox at Remie’s. The Midwest does après different—and we’re all for it.

Set on the shores of Lake Superior, Marquette turns students into long-term residents. Presque Isle Park and the Black Rocks offer up stunning, cliffside trails and wild forest, while biking in the NTN trail systems features a little something for every ability level. Cutting right through town, the Iron Ore Heritage Trail stretches 47 miles across the region, connecting caves, ruins, and historical stops related to the area’s mining roots.

This pick’s a little spicy, but hear us out. Boulder and Crested Butte have the skiing and mountain biking thing on lock. Salt Lake City has mountain proximity in spades. But Durango? This college town has a bit of everything—minus the people and mountain-bound bustle. Home to Fort Lewis College, Durango sits in the cradle of Colorado’s San Juan Mountains, an overlooked corner of the state with some serious vertical relief. Students attending Fort Lewis have their pick of Purgatory, Wolf Creek, and Hesperus within an hour or so of campus, but for those seeking bucket-list turns, Silverton and Telluride are a respectable day trip. If class is letting out late, there’s even Chapman Hill, a small (but steep) community ski area at the edge of campus, which offers an entire season of skiing and riding for $130.

Durango is a locals’ town right down to its libations. While several breweries have popped up over the years (Animas, Steamworks, and Ska, to name a few), it’s hard to pass up a post-mountain beer at Anarchy Brewing Co. Aside from some seriously strong IPAs (we liked the Sex Panther), this spot is four-legged friendly and has good food served on its outdoor patio.

If you just can’t wait to wet your whistle, the town’s biggest cheat code is the Durango Hot Springs Resort and Spa. It may sound bougie, but this series of naturally heated pools is a hangout that will soothe the soul without breaking the bank. The facility offers beer and wine for purchase and is only 20 minutes from Purgatory Ski Resort.

It’s deals and access like this that keep Durangatangs in town after they graduate, but while winter is a highlight, this locale is all about summer recreation. The Animas River runs right through the heart of Durango, often dotted with rafters and flotillas of varying sizes. Further downstream, the town has its very own whitewater park, and Durango Whitewater is a direct feeder program to the U.S. Olympic whitewater teams.

Beyond the river, Durango features hundreds of miles of mountain biking trails ranging from flowy beginner to some of the gnarliest technical terrain in the West. In fact, in 2030 the town is slated to host the UCI Mountain Bike World Championships.

Set between Montana’s two biggest mountain resorts and home to a robust network of in-town Nordic skiing, Bozeman has quietly been cultivating its ski community for decades. Students at Montana State University can take a free shuttle bus to nearby Bridger Bowl or unlock a discounted season pass at Big Sky (though it won’t include Saturdays or tram access). But Montana State skiers and snowboarders are not just on-piste enthusiasts. For the backcountry curious, the school has a strong backcountry scene including a dedicated Backcountry Club for avalanche education, gear help, group trips, and networking with professionals in the nearby Custer Gallatin National Forest and beyond.

It just so happens that some of Montana’s most epic skiing is located above some of Montana’s most epic après bars, so lucky you. Grab a pint at Scissor Bill’s in Big Sky or sip a Cold Smoke Scotch Ale at the Grizzly Bar off the Bridger Bowl parking lot—either way, you can’t lose.

The key to a good night is making it back to Bozeman, though, where Main Street offers up one of the more fun Eagles Club experiences around, complete with cheap drinks and rowdy live music. The Zebra is another community staple, where college students, ranchers, and old-timers can mingle over good local tunes.

Bozeman is an outdoors town with a frontier soul. The changing demographics lean toward outdoor recreation, but there’s an underlying and common current dedicated to protecting and enjoying the wild places in its periphery, and it shows. Hyalite Canyon is home to some of the best ice climbing in the world, and hosts a slew of climbers, runners, Nordic skiers, and hikers year-round. The Gallatin and Madison Rivers and Bozeman Creek all pass through or near town as well, giving anglers and kayakers primo access right out the front door, and plenty of options for anyone looking to play a little midweek hooky.

This Northwest representative was a close call with Bellingham and its coastal charm, but there’s something about the high desert that just doesn’t quit. Bend didn’t start out as a college town, but with the arrival of Oregon State University’s Cascade Campus (and the continued local support for Central Oregon Community College), this outdoors hotspot has become one heck of a diploma destination. Just 30 minutes from the 360-degree volcano skiing and riding of Mount Bachelor, Bend is a college town wrapped in mountain pedigree. We’d be silly not to mention the extensive backcountry access in Deschutes National Forest, and the Olympians that have come out of Mount Bachelor’s powerhouse Nordic skiing program. All of that and 300-plus sunny days a year? Where do we enroll?

Ski bar legend has it that before après was a French word, it was a guiding light in Central Oregon. Kidding aside, few ski towns have as many post-ski food and beverage options as the mighty Bend. While the boom has curbed in recent years, there are still over 30 breweries and taprooms within Bend city limits. Crux Fermentation Project and Monkless Belgian Ales rest on the banks of the picturesque Deschutes River, but the cozy climes and kickass beer at Terranaut Beer won Oregon’s Small Brewery of the Year in 2025.

For folks who turn college degrees into outdoor family roots, there are few spots quite like Bend. With year-round river recreation (including a retractable surf wave co-designed with surfer Gerry Lopez) and multiple trail running and mountain biking trail systems around town, Bend residents don’t know how or care to sit still. Beyond city limits, Central Oregon is an absolute natural playground, whether it be rock climbing and hiking around Smith Rock State Park, wandering the woods around Sisters, or getting out on the water in Prineville. If your friend says they’ve gotten bored of Bend, it’s time to find a new friend.
