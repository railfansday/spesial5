Here’s How Many Shares of Nike You Need for $20,000 in Yearly Dividends

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Nike’s 4.6% yield sits near a record high as management targets $3 in adjusted earnings per share by fiscal 2031.

By John Ballard – Nov 20, 2026 at 5:45AM EST

Nike (NKE +0.12%) stock closed at $36.31 on Nov. 18, down 42% during the past year, which puts the dividend yield at 4.6% -- the highest yield in Nike's history.

Nike pays $1.64 per share annually, so collecting $20,000 a year takes about 12,195 shares, which would cost roughly $442,800. The company's recent Investor Day presentation gave those shareholders reason to be patient.

At the Nov. 17 presentation, Nike said it plans to return about $12 billion through dividends from fiscal 2027 through fiscal 2031. It also targeted adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of $3 to $3.50 by fiscal 2031, versus a fiscal 2027 forecast of $1.15 to $1.35.

The first-quarter results for the period ended Aug. 31 show where that growth starts. Performance revenue rose in the high single-digit percentages, led by double-digit gains in running and global football.

Importantly, gross margin widened to 42.8%, and selling and administrative expense fell 3%, showing management's progress in reducing costs.

During the past year, Nike generated about $2.1 billion of free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capital spending) and paid $2.4 billion in dividends, a payout ratio of roughly 115%.

Normally, a payout like that exceeds free cash flow could open the door for a potential dividend cut. However, Nike ended the quarter with $8.4 billion in cash and short-term investments, providing more resources to support cash returns. On the Oct. 1 earnings call, Chief Financial Officer Dave Denton said, "Under all scenarios, we have support for maintaining and ultimately growing the dividend over time."

Still, Nike expects fiscal 2027 revenue to fall by a high-single-digit percentage, with Greater China down 26% last quarter and Sportswear and Jordan continuing to shrink. It also said it doesn’t expect revenue to resume growing until fiscal 2029.

There is a risk that Nike may be unable to increase free cash flow and may have to reduce the dividend. Some investors may want to wait for better signs of progress in the turnaround before buying shares. But assuming the company meets management's EPS target of $3 by fiscal 2031, the stock could deliver excellent returns on top of the dividend.
