BBC Being Investigated Over Antisemitism By Government Adviser

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The UK government’s antisemitism tsar is probing the BBC.

John Mann told the Today programme he has opened an investigation to see how “widespread” Jewish staff’s “feeling of discomfort” is within the corporation. According to The Times, whistleblowers have already told him they consider the BBC a “hostile environment.”

Mann said his investigation should complete by Easter before he reports back to the broadcaster. He invited “anybody who is Jewish inside the BBC past and present with something to say” to come forward and speak with him. On Today, he said he had already heard from employees who had read about the investigation in The Times just three hours prior. Rather than listeners or viewers, he said the probe concerns “the lived experience of Jewish staff past and present at the BBC,” and will “report facts, not opinions.” Watch on Deadline

Deadline understands the BBC has not yet been notified about Mann’s investigation. Mann has been the UK government’s adviser on antisemitism for seven years.

Speaking at a press lunch earlier this month, the BBC’s deputy chief Rhodri Talfan Davies said he did not think an internal inquiry into antisemitism was “necessary.”

“Of course, in any large organisation there can be instances of discrimination, which you deal with when they arise, but there is no evidence of any systemic issue within the BBC that would merit anything like that,” he added. “I don’t believe there is institutional discrimination of any sort in the BBC, but I think as an organisation you always have to look harder and review how you go about identifying issues, and that work is never done.”

In the wake of October 7, antisemitism has been an issue the BBC has struggled to get to grips with. At times, this has spilled out in the open. Last month, one of the exec producers behind Oscar-winner Mr. Nobody Against Putin was reported to have settled with the BBC for £400,000 ($523,000) over antisemitism claims, although an internal BBC investigation did not uphold her complaint. “I think I made too much noise about being a Jew inside the BBC,” Lucie Kon told The Telegraph a few weeks ago.

A BBC spokeswoman said: “We take any and all allegations of discrimination extremely seriously – there is no place for it at the BBC. We have introduced compulsory training on antisemitism for all our staff and we actively encourage colleagues to report any incidences or concerns they have. We are determined to ensure the BBC is a place where everyone can be themselves and feel accepted.”
