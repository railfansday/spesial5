The pages of a site form a graph. Think of it as many nodes connected to each other via edges. Each of the edges are the links between the pages, and the nodes themselves are the pages. Now if you start to visualize this as a graph, you will quickly see that there are multiple ways to traverse this graph and reach a particular endpoint.

A search crawler usually uses one of these pages as a starting point and crawls through all of the pages (especially when pages are not fully covered in a sitemap).

If a page lies very deep from the home page, it means that it's not an important page. It's an indication to the search engine that this is not something that you yourself consider important, and the search engine has a lesser incentive to rank this page over pages that are linked better within your site.

The key metric here is link depth. That is the number of clicks it takes to reach a particular page from your home page.

Another key metric is broken links. If you have too many broken links, it indicates to the search engine that your site is outdated and not well maintained, and the search engine will then not want to rank your site.

The way to identify both these problems is the same: to actually walk through your site, do a crawl of your site outside in, and try to figure out what the link depth is and if there are broken links on your website.

Seed the crawl with the home page and all URLs mentioned in the sitemap. Technically, the sitemap is not required if you're doing a crawl, but going from the sitemap will allow us to detect orphaned pages as well.

Fetch the pages and follow redirects as required. Record when you're following a redirect: we do not let fetch follow it silently, we record each hop and add its target to the crawl. Record HTTP errors as they occur.

Drop URL fragments and tracking parameters. Keep trailing slashes because sometimes there can be mismatches around expectation of trailing slashes in different URLs of a site.

In order to navigate from the home page, we can use BFS. So we know that the first time we reach a page, that is the page's shortest path.

We can cut off the depth over a certain threshold, say 5, to indicate that pages beyond that are too far or too deep to be reached.

Noindex pages will still be crawled. However, we need to make a record of them being not indexed, to reflect that they will not be present in the search engine.

The desired page depth is as close to the home page as possible. Important pages should be close to the home page. The deeper you go from the home page, it signals to the search engine that the page is less important.
