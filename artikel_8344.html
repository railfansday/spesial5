Most homemade dog treats cost less than their store-bought versions, but a homemade daily diet often costs more than canned dog food and falls short on nutrients. Price each batch against the food you buy now, and have a veterinarian balance any recipe you’ll feed every day.

In a study published in the Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association in 2013, veterinary nutritionists at UC Davis checked 200 home-cooked dog food recipes from books and websites. Ninety-five percent fell short on at least one essential nutrient, most often zinc or choline.

That shortfall matters most in food a dog eats every day, and those meals are also where cooking at home stops saving money. Batch by batch, three of the five treats below cost less than their store-bought versions, while all four meals cost more per pound than store-brand canned dog food. None of the ten recipes has been balanced by a veterinary nutritionist.

Treat the four meals above as an occasional change, not a diet. In the UC Davis study, all four recipes written by board-certified veterinary nutritionists met the recommended nutrient ranges for adult dogs. Before a recipe becomes your dog’s everyday food, have your vet or a board-certified veterinary nutritionist balance it for your dog’s age, size, and health.

The American College of Veterinary Internal Medicine’s directory lists board-certified nutritionists by ZIP code, including ones who consult remotely.

If store food still comes out ahead, a commercial food labeled complete and balanced for your dog’s life stage needs no balancing.

Bring the recipe you’d feed most to your dog’s next checkup, and price the balanced version per pound against the food you buy now.
