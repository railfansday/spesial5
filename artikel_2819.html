Lions CB D.J. Reed won’t rush back after last year’s injury issues

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

When Detroit Lions cornerback D.J. Reed went down with a hip injury in the team's Week 8 game against the Minnesota Vikings, he couldn't help but feel some frustrating déjà vu. Like last year, when a serious hamstring injury caused him to miss six games, he was again sidelined while at the top of his game. In an odd coincidence, in both games he was injured, he even tallied his first interceptions of the season. Instead of building on that progress, he now had to watch from the sidelines again.

But this time around, he's going through the process with a little more wisdom.

In 2025, the hamstring injury was enough to land him on injured reserve—he estimates either a Grade 2 or 3—but he thankfully avoided surgery. In retrospect, though, he believes he rushed his way back onto the field.

"When you get hurt, you obviously want to get back on the field as soon as possible. I kinda undermined the severity of a hamstring strain, especially a high-grade one, because I never hurt a hamstring that bad," Reed said. "In hindsight, yes, I do feel like I came back (too) early."

When Reed returned to the field, he thought he was fine, but in the offseason, he noticed his clear lack of burst. His speed was gone, and it led to a tough stretch of football. PFF credited Reed with allowing 24 catches for 344 yards and a touchdown in the final seven games of the season.

So this time around, he's conscientious about returning too soon. The stakes are too high, and the defense is playing too well for him to jeopardize things by thrusting himself into the lineup before his body is ready.

"I definitely want to make sure I come back and I feel like myself. I don't want to rush it, especially because our defense is playing well," Reed said. "So I don't want to be a hindrance. So I just gotta be honest with myself, be honest with the trainers and my coaches to see if I'm ready to go, whenever that is."

Without Reed in the lineup, the Lions have allowed just 10 points against the Dolphins and 10 against the Patriots. Sure, that was against a poor Dolphins offense and a Patriots team without starting quarterback Drake Maye for most of the game, but Reed's supporting cast of Rock Ya-Sin, Roger McCreary and a pair of solid safeties Brian Branch and Chuck Clark have undeniably been balling out.

"I'm very proud of them," Reed said. "Not just the DBs, but the whole defensive unit. Guys just continuing to step up. Like we always say, it's a next-man-up business. Roger's playing very well. Rock played amazing last game. Chuck is playing phenomenal. BB's playing great, so we've just got to keep it up."

Reed wouldn't offer too many details on the current hip injury, noting just that he thinks it's a Grade 2 and he doesn't think he'll play this week against the Buccaneers. But he also made it clear he doesn't think he's going on injured reserve and he is getting better.

"I'm progressing, I'm healing, I'm going to be fine. Just rehabbing and working it," Reed said.

On Wednesday, he took his first significant step toward coming back, being listed as a limited participant during Detroit's walkthrough practice. But he won't know for sure until he's out there going full speed and can tell whether that explosiveness is truly back.

"I think that's the number one thing at my position is making sure I've got my burst, making sure that I could get the top-end speed, making sure that I can turn and flip the receivers like this and everything feels normal. I think that's the most important thing."
