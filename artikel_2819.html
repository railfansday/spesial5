LeBron James records first triple-double with 76ers: Where he ranks on all-time list

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

In case you forgot, LeBron James is great.

The NBA's all-time leading scorer notched his first triple-double with the Philadelphia 76ers Wednesday, Nov. 25 in an NBA Cup matchup against the Miami Heat.

He completed the feat with 1:43 left in the fourth quarter, when he scooped his 10th rebound. With Joel Embiid sidelined to start the game and Jaylen Brown getting banged up just seconds before the end of the first half, James took over. He erupted in the second quarter, burying 3-pointer after 3-pointer. During one stretch in the second period, he scored 14 consecutive points for the 76ers.

"It's not my job to dominate every night for this ball club," James told reporters after the game. "We have a Finals MVP (Jaylen Brown), a regular season MVP (Joel Embiid), an up-and-coming superstar point guard (Tyrese Maxey), a young fella in VJ (Edgecombe), who is up and coming as well. My dues is paid in what I've done, but if they need me to dust off the cape from time to time, I can still accomplish that. But it's not my job. I don't think it's my job, but if they need it, I can do it."

James finished the game with 25 points on 9-of-15 shooting, adding 12 assists and those 10 rebounds. His point and assist totals were both season highs.

This is the 126th triple-double in James' career, which has now reached an NBA-record 24 seasons. Russell Westbrook holds the record for most triple-doubles in NBA history with 209. James ranks fifth behind Westbrook, Nikola Jokic, Oscar Robertson and Magic Johnson. No other player in NBA history had recorded a triple-double after turning 41 years old; James has now done it four times.

In fact, James now has more triple-doubles than Michael Jordan, Kobe Bryant and Larry Bird — combined.

James was ranked the 19th-best player in the league in USA TODAY's preseason rankings of the top 100 players.

The four-time MVP and NBA champion joined the 76ers this offseason as a free agent after leaving the Los Angeles Lakers.

"We all know, and everybody always says it, but Father Time wins," Heat coach Erik Spoelstra said. "But right now, he's giving Father Time hell."
