Where is Red Wings Fire?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Detroit Red Wings have won just two games in the month of November and find themselves circling the bottom of the Atlantic with only the dysfunctional Toronto Maple Leafs below them in the division standings for the moment. It's not that the Red Wings haven't been in games, however, for a team with very little room for error, it continues to find them, despite their best efforts.

"This is who we have right now, and this is what we're playing with. And we're going to keep working hard and give it our best effort each night," Head coach Todd McLellan said, "I can't hold their feet to the fire for anything other than a good effort."

Detroit has had a tough run in the last month, with every team they've faced ahead of their matchup with the Vancouver Canucks on Nov. 25 at a .500 record or better so far this year. Despite the Red Wings improved defense on paper, the reality has been that in all but one their matchups since Halloween, they've allowed in two or more goals to opposing offenses.

Meanwhile, the Red Wings have only scored more than two goals three times since their first loss to the St. Louis Blues. McLellan described a "gap" between the Red Wings and the elite teams of the league after their loss to the Boston Bruins.

"You know, the same problems arise. We were short on offense, and I thought today we had some good luck early because they hit the post and that helped us. But we had some bad luck on the first one, the way it ricocheted around, ended up in our net," McLellan said,

"And the second one was flat out too many men on the ice, so the officials missed that. But, you know, sometimes when it's not going well, those things come back and go against you rather than for you, so, that's where we're at."

It's a far cry from McLellan's message when he first came to Detroit, firing up a Red Wings squad that was floundering and playing far too mechanically under former head coach Derek Lalonde. Now, for a different set of reasons, the Red Wings find themselves struggling as they head into December and it seems fair to wonder what exactly can be said or done to inspire this Red Wings squad.

The biggest issue of course, is that this team doesn't seem to have enough stars to keep a rotation going. McLellan likes to compare Red Wings stud defenseman Moritz Seider to a diesel engine.

Well, anyone with a diesel in their truck will tell you; You need two batteries to get that thing going.

Diesel is a relatively stable fuel, so it's hard to ignite and get things running like you want it to, especially out in the cold. The thing about it is that it needs a little pre-loaded pressure and heat to actually turn the engine over.

Right now, it doesn't seem like the Red Wings can find the energy to turn over a new leaf and start winning games. For that, they'll need to find raise the pressure they put on not just their opponents, but themselves to win in the gritty, hard areas of the game they haven't so far.
