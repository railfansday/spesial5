7 Years On: Airbus A321XLR Launch Customer Middle East Airlines Finally Gets Its 1st Jet

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Airbus A321XLR's launch customer, Middle East Airlines, has finally received the first of five of the aircraft that it has ordered. This carrier was the first to order the A321XLR back in 2019. A combination of local political and economic crises, certification delays, and option deferrals have caused the delay.

The aircraft builds upon Middle East Airlines' current fleet of modern Airbus jets. It gives a longer-range alternative to the 11 Airbus A321neos that are currently in the airline's fleet, according to Planespotters.net.

The initial order made by Middle East Airlines in 2019 was for four A321XLRs, which has since been upgraded to five jets. Airbus says that the aircraft will allow the carrier to complete longer narrowbody missions while delivering a 30% reduction in fuel burn.

According to Airbus, Middle East Airlines' A321XLRs will be configured in a two-class layout. The jet will feature 160 seats, with 22 people enjoying the luxury of lie-flat business class.

The remaining 138 passengers will be in economy class. The airline generally offers 31 to 32 inches (79–81 cm) of seat pitch and an 18 inches (46 cm) of seat width in economy class, although the exact economy and business class product that will be available on the aircraft is not yet known.

Just one Embraer jet prevents the fleet of 27 jets from being exclusively Airbus. The fleet features Airbus A320 family jets, with widebody capability provided by the Airbus A330. The airline wrote in a press release at the time of the original order:

"The flag carrier of Lebanon has signed a firm order for four A321XLRs, making it the launch airline customer of Airbus' latest evolution of the winning A321neo family. The agreement takes Middle East Airlines’ cumulative single aisle orders with Airbus to 15 A321neo family aircraft, including 11 A321neos and 4 A321XLRs with deliveries starting in 2020. MEA will use the A321XLR to strengthen its network in Africa and Asia."

The aircraft reportedly remains in Europe due to supply chain issues.

The ongoing geopolitical crisis in the Middle East is a clear reason that the A321XLR's arrival has been delayed. Yet, Middle East Airlines is also clearly trying to make the best of a bad situation. The carrier has kept flying throughout the conflict, Aviation Week reports, although with passenger numbers falling by over 50%. The airline was working to get these numbers back up to 70% during the summer.

Speaking with Aviation Week, Chairman and CEO Mohamad El-Hout said that it's all about flexibility and balanced growth. He said that long-term planning has been replaced with year-by-year thinking, aiming to respond to unpredictable conflicts and instability in the region and involving Lebanon.

Another issue that Middle East Airlines has been struggling with is problems with Pratt & Whitney geared turbofans, and it is thought the airline has dealt with groundings as a result. El-Hout hopes the fact that its A321XLRs will be fitted with the Advantage version of the GTF engines will mean the new aircraft don't face the problem. Despite all these internal and external challenges, the airline is currently working to launch FlyBeirut in 2027. This subsidiary will compete with Middle East Airlines' low-cost rivals.
