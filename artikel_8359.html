If your cell phone holds calls in every room, cancel the landline and make the cell your only phone. To keep the home number and your old phones instead, move the number to an internet calling service before you cancel, then register your street address for 911 and add a battery backup.

Eight in 10 U.S. adults lived in homes with cell phones and no landline in the second half of 2025, the National Center for Health Statistics reports. Basic home phone service averages $33.99 a month in urban areas, by the FCC’s 2026 Urban Rate Survey.

Both internet options may not work in a power or internet outage, the FCC warns, so plug the modem and adapter into a battery backup and keep a charged cell phone. Register your street address with the provider, too, since it passes that address to dispatchers when you call 911.

If a home alarm or medical alert dials out over the landline, ask its monitoring company whether it works on the new service before you switch.

Keep the landline if your cell signal is weak indoors and your internet often drops. A traditional copper line may keep working through a power outage, though you may need a corded phone.

Switching to a cell or internet provider in the same area lets you keep your home number, the FCC says. Sign up for the new service and have it move the number before you cancel the landline, since moving it to a cell phone may take a few days, and Vonage says its transfers take about 10 business days.
