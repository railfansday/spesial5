🥾 Further Than I’ve Gone Before 🌄

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The cloudless night and new moon would have been a good night to cowboy camp.

Waking up to birds singing, the morning was peaceful and cold. Struggling to get out of my warm quilt, I walked to the stream to collect water.

With my leg a little sore, I crawled back into my tent. My food bag was feeling light, i made oatmeal and tea for breakfast.

As Specs, then Sunshine, walked by, we greeted each other. Later I was cleaning my pot, as NBC, All-Star, and Joker hiked past.

It was almost 10 a.m. before I left camp. I’m an idiot. I could already feel the heat of the day.

As I hiked, the volume of my music kept increasing, and I was unable to tell what was causing it. Annoyed, I pushed through some overgrown bushes, then restarted my phone.

A raven flew through the clear blue sky. The desert was absolutely stunning, with purple, white, and yellow flowers blooming on green plants against the tan sand.

As I started my 1,400-foot descent, Sunshine was sitting on a big, flat rock in the shade. I joined her for a minute and took off my shoes. I dug through my pack, finding a bluetooth dongle.  I had taken it out of my selfie stick tripod when I put it in the hiker box back in Tehachapi. Sunshine offered me a half-liter of water, which I gratefully accepted.

Down at Kelso Valley Road, about 15 full water jugs sat along a fence. The blue five-gallon jugs were hot from the sun. With no shade, Sunshine, All-Star, Joker, and NBC all stopped for water, then left while I stayed, hoping for a place to blog while I waited out the heat of the day. An SUV stopped and rolled its windows down to take a picture of the PCT sign and trail. The four men were in the area checking on an emergency glider landing strip, and one of them had hiked on the PCT before. They asked if I needed anything. “I’m fine, but if you have any extra snacks, I would take them.” In a few minutes, I was holding two peanut butter and jelly sandwiches (with crunchy PB), a packet of trail mix, an apple, and a hard-boiled egg. After they drove off, I felt very alone in the middle of the desert.

I watched a bunny hop by, then a hawk fly over as I ate the apple and both peanut butter sandwiches. I had 3.75 liters of water in my backpack. I mixed coffee in a bottle and hiked out at 5:40 p.m.

Passing a trail register that had seen better days. I had fifteen exposed desert miles to the next water cache.

I sighed deeply. My shin felt a little sore. This was the last section I hiked in 2018. Maybe the pain was all in my head.

Pushing through the sand after a few miles, I saw Sunshine at her camp, tucked near a Joshua tree. She was surrounded by little purple flowers scattered around.

We traded half her dinner for my solar panel (I kept forgetting about it anyway). Sitting in the sand, I ate the food while we chatted.

As I hiked into the setting sun, little white moths were flying around the blooming plants.

As the sun set, I realized how tired I was. I crossed Spring Canyon Road and found a windy flat spot to set up my tent.

With no rocks to help hold my tent stakes in the sand, I hoped the tent would hold. Dirty, sweaty, and greasy from sunscreen, I lay down without changing. Exhausted, I passed out.

Waking up, I was surprised my tent had not collapsed in the wind. I was packed and on trail at 4:30 a.m. The waning crescent moon was smiling down on me. Other than navigating a couple of washouts, I was able to hike by the glow of the moon. This is an absolutely beautiful section of the trail.

I was hiking along the ridgeline when the sun began to reveal sweeping views of the desert valley. I feel grateful to be out here hiking this trail.

At 8:00 a.m., I passed Bird Spring Pass Trailhead, where an empty Jeep was parked. The water cache was almost identical to the previous one. Originally started and maintained by legendary hiker Devilfish to aid hikers in this dry 35-mile stretch. The caches are now maintained locally by legendary trail angels Mike and Terry.

Filtering water, I sat in a small patch of shade. I watched a hare hop across the road as I ate oatmeal, drank coffee, and ate a hard-boiled egg with some habanero garlic hot sauce.

My food bag feeling light as I stuffed it into my backpack, preparing for the 1,500-foot exposed climb out of the valley.

At 8:40 a.m., Worms, Sea Dog, Double Down, and Specs arrived at the cache.

At 9:25 a.m., I began this new stretch of trail, excited to hit 10 miles by 10:00 a.m.!

An hour and a half later, I reached the top of the valley, immediately taking an hour break.

The heat of the day hit me as I left the shade of my lunch spot, partially regretting such a long break.

The sandy mountainside was speckled with little purple and yellow flowers.

Hearing helicopters, I looked across the valley to see a black scar with a red outline in the distance. Fire season had already started.

Hitting a wall, I stopped at a shady camp spot, taking a nap. With over 5 miles left and only a few sips of water in my bottle, I made one more push.

Hiking on an exposed service road, I saw lizards and horned toads brought out by the heat. I’m glad the day was starting to cool off.

An adorable baby cottontail bunny hopped out in front of me on the road. It was so tiny. I stopped to watch it for a second.

At 6:30 p.m., I dropped my pack on the porch of McIver’s cabin.

Crossing to the other side of the pipe spring, I filled my water bottle and spotted a flat place to set up my tent.

Tired enough to cowboy camp, I didn’t want to be annoyed by bugs, so I set up my tent.

Exhausted, I made hot tea and forced myself eat dinner. After all, it was a 21-mile day.

Unable to get comfortable all night, I tossed and turned while my sleeping pad slid to the bottom of my tent.

Waking up at 4:30 a.m., I was thankful that at least the stars were beautiful.

I was tired and hungry with had no oatmeal or coffee left. Eating some trail mix as I hiking down the road at 7 a.m., I had a handful of Skittles, a half cup of trail mix, two Clif Bars, and one electrolyte, along with the electrolyte in my water.

Ridge walking toward Walker Pass, I stopped in the shade to apply sunscreen just before the trail curved. I enjoyed the beautiful views of the desert valley and the Sierras beyond, although it was smoggy, possibly from fires.

As I was signing the trail log at the junction to Walker Pass Campground, I heard voices. Turning around, I saw All-Star, Joker, and NBC walking down the trail. They were rushing to catch the bus into Ridgecrest. Reaching the bus stop together, we decided to drop our backpacks and hitchhike while waiting for the bus.

A driver in a small SUV pulled over. With the passenger seat already filled, we were surprised when all four of us fit in the back seat. Our packs in the small cargo area.

The valley floor of the Mojave Desert was hot in the afternoon sun. Being dropped off at Starbucks, we thanked our new friends and slung our packs. The four of us walked off in search of lunch. A mile down the bright, shadeless city sidewalk, we walked into Saigon Flavor (a Vietnamese restaurant), where we were welcomed, hiker stink and all. We talked until our food arrived, then silence as we gobbled up our meals.

Full, we walked next door to the Orient Outlet. I purchased coffee candy, a mango, and a coconut water.

The afternoon heat was unbearable. When we were unable to find a rideshare, NBC went back into the restaurant. She returned with our server, Lilliana, who offered to drive all four of us to our destinations.

Arriving at the address given to me, I saw trekking poles and boots outside. I knew I was at the correct address. Inside were Sherpa and Nobody, along with Oliver the poodle, Hopper the terrier, and the calico cat Luna.

I was offered vegetables from the table: broccoli, carrots, radishes, and cauliflower with a sour cream dip.

I spent some time socializing even though I was stressed about having time to blog and other chores while in town.

Nobody made a delicious spaghetti dinner with bread and side salad to share with our new friends, TA Jellyfish and Burn Out. It was nice sitting down together and enjoying a home-cooked meal after a long day on the trail.

I soaked my foot in Epsom salt to get the sand out. Before bed, I spent some time going through the hiker box. I took the snow baskets off a pair of trekking poles and grabbed a few stickers. Never know what you’ll find in a hiker box!

Waking up refreshed. A sliver of light sneaking into the dark room from behind the curtains. How grateful I am for this morning. Wearing clean clothes, sleeping in a clean bed.

I opened the bedroom door to be warmly greeted by Oliver and Hopper. Nobody was in the kitchen scratching up some breakfast. Sipping on coffee, we chatted over our heavenly meal of poached eggs, homestyle potatoes topped with sour cream and parsley, with fruit on the side.

Nobody let me pick through his food bag. This came in handy during today’s resupply mission.

Retreating to the dark bedroom, I spent some time on my writing.

Leftover spaghetti for lunch, then off to run some errands.

Meeting Sunshine to pick up my solar panel, then heading to the post office to mail home some extra gear and stickers. I picked up a few snacks for my food bag and a gallon of milk to drink.

Sandy had some gear in the back of her car on its way to the hiker box. I took out six Pachallama 2g Carbon-Pin Tent Stakes. I can’t wait to try these out!

Nobody rehashed the spaghetti into a bake with cottage cheese. Another delicious meal! I’m going to miss him being around.

After dinner, I posted on my Instagram while drinking hot tea and soaking my feet. Finished with today’s chores, I crawled into my dark cave of a bed.

Today I am thankful to have met Nobody. To call a man with his earthy energy my friend. He is a kind soul who shared his wisdom about finding happiness in life. I’m also thankful for Sandy, aka Wings a true trail angel in Ridgecrest where I felt right at home the moment I stepped in the door.
