Warriors vs. Pelicans player grades: Yaxel Lendeborg leads the way in a rough game

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Well, that was a dud. The Golden State Warriors have looked so impressive when Steph Curry is off the court this year … but only in the games where he starts on the court. In their second game since Curry's abdominal injury, the Warriors once again looked completely lost … and once again needed more than six minutes to score their first field goal.

The end result was a 115-94 loss to the New Orleans Pelicans but, if we're being honest, the game wasn't nearly that close. So let's run through the grades, because nobody wants to spend much time on this one. As always, grades are based on my expectations for each player, with a "B" grade representing the average performance for that player.

Note: True-shooting percentage (TS) is a scoring efficiency metric that accounts for threes and free throws. Entering Monday's games, league-average TS was 57.4%.

33 minutes, 28 points, 8 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 steal, 2 blocks, 4 turnovers, 2 fouls, 9-for-23 shooting, 2-for-9 threes, 8-for-8 free throws, 52.8% TS, -30

Lendeborg had the worst game of his young career on Friday, in his first taste of being The Man at the NBA level. He responded by calling himself out and cutting his hair.

For a while it seemed like this game might be a similar outcome, but as time wore on, he really started to figure things out. Lendeborg, like all of his teammates, couldn't find the bottom of the hoop in the first half, but he poured in 20 second-half points, including 10 in the first few minutes of the third quarter as he almost single-handedly tried (albeit unsuccessfully) to get the Dubs back in it.

The resilience was noteworthy. As was the defense which, while far from perfect, featured some really impressive hands. Lendeborg has a bit of Andre Iguodala's ability to swipe at the ball while moving backwards and still avoid fouling, and it's very impressive. It disrupted Zion Williamson on a few occasions.

There's still a lot of work to do if he wants to lead the team to victory in Curry's absence, but he showed some signs in this one.

Grade: B–Post-game bonus: Led the team in points; tied for the team lead in rebounds; worst plus/minus on the team.

25 minutes, 2 points, 8 rebounds, 5 assists, 1 steal, 1 block, 4 fouls, 1-for-3 shooting, 0-for-1 threes, 33.3% TS, -13

Just kind of a whatever game for Green. He didn't really do much on either side of the ball to put the Warriors in decent position, but also didn't do anything to hurt them. His defense was okay, but not as good as it often is. He made some nice passes and didn't turn the ball over, but wasn't really able to create any offense of his own. Not bad, all things considered; just not noteworthy, either.

Grade: B-Post-game bonus: Led the team in assists; tied for the team lead in rebounds.

14 minutes, 0 points, 5 rebounds, 3 blocks, 0-for-4 shooting, 0-for-2 threes, 0.0% TS, -17

Not a great matchup for Horford, and it didn't help that his shot wasn't falling, either. But he did have some tremendous plays on defense, which included a highlight-reel block of Williamson in the opening minutes. Three blocks in 14 minutes is pretty noteworthy!

Still, while he was patrolling the paint on some possessions, it did feel like New Orleans was just too fast and athletic for him in this game, which is why he was replaced by Charles Bassey in the starting lineup for the second half.

30 minutes, 15 points, 6 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 steal, 1 block, 5 turnovers, 2 fouls, 3-for-8 shooting, 3-for-7 threes, 6-for-7 free throws, 67.7% TS, +1

Podziemski had some struggles in this game, though it's worth highlighting just how strong his offensive efficiency was. Still, against the length, speed, and athleticism of the Pelicans, Podz had a difficult time penetrating the defense, and took just one shot inside the arc all night long (though he did draw some fouls). Podziemski has a nice jumper, and his three ball has been good this year, but his game works best when it's working inside out, rather than outside in. The lefty is at his best when he's penetrating the defense, getting looks in the lane, and then parlaying that threat into outside looks, and that wasn't the case today.

The bigger issue, though, is that with Curry out, the Warriors need Podziemski to function as a facilitator of sorts, and it's hard to do that when you turn the ball over five times. He's usually very steady with the rock, but was a bit of a liability in this game when dribbling and playmaking. And it was not his best defensive effort either, I thought.

But you do have to love 15 points on 67.7% true shooting…

24 minutes, 7 points, 1 rebound, 3 assists, 1 turnover, 1 foul, 2-for-10 shooting, 1-for-8 threes, 2-for-2 free throws, 32.2% TS, -10

You can certainly understand the idea behind putting Cryer in the starting lineup, so the Warriors could have an extra dose of shooting in Curry's absence, especially with how well Cryer played last week. But it's hard to have an impact when the primary thing you bring to the table is shooting threes, and you go 1-for-8 from deep.

Cryer showed some of the things he can do to help open up this offense with Curry sidelined, but ultimately it was a very tough game for him.

I've said it before and I'll say it again: Niang is the type of player who can look really good when the team is playing well, but is unlikely to look good when they're playing poorly. He can elevate a team from good to great much more easily than from bad to good.

16 minutes, 4 points, 1 block, 2 fouls, 1-for-2 shooting, 2-for-2 free throws, 69.4% TS, -5

This was Bassey's worst game in a while, though even at that, he replaced Horford in the starting lineup for the second half as the team sought more athleticism in the paint. He had some nice plays, but it's hard to impact the game as a center when you play 16 minutes and don't get any rebounds, though he had some good defensive possessions still.

21 minutes, 8 points, 2 rebounds, 1 assist, 3 turnovers, 3 fouls, 3-for-7 shooting, 2-for-6 threes, 57.1% TS, -20

An okay scoring effort for Melton, but, like Podziemski, he really struggled to do anything against New Orleans' defense. He couldn't penetrate to get shots at the rim or create … one shot inside the arc and one assist, against three turnovers, just won't cut it for Melton, especially when Curry is out. Also one of his more poor defensive games of the year, I thought.

19 minutes, 12 points, 7 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 turnover, 1 foul, 2-for-6 shooting, 1-for-3 threes, 7-for-8 free throws, 63.0% TS, -7

It was a really tough game for the bench, but Santos did some very nice things. He looks much healthier than he had in recent weeks, and this was his first double-digit scoring output since the fourth game of the season. It's funny: sometimes Santos seems to be bothered more than most of the team by length and athleticism, and sometimes he seems to handle it better than the rest of the team. This game was squarely the latter, as he combatted New Orleans' size and speed by being extra aggressive, and couldn't be kept off the glass.

23 minutes, 11 points, 3 rebounds, 1 assist, 2 steals, 1 turnover, 4 fouls, 4-for-11 shooting, 2-for-6 threes, 1-for-1 free throws, 48.1% TS, -8

First and foremost, Richard's toughness needs constant praising. It seems quite possible that the plan for Richard is to play through the pain until the team gets healthier, and then forgo the second half of the season to have surgery for the torn UCL in his thumb. It could end up costing Richard the chance to play during a stretch run, and he deserves to be commended for toughing out the pain, and for putting the team first in just his second NBA season.

He's on the court for defense primarily, but he was one of the team's most aggressive scorers in this game. It didn't always work, as the shooting efficiency points to, but it was very notable, and it helped keep the Pelicans' defense honest. I liked seeing him in attack mode, and hopefully we get more of that going forward.

8 minutes, 2 points, 1-for-1 shooting, 100.0% TS, +6

Conventional wisdom might make it seem like Payton should be getting more minutes with Curry and Brandon Williams sidelined, but in reality, it seems to push GPII further down the depth chart, since the Dubs really need some offense to replace that high-octane duo, and Payton is a defense first (and second) guard. Still, he did some good things in his limited time in this game.

I don't like that it usually comes under such unfortunate circumstances, but I really am enjoying every chance we get to watch Ike. The undrafted rookie, who is on a two-way contract, really filled out the box score in his limited time in this game.

6 minutes, 2 points, 1 rebound, 1-for-3 shooting, 33.3% TS, +8

Even though he was on the team last year, Leons is still a bit more raw than the other two-way contracts on the team, Ike and Cryer. But he's still good for a few eye-opening plays when he gets on the court. He definitely has a lot of talent.
