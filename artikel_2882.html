Knicks Bulletin: ‘Ball don’t lie!’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Just when you thought the Knicks would make you fall for another fake comeback attempt…

…it was Toronto who whiffed miserably, with Scottie Barnes bricking the Raptors' road to a loss.

Here's what I gathered from Sunday's overtime dub.

"Good win. I played Jalen 41 minutes, KAT 43 and OG 44.5—I prefer not to do that, but I felt I needed them on the floor

"Landry, big spark on both ends of the floor, shot very well. Mikal Defensive Player of Game, really good, especially in the second half and late, fantastic.

"Our screening down stretch, fantastic. Talked about being physical and we were—screening, putting pressure on the paint in off-ball situations… It was beautiful.

"Last two games we lost the possession game bad––tonight we won it 23-19 (in terms of FTA + offensive rebounds – turnovers). The whole second half and overtime, seven turnovers, huge for us."

On the importance of having versatile bench players who can contribute in lesser roles:

"In order to be a good team, you have to have guys who can contribute in different ways off the bench. Whether it's defensively or changing up the tempo or style of play. Offensively, knocking down shots, setting screens, getting offensive rebounds or whatever it is. You want to have guys who can impact the game without them getting outside of their box and causing them to do something that's got everyone like, 'Woah!' That's what we feel we have with our bench. I can throw this guy in, and then I can throw that guy in."

On Josh Hart's struggles and his confidence that the veteran will recover:

"Josh is a pro. And he's been in this league long enough to understand what he needs to do to continue to get himself back on track because at the end of the day, we need him.

"And he's a big part of what we do on both ends of the floor. And everybody knows that. And it's only a matter of time before he works his way out of it. Same thing for everybody else. Mikal was going through it a little and the last couple of games he strung some good ball games together. And I don't see it any different for Josh."

On Landry Shamet's offensive impact and importance in the Knicks' comeback against Toronto:

"Landry was huge for us because offensively he's got to be guarded so he creates space for everybody else to be able to attack the rim. He shot the ball well. If you relax defensively on him, he's going to go from point A to point B at a pretty high clip. He's got a really good sidestep dribble to shoot a 3. He was extremely important for us — especially down the stretch."

On the importance of winning an ugly overtime game after consecutive losses:

"Yeah, it's always good to win close ballgames like this. Especially when you don't feel like you played particularly well. And you give Toronto a lot of credit because they were physical and they try to push the pace and they got their hands on the ball a couple of times. So for us to be able to find a way to get a win in a very ugly game was huge.

"You want to win games like this, because any time you can, especially when you lost a couple in a row, it helps with that belief. And that's a huge component of what our standard or what our culture is about. So any time you can do that, it's a good thing."

On preferring to preserve his starters a bit more and avoiding Toronto-like games:

"Good win by our guys, man. Obviously, if I can help it, I don't want to play OG, KAT and Jalen – I played Jalen 41 minutes, KAT 43 minutes and OG 44.5 minutes.

"If I cannot do that, I prefer not to do that, but I felt I needed them on the floor tonight, and so they got extended minutes."

On Mikal Bridges earning Defensive Player of the Game against Toronto:

"Mikal was DPOG. Could've also gone to OG"

On the Knicks' bench players consistently stepping up and helping the team develop:

"It's really unique for our team, and it's helpful. Guys come in ready to play. The things we've been able to do as a team in this short season, and obviously, we've had ups and downs, but guys have been ready. We're building. Just because we don't win every single game, I do think we do things right in games. It might not result in a win but it helps us grow as a team.

"Guys come in ready to play every single days, and that's been the journey of our season."

On making the decisive overtime jumper against the Raptors:

"I work on that shot, and I'm happy it went in."

On the Knicks refusing to give up despite trailing Toronto late:

"We know what we are capable of in those situations. I'm just happy the way we fought. We could've easily given up, chalked it up to the next game. But we kept fighting."

On the Knicks rediscovering their identity during the comeback against Toronto:

"We worked hard for this one. We played like the Knicks again. This team never stops playing."

On the Knicks' resilience and fighting spirit against the Raptors:

"This team never stops fighting man and we did a great job tonight of continuing to fight, show that resilience that was so special and magical about our team last year in a hostile arena, too, against a team that's playing really well."

"When we execute at a high level and play with that physicality and energy that makes us so special last year, we're a very hard team to beat."

On the need for developing championship habits throughout the regular season:

"You win a championship, you see how important a lot of things are, and every day, you want to grow those championship habits. Winning a championship doesn't start after Game 82; it starts way before Game 82. Game 5, game 10, game 20, those games really matter. Championship habits are built every single day, brick by brick."

On feeling physically younger and healthier after slimming down:

"My body feels better than it has in a long time. Science, scans, they all show it. A lot of science shows that I'm doing a good job of taking care of my body, aging backwards."

On losing weight last summer to protect his knees and maintain his athleticism:

"As much as I could get some pounding and weight off my knees, it's helped me be athletic still 12 years in. It's just a philosophy, something I believe in. It's done me pretty damn well in my career. God has been good to me to have the health that I've been able to have, even with the little things, the nicks and bruises I've had in my career, it's always been something that's been very good."

On prioritizing his availability and taking care of his body:

"As long as I can play, I play. I just do a great time taking care of my body, I always have."

On breaking Willis Reed's franchise record for consecutive double-doubles to start a season:

"Willis Reed, in my opinion, is always going to be a better Knick than me. A kid from Hico, Louisiana. Came over to New York, set the precedent, set the table for someone like me to give me an opportunity to even see the baton. … So I don't look at it as breaking a record. I'm thankful that the Willis Reed family has allowed me to live this dream and be even in the same breath as him."

On the Knicks refusing to give up despite having several opportunities to concede defeat:

"We had a number of opportunities to say 'not our night' but stayed in it. Think that's what I'm proud of. Our mentality and approach didn't waver. We buckled down, and got better"

On the importance of winning a close game after struggling in similar situations:

"That feels good. That was our time of the season where it's like, 'Let's buckle down. Let's go and work our way back for this one.' We had a few early in the year last year and obviously in the playoffs, but to get one under the belt and win one that way — that's an encouraging win for the group where maybe things didn't go the way you wanted to at times."

"They won the championship, and that's what championship teams do. They stay resilient; they stay through those moments. That's what they did; they just figured out a way to come back into that game and win."

"His footwork creates angles and he attacks those angles. He's got the turnaround, he's got the step through… They have KAT, great 3-point shooter. Still gotta worry about those two. He's got the pull-up—left pull-up, right step-back, both ways. That's what he does"

On Mikal Bridges' defensive intensity changing the game against Toronto:

"Then Mikal really in that, what was that, that fourth quarter, I think third or fourth quarter, Mikal, he got like four or five stops, probably like two steals. He was just being super aggressive, being super physical, which kind of really changed the game for them. I feel like, and letting them come back into the game."
