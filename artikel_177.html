Kenya cult leader Paul Mackenzie sentenced to 200 years

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

A court in Kenya has sentenced Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison for the murders of 190 children and an adult. He told them to starve themselves to death as part of his apocalyptic religious cult.

Kenyan religious sect leader Paul Mackenzie was sentenced to 200 years in prison on Friday, at the culmination of a trial following the discovery of hundreds of corpses in the woods.

A self-proclaimed pastor and longstanding leader of a new age Christian apocalyptic cult called the Good News International Church, Mackenzie was convicted in October of 191 counts of murder in the deaths of one adult and 190 children.

Justice Diana Kavedza said the sentence was comprised of 100 years for 183 counts of murder by starvation and another 100 years for eight counts of murder by direct homicide.

He could have faced the death penalty, which is still technically on the books in Kenya.

However, like several countries that have kept capital punishment in their legal code but stopped actually using it, Kenya has not executed anybody since 1987.

While the charges against Mackenzie pertained to 191 deaths, some 400 people were found dead in what became known as the "Shakahola Forest Massacre." Mackenzie's so-called church had been operating since 2003, based in the coastal town of Malindi.

Mackenzie had denied the charges, saying that he did not force the cult followers to starve.

A total of 28 accomplices were given various prison sentences for their roles in the deaths.

They included guards who enforced his fasting directives and gravediggers who helped conceal the dead in shallow graves. They all denied the charges, except for one who pleaded guilty and asked for leniency.

Among them was Mackenzie's assistant "pastor," Smart Mwakalama, who was sentenced to 160 years.

Judge Kavedza dismissed the argument from some of the accused that the fact they had lost their own children altered the nature of their involvement in the deaths of others.

More than 60 of the bodies were positively identified and reburied by their relatives, but many of the remains were so decomposed that they could not be identified or claimed.
