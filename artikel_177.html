Hundreds protest against Nathan Cofnas talk on DEI at Oriel College in Oxford

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Students oppose appearance of self-described ‘race realist’ who made plagiarism claims against Jason Arday

Hundreds of protesters filled the cobbled streets around Oriel College, Oxford, to voice their opposition to an appearance by Nathan Cofnas, the self-described “race realist”.

The event organised by the university’s Reform UK student society – billed as “An evening with Nathan Cofnas: is DEI undermining higher education?” – went ahead on Tuesday evening despite opposition from students, college staff and local politicians concerned that his appearance would create a hostile environment.

Oriel, which allowed the Reform society to use a lecture theatre, declined to cancel the booking, saying it had a “statutory duty” to allow the blogger and academic to appear.

Earlier this year, Cofnas was the first to raise accusations of plagiarism against Prof Jason Arday.

Arday died weeks later, having resigned from his post as professor at the University of Cambridge, amid sustained media scrutiny which began with Cofnas’s claims.

The crowds outside sought to block the college’s main entrances, with hundreds chanting “Say his name – professor Jason Arday”, while several protesters held signs reading “Remember professor Jason Arday”.

One of the protesters, a first-year student studying English and German, who declined to give her name, said: “I think, given the situation at Cambridge with Jason Arday and given what happened, that we as an institution would be looking to not replicate that in any way or hinting that we could support that notion, [but] acting as an example in opposition.

“I think [Oxford and Cambridge], as two academic institutions, should be working together to banish this from ever happening again. And the fact that we can’t even act as the example, I think, makes me almost ashamed to be a part of this.”

Tommy, a second-year maths student at Oxford, said: “This is not the first of this kind of event. There was Tommy Robinson and the Oxford Union, and obviously there were protests for that as well. So I think it’s quite a nasty pattern that we’re seeing with these Oxford societies pandering to the far right, which is just really dangerous.”

Inside the lecture theatre, Cofnas was asked if he wanted to apologise to Arday’s family, but he declined. He said the implication behind the question was that “we shouldn’t criticise powerful figures”.

Cofnas added: “I would ask the people at Cambridge University who protected him for three years, who suppressed all criticism … I would ask them these questions,” earning a brief round of applause.

About 40 people made it into the venue, with less than a third of the space filled. Protests outside could be heard throughout Cofnas’s talk. At times some members of the audience appeared disengaged, looking at their phones.

Cofnas spent the majority of an hour talking about the history of what he called the “race equality thesis”, and his reasons for questioning it.

He has previously written that black people would “disappear from almost all high-profile positions outside of sports and entertainment” under a meritocratic system.

Anneliese Dodds, the MP for Oxford East, said she had contacted Oriel College after receiving complaints from constituents.

Dodds said: “Cofnas’ views are both repellent and contrary to the facts. Many of my student constituents have therefore contacted me to express their concerns about the student reform society choosing to provide him with publicity.

“I have written to Oriel College to ask what they are doing to act on students’ concerns about this meeting, including on safety grounds.”

While the protests were largely peaceful, a group of police appeared towards the end of the event to clear protesters from the entrances.

Hannah, a first-year law student, said that the size of the protest was proof that “even if Oxford won’t listen to us and won’t cancel the event, we’re still going to show up and we’re going to show that we won’t let that decision silence us. And we will still show that we don’t approve of this and we don’t want this type of behaviour here.”

Misan Harriman, the photographer and former chair of trustees of the Southbank Centre, said he attended the protest because he was concerned by the “damaging” rhetoric that Cofnas espoused.

“The university has failed all the students, not just the black students, by having them see this as something that could be remotely seen as part of the course of free speech,” Harriman said.

Additional reporting by Stanley Smith and Beatrix Arnold.
