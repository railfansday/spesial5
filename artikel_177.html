Friday night will be ‘coldest of the autumn so far’, UK forecasters say

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Lows will be ‘severe’ in the north, down to -9C, with some sub-zero temperatures farther south

Friday night will be the coldest night of the autumn so far, forecasters have said, surpassing the previous chilliest: Thursday.

A low of -8.4C was recorded at Tulloch Bridge in the Scottish Highlands overnight, according to the Met Office. Sub-zero temperatures were also recorded in Northern Ireland. More southerly areas stayed substantially warmer overnight on Thursday.

The Met Office said frost and rain, particularly present across northern parts of the UK in the morning, are largely expected to ease through the day on Friday, with some sunshine. But the frost will return fairly quickly as the sun sets, before another cold night.

“It will turn a lot colder as we head through into the weekend,” said meteorologist Clare Nasir.

“Through this evening and overnight, any residual spits and spots of rain will tend to clear as we see starry skies evolve and that frost extends southwards through the night.

“It will be severe in the north, so we could see temperatures as low as around -8C, -9C in one or two glens. And, farther south, we’re expecting some sub-zero temperatures. A cold end tonight, even some freeze and fog patches, particularly across central, southern areas and in Northern Ireland.”

For most, Saturday is forecast to be dry with some sunshine. It follows the first snow warning of the season, with more than an inch recorded in parts of northern England on Tuesday.

A yellow cold health alert is in force for the north-west, the north-east and Yorkshire and the Humber until 9am on Monday. The alerts mean weather conditions could result in an increased use of healthcare services and a greater risk to life for vulnerable people, according to the UK Health Security Agency. Yellow alerts indicate a moderate likelihood and level of impact.

There are 12 flood alerts in place for England, indicating flooding is possible, after a spell of wet weather.

On Friday it was reported that sales of clothing had increased at large supermarkets – linked to more typical autumn weather after a warm September had affected initial demand for autumn and winter clothing lines. Clothing and footwear stores reported 1.5% growth in sales volumes as a result.

This contributed to an unexpected recovery of sales overall across the retail sector, the ONS reported, with economists having predicted a marginal decline of 0.1% for the month.
