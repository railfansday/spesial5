Grand Theft Auto VI has arrived

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

More than a decade after Grand Theft Auto V was first released, its sequel is finally launching. On November 19th, GTA VI will be available on both the PS5 and Xbox Series X / S, and it’s quite possibly the most anticipated video game launch of all time.

Part of this has to do with the success of its predecessor, as GTA V sold more than 230 million copies over the last 13 years. Some of it has to do with the sheer length of time between releases, a period that was plagued by leaks and a handful of delays that made the wait even harder for fans.

But GTA VI is now a real thing you can play, and you can also keep track of all of the important post-launch information right here. That includes our own review and interviews with the development team, as well as news on just how the game ends up resonating with players.

We finally have more details about GTA VI’s graphics, including that it’s upscaling 1440p to 4K on base PS5 and Xbox Series X, but PSSR2 on the PS5 Pro.

AMD’s FSR Upscaling 4 is used to scale the resolution from 720p to 1080p on Series S. Digital Foundry explains how input lag factored into those choices, as well as the differences between platforms, how well it holds to the target 30fps framerate, plus the impact of VRR.

With Grand Theft Auto VI set to finally launch tomorrow, November 19th, our long-awaited return to Vice City is nearly here. But after all this time, some of the gear you’ve used in San Andreas might feel out of place on the glamorous streets of Leonidas County. Luckily for you, we’ve gathered a few of our favorite Xbox and PlayStation 5 accessories to help your long gaming sessions run as smoothly as possible.

The 8BitDo Ultimate 3E — an upgraded pick for Xbox Series X and Series S consoles — is a very capable, comfortable controller with elite-level features, including a high polling rate and adjustable triggers. It’s on the expensive side at $149.99, but the customization and clean execution justify the cost. Read our review.

It took 13 years, a reported 10-figure budget, and the weight of the world’s expectations, but Grand Theft Auto VI is here. And it’s fantastic. The Verge’s Andrew Webster takes a break from the game to tell us all about life in Vice City, and why GTA VI’s approach to coffee tells us everything we need to know.

Lost in the excitement of GTA VI finally launching is the fact that Rockstar employees are in the process of voting on whether or not to unionize. Developer Nestor Uriarte explains the importance of the moment in an essay in The Guardian:

Winning union recognition would be a huge step forward in securing a meaningful say over our conditions at Rockstar, but its significance would reach far beyond that. As one of the biggest and most influential game studios in the world, what happens here could open the door for other workers across the UK to imagine an alternative future for the industry – one in which workers’ voices are taken into account more.

Now that reviews have dropped for the game, it has skyrocketed to the top of Metacritic’s highest-rated list, surpassing Ocarina of Time (the original, not the remake). Of course, this could change as more reviews trickle in, but for now it’s safe to say that GTA VI was worth the long wait.

Grand Theft Auto VI isn’t just the biggest GTA game to date; it’s also the most romantic. Though the series is well known for its sordid assortment of criminal leads, GTA VI switches things up by starring a pair of thieves who also happen to fall in love after meeting while stealing a car. It’s still an open-world action game full of chaos and mayhem. But many players will also be attempting to nurture the budding relationship in between heists. The sprawling game is designed to support both audiences.

“It’s a risk,” explains Rob Nelson, producer on GTA VI and co-studio head at developer Rockstar North. “It’s a real change for Grand Theft Auto. You can still play it the old way, you can reject all of this stuff. But for people who do engage, it’s meaningful — you’re earning it, you’re working at it, just like in a normal relationship.”

How do you follow a game like Grand Theft Auto V? Thirteen years ago, Rockstar’s crime-fueled epic went on to redefine what an open-world game could be and became an enormous success, selling more than 230 million copies across three generations of game consoles. It sits just behind Tetris and Minecraft on the list of the best-selling games of all time, and its lucrative online mode has helped it remain popular for well over a decade.

That level of success has allowed Rockstar to take its time with a sequel, which has further increased the hype around the next GTA. A series of major leaks and delays only exacerbated this. At a time when much of the video game industry is struggling, the next edition of GTA stands alone, a cultural moment so big (almost) everybody has been forced to get out of the way. The buildup to Grand Theft Auto VI’s launch is unlike anything the game industry has ever seen.

Sony has confirmed the 1.267GB download is mandatory to play the new Grand Theft Auto when it launches at midnight tonight. As with most PlayStation firmware patches, we don’t know what it does though, beyond “some security fixes to the system software.”

Rockstar hasn’t publicly said who is playing the two leads in GTA VI, but The Game Awards’ nominee video says that Manni L. Perez has been nominated for Best Performance for her work in the game.

GTA VI earned eight nominations, the most of any title, including one for Game of the Year.

Arion Kurtaj, a member of the Lapsus$ cybercrime group who leaked 90 videos of Grand Theft Auto VI in 2022 and hacked other big companies, pleaded guilty on Monday, according to the UK’s Crown Prosecution Service (CPS).

Kurtaj, who is now 21, hacked BT/EE sometime between August and November 2021, and starting in early November of that year, the company “noted a marked increase in unauthorised SIM swaps of their customers mobile phones.” He was charged for those offenses and moved to a hotel for his “own protection” in September 2022, but “breached his bail conditions to carry out further hacks on major companies using a mobile phone, the hotel TV’s Wi-Fi and a firestick,” including hacks on Rockstar Games, Uber, and Revolut, the CPS says.
