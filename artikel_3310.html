‘Percy Jackson’: How A Quest To Save Annabeth Makes Everyone Reevaluate Their Relationships In Season 3

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

SPOILER ALERT! This post contains some details from the first two episodes of Percy Jackson and the Olympians Season 3.

If there is one thing that the kids of Camp Half-Blood can count on, it’s that their lives are only going to get more complicated.

Percy Jackson and the Olympians returned to Disney+ for Season 3 on Friday, and already the dynamics have shifted. The first episode opens with Percy (Walker Scobell) and Annabeth (Leah Sava Jeffries) recruiting the recently revived child of Zeus, Thalia (Tamara Smart), to help them rescue two younger demigods — Nico and Bianca di Angelo, the children of Hades — and the tension is palpable. “I think that Annabeth’s presence is actually incredibly divisive between the two of them,” Smart tells Deadline of the dynamic between Thalia and Percy. Watch on Deadline

In terms of sowing that division on screen, she adds that “a lot of it is based in our actual relationship in the best of ways.”

“Walker also has the exact same personality and sense of humor. So, right right off the bat, we were definitely going back and forth and joking, and I think the only thing we had to do was just up the stakes and be serious about it, which was actually more difficult than we planned,” she says.

Percy and Thalia can’t agree on almost anything, except their loyalty to Annabeth, who has unwittingly been thrown in the middle of their power struggle at the start of the season.

So, when Annabeth is captured and brought to an enemy stronghold where she’s forced to bear the weight of the sky (the eponymous Titan’s Curse), those two have to set aside their differences quite quickly.

“She kind of kept them at a safe distance away from each other, because, as I guess we’ve seen the trailer, bad things happen when they’re not at that safe distance,” Scobell says. “So I think without her there, they’re forced to actually work together and try to figure each other out and attempt to understand each other.”

Adds Smart: “They actually have to be forced into proximity to work together to get her back, because that’s their mutual interest. So, it’s really beautiful in a way that her absence brings them closer, which is a very human thing for us. I think as people, we’re just always brought closer by tragedy. But I think that for them specifically, a lot of ego has to get out the door for them to work together to achieve their mutual goal.”

Rather than put a wedge in Annabeth’s relationship with either, Jeffries thinks that the tension between Percy and Thalia also provides some clarity for the daughter of Athena. Whether she’s ready to face that realization yet is another story.

“I honestly feel like you get to see more Percabeth when it’s between Percy and Thalia,” Jeffries says. “I feel like her eyes open up a little bit. Annabeth is very smart. Even though she’s making it look like she’s not noticing, she knows [she’s developing feelings for Percy]. I don’t know if that’s more of like a denial between her of like, ‘No, no, I’m working right now. I’m on missions. I don’t have time to be falling in love…’

She continues: “You get to see the fight [in her] more, not physically, but just like ‘No, I love this person. You’re not gonna take him away from me.'”
