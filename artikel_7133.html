Dallas ISD to close 6 schools as it grapples with $128M deficit

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Texas district fell 3,099 students short of projections this school year as the state faces broader enrollment declines.

Districts nationwide have had to approve school closures and consolidations in recent years as declining birthrates and other factors have fueled drops in enrollment.

Among the six Dallas ISD schools set for closure are three early childhood centers, two elementary schools and one middle school.

Throughout Texas, districts in Houston, Austin, Fort Bend, Judson and Corpus Christi have all approved school closure or consolidation plans since the 2025-26 school year.

The state’s total public school enrollment is about 5.3 million this school year, down from 5.5 million in the 2024-25 school year, according to Texas Education Agency data. But this year’s enrollment is still higher than in 2012-13, when there were nearly 5.1 million students.

Morath also pointed to declining birthrates as a key factor in Texas’ decreasing enrollment. But he noted, however, that the share of school-aged children enrolled in public schools has dipped over a 10-year period.

For instance, almost 90% of school-aged Texas children attended public schools in 2015 versus about 86% in 2025. That decline of that share of students has accelerated since the COVID-19 pandemic, Morath said.

The same week Dallas ISD approved its school closures, Ohio’s Mentor Public Schools finalized its own consolidation plan, which includes closing three elementary schools in 2027-28. Mentor Public Schools also cited ongoing enrollment declines in its decision to close schools.

“The reason we're consolidating our schools is to reduce our operating costs by running fewer school buildings across the district,” said Craig Heath, Mentor Public Schools superintendent, in a Nov. 18 district video. “Because the more we can save on operating our buildings, the more resources we can dedicate to preserving the programs and opportunities we offer our students.”

The Ohio district anticipates saving $3.4 million from its consolidation plan during the 2027-28 school year, which would amount to $17 million in total savings in the next five years.
