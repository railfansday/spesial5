Tokenized stocks have often promised easier access and faster settlement, but a less glamorous question keeps surfacing: can the holder vote? Broadridge and Dinari announced an integration on November 5 that aims to connect eligible holders of Dinari's dShares to established proxy-voting infrastructure. The development highlights shareholder governance as a core feature of credible tokenization, not an optional extra on a trading app.

According to the companies, eligible dShare holders can exercise voting rights associated with the underlying US equities through Broadridge's proxy systems. Those votes are intended to enter the same formal processes used by traditional shareholders. This is more concrete than giving token holders a separate community poll about a company. Shareholder resolutions, board elections and other corporate actions depend on recognized ownership records, deadlines and entitlement calculations. The release describes an integration of these processes, although it does not publish a full end-to-end test record for each supported company.

A user buying exposure to a company's share may assume the resulting token comes with the same rights as a share in a conventional brokerage account. That assumption is dangerous unless the product's legal structure supports it. Some tokens are claims on intermediaries, while others are issued and serviced in ways designed to preserve shareholder entitlements. Dinari's integration addresses part of that problem by moving voting instructions through established channels. Investors should still verify which tokens qualify, whether voting is available in their jurisdiction and how any intermediary custody arrangements affect their position.

Broadridge already handles a large volume of investor communications and governance workflows in traditional markets. Connecting these systems to a tokenized product can be more practical than reconstructing every corporate-action process from scratch. That matters because an issuer cannot simply ignore events such as stock splits, tender offers or changes in the shareholder register. There may also be timing conflicts between token transfer windows and voting record dates. The harder task is keeping balances, entitlements and instruction chains aligned as holders move between wallets and supported intermediaries.

An improved voting experience does not automatically make a tokenized share freely transferable, exchange-listed or protected identically in every jurisdiction. Eligibility, investor verification and local securities rules may still apply. Likewise, a voting feature says little about trading liquidity or the robustness of the underlying custody model. Broadridge and Dinari's announcement is best understood as one part of the market's operational maturation. It makes a necessary ownership function more visible, but cannot settle every legal or commercial question around tokenized equities.

Useful evidence would include the number of supported issuers, actual voting participation by eligible holders, records of successful proxy delivery and support for other corporate actions. For asset managers and platforms considering tokenized stock distribution, governance infrastructure is a test of whether the product behaves like an investment or merely tracks its price. That distinction will become more important as institutions offer these instruments to a broader audience.
