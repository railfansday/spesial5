Former White Sox, Mets Pitcher Lance Broadway Dead at 43: 'Heartbreaking'

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Lance Broadway, a former pitcher for the Chicago White Sox and New York Mets, died unexpectedly on Tuesday, November 17. He was 43.

“Some heartbreaking news: Former TCU and DBU pitcher Lance Broadway has passed away,” Jamie Plunkett, a Texas Christian University sports reporter for HornedFrogBlitz.com, announced via X on Wednesday, November 18.

“Broadway earned 2005 CUSA Pitcher of the Year and First-Team All-American honors from Baseball America, posting a 1.61 ERA and 151 strikeouts in 117 innings in his lone season as a Horned Frog,” Plunkett continued. “Following that season, he was selected 15th overall in the 2005 MLB Draft by the Chicago White Sox. He made his MLB Debut on Sept. 7, 2007, and also spent time with the Mets and Blue Jays organizations.”

The post concluded, “Broadway also briefly stepped into acting, appearing in the 2013 film Olympus Has Fallen as well as a handful of television and short film projects.”

A cause of death has not yet been revealed.

Broadway was born in Bryan, Texas, in August 1983. He began his college career as a pitcher for Dallas Baptist University while majoring in communications. He earned two all-conference honors at the school before transferring to TCU his junior year.

After college, Broadway spent two years with the White Sox from 2007 to 2009 before he was traded to the Mets in exchange for veteran catcher Ramon Castro.

During his time with the Mets, Broadway posted a 6.75 ERA in 14 and two-thirds innings in the 2009 season. He was non-tendered after the season ended and briefly signed with the Toronto Blue Jays in December 2009.

Broadway retired from baseball after the 2010 season and ventured into acting. Along with Olympus Has Fallen, he appeared on the TV shows Saint George, Kevin From Work, Lucifer, Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. and Colony.

“I originally … in 2010 was my last [MLB] season and in every offseason I wanted to get involved with something,” Broadway told Screen Slam’s Shawn Cauthen in 2013. “And I don’t know why, but I just wanted to go take an improv class. The improv class I wanted didn’t start until January 2011, so I asked a friend who told me to go take an acting class. That way, at least I will be in front of people doing something while they watch. So I got into an acting class in November 2010. I loved it and stuck with it.”
