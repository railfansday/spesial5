Arsenal nous frustrates Liverpool as 14-year Anfield hoodoo ends with statement win

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

It is clear and obvious that Arsenal will be the team to beat in the Premier League title race, especially given that we do not yet know the fate of faltering Manchester City and their looming punishment.

A statement victory against Liverpool, their first in the league at Anfield since 2012, took Arsenal back to the top. And who would bet against them staying there and retaining their title?

Few would when they defend and manage a game – and it was game management in every sense – like this. From the formidable to the cynical – that was the statement. Against the Scousers, Arsenal had the nous.

“The mission was something we didn’t complete in 14 years,” Mikel Arteta said, having famously – in an Amazon documentary series – played You’ll Never Walk Alone to his players as they walked out to train to try and overcome a perceived mental block.

“There are reasons for that. Some of it psychological, some of it moments we did not nail on the pitch,” the Arsenal manager added of the winless run.

The result means that the north Londoners have already now won away at Villa Park, the Stadium of Light, the City Ground and Anfield. Stadiums where they dropped points last season. And that feels ominous.

A win would maybe have thrust Liverpool into the title race. Instead, under Andoni Iraola, and with an evolving young team – with promising 19-year-old midfielder Trey Nyoni handed his league debut – they are already nine points behind after just nine matches.

It is also clear, however, that they could have prevented this defeat having somehow been denied a first-half penalty after Alexander Isak’s shirt was blatantly pulled by Jurrien Timber as the striker tried to head home a cross.

Referee Andy Madley may have missed it, but what was the excuse of Stuart Attwell, the VAR? It was a stunningly poor decision – another bad day for Pro Ref and the use of technology.

It might be argued that Timber did not pull hard enough to prevent Isak from reaching the ball. But the defender grabbed the shirt and continued to hold it for a long time. It had to be a penalty. Little wonder Iraola said Isak should dive next time. Who can blame him?

What is also clear and obvious is that, once again, Arsenal’s resilience, defensive determination and appetite for toughing it out will be their greatest weapons. They are certainly, under Arteta, not here to win friends – just to win matches and trophies.

“That defensive line, that togetherness we have is undefeated. It’s something I have never seen before,” Declan Rice, who was also a stand-out performer and delivered a fine assist for the winner, said.

Arsenal’s version of the Yellow Wall was, indeed, hugely impressive – it is now six clean sheets from their nine league games and, despite all their pressure, Liverpool did not have a shot on target. That has never happened in the Premier League since such statistics started to be compiled in 2003 (422 games ago).

“That’s incredible. It did not feel like it on the pitch. Again, it’s the commitment of every single player,” Arteta said. And that was indisputable.

Even the expected second-half momentum, the expected surge of attack after attack as Liverpool faced the Kop, crashed on Arsenal’s back line. The visitors were so well-drilled that they stifled space – they refused to crumble.

Arsenal put their bodies on the line with blocks and tackles and a bit of time-wasting. There were dark arts, especially from both centre-halves with Gabriel going unpunished after claiming he was struck in the face by Isak early on – VAR anyone? – and the otherwise outstanding Ezri Konsa throwing himself to the ground after minimal contact from the Liverpool centre-forward.

In fairness there was also a bit of that from an increasingly frustrated Liverpool with Alexis Mac Allister going down after touching heads with Noni Madueke, having already tried to provoke Martin Odegaard with a hand to the head when the Arsenal captain was on the turf. That was not good from Mac Allister either.

And the only goal? Again, more frustration for Liverpool. It was well-constructed from Arsenal, but only after Alisson Becker’s loose goal kick, aimed towards Jérémy Jacquet, who was slow to react, was intercepted by Odegaard with Bruno Guimarães poking the ball to the right, where it was collected by Madueke.

There was an overlap by Rice and his low centre was precise and dangerous across the face of the six-yard area, with Virgil van Dijk unsure what to do. It ran beyond the far post where Kai Havertz side-footed home.
