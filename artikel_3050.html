Global Net Lease: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Office exposure is being cut, and noncore sales, not new stock, are funding buybacks that management says keep equity issuance off the table.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 21, 2026 at 5:13PM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Global Net Lease (GNL -0.17%) raised the lower end of its full-year adjusted funds from operations (AFFO, a real estate cash flow metric) guidance to $0.83 per share. For long-term investors, this focus on disciplined capital allocation helps protect dividend safety and long-term value.

The company is selling office properties to eliminate carrying costs and protect straight-line rent, an accounting method that averages lease payments across contract terms. Shifting capital away from office buildings protects cash flow durability across the broader portfolio.

Low stock prices make issuing new equity unappealing for funding growth. Instead, management sells noncore assets like gas stations and car washes at favorable capitalization rates (cap rates, the annual return expected from an income property) to repurchase undervalued shares.

Rising interest rates create headwinds for real estate debt refinancings. Global Net Lease insulated itself by swapping most borrowing to fixed terms, leaving zero debt maturities until late 2027.

Capital allocation stays disciplined even as borrowing costs remain volatile across commercial real estate markets. The company holds strong liquidity while prioritizing debt reduction and high-return industrial acquisitions.

Contractual rent increases anchor cash flow durability. The strategy centers on reducing office exposure while using property sales to pay down debt.
