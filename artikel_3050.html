Greg Abel's Berkshire Hathaway Still Holds About $18.6 Billion of Chevron Stock After Selling More Than a Third of It

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Berkshire still holds Chevron as its sixth-largest investment in its equity portfolio.

In the first quarter of 2026, Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA +0.47%) (BRKB +0.53%) sold 35% of its position in Chevron (CVX -0.41%), one of the world's largest integrated energy companies. But it increased its position by nearly 7% again in the second quarter, and left it unchanged in the third quarter. As of this writing, Berkshire still holds $18.6 billion in Chevron shares.

That's equivalent to a 4.3% stake in Chevron. It accounts for 5% of Berkshire's portfolio, and it's the company's sixth-largest stock investment. Is that a bullish signal for Chevron's future?

Chevron owns upstream, midstream, and downstream assets. However, it generates most of its profits from its upstream extraction business, which benefits the most from rising oil prices. It has a presence in 180 countries, but it still gets most of its oil and natural gas from the U.S., Kazakhstan, and Australia. It has less exposure to the Middle East than many of its big oil peers.

Chevron expects to increase its oil and gas production by 2%-3% annually through 2030. That growth will be driven by the expansion of its Tengiz Field in Kazakhstan, drilling upgrades for its largest field in the Permian Basin, its deepwater projects in the Gulf of Mexico, and its increased exposure to Guyana, one of the world's fastest-growing oil regions, through its takeover of Hess.

Chevron's stock has rallied more than 40% over the past 12 months. Most of that rally was fueled by rising oil prices after the outbreak of the Iran war in late February. However, Chevron can still easily cover its capex and dividends as long as Brent crude oil -- currently at $95 -- remains above its corporate breakeven price of $50 per barrel.

That's why Chevron has raised its dividend annually for 39 consecutive years, and why it has a clear path toward achieving the 50 straight dividend hikes that would make it a Dividend King.

Berkshire's new CEO, Greg Abel, was previously the CEO of Berkshire Hathaway Energy. Even though Abel isn't a celebrated stock picker like Warren Buffett, it would make sense for Abel to keep Berkshire exposed to the broader energy market through Chevron.

From 2025 to 2028, analysts expect Chevron's revenue and EPS to grow at CAGRs of 5% and 26%, respectively, even if the Iran war ends and oil prices normalize. Those are stellar growth rates for a stock that trades at 14 times next year's earnings and pays a forward yield of 3.2%. That's probably why Abel kept Chevron as a top position in Berkshire's portfolio, even though it shrewdly reduced that position to more balanced levels at the beginning of the year.
