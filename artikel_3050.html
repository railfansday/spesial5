Why Isn't Walmart's Stock Bouncing Back After Weak Earnings Results?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The company barely beat analyst estimates for revenue and profitability in its third quarter of fiscal 2027.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 21, 2026 at 8:37AM EST

Walmart's (WMT +1.51%) third-quarter results, which were posted Thursday morning, certainly weren't bad. The massive retailer topped analyst estimates, if only slightly, and delivered growth in both revenue and non-GAAP, or adjusted, earnings per share (EPS).

But the company's growth rates for key metrics were lower than in the second quarter, including two that had soared in previous frames. I think this is why Walmart's stock slumped during Thursday's trading session, only to recover the following day amid investor rotation out of beleaguered tech stocks and into defensive sectors like retail.

To put some concrete numbers and percentages on this, Walmart's third-quarter revenue totaled $188.8 billion, which was 5.2% higher year over year. This was on the back of comparable sales in its core Walmart U.S. operations that rose by 2.9% when stripping out fuel sales. On a constant-currency basis, revenue was 4.5% higher at $187.6 billion.

On the bottom line, attributable net income under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) sank in that one-year stretch, falling to just under $4.95 billion from the year-ago $6.14 billion. Yet on the non-GAAP (adjusted), per-share basis that analysts and many investors consider a truer indication of profitability, there was an improvement. Adjusted EPS rose to $0.65 from third quarter 2026's $0.62 for a 4.8% increase.

Those leading fundamentals were barely high enough to outpace the consensus analyst estimates of almost $187.3 billion for headline revenue and $0.64 per share for adjusted net profit.

CEO John Furner flagged the quarter as a resounding success. This, he was quoted as saying, derived from "growing our core business and gaining market share. The strength of our omnichannel model gives us room to do even more."

That growth, however, was down from the second quarter's headline revenue increase of 5.9% and the 19% rise in adjusted EPS to $0.81. At least frame No. 3's 2.9% bump in U.S. Walmart ex-fuel "comps" (modestly) topped the previous quarter's 2.6%.

Another major growth driver, global e-commerce sales, advanced 17% year over year. This sounds very robust until it's compared to the second quarter's 23%. A smaller but fast-emerging revenue stream, the company's global advertising business, slowed to 25% year over year from the quarter-ago gain of 38%.

Walmart noted that its results were adversely affected by Big Billion Days (BBD), which occurred later in the quarter than in fiscal 2025, due to the timing of the Diwali holiday in the Hindu calendar. This meant that a large chunk of the increase in sales from BBD -- a monster shopping holiday in advance of Diwali for the company's majority-owned e-commerce business Flipkart in India -- will be booked in the current (fourth) fiscal quarter.

CFO John David Rainey said in Walmart's quarterly conference call that the growth of net sales (the bulk of its revenue) on a constant-currency basis, which was 4.4%, would have been around one percentage point higher. The timing shift also affected global e-commerce and advertising growth, although the company didn't provide estimates on the impact to those overall metrics.

This is all to say that Walmart's third quarter wasn't materially better than its predecessor, and investors are expecting more from such a powerful and historically innovative presence in the retail industry.

To its credit, for the third time this calendar year, the company's management raised certain guidance for fiscal 2027. The period's net sales on a constant-currency basis should rise from 4.9% to 5.2% over 2026; the previous range was 4% to 5%. Adjusted EPS is forecast to range from $2.84 to $2.89 per share, compared favorably with the prior $2.80 to $2.87 estimate.

Perhaps the zip the lingering BBD lift provides to fourth-quarter results will be a pleasant surprise to Mr. Market. And looking past that, the company seems poised to continue cranking out growth rates that are high for an already-huge enterprise operating in the tough and competitive retail industry. I think Walmart can still deliver for its shareholders, and I remain bullish on its stock despite the ultimately forgettable third quarter.
