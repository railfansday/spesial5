Joe Newell makes Hibernian 'new manager bounce' admission after Hearts win

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Joe Newell reckons Hibernian triumphed over Hearts in the Premier Sports Cup semi-final on Sunday despite their lack of a "traditional new manager bounce".

Hibs have struggled in the Scottish Premiership so far this season and sit rock bottom of the division. For some it remains a false position for the Easter Road outfit whose performances have perhaps merited more.

But the stats don't lie and they have only managed two wins from 11 matches in the league. As is so often the case, the form book goes out the window on derby day and Marink Reedijk's charges proved just that with their 2-1 victory over their rivals.

After David Gray was sacked back in September, Reedijk, Newell confesses, has not had the usual immediate impact on a squad that some new bosses do.

But that, the Englishman says, did not stop them from roaring into gear against their foes at Hampden and securing a spot in the final next month.

Speaking on Premier Sports' Scottish Football Social Club, Newell said: "It was two teams that went into it, they're in better form and have been doing really well with momentum from last year so they'd have been full of confidence.

"But we genuinely had that internal belief that we were gonna win the game. There's been a lot of change obviously a new manager comes in but we felt we've been trending in the right direction and progressing throughout the matches.

"The training sessions we've been having. So that's probably what it came down to, two teams that had genuine desire and expectation to go and win the game. That's probably what made it a good match."

He continued: "How difficult was it to believe despite poor results? I think that's why it is internal belief. Number one, if you don't have that you're struggling, you need to have that. But there is genuine signs from the games and stuff that we've been working on.

"That has been getting better. Results-wise it's not a traditional new manager bounce, let's say. I think that's fair. But it's hard for the outside, external world to look at it and agree with what we were thinking. But we always had that belief and fortunately there was no better occasion for it to click."

Reedijk may have taken some time to get his messages across in terms of tactical planning and set-up and that's down to a number of reasons.

Not least of all the fact that former head coach Gray had been in the Easter Road dugout for two years plus his spells as caretaker and first-team coach. The squad, perhaps used to one way of performing, knew the turnaround would be vast.

Newell reflects on Gray's departure with a sense of disappointment as he admits it was an exit which perhaps hurt more due to the 38-year-old's longevity in Leith.

"Yeah he's been there such a long time, he had the respect and love of everyone at the club so it was hard," Newell said on Gray. "So it probably did feel a little bit more than just a normal manager losing his job, even though that's never nice.

"Someone who was a player, coach, manager, he's been there for everything. So maybe that had a little bit of an effect, but you know there's no hangover in football, you've got to move on. It took a little bit of time but I think we're trending upwards now."
