All 2026/27 EFL Championship clubs ranked by how much they spend on wages every week

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The 2026/27 Championship season has already sprung surprises at both ends of the table, with clubs who weren't necessarily expected to mount promotion challenges already setting the pace, whilst others find themselves in dire straits in the bottom-half.

That in itself is one of the beauties of the second tier, that any side is capable of beating another on their day, regardless of the amount of investment which has been ploughed into squads or the strength-in-depth at any manager's disposal.

Football finance has become a dominant topic in recent times, with some clubs finding themselves in extremely concerning circumstances due to spending beyond their means in a bid to achieve short and long-term ambitions.

With that being said, Football League World have looked at how much each of the Championship's 24 clubs are ESTIMATED to be spending on wages per week, according to Capology.

It may come as no surprise that newly-promoted Lincoln City are believed to have the division's lowest wage bill.

Despite breaking their transfer record for the signing of Danish forward, Mikkel Ladefoged, the Imps are believed to be paying out just £161,750 per week.

Portsmouth boss, John Mousinho, was backed in the summer transfer window to add further quality to his squad, and although the South Coast club have shown signs of progression, they are said to have the second-lowest wage bill in the Championship.

According to the aforementioned site, chairman Michael Eisner is only spending £222,000 per week on wages at Fratton Park.

Bolton Wanderers are said to have the division's third-lowest wage bill following their return to the Championship after a seven-year exile.

It has been debated whether Steven Schumacher was backed enough in the transfer window amid the Whites' mixed start to the campaign, although the fact the ownership are said to be spending just £231,000 per week on wages, some will say that survival is a strong achievement.

Next up are Charlton Athletic, who returned to the Championship last summer after five years in League One.

Having survived at the first time of asking, Nathan Jones will be hoping his side can progress up the Championship table in time, even if a weekly wage bill of £255,800 is believed to be the fourth-lowest in the second tier.

Queens Park Rangers have enjoyed a largely positive start to the season, but will be hoping the remainder of the campaign doesn't follow a similar pattern to 2025/26.

The Hoops have certainly cut their costs in recent years, though, having failed to trouble the promotion picture since being relegated 11 years ago, with it now being estimated that the club are only paying £290,200 a week in wages.

Millwall have invested rather heavily in the squad at Alex Neil's disposal in recent times, meaning it may surprise people that they are estimated to have the sixth-lowest wage bill in the Championship.

The Lions surprised many by finishing third last season, and if they did achieve an unexpected promotion, there is no doubt the payroll in SE16 would have been significantly higher than £296,000 per week.

Blackburn Rovers are up next, with the East Lancashire club's financial situation under the Venky's being well-publicised in recent years.

The one-time Premier League champions have, in recent seasons, fluctuated between play-off and relegation battles, and this season will be hoping to upset the odds whilst having a wage bill of £297,500 per week.

Preston North End's start to the season has been nothing short of dire, even after a summer in which the Lilywhites broke their transfer record for the permanent signing of Alfie Devine from Tottenham Hotspur.

Although still early days into his reign, Graham Alexander is yet to turn the tide following the sacking of Paul Heckingbottom, and relegation to League One would certainly result in cuts to an estimated weekly payroll of £302,000.

Bristol City endured a summer of change, with Michael Skubala taking over at Ashton Gate whilst recruiting some exciting names such as Sam Tickle, Lorent Tolaj and Dom Ballard.

The Robins will be hoping to put a disappointing 2025/26 campaign behind them and challenge for the play-offs once more, even if such ambition isn't entirely reflected by a weekly wage bill of £306,500.

Despite seeing high-profile investors such as Snoop Dogg and Luka Modrić involved with the club, Swansea City still sit in the bottom-half when it comes to estimated wage bills in the Championship.

However, a figure of just £323,000 per week in that regard hasn't stopped Vitor Matos' side from enjoying an extremely impressive start to the season, and one they will hope continues right up to matchday 46.
