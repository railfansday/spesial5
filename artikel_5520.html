FHA’s MMI Fund stays 5x above floor, fueling calls to cut insurance premiums

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

FHA’s 2026 Annual Report to Congress shows the Mutual Mortgage Insurance Fund’s capital ratio at 10.29%, down from 11.47% in 2025 but still far above the 2% statutory minimum for the 12th straight year. Economic net worth slipped to $182.6 billion while capital resources rose to $148.9 billion.   AI Summary

The Federal Housing Administration (FHA)’s Mutual Mortgage Insurance (MMI) Fund posted a strong financial performance in fiscal year 2026, even as its capital ratio declined from 2025 levels, renewing industry pressure to cut annual mortgage insurance premiums amid affordability concerns.

The MMI Fund had a capital reserve ratio of 10.29% as of Sept. 30, down from 11.47% a year earlier, according to FHA’s Annual Report to Congress released Monday. It’s the 12th straight year the fund has remained above its 2% statutory minimum.

“Over the past year, the MMI Fund remained strong despite a year-over-year decline,” the report states. “Both the Single Family forward and Home Equity Conversation Mortgage (HECM) programs have separately maintained capital levels above the 2% ratio for the last six years.”

The fund’s economic net worth ended FY 2026 at $182.6 billion, a $6.3 billion decrease from 2025. Total capital resources, however, increased by $9.3 billion to $148.9 billion, signaling continued strength in FHA’s insurance capacity even as modeled future performance softened.

Scott Turner, secretary of the Department of Housing and Urban Development, wrote in the report that under the Trump administration, HUD has continued “our work to streamline and deregulate FHA Single Family programs, while remaining vigilant stewards of taxpayer resources by eradicating fraud, waste, and abuse within our programs.”

Joseph Gormley, assistant secretary for housing and president of Ginnie Mae, wrote that the administration has eliminated roughly 150 “burdensome and unnecessary” policies, implemented alternative credit scoring models and updated FHA’s appraisal technology submission processes.

Gormley added that HUD has proposed a reform to the partial claim process through a Reinstatement Advance Payment, “which we intend to implement in Fiscal Year 2027.”

In a recent interview with HousingWire, Gormley said the administration is “comfortable” with the fund’s capital levels, signaling no immediate changes to FHA premiums or broader pricing. But the fund’s continued strength is fueling fresh calls from the mortgage industry to reduce FHA’s annual mortgage insurance premiums and ease monthly payments.

Bob Broeksmit, president and CEO of the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA), said in a statement after the report’s release that the 2026 results create room for “targeted changes that improve affordability for borrowers.”

“FHA should modestly reduce annual mortgage insurance premiums, lowering monthly payments for moderate- and lower-income homebuyers,” Broeksmit said. “Given the slowdown in the housing market, any MIP reduction should be carefully calibrated and paired with additional safeguards to limit multiple layered risk factors on individual loans and protect the fund’s long-term health.”’

The Community Home Lenders of America (CHLA) also weighed in on the report, urging the FHA to end its life-of-loan policies on mortgage insurance.

“FHA Life of Loan premiums were intended as a stop-gap fee hike in 2013 to respond to financial concerns resulting from the 2008 Housing Crisis.” the group said in a statement. “With today’s MMIF report showing a long-term 12 year run of solid financial performance and bulging FHA reserve levels, it is long past time to stop overcharging homeowners for FHA loans, in order to help them in asset building.”

Within the overall fund, the single-family forward loan program reported a standalone capital ratio of 10.47% in FY 2026, compared with 10.95% in 2025. HUD attributed part of the decline to the growing share of loans with reduced annual MIPs following a premium cut in 2023, as well as slower home price appreciation and updated macroeconomic forecasts that increased projected losses.

The reverse mortgage book saw a much sharper move. The standalone capital ratio for the HECM program fell to 5.43% in 2026, down from 24.06% in 2025. HUD noted that HECMs, which account for less than 4% of the MMI Fund, are more sensitive to home price movements. Meanwhile, loan payoffs — viewed as the most favorable recovery outcome for HECMs — have become less common.

“Lower actual and forecast loan payoffs increased losses,” the report stated. “Finally, the HECM program spent substantial capital resources on assignment claims in FY 2026, reducing available cash.”

Overall, in FY 2026, FHA insured more than 935,000 single-family home mortgages, 83% of which were used by first-time homebuyers. FHA also endorsed more than 24,000 reverse mortgages through the HECM program.

As of Sept. 30, the agency managed active insurance on more than 8.4 million forward mortgages with $1.7 trillion in unpaid principal balance and $63.5 billion in outstanding HECM obligations.
