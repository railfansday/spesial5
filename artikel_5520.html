JetBlue creditors are hiring restructuring advisers as bonds crash to 42 cents

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

JetBlue's $2 billion of notes due 2031 traded near 80 cents on the dollar this month; its stock has fallen more than half since August

JetBlue Airways has a group of senior creditors engaged with PJT Partners, a restructuring adviser, and the law firm Akin, as the airline deals with heavy cash outflows and high oil prices, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The airline's $2 billion of notes due 2031 sank to 42 cents on the dollar on Thursday and traded in the mid-40s on Friday, according to The Wall Street Journal, which cited market participants and Finra data. The decline followed a recent bondholder call. Finra data shows the notes changed hands at roughly 80 cents per dollar of face value earlier in October.

JetBlue stock closed at $2.62 on Friday, down 0.94%, and has fallen 42% this year. It has lost more than half its value since the start of August.

"JetBlue remains confident in its JetForward strategy and the progress being made toward returning to sustained profitability," the company said in an emailed statement, according to Reuters. A JetBlue representative told The Wall Street Journal that management's focus is on boosting revenue and developing a business that earns more. JetBlue plans to give an account of how it is progressing and where its finances are headed when it hosts its call on third-quarter results on Tuesday.

Creditors bring in advisers once they fear a borrower may not return their money in full or on time, according to The Wall Street Journal. Spirit Airlines shut down operations this year after repeated bankruptcy filings, and investors holding airline debt have been wary ever since, the Journal said.

JetBlue held $1.66 billion of cash, plus cash equivalents, as of June 30, set against $8.5 billion of borrowings and finance lease commitments, according to its filings. Much of that debt is secured by JetBlue's aircraft fleet and its TrueBlue loyalty program, according to The Wall Street Journal. Close to two-thirds of the company's assets, spanning property, equipment and intangibles, have been put up as security for lenders, according to its quarterly filings.

Some market participants fear that piling on more debt could leave JetBlue little room to maneuver if a turnaround fails, according to The Wall Street Journal. The airline also faces possible pressure from credit-card processors, which may demand that carriers hold back collateral should conditions deteriorate, the Journal said. JetBlue said it was under no such obligation when June ended.

Higher jet fuel costs tied to the Iran war have squeezed margins at U.S. airlines even as fares rose and travelers kept flying at a consistent pace, according to Reuters.

JetBlue reported a first-quarter net loss of $319 million in April, wider than the $208 million loss a year earlier. Jet fuel cost 15.2% more per gallon than in the same period a year before, and the company ended the quarter with $2.4 billion in liquidity. JetBlue arranged $500 million of financing secured by aircraft in April, and the full amount had been tapped by June 30, according to The Wall Street Journal.
