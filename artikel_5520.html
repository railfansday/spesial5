Oil prices slip as IEA sees first supply surplus since Iran war began

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Brent crude fell 0.5% to $95.32 a barrel after the agency said the recovery in Gulf exports outpaced global oil demand

The International Energy Agency forecast a fourth-quarter surplus in the global oil market on Friday, and crude prices edged lower on Monday.

Brent crude futures fell 0.5% to $95.32 a barrel as of 6:14 a.m. ET. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures dropped 0.3% to $88.29.

The IEA said global oil supplies surpassed demand in October for the first time since the war in Iran began. Global supply rose by 2.3 million barrels a day to 105.4 million barrels a day, and observed oil inventories rose by 52 million barrels, the first increase since June.

Gulf oil exports, excluding Oman, reached 18.4 million barrels a day in October, according to the agency. That is 26% below pre-war levels.

North Sea Dated crude fell 32% from a high of $136 a barrel on Oct. 8 to $93 at the time the IEA wrote its report. The price remains $22 above its pre-war level, the agency said.

The IEA forecast that world oil demand will fall by 2.7 million barrels a day in 2026 and rebound by 2 million barrels a day in 2027. It expects global supply to rise by 6.6 million barrels a day to 108.1 million barrels a day next year, with a larger build in surplus in 2027.

Fourth-quarter supply will average 105 million barrels a day, outpacing expected demand by 1.7 million barrels a day, according to Reuters. The agency, in its earlier outlook, had anticipated a shortfall for the quarter, Reuters said.

Tim Waterer, chief market analyst at KCM Trade, said traders have started to treat a glut as a real prospect, a shift from the tight supply that has characterized the conflict until now, according to Reuters.

The IEA said downside risks to its forecast persist because attacks on shipping undermine the maritime security needed for a sustained recovery. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said mines hit two oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, and that six tankers burned in comparable incidents across Sunday and Monday, according to Reuters, which said it could not independently verify the statement.

Last week, crude prices reversed earlier declines after President Donald Trump said in a Truth Social post that Washington has no need for a deal with Iran. Brent briefly moved above $94 a barrel that day.
