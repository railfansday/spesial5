Leading AI Models Still Missing Critical Details When Giving Financial Advice, New Research Finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

New benchmarks from Vals AI and NextLadder Ventures, advised by the Financial Health Network, tested 28 AI models across 256 simulated financial counseling scenarios involving eviction, medical debt and other challenges facing low-income families.

SAN FRANCISCO, Nov. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Today, Vals AI and NextLadder Ventures released a new benchmark analysis examining general-purpose AI models' performance in providing financial advice in high-stakes scenarios often faced by low-income Americans, compared against best practices identified by financial counseling experts. The evaluation tested 28 models across 256 simulated financial counseling scenarios developed with practicing financial counselors.

The benchmarks are part of a broader effort spearheaded by NextLadder Ventures to accelerate the development of AI tools tailored to the unique needs and challenges facing low-income Americans navigating fork-in-the-road moments with disproportionate impact on their economic mobility.

"Every day, millions of Americans are struggling to make ends meet on tight budgets without much guidance or support," said Ryan Rippel, CEO of NextLadder Ventures. "Effective financial guidance that helps families make sense of their unique circumstances with reliable, tailored recommendations can make a world of difference. But too little of that guidance is available to low-income families, and the counselors who do work with these families are wildly over-stretched. AI-powered financial health tools have monumental potential to make a real dollars-in-pockets difference for low-income families, but far too many gaps remain in the general-purpose models that most people turn to today. We should not accept that the true measure of success for leading AI models means shattering the frontiers of mathematics, but not whether they can do the kitchen table math that faces tens of millions of families. It's our hope that these benchmarks will provide unbiased and actionable insights on how to make these tools better equipped to help low-income Americans move up the economic ladder."

"I'm optimistic that AI can make useful financial guidance available to more people," said Rayan Krishnan, Vals AI's cofounder and CEO. "Our evaluation shows there is still work to do. Models sometimes gave advice without understanding someone's circumstances, missed important deadlines or failed to connect them with available help. Independent evaluation makes those gaps visible and gives developers a way to measure whether their systems are becoming more reliable."

Vals AI worked with practicing financial counselors to develop 256 test scenarios drawn from the questions people bring to financial counselors. Twenty-eight AI models were tested in the role of financial counselor between September 14 and October 12, 2026. A second AI played the client and disclosed key facts, including income, debts, deadlines, family size, only when the counselor asked about them. Each of the 7,167 completed conversations (one per model per scenario) was scored against a checklist developed by financial counseling practitioners. An AI model did the grading; on practitioner-graded checklist items, it agreed 85% of the time.

The benchmark tested models from 11 developers: OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Luna and GPT-5.6 Terra; Google's Gemini 4 Argon, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Gemini 3.6 Flash, Gemini 3.5 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite; Meta's Muse Spark 1.2, Muse Spark 1.3, Muse Spark 1.3 Max, Wonder and Wonder Full; Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1; xAI's Grok 4.7; DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash; Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 Max; Moonshot AI's Kimi K3; Zhipu AI's GLM 5.3; Xiaomi's MiMo V2.6 Pro; and MiniMax's MiniMax-M3. Each model was tested directly at its standard settings, outside the consumer apps that run on it. The results measure model performance in simulated conversations, rather than the complete experience offered by consumer apps.
