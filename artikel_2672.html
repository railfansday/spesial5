Peoria Javelinas win AFL championship

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

In the final week of the Arizona Fall League season, the Peoria Javelinas played two games to finish the regular season and lost one. By losing that game, they dropped out of a tie for first in the league and finished second to the Surprise Saguaros.

By being the top two finishers, the Javelinas and Saguaros both had a bye for the first round of the playoffs. For the semi-finals, the Javelinas played the Salt River Rafters after the Rafters defeated the Mesa Solar Sox the day before.

In their match, the Javelinas won 6-5 and advanced to the final. In the other semi-final game, the Surprise Saguaros defeated the Glendale Desert Dogs 10-4. The two top finishers, who finished a game apart in the standings, met for the championship on Saturday at Peoria Sports Complex in a game televised nationally on MLB Network.

It was never really a contest as the Saguaros' starter, RHP Justin Lampkin, struggled out of the gate and was backed up by a leaky defense. The Javelinas scored five runs in his two innings, with only one of those being an earned run.

The Javelinas starter, Reds prospect RHP Luke Coleman, was masterful over five innings of work and allowed four hits and no runs with three walks and six strikeouts. He was backed by great defense, with Padres prospect Justin DeCriscio playing second base and being instrumental in many infield outs.

Padres No. 13 prospect RHP Kannon Kemp came on in the sixth inning in relief of Holman and struggled with his pitches against the first batter. Kemp features tremendous stuff. His four-seam fastball sits 96-99 mph with movement. His sinker runs in the same range; his hard slider sits in the high 80s, and he adds a high-80s changeup. His command is the issue after missing time in the past two seasons with injuries.

After walking the lead-off man in the sixth, Kemp got outs on a ground ball double-play and a strikeout. He also pitched himself into trouble in the seventh inning with two walks and a single to load the bases but then got three infield outs to strand the runners. Commenting on the broadcast for MLB Network were Jim Callis and Dan O'Dowd wtih Callis the prospect expert and O'Dowd the former GM of the Rockies. They agreed Kemp has great stuff but inconsistent command. He profiles as a high-leverage reliever if the Padres want him to move quickly but could also be a starter if he gets better command of all his pitches.

RHP Francis Peña came in to protect the lead in the ninth inning. He allowed a run on a walk, a hit, a grounder, and then a sac fly before retiring the side on another groundball to end the game.

On the offensive side, 2B DeCriscio went 1-for-3 with an RBI. He walked and struck out for his three appearances. OF Alex McCoy was 0-for-3 with a run scored after being hit by a pitch in his first at-bat. He also struck out twice.

For the two playoff games, Peña pitched in both for 1.2 innings with one run and two hits. He finished the AFL with a 1.59 ERA in 11.1 innings for the regular season, second to Luke Holman. The starter for the final game pitched 13 innings for the entire AFL season with a 0.69 ERA. He then went five innings in the final game to shut out the Surprise team.

Kemp finished the season with a 7.71 ERA in 14 innings with nine walks and 16 strikeouts. His command issues were the culprit for his high ERA. LHP Igor Gil finished with a 3.86 ERA in nine innings pitched with 10 walks and 13 strikeouts. RHP Winyer Chourio also featured inconsistent command and finished with a 4.60 ERA in 15.2 innings pitched with 14 walks and 13 strikeouts. RHP Jordan Valenzuela had difficulties and only appeared in seven games with 6.1 innings pitched and had a 9.95 ERA with seven walks and six strikeouts.

Outfielder Qrey Lott was a late addition to the Javelinas roster and only appeared in two games with four at-bats and one strikeout. He was added on Nov. 9, just for the last two games of the schedule. Lott played in 95 games for Low-A Lake Elsinore in the 2026 season.

McCoy finished the six weeks with a .250/.372/.578 line and was noted to have tremendous power by the commentators on the broadcast but struggles versus breaking balls and that leads to a high strikeout rate. They noted his ceiling to be high if he can learn to handle breaking balls to go with his power.

Utility man DeCriscio was drafted as a defense-first shortstop in 2025. He has since been used by the Padres as a super utility player. For the Low-A Lake Elsinore Storm, DeCriscio played all over the infield except first base and also played the corner outfield spots. He was promoted to Single-A Fort Wayne in May and finished the season with Double-A San Antonio. In the AFL, he played second base for the Javelinas. The second baseman finished the AFL season with a .288/.403/.519 hitting line and then hit .600 in the playoffs with a strong defensive effort.

Catcher/DH Ty Harvey played in 20 games for Peoria with a .241/.389/.276 line. He had two doubles and no home runs for a player drafted with plus power. He lost a lot of the regular season to injury and needed the AFL for reps before the offseason began. His lack of power could be a reflection of his loss of strength from a broken hand.

The previous weekend featured the AFL Home Run Derby and Fall Stars game. OF Alex McCoy participated in both, while 2B Justin DeCriscio was one of the final two added to the team by fan vote. RHP Kannon Kemp was also selected for the game.

McCoy was eliminated in the first round of the derby but distinguished himself by hitting a ball 503 feet in the elimination round. That was the farthest home run of the night.

In the Fall Stars Game, the NL defeated the AL in a 7-4 final. McCoy was inserted in the seventh inning as a pinch hitter and DH. He had two at-bats and went 1-for-2 with a run scored. DeCriscio was a sub and had three at-bats going 1-for-3 with a bunt single that drove in the go-ahead run in the fourth inning.

The two players to benefit the most from the AFL for the Padres were DeCriscio and Peña. They both distinguished themselves with their performances and made a case for Peña to be added to the 40-man roster. DeCriscio will be a fun watch as he works his way through the minor league system.

Fernando Tatis Jr. had a strange start to his season, but he rounded into form over the second half. He did well enough to be named to the All-MLB First Team and like many of his teammates he also received a grade from Lincoln Zdunich of Gaslamp Ball.

The Milwaukee Brewers made a trade with the San Francisco Giants for reliever Ryan Walker.

Reliever Tim Hill agreed to a one-year deal with the New York Yankees.

Pablo Sandoval announced his retirement from baseball.
