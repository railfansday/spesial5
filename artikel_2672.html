‘You just offended most, if not all, of Mexico’: Nick Bosa faces criticism after ‘condescending’ comments about Mexican fans

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The San Francisco 49ers and Minnesota Vikings will meet in Mexico City on Sunday Night Football in a game that could go a long way toward deciding the NFC's No. 1 seed. But ahead of the big matchup, 49ers superstar defensive end Nick Bosa managed to make headlines for all the wrong reasons.

Bosa drew backlash this week when he suggested that fans in Mexico City need to be told when to cheer during the game. Now, longtime 49ers reporter Grant Cohn has offered Bosa some "friendly advice," saying that his comments came across as "condescending" to the host country.

During a press conference this week, Bosa was asked about the atmosphere when the 49ers last played in Mexico City in 2022. He remembered the crowd being loud, but he had a request for the fans this time around.

"Yeah, I kind of want … I think we need some information given to the fans so they know when to cheer," Bosa said, via NBC Sports Bay Area. "Because they just chant the whole time. If we can kind of teach them, no offense to them and their soccer cheering techniques, but if they can cheer when we're on defense, that would be great."

Needless to say, those comments didn't go over well with a lot of people, including plenty of reporters who cover the NFL. Several members of the media were quick to call Bosa out, including Cohn, who said he "was not expecting Nick Bosa to offend the entire country of Mexico before the 49ers even arrive."

On Saturday, Cohn followed up with a two-minute video offering Bosa some advice. He opened by suggesting that Bosa probably didn't mean to offend anyone.

"Nicholas, Nicholas, Nicholas, you've done it again," Cohn said. "You're a man of few words, but when you do talk, you often get yourself in trouble. And I feel like sometimes you're just misunderstood, like this time. Like, now, you just offended most, if not all, of Mexico. The entire country. And I don't think you meant to do that."

Cohn acknowledged that Bosa was likely "just talking ball," but he made it clear that the comments still came off the wrong way.

"But see, it comes across as condescending," Cohn said. "Because they know. They watch football. They know exactly how Americans cheer; they just don't cheer like that because they want to cheer their way. Frankly, it's a lot more fun."

Cohn then offered Bosa a simple rule to follow in the future to avoid these sorts of situations.

"In the future, Nicholas, anytime you're invited somewhere and you're a guest, it's bad form to tell the host what to do. In any walk of life. You're the guest. What you say is, 'It's so nice to be here. Thank you to the entire country of Mexico for being so generous and welcoming. Can't wait to play at Stadium Azteca.' That's all you say. Period!" Cohn said.

"Just like if you were to go to someone's house for dinner. You'd say, 'Hey, man, thank you for dinner and thank you having me.' Not like, 'Yo, turn the heater up to 78 and give me eight ice cubes in my drink. And make it a half-Coke, half-orange soda mixture right now. Actually, I want peanut butter and jelly.' That's what you don't do!" he added.

Clearly, Bosa will have plenty of motivation to get that first sack of the season on Sunday night. It will be interesting to see how the crowd in Mexico City treats him when he does.
