Knicks Bulletin: ‘Sometimes your mind or your body just takes a second’

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

If you still have leftovers in the fridge, congratulations. That means you'll be able to celebrate twice today.

First, a Knicks win over the Heat. Then, feasting on another round of reheated turkey, mac and cheese, and mashed potatoes.

Here's yesterday's post, slightly padded with a few more quotes.

On the Knicks still having room for improvement:

"I've said this before, we play so many games. You can never get too high. I guess we're 12-3 since the start, but our [performance] does still stink. We haven't arrived. We still have to embrace the little things.

"They do this (points downward), but eventually they're going to do this (points upward). When we do that, handling the challenges in front of us the right way is going to be big. You can't get too high in this league because you're that close to going the other way. Just like if you're down, it's not the end of the world; you're that close to flipping it. So you try to stay even-keeled and kind of stick with your process, especially if it's something that you believe in. And we believe in it. Eventually things are going to change or turn in the right direction."

On Jordan Clarkson being instrumental in establishing the Knicks' flexible bench rotation and wanting him to remain with the organization beyond his playing career:

"As a coach, you could want to do that, but you have to have others around you that will at least support it initially to see how it turns out. And a huge, huge, huge catalyst for that was Jordan Clarkson. Jordan Clarkson was the one, if you're talking about giving 100 percent out there to what allows me to do that, I think Jordan Clarkson gave me at least 90 percent of the opportunity to do it, because here's an accomplished veteran that could be in the rotation for almost any team out there, let alone a championship team, and I can literally play him six straight games and then sit him 10 straight games and he's gonna be the same dude every fricken' day. And he's gonna talk the same way to his teammates. Not 'can you believe what Mike Brown did?' Nah, he's gonna be the same dude, uplift the group, and then when I call his number again, he's gonna be ready to go. He's a guy that, even when he gets done playing, it's one of those things where you hope that he can just be a part of this organization in some way, shape or form until he doesn't want to be part of this organization. He's that type of person, in my opinion."

On the benefits of potentially missing the NBA Cup knockout rounds:

"It is important (to win and potentially advance), but the reality of it is, when you look at the schedule, and you see that our percentage [to advance] is not that high, and so they kind of shared with me what our schedule would look like if we don't go and if we do go. And you look at it, you're like, 'Whoa, that's a lot of days off if you don't go.' And so again, you want to compete; you want to win. But if you don't, in my opinion, when you look at the schedule… you're like, there's an opportunity if you got some guys banged up to get rest and/or to work on some things in between the games that you have if you're not involved in the Cup."

On the physical and mental toll of competing in the NBA Cup:

"I didn't really break it down to an 83rd game, but when I talk about the Cup, it adds a level of stress maybe on your mind and your body because the games are intense as you go along and you're playing more games in a short amount of time.

"I remember it was like, game, game, game, and then you win the Cup, and you celebrate it that night, and you hop on a plane and go play in Indiana the next night. And so it's great. You like the competition and the setup, but it adds a lot of stress to you physically and mentally. I'm saying this because especially with the extended season we had last year and how taxing the games were for our playoff run mentally and physically, I'm trying to be smart, so if — knock on wood — we end up advancing, I'll try to be smart about it."

On refusing to chase regular-season wins at all costs and learning from Golden State's 73-win season:

"I try not to chase wins, especially early in the season. You want to win, but I'm not going to win every game at all costs.

"I wasn't part of the staff [for the first Golden State title] [Golden State] won Steve [Kerr's] first year. Then they lost in the second year, but they set a regular season record of 73 wins, and one of the things that he used to tell me, they gassed themselves trying to get this record and come playoff time they just didn't have enough in the tank to close it. And so that always sits right here for me."

On calling Pacôme Dadiet into his office and threatening to remove him from the rotation over his lack of effort:

"I didn't think he played hard in the game before. And I actually called him into my office and showed him some film. I told him, 'As a young guy, if you don't play hard, I can't play you.'

"And I said, 'Believe me, I got a lot of guys I can go to. I'm not afraid to go to somebody else. So the least you could do is play hard.' And I showed him how he wasn't. And I thought [Wednesday], he played hard."

On leaving OG Anunoby's injury management to the medical staff:

"There are a lot of medical terms that are above my head. They tell me and I don't really pick up on the injury. If he's out, I change my minutes sheet. And then I'll just wait for them to tell me when he's back. [Wednesday], OG's individual work happened before shootaround and I didn't even know he had individual work until his player development guy told me later on. You never want a guy to hurt himself more. But I don't think about it."

On the Knicks still having plenty of room for improvement despite winning 12 of their last 15 games:

"We're 12-3 since the start, but our s–t still does stink. We haven't arrived, and we still have to embrace the little things."

On the Knicks never panicking after their 0-3 start and gradually finding their rhythm:

"I don't think we had any panic. It wasn't like we started 0 and 3 and we were getting blown out by 15 and 20 every game. It was a possession game each of those three losses and could've gone either way. So we knew that, and we knew it was just a play here or a play there that could've changed the game. We just tried to make sure we're focusing on every possession. Sometimes your mind or your body just takes a second at the beginning of the season. I feel like we're in a good space, physically and mentally, continuing to get better. Really diving into our core principles."

On Brown experimenting with an unusually deep rotation against Cleveland:

"I looked at the bench in the first quarter and realized, 'Wow, we played like 10 people in that first quarter.'"

On the Knicks' supporting cast stepping up and trusting Karl-Anthony Towns and Jalen Brunson as their leaders:

"I mean, it's big for our team. Those guys [Towns and Brunson] are going to carry us enough, so today obviously it was an all-around game. We're always going to have their backs, and they always have ours, and it just shows you what type of team we are. It could be anybody on any night, but they're going to carry us to the promised land."

On Clarkson encouraging Pacôme Dadiet and reminding his teammates to stay ready:

"JC is always big on that, telling guys to stay ready. He was in [Dadiet's] ear the whole shootaround [Wednesday morning]. Just telling everybody to stay ready because you never know when your name is called."

On endorsing Tom Thibodeau as a potential head coaching hire for struggling NBA franchises:

"I made three playoffs with Thibodeau as my coach so his track record is one of success. So of course if I was a team that wanted to get back on track, I'd look at Tom Thibodeau to be my coach."

On Thibodeau's tactical knowledge, dedication and ability to prepare his teams to win:

"It's the X's and O's and the amount of care he puts into his craft, his team. He steps into that gym every day and, you know, you can say anything you want about the way he coaches, but one thing I know is his team is prepared to win a game. So that's a testament to who he is as a coach, the preparation he puts his team through and also his ability to have his team ready for the moment."

On the Knicks' bench players embracing fluctuating minutes and not having egos:

"We care about each other. We want to win for each other. We're willing to take on different roles and we know that we've got each other's back. If Jose's got it going and my minutes are shortened, awesome. And vice versa. All of us, Deuce [McBride] included in that, I would throw him in that as well, all of us kind of feel that way. That's really cool.

"I've been saying it, I've been a broken record about it, but you don't see that — that's very rare where no ego or ulterior motives get involved. That's something special that you want to be a part of."
