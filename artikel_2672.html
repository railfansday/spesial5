Big Ten Rankings and Projections After Week 12

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

It was a crazy Saturday in the Big Ten last week. Not only did UW get the upset over Indiana, UCLA got their first loss of the season at Michigan-and both of those games went to overtime. Nebraska got their first win over a top-25 opponent in over a decade, knocking off Ohio State to give Ohio State their first 4+ loss season since 2011. And Rutgers ended a 38-year losing streak against Penn State.

The result of those upsets is that the final weekend games will decide which teams make the Big Ten Championship game, and which team(s) from the Big Ten make the CFP. At this point, there are a lot of possibilities.

The only teams at the top of the CFP rankings to lose last weekend were in the Big Ten (UCLA and Indiana). That resulted some drops by those teams. It also led to some new teams being included in the rankings (wait for it…)

The full list of rankings can be found here: https://collegefootballplayoff.com/sports/2026/8/9/wk-x-wk-rankings

That gives the Big Ten 8 teams in the top-25. Obviously the committee rewarded both UW and Nebraska for their wins over ranked teams. That's the good news. The bad news is that only UCLA would currently make the 12-team bracket as it is today. (Oregon wouldn't make it at #12 because a G6 champion would be included.) But, there are 4 Big Ten teams that just outside, any of which could still move into the CFP bracket depending on what happens in the next two weeks.

In the other major polls, here are where the Big Ten teams are ranked (AP/Coaches):

Ohio State got enough votes in the Coaches Poll to be #26. Both Nebraska and Washington (!) are receiving votes in both polls. Nebraska is #27 in the AP and #29 in the Coaches. UW is #30 in both polls. In addition, Wisconsin (#38), Illinois (#42), and Minnesota (#43) got votes in the Coaches poll.

This week the Massey Composite (https://masseyratings.com/ranks) includes 92 different rankings (as of the writing of this article), including the AP and Coaches polls. The following "Cool Chart" shows the changes since the preseason for the teams in the Big Ten.

The Big Ten teams are largely separated into three groups. The top 6 teams are the ones that are getting votes in the AP. Then there's the next 8 teams. And the final 4 teams are clearly a huge step below.

UCLA is still the top team in the Big Ten despite their loss to Michigan. And they have still improved the most since the preseason-up 49 places. Nebraska, Northwestern, and Wisconsin are the only other teams that are up by more than 5 places since the preseason.

UW is one of 7 Big Ten teams that are down more than 10 places since the preseason. Rutgers continues to be down the most (-43). Maryland (-25), Michigan (-26), and Penn State (-27) are down more 20 places since the preseason.

The table below lists the rankings for each of the Big Ten teams in the Massey Composite plus 6 other rankings: SP+, FEI, ESPN's FPI, Sagarin, CBS Sports, and SRS.

The Sagarin rankings again include FCS teams. Both Purdue and Rutgers rank below FCS South Dakota State (#68). In Bill Connelly's ranking of all college football teams (FBS, FCS, D2, etc.), Minnesota, Maryland, Michigan State, Purdue, and Rutgers are all ranked below South Dakota State (#75), and 5 other FCS teams are ranked above Rutgers.

The top 6 teams in the Massey Composite are in the top-25 in all of these rankings. Nebraska is in the top-25 in all of the rankings other than the Massey Composite. Wisconsin is in the top-25 in two of the rankings (FEI and Sagarin).

There are 6 Big Ten teams that vary by more than 20 places. Four of those teams are the bottom 4; it isn't surprising that they vary that much. Minnesota varies from #49 to #76; and remember that Minnesota is one of the teams getting votes in the Coaches poll (#43). Penn State is just outside of the top-25 according to ESPN's FPI (#26) and SP+ (#27), but as low as #55 according to CBS Sports.

The F+ rankings are a combination of the FEI rankings from Brian Fremeau and the SP+ rankings from Bill Connelly. The table below includes the overall ranking, the ranking of the offense, the ranking of the defense, and the ranking of the special teams.

Only two Big Ten teams have a top-10 offense (USC and Ohio State), and only 3 are in the top-25. The offensive rank for 7 of the Big Ten teams is down more than 20 places since last year, led by Michigan which is down 70 places. UCLA (+48), Wisconsin (+46), and Northwestern (+40) have all improved their offensive rank by more than 25 places since the end of last season.

Half of the Big Ten teams are in the top-25 in defense-and Washington is close at #29. In contrast, both Purdue and Rutgers are ranked near the bottom of all FBS teams in defense. UCLA's defense has improved significantly from last year; they are up 84 places. Nebraska's defense has also improved a lot; they are up 41 places. Purdue's defense (-37) is the only other defense that has changed by more than 20 places since the end of last season.

Five Big Ten teams are in the top-25 for Special Teams, led by Purdue. Three Big Ten teams are ranked below #100. UW's Special Teams have improved 44 places since the end of last season. That isn't the best in the Big Ten; both Michigan State (+58) and Minnesota (+96) have improved more. Six Big Ten teams have seen their Special Teams rankings go down by more than 35 places, and five of them are down more than 60 places.

Ohio State and Oregon are both in the top-25 in both offense and defense.

Unlike last week when most of the visiting teams were favored, this week most of the home teams are favored. The exceptions are Rutgers and Maryland, two of the bottom teams in the conference.

With the exceptions of Massey's win percentage for Iowa and SP+'s win percentage for UCLA, the win percentages are very close in all of the games.

There have been a lot of surprising results in the Big Ten this season, especially in the last couple of months. There has been at least one upset each week since the beginning of October, and most weeks there have been at least 2. The question is which of the games this week will result in an upset?

This is the last season that there will be a Big Ten Championship game. And going into the last games of the regular season, there are 6 Big Ten teams that still have a shot at playing in that last Big Ten Championship game, including 3 of the former Pac-12 teams. In fact, the most likely outcome based on those win percentages is a rematch between UCLA and Oregon. Here is a look at what needs to happen for each of those 6 teams: https://superwestsports.com/breaking-down-the-2026-big-ten-football-championship-game-tie-breakers/

(Note that a win by UW over Oregon doesn't mean the Oregon is out, but it would take losses by Indiana and Iowa and UCLA for them to get in.)

With Stanford and Oregon State both winning last weekend, there are now 9 of the former Pac-12 teams that are eligible for bowl games. Utah is probably the only one of the former Pac-12 teams that is all but guaranteed a spot in the CFP. There are only 6 bowl games for the remaining teams, but there should be more than enough bowl games that will want one of the former Pac-12 teams. We just may not know which one UW will be in until after all of the conference championship games are played and the final CFP rankings are released.

It was a (pleasant) surprise to see UW included in this week's CFP rankings. That should give the players and coaches even more incentive (as if they needed it) to beat Oregon this week. It is extremely unlikely that even a win over Oregon would get them into the CFP given where they are currently ranked, and a loss will very likely get them dropped out of the next ranking. But, this is obviously a huge boost for the program considering where they were just a month ago.
