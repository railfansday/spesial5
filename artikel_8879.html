designboom listens: the sounds behind alastair philip wiper’s industrial visions

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

moving between personal memories, unexpected visual references, and the energy of disco, wiper reveals how music accompanies his work and everyday life.

An album cover encountered in childhood can stay with you for decades. In the case of photographer and creative director Alastair Philip Wiper, the mirrored industrial landscape on The Alan Parsons Project’s Ammonia Avenue became a lasting reference and, eventually, an inspiration for his work. Based in Copenhagen, Wiper turns his camera toward factories, laboratories, scientific equipment, and industrial infrastructures, finding unexpected beauty in the systems people build and the often-absurd ways they operate. His photographs reveal the unexpected visual qualities of places built for function, drawing attention to their symmetry, patterns, and overlooked details.

This week on designboom listens, Wiper shares the music that accompanies his creative moments, moving between disco, jazz, electronic music, and 1980s pop. Some tracks are tied to personal memories, like Cannonball Adderley’s Autumn Leaves, which he played while trying to get his first son to sleep, while others help him find energy or shift his mood. ‘I need something to get me excited and get some energy going. Disco usually does that for me,’ he says of Sister Sledge’s Lost in Music. Kraftwerk’s Radioactivity connects with a book he is currently working on about nuclear technology and culture, while Billy Idol’s Eyes Without a Face echoes some of the more uncanny subjects he has photographed, from robotics to medical and scientific research. Read on for Wiper’s answers and discover the sounds, memories, and visual references behind his practice, then listen to the full playlist below.

What song best captures your creative practice?

Alastair Philip Wiper (APW): Wonderful Life – Black. There are a lot of times when I think I have a wonderful life when working. Not all the time. But then I can just put this song on and I would be back to my wonderful life.

What is the very first track you play to clear the air and get inspired when starting a new project?

APW: Lost in Music – Sister Sledge. I need something to get me excited and get some energy going. Disco usually does that for me.

Is there a song that instantly takes you back to a specific memory or place from your past?

APW: Autumn Leaves – Cannonball Adderly. I played this all the time when I was trying to get my first son off to sleep as a baby, 15 years ago. I would rock him in my arms and bop slowly around the room. Sometimes it worked, a lot of the time it didn’t but I still love it.

What song is stuck in your head right now?

APW: The Captain of Her Heart – Double. Can’t get enough of this 80s beauty.

What is your ultimate 2 AM studio track — the one you secretly know every word to?

APW: Runaround Sue – Dion & The Belmonts. If you want to see me karaoke like an idiot this is probably it.

What song do you put on when you’re stuck and need a breakthrough?

APW:  If You Really Love Me – Stevie Wonder. I’m a massive Stevie Wonder fan and I could have chosen many of his tunes to lift me up, but settled on this. Always puts me in a good mood.

What track is dominating your speakers this week, and is it influencing what you’re making right now? APW: You Always Start it – XXXY. I tend to listen to this tune on repeat for a while, then have a break, then come back to it after a year or so. It’s back this week.

What song helps you slow down and focus on the tiny, quiet details? APW: Within – Daft Punk. I listen to a lot of modern classical like Michael Nyman or Philip Glass when I am trying to focus but want something in the background, but today I decided on this slow one by Daft Punk.

If people were wearing headphones while experiencing your latest work, what one song should be playing? APW:  Radioactivity – Kraftwerk. I’m working on a book about nuclear technology and culture and I love Kraftwerk so it has to be this one.

What is your favorite album cover art? APW:  Island Life – Grace Jones. Jean-Paul Goude is one of my all-time favorite photographers. He was the boyfriend of Grace Jones and was responsible for her whole image around the time she was about the coolest person in the world. All the cool images you have seen of her, looking super sharp, androgynous, robot-like – are down to him. He used to cut up negatives and stitch them together to exaggerate her features – for this image he extended her legs and arms so she seems like a sculpture. Also, The Alan Parsons Project – Ammonia Avenue. I saw this when I was a kid – a photo of some industrial facility which has been mirrored. It stuck with me forever and has been an inspiration for the kind of work I do today.

What track best describes your favorite project you’ve worked on so far? APW: Eyes Without A Face – Billy Idol.  I’ve done a lot of work with sex dolls and robots and cryonics and surgery.
