Sumayya Vally Unveils First Permanent UAE Project With Dubai’s Elie Khouri Art Foundation

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The Elie Khouri Art Foundation (EKAF) has appointed Sumayya Vally of Johannesburg- and London-based practice Counterspace to design its permanent home in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. Scheduled to open in late 2028, the institution will occupy a repurposed villa in the Jumeirah 1 district, transforming the existing residential property and its surrounding grounds into a space for contemporary art exhibitions, education, and public programming. The appointment forms part of EKAF's plans to establish a permanent platform for contemporary art in Dubai, with architecture and curatorial programming developed in parallel.

The Elie Khouri Art Foundation (EKAF) has appointed Sumayya Vally of Johannesburg- and London-based practice Counterspace to design its permanent home in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. Scheduled to open in late 2028, the institution will occupy a repurposed villa in the Jumeirah 1 district, transforming the existing residential property and its surrounding grounds into a space for contemporary art exhibitions, education, and public programming. The appointment forms part of EKAF's plans to establish a permanent platform for contemporary art in Dubai, with architecture and curatorial programming developed in parallel.

Vally will lead the architectural transformation of the existing villa, incorporating galleries, educational spaces, pavilions, and a garden, alongside hospitality areas and outdoor spaces for seasonal events and gatherings. The project will retain the site's domestic character while adapting its spatial organization and landscape to accommodate public use. Rather than approaching the commission as a conventional museum building, the design will explore the relationship between the existing house, its architectural features, and its future role as a cultural institution.

The architectural concept draws on two spatial references: the street architecture of Beirut and the inward-facing organization of traditional Gulf courtyard houses. According to the practice, the existing villa's doors, cornices, balustrades, and room arrangements will inform the development of a contemporary architectural language, translating elements of domestic architecture into a setting for public and cultural exchange. The project is intended to establish a relationship between the memory of the existing building and its new institutional function.

Founded by French-Lebanese art collector, entrepreneur, and philanthropist Elie Khouri, EKAF builds on more than two decades of collecting and engagement with the international art world. Under the direction of Caroline Louca, the Foundation is developing a programme that will bring together works from its collection, temporary exhibitions, institutional collaborations, and loans. Its activities will extend beyond the presentation of artworks to include learning initiatives and public events intended to encourage dialogue and exchange around contemporary art.
