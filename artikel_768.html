Scratchy Ireland still prove too strong for Australia at Twickenham

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Tries: Henshaw (26), Timoney (37), Baloucoune (43), McNabney (73,81) Conversions: Prendergast (27,39,44,74,81) Penalties: Prendergast (43)

Tries: Williams (22), Thomas (56) Conversions: Gordon (23,57)

Ireland vs Australia Nations Championship 8:10pm Friday 27th November

Ireland rounded off their Nations Championship campaign with a sixth victory from seven Tests, defeating Australia 38-14 at Allianz Stadium Twickenham.

Such a record can only be matched by England and France, should either or both defeat New Zealand and South Africa this weekend, respectively - Ireland finished behind England on points difference and France due to bonus points in the northern hemisphere standings.

On Friday, centre Robbie Henshaw, back-row Nick Timoney, wing Rob Baloucoune and replacement No 8 James McNabney (two) scored tries, with Sam Prendergast adding all five conversions and a penalty.

Ireland - Tries: Henshaw (26), Timoney (37), Baloucoune (43), McNabney (73,80+1). Cons: Prendergast (26,39,44,74,80+1). Pens: Prendergast (40+3).

Australia - Tries: Williams (22), Thomas (56). Cons: Gordon (23, 57).

Andy Farrell's Ireland were far from their best on the night, but the Wallabies could only score two tries through lock Jeremy Williams and scrum-half Kalani Thomas, wasting a further nine 22 entries up against an extremely tough Irish defence.

The victory also saw Ireland claim the Raeburn Shield from Australia - rugby's lineal world title which dates back to the first Test match ever played in 1871. Australia had won it by beating Wales, who claimed it from New Zealand in Cardiff a fortnight ago.

Ireland began the contest well off the standard they produced in victory over world No 1-ranked South Africa last week, with their attack struggling to click and errors widespread.

Australia, despite a wealth of early possession and territory, produced a number of mistakes themselves, though, with fly-half Carter Gordon guilty of missing touch with a penalty from hand in the most glaring example.

In the end, it took until the 22nd minute for the Wallabies to strike for the opener: Williams scoring from close range after Tadhg Beirne had actually stolen an Australia five-metre lineout, but Ronan Kelleher had spilled to surrender possession back.

Within four minutes, Ireland were level, as they struck for a try off their very first 22 entry: a clever lineout move to the tail setting scrum-half Jamison Gibson-Park breaking back down the blindside, before Kelleher was stopped just short.

Centre Stuart McCloskey did the damage from there, as he took on the ball and fed midfield partner Henshaw, who slid over untouched.

Three minutes from the end of the half, Ireland displayed their clinical nature again as Timoney scored following a well-constructed rolling maul to move Farrell's side in front.

Ireland almost had a third try before the break in the final play, only for centre Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii to stick out a hand in a deliberate knock-on. The Wallaby avoided a yellow card, but Prendergast stretched the lead to 10 points off the tee.

Into the second half, Ireland started far brighter, with Jacob Stockdale almost scoring down the left less than two minutes in, and Baloucoune then scoring a minute later after collecting a Prendergast cross-field kick James Ramm could only deflect into his path.

Prendergast converted for a 24-7 lead, and what ensued was virtually 10 minutes of sustained Australia attack in the Irish 22 for no points.

The Wallabies did finally cross for their second try through Thomas in the 55th minute, with the score surviving a TMO check into the grounding.

Gordon narrowed Ireland's lead to 10 points with the conversion, but they failed to build from there.

Instead, Ireland's pack tore forward for a scrum penalty against the head in the lead-up to their fourth try - impressive back-row McNabney showing his power off the bench to wrestle over by the posts.

In the final attack of the half, Ireland then blew out the scoreline beyond what had truly transpired in the contest, in truth, with the exciting McNabney finishing again.

Ireland head coach Andy Farrell told ITV Sport:

"Everyone could see it was a bit error-ridden with all sorts of things going on.

"But I suppose the way that we stayed composed and did it in our own way was a credit to how we kept on going.

"That was epitomised in the first half where we had no territory and no possession whatsoever, and we go in with the lead, which was very pleasing.

"It wasn't a brilliant performance, but at the same time I'm very pleased with the composure to make sure we keep on going.

"We would have taken that [going four from four] at the start, wouldn't we? Sport, when you're playing good sides in international rugby, doesn't always go your way.

"It didn't last week, but we still found a way. We did that today. We'll take that and we'll move on to the Six Nations.

"We're very pleased with the competition that we're building, and we've got a lot of good players back home who are dying to get back into the room.

Giving his verdict on Ireland's year, Brian O'Driscoll told ITV Sport:

"It was a disappointing Argentina, so you can't really gauge a huge amount. It was a good victory against them. Fiji, the expectation there is to win.

"The big one was South Africa, and they stood up to the plate there. They delivered a performance they should be proud of.

"This is a very well-settled team, except in one or two positions, and mainly your pivotal position at No 10.

"To give him [Prendergast] the confidence to guide the team around in a big game like that will go a long way towards restoring belief in all the players that have played 70, 80 Tests.

"But mostly for him as he's kind of forging his path as a relative newcomer to the Test Arena.

"I think, on the whole, very pleasing. If you had given us 10 victories in 12 games this calendar year, we'd have bitten your hand off, considering how they lost to France in the opening game of the Six Nations.

"The only other loss being to New Zealand in Eden Park.

"Are we World Cup contenders yet? I wouldn't say yes, no, I wouldn't. I think there's other teams a little bit ahead of us, but a year is a long way to go, and hopefully we can narrow that gap."

Ireland fly-half Sam Prendergast told ITV:

"I think we stuck with it pretty well. There were some scrappy parts we'd like to be a bit better at.

"I thought we were quite clinical in their 22 but scrappy at times. I think he [Andy Farrell] will have a similar gist; there's definitely some good parts. There's loads we can be better at and want to be better at today.

"Coming into the autumn, we knew we had a couple of big games to start. We went four from four and that's what we were targeting. There was no lack of motivation.

"I'm really enjoying [playing for Ireland]. We're building towards the Rugby World Cup. Loads of highs and lows at the moment so, I won't get too high."

Australia head coach Les Kiss told ITV Sport:

"The first half we got some traction but we're disappointed with our end zone. Ireland were very clinical and took their chances when they came up.

"Credit to them, we needed to persist a bit longer. They were up for it. That was the difference; they took the key chances when they had to.

"Fair play to them, they put the scoreboard pressure on us. There's still a lot of good things there and a lot of things to look forward to, we've just got to build more consistency throughout our game."

Ireland are next in action during the 2027 Six Nations in February, when they start with a huge Test against England in Dublin at the Aviva Stadium on Friday February 5 (8.10pm kick-off).

Australia now enter their offseason and are not in action again until April 25 2027 when they face New Zealand at Suncorp Stadium in Brisbane. Australia are hosting the Rugby World Cup in 2027 between October 1-November 13.
