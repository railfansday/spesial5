Jayson Tatum deserves better than what Celtics are giving him — much, much better

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

As the final six seconds wound down before halftime Sunday night, Jayson Tatum pulled up from 32 feet (aka Stephen Curry range), with his left foot on Golden State's logo and drained it. He raised his right hand and took his business-as-usual stroll into the players' tunnel, as he passed Bill Russell for eighth on the Celtics' all-time scoring list.

"That's cool," Tatum told reporters, per NBC Sports Boston, after the Celtics' 88-87 loss in Golden State.

"What [Russell] meant to society, what he meant to the game of basketball here in the Bay, and obviously to this organization. So, obviously, anytime you're mentioned with names like that, it's an honor. You're doing something right."

In the second half, smiles were at a premium on Boston's sideline.

Tatum's return from a rest night in Sacramento was the only thing keeping the Celtics competitive in Golden State, even against a Curry-less Warriors team. He recorded his third double-double of the season, finishing with 27 points, 10 rebounds and six assists, albeit on 8-for-20 shooting. He was also the only Celtic to score more than 14 points and make at least three 3-pointers (4/11), as supporting help was in short supply for the fourth straight time, dooming Boston again.

He had a chance to sink the game-winner, but missed. And that wasn't an indictment of Tatum's lackluster execution or inability to find a good look. Rather, it was a situation the Celtics could've avoided altogether.

Without Paul George by his side, Tatum received very minimal assistance.

Derrick White was the team's second-leading scorer with 14 points, but his contribution wasn't pretty. White missed his first five 3-point attempts before finally getting his sixth to fall. He shot 6-for-21 overall, including 2-for-12 from three, continuing his ice-cold shooting slump (30.6 percent over his last three games) while becoming a detrimental non-factor.

He continued firing away to no avail, adding to the Celtics' mounting inefficiency as they finished 14-for-47 from long range.

After White, Luka Garza was Boston's third-leading scorer, providing 10 points off the bench.

The scheme of hucking and chucking in hopes of lighting a fire within the team has been an utter failure ever since the Celtics boarded their flight from Boston to Denver a few days ago. They went 11-for-44 against the Nuggets, 13-for-58 against the 2-14 Kings, setting a franchise record with 45 missed 3-pointers, and then 14-for-47 in Golden State.

Over their last three games on the road, the Celtics went 38-for-149 (25.5 percent) from three, and 68-for-205 (33.2 percent) since the start of the four-game losing skid.

It's only held the Celtics back. They suffered a four-point loss to Nikola Jokić, followed by back-to-back one-point losses to Sacramento and Golden State, sending Boston to a fourth straight loss for the first time in five years and down to third in the Eastern Conference.

Shot selection and discipline have been the difference throughout the road trip.

"I think if the season ended today, I'd be pretty concerned," Celtics head coach Joe Mazzulla told reporters. "But we have [65] games left, so you have to continue to trust the fact that if we're getting good shots, which more times than not we are, there's obviously times out there when we can get better shots, right? I'd say every game there's probably 10 to 15 that we can improve upon. So you try to narrow that down every game to taking less not shots that you want to take.

"But if we're looking at it and saying, 'OK, these are the shots that we're getting, and we're good with these shots,' you have to be able to trust, over the course of a season, that that's going to work out. I think last night's a good example. We got [23] more shots than [Sacramento]. So at some point you have to make it. Just because you get more shots than the other team doesn't mean it guarantees you a win."

Mazzulla's tried to do right by the group by divvying up rest days in between back-to-backs to keep everyone fresh. But so far, it hasn't worked out. The Celtics aren't receiving the depth production needed on nights where Tatum or George or another starter(s) sits to rest. They've gone 2-3 this season on the second night of back-to-backs, losing to the Rockets, Magic and Warriors.

In Denver, Tatum took responsibility for his poorly timed technical foul in the final 6.4 seconds of regulation after committing four turnovers in the third quarter. This time, however, the loss wasn't on him. Tatum spent the entire night doing his part in every way possible, while those around him made getting into the win column feel like climbing Mount Everest.

Had Tatum scored just one more basket, he would have matched or surpassed the 29 combined points scored by the other four starters — White, Sam Hauser, Neemias Queta and Hugo González.

Payton Pritchard took ownership after airballing a potential game-winner in Sacramento, but failed to respond 24 hours later in Golden State. He played 28 minutes off the bench, scoring only five points on 2-for-8 shooting while committing three turnovers.

He's an unquestioned vital piece with George on the floor. So, on nights when George isn't available, it's even more critical for Pritchard to rise to the occasion and help shoulder some of the weight that Tatum had to carry all by himself on Sunday night.

Mazzulla emphasized that his level of concern for the team remains low at the moment.

"I'll be concerned when we stop making shots and having a lack of effort on defense," Mazzulla added. "That, to me, is a concerning issue that you would be worried about. Our first-shot defense has been very good. Our effort has been very good. We have to play efficient offense, and we have to rebound. When effort becomes an issue, that's when I'll be concerned."

As Mazzulla mentioned, there's far more basketball left to be played than has already been played. But while Tatum bears much of the responsibility as Boston's leading man, that doesn't mean everyone else is absolved when the team collectively lays an egg.

Tatum is on the hook as the No. 1. The Celtics are on the hook for making his job easier, not more difficult.
