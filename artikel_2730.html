Aaron Rodgers brought his humiliating benching on himself

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

If I was the head coach of the Pittsburgh Steelers, I'm not sure I would have made the same decision as Mike McCarthy. Benching Aaron Rodgers took some real chutzpah.

Not because Rodgers is a legend destined for first-ballot entry into the Pro Football Hall of Fame one day. But because the Steelers don't actually have a sure-fire better option.

You benched Aaron Rodgers for Mason Rudolph?! How humiliating. I understand why Rodgers didn't show up to work today.

That said, Pittsburgh had to try something. Because the season is dangerously close to slipping away. The Steelers are 6-4 through 11 weeks and clinging to the final AFC wild card spot. After what we saw from them in Sunday's loss to Philadelphia, they don't appear likely to stay there. Friday's game against the Broncos is close to a must-win.

Rodgers might not have been the team's biggest problem (though that's not out the question), but he definitely hasn't been the solution for a dead offense -- no matter how much he points the finger at others. Rodgers ranks 26th in the NFL in both QBR and passing yards per attempt. He's 29th of 32 quarterbacks in EPA per play, according to RBSDM.

Those type of numbers would get any other quarterback on a playoff hopeful benched. Just ask Kyler Murray, who had an identical passer rating as Rodgers and lost his starting job despite a 5-1 record for the Vikings. And he's 14 years younger.

At this stage of Rodgers' career, in what was announced as his last season, he's not above the same treatment. In fact, we maybe should have expected it. He's been in decline at least five years and turns 43 next week. Even Peyton Manning ceded games to Brock Osweiler his final year in the NFL. If Rodgers wanted to avoid this outcome, he needed to take some accountability and play better -- or retire sooner.

The only thing wrong with Pittsburgh benching Rodgers is how it was apparently handled. On Monday, McCarthy said Rodgers was still the team's starter. One day later, the benching was announced in a late-night social media post before McCarthy could discuss it with the team Wednesday morning. It isn't going over well with some players, according to The Athletic's Mike DeFabo.

Rodgers can be upset with that part. But not the decision. Most aging players don't get to go out on their own terms. That's something he needed to accept before deciding to come back one last year.
