USC's defense has no time for feeling sorry for itself

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

LOS ANGELES -- Indiana's offense had looked questionable for weeks. Against USC, it found what it was missing, coming up with four plays of 35 yards or more. Quarterback Grant Wilson threw for 305 passing yards as the Hoosiers totaled 451 yards in a 27-13 win. USC's defense says the film was less damning than the box score, but knows where it slipped. The more important question, the defenders said, is how they answer.

Defensive tackle Alex VanSumeren said the message came from coach Skyler Jones, known to the players as coach Sky, after the Indiana game.

"How you respond is how you're going to be judged," VanSumeren said. "You're not judged in times of when everything's peaches and cream, but in times of adversity."

That framing helps the group look at the tape fairly. VanSumeren gave the defensive line credit for limiting the Hoosiers' inside run game, saying "everything bounced." Nonetheless, he made no excuses.

"We did things well. It's not all negative," he said. "At the end of the day, we didn't get the job done. We realize that, and we're not happy about it...control what we can control."

Safety Prophet Brown said the job slipped on leverage and communication, and he praised Indiana for disguising its looks. The numbers reflect that split, with USC currently ranked 38th nationally against the run and 101st against the pass, according to ESPN. Brown's suggested fix is about speed.

"It's happening very quick on that field," he said. "For us to just speed up our process of communication...just speeding it up a little bit, we could definitely dominate on third down."

Defensive tackle Jide Abasiri was just as candid about the run defense in the second half, when Indiana began to find more room. He said the interior group measures itself by things that don't always make the stat sheet, such as "blowing up plays" or "causing the running back to cut back or stop his feet." Coach Sky's lesson for his linemen, Abasiri said, is the same one.

"You take care of the little stuff, the details, and that's when the big things take care of themselves."

Their ideology is visible in practice, too. Center Tobias Raymond, who lines up against VanSumeren on the practice field, said he never takes a rep off.

"He's just one of those guys that always shows up and just lines up against you, and you know he's going to go full speed," Raymond said. "He's going to be doing super aggressive things and playing hard, and so he's always a fun guy to line up against next to at practice."

That effort is the point, VanSumeren said. He described the mood as a team without any room for self-pity.

"There's no laying down or feeling sorry for ourselves," he said. "That's not flying in this program...that's been reinforced from the top to the bottom."

Maryland will test its response quickly. Having won two straight, backup quarterback Cardell Williams completed 21 of 23 passes last Saturday against Wisconsin, throwing for a touchdown and rushing for two more in a 24-21 victory. Usual starter Malik Washington was listed as questionable for this week's contest on Wednesday's availability report. VanSumeren said the Terrapins "should have had almost three wins" in a row after they fell just short in a 20-17 loss against Illinois on Halloween.

Brown remains confident that the Trojans' defense will be ready for either quarterback.

"They got two good dynamic quarterbacks that could hurt you with their legs, and so we're just prepared for either one."

It's also the fifth-year senior's final game in the Coliseum. When he looks back, he said, the hard stretches will matter the most to him.

"I've had a lot of highs and lows, but I'm definitely going to remember the lows, and not in a negative light...it helped form who I am today, and that's a life lesson I could take throughout the rest of my life..." Brown said.

Abasiri, who has played alongside Brown for three seasons, called him a "great leader."

"He's a veteran, so just seeing him and his presence across my three years has been really encouraging," Abasiri said.

The talking is done, and on Senior Day at the Coliseum, the Trojans' defense gets its chance to answer.
