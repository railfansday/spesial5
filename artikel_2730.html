How a baseball coach helped build a national esports contender at Choctaw

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For Oklahoma's Choctaw High School esports team, everything has happened pretty quickly.

At the beginning of last school year, the newly minted program had a few students trickling in, some interest here and there. Just one season later, the Yellowjackets are ranked No. 25 in the nation and sitting at fourth in the USA TODAY Sports esports national title race.

"It's just kind of finding the right pieces to put the puzzle together," said head coach Tim Ackerman, who has built the team up over the past year. "Everyone here has been super, super helpful every step of the way."

Ackerman spent years as a high school baseball coach, a role he still reprises each spring, before taking up the mantle as Choctaw's inaugural esports leader. He's infused some of that more traditional coaching experience, namely his emphasis on competitive practice, into his new role.

"I've got really good gamers here who know a lot about their games, play their games well. But when it comes down to it, I think competing is a very important gap that a lot of schools miss on," Ackerman said. "Understanding how to control momentum, how to control your emotions as you go…a professional approach when you're going into a match, rather than just you're at home with a controller."

It took a minute for the program to find its footing, but over time, students who might not have been attracted to it at first glance started to trickle in. They'd pop their heads into Ackerman's room, telling him about their favorite games and scoring invites to "show me what you got" as he collected the right players for the team.

Senior Blake Wallace, the team's "Marvel Rivals" captain, was convinced to give it a shot though it did take second.

"When I saw it my sophomore year in the classes you could take, I was like, 'Only nerds are going to take that. I'm not going to do that," Wallace said. But he came around. Now as a captain, he's helped chart the course for the ever-growing squad.

"My favorite part is that we're always improving," he said. "We do things where we go through games we lost and just break down everything we did wrong, and what we could do better next game."

The success and popularity of Choctaw's esports squad has already extended to the middle school level, where Ackerman said nearly 70 students tried out for the small number of spots available on the Choctaw teams there.

"I think the future is pretty bright over here," he said. "There's a lot of really excited, good players coming in, showing interest and wanting to be a part of it."
