Miami HC Mario Cristobal has strong words for officials after Notre Dame loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Elija Lofton came down in the corner of the end zone at Notre Dame Stadium with the ball in both hands and 3:43 left on the clock, and for a moment the Miami Hurricanes thought they had tied the game. Luke Talich of the Notre Dame Fighting Irish knocked the ball loose as Lofton finished the play, the officials ruled the fourth-and-6 pass from the 18 incomplete, and a short replay review left the call alone. Notre Dame held on, 31-24, and handed top-ranked Miami its first loss of the season.

Notre Dame led 24-3 early in the third quarter behind CJ Carr, who finished with 319 passing yards and three touchdowns, and the Hurricanes were playing without Malachi Toney, who was hurt in practice.

Darian Mensah accounted for three touchdowns to pull Miami within 31-24 with 9:53 left. After the Irish missed a 53-yard field goal, the Hurricanes drove to the Notre Dame 18 and gave Mensah one chance to find Lofton.

Miami Head Coach Mario Cristobal heads off the field after the College Football Playoff National Championship college football game at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens on Monday, Jan. 19, 2026.

Mario Cristobal went straight to that play when his postgame news conference began.

"You know I'm pissed off. So I mean, what do you want me to say? I mean, it's b -hit. And I'll leave it at that. And again, that's the way it was seen by them. So I gotta live with it and respect it. And our players gotta live with it. And we gotta do better. And you know what? Again, don't leave in the hands of the officials. Play better, coach better, find a way to win. But I again, I don't need to be criticizing officials. Just move on." Cristobal said.

He later apologized for letting the call take over the start of his remarks.

"I didn't even see him knock the ball out at the end, so I thought it was a touchdown. You can't leave it up to one play. Can't leave it in the hands of the officials," Mensah said.

The last time these teams met, the result carried all the way to Selection Sunday. Miami beat Notre Dame 27-24 in the 2025 opener, and in December the playoff committee used that head-to-head win to place the Hurricanes in the field over the Irish for the final at-large spot, and committee chair Hunter Yurachek pointed to that game after the committee rewatched it.

Notre Dame now holds that same tiebreaker.

Miami has three regular-season games left, starting with the Duke Blue Devils on Nov. 14, and Toney will miss that one. Cristobal said Toney's ankle surgery "went great" and that the receiver should return in about four weeks.
