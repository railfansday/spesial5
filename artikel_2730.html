Micah Parsons’ eye-catching request of Rams star prompts ‘big bro’ troll job

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Myles Garrett and Micah Parsons have spent years arguing over who is the best defensive player in football. On Wednesday night, they'll finally get another chance to settle the debate where it matters most.

With the Los Angeles Rams preparing to host the Green Bay Packers in a Thanksgiving Eve showdown on Netflix, Garrett revealed Monday that the latest exchange between the two superstar pass rushers had nothing to do with sacks, quarterback pressures or bragging rights.

"The dialogue was, 'Hey, big bro, you got any extra tickets?'" Garrett said, recounting their conversation. "'Yeah, I got you, man.'"

Micah Parsons of the Green Bay Packers looks on prior to a game against the Carolina Panthers at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin, Thursday, Oct. 29, 2026. Getty Images

Apparently, even one of the NFL's most feared defensive players needs a hookup from his 'big brother.'

The friendly exchange was considerably tamer than the warning Garrett delivered during an appearance on The Rich Eisen Show last week.

Asked what would happen when the two finally met after Wednesday's game, Garrett predicted a familiar conversation.

"I'll probably shake his hand and laugh and joke with him," Garrett told Eisen. "He'll tell me about how my light is dimming, and I'll point to the scoreboard and the stat sheet and how I had a great game and he had a good game.

That's the kind of trash talk that makes their relationship so entertaining.

Myles Garrett on "The Edge with Micah Parsons" podcast. Bleacher Report

The two have traded verbal jabs for years, including on Parsons' podcast, The Edge with Micah Parsons, where debates over defensive supremacy have occasionally sounded like two brothers arguing over who gets to sit in the front seat of their parents' car.

Parsons once declared himself the best overall football player in the world, prompting Garrett to call him delusional.

Garrett, a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year, has never been shy about defending his throne. But underneath the competitive banter is genuine admiration for each other.

Both players have battled knee injuries this season, making their highly anticipated meeting at SoFi Stadium even more meaningful.

Los Angeles Rams Myles Garrett (95) plays against the San Francisco 49ers in Melbourne, Australia, Thursday, Sept. 10, 2026. Sports Illustrated via Getty Images

Green Bay Packers Micah Parsons (1) leaves the field injured during the second half of an NFL football game against the Denver Broncos at Empower Field at Mile High in Denver, Sunday, Dec. 14, 2025. Icon Sportswire via Getty Images

Garrett underwent arthroscopic knee surgery earlier this season and spent weeks on injured reserve before returning to the Rams' defense. Parsons tore his ACL and had meniscus damage suffered last December during a game against the Broncos and underwent season-ending surgery.

Through the first 11 weeks of the season, he's been rehabbing and recovering and is expected to make his season debut against the Rams on Wednesday.

On Monday, Garrett made it clear that having his longtime rival healthy and back on the field means more than winning their personal statistical showdown.

"The game is better when Micah Parsons is on the field doing his thing," Garrett said. "So I'm happy to see him back out there."

Green Bay Packers edge rusher Micah Parsons practices Wednesday, Nov. 4, 2026, in Green Bay, Wis. AP Photo/Steve Megargee

Asked what makes Parsons so dangerous, Garrett offered a scouting report.

"He's smaller. He moves well. He has great get-off. He has supreme instincts," Garrett said. "He really is the full package."

For all the jokes about who's better, Garrett understands that Wednesday's game carries consequences beyond their personal rivalry. The Rams and Packers are fighting for NFC playoff positioning, with the head-to-head tiebreaker potentially proving decisive.

And while the two won't actually line up against each other, every sack, pressure and quarterback hit will add another chapter to their ongoing debate.

Los Angeles Rams defensive end Myles Garrett (95) sacks Washington Commanders quarterback Jayden Daniels (5) in Landover, Md., Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026. AP Photo/Nick Wass

"I'm proud of him, no matter what the outcome is," Garrett said.

Of course, Garrett has already made his preferred outcome abundantly clear.

He wants the victory, the better stat sheet and the last laugh.

And apparently, he's even providing the tickets for Parsons' friends and family to watch it happen.
