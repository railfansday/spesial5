Pokemon cards are basically impossible to get hold of right now. I am terminally online, have friends who own and work in TCG stores, and regularly frequent supermarkets, and I can barely get hold of them, so I know just how hard it is for the average collector.

The trading card game is now seen as a prime investment opportunity by crypto bros and scalpers, and as such, nefarious techniques, like bots, are deployed to scoop up as much stock as possible. That's also why we see queues forming outside stores 12 hours before they open, since people have no other option unless they want to miss out. Unless...

It turns out, avid Pokemon TCG fans/scalpers/people with too much money are using services and paying hundreds of dollars for people to queue for them to get the hottest products.

The Pokemon Trading Card Game's 30th Anniversary expansion is one of the best in a long time. I called it "the most refreshing in years," and aside from Ascended Heroes, I think it's the best set produced since Evolving Skies. Naturally, such a set has drawn major attention. Lapsed Pokemon TCG fans are returning for the 30th anniversary, long-time fans are, of course, there, and scalpers are seeing it as the perfect chance to make a quick buck—despite tons of stock, competition has never been fiercer.

Things have gotten so bad that people are actually willing to fork out their hard-earned cash, just so they don't have to queue.

Odd-job websites like Airtasker have been flooded with requests from said Pokemon TCG fans/scalpers/people with too much money, asking people to queue for cards.

One listing, which has, unsurprisingly, been filled, is paying $300 for somebody to queue at a Best Buy in California for Pokemon cards. It read, "November 6, there's a Best Buy release of Pokemon cards. Apparently it will start much earlier, so will budget for a 3am start. I can no longer go with my son and I don't want him to miss out. I am open to paying for a couple or two friends to go..."

That's right, somebody has paid $300 on top of RRP just to get their hands on the latest product. At this point, it's almost certainly cheaper to pay scalper prices.

That's far from the only listing, either. A second in California offered $200 for somebody to queue for Best Buy's UPC drop, and multiple other listings for $100+ were made in Texas, Pennsylvania, and Virginia. The world has gone mad.
