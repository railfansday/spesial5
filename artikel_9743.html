An embedded form or survey builder still ships skip logic and scoring in the browser

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Form and survey builders often embed a runtime in the customer’s page: a script tag, a webpack chunk, or a Web Component. The pitch is instant skip logic, live validation, calculated scores, and no round-trip between questions. That runtime is JavaScript, and it usually includes the definition of the form itself.

If the value of your product is the engine — branching, scoring, price quotes, qualification — then that engine is in the browser. This article is about what an embedded form runtime actually exposes, what must be re-checked on the server, and how to protect the engine you still have to ship.

Minifying the runtime will rename pickNext. It will not hide "disqualify", a distinctive weight, or a question you thought was secret because the UI had not reached it yet.

This is the part teams regret after a launch. A hiring screen, a pricing quiz, a medical-style symptom tree, or a lead-qualification form sometimes puts the "real" criteria in client-side skip logic: reject if salary is below a number, skip the expensive plan if the company size is small, show a deluxe upsell only when the score is high.

Assume anyone can read those criteria. If they must stay unpublished — interview answer keys, fraud signals, medical cutoffs, unpublished prices — do not put them in the embed. Fetch the next question from the server, or at least re-evaluate the result on the server before it means anything.

Submitted answers should also go to your server in raw form. A client-computed score: 97 is a suggestion.

Pretty-printing the chunk no longer reconstructs the original scoring routine; you get bytecode and a dispatcher. That is slower than a thin JSON walker, which is why you protect the engine, not the entire form UI.
