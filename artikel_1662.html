Manchester City found guilty of 114 charges

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Manchester City have been found guilty of 114 of the 115 charges brought by the Premier League over breaches of its financial rules. It is a decision that, if upheld, would equate to one of the biggest scandals in football history.

City face a range of sanctions from a fine to points deductions and even expulsion from the Premier League. Or a combination of all, under rule W51 of its handbook. No punishment has yet been announced.

If the verdict is upheld, with City expected to appeal, it will also raise question marks over the 20 trophies the club have won under Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan, whose Abu Dhabi group took over in 2008.

The charges can be broken down into five categories and relate to a period from 2009-23 in which City won six League Cups, three FA Cups, seven league titles and the Champions League. There is the provision for removing past titles, but that would appear remote.

City have been the dominant force in English football for most of that time, but that status is under threat with a verdict that will send shockwaves around Europe and could have huge ramifications for the club.

That is unless they can win an appeal, with sources cautioning it still may take up to another two to three years for the case to be resolved, following on from an already five-year investigation.

The Premier League’s rules provide for an independent appeal board with broad powers, including the ability to uphold, vary or overturn the independent commission’s decision.

It would therefore appear that despite the verdict, the case is far from being resolved and punishment decided. The details of the commission’s findings, when published, will be crucial to determining what happens next.

But if it is upheld, the verdict also opens up the possibility of City facing legal action from other clubs who feel they missed out on trophies and even merit money from league placings.

It is understood Premier League clubs are already discussing legal action and possible compensation claims as a result of the guilty verdict.

Each of those cases would have to be held individually, potentially leading to years more of litigation. Conversely, if City win their appeal, they too could take legal action against the Premier League the damage caused to their reputation.

There has not yet been an official confirmation of the independent panel’s verdict from the Premier League. The Premier League declined to comment.

However, several sources have told Telegraph Sport that the panel has found City guilty of all but one of the charges they faced. The one that has not been proved is said to be one of the most serious of the 115.

The damning verdict for City is a major new test of fragile UK relations with the United Arab Emirates, with whom bilateral trade was worth £25bn last year. Redacted freedom of information documents, seen by Telegraph Sport, show Abu Dhabi hosted UK government officials including Lord Stockwood, then investment minister, as recently as February. Khaldoon Al Mubarak, the City chairman and trusted adviser to the UAE president, was present at the meeting. Government notes from the meeting recorded that he “noted they are very proud of what they have achieved through their investments in Manchester”.

The Government previously caused acrimony among senior leaders in Abu Dhabi after putting forward legislation to ban foreign states from owning UK newspapers and news magazines. The intervention prompted the collapse of a takeover of The Telegraph in April 2024, which was largely funded by Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan, the owner of City and vice-president and deputy prime minister of the United Arab Emirates.

The previous UK government has admitted its embassy in Abu Dhabi and the Foreign Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) in London discussed the charges levelled at City by the Premier League. However it refused to disclose the correspondence.

City have steadfastly maintained they are innocent, promising “irrefutable” evidence in support of their position, and had assembled some of the most expensive lawyers in the country to argue their case. Despite the verdict, the club are understood to be confident that it will win the argument in the end. Senior figures are said to be calm about the latest development, as significant as it seems.

City rebuilt their team this summer and it is understood that signings were brought in on conventional player contracts, rather than terms including clauses triggered in the event of a heavy punishment. Given the club have consistently denied breaching financial regulations, and their deals for new players to work with head coach Enzo Maresca underlines their position.

On Friday, a City spokesman said: “The Premier League process remains ongoing, with significant elements to be completed, and subject to strict confidentiality. As such, Manchester City FC’s position remains consistent with the club’s statement of February 2023.

“The club have diligently respected due process for eight years on the basis that the Premier League board and executive would behave as an independent, impartial and fair-minded regulator, free from partisan influence.”

Sources close to Pep Guardiola, the former City manager who stepped down last summer after 10 years of remarkable success at the club, including a treble triumph in 2023 and an unprecedented fourth straight league title in 2024, insist he has not been informed of any verdict.

In the days after City were first charged, Guardiola mounted a fierce defence of the club, claiming: “I am fully convinced we will be [proved] innocent.”

The verdict vindicates the Premier League’s decision to launch an investigation after explosive allegations that City had routinely broken financial rules were first made in Der Spiegel in 2018 based on the Football Leaks documents.

Uefa previously accused City of breaches of its financial regulations and announced in 2020 that it was suspending the club from the Champions League for two years for “overstating” sponsorship revenue between 2012 and 2016.

City denied the Uefa charges and, after an appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport, the two-year ban was overturned. Cas found that a number of the claims brought by Uefa had fallen outside a five-year “time bar” and others were “not established”.

It did, however, still impose a €10m fine after ruling that City failed to cooperate with investigators. City had previously also agreed a settlement with Uefa of €20m in 2014 following an investigation into their finances.

Legal experts agreed that the commission’s verdict is far from the end of the case. Yasin Patel, barrister at Church Court Chambers, said: “City’s lawyers will now analyse this decision and look at the options open to them to appeal, which may include procedural impropriety, wrongful interpretation of law, and legal errors – importantly, they can guarantee an appeal.

“Sanctions could include a major fine, points deduction, relegation or even all three. Factoring in whether titles and trophies should be stripped, whether other clubs have been robbed of further revenue and opportunities due to City’s wrongdoings and losses incurred, we safely say that this is only the first chapter of a long-running legal saga that will not end any time soon.”

Ian Hargreaves, partner with specialist disputes firm Quillon Law, said the verdict was “not the end of the story but the beginning of a new phase”, and added: “There is also the question of whether the panel’s decision can withstand a hard-fought appeal, and on what grounds any challenge might be brought. Much will depend on the detail of the panel’s findings and reasoning, which will inevitably be closely scrutinised by both sides.”

Former Manchester City midfielder Rodri, who won the Ballon d’Or during his trophy-laden spell at the club, said he believed the club were innocent.

“For the players, it’s different than it is for journalists. I can understand all opinions, but we try to stay positive,” the Spain midfielder said while on international duty.

“When you see all the people who worked hard at City to achieve success, you know what we achieved can’t be taken away. It wasn’t paid for with money, but with effort and teamwork. Everything we achieved was deserved; we marked one of the great eras of English football. I believe in the club’s innocence – we already went through this in 2020.”

Andy Burnham, the Prime Minister, said: “I think we would all need to study the independent commission’s report in some considerable detail before reaching any conclusions.”

Zohran Kwame Mamdani, the mayor of New York, posted “114” in relation to the charges while former Manchester United players David de Gea and Rio Ferdinand both asked whether they should be due winner’s medals from past seasons.

The Premier League statement in February 2023 said that “a number of alleged breaches” had been referred to an independent commission. It then detailed each specific rule against each season in which the alleged breach took place. The precise number was not clear from the statement and led to varying reports between 115 and 130. Friday’s news suggested the figure was 115.

All the way to 2009-10, the first full season following the club’s takeover by Sheikh Mansour, who is a member of the Abu Dhabi Royal family and the vice president of the United Arab Emirates. There were charges relating to all 14 seasons until the Premier League completed their investigation during the 2022-23 campaign. In that time, City won seven Premier League titles, six League Cups, three FA Cups and the Champions League. They have since won another league title – the first club to do so four times in a row – as well as the Uefa Super Cup, the Fifa Club World Cup and the FA Cup and League Cup double last season.

They can be split into five groups. The biggest relates to the seasons from 2009-10 until 2017-18 and the requirement for clubs to provide accurate financial information and act in the utmost good faith.

The next set of rules concern the full disclosure of manager and player payments. The alleged manager breaches related to the period between 2009-10 and 2012-13, when Roberto Mancini was leading the team. The alleged breaches with respect to player payments relate to all of the seasons between 2010-11 and 2015-16.

As well as providing financial detail, clubs were required to meet Uefa and Premier League rules which were designed to bring a club’s spending broadly in line with their income by placing set limits on losses. City were charged with breaches of Uefa’s licencing and financial fair play rules from 2013-14 until 2017-18, and the Premier League’s profit and sustainability rules from 2015-16 until 2017-18.

The final set of charges spanned the five seasons during which City were being investigated by the Premier League and rules that require members to cooperate in the utmost good faith by providing information and documents.

A full picture will only emerge when the commission publishes its findings but we know that the Premier League investigation began in December 2018 following allegations in the German newspaper Der Spiegel based on the Football Leaks documents. These included alleged payments to former manager Mancini for consultancy work at an Abu Dhabi-based club that was separate to his regular contract at City.

We also know that City were alleged to have breached rules over setting out image rights payments to players in their club contract. Der Spiegel reported that the Premier League investigation also focused on payments to under-age players, as well as whether club sponsors were paying for commercial deals themselves or a significant proportion was in fact effectively coming from owner Sheikh Mansour.

City have always denied breaking any rules. In response to the Premier League statement about the alleged breaches, they said that they have “irrefutable evidence” to support their position and “look forward to this matter being put to rest once and for all.”

At the start of the hearing in September 2024, Guardiola also said: “Wait for the independent panel. We believe we have not done anything wrong.”

Of the Football Leaks documents, City said: “We will not be providing any comment on out-of-context materials. The attempt to damage the club’s reputation is organised and clear.”
