Liquid staking has become a big part of decentralized finance (DeFi). It lets crypto holders earn staking rewards without locking their assets away completely. And as more blockchains look for ways to put capital to better use, liquid staking is spreading beyond Ethereum into ecosystems like Solana and Casper Network.

With traditional staking, you commit your tokens to help secure a proof-of-stake blockchain, and in return you usually canât do much else with them until you unstake. Liquid staking takes a different route. You get a token that represents your staked assets, and these liquid staking tokens (LSTs) can often be traded, moved, or used in supported DeFi apps while the original tokens keep earning staking rewards in the background.

Todayâs market includes decentralized protocols, exchange-run services, and solutions built for specific blockchains. Here are seven liquid staking platforms and ecosystems worth knowing in 2026.

Lido is one of the longest-running liquid staking protocols on Ethereum. It lets users stake ETH without running their own validator or meeting Ethereumâs 32 ETH minimum for a solo validator. Stake ETH through Lido and you receive stETH, a token that represents your position.

Unlike traditional staking, where your assets sit idle, stETH can be put to work across supported DeFi platforms for things like lending and providing liquidity. Lido is now a core part of Ethereumâs staking setup and a clear example of how liquid staking links network security with wider DeFi activity. Its size has also fueled an ongoing debate about validator concentration and decentralization.

Casper Network is bringing liquid staking to its proof-of-stake ecosystem, giving CSPR holders a new way to help secure the network while keeping their funds liquid. With Casper 2.0, liquid staking is built directly into the networkâs core protocol rather than added on through a third party. Users stake CSPR and receive sCSPR, a token representing their staked position.

Casper has partnered with WiseLending, a lending and borrowing protocol built on the network, to extend what sCSPR can do. The underlying CSPR keeps securing the network and earning rewards, while sCSPR can be held, swapped, or used in supported apps across the Casper ecosystem.

This matters because networks increasingly want staking to do more than generate passive income. By tying staking to DeFi, Casper aims to give token holders more freedom in how they manage and use their assets. For younger DeFi ecosystems, liquid staking can also help new financial apps grow. It creates assets that represent staked capital, so users donât have to give up their staking positions to take part.

Rocket Pool offers another take on Ethereum liquid staking, with a strong focus on decentralizing who runs validators. Users stake ETH and receive rETH, a token that reflects the value of their staked ETH plus rewards earned. Rather than paying out rewards by adding more tokens to your balance, rETH is built to grow in value relative to ETH as rewards build up.

Rocket Pool also supports independent node operators, which spreads staking across a wider group of participants. For anyone who wants to stake ETH without managing a validator, itâs an alternative to the larger providers, with a liquid token included.

Jito has become one of the best-known liquid staking providers on Solana. It pairs regular staking rewards with extra rewards from maximal extractable value (MEV). Stake SOL through Jito and you receive JitoSOL, which represents your staked assets and the rewards they earn.

JitoSOL works across supported Solana DeFi apps, so you can use it for lending, providing liquidity, and more. Jito shows how liquid staking providers can stand out through the way they generate rewards. Its deep ties to Solana DeFi also show how an LST can become a useful asset in its own right, well beyond its original staking role.

Marinade Finance is another established liquid staking protocol on Solana. It lets users stake SOL while keeping flexibility through its mSOL token. Deposit SOL into Marinadeâs liquid staking product and you receive mSOL, which represents your staked position and reflects rewards earned over time.

You can use mSOL across supported Solana DeFi apps, including lending protocols and liquidity pools. Marinade puts a lot of weight on spreading stake across many validators, which supports network decentralization while still giving users access to staking rewards. Together with Jito, it shows how varied liquid staking on Solana has become, with users able to pick from different validator strategies, reward structures, and DeFi integrations.

Coinbase offers liquid staking through its Ethereum staking service and Coinbase Wrapped Staked ETH, better known as cbETH. Eligible users can stake ETH on Coinbase and wrap their staked position into cbETH. That lets them transfer the token or use it in supported apps without unstaking the underlying ETH first.

Unlike decentralized protocols such as Lido or Rocket Pool, Coinbase is a centralized service where a major crypto exchange manages the staking for you. That makes it a good example of how liquid staking has grown beyond DeFi protocols to include exchange-based options. Users should still weigh the custody setup, reliance on the platform, and eligibility rules that come with a centralized provider.

Binance offers Ethereum liquid staking through WBETH, or Wrapped Beacon ETH, a token that represents staked ETH and the rewards it earns. Like rETH, WBETH is designed to rise in value relative to ETH as rewards build up, rather than adding tokens to your balance. It can also be transferred or used in supported DeFi apps, depending on network and platform compatibility.

As with Coinbase, Binance shows how centralized exchanges now compete in liquid staking alongside independent DeFi protocols. It may suit users who already stake through exchanges, though custody, withdrawal terms, and platform risk are all worth thinking about.

All liquid staking platforms aim to free up staked capital, but how they do it can vary a lot. Some, like Lido and Rocket Pool, are decentralized protocols. Others, like Coinbase and Binance, are run by exchanges. And ecosystems such as Casper and Solana show how liquid staking can be built into different network designs and DeFi setups.

Key things to compare include validator decentralization, staking rewards, protocol fees, smart contract security, token liquidity, and how many DeFi apps support the token. Liquid staking also comes with risks that traditional staking doesnât carry in the same way. LSTs can trade below the value of the assets they represent, smart contracts can have bugs, and using LSTs in other DeFi activities can expose you to liquidation or liquidity risk. Staking rewards also vary and are never guaranteed.

Liquid staking is changing how people engage with proof-of-stake networks. Instead of choosing between staking and DeFi, users can now do both, helping secure a network while keeping more control over their assets. Lido and Rocket Pool helped build the Ethereum liquid staking market, while Jito and Marinade brought the model to Solana. Casper Networkâs partnership with WiseLending shows how other proof-of-stake ecosystems are adding liquid staking to their expanding DeFi infrastructure.

As blockchains keep building new financial tools, the ability to earn staking rewards without giving up liquidity may become an increasingly important feature of the crypto world.
