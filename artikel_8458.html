Return Unwanted Holiday Gifts Before January’s Deadlines – Electronics Come Due First

Most big stores take back holiday purchases until mid- to late January, so sort out the gifts you won’t use in the first weeks of the year. Return or exchange each one with its gift receipt while its store’s window is open, and regift, sell, or donate what can’t go back.

This season’s return deadlines are already set, and the earliest fall on electronics.

The gift receipt decides what you get back. Best Buy gives store credit for what the giver paid on a gift returned in the store, though one returned by mail is credited to the giver, who gets an email. Without proof of purchase, Macy’s says you may get store credit for the lowest price the gift sold for in the past 180 days. A $60 sweater that went on sale for $35 in that time would get you $35.

Amazon lets you start a gift return with the 17-digit order number on the packing slip or the digital gift receipt, as long as the giver marked the item as a gift. The refund comes as an Amazon gift card.

Leave electronics sealed until you know you’ll keep them. In most states, Best Buy charges 15% of the price to take back an opened drone, camera, or projector, and nothing for a sealed one.

Where the store has something you’d use, exchange the gift for it. Of what can’t go back, regift what’s new and unopened to someone it suits, sell other sealed things, and donate the rest. Sell or swap a gift card you won’t use, too.

Sort the pile the first weekend of January, and bag each store’s returns with their receipts so you make one trip per store.
