How does Camping work in WoW Forever? (Camp Buffs, Cozy Sleeping Bag explained)

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Make the most out of the Camping System in WoW: Forever, which is easily its best addition to build communities.

The Camping System is one of the newest gameplay features to come out of World of Warcraft: Forever, and it’s one of the best representations of Blizzard’s aim to reimagine the OG WoW release for a modern audience. So instead of just re-exploring Azeroth from scratch, you also get encouraged to be part of a wider adventuring community.

You might have already encountered the Camping System at some point. Maybe you sat near someone else’s Campfire or got to a point where you learned to make one through a quest. And while you’re not required to always participate in Camps you encounter, there are certainly benefits to enjoy if you take the time to contribute. Plus, there’s an item in the game that can take this immersion even further, and it’s nowhere near anyone’s Camps.

While it’s true that everyone’s first priority is to level up their Class to enjoy the endgame WoW: Forever has to offer, it’s going to take a long while before everyone can start raiding on the regular. At this point of the launch experience, we’re going to encounter the occasional adventurer who will give us buffs or others who are pursuing the same quests or mobs. In the rare instances that we feel friendly, we might party up and do these things together, which is somewhat the point of the “multiplayer” part of the MMO acronym.

But with the Camping system, Blizzard provided more ways of facilitating these encounters. With just a simple Campfire, players can have a place to rest up and even encourage others to stop by and contribute.Gameplay-wise, think of the Camp as a shared micro-Garrison from Warlords of Draenor, where instead of giant structures, you can create interactive elements instead. According to Blizzard’s explainer, “The Camping system gives those moments (meeting strangers) room to grow by turning a simple Cooking Fire into a place where players can rest, prepare, and connect.”

Normally, just the fact that you can create a Campfire pretty much wherever is a unique enough “visual” for adventurers. Imagine ending a tough encounter and settling in for the night, only to realize other adventurers are also relieved to see a fellow traveler there. But not all adventurers are built the same.

Some might be out there to gather materials, others to slay monsters, or just explore the wilderness in general. Flavor-wise, that’s how Professions are meant to be roleplayed. And Camp Features enhance this layer of immersion by providing a tangible gameplay element we can interact with. These features come in two forms: stat boosts from items and facilities, and other facilities that provide crafting opportunities.

If you’re more of a traveling adventurer who prefers to be “on the go,” you might not have a lot of time setting up a Campfire and constructing a lot of Camp Facilities. But you don’t necessarily have to retreat to the nearest Inn or force yourself to mingle with others at their own Campfires. For lone wolves and characters with mysterious backstories (or just those who play at their own place), the recent talk of the town is the Cozy Sleeping Bag.

This is a rather “sleeper build” of an item, as it’s a pre-midgame acquisition that you wouldn’t necessarily notice unless you trigger the right quests. And surprisingly enough, it has a massive benefit, in that you gain +1% XP for every minute you lie down, up to 3%. This benefit also lasts for two hours, which can be a huge deal for those who want to level up as soon as possible.

Getting the Cozy Sleeping Bag, unfortunately, is nowhere near cozy. It’s part of a rather convoluted quest chain, and finishing it can be rather complicated, as it can take you to dangerous territories early on.

Also, do take note that this questline is only available once you hit Level 14.

Here’s a quick rundown on getting the Cozy Sleeping Bag, separated based on some steps that start differently if you’re playing an Alliance or Horde toon:

The quest starts with the charred remains of a note. All you could read was, “Not all that burns is lost. Find the twin land, find the place.”

For lore fans, the “twin land” refers to the Stonetalon Mountains. The region is sacred ground in Tauren culture, as it’s said to be a holy site of the Earth Mother’s twin children, An’she the Sun and Mu’sha the Moon.

“Stepping Stones” will take you to Stonetalon Mountains. The quest is from a note that hints at deliveries involving the New Plague, but the mysterious quest giver thinks they’re just “simple apothecary accidents.”

The only other reference to a “New Plague” in WoW lore is “the New Plague” that the Forsaken are developing. Unlike the Plague of Undeath that resurrected the dead, the New Plague can kill both the living and the dead. It’s been deemed unethical to use after failed experiments.

Either way, they want the recipient (in this case, you) to go to their favorite spot “between The Barrens and Desolace” “to talk.”

This location is the Sun Rock Retreat. Upon getting there, look for a path to its northeast that will lead to an abandoned camp. You know you’re in the right spot if you can click Pocket Litter.

Turn in “Stepping Stones” so you can start “Scramble” (3/6). And do take note:

From the abandoned camp, the new quest will instruct you to head north and climb the hill. There should be a clickable Mound of Dirt nearby; try to reach it by jumping. It might take some time to do this, but it’s totally doable. Click that to complete “Scramble” and start “Wet Job” (4/6).

“Wet Job” reveals yet another note. This time, the quest giver implies they always kept a stash of “something” nearby if in case they had to run. They imply a location on the mountainside that may have just been “carved from the waterfalls of time” due to their beauty.

The only place in Azeroth with an obvious carved mountainside is Loch Modan, specifically Loch Modan Dam. From your map, locate the giant carved heads that are facing the Wetlands. This is where the Carved Figurine is located, and you can complete “Wet Job” from here. Based on the rewards, the “stash” is booze.

You will find a note in the toolbox, which triggers “Eagle’s Fist” (5/6). It will tell you to go to a place where it might be perfect to “launch a parachute glider.” They need to create distance to help serve the Eagle and Fist. The last destination is a barricade that existed from the Old Kingdoms.

There’s no major lore indication of what “The Eagle and Fist” could be. But the “barricade” that defended the Old Kingdoms pertains to Thoradin’s Wall. Back in the day, King Thoradin of Arathor built this wall to shield the early human kingdoms from the Amani Trolls in the highlands. Today, it separates the dangerous Arathi Highlands from the Hillsbrad Foothills.

The location is massive, but if you go to the Hillsbrad side, you can look for a cart that can give you a boost to start climbing the wall. There is a room there with a Messenger Bag on the right side, which you should click to complete the quest. It starts “This Must Be The Place” (6/6), with the final note stating that the quest giver has decided to “take their work underground” to prevent it from “falling into the wrong hands.”

We have no idea what this “work” is, but you will receive the coveted Cozy Sleeping Bag from here.
