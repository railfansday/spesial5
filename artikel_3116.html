When Can Anthropic Insiders Sell? What the Post-IPO Lockup Period Means for Shareholders.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

There are more important long-term drivers for the stock price than the expirations of its lockup periods.

By Jack Delaney – Nov 22, 2026 at 3:09PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

After nearly every initial public offering (IPO), insiders and pre-IPO stakeholders have to wait for a period before they can sell shares on the open market. These lockup periods can last for up to a year, but their expirations always cause some concern among new shareholders. After all, if insiders flood the market with their shares and demand from buyers doesn't keep up with supply, it can put downward pressure on the stock price.

For Anthropic (ANT -8.50%), which went public on Nov. 19, employees and executives will eventually have the opportunity to sell more of their shares. But instead of lockup periods ending, there are higher priorities for shareholders to focus on.

If employees start selling when a lockup period ends, that shouldn't be viewed as an automatic red flag or a signal that they have lost faith in their company. Rather, it's one of the first times they can turn the paper wealth they've accumulated over the years into actual liquid wealth.

For Anthropic, the lockup expirations are more staggered than a traditional IPO, with shares unlocking at several intervals over the next year — including one that took place Friday unlocking up to 315 million shares, and several more large events over the next couple of months. That just means that, instead of one selling date to focus on, there will be multiple selling points. That's important to note for anyone planning to invest in Anthropic, as they may see some choppy price action around those dates.

That said, lockup-related volatility is typically a short-term factor. The bigger drivers of the stock price over the long term will be revenue growth, spending, and profitability.

Anthropic is showing explosive top-line growth.

Revenue jumped roughly 1,200% in the first nine months of 2026 to $33.2 billion, and it now has approximately 250 customers spending $10 million or more on a 12-month trailing basis. In addition, its net revenue retention rate of approximately 900% as of Sept. 30 shows that existing customers are dramatically increasing their spending with Anthropic over time. These types of numbers support the case of a rapidly expanding business that can become more valuable.

However, while rising revenues are good, there is also something to watch out for: spending. In its S-1 filing, Anthropic disclosed that it has committed to  $895.4 billion in spending in the coming years, largely connected to infrastructure and compute. And of that spending, around $447.5 billion is expected to occur before 2030. If demand for artificial intelligence (AI) slows or if competitors win market share away from Anthropic’s models, these costs could become a financial drain.

Aside from revenue growth and spending, Anthropic is still losing money, reporting a net loss of $25.1 billion for the first nine months of 2026. But its losses are shrinking, as its net loss was $104 billion for the first nine months of 2025.

The bull case for Anthropic as an investment is that it will keep turning widening AI adoption into growing revenues, which it could eventually convert into profits. The bear case is that demand for Anthropic’s models and tools won’t be as great as the company is anticipating, or that it starts losing market share. Either case would ultimately leave its massive spending unjustifiable.
