7 Simple Steps for Winterizing Your Lawn Mower

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

You can’t just park it in the shed and forget it. Both gas-powered and cordless mowers need to be prepared for winter storage.

Somehow the end of the year is upon us and mowing season is winding down. And while you should still be mowing your lawn until the first hard frost hits, soon it’ll be time to retire it for the winter, and you get to give that chore a rest for a bit. But you can’t just store the mower in the shed and forget about it. To ensure your lawn mower is ready to go next season, you need to winterize it before sending it into hibernation.

Winterizing is especially important for gas-powered mowers, which have engines that need fuel, oil, and an air filter to run properly. You should do some light maintenance on them before the cold weather arrives. While cordless electric lawn mowers are easier to maintain, there are still a few steps you should take before storing them away.

Here are our tips for winterizing any lawn mower so it’s ready to go in the spring. Note that you don’t have to do them in this exact order; for example, you might want to leave fuel stabilization until later in the process, but we wanted to keep it at the top, since it’s one of the most important steps.

Prepping your gas-powered lawn mower for winter storage isn’t hard; there are just several things to consider, and if you cover them all, it should only take an hour or so. You will be dealing with engine fluids, however, so choose a work area and clothing that you don’t mind getting dirty.

When left for long periods, gasoline can become “stale,” meaning it evaporates and oxidizes, making it less combustible. It can also create residues that could damage the engine. Also, the ethanol found in most gasoline can break down if left to sit, causing performance issues and attracting water that can cause corrosion (one of the reasons ethanol-free gas is better for lawn mowers).

You can avoid the problems by adding a fuel stabilizer, but if there is any air in the tank, it’s not a perfect solution. Meanwhile, siphoning the gas from the tank is not truly going to remove all of it, so here’s our advice, in steps:

Not everyone will recommend this method, but we believe that, since a full tank has almost no air in it, there is little space for fuel to evaporate or for condensation to form due to temperature changes. Therefore, you are less likely to encounter problems than if you had drained the tank.

Large gas-powered mowers, such as lawn tractors and zero-turn mowers, typically have a battery to power the starter, lights, and other accessories. Gas walk-behind mowers with an electric start feature will also have a battery. It’s a good idea to remove it for the winter and clean it to remove corrosion on the terminals or battery posts. When performing this task, wear nitrile gloves and eye protection. If your battery has a lot of corrosion, you don't want to remove it in a way that it gets airborne. You can use a solution of water and baking soda to neutralize the corrosion and wipe it off with a rag or microfiber cloth.

Most experts recommend replacing your spark plug every year. Changing your spark plug can vary depending on the model of lawn mower you own, so always consult your manual. If you do not feel it’s necessary to replace it, at least remove it to check for any damage. If you see a lot of carbon buildup, rust, or cracks, it’s best to change it.

Drain the oil from the mower after letting it run for 5 minutes—warm oil flows more easily. For walk-behind mowers, this will require you to tip the mower over, so it’s probably better to remove the gas first or at least make sure the gas tank is securely sealed. When tipping the mower, the carburetor and air filter sections should be facing upward to avoid oil contamination—since the oil spout is usually on the opposite side of the carb and filter, you’ll naturally be following this rule anyway. But it’s good advice for when you tip the mower to clean the underside of the deck as well.

This is pretty easy to do, but it helps to know what the filter is made of. Paper and fiber filters should be replaced. If it’s a foam filter element, you can use water and Dawn dish soap, but make sure to rinse it well and let it air dry completely before reinstalling it.

You should be cleaning your mower deck throughout the summer, but if not, make a point to do it before storing it; otherwise, the caked-on grass may cause corrosion or attract bugs and vermin. As mentioned above, make sure to tip your mower so that the air box and carburetor are on the high side to avoid fuel contamination.

After you’ve completed the task of cleaning the deck, it’s a good idea to wipe down your engine with a damp cloth, removing any dirt or debris, and dry it with another towel.

The end of the season is a good time to sharpen your mower blade or replace it altogether. You can take it to a lawn mower repair shop to have it done, but if you have a few basic tools, it’s very simple to do it yourself.

The beauty of a cordless mower is that there’s less maintenance you need to perform. But that doesn’t mean there aren’t steps to take before storing it away for winter.

Batteries are the lifeblood of a cordless mower, so you need to take care of them. Don’t leave them in the mower over winter, especially if you’re keeping it in an uninsulated shed that sees cold temperatures. Remove your batteries and charge them to between 40 and 60 percent. Check the charge level every two months or so, as lithium-ion batteries lose power over time. Partially recharge the batteries if they dip close to 20 percent or lower.

Store your batteries in a cool, dark place such as a basement or insulated garage (but not near a furnace or heat source). For a riding mower that has a built-in battery you cannot remove, park it in a garage or at least a covered area. If stored in a place that gets below freezing, don’t leave it on a charger or try to recharge it, as some batteries (lithium-ion) should not be charged below 32 degrees Fahrenheit. Get it to a warmer place or wait until a warmer day.

This isn’t much different from how you do it for a gas mower; just be careful if using water to do any cleaning, as this can potentially damage electrical components. Also, be sure to remove the batteries before you do any kind of cleaning on a cordless mower.

Again, this isn’t a different process than a gas mower; just make sure the batteries are removed first to avoid the chance of the mower starting up accidentally.
