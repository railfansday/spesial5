Sandisk Splits Its Stock 8-for-1 on Nov. 19. Flash Memory Prices Still Matter More Than the Share Count.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Eight shares for every one won't change the business. What will move the flash memory maker's stock instead?

Sandisk (SNDK -5.59%) shareholders will soon own many more shares. After market close on Thursday, Nov. 19, the flash memory maker will issue seven extra shares for every share held. The stock is expected to start trading on a split-adjusted basis at the market open on Friday, Nov. 20.

At its Nov. 12 close of around $937, that comes to about $117 per share. Even then, the growth stock is about 60% under the record close of $2,335 on June 25 -- and Sandisk's revenue has kept rising since then.

Could a cheaper-looking share price help? I don't think it changes much.

What matters for Sandisk's stock is what the company can charge for its flash memory. And its latest results show just how much of its growth has come from price.

Not much besides the share count. Sandisk said the split won't affect its market value or any shareholder's ownership percentage. Ten shares at around $937 would become 80 shares at about $117, before any move in the stock.

There's also nothing for shareholders to do. According to Sandisk's stock split FAQ, the extra shares will be automatically deposited into brokerage accounts. And investors who buy after the Nov. 9 record date but before the Nov. 19 close still get the extra shares, because the right to them goes with the stock. The company said those shares could take a bit longer to arrive.

Sandisk also said the split is meant to qualify as tax-free for U.S. federal income tax purposes, which would keep a shareholder's total tax basis and holding period unchanged. It won't issue fractional shares, either.

Sandisk's revenue grew 378% year over year to $11.0 billion in its fiscal Q1 of 2027 (which ended Oct. 2, 2026). But its quarterly filing shows it sold almost 10% more storage, measured in exabytes, than a year earlier. In its edge business, which supplies flash for PCs, phones, cars and other devices, revenue per gigabyte jumped over 450% even as the total storage sold dropped 20%.

Simply put, almost all Sandisk's year-over-year growth came from charging more for every gigabyte.

That isn't new, either. In fiscal Q3 of 2026, which ended April 3, 2026, revenue rose 251% year over year as the average selling price per gigabyte climbed 248%. The total storage Sandisk sold was flat.

Customers have also been getting fewer breaks. Sandisk treats price protection and other sales incentives as a reduction to revenue, and the programs dropped to 9% of gross revenue in fiscal Q1. That's down from 12% in fiscal Q3 of 2026 and 16% in the fiscal first quarter of 2026.

By that measure, Sandisk's pricing power has arguably kept growing over the last year.

But dropping prices might hit the business just as hard. Its gross margin rose from around 30% a year ago to 85% in fiscal Q1, because the cost to make its products hardly moved as revenue surged.

For the fiscal second quarter, management guided for revenue of $12.2 billion to $12.6 billion. The midpoint implies around 12% sequential growth -- a step down from 23% in fiscal Q1 and 51% in fiscal Q4 of 2026. Management also guided for a GAAP gross margin of 84.4% to 85.4%, close to fiscal Q1's 85%.

The forecast seems to assume prices hold up and inch higher, not another big jump.

Kioxia Holdings (KXIAY -4.49%), whose joint ventures with Sandisk produce almost all of Sandisk's flash wafers, is also expecting a smaller step.

Kioxia's blended average selling price climbed by a percentage in the mid-20s from the June quarter to the September quarter. For the December quarter, Kioxia expects shipments to be about flat and its blended price to rise only moderately, partly because chips for phones and other smart devices will account for a larger share of its sales.

Still, Kioxia expects supply shortages to last through 2027, with AI demand in data centers keeping the market tight, and said it expects prices to remain on an upward trend.

In the end, the split won't change Sandisk's valuation or make the stock any cheaper. What could move it is whether flash prices keep rising more slowly, as both companies expect, or start dropping.

With the stock off around 60% since June, many investors seem to be bracing for the second. I think this worry makes sense for a firm whose growth in the last year has come almost entirely from price, even if the latest guidance doesn't show prices sliding yet.
