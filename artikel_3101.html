BYD eyes a French plant days after a Dutch court bars its Ti7 from the EU

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The ruling also stops production of the BYD Ti7 at BYD’s new Hungarian plant. BYD says it will appeal, and a final decision on JLR’s claim is due next year.

BYD has entered exclusive talks to take over Ineos Automotive’s car plant in Hambach, France. It would be the Chinese carmaker’s second factory in Europe. The talks come days after a Dutch court barred BYD from selling its Ti7 SUV in the EU. The court found it infringes JLR’s design for the Land Rover Defender.

BYD announced the Hambach talks in a statement on Tuesday, Daniel Ren reported for the South China Morning Post. It is still consulting works councils before any deal can close. Ineos builds its Grenadier off-roader at the site, according to Ineos.

“Our commitment to Europe is long term. Our proposed investment in Hambach reflects our confidence in France and our ambition to keep growing in Europe,” said Stella Li, BYD’s executive vice-president.

The District Court of The Hague issued the injunction on Friday, in a fast-track case brought by JLR. Nick Carey reported the ruling for Reuters.

“In these summary proceedings, the preliminary relief judge rules that the Ti7 infringes the EU design of Jaguar Land Rover. The characteristic and distinctive elements of the design are reflected in the BYD Ti7,” the court ruling said.

The court also barred BYD from making the Ti7 at its new factory in Hungary until the case ends. That plant is due to start mass production early next year. The court said it had no jurisdiction over BYD’s UK affiliate, Reuters reported. Major carmakers in Europe rarely go to court over design rights. A ruling this early in a case is also uncommon.

JLR, owned by India’s Tata Motors, sued after BYD opened UK orders for the seven-seat Ti7. Deliveries are due in January. The Defender is JLR’s most popular model, with global sales of more than 100,000 in 2025.

BYD said it strongly disagrees with the ruling and will appeal.

“The BYD Ti7 is the result of BYD’s own independent design work and infringes none of JLR’s rights. We will pursue every legal avenue to demonstrate that this decision is incorrect,” the company said in a statement.

JLR said it protects its intellectual property and takes legal action when necessary. It declined to comment further while proceedings continue.

The injunction stays in place unless BYD wins its appeal. A final ruling on the claim is due next year, Kana Inagaki and Edward White reported for the Financial Times. BYD’s Hong Kong shares fell as much as 4% on Monday and closed 1.9% lower. JLR’s bailiffs raided BYD’s Dutch offices in August. Earlier this month, a Dutch court ordered JLR to destroy the seized files, saying the raid went too far.

The ruling does not apply in the UK. There the Ti7 starts at £47,995, against £64,315 for the cheapest five-door Defender 110, The Independent reported.

BYD’s share of the EU and UK car market rose to 2.7% this year from 1.2% last year, above JLR’s 0.9%, according to European carmakers’ association data cited by the FT. Chinese brands as a whole took a record 14.5% of European sales in September, market researcher Dataforce found, as Bloomberg reported. The EU has already moved to curb hybrid imports from China.

BYD has said it will put a solid-state car on the road next year. JLR, meanwhile, cut 4,000 jobs in September.
