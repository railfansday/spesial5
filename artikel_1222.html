Manchester City face an uncertain future after being found guilty of breaching the Premier League’s financial rules.

Speculation is rife that City will be severely punished if their appeal fails after they were declared guilty on all of all 115 charges following a four-year investigation by an independent commission.

Huge fines, hefty points deductions, relegation from the Premier League, kicked out of football altogether… nothing is off the table, as things stand. However, it’s claimed there’s currently ‘little appetite’ in the eyes of the Premier League and its teams for past titles to be stripped away from the club.

Man City have appealed the verdict, and that process is set to conclude in December. The latest news is that Premier League clubs Manchester United, Liverpool and Spurs are using the same law firm to prepare their legal claims against the Etihad side, should the appeal be unsuccessful.

A study from data specialists OLBG - per The Sun - claims City could owe its domestic rivals more than £527 million in lost Premier League prize money.

The figures have been calculated based on data from the 2011-12 campaign up until last season. They take into account the extra cash each team would have received after being bumped up one position every season with City taken out of the table.

The higher teams finish in the Premier League table, the more prize money they receive.

Sergio Aguero's legendary Premier League title winning goal has been subject to a conspiracy theory, with Wayne Rooney even believing it.

A total of 38 clubs could theoretically be owed money from City. Let’s take a closer look at the findings…

We start with clubs who have only spent a season or two in the Premier League since 2011.

Several of these teams have battled serious financial issues in recent years, meaning the money they could theoretically be owed has the potential to go a long way.

Three of the clubs - Reading (£755k owed), Wigan Athletic (£1.5m owed) and Luton Town (£3.1m owed) - are currently down in League One, the third tier of English football.

Now we get to the serious money, with three clubs (Leeds United, Watford and West Brom) potentially owed as much as £10 million apiece.

Swansea City and Stoke City aren’t far behind as £9.1 million each, followed by Norwich City (£7.4m) and Sheffield United (£6.6m).

That we haven’t even reached the top 20 yet shows just how serious the situation could prove to be for Man City if their appeal fails.

The calculated figures rise steeply again in this section, with four clubs (Wolves, Southampton, Arsenal and Bournemouth) potentially owed in excess of £20 million each. The Gunners are said to be using their retained lawyers, HFS Kramer, to prepare a claim, having lodged legal notices in November 2024 reserving their right to seek compensation against City.

The clubs in this section have all spent numerous seasons in the top-flight during Man City’s period of dominance.

Other clubs impacted include Burnley (£19.2m owed), Fulham (£19.2m owed) and Leicester City (£17.9m owed), who won the Premier League title in 2016 but have since slipped down to League One.

Finally, we come to the clubs who have missed out on the most prize money due to City’s alleged cheating.

Local rivals Manchester United are second on the list with a whopping £28.1 million owed, while Everton top the list at £28.9 million owed.

Tottenham, who would have qualified for the Champions League on four occasions without City’s presence, are in seventh place with £25.5 million owed.
