Target vs. Walmart: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Walmart's automation bets and Target's brand partnerships pull in opposite directions, and their valuation gap is wide enough to shape the choice.

By Jake Lerch – Updated Nov 24, 2026 at 9:09AM EST

As the retail landscape shifts toward automation and digital convenience, investors are weighing the value of brand loyalty against the benefit of massive global scale. Choosing between Target (TGT +4.31%) and Walmart (WMT +1.59%) requires examining their unique paths.

Target attracts shoppers with its curated merchandise and exclusive shop-in-shop partnerships, while Walmart dominates through sheer volume and a rapidly expanding e-commerce network. These retail titans compete for the same household dollars but rely on different operational engines to drive shareholder value, making their comparison a study in efficiency versus brand loyalty.

Target relies on its store network and digital services, such as Shipt, to provide a differentiated guest experience. The company leverages high-profile partnerships with Apple (AAPL +0.76%), Starbucks (SBUX -1.11%), and Ulta Beauty (ULTA +2.06%) to drive traffic to its physical locations and its Target Plus digital marketplace. While the shop-in-shop operating agreement with Ulta Beauty is scheduled to end in August 2026, these brand collaborations remain core to its merchandise strategy.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 31, 2026, revenue for this player in consumer staples stocks reached nearly $109.5 billion. This represented revenue growth of roughly 4% compared to the previous 12 months. Net income for the period was close to $4.9 billion, showcasing the company's ability to remain profitable while expanding its digital footprint.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 1.3x, meaning total debt was 1.3 times shareholder equity. The current ratio was nearly 0.9x, measuring the ability to pay short-term obligations with short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $2.8 billion, which is the cash remaining after subtracting capital expenditures from operating cash flow.

Walmart operates as a tech-powered retailer with over 10,750 locations worldwide. The company uses automation through partners like Symbotic (SYM +5.79%) to streamline its massive fulfillment network and improve distribution efficiency. This immense scale allows it to serve roughly 280 million customers every week through a combination of physical stores and a growing global omnichannel model.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $745.2 billion. This figure reflects revenue growth of approximately 6% compared to the previous 12 months. Net income for the company reached nearly $20.9 billion, underscoring its status as the world's largest retailer by total sales volume.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 0.7x, indicating that total debt was less than shareholder equity. Its current ratio was approximately 0.8x, calculated by dividing current assets by current liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow totaled nearly $14.9 billion, reflecting cash generated after accounting for necessary capital investments.

Target faces significant operational pressure from inventory shrink, including theft and organized retail crime, which impacts asset impairment and store closures. The company relies heavily on imports from China, making it vulnerable to shifting trade policies and evolving tariffs. Additionally, it depends on third-party technology and fulfillment services to maintain its digital guest experience, adding a layer of logistical complexity.

Walmart must manage the high costs and uncertain returns of its heavy investments in artificial intelligence and automation tools. The retailer faces complex regulatory risks, including ongoing litigation over labor practices, product safety, and pharmacy dispensing. Macroeconomic factors such as currency fluctuations and geopolitical instability also affect its international operations across the 19 countries where it maintains a presence.

Target looks cheaper using its Forward P/E, based on future earnings estimates, and its P/S ratio measuring sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

When comparing Target and Walmart, investors should consider a few key factors. Let's have a look at them and see what that tells us about each stock.

Firstly, we should consider revenue growth. Walmart has delivered higher average year-over-year revenue growth over the past three years at 5.3%, while Target's rate has been 1%. Yet in recent quarters, Target has not only closed the gap but also surpassed Walmart. In Target's most recent quarter, revenue growth surged to 7.1%, while Walmart's dipped slightly to 5.2%.

Another factor to consider is each company's profitability. Both companies have relatively thin margins given their enormous revenue bases. However, Target has a slightly higher operating margin of 6.3%, while Walmart's is 4.5%. That gives Target a small but durable edge in profitability.

Lastly, there's valuation. Both companies have been consistently profitable, so we'll examine their respective price-to-earnings (P/E) multiples. Walmart has a P/E ratio of 49.6x, while Target's is 15.6x, making it far more affordable than Walmart. That gives Target stock a key advantage in the overall matchup.

To sum up, both of these consumer stocks have their strengths. Both exhibit solid revenue growth and consistent profitability. However, valuation tips the scales in Target's favor, in my view.
