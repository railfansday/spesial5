UK Actors Union Equity To Kickstart Negotiations With Streamers Early Next Year

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

EXCLUSIVE: Having nearly signed off on its long-gestating deal with Pact, Equity will soon sit down with a trio of streamers.

Deadline is told that the UK actors union intends to enter the negotiating table with Netflix, Disney and Apple at the start of next year.

Equity’s landmark deal with producer trade body Pact, which governs series for British broadcasters plus UK movies, took almost three years to rubberstamp but the union last week revealed it has a final offer in place and is recommending its members vote “yes.” Related Stories Sports NFL Thanksgiving Week Football Schedule: How To Watch & Livestream All Games Documentary In Joanna Natasegara's Oscar Contender 'The Disciple,' Wu-Tang Clan Superfan Cilvaringz Morphs Into A Killer Bee

Deadline understands one key priority for Equity will be improved upfront usage fees for actors on streamer productions. In terms of longer-term compensation, Equity General Secretary Paul Fleming previously told us the union wants compensation to focus on how long a show or movie is licensed to be on a particular platform, with a move away from a model that rewards viewership. Streamer residuals dominated U.S. actors union SAG-AFTRA’s 2023 negotiation with the AMPTP. We are told Equity is in contact with SAG over these streamer agreements to make sure they both align.

Equity is also seeking to end the “misuse of special stipulations” from the streamers, a reference to demands in areas such as availability, holiday pay and sick leave that come with the streamers’ higher pay rates.

The Equity-Pact deal, which was revealed last Monday, negotiated a 3 percentage point uplift to “streaming platform use fees,” which refers to when a show or movie is given a second window on a streamer. This could equate to a real term rise of as much as 50% for its members, Equity said. That deal also includes “groundbreaking” AI provisions and pay rate rises of 24% for bigger-budget movies and 9% on TV shows.

Apple and Disney declined comment. Deadline has reached out to Netflix for comment.
