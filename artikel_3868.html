Final Fantasy Resonance Devs Surprised by How Many Fans Wanted a Traditional, Turn-Based RPG

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Final Fantasy Resonance has been one of the most positively-received Square Enix games in ages — but its developers were never fully convinced that it would, er, resonate on the scale that it has.

Producer Keisuke Nakashima told Japanese outlet Game*Spark (as translated by Automaton): "I was surprised to see just how many people, not only in Japan but overseas as well, are eager for the kind of FF they remember from back in the day, with classic turn-based battles and pixel art."

Of course, this quote feeds into a debate that's been raging amongst fans of Final Fantasy for over a couple of decades at this point.

There are many who believe that the series has long since lost its way, pushing to innovate with more and more action-focused gameplay instead of drawing from the turn-based and command battle systems that helped establish the franchise as a world-beater in the first place.

Titles like Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 are constantly brought up as living proof that turn-based RPGs can still be blockbusters — but Square Enix has only veered further and further away from Final Fantasy's traditional blueprint with each mainline instalment.

Resonance may be a lower-budget spin-off, but at least to some extent, it does go against the publisher's line of thinking that Final Fantasy must appeal to a "wider audience" if it's to maintain its flagship status.

"We figured [Resonance] was something we ourselves would enjoy, and that there would be players out there who’d feel the same way," says Nakashima.

Director Hiroto Furuya adds: "I thought this was a rather unconventional title, so I expected opinions on it might be divided."

He then references Resonance's foundations, having been built on the bones of now defunct mobile title Final Fantasy Brave Exvius: "I think the fact that a game with such an unusual background has been embraced by so many people is a positive for the industry as a whole, and I’m personally very happy to see it."

Without wanting to be overly critical of games I really enjoy, like Final Fantasy 16, I do think Square Enix has been guilty of chasing a goal that simply may not exist.

This mythical "wider audience" has been the downfall of many games over the years, and you can argue that Final Fantasy has chipped away at its own identity and strength of brand in the (pretty desperate) pursuit of new fans.

Resonance isn't going to sell millions upon millions of copies, but the way it's been embraced says a lot about the current state of Final Fantasy as a whole, I feel.

And remember, recent reports strongly suggested that the suits at Square Enix wanted Resonance to be a basic port of the aforementioned mobile title, presumably in a bid to keep costs down.
