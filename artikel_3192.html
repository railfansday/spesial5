Here's How Many Shares of Procter & Gamble You'd Need for $1,000 in Yearly Dividends

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Even if generating $1,000 in dividend payouts from Procter & Gamble isn't in an investor's budget, it's still a stock worthy of investment consideration.

From paper towels to razor blades, people can look around their homes and likely spot a few products from Procter & Gamble (PG -1.02%). And selling those consumer-goods essentials helped P&G haul in $15.9 billion in net earnings for fiscal 2025 and pass some of that on to shareholders as dividends.

With a consistent payout yielding 2.6% as of this writing, investors may be wondering whether P&G could help them generate extra income amid higher fuel and grocery costs. We'll look at what it would take to generate an extra $250 every three months, or $1,000 a year, from the company.

For Procter & Gamble to pay a shareholder $1,000 in yearly dividends, it's going to take quite a few shares. With a quarterly dividend payout of $1.09, it would take roughly 230 shares to earn $1,000 in yearly dividends.

At an opening stock price of $164.65 on Nov. 4, that would require purchasing approximately $37,869 worth of stock, so it will take a fairly large investment to generate $1,000 in yearly dividends.

That said, there's still a reason Procter & Gamble may be worth considering as an investment even with a smaller budget: stability.

As investors worry about the same level of uncertainty swirling around the markets in 2027 as in 2026, companies that allow their shareholders to sleep well at night are receiving more attention. Some of those types of stocks include Dividend Kings.

To earn the title of Dividend King, a company has to have increased its dividend payouts for 50 or more consecutive years. Procter & Gamble has easily cleared that hurdle, increasing its payout for 70 consecutive years.

That shows a strong business model with products that people will always buy no matter what's happening in the economy. Its brands include Tide laundry detergent, Bounty paper towels, Charmin toilet paper, Gillette razors, and more. And while not known for its stock price appreciation, shares are up 16.5% thus far in 2026, outperforming the S&P 500's (^GSPC +0.31%) 8.2% return.

Ultimately, while it may not work out for some shareholders to receive $1,000 in annual dividends from Procter & Gamble, it can still serve as a valuable portfolio addition thanks to its dependability.
