Marvell Reports Dec. 1. History Says What Its Stock Usually Does Next.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The chipmaker's stock has swung an average of around 12% the day after its latest reports. Which way will it go this time?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 20, 2026 at 4:23PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Marvell Technology ›

Marvell Technology (MRVL -11.69%) will release results for its fiscal third quarter of 2027 (the period ended Oct. 31, 2026) on Tuesday, Dec. 1, with a conference call at 1:30 p.m. Pacific time.

The chipmaker's stock comes into the report at around $138 as I write, off about 56% from its June 4 record close of $316.43. And most of that drop is recent. Marvell shares ended Oct. 28 at around $252, the last trading day before speculation started about a deal for optical parts maker Lumentum Holdings.

On Nov. 2, the two companies announced an all-stock deal. After it closes, Marvell's current shareholders will own around two-thirds of the combined company. The growth stock is down about 45% from that Oct. 28 close.

The Dec. 1 report will be Marvell's first full quarterly update since it agreed to buy Lumentum. And if the past three years are any guide, the shares might make a big move the day after.

Marvell reports after the market closes, so the reaction shows up in the next day's trading. Here are its last 12 reports, the stock's close-to-close move the next day, and the revenue growth management guided for the following quarter over the quarter just reported.

Shares dropped the next day seven times and climbed five times.

And the moves were big either way. The average swing was around 12%, and seven of the 12 moves topped 10%.

Interestingly, the quarters themselves were almost never to blame. In all 12 reports, Marvell's revenue came in above the midpoint of its guidance.

I think the outlook mattered more. In the six reports in which management guided to next-quarter revenue growth of 6% or lower (with one guide for a decline), the stock fell the next day five times, with an average drop of around 9%.

In the six where it guided for about 8% or more, shares climbed four times, with an average gain of about 6%.

But six reports on either side doesn't prove much, and the pattern has a clear exception. In August 2026, management guided to third-quarter revenue of $3.15 billion, up 15% from the second quarter's $2.74 billion, the second-largest increase in the table. The stock still dropped 10% the next day.

A possible reason is that expectations had risen with the tech company's share price, which had more than tripled since the March 2026 report.

This time, the quarter itself could hold even less surprise than normal. When it announced the Lumentum deal on Nov. 2, Marvell said preliminary third-quarter revenue was in line with its guidance. It kept the $3.15 billion midpoint and tightened the range to plus or minus 2%.

That puts the focus on the fiscal fourth quarter, and the bar is high for a chipmaker helped by demand for artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure. Marvell's revenue climbed 22% year over year in the final quarter of fiscal 2026. Growth then rose to 28% and 37% in the first two quarters of fiscal 2027. And the $3.15 billion third-quarter midpoint means growth of around 52%, an acceleration for a third straight quarter.

"[W]e expect our revenue growth to accelerate further through the remainder of fiscal 2027," Marvell CEO Matt Murphy said in the company's fiscal second-quarter earnings release.

For growth to keep accelerating in the fiscal fourth quarter, revenue would have to exceed $3.4 billion, or about 7% above the third-quarter midpoint. In other words, a guide that just keeps pace with the third quarter might land right around the line that has split the stock's past reactions.

In the end, I'd expect a big move on Dec. 2 either way, and a down day has been a bit more common than an up day. But I wouldn't read much into one day's move when the Lumentum deal isn't expected to close until the first half of 2028.

Personally, I'd pay more attention to the size of the fourth-quarter guide than to the stock's reaction to it. Shares trade at around 20 times the earnings analysts expect for fiscal 2028, a price that seems reasonable if growth continues to accelerate. And if shares drop again on a guide that still shows accelerating growth, I think the lower price might be worth considering for investors who can ride out more swings like these.
