The 2026 RMD Deadline Is Approaching Quickly. Here's What to Do If You Haven't Taken Yours Yet.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Now's the time to act if you haven't made this important retirement move yet.

If you'll be 74 or older by the end of 2026, you only have a few weeks left to complete your required minimum distributions (RMDs) for the year. Failing to do so could result in a 25% penalty on the amount you were supposed to withdraw, so it's best to act quickly if you haven't taken yours yet.

If you are turning 73, you technically have until April 1, 2027, to take your first RMDs. But it doesn't hurt to get them out of the way now, too. Here's what you need to do.

You're legally required to take RMDs from all tax-deferred retirement accounts except your current 401(k) if you're still working and own less than 5% of the company. You can skip RMDs from Roth accounts, as you're not required to pay taxes on these withdrawals anyway.

If you have multiple traditional individual retirement accounts (IRAs), you're not technically required to take an individual withdrawal from each account. You still have to calculate your IRA RMDs individually, but you can withdraw the total from any combination of accounts you'd like. For example, if you have a $5,000 RMD for one IRA and a $10,000 RMD for another IRA, you can take $15,000 from one, $7,500 from each, or any combination you like, provided the total is at least $15,000.

Your IRA or 401(k) plan administrator may be able to help you calculate your RMDs. You can also do this manually. Start by looking up the account balance as of Dec. 31, 2025. Divide this amount by the applicable denominator next to your age in the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. The result is your RMD.

For example, if you had $100,000 in an IRA on Dec. 31, 2025 and you'll be 75 by the end of the year, you'd divide that $100,000 by the 24.6 applicable denominator for 75-year-olds to get an RMD of $4,065 from that account. Repeat this process for all the accounts from which you must take RMDs.

It's possible you've already fulfilled your RMDs for the year if you've made retirement account withdrawals throughout 2026. Check with your plan administrator to see how much you've withdrawn so far this year. Subtract this amount from your RMD to figure out how much more you must take out. Then withdraw the remaining funds before the end of the year.

It's best to act as quickly as possible in case there are problems with your withdrawal. You don't want to miss the deadline, or you could lose even more to taxes.
