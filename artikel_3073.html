3 Glorious AI Stocks to Buy Hand Over Fist If the Stock Market Crashes

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A crash would put these dominant businesses on sale, and one already looks cheap.

By John Ballard – Nov 25, 2026 at 2:42PM EST

Crashes may feel scary for investors, but they're also the best time to buy certain stocks. Companies that provide essential services, earn substantial profits, and have ample cash on their books can keep investing through downturns. The strongest companies often come out of a downturn stronger than they went in.

The stock market falls by at least 10% around once every 16 months, on average, according to Hartford Funds. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) recently closed 11.6% below its Oct. 6 record high on Nov. 20 and currently sits about 8% below that high. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite, meanwhile, is still down by about 14% from its recent peak.

We could have already passed the bottom of one of those typical corrections, or we could still be near the start of a bigger decline: No one knows. But if the market falls further, Alphabet (GOOGL -2.13%)(GOOG -2.02%), Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -1.96%), and Amazon (AMZN -3.12%) would be artificial intelligence (AI) stocks worth buying. Here's why.

Alphabet is the company behind Google Search, YouTube, and the Gemini AI model. Billions of people use its products. Search is how much of the world finds information online, and YouTube is a go-to destination for video. And the Gemini app now has 1 billion users.

That reach allows Alphabet to rake in substantial advertising revenue across its platforms. Google Services, which includes Search, YouTube, and subscriptions, generated $96 billion in revenue in the third quarter and earned about $0.41 of operating profit per dollar of sales. A recession might cause advertising spending to decline, slowing Alphabet's revenue growth. Still, the massive reach of its services business means the company should see its revenue march higher over the long term.

Moreover, AI appears to be enhancing its long-term growth prospects. Alphabet said Search queries grew at their fastest pace in five years last quarter. That matters because earlier in the generative AI revolution, many investors and analysts feared that users asking AI chatbots questions would lead to a reduction in traffic to Google Search.

The stock trades at about 18 times next year's earnings estimates, in line with the S&P 500, which also trades at about that ratio, while analysts expect Alphabet’s earnings to grow about 17% annually. If a broader market crash took the tech stock down with it, that would turn a market leader trading at a fair price into a bargain.

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSMC) is the world's leading contract chipmaker. Its foundries physically produce the chips designed by other companies, including almost all cutting-edge AI processors. When companies need the most advanced chips in large quantities, TSMC is the partner they turn to, because no rival has its massive production capacity or manufacturing expertise.

That position gives TSMC pricing power. Its operating margin was 59% last quarter, meaning it kept about $0.59 of every dollar of sales as operating profit. Management expects revenue to grow by about 44% in U.S. dollars this year as demand for AI chips continues to climb.

The biggest risk to the company is geopolitical. Most of TSMC's leading-edge factories are in Taiwan, and a conflict involving China could disrupt its chip production.

But that's why TSMC is working to expand its foundry operations geographically. Its new manufacturing facility in Arizona is expected to begin volume production in the third quarter of 2027. The company is also evaluating expansion in Texas, which would help mitigate this risk over the long term.

Trading at about 15.5 times next year's expected earnings, below the market's roughly 18 times, the stock looks undervalued. Analysts expect about 44% annualized earnings growth in the coming years. A further drop in the share price would make the stock a steal.

Amazon runs a massive online store, an advertising business, and Amazon Web Services (AWS), its cloud platform. It just posted its highest operating margin ever, nearly 14% in the third quarter. Cash from operations rose 19% year over year to $42 billion in Q3, and grew by 29% to $168 billion over the last 12 months.

Amazon Bedrock, the AWS service that lets businesses run AI models, saw token volume (a key measure of AI usage) grow nearly 17 times year over year. This makes Amazon one of the best stocks for the average investor to profit from enterprise AI adoption, since AWS generates most of Amazon's profit.

The stock's valuation is not as low as the others. At about 23 times 2027 earnings, it trades at a premium to the market, yet analysts expect earnings to grow roughly 23% annually. Softer demand for cloud services could send the stock lower. That's the risk, but a sluggish year of growth wouldn't derail the company's long-term trajectory.

Amazon's margins are trending higher, and rising cash from operations would let it keep investing in growth through a downturn. A market crash wouldn’t keep tens of millions of people from shopping on Amazon.com, nor would it stop enterprises from needing to store their data on AWS. It would only bring the stock's valuation down to more attractive levels, making it potentially a bargain for patient investors.
