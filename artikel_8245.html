Te Araroa blog: a few big days till rest

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

I woke up in my tiny cabin at Whananaki holiday park excited for one of the icons of the north island – the “longest footbridge in the southern hemisphere”. In the course of our 24 beers the night before we had laughed at that a lot, the bridge did not look particularly long so the footbridge qualifier was doing a lot of the heavy lifting there.

On the footbridge, photo courtesy of Sven

We squeezed Lucy’s ginormous and extremely heavy pack through the very skinny gap in the fence and made our way to the bridge. It sure was a footbridge. What followed was a chill dirt road walk, followed by a coastal path and through a lovely forest and past some very fancy houses. The rain came and went, and we ran into a gaggle of day-hikers who, after a couple of days around only thru-hikers, sort of felt to me like running into a flock of chickens all chattering to each other.

By the time I got to the end of the path I could see Sven getting into a car so I chased him down and wedged myself into the car on top of some construction supplies (deeeeffinitely wore a seatbelt, don’t worry mum). That hitched dropped us off at the junction for the road into Ngunguru (a town nobody pronounces correctly) where some cyclists told us that hitching would be impossible. There was only one solution: Sven once again put on his lucky wrist brace and within fifteen minutes a lovely couple loaded the two of us into their car and took us down to KM 339 where we got back on a non-dodgy road section. This was the final 10km into Ngunguru. We made it by about 1:30 and had time for a coffee and cake in the cafe and to do a resupply at the dairy, I’ll never go into a supermarket with Sven again because I came out of that shop with about twice as much food as I needed because he was so hectic in there and I lost focus.

Our group reassembled and we texted our trail host for the night: James. James should actually change his name to king of the TA. James’ place in Nikau bay is an oasis. It has space for camping and a few very cute cabins, as well as a very comfy shelter where he does a nightly presentation on the next 60 or so kilometres of trail. James picks you up from across the bay in his boat and you have the option to either ride in the boat or to swim.

I, of course, chose to swim. This was probably a highlight of the whole trip for me. It was totally magical handing off my bag and swimming to my home for the night. We felt like we were on survivor or some kind of reality tv show. And James was not lying about the BIG HOT shower.

It was at James’ that we hatched our plan for the next two days: we’d combine two days by hitching down a 15km road to a big tidal crossing and then walk all the way to Ocean beach. It would be a 32km day even with the hitch cutting out 15km and we knew if we didn’t get a hitch we’d have to wait for the crossing at about noon the next day. We’d then do another big push to Ruakaka. This would allow us to take a zero in Ruakaka and conveniently celebrate my birthday in an Airbnb where we could do endless rounds of laundry.

Campfire at Nikau bay after our swim. Photo courtesy of Eve.

We woke early and powered through the first 14km of the day, making it to the road by about 9:30am. This time it was not Sven’s hitching powers that worked, but the sweet and un-intimidating faces of Alex and Steven (who Sven calls the American GOATS). We loaded four of us into a car being driven by a 17 year old making his way home the morning after a graduation party and made it in time to have a coffee before the tidal crossing.

Our group reassembled just before the crossing and ate lunch, then were escorted by a little dog through the first half of the crossing along mangroves. We timed it perfectly and the highest the water got was mid thigh. Yay for a dry pack!!

The rest of the day felt like it would be easy and fun, I was so excited! And then we hit Kauri Mountain, which is just a hill but in the cloudless sunny afternoon it felt like hell on earth. My peanut butter and cheese wrap was sitting heavy in my stomach and I had to eat a whole bunch of my sour lollies to make it through the climb. But we all did it, and dropped to Ocean beach where we swam in the cold water and were able to reset for the final 7km walk down the beach. This felt like it took forever, though I did get to run into Sinèad who I had met in week one which really boosted my energy.

Me unimpressed that this was the last few hundred metres of the day. Photo courtesy of Cody.

After a quick rock scramble we made it to the camp at kilometre 393 and pitched our tents. Dinner was a struggle but I managed to eat all my mash and peas and jerky and have a shower before crashing. Tomorrow we’d climb Bream Head, allegedly a real highlight of Northland.

The only real view of Bream Head that we got

Unfortunately we woke to misty thick rain clouds and not a single view. This did allow us to just lock in and speed run the hill, we had been told it would take 5-6 hours but we finished it in 4. Then it was a hitch down some gross road walking to Reotahi bay where we’d catch a water taxi to Marsden point.

The water taxi driver told us we might see orca so I walked the rainy beach obsessively staring out at the water, only to see nothing and get chafing from the salt water soaking my legs. I’ll never forgive the water taxi man.

I made it to the Airbnb and was handed a beer, a towel to shower and some snacks. So in the end it was all worth it.
