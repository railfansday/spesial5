3 Dead in Plane Crash on Remote North Rim of Grand Canyon National Park

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Authorities have not released the identities of the people aboard the plane or details about the accident (Photo: Nico De Pasquale Photography/Getty Images)

Three people are dead after a plane crashed on the North Rim of Grand Canyon National Park on November 15, 2026.

Authorities have not released the identities of the people aboard the plane or details about the accident. The National Transportation Safety Board is investigating the accident, the National Park Service (NPS) wrote in a statement.

According to the NPS, the Grand Canyon Regional Communications Center received a call about a missing Piper Comanche at 11:30 P.M. on November 15. Produced between 1958 and 1972, the Piper Comanche is an older single-engine, low-wing aircraft that typically seats between four and six people.

The Arizona Department of Public Safety conducted an aerial search in the early morning hours of November 16 using infrared and night vision, according to the NPS.

“The aircraft and debris field were located by NPS at approximately 10:15 A.M. on November 16 in the Basin area,” officials said. “There were no survivors.”

The North Rim is an isolated part of the Grand Canyon that sees just about ten percent of the park’s visitors annually. It’s a rugged, higher-elevation area on the Kaibab Plateau that averages about 8,300 feet—roughly 1,000 feet higher than the South Rim. All park amenities on the North Rim are closed November through April, and the road into the park closes in December.

The Arizona Department of Public Safety, Coconino County Search and Rescue, Kane County Search and Rescue, and Air Force Rescue Coordination Center participated in the search efforts.

Since 1986, NPS data show 12 aircraft incidents within Grand Canyon National Park: nine crashes and three forced emergency landings. At least 21 people have died. Twenty-five people were killed in 1986 when a Grand Canyon Airlines sightseeing plane and a tour helicopter collided over the park. In 1989, ten people died when a Grand Canyon Airlines Twin Otter crashed near the Grand Canyon Airport during an attempted go-around.

Most notably, in 1956, a TWA Lockheed Super Constellation and a United DC-7 collided and crashed into the Grand Canyon. All 128 passengers and personnel onboard were killed. The disaster helped spur the creation of the Federal Aviation Administration, a government agency that regulates aviation in the United States.
