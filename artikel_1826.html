Rangers half-time surgery sparks second half tempest to subdue Ibrox anxiety

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

There are evenings when tactical blueprints dissolve into sheer, unscripted theatre, and for 45 minutes at a rain-soaked Ibrox, it appeared as though Derek McInnes’ best-laid plans had conspired against his own side. Yet by the time a frenetic second half had run its course, Rangers had conjured an epic turnaround to claim a breathless 3–2 victory over Aberdeen that saw them move within a point of idle Premiership leaders Celtic.

In testing conditions, both sides abandoned any pretence of caution to serve up a thrilling second half that was in stark contrast to the opening 45 minutes, which offered little beyond frustration and creeping anxiety for the Rangers manager.

Facing the club where he spent eight formative years in the dugout, McInnes had tinkered with his starting formation, handing Ben Godfrey a first start since July 31st as part of a three-man defence designed to counter their opponents narrow, winger-less system, However, the hosts looked entirely disjointed.

Rangers, playing their first home match in a full month following an unusual sequence of five consecutive away fixtures, began on the front foot. However, despite Dan Neil squandering a glorious opportunity from six yards inside the opening 60 seconds, his rebound subsequently struck off teammate Ryan Naderi, the home side struggled to establish rhythm or coherence.

Aberdeen, aiming to build on their first win under new head coach Mario Frick at the weekend, sensed vulnerability and grew steadily in conviction. Organised in a compact 4-3-1-2 shape, the visitors frustrated Rangers before delivering a clinical blow on the half-hour mark. Brad Lyons capitalised on Rangers’ hesitation from a corner to produce a diving header that was hooked off the line by Nico Raskin. However, after VAR intervened, the ball was adjudged to have crossed the line and referee John Beaton awarded the goal, sparking wild celebrations scenes in the away end.

Worse almost followed for the hosts. Only profligacy in front of goal, most notably from Moroccan striker Ayoub Mouloua, who twice spurned clear openings before the interval, prevented the Dons from carrying a more commanding lead into the break as the Rangers supporters made their dissatisfaction known.

If the first half belonged to Aberdeen’s disciplined industry, the opening 12 minutes of the second belonged to Rangers’ ruthless efficiency. McInnes wasted little time in rectifying his questionable team selection, executing a bold triple substitution at half-time that saw Kim Min-su, Tuur Rommens and Findlay Curtis replace Kevin Kelsy, Godfrey, and Badredine Bouanani.

The transformation was instantaneous. Within 60 seconds of the restart, Min-su tested Aberdeen goalkeeper Marius Müller with a dipping shot. Müller could only parry the ball directly into the path of Ryan Naderi, who gleefully dispatched the rebound from close range to restore parity.

Before Aberdeen could draw breath, Ibrox erupted once more. Just 64 seconds after the equaliser, Naderi turned provider, threading an incisive pass into Djeidi Gassama. Outpacing his man, the winger kept his composure to drill a low finish across Müller into the far corner. In barely a minute, a packed stadium that had been growing increasingly restless was suddenly alive again.

Rangers’ third arrived in the 56th minute via a devastating counter-attack. Min-su, whose introduction had injected vital urgency into the hosts' attacking play, won possession deep inside his own half and drove up field before releasing Gassama. Cutting the ball neatly across the penalty area, the Mauritanian international teed up Raskin, who coolly side-footed low across Müller to establish a seemingly decisive 3-1 advantage.

Aberdeen, however, refused to crumble. Barely a minute after Raskin’s goal, Alexander Briedl drifted inside from the left flank and slipped a weighted pass into the path of Findlay Marshall. The youngster met it cleanly, sending a crisp drive crashing in off the post to reduce the deficit and reignite a pulsating contest.

What followed was a nerve-shredding final half-hour punctuated by transitions and agonising near-misses. Aberdeen’s Nesta Guinness-Walker endured frustration when his late penalty appeal was waved away. In between, Marshall came within inches of securing a share of the spoils, dragging a left-footed effort agonisingly wide of Pandur's post in the closing stages after the Croatian had made a spectacular save to deny Nicky Devlin from a corner at his front post.

If Scottish football occasionally stands accused of predictability, nobody among the 50,738 crowd could reasonably complain of short measure. Rangers survived, although not without making their supporters endure another uncomfortable finale as they took advantage of their game in hand to move closer to keeper their title challenge firmly on track.
