FAA Technician Only Unlocked The Door: Inside The Oversight Gap Behind The Nationwide Ground Stop

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Last Friday we saw the worst disruption to air travel that we’ve seen in the United States in over 25 years, as air traffic shut down nationwide for over four hours. We quickly learned that this happened because L3Harris, a government contractor, was doing maintenance to switch telecommunications lines from copper to fiber.

The logical question is how something going wrong with the work of one contractor could cause our entire aviation ecosystem to shut down:

So, what’s actually going on here? A new report sheds some more light on this…

CNN has some insights into how little oversight there was of the work being performed by FAA contractor L3Harris, which led to the ground stop disaster that we saw. When was oversight reduced? Well, in early 2025, coinciding with the FAA laying off a large number of employees, as part of Elon Musk’s “Department of Government Efficiency” (DOGE) stunt.

According to this report, last Friday morning, an FAA technician escorted a contractor from L3Harris into a remote radio communications site for air traffic control in southern Arkansas, to replace a decades-old router. The FAA technician’s belief was that his role was done once the contractor entered the facility, according to people familiar with what happened, plus the union representing FAA employees.

That router replacement ended up being what triggered the communications issues across the country, ultimately leading to the nationwide ground stop.

FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford insists that the L3Harris engineer performing the upgrade “was monitored by an FAA technician” and that a risk assessment was performed, but sources dispute that characterization, pointing out that the technician “was only there to unlock the door,” and that “there was no monitoring.”

Funny enough, an FAA spokesperson later contradicted Bedford, saying “the FAA escort is responsible for providing the L3Harris technician access to and from the building,” and “because it is L3 equipment, the escort is not there while the technician is doing work.” It’s amazing how they can’t even keep their narrative straight.

This type of hands-off arrangement is apparently a recent development that happened under the Trump administration, and a change to how the FAA used to handle things. In March 2025, an FAA memo laid out changes to how FAA maintenance and upgrades would be carried out, in order to speed things up. The memo stated that “it is critical to approve maintenance performed by the Telecommunication Vendors without delay.”

With this policy, the FAA also went from receiving maintenance “requests” from private contractors that needed to be approved, to receiving “notifications” of intended work, noting that “this work will proceed without FAA approval,” if not processed in time.

While the memo claimed that an FAA liaison will evaluate the “purpose, severity, criticality and impact” of the work, there are reportedly only a handful of liaisons nationwide, processing thousands of notifications. In this case, L3Harris reportedly submitted its “notification” with just a few days notice, meaning there was no meaningful oversight.

A spokesperson for the union representing FAA employees explains that “orders were to simply let the contractor in the building,” that he “had no direction to help or assist in any way,” and that “no PASS-represented employee was asked to conduct a risk assessment on this particular upgrade.”

We’re getting a clearer picture of the circumstances that caused the nationwide ground stop last Friday, the biggest disruption to US air travel since 9/11. A contractor with L3Harris was given access to a site to replace wiring, with virtually no oversight from an FAA employee.

While the FAA claims that there’s supposed to be oversight of contractor work, in this case the FAA employee was reportedly just tasked with unlocking the door. The approach to overseeing contractor work is reportedly a new system as of March 2025, where contractor work went from being viewed as “requests” to being viewed as “notifications,” with just a handful of people to oversee these requests. This came just weeks after hundreds of employees were laid off from the FAA, thanks to Musk’s DOGE.
