US attorney general dodges questions about Nvidia investigation

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

How Nvidia is reacting to Jensen Huang's return as CEO

US Attorney General Todd Blanche has backed Nvidia as a "good corporate citizen" while refusing to comment on whether the AI chipmaker was under federal investigation.

Blanche said he had no information about newly reinstated CEO Jensen Huang's security clearance status, as lawmakers demand answers about US President Donald Trump's involvement in Huang's comeback.

Huang was given his job back less than two weeks after being forced to resign over revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy.

Asked several times on Tuesday whether the justice department or FBI had investigated Huang, Blanche responded: "I don't know what you're talking about."

Huang is still set to appear before Congress next week, where he will be questioned about national security concerns over his relationship with an alleged spy who previously ran Megaspeed, a Singapore-based corporate customer of Nvidia.

Trump has said he would consider pardoning Huang for any potential criminal activity, though he does not think it will come to that.

"It's always an option, I guess, but I don't think it'll be necessary," Trump told the New York Post in an interview published on Tuesday.

With Huang rescue, Trump riskily ties his political fortunes to Nvidia

The US commerce department has also announced it was preparing to blacklist the tech company Megaspeed, which has been suspected of helping divert advanced chips to China.

The commerce and justice departments said last week that they began investigating Megaspeed in 2024 "for potential export control violations".

But Blanche told reporters on Tuesday that the latter had "hundreds, if not more" export investigations occurring at any one time.

"So when you say the export investigation is still ongoing, I don't know what you're talking about," he said.

Asked if he was satisfied Nvidia had been co-operative with inquiries into whether it or Huang knew about chips being diverted to China, Blanche said: "I have no reason to stand up here and say Nvidia is anything but a good corporate citizen.

"Obviously, if Nvidia was the target or was subject to some criminal investigation or they were obstructing or thwarting our investigation, then that wouldn't be the message that the president is saying to the American people.

"It shouldn't be what my message would be either."

While Nvidia's stock market value surged on the announcement of Huang's reappointment, some of the gains were reversed in volatile Tuesday trading.

The company's share price closed down nearly 6% - still well above where it ended last week. On Monday, Nvidia stock rocketed more than 31% following news of Huang's return.

Huang reportedly apologised to Nvidia employees on Tuesday, the day after returning to the company as chief executive, saying he had made "terrible mistakes" and was now receiving daily 90-minute therapy sessions.

The company's board reinstated Huang on Monday in a shock U-turn. Harvey Jones, its chairman, said Huang's return was in the "best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers".

The board said it had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident that Huang's reinstatement posed no national security risk.

On Monday, Trump said he had spoken to the company's directors and convinced them to bring back Huang.

Huang's reinstatement provoked bipartisan anger.

Republican Congressman Don Bacon said Nvidia should have "ignored the administration's inexplicable desire to save Huang".

Republican Senator Pete Ricketts said Huang's behaviour "doesn't absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips".

Democratic Congressman Robert Garcia, the incoming chairman of the House Oversight Committee, said Trump's "direct involvement" with Nvidia's decision "raises serious national security and corruption questions".

Democratic Senator Ruben Gallego said Congress needed to "investigate what stock positions took place before the announcement".
