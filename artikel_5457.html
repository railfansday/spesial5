NY Private-Listings Bill Becomes Law, Includes Opt-Out Option for Sellers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

After passing both the state Assembly and Senate this past summer, the Fair and Transparent Real Estate Listings Act is now law after receiving New York Governor Kathy Hochul’s signature on Friday.

The law requires the “timely public advertising or marketing of listed residential properties on platforms accessible to the general public” but does provide an “opting-out” avenue for sellers who wish to market their listings privately, through a standardized state disclosure and process.

StreetEasy General Manager and Zillow Group (NYC) VP Caroline Burton applauds Hochul and describes the state of New York City housing, pointing to scarcity issues keeping affordability out of reach.

“This is a win for all buyers, sellers, and the agents who serve them,” she states. “When homes are kept out of public view, New Yorkers lose out, especially in a market where homes for sale are already scarce. Our research shows that the overwhelming majority of NYC buyers want to see all of the options available to them, and many NYC sellers who listed privately ended up regretting it.”

A Compass spokesperson commended Hochul for signing the bill into law, commenting the new law “preserves homeowners’ right to choose how they market their homes.”

The New York State Association of Realtors® (NYSAR) praises the governor and stated that the legislation “promotes greater transparency and consumer protection in the real estate marketplace by addressing the use of private listing networks.”

“This new law helps ensure that property owners are fully informed of the potential consequences of limiting the public marketing of their properties, including the possibility of reduced exposure to prospective buyers or tenants,” the association states. “Importantly, the legislation strikes an appropriate balance between promoting transparency and protecting consumer choice by preserving the ability of property owners to determine how their properties are marketed.”

During the law’s drafting and path through each of the legislative bodies, Mike Kelly— NYSAR VP and chief lobbyist of Government Affairs—expressed the association’s support for the bill to RISMedia and had high hopes for the changes to come for the state’s housing market.

“The legislation starts with the premise that the real estate licensee, the professional representative, seller or lessor is required to widely market their property,” he said. “But in cases where the lessor seller wants to enter into a private listing network or limited marketing, they can do so, but they can only do so by acknowledging and signing a state required disclosure form.”

The act was originally introduced by Assemblywoman Michaelle C. Solages (D-NY)— who represents New York’s District 22 in the state legislature—and she described “transparency” as her main intention for the bill to RISMedia.

“It’s always been my goal as a state legislator to ensure that folks have access to the housing market,” she said. “So whether it’s finding discrimination, building more supply, or making sure that we have transparency with private listing networks, this is a goal to ensure that we are giving people access to the housing market. The legislation really is about transparency, fairness and access.”

Anthony Domathoti, treasurer and incoming president-elect 2027 for the NYSAR, states the law is a “real win for consumer and for transparency” in the market.

“In my 27 years as a broker, I’ve seen how much exposure matters when you sell or rent a property,” he added. “When an owner keeps a listing off the open market, fewer buyers and tenants ever see it. This law makes owners understand that tradeoff before they decide. The Fair and Transparent Real Estate Listings Act’s signing is just the latest in changes to private listing networks. What I like most is that it doesn’t take the decision away from them. Owners still choose how their property gets marketed. They just make that choice with full information.”

In May of this year, Connecticut Governor Ned Lamont also signed a similar bill into law to reform real estate in the Constitution State. Like New York’s version, the Connecticut law does not outright ban the use of private listing networks, rather it mandates that if a listing is added to such a network, it must also be marketed on a public platform.

Connecticut’s law also includes an opt-out option from public marketing, ensuring that sellers are informed and educated prior to making their decision on whether to publicly or privately market their properties.

“Finding a home in the most challenging market shouldn’t depend on privilege, who you know, or who you work with,” Burton added. “In one of the biggest decisions in a person’s life, New Yorkers deserve an open and transparent marketplace so they can make an informed choice.”
