KeyBank Offers Tips to Help Consumers Spot and Avoid Holiday Shopping Scams

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

KeyBank shares warning signs and practical steps for avoiding phishing, fake shopping sites, payment app scams and other holiday fraud

CLEVELAND, Nov. 27, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- As holiday shopping picks up, so do opportunities for scammers.

Consumers shopping online, tracking packages, buying gift cards or donating to charities can encounter increasingly convincing fraud attempts. Scammers are also using artificial intelligence to create fake retail websites, emails, text messages and even AI-generated voices designed to impersonate trusted companies or people.

Knowing what to look for can help consumers recognize potential fraud before they share personal information or send money.

What are the most common holiday shopping scams?

Here are several scams consumers should watch for during the holiday season:

Bank imposter scams. Fraudsters may call, text or email pretending to represent a consumer's bank. They may claim there has been a suspicious transaction or login attempt, then ask for a one-time passcode, login credentials or debit card information. KeyBank will not initiate a call or text asking for your online banking username or password. KeyBank also will not ask a client to send money to themselves using ZelleÂ®, an account transfer or a wire payment, or request remote access to your computer or mobile device. However, in some cases, we will call to verify your identity by asking for a one-time passcode that is sent to your mobile device.

Phishing and smishing scams. Fraudulent emails and text messages can look like legitimate communications from retailers, banks or delivery companies. Scammers may also use AI to create convincing fake retail websites and communications intended to capture debit card, credit card or other personal information. A common example is a fake package-delivery message asking for payment to release or redeliver an order.

Vishing and AI voice scams. Criminals can use AI-generated voices in phone calls to impersonate customer service representatives, delivery companies, family members or others in an effort to obtain sensitive information.

Payment app scams. Consumers should be cautious when an unfamiliar seller asks for payment through ZelleÂ®, Venmo, Cash App, wire transfer or cryptocurrency. Recovering money sent to a fraudulent seller through these methods can be difficult.

Online marketplace scams. Fraudsters may advertise popular holiday gifts at unusually low prices, then request advance payment through a payment app, gift card or cryptocurrency before disappearing.

Gift card scams. Criminals may tamper with gift cards displayed on retail racks or convince victims to provide gift card numbers over the phone to make supposedly urgent payments.

Charity scams. The season of giving can also attract criminals impersonating legitimate charities or disaster-relief organizations through phone calls, texts, emails and social media.

What should you do if you think you've been scammed?

Consumers who believe they have been targeted or have already sent money should contact their financial institution immediately and report suspected fraud to the retailer and appropriate authorities. KeyBank clients can contact the KeyBank Fraud Client Service Center at 1-800-433-0124. Dial 711 for TTY/TRS.

Additional information about emerging scams, account security and fraud prevention is available at key.com/fraud.

Ultimately, one of the strongest defenses against holiday fraud is taking a moment before responding. An unexpected message, urgent request or unusually attractive deal is worth verifying independently before clicking a link, providing information or sending money.
