Microsoft is unveiling cheaper coding and cybersecurity AI models to rival OpenAI and Anthropic

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Microsoft says its MAI Code 2 model delivers 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol's performance at 40% of the price, according to Axios

Microsoft $MSFT unveiled new coding and cybersecurity AI models at its Ignite conference in San Francisco on Tuesday, saying they run at a fraction of the cost of frontier models. CEO Satya Nadella announced them in the opening keynote.

The company introduced Code 2, a family of agentic coding models, and Code 2 Flash, a version for fast, high-volume work such as code explanations, targeted edits and routine fixes. Microsoft said both are generally available in GitHub Copilot and in Foundry, its AI model platform.

Microsoft also introduced Cyber 2 Flash, a security model it says outperforms frontier models at a fraction of the cost per task. The company is integrating the model with Perception, its agentic security system, which Nadella said became generally available Tuesday. Microsoft intends to restrict access to the cyber model to a small set of customers, according to Reuters.

Microsoft says Code 2 delivers 98% of the performance of OpenAI’s GPT-6.1 Sol at 40% of the price, according to Axios. Against Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, it reaches 96% of the performance at 5% of the cost. Microsoft says Code 2 Flash matches OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna while costing 65% as much and matches Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5 at one-fifth of the price, and that on a security benchmark Cyber 2 Flash outscores OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra while costing under 2% as much for each task. Those comparisons draw on a hand-picked set of benchmarks, and part of the price gap stems from promotional discounts, according to Axios.

Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman told Reuters that tailoring technology to particular domains holds the company's expenses down, yet still lets it equal competitors and occasionally beat them on model benchmarks. "This isn't just one model. It's not a flash in the pan," he said. "This is a system, a hill-climbing machine that we have built, which co-optimizes performance and price."

Suleyman told Axios that specialization lets Microsoft fit more knowledge into the same number of parameters. A model built around routine coding work will handle complex mathematics or scientific research less well, according to Axios.

Microsoft also announced MAI Licensed Weights, which will give trusted customers access to its model weights. Customers must deploy the licensed models within Azure, according to Axios.

The launch follows Microsoft's decision in June to introduce seven in-house MAI models at its Build conference, including the coding model MAI-Code-1-Flash. The company framed that effort as a push for long-term self-sufficiency and less reliance on outside providers such as OpenAI.

Microsoft's Copilot app, which the company combined with coding tools and autonomous agents in September, continues to draw on models from OpenAI and Anthropic alongside Microsoft's own.
