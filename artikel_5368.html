Chainlink (LINK): Trading near $11.61, LINK is testing support around $11.00 and looks neutral to bearish in the short term.

Near Protocol (NEAR): Near $4.40, NEAR has a healthy longer-term chart but must hold $4.00–$4.20 to avoid a deeper correction.

Binance Coin (BNB): At about $714, BNB is defending the $690–$700 support zone with a neutral-to-bearish short-term outlook.

Bitcoin (BTC): Near $77,871, BTC is testing $76,000 support, and the short-term outlook is cautiously bearish.

Chainlink is trading at about $11.61, well off its late-September peak near $15.75. On the daily chart, what was a strong bullish run has turned into a corrective consolidation, and right now the price is testing support.

LINK's bullish reversal came in August. It broke above its major moving averages and ran from roughly $8.00 to $15.75. The decline that followed has carved out lower highs and lower lows, and short-term momentum has deteriorated along the way.

The price is now under the shorter-term moving averages, which are bunched around $12.00–$12.60, so sellers still control the near-term trend. Still, LINK is holding above its 200-day moving average near $11.00, and as long as it does, the wider recovery stays intact.

The first support is $11.40–$11.50. Below that sits $11.00, a psychological level. If that gives way for good, the $10.40–$10.60 region comes into view; buyers showed up there recently.

Resistance starts near $12.00–$12.20, and the $12.50–$12.70 supply zone above it is the stronger barrier. Win those back, and a recovery toward $13.30 starts to look more likely.

Daily RSI is around 40–45, on the weak side of neutral. Buying momentum is subdued, but LINK isn't deeply oversold.

There was a sharp wick down toward $10.30 recently, and the price climbed back above $11.50 afterward. That could mean demand is soaking up the selling. One rejection candle doesn't confirm a reversal, though.

Short term, LINK looks neutral to bearish. Its next real move will probably be decided by the $11.00 support and the $12.20–$12.70 resistance zone.

NEAR Protocol trades near $4.40. It had a big rally off its August range of $1.60–$1.80, and even after the latest pullback the bigger structure still looks healthy. Short-term momentum is fading, though, so it may need to consolidate for a while before the next sustained leg up.

The breakout came in September. NEAR tore through several resistance levels and topped out around $6.00 in early October, on much heavier volume than usual, a sign that a lot of the market was taking part.

Since that peak it's been correcting: lower highs, and one test after another of the $4.20–$4.40 support region.

Right now NEAR is trading under its short-term moving averages, with resistance around $4.50–$4.80. The intermediate and long-term averages are still rising and sit well below the price, so the broader bullish structure doesn't look broken yet.

First support is $4.20–$4.30, the area that kept getting tested during the recent consolidation.

Under that, $4.00 is both a psychological and a technical support level. A decisive break there could expose $3.60–$3.70, roughly where the rising longer-period moving average sits.

On the upside, the first resistance is around $4.55–$4.70, then $4.90–$5.00. Break above $5.00 and hold, and the way could open toward $5.40, eventually $5.80–$6.00.

Volume exploded during the September breakout, so there was real buying behind it. It has cooled off since, as the price consolidates, and that points to less speculative interest.

The daily RSI has dropped back to around 40–45. Momentum is weaker, but there's no clear oversold signal. The latest bounce from roughly $4.00 shows buyers are still active lower down. It needs stronger follow-through to count for much. Getting back above $4.70, then $5.00, could restart the upward momentum, with $5.40–$5.80 as the target.

A confirmed daily close under $4.00 would make a deeper correction toward $3.60, and possibly $3.20, more likely.

NEAR's longer-term chart still looks healthy, but the short term is under corrective pressure. It has to hold $4.00–$4.20. A recovery to $4.70–$5.00 would be stronger evidence that buyers are taking back control.

Binance Coin is trading near $714 after a sizable pullback from its October highs around $800–$810. Short-term momentum on the daily chart is weakening, but the recovery that began in August hasn't been broken technically.

BNB broke out hard in late August and climbed from about $600 to $800. October was a different story, with a run of lower highs as buying pressure faded. The latest decline knocked BNB under its short-term moving averages at $730–$750, which tilts the near-term picture bearish.

Recently BNB tested $690–$700, where support lines up with the rising 200-day moving average, and bounced back to $714. Buyers look like they're defending that zone.

Resistance comes first at $730–$740 and then at $750–$760. A sustained recovery above $760 would improve the outlook and could open the way toward $780–$800.

On the way down, $700 is the first support, and $690 is the next. A confirmed daily close below $690 could speed up selling toward $670–$650.

The daily RSI is around 40–45. Momentum has weakened, but it isn't at extreme oversold levels, so a technical bounce is still possible. It would need stronger confirmation, though.

Bitcoin is trading near $77,871 after a sharp correction from its October peak around $87,000. Short-term momentum on the daily chart is getting worse, and BTC is closing in on a support zone that could decide where it goes next.

The August reversal was a strong one. BTC broke above its major moving averages and rallied from roughly $63,000 to $87,000. Then October brought a string of lower highs, as demand weakened and selling pressure built.

The latest drop took BTC below its short-term moving averages, which are concentrated around $80,500–$82,000. That area is now heavy overhead resistance.

The price recently dipped to about $76,000 before recovering toward $77,900. The rising 200-day moving average runs through the $76,000–$77,000 area, and another intermediate-term average is close by, so support is stacked there.

Immediate resistance is around $78,500–$79,000, then $80,500–$82,000. A sustained breakout above $82,000 would strengthen the case for a recovery and could bring $84,000–$86,000 into reach.

If BTC loses $76,000, the broader bullish structure weakens and a correction toward $74,000–$72,000 becomes more likely.

Daily RSI has dropped to about 40. That's bearish momentum, but it's getting near the levels where short relief rallies can start.

Selling volume has been high lately, so plenty of traders took part in the decline. That's why any return of buying demand needs to be confirmed.

Holding $76,000–$77,000 and taking back $80,500 could set off a recovery toward $82,000–$84,000.

A confirmed breakdown below $76,000 could speed up selling toward $74,000, possibly $72,000.

Bitcoin is testing a major long-term support region, and the short-term outlook is cautiously bearish. Defending $76,000 is what keeps the broader recovery intact. Reclaiming $82,000 would be a clearer sign that bullish momentum is back.
