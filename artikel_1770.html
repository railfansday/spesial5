‘Not enough for me’ - Ex-Premier League referee weighs in on Chelsea v Leeds United controversy

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Former Premier League referee Keith Hackett would not have awarded Leeds United a penalty when Dominic Calvert-Lewin was sent to ground by Chelsea goalkeeper Emiliano Martinez.

Leeds ran out as 4-1 winners at Stamford Bridge on Saturday (November 21) in a game that had its fair share of controversial moments.

Early in the second half, Martinez appeared to wrap his arms around Calvert-Lewin and drag him to the ground.

Referee Michael Oliver did not award a spot-kick to the Whites and Hackett, who officiated in England’s top flight between 1992 and 1994, would have also ignored protests.

He told Football Insider: “When I look at the law, the law talks about a careless challenge, and the result of that is no sanction, but would be a free-kick.

“So, although it might seem harsh, there are grounds for the award of a penalty kick, on the basis of the goalkeeper and the defender being careless.

“But then I switch to the fact that football's a contact sport - and these things happen. What I think is the 18th law of the game, which is rarely used now, is common sense.

“I'm not going to award a penalty kick for that. I'm just going to go, ‘right, fine, carry on'. But if it's a penalty, well, that's your opinion. Mine's slightly different. I'm not giving it. Not enough for me.”

Leeds were awarded a penalty minutes later, when Morgan Rogers was judged to have handled a Jayden Bogle shot.

Calvert-Lewin stepped up and slotted home from 12 yards, putting the Whites ahead in the game. Noah Okafor later registered his second of the afternoon to extend the lead and Brenden Aaronson delivered the final blow late on.

The win pushed Leeds up to sixth in the Premier League table, above Xabi Alonso’s underperforming Blues.
