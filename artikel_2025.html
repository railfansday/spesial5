Ex-refs chief: VAR should have 'intervened' over Sunderland penalty in win vs Chelsea

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The penalty that ultimately saw Sunderland beat Chelsea 1-0 on Sunday should have been overturned.

That is according to former head of PGMOL and FIFA referee, Keith Hackett, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

Enzo Le Fee made no mistake from the penalty, which was awarded by Jarred Gillett for Levi Colwill's foul on Brian Brobbey.

Xabi Alonso was incensed, as were his players, who felt that the defender had been dragged down by the Sunderland striker.

The Black Cats continued to threaten and were ultimately unlucky not to add to their advantage, with Chelsea struggling to make an impact.

The defeat leaves the Blues behind Liverpool and already 11 points adrift of Premier League leaders, Arsenal.

Upon seeing the replays, many would have almost assumed that VAR would overturn the decision.

While Colwill was behind Brobbey and certainly had a hold of his shirt, there was also sustained holding by the Sunderland striker.

The Dutchman quite clearly had hold of the England international's sleeve, before ultimately clipping his own legs on his way down.

But Gillett awarded the penalty after a brief pause and VAR Nick Hopton ultimately confirmed the decision, much to the anger of Chelsea.

"In my opinion, the Sunderland penalty kick awarded by referee Jarred Gillett should've seen the intervention of VAR," Hackett told Football Insider.

"Review of the video shows that the Sunderland player is in fact the offender, holding his opponent by the shirt and dragging him to the ground behind him. Not a penalty kick for me."

Interestingly, Sunderland also benefitted from a similar incident earlier in the season at the Stadium of Light.

The visit of Arsenal had been closely-contested, before John Brooks pointed to the spot for a penalty for the home side for a seemingly obvious foul.

Replays clearly showed that Dan Ballard had grabbed hold of Ezri Konsa's shirt and pulled him to the ground in a judo roll, but the penalty stood.

David Raya produced the save that day and Arsenal would ultimately win - Chelsea were not so lucky on Sunday.
