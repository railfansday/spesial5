This Colorado Mountain Escape Is Home to a Plum Kitchen, “Quilted” Bedroom, and the Occasional Grizzly Bear

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Designer Emily Tucker designed her family’s rustic retreat with hosting in mind.

The first guest to make himself at home at designer Emily Tucker’s Colorado mountain retreat was a bear. While the property was still under construction, the curious visitor took to wandering through the unfinished rooms, even leaving a few paw prints on the architectural drawings. It was a fitting introduction to life at Camp Grizz, Tucker’s family getaway in Ward, Colorado, where the surrounding wilderness has always had a say in things.

Tucker and her family purchased the wooded parcel, which backs up to a national forest, in 2020. The existing cabin offered rustic accommodations (read: no running water), but the family had bigger plans for the property. “The goal was to build a house as modest as possible, but able to fit two families at a time,” says Tucker, who regularly hosts friends and their children for weekends away. Building on a landscape composed almost entirely of granite, however, was no small feat. With no existing infrastructure to tap into, everything from the well to the driveway had to be created from scratch. “It was a beast,” the designer adds.

Tucker envisioned interiors that felt “colorful, warm, Scandinavian-ish, but with a lot of collected touches,” taking inspiration from the mismatched charm of a patchwork quilt. That influence appears everywhere, from the primary bedroom’s pieced-together grasscloth walls to the checkerboard bathroom tile and eclectic mix of textiles. Designing her own home also offered Tucker the opportunity to indulge ideas that hadn’t quite found an audience with her clients. Chief among them? An eggplant-purple kitchen, complete with plum cabinetry and glossy zellige tile. “I had proposed purple kitchens and mudrooms for many clients who had all said no,” she laughs.

But Camp Grizz’s greatest luxury has little to do with its finishes. “My favorite thing about the house is the way we feel in it,” Tucker says. “It’s cozy, layered, collected, and holds our family and friends for celebrations, relaxing, and just enjoying life’s small moments.”

A chance discovery in a stair-parts catalog led Tucker to the charming Swiss-style balusters that distinguish the staircase. The decorative stenciling came later, inspired by a trip to London during construction. Tucker and her son worked out the pattern together on a spare baluster before local decorative painter Annie DeCamp brought their design to life.

A mix of existing furnishings (a “hodge-pod of things I already owned,” says Tucker) gives the living room its collected, unfussy character. Flanking the wood-burning stove, a pair of floating live-edge wood benches act as a happy departure from Tucker’s original plan for concrete seating. “When the opportunity came up to use the live-edge wood slabs, it seemed the perfect fit,” she says.

“The vintage Thomas Moser chairs were purchased before we even broke ground on the house; I love these chairs and always have,” Tucker says of the seating in the dining nook. “The classic but modern, pared-back Americana design was perfect for the space.” Nearby, trim clad in Rattan by Benjamin Moore unites the open floor plan.

After years of pitching purple kitchens to clients without success, Tucker finally took the plunge herself. “Once I landed on eggplant purple, I couldn't get it out of my head,” she says. Benjamin Moore’s Plum Royale cabinetry meets glossy Purple Iris zellige tile from Zia Tile, with soapstone countertops providing a moody counterpoint. Behind the saturated facade, clever cabinetry conceals appliances and pantry essentials, leaving the countertops streamlined and clear.

For her own bedroom, Tucker gathered leftover Phillip Jeffries wallcoverings from previous projects, supplemented them with her installer’s remnants, and pieced together a one-of-a-kind patchwork-style wall treatment. The room’s generous ceiling height also gave her the chance to indulge another longtime wish: a canopy bed, which she dressed with modified Anthropologie curtains.

A length of cowboy-print fabric gifted to Tucker by a client became the unexpected starting point for the bunk room’s spirited palette. Picking up on the textile’s red accents, she painted the built-in bunks in Benjamin Moore’s Caliente and layered them with a deliberately mismatched assortment of Pendleton blankets and pillows from Target, St. Frank, and IKEA.

The home’s patchwork inspiration finds another expression in the bunk bathroom, where blue and green tiles from Wow Tile form an intentionally irregular checkerboard. Leftover green-veined marble flooring from another project adds another layer of color.

A lucky auction find meant this compact guest bedroom needed an unconventional layout. Because the painted dresser was too large to fit alongside a traditionally centered bed, Tucker aligned it to one edge instead, installing a wall-to-wall upholstered headboard to restore visual balance.

Emily Tucker is the founder and principal designer of Emily Tucker Design, a Boulder, Colorado–based interior design firm specializing in colorful, collected, and deeply personal residential interiors. A California native, Tucker earned her degree in Architecture and Planning from the University of Colorado Boulder before honing her eye under celebrated designers Ruthie Sommers, Kelly Wearstler, and Steven Gambrel in Los Angeles and New York City. In 2015, she returned to Colorado to establish her eponymous studio, where she now oversees projects across the country, ranging from ground-up construction to thoughtful renovations.
