NYC college put on notice after Jewish campus group shunned by student government: 'Rude awakening'

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Shabbos Kestenbaum called for Hunter College to lose federal funding after its student government was accused of shutting down an event involving Jewish students.

A Jewish American activist in New York City decried what he calls "anti-Jewish discrimination" after the student government at a school that is part of the City University of New York (CUNY) system, canceled an event that was supposed to include the school's Hillel group when it received backlash from other student organizations.

Hunter College's undergraduate student government (USG) canceled a "Cultural Arts Night" involving several student groups, including Hunter Hillel, the Chinese Student & Scholar Association, the Taiwanese Student Association and the Center for Puerto Rican Studies, according to the New York Post. The radical far-left group Students for Justice in Palestine reportedly took part in planning the event.

In a statement to The Post, the school itself admitted that some of those groups withdrew from the event because Hunter Hillel was planning to participate.

"They were establishing a cultural arts night, which I might add also included countries with far worse civil rights violations, like China, and none of that was a problem until, of course, the Jewish organization was involved," said Shabbos Kestenbaum, who described himself as a Jewish American activist and who works for PragerU.

"So this is blatant anti-Jewish discrimination, and we as New Yorkers, we as Americans, and yes, we, as Jews, will do our best to hold the university accountable," he said.

The USG apologized for sponsoring the event, saying it had "failed" and that the event "should never have moved forward.

"To be clear, USG does not endorse this event or this collaboration," the apology said. "We hear what the student body has said, and we have made the decision to cancel the event as it doesn’t align with our values or the values of the students we represent as the Hunter Community. We apologize to the clubs and students who were affected by this instance."

Hunter College faced claims of antisemitism after student leaders canceled an event featuring Hillel, a Jewish organization. (Rachel Wolf/Fox News Digital)

The apology said the group was actively working to ensure that such a mistake never happened again.

Kestenbaum believes that the school might have run afoul of anti-discrimination laws, including Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, which prohibits any entity receiving federal funding from engaging in discrimination based on sex, race, ethnicity or other immutable characteristics.

He advocated for the federal government to pull its funding from Hunter College.

"It's just enforcing the laws already on the books," he said, adding that President Donald Trump and his administration have been making progress on punishing discriminatory behavior on college campuses.

Graduates of Hunter College walk out of graduation ceremonies to protest Israel's continued war in Gaza on May 30, 2025, outside Barclays Center in Brooklyn, N.Y. (Andrew Lichtenstein/Corbis via Getty Images)

"It's not something you pick and choose if you feel good that morning," he said. "It's the law and the expenditure of federal dollars is a representation of our values. And so if you're at university and you're discriminating against American citizens based on their ethnicity, based on the religion, based on their politics — not only is that a violation of law, but that's not a representation of where we want our money to go to."

Hunter Hillel responded to the cancellation in a statement to Fox News Digital.

"Hillel students were looking forward to taking part in the Cultural Arts Night, and our organization had no role in the student government’s decision to cancel it. Antisemitism has no place at Hunter College," the statement said. "The antisemitic response to the cancellation notice has been egregious and inappropriate. Rita Levine Rabin Hillel at Hunter remains committed to building joyful, strong, and vibrant Jewish life on campus."

Kestenbaum also referenced Rep.-elect Darializa Chevalier, D-N.Y., a prominent anti-Israel activist who took part in the infamous 2024 Columbia University encampment, and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, himself a former anti-Israel activist.

He said students at New York's colleges are simply following the examples set for them by their elected officials.

CUNY has previously settled with the Department of Education after discrimination claims.

"And all these elitist universities that think that they're higher and better than the law, they're in for a rude awakening," Kestenbaum said. "And again, you don't need to radically pass pieces of legislation that overhauls the bureaucracy. All you need to do is make it incredibly clear to these colleges, we are not giving you money if you discriminate. It is literally that simple."

The CUNY system did not return a request for comment.

"There is no place for discrimination and harassment in our college community," Hunter College told the New York Post. "We have policies in place to safeguard that principle and will not hesitate to enforce them."

Neither Hunter College nor its USG returned Fox News Digital's comment requests.
