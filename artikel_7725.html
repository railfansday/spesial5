There’s always something new and exciting on the horizon in the world of gardening. Around this time every year, various organizations release their favorite gardening and landscaping trends for the coming year. From the color of the year to new plants and outdoor décor, the list below represents our favorite 2027 gardening trends for you to consider as you plan or update your outdoor space in the coming months.

Garden Media Group has identified ancient blue as the garden color of the year for 2027. As the first manufactured color in history, blue was created by the Egyptians 5,000 years ago to imitate precious lapis lazuli. While blue is consistently the favorite color around the world, it is the rarest color in the garden, showing up in fewer than 10 percent of flowers. “This makes growing it in the garden feel like victory,” says Katie Dubow, president of Garden Media Group.

Gardeners prefer more from their edible plants than just fruits and vegetables to harvest, according to Monrovia’s 2027 Garden Design Trends Study. “We’re seeing shoppers be more discerning in their edible choices,” says Katie Tamony, chief marketing officer and trend spotter at Monrovia. “They’re looking for plants that add a design element or feel more exotic than the common choices their neighbors grow. People are also interested in edible plants that look good all season long.”

Adding an edible garden ranks among the highest-interest projects in Monrovia’s survey, with more than 45 percent of respondents saying they are looking for more unique selections. Enhance your edible garden with specialty herbs; berries with year-round interest; and conversation-starting selections like pomegranate, figs, kiwi, and olives.

After years of beige minimalism and tidy rows of shrubs, the “Let’s Go Rococo” theme is all about exuberance over restraint, says Dubow. Emerging in early 18th century France, rococo design was a deliberate rebellion by artists and craftsmen against the stiff grandeur of Baroque and societal wealth inequality. Think flowers, shells, gilded flourishes, ruffles, jewel-tone palettes, and asymmetry. “It’s ornamental, romantic, a little over the top, and joyful,” notes Dubow.

Garden Media Group’s OutdooRx trend positions the garden as medicine, says Dubow. “In the United Kingdom, doctors now “socially prescribe” gardening programs to patients, and research continues to show that gardening lowers stress hormones like cortisol while boosting mood-lifting dopamine and serotonin,” she explains. National Garden Bureau also tracks garden trends, especially when it comes to which specific plants are the most popular in a given year. “Coming up in 2027, we see an enormous interest in hellebores, and that is our Year of the Crop for Perennials,” says Diane Blazek, executive director for All-America Selections at National Garden Bureau.

Often called the Christmas rose or Lenten rose, the hellebore is a hardy perennial that does well in full shade and brings beauty to winter and early spring gardens when most other flowers have faded. It produces elegant 2- to 3-inch blossoms in a cup shape that resemble roses. Hellebore flowers grow in a variety of colors, so you have plenty of options. It grows in USDA Hardiness Zones 5 to 8, and does best in cottage gardens, winter gardens, or woodland gardens. Take note that this plant is toxic and should be handled with caution.

As another season of the popular show The Gilded Age is released this month, romance and beauty are alive and well in gardens, too. This look maximizes the popularity of European-inspired garden design with an elevated, modern twist, shining a spotlight on classic plants like roses, peonies, hydrangeas, and boxwood. In addition to these timeless plants, this trend showcases artful hedges, chartreuse foliage, and collector statement plants.

“This trend appeals to established gardeners,” says Tamony. “Homeowners who have invested in their interior design are taking that high-end look to the garden.” Their interest in rare, collector plants is increasing, and Monrovia’s research shows their willingness to pay for premium plants and exquisite blooms is up between 5 and 9 percent since 2025.

Another style that is expected to be popular is the Palm Springs outdoor space. This works well for gardeners looking to create an attractive desert oasis through containers as well as the landscape. “The Palm Springs Style trend is all about creating a calming, wellness-focused outdoor space that feels more upscale,” says Tamony. With this aesthetic, gardeners can transform their landscape by choosing structured succulents and lush, tropical colors found with species from Agave, Bougainvillea, Mandevilla, and tropical hibiscus.
