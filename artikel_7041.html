The Best Mini PCs to Buy in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Every product was carefully curated by an Esquire editor. We may earn a commission from these links. Here’s how we test products and why you should trust us.

These high-performing, small-footprint models will clean up your workspace.

Tiny computers are nothing new. I know you have one in your pocket right now—that is, if you’re not reading this on it already. The smartphone proved that computers can be anything, and anything can be a computer. Even putting aside the smartphone and the laptop, desktops have been getting smaller and changing form over time. The mini PC (sometimes also called a NUC) is a term that’s getting a lot of use lately, but it’s for something that’s more or less existed for decades: a small, prebuilt computer.

Traditionally, a PC runs Windows, but you can find Linux-based and MacOS-based mini PCs too. Of course, Apple makes its own in the form of the Mac Mini, probably the most popular one on the market. The original version debuted back in 2005, proving my point that these are nothing new, and has made its way into millions of homes without needing anyone to utter the words “mini PC.”

Point is, if you’re looking for something with the capability of a desktop without wanting a space-hogging tower, this is what you need to be looking at. Whether you need a machine for school, work, or gaming, these are the most capable desktops that come in shockingly small packages. These four machines are the best mini PCs to buy right now.

Starting off, it’s the 2026 Mac Mini. For the average person, you can start and stop the shopping journey here. If you want a compact home computer and you’re already deeply embedded in MacOS and Apple’s ecosystem, it’s hard to resist the simple appeal of the Mac Mini M6. It’s unassuming, smaller than a cable box and about the size of your internet modem. Ninety percent of the time, it’s as quiet as either of those too, despite being a fast mid-range computer optimized for AI workloads (more so than any generation before it). In terms of connections, you get a full stack; three Thunderbolt 4 ports, an HDMI port, and an Ethernet port in the back, as well as two USB-C ports and a headphone jack in front. More than plenty for all your accessories.

Like I said, this is a mid-range Mac. It’s just as suited for a home office as the main family computer in the living room. For something with professional level capabilities (we are all professionals, but I’m talking video editing, software development, any 3D model rendering), you’ll want the one with the M5 Pro chip instead. As always, with either of these, the biggest downside is Apple’s walled garden. You can’t upgrade the RAM or storage capacity like you can with other PCs, and Apple isn’t afraid to charge an arm and a leg for the versions that come with extra.

If you’re in both a time and money crunch, this is the kind of computer you want. For less than $400, it’s a Windows-capable machine with 16 gigs of RAM that can output to up to three 4K displays at a time. And unless you are pushing its processing power, it stays cool and relatively silent. It’s got all the ports you’d want—USB, HDMI, DPI, headphone jack—plus two high-speed USB-A ports in the back. These are decent for when you have large files to transfer, but they admittedly pale in terms of speed compared to a Thunderbolt port.

At the price it’s currently at, this is also cheaper than any gaming console out there. Not even as powerful as a Nintendo Switch (the old one), but it can run Minecraft and other casual or indie titles with little trouble.

If your job or home business requires running more and more local AI models (as many do), you might want a mini PC designed with these workflows in mind. Plus, it has every kind of port you’d want and more. With HDMI, USB, Thunderbolt, Ethernet, even an SD card slot and Copilot button, it’s fully kitted out for even the more niche uses.

It’s not a perfect solution. This machine will struggle with more intensive tasks and you’ll hear about it. For its size and light frame (it’s easily mountable to a monitor stand), though, it’s nothing to sneeze at.

Any machine can run some video games, but if you’re the type who considers gaming your hobby of choice, and you need something to play all the latest releases in 4K, well, then you need something as dedicated as you. For a reasonable price tag (trust me, it is), you’re getting a powerful PC with an RTX 5060 graphics card and 32 gigabytes of RAM. For the completely uninitiated, that’s a mid-range 2025 dedicated graphics card—sure to run any modern game, but when it comes to more demanding titles you just may have to choose to prioritize either frame rate or visuals.

Beyond gaming, this is a capable machine due to the decent GPU. And for those who don’t want to tinker at all, the G1 Pro offers three default modes to quickly swap between. Swap from Office to Gaming when it’s time to play, or go into Beast mode when you’re really trying to throttle the machine and get the most out of it.

The real reason to get something like this over a console or Valve’s Steam Machine is that it’s upgradeable. The storage and RAM can both be swapped out for newer or higher-capacity options. It’s not a full-on PC, though. With the CPU and GPU, you’re stuck with what is on board.
