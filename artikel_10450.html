Starbucks’ viral Bearista cup is back for the holidays. Here’s how they turned the cute cup into a collector frenzy

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Starbucks obviously knows coffee, but the chain has also mastered the ability to sell endlessly collectible merchandise. Collecting its mugs and cold cups has been a hobby of die-hard fans and online content creators who make videos about hunting for the latest drop, and interest in Starbucks merchandise reaches a fever pitch around the holidays.

In November 2025, the company introduced the Starbucks Bearista Glass Cold Cup in the U.S. The handblown, bear-shaped glass cup for cold drinks came with a Starbucks-green beanie and a green-and-white striped straw that proved irresistible. Lines snaked around stores before their early-morning openings. More than one store witnessed physical fights, and videos on social media of people “hunting” the cup racked up views.

The Bearista has been a recurring mascot for the brand since 1997, when Starbucks introduced a plush, green-aproned bear for the holidays. In 2023, it morphed into a cold cup in Korea, then spread throughout Asia before hitting the U.S. last November. By then it was already on cup collectors’ radars.

Dana Pellicano, SVP of global product experience, Starbucks: We were seeing this trend of kidulting, adults leaning into playful, fun products. The confluence of that, [the bear’s legacy], and the rise of cold drinks made it the right time for us to bring it to the U.S.

Carol Casey, aka RayahSunshine, cup collector/content creator: In the cup community, we see lots of cups coming out of other countries that are really enticing. We noticed that Starbucks Asia had a very unique set of cups. Their cups were more cutesy.

The artisan quality of the cup meant its supply was very limited. That created demand for social media videos of how to find one, and even created a secondary market on eBay. In a statement, the company apologized to those who weren’t able to snag one.

Pellicano: What makes it special is also what makes it logistically hard and part of the reason we had to be really disciplined about how we launched it. When you say yes to something, you’re saying no to something else. So we are saying yes to this really premium, elevated offering and therefore unintentionally saying no to everybody being able to have one. These are the choices that you have to balance.

Lindsay Ream, Starbucks coffeehouse leader, Grand Rapids, Michigan: Everybody wanted it. We get to the store at 4 a.m.—we open at 4:30—and when we got here, we had six cars in the parking lot waiting for us to open the doors. All day long the phone rang. We had someone answering all day to say “sorry, we’re sold out.”

Casey: Because stores stagger their openings and resellers pop from store to store, it’s very competitive. I like to go where I know I can for sure get the cup. With the limited editions, you’re there at 3 a.m., and some people were getting there at 2. The Bearista bear went nuts. Everybody was trying to get it—and people were willing to spend $250 on eBay for it. It was so limited that it upset people more than not, but it did bring a lot of interest.

Since last year, Starbucks has launched four more Bearista cups—the Pink Drink Bearista with a white hat and strawberry straw topper, an online-exclusive, soccer-themed Bearista for Starbucks Rewards Reserve tier members, and a Snoopy-shaped cup for fall. For the holiday season, the original Bearista is making a comeback, with a surprise box that contains one of four variations on the original, with the potential to include a rare golden Bearista..

Pellicano: We’ve really worked to refine our processes, and we’ve had a lot of success. We try to match the natural demand of where our stores see real volume in driving merch. We capped how many cups customers can purchase [at two per person].

Ream: In my district, we talked about partners not buying cups ahead of time. We have “partner access” where employees only were offered an early launch, but it didn’t come out of the amount of cups that we had for customers.

Casey: I may hunt it [again] just for the sake of the hunt and the content—just because that’s what I do.
