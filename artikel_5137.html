Nvidia’s CEO, an alleged romance and a suspected Chinese spy: Congress wants answers

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The reported intelligence memo adds detail to an earlier House briefing request, with an answer sought by November 11.

Congress is seeking answers about Nvidia’s defenses against espionage as Financial Times reporting describes an intelligence-based memo alleging a romantic relationship between CEO Jensen Huang and Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Nvidia customer Megaspeed.

FT excerpts shared by @AsiaLens on November 7 report that US intelligence believes Alice Huang was recruited by China’s Ministry of State Security, citing people familiar with the matter.

Three House committee chairs signed a November 4 letter requesting a joint, closed-door briefing on Huang’s interactions with Alice Huang and Nvidia’s safeguards against foreign espionage. Published on November 5, the request predates the FT excerpts.

Nvidia has described the contacts as ordinary customer business and said it does not share classified information. The central security question is whether the alleged relationship provided access beyond ordinary customer contact.

The FT excerpts shared by @AsiaLens say US officials wrote the memo earlier in 2026 using information obtained by American intelligence. People familiar with the memo said it alleged Huang had been romantically involved with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer.

According to the same excerpts, the memo reached a small group of senior US officials shortly before President Donald Trump traveled to Beijing for a summit with Xi Jinping. The memo itself is not public, and its exact date and authoring agency are not established. The reported assessment and relationship allegation have not been publicly proven.

The letter was signed by Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, who chair the Foreign Affairs, China Select and Intelligence committees, respectively. Their public release identifies possible foreign-adversary access to US technology and information as the reason for seeking the briefing.

In its November 5 statement to CNBC, Nvidia described ordinary customer information and denied sharing sensitive trade secrets or national-security information.

Megaspeed belongs to Nvidia’s cloud partner network, buying equipment and renting computing power to AI developers, according to Bloomberg’s November 7 report. That commercial role puts the relationship within Nvidia’s customer network and the wider debate over access to US AI technology.

Nvidia told CNBC on November 5 that regional cloud customers receive routine business information rather than sensitive trade secrets or information relevant to national security. The company also said Huang’s meetings with customer executives were a normal part of his job.

In the newer Bloomberg report, Nvidia said its cloud partners do not ask it to seek changes in US law or advocate chip sales to China. It also said: “We neither have nor share classified information with customers.”

Asked about FT’s relationship allegation, Nvidia referred Bloomberg to Huang’s earlier description of a typical business relationship with Alice Huang.

The FT excerpts shared by @AsiaLens describe Nvidia declining comment on the memo and alleged relationship, and the White House and CIA declining comment.

The House chairs requested a November 18 briefing, asking Huang to accept or propose another congressional session date by November 11. CNBC reported that Nvidia had not directly confirmed attendance when asked on November 5, while Megaspeed had not immediately answered its request for comment.

Other official responses address narrower, earlier reporting. China’s foreign ministry dismissed an earlier New York Times report on October 30 as unsubstantiated. That statement predates the latest memo and relationship reporting.

Singapore’s Ministry of Home Affairs said on October 31 that no Alice Huang-specific investigation of the kind described by the Wall Street Journal had been conducted. Its annex separately confirmed an ongoing investigation into Megaspeed. The denial concerning Alice Huang did not extend to the separate Megaspeed investigation.

Huang’s next deadline is November 11: accept the proposed briefing or offer another date.
