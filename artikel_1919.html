Leeds United midfielder Sean Longstaff lifts lid on ‘tough year’ and makes Newcastle United comparison

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Leeds United midfielder Sean Longstaff has conceded his move to Elland Road has not gone completely to plan.

The 29-year-old signed for the Whites as part of their post-promotion recruitment drive in the summer of 2025, making a £12m move from his boyhood club Newcastle United.

He started his maiden campaign as a midfield regular but missed the weeks in which Leeds turned their season around through injury.

Opportunities were limited upon his return and competition in the middle of the park has only intensified in his second season.

He told The Official Leeds United Podcast: “I’d be lying if I said it’s been the easiest year I’ve ever had. It’s been a tough year.

“There’s probably been times, at home, especially, there’s times where you’re not that nice to be around. Ultimately, I made the decision to come to Leeds. I took my family away from the comforts of being at their home. I’ve took a newborn baby away from his grandparents to bring him down here for me to enhance my career.

“Obviously, at the minute, it’s not gone probably to the perfect plan. But I don’t think life ever does.”

Longstaff was handed his first league start of the season before the international break, completing 90 minutes in Leeds’ 1-0 win over Tottenham Hotspur.

He made his 200th Premier League appearance in the game and the reaching of the milestone in such a valuable win served as a reward for his perseverance.

The midfielder said: “For me, I take training as serious as possible. Whether that’s the day after a game, whether it’s during the week, you’ve got to train as if you’re going to play.

“And then ultimately, if you don’t play, that’s not in my control. I can’t control it. All I can control is, make sure I’m fit enough to play when I get a chance, make sure I’m sharp enough, make sure my mentality is right.

“If I get a chance, I want to try and show what I can do, whether that’s for 30 seconds whether that’s a for, come on at half-time, or whatever the case may be. I can’t control other people’s decisions. All I can control is the controllables.”

Longstaff may not have seen the level of action he wanted in West Yorkshire, but has spoken of the club with great affection.

He has compared the Whites to Newcastle, who he made 214 appearances for before departing.

He said: “It’s still close to home so it didn’t feel like you were moving away miles. It’s not until you probably get down here, everyone says Leeds is a massive club, but until you get here, you don’t realise how big it is.

“I came with an expectation of ‘it’s going to be Newcastle’s this and Leeds is that’. Then you get in, you think ‘oh actually, Leeds is so similar.

“Apart from the kit, everything else felt exactly the same. Obviously the only difference is me being from Newcastle and not from Leeds, but actually got to know a lot of people from the city and they were telling us stuff. The way they speak about Leeds is the way I felt about Newcastle. The more you meet people, the more you realise it’s massive.”
