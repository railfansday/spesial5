Bruno Mars Mourns His Father’s Death: ‘The Man That Taught Me Everything I Know’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Throughout his prolific and wildly successful career, Bruno Mars has always made sure to credit his parents and upbringing for his success today, highlighting how watching his father, Peter “Dr. Doo-Wop” Hernandez, as a kid would impact his signature style as he rose to fame. “My whole sense of rhythm is because my dad was teaching me bongos as a kid,” Mars told Latina magazine in 2017, per Remezcla. “He’s an old-school working musician, so that’s where the pinky rings come from, the patent-leather shoes, the suits, and the pompadour. It all stems from watching my father.”

On Thursday, Mars shared the devastating news that his father had died. In a social media post, Mars included photos of his father hugging him from a hospital bed and images taken over the course of his lifetime, ending with a snapshot of his father and mother, Bernadette Hernandez, who died from a brain aneurysm in 2013. In the caption, Mars wrote, “Last week I got to hug my father one last time. If you’ve ever seen me talk about him I’ve always said that he’s the man that taught me everything I know, especially when it comes to music.”

Mars continued, “He raised my four beautiful sisters, my brother, and me. In my eyes he was the greatest dad in the world, and I will miss him everyday until we meet again. I Love you Pops. Kiss mom up there for me.”

When speaking to Rolling Stone in 2013, Peter recalled dimming the lights in the delivery room as his wife gave birth to Mars. He described the setting as “almost like a nightclub,” and said he played “oldies but goodies” on a cassette boombox to usher Mars into the world. Mars often discussed how he grew up surrounded by music as a kid, playing at gigs alongside his father, mother, and siblings. Peter was a percussionist from Brooklyn who packed his congas and headed to Hawaii in the Seventies. While playing drums at the Hilton in Waikiki, he met Mars’ mom, Bernadette, a hula dancer and singer who’d moved from the Philippines with her family at a young age. When Mars arrived a few years later, his father had already shaped the family for the stage. While his father led the band, his mother sang with some of his uncles; and his older sister and brother also performed. Mars, as many know by now, impersonated Elvis as a child and at four years old, he appeared as a tiny King in Honeymoon in Vegas.

“This is something I never thought about until recently,” Mars said in a 2013 Rolling Stone interview. “But because of my upbringing performing for tourists, I had to entertain everyone. Not just black people, not just white people, not just Asian people, not just Latin people. I had to perform for anybody that came to Hawaii.”
