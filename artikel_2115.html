One league appearance despite trio of assists: How is former Burnley and West Ham target faring since failed summer move

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Burnley were a busy side at the end of the transfer window as they looked to strengthen following a disappointing summer and start to the window. The Clarets came close to signing Sunderland winger Jocelin Ta Bi on deadline day in September, but the move ultimately collapsed over the Clarets’ squad requirements.

The 21-year-old had travelled to Turf Moor as Nicky Hayen’s side explored a loan deal, only for the transfer to fall through late in the window. Burnley were not alone in their pursuit, with West Ham, Hull City and several French clubs also expressing interest.

According to reports from Sebastian Denis, the Hammers were among those monitoring Ta Bi throughout the summer, underlining the competition for his signature.

The Ivory Coast youngster joined Sunderland from Maccabi Netanya in January 2026 on a four-and-a-half-year contract. However, regular first-team opportunities have been difficult to come by under Regis Le Bris.

Ta Bi has made limited Premier League appearances, totalling fewer than 60 minutes across his senior outings in England’s top flight. His opportunities have largely come in cup competitions, where he has shown encouraging signs of his potential.

His standout performance came against Brentford in the Carabao Cup on 28 October. Starting on the right wing, Ta Bi was heavily involved in all three Sunderland goals during their 3-2 victory, creating several opportunities and showcasing his ability to beat opponents in wide areas.

Le Bris was particularly impressed by his technical quality, bravery and work rate, highlighting how the youngster managed to combine his attacking contributions with considerable defensive effort.

The Sunderland head coach has previously spoken positively about Ta Bi’s potential, although regular minutes remain important for his development. A temporary move could therefore still make sense, particularly given the player’s limited Premier League involvement.

That is something Burnley should continue monitoring. Ta Bi possesses explosive pace, close control and the dribbling ability to trouble Championship defenders. His preference for cutting inside from the right onto his stronger left foot could also offer a different attacking dimension.

Those qualities would have suited Burnley’s need for greater pace and penetration in the final third under Hayen. Although the Belgian has since departed, the Clarets’ need for more attacking threat has not disappeared.

Ta Bi still has plenty to prove at senior level, but his performance against Brentford demonstrated why Sunderland rate him so highly. If first-team opportunities remain limited, Burnley could have another opportunity to revisit their interest in a player they came so close to signing.
