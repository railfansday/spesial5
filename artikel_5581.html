Historic New England City Reclaims Top Spot as America’s Hottest Market

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A Connecticut metro known for its insurance headquarters and the Pratt & Whitney aerospace company has reclaimed its title as America's hottest housing market.

The Hartford, CT, metro area has shot back to the No. 1 spot in October for the first time since a summer run at the top from May through July 2026, according to the Realtor.com® October Hottest Markets report. Hartford has ranked in the top 20 a total of 57 times, claiming the crown eight times.

The Nutmeg State accounts for four of the top 20 markets, with Waterbury, New Haven, and Norwich joining Hartford with median list prices between $400,000 and $460,000, says the report.

The No. 1 market encompasses the cities of Hartford, West Hartford, and East Hartford, but also a large swath of quintessential historical towns that look like they stepped out of a Hallmark movie.

Those small towns—including Wethersfield, Windsor Locks, Avon, and Farmington—draw homebuyers with their small-town charm, stunning nature, and commutable locations.

They also offer relative affordability compared to the elite suburbs dotting the Long Island Sound, such as Greenwich, Westport, and Darien, which are closer to New York City.

But Hartford, the state's capital, is still close enough that it embraces some spillover from the more expensive areas. If residents need to commute to New York City only a couple of times a week, the approximately 2.5-hour drive or Amtrak ride may be worth it for the nature, safety, quiet, and relative affordability these small towns offer.

"Located in New England, midway between Boston and New York, Hartford is an ideal landing spot for homebuyers looking to take advantage of the relatively low home prices in an otherwise pricey part of the country," says Realtor.com senior economist Hannah Jones.

And home browsers are most definitely interested.

The Hartford metro had 4.1 times the viewers per property as the rest of the country, by far the highest. Median days on the market are a swift 37 (only one more than last year and a month faster than the national time) and the median list price is $440,000, the second-highest among the top 10, behind only Manchester-Nashua, NH, at $549,000.

Homes in the most wanted markets moved significantly faster than the national norm, with median time on the market of 37 days, more than three weeks faster.

The hottest markets for October 2026 are Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford, CT (4.1 times the national average); Kenosha, WI (2.8); Lancaster, PA (2.4); Rochester, NY (2.5); Manchester-Nashua, NH (2.8); Peoria, IL (2.4); Erie, PA (2.8); Rockford, IL (2.6); and Waterbury-Shelton, CT (2.8).

Incorporated in 1693, Glastonbury (named after the town in England) boasts the country's oldest continuously running ferry, to Rocky Hill across the Connecticut River. It also has the second-highest concentration of surviving pre-1800s Colonial homes, about 154.

An easy 15-minute commute to Hartford via Route 2, and abutting both West Hartford and East Hartford, Glastonbury has long been a home base for the higher-ups in both the nearby insurance industry and Pratt & Whitney ("Pratt rats," in local lingo).

The past few decades have seen major changes for the formerly rural town as sprawling, multimillion-dollar estates began flecking the hills that were only farmland and tobacco fields not long ago.

Glastonbury has managed to retain much of its historical and rural appeal even as the town gained both a Whole Foods and a Trader Joe's, and its median home price skyrocketed, currently standing at $530,300, a 6.2% increase over last year.

Wendy Anderson of William Pitt Sotheby's International Realty, who represents the town's most expensive listing, a $7.2 million French chateau-style estate in South Glastonbury, says the town continues to draw high earners not only from Hartford, but from New York City and New Haven as well.

"It's always been a strong market, a sought-after town," she tells Realtor.com. "It's an easy commute."

Beyond the commute, the town—which is fairly large for a "small town" at about 52 square miles, with three ZIP codes—seems to have it all.

Beyond its bustling commercial center—complete with shops, restaurants, coffee spots, and an urgent care clinic—the area still boasts working farms, orchards, two active wineries, a sandy-shored pond, and a riverfront park.

All public schools have a rating of 8 or higher out of 10 by Great Schools, drawing families.

The housing stock is diverse: In addition to the aforementioned Colonial homes, there are small ranches, grand estates, classic midcentury homes, newer townhomes, and upscale rentals. There are even senior and lower-income communities.

"A lot of towns in Hartford County have a lot of amenities and activities," says Anderson, who has lived in Glastonbury for 45 years. "And a great sense of community. There's town football, soccer, tennis. So many activities for children, and concerts. And it's easy to get to the shoreline, or to get to Boston or New York."

Anderson doesn't think the market will go up indefinitely.

"I think the market has peaked, the wave has crested," she says. "But the market is still holding strong."

Since listing the $7.2 million French chateau on nearly 2 acres in July, she has already shown the estate to a dozen potential buyers—and just closed on a $1.4 million home that was sold in less than a week.

Buyers, usually business owners, are lured to the town by its spectacular fall colors, its rolling hills and dense forests, its fruit orchards, its gourmet restaurants and cosy taverns—as well as its relative affordability.

Adds the agent: "I just saw a TikTok video that said, 'This town, Glastonbury—it's like Greenwich, but half the price!'"

West of the river, a mere 10 miles away from Glastonbury, is West Hartford. An undercurrent of rivalry has long existed between West Hartford and Glastonbury, with both attracting Hartford's top earners from the healthcare, insurance, and education industries.

Although less than half the size of Glastonbury, West Hartford offers many of the same charms with its mix of idyllic small-town life and modern convenience.

Both towns are considered very walkable compared to the average Connecticut suburb, have highly rated public school systems, and have beautifully maintained their share of pre-1800s historic homes.

West Hartford, however, tends to be more expensive at its high end, standing at a median of $712,000 for its 06107 ZIP code, a slight dip from last year.

Robert Levine of ERA Real Estate, who lives and sells in West Hartford and the surrounding areas, says the two upscale suburbs have a few key differences.

Glastonbury has larger plot sizes, with homes often sitting on an acre or more of land, and has room to grow. West Hartford, a more suburban enclave with no real room left to build, has a denser, more bustling center of town.

"We're a destination. People come from 50 miles away to have dinner in our center," Levine says.

As for the metro being the hottest one in the country, the agent says he isn't surprised—he still sees multiple offers on turnkey homes that check all the boxes: good location, backyard, basement, two-car garage, etc.

But he declares that the area is in a "transitional market," with some homes sitting for several months.

"Homeowners are scratching their heads, like, 'Wait, my home won't sell in a weekend?'" he says.

"We're transitioning to what I call an 'equilibrium' market," he adds. "It's not necessarily favorable to either buyers or sellers. It's all about the property."

As for why central Connecticut keeps luring buyers, the agent notes the scenery, the small-town vibes combined with modern ease, and the tight-knit communities.
