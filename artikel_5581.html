Anthropic stock slips after record IPO as AI selloff drags down Nvidia and AMD

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Anthropic stock fell 8% in premarket trading on Friday after closing up 17% at $97.17 a share in its first day on the Nasdaq

Anthropic stock fell 8% in premarket trading on Friday, a day after the AI lab debuted on the Nasdaq $NDAQ in the largest initial public offering on record.

The stock was down 8.1% at 7:05 a.m. ET. Nvidia $NVDA stock fell 2.4% and AMD $AMD stock fell 6.8% as fears about AI continued to fuel a selloff, according to CNBC.

Anthropic closed at $97.17 on Thursday, 17% above its $83 IPO price. Wall Street's major benchmarks closed Thursday deep in the red, with futures signaling more losses for Friday.

Investors had been counting on the debut to give the AI trade a lift after months of strain tied to worries about capital spending, according to CNBC. The sector is also grappling with the aftermath of Jensen Huang stepping down as Nvidia CEO, as well as worries that Washington's tightening of an export-control loophole could cut into AI chip shipments to Asia.

AMD stock ended Thursday more than 7% lower after Hunterbrook Media published a report claiming that AMD firmware had been found at companies on U.S. blacklists, and its affiliate Hunterbrook Capital disclosed a short position, according to CNBC. AMD told CNBC it complies with U.S. law.

Anthropic opened trading on the Nasdaq at $111.50 on Thursday under the ticker ANT, 34% above its $83 IPO price. The offering topped the $75 billion SpaceX raised at the start of its June IPO. Anthropic stock hit a high of $115.10 after the open and at one point fell below $100.

The House Foreign Affairs Committee on Thursday released export license information showing the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security approved more than 879,000 Nvidia and AMD AI chips for sale to China. The figures run through May. Huang, an Nvidia co-founder, resigned as CEO on Nov. 12.

According to CNBC, the committee's documents suggest that sales could cool if tighter export restrictions take hold.
