Iran War Strains Gulf Hotel Contracts, but Owners Aren’t Walking Away Yet

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Six months of weak trading has exposed how lopsided Gulf hotel management agreements are, with owners carrying the losses while operators keep collecting fees. For now, both sides would rather renegotiate than fight, but that patience depends on a recovery that hasn't come yet.

Gulf hotel owners have begun to reassess their hotel management agreements (HMAs) with operators, as the U.S.-Iran war that began in late February continues to weigh on the region’s hotels. So far, they are busy renegotiating terms rather than heading to arbitration.

Under an HMA, an owner pays a brand to run the property, typically through a base fee tied to revenue that gets paid even when the hotel loses money. This leaves owners carrying most of the downside when performance falls short.

And many hotels in the Gulf have been underperforming for more than six months. Dubai hotel occupancy, for example, fell to 68.4% in October, down 16.5 points from 84.9% a year earlier, according to CoStar’s STR. Average daily rate dropped 28.1% to $181 and RevPAR fell 42% to $124.

Gulf hotel owners are reopening their hotel management agreements with operators as the U.S.-Iran war that began in late February continues to depress regional demand. Dubai hotel occupancy fell to 68.4% in October, down 16.5 points year over year, with average daily rate down 28.1% to $181 and RevPAR down 42% to $124, according to CoStar's STR. International visitors to Dubai fell 38% to 9.7 million in the first 10 months of 2026, said Issam Kazim, CEO of the Dubai Corporation for Tourism and Commerce Marketing. Jennifer Eakins, legal director at Clyde & Co, said she wouldn't say there are more disputes than usual, but there is a reassessment of contractual obligations and risk allocation within HMAs, focused on performance tests, when management fees are paid, and how capital spending is handled. Under an HMA, the owner funds the asset and carries the loss while the operator collects fees — a split a June Cavendish Maxwell report called "particularly asymmetric." During Covid, these contracts were renegotiated rather than abandoned, with performance tests suspended and operators giving up fees while keeping their flags. Colliers expects the toughest HMA negotiating environment in a decade, with more franchise conversions, tougher performance tests, and shorter contract terms ahead.
