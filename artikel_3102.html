Is This 6%-Yielding Dividend Stock a Bargain or a Trap? Its Payout Eats Up About 90% of Cash Flow.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Here's the case for and against buying the high-yielding logistics stock.

By Matt DiLallo – Nov 21, 2026 at 7:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than United Parcel Service ›

UPS (UPS -2.65%) stands out for its dividend yield these days. At 6%, it's several times higher than the S&P 500's 1.1% yield. That wide divergence might have you wondering whether it's a trap or a bargain.

Here's a look at the case for and against investing in UPS stock right now.

UPS recently reported its third-quarter earnings, giving investors a glimpse into the safety of its dividend, which the company has maintained or increased every year since its 1999 IPO. The metric that matters most for dividend investors is its cash flow. UPS has generated $6 billion in cash from operations through the first nine months of this year. Meanwhile, its free cash flow, after accounting for payments made under its Driver Choice Program, was $4.2 billion. That was just barely enough to cover its dividend, which cost the company $4.1 billion.

UPS now expects to generate about $6.1 billion in free cash flow for the full year and anticipates paying $5.4 billion in dividends, subject to board approval. That would put its payout ratio at roughly 89%, which is high. If market conditions unexpectedly deteriorate, its dividend could be at risk of a reduction. On a positive note, the company has $6.8 billion in cash on its balance sheet, providing a bit more cushion.

UPS is in the middle of a strategic transition away from less profitable Amazon volumes toward higher-margin business, including healthcare logistics. The company is nearing the completion of its Amazon glide-down and the reconfiguration of its cost structure to align with these lower volumes. Its third-quarter report showed that this strategy is delivering results. Its revenue rose 6.7%, with growth across all segments. That gave the company the confidence to boost its full-year outlook, including for free cash flow.

The company is now on track to deliver $7.28 per share of adjusted earnings. At its recent $110 share price, UPS trades at about 15x earnings. That's a bargain compared to the broader market (the S&P 500 trades at over 23x earnings).

UPS wouldn't have to grow very fast to deliver strong total returns from here. With a 6% yield, the company could deliver double-digit total returns with around 4% annual earnings-per-share growth (assuming it maintained its current low valuation multiple). Meanwhile, if earnings grow faster and its valuation improves, UPS could deliver much stronger total returns, potentially in the mid-teens.

I lean toward bargain, but this isn't a dividend stock you can bank on just yet. I'm watching three things. First, revenue needs to continue rising. Second, I want to see next year's free cash flow guidance, since it needs to remain well above $5.4 billion to cover the dividend. Finally, I want to make sure debt doesn't creep up to fund the payout. If UPS continues executing its turnaround strategy and gets back on a sustainable growth trajectory, it's a bargain at the current price. However, if it faces a setback, the dividend could be at risk.
