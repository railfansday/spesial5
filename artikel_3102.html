Abercrombie & Fitch Grew Sales for the 16th Straight Quarter as Earnings Jumped 62%. Here's What's Behind the Streak.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The company effectively serves a key customer demographic.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 25, 2026 at 2:16PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Abercrombie & Fitch ›

Abercrombie & Fitch (ANF +5.27%) set a new record and extended an impressive winning streak in its third quarter. So it was little wonder that investors couldn't wait to pounce on the stock following the earnings announcement Tuesday morning. On the back of that demand, it closed the trading day nearly 20% higher in price.

While not every one of the quarter's many growth figures was as remarkable as it looked, there's no denying the power of the aforementioned streak. Read on for more about the clothing retailer's memorable third frame, and how it extended that already-long run.

The new all-time high Abercrombie set was third-quarter net sales, which in the 2026 version rocketed 11% higher year over year to $1.4 billion. It didn't hesitate to mention that this was no less than the 16th consecutive quarter of growth. This was powered by both of the company's leading brand families, its namesake Abercrombie & Fitch, whose sales rose by 12%, and Hollister, up 11%. Meanwhile, the company's overall comparable sales notched an impressive, though smaller, 6% gain.

Equity analysts had underestimated Abercrombie. On average, they were modeling net sales of $1.37 billion.

As for profitability, attributable net income climbed by a meaty 45% to over $164 million, or $3.82 per share. The company emphasized that the latter figure represented a 62% year-over-year increase. Yet some of this was due to a sharp reduction in share count; the weighted-average basic shares outstanding figure for the quarter was almost 42.3 million, compared with third-quarter 2025's 46.8 million-plus. That's because Abercrombie replenished its stock repurchase program last March, with its board authorizing a massive $1.3 billion for the purpose. Ever since, it has been steadily executing on that initiative.

Another factor to consider when examining the company's bottom line is the impact of tariff refunds. During the quarter, it was reimbursed by the federal government for duties it paid under a set of 2025 tariffs that were later ruled illegal by the Supreme Court. This added $0.40 to the company's per-share net income figure. Adjusting profitability by that amount reduces it to $3.42, bringing the year-over-year improvement down to "only" 45%.

That figure still crushes the consensus analyst projection of $3.09. In other words, while tariffs made a bit of difference to profitability, Abercrombie did smashingly well in the quarter regardless.

Abercrombie's fashion is, as ever, geared toward younger consumers. It blankets that demographic rather well, with the Abercrombie & Fitch brand group targeting older, theoretically more choosy and sophisticated customers, and the relatively casual Hollister lines aimed at their younger counterparts.

Both manage to stay in fashion and on the wish list of such people, buttressed by clever marketing. When a clothing company's wares are in vogue, it has less of a need to discount them (a common practice in the business). In its earnings release, Abercrombie also pointed to good discipline in its inventory management as a secret to its third-quarter success. Hence the double-digit improvements in both sales and profitability.

The timing of this is fortuitous, because the consumer discretionary sector is entering the eternally important holiday season. With its considerable growth momentum, the company felt confident enough to raise its annual guidance for the second quarter in a row.

It's now modeling sales growth of 6% over the 2025 result, up from the preceding prediction of 5%. EPS should come in at $13.70 to $13.90, encouragingly above the former range of $13.10 to $13.60. Management maintained its projection for the impact of tariff refunds; these should add 220 basis points to its operating margin. Guidance for yearly spend on those share-count-reducing stock buybacks was also kept level, at $500 million.

Sales growth streaks are hard to achieve and often harder to maintain. That goes double for the ever-fickle retail fashion industry. But if any company's going to keep one alive, it's Abercrombie, which always seems to have its finger on the pulse of what its youthful customers like to wear. I'd be a buyer of this stock, particularly considering its modest forward P/E of 12.5, even after Tuesday's monster pop.
