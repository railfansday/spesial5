Stock Market Today, Nov. 18: Opendoor Extends Rally for Second Straight Session as Investors Watch Housing

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Shares climbed for a second session as investors weigh housing conditions and the stock's upside potential, today, Nov. 18, 2026.

By Robert Izquierdo – Nov 18, 2026 at 5:24PM EST

Opendoor Technologies (OPEN +7.63%), a digital home buying and selling platform, closed at $1.27, up 7.63%. Shares rose as gains extended for a second straight session, and investors weighed housing conditions and a new deal with FSBO.com, one of the country's oldest real estate platforms dedicated to for-sale-by-owner listings.

Trading volume reached 50 million shares, coming in about 48% above its three-month average of 33.8 million shares. Opendoor Technologies IPO'd in 2020 and has fallen 88% since going public.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.23%) fell 0.23% to 7,324, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +0.42%) rose 0.42% to 24,430. Among residential real estate services and digital home transactions platform peers, Offerpad Solutions (OPAD -3.11%) closed at $1.56, down 3.11%, and Zillow Group (ZG -0.32%) closed at $28.45, down 0.32%.

On Wednesday, Nov. 18, Opendoor Technologies extended gains for a second straight session and moved further from its 52-week low of $1.09 as investors decided shares had been oversold. Moreover, on Monday, Nov. 16, a deal between FSBO.com and Opendoor was announced, boosting Wall Street's hopes for a turnaround and contributing to this week's share price gains.

Also on Monday, company director Dana Hamilton bought 200,000 Opendoor shares, according to a SEC filing reported the next day. The purchase adds a governance signal, but the market still needs operational improvement to sustain this week's stock price move.

On Nov. 5, Opendoor Technologies reported third-quarter results that brought fresh guidance and profitability updates. Although the company saw sales grow 12% year over year to $1 billion, its net losses expanded to $218 million, up from a $90 million loss in Q3 of 2025.

The stock has stayed sensitive to margin pressure and loss trends since that release, especially amid a macroeconomic backdrop of rising interest rates. As rates go up, this hurts Opendoor's capital-intensive home-buying model, which depends on cheap debt, fast home turnover, and healthy housing demand.
