Breakfast News: What NVDA’s CEO Exit Means for You

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Huang built a company with the products, people, and customers to outlast him, and we expect Nvidia to come through this.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's (NVDA -4.34%) chief executive amid controversies over his personal conduct and questions about whether Nvidia's chips reached China through a customer in Singapore.

His exit followed a week of reports about his personal life. The first concerned an affair with an executive at Megaspeed, one of Nvidia's customers in Singapore. U.S. officials suspected the executive of spying for China, which China denied. On Wednesday, a second relationship, with one of Huang's direct reports, came to light. The board met that night, and the company announced Huang's resignation the following morning.

We think investors are right to take Huang's departure seriously. In our view, though, the sell-off prices in more damage than the business has shown so far.

If you've followed Nvidia for any length of time, you know how central Huang has been. He co-founded the company in 1993 and led it for all 33 years. His leadership structure was also known for being unusually flat, as he had 33 direct reports at last count, according to CNBC.

Huang pushed Nvidia as an innovator and technologist. He launched CUDA (software that lets developers program Nvidia's chips) in 2006 and boldly committed the company to artificial intelligence (AI) computing long before it became a mainstream market. He also managed Nvidia's largest customer relationships himself and represented the company in Washington on export rules for China.

That is a lot for one person to hand off, let alone overnight, and Nvidia has no named successor to take it on. For now, CFO Colette Kress, who has worked alongside Huang since 2013 and has joined him on every earnings call, will serve as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement.

We understand why the market reacted so sharply. A company that leaned this heavily on one person's judgment now has to make its next big decisions without him, and under federal scrutiny. Analyst Dan Ives estimates Nvidia is worth 25% to 30% less without Huang, and some discount is warranted. With shares dropping to $148.80 in Thursday trading and closing at around $166, the market has already applied much of it.

What reassures us is that Huang's exit on its own is unlikely to change Nvidia's revenue much over the next two years. Demand for its chips is still growing quickly. Last quarter, revenue reached $96.2 billion, up 106% from a year earlier, and the company guided to $108 billion for the quarter that ended in October. By any measure, that's a business in good health.

Chips take years to design, so the products Nvidia will sell through 2027 were planned while Huang was still in charge, and customers have already ordered many of them. Even if regulators cut off sales routed through Singapore, we estimate Nvidia would lose no more than about 5% of its revenue.

A broader crackdown would cost more, and the investigations will help decide whether one comes.
