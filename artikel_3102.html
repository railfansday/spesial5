2 Ways Anthropic's IPO Pays Off for Alphabet Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A large equity stake and nearly guaranteed cloud sales make Anthropic's future success a boon for Alphabet.

By Chris Neiger – Nov 26, 2026 at 12:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Anthropic (ANT +1.08%) recently completed the largest initial public offering (IPO) in history, raising $81.3 billion -- beating the previous record set by SpaceX by $6.3 billion in June.

Many investors were no doubt pleased with Anthropic's public debut, but individual retail investors aren't the only ones to benefit. Alphabet (GOOGL -2.13%) (GOOG -2.02%) holds nearly 14% of Anthropic's shares, making it one of the largest shareholders in the artificial intelligence (AI) company.

What's more, Alphabet has significant cloud computing and processor deals with Anthropic, further entangling the two companies' futures. Here's how Alphabet and its shareholders benefit from the recent Anthropic IPO.

Starting in 2023, Alphabet made a series of investments in Anthropic over multiple funding rounds that eventually totalled about $33 billion. At the time of Anthropic's IPO, Alphabet held 13.7% of Anthropic's stock.

That investment was a very wise move, considering that Anthropic's shares were priced at $83 at the IPO, making Alphabet's position worth about $149 billion. Of course, as Anthropic's shares rise and fall, the value of Alphabet's stake shifts.

Holding Anthropic's shares boosts Alphabet's reported net income on paper each quarter (if Anthropic's returns go up), without generating actual cash. Alphabet could eventually sell some of its Anthropic shares to reinvest in the company, but it's likely to hold on to them, or at least the bulk of them, for a while.

If Alphabet holds onto its stake, it could add significant unrealized gains to its reported income. When it sells some of its shares, it will directly benefit shareholders by providing fresh capital for growth or buybacks. So, even though Alphabet and Anthropic compete with their AI models, Alphabet shareholders will still benefit as Anthropic grows.

Anthropic said in its S-1 filing before going public that it had about $121 billion in spending committed to Google between April 2026 and July 2033. The good news for Alphabet was that Anthropic was locked into that spending "regardless of actual usage."

What's more, Anthropic also buys Google's tensor processing units (TPUs) for AI processing. The AI company will reportedly spend $2.5 billion on Google's TPUs this year and up to $7.2 billion next year.

Altogether, Anthropic is estimated to spend up to $200 billion on Google Cloud and TPUs over the next five years.

With Anthropic being a core Google Cloud and TPU customer, its financial stability is a good thing for Alphabet. Now that Anthropic has raised more than $81 billion from its IPO, the deals between the companies are essentially de-risked because it can use the proceeds to pay for some of its commitments.

And Anthropic could expand its deals with Alphabet in the future, as demand for its services increase. Anthropic's third-quarter revenue rose 47% to $17 billion, indicating that the company is already on the right growth trajectory.

Alphabet is positioning itself well in the AI era as the company expands sales of its TPUs and grows its Google Cloud service. Morgan Stanley estimates that Google could generate $84 billion in graphics processing unit (GPU)-related sales next year alone. That would be an enormous opportunity for the company, considering that it only just began selling TPUs to customers earlier this year.

What's more, the company's Google Cloud revenue is rising fast and more than doubled in Q3 to $30.7 billion. The company ended the quarter with $561 billion in Google Cloud backlogs, 55% of which management says will be fulfilled over the next 24 months.

While Alphabet certainly benefits from its significant stake in Anthropic, I think the company's growth from Google Cloud and massive opportunity from selling TPUs make it a compelling AI stock on its own.

And given that Alphabet's shares trade at just 15 times the company's trailing earnings -- compared to the tech sector average of about 24 -- the company's stock looks like a good buy right now.
