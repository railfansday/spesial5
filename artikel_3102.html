Prediction: Nov. 18 Will Be a Big Day for Nvidia for 2 Reasons

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Turmoil shook Nvidia and its shareholders last week.

Nvidia (NVDA -1.39%), once a market sensation, has been the subject of turmoil in recent days. Chief executive officer Jensen Huang stepped down following allegations of two affairs, leaving investors with many questions about the company's next moves and overall strategy.

Prior to the current troubles, Nvidia delivered quarter after quarter of earnings growth, with revenue and profit reaching record levels in the billions of dollars. As a result, the stock soared too, climbing 1,100% over the past three calendar years.

Now, I predict that Nov. 18 will be a big day for Nvidia and its shareholders for two reasons -- let's check out the key events to watch this week.

First, though, a quick summary of the Nvidia story and current troubles. Nvidia has become almost a household name in recent years thanks to the company's dominance in the AI market. This tech giant sells the most powerful AI chips, called graphics processing units (GPUs), and has innovated on an annual basis to stay ahead of rivals. The company also expanded into a variety of related products and services and struck partnerships and signed deals with key players to broaden its reach.

All of these moves helped Nvidia construct an AI empire. Founder and chief, Jensen Huang, played a major role in this transformation of Nvidia from a seller of chips mainly to video gaming companies to a giant in the AI market. Huang spoke extensively about AI at events around the world and had strong visibility into where the technology was heading next -- this knowledge, along with his relationships with other tech leaders, made Huang a major figure in the AI space, and a leader capable of driving Nvidia's next wave of successes.

Last week, however, allegations of affairs arose; one involved a woman U.S. intelligence is calling a Chinese spy, and the other involved an Nvidia executive. Nvidia's board, which immediately launched an investigation into Huang's relationship with the former, then went on to meet to discuss Nvidia's future. Huang resigned soon afterward, and Nvidia named Colette Kress, who had been chief financial officer, as interim CEO as the search for a permanent leader is conducted.

Meanwhile, Nvidia's stock, which was up about 25% this year through the end of October, is now down 12%. After Huang's resignation, the shares fell about 4% in the trading session. The recent news clearly put pressure on the stock, but investors are also looking at the company's solid technology and customer relationships -- these elements haven't changed.

Now, let's consider the two events that I predict will make Nov. 18 a big day for Nvidia. On Nov. 17, after the closing bell, Nvidia reports third-quarter earnings, and the next morning, Colette Kress is holding a company meeting.

"You will hear more from leadership in the coming days about our future together, and we will host a company meeting on November 18 at 9 a.m." Pacific time, Kress wrote in a staff email obtained by CNBC.

The earnings report and call, as well as the company meeting, may offer investors clues about Nvidia's strategy in the months following Huang's departure. Though investors know Kress well from previous earnings calls, where she's played a big role, this week, she will be in a different position. Investors will get to know Kress as the interim leader of the company. It's important to note that Nvidia's search for a permanent CEO is considering internal and external candidates, so Kress may be on the list.

My prediction is that any significant messages about strategy, what's next for the company, or forecasts for revenue in the coming quarters could offer the stock direction in the following days. The stock's performance so far shows that some investors are worried, but this isn't a sharp and general sentiment; it's clear that other investors are waiting to learn more before making a move.
