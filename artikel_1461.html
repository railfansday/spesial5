Xabi Alonso hits out at ‘soft’ Chelsea players

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Xabi Alonso accused his Chelsea players of being “soft” after they failed to keep a clean sheet for the 20th successive Premier League game.

Chelsea threw away an early lead to draw 2-2 with Hull City and have conceded two or more goals in each of their four Premier League games.

Alonso has tried different systems and different players in those games, but suggested Chelsea’s biggest problem is mental.

The club’s dreadful clean-sheet record pre-dates him and is the first time in 65 years Chelsea have gone 20 league games without keeping the opposition out.

Hull’s first goal from Mohamed Belloumi came after the ball had bounced over Jorrel Hato and Alonso said: “We need to be aware that from any situation, any team in the Premier League can hurt you. Today it was from a long ball, two bounces, the way it happened. You need to be very aware, very ready that the danger can come from any situation. If you are soft or if you are not prepared, you will get punished and we have been punished that way.”

Chelsea have shared 30 goals with their opponents in the club’s six games in all competitions so far this season.

“Yeah, it’s all maybe entertaining, but I hope that one day you say it was a boring game and we win and we don’t give these entertaining moments,” he said.

“But, for sure, it kind of repeats some patterns that we are making. It will take time to... or it will take moments to fix them when we get the lead and you still need to be competing and not give the chance so easily for them to come back, especially in the first goal, we can do much better.

“It was too soft to give them this hope because until then we were controlling, we were creating chances. But that momentum that we had changed the mood and the score of the game, and it’s something that we need to be talking [about], we address and we will get better. But it’s having an impact on our performances at the moment.”

Asked if his players have a real desire to defend and keep clean sheets, Alonso said: “We can do better, we can do better for sure. We need to know that even when we have the lead, a very important part of the game is to hold together, to keep the distance, to go intense in the situations to defend. I’m sure that we can do better.

“Overall, I think that we are attacking better than defending in every part. We know that it’s a big topic and a big theme for us. For sure, it’s just four games. But the dynamic in terms of conceding, I don’t like it and we need to do much better. Open play, set-pieces, long balls, this toughness, this competitive sense, we can do better.”

Needing three goals or more to win games is not sustainable, as Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso is finding out.

This is not a problem of Alonso’s making, given Chelsea have now gone 20 Premier League games without a clean sheet. But he is certainly not improving the issue yet.

Chelsea beat Fulham with three goals and Brighton with four but left themselves far too much to do against Arsenal and were taken advantage of by Hull City, who will be slightly disappointed they did not leave Stamford Bridge with all three points.

Goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez has now conceded seven goals in three games since joining from Aston Villa, while central defender Maxence Lacroix, who joined for £52m from Crystal Palace in the summer, was relegated to the substitutes’ bench.

Martínez was at fault for Mohamed Belloumi’s second goal, but the Argentine did make a crucial save at his back post to stop substitute Mohamed-Ali Cho grabbing a winner that Chelsea could have had no complaints about.

Hull finished the game with more shots on goal than their hosts and a higher expected-goals rate. Chelsea are far too open and in some ways far too entertaining.

Defensive midfielder Moisés Caicedo cannot return from injury quickly enough, but this does not feel like a problem that will be solved by one man. Alonso needs to find a balance and instil a real desire in his team to defend.

Caicedo has that desire, but there are no defenders at the club who have shown that absolute desperation to keep the ball out of the net that Chelsea fans became so used to in the days of John Terry, Gary Cahill and César Azpilicueta.

None of this should diminish Hull’s performance. They were not only spirited and full of character but also showed quality and a threat. The joke is currently on those who laughed at their hopes of survival before a ball had been kicked.

It is now two wins and two draws for Sergej Jakirovic’s team and they are already a fifth of the way to 40 points. Jakirovic had promised his staff a week off in the international break if they reached eight points after five games. With that target already met, there may be a new reward if Hull can make it 11 points against Newcastle United next weekend.

“Before the season, I said to my staff that we will take seven, eight points in these first five and then I will give them seven days off,” Jakirovic said. “That was our target and now we are in a very good position, eight points.

“Especially today, it’s very, very difficult to play here, and now we will try to get even more. Of course, I’m a little bit worried about the international break because we have 13 national team players. But we will see who will come back and in what condition. So this is my concern right now.” Asked if his staff’s seven-day break will be extended if Hull can beat Newcastle, Jakirovic added: “I will tell them. But I know already.”

While his five-game target may need modifying, Jakirovic is not deviating from his goal for the season for Hull. “Our target is always to stay in the league and collect as many points as possible,” he said. “Forty points, 38 to 41, this is the average we need. Then it’s easier to play. You can play more relaxed, with more joy and happiness. So [get to] this as soon as possible, so that we are safe.”

Hull had not conceded a league goal before this and there had been predictions they might crumble when they finally let the ball into their net. They did so after just seven minutes but they kept their heads and came back stronger.

It was far too easy for Morgan Rogers to make a yard of space for himself to shoot after collecting the ball from João Pedro, and Hull goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis may not have been happy to have been beaten at his near post.
