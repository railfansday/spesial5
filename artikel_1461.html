Outstanding Leeds cut short Jaissle’s Newcastle honeymoon

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The noise started in a corner of the South Stand, early in the second half. It was muffled at first, but as the ball popped from one white shirt to another, it became louder and louder, sweeping around the stadium: here was the soundtrack for a humiliation.

“Olé! Olé! Olé!” they roared and why not? Leeds were ripping Newcastle to pieces, knocking passes around them as if they were training-ground mannequins. Football has become a sport that leaves people increasingly vexed, but this was a night when – for the locals, at least – it was almost a throwback.

There was energy and ferocious tackling, a No 9 soaring like centre-forwards used to in the not-too-distant past and lots of goals as Leeds cut loose and abruptly ended whatever honeymoon Matthias Jaissle had been experiencing in charge of Newcastle.

“I suppose in football you can always find something to moan about,” Daniel Farke, the Leeds manager, said. “But this was a top-class performance.”

It has felt suspiciously like Newcastle’s start has been like an overstuffed plastic shopping bag, the handles straining to the point of snapping. Eventually they fell asunder. Leeds ran all over them and were too smart, slick and hungry, not to mention ruthless. No wonder Jaissle had nowhere to go.

The home fans were adamant this was one of the best nights Leeds have experienced under Farke and if they were prepared to say as much, you had an obligation to listen. Forget how wretched Newcastle were, Leeds had produced a performance to enable imaginations to run wild.

Centre to it all was Ao Tanaka, with his desire to keep Leeds playing forward, but it felt wrong to talk about one man when you had the unstoppable Dominic Calvert-Lewin, who would be substituted to a standing ovation, and the ubiquitous James Justin, among others.

The foundations are being put in around the stadium for a cavernous new West Stand, and Farke’s remit is to ensure that Leeds will be firmly established in the Premier League by the time it is completed in the summer of 2028.

To be fair to Farke, he has laid a sturdy base for this team to thrive and it felt before kick-off that this was going to be a big evening for Leeds, who had made a quietly encouraging start to the campaign and had negotiated three challenging away trips without defeat.

Big? It turned out to be spectacular. The first half was so one-sided that a boxing referee would not have allowed Newcastle to come out for the second, such was the pummelling they received; wave after wave of white-shirted raids left the visitors black and blue.

Aside from one little rick inside the opening 10 minutes, when James Trafford dithered and presented Yoane Wissa with a chance to mug him, Leeds were every bit as sensational as Newcastle were shocking, seizing on the opportunity to go on a rampage.

Leeds would have been in front after 11 minutes but Ethan Ampadu’s fierce drive battered into Calvert-Lewin’s legs and bounced away. Normally in such circumstances, players will wave an arm to berate their bad luck, but Ampadu, celebrating his 26th birthday, smiled: he, like everyone else, sensed what was coming.

Jayden Bogle, Justin and Nico Elvedi all had opportunities to open the scoring earlier, but eventually, they found the weak spot. Newcastle, who had lost the influential Nico González to a potential concussion, had replaced him with Sean Steur and what an influence he would have on matters.

On the half hour, Steur was a fraction too late getting out to close down Tanaka, whose shot ricocheted off him and to Lewis Miley, before looping away from Lukas Hornicek and dropping into the corner of the net in dam-busting fashion.

Leeds were running even quicker by this point, pressing as if their lives depended on it and making tackles that were lustily acclaimed in the stands. Steur completely short-circuited in the bedlam and a ridiculous pass on the edge of his area was pounced upon by Bogle.

Out went a ball to Justin, whose cross was whipped into the penalty area, where Calvert-Lewin had climbed to get his head to it, though replays indicated his effort glanced off Bogle’s head – determining the true scorer will make for an interesting conversation.

Calvert-Lewin, at least, got the reward his efforts deserved on the cusp of half-time, spinning away from Miley and chopping inside Sven Botman before delivering the kind of finish that should make Thomas Tuchel take note when selecting his next England squad this week.

The gloss, after those “olés”, was applied in the 59th minute when Noah Okafor put a realistic hue on the score, ramming in a finish in front of the Kop and leaving Newcastle open to the crudest mocking for the amount of money they have spent to resemble such a disorganised rabble.

Bazoumana Touré, at least, scored a consolation goal, but Jaissle barely flickered as it went in and the emotion belonged to Farke, who thundered on to the pitch at the final whistle and sought out his captain, Ampadu, who had erred in allowing Newcastle’s substitute to profit.

“That was an unnecessary goal to concede and I so wanted us to keep a clean sheet,” Farke sighed. “We deserved a clean sheet and I couldn’t help myself. I had to tell him because I am sometimes a perfectionist. We must try to stay humble, but we can be an outstanding team.”

And this, for certain, was an outstanding night.

Tonight we faced that challenge. It’s tough and difficult to digest. But there is a chance to learn out of that and there’s a long time until our next match at home and that is good.

It’s about having an understanding with the players around you. With Dom up there, you know he’s going to win everything so you have to get the runners around him.

It’s about the rotation like today. When James Justin goes wider there’s space inside. Dom occupies two centre-backs so there’s always pockets.

Regardless of the scoreline it was a tough game physically. They’re a very good side. We take it game by game, not to get carried away. Everyone’s ambitious in their own way but we have to take it game by game.

There will be a lot of excitement surrounding Mathias Fernandez-Pardo’s first start for Newcastle United following his deadline day move from Lille but also keep an eye on Jacob Murphy on the right wing.

Fernandez-Pardo starts with a clean slate at Newcastle, Murphy has a lot of baggage and he has been a popular online target for criticism over the last year or so.

Indeed, some sections of the fanbase were stunned Newcastle rejected a £15m offer from Nottingham Forest on deadline day for a player who has entered the final 12 months of his contract.

Murphy has his limitations, sure and he can be infuriating sometimes when he gives the ball away cheaply, but there is a reason Matthias Jaissle was adamant he could not be sold.

Part of that is due to the fact he does not have another specialist right winger behind Anthony Elanga who misses the Leeds game through injury.

But Murphy is also valued for his experience and his training ground presence. He has been at the club a long time, working under Rafa Benítez, Steve Bruce and Eddie Howe and is a boyhood Newcastle fan.

Jaissle was not willing to lose that from his dressing room. Ultimately, though, Murphy has to perform on the pitch to justify the faith shown in him.

There are not many other options to fill that position while Elanga is missing. Harvey Barnes does not like playing on the right, Amar Dedic is a full back and so is Tino Livramento.

This is an opportunity for Murphy to silence his detractors and prove the faith Jaissle has put in him is not misplaced. At the age of 31, he knows he is not the most popular or appreciated player at the club but he has never shirked or complained about that.

He formed a potent partnership with Alexander Isak when he was leading the line for Newcastle. Can he do the same with the new boy Fernandez-Pardo?

Mind you I remember his visit to Elland Road in 1982-83. Not for the faint hearted. In fact it was a disgrace when he was hit by a coin. And yet he was always gracious about Leeds and particularly fond of Don Revie. He was one of very few football people without a strong Leeds United connection who went to his funeral in 1989.

Apparently he was injured in training, the nature of the injury is unspecified.

Eddie Howe has left a legacy and the culture he created is still around. Matthias has to work with it. They are unbeaten. they are very dangerous with many pacy players up front. It’s important not to lose the ball too easily and always be switched on against Newcastle.
