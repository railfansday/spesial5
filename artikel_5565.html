Three charged with using AI to impersonate executives and steal $129 million

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Prosecutors say the group posed as CEOs and law firm attorneys to trick employees into wiring money; extradition requests are pending

Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit are accused of using AI to impersonate corporate executives and attorneys and steal more than $129 million from companies worldwide, U.S. prosecutors said.

The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York unsealed an indictment against the three foreign nationals. Soall, 41, is from Marbella, Spain, and the U.K. Hazan, 54, is from Israel. Sheetrit, 42, is from Israel and the United Arab Emirates.

Soall was arrested in Spain on June 17, Hazan was arrested in Spain on Aug. 5, and Sheetrit was arrested in the U.K. on Sept. 7. Extradition requests for all three are pending, the office said.

Between 2024 and 2026, the defendants and their co-conspirators used AI to copy how top company officials sounded or looked, and did the same for lawyers from prominent law firms, according to the indictment. They posed as CEOs and asked employees to assist with a financial transaction that had to be kept confidential. They then referred the employees to lawyers from big multinational firms, and those lawyers were impersonated too. A person posing as one of those lawyers told the employee to send a big payment by wire to an account the conspiracy controlled.

After the transfer went through, the group changed the funds into cryptocurrency to hide where they came from, then divided them up among its members, the office said. The accounts were located in the United States, Spain, Portugal, Greece, the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, Austria, Slovakia and China.

Victims included a Missouri hospital system, which lost $4,829,580, and a Rhode Island bank, which lost $2,684,580. A California hospital system lost $2,968,497, and a U.K. financial technology company lost $11,578,474. A Massachusetts mutual fund company lost $4,639,918, and a Florida pet food company lost $5,242,747.

Soall mainly impersonated U.K. attorneys and kept a handwritten script titled "Lawyer Call," the office said. Hazan and Sheetrit laundered proceeds. Hazan bragged of laundering more than $400 million during the previous three years, according to the indictment.

Each defendant faces seven counts, covering a wire fraud conspiracy, substantive wire fraud, two aggravated identity theft counts, a money laundering conspiracy and two substantive money laundering counts. Each charge carries up to 20 years in prison, with the exception of aggravated identity theft, which has a ceiling of two years. Hazan was convicted in the Southern District of New York of conspiracy to commit bank fraud in 2008.

The office's release calls the technology super intelligence, a term that is the White House's preferred label for AI, according to NBC News.

"I encourage individuals and the business community to consider how to best protect themselves from the emerging threat of SI-enabled crime," U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald said in a statement.

FBI Assistant Director in Charge James C. Barnacle Jr. said in a statement that the FBI “remains committed to rapidly adapting our capabilities to identify, investigate, and disrupt those who seek to harm New Yorkers and businesses operating in our communities.”

The charges are accusations, and the defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty, the office said.
