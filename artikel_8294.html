The Most Beautiful State: On the CDT in Wyoming

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

We crossed the border and I already felt more relaxed. The trail became more manageable, and I began to enjoy hiking again. We began to get closer to the Basin.

The Great Divide Basin is a long stretch of nothing but sagebrush for over a hundred miles. No trees in sight and barely any water along the way. Before we started our trek through the desert, we only had a day or two before we reached Rawlins, which became a haven for a double zero. Rawlins was more affordable than Colorado, and had a Pizza Hut, which JC and I discussed often during long hiking days. From there we set out.

120 miles. There were almost no natural water sources, so we had to rely on water caches. We planned our campsites around them, and timed our hikes in the early morning, late evenings, with a siesta during the hottest time of the day. JC got creative with our tent a few times for our siestas. On one particularly hot day, he threw the tent over a ditch, and we got to rest in the shade. Another time, he set it up in a way where it provided shade, but the wind blew it down on top of me.

When we were in Rawlins, I picked up my prized possession: a cheap umbrella from Walmart. I was so happy to get a little bit of shade as I was hiking, though the wind blew it around a bit.

The heat was so intense, just as it was in the desert in New Mexico. We passed sagebrush and mysterious holes, one of which JC found a black widow spider. We witnessed beautiful sunrises while listening to the worst fan fiction audiobooks we could find. The terrain, luckily, was incredibly flat, so the heat and exposure and the 28-mile stretch of no water at all were the only things we needed to worry about.

The first storm came as we had just left a solar well oasis, so we were able to turn around quickly and put up our tent near some trees. The second storm came as we decided to camp on a higher hill to enjoy the sunset. We had to quickly pack up and find a slightly lower spot and put up the tent as dark clouds descended. We huddled in the tent and waited for the storm to pass. And kept waiting. I unzipped the tent and stuck my head out. The storm that had looked so threatening had disappeared.

The last storm came on our last night in the Basin. Our last few days in the Basin were idyllic, as the temperatures decreased a bit and the clouds came in for a more overcast couple of days. It was perfect. As we were looking for a spot to camp, it seemed like there were three different storms brewing on the horizon. Luckily, the way forward didn’t have any storms ahead, so we sped up into the dark. We rushed as fast as we could, and JC found a spot with trees and a ditch to camp for the night. The storm raged on, and we were in the safest place we could possibly be.

Our trek through the Great Divide Basin ended in South Pass City, a tiny town that used to be a bustling hub during the Gold Rush. There was a store with some old-fashioned candy, where we chugged some root beer and tried candy cigarettes.

From there, we got a ride to Lander for the night. This was where we entered grizzly country, and we needed to get bear spray and an AdoTech at the outfitter. From there, we entered my favorite section of the whole trail: the Wind River Range.

So far, both New Mexico and Colorado, while very beautiful, were arid, rocky and kind of empty, with a lot of wide open spaces. The Wind River Range was lush, full of wildflowers, lakes, and trees, with sweeping mountainous landscapes. It was stunning and I could have stayed there so long. It really made me fall in love with hiking again.

JC promised to do the low routes with me, with the exception of the Cirque of the Towers. I wasn’t too sure about it, as people were talking about the descent from Texas Pass. I figured I would try the first pass on the alternate and see how it would go, as there was an alternate that would take me back to the low route. We started the alternate and began heading toward Temple Pass, which was not the best marked or maintained trail. JC and I spent a lot of time trying to find our way on the trail, and as we began to climb the switchbacks, the trail gave way to a high, eroded section that was barely trail at all. After a steep, gravelly section that seemed to go straight down, I decided to get off the alternate on my own. I was nervous to do this in grizzly country, but it would only be for half of the day. After that pass, we explored some lakes and stuck our feet in the frigid water.

The next morning, we split our different ways for the next ten miles. JC hiked on and did Jackass Pass and Texas Pass, whereas I had a calm, flat hike, through wooded alpine lakes with misty mountains against the against the overcast sky. Looking back, I am so glad that I took this alternate back to the CDT. It was one of my favorite days on trail. In the afternoon, JC and I met up again, and he talked about being underwhelmed by the Cirque of the Towers.

We hiked the rest of the Wind River section together as the trail winded through canyons and lakes. It felt like a dream, and as we exited the Wind River Range, we noticed a stark lack of lakes and wildflowers. Knowing that we were now in grizzly country made us more jumpy, especially as there were more cows making bear-like noises. We hiked through a wooded section and diverted ten miles off trail to get to the road to Pinedale.

I was so excited for Pinedale. JC and I were interested in seeing the Tetons, but we didn’t have time to do the Teton Crest Trail, so we decided to get off trail, rent a car, and spend a couple of days in Grand Teton National Park. We camped just outside of the park, and JC and I had a wonderful day, seeing the sights and doing much milder hiking. We drove around and ate snacks on Jackson Lake. It was nice to just enjoy each other’s company and be in way less life-and-death situations.

When we returned to Pinedale, we ran into NoCo, who had to get off trail due to an injury, and Leaky and Temu, who we hadn’t seen since Colorado. We also met a French hiker, Elvis, who worked hard to convince JC and I to never do the Tour du Mont Blanc in France. We spent the night at the bar, giving NoCo one last sendoff before he got off trail.

Before we knew it, we were almost done with Wyoming. The last stop before Idaho and Montana was Yellowstone National Park. I have traveled a lot, but I had never seen anything like Yellowstone. There were so many geyser basins and geothermal features. Yellowstone is one of the busiest national parks, but there were some backcountry spots where it was JC and I and the boiling, rainbow-colored pools.

As we hit Day 3 in the park, JC got the news of a family emergency back home. We got off trail for a day to figure out our options. Would JC need to fly back to the UK? It became a waiting game, and time began ticking on JC’s visa as well.

JC and I decided to keep going, knowing we may have to get off any moment due to the ongoing emergency. But we weren’t going to be able to do the whole thing. He came to an idea: we would leave Yellowstone, rent a car, and drive to Butte, MT and continue on from there. It was a dream of both of ours to see Glacier National Park, and we wanted to see it before it was impassable with snow. We took an amazing road trip through Idaho, stopping at Swig for a dirty soda.

And then we arrived in Montana, the last state of the CDT.
