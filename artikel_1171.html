England 2-1 Croatia PLAYER RATINGS: Whose position is under 'serious' threat? Which two regulars need to show more? And who was at the heart of Three Lions' best attacking play?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Published: 17:06 EST, 12 November 2026 | Updated: 20:16 EST, 12 November 2026

Harry Kane scored on his record 126th appearance for England as they beat Croatia 2-1 at Wembley in the Nations League.

The Bayern Munich striker, 33, scored his 92nd goal for the Three Lions from the penalty spot to give them the victory.

Morgan Rogers had opened the scoring in the first half before Petar Sucic equalised.

Thomas Tuchel's side go to Spain on Sunday, where they will be in a shoot-out to qualify for the League A finals.

CRAIG HOPE was at Wembley to bring you our player ratings.

Harry Kane scored on his landmark 126th England outing to give them a 2-1 win over Croatia

Was left exposed for goal and not much he could do. Made four fairly routine saves and kicking was fine. Tuchel says he’s the No.1 but that will only become apparent should he start in Spain on Sunday. It feels like his position is under serious threat from James Trafford, not that he did anything wrong here.

A very bright start, including one delightful drop in behind for Rogers, but he faded thereafter. Not the creative force he should be in a game like this and questions remain over both Alexander-Arnold’s current level of performance and whether anyone will emerge as England’s first-choice right back.

He was enjoying a trouble-free night until mistake leading to Croatia equaliser with baffling decision to head back to Pickford amid crowded goalmouth. He did, in part, make amends by winning penalty for Kane goal, as well as timely late slide to block shot. Doesn’t convince as a starter, though.

Like Quansah, all was going well until Croatia goal, and he’ll be disappointed not to get the first header on the corner before ball dropped. He looked shaky after that and was nearly outdone for a Croatia equaliser late on. Could have done with a breather after 70 minutes or so given recent return from injury.

Trent Alexander-Arnold was not the creative force he should have been in a game like this

In a position that is up for grabs he did his chances no harm at all and was the pick of the backline by far. Got forward at every opportunity and rarely wasted a ball. Was also there at the other end with several key interceptions. A solid night.

A lot of huff and puff but, as too often in an England shirt, not enough to show for it by way of creation or penetration. With some bright young midfielders like James Garner and Alex Scott emerging, Rice has to do more, you feel, to make sure he stays in the XI. This was an underwhelming night in that regard.

Lots of involvement and omnipresent across the park, as ever. One superb dribble that drew applause, but we need to see more of that inspiration higher up the pitch. A good display, but no chances created. Partnership with Rice still feels a little forced at times.

Morgan Rogers was his team's brightest player when he forced the opening goal

Was already his team’s brightest player when he forced opening goal with close-range finish. Clearly a man in form and brought that brightness to this stage. With doubts over Saka’s durability, he looks the best bet as the starting right winger. Was at the heart of all good about England in final third.

Provided assist with confident cushion for pal Rogers and he was a threat throughout, even if final shot or pass didn’t quite come off, the assist apart. Felt like he was in second gear a little, especially knowing booking would have ruled him out of Spain on Sunday in his home city of Madrid.

Didn’t happen for Barcelona winger and he’ll know he should have done better when twice cutting in on right foot and having a good sight at goal. He missed the target on both occasions. Gordon can’t get cosy knowing he’s Tuchel’s first choice on the left, and more is needed.

The quarterback! Was this a glimpse of the future? Kane operated even deeper than he usually does at times and one long-range pass from 30 yards inside his own half drew gasps from the Wembley crowd. It was his playmaker’s dink in behind that led to opener. Got his goal with penalty dink on his big night.

A win, but it was not comfortable and Tuchel leaves this with yet more questions about how he builds a defence for the European Championship. A sold-out Wembley weren’t exactly thrilled by England’s attack, either. Job done, but it was dull.
