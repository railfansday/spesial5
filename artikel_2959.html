The Fed Is Raising Rates the Slow Way. History Says the Pace Matters More Than the Hike.

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Past tightening cycles offer clues about how stocks may fare from here.

Inflation is heating up, and the Federal Reserve is preparing its response.

The central bank already raised the federal funds rate a quarter point at its September meeting before holding steady in October. According to the fed funds futures market, there's a 100% chance that the Fed will hike at least another quarter-percentage point next month.

We know that stock prices usually react negatively to higher interest rates. But how the Fed chooses to change rates can have a big impact on how stocks react. If it can raise rates slowly and steadily, the potential downside might not be as severe.

Image source: Official Federal Reserve Photo.

A recent study by Charles Schwab looked at more than a dozen rate-hiking cycles since World War II. It categorized six of them as "slow" hiking cycles and eight of them as "fast."

Those returns would clearly fall into correction territory and don't paint a positive outlook for stocks. Right now, the S&P 500 is up about 1% since the September meeting but down about 3% from its high.

The fed funds futures market is currently expecting the fed funds rate to be in the 4.50%-4.75% range by the June 2027 meeting. That would put the current market in the "slow" rate hike cycle category.

In general, the markets prefer slow changes, not sudden ones. Sudden changes cause uncertainty, and the market hates uncertainty. Rapid increases in the fed funds rate, like the cycle we saw throughout 2022, leave investors guessing about how much the economic outlook will change. And when investors have questions about what happens next, they tend to turn defensive.

The one thing that could make 2026-2027 different is an end to the Iran war. Inflation is being driven by energy prices, and it would seem logical that prices would come down if the conflict came to an end. There's no sense yet that this could happen soon, but it's a catalyst that could quickly change the Fed's plans.

History shows that U.S. stocks could be in for a rough road ahead. But given that there's no guarantee how this plays out, staying the course might be the best option.
