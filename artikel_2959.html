Stock Market Today, Nov. 25: SpaceX Gets Historic FCC Approval for 1 Million Satellites. Is This Its Next Growth Engine?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

SpaceX has secured a landmark regulatory approval that could expand its business beyond rockets and satellite internet.

By RJ Fulton – Nov 25, 2026 at 4:45PM EST

Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%) surged into the spotlight on Wednesday, November 25, after securing landmark Federal Communications Commission (FCC) approval to deploy up to 1 million satellites designed to function as orbital data centers. The authorization represents a significant step toward expanding the company's business beyond rocket launches and satellite communications into artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure.

Shares of SpaceX climbed 1.30% to close at $127.44 as investors assessed the commercial potential of the ambitious project. Trading volume reached 145.6 million shares, approximately 50% above its three-month daily average of 97.2 million, reflecting heightened investor interest following the announcement.

Wall Street finished modestly lower on Wednesday, November 25, as continued weakness in technology stocks weighed on major indexes. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) declined 0.21% to close at 7,186, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.62%) fell 0.62% to 23,586, reflecting persistent uncertainty surrounding technology valuations and the broader economic outlook.

Aerospace and satellite communications stocks fared better despite the broader market downturn. Rocket Lab (RKLB +2.26%) gained 2.26% to $47.02, while Viasat (VSAT +0.91%) climbed 0.94% to $61.03.

The FCC's approval represents an important step toward expanding SpaceX's business beyond rocket launches and satellite communications. By combining its reusable rocket technology, satellite manufacturing capabilities, and Starlink infrastructure, SpaceX could establish a competitive advantage in orbital computing that would be difficult for traditional data center operators to replicate.

However, regulatory approval does not establish commercial viability. Building and maintaining orbital data centers would require substantial capital investment. Before assigning meaningful value to this opportunity, investors should look for concrete evidence of customer demand, competitive operating costs, and a credible path to profitability.
