Research moves closer to identifying CTE in living patients

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The ability to detect CTE only through autopsies prevents football players from knowing whether they have it during their careers. Work continues to change that.

Via ESPN, a pair of studies published by The Journal of the American Medical Association show that a new blood test could detect advanced stages of CTE in a living patient.

A study from Boston University and Banner Health was based on blood drawn from a small sample of former players during their lifetimes. Autopsies performed after they died showed that they had CTE. The researchers then found a marker for CTE based on the levels of a specific type of protein in their blood.

“I’m cautiously optimistic that we can really understand this finding and move it forward in the next five years or so,” Dr. Michael Alosco, one of the authors of the study, told ESPN. Alosco described the test as a “game changer.”

The study applies only to advanced stages of CTE.

“We are still in the discovery phase and it’s a small sample size,” Alosco said in a statement released regarding the study. “But if future studies validate these findings, this blood test will allow us to detect CTE with a high level of accuracy and open the doors to clinical trials and treatment.”

The ultimate goal remains developing a test that would show any degree of CTE in a living patient. It would allow football players to know whether they already have CTE, and possibly the extent of the disease.

Football players would first have to decide whether they want to know if they have CTE and, if they do, whether they will keep playing.

More broadly, football leagues at every level would have to decide whether players would be prevented from participating if they have CTE.

The development of a test to diagnose CTE could also lead to research aimed at identifying potential treatments for it.

Where the work leads will be determined over time. And the knowledge acquired by football players will allow for informed — if not complicated — decisions as to whether they will choose to keep playing, and whether they will be medically cleared to do so.
