Colts' mismanagement continues with release of QB Daniel Jones

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Colts arguably mishandled the Jonathan Taylor situation, when it comes to the possibility of trading him. As to the possibility of trading Daniel Jones, there’s no argument to be made.

He has played two games since the deadline came and went, and now he’s been cut. They should have seen it coming.

They cut him to avoid an injury that would have triggered a $10.51 million guarantee in 2027. A trade would have avoided that guarantee. And they would have gotten something.

Now? They get nothing. They’ll pay him $50 million for 11 games and have three wins to show for it.

The Vikings were interested, and they were talking to the Colts. While Minnesota wanted Kirk Cousins, the Colts could have lowered the price to a point that the Vikings couldn’t have refused.

Then again, the Vikings may have used the possibility of pivoting to Jones to get the Raiders down to a third-round pick for Cousins.

Regardless, the Colts blew it. It’s no surprise. They have no General Manager. Which makes it hard to generally manage the decisions that come with the opportunities to trade players.

Our guess is that the trades of guard Quenton Nelson and defensive tackle DeForest Buckner created the perception that the Colts were having a fire sale. Which resulted in lower offers for players like Taylor and Jones.

In the end, they traded neither. Now, Jones is gone. Taylor possibly will ask to be traded after the season, or they’ll decide to do it again on their own. Chances are that, when they do, they’ll get less than they could have gotten if they’d traded him at the deadline.
