Saylor’s Strategy Keeps Buying Bitcoin, Adding 'A Little Orange' Through the Dip

Strategy kept buying Bitcoin through a brutal market stretch, adding 485 BTC worth roughly $33.4 million and repurchasing $228 million of its preferred stock, as chairman Michael Saylor urged the market to rally with a characteristically cheeky nudge.

The company disclosed the purchase in a filing Monday. The modest addition, small by the standards of a firm that once bought tens of thousands of coins at a time, lifted Strategy’s total holdings to 850,849 BTC as of Nov. 15, alongside $4.9 billion in U.S. dollar assets.

Strategy, the largest corporate holder of Bitcoin, remains far ahead of any other treasury company.

The buy caps a stretch of smaller, steadier accumulation paired with stock buybacks. Strategy recently added $82 million in Bitcoin while repurchasing $188 million of its STRC preferred shares, part of a shift toward what it calls a capital-management approach, buying back stock when it views the shares as undervalued rather than relentlessly stacking coins.

Bitcoin has slid sharply in recent days, trading well below the six-figure highs it touched earlier in the cycle, and the downturn has squeezed Bitcoin treasury firms whose valuations rise and fall with the asset.

Saylor, undeterred, posted the holdings update to X alongside the firm’s trademark Bitcoin tracker, and followed it with a quip aimed at the slumping market: “This dip could use a little orange,” a nod to Bitcoin’s signature color.

The purchase lands during a precarious moment for the treasury-company model Strategy pioneered. Index provider MSCI has finalized rules that could eject Bitcoin treasury firms like Strategy from its widely tracked equity indexes, classifying companies whose balance sheets are dominated by a single asset as “non-operating” and therefore ineligible.

Removal would force index-tracking funds to sell the affected shares, potentially pressuring prices and shrinking the premium these firms have long commanded over the value of their Bitcoin.

For now, Strategy is doing what it has always done, buying Bitcoin and talking it up, even as the market tests its conviction.

The combination of a small purchase and a sizable buyback underscores the balancing act: keep accumulating, defend the stock, and weather a downturn that has turned the once-unstoppable treasury trade into a far more fraught proposition.
