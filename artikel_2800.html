Prime Joel Embiid is a thing of the past, and that’s OK for the Sixers

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

For a grand majority of Joel Embiid's tenure in Philadelphia, the Sixers have only gone as far as he dragged them. His on/off splits are the stuff of legend, particularly in the playoffs.

But as his injuries piled up in recent years, the Sixers began the process of pivoting away from him as their focal point. Tyrese Maxey, whom Embiid nicknamed "The Franchise" a few years ago, started to live up to that promise following the departure of James Harden.

The additions of LeBron James and Jaylen Brown this offseason led to an onslaught of questions about "alphas" and whether the Sixers could share the ball well enough to keep all of their stars happy. One 14-4 start later, their vibes are immaculate for now.

However, one rival scout doesn't believe it'll last.

"I think it's going to be a problem, and that could railroad them," the scout told ESPN's Tim MacMahon in reference to Embiid taking more of a backseat on offense this season. "I just don't buy it right now. He's going to want the ball more. He's going to get pissed. He's going to stop playing defense. He's going to stop running."

Thus far, the exact opposite has played out.

Through his first 14 games this season, Embiid is averaging only 20.4 per outing while shooting a career-worst 40.0 percent overall and 14.3 percent from deep. That hasn't caused him to check out on defense, though. Instead, he's averaging 8.9 rebounds per game—his most since the 2023-24 campaign—and he's looking more spry defensively than he has in years.

After the Sixers' season-opening win over the New York Knicks, Embiid told reporters that he planned to vie for a spot on the All-Defense teams like he did earlier in his career because "that changes everything for our team."

That is the exact right mentality for Embiid to have this season.

For years, he's quietly bemoaned the lack of offensive support that he's had around him. Yes, the likes of Harden, Paul George, Jimmy Butler and Ben Simmons have all cycled through Philadelphia over the past decade, but more often than not, the Sixers were drawing dead without a big game from the big fella. This year is the first time where that isn't the case on a night-to-night basis.

Look no further than their NBA Cup win over the reigning-Cup-champion Knicks. Embiid finished with 10 points on 3-of-12 shooting. Prior to that game, he had finished with 10 or fewer points only 15 times across his career, many of which he left early because of injuries. (Somewhat surprisingly, the Sixers went 7-8 in those games.)

Despite his cold-shooting night, Embiid found other ways to make an impact against the Knicks. He finished with seven rebounds, seven assists (trailing only LeBron James' eight dimes on the night), one steal and one block in 33 minutes. He was a plus-eight on the game, which was tied with LeBron for the best mark among the Sixers' starters.

Embiid already had a triple-double in the third game of the season, and he nearly notched another one against the Milwaukee Bucks on Saturday. Instead, he wound up settling for a 26-17-9 night. Ho hum.

Heading into the season, it was fair to wonder whether Embiid would be willing to take a step back on offense to focus on traditional big-man tasks such as rebounding, rim protection and screening. It was also unclear whether he still had the lateral agility to switch out on the perimeter without getting roasted alive. If not, would the Sixers be forced to keep him in drop coverage? What other tactical adjustments would they have to make to account for that?

It's still far too early in the season to take a full victory lap, but so far, so good on that front. Better yet, we're still a ways away from seeing this group's ceiling on offense as they continue to work on developing chemistry with one another.

"I think we've got a long way to go offensively," Embiid told reporters after the Sixers' win over the Chicago Bulls in early November. "A lot of it is efficiency, starting with me. A lot of guys have picked up, so it's up to me to kind of pick it up."

Embiid added that the Sixers were "on the right path" because they have the "right mentality, making plays for each other." He followed up with some revealing comments about why he might be struggling with his efficiency in the early going.

"For me, it's a little bit of an adjustment," he said. "I'm such a rhythm guy. Over the years, obviously I've had a lot of opportunities to just touch the ball almost every other possession. Sometimes you can just go four or five possessions where you don't touch the ball. That's where it's kind of like an adjustment. I'm not used to catch-and-shoot as much, I'm used to more creating for myself. So I'm working on that."

But lest that anonymous scout who spoke with MacMahon feel the need to start crowing about his prophecy, Embiid quickly shot down the notion that he'd allow his own offensive struggles to impact the rest of his game.

"My mindset is really whenever I have the ball, make plays," he said. "Make the game easy for all these guys. Making the right plays over and over. Setting screens. Get Tyrese as open as much as possible. Bron. All these guys. Just kind of being the hub and trying to get these guys open and make plays for them. That's really what the focus is on.

"And then on the other side, also, working on myself. How can I get back to that level of dominance without having as much touches as I'm used to? That's a work in progress. That's where really the struggle has been because, like I said, I'm a rhythm guy. I need to feel the ball. I need to touch the ball. When you don't, it's a little different. But it's a fun process, and I'm enjoying it, trying to find ways to get over the hump. But I feel like once I do and I figure it out and I can really be dominant, then the sky's the limit for us."

The version of Embiid who averages 30-plus points per game might be gone for good, particularly with this compilation of talent around him. That's completely OK. In fact, that's the point of assembling this level of talent around him.

There will still be some nights where Embiid has it going and the Sixers need him to bully an overmatched big man in the post and/or draw a parade of trips to the free-throw line. Against the Detroit Pistons in early November, he dominated poor BBall Paul and finished with 26 points and a season-high 15 rebounds. As Liberty Ballers' own Paul Hudrick noted, he hadn't pulled down that many boards in regulation of a regular-season game in almost three years.

The Sixers will need Embiid's threes to start dropping at some point just to keep defenses honest. But they no longer need him to be the MVP version of himself on a night-in, night-out basis. Maxey, LeBron, Jaylen Brown and VJ Edgecombe are here to shoulder some of that offensive burden, not to mention snipers like Anfernee Simons and Kentavious Caldwell-Pope off the bench.

Individually, Embiid has already done everything that he could have dreamed of accomplishing in the NBA. An MVP award, five All-NBA nods and seven All-Star selections later, the only thing left to do is win it all.

As long as he remains committed to focusing on the role that the Sixers need him to play this year, he'll have his best shot yet of doing so.
