Improvement in this key area taken Wembanyama to the next level

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

No one can stop Victor Wembanyama when he aims for the heart. The whole league should be on notice because he has more lobs than each team and another leap is happening in real time. A good chunk of it has to do with his screening.

Former NBA player and Harvard man, Jeremy Lin, highlighted Wembanyama's screening on his Instagram page, which all San Antonio Spurs fans should check out. He touched on four distinct screens — the 45° angle, flat-ball screen in transition, traditional and the 45° angle with his back facing the backcourt line.

Setting picks obviously gets teammates open, but doing it correctly and quickly enough also gets the screener in great position to make a play. Wembanyama's wide base is a significant advantage in this area and so is his catch radius as it is often out of reach of defenders.

The 45° angle is nice because it starts at the blind side and it blocks the defender's inside leg from moving cleanly; the flat-ball screen in transition gets helped by a roll and that movement helps set the space for the pick; Wemby's traditional screen is more effective than most due to his size; and the hardest of these to guard is the 45° angle with his back facing mid-court. As Lin explained, this is done close to the baseline, limiting the on-ball defenders ability to go underneath. Yet, in fairness to the defenders, what are they supposed to do when they run up against a mountain of an offensive lineman? This play is a cheat code that can only be neutralized at the expense of the 3-point line — and he might still go over the top.

This season, Wembanyama has upped his percentage of shots at the rim to 36 percent from 32.8 percent last year. He converts 80.3 percent of the shots he takes there and is on pace to record the most dunks of his career. Stephon Castle has been the playmaker with the best connection with him this season, averaging the most passes and the highest percentage leading to shots from those passes.

Castle also deserves some recognition in the dunking area, because of all the registered dunks in the NBA this season, the five highest vertical leaps recorded were his, between 38.3 and 41.5 inches. Wemby, on the other hand, does not need to get off the ground that much and has even recorded one this year, lifting off 11.6 inches from the ground.

Furthermore, Wemby deserves props because on top of being ultra efficient, he has done it in an exciting way. Alley-oops are arguably the most entertaining plays in basketball, so it's a privilege to see a team and specifically one player finish them in high volume. One would probably need to ask the NBA's communications office for help tracking which player has finished the most lobs to himself, but I'm sure it's him, too. He's done some of those as he improvised, making them more special.

Screens, like rebounds, are winning plays that make life for everyone easier. Now that Wembanyama has gotten great at using them for himself, the next area is using more of those to free up his teammates with some off-ball screens. He's lower than he should be in screen-assists created (1.8) and can easily pick this area up if he's told to. It would help create mismatches which could lead to more lobs.

He's the league leader in points in the paint (270 total), and he has done all of this without sacrificing his style of being an outside player, as he is averaging the most 3-point attempts of his career (6.5). He's not quite doing it all yet, since his efficiency is low from behind ghe arc, but he is close to getting there.
