Chelsea in 10-goal thriller as ‘Alonso ball’ brings risk and reward

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Chelsea’s first match under the management of Xabi Alonso was a 10-goal thriller in Australia, as the Premier League side eventually showed their class in a 6-4 win over Western Sydney Wanderers.

Star striker João Pedro, who was controversially left out of Brazil’s World Cup squad, scored a nine-minute hat-trick after coming off the bench with half an hour of the friendly remaining.

Alonso had selected a youthful team for the first hour before bringing nine senior players, including João Pedro, into the side in the second half. According to Transfermarkt, Chelsea’s squad is valued at over £1.3bn while their opponents a more modest £5.86m.

Teenage signing Dastan Satpayev, midfielder Dario Essugo and winger Jamie Gittens also found the net for Chelsea, who were defensively vulnerable for long spells for the game.

Here are five things Telegraph Sport learned from Alonso’s first match.

João Pedro scored 13 goals in the second half of last season and his impressive performances resulted in the 24-year-old being named Chelsea’s player of the campaign. It was something of a surprise, then, when he was left out of Carlo Ancelotti’s Brazil squad this summer.

The good news for Chelsea is that João Pedro’s absence from the World Cup has given him plenty of time to recover ahead of the new campaign, and he looked thrillingly sharp in his second-half performance against Western Sydney Wanderers. The difference in class between João Pedro and Liam Delap, the man he replaced, was clear for all to see.

Two of the Brazilian’s three goals were classy, composed finishes — and he also created a goal for Gittens after surging past three defenders on the left wing. João Pedro looks like he means business.

As expected, Alonso tasked his players with deploying a possession-based strategy in which the ball is worked from back to front between the lines. At times, Alonso’s vision was executed in style: Chelsea’s two goals in the first half were both the consequence of slick passing moves.

There were plenty of other occasions, though, when Chelsea were sloppy in their passing and defensively exposed when they lost the ball. The best example of this was in the second half, when Estêvão’s error allowed Western Sydney Wanderers to score their fourth goal of the game.

Admittedly, Chelsea’s attempts to play passing football were not helped by the poor quality of the pitch in Sydney. A recent rugby game had created a bobbly, unpredictable surface which caused no end of problems for Chelsea’s midfield players. That may sound like an excuse for their sloppiness, but it is probably a fair one.

Chelsea started the game with two Reggies in their midfield. Reggie Walsh is a 17-year-old technician who last year became the club’s third-youngest player, and was loved by former head coach Enzo Maresca. He played as the No 10.

Reggie Watson, meanwhile, is the son of former Premier League midfielder Ben and is one of the most exciting talents in English football. A composed central midfielder, Watson is a regular for England’s youth teams and is the youngest player on Chelsea’s pre-season tour.

Both players showed glimpses of their talent in this match in Sydney. Watson was trusted with set-piece duties and produced some excellent deliveries, while also playing some lovely passes through the lines. Watson was the only Chelsea player to feature for the full 90 minutes, which spoke volumes of the quality of his performance.

Walsh was less involved but caught the eye in a couple of moments, not least when playing a delicate one-two with Essugo in advance of the midfielder’s first-half strike.

Satpayev is technically not a Chelsea player yet, as his deal officially commences when he turns 18 in August. His former club, Kairat, gave special dispensation to allow the teenager to join Chelsea’s pre-season tour ahead of the completion of his £3m transfer, which means he is officially in Australia as a “triallist”.

Satpayev is a player of obvious talent and has already played nine games for Kazakhstan. Before this move to Chelsea, he scored 21 goals in 41 games in the Kazakhstan Premier League.

The first sighting of Satpayev was an exciting one for Chelsea supporters, who saw the new man’s running speed and shooting power as he scored the opening goal of the game. Running in behind the Western Sydney Wanderers defence, Satpayev surged clear of the opposition and launched a scorching shot into the roof of the net.

Gittens endured a hugely disappointing first campaign at Chelsea last season, following his £52m move from Borussia Dortmund. Injury issues did not help his cause but he struggled to make much of an impact when he was fully fit, scoring just one goal in 27 appearances in all competitions.

Gittens is therefore a man with it all to prove at Stamford Bridge this season, and he has started the new campaign in promising form. His goal here came after two strikes in two behind-closed-doors friendly games before Chelsea left for Australia. Gittens was a constant threat after coming onto the pitch in the 63rd minute, showing his pace on the wing and his ability to go down the touchline or cut inside. His challenge now is to replicate this level of performance against better opposition.

Chelsea started their pre-season with a 3-0 victory over Bromley in a behind-closed-doors friendly, but this morning we will have our first proper look at Xabi Alonso’s team as they take on Western Sydney Wanderers.

There was some interesting news from Stamford Bridge on Monday when it emerged the club are in talks to sign Danny Welbeck and Jordan Henderson. Coming after the fruitless pursuit of Granit Xhaka, the strategy suggests that Alonso has made cultural improvement a priority in a dressing room that has been accused of lacking discipline and leadership.

As ever, Chelsea have been busy in the transfer market with Morgan Rogers and Marco Palestra their headline signings, and Geovany Quenda joins up with the club from Sporting CP having been signed in March 2025.

Rogers is yet to join up with his new team-mates following his World Cup exertions with England, but fans will be looking forward to seeing how he combines with Cole Palmer.

“In that position, we needed an important player, and I’m sure that there were not many more better options than Morgan,” the 44-year-old manager told reporters. “It means a lot for everyone.”
