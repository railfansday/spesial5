The Flu Shots Are Here! The Flu Shots Are Here!

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Every year, we wait on the arrival of this season's annual flu vaccine, patiently biding our time and explaining to our patients that it's coming, any day now, while the manufacturers finish their processes of selecting the candidate strains, making the vaccine, packaging it up, distributing it, and getting it into our hands at the practice through a complex system of logistics.

Every year, it seems to arrive later and later, and often in shorter supplies, than we would hope.

Many of our patient visits in late summer include specific questions: when is the flu vaccine going to be here, when can I get mine?

We usually tell them we're hoping for September but it might be October, but we've had some even later years for delivery, we're hoping to send out an announcement through the patient portal, so as a patient, please be patient.

Sometimes folks can't wait, they are really anxious to get that protection; the minute their pharmacy announces they have it in stock, they go on in and get it.

We're often deluged with messages: please update my chart to show I got the flu vaccine on September 3, at my workplace. And usually within a few days the statewide registry will catch up and dump this into their vaccine immunization history in our electronic medical record.

There always are lots of patients who don't want a flu vaccine, who've never gotten one and never will. No matter how much we try to address their hesitancy and answer their questions, some have a real reason -- some have fears that have grown from social media or friends and family, and some have had a prior severe reaction or side effect. Guillain-Barré. Optic neuritis. Anaphylaxis.

Try as we might, we never get everybody vaccinated, but I'm incredibly grateful that we do such a good job of getting it to everyone we can before flu season is really barreling down in full force.

When I first started out in practice, I never really bothered getting the flu shot.

While I'd had the flu a few times in my life (once a miserable few days spent in bed feeling like death-warmed over), I always figured it wouldn't be that bad and I've always been healthy and I didn't really need the vaccine.

As I matured into my practice, I became more convinced by the arguments of my colleagues and our infectious disease friends and epidemiologists and public health experts, showing that I was actually putting patients at risk, including during that subclinical phase when I would be carrying and shedding the virus and potentially spreading it to my patients, who could fare far worse with a case of influenza than I might.

Now that I've matured even further (and by "matured" I mean "gotten older"), I suddenly find myself qualifying for the "senior flu shot," a milestone I once looked at as being far off in the distance, now suddenly upon me.

Since we got the flu vaccine in our practice this season, it has been incredibly busy, and I haven't had time to get mine. I keep making excuses: I'm going out to dinner or playing squash tonight, big presentation in the morning, I'll get it later.

Suddenly this week, I realized the deadline for all of us at our institution getting vaccinated was upon us, and it was time to get my jab.

In the old days, one of my partners used to give me the flu shot, and I would give him his, but now we want to make sure it gets accurately registered in the system so that Workforce Health and Safety can close our files, mark us as compliant with our mandatory annual influenza vaccination.

So, I dutifully scheduled an appointment at a local pharmacy, and stopped in to check off that box.

Overall, everything went smoothly. One of the folks working behind the pharmacy counter checked me in after confirming my name and date of birth, and I was the only one waiting so the nurse called me right in.

She went through the same process of confirming my date of birth, asked which arm I wanted it in (my right deltoid, since I'm left-handed), a quick pinch, and I was done.

Think how amazing this is: I'm able to get this done, to protect myself and my patients and my family and my friends and community from what could be a potentially deadly illness.

Science and public health and our amazing healthcare system have so much to offer to keep people healthy, to get them healthy, to prevent and treat and cure.

I know some people will read this and scoff, have their arguments about why vaccines are terrible, say we've all been deluded by some secret cabal of people trying to make money and causing some disease or changing my DNA, but for the most part it would be wonderful if everyone would just believe us, let us do right by providing the best care with the best evidence.

Someone can say no, but we should never deny any group of people, any community, any individual, the opportunity to seek the best healthcare they can get.

I'm hopeful as we've seen the results of the midterm elections last week that opportunities to improve healthcare, to bring calmer and saner voices to the table, will revitalize and transform our healthcare system, have it live up to the potential it holds, and take us down a series of brilliant pathways that will allow us to serve our patients as best we can.
