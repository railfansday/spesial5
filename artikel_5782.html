Google signs 50-MW superhot geothermal PPA with Quaise Energy

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Let Utility Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The agreement will support Google’s Oregon data center operations beginning as early as 2030, advancing development of a technology designed to access geothermal resources deeper underground.

Google's agreement with Quaise gives superhot geothermal an important commercial partner as developers seek to move enhanced geothermal systems from demonstration projects to utility-scale deployment.

“Geothermal energy remains largely underutilized. It supplies less than 0.5% of global electricity and is limited to just a handful of states in the U.S.,” Diane Hughes, senior vice president of marketing and communications at Quaise, said in an email.

Unlike conventional geothermal plants, which depend on naturally occurring underground hot-water reservoirs that are geographically constrained and rarely exceed 400 degrees Fahrenheit, enhanced geothermal uses engineered reservoirs to access heat in deeper rock formations.

Quaise is developing millimeter-wave drilling designed to ablate rock without mechanical contact. The company is designing wells to reach depths of up to 65,000 feet, where rock temperatures can exceed 932 degrees Fahrenheit and conventional drill bits cannot withstand the heat, according to Hughes.

“Superhot wells like these can produce up to 10x more power per well than what's currently in play and bring geothermal to places where it isn't viable or economical today," she said.

The agreement comes as technology companies increasingly seek firm power sources to meet data center demand. Google has signed nearly 35 GW of clean energy purchase agreements globally since 2010, including previous investments in next-generation geothermal, according to its most recent environmental report. Google says its three Oregon data center campuses are sourced with 83% carbon-free energy.

Google is also pursuing other emerging technologies, including advanced nuclear power and long-duration energy storage, as part of its efforts to achieve net-zero emissions.

Quaise said in its 2025 year-end update that it had drilled 118 meters through granite at its central Texas test site and is now working toward a depth of 1 kilometer.
