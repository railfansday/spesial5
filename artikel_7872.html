Fall Color in Nancy’s Massachusetts Garden

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Hi GPOD! Today we’re heading to Hingham, Massachusetts, for some beautiful fall color from Nancy. Nancy sent in photos of the trees and shrubs winding down the season in her garden, along with one of the last flowers still hanging on.

This is our sugar maple (Acer saccharum, Zones 3–8) showing off its fall foliage before it drops its leaves to keep my gardens warm this winter.

My ‘Forest Pansy’ eastern redbud (Cercis canadensis ‘Forest Pansy’, Zones 5–9) is maroon most of the year until fall, when it turns a lovely yellow.

My paperbark maple (Acer griseum, Zones 4–8) has had a hard time coping with our summer droughts and water restrictions, but it looked a lovely red this fall.

This is another native tree that many people are not familiar with, fringe tree (Chionanthus virginicus, Zones 4–9), which has fragrant white flowers hanging from its branches in late spring to early summer. As you can see, it also looks beautiful in its yellow fall foliage.

In the front of this picture is a young sweetgum (Liquidambar styraciflua, Zones 5–9), which has spectacular red color in the fall and develops spiky seed balls as it matures. In the distance is a blackhaw viburnum (Viburnum prunifolium, Zones 3–9) showing its orange fall color.

‘Mount Airy’ fothergilla (Fothergilla ‘Mount Airy’, Zones 5–8) glows red and orange in front of fragrant abelia (Abelia mosanensis, Zones 4–8), whose fall color is beginning to fade. The abelia will charm us all again in spring with its fragrant blooms.

Sweetshrub (Calycanthus floridus, Zones 5–9) on the right has turned a lovely yellow. Amazingly, all three sweetshrubs shown here had the bark eaten off around every trunk by rabbits last winter, killing every one. They all grew back from the roots, showing just how resilient they are. On the left, ‘Mount Airy’ fothergilla is showing off its red fall foliage, and in back is ‘Alice’ oakleaf hydrangea (Hydrangea quercifolia ‘Alice’, Zones 5–9) with maroon fall foliage.

One of the only flowers left blooming is ‘Sheffield Pink’ chrysanthemum (Chrysanthemum ‘Sheffield Pink’, Zones 5–9), which complements the dark foliage of Diabolo ninebark (Physocarpus opulifolius ‘Monlo’, Zones 3–7) behind it.

Thanks for sharing your fall garden with us, Nancy! Nancy says she’s ready to wander the garden, make her list of what needs dividing in spring, and figure out what new plants need to be bought. Sounds like a good way to spend the off-season to me!
