Jennifer Svedberg-Yen, lead writer of the hit 2025 RPG Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, has revealed that she is no longer with developer Sandfall Interactive. In an interview with Gamesbeat, Svedberg-Yen said she wants to return to her own creative efforts, including stories for film and television, and also aims to set up a new fund specifically to support the advancement of narrative design in games.

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 was universally acclaimed and won an astounding list of awards, including just about every narrative-focused prize you can name: Best Storytelling at the Golden Joysticks, Best Narrative at The Game Awards, Outstanding Achievement in Story at DICE, Best Narrative at the GDC Awards, Best Narrative at the BAFTAs, and Hugo and Nebula awards. That makes it particularly interesting that Clair Obscur is Svedberg-Yen's one and only game credit: Prior to joining Sandfall Interactive in 2020 as one of its earliest members, she spent years working in the finance industry.

Despite Clair Obscur's success, Svedberg-Yen revealed that she moved on from the game, and the studio, earlier this year. "I left Sandfall in July, after wrapping up some final stuff for Expedition," she said. "We’ve parted ways."

That has to hurt for Sandfall, but Svedberg-Yen still has ambitions within the game industry, including the creation of an investment fund for "narrative-focused game creators, trying to bridge the gap between the seed stage and going to publishers."

"This is the chicken and egg question a lot of people face," Svedberg-Yen said. "To get money from publishers you need a demo, but to make a demo you need money. To get money you have to go to publishers. That’s an area where I would like to help a lot of these smaller creatives to get that seed funding so they can, in a way, de-risk themselves in the eyes of bigger investors.

"In that way I can also help them build a stronger narrative foundation from the beginning. I’ve done a lot of consulting work with different studios. I’ve discovered that quite a lot of studios don’t really know how to think about narrative, or how to integrate it into their pipelines and their processes. They tend to think it’s a very narrow thing, when in fact it can influence all aspects. Part of this would also be an incubation program to help these studios develop those narrative foundations."

Svedberg-Yen said a lot of studios think of narrative as "a skin," something thrown on near the end of the development process once everything else is assembled. But that's like trying to solve a puzzle without knowing whether there's actually a solution at all, she continued, "whereas if you start from the very beginning, when everyone is starting from the same jumping-off point, everything will naturally be able to tie together."

The goal is not to compete with publishers, she explained, but "to find a way to work with them to get us back on solid ground. Above all, I really would like to champion other people and other creatives to have a chance to pursue their dreams, have a chance to make the games they want to make."

That investment fund, alongside her own projects and stories, will be Svedberg-Yen's primary focus in the coming year: "The strongest feeling in me right now for 2027 is I want to help. That sounds so cheesy and idealistic. I always cringe at myself for saying it. But I don’t know how else to say it and be truthful. I want to help in some way, help the industry."
