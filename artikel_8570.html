Staying in your home as you age works only if the house is safe and you can afford help when you need it. Make the key changes, like grab bars, a no-step entry, and an accessible first-floor bathroom, before a fall forces them. Then price help at home and decide how you’d pay for it.

Start with the house, since more than one in four adults 65 and older falls each year, the CDC reports.

Angi’s accessibility cost guide puts grab bars at $100 to $500 each, a wheelchair ramp at $2,300 on average, and a bathroom made fully accessible at $9,000 to $40,000. Angi’s stair lift cost guide puts the average stair lift at $7,050 installed.

Say a couple in their late 60s spend $14,000 now on a walk-in shower, grab bars, and a ramp. If one of them later needs help at home 15 hours a week, that’s about $2,275 a month at the $35 hourly median in CareScout’s 2025 survey. At 44 hours a week, help costs about $6,673 a month, more than the $6,200 median for assisted living, which also covers meals and a place to live.

Pay for the changes from savings or a home equity loan, or a reverse mortgage if you plan to stay for good.
