Kraken Review – One Account for Crypto, Stocks & Pro Trading

Open a Kraken account if you want crypto, U.S. stocks, and pro-grade trading under one login, from an exchange that publishes proof of reserves. Place your regular buys on Kraken Pro rather than the app, whose 1% fee and built-in spread make it the account’s costliest way to trade.

Buying crypto in a simple app is easy but costly. Trading on an exchange’s order book, the live list of buy and sell offers, costs less but takes more know-how, and stocks usually sit at a separate brokerage. Kraken puts all three behind one login, so the decision that matters is where you place each trade.

Kraken is the trading name of Payward Inc., founded in 2011, and it says more than 13 million people worldwide trust it. In the U.S., it’s licensed to transfer customers’ money in 46 jurisdictions and offers stock trading through Kraken Securities, a broker-dealer registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). It also publishes proof-of-reserves reviews, which check that it holds the crypto its clients’ balances show, a practice it says it began in 2014.

The account has five parts: a simple app, a pro exchange, stock trading, ways to earn on idle coins, and two companion apps.

The Kraken app is the simple side of the account. You buy, sell, or convert crypto at the price it quotes, set recurring buys daily, weekly, or monthly on more than 200 coins, and pay by bank transfer, debit card, or PayPal. Kraken says it lists more than 600 cryptocurrencies, though it restricts 83 of them for U.S. clients. The app suits investors who buy now and then and don’t want to learn an order book.

Kraken Pro, on the same login, is the full exchange, with stop-loss and take-profit orders, charts with 25-plus trading and data widgets, a desktop app, and API access for your own trading software. Eligible U.S. traders can also trade on margin, borrowing against their crypto for positions worth up to 20 times their collateral on some coins, and trade perpetual futures, contracts with no expiration date. Both run through Kraken Derivatives US, a futures broker registered with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), and Kraken warns that margin losses can exceed what you put up.

The Kraken app and Kraken Pro also trade more than 11,000 U.S.-listed stocks and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) with no commission, through Kraken Securities, in every state Kraken serves. Orders placed outside market hours wait for the next session, Kraken Pro adds trading 24 hours a day on weekdays, and Kraken charges nothing on its end to move shares in from another broker.

Once you switch it on, Auto Earn stakes eligible idle coins, committing them to help run their blockchains in return for rewards. It pays weekly and keeps the coins free to trade or withdraw, and on coins that make you wait to unstake, it pays rewards on up to half of what you stake. Bonded staking pays more for locking coins up for a set term.

Rates on the biggest coins are low. Kraken estimates Auto Earn’s annual percentage yield (APY) at 0.02% for bitcoin and 1.47% for ether, before its commission. DeFi Earn puts USDC, a stablecoin pegged to the dollar, into vaults that lend it on blockchain lending markets for up to 5.33% APY, though the rate floats, the product isn’t regulated, and you can lose money.

Kraken Wallet is a free self-custody app, meaning you hold the keys to your coins instead of Kraken, on Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, and other networks. Krak, a separate money app, sends money free between Kraken users and lets you spend through the Krak Card, a Visa debit card from Lead Bank. The card pays up to 2% cash back, at a rate set by the average assets you hold across Krak, Kraken, and Kraken Pro, and because Krak isn’t a bank, its balances carry no FDIC deposit insurance.

The app’s convenience is the account’s biggest cost. Every app price includes a spread, a markup over the market rate that Kraken doesn’t publish and may keep, on top of these fees.

Counting fees alone, since Kraken doesn’t publish the app’s spread, here is what $500 of bitcoin a month costs by our math. The app charges $5 a month, or $60 a year. Kraken+ costs $59.88 a year on monthly billing, so it pays off once app trades top about $500 a month, or about $417 on the annual plan. Kraken Pro charges $2 for the same buy as a maker order or $4 as a taker order, and Kraken says Pro prices carry no hidden spread.

If you want only bitcoin or ether as a long-term slice of a portfolio, a spot exchange-traded product (ETP) is the main alternative. It holds the coin itself and trades on a stock exchange, so you skip the crypto wallet and keys, though you pay the fund’s sponsor an ongoing fee that direct holders don’t.

Kraken says no custodial wallet it manages has been breached since its founding in 2011, and it keeps most client crypto offline, in cold storage in guarded facilities. Accounts sign in with passkeys and two-factor authentication and can’t be recovered by text message.

An independent accountant’s proof-of-reserves review for Sept. 30, 2026, found Kraken held 103.2% of its clients’ bitcoin and 100.7% of their ether. Kraken notes that such a review can’t rule out hidden claims on the coins or prove they weren’t borrowed, and it covers only the coins it names, not dollar balances.

Crypto at Kraken carries neither FDIC deposit insurance nor the brokerage protection of the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC). Stocks are different. Kraken Securities is a member, and SIPC covers up to $500,000, including $250,000 in cash, if a member brokerage fails, though never market losses.

Kraken’s record with regulators is mixed. In 2023 it paid $30 million to settle SEC charges over its old U.S. staking program and shut that program down, relaunching U.S. staking as a new product in 2025. A separate SEC lawsuit claiming Kraken ran an unregistered securities exchange was dismissed for good in 2025.

Kraken serves every state except New York and Maine, and some features run on shorter lists. Staking is open only in 37 states, Washington, D.C., and Puerto Rico, which by our count leaves out 11 states Kraken otherwise serves, including California, Texas, Illinois, and New Jersey. The Krak Card isn’t offered in Massachusetts or Indiana, and Krak’s cash transfers aren’t available in Indiana, Louisiana, Massachusetts, or Utah. Margin and perpetual futures go only to traders who meet Kraken’s eligibility rules.

Support runs around the clock by live chat and email, with phone calls only in select cases. Kraken calls you only after you’ve contacted it and arranged the call, so treat any other call, or a Kraken number found in a search ad, as a scam. Of Kraken’s 9,583 reviews on Trustpilot, 49% give one star and 33% give five, for a TrustScore of 3.1 out of 5.

Kraken is a strong pick for investors who want crypto, U.S. stocks, and serious trading tools in one account. Its best case is Kraken Pro’s low fees beside the beginner app, backed by proof of reserves and a clean custody record.

It’s the wrong account for New York and Maine residents, who can’t open one, and for anyone in the 11 other states without staking who wants rewards on idle coins. Long-term holders who want only bitcoin or ether can own them through a spot ETP instead. Everyone else should open the account and route regular buys through Kraken Pro, or add Kraken+ once app trades top about $500 a month.
