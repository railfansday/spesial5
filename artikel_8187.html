11 Early Holiday Deals for Hikers Our Editors Would Actually Buy

If it feels too early to start thinking about holiday shopping, well, I agree. Halloween is just behind us, Thanksgiving is still weeks away, and yet every store has already decked their halls with fairy lights, Christmas trees, and giant faux candy canes. But, setting aside my Scroogey (and maybe slightly Boomer) complaints for a minute, there are some real advantages to getting your holiday shopping early. For one, you don’t need to worry about shipping times. For another, you can browse the season’s best deals at your leisure. While not all brands have officially started their Black Friday or winter sales yet—REI’s doesn’t begin until the 13th—eager bargain hunters can find heavy discounts on everything from tents to boots already. These 11 Backpacker-tested and -approved shelters, bags, trail runners, and other assorted goodies are on sale for 25 percent or more off right now.

For beginners and anyone who prefers to linger in camp, a tent’s weight matters less than its durability and livability. The Marmot Limelight has both in spades: With 34.4 square feet of floor space for the 2 (42.5 for the 3), double doors and vestibules, and additional gear storage space in the included wings, the tents have enough room for even tall backpackers to spread out comfortably.

A legit backpacking tent for less than $200 is a steal in this economy. Like the Limelights, the Tungsten is a solid option for anyone who prefers to live a little larger in the backcountry. The freestanding tent features an 8.72-square-foot vestibule and a 38.2-inch peak height, making it comfortable for long nights or waiting out a storm. Bonus: The color-coded clips make setup easy.

Swapping to a trekking pole tent is an easy upgrade for anyone who wants to cut their pack weight. While the REI Co-op Flash Air 2 lost out to models from Durston, Zpacks, and Six Moon in this year’s ultralight tent test, at this price, it’s still a legit option for anyone searching for an upgrade. The 2 lb, 6.5 oz tent pitches with a pair of adjustable trekking poles, while two additional small poles at the feet and crossbar create extra room.

The Copper Spur line is a perennial favorite of our testers; we’ve tested it in almost every kind of conditions, from South American rainstorms to clear desert nights. The HV UL1 Bikepack features 20 square feet of floor space; at 2 lb, 5 oz, it’s light for a freestanding tent, too.

From thin sleeping pads to inconveniently-placed zippers, we side sleepers have a lot stacked against us in the backcountry. The Sidewinder is specifically made for those of us who prefer to curl up, with a center zip that you won’t end up laying on when you toss and turn.

The REI Co-op Magma 30 doesn’t have any gimmicks: It’s just a down-the-middle, well-made sleeping bag suitable for 3 season use. The 850-fill-power down lets it compress handily, with a long taking up just slightly more room than two 2-liter Nalgenes.

The Aspire was our testers’ favorite women’s rain jacket of 2026 (the Foray is the men’s version), so at half off it’s an easy buy. Our testers praised the breathability of the 3-layer Ascentshell construction, while the 40D, 100-percent recycled nylon face fabric shrugged off close encounters with trailside foliage.

We named Black Diamond’s Fineline the best-value rainshell for women in our 2026 test. But value isn’t all this lightweight shell has going for it. The 2.5-layer shell’s light stretch made it comfortable to wear on everything from high-tempo hikes to mountain bike rides, and we appreciated the helmet-compatible hood and hand pockets.

With their wide, splay-friendly toebox and zero-drop design, Altra’s shoes are popular enough on hiking trails that they need no introduction. With its 33mm stack height, the Olympus 6 is an excellent pick for backpackers who want a little more cushion than the popular Lone Peak provides.

I’m personally a big fan of Adidas’ Terrex Free series, which I’ve taken everywhere from the Panamanian jungle to the Appalachian Trail. Testers who tried this speedy shoe out agreed. A Gore-Tex bootie shuts out slop, while the DreamStrike+ EVA midsole kept them feeling bouncy underfoot.

Don’t be fooled by the space-age looks: Our testers reported that these kicks from Merrell were at home on the trail, with a “ridiculously thick” EVA foam midsole that absorbed shock on rough treadpath without ever feeling unstable.
