Kelly Bishop Hints at Tension Between ‘Stars Hollow’ Group on ‘Gilmore Girls’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The unauthorized Gilmore Girls documentary, Drink Coffee, Talk Fast, is here to celebrate more than 25 years of the show’s iconic legacy.

Produced by Ink On Paper Studios and directed by Meghna Balakumar and Kevin Konrad Hanna, the doc features interviews with original cast and crew members, including Kelly Bishop, Scott Patterson, Jared Padalecki, Keiko Agena, Chad Michael Murray and more.

Keep scrolling to see the biggest revelations made in the film:

Diner owner Luke Danes, played by Scott Patterson, was originally intended to be a woman named Daisy. However, the character was ultimately rewritten when The WB requested “more testosterone” in the show to balance out the female characters. Of course, that decision brought fans the beloved romance between Luke and Lorelai Gilmore (Lauren Graham), so we can’t be too upset that Daisy never came to be.

Before Jared Padalecki took on the role of Rory Gilmore’s (Alexis Bledel) first boyfriend, Dean Forrester, two other actors had the role.

“We had to recast the role of Dean,” casting director Jami Rudofsky recalled in the doc.

Casting director Mara Casey added, “There was a Canadian Dean and then there was this other Dean. I think they fired two dudes.”

Padalecki, who was 17 years old when he auditioned for Gilmore Girls, was ready to return home to Texas if he didn’t land the part. Thankfully, Casey, Rudofsky and series creator Amy Sherman-Palladino were all instantly sold on him playing Dean.

Likewise, Lorelai’s best friend, Sookie St. James, was initially played by Alex Borstein in the unaired pilot episode before Melissa McCarthy took on the role. Borstein was unable to play the character due to a scheduling conflict, although she went on to appear in the show as Drella, the harp player, and Miss Celine.

Several actresses auditioned for the role of Lorelai, but one crucial line in the script solidified Graham as the lead.

“In that first scene in Luke’s Diner, there was a reference to Jack Kerouac,” director Lesli Linka Glatter said in the doc, recalling how Graham was the only actress to pronounce the famous novelist’s last name correctly.

“Amy’s writing had so many references to theater and literature and contemporary music,” Glatter recalled. “So we said, ‘You know what? We cannot have someone that doesn’t know who Jack Kerouac is.’”

During the early days of filming Gilmore Girls, The WB frequently called Sherman-Palladino with complaints about the script because the show didn’t fit the network’s target audience. She finally got the calls to stop when she issued an ultimatum: fire her or quit complaining. It worked.

Many of the cast members in the doc recalled having to be “word perfect” when reciting their lines on set. Even replacing “a” with “the” wasn’t allowed. They also had to talk very fast, which is where coffee came in handy.

“We used to have delicious coffee drinks on set,” Rose Abdoo, who played Gypsy, recalled. “We all talked even faster, and I thought, ‘OK, they’re not just trying to treat us. We’re really all jazzed up now.’ … It wasn’t one of those shows where people could improvise… no, every word is there for a reason.”

Murray, who played Chilton student Tristin Dugray, remembered being told by Sherman-Palladino, “Talk faster. I don’t want to cut any pages out of this show.”

Season 3 of Gilmore Girls featured an episode where Jess Mariano (Milo Ventimiglia) traveled to California to get to know his father. The episode doubled as a backdoor pilot for a spinoff called Windward Circle. Though Ventimiglia was on board with the spinoff, the WB ultimately canceled the project after the California episode received poor feedback. It was also reportedly too expensive to film in Venice Beach.

In the doc, Sherilyn Fenn, who played Sasha in the episode and was later recast as Anna Nardini, claimed that The WB thought Ventimiglia couldn’t lead a spinoff.

“The studio was so silly. They said, ‘Oh, Milo’s not strong enough to carry a show,’” she said. (Ventimiglia went on to star as Jack Pearson in the NBC drama This Is Us.)

Gilmore Girls had many cameos from actors who would go on to become famous, including Seth MacFarlane, Jane Lynch, Jon Hamm, Rami Malek and more. Ryan Gosling was almost on that list, too, but he bombed his audition, Rudofsky revealed.

Bledel’s first major role was playing Rory in Gilmore Girls when she was 18 years old. Though she expressed gratitude for the opportunities the show brought her, cast and crew members recalled in the doc that she sometimes struggled with the grueling filming schedule.

“She was just like 18 years old, and ‘I’m here for 60, 70 hours a week. What am I doing with my life?’ And it’s pretty, it’s gruelling,” Mark Strasburg, first assistant camera, said. “It’s something you really had to want to do. There were definitely moments she knew she didn’t want to be there.”

The doc also featured footage of an older interview with Patterson in which he admitted, “Alexis was really overwhelmed by being away from her family, maybe having some second thoughts about what she had gotten herself into. ‘Is this really a way to spend my youth?’ She was terribly homesick. And what was nice to see was the bond that formed off the set between Lauren and Alexis, because Lauren really helped her out.”

While the doc’s participants had mostly fond memories of their time on Gilmore Girls, Kelly Bishop, who played Emily Gilmore, alluded to some behind-the-scenes drama between cast members.

“I loved working with that little nucleus of a family. The Hartford group got along great,” she recalled, referring to herself, the late Ed Herrmann (who played Richard Gilmore), Bledel and Graham. “I guess there was a little bit of problems here and there in Stars Hollow, and I’m not gonna tell you what they were because it’s none of my business, and it was hearsay and I don’t do that.”

While Bishop did not name-drop anyone, others suggested that Graham and Patterson didn’t get along off camera. George Anthony Bell, the show’s dialogue coach, noted that he didn’t think the pair “hung out after the show,” while Strasburg added, “Off screen, I’m not sure if they’re besties, but they don’t really seem to really like each other. But on screen they’re amazing.”

Graham and Patterson previously shut down rumors of a feud. In 2007, Graham told TV Guide that their friendship was a “working relationship like most of them are. But he was so great in that part. I really loved my scenes with him and the chemistry we had. Our banter was among the most fun stuff to do.”

Patterson told 7News in 2023, “I love her, she loves me, we get along very, very well.”
