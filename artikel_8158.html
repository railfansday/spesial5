🥾 More Than a Trail, More Than Miles 🌄✨

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Waking up to my watch alarm at 5:45 a.m., I grabbed a cup of coffee, then disappeared back into my dark room to write.

With laundry in the machine, I sat down with Nobody at the table. Another amazing meal: eggs and oat pancakes, fresh mango, and orange juice.

After eating a hot-out-of-the-oven brownie, I took one last foot bath.

Backpack packed, my tummy started rumbling.

I grabbed a few more brownies to eat on the road.

Almost to Walker Pass Campground, we passed a roadside burn from the night before. The sight of the burnt Joshua trees was devastating.

Arriving at Walker Pass, my stomach was still churning. After making two trips to the pit toilet, I swallowed an Imodium. I knew I could always turn around if I didn’t start feeling better.

As the sun began to dip toward the horizon, the trail leveled out. I set up my tent in the wind, wondering whether I should eat my leftover Pad Thai. I took half an Imodium, then ate my cold dinner.

Happy to be back in the wilderness, I fell asleep in my tent under the stars.

Pushing snooze on my 4 a.m. alarm, I fell back asleep, then repeated the process. I boiled water for oatmeal and coffee.

I took one more Imodium, then began my trek north at 7:20 a.m.

I thought about everything I was grateful for, especially being able to hike this trail. I was grateful to try those new tent stakes. I was also relieved that the fire near the trailhead had been extinguished, knowing how it could have affected the PCT.

Meeting Wings and Nobody was another thing I was grateful for.

Up, up went the trail. The sandy terrain gave way to the slate of the Sierras. The agave and blooming prickly pear disappeared, replaced with purple and red flowers.

Cresting a saddle, I pulled out my phone. Joshua Springs was three miles downhill. With my bottles empty, I crossed a small flowing stream, remembering Joshua Springs fondly from my 2022 hike. I planned a long lunch at the spring.

At a trail junction, I took a left and began my quarter-mile downhill hike. When I reached the piped water trough, I quickly realized that I could not expect this hike to be the same as my 2022 Sierra hike.

Hogs had come through and torn up the dirt. Since water was flowing along the trail above, hikers hadn’t needed to make the extra trip down to the spring.

Using my groundcloth to flatten the dirt, I sat down and took my shoes off while I filtered water. Mosquitoes and tiny gnats quickly became a nuisance while making a cheese, mayo, and barbecue chip burrito.

I preferred avoiding the midday heat, especially when I had access to shade and water. The conditions at the spring made it difficult to enjoy a restful break. Once my water was filtered, I hiked back up into the heat of the day.

A few miles later, I was sitting in the shade when I heard voices. Expecting to see hikers coming down the trail, I was surprised when two people popped out of the shrubs. Vic from PCTA and Andrea from the ACE crew were out for trail maintenance. We stopped to talk for a few minutes before I continued my hike.

At Spanish Needle Creek, I stopped to make dinner and filter water. I considered camping near the trail crew, but I still had a few miles left in me. I wanted to get a jump on the 11-plus-mile water carry.

As I hiked up a four-mile climb with 1,600 feet of elevation gain, I pulled out my headlamp. Eventually, I reached the Lamont Peak spur trail. I found a flat, rocky spot tucked away from the wind.

Setting up a freestanding tent in a rocky, windy location could be difficult for a beginner. I had always backpacked with freestanding tents and was proud of moments like this.

First, I wrapped the corner guyline around a small rock. Then I placed a larger rock on top of the guyline to keep the smaller rock from moving. I repeated this process on all the corners and then the doors. No stakes, my tent stayed up all night.

With no moon, the Milky Way was visible behind the bright stars.

Turning off my alarm, I fell back to sleep until the birds started singing.

While cold-soaking oatmeal in a bag, I took down my tent and talked with the couple I had camped near.

I crossed an unmarked water source, where I stopped to collect water and eat my oatmeal.

I passed near the jagged Sawtooth Peak, then switch-backed downhill. In the distance, I could see Chimney Mountain.

When I reached the wide-flowing Chimney Creek, I filtered water and grabbed a snack. After applying sunscreen, I was about to leave when I heard voices. I was surprised to see Joker, NBC, and All-Star behind me.

Mosby wasn’t feeling well and needed to get off trail. The five of us walked to Chimney Creek Campground, where we set our gear on a picnic table with a view of the gravel road.

When a vehicle approached, we flagged down the truck. Dulce and Chuck were going fishing and driving through Kennedy Meadows South.

With our backpacks in the bed of the truck, we all squeezed into the cab. We stopped at Grumpy Bear’s for a picture.

After being dropped off at the General Store, I saw a few hikers I knew: Sherpa, 8 Mile (previously Big Pharma), and Rooster.

After hearing about trail magic at the campground, I hitched one more time.

Hotdog and the Hike Beyond the Hills group had tacos for trail magic. TA Sprinter plucked at his guitar. Mary from 2 Foot stopped to visit for a while. Worms grabbed a bite on his hike north. Later, Snake Charmer, aka Mama Bear, made beef ramen with mushrooms and bok choy.

The evening was filled with good food and good company.

Borrowing trekking poles, I left my tent set up at Kennedy Meadows Campground. My bag was packed with the bare necessities and bulked out with my quilt.

Meeting up with Joker, Hotdog kindly drove the two of us back to Chimney Creek.

Sunshine (WA) was at Chimney Creek Campground, and we talked briefly. She was excited to be finishing her final PCT section!

Joker and I started our NOBO hike at 6 a.m.

As I passed a log swarming with black California carpenter bees, I noticed monarch butterflies everywhere.

Crossing over a stream, I met Marmot, Brave (Czech Republic), and Shake Head (Israel).

In the distance, I could see snow-covered, white-stone mountains dotted with pine trees.

Passing Mile 700, we stopped for a picture.

We talked with Michael, a day hiker and bird watcher.

When we reached the road, we walked to the General Store. There, Joker and I caught a ride back to the campground for more trail magic: grilled hot dogs.

That night, I met Cool Turkey, Honeyman, and Oliver.

That morning, I was able to sleep in and spend some time on my blog.

The girls—CeeCee, Vicki, and Rochelle—packed up camp and left.

We gave trail magic to Meta, a hiker attempting the Calendar Triple.

At Grumpy Bear’s, I retrieved my packages and unhappily paid to pick them up. After signing the logbook, I left.

Across the street, I picked up my PCT 2026 bandana and bought a HYOH sticker.

Back at the General Store for a shower and laundry, I purchased a green Alpha hoodie from 2 Foot Adventures.

I hung out with other hikers: Hellmann, Cactus, DQ, BangBang, Google, Rooster, Power Finger, Spice, Socks, and Bonus.

Back at the campground, I hung my clothes on a line to dry.

The wind blew a fine layer of sand inside my tent. My recently cleaned backpack had sand in the zippers, and I cleaned the sand out of my tent.

Seeing a shooting star was the cherry on top of my evening.

I was up way too early for a non-hiking day. At 4:30 a.m., it was a cold, gusty morning. Happy to have my new Alpha hoodie, I blogged and sipped coffee in the comfort of my tent.

Building a small fire for warmth, and the neighbor’s dog stopped over for a visit.

Around 8 a.m., the hikers started arriving for pancake trail magic on their way into the Sierras: BangBang, DQ, Joker, NBC, All-Star, Hellmann, Cactus, Brave, Rado, and Caroline.

After running out of pancakes, we made hot dogs. Google and more hikers continued arriving until all the food was gone.

Oyster came up needing a ride into town. She thought her filter was compromised.  Still a little while before we left, we asked the neighbor with the dog if he could give Oyster a ride.

At the General Store, I saw Fairy, and met a female hiker names Pickles.

Finally meeting back  up with Marble we talked about possibly going rafting down the Killer Kern. Excited for a possible side trip, I decided to hang out in KMS for a few more nights.

The afternoon was spent sorting my resupply into my bear can. Later, at the restaurant window, I ordered a cheeseburger with guacamole and a basket with half jalapeño poppers and half french fries.

Front Porch was having issues with his tent zipper. Fairy helped him fix it. I gave him my solar panel.

I spent the remainder of the evening painting my nails and watching YouTube.

I enjoyed coffee and pancakes with Front Porch, Marble, Pickles, and Electrolyte (MS).

With my resupply running low, I was grateful when hikers who were getting off trail offered to share their food. The extra meals and bars were much appreciated.

I back-flushed my filter and talked with Front Porch.

Hungry, I dug through the hiker box and threw together tomato soup with refried beans and ramen noodles. I added the chicken packet for flavor. It was really good for a hiker box meal.

That night, I met a lot of new hikers, including more women. It was nice to see how many women were on the trail this year.

As I crawled into my tent, the zipper started to act up, and it took me a while to get all the teeth to catch.

I am grateful for my ability to backpack.

I am grateful for humanity and the compassion people show one another.

I am grateful for the hiking community. There are some amazing people out there who help you along your journey. Their kindness is heartwarming.

I am proud to be a part of this community.

I am grateful for waking up on the trail each morning.
