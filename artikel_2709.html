'Somebody leaned against the wall': Power outage interrupts MNF

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Monday night's game between the Washington Commanders and Cincinnati Bengals was interrupted by a sudden blackout at Northwest Stadium just outside of D.C.

With the Bengals holding a 10–7 lead in the third quarter, the Commanders were preparing to take the snap on a third-and-14 from inside the red zone when the stadium suddenly went dark.

"Somebody leaned against the wall," Joe Buck joked as he and Troy Aikman attempted to make sense of the scene.

"We're being told that it's power related, and not the stadium," Buck later assured viewers, though that felt like a distinction without a difference to anyone who was just trying to watch some football.

It was a delightful bit of direction from the ESPN production crew, shuffling between shots of players staying warm in the dark, fans holding out the flashlights on their cellphones, and a shot of commissioner Roger Goodell looking on from the owner's box as a guest of Josh Harris.

Thankfully, the interruption didn't last long. After throwing to commercial break, the lights were back on by the time the broadcast came back, with the relighting of the stadium earning massive cheers from the crowd and presumably a huge sigh of relief from Goodell.

On third down, Commanders quarterback Jayden Daniels was forced to throw the ball away, and Washington settled for a field goal to tie the game 10–10.

In the waning seconds of the third quarter, we finally found out what happened to shut the lights out.

"If you want to know the reason why the lights went out earlier, the official word is the lighting vendor never disabled the regularly scheduled maintenance for 10 p.m.," Buck explained. "It's a weekday thing, and they forgot it was a Monday night game."

That is a hilariously simple reason, and means that someone in the Washington operations staff is having the worst day at work imaginable. Thankfully, it all worked out.

Hopefully the rest of the game can carry on without any further interruptions.
