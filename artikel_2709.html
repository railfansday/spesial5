Remembering legendary Broncos linebacker Tom Jackson

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

As the Denver Broncos enjoy a six-game winning streak and an 8-2 record, there is plenty for Broncos Country to be happy and excited about. This team may really have what it takes to get to and win Super Bowl LXI.

However, as the team and its fans ride that exciting wave, the news came in over the weekend that one of the team's greats, linebacker Tom Jackson, had passed away at the age of 75. Jackson's loss is not an ordinary one for the Broncos. It's not an ordinary loss for the game of football, either.

Jackson made a lasting impact on the game. He made a lasting impact on fans like me.

Born in Cleveland, Ohio, Jackson used to attend Browns games with his father as a kid. Football was an early part of his life, creating a work ethic and drive that led to him playing the game professionally. He was chosen in the fourth round of the 1973 NFL Draft out of Louisville. Jackson joined a Broncos team that had never had a winning season, let alone been to the playoffs.

The Broncos had only been part of the NFL for three years at that time, but they had a 10-year run in the old AFL. Jackson was there to add a hard-working, grinding linebacker to a growing defense. That defense would eventually become known as the "Orange Crush," and along with players like Randy Gradishar, Lyle Alzado and Louis Wright, Jackson was one of the key figures within that unit.

To this day, it's one of the best defensive groups in NFL history.

Jackson helped the Broncos reach the postseason for the first time in 1977. After defeating the Pittsburgh Steelers in the divisional round, the Broncos beat the Oakland Raiders in the AFC Championship Game, a thrilling victory that was the biggest in team history to that point.

The Broncos went on to Super Bowl XII, losing to the Dallas Cowboys. Jackson, or TJ as he was affectionately known by many, had a sack and a fumble recovery in that game. But the loss stuck with him.

The Broncos reached the Super Bowl nine years later. Following "The Drive," which famously helped win the AFC Championship Game against the Browns in 1986, the Broncos went to Super Bowl XXI. It was in that game, the first football game I ever watched, that I was introduced to Jackson. It also happened to be his final game as a player.

The Broncos lost 39-20 and Jackson retired after that season. However, his contributions to the game were only beginning. In 1987, he was hired by ESPN to help cover the league.

Jackson would soon be partnered with Chris Berman at ESPN. If you don't remember Jackson from his days on the field, you almost certainly saw him sitting at a desk doing highlights or discussing what was going on in the NFL at that time.

For fans of the league across the country, Berman and Jackson were the voices of the show NFL Primetime. Though the show still runs, it's never been the same without the duo of Berman and TJ. Berman spoke about their friendship on Sunday before the games kicked off for the week.

It was a tough watch for NFL fans, as it signified the end of a beautiful era, even if that era ended a long time ago. There was no way to listen to the emotion in Berman's voice without getting teary-eyed.

That's because NFL Primetime was much more than just a highlights show. The connection between Berman and Jackson was incredible. Long before smartphones and social media, this was how fans stayed connected to what was going on in the league. It was a much different world and the realization that it's all just a memory now can be tough to swallow.

Jackson wasn't just a guy who sat at a desk calling highlights. He played in two Super Bowls. He is a member of the Broncos' Ring of Fame. He brought knowledge and insight to the game that all fans appreciated. But most of all, he was a genuinely great person, and that was evident at all times. You'll never hear someone say a bad thing about Tommy, because there are none. That is reflected in the way his former ESPN colleagues speak of him.

Adam Schefter, who is now ESPN's No. 1 football analyst, was in Denver during the early part of his career, writing for multiple news outlets. He knew of Jackson because of the lasting impression he left before joining him at ESPN. He paid tribute to Jackson on Sunday.

For those who were not there for the NFL Primetime era, it was special. It was instrumental in helping the game become what it is today. Between Berman's nicknames for players and terrific calling of the action, Jackson added just enough to make the show flawless. In those days, you'd often look more forward to those two calling the highlights than you would the games that were played that day. Here's a small snippet of what those days were like.

Those days have been gone for some time now, but this seems to be a definitive end to them. They should be remembered as a magical time in professional football history and TJ was an integral part of that history. Fortunately, he and his old partner, Berman, were able to see each other one final time recently, in the same setting where they created so many lasting memories for fans of the game.

It's a sad day for Broncos fans. It's a sad day for ESPN. It's a sad day for football. But when our time comes, the best that we could ask for is that we left a lasting, loving impression on those who mattered the most to us. There is no doubt that Tommy did that.

For sharing a unique knowledge of the game. For being part of something that changed the way we looked at the game and forming a duo that will never be duplicated, Jackson gave everything he possibly could have to the game he grew to love and in turn, his love for the game spilled over and helped to create new fans of the game.

It doesn't get more lasting and loving than that. Thank you, TJ, for helping me become a lifelong fan of the game, and for doing the same for so many others. You were truly one of a kind.
