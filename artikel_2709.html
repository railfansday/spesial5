How early-season shot selection shifts are impacting fantasy basketball value

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

We're far enough into the season to notice something useful, as long as we're careful about what we're measuring. Scoring averages and shooting percentages can swing a lot over the first few weeks. Where a player takes his shots tends to settle faster, so it's a better place to start.

In testing this for 2025-26, Tall Order Digital found that among 156 qualifying players, shot-zone shares over the first four games correlated with the rest of the season at 0.72 to 0.86 (a perfect match would be 1.0). Field-goal percentage correlated at just 0.46.

To see what the shifts are doing to fantasy basketball value, I've listed each player's preseason ADP next to his current 9-cat rank (through Nov. 11). Shot data is Basketball-Reference totals as of Nov. 11, compared with last season or a career mark.

Luka Dončić and De'Aaron Fox are ranked higher than where they were drafted, and Michael Porter Jr. has climbed despite scoring less. In all three cases, the revised shot profiles are landing and netting positive results.

He's launching 13.4 3s a game, up from 10.8 last season (which led the NBA). His 3-point share has risen from .476 to .549, and last year's .476 was already a career high. The bigger move is at the rim, where his share has dropped from .076 to .060. He's making 40.8% of his 3s and averaging 38.6 points in 11 games.

Fox is getting to the line far more often. He's attempted 53 free throws on 111 field-goal attempts, a free-throw rate of .477, compared with .235 last season and a .316 career mark. His career high is .420, set in 2019-20, so this is a new peak.

It isn't coming from more shots at the rim, though. His rim share is flat (.207 vs .201). What changed is the middle of the floor. Shots from 3-10 feet jumped from .204 to .315 of his attempts, while long twos nearly vanished (.067 to .018). He's also attempting 3.1 3s a game, down from 5.5, so his average shot is 12.4 feet from the basket instead of 14.4.

He's traded long twos and 3s for more floaters and short jumpers, and the whistle is doing the rest. He's backing it up with 51% shooting and 6.2 assists, though his scoring is 14.2 points, down from 18.6.

Porter is taking 3s on .615 of his attempts, up from .506 last season. He had never topped .556 in a season. His free-throw rate has climbed from .237 to .349, and his 10-to-16-foot share has collapsed from .075 to .018. He's averaging 21.5 points, below last season's 24.2, yet he's still up five spots in his draft position. A more interesting shot profile doesn't automatically mean more points. Playing alongside a player like Julius Randle likely also affects how a player navigates getting shots up.

These two are still scoring, but their rank has dropped since they were drafted. A new shot mix doesn't always show up the same way in the box score.

Durant is a free-throw story. His FTr has risen from .339 to .582 (92 attempts in 12 games, 7.7 a game), above his previous career best of .523 in 2012-13 and his .392 career mark. His 3-point volume has fallen from 5.8 a game to 3.8, and he's turning it over 3.3 times a game. Durant is at 20.9 points on 49% shooting. Having Fred VanVleet back and running more offense through Alperen Şengün is putting less dependency on Durant – which is probably a good thing in the long run. Fantasy-wise, Jabari Smith Jr. and Şengün's growth has Durant adjusting his shot profile, which could lessen his value as a scorer.

Gilgeous-Alexander's rim share has fallen from 22.8% to 13.1% and his 10-to-16-foot share has risen from .273 to .303. His 3s are up to .292 on 6.7 attempts a game from .226 on 4.4 last season. SGA's free-throw rate has dropped from .465 to .339. He's still scoring 29.7 a night, so this is a small slide from a very high perch.

That's an observable redistribution that likely comes down to how much attention he's drawing without co-stars Jalen Williams and Chet Holmgren to balance out the Thunder's offensive attack. SGA is having to settle for more contested shots and rely more on his jumper. SGA has proven to be one of the most efficient guards in the league, but fantasy managers may have to accept that this could be a down season. He's shooting 45.6% from the field as of Wednesday.

This is where the shot shift is costing the most fantasy value. A big jump in one kind of shot usually takes a few weeks to settle and the efficiency tends to lag the volume. Whether that's what's happening here, or whether the new role simply isn't a good fit, is something we'll know more as we move further into the season.

In 10 games with Orlando, 100 of his 148 attempts have been 3s: 67.6%. His 3-point share had slid from .543 as a rookie to .354 last season, against a .441 career mark. His two-point attempts have fallen from 9.5 a game to 4.8 this season. His average shot distance is 23.4 feet, up from 13.5.

The results haven't been good: 39.2% from the field and 18.0 points a game, down from 20.1. The key question is whether Orlando keeps asking him to operate on such a narrow diet. If it does, last season's scoring profile may be the wrong baseline even after his shooting recovers.

Markkanen has the second-biggest 3-point jump on this list, making up .646 of his attempts, up from .402 last season. His previous career high was .570 in 2024-25. His rim share has halved, from .210 to .106, and his average shot distance has gone from 13.9 feet to 18.9. And now, he'll miss at least a month with two non-displaced fractures in his shooting hand.

He's scoring 18.0 points a game on 39% shooting, down from 26.7 in 42 games last season. A bigger share of 3s didn't produce more points here, at least so far.

Buzelis is a different case from the others in this group, because his shot mix barely changed. His 3s are .520 of his attempts, almost identical to last year's .514. What changed is how many shots he's taking. He's down from 12.5 field-goal attempts a game to about 9.8, with both his 3s (6.4 to about 5.1) and his twos (6.1 to about 4.7) falling, and his minutes slipped from 29.2 to 26.3.

His free-throw rate jumped from .247 to .429, but that's partly a smaller denominator. He's getting to the line about 4.2 times a game, up from 3.1, on far fewer shots. The extra trips haven't made up for the lost volume. He's shooting 43% with 2.8 turnovers, and he's ranked 226th. A higher free-throw rate doesn't help much when the shots aren't there to begin with. Caleb Wilson has a fractured hand and is reportedly expected to miss 4-6 weeks, so expect Buzelis to see a short-term boost in his volume.

Shot diets settle faster than shooting percentages, and that's the lens for this group. Bane and Markkanen have the clearest case: 3s are 68% and 65% of their shots, and that's likely to stick, while their 39% shooting is the part more likely to improve. More 3s  also means a lower field-goal percentage but more 3PM, so expect a trade-off in 9-cat rather than a clear gain.

Fox and Durant are getting to the line at career-high rates, which gives them some cushion on off nights, though it hasn't lifted either one's rank. Buzelis is the caution. His free-throw rate is up, but his shot volume is down, so the better rate hasn't helped his fantasy value.

Bet on the shot profile, then check whether the production is catching up.
