Your eye doctor has to hand over your contact lens prescription when the fitting ends, at no extra charge, whether or not you ask. Take it, and before you reorder, compare what a year’s supply costs at a few online stores after shipping, rebates, and what your vision plan pays.

The requirement comes from the Federal Trade Commission’s Contact Lens Rule, which exists so you can shop around.

Your eye doctor can’t make you buy lenses at the office, pay an extra fee, or sign a waiver in exchange for the prescription. The prescription must also stay valid for at least a year, unless the doctor has a medical reason to make it expire sooner.

A store can ship only after it gets a copy of the prescription or checks it with your eye doctor. If the office doesn’t answer the store’s request within eight business hours, the prescription counts as verified. Your copy also names one brand. Switching to another takes your eye doctor’s approval, unless the new box is the same lens from the same maker, sold under a store’s name.

So compare stores on what a year of your exact lenses costs, not on the price of one box. Shipping fees, a maker’s rebate, and whether your vision plan pays the store directly can each change the total. A store in your plan’s network applies the benefit at checkout. Anywhere else, you usually pay up front and file the claim yourself.
