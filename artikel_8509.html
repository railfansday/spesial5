Pick a budgeting app by the work you’ll do each week, not its feature list. Quicken Simplifi suits most people, since it sorts your spending into a plan on its own. Pay for YNAB only if you’ll plan every dollar and check in most days, or use EveryDollar’s free version if you’ll type in purchases yourself.

Intuit shut down Mint, its free budgeting app, in March 2024, and most of the apps below that pull in your bank transactions charge for it, such as YNAB at $109 a year. What the fee mostly buys is time, since the free versions of EveryDollar and Goodbudget don’t link to your bank, so you add each purchase yourself.

Each pick suits one way of budgeting and earned its spot on what it costs a year, how reliably it connects to your bank and card accounts, and how much work it asks of you each week. If you’d rather not use an app at all, make a budget by hand in a spreadsheet.
