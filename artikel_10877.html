Google will block Search for students if schools turn off its AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Google has announced that schools choosing to completely disable artificial intelligence tools for students will also lose access to Google Search, as the company prepares to introduce new AI controls in early 2027.

The change comes as schools across the US reconsider how students interact with AI, with educators and parents raising concerns about cheating, misinformation, and the technology’s impact on learning.

While Google plans to give administrators more control over AI features in its education software, schools that decide to block the technology entirely will face restrictions on one of the most widely used research tools in classrooms.

According to The New York Times, Google will begin disabling AI tools for K-12 students by default in early 2027, requiring school administrators to explicitly enable them.

However, districts that choose to prohibit AI entirely will also lose access to Google Search through school-managed accounts.

“Search and A.I. are deeply integrated,” said Chris Phillips, Google’s vice president for education.

Phillips explained that students at schools banning the technology “will not have access to search, and they’ll see a message from their school that indicates it’s not available.”

For schools that permit AI, Google will offer two search experiences. The first will include AI Overviews and conversational AI features, similar to the standard consumer version of Google Search.

The second will incorporate Guided Learning, a version of Google’s Gemini chatbot designed to help students work through questions and understand subjects without immediately providing answers.

Google says the educational version of Guided Learning will include additional restrictions, with teachers able to review student chatbot conversations.

The upcoming changes also reverse an August decision that automatically enabled AI features within Google Classroom for schools that had already allowed students to access Gemini.

That move prompted complaints from educators who wanted greater control over when and how AI tools were introduced to students.

The announcement comes amid growing restrictions on generative AI in American classrooms.

In September, the New York City and Los Angeles school districts broadly restricted student access to generative AI on school devices. Los Angeles officials had hoped students could continue using Google Search without AI Overviews appearing in results.

Google’s latest policy means districts will no longer have that option in their school-managed search experience.

Morgan Polikoff, an education professor at the University of Southern California, criticized the decision.

“This is a particularly clear example of the tech industry going out of its way to force A.I. on us all — even if we have said we don’t want it,” he said.

Meanwhile, Josh Golin, executive director of the child advocacy organization Fairplay, encouraged schools to consider alternative search engines rather than accepting Google’s AI requirements.
