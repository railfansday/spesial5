Lock of former US President George Washington's hair sells for $4,000

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

George Washington died in 1799 after serving for two terms as America's first president

A framed lock of hair from America's first president, George Washington, has been sold at auction for $4,000 (Â£3,058).

The hair once belonged to John Gadsby, who entertained Washington in 1799 just before the latter's death the same year, and was later obtained by a collector called John K Lattimer from a descendent of Gadsby's, according to Richmond Auctions, which sold the item.

Saturday's sale is not the most expensive for Washington hair at auction. Three strands thought to have been removed from him close to or after his death stored in a locket sold for nearly $40,000 in 2021.

Gadsby, a resident of what in 1799 was the town of Alexandria in Virginia, entertained Washington for dinner shortly before his death later that year, according to a testament given in 1928 by Gadsby's granddaughter, Julia Ten Eyck McBlair, and which was posted on the Richmond Auctions website.

McBlair stated that her grandfather came into possession of Washington's hair "in view of the friendship and association with his last days" and had it framed and hung "under General Washington's portrait in our home in Washington City, there I have seen it all my life".

Gadsby hosted Washington's two birthday celebrations in 1798 and 1799, Richmond Auctions states.

Washington led American forces to victory against the British in the War of Independence and helped draft the US Constitution before becoming the country's first president in 1788. He stepped down in 1797 after serving two terms.

Several locks of Washington's hair have been preserved, including at least 50 examples that are in a collection at his former residence, Mount Vernon, which is now a museum.

"Public displays of hair from friends or loved ones peaked in the 19th century, but had long been popular among affluent families like the Washingtons," a blog on the Mount Vernon website states.

As well as lockets of his hair, other items associated with Washington have also done well at auction.

In July, a signed letter by Washington from 1776, in which he asks for soldiers for the Continental Army to fight the British, sold for $109,800.
