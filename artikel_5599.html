Rare Muscle Disease Gets Its First Approved Therapy

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

— FDA approved ribitol to treat limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9

The FDA approved ribitol (Welgemda), an oral small molecule, to treat limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9 (LGMD2I/R9) in adults and children weighing at least 30 kg, the agency said Monday.

The treatment is the first therapy approved for any form of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, the FDA noted.

"Limb-girdle muscular dystrophy is a progressive muscle disease that gradually takes away a patient's ability to walk, breathe, and live independently, often beginning in childhood," said Laura Jawidzik, MD, of the FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research. "The clinical data supporting Welgemda's approval show meaningful improvements in walking speed and lung function, and we are encouraged by what that can mean for patients' daily lives."

LGMD2I/R9 is caused by mutations in the FKRP gene. In healthy individuals, the FKRP enzyme helps attach sugar molecules to alpha-dystroglycan to connect muscle cells with surrounding tissue. Ribitol is a naturally occurring, five-carbon sugar alcohol that may help support the glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan.

Clinical manifestations of LGMD2I/R9 include skeletal myopathy affecting the lower and then upper limbs, often accompanied by subsequent pulmonary muscle and cardiac muscle involvement.

The FDA's decision to approve ribitol was based on key measures from a planned interim analysis of data from the phase III FORTIFY trial, according to drugmaker BridgeBio. At 12 months:

"Families living with LGMD2I/R9 have never had disease-specific treatment and only had supportive care to manage progression," Matthew Wicklund, MD, of the University of Texas at San Antonio Health Science Center, said in the drugmaker's news release. "What we saw in FORTIFY was different: individuals became measurably better at the things that matter most day to day, including breathing easier, walking faster, walking farther, and less fatigue."

The double-blind FORTIFY trial randomized 112 patients with genetically confirmed LGMD2I/R9 to either twice-daily oral ribitol dissolved in water or to placebo for up to 36 months. Participants were 12 to 60 years old.

The most common adverse reactions included gastrointestinal effects (e.g., diarrhea or stomach pain) and joint pain, the FDA noted. Patients treated with ribitol showed significant increases in glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan, indicating the drug reached its intended target.

With the FDA's approval, BridgeBio plans to transition FORTIFY participants in the U.S. to an ongoing open-label extension study. Trial sites outside the U.S. will continue the trial as planned until each participant completes 36 months of treatment.

Ribitol is not approved or available in any other market. BridgeBio anticipates an immediate U.S. launch.
