If you fly United Airlines regularly, the (see rates and fees) can be a powerful way to earn more miles and reduce your out-of-pocket travel costs.

As someone who frequently flies United, I've found that the Quest hits a practical middle ground. It offers meaningful airline perks, including a $200 annual United TravelBank credit, two free checked bags and discounts on award flights, without the hefty annual fee of a premium airline card.

But with a $350 annual fee, the Quest isn't necessarily right for everyone.

Here's who I think can get the most value from the card and who might be better off with another option.

New United Quest cardholders can currently earn 500 PQP after their first purchase and 80,000 bonus miles after spending $4,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening. Plus, you can earn another 10,000 bonus miles after adding an authorized user in the first three months from account opening.

Based on TPG's November 2026 valuations, which peg United miles at 1.2 cents apiece, the full 90,000-mile bonus is worth $1,080. That's not including the additional value of the 500 PQP, which can help you get closer to United Premier status.

While not the strongest offer we've seen in the card's history, it's still a great time to apply if you've already been considering the Quest, particularly if you have upcoming United travel planned.

If you fly United several times per year, you'll want to take a closer look at the Quest.

That’s because many of the card’s most valuable perks are tied directly to flying United. Cardholders receive:

One of the card's strongest perks is its earning structure. The United Quest earns a strong 10 total miles per dollar spent on eligible United flights as a United MileagePlus member — that's a whopping 12% return, according to TPG's valuations (mileage earning may differ for basic economy and base option tickets).

As someone who holds Premier Silver status, I can earn a total of 12 miles per dollar spent on eligible United flights when using my Quest, which is an even better return.

But what I find especially valuable are the discounts United cardholders now receive when booking award flights. Eligible United cardholders without Premier status save at least 10% on qualifying United-operated award flights, while cardholders with Premier status save at least 15%.

I take advantage of these discounts pretty regularly. Recently, I booked two tickets from Washington, D.C.'s Dulles International Airport (IAD) to London's Heathrow Airport (LHR) for a mother-daughter trip to Europe. Thanks to my cardholder discount, each ticket cost just 34,000 miles plus $11.20 in taxes and fees, rather than 45,000 miles plus taxes and fees.

That's a savings of 11,000 miles per ticket, or 22,000 miles total, worth $264 according to TPG's valuations.

The Quest also offers two additional award flight discounts: 10,000 miles beginning with your first card anniversary and another 10,000 miles after spending $20,000 on purchases in a calendar year.

I recently used my 10,000-mile award flight discount toward a transatlantic flight from Amsterdam Airport Schiphol (AMS) to IAD. The flight would have cost 34,000 miles, but the credit brought it down to just 24,000 miles plus $112.83 in taxes and fees.

That's another $120 worth of miles saved, based on TPG's valuations.

Between the cardholder award pricing discounts and the annual award flight credits, the Quest gives me several ways to stretch my United miles further, which is why it's a card I plan to keep in my wallet long-term.

This is one of the most underrated — and potentially most valuable — perks of the United Quest Card.

The card includes two free checked bags for you and a companion on the same reservation. On domestic economy flights, United typically charges $50 ($45 if prepaid) for the first checked bag and $60 ($55 if prepaid) for the second bag.

That means you could pay up to $110 per person each way if you check two bags without a card benefit.

If you and a companion each check two bags round-trip on just one domestic flight, that could cost up to $440 total. That alone covers the Quest Card’s $350 annual fee.

Personally, I frequently fly United and regularly check two bags free thanks to my Quest Card. For travelers who often travel with a companion, don’t pack light, take longer trips or occasionally travel with bulkier items like skis or golf clubs, this benefit alone can justify holding the card year after year.

Each account anniversary, the Quest Card provides $200 in United TravelBank cash. This credit can be used toward United-operated flights and is automatically deposited into your TravelBank account.

I recently used my $200 TravelBank credit to offset the cost of a round-trip flight from San Francisco International Airport (SFO) to IAD to visit family. The ticket originally cost $560, but my TravelBank credit brought my out-of-pocket cost down to $360.

And the TravelBank credit isn't the only statement credit available with the Quest.

For me, the TravelBank credit is the easiest to use. When you subtract its $200 value from the Quest's $350 annual fee, that leaves an effective cost of $150 — assuming you can use the credit in full — before factoring in free checked bags, award flight discounts and other benefits.

If you're working toward United Premier status, the Quest Card can also help you get there.

Cardholders can earn 1 PQP for every $20 spent on purchases with the card, up to 18,000 PQP per calendar year.

Those PQP count toward United Premier status qualification, potentially helping you close the gap to your next status tier.

Plus, beginning in 2027, eligible United cardholders will receive a head start on status qualification. United will deposit PQP equivalent to 5% of the amount required for their current Premier status tier into their accounts at the start of the year. That means Premier Silver members, for example, will receive a 300-PQP boost.

I earned United Premier Silver status for the first time last year, and I've already secured it again for next year, thanks in part to the PQP boost I received at the beginning of the year and the PQP I've earned through flying and spending on my Quest.

I generally don't put much spending on the card outside of United purchases, since I have other cards that earn more flexible rewards on everyday expenses. However, I did intentionally increase my Quest spending over the past few months to earn the additional PQP I needed to secure Silver for another year.

For me, that was worth it because I regularly fly United and appreciate the benefits that come with Premier status.

Of course, I wouldn't recommend putting unnecessary purchases on a card just to earn PQP. But if you're already close to qualifying for status, the Quest can give you another way to get there.

The United Quest Card is a strong step up from the (see rates and fees) — especially for travelers who fly United more than once or twice per year.

The Explorer is a great entry-level option, with a $0 introductory annual fee for the first year (then $150) and useful perks like a free checked bag and two one-time United Club passes each year. For occasional United flyers, that may be all you need.

But if you’re booking multiple United flights annually and check bags or redeem miles, the Quest’s incremental perks can quickly outweigh the difference in annual fees and deliver stronger long-term value.

In short, the Explorer fits the occasional flyer. The Quest is designed for frequent United travelers who can maximize its recurring value.

While the is packed with benefits for United fans and frequent travelers, there are a few reasons why the card might not be the right fit. Let's take a deeper look at who the card isn't the right fit for.

While the United Quest Card has many travel-related benefits, it does not come with any lounge privileges, and you don’t get any guest passes.

If lounge access is a priority, the (see rates and fees) may be a better fit. It includes a United Club membership as part of its annual fee, which grants access for the primary cardholder and eligible guests.

And for those who don't want to pay the United Club's steep $695 annual fee, remember that the offers two guest passes annually for a much more approachable fee ($0 introductory; $150 after the first year).

That said, not everyone needs airline-specific lounge access. For example, I personally get lounge access through the Capital One Venture X Rewards Credit Card, so I don’t need the Quest Card to provide that benefit.

While the number of Capital One lounges is limited, the Venture X also includes a Priority Pass lounge membership (at participating lounges), so I rarely feel short on options.

If you also already have lounge access through another premium travel card, the lack of United Club access here may not matter.

The United Quest Card is subject to Chase’s 5/24 rule. If you’ve opened five or more personal credit cards across all issuers in the past 24 months, you’re unlikely to be approved.

If you’re currently over 5/24, you may need to wait — or consider a card from another issuer.

The United Quest Card earns United MileagePlus miles. If you don’t regularly fly United — or you prefer more flexibility in how you redeem your rewards — a transferable points or miles card could be a better option.

Flexible points and miles can typically be transferred to multiple airline and hotel partners or redeemed in other ways, giving you more options.

If you fly United multiple times per year, check bags regularly and can easily use the $200 annual TravelBank credit, the United Quest Card’s ongoing perks can quickly offset much of its $350 annual fee.

However, if you prioritize lounge access, prefer flexible points or miles over airline-specific miles or only fly United occasionally, another card — either within United’s lineup or outside of it — may be a better fit.

Ultimately, the Quest Card shines for travelers who are loyal enough to United to benefit from its airline perks but don’t need the full premium treatment of a club membership.
