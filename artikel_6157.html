Virgin Atlantic Cancels 3 Long-Haul Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Virgin Atlantic, like many other airlines, has been making several changes to its route network this year. Since March, the airline has completely withdrawn from the Middle East, and it recently confirmed that it will not resume flights between Manchester Airport (MAN) and Las Vegas Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) for summer 2027.

The decision comes as the SkyTeam member is basing a fifth aircraft at its Manchester hub next summer. It has also confirmed plans to increase frequencies on several existing routes as part of its summer 2027 schedule.

Since tensions in the Middle East escalated in late February, several airlines have temporarily paused or cut flights to the region. Virgin Atlantic was no exception. Before its withdrawal, the airline served two destinations in the region: Dubai International Airport (DXB) in the United Arab Emirates and King Khalid International Airport (RUH) in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. The latter was a relatively new addition to its network, having launched in March 2025.

The airline operated daily seasonal flights between London Heathrow Airport (LHR) and Dubai using its Airbus A350-1000 aircraft. Its Riyadh service, on the other hand, operated daily throughout the year using the Airbus A350-900. Initially, the UK airline announced that it was temporarily suspending both routes due to escalating tensions in the region. However, it subsequently decided to cancel them permanently.

According to data from aviation analytics provider Cirium, Virgin Atlantic's last scheduled flights to both destinations were in mid-March 2026, and no further services are currently scheduled. Separately, its London Heathrow–Tel Aviv route (which it last operated in 2023 when the conflict began) remains suspended, and flights are not expected to resume.

Furthermore, another notable change came recently when Virgin Atlantic confirmed that it would not return to Las Vegas from the Northwest England airport next summer. The airline first launched flights between the two cities in 2011 and continued operating the route until 2019. It then reinstated services in June 2024.

This year, the SkyTeam member operated the route between April and October and was scheduled to return on April 13, 2027, running through October 31. The airline had planned three weekly services, reducing to two during the peak summer months. However, these flights have now been canceled. Well, the route's performance may help explain the decision.

According to data from the US Department of Transportation (DOT), Virgin Atlantic filled just 72.3% of its seats between Manchester and Las Vegas in the 12 months to July 2026. This was around ten percentage points lower than its London Heathrow–Las Vegas route, where it operates daily year-round flights using a 258-seat Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner.

Instead of daily flights to Dubai, the airline will increase its capacity to South Africa, adding a new destination and increasing frequency.

The Las Vegas cancelation comes as Virgin Atlantic has announced plans to increase frequencies on its three remaining US routes from the airport. According to the airline's original schedule for July 2027, it had planned 24 weekly departures across these three routes. That number will now increase to 40.

Flights to Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) will increase from daily to 11 weekly, while services to New York's John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) will rise from seven to up to ten weekly. Orlando International Airport (MCO) will see the largest increase, from 12 to up to 19 weekly flights.

That said, London Heathrow will continue to account for the majority of Virgin Atlantic's transatlantic capacity, representing roughly 84% of its transatlantic flying. The airline will continue to offer regular flights to 11 US destinations from the West London airport, including JFK, Boston, Atlanta, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Las Vegas, Miami, Orlando, Seattle, Tampa and Washington, DC.
