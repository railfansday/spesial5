From Texas To France: Southwest Expands Global Reach With New French bee Partnership

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Southwest Airlines has announced a new international interline partnership with French bee, allowing passengers to combine flights on both carriers under a single ticket. The new agreement provides connections through Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), Miami International Airport (MIA), and San Francisco International Airport (SFO), linking Southwest's domestic network with French bee's long-haul services to Europe and beyond.

French bee becomes Southwest's 12th international airline partner, continuing a remarkable expansion of the carrier's overseas connectivity since its first agreement with Icelandair in early 2025. The partnership comes as French bee prepares to expand into two new Asian markets, opening additional travel opportunities for Southwest passengers.

The new partnership takes effect immediately, with bookings available through French bee, travel agents and third-party booking platforms. Southwest Executive Vice President and Chief Commercial Officer Justin Jones welcomed the agreement, highlighting the opportunity to expand the airline's international reach without operating long-haul flights itself.

"French bee shares our philosophy of pairing value with a welcoming travel experience, and this partnership opens new possibilities for travelers to connect across oceans on a single ticket."

Unlike a codeshare, an interline agreement allows airlines to coordinate connecting itineraries and checked baggage without placing their flight numbers on each other's services. Passengers therefore benefit from a single booking. However, it's worth noting that interline tickets are not yet available through Southwest.com, and reciprocal loyalty earning and redemption are also not included.

French bee, owned by Groupe Dubreuil, operates an all-Airbus A350 fleet from its Paris-Orly base. Its existing network includes Newark, Los Angeles, Miami, San Francisco, Montreal, Réunion Island and Tahiti. It is also famous for operating the world's longest nonstop domestic route, from Paris to Roland Garros Airport (RUN) in Saint-Denis de la Réunion, covering 5,048 nautical miles (5,810 statute miles / 9,349 km).

For Southwest, the partnership provides access to destinations far beyond its Boeing 737 network, including Paris and French Polynesia. French bee is also preparing to launch new routes to the Maldives and Sri Lanka on December 19. Initially, the services will operate via a triangular Orly–Malé–Colombo–Orly routing, with additional nonstop Paris-Colombo flights beginning January 11, 2027. French bee also stands to benefit from access to Southwest's extensive domestic network, with travelers originating from other US cities able to connect through Los Angeles, Miami or San Francisco onto French bee's long-haul flights. This additional feeder traffic is particularly valuable to a relatively small long-haul airline seeking to improve load factors and reduce its dependence on passengers originating in its three US gateway markets.

Looking further ahead, the partnership could also strengthen the business case for French bee to launch additional US routes. Aviation Week reports that the airline is actively examining new destinations in the US and Asia to reduce seasonal fluctuations in demand. Cities like Las Vegas and Orlando could therefore be particularly interesting candidates, given Southwest's strong presence in both cities and with them being substantial leisure markets.

France's long-haul low-cost carrier has a unique operating model.

Southwest's international partnership strategy began when it signed its first agreement with Icelandair in January last year, with operations beginning the following month. Since then, agreements with multiple Asian and European airlines have rapidly expanded its international reach, including with global giants such as Turkish Airlines and Singapore Airlines.

The partnerships are also starting to evolve beyond basic interline arrangements. In October, Southwest and Icelandair announced an expansion of their loyalty partnership, allowing reciprocal points earning and redemption from Spring 2027. With further international agreements under consideration, Southwest is increasingly positioning itself as a feeder airline for global carriers, gaining long-haul connectivity without the costs and complexities of operating widebody aircraft.
