On Personalized Pricing, the FTC Got Ahead of the Evidence

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The agency pulled a market failure out of thin air and tasked itself with solving it.

Delta Airlines passenger jets at LaGuardia Airport in Queens, N.Y., in 2022.

Americans should expect their federal agencies to be staffed by experts who gather evidence meticulously before proposing regulations disinterestedly. But that’s not what the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has done when it comes to personalized pricing.

Last month, the FTC ordered nine major companies — Delta Air Lines, Uber, and Instacart among them — “to provide detailed information that will allow the agency to study the use of personal data to set prices and compensation.” The agency, however, seems to have reached its conclusions already, given the contents of both its order and an enforcement policy proposed in August, which appears partial against personalized pricing and the businesses that employ it.

Vladlena Klymova is a policy analyst at the Taxpayers Protection Alliance.
