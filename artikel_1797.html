Chelsea’s feisty friendly ends in penalty shoot-out confusion

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Chelsea’s Far East tour ended in farce and confusion with players waiting around for a penalty shoot-out that never happened.

The chaos came at the end of a hard-earned, bad-tempered draw against Malaysian champions Johor Darul Ta’zim, with an own-goal in the 89th minute sparring the west London side’s blushes.

That goal also was supposed to set up penalties. But after minutes of waiting around – and Kevin Campello, Chelsea’s head of first-team operations, saying “it’s written in the contract” – the players walked off the pitch.

That confusion ended a feisty affair in which tensions threatened to boil over and that, at certain points, paid little attention to the “friendly” aspect of the clash with seven yellow cards shown. At one point the visitors’ new set-piece coach Austin MacPhee exchanged angry words with some of Johor’s coaching staff. The CFC TV commentary team suggested that the spot-kicks were abandoned because of the match’s heated atmosphere.

Having played just 24 hours earlier in Jakarta – a 3-0 win over AC Milan – it was no surprise to see Xabi Alonso name a fresh XI, a mixture of players who may well be offloaded before the end of the transfer window, those needing to prove themselves, and youngsters given much-needed game time.

And considering Johor are one game away from setting a new world record of 109 matches unbeaten in the top flight – a run that would surpass the current mark set by Ivorian side Asec Mimosas in the early 1990s – it was also no shock to see the Malaysian team offer a stern, pre-season test for the west Londoners.

After a good start Chelsea were second best in a first half in which Johor should have been at least two goals to the good. The Malaysian champions controlled the first 45 minutes and were 1-0 up after 14 minutes – a low cross from the left side-footed home by Arif Aiman.

The hosts gave the young, inexperienced Chelsea backline a torrid time, especially on the flanks and Óscar Arribas was guilty of not capitalising on a great chance to double Johor’s lead, his shot after cutting in from the right going wide.

While they looked vulnerable at the back, Chelsea also struggled to keep possession in attack. But they got an equaliser they scarcely deserved after Liam Delap was manhandled to the ground in the box by Kevin Medina. The striker, who may well leave Stamford Bridge before the transfer window slams shut, fired home the spot-kick allowing the visitors to go into the break on level terms.

For the most part, the second half followed a similar pattern to the first – Jofor having the better chances, while tensions on the pitch continued to run high.

It was, however, Chelsea who took the lead after the break, Delap once again scoring from the spot, this time after Jamie Gittens was brought down in the area.

Johor, though, kept on creating, thanks in part to a fairly porous visitors’ backline, and levelled through a deflected Arribas free kick, before going 3-2 up thanks thanks to a well-taken Bérgson strike.

Had it stayed that way Chelsea could have had few complaints. But as the clock approached 90 minutes, the visitors had one last attack. Mykhailo Mudryk, just on as a substitute, delivered a cross from the left that Antonio Glauder could only steer into his own net. That was supposed to set up the penalty shoot-out that did not take place and the chance for one of the sides to claim a hollow victory.

Considering a raft of changes and that fact this was far from the first XI, it is hard to know what to make of this. But among the positive things Alonso could take from the game was the impressive form of Nicolas Jackson and good centre-forward play of Delap. The negative was, however, a poor defensive display.

A mixture of players who may well be offloaded before the end of the transfer window, those needing to prove themselves, and youngsters you may not have heard of.

Johor Darul Ta’Zim – more commonly known as JDT – are the Man Utd of Malaysia (at least that’s according to a Malaysian friend of mine). In 2013, they were going nowhere and were the dictionary definition of a stagnant club, low on confidence and trophies. That was until the Crown Prince bought the club and, over the next 13 years, transformed it into the powerhouse it is today.

As if to underline JDT’s dominance over Malaysian football they are one game away from the world record unbeaten run in a top flight. The new season of the Malaysian Super League gets under way later this month and should they avoid defeat in their opening match they will extend their unbeaten run - which began in April 2021 - to 109 games and surpass the world record set by Ivorian side Asec Mimosas in the early 1990s.

Chelsea’s pre-season tour of South East Asia ends today with a match against Johor Darul Ta’zim in Malaysia. The clash comes barely 24 hours after the west Londoners beat AC Milan 3-0 in Jakarta, a much-needed win coming on the back of defeats against Juventus and Tottenham.

A fine volley from Moises Caicedo and a double from João Pedro either side of half-time led Xabi Alonso’s side to victory in the Indonesian capital.

The game was fairly even in the first half and looked set to head into the break level at 0-0 but Pedro’s header from Caicedo’s corner put Chelsea in front.

Alonso’s side then doubled their advantage seconds into the second half as Pedro scored his second, assisted by Pedro Neto.

Just minutes later, Chelsea had their third courtesy of a well-worked corner routine. Romeo Lavia’s delivery was met by Caicedo on the volley, with the Ecuadorian’s finish with the outside of the boot finding the bottom corner.

Today, the west London side face Malaysian powerhouses Johor at the Sultan Ibrahim Stadium, more on them later, but considering the tight turnaround from yesterday’s match today’s clash will provide a stern test.

“The idea, with the game against Milan and then one in Johor, was to arrive at this weekend with players not being ready for 90 minutes until now,” Alonso explained after the win in Jakarta.

“We wanted to have a logical progression in their loads; so we had our plan and we’ve stuck to that.

“We have another idea [against Johor]; that was decided yesterday [Friday]. The idea is that many young players were coming to have chances to play tomorrow. If not, we’d have been short, as playing two games in two days is quite intense. So we will need them.”
