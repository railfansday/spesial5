How to Not Get Sick This Season, According to <i><em>Bazaar </em></i>Editors

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

There are the tried-and-true tricks, but what about the weird, wacky, outlandish ways to prevent or fight off a cold? Ahead, we share the 14 hacks that keep us healthy all season long

Here at Harper’s Bazaar, we are game to try anything and everything in the wellness world—from daily digestion walks to a $500 full-body assessment, and even supplements to boost ferritin levels—to stay healthy. But, like anyone else, we’re always hunting for new ways to avoid getting sick.

We’ve all been there: someone coughing on the subway immediately makes you jump, or you hear a friend you just sat next to at dinner has come down with a cold, and suddenly you’re overdosing on vitamin C. Warding off a cold or viral sickness is not always possible—especially once cold and flu season rolls around.

There are classic tricks like upping your vitamin C intake, washing your hands frequently, and drinking hot liquids like soup and tea, but what about the extreme hacks that hit the SOS button on your immune system—those under-the-radar, witchcraft-adjacent tricks? We have tried almost every tip and trick in the book—some work like a charm, and others are all hype and anecdotes—but ahead are the 14 hacks our editors swear by for staying healthy this cold season—and beyond.

“Tongue scraping has changed my life. Not only does it make your mouth feel much cleaner and support overall oral health, but I swear that since I started scraping religiously a few years back, I've been sick WAY less often. And I have a toddler! In daycare! My theory is that it scrapes away bacteria before it has a chance to settle in your throat—but who knows? No matter what the science says, there are only upsides—fresher breath, better teeth, and the satisfaction of seeing all that gunk removed." —Jenna Rosenstein, beauty director

“As an out-and-proud hypochondriac, I have a long laundry list of cold-fighting hacks. The most important is washing my hands frequently (as soon as I arrive somewhere new, after taking the subway, after touching a public surface, etc.) and avoiding touching my face. I keep a compact hand sanitizer in every purse I own in case of emergency!” — Olivia Alchek, senior designer

“I consider a good night's sleep a cure-all for just about anything, including incoming sickness. Not only does adequate rest help your body fight off infection by way of antibody and immune cell production, but it just makes you feel better in general, which is all you want when you're feeling borderline sick.” —Tiffany Dodson Davis, beauty commerce editor

“Most of my heavy-hitting cold fighters are from Europe (they’re just exponentially better over there), but when it comes to sick solutions stateside, I always have Wellness Formula stocked in my house. I don’t know what sorcery is in these pills, but once I take this, whether it’s when a sniffle or tickle in my throat comes up, or if I am already deep into a cold, I feel better the next day. The pills contain all the gold-standard immunity ingredients: elderberry, zinc, propolis, echinacea, and vitamins C and D, plus vitamins and minerals that are good to take generally, like selenium, calcium, and vitamin A. The only downside is a pungent aftertaste (likely from the garlic bulb in the formula), and the daily dose is 6 pills, which is more than many can handle. If you don’t like pills, there’s a liquid version that works just as well, but I prefer the pills for when I travel.” —Katie Intner, beauty market and commerce editor

“Even in the post-COVID era, I still wear K-N95 masks on public transit. It’s the most successful way to avoid unwanted germs. Thinking about thousands of New Yorkers on a crowded subway, sniffling in the winter months—I’ll never go back to being mask-free again!” —OA

“As soon as I feel a scratchy throat coming on, I break out the Vicks. Slather it on and let it do its old-timey magic.” —Lillian Dondero, assistant managing editor

“When people say to ‘flush out a cold,’ they typically mean drink lots of fluids, but I love dry brushing to get the lymphatic system moving and grooving to rid any toxins from the cold. I do it for two minutes before I shower, and I instantly feel my lymphatic system activated and more energized. Plus, it doubles as exfoliation, which is key during the winter months.” —KI

“Throat Coat tea is my holy grail whenever I’m feeling under the weather. I love the subtle licorice flavor, and it’s incredibly soothing on an irritated throat. I always keep it stocked at home and reach for it at the first sign of a cold. Honestly, it’s so good that I even drink it when I’m not sick!” —Jackie Judd, executive assistant

I don’t know if this is the official tagline, but in my head it is ‘stops a cold before you get it’ because IT. DOES! The trick is to buy the kind that goes up your nose—spray or swabs—and be diligent; your symptoms won't progress, and then they'll go away completely. It's magic!” —Laia Garcia-Furtado, deputy culture director

“Liquid echinacea everywhere! I keep it in my bag and squeeze it into everything—even my coffee.” —Lynette Nylander, executive digital director

Life is hard enough! I want a supplement that works and tastes good.
