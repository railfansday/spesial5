Ford's Privacy Promise Includes A 'Quasi-80s Mode' To Dumb-Down Connected Cars

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Modern cars are enormously talented at producing gobs of information about drivers. How hard they smash the brakes, where they frequently navigate to, or even what in-car features they use the most. It makes it incredible difficult to draw a line between what portion of an automaker is a car company or a tech company.

Ford would like you to know that it won't be that difficult to tell your car to zip its mouth about driver data—if it's wearing a Blue Oval on the grille, that is.

Ford also shares what it does with that data and (most importantly) how you, the driver, can turn data sharing off.

A car cannot simultaneously refuse to tell Ford where it is and also ask Ford to give it information relevant to the world around it (like live traffic data). That means if you choose to turn off connected navigation, internet connectivity, and location features in the Ford app, you'll lose some functionality within the car. And doing that would certainly make the car have a more '80s experience, after all.

The trade-off for Ford is that it uses that data to help tailor its vehicles to include features that owners want. No data means that Ford will need to find another way to customize in-car features to fit a less-connected digital experience.

Ford CEO Jim Farley also recently mentioned that the company will lean on digital subscriptions to help boost revenue with vehicles like the uber-cheap Ford Fathom. The caveat there is "digital," which means that vehicles that ditch connectivity could also be a lost cause on digital subscription revenue. And that's the ultimate compromise that owners will need to decide.

The good news is, Ford is at least being transparent about owner choice. That's the entire point according to Jones: "The trust really comes from transparency. It’s our job to be as radically transparent as we can be."
