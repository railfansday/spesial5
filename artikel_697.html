The Next Bond Theme: From Taylor Swift to Raye, 16 Singers Who Could Croon for Robert Pattinson’s 007

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The James Bond films are filled with traditions: the martinis are shaken, not stirred; the tuxes are immaculately tailored; the women are gorgeous; the villains are fascinating. Importantly, the films wouldn’t be true Bond stories without a dramatic theme song written and performed by a star. Nancy Sinatra, Shirley Bassey, Paul McCartney & Wings, Madonna, Jack White and Adele are just some of the acts who have brought soaring anthems to the films’ introductions. With Robert Pattinson’s Bond just announced, it’s prime time to dreamcast a new singer for the new film. Check out Variety‘s top picks below.

Many Bond numbers are heavy on brass and horns. Insert Marc Anthony, who can make any song feel cinematic. In 2024, Lin-Manuel Miranda penned an op-ed for Variety calling the 58-year-old the Latino community’s Frank Sinatra, writing: “He is our Sinatra and when we hear him, we also hear the echoes of all the unforgettable moments in our own lives.” The salsa king will have his “The Last Dance World Tour” in 2027, so perhaps he can fit in a nice writing credit to bring some excitement to the new iteration of 007? Let’s give him a sweeping bilingual ballad about loving someone who’s going to betray you, and the audience will melt in their seats. — C.D.

Bond can always use some Latin flavor! Who wouldn’t be more iconic than the biggest artist on the planet? Bad Bunny already conquered the Super Bowl, and his album “DeBÍ TiRAR MáS FOTOS” became the first fully Spanish-language album to win the Grammy for album of the year. The Puerto Rican superstar already has a bit of assassin experience with his scene-stealing turn in the film “Bullet Train.” Can you imagine a moody, reggaeton-laced theme in Spanish playing over the opening titles, followed by a revolutionary performance on the Oscars telecast? A global event, indeed. — C.D.

The Queen can do no wrong. Beyoncé already has some experience in the spy business, having played Foxxy Cleopatra in “Austin Powers in Goldmember.” Now it’s time for her to take on the real deal. Fresh off winning Album of the Year at the 2025 Grammys for “Cowboy Carter,” and with two Oscar-nominated songs to her credit— “Listen” from “Dreamgirls” and “Be Alive” from “King Richard” — expectations would be sky-high. A Tina Turner-inspired Bond theme, complete with powerhouse vocals and a rock-and-soul edge, could be the perfect way to usher in a new era of 007. — C.D.

Do we remember the Winter Olympics in Milan? That soaring high note on the Italian classic “Volare” reminded us that Mariah Carey still has it, and it’s almost criminal that she hasn’t conquered this kingdom yet. That five-octave range is tailor-made for an iconic Bond finale: the orchestra drops out, the tension builds, and Mariah unleashes one final, earth-shattering note on a slow-burning ballad. Give us Mariah! — C.D.

The Bond universe isn’t averse to fresh blood when it comes to picking younger, newer artists for theme songs (see: Billie Eilish and Adele), so it’s not entirely out of the realm of possibility that Olivia Dean could be tapped to record an original for the new Bond film. She’s got the range and poise that live up to the most classic of Bond themes, plus her British pedigree suits her cleanly for the role. Put her in the studio with an orchestra and you’ve got yourself a formula for Bond magic. — S.H.

Few artists make more sense to record a Bond theme than Lana Del Rey, whose sweeping ballads and cinematic songwriting scream 007. And it would be something of a redemptive arc for her, considering that she previously revealed that she had penned and recorded the song “24” from her “Honeymoon” album for “Spectre,” only for Sam Smith to get the job. Del Rey has since inched closer to Bond glory earlier this year with “First Light,” the theme for the video game “007 First Light,” but the big ticket is just out of reach. — S.H.

Earlier this year, Cynthia Erivo performed at the Hollywood Bowl and delivered a remarkable rendition of Gladys Knight’s “Licence to Kill” from the James Bond film of the same name. Dear Denis Villeneuve and the Bond producers: you must watch the performance on YouTube and consider Erivo. She’s perfectly British, and her vocals are elegant, soulful and filled with grandeur. She also has a cinematic style and would be a perfect entry into the Bond franchise. — J.T.

The Canadian chanteuse is back in the public eye after struggling with health issues, and who better to deliver a soaring anthem that unites the world? She’s no stranger to the genre, delivering gems such as the indelible “My Heart Will Go On” for “Titanic” and the underrated “Ashes” for “Deadpool 2.” Plus her powerful four octave range is sure to match the intensity of any orchestra she’s singing alongside. Step aside and let Dion put the “power” in “power ballad.” — W.E.

Lady Gaga knows how to write music for film. She won an Oscar for “Shallow” from “A Star Is Born,” and she wrote “Harlequin,” an album inspired by her “Joker: Folie à Deux” character Harley Quinn. This week, she landed two Grammy nominations for “Runway,” from “The Devil Wears Prada 2.” She also has the vocal range to deliver jazz standards, as displayed during her Las Vegas Residency, “Jazz and Piano.” Gaga checks the boxes to write a soaring classic 007 theme. — J.T.

Raye, who was recently named Variety’s 2026 Hitmaker of the Year, is one of music’s hottest stars. And yet her voice has a timeless quality which would certainly speak to old-school Bond fans who are looking for a more classically-sounding theme. Plus, her smoky vocals can stand alongside complex arringements, which come with the territory on Bond songs. Plus, she has the secret agent bona fides down, as she’s best known for solving a mystery … after all, where the hell is her husband? — W.E.

We already have a James Bond theme song preview from Rosalía if anyone has listened to “Lux,” her fourth studio album, where she sings in 13 languages, paired with operatic vocals and a thundering choice with Björk. The Spanish-born pop star is incredibly hot at the moment, as shown by pulling in nine Grammy nominations this year and a résumé that includes 13 number-one hits. She can bring the drama, the intrigue and a little flamenco flair to the next 007 adventure. — C.D.

She’s not quite the superstar, yet, that virtually all the others on this list are, but she’s certainly on or approaching that bubble. That the British upstart styles herself like someone right out of a ’60s swinging-London film helps set the mood for thinking of her as a Bond girl: You could visually picture her silhouette fitting right into the title sequence, without having to hire a ringer. But it’s not so much about her nostalgic look — it’s that voice, which one of her obvious inspirations, former Bond theme-stress Adele, has cosigned. The most obvious sign it could or should be Spiro: one great song on her new album, called “Time, You and Me,” already sounds like a classic Bond theme. If it were, it’d be one of the two or three best 007 songs this century. Spiro could surely find another one where that came from. — C.W.

Harry Styles would be the perfect candidate to write the new James Bond theme. Having gone from One Direction breakout to a hugely successful solo performer, Styles can deliver both pop and rock with strong vocals. He also co-wrote the Bleachers song “Alfie’s Song (Not So Typical Love Song)” for the 2018 film “Love, Simon,” but that was more of a pop bop. Think instead of 2017’s “Sign of the Times.” The song is a perfect power ballad. For Bond, Styles would be matched with a large orchestra. And despite a screen test, if he can’t get to play James Bond, delivering a Bond song for the franchise would be the next best thing. — J.T.

Hiring an A-list Bond? Consider an A-list superstar to do the theme. Swift is certainly ambitious enough to consider doing a 007 song, an elite club of music’s best and brightest. She’s certainly open to writing for films, as her current hit “I Knew It, I Knew You” is from “Toy Story 5” — and she’s already hitting the promotional trail to ensure it gets ample Oscar consideration. But we know she could have an interesting take on the drama, intrigue and romance that fuels every Bond song … after all, her catalogue is filled with big emotions. — W.E.

Sure, there’s novelty in getting Pattinson’s longtime fiancée in the mix to sing his theme song. But Waterhouse is a respected musician with three albums and plenty of touring under her belt, and a sultry alto that adds texture to her indie rock hits. Her most recent album, “Loveland” has some complex arrangements, and she’s still able to hold her own, which is necessary as the inevitable Bond theme orchestra swells. Hell, while we’re at it, make her a Bond Girl, too, and tickets will sell like crazy. — W.E.

The Paramore belter has shown, between her band’s genre experimentation and her own freewheeling solo material, that she’s not constrained by sound. Although 007-style bombast would be yet another gear, it’d be fascinating for to try that style on for size. Besides, the Villeneuve Bond will inevitably be edgy: Williams is the perfect choice to bring some grit to the secret agent glam. Besides, Pattinson ushered in an era of the emo Batman … could an emo Bond be next? If so, who better than Williams to sing his feelings! — W.E.
