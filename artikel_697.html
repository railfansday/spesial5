Ava DuVernay to Receive London Critics’ Circle Inaugural Laura Mulvey Female Trailblazer Award

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The newly created honor, which DuVernay will collect from Mulvey herself at the London Critics' Circle Film Awards, recognizes the most influential women in film working to break the glass ceiling.

American filmmaker Ava DuVernay is the first recipient of the London Critics’ Circle’s inaugural Laura Mulvey Female Trailblazer Award.

DuVernay, who this year has taken her most recent documentary, 14th, on the festival circuit, will accept the newly created honor from Mulvey herself at the London Critics’ Circle Film Awards ceremony in the British capital on Feb. 7.

The Laura Mulvey Female Trailblazer Award is designed to recognize “the most influential women in film, who are working to break the glass ceiling, change the cultural conversation, and be an example of what is possible for future generations,” according to London Critics’ Circle. DuVernay’s 14th — set to release in U.K. cinemas Nov. 27 before dropping on Netflix Dec. 4 — is an exploration of American democracy through the lens of the foundational 14th Amendment, exposing an unfinished battle to define America, now taking on drastic new dimensions. “I’ve long been interested in what happens to cinema when more kinds of people have the power to make it and have the access to watch it,” said DuVernay. “How much richer our understanding of the world becomes when we experience it through different voices and perspectives. Laura Mulvey’s work helped shift that conversation in profound ways, and I’m delighted to be the first recipient of an award in her name. Thank you to the LFCC. And here’s to the many women who will follow.”

DuVernay has a vast range of other credits to her name, including film work Selma, 13th, Middle of Nowhere, A Wrinkle in Time and Origin, and groundbreaking series such as When They See Us and Queen Sugar. The Emmy, BAFTA, Sundance, Image and Peabody Award-winning DuVernay is also the creator and driving force behind Array, an arts and social impact collective dedicated to narrative change.

The award is named after film theorist and filmmaker Laura Mulvey, who is herself a member of the LCC. She is widely credited with legitimising feminist film studies as an academic discipline. Mulvey is also renowned not only for films Penthesilea: Queen of the Amazons (1974) and Riddles of the Sphinx (1977), but also for essays and books, the most famous of which is 1975’s Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema.

Chair of the London Critics’ Circle Film section, Jane Crowther, added: “In our industry, there is a plethora of women making exceptional art, but so often budgets, awards, festival line-ups and chances are still disproportionately being awarded to their male counterparts. We are therefore delighted to have introduced this new special award, to honour and celebrate these women’s outstanding contributions.”

“Just like the award’s namesake Laura Mulvey, Ava DuVernay is a true pioneer of the industry,” Crowther added, “from her exceptional filmmaking output to her extensive social impact work through Array, she is a natural choice for the inaugural Female Trailblazer Award.”
