Chelsea and Manchester United lead tributes as Juan Mata announces football retirement

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Your weekly briefing on Chelsea, featuring team news and expert analysis from Dom Smith

Chelsea and Manchester United led the tributes to Juan Mata after he announced his retirement from professional football.

The former Spain international, who lifted the 2010 World Cup and 2012 European Championship, has hung up his boots at the age of 38 after leaving Australian giants Melbourne Victory in September.

Mata started his career at Real Madrid’s academy before moving to Valencia in 2007, where he won the 2008 Copa del Rey alongside fellow future World Cup winners David Silva and David Villa.

He moved to Chelsea in 2011 and helped the Blues win the Champions League and FA Cup in his first season at the club. The diminutive midfielder was crowned Chelsea’s Player of the Year in both of his two full seasons in west London before leaving for Manchester United in January 2014.

Mata helped United win the Europa League, FA Cup and League Cup before his departure in 2022. He then spent the latter years of his career with Turkish side Galatasaray and Vissel Kobe in Japan before he played for Australian sides Western Sydney Wanderers and Melbourne Victory.

Mata announced his retirement with a 50-second video posted on his social media channels.

In the video, Mata sits in a changing room, staring at a pair of football boots. He then turns around and hangs them up on a peg, with the caption: “Sometimes to love is to let go. Thanks, football.”

A full statement was also posted on Mata’s official website, in which he admitted “Football will never leave my life” and that he would continue to use football “as a vehicle for development.”

Mata is best known for co-founding Common Goal, a global movement in which players, coaches, and clubs pledge at least 1% of their salaries to fund grassroots football charities.

“I was able to keep going for so many years because of the sheer joy of playing. Waking up every morning looking forward to training and playing, whether at magnificent grounds such as Old Trafford, Stamford Bridge or Mestalla.

“Or back when I was starting out at Juventud Estadio, where the pitch turned into a mud bath every winter. I believe that kind of passion is fundamental to everything in life.

“Over time, I realised football was about more than just kicking a ball. As I grew older, travelled and lived in different countries, I came to understand the power of sport in general, and football in particular, to change people’s lives.

“Football will never leave my life. There are so many ways to continue loving the game, so many ways to keep using it as a vehicle for development. I may stop playing professionally, but I will never stop being a footballer.”

Manchester United paid tribute to Mata, who scored 51 goals in 285 games for the Red Devils, with a post on social media.

“Great player, better person. Thank you for representing our colours with class, Juan,” they wrote on social media.

Chelsea also posted a tribute to Mata on their social media pages.

“What a player,” Chelsea wrote in response to his retirement announcement.
