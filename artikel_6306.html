White House budget aide Ueland slated to head GAO

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Eric Ueland, No. 2 official at the White House Office of Management and Budget, arrives for his confirmation hearing in the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee on April 3, 2025. (Bill Clark/CQ Roll Call)

President Donald Trump on Monday nominated Eric Ueland, deputy director for management in the White House budget office, to be the next comptroller general to lead the Government Accountability Office.

The nomination fills in the blanks after Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Chairman Rand Paul, R-Ky., had scheduled a Nov. 19 hearing to examine more than a dozen of Trump’s nominees for executive and judicial positions but without identifying who the comptroller general candidate would be. Ueland's name has since been added to the hearing agenda.

The move sets up an unprecedented scenario where a top official of the Office of Management and Budget, a White House office that has tangled with the Government Accountability Office since Trump’s first term, is put forward as the next head of the legislative agency.

The GAO, a congressional watchdog with some 3,000 employees, has repeatedly accused the administration of violating the 1974 budget law by withholding or "impounding" enacted, appropriated funds. In September, for example, the GAO said Trump’s cancellation of $810 million in spending without the approval of Congress through a so-called pocket rescission was illegal.

White House Budget Director Russ Vought was quick to respond on X, writing that GAO “has become hyper partisan and ... they make their legal assessments based on whether Democrats are in the White House.” The administration has dismissed GAO opinions, arguing the agency has no authority over the executive branch because it is part of the legislative branch.

Democrats immediately denounced Ueland's nomination, saying it promised to further inflame tensions between the executive and legislative branches over the power of the purse.

“It would be lunacy for the Senate to hand a 15-year term leading Congress’ nonpartisan watchdog to a Russ Vought acolyte who spends his days plotting new ways for this administration to defy Congress," said Senate Appropriations ranking member Patty Murray, D-Wash., who is likely to chair the panel next year.

"Ueland orchestrated the president’s destructive mass firing campaign, is presiding over the razing of USAID, and is the last person who should be leading GAO," Murray said in a statement.

Sen. Gary Peters, D-Mich., ranking member of the Homeland Security committee, urged all senators to oppose the Ueland nomination, saying it would provide “further power to the Executive Branch at a time when independent oversight from Congress is needed more than ever.”

Peters also reiterated his previous criticism of Republicans for skipping bipartisan deliberations by a special 10-member legislative commission that is charged under statute with recommending candidates to the president for nomination.

“All three times the Commission process has occurred in the past, the president has selected a nominee with bipartisan support,” Peters said.

In a sign of potential trouble for the nomination, a few Republicans expressed qualms about Ueland and what critics described as a rushed process for picking a GAO chief.

"I'm not a fan," said retiring Sen. Thom Tillis, R-N.C., who faulted Ueland's boss for not providing proof of the cost savings claimed by Trump's Department of Government Efficiency, known as DOGE.

"To me, there's a lack of discipline or business experience in there to answer that simple question, or the reason they don't answer it is they don't have an answer," Tillis said. "Both are bad."

While Tillis stopped short of promising to oppose Ueland, he said the nomination "deserves individual consideration" instead of being taken up in a group with several other nominees.

And Sen. John Curtis, R-Utah, "has concerns that the nomination is too partisan, particularly given how independent and long-term the appointment of the Comptroller General is," a spokesman said in a statement.

Ueland has served as deputy director for management at the White House budget office since last year following his confirmation by the Senate on a party line 52-45 vote. He also has been performing the duties of the OMB controller since September 2025, according to his LinkedIn profile.

An expert on legislative procedure and strategy, Ueland has worked in a wide range of administrative and policy-oriented positions in the executive branch — including at OMB, the State Department and White House — and before that in the Senate.

He served as director of legislative affairs for Trump in his first term, as well as the State Department undersecretary for civilian security, democracy and human rights, and director of the department’s Office of U.S. Foreign Assistance, among other positions.

Before that, he was the Senate Budget Committee’s GOP staff director under then-Chairman Michael B. Enzi, R-Wyo., for four years through the end of 2017.

Earlier, he worked as chief of staff to then-Senate GOP Whip Don Nickles of Oklahoma and then-Senate Majority Leader Bill Frist of Tennessee.

Ueland, who has a bachelor’s degree in history from the University of San Francisco, lacks the financial background of recent past comptrollers. He has, however, gone deep in the weeds with numbers at OMB and the Senate Budget Committee, and has extensive experience as an administrator.

Former comptroller Charles A. Bowsher was a CPA, a partner at Arthur Anderson and assistant secretary of the Navy for financial management before President Ronald Reagan tapped him to lead the GAO in 1981.

David M. Walker, his successor, was a CPA, a partner at Arthur Anderson, assistant secretary of labor in the Employee Benefits Security Administration, and deputy executive director of the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation before his nomination by President Bill Clinton.

Gene L. Dodaro, whose 15-year term ended last year, had a bachelor’s degree in accounting and had worked at GAO for some 35 years before President Barack Obama nominated him to succeed Walker.

When Dodaro left, he designated Orice Williams Brown, who was GAO’s chief operating officer, as acting comptroller.

Before Trump nominated Ueland, the six Republican members of the legislative commission sent a letter to the White House last week recommending three candidates for the position — Ueland, Brian C. DeBusk and Roland Foster, according to sources.

DeBusk is president and CEO of DeRoyal Industries, a privately held medical equipment manufacturer. He served on the Medicare Payment Advisory Commission from 2016 to 2022 after being appointed by Dodaro. Foster is a longtime GOP aide currently serving as senior adviser to retiring Sen. Joni Ernst, R-Iowa.
