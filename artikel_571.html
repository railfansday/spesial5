NBC Pulls Spot Advocating for End of U.S. Military Aid to Israel, Will Review Approval Procedures After Outcry

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

NBC has pulled an ad from a Middle East advocacy organization that calls for the end of U.S. military aid to Israel. The network will review its ad approval procedures after criticism from the Simon Wiesenthal Center and other organizations.

The spot, purchased by the Institute for Middle East Understanding Policy Project, pushes for the end of U.S. military aid to Israel amid the humanitarian crisis in Gaza. The spot titled “Every Three Seconds” ran Nov. 25 on NBC in primetime during the network’s “Saturday Night Live” Thanksgiving special. The ad makes a jarring juxtaposition between the suffering in Gaza and scenes of a typical American preteen girl doing chores and odd jobs to save money for a bicycle amid tough economic times. Just as the American girl brings her jar full of money into a bike shop, the spot cuts to a scene of a girl standing in a pile of rubble in Gaza. As the camera pans back, the girl is revealed to be leaning on a crutch, having lost one of her lower legs. The spot tells viewers that “Every three seconds” the U.S. sends about $400 million in military aid to Israel under a long-term deal set to expire in 2028.

NBC’s top brass were unaware of the spot before it aired. As a result, there will be a review of the network’s ad approval processes, according to a person familiar with the situation.

Jim Berk, CEO of the Wiesenthal Center, issued a lengthy statement on Thursday questioning NBC’s decision to air the ad during an “SNL” entertainment special in primetime. He pointed to the ad’s potential to fuel hostility against Israel and Jews.

“The advertisement’s imagery, an American child’s hardship set against the anguish of a child in Gaza, offers viewers a seductive but unsubstantiated narrative: that the struggles of American families are somehow bound up with U.S. support for Israel,” Berk stated. “In collapsing a complex foreign policy into a single, emotionally charged image, it erases the deeper realities, including the imperative to protect Israeli lives and the very existence of the country itself. By manipulating emotion and stripping it of context, this ad seeks to become a catalyst for hostility. … Without scrutiny, insinuations and half-truths are allowed to seep into the consciousness of millions, unchallenged and unchecked. Turning children’s suffering into a prop for political theater is an unacceptable manipulation.”

The Institute for Middle East Understanding Policy Project did not immediately respond to a request for comment on NBC’s decision. It’s unclear how many more times the spot had been scheduled to run on NBCUniversal platforms. According to the organization’s website, IMEU “was founded in 2005 by a group of concerned Americans who want to foster an increased understanding among Americans about Palestine and the Palestinians.”
