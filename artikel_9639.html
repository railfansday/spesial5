Bothy: Building Verifiable AI Decisions With Arweave Receipts

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Every winter, somewhere in the UK, a person with a pager has to decide whether to close a mountain road. Picture a Cumbria County Council winter duty officer with the A5094 and the B5311 on their desk: narrow, steep, single-lane passes that ice fast and plough badly. Close too early and you strand a valley of commuters and farms. Close too late and a family of four meets a snowdrift on a blind bend.

The Met Office warnings, the gritting schedule, the gradient map and the incident history all exist, scattered across systems that were never joined. The officer has to hold it all in their head under a deadline and be personally accountable for the call.

That is the decision Bothy is built for. It is an open-source, evidence-first agent. It watches a curated evidence graph (Met Office warnings, live weather, route topology, incident records) and produces a cited risk assessment: a score, a label, a draft for the duty officer, and the reasoning chain behind every claim, each linked to a real data point. Then it does what dashboards don't: it hands the decision to a named human, who approves or rejects it, and it keeps the whole trail.

The same agent has a second surface. For supply-chain analysts it produces defence briefs, cited investigations of what would happen to a logistics programme if a dependency went dark, reviewed by a named reviewer before anything counts as decided.

The interesting problem here isn't the AI. It's what happens after.

Every "AI safety" feature in every dashboard I've seen asks you to take the vendor's word for it. The model says it cited. The platform says it audited. The log says the approval happened. But when a duty officer's name goes on a closure, or an analyst's name goes on a risk call, the question that matters is: can anyone independently verify that this exact output existed at this exact time, and that nobody edited it before the human signed?

Usually the evidence behind a decision evaporates. Logs rotate, dashboards version, decisions get re-edited. So my answer this week was to stop asking for trust and start publishing fingerprints.

At the two moments that matter, when the agent generates an output and when a human decides on it, Bothy anchors a SHA-256 receipt to Arweave mainnet. The record itself never leaves our systems. Only its fingerprint does, permanently.

Open the case link and click Verify. Your browser fetches the anchored receipt, recomputes the fingerprint of the record on screen with crypto.subtle, and compares the two. My server is not trusted for the answer, and that is the whole point.

A receipt on generation proves the AI's output existed, unaltered, at time T. That is table stakes.

The second moment is the human decision. The decision receipt chains back to the generation receipt (subjectDigest plus generationTx), records the decision and its timestamp, and stores the reviewer's identity only as a hash: committed, not revealed. On the defence-brief side it also pins the head of the case's hash-chained audit log.

So the anchored statement isn't "an AI said X on Tuesday." It is "a named responsibility-holder signed off on X on Wednesday, and the audit trail behind it hashes to this value." If anyone later edits the brief, the draft or the log, recomputation fails loudly.

This was the subtle part. The naive version, sha256(JSON.stringify(assessment)), doesn't survive contact with a real database. Postgres real columns round-trip a few ulps off the in-memory value, jsonb doesn't preserve key order, and fields like status and decidedAt legitimately change after anchoring.

So the digest covers a canonical payload: immutable fields only, keys sorted recursively, scores rounded to four decimals.

Because the agent and the browser share the same canonicalization module, a judge recomputing in devtools gets the same fingerprint the server anchored. It is tamper-evident by construction, not by promise.

Three things went wrong on the way here, and they're worth more to readers than the wins.

The duty officer's problem isn't "AI." It's that when their name goes on a closure, the proof of why usually disappears. Bothy makes that impossible by design: the agent produces the draft, the human owns the call, and Arweave holds the receipt. The accountability belongs to the person. The proof belongs to nobody. It's just there, permanently, for anyone to check.
