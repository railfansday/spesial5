New York, USA, November 10th, 2026/CyberNewswire/--Kali Linux. OSCP. Try Harder. For 20 years, OffSec has set the standard for the tools security practitioners use, the skills they prove, and the mindset they bring to the work. This month OffSec is marking its 20th anniversary as a global leader in hands-on cybersecurity training and certifications for individuals and enterprise security teams.

"Our founders built an incredible brand and an incredible product,” said OffSec CEO Ning Wang. “Our mission has stayed the same. We develop critical thinkers, cyber warriors, and problem solvers who are ready to excel in their jobs under stress. Hands-on is the only way we believe you can effectively train someone.”

OffSec began in 2006 when cybercrime was becoming big business. OffSec grew out of the security community, founded by practitioners who believed the best way to teach cybersecurity was to share what they'd learned through hands-on experience. That origin story still shapes how OffSec develops cybersecurity practitioners and teams. Through hands-on training with real systems, learners build practical skills, strengthen their problem-solving abilities, and gain the confidence to tackle challenges when the obvious approach fails.

BackTrack Linux brought penetration testing tools together in one open-source platform. In 2013, OffSec rebuilt it as Kali Linux, a free environment for penetration testing, research, forensics, and reverse engineering. In 2023, Kali Purple extended it to defenders.

OSCP earned its reputation as the gold standard in penetration testing certifications because candidates must work through live systems, adapt their approach, and document what they accomplish. Employers trust it as evidence of real capability. For many practitioners, it is a career-defining milestone. OSCP+ added a renewable credential in 2024. Now, for a limited time, individual practitioners can save 20% off of Learn One annual subscription.

The Research and Practice They Learn From

In 2009, OffSec launched the Exploit Database, a non-profit, public-service archive of public exploits, proof-of-concept (PoC) code, shellcode, and security papers, which it still maintains as a free resource. It also acquired the open-source training project VulnHub, offers free labs in Proving Grounds Play, and recently added HackTrack. HackTrack offers live weekly training over a 10-week stretch. The first round of HackTrack focused on OSCP and was offered free to those with a Learn One or Course and Certification Bundle. A new HackTrack focused on OSAI is set to launch soon.

Along the way, Try Harder became the security community's shorthand for the persistence and dedication that a career in cybersecurity demands. In October 2026 at Singapore International Cyber Week, CEO Wang urged her Women in Cyber keynote audience to do hard things early that build confidence and resilience, noting, “If you don’t struggle through something, you will never become really great at it,” she said.

Attackers now use AI to discover and weaponize vulnerabilities at speeds unimaginable 20 years ago. The time between a vendor patch and a working exploit continues to shrink. Cybersecurity skills gaps remain a growing challenge. According to ISC2’s 2025 Cybersecurity Workforce Study, 95% of organizations report at least one skills gap, with 59% describing those gaps as critical or significant, up from 44% in 2024. These findings reinforce the need to move beyond once-a-year training toward continuous, hands-on skill development, an approach rooted in OffSec’s philosophy since day one.

“Frontier AI models are evolving at unbelievable speed. If you don’t keep up, you won’t be able to do the job,” said CEO Wang. “Certification still assesses capability. But you need to constantly upskill your teams so they are ready to defend your organization. It is simply no longer a nice-to-do. It’s a must-do.”

OffSec has grown from a specialist offensive security provider into a full cybersecurity learning and workforce development portfolio. Every course teaches the why, the what, and the how so practitioners learn to think through problems they have never seen.
