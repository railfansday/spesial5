7 Modern Western Shows That Are 10/10 Masterpieces

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

When it comes to the Western genre on television, the classics are always the best. Old-school programs like Gunsmoke, Bonanza, and The Virginian are still so beloved for their timeless charm and almost mythic sensibilities that speak firmly to the traditional values of the genre. But if you go beyond the 20th century, the Western has adapted to the tastes of modern viewers, and it's with those in mind that we've rounded up some of the best period masterworks that bring the Old West (and the modern West) to life.

Now, to run that elephant out of the room ahead of time, you won't find Deadwood on this list. No apologies will be offered here, despite the protests that may ensure. With only seven slots to choose from, it fell off the list and into television jail, with no possibility of parole. Nevertheless, we hope you'll give these other stellar Western (or neo-Western) television shows a go, because they each have their own merits that make them worthy of the masterwork label.

Although Taylor Sheridan's contributions to the genre are not without their controversy, there is no denying that 1883 is his seminal work. Yellowstone may be his most popular, but 1883 feels like Sheridan's own take on a groundbreaking epic like Lonesome Dove, as he chronicles the Duttons' initial journey from Fort Worth, Texas, to Paradise Valley, Montana. It's a breathtaking endeavor, one full of tragedy and heartbreak, love and loss, wonder and excitement. There's nothing made in recent years that's been quite like it.

1883 infuses real Western history with a fictional account of what it was like for wagon trains headed northward into the State of Oregon. With a phenomenal cast that includes Sam Elliott, Isabel May, Tim McGraw, and Faith Hill, among others, Sheridan's 10-episode masterpiece is everything about the Old West that you can dream. It's one of those Westerns worth watching over and over.

The Utah War may not be a Western conflict often touched on in the genre, but The Revenant screenwriter Mark L. Smith goes above and beyond for this provocative take on the true history associated with the Mountains Meadow Massacre. American Primeval is a riveting story of Sarah Holloway (Betty Gilpin) as she seeks to escape with her son into the wild frontier, led only by distant mountain man Isaac Reed (Taylor Kitsch). The results are brutal, gripping, and unrelenting, and viewer discretion is certainly advised on account of violence.

Yet, this was the truth about how bloody the West could be, and American Primeval is an exceptional Western miniseries that pulls no punches. Historical figures like Jim Bridger (Shea Whigham) and Brigham Young (Kim Coates) are heavily featured, as the Mormon militia comes in contact with the local Shoshone tribe. While some have called for a sequel, these six episodes stand tall enough on their own.

Produced by Steven Spielberg himself, Into the West is an underrated TNT drama that attempts to cover the majority of the 19th century in the span of six 90-minute episodes — and it does so quite mightily. The narrative follows the Wheeler family and their Native American counterparts as they experience the settling of the West from differing perspectives and points of view. Eventually, the families unite in Jacob Wheeler (Matthew Settle) and his wife, Thunder Heart Woman (Tonantzin Carmelo), who bridge these peoples together.

As far as televised epics go, Into the West deserves its place in the annals of Western history. The six-part miniseries boasts a truly stacked cast that includes folks like Josh Brolin, Wes Studi, Keri Russell, Zahn McClarnon, Graham Greene, Sean Astin, and many more, playing mixtures of historical figures and original characters. It's a criminally underrated Western series that accurately explores the trials and tribulations of those who bled and died for this land, no matter which side of the conflict they fell on.

Okay, now some may consider Justified a bit of a stretch here, and perhaps rightfully so. After all, it takes place east of the Mississippi in rural Kentucky. Nevertheless, U.S. Deputy Marshal Raylan Givens (Timothy Olyphant) is everything you'd expect from a Western hero; he just happens to reside a little bit further east, is all. To make a greater case, Raylan himself cites episodes of Rawhide and Have Gun – Will Travel as influences on his chosen profession and penchant for cowboy hats, so we think he ought to get a pass.

Justified is based loosely on the works of Western crime author Elmore Leonard, which likewise gives Justified an Old West-branded edge. For six seasons, Raylan has performed his duty to uphold the law in Harlan County, no matter the personal injury or cost. His lawman-style duels are some of the coolest on television, and there's a reason he ranks high on our list of the greatest gunslingers in Western TV history. Even if you're unsure if Justified should be considered a neo-Western, Olyphant's performance here rivals (and arguably exceeds) his work on Deadwood.

Speaking of neo-Western crime dramas, one that is far more unashamedly Western in nature (not to mention location) is Longmire. Another six-season take on a modern lawman with true Old West sensibilities, this A&E-turned-Netflix drama was based on an ongoing series of mystery novels by author Craig Johnson. The show, however, is a loose adaptation that follows the titular Sheriff Walt Longmire (Robert Taylor), who patrols Absaroka County, Wyoming, best as he knows how — and it's perfect from start to finish.

Not only does Walt engage in the typical shoot-'em-up action bits that make up these sorts of programs, but as an educated and intelligent lawman, he often finds ways around resorting to violence (justified or otherwise) to see his duty through. Many of his adventures take him and his deputies to the home of his best friend, Henry Standing Bear (Lou Diamond Phillips) on the local Cheyenne Reservation, contributing to the age-old conflict between "cowboys and Indians." If there was ever a Western series set in modern-day that aimed to capture the spirit of those classic tales, it would be Longmire.

Created by former Longmire writer Tony Tost, Damnation is a heavily underrated historical drama that deserves its place here. Although it takes place during the Great Depression — a few decades after the Old West era — it's a period mix between a historical Western and a neo-Western that settles on the same themes and ideas. When political preacher Seth Davenport (Killian Scott) shows up in Holden County to make a stand, it puts him at odds with a familiar strike-breaker, Creeley Turner (Logan Marshall-Green), who does all he can to tear the movement down.

Damnation was once described by Tost to The Lafayette Flyer as "1/3 Clint Eastwood, 1/3 John Steinbeck, 1/3 James Ellroy," and that about sums it up. The Netflix/Universal co-production only ran a single season of 10 episodes, but there was plenty of meaty material within that inspired a cult following. Damnation may not have been a long-running series, but it's easily one of the grittiest Western shows out there that had something a bit more philosophical to say. That said, just like American Primeval, viewer discretion is seriously advised.

What began as an Epix original eventually moved to MGM+ and spanned three seasons total, retelling the mythic history of the Wild West's most legendary and memorable outlaw. Billy the Kid was created by Michael Hirst and followed Tom Blythe's portrayal of the notorious gunslinger. At the advent of the Lincoln County War, Billy and his Regulators find themselves nestled on the side of the conflict that just so happens to be opposite the secretive Santa Fe Ring. The results are explosive, and not for the faint of heart.

As far as historical accuracy goes, Billy the Kid changed plenty of things from the real-life story, but the narrative Hirst weaves makes it all worth it. It may not be a perfect Western series in that regard, but the MGM+ drama will grip you tightly through all three seasons, revealing a side to the infamous Kid that doesn't always come out on the screen. History buffs may be a bit disappointed (and perhaps rightly so), but if you're looking for a guns-a-blazing Western adventure, Billy the Kid may be right for you.
