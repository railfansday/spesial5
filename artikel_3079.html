If a Bear Market Is Coming, History Says This Warren Buffett-Approved Investment Is the Smartest Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

For years, Warren Buffett has suggested one fund above all others.

By David Dierking – Nov 18, 2026 at 7:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Vanguard S&P 500 ETF ›

Many people view a bear market as an opportunity to sell stocks. After all, if the market is about to drop 20% or more, why would you want your portfolio to ride it on the way down?

The reason is that no one knows if or when a bear market might arrive. In the end, many folks thought the declines would continue after stocks and long-term Treasuries both fell more than 20% at the same time. Instead, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.38%) posted three straight years of double-digit gains, bigger gains for tech stocks, and another 11% year-to-date gain in 2026.

That's why Warren Buffett advocates a simple, long-term buy-and-hold approach for most investors, using the S&P 500. Even during bear markets, holding and continuing to accumulate shares of the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -0.35%) has been the best strategy for maximizing the opportunities presented in severe drawdowns.

Former Berkshire CEO Warren Buffett. Image source: Getty Images.

In 2013, Warren Buffett famously suggested that for his wife's eventual inheritance, he instructed his advisor to put 90% of the money into an S&P 500 index fund ("I suggest Vanguard's.") and the remaining 10% in Treasury bills.

The logic is straightforward. An ultra-low-cost S&P 500 index fund provides broad exposure to the U.S. economy's biggest companies and allows participation in their long-term growth stories. The cash position gives you the means to fund additional purchases in your portfolio, ideally at lower prices, and does so without requiring you to sell stocks at a loss.

There's a big difference between weakness in individual companies and weakness in the economy.

When there's a recession, all companies are affected, even the largest and most successful. But that doesn't mean the long-term fundamental strength of these companies has necessarily changed. The economic environment as a whole goes through a period of weakness, as it does from time to time. Sometimes the economy is strong. Sometimes it's not. It's normal behavior when you own something economically sensitive.

It's because these swings are normal that you want to hold the S&P 500 throughout them. If a company is on the path to bankruptcy, you have a case for selling that stock. But you don't want to essentially sell off the entire U.S. economy because it's going through a temporary period of weakness. You get the good and the bad when you invest in stocks, and that's why long-term buy-and-hold is so important. This is Buffett's point of view as well.

The best approach is usually to just keep doing what you're doing. Avoid the temptation to sell because you see stock prices going down. Keep contributing to your investment accounts monthly. Make sure your portfolio allocation aligns with your personal goals.

Buffett's past statements suggest that the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF would be the ideal product to accomplish this.
