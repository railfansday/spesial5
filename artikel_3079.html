Bloom Energy Is Down 50% From Its High. Should You Buy the Dip?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bloom has lost half its value. Is this a buying opportunity, or should you stay away?

By Steven Porrello – Nov 20, 2026 at 9:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bloom Energy ›

My, how the tables have turned on Bloom Energy (BE -7.46%), a stock that traded above $340 as recently as this June. Today, Bloom trades at about $172.

Obviously, when a stock drops 50% while also experiencing surging revenue growth (138% year over year in the third quarter) and raising full-year revenue guidance for several quarters in a row, the question naturally arises: Is this a buying opportunity?

The thing is, Bloom's extraordinary growth has come at an extraordinary cost. Last quarter, it reported $225 million in operating income, yet it also burned through $27 million in operating cash and spent roughly $153 million in capital expenditures. All in all, free cash flow was negative by about $181 million.

Lucky for Bloom, it has about $2.5 billion in cash and equivalents, so it's not up a creek without a paddle. Still, I believe it's important to distinguish between a company that's growing quickly and one growing profitably. This is especially pertinent for Bloom, whose capital requirements have historically been substantial and whose ability to generate sustainable profits and free cash flow has long been a question.

It's also important to consider that Bloom stock, even after its 50% decline, still trades at roughly 11 times its projected 2026 revenue -- a sizable valuation for a company whose primary customers, data center developers, could face delays if the enormous investments in AI fail to deliver the returns expected of them.

Bloom might be trading lower than it was over the summer, but it's not necessarily a bargain. I would continue to treat it as a growth stock with the potential to supply power to data centers, but with plenty of challenges to overcome before that growth translates into profits.
