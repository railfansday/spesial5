All 8 Episodes of 'VisionQuest,' Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Spoiler Alert: This list contains spoilers for the VisionQuest finale.With VisionQuest finally finishing up its limited series run, Marvel fans are extremely happy. The finale to the trilogy that began with WandaVision is making its rounds as a fan-favorite Disney+ series. This is all thanks to the fact that the show has all the makings of a good superhero television series, while still staying extremely true to itself.

The character of Vision (Paul Bettany) has been through the ringer since his birth in Avengers: Age of Ultron, and while many had thought his story ended with his death in Avengers: Infinity War, the likes of WandaVision revived him (in a way). While, at first, there were lots of people who weren't happy about this new Vision being introduced, VisionQuest's eight awesome episodes have given them ample reason to be.

VisionQuest Episode 5, "Firewall" is a bit more of a sit-down-and-breathe episode, so it takes itself a tad slower than the rest of the others. This doesn't mean it's any kind of snooze-fest, though. Episode 5 is a lot more character-driven, especially when it comes to the likes of antagonists Jocasta (T'Nia Miller) and Ultron (James Spader). This fifth entry does quite a bit of legwork to make them more understandable as characters moving into the latter half of the season.

Having good, relatable, and sympathetic characters as antagonists is wildly important, and the Marvel Cinematic Universe can either be really good at this, or quite bad. Episode 5 makes it so VisionQuest truly succeeds on the antagonist front (even though the true villain of the series had yet to be truly introduced). It's not a perfect episode, and sometimes the pacing really keeps it from being everything it could be. But, all-in-all, it's still a decent episode that develops the characters effectively.

The first few episodes of a new show are extremely important. If the audiences are hooked by the first or second episode, the crew behind the series needs to make sure that hook stays and that was the important job that VisionQuest Episode 3, "Reboot" had. Thankfully, things are really kicking into high gear by this point in the plot, with Tommy Shepherd/Maximoff (Ruaridh Mollica) and Vision finally paired up and working together in full force.

The third entry also further develops the mystery behind whom the so-called "Butcherbird" is, and why they have been killing so specifically. This mystery truly engages viewers and keeps them guessing. This, combined with the great character work written between Tommy and Vision, makes Episode 3 a great continuation to the borderline perfect introduction that was in the first two episodes (which dropped together).

A good pilot for a television show is everything. If a pilot fails, the potential continued viewership of the show drops drastically. While the MCU has pre-installed fans that will watch whatever they put out, a bad pilot can still crash the plane and detract even the most dedicated viewer. Thankfully, director Christopher J. Byrne did a wonderful job bringing this new story to viewers.

Byrne reintroduces the audience to Vision and where he's been in the time since WandaVision with prowess and grace. Not to mention, it wastes no time introducing all the main players and the conflict. This faster pacing doesn't keep it from feeling too fast, though, and it still takes ample time to slow down and establish these characters, where they're at in their journeys, and what's to come. VisionQuest Episode 1, "No Strings" is a great example of an effective beginning.

The truth of Tommy's past is a mystery (to him) that lingers over his entire character arc. That presence is very prominent in VisionQuest Episode 6, "Deadlock" as they head to Madripoor to meet with Paladin (Todd Stashwick) for a tense alliance. If it isn't obvious from online discussion, the son of Vision is a fan-favorite character in the series, and this is a big episode for his character arc.

VisionQuest does a great job making reveals while still maintaining as much mystery as they can, and it doesn't just do that with Tommy's past in the sixth installment, but regarding the mysterious man behind all the shenanigans thus far. Not to mention, it's got a wonderful MCU callback cameo in Faran Tahir returning as Raza for the first time since Iron Man in 2008. A super fun inclusion that awards MCU fans who have been here since the beginning and still made it this far into such an expansive universe.

Four villains, and four arguments the films were honest enough to actually let them make. Here is the one you lean towards, the part of it that holds up, and the exact point where it stops holding up.

You align with the one who was right about the problem and wrong about the solution. Killmonger is the most persuasive villain Marvel has produced because the film never pretends his anger came from nowhere.

You align with the protector, which is the most understandable and most dangerous position on this list. Namor is not chasing power or revenge, he is doing whatever keeps his people hidden, and he has been doing it for centuries.

You align with the patient one, the only man on this list with no powers, no army and no hurry. Zemo lost his family in Sokovia and then spent years working out that you do not fight the Avengers, you introduce them to each other's secrets.

You align with the one who decided the answer was arithmetic. Thanos is not angry and not grieving in any way he will admit to, he simply reached a conclusion and never once revisited it, which is precisely what makes him frightening.

Just as a good pilot is important, a good finale is just as important—if not more so. VisionQuest had a lot it needed to accomplish by the end of the runtime of VisionQuest Episode 8, "Forever" and, thankfully, it managed to execute on it. MCU Disney+ finales have definitely struggled in the past, so this story had a lot to prove, with high stakes. There was a lot to be screwed up here, and, if everyone on the team—cast and crew alike—didn't give their A game, it could have turned out catastrophically. A bad finale can tarnish how people view a series as a whole forever, unfortunately.

The show's first seven episodes are incredibly strong, so to fumble in the final hour would have been horrific for the show's reputation and overall quality. "Forever" wraps up every plot thread relatively well, being able to weave the plots together into one cohesively. Everyone's character arcs feel like they've reached a natural conclusion, and the way that Vision finally bests Ultron is quite creative, as it doesn't rely on just action to wrap the conflict up. Plus, it gets huge points for finally bringing the Maximoff boys together, with Billy Maximoff (Joe Locke) making an appearance to help Tommy revive their father.

VisionQuest Episode 1, "No Strings" focuses mainly on introducing Vision and his AI crew, but they're not the only protagonists of this show. On the other hand, VisionQuest Episode 2, "Theseus", takes its entire runtime to introduce the character of Tommy and develop his side of this world and his character arc. Ever since WandaVision and Agatha All Along, people have been wondering where he's at. Is he alive? Where did he end up? What does he even look like?

This second episode answers all those questions. So, in a way, this is a bit of a "second pilot"—a job that it wonderfully succeeds at. The young man was always going to be one of the most important characters of this show, so giving him an entire episode to be introduced and established was an incredible call on the VisionQuest team's part. This is especially true with the great direction, as per usual, from Christopher J. Byrne.

Comic book fans were abundantly excited when it was announced that the awesome villain, Jocasta, was going to be making her MCU debut in VisionQuest. If there's an episode to consider the "Jocasta Episode," it would be VisionQuest Episode 4, "Malware" as it's her big introduction and the reveal of her major plans for the future. This episode also throws a bit of a wrench in the father and son's relationship, as the young speedster discovers that Vision wasn't wholly honest about who resides in his mind.

With Tommy putting it together that Ultron is in Vision's mind, he's obviously not very happy with his father. Things get worse, however, when Jocasta makes her move, and Vision is forced into letting Ultron take control to stop her—which, to no one's surprise, does not go well. Tommy also gets to finally show off his true speed in the climax of this episode, which is abundantly cool to finally see on display.

A good penultimate episode needs to tee up what's to come in the big finale, and—genuinely—VisionQuest Episode 7, "Immolation" is one of the best penultimate Disney+ MCU episodes of all time. The way this seventh episode sets up what's to come and reveals such massive information to the audience is masterful in every way. It truly puts Vision and Ultron at odds, brings Jocasta one step closer to her goal, sets Tommy up for a huge arc conclusion, and so much more.

VisionQuest Episode 7, "Immolation" has everything that makes this show as great as it is. It has some of the most raw, vulnerable dialogue and character moments out there that bring these characters to where they need to be perfectly. It's got action that ups the ante with spectacle and wonder. Not to mention, the visuals continue to be astounding, thanks to Director of Photography, Neville Kidd. With this in mind, there's just no question that the penultimate entry to the VisionQuest journey is the best the show has to offer. The stakes are set, the characters are ready, and the crew executed everything they needed to with so much prowess.
