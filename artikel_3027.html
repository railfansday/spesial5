Grants don’t have to be repaid, but the money can only go toward school-related costs. Here’s how to use grants to pay for school and how to make them go further.

Grants are one of the best ways you can pay for your education. It’s free money you aren’t expected to pay back.

Grants can be used for tuition, course materials and other cost-of-attendance expenses. Some grants, depending on who awards them, require an application or have requirements you must meet before you can get your money.

Here’s how to use a college grant and what it can pay for.

Federal grants come from the federal government.

State grants come from state programs and often can only be used at schools in that state.

Institutional grants come from your college or university, or even your program or major.

Private grants come from nonprofits, community organizations and individual donors.

To receive a federal grant, you must complete the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA). Most states require the FAFSA for state grants, and a few require a separate state application. You’ll need to complete the FAFSA for each year you’re enrolled in school. You may need to meet certain eligibility requirements, such as income limits, to get some grants. The FAFSA is how you apply for the Pell Grant, which is a financial need-based grant. The FAFSA also determines eligibility for federal work-study, which is a job program rather than a grant.

Federal aid, including federal grants, typically requires students to meet satisfactory academic progress. This means you have to maintain a minimum grade point average and be on track to finish your degree on time, as determined by your school. Your school also sets how many credits you must take to qualify for grant funding.

Some grants — typically private or institutional grants — require an additional application that asks for transcripts, letters of recommendation and personal essays. Your school’s financial aid office or the grant provider will have details on applying for specific grants.

After you submit the FAFSA, you’ll receive a financial aid award letter from each school you applied to, which outlines how much in federal, state and institutional grants you’ve been offered. You must accept your aid to receive it.

Most grants will be disbursed directly to your college or university. Your school will apply the grant money first to tuition, fees and any other charges it bills, such as housing or a meal plan.

In some cases, you may receive more grant money than your school bills, which is the best-case scenario. If that happens to you, any leftover funds will likely be refunded to you. Refund timelines vary by school, so it may take some time to get your money. You can get your refund faster by setting up direct deposit with your school. You can then use the funds for other costs approved by your grant — some examples are detailed below.

If you have questions about your grant money, contact your college’s financial aid office.

Grants can help cover cost-of-attendance expenses. Generally, you can use grant money to pay for tuition, textbooks, class materials, transportation, housing and food. Depending on your school’s policies, you can use grant money to cover summer classes or study abroad programs, too.

Some grants come with specific terms about how you can spend them. Check your financial aid award letter or, for a private or institutional grant, the school’s or organization’s website to find specifics.

After your grant is applied to your tuition and school expenses, you can options to fill the gaps between what the grant covers and the total cost of your program. Some options include savings, scholarships, work-study, payment plans, federal student loans or, as a last resort, private loans. By using grants to pay for your college education, you may be able to borrow less.

To maximize the grants you’re eligible to receive, enter accurate information on the FAFSA and complete it as soon as you can — some funds are first-come, first-served. Also, don’t limit yourself to federal or state grants — search and apply for as many privately funded grants as you can find.

You don’t have to pay back a grant as long as you continue to meet all requirements for the grant, such as staying enrolled. You can use grant money on school and school-related expenses. Some grants may require you to meet eligibility requirements such as a minimum GPA or state residency. If you receive more grant money than your cost of attendance for a given school term, ask your financial aid office if you can put that grant money toward next year’s cost of attendance.
