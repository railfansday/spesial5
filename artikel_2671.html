Everyone on the internet loves to overreact, and that's especially true when we're talking about the NFL. Zach Pereles looked at several things that happened during Week 9, so decide for yourself whether we're all overreacting.

Statement: The Browns should stick with Shedeur Sanders as their starting QB.Overreaction or reality: Reality. The 23-22 loss to the Saints will leave a bad taste in the mouth after what was otherwise a solid performance, especially given the circumstances. Cleveland's offensive line was abysmal, as it has been much of the year, and the run game didn't help at all. There were also multiple drops. But Sanders delivered several things Deshaun Watson simply doesn't. How about three completions on 30-plus-yard throws downfield? Watson has one ... all year! Sanders also did excellently in several moments navigating the pressure. Coaches rarely bench players due to injury, so I wouldn't be surprised if Watson returns to the lineup. Todd Monken refused to talk about it postgame. But in this writer's eyes, Sanders should keep the job.

Statement: Matthew Stafford is holding back the Rams.Overreaction or reality: Reality. The Rams are above .500 for the first time this year after outlasting the Commanders, 20-13. The Rams force-fed Puka Nacua (19 targets for 14 catches, 150 yards and a touchdown) and got just enough for the run game. Myles Garrett came back in full force with three sacks and a forced fumble. At 5-4 and winners of three straight -- with the Cardinals up next -- Los Angeles should feel OK. But Matthew Stafford just does not look good. Plain and simple. He threw an awful interception after locking in on Nacua, with no chance of completion. Stafford missed a ton of throws he made routinely during his 2025 MVP campaign. Sunday alone, he missed an open Tyler Higbee on an out-breaking route; he missed Tutu Atwell twice -- once wide, once high -- and missed Nacua once, too, this time low. The Rams can still be a good team, but at some point, they will need Stafford to play like his 2025 self, and it just doesn't look like he has that anymore.

Statement: The NFC East race is over. Overreaction or reality: Overreaction. The Giants' win puts them at 5-2-1 on the season, a truly remarkable start. J.J. McCarthy wasn't good, but he didn't have any huge mistakes. That's enough against a team like the Eagles. The Cowboys avoided a DEFCON 1-level loss, surviving a mistake-riddled game to beat the Colts 27-23. Dallas lost a pair of fumbles in awful moments, committed several terrible penalties, and seemed to spend half the game trying to give it away. But they won and now sit at 4-5. Yes, that's 2.5 games back of New York, but it's not impossible. I don't think the Cowboys are very good, but they might not need to be very good to win this division. A Week 17 showdown between the two teams still looms large, even if Dallas underwhelmed very much (again) in Week 9.

One notable injury for the Bills is left tackle Dion Dawkins (ribs). He didn't practice all week and is listed as questionable. If he can't go, that will be a huge hit, with the Bills set to face one of the best defenses in the NFL.
