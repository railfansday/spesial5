Winners & Sinners: Week 5

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Welcome to Winners & Sinners, where we reflect on the week that was for the Milwaukee Bucks. Week 5 saw the Bucks go winless yet again, with respectable losses to the Atlanta Hawks and Portland Trail Blazers bookending a Vice City beatdown at the hands of Giannis Antetokounmpo and the Miami Heat, and a heartbreaker to the Philadelphia 76ers. But for all the losing, this week was still quite fun. Seriously.

The 76ers game threw the MVB voters into hysterics, with Tyler Herro exploding for a career-high 50 points, to go along with 11 rebounds and seven assists, and Brayden Burries also setting a new career-high with 26 points (plus eight rebounds and four assists). But a single game does not make an MVB and Herro was rough to begin the week, putting up single-digit performances against the Hawks and Heat, while also playing ball-stopper against the Trail Blazers. Burries' case is more compelling; he put up 18.3 PPG, 6.5 RPG, 3.0 APG, and 1.5 SPG on .510/.429/.750 shooting. But Kasparas Jakučionis was the Bucks' best in three straight—21.0 PPG, 3.7 RPG, 4.0 APG, 2.0 SPG and just 1.3 TOV (on .600/.476/.786 splits)—before seeing just 10 minutes of court time against the 76ers. Really, it's a coin flip, but since Jakučionis had the better week-long plus/minus (+2, to Burries' -9), he gets the nod. Regardless, that Burries and Jakučionis are already putting up weeks like these is a massive step in the right direction—and a sign that it's not all doom and gloom in MKE.

The Contenders: Kasparas Jakučionis, Kyle Kuzma, Nate Ament.

The Decision: Kuzma continues to get the toughest assignments, and he's trying hard (more on that later). Ament wants more of 'em and is doing a darned good on-ball job when he gets them. But this week's showdown comes down to the point of attack: Rollins versus Jakučionis. And, truthfully, it can only go to one. Jakučionis not only played twice as many games as Rollins (that matters), he also led the team in defensive rating (112.0) and defensive win shares (0.3). Beyond the numbers, Jakučionis' grit and hustle was on full display. His full-court pressure, screen navigation, and active hands were a sight to behold. This guy truly cares (more on that later in the week) and while he'd much rather have earned a W or two, he'll have to settle for the Brew Hoop double: MVB and Defensive Belt.

Otherwise, these would've featured. Still, they weren't the only replay-worthy highlights, and one, in particular, provided glimpses of what we've been waiting on all year.

Against the Blazers, for a moment at least, Kel'el Ware remembered he is a bad dude. Just look at the form: elbows locked in full extension, hands snatching the ball at the apex. Then the reload, the flush, the recoil, sending 7'2", 280-pound Donovan Clingan ricocheting like he was leaving the club at 5 a.m.

"Kobe" Kuzma: Kyle Kuzma has done some things well this year. He's tried to show leadership and help unite the team. He's tried hard to attack the rim in a bid to put pressure on opposing defences. And he's tried hard on the boards (mostly). But he's also been oh so trying. Head-scratching passes, mouth-gaping turnovers, and way too many "10-year vets shouldn't be doing this" type decisions. For at least the second time this year, more of that reared its ugly head against the Hawks when, after a blistering early shooting display, Kuzma decided to test the heat-check button several times as if he were Kobe Bean Bryant, helping the Hawks come back from a 22-point deficit. It got even worse against the 76ers. In a back-and-forth affair, Milwaukee down one, Kuzma misses a one-pass three with 18 seconds left on the clock, leading to a 9-0 Philly run that the Bucks can never recover from. Kuz, my man, you're not Kobe. They don't love you like that.

Matinee Tip-Offs and Missed Calls: Look, it's hard enough to cover the Bucks from a different continent, let alone when they're struggling to new franchise lows. But when the NBA schedules a 12:00 p.m. central start time—4:30 a.m. in the land of Oz—it's just rude. And when the refs miss a blatant out-of-bounds call that would give the Bucks a legitimate shot at the win? Well, there's just not enough coffee in the world to deal with that. (Of course, if you asked my wife, I'd be the sinner for setting the alarm clock).

If Taylor Jenkins has thrown damn near everything at the wall to see what might stick—rotation-wise at least—then you have to wonder why he hasn't given the twin towers frontcourt more of a look, especially with the Bucks' escalating rebounding woes. Myles Turner and Kel'el Ware, for all intents and purposes, have just not seen any playing time together (just four minutes in total). In their lone preseason start together, Turner dropped 19 points, five rebounds, and two blocks, while Ware had 18 points, 12 rebounds, and one block.
