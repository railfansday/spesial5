Fantasy Football: George Kittle, 49ers offense win the day versus Vikings' league-best defense

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

I've been married for almost 20 years, and you'd think I'd be used to things changing in an instant. I mean, I've had plenty of practice. Just last Friday, my evening plans switched from nothing (my default choice) to my wife inviting a couple over for board games, to her going to play cards with her sisters. It did turn out to be a quiet night for me. But it reminded me that in our house, a plan is, well, more of a suggestion.

I should've remembered that lesson as we headed into Sunday. From the kickoff to the early games, Week 11 reminded me how quickly things can flip in just a week. Honestly, I came away with more questions than answers. Let's dig into the storylines on some of the things that changed the most.

Before SNF, nobody wanted their QB to face the Vikings. The only passer to throw for multiple TDs was Jordan Love back in Week 1. Jared Goff in Week 8 was the first to complete over 70% of his passes against Minnesota's defense. And for TEs specifically, Keleki Latu was the sole player at his position to score against DC Brian Flores' unit.

Brock Purdy and George Kittle raised the bar in Mexico City.

Of course, the Vikings came in with their usual approach. Purdy saw five or more rushes on 73.3% of his passing attempts. That didn't matter. One, the blitzes only got home once. And two, only one incompletion was the result. Eight of his 10 completed passes turned into a first down or a score. Kittle was the primary fantasy beneficiary of Minnesota opting to treat Purdy like every other QB. But the rest of the receiver corps chipped in, too.

Target Rate: 26.9% (Evans), 15.4% (Samuel), 15.4% (Allen)

The "Unc Bowl" was in full effect for HC Kyle Shanahan. None of his aged WRs ran more than 50% of the snaps. But when they were out there, you knew where the ball was going. But the Vikings' secondary couldn't stop them. All four of the 30-plus-year-old pass-catchers were responsible for at least one explosive play. Only Christian McCaffrey got left out in the cold (he still found his way to the end zone). Minnesota's defense looked mortal for the first time all season.

But the state of the offense is a bigger issue.

Passing Success Rate: 43.6% (Week 11), 47.1% (Week 10)

After a confidence-building week against the Packers with Kirk Cousins fresh off the trade, everything came to a halt against San Francisco. Aaron Jones Sr. and Jordan Mason combined for a 35.3% rushing success rate on early downs, leaving Cousins with 8.6 yards to go in obvious passing situations. Justin Jefferson lost a fumble on a play where he (maybe?) would've scored if he hadn't changed directions. Cousins threw a pick-six. That's two possessions that could've tightened up the score. But as the Vikings were trying to catch up, an old fantasy option returned.

T.J. Hockenson came back to a part-time role after missing Week 10 with wrist and chest injuries. However, despite running the fewest routes of any of the starting pass-catchers, only Tai Felton had more targets. Hockenson should be in starting TE slots moving forward. But for Felton, I'd be more wary. The team opted not to place Jordan Addison on injured reserve, expressing optimism that he can be out on the field sooner than the IR designation. So, unless you're in dire need of a receiver, Hockenson should be the only addition from the Vikings off waivers as we head into Week 12.

Let's review Sunday's expectations for the Bears. The Saints' run defense came into the game allowing the 10th-most yards per game on the ground over their last six contests. Quinshon Judkins was the only starting RB to face New Orleans and exit with single-digit PPR points. HC Ben Johnson not only had Kyle Monangai returning from injury, but also the bye to plan.

Even in the first half, the results were concerning.

The Bears' approach to play-calling to start the game was simple: let's establish the run. D'Andre Swift touched the ball on the first four plays. Five of the nine plays on their opening drive were runs. Chicago finished the day with an -8.8% PROE (a season-low mark). Said another way, the volume was there. But Johnson split the pie up a few ways.

Swift: 48.0% (Early-Down Touch Rate), 37.0% (Route Share), 53.8%(RZ Snap Rate)

Swift and Monangai swapped series throughout most of the contest. Luckily for gamers rostering Swift, it was No. 4 who was on the field for Chicago's first TD drive after CJ Donaldson fumbled on their side of the field. Swift got the lone touch from inside the 10-yard line and converted for the score. But therein lies the problem. The combination of a healthy Ben Johnson running game and (theoretically) poor resistance from New Orleans should've garnered more production from anyone carrying the rock. Instead, we got the opposite.

Credit to the Saints' defensive line. They met the trio of Bears' rushers at or behind the LoS on 51.9% of their attempts. Monangai wasn't known for being the shiftiest of runners (0.0% forced missed tackle rate), but averaging 1.9 yards after contact didn't help. Swift took all of the short-yardage totes and only came out with one conversion on three attempts. And to make matters worse, Salvon Ahmed mixed in on early downs, siphoning yards away from both.

I could use the Bears' quick turnaround to face the Lions on Thursday to explain Ahmed's usage. However, season-lows in efficiency, leading to Caleb Williams' third-down environment getting more chaotic (fourth-highest average distance to convert), aren't just about the rotation. And after watching the Lions keep the Bucs' ground game in check, the Bears will need to adjust their run game in order to stay in the race for the top spot in the NFC North.

I understood Pittsburgh coming out flat against the Bengals in primetime last week. Don't get me wrong, I was surprised in the moment. But I've seen these games between division rivals before. I didn't expect to see the output continue a week later.

Aaron Rodgers' scramble to close out the first quarter was the longest running play for the Steelers on Sunday. If you're rostering Jaylen Warren, apologies in advance. After enjoying the backfield to himself for a stretch, maintaining the starter role and putting up three RB1 finishes, Warren was becoming a staple of fantasy rosters. However, everything's taken a step back.

Rushing Success Rate: 54.6% (Weeks 1-8), 28.6% (Week 10), 22.2% (Week 11)

Adj. Yards After Contact per Att.: 4.97, 2.14, 2.67

Pittsburgh's biggest play-calling shift was in how often Rodgers dropped back to pass. After having a negative PROE for three straight games, the Steelers were up to +6.3% (fourth-highest on the season). Accordingly, Warren carried the ball just nine times (second-fewest in '26). Philadelphia held him to zero yards before contact on two-thirds of his carries and stuffed him on a third. But that's OK. With more passing, Warren's involvement as a receiver should've kept his fantasy value afloat. However, his QB was also still in a slump.

Pressure-to-Sack Ratio: 62.5%, Season-high

The worst part is that the Eagles largely left Rodgers alone. They blitzed him just once. And yet, Rodgers still hit his season-high of five sacks. But I can't blame him. DK Metcalf decided that fighting with the defense was more important than catching the ball. And sure, Germie Bernard earned 10 targets. But again, six of them were on throws of five air yards or less. Rodgers didn't regularly look for him on intermediate or deep throws until the second half.

The knee-jerk reaction from gamers might be to start Bernard in Week 12. Metcalf might incur a suspension. Rodgers has been more efficient targeting Bernard over Metcalf in multiple games. However, with the Broncos and Texans on their upcoming schedule, I'd keep everybody in black and gold on your bench.

My largest concern with Fernando Mendoza starting during this stretch of games for the Raiders was the absence of Brock Bowers. I realize neither he nor HC Klint Kubiak could plan for an injury. But the chasm between Bowers and the rest of the pass-catchers would put anyone in a tough spot. And having to face Denver, coming off its bye, without his de facto WR1 would be a challenge. However, I didn't give enough credence to the idea that Kubiak could prepare his QB to do more with less.

To be clear, it wasn't all pretty. The Raiders punted on their first three drives. Mendoza ended the first quarter with just one more completed pass than sacks taken. It wasn't until the half was winding down that Mendoza started to get into the flow. But if there were concerns about whether or not Kubiak was giving Mendoza answers to the defense's questions, the rookie alleviated some of them.

EPA per Dropback: -0.02 (Week 11), -0.18 (Week 10)

Instead of forcing the ball to the middle of the field without his best receiver, Mendoza looked to the perimeter. It was the ideal adjustment given the strengths of the skill players he had on hand. Plus, he's pretty good at it. Mendoza averaged 0.68 EPA per dropback with a 55.6% completion rate on boundary throws. But Mendoza wasn't just aggressive — he was decisive, averaging more throws under 2.6 seconds. Oh, I guess I buried the lede there. All of his TD throws were to the perimeter. And at least one of his target-earners shouldn't be a surprise.

Unfortunately, not much changed for Ashton Jeanty as a runner. Six of his 11 carries came with a defender in the backfield. Denver held him to three or fewer yards on 54.5% of his attempts. But Jeanty came into the game behind only Christian McCaffrey in YPRR and was fifth in YAC per reception. If there was going to be a way to get Mendoza comfortable with the offense, using Jeanty as a receiver was the right call. But there's one more pass-catcher who should be on our radar.

Jack Bech's routes and targets have gone up in three straight weeks. He led the team in looks after Bowers left in Week 10 and was just one shy of Tre Tucker's total in Denver. But with receiving aDOTs of 9.3 and 8.1, Bech won't have the same volatility as a downfield option like Tucker. So even with Bowers likely to return, Bech should be a speculative add if you have the bench space.

Talk about narratives changing in a week. Jacksonville made multiple additions to the defense before the trade deadline, needed the full four quarters to get by Tennessee but, on paper, looked like one of the favorites to get to the Super Bowl.

And then the Giants reminded them there are no easy matchups in the NFL.

To be fair, the signs were there for the Jaguars to face some problems on offense. Starting LT Anton Harrison tore his ACL in Week 5, and C Robert Hainsey missed this game with a knee sprain. Minor injuries to RT Cole Van Lanen and RG Ezra Cleveland only complicated the situation further. Coincidentally, the G-Men were second to the Vikings in blitz rate and ninth in sack rate over their last six games. The outcome was one of Lawrence's worst outings this season.

Completion Percentage Over Expected: -2.1%, 3rd-lowest

Jacksonville's issue wasn't just that Lawrence's protection kept breaking down, but how fast things deteriorated. DC Dennard Wilson had his unit getting to Lawrence in 2.5 seconds or faster on 12 of his dropbacks. That's four more than his previous season-high. Accordingly, Lawrence's average time to throw skyrocketed to 3.3 seconds. He started taking more shots downfield (12.1 passing aDOT). Of course, the Giants mounting a multi-score lead by the end of the first half prompted the aggression. But New York reducing them to a one-dimensional offense was the larger issue.

Rushing Success Rate (on early downs): 27.3%, Season-low

Distance to the Sticks (on third downs): 10.8, Season-high

Bhayshul Tuten entered the game with foot/ankle injuries, missed time earlier in the game and then left early with a limp. Meanwhile, Chris Rodriguez Jr. ran into a stacked box on every single one of his early-down rushing attempts. Without a running game, Jacksonville turned into a dropback passing unit.

Let's assume Tuten misses Week 12. Jacksonville has its rematch against the Titans. Rodriguez is healthy. He should be at or near the top of your waiver priority. But I'd be wary about expecting similar target totals for Jakobi Meyers (10) and Josh Cameron (7). Lawrence just attempted more than 45 passes for the first time since Week 7 of last season. Unless you think the Titans can do the same, the offense should go back to its balanced ways in Week 12.
