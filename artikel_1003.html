England squad live: Tuchel explains shock Palmer omission as Jobe Bellingham receives first call-up

Thomas Tuchel has left Cole Palmer out his England squad while Jobe Bellingham, the brother of Jude Bellingham, has received his first senior call-up.

Tuchel was critical of Palmer for withdrawing from September’s squad due to injury, with the England manager saying the Chelsea star missed “too many camps with injuries”. Palmer did not miss any games for Chelsea following the international break, but was sent off against Tottenham, and the 24-year-old has now been left out for the Nations League games against Croatia and Spain as the Three Lions aim to book their place in the quarter-finals.

Speaking at a press conference at Wembley, Tuchel explained: “It counts when you are in camp and I just said it last time, he [Palmer] misses too many opportunities to impress. It was a long camp. It was two-and-a-half weeks and the players and the boys who were in camp built something extraordinary throughout these weeks. That's why the credit goes to them. The trust is for them. If you are in camp, this is your chance to impress. So it's not personal.”

Elsewhere, Jobe Bellingham, 21, joins brother Jude in Tuchel’s squad after impressing for Borussia Dortmund this season, while there is also a first-time call-up for Fulham midfielder Josh King, 19. Trent Alexander-Arnold keeps his place despite his lack of minutes for Real Madrid while Arsenal right back Ben White also returns following injury, but there is no place for winger Noni Madueke despite the absence of Bukayo Saka due to injury.

England are third in Group A3 after four games, having won three and lost one, to Spain at Wembley. They host Croatia on Thursday before travelling to Madrid to take on Spain on Sunday, and a point from either of those two games would be enough to qualify for the knockout rounds in March.

Follow all the latest updates from England’s squad announcement and Tuchel’s Wembley press conference below.

From childhood Champions League finals on a patch of grass in Stourbridge to the biggest stage in international football, Jobe Bellingham is determined to step out of his brother’s shadow.

Myles Lewis-Skelly is absent for England’s match against Croatia due to suspension.

Thomas Tuchel addressed this saying: “We have a slight issue in central midfield because Alex Scott is not ready. Unfortunately he picked up an injury in the last camp and can’t be with us.

“Myles, who featured in all four matches, picked up a yellow card and is suspended for the Croatia match and it leaves us a little bit short in central midfield, especially for the first match.

“(Under-21s boss) Lee Carsley has spoken highly of Jobe for weeks and months that if there is a spot and possibility to join and make an impression in the first team.

“He had good matches, and he is on a good run of form in Dortmund, so he deserves to be with us.”

Jobe Bellingham and Josh King have been included in the final England squad of the year, but Thomas Tuchel has overlooked Cole Palmer and Kobbie Mainoo for November’s internationals.

Djed Spence and Ben White were brought into a 25-man squad that defender Nico O’Reilly has dropped out of.

Declan Rice returns after injury but Bukayo Saka is absent with a thigh injury and Tuchel decided not to bring in Noni Madueke as his Arsenal team-mate’s replacement.

There was no place for World Cup squad members Dean Henderson, Dan Burn and Jordan Henderson after missing the last camp.

Alex Scott, Marcus Rashford and John Stones are ruled out of this meet up through injury, as are Tino Livramento, Reece James and Trevoh Chalobah.

England will play the final two matches of their Nations League group stage in this break.

First up is Croatia on Thursday 12 November before a blockbuster clash against Spain on Sunday November 15.

The England boss ended his press conference by answering a question on right-back Trent Alexander-Arnold.

He said: "He was very good in the last camp and in the games he played, and he deserved to come back.

"The competition is on, as always. The door is always open. He competes with Jarell [Quansah] and Ben [White] for the right-back position."

Thomas Tuchel was asked about the inclusion of younger players such as Jobe Bellingham and Josh King and whether that signals a new phase for the team.

"I think we did naturally. We were a little bit forced. I was a bit concerned before the last camp because we missed Dean Henderson, John Stones, Dan Burn, Jordan Henderson, Reece James - players who were really responsible for the attitude and the energy at the World Cup and played a huge part in the standards throughout the camp,” Tuchel explained.

"I was a bit worried that we missed so many of them but the group found a new way. They found new leadership, a new way to engage with each other. We built another camp with a good atmosphere, new voices to set the standards.

"Now we have more choices and I had to give Dan Burn, Jordan Henderson, Dean Henderson difficult phonecalls. I am very aware of what they did for us and they built a very strong foundation on which these players now are benefitting from.

"Naturally, we went forward. But we always want to go forward and embrace change as something positive. It does not mean the door is closed for these kinds of players. But we decided to give this group kind of a second push."

The England manager has opted to ‘trust’ the players who impressed him in the last squad, while also offering first-time call-ups to Josh King and Jobe Bellingham.

On Jobe Bellingham’s call up, Tuchel said: "It's an incredible achievement for the brothers and the family to be both nominated for the national team, which is an incredible story in itself.

“I was very happy to give him the call-up and I saw his excitement straight away in his smile. He is a very open and calm personality so it was a very nice phone call, of course, because it was very good news.

"He is one of five players for the central midfield. He is a more defensive player than his brother Jude. Good on the ball, good physique, very physical player who can keep possession. Now it is up to him to impress."

“Me personally, I’m targeting top spot and I would be very surprised if my group doesn’t.

“This is clearly the target, we want to have a final in Spain because we want to have these pressure moments and these matches when we play for something.

“We want to play under pressure and in the big occasions. We want to use the Nations League for exactly that to be prepared in the best possible way for the Euros.

“For me, Croatia is a crucial match to win especially after the result we got. We got a deserved result [7-0 in Croatia] but it was kind of a freak result that does not reflect the quality of Croatia in general.

“It reflected the way the match went last time but we have to forget that result and remember what we put in and what quality we put in and what effort we top.

“We need another top performance because Croatia showed an excellent response against Spain straight after.”

"I have little a standby list and the clubs and the players know it, players who cover us on a lot of positions,” Tuchel explained.

"But we have 22 outfield players so this should not affect us. It is a short camp and we should be fine number-wise.

"It is always about the ones who are in camp, who don't withdraw and walk the talk, come to camp and show that it matters.

"I think that [message] is naturally coming through. That was always the message very, very early since we took charge that is matters if you are in camp and what you do in camp.

"The last camp was an excellent one and the trust and belief is in the players who were there."
