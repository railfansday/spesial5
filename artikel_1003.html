Humphries shocked by Sykes in last-leg thriller at Grand Slam of Darts

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Luke Humphries is out at the last 16 stage of the Grand Slam after losing to Tom Sykes; Humphries was struggling with a trapped nerve in his shoulder; you can watch the Grand Slam of Darts in Wolverhampton live on Sky Sports until November 22

Luke Humphries suffered a last-leg 10-9 loss to debutant Tom Sykes in the second round of the Grand Slam of Darts in Wolverhampton.

It is the second big shock in the space of two days following world No 1 Luke Littler's loss to Jermaine Wattimena. Now, with the world No 2 also out, the competition is well and truly blown wide open.

Humphries looked to be struggling with a problem in his shoulder throughout the contest but managed to find some strong darts to take the contest all the way.

But Sykes, who is now ranked 72nd in the world, held on and prevailed to pick up the biggest win of his career and reach his first ever major quarter-final.

It means Humphries will have to wait to try and clinch his second Grand Slam title following his victory back in 2023.

"I was a bit nervous, but obviously I thought the game was over when I turned around and saw Luke hit the first treble 18 there, and hit the second one and then wired it," Sykes told Sky Sports following his win.

"I was so cold on that last one. I just thought, just hit it first out, no messing about, and get into it. And I did.

"I'm just riding a wave at the minute. I'm not really bothered who I play. I'll just come back up here and we'll see what happens."

For Sykes, a mouth-watering clash with Michael van Gerwen awaits after he came through with a remarkable contest against James Wade with a 10-8 victory.

It marks Van Gerwen's first ranking quarter-final of 2026 and he earned it in some style with a near 104 average. There was nothing separating two darting juggernauts throughout the high-quality bout, a massive 121 checkout from the Dutchman levelling the game at 7-7 before rattling off two more legs to move 9-7 ahead.

Wade did not give up and a stunning 130 checkout, his second of the match, broke Van Gerwen's throw but the three-time Grand Slam champion broke straight back and pinned D16 for the win.

"To play in these types of games was even worse. I made it incredibly difficult for myself. That's sometimes so annoying," Van Gerwen told Sky Sports.

"I couldn't hit a double after the second break and with James Wade standing behind me - it's so annoying - but it's also his class. I dealt with it the right way, and now I feel comfortable now.

Sykes made it clear he would be making the match a real contest in the first session. He averaged 107.42 in a staggering opening five legs that saw him win four with incredible ease against the in-form player in the darting world.

Although still looking uncomfortable, Humphries fought back as Sykes went slightly off the boil. That saw him pick up four of the next five legs to level the game at 5-5.

The game continued to go back and forth as Humphries found some of his top levels, but Sykes would not back down, fighting back despite some strong finishing from 'Cool Hand', a 114 finish on tops the highlight.

It then looked like it was all going to go the way of Humphries when he powered in back-to-back 11-darters to take the game to a final-leg showdown but the 'Castleford Tiger' showed why he has become a formidable player.

Humphries missed D10 to finish off a 128 checkout which allowed Sykes to have his match dart at 36. He pinned that first dart for a win to remember.

Humphries took to social media after the contest to congratulate Sykes and admitted he has been through a tough 48 hours.

Humphries said: "Well done Tom Sykes absolutely amazing performance!

"So proud of the way I played tonight with what I've had to go through with injury the last 48 hours gave it my absolute all there.

"Wish everyone all the best for the rest of the tournament."

Sykes has been on the darting circuit since 2013 when he competed on the PDC Challenge Tour and then followed that up with a year on the PDC Development Tour.

It is over the last two years that Sykes has truly flourished. He qualified for the 2025 UK Open and made it all the way to the third round.

Earlier this year he clinched his Tour card at Q-School and has since made it to the fourth round at the UK Open before picking up the win of his career with the scalp of the world No 2 on the Grand Slam stage.

The 34-year-old hails from Castleford in West Yorkshire and his nickname 'The Castleford Tiger' is an ode to the rugby league side from his hometown.

Nathan Aspinall made it through to the quarter-finals with a hard-fought 10-7 victory over Gabriel Clemens.

Aspinall apologised post-match for "writing off" the German player before the game and it was clear why, with Clemens averaging 96.55 and hitting 10 180s.

It was a topsy-turvy game throughout the first two sessions and at the end of them, the scores were level at 5-5.

However, it was from there that Aspinall took control. From 6-5 down, he took out four legs in a row and found a massive 'Big Fish' finish for a break of throw to send the Wolverhampton crowd wild.

Clemens did manage to get one more leg on the board but in the 17th leg, Aspinall scored highly and clinched D16 for a spot in the last eight.

He will take on Gian van Veen after he held off a fightback from Martin Schindler for a 10-7 win.

It was in the second session that the world No 3 stepped things up. Averaging over 104, he took advantage of Schindler's clear issues on the doubles to take out four of the five legs to be 7-3 ahead.

Van Veen then made that seven out of eight legs and was one leg from victory before a period of poor scoring for the third seed and a massive improvement from Schindler saw the German take out four legs to put the pressure on.

Eventually, Van Veen found back-to-back 134s and took out 93 to book the showdown with Aspinall.
