Your PL club's 'Unfinished Business' revealed ahead of January window

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Watch our 'Unfinished Business' Transfer Show special on Sky Sports News at 4pm and 7pm; the transfer window will re-open for Premier League clubs on January 1, 2027 and close on February 1 at 11pm; how many signings does your club need? Vote in our poll below

Arsenal failed to bring in a marquee forward in the summer and it remains one of their big objectives ahead of January as they prepare their recruitment plans for 2027.

The Premier League champions are understood to be tracking RB Leipzig's Antonio Nusa and Brentford's Kevin Schade as potential options. Arsenal want a forward who primarily plays on the left but has the capability to feature across the front line.

Morgan Rogers, Vinicius Junior and Julian Alvarez were pursued over the summer, but Arsenal failed to agree deals for any of their top forward targets.

Arsenal are believed to be moving on from Atletico Madrid's Alvarez as he is not keen to leave Spain. Meanwhile, Rogers signed for Chelsea in a £117m deal, a fee Arsenal were unwilling to reach, and Vinicius signed a new deal with Real Madrid.

Bournemouth's Alex Scott is also of interest to Arsenal, who are understood to be willing to move on a midfielder to beat Chelsea and Liverpool to the 23-year-old's signature.

Aston Villa ended the summer happy with their business after a window that, for a long time, looked like it would be a difficult one.

There were a host of high-profile departures but a string of arrivals to replace them have left Unai Emery with an exciting, younger squad and all positions filled.

One bit of unfinished business that will be arguably more important than the signing of any player is the new contract for manager Emery.

Bournemouth completed the business they planned to do over the summer transfer window and any further additions in January will likely depend on outgoings.

Alex Scott has interest from Arsenal, Chelsea and Liverpool with Bournemouth's stance over a potential sale softening since the summer having been unable to agree a new contract. It is understood Bournemouth would be seeking up to £100m, including add-ons.

Scott's departure is by no means a foregone conclusion in January but if he was to leave then Bournemouth would like to sign a midfielder.

Bournemouth want to ensure Marco Rose's squad is in a strong position to fight on in the Premier League and Europa League for the rest of the season before what is expected to be a big summer of upheaval.

Brentford are continuing to monitor Archie Gray's situation at Tottenham. The club were interested in signing him in the summer and are keeping an eye on his rate of minutes under Roberto De Zerbi.

Gray has been a focal point of De Zerbi's team so far this season, making eight starts in their 10 games. He has found an opening at right-back due to Pedro Porro's injury.

Porro returned to the starting line-up against Leeds, which saw Gray start in midfield, as Spurs were beaten at Elland Road. Brentford will be keeping an eye on whether Gray keeps his place or finds minutes harder to come-by in the run up to the January window.

Brentford are not expected to be busy in January having improved their squad in the summer and made an excellent start to the campaign under Keith Andrews

Brighton don't really have any "unfinished business" in terms of incomings from the summer to complete in January. They looked at a number of players in different positions and ended up signing a player in each role.

With Brighton's outstanding displays taking them to second in the table after 10 games, very little if anything is likely to happen in terms of first-team ready signings. Historically, owner/chair Tony Bloom prefers to do business in the summer when targets are more obtainable and more affordable than mid-season.

The January window is sometimes used to sign prospects from abroad and begin their development on the south coast. In terms of outgoings, they will not listen or entertain any offers for main first team squad players with the club being tipped as contenders for a Champions League place.

Some of the young players at the club may go out on loan for game time.

Manager Xabi Alonso remains open to signing another midfielder after the departure of Enzo Fernandez in the summer and seeing a Deadline Day deal for Monaco's Lamine Camara collapse.

Chelsea are interested in Bournemouth's Alex Scott and are believed to have the financial edge over other clubs in the race to sign the England international.

However, he is not Chelsea's only target while Romeo Lavia's improvement could reduce their urgency to reinforce their midfield options.

Coventry City made 12 major signings and spent over £130m during the summer, but results so far suggest further investment will be needed at both ends of the pitch to boost their chances of retaining Premier League status.

One situation to watch could be the future of attacking midfielder Jack Rudoni - a player Coventry will be determined to keep hold of but who is attracting attention after an impressive start to the season.

Club Brugge's Joel Ordonez saw his proposed summer move to Crystal Palace break down after he had a medical. The Ecuadorian is yet to play this season, so any renewed January move by the south London club is thought to be unlikely.

Palace signed centre-backs Axel Disasi from Chelsea and Honest Ahanor from Atalanta on loan, plus free agent defender Takehiro Tomiyasu, but further additions in that area, and also up front, cannot be ruled out.

Jean-Philippe Mateta's situation is one to watch too as he enters the final six months of his deal in January. Palace cashed in on Marc Guehi 12 months earlier before he became a summer free agent, and Palace may now have a similar dilemma over striker Mateta.

The threat of Premier League relegation - and the cost that comes with it - could also play a factor when deciding Mateta's future.

There will also be more talk about the future of Ismaila Sarr. He had his head turned by summer interest from Liverpool and then Galatasaray, while talks - described as "positive" and "good-natured" - have also taken place about extending the winger's contract, but a new agreement has yet to be confirmed.

Everton tried desperately to bring in a striker on Deadline Day. They were left frustrated when a move for Monaco's Folarin Balogun collapsed after the window closed having also failed with a loan bid for Joshua Zirkzee.

Thierno Barry's impressive form - 10 goals in 13 games this season - has softened the blow of missing out on a striker but he remains Everton's only out-and-out option up top, and a striker remains a priority.

Right-back was also a problem position for Everton. They have filled it this season with Merlin Rohl, Ainsley Maitland-Niles and Jake O'Brien but doubts remain about whether Everton have the role covered long term.

Fulham will closely monitor the transfer window for viable opportunities to improve their squad but do not currently feel they have any immediate need to reinforce in a single position.

With more midfielders than he can give game time to, head coach Alvaro Arbeloa may look to send a one or two out on loan.

Should Man Utd come in for centre back Calvin Bassey, Fulham may be forced to enter the market. Although it would require a very big fee to convince them to part ways with such an important player and leader.

Arbeloa has said Fulham are keen to agree a contract extension with Bassey to keep him in west London.

Hull City recruited heavily in the summer and are expected to have a much quieter January transfer window. But one area where they may look to strengthen is on the left side of their attack.

They continue to assess the fitness of Ilyas Ansah, who has been sidelined with a back injury since joining in the summer. Ansah has yet to make his competitive debut but could return to training next month. Several fringe players could also leave the club in this window.

Ipswich Town went big in the summer in a bid to have all angles covered on their return to the Premier League. After splashing out over £200m on 16 players they had the second-highest net-spend (£190.7m) in the Premier League behind Liverpool (£219.4m).

Their chairman Mark Ashton told Sky Sports News: "We have never shied away from the transfer market. We were very aggressive in the summer but if the right things come along we will look at them in the right way."

Leeds United are happy with the current state of their squad after addressing the key areas of need in the summer transfer window, but will remain open to market opportunities in January.

There was interest over the summer in signing a striker who could support Dominic Calvert-Lewin, with a younger profile viewed as ideal.

Leeds tried to sign Jorgen Strand Larsen for a then club-record fee last January, but there is no longer any appetite to bring in a player that would potentially limit Calvert-Lewin's role.

Sky Sports News understands Calvert-Lewin is expected to extend his stay at Elland Road beyond his current deal, which has 18 months to run. There was interest from Manchester United but they have no plans to explore a potential move further.

Liverpool were interested in Crystal Palace's Ismaila Sarr and Brighton's Yankuba Minteh late in the summer window but were unable to sign either winger.

Any renewed move will depend largely on whether their clubs are more open to doing business than they were in the summer though Liverpool's plans are yet to be firmed up.

With Sarr and Minteh staying put, Cody Gakpo remained at Anfield despite interest from Manchester City and Tottenham.

That left head coach Andoni Iraola with the pool of five wide options that he ideally wants, though that does include Federico Chiesa who has only been a bit-part player this season.

Manchester City will be braced for any interest that comes in Rayan Ait-Nouri this January, given Manchester United tried to sign him on loan on Deadline Day.

But they are not making any decisions on the former Wolves left-back given there is still more than a month to go until the window opens and they may need all their players.

Ait-Nouri has not played a single minute in the Premier League so far and has failed to even make the squad for four of City's 10 games.

Meanwhile, City are not looking to sign Malo Gusto, Sky Sports News understands, amid widespread reports about the right-back's future at Chelsea.

City have reportedly been interested in Gusto given boss Enzo Maresca worked with him at Stamford Bridge - but that is not the case.

Right-back is a position City have been assessing whether or not to strengthen further for some time but have been looking at younger development players such as Feyenoord's Givairo Read.

Man Utd prioritised the midfield rebuild over signing a left-back and striker in the summer but, after their worst start to a league season for 40 years, there is an acknowledgement from the hierarchy that they could have strengthened further in the last window.

New head coach Mauricio Pochettino, who replaced the sacked Michael Carrick last week, is expected to be backed in January and have input on potential targets with left-back, centre-back and a forward on the agenda.

Everton's Jarrad Branthwaite and Fulham's Calvin Bassey are among the left-sided defenders being monitored with Lisandro Martinez's future in doubt. The Argentina international is into the final year of his deal, which includes an option for a further 12 months.

Forward options are being explored with Benjamin Sesko, the club's only recognised No 9, out injured until at least January, while left-back cover is another potential area of focus with five different players used in that position this season with limited success.

Matthias Jaissle wants to add more goals to his team this January, with a No 9 and a No 10 top of his wish list.

He doesn't have an out-and-out No 10 in his current squad, despite persisting with the 4-2-3-1 formation since he joined the club in the summer.

Joe Willock and Yoane Wissa have both been deployed in that position so far, but neither is natural in that role.

However, the chances of signing players for both positions are slim, unless Newcastle offset that with sales.

The winter window is notoriously difficult to operate in, with difference makers being the hardest players to sign.

Signing a player for one of those positions is likely to be as good as Newcastle can achieve, given the financial restraints.

Jaissle wants to add experience in January, having lost a clutch of experienced players in the summer. This will be his first window to operate in, having arrived late in the summer.

Ivan Toney is one striker he admires and would like to sign, having managed him previously with Al Ahli, but he knows it is unlikely to make economic sense for a club trying to invest in younger players with high ceilings.

Central midfield and goalkeeper are two positions outstanding from the summer that Nottingham Forest could look at if they go back into the market in January.

Forest were prepared to spend big in midfield but were unsuccessful with offers for Tottenham's Lucas Bergvall, Everton's Harrison Armstrong and Liverpool's Trey Nyoni.

It was a similar story in goal. Forest were linked with Crystal Palace's Dean Henderson, Chelsea's Robert Sanchez and Liverpool's Giorgi Mamardashvili but did not sign any of them.

The form of Oliver Glasner's players and team between now and January will determine which situations Forest revisit in the winter window.

Sunderland were frantically trying to add to their squad on Deadline Day and winger Malick Fofana was their main arrival from Lyon. They were keen to add another winger and tried for Benfica's Dodi Lukebakio but failed to secure his signature.

Head coach Regis Le Bris also wanted an additional attacker, to give him another genuine option behind Brian Brobbey and Wilson Isidor. Sunderland signed teenage prospect Juan Riquelme Angulo to plug that gap but he has barely featured this season, leaving that as one role they may still want to fill.

Tottenham were one of the Premier League's biggest spenders in the summer with an outlay of more than £330m on new signings but that does not rule out the possibility of a fresh addition in the January transfer window.

Goals have been an issue for Roberto De Zerbi's side this season, with only two Premier League sides scoring fewer after 10 matches, and Spurs are open to bringing in another forward if the right player becomes available.

Bournemouth's Eli Junior Kroupi was a target in the summer before his metatarsal injury ruled him out for the first three months of the season. The 20-year-old has now returned to action, but it would take a huge fee to prise him from Bournemouth in January.

Spurs will also seek to find a new club for Richarlison, who rejected a move to Everton at the end of the summer window after he was omitted from De Zerbi's Premier League squad. The Brazilian, whose contract is up in the summer, has not played for the first team since August.
