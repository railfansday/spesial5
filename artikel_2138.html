Don Goodman names the 3 EFL clubs that have shocked him the most so far

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

From unprecedented promotion pushes to shock relegations and unexpected free-scoring campaigns to the harshest of managerial sackings, the EFL prides itself on providing innumerable surprises, curveballs and twists and turns each campaign.

The 2026/27 term still remains in a relative infancy but has not fallen short of meeting such standards of saga, drama and surprise heading into the November international break, with various plot twists and odds-upsetting developments continuing to take centre-stage across the EFL sphere.

Whether it's the Championship, League One or League Two, the EFL simply never disappoints when it comes to conjuring up drama and shock, and the excitement among supporters is often fuelled by the unnerving - yet just as thrilling - reality that you really don't know what to expect outside of the Premier League.

But which clubs have produced the most significant surprises so far in 2026/27? EFL expert Don Goodman has offered his take...

One of the most recognisable faces and voices throughout the EFL landscape, Goodman scored freely for the likes of West Bromwich Albion, Wolves and Sunderland before entering the media after hanging up his boots and is now at the forefront of Football League proceedings as a pundit for Sky Sports.

Goodman is well-acquainted with the EFL's pulsating drama and unpredictability, then, and he has named the 3 clubs throughout all three divisions to have left him with the most surprises in the opening quarters of the current campaign.

The pundit initially raised Burnley's struggles towards the foot of the Championship table in the wake of from the Premier League in May before paying particular attention to Bromley's controversial and widely-condemned sacking of Andy Woodman after rising from the fifth-tier to a first-ever EFL promotion and then up to League One as champions last term during the 55-year-old's legendary reign.

Goodman has been surprised by Salford City, too, with the Ammies having soared to League Two's summit playing a swashbuckling brand of attack-minded football under ex-Malaysia boss Peter Cklamovski following defeat in last term's play-off final against Notts County.

Speaking to Football League World via Betway, Goodman explained: "We'll start with the bad which is obviously Burnley.

"Conceding 31 goals in 15 league games is poor, especially when two seasons ago in the Championship, in the whole season they conceded 16.

"I know that there's different personnel, but that is how far off it they are to being a top team in the Championship, so Burnley have shocked me how poor they’ve been.

"I think Bromley have shocked me by sacking Andy Woodman, who's brought them from the National League into League One.

"I am really shocked that they didn't have faith and a bit of patience with him having done what he's done for that football club. I think that's a shocker from Bromley and their hierarchy."

His surprises weren't limited to the negatives, though, as Goodman then talked up Salford's early-season promotion ascent.

It may not surprise others as much considering the Greater Manchester outfit's run to Wembley at the end of the previous campaign before acquiring top-grade League Two talent in the form of Macaulay Langstaff and Luke Molyneux from Millwall and Doncaster Rovers respectively over the summer, of course, but the manner in which Cklamovksi's side have hit their rhythm has still left Goodman shocked for all the right reasons.

"I think a positive one is Salford City," he continued.

"After losing the first three games of the season, for them to win 10 and draw one of the last 11, winning eight in a row, they look unstoppable under Peter Cklamovski.

"The football that they're playing doesn't belong in League Two. It's brilliant to watch. They're far and away the best team, and I'm a little bit surprised by that in a good way."

Bromley's shock aforementioned dismissal of Woodman is certainly a worthy discussion point, and we're unlikely to see a harsher sacking in any of the three divisions all season despite the proclivity for brutally trigger-happy sackings within many EFL boardrooms.

The plucky Ravens had never previously competed above the fifth-tier in what had been a 132-year history before winning the National League play-offs under Woodman in 2024.

A respectable eleventh-placed finish left some surprised, but the real shock was in store last time out when Bromley saw off the likes of Cambridge United, Salford and Paul Warne's star-studded MK Dons side to lift the League Two title against all odds.

The South Londonders had struggled to find their feet at League One level, it must be said, though that was actually following the script of expectation after completely overhauling the squad which had spearheaded the club's shock upwards direction of travel in the first place.

What wasn't in the script, of course, was for Woodman - the most successful manager in Bromley's history after ascending to unimaginable peaks - to be relieved of his duties merely 10 games into the campaign in a meeting that, according to the man himself, lasted just four minutes.

Bromley, who were positioned in 23rd at the time following two wins from 10 but have lost three of their last four encounters across all competitions since parting company with Woodman, left the now-ex Ravens boss feeling "disappointed, upset and misled" by a brutal decision that undermined all his unprecedented achievements and has been slammed by the broader EFL community.

Woodman did, however, issue a telling reminder that the game's inability to follow expectation means that it never truly surprises us - and he may just be onto something there, after all.

Burnley's positioning inside the Championship relegation zone just months after dropping out of the Premier League and with a managerial casualty already on the list means that few clubs have surprised the masses more than the Clarets, and Woodman's departure from Bromley may not be topped in terms of sheer shock and harshness, yet plenty of other EFL clubs have also upset the odds for either the right or wrong reasons.

Level on points with the Clarets in the second-tier's bottom-three is Derby County, who were widely expected to plot a successful march into the expanded play-offs after finishing in eighth and just four points shy of sixth-placed Wembley winners Hull City but have instead endured a bitterly-disappointing campaign to prompt mass backlash towards under-fire manager John Eustace.

Higher up the Championship, however, both Stoke City and Swansea are bidding to defy the odds with somewhat-unexpected promotion pushes following mid-table finishes, with the latter representing fair value to compete for a top-two finish at this moment in time.

As for the League One promotion race, it's pretty much as you would expect, although Aaron Ramsey's under-embargo Oxford United side are earning plaudits in third position and find themselves ahead of larger, more free-spending clubs without their current transfer restrictions. Neil Harris' eighth-placed Cambridge, fresh off the back of promotion behind Bromley, also deserve some flowers.

Then in League Two, the real surprise position may just be Cheltenham Town's in fifth after winding up in 15th and 18th over the previous two campaigns.

Rotherham United, meanwhile, are facing the threat of successive relegations, which may offer some surprise in light of the Millers' previous status as a yo-yo outfit between the second and third-tiers, although the broader deep-seated issues at the ASSEAL New York Stadium provide more context behind a sudden nosedive.

More surprises, mind you, are only in store as the season toils on, and whereas the ecstasy and enjoyment for some will be rivalled by disappointment and frustration for others, the EFL is only promising to retain its peerless aversion to predictability.
