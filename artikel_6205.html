If you typically fly with American Airlines and its partner airlines, earning elite status in the AAdvantage program can unlock many perks, from complimentary upgrades to priority boarding.

In 2022, American AAdvantage revamped the method for earning elite status to a system based on Loyalty Points.

Nowadays, members earn Loyalty Points through eligible flights, credit card spending and purchases with participating partners.

Here’s what you need to know about American Airlines elite status, including how to earn it and what benefits it brings.

American Airlines elite status gives perks to AAdvantage members who interact with the program and its partners. The program lets you earn Loyalty Points toward elite status through many activities unrelated to travel, including shopping, dining and spending on cobranded American Airlines credit cards.

You can earn each American Airlines elite status tier by accumulating a set amount of Loyalty Points during the elite-qualification year that runs for 12 months from March 1 through the end of February. Once you reach the threshold, your status takes effect immediately and remains valid through March 31 of the following full year.

Note: Loyalty Points and AAdvantage miles are not the same thing (but more on that below).

You’ll also earn Loyalty Point Rewards as you reach specific Loyalty Point thresholds, including some that fall between elite status tiers. You’ll earn your first Loyalty Point Rewards choice after earning 15,000 Loyalty Points within the 12-month qualification period beginning March 1.

To qualify for American Airlines elite status, you must earn the required number of Loyalty Points during each 12-month qualification period following March 1. As discussed, you’ll need to earn 40,000 Loyalty Points for Gold status, 75,000 for Platinum, 125,000 for Platinum Pro and 200,000 for Executive Platinum each qualification period.

We have a full guide to earning Loyalty Points. But, in short, you'll earn two types of American Airlines miles: base and bonus. And while both base and bonus miles are redeemable, generally only base miles earn Loyalty Points. The exception is the elite status bonus on flights, which also counts (more on that below). So, each time you earn a base mile with AAdvantage, you'll also earn a Loyalty Point.

Bask Bank mileage accounts are one example of an activity earning only bonus miles — hence, no Loyalty Points. Basic Economy fares on American don't earn Loyalty Points either.

Note: American and Hyatt ended their enhanced loyalty partnership in 2026, so World of Hyatt status is no longer offered as a Loyalty Point Reward. You can still earn 500 AAdvantage miles per eligible Hyatt stay by giving your AAdvantage number at check-in.

American Airlines elite status comes with some valuable benefits. Here’s a look at some of the most noteworthy benefits, including how they differ by status tier.

American Airlines elite members receive complimentary upgrades on eligible flights to select destinations within North America, the Caribbean and Central America, subject to availability. Depending on the aircraft, these upgrades can move members from Main Cabin to premium economy, from Premium Economy to business or domestic first class, or directly from Main Cabin to business or domestic first class (on aircraft without a Premium Economy cabin).

These upgrades are automatically requested and are available on paid tickets and award tickets. Members can also receive a complimentary upgrade for one companion traveling on the same flight. However, Basic Economy tickets purchased on or after May 18, 2026, aren't eligible for complimentary upgrades.

American will start processing these upgrades at different times depending on your American Airlines elite status tier. Executive Platinum members may have their upgrades confirmed as early as 100 hours before departure, Platinum Pro members as early as 72 hours, Platinum members as early as 48 hours and Gold members as early as 24 hours.

Members with American Airlines elite status — and up to eight passengers traveling with you on the same reservation — can select Preferred seats without any extra fees during booking or anytime after booking (based on availability). Preferred seats have standard legroom but are located in more desirable locations throughout the economy cabin. This benefit does not apply to Basic Economy tickets purchased on or after May 18, 2026.

Main Cabin Extra seats, which offer additional legroom, are also complimentary for AAdvantage elite members and up to eight companions traveling on the same reservation (based on availability). Executive Platinum, Platinum Pro and Platinum members can select these seats at booking, while Gold members must wait until within 24 hours of departure. Basic Economy tickets purchased on or after May 18, 2026, are not eligible for complimentary Main Cabin Extra seats.

The status bonus is added to the base miles earned on eligible flights, and unlike most bonus miles, it counts toward Loyalty Points as well. For example, on flights marketed by American, a member without status earns 5 miles and Loyalty Points per dollar, while an Executive Platinum member earns 11. That makes it easier for higher-tier members to requalify through flying.

AAdvantage elite members enjoy priority access at check-in, security and boarding on eligible American Airlines flights. Specifically, Executive Platinum members board in Group 1, Platinum Pro in Group 2, Platinum in Group 3 and Gold in Group 4. Boarding with any of these groups might mean there’s still space in the overhead bin for your carry-on suitcase.

AAdvantage status members also get priority when standing by for a different American Airlines flight on the same day of travel (and can standby for a later flight the same day) on eligible flights within the U.S. and between the U.S., Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

Executive Platinum and Platinum Pro members — and up to eight companions traveling with them on the same reservation — can enjoy complimentary same-day confirmed flight changes on American when flying to or from the U.S., Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

AAdvantage elite members also enjoy complimentary checked bags when flying American Airlines: Gold members get their first checked bag free, Platinum members get their first two checked bags free, and Platinum Pro and Executive Platinum members get their first three checked bags free. Best of all, this checked baggage allowance is also offered to guests traveling with the status member on the same reservation.

American Airlines elite status is worth it for some travelers. I've held Platinum Pro or Executive Platinum status since 2016 because I value the domestic upgrades, waived checked bag fees, Priority check-in lines and Oneworld Emerald status these tiers provide.

But, after reconsidering what I value in American Airlines elite status, I've only strived to qualify for Platinum Pro in recent years. In short, I decided the incremental value of Executive Platinum over Platinum Pro didn’t justify the extra costs I’d incur to earn the higher status.

It’s important to consider how much you’ll use your AAdvantage perks and the cost of earning American Airlines status. If you’ll organically earn American Airlines status through flights, then it’s likely worth earning. But if you’ll have to earn a significant number of Loyalty Points through other means, such as using the AAdvantage eShopping portal or spending on an AAdvantage credit card, there’s an opportunity cost to doing so.

Finally, if you’ll only use your American Airlines elite status perks occasionally or only need a specific perk, consider whether you can get what you seek through booking premium-cabin fares or applying for a credit card that offers that particular benefit.

American Airlines offers four elite status tiers you can earn by accumulating Loyalty Points: Gold, Platinum, Platinum Pro and Executive Platinum. These tiers offer perks including Preferred seats, complimentary space-available upgrades on eligible American Airlines flights, and Oneworld perks on select partner airlines.

If you frequently fly with American Airlines, British Airways or Iberia on expensive paid fares or spend a significant amount on American Airlines credit cards or when shopping through the AAdvantage eShopping portal, you’ll likely find it easy and worthwhile to earn American Airlines elite status. Otherwise, it's smart to consider whether the value you'll get from American Airlines status is worth the cost you'll incur to earn it.
