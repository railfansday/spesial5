Observation of tunable superradiant frequency combs

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Cavity quantum electrodynamics1 with quantum emitters coupled to resonators provides a powerful platform for engineering light–matter interactions2,3 and exploring many-body phenomena4,5. In particular, superradiance6—arising from collective quantum interference among emitters—has been explored as a route to ultrastable continuous radiation7. However, engineering superradiance in the time domain to realize periodic pulsed sources or frequency combs remains largely unexplored. Here we investigate the non-equilibrium many-body dynamics of a driven spin ensemble coupled with an on-chip superconducting resonator and uncover a dynamical phase transition from continuous-wave to periodic pulsed superradiant emission. To quantitatively capture the observed dynamical phases, we introduce a driven-dissipative cavity-quantum electrodynamics model that elucidates how the periodic pulsed superradiant phase emerges from collective, periodically repeating spin dynamics stabilized by the interplay of coherence growth, disorder and dissipation. We also find that rare-earth ion spin systems with both optical and microwave transitions enable phase-synchronized, dual-rail superradiant frequency combs in both the microwave and the optical domains. Our results not only open new opportunities for atom-based frequency-comb sources and programmable superradiant generation, but establish a fundamental connection between periodic pulsed superradiance and the emergence of a continuous time crystal8,9,10 as a new non-equilibrium phase in driven open systems.

a, Detailed device schematic. A niobium film is patterned on a 500 μm thick 171Yb3+:YVO4 crystal. A 100 nm gold layer is evaporated on the back side, forming a weak optical Fabry-Pérot mode between the front and back chip reflections. b, Calibration of spin-cavity coupling strength. By optically pumping the population into the \(| \downarrow \rangle \) state, we probe the resonant spin-cavity coupling spectrum and its strength while avoiding superradiant effects. The blue data in the bottom subpanel correspond to the continuous-wave (CW) pump being off, while the black data are taken with a 400 μW continuous-wave pump. By fitting to a spin-cavity coupling model, we extract an ensemble coupling strength of \(\sqrt{N}g\approx 2\pi \times 1.1\) MHz, corresponding to a normalized coupling gnorm ≈ 0.6 < 1. The single-spin coupling, g = 2π × 10 Hz, and the effective ensemble size, N = 1010, are estimated from Comsol simulation and dopant concentration. c, Ensemble coupling strength, \(\sqrt{N}g\), as a function of pump power. We observe that the ensemble coupling strength increases with pump power, indicating a larger population in the microwave spin manifold.

a, A pulsed, transient optical pumping scheme that drives 171Yb3+ spins into the \(| \uparrow \rangle \) state. The resulting inverted spin polarization is resonantly coupled to a microwave cavity, leading to a superradiant burst in the microwave domain. b, Example of a delayed superradiant burst. A 1 mW pump is applied for 50 μs, after which a microwave superradiant burst is observed with a delay time τd. The superradiant signal is normalized by its peak-to-peak amplitude. c and d, Burst delay times, τd, and burst width, τw, measured as a function of the optical pump detuning, Δpump, which controls the optical transition strength from \(| g\rangle \) to \(| \uparrow \rangle \). A symmetric dependence of τd and τw is observed, with shorter delay times and narrower widths near resonance. This behaviour is consistent with the expected scaling τd, τw ∝ 1/N, as the pump detuning effectively controls the ensemble size, N, in the microwave spin manifold. Data points represent mean values, with error bars indicating the standard deviation over 300 repeated measurements.

a, Frequency-domain measurement of continuous-wave microwave superradiance, showing a much narrow linewidth of  ≈ 30 Hz. The inhomogeneously broadened ensemble line profile (dashed line) is overlaid for comparison. b, Dependence of the linewidth of continuous-wave superradiance on emission power, PMW. An inverse relationship between the linewidth and the emission power is observed, consistent with a noise-broadened Schawlow-Townes linewidth. The lower dashed line denotes the pump laser noise-limited bound. These results confirm that the disordered spin ensemble emits continuous-wave superradiance with a much narrower spectral linewidth. c, Continuous-wave superradiance emission frequency at different optical pump detunings. A linear correlation between the microwave emission frequency and optical pump detuning is observed, with a slope of  −1.23 kHz/MHz.

a, Four representative data traces under a 1 ms continuous optical pump. Random onset times of the emergent periodic pulsed phase are observed. b, Zoom-in of individual burst trains showing the distribution of burst onset times, {τk}, where k denotes the experimental repetition index. After an initial settling time, both the amplitudes, {Ak}, and the periodicity, T, become independent of k, indicating the establishment of a robust pulsed superradiance phase. c, Burst onset phases, defined as θk = 2π × τk/T (mod 2π), measured over 50 repetitions under identical experimental conditions. The phases are randomly distributed over 2π, demonstrating spontaneous breaking of continuous time-translation symmetry in the absence of any external timing reference.

a, Schematic of periodic pulsed superradiance, characterized by three parameters: the pulse-train periodicity T, the peak emission power Ppeak, and the average emission power \({\overline{P}}_{{\rm{MW}}}\). b-d, show fits to the experimental data presented in Fig. 3 of the main text, where T, Ppeak, and \({\overline{P}}_{{\rm{MW}}}\) exhibit good agreement with the expected scaling behaviours of T ∝ 1/N, Ppeak ∝ N2, and \({\overline{P}}_{{\rm{MW}}}\propto {N}^{2}\), respectively. Here, N is the effective number of spins in the microwave manifold, tuned via the optical pump detuning, Δpump. When sweeping Δpump, the optical pump power is fixed, corresponding to an excitation over an optical inhomogeneity Γop = 2π × 92 MHz in all three cases.

a, Relative contributions of superradiance (blue) and stimulated emission (red) to the emission strength in the periodic superradiance phase. b, Comparison of emission patterns between the many-body case with N = 1010 atoms (blue) and the single-atom case with N = 1 (red). For a fair comparison, the total effective cavity coupling strength is fixed to \({g}_{{\rm{tot}}}=\sqrt{N}g=2\pi \times 1\,{\rm{MHz}}\) for both cases. c, Comparison of emission patterns in the presence (blue) and absence (red) of spin frequency inhomogeneity, i.e., disorder. All simulations are performed using experimental parameters corresponding to the periodic superradiance phase in Regime III.

a and b, show simultaneously recorded microwave periodic superradiance and transferred optical periodic superradiance, respectively, over a duration of up to 100 ms. c, Zoom-in windows near 1, 50, and 100 ms showing synchronized dual-rail emission patterns, maintaining persistent and robust pulse periodicity without decay.

a, Microwave (blue) and optical (yellow) amplitudes, A(t), extracted from IQ detection as A(t) = ∣I(t) + iQ(t)∣. The I and Q signals, I(t) and Q(t), are measured continuously over 2 ms, capturing 30 consecutive superradiant bursts. The microwave and optical channels exhibit synchronized and reproducible temporal profiles. The grey shaded area indicates the noise floor determined from the optical signal, below which the phase extraction is not reliable. b, Normalized amplitude stability over 30 consecutive superradiant bursts. τ denotes the peak emission time, identified for each burst from the time-domain pulse train. The error bars denote the standard deviations of the microwave and optical normalized amplitudes, which are 0.009 and 0.05, respectively. c, Time-domain phase profiles, \(\phi (t)={\tan }^{-1}[Q(t)/I(t)]\), extracted from IQ detection. The microwave and optical pulse trains exhibit synchronized and reproducible phase profiles over time. Phase values in regions dominated by the optical-signal noise floor are greyed out, where large fluctuations are observed. The chirping behaviour during each pulse qualitatively follows the amplitude profile, which can be attributed to an effective Kerr nonlinearity induced intensity dependence. d, Phase stability over 30 consecutive superradiant bursts, zoomed in on the region above the noise floor. The error bars denote the standard deviations of the microwave and optical phase values, which are 0.03π and 0.11π, respectively. See Methods for details.
