This move will prove to pay off for the Suns

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

As we waited for the Phoenix Suns season to start, many questions arose ahead of the final decisions about the team. With them having 15 players under contract, one of them, Mark Williams, out for an extended period before the season starts, and only two centers on the roster, changes were inevitable. That is where we saw Haywood Highsmith get waived, giving the Suns flexibility with their 14-man roster, and we also saw moves on two two-way contracts. These were C.J. Huntley and Koby Brea, who were waived to add more size with Duop Reath and Drew Timme. Both Huntley and Brea remained with the Valley Suns after being waived, but they brought in two new faces who have both seen some time in the NBA.

Reath spent the last three years with the Portland Trail Blazers and brings versatility to the frontcourt with his shooting. In the past, I highlighted him as a low-end free-agent target for the Suns, and I am happy to see him in the Valley this year. Even if he is just there as insurance if another big goes down, the Suns need that, and Reath can provide valuable shooting to a bench unit that already excels at that if he does get an opportunity.

The name that went unnoticed, though, was Drew Timme, who joined the Suns after being waived by the Los Angeles Lakers. As with Jordan Goodwin last season, the Suns brought in someone from the Lakers, and this could prove a strategic move. After Goodwin paid off last offseason, the investment in Timme may also pay off, given his run in the G League so far.

Now, Timme is no stranger to the spotlight; he was a dominant collegiate player at Gonzaga before going undrafted in the 2023 draft. After bouncing around the Nets and Lakers, he now finds himself in the Valley with an opportunity to impress. Even though it is just the G League, these numbers prove that Timme is better than his current standing. We saw this trend with Collin Gillespie his first year in the Valley, and he turned out to be a key piece for the Suns. Timme could be that next buy-low product that the Suns invest in that pans out, and over the last few seasons, that is how they have found key depth pieces.

Both Gillespie and Bouyea were on two-way contracts before landing NBA deals, and with the Suns still having that open roster spot, could that happen by the end of the season? Only time will tell if Timme gets an opportunity, but with his recent play, I wouldn't rule it out. Especially with Mark Williams still out, the Suns lack some size in the frontcourt and have elected to give Koa Peat some small-ball minutes to replicate their third center for now. Could Timme end up fighting for that, depending on whether the Valley Suns are home? Personally, I'd like to see it.

Timme, even though he hasn't had overwhelming success in his 36 NBA games, has shown flashes of impact. Sadly, he played for a pretty poor Nets team, but with the Lakers, he made plays to help Luka Doncic and LeBron James. If you can get those quotes from the GOAT, that must mean something, right?

Timme can come in and be another solid passer and playmaker in this frontcourt. Like Ighodaro, he has the basketball IQ to make not only smart plays but the right ones, and he would help open up more opportunities for those bench scorers. The team is already deep, and as a two-way player, it is not shocking that he hasn't seen much time with the Suns yet, but if injuries open a spot, the Suns should give him that shot.

This shows the Suns' ability to build on the margins. They identify talent that can stand out, and even if it ruffles some feathers, they don't care. They will give up on draft picks or guys who haven't panned out if there is a better name out there, and you have to appreciate that from a business standpoint: they have gotten guys like Gillespie and Bouyea, and they only saw time with injuries. Timme's time may relate to that, but once again he is proving, like those guys, he is better than the G League, which is what the Suns want. They need these small-impact guys to pan out and be better than anticipated to build out the bench and depth, and they've done that.

All in all, as a fan of the team, it is nice to see them make these smart moves compared to the ones that have not worked. It shows they can succeed with these marginal moves and offers some hope that, if there is someone you don't know who is signed to this team, they may actually pan out.
