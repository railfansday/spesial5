Recap: The lights in LA were too bright for Jazz

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

If you went to the middle of Main Street and even suggested that the Jazz would have one of their worst seasons through November, you would have likely been beaten to a pulp. But now, this is our reality. It's nearly comical how sonorously the wheel has fallen off the wagon.

In a game without Jaxson Hayes — Utah's starting frontcourt also watching in street clothes — the LA Lakers were in no room for any funny business, serving the Jazz their 16th loss of the year in a 109-94 finish. This game marked potentially the end of a season series (pending on NBA Cup consolidation matches), which is a bit silly to say in late November.

Will Hardy mentioned pregame that his goal was for the Jazz to limit their turnovers to under 12:

…The team proceeded to record eight turnovers in just the first quarter, and the Lakers scored nine points off of them. It felt like a one-sided battle right from the get-go, as LA toppled Utah by double digits after 12 minutes of action. Our only silver lining was Ace Bailey giving Walker Kessler a piece of him at the rim:

The Jazz have had trouble containing teams and building early advantages ever since their two-game winning streak, which has really struck them where it hurts when they start getting hot.

The second stint saw more of the same, leaving Utah hanging on by a thread. Utah dominated the paint and offensive rebounding, but there wasn't enough scoring (2-for-15 from deep) to allow them to close the gap. Rookie Darryn Peterson was the hot hand for the Jazz in the first half and led the way with 11 points for Utah. They got some impactful minutes from Kyle Filipowski, shooting an efficient 4-for-7 from the floor to rack up 10 points. It's always a pleasure to watch Flip get going, considering his fluctuating minutes and uncertain role on the roster.

The Jazz came out of halftime swinging, showcasing an elite defense anchored by Mo Bamba to come all the way back down 16. They took advantage of Luka Doncic, who had previously recorded four fouls at the half, attacking his side of the floor and giving him no space on the other end of the floor. Kyle Filipowski was completely cooking—he scored 6 of his points in the third period.

The Jazz struggled to spur a fourth-quarter run to build an advantage in the final outing. A 15-3 run by the Lakers gave them a double-digit lead in an instant. After exploding for 35 points in the third quarter, the offense finally dried up. The Jazz could not buy a bucket from deep. from three-point range. Svi Mykhailiuk (0-for-6 from three after hitting 10 triples on Sunday), Darryn Peterson (0-for-3), and Josh Okogie (0-for-3) have combined to go 0-for-12 from beyond the arc.

While Utah has only committed two more turnovers than Los Angeles (15 to 13), their mistakes have been live-ball turnovers that fuel the Lakers' running game. Luka stopper Josh Green fouled out, forcing Utah into heavy minutes for an already depleted rotation.

In his rookie year, it seemed like Kyle Filipowski was destined to be our mini-Lauri Markkanen. The size, the footwork, the athleticism, the jump shot—it was too good to be true. Then, Flip gradually fell out of the rotation over the next two seasons, and the emergence of Mo Bamba seemed to bury him for good. But with the Jazz being extra shorthanded, Kyle Filipowski finally got his shine.

He did most of his damage inside the restricted area, mixing rim finishes with a three-pointer and three assists while limiting turnovers to just one. Will Hardy gave him the Jusuf Nurkic playbook and he took that role with ease. Passing and handling bigs make the offense run with no squeaks.

The first half was as atrocious as you could ask for. The 10 turnovers in the first half had the Jazz playing uphill all night long. The Lakers turned Utah's live-ball mistakes into 18 points off turnovers and fueled a massive 26–9 fast-break advantage late in the fourth quarter. JJ Redick's aggressive defensive pressure and zone looks disrupted the Jazz's style of play.

The lack of defensive discipline was also a major concern—it's mind-blowing how frequently the Jazz put opponents into the bonus early in quarters. Utah sits in the bottom 10 in opponent free-throw rate and averages roughly 23–24 personal fouls per game. Giving away free points at the line prevents Utah from setting their half-court defense and compounds their own poor free-throw shooting (ranking 28th in the NBA in FT%).

The stagnant iso-ball continues to be the worst offensive set this team has ever run. When shots stop falling or defenses turn up the physicality, Utah abandons ball movement. Too many possessions devolve into zero- or one-pass isolations late in the shot clock.

The fourth quarter encapsulated all three of these weaknesses into 12 minutes of ugly basketball. The great defense in the third quarter was wasted the moment they took their foot off the gas.

Utah drops to 3-16 with this loss and lines up their next matchup in Denver on Thursday night, marking the first matchup between these two teams.
