Louisville women's basketball has only 'scratched the surface' of potential

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Who needs a calendar when you've got Jeff Walz?

The Louisville women's basketball coach was quick to remind media members that it was only Nov. 15 after the seventh-ranked Cardinals' 18-point win over then-No. 4 UConn on Sunday at the KFC Yum Center.

"We've got to have the mindset of, if we're going to be all thrilled and excited, I want them to enjoy it tonight. They should," Walz said. "Tomorrow, it's over. Now we've got to get better."

"Better" starts with facing Tennessee State on Nov. 17 and is certainly relative. But it's also a reminder of just how far the Cardinals can go this season. They're 6-0 for the first time in six years. Monday, they rose to No. 3 in the AP poll for the first time since the 2021-22 season. Yet they have only played with all their starters for a full game twice so far this year — against UConn and Ball State — after forwards Elif Istanbulluoglu and Mackenly Randolph, the two biggest vocal leaders on the team, got hurt in the opener against Bellarmine.

They only played 13 and 11 minutes on opening night, respectively, with Randolph missing the second half. But Louisville had built a 72-21 halftime lead by that point. It was all former Louisville standout and current Bellarmine coach Mo Reid needed to see to know that this team can be one of the best Walz has ever had.

"This is the deepest team I've ever seen from him," Reid, who made two Final Four appearances with the Cards, said Nov. 2. "I even told him at halfcourt after the game, 'I hope you win a national championship.' It makes me smile seeing it because I'm still prideful about my school. ... Some of the things wowed me that I saw tonight."

It's too early to outrightly say that this group will be the one to win Walz his ever-elusive national championship. And as Walz alluded to, it's only the third week of the women's basketball season. But his squad is putting together a strong case for at least being in the national title conversation. Whether the Cards live up to hype is on them.

"They have a little bit of everything," UConn coach Geno Auriemma said of Louisville after Imari Berry hung 21 points and six rebounds on his team en route to ACC Player of the Week honors. "They've got size. They've got quickness. They've got speed. They've can shoot. Certainly (Walz's) Final Four teams that we played against were great. But I think, in the last ... I would say in the last four or five years, this is probably one of his best teams that I've seen. They play together, they play hard. They complement each other well. They play really smart basketball."

Berry (16.2 points per game), Taj Roberts (15.5 ppg) and NC State transfer Zam Jones (10.2 ppg) make up a three-headed guard monster that has wreaked havoc on opposing defenses. They've combined to contribute to 48.3% of the team's points this season. That has helped the Cardinals rank 18th nationally in scoring margin (+37.0), 25th in scoring offense (86.7 points per game) and 36th in field goal percentage (47.7%).

And for years, fans have been clamoring for size in the post. The guard play has always been there, but the bigs have been ... on the smaller side. This year, Louisville has five players over 6 feet tall, including the 6-foot-3 Istanbulluoglu and 6-2 Virginia Tech transfer Carys Baker, plus two players listed at 6-4 in underclassmen Grace Mbugua and Ayse Melek Demirer. That size has helped Louisville record a +14.5 rebounding margin over its first six opponents, which ranks 24th nationally.

But with all that said, Louisville still goes back to its head coach's words. It was only Nov. 15. Beating UConn was monumental (and fun) for UofL. The Cardinals' rebounding performance was their best of the season. Defense? Sensational.

But it's one win less than a month into the year. Louisville can still get better with a whole season and postseason ahead of it. That alone should be enough to make fans wonder whether Reid's hope will be fulfilled.

There's still so much that can and needs to happen. For starters, the Cardinals have to stay healthy. Then, they have to stay committed to making the improvements they're capable of while staying consistent to what's earned them six consecutive wins to start the year.

"I feel like we just scratched the surface of how good we can be this year," Berry said. "We've got some more coming for you."
