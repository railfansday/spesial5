‘Smartest guy in the game’ Israel Adesanya blames four-fight skid on terrible fight IQ — ‘I stopped using my greatest weapon’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

"The Last Stylebender" has been doing some serious reflecting.

Former two-time Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) Middleweight champion Israel Adesanya returns to action against Shara Magomedov in just over two weeks (Sat., Dec. 5, 2026) in the UFC Saudi Arabia main event inside The Venue in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia.

Adesanya enters the matchup riding the first four-fight losing streak of his decorated career. He has not won since knocking out Alex Pereira in their April 2023 rematch (watch highlights).

"The Last Stylebender" lost his Middleweight title to Sean Strickland before suffering consecutive stoppage defeats to Dricus du Plessis, Nassourdine Imavov and Joe Pyfer (watch highlights).

Now, the 37-year-old believes he has identified one of the biggest causes of his downfall: he stopped fighting like himself.

"I wanted to prove through many fights that I could knock people out and that I had power because, earlier in my career during my kickboxing days, they said I was too skinny and didn't have any power," Adesanya said on his YouTube channel.

"But then I got into this thing where I wanted to get jacked and just flatline dudes to prove how rugged I am," Adesanya continued. "I didn't have to. That's not my shit. My shit is stylebending — hit and not get hit."

Adesanya was celebrated throughout his championship reign for his fight IQ, mid-fight adjustments and ability to control opponents from distance. During his current skid, however, that patient and calculating approach disappeared.

Instead of relying on the movement and creativity that carried him to the top, Adesanya found himself trying to prove that he could win through toughness and raw power.

"I was trying to prove that I'm tough," Adesanya said. "I don't need to prove that I'm tough, but I don't really know why I thought that. I need to dive deeper into my psyche on this one. Did I let them get to me? Did they make me feel like I had to prove I'm tough?"

"I just need to be who I was — the smartest guy in the game, the most cunning guy, the slickest motherf—ker in the game," Adesanya continued. "I just need to outsmart these fools because I can beat them all — but not at being tough because a lot of them are built tough."

"I'm tough when I need to be, but this [my mind] is my greatest weapon. I stopped using it and made the dumbest decisions in my fights. I don't regret it, but I've learned from it."

Identifying the problem is one thing. Correcting it under pressure is another.

Adesanya has assembled a new team for his latest training camp after spending virtually his entire UFC career with City Kickboxing. The change has apparently helped him rediscover some of the enjoyment that had disappeared during the past few years.

Magomedov should provide Adesanya with an opportunity to return to his preferred style. "Shara Bullet" is a dangerous and creative striker who will likely be willing to engage with the former champion at range rather than build his game plan around wrestling.

Adesanya also believes his close friend Alexander Volkanovski has demonstrated that an aging former champion can still turn back the clock and compete at the highest level.

Now, "The Last Stylebender" must prove he can do the same.

Adesanya no longer wants to show everyone how tough he is.

He wants to remind them how smart he can be.
