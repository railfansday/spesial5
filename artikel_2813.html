Hawks excel off of turnovers, eliminate Hornets in NBA Cup win

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Atlanta Hawks picked up another victory in the NBA Cup on Friday night, eliminating the Charlotte Hornets from cup progression with a 111-97 victory at State Farm Arena. Jalen Johnson led the Hawks with 26 points while Aaron Wiggins added 19 points. For the Hornets, Moussa Diabate scored 21 points, Brandon Miller added 19 points.

After suffering a disappointing loss to the Bulls in overtime in their last NBA Cup appearance, the Hawks were presented a good opportunity to steady their position in the standings with a home tie against a struggling Charlotte side. Naturally, it was the visitors who were in the ascendancy for the majority of the first half. Rallying behind their size, the Hornets secured 12 offensive rebounds leading to 20 second chance points in the first half alone, contributing to the Hornets taking not just double-digit lead in the second quarter but a lead that ran as high as 14 points.

"Diabate was part of it," said Hawks head coach Quin Snyder of the Hornets' rebounding prowess. "Not only do they have he and Kalkbrenner, two guys that it's very difficult to keep them off glass by yourself. If you are able to put a second body on, which we didn't do enough. When we did, he may not get it, but then everybody's got to clean it up. The first part of that, we need to do better. The second part of it, I'd like to get to the second part where he actually doesn't get it (the rebound). A credit to him as far as how relentless he is on the glass. I thought as the game went on, we had some possessions, and those would be guard rebounds. I think RayJ got five (rebounds), he's the smallest guy on the court. We've got to have everybody in the mix to be effective on the defensive glass."

A cold stretch from the Hornets allowed the Hawks the opportunity to chip away at the lead; by the end of which the Hawks had whittled the lead down to just two points heading into the locker room. This momentum carried through into the second half, with the Hawks not only to re-take the lead but established a double-digit lead by the end of the third quarter, progressing to a lead as high as 19 points in the fourth quarter. While a Hornets run briefly brought the lead down to 10 points, it never dipped below double-digits and the Hawks would go on to secure a comfortable victory.

Postgame, Snyder was asked about the Hornets being limited to 40 second half points, and outlined the details that the Hawks tightened up on in the second half.

"I think our rotations were not sharp early, we were just a step behind," said Snyder of the second half. "That's really important for us in our pick-and-roll defense. As we're trying to keep the ball out of the paint, we're going to find ourselves in those situations. Some of it was close-outs, when we switched pick-and-roll we weren't aggressive enough early. They shot just right behind the screen. I just think we weren't as sharp as we needed to be."

One of the keys to victory in this contest were the Hawks' points off of turnovers, scoring 36 off of the Hornets' 17 turnovers.

With Kingston Flemings, Jock Landale, Asa Newell, and Mo Gueye absent, there were opportunities that may not normally be available for RayJ Dennis and Zuby Ejiofor. Both players made meaningful impacts in their time on the court, and we've looked at a few already with the turnovers.

Ejiofor played 13 minutes and scored five points, but his impact goes beyond the boxscore as Snyder outlined postgame.

"I think it's timely as well," said Snyder of Ejiofor's energy off the bench. "There's plays, whether it's a block— you see him dunk but what you don't notice all the time is he sprinted to get to that position. Those are the things ,in my mind, that are hard, because he's running without the promise of a reward. Sometimes for gratification, it's hard. The things that our team needs to do to be good, that's consistent, there's not a payout in the moment. You have to run, even when you don't get the ball, you have to get a hit, even when you don't get the rebound, you have to space, even if you don't get the shot. Those things are important to him. As a result, he puts himself in position to make those kinds of plays. It's the combination of the two things together because what he's doing is selfless, and then he actually gets rewarded for it. It's a really good thing. It's something we talk about and I want our whole team to emulate.When we do it, we're a much better team."

Ejiofor did not back down when confronted with Diabate while on the floor; it was this, in addition to the energy he provided, that stood out to the Hawks last night.

"It was good, rook coming in, you know, not backing down," said Dyson Daniels of Ejiofor. "It's what we like, another big fella getting the mix with Mo (Gueye) being out, that's usually Mo doing that sort of thing. So, Zuby in there, it was really cool to see. He's just come in, giving us really good minutes, giving us good energy around the floor, and he's making some big shots for us. He's really stepped up since Jock's gone down, which has been good."

Dennis, similarly, played 13 minutes, scoring eight points but, similar to Ejiofor, his impact was felt beyond his boxscore.

"It's great when you have guys in the program," said Snyder of RayJ Dennis. "Last year in College Park, this summer, throughout the course of September, he's got a good feel for how we want to play. He played well in Oklahoma City, is all he didn't make a shot. He can get in the lane and when he gets in there he's capable of finishing. He also has good vision, I think he's worked on that, too. Big minutes from him tonight gave us a lift."

Without CJ McCollum and and Kingston Flemings in the lineup, the Hawks have been operating without a primary point guard. It was Dyson Daniels who stepped up with 10 assists last night to lead the Hawks from a playmaking perspective. Daniels himself believe it has been done by committee.

"I think everyone's stepped up," said Daniels of the point guard play without McCollum and Flemings. "Obviously, we don't have like Kingston or CJ, like a true point guard out there but we've just been pushing the pace. Everyone's getting touches, we've got guys that can play with the ball, guys that can play off the ball. So I think everyone's just filled those voids. Guys like RayJ (Dennis) have been really good coming in as well, playing, some good minutes for us. Corey (Kispert) coming as well, playing some really good minutes. Everyone's kind of stepped up, which has been good."

While Daniels starred in his own role, there was little doubt as to who the MVP was for the Hawks last night, and that was Jalen Johnson. Johnson scored 26 points on 10-of-15 shooting from the field and 3-of-4 from three:

As we looked at earlier, Johnson excelled in transition/off of Charlotte turnovers. The manner of which he was able to manoeuvre through bodies in transition to get to the rim looked effortless at times; he just looked in total command in the open court.

It hasn't been an easy season for Johnson shooting the three-pointer, shooting 22% on the season, and while it was encouraging to see Johnson hit 3-of-4 from, the poor percentages so far are't going to dissuade the Hawks and Johnson from shooting.

"I think he knows that we want him to keep shooting," said Snyder of Johnson. "The catch-and-shoots that he hit were quick decisions. I thought the thing that he did tonight where he was really impactful was he played without the ball. That's important for him and for us, particularly when people are game-planning for him, whether it was a matchup on the ball, his ability to do both, to get out in transition, to get to the foul line, to offensive rebound, all those things add up. I think it calms your nervous system a little bit, too. He knows how we feel about him. His shooting the ball is important for us, so it's good to see a couple go in, no question about it. I don't want to obsess about it either because there's lots of ways he can help us."

"I mean it happens," added Johnson of his three-point shooting. "Obviously I haven't shot the ball the way I wanted to but I put in the work. I'm not worried about the percentage or whatnot, I know my shot is going to fall. It was good to see a couple go in for sure. I'm going to keep shooting the shots that I work on and just continue to let them fall."

The Hawks move to 2-1 in the NBA Cup and are currently 2nd in the group, trailing the Boston Celtics who have played just two cup games (2-0). The Hawks and Celtics will face-off next Friday at the TD Garden, the winner practically guaranteed to advance from Group C. The Hawks are obviously motivated to win, but the mechanisms of the NBA Cup provide another incentive to win.

"I mean, obviously we want that money," said Jalen Johnson when asked how the Hawks prepare for a cup game. "So, I think it's as simple as that. Obviously we understand that the stakes are higher considering what's for the reward. So, I think we all young, we all like an extra prize and money. So, I think just upping our intensity, upping our focus."

The Hawks (10-6) are back in action on Sunday afternoon where they host the Detroit Pistons (10-5) at State Farm Arena.
