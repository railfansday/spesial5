Lakers guard mimics Paul Pierce’s iconic line after burying Suns

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

PHOENIX — Quentin Grimes broke out of his early-season shooting slump in a major way during the Lakers' 131-123 win over the Suns on Sunday at Mortgage Matchup Center.

And he arguably had the biggest sequence of the game to seal the victory.

Quentin Grimes of the Los Angeles Lakers handles the ball against the Phoenix Suns in Phoenix, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

After the Suns cut what was once a 31-point Lakers lead late in the third quarter to five points with just over a minute left in the game, Jake LaRavia missed a corner 3-pointer that could've given the Lakers a larger cushion or given the Suns an opportunity to make it a single-possession game.

Grimes made sure the latter didn't happen, crashing the offensive glass and grabbing the rebound off LaRavia's miss to give the Lakers another shot at scoring.

Quentin Grimes (5) of the Los Angeles Lakers dribbles the ball against the Phoenix Suns on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, at Mortgage Matchup Center in Phoenix. NBAE via Getty Images

The veteran wing took matters into his own hands when he banked in a pull-up 3 to put the Lakers up 129-121 with 32.2 seconds left to ice the game.

Did Grimes call bank before making the game-sealing 3?

"I called game," Grimes said before repeating the line twice. "I mean, the bank ain't open on Sunday. I called game. Trying to get out of here and get back to L.A."

Paul Pierce, who was playing for the Wizards at the time, famously responded "I called game!" after banking in the game-winning shot of Game 3 of the Wizards' Eastern Conference Semifinals playoff series against the Hawks.

Los Angeles Lakers guard Quentin Grimes speaks to reporters following a game against the Phoenix Suns in Phoenix on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

While Grimes had fun with his response to whether he intentionally banked in the 3, Sunday was an important step for him to break out of his shooting slump.

The sixth-year guard made a season-high five 3s against the Suns, with his performance on Sunday increasing his 3-point percentage from 24.2% to 33.3%.

The Lakers are confident Grimes, who's shot just over 36% on 3s for his career, will continue to find his groove with his perimeter shot.

"Watching him every day, when he is in the gym, he's very consistent with his routine," coach JJ Redick said. "And we tell players this all the time: Whether you're shooting well or you're shooting poorly, you should just keep the same routine. Because otherwise, then it becomes, 'is shooting a superstitious thing?' I don't think it is, right? Shooting is based on form and reps and confidence, and the only way to build that confidence is through the reps.

"He's just handled it really well. He's been in the gym, he's got the shots up, he's done his routine. He's historically had some really good shooting seasons both from catch-and-shoot and off-the-dribble. We don't think he's a 26% [3-point shooter] or whatever he is, I don't know what he's been before [Sunday] but somewhere in the 20s. He's a high 30s guy."
