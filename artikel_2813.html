PFT's Week 9 2026 NFL picks

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Through Sunday, I’d somehow managed to go 13-0 in the Week 8 picks. The only thing standing in the way of unprecedented PFT perfection was Monday night — Bears at Seahawks.

I’ll still take 13-1. And I’ll keep trying to run the table.

For the year, I’m now 73-47-1. That’s 60.3 percent.

The goal this week? To not backslide to 1-14, with 30 teams playing and the Steelers and Titans off.

It’s a short-week trap for the Jaguars, who managed to not fall into the Week 8 trap against the short-staffed Colts.

The Falcons are way too inconsistent to trust in any given game, especially against a Bengals team that is clicking far more often than it isn’t. Besides, the mini-Super Bowl vibe of an international game will likely push quarterback Joe Burrow to a playoff level of performance.

It’s a Super Bowl 50 rematch, between one team that is a Super Bowl contender and one team that is not.

It’s a Super Bowl V rematch, between one team that knows it’s not a Super Bowl contender and one team that continues to delude itself.

The Chiefs may play with their food (again) on Sunday, but they’ll eventually eat the whole meal.

The Dolphins should not be taken lightly. The Lions can’t afford to do that, given the quality of the division they occupy.

The Saints still have a chance to get things right, and they’re desperate to avoid starting 0-4 in the Superdome.

It’s suddenly the Bizarro NFC East, with the Giants in first place and the Eagles in the basement.

The Rams have fully emerged from their early-season slump.

There are some reasons for hope in Houston. There is no reason to expect any positive change from the Chargers, who continue to behave as if they’re closer to 7-0 than 0-7.

Did you hear Tom Brady is returning to Foxboro? (If you didn’t, we envy you.) The Patriots need to keep pace with the Bills, and the Packers (while good) need Micah Parson back before they’ll have a shot at being great.

If the Seahawks pick up a fifth loss before Veteran’s Day, they’ll almost definitely be sitting at home on Valentine’s Day.

In a renewal of a rivalry so intense that they had to stop playing each other in the preseason, the Raiders will make it interesting.

The Bucs have found their formula for winning, and they’re in position to run away with the NFC South. That likely won’t be enough to overcome a Bears team that is now Super Bowl-ready.

Josh Allen is good enough to find the holes in the Minnesota defense before the rush can get to him. And the Vikings’ offense isn’t good enough to keep pace.
