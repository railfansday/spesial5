Lewis Hall outclassed Lamine Yamal in Madrid.

The match ending Spain 2 England 3 but that only tells a very small part of the story.

England by a huge distance the better team and going 3-0 up in less than an hour, it should have been more.

Spain massively got away with one as moments of madness from Jarell Quansah, Elliot Anderson and Anthony Gordon, threatened to destroy what had been a supreme team performance.

With England so much on top, their individual errors in a 12 minute second half spell directly led to the two undeserved Spanish goals and Thomas Tuchel’s team having to play the final 30 or so minutes a man down.

Somebody who didn’t put a foot wrong was Lewis Hall.

We have to now pay tribute and give our thanks to those in the media who have now suddenly ‘discovered’ the brilliant 22-year-old.

I wonder what lifelong Newcastle United fan Lewis Hall has been up to these last few years…?

Lamine Yamal had played in 32 competitive matches for Spain, where they had won 27 of them, drawing five and losing none.

Then the winger ran into Lewis Hall, again.

Lamine Yamal losing in his 33rd competitive appearance for Spain, his first ever.

The only surprise about this Lamine Yamal v Lewis Hall contest was that anybody should be surprised.

It was only in March this year that the pair were up against each other when Newcastle United hosted Barcelona at St James’ Park, Lewis Hall up against Lamine Yamal.

Lamine Yamal scored a fortunate late equaliser from the penalty spot, however, there was no doubt about who won this duel of top class winger and elite lifelong Newcastle United fan.

Barcelona star man Lamine Yamal wasn’t able to dribble past Lewis Hall even once in the 90+ minutes. Whilst the United defender was superb going forward, creating five chances, the most created by any player on the pitch.

On top of all of that, the appalling referee failed to book Lamine Yamal for a cynical foul on Lewis Hall, after yet again the NUFC player got the better of him. A yellow card which would have been Yamal’s third in the competition and seen him suspended for the second leg.

Last night in Madrid was very reminiscent of St James’ Park in March, the only joy Lamine Yamal got was when he was marked by somebody else out wide, or when he came inside to get away from Lewis Hall. Plus of course those two awful errors from Quansah and Gordon that led to the Spanish goals.

It was also amusing to see once again last night, what happens when things go against a young prima donna such as Lamine Yamal. His diving was so impressive and his petulance was a joy to see as he reacted to Hall and England’s dominance with fouls of his own as things weren’t going his and Spain’s way.
