Dave Challinor sends Sheffield Wednesday message after Barry Bannan captaincy query

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Sheffield Wednesday's captaincy situation has been played down by Dave Challinor, despite Barry Bannan handing the armband to Liam Cooper and not Liam Palmer in last Friday night's win at Barnsley.

Henrik Pedersen installed Palmer as the club captain in January when Bannan left Hillsborough to join Millwall. After returning to Sheffield Wednesday in the summer, Bannan didn't pick up the armband he left behind, with Palmer instead continuing in the role.

However, the defender has been in just three starting line-ups this season, meaning Bannan has taken over the armband a considerable amount of the time under both Pedersen and Challinor.

In last Friday's win against Barnsley at Oakwell, Bannan wore the armband until he was replaced by Harry Gray in the 78th minute. Despite Palmer coming off the bench seven minutes earlier, it was Cooper who took the armband.

In the wake of that happening, Challinor was quizzed by Joe Crann of The Wednesday Word about the captaincy situation.

He responded by confirming he'd not given who wore the armband at Oakwell much thought at all.

"I've given it zero thought," he said. "Baz came off and whoever he passes the armband to (is the captain). I've said before, I'm not huge on — I know some of them (the players and fans) will be — who wears the armband."

Challinor also offered an insight into his leadership group: "We have a group that I spoke to on the first day I came into the building — the leadership group. Baz, Palms (Palmer), Coops (Cooper), Lummers (Joe Lumley), Billy (Mitchell) and Dish (Di-Shon Bernard) are all part of that. It makes no odds, I want leaders on the pitch.

"If you're wearing an armband, it makes no difference to me."

Wednesday are back in action on Saturday afternoon when they host Reading FC at Hillsborough.

Pedersen was in a difficult position when Bannan walked out of Hillsborough to go to Millwall, and the obvious choice was to give the captaincy to Palmer — a long-standing member of the squad and someone who had stuck with Wednesday through difficult periods.

Bannan's return to the club in the summer led to many believing he would take on the captaincy again, but Pedersen rightly stuck by the man who had stuck by Wednesday in Palmer.

The fact Palmer isn't a guaranteed starter makes this a topic of discussion once again. If Bannan is starting the majority of games and Palmer isn't, he will naturally wear the armband more. The same can be said of Cooper, who has started 13 times in the league this season. He was the club captain at Leeds United for a decade and led them to the Premier League — he's another strong candidate to captain Wednesday.

A new manager also brings new ideas, and Challinor won't have the loyalty to Palmer that Pedersen did. He's also clearly got a few contenders in Lumley, Mitchell and Bernard, who could be longer-term options for the captaincy.

A change in the coming months would be no surprise.
