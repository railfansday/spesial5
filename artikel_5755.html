Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Closing the last mile of value-based care with AI" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

One of the most challenging parts of value-based care is its timing. Too often, diagnosis gaps or risks arrive late, after an appointment or follow-up, when there is less opportunity to improve care and results.

This is a persistent last-mile challenge in VBC: translating population-level information into action within everyday workflows. Reports, payer portals, and standalone analytics tools may identify important opportunities, but practices are often left on their own to connect those insights to the clinicians and teams responsible for addressing them.

AI can help close that distance. Instead of simply generating more insights, AI combined with clinical and payer data can surface actionable intelligence at the point in time when teams address care gaps, evaluate diagnoses, and monitor VBC performance.

Put VBC insights where clinicians can act on them

Identifying a potential diagnosis gap is only part of the job. A clinician still needs enough information to determine whether the diagnosis is supported by the patient’s record and, if so, document it appropriately. If finding that evidence requires searching through the chart or consulting a separate tool, a useful insight can create additional work before it leads to action.

The stakes are also rising. As scrutiny of risk-adjustment coding increases, practices need confidence that every documented diagnosis is supported by the record. An AI suggestion surfaced without clearly documented rationale can add risk, instead of the opposite.

This raises the standard for AI in diagnosis-gap workflows. Each suggested gap should come paired with clinical evidence that references relevant lab and imaging results, vital signs, and clinical notes, so that clinicians can quickly evaluate recommendations within the visit time.

athenahealth’s Clinically Inferred Diagnosis Gaps was built around this model. It uses generative AI to analyze existing chart information and surface potential gaps related to the CMS-HCC risk-adjustment model, along with supporting evidence, inside the clinician’s existing workflow.

Bring payer insights into the clinical workflow

With visibility across care settings, payers have a unique line of insight into patients’ overall care journeys. This more comprehensive view may reveal potential diagnosis gaps beyond what a clinician encounters during an individual visit. Bringing those payer-supplied gaps into the clinical workflow can give clinicians additional information to consider while the patient is in front of them.

New workflows connect payer insights with point-of-care clinical actionability. athenahealth’s moment of care diagnosis-gap workflow allows payers to flag a potential diagnosis gap prior to an upcoming appointment, allowing clinicians enough time to review during the encounter. If the clinician determines that a diagnosis is appropriate, they can document it through the existing workflow and can even close the loop by sharing information back to the payer about how the gap was addressed.

Find VBC opportunities across your patient population

For true value at scale, organizations need to be able to look beyond individual patient visits and at their broader populations. Quality and population health leaders need to answer practical questions across entire patient populations: Which patients still have an open care gap? How are clinicians performing on specific quality measures? Where should the team focus its attention next? Getting those answers can require configuring reports, combining results, or exporting data for further analysis.

AI tools offer powerful new ways to facilitate a macro view, plumbing data to surface trends, emerging needs, and risk profiles that can help direct strategies. Rather than requiring users to translate each question into a predefined report, Sage™, athenaOne’s AI assistant, gives them another way to investigate VBC performance and zero in on the patients, clinicians, or measures that need a closer look. For independent practices, that can make population-level information more accessible to the people responsible for turning it into action.

Reduce the work between VBC insight and action

The value-based care burden can be heavy for independent practices with thinner teams and fewer resources. Integrated AI-native platforms with smarter insights, payer data, population analytics, and performance reporting can help practices manage the coordinated efforts required to succeed in VBC.

When these stakeholders and systems can communicate, their independent value becomes exponentially greater. When information is spread across separate reports, payer portals, analytics tools, and clinical systems, practice teams may have to find and reconcile data before they can decide what needs attention.

Bringing more of that intelligence into the environment where clinicians and quality teams already work creates a more direct path: Find an opportunity → add relevant context → get the information to someone who can act → understand performance.

That is the larger opportunity for AI-enabled VBC: not simply generating more information, but making useful clinical, payer, and performance insights easier to put to work.
