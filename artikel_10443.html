The Missing Layer In Enterprise AI Is The Human

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Vivek Bhaskaran is founder & CEO of QuestionPro, a global leader in survey and research, data and insights services.

In 2025, a request kept showing up in our customer conversations: “Please disable AI in your app. We’re not allowed to use it.”

Twelve months later, same customers, opposite request: “Turn AI on. Teach us how to use it.”

I have been running QuestionPro since 2005. I have never seen buyer sentiment flip that fast. The wait-and-see crowd went all in, almost overnight. The models got genuinely good at reasoning, and agents started doing the work instead of just chatting about it. The fog burned off.

But once the fog lifts, you can see what is actually missing. And what is missing in most enterprise AI is not more intelligence. It is people.

Consider the pace. In 2018, AI models could barely string together a coherent sentence. By 2022, they could write basic HTML. In 2026, they are working through math problems that sat unsolved for 90 years. It took humanity 66 years to go from the first powered flight to landing on the moon. We are compressing that kind of leap into a few product cycles.

Every company now has access to roughly the same brilliant brain. You rent it. Your competitor rents it. That brain knows everything about the world, but almost nothing about your company.

Think of it as the smartest new hire you have ever made, permanently stuck on day one. Today, most enterprise AI works like this: You type a prompt, it gives a response. The AI only knows what you tell it, and the answer is only as good as your prompt.

The next phase will likely be different. AI is expected to pull in organizational context on its own, including your customers, market, people, strategy, rulebooks and regulatory and compliance obligations. Context is what turns general intelligence into an answer that actually fits your business.

What Leaders Can Take From This: Stop measuring your AI strategy by which AI model you picked. Measure it by what your AI knows about your organization that a competitor’s AI does not.

Much of that context is documents, or policies, contracts, playbooks and compliance rules. Those are relatively easy to plug in. Point the agent at the folder and you are halfway there.

The hard part is human context. Why customers actually churn. What employees say when they think leadership is not listening. How a market feels about your price increase before it shows up in the revenue line. None of that lives in a PDF. It lives with your voice-of-customer programs, your research teams and your employee experience teams.

For years, insights teams have been treated as a reporting function. They are the people who send the 40-slide deck that gets skimmed five minutes before the board meeting. That era is over. In an AI-driven company, insights teams become infrastructure. They supply the human signal that every AI system and every AI-informed decision depends on.

This changed how I lead. I stopped asking my team, “What AI features should we ship?” and started asking, “What does our AI still not understand about humans?” The second question produces far better roadmaps.

What Leaders Can Take From This: Find out where your insights, CX and EX teams sit in your AI roadmap. If the answer is “They get the output later,” then you have your org chart upside down.

Here is a test I like. Take two customers who look identical in your data. Same age bracket, same ZIP code, same purchase history, same plan tier. On a spreadsheet, they are twins.

Now, ask them why they bought from you. You will often find they are worlds apart. One is a loyal fan. The other is one bad support ticket away from leaving.

That is the difference between being profiled and being understood. Behavioral data profiles people. Only asking people, and listening to the answer, produces understanding. AI is extraordinary at the first. Without a human-context layer, it is guessing at the second, very confidently.

There is a data quality trap here, too. As AI gets better at generating responses, it also gets better at faking them. Bots and synthetic answers are flooding research the same way spam once flooded inboxes. You need to know which data came from a verified, real human being. Feed an AI fake people and it will make very precise decisions about customers who do not exist.

What Leaders Can Take From This: Ask your vendors and your own teams how they verify that human data is actually human. If nobody can answer quickly, that is your answer.

I ride motorcycles, and I have ridden in more than a dozen countries. Riding is a great equalizer. It puts you far from the boardroom and close to how most people actually live. Every trip reminds me that people are more surprising, more contradictory and more interesting than any dataset suggests.

One of our customers summed it up better than I can: Customers like talking to people.

Technology can accelerate our knowledge and expertise, but it will never replace authenticity and connection. The companies that prove most successful in the next decade will not be the ones that remove humans from the loop. They will be the ones whose AI understands humans best.

That is the missing layer. It is human. And the good news is that you already have it. You just have to plug it in.
