Norse Atlantic Faces Fight for Survival as Cash Crisis Deepens

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Norse Atlantic Airways is fighting for its survival after warning on Wednesday that it needs fresh funding to continue operating. In a short stock exchange filing, the Norwegian low-cost, long-haul carrier described its financial situation as “critical.”

The company said it had explored various strategic alternatives with its advisors and was open to discussions over a potential takeover.

More immediately, Norse is in talks with selected parties to secure short-term financing. However, the airline warned that “there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution.”

Shares in Norse plunged to an all-time low following the announcement, down more than 70% as of 1 p.m. Oslo-time on Wednesday. The company did not disclose how much money it needs or how long it can continue operating without additional funding.

Norse Atlantic Airways told the market on Wednesday that its financial situation is "critical" and that it needs fresh funding to keep operating, sending its shares down more than 70% to an all-time low. The Norwegian low-cost, long-haul carrier said it is in talks with selected parties over short-term financing and is open to discussions about a takeover, but warned there is no assurance the talks will produce a viable solution. It did not disclose how much money it needs or how long it can operate without it. The warning comes despite strong commercial indicators: planes were almost 95% full in October and unit revenues on its own network rose 19% year over year, though on a much smaller schedule of 199 flights versus 355 a year earlier. Norse has been shifting its 12 Boeing 787s toward ACMI work for other carriers — most recently a deal with Pakistan International Airlines covering two 787-9s on UK-Pakistan flights — alongside a rights issue in June, a $52 million financing agreement in August, and Project Falcon, a cost program targeting $50 million in annual savings from 2027. The warning lands at the start of winter, the weakest period for European leisure-focused airlines.
