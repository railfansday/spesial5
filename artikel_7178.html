The 3 Paths to Being a Better Marketer That Have Nothing to Do with Marketing.

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Every CMO knows the drill: as a brand scales, founders and the executive committee reach out asking how to spot true marketing leadership in a sea of impressive resumes.

My answer is always the same: It’s not easy, but it is simple. Finding the right person requires looking beyond pedigree, predictable career steps, and whether they have done the exact job before. When I was hired by my CEO, he said 10 words I will never forget: “It’s not about what you’ve done, but who you are.” That sentiment still holds true today.

Only 6% of CXOs think the best profile for effective CMOs is a strong academic foundation. In other words, 94% of leaders who have a CMO reporting to them don’t think this is the best profile. But exclusive research conducted by NewtonX and ADWEEK for the Marketing Vanguard program reveals there is no single path to CMO effectiveness. Instead, it suggests the experiences gained outside of marketing are the secret to success.

Whether you’re formally educated or not, becoming a great top marketer is about the ability to gain and apply cross-functional experience. For example, it isn’t surprising that the top profile (24%) for a CMO leader is the “Hybrid Builder,” someone who combines academic marketing credentials with experience in product, sales, or consulting.  The second most popular profile (20%) is ‘no clear pattern,’ indicating effective CMOs can come from diverse backgrounds.

So what then are the diverse backgrounds that interest leadership? Commercial Operations, Entrepreneurship, and Data Science/Tech all rose to the top.

Those with Operations backgrounds represent 18% of responses. They have experience owning revenue, P&L, or general management. “It serves as evidence that they can connect marketing decisions to commercial performance and enterprise level outcomes,” says Jasmin Siegle, Senior Director of Strategic Insights & Analytics at NewtonX.

Entrepreneurs represent 16% and have experience operating under pressure. Having built or scaled a business signals comfort with ambiguity, resource constraints and accountability. All these are qualities that can help CMOs move beyond campaign execution and contribute more broadly to business strategy.

And in an increasingly measured and mar tech driven world, those with a data-science background are considered attractive cross-overs. Digitally native leaders also represented 16%. “Their value is not simply technical expertise; it is the ability to use data, technology, and analytics to guide decisions and demonstrate marketing’s contribution to measurable business outcomes,” says Jasmin Siegle, Senior Director of Strategic Insights & Analytics at NewtonX.

Taking a step back and looking at the similarities across disciplines, a picture emerges that suggests marketing effectiveness is defined more by behaviors and skills, not a single background or education.

For those that have it, appreciate your marketing education but don’t be complacent. The role is being rewritten in real time. There will always be something to learn. If your experience lies in a different field but you’ve always been interested in marketing, your skills are more transferable than you think.

Those who are best positioned for success are the ones who have the marketing background and education, and diverse cross-functional experience.

The research tells us that above all, everyone is on their own journey. Success rewards those who are proactive in benchmarking themselves and then creating steps-to-the-goal so they can possess the necessary skills for modern marketing leadership.
