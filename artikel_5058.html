Bitcoin faces fresh $60,000 threat as Fed minutes reveal overwhelming support for more rate hikes

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Federal Reserve officials favor further tightening as Bitcoin struggles near $62,000, with shrinking stablecoin supply and leveraged positions increasing downside risks.

Bitcoin, gold, and silver had already fallen on Wednesday before the Federal Reserve released its October meeting minutes.

Almost all participants at the October meeting judged that further rate increases would likely be appropriate in the months ahead under their baseline outlooks for inflation and employment.

Bitcoin traded in the $62,000 zone for most of the day after the minutes, while spot gold closed down 3% to $3,741.82 an ounce, and silver fell 5.3% to $44.88 an ounce. Gold had touched its lowest level since September 2025 earlier in the session.

Even after the minutes, CME FedWatch odds were 92% for a quarter-point hike in December and 8% for a half-point move.

Analysts linked the drop in bullion to the stronger dollar, rising Treasury yields and expectations of tighter policy. Gold pays no interest, so rising yields increase the cost of holding it instead of putting cash into interest-bearing assets.

The Silver Institute’s 2026 market forecast projects a 3% decline in overall industrial use, even as demand outside photovoltaics is expected to grow 4.5%. The institute still expects total silver demand to exceed supply by 35 million ounces this year.

The October minutes show a committee that held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% by a 10-2 vote.

Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack preferred a quarter-point increase, while a couple of participants said a move larger than 25 basis points could warrant consideration at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavorable.

Bitget analyst Lewis Huang shared in a note that the record points to wider backing for a hike than the vote count shows. He said:

“The minutes suggest broader support for an October hike than the 10–2 vote alone implied—and that was before payrolls and CPI both came in hot. Governor Waller has said a half-point increase should be considered; evidence that other officials were already open to that scale of tightening in October could push the odds of a 50-basis-point move higher.”

Huang pointed to yields and the dollar as the channel into Bitcoin, saying that a further rise in yields and the dollar would add pressure to Bitcoin, which is now trading well below its 200-week moving average near $68,000. Gold could also struggle if real yields keep climbing.

Fabian Dori, chief investment officer at Sygnum Bank, sees the same path. He said:

“Markets already price a run of hikes into next year, and the minutes suggest the committee was leaning that way even before October’s hot CPI.”

Dori added that this likely keeps long-term yields and the dollar firm, and the question for Bitcoin is whether debasement concerns and advancing institutional adoption can outweigh it.

Fed staff also outlined that falling prices could force investors to sell assets to cut borrowing, while withdrawals could force fund managers to raise cash.

In very severe scenarios, sales by hedge funds, bond mutual funds and target-date funds could be large enough to impair market functioning. Staff were concerned that selling into a falling market could push prices down further and strain trading.

A softer dollar and lower Treasury yields would ease some pressure on markets, and Bitcoin’s own demand gauges give investors another way to judge a rebound.

Farside Investors’ ETF flow table shows over $310 million in net outflows from US spot Bitcoin funds on Nov. 17 and 18.

Glassnode’s Nov. 17 analysis estimated that about 20% of the coins held by short-term holders were acquired between $62,000 and $66,000. That puts Wednesday’s reported price near the low end of a range where many recent buyers entered.

Using the same Nov. 17 data, Glassnode modeled a cluster of potential long liquidations between $59,500 and $62,200, where a renewed decline could force some leveraged positions to close.

Glassnode puts short-term holders’ overall cost basis at $74,100, and a return to that level would bring recent buyers back around break-even as a group.

Huang named the weekend low as the level to watch:

“For Bitcoin, a break below the weekend low near $62,000 could extend the sell-off,” he said.

Stablecoin supply had shrunk 4.2% over 90 days, and Bitcoin briefly traded at a premium on Coinbase around the November low before slipping back to a small discount relative to the global price.

Georges Chouchani, founder and CEO of Euclid, links the minutes to that cash shortage:

“The minutes show support for a hike went well beyond the two officials who voted for one in October, so markets start pricing more hikes after December.”

He said that this keeps yields and the dollar firm, and it hits a crypto market already short of cash. Stablecoin supply, the dollar tokens crypto trades with, has fallen by about $16 billion over the past month, its steepest drop since June 2022, so there are fewer buyers to absorb the selling, and Bitcoin takes the hit.

A premium that holds, sustained ETF inflows, or a leveling off in stablecoin supply would give investors more evidence that demand is returning.
