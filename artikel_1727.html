Prediction: Hungary vs Northern Ireland

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Everything is on the line in Budapest on Tuesday evening as Hungary and Northern Ireland meet in the decisive match of their UEFA Nations League campaign.

The visitors lead the group by a single point heading into the final round of fixtures, meaning a draw would secure promotion for Northern Ireland, while Hungary must win to leapfrog their rivals and clinch top spot.

Hungary head into the final round of Nations League group matches knowing that only a victory will be enough to secure automatic promotion to League A.

Marco Rossi’s side currently sit second in the group with 10 points, one behind leaders Northern Ireland, meaning the equation is straightforward at the Puskas Arena. A win would send the Magyars into first place and guarantee promotion, while any other result would leave them depending on a promotion playoff.

Hungary boosted their chances with a 2-0 victory over Georgia on Saturday, although they were made to work hard for the breakthrough and relied on an own goal from Lasha Dvali to open the scoring.

That success was their third consecutive Nations League win and continued a strong run of form that has seen them lose only once in their last nine matches in all competitions. During their recent three-game winning streak, Hungary have scored five goals and kept two clean sheets.

Home form has also been encouraging. The Magyars have won four of their last six matches when playing on home soil and will believe they have a genuine opportunity to overturn the one-point deficit separating them from Northern Ireland.

Dominik Szoboszlai revealed he almost suffered an injury before the victory over Georgia, but the Liverpool midfielder is expected to feature and could operate in an advanced role.

Hungary are without Milan Vitalis through suspension, while captain Willi Orban is also banned, potentially forcing Attila Szalai into the starting lineup.

Barnabas Varga is expected to lead the attack and will be aiming to score his 17th international goal.

Northern Ireland arrive in Budapest sitting at the top of the group and knowing that a draw will be enough to secure promotion to League A for the first time.

Michael O’Neill’s side strengthened their position with a hard-fought 1-0 victory over Ukraine on Saturday, courtesy of a long-range strike from Terry Devlin. That result moved them onto 11 points and left them in control of their own destiny heading into the final matchday.

The Green and White Army have become extremely difficult to beat. Their only defeat in their last eight matches came against France in June, with four wins and three draws recorded during that period.

While Northern Ireland have not always been prolific, they have consistently found ways to collect results. They have scored exactly one goal in five of their last eight games and have developed a reputation for staying competitive in tight contests.

Their record against Hungary is reasonably encouraging too. Northern Ireland have only lost one of the last four meetings between the nations and held Hungary to a goalless draw earlier in the group stage.

The visitors have also won both of their away fixtures in this Nations League campaign, although their broader away record remains mixed.

Jamie Donley will miss the game after picking up a yellow card against Ukraine.

His absence could see Josh Magennis lead the attack, with Ethan Galbraith and Isaac Price expected to provide support from wide areas.

Shea Charles should once again be tasked with controlling the midfield.

This is effectively a winner-takes-all contest for automatic promotion, although Northern Ireland hold the advantage of knowing that a draw will achieve their objective.

Hungary’s recent form and strong home record make them slight favourites to claim victory, but Northern Ireland have built their campaign on defensive resilience and consistency. The visitors have proved extremely difficult to break down throughout the group and already held Hungary scoreless earlier in the campaign.

With the stakes so high and neither side likely to take unnecessary risks, a closely contested encounter seems to be on the cards, and the visitors are likely to leave with what they come for: a point.
