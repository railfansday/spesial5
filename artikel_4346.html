8 Mods That Already Exist For The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake has only been around for a short while, but yet there are people who have been working diligently to change the game. They have already ported the game over to PC, allowing people to play it if they don’t have a Switch 2.

Of course, since the PC community has its hands on the Ocarina of Time Remake, that means that they are going to mod it. After all, nothing can remain unchanged when it comes to those playing on PC.

Do you want a photo mod in the game? Would you rather the auto jump be back instead of the manual jump? Well, there’s a mod for that.

You are quite limited on where you can take Epona in Ocarina of Time. You can really only ride Epona around Hyrule Field, which is fine, but it would be nice to take her to different locations.

Well, there is a mod that allows you to do just that. It is called Epona Everywhere, and it removes all the limitations around Epona, allowing you to take her anywhere you want.

This means that you can ride Epona into Kakariko Village or into the Lost Woods. You can even take her into the dungeons to give her the full experience - though I don’t think she is going to help you solve the game’s puzzles.

Instead of there being a traditional auto jump in the Ocarina of Time Remake, Nintendo decided to make it a manual jump, allowing Link to jump whenever the player decides rather than hitting the ledge of an object.

While many think of this as a great change, there are some purists out there who miss the days of old when Link could only jump at specific times. Because of this, modders wasted no time change the game, making it so auto jump is once again alive and well.

With this mod, it returns the game to its classic format, making it so you can’t jump unless on the edge of an object or piece of the environment. It’s an odd choice, but oh well.

Thomas Mahler believes Nintendo missed an opportunity to improve the level design in the Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake.

Controllers are just nice to use. Keyboard and mouse are good for certain games, but a game like Ocarina of Time is a better experience when using a controller, and if you are playing on PC, you have access to any of the controller types.

Thanks to modders, not only can you play the Ocarina of Time Remake on PC, but you can also use any controller you want, meaning you can use a Switch Pro Controller, Dualshock Controller, or an Xbox Controller.

It also means that the game now has full button mapping, allowing you to switch button commands. Thankfully, you don’t need to use the X button to jump anymore.

The original Ocarina of Time game was beautiful for its time, and many still remember it as that gorgeous classic they played as a child. However, you have to admit that the remake surpasses it in every way when it comes to visuals.

Unfortunately, the enhanced graphics and visuals aren’t enough for some people, and they need to make the details even sharper. Hence the reason why the Soul Restored mod already exists.

This mod enhances the game’s lighting, removes some of the fog effects, and makes it so that the dungeons are darker and more creepy, while the sunlit areas look sharper and more focused. I have to admit, it makes the game look even better than it already did.

Since the dawn of the original Ocarina of Time, people have been telling Navi to shut up. I have personally never found her to be annoying, but the remake has changed my opinion. Numerous text bubbles appear from her whenever she sees something or you are hurt.

These bubbles can be a bit annoying, and there is no way to turn them off, which is a quality of life feature I would love to see Nintendo add. Luckily, a modder has already done this.

One of the mods that already exists for the Ocarina of Time Remake is one that shuts Navi up, making it so the text bubbles don’t appear and so there are no longer any voice prompts. You can explore peacefully without Navi making a statement about it.

The remake's dev team included younger folks, several of whom never played the original Ocarina of Time, who provided a much-needed modern view of things.

It has become more commonplace nowadays to see games that come with an arachnophobia mod. For instance, Monster Hunter Wilds has one, as does the game Grounded. Weirdly enough, the Ocarina of Time Remake does not.

While this is fine for most players, there are plenty who probably wish they could play through the game without coming face-to-face with its numerous spider enemies that love to fall from the ceiling.

Those who are playing the game on PC can now download an arachnophobia mod, allowing them to play through the game peacefully without seeing massive spiders in front of their eyes. It is a nice addition for those who have never been able to play through the game before.

After beating the game 100 times back in the day, gamers needed more of a challenge. When Ocarina of Time was emulated on PC, a randomizer was quickly created, making it more difficult to complete the game.

Well, history is repeating itself, as a randomizer is already in the works. Yes, that means that as of writing this the randomizer mod is not complete, but it is being worked on, and is estimated to be around 70% complete.

This mod is similar to the classic randomizer, making it so everything you get is randomized. Quest rewards, Gold Skulltula Tokens, and chest items are all mixed up, meaning you never know what you will get. Just give it a few days and this randomizer will probably be ready to go.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is a single-player game. When you boot it up, you can’t bring a friend into the experience. Well, that is what I would be saying if there wasn’t already a co-op mod for the game.

Currently, the co-op mod is in beta form, but it has come a long way. It allows you to play online with a friend, saving Hyrule together rather than being a lone Link taking on the big bad.

So far, NPCs, enemies, and animals are all operating within the mod, and you can even share items between each player’s game. However, there is still a lot of work that is needed to be done to make it a perfect co-op experience. It shouldn’t take extremely long for it to all come together though.

With Nintendo planning to continue remaking past games, these games should be considered for future remakes.
