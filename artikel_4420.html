Last week, General Motors agreed to pay $220 million to settle allegations that it improperly sold customers’ driving behavior and location data to third-party data brokers, which subsequently passed the information to insurance companies. The controversy has put a bigger question in front of the automotive industry: How much information should automakers collect from connected vehicles, and how much control should owners have over it?

Second, Ford promises to ditch the legalese and actually explain what data your car is collecting and where it’s going, instead of burying it in a mountain of fine print.

One particularly relevant clarification is that Ford says it never sells information about how customers drive their vehicles to data brokers.

It also helps with troubleshooting, product tweaks, and those personalized touches. But if you’d rather keep your data to yourself, you can opt out right from your car’s screen. Just know that some features – like BlueCruise, internet access, and tracking your car with the Ford App – might stop working.
