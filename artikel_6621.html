28 Black-Tie Wedding Guest Dresses That Are Perfectly Formal

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

So you’ve been invited to a black-tie wedding. Before descending into a formalwear-induced anxiety spiral, consider the benefits—this is your chance to go all out with a statement evening look you’d never otherwise get the chance to wear. (Plus, the people-watching is guaranteed to be incredible.)

The main thing to keep in mind is the definition of the dress code. Put simply, “black tie” means floor-length hemlines, so leave the midi dress at home in favor of something ankle-sweeping. From there, you’ll find there’s a lot of variation within the black-tie wedding guest dress options.

If you’re stuck on where to start, begin by considering the setting. A black-tie event in an upscale city venue requires a different sort of formal dress than a black-tie event in a bucolic setting. Think about fabrics and the season, as well as silhouette: Do you go light and dreamy, with layers of tulle and pleated chiffon? Or moody and sophisticated with luxe silk and embroidered jacquard? When in doubt, a classic A-line or halterneck style offers just enough formality. And for winter weddings, anything in rich tones, from golden saffron to oxblood to inky navy, is a safe bet.

From simple black dresses to beaded sequins, shop our edit of the best black-tie wedding guest dresses, ahead.

You can’t go wrong with a satin dress. Elegant and polished, these slinky gowns feel like the ideal canvas for all sorts of accessorizing. SIR’s short-sleeve dark plum dress calls for minimalism with sculptural gold jewelry; something sleeker, such as Amsale’s liquid gold gown, is an opportunity to play with semi-precious gemstone jewelry (like amethysts, citrines, tourmalines, and aquamarines) to enhance the richness of the hue.

Come winter, velvet is hard to beat. Its plush texture and light-catching sheen add instant depth to a simple silhouette, as seen with Bernadette’s embellished column dress and Liberowe’s halter maxi. Just be sure to pair your look with minimal jewelry to let the fabric take the lead.

It’s in the name: If you’re not sure what to wear for this dress code, black is generally a safe bet. Experiment with the shape to bring interest to a restrained palette. Norma Kamali’s gown, which has a plunging cowl back, and Staud’s blouson bodice dress, which has a cinched waist tie, are both good options; then, use your accessories to bring personality to your look.

Take inspiration from the champagne toast with your black-tie gown. Solace London provides a relaxed fit with lots of shine, while Ralph Lauren’s silver style is great for those who prefer something a little less flashy. If you’re searching for after-hours glamour, look no further than Aje’s feather-trimmed sequined gown or Taller Marmo’s radiant gold mockneck.

There’s something especially sculptural about a draped dress. Whether it’s expressed throughout the silhouette, as on Tory Burch’s design, or shows up in the one-shoulder asymmetry of Simkhai’s dark spruce gown, the fluid finish feels undeniably artistic.

No one said long-sleeve formal dresses had to be frumpy. Try a sleeker silhouette, like Victoria Beckham’s black twist-waist gown or Kallmeyer’s minimalist burgundy maxi. For a looser fit, Proenza Schouler’s cold-shoulder piece with an open back is just what you need.

Need proof that floral dresses aren’t just for garden weddings? Consider, if you will, the following: burgundy versions from Rodarte and A.L.C., a moody but romantic ruffled option from Rixo, and an elegant floral-cloqué gown courtesy of Emilia Wickstead.

Can I wear colors other than black to a black-tie wedding?

Yes! This dress code has more to do with length and style than color—and while wearing a black gown is certainly a shortcut to looking put-together, sometimes your host will actually prefer color. A summer destination wedding is the perfect occasion for bright hues, for instance, while a fall event calls for a moody, autumnal palette.

Can I wear a jumpsuit instead of a dress?

It all depends on the jumpsuit. Something in a luxe fabric, like silk or taffeta, with sharp tailoring is probably alright (though to be safe, you can always double-check with the couple). And if you’re unsure, the staunchly anti-gown guest can opt for a polished suit in similarly upscale materials for a sophisticated twist on the dress code.

Should I choose my black-tie dress depending on the venue or season?

When in doubt, take context clues from the invite. The season, venue, and even time of ceremony are all helpful indicators of what kind of dress to pick—you wouldn’t wear a velvet floor-length gown to a morning ceremony in July, just as a strapless style in floral crepe might be a poor choice for a snowy winter fête.

What are the key guidelines for black-tie wedding guest dresses?

Can a wedding guest wear a midi dress to a black-tie wedding?

Absolutely not. Black tie equals floor-length hemlines, and a midi will look out of place in a room full of gowns.

What are the differences between a black-tie and a black-tie optional wedding guest dress?

Black-tie optional gives you more flexibility with your eveningwear, whereas black tie is stricter about what you can wear. In other words, a sophisticated midi is acceptable at a black-tie optional wedding, though a gown is still your best choice. (Minis, on the other hand, are always off-limits.)
