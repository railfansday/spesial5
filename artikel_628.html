SpaceX's latest Drago cargo mission is scheduled to head back home toward Earth today (Nov. 20), and you can watch the action live.

The robotic Dragon capsule will undock from the International Space Station (ISS) today at around 12:05 p.m. EST (1705 GMT), if all goes according to plan.

You can watch Dragon's departure live here at Space.com, courtesy of NASA. Coverage will begin at 11:45 a.m. EST (1645 GMT).

If Dragon does indeed leave the ISS today, it will splash down in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of California on Saturday (Nov. 21) at about 4:55 p.m. EST (2155 GMT). There will be no livestream for its reentry and splashdown.

A safe return to Earth requires relatively calm seas, so NASA and SpaceX are keeping a close eye on the weather in the splashdown zone. If conditions aren't great for a Saturday afternoon return, Dragon will stay docked to the ISS for a bit longer, until things improve.

This particular Dragon launched Oct. 13, on a mission called CRS-35 because it's the 35th that SpaceX has flown for NASA's Commercial Resupply Services program.

The capsule arrived at the ISS on Oct. 15, delivering more than 6,300 pounds (2,860 kilograms) of food, scientific gear and other supplies to the astronauts aboard the orbiting lab. It will bring a lot of stuff down to Earth, too.

"Thousands of pounds of cargo are returning from the space station, including research samples that could advance future space exploration and improve life on Earth," NASA officials wrote in an update on Tuesday (Nov. 17).

"The returning research includes a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct scientists used to study vascular aging and cardiovascular disease in space and on Earth," they added.

Dragon is the only cargo craft flying today that's capable of making such downward deliveries. The others — Northrop Grumman's Cygnus, Russia's Progress vehicle and Japan's HTV-X — are all expendable and burn up in Earth's atmosphere to wrap up their first and only spaceflight.
