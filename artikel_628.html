‘One Last Order’: Beloved Drive-Thru Worker Sighle Williams Says ‘I Miss the Community’ as Viral Netflix Doc Streams

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Sighle Williams served customers at a Florida burger joint for 37 years. And on her last day at Mac’s Drive Thru, it seemed like the entire city of Gainesville came out to say goodbye.

“A lot of people told me they were going to come. But I wasn’t expecting that many people,” Williams tells Variety. Mac’s had a three-hour line of cars during her final shift. Williams is the subject of “One Last Order,” a 20-minute documentary that hit Netflix on Tuesday. The film, co-directed by the restaurant owner’s daughter, follows Williams as she hugs emotional customers and recounts spending half her life behind the drive-thru window.

Williams says the cameras didn’t faze her, but she got emotional during “a moment when I sat in my car and realized it was my last day of work, and how all the people came out to see me off. It was awesome.”

“It was overwhelming as well,” Williams adds. “I was so hyped up, I didn’t eat all day. I didn’t even go to the restroom.”

Mac’s made three times its normal sales on Williams’ last day. As a retirement gift, the owners gave her every dollar that passed through the fast-food window. Between the day’s earnings and generous tips, Williams made nearly an entire year’s salary in a single day, according to the documentary. Williams tells Variety the gift has allowed her to “catch up on some things and save for a rainy day.”

Since retiring, Williams has been back at Mac’s a handful of times — mostly to catch up with her friends. “I miss working, yes, but I also miss the community,” she says. “I miss the customers. That is what I mainly miss.”

Williams became a beloved fixture in Gainesville for her sunny demeanor and her unique ability to memorize repeat customers’ orders. One customer in the documentary says seeing Williams in the drive-thru window is “like seeing a rainbow every day.”

“Everybody has their own things going on in their life, and sometimes you just have to take a moment to say, ‘Hey, how are you doing?’ They could be having a rough day, and it made a difference,” Williams says. “Sometimes you have a car full of kids and the mama trying to get the order right. Some people come and they don’t really know what they want. You just have to be patient. People appreciate that.”

Williams worked for 37 years while raising five girls on her own. When her sister struggled with drug addiction, she stepped up to parent her two daughters. And a few years later, she took in her niece’s three kids.

Asked how she remained so cheerful despite difficult circumstances, Williams says, “I’m just a positive person, I guess. They had to go to school. I had to go to work. I had to make sure we had the lights on, doctor’s appointments and everything. I can’t explain it.”

When Netflix posted the trailer for the film in early November, it went viral on X, gaining more than 11 million views. Williams, who has accompanied the documentary at seven or eight festivals, says she felt like a celebrity before the film even premiered. The week it launched on Netflix, Williams appeared on “CBS Mornings” and “The Jennifer Hudson Show.”

“I’ve been practicing for the Spirit Tunnel,” she says of the talk show’s viral entrance videos. “I can dance a little bit. I’m going to be cool with it and rock with it.”
