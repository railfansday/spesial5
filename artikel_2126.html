EFL League One's top 9 toughest fanbases named and ranked by AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The League One lineup of clubs this season is, on paper, one of the strongest it has ever been, with nine former Premier League sides and a few more who have spent time in the top flight previously, too.

With that, there are a lot of big, tough fanbases who have seen their sides reach the highest heights in English football, and have followed them through the more frequent lows that the sport has served them up in recent years.

But there are also some, quote, unquote, "smaller" fanbases who pack out both their own stadiums and away ends to create a tough, hostile environment. That's what this list focuses on.

Football League World has asked the AI-based service ChatGPT to rank the toughest fanbases in the third tier this season. When deciding, AI took into account intimidation, numbers, noise and the fanbases' reputation. Here are the top nine.

Wigan Athletic's fanbase is often looked down upon by certain critics given the history of rugby league in the town and the fact that they play in a ground which holds over 25,000, yet their average attendance hovers around the 10,000 mark.

However, when comparing that to other clubs in the division, their average attendance is near the top half, and it's a loud, passionate fanbase that follows the club well up and down the country.

We rank all 24 League One stadiums by their ground's distance from the city or town, starting with the longest distance.

AI also made sure to reference all the turbulent times that have surrounded the club in recent years, through numerous ownership groups and administrations. They're still vocal in support of their side, and it creates quite an underrated, gritty fanbase in League One.

Another club who have gone through tough times in the past decade, but still have a loyal fanbase who stick by them are Blackpool.

ChatGPT outlines the Tangerine Army as a 'proud fanbase' with a strong identity, and with their recognisable branding and colour scheme, it's not wrong.

When Bloomfield Road gets going, there aren't many more intimidating grounds in the EFL. Last season, Blackpool's survival could almost be attributed to their decision to slash ticket prices, giving fans the chance to buy a five-game bundle for £65 and pack the stadium ahead of the final push. They won all five.

This season has been difficult once again under Ian Evatt, and you feel that this tough fanbase will have to stick with their team again to try and avoid a similar scare to last year.

Barnsley are arguably the fanbase in League One going through the most right now regarding their ownership. The ire towards Neerav Parekh is as loud as it ever has been, with the Tykes regressing year by year and now staring down the barrel of a potential relegation.

There have been murmurs of protests during games, as a young, inexperienced squad does its best under the leadership of a returning Daniel Stendel. Whether that happens or not as the season goes on is to be seen.

Nevertheless, when Oakwell is full, which hasn't been the case for several years now, the loud South Yorkshire fanbase can really make a difference. AI hasn't forgotten that, hence Barnsley's place on this list.

There aren't many more difficult places to come in the EFL than the Kenny on a Tuesday night — or any night, for that matter.

The tight ground at Kenilworth Road creates a feeling of the fanbase being right on top of you, and even last season, with there being discontent at Luton Town amongst the fanbase due to their consecutive relegations, home form was at play-off level; it was away from home which let them down.

That's arguably why the Hatters don't rank closer towards the podium on this list, but their recent experiences in the top flight and in Wembley finals create quite the reputable, 'tough' fanbase to beat.

There'll be hopes that it doesn't fade away once they move out of their famous home and into the stadium at Power Court in 2028.

"A historically established football town" seems quite an understatement from ChatGPT when describing Huddersfield Town. The Terriers were the best team in England in the infancy of the Football League, and that's created a fanbase which has passed down through generations.

Like some of the other clubs on the list, Huddersfield have had those Premier League years recently before sinking into League One, and while there's still room to grow the attendance average at this moment in time, it's still a loud atmosphere at the Accu Stadium.

It's a fanbase that doesn't accept mediocrity, especially at this level, which can sometimes create a negative feeling around the club, but when things are going well, there aren't many in the division who are better.

It must be incredibly tough for Plymouth Argyle fans to travel quite literally up and down the country every other Saturday for away games, and that alone is enough to see them on this list, per ChatGPT.

Even their closest away day in the league this season takes them to Reading, which is over three hours away in the car. It's quite some effort, and the fact that their following on the road is better than most is quite frankly ridiculous.

The Green Army is incredibly intimidating at Home Park, too, and while form at home hasn't been as strong under Tom Cleverley as it has in recent years, there was a time under Steven Schumacher in the Championship where all they did was win at home.

They're one of the more reputable fanbases in England, and well worthy of a place on this list.

Not all fierce, tough fanbases have been sharpened by a fall from grace; others build their own from the ashes of what they used to be. Stockport County epitomises that.

Even back during their time in the EFL in the 2000s, before they fell all the way to the National League North, Edgeley Park wasn't nearly as full as it is now, and the plans to expand the stadium would have been laughed at.

Whether it's due to fans of the big Manchester clubs deciding to follow a local club on the rise due to being disconnected from the top level, or just the success breeding new fans, this is a passionate supporter base that makes their ground a very difficult place to come.

AI states that "Valley Parade is a seriously difficult away day." Once again, a rather simple analogy. Up until their recent defeat in the West Yorkshire Derby against Huddersfield, it was a fortress that, if you did somehow come away with anything, it'd be by some stroke of luck.

Since December 2024, Bradford City have won 31 league games at home, dropping points just 14 times. That's a seriously good record, and it's almost all down to the sheer noise that the Bantams fans create in the ground.

Their standing as a big-city club helps build a passionate set of supporters, who fill away ends, too, and if they end up climbing back towards the Premier League, they'd more than deserve it.

But there was always going to be one side topping this list. In terms of fanbase size and their recent troubled past, Sheffield Wednesday have a really tough, hardened set of fans.

The fact they were able to stay away from Hillsborough during the final days of the Dejphon Chansiri reign in protest, losing out on what is, for some, a real escape from day-to-day struggles, was incredibly commendable, and it was their support which has helped the club get back on its feet under David Storch.
