Bristol City & West Ham ticketing twist has caused a huge backlash

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

West Ham have enforced a major ticketing decision involving away supporters ahead of next month's home clash against Bristol City - and it's causing quite the reaction online.

Following West Ham's unprecedented relegation from the Premier League back in May, the London Stadium represents the away day for away fans throughout the Championship.

Indeed, the second-tier has never seen a larger ground than the London Stadium's 60,000-capacity, and for virtually every fanbase, an away trip to this corner of East London is one of the most notable fixtures on the calendar all season.

Fans of Bristol City, however, have just received a fresh twist that will impact their fixture away to West Ham next month, with the decision stirring up mixed reaction among supporters online.

Bristol City fans will be ticking off a major Championship away day when they travel to West Ham on December 12, though trips to Middlesbrough, West Bromwich Albion and Derby County are all on the agenda before their journey down the M4 and into East London.

But it has come with a twist that will now see a reduced number of supporters in attendance within the London Stadium away end, with West Ham having reciprocated the Robins' travelling allocation.

A ticketing announcement from the West Country outfit has confirmed this, disclosing that tickets will be on sale to supporters from next Wednesday before revealing the allocation.

Now, the Robins have informed fans that they have received an allocation of just 2,110 tickets including rail seats and general seating. This, of course, is a fairly sizeable reduction on the Hammers' typical away allocation to travelling supporters of around 3,000, yet they've simply reciprocated what City have already done.

The development comes following an announcement in September which revealed that Bristol City have opened the Atyeo Stand to home fans for high-profile fixtures against West Ham, Stoke and Severnside rivals Cardiff, thus reducing the number of tickes available to away supporters.

Therefore, West Ham have reacted by reducing their own allocation for the Robins faithful, and unsurprisingly, it's sparked something of a stir.

With every other Championship club to have travelled to West Ham so far this season having received a larger allocation, fans were quick to react following the Robins' announcement.

A lot of the reaction actually came from Hammers supporters, who wasted no time in lauding their club's reciprocal efforts.

This one fan, for example, applauded his side and urged them to do the same for every game against an opposing club that doesn't provide the same allocation.

Another, meanwhile, kept it direct and straight-to-the-point in response to a Bristol City punter who had questioned the allocation.

Indeed, this West Ham supporter believes the decision is evidence of the East Londoners actually getting something right, with many simply all-too-happy to see that their club has refused to simply roll over and give out the largest possible allocations while being restricted in some of their own travels on the road.

By contrast, Bristol City fans have naturally failed to share the same sentiment and believe that the Robins have been hard done by with this particular decision.

A Robins supporter here pulled no punches by describing it as an "absolute joke" - to which the Hammers' lot were, of course, swift in reminding of why the allocation has been shaved.

Emojis can sometimes tell the story, too, and in this case, they summed up exactly how another supporter of the Ashton Gate side is feeling right now.

However, another punter saw sense behind West Ham's decision and has backed the reciprocation.

There has been a split of opinion over West Ham's allocation for the travelling Bristol City contingent next month, even if many do believe that the Hammers are perfectly within their right to match the reduction.

However, there's absolutely no division when it comes to the prices that West Ham are charging for Robins fans who intend on making the trip to the London Stadium, which may just work out among their cheapest away days all season.

Adult tickets come cheaper than virtually anywhere else in the Championship at just £25, whereas 66+ and under-21 ones are even more affordable at a price of £12.

Under-18 tickets, meanwhile, will cost no more than £10, with these prices across the board paling in comparison to some other second-tier clubs.

All in all, then, Bristol City supporters perhaps can't complain much, and in terms of how much the fixture will cost to attend, there can be no faulting the Hammers.

That's why this fan, despite the noise, took time to praise the prices for City's trip to face Scott Parker's side away from home.

It's also no shock that one West Ham supporter - and many more silently, one can only imagine - hopes that Bristol City undertake some reciprocation of their own by charging the same prices when the promotion-chasers travel to Ashton Gate on New Year's Day.
