I use ChatGPT most days and am just getting started with using Instinct AI via WhatsApp. And I'm increasingly using AI to plan and improve my travels. For example, I frequently ask ChatGPT to plan walking tours in new destinations, use Instinct to find stores selling specific items and even tried connecting Gondola to ChatGPT for more personalized travel advice.

But as AI tools become increasingly capable of booking trips and managing accounts on your behalf, airlines and banks are becoming increasingly concerned about how consumers use them. For example, United Airlines recently announced restrictions on unauthorized third-party AI agents, while Bank of America warned customers against sharing their banking passwords with AI assistants.

These developments don't mean you should stop using AI to plan your travels. But they highlight why it's important to understand — and thoughtfully consider — what information you're sharing, what you're allowing AI to do and when you should verify its recommendations.

One way travelers can use AI is to help them research and plan trips. I've used AI to research and plan trips with extended family to both Iceland and Venice. And I use AI most days to help me plan walking routes in new cities, decipher restaurant menus and figure out how to operate appliances in Airbnbs.

Besides the time you can save and the experiences you can discover, the best part of using AI to research and plan your travels is that you can often provide enough context to help the AI improve its recommendations without sharing unnecessary personal information. As such, using AI to plan and research your travels is often relatively low-risk.

You can also use AI to help you with your points and miles. For example, AI can suggest ways to stack earnings and help you decide the best card to use before you make a purchase. However, you have to provide the AI with the proper context, such as the merchant you want to buy from, whether you want to prioritize having extended warranty protection, whether you're working toward any minimum spending requirements and which cards you currently have in your wallet.

AI could also help you compare cards when you're ready to open a new card. And it can also help you with many of the calculations travelers often struggle with, such as comparing cash versus points bookings (you can also use TPG's calculator for this), calculating redemption values, evaluating whether transferring points makes sense and assessing elite status benefits.

Best of all, it can do all these tasks without needing access to your accounts. Sure, the more information you give the AI agent, the more accurate and relevant its responses may be. But you don't need to give an AI agent access to your accounts for it to compare properties at your desired destination, calculate whether it's better to redeem 25,000 Hyatt points or pay $600, or compare the perks of using two different cards for a purchase.

Even as many travelers start to use AI for more travel decisions and planning, it's important to fact-check or verify anything an AI agent tells you that you plan to rely on.

Sure, it doesn't really matter if ChatGPT gets a few details about landmarks incorrect during a walking tour I asked it to plan. And it's only a minor inconvenience if it sends me to a closed restaurant or suggests I get off at a slightly farther away bus stop.

But many travel decisions do matter and should be verified. For example, verify driving routes and travel times, entry requirements and whether a hotel offers the amenities you need. I also recommend ensuring that any redemption rates or other calculations pass the "sniff test" and appear correct. And if you ask an AI agent to find sweet spots in a specific loyalty program, be sure to fact-check that the sweet spots still exist at the stated rates and that you can find availability on at least some dates.

Also keep in mind that AI may give you overly conservative advice. For example, it will often recommend arriving at the airport three to four hours before your flight even when the bag drop counter doesn't open until two hours before departure. So, double-check any information that you'd be upset if it were incorrect.

Although sharing information with AI can help you get more personalized advice, you should carefully consider what to share. In particular, avoid sharing passwords, security codes, full credit card numbers and other sensitive information with AI tools.

Bank of America recently warned customers not to share their banking usernames or passwords with AI assistants, noting that customers could be responsible for certain activity resulting from that access. This is good advice for your loyalty accounts, too.

However, you don't necessarily need to avoid sharing any personal information with AI agents. For example, uploading a flight itinerary or hotel reservation could help an AI assistant plan your travels. Consider removing unnecessary details, such as confirmation numbers, account numbers and other identifying information, before uploading documents.

If you've already shared a password with an AI tool, change it immediately and review your account for unauthorized activity.

As AI agents become more capable, you may be tempted to let them access your loyalty and banking accounts to search for award availability, manage reservations or even book travel on your behalf. But just because an AI agent can access an account doesn't mean the account's provider allows it.

For example, United Airlines recently announced restrictions on unauthorized third-party AI agents accessing its systems, citing concerns about unauthorized purchases, MileagePlus redemptions and automated check-in. Meanwhile, American Airlines explicitly prohibits "automated re-shopping," and Marriott prohibits automated tools from monitoring or retrieving website information without written permission.

Likewise, some financial institutions restrict how third-party tools can access customer accounts. For example, Chase updated its digital services agreement last month to prohibit third parties, including AI agents, from using customers' login credentials to access accounts.

However, this doesn't mean all AI integrations are prohibited. Some companies allow customers to connect their accounts to approved third-party services through secure connections that don't require sharing passwords.

So, before connecting an AI agent to your travel or financial accounts, check whether the company permits the integration. And even if an integration is authorized, understand what information the AI tool can access, what actions it can take and how to revoke access if you change your mind.

As I was using Instinct AI to research clothing I needed to buy during a trip, its responses kept saying "Nothing has been ordered." This was reassuring on one hand, but I also found myself thinking, "I definitely don't want it ordering anything without my approval."

For each AI tool you use, take the time to understand how the company collects, stores and uses your data. After all, you may be sharing more information than you realize, especially if you're uploading documents or connecting third-party accounts.

For example, some AI services may use your conversations to train or improve their models. Others may retain information even after you delete a conversation. And if you're using an AI tool through another platform, such as WhatsApp, or connecting a third-party service, multiple companies may have access to your information.

AI tools can be incredibly useful for planning trips, maximizing points and miles, and making travel easier. I use them frequently and expect I'll continue finding more ways to incorporate them into my travels.

But as AI tools become more capable, it's increasingly important to understand their limitations and the risks of sharing information or granting them access to your accounts.

My advice is to start with relatively low-risk tasks, such as researching destinations and comparing redemption options. Then, as you become more comfortable with AI tools, consider whether you're willing to share more information or allow them to take actions on your behalf. Just be sure to understand what you're authorizing and maintain control over your accounts and reservations.
