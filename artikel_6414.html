Scientists Develop a Powerful Two-in-One Treatment for Gum Disease

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Gum disease can eat away at the bone that holds teeth in place, and a tiny cage made of DNA helped slow that damage in rats.

Researchers at Sichuan University built a DNA carrier that delivers two compounds together to treat periodontitis, a chronic form of gum disease and a leading cause of tooth loss in adults. The team, led by Geru Zhang, Yunfeng Lin, and Xiaoxiao Cai, published its findings in the International Journal of Oral Science.

Periodontitis starts when bacterial plaque builds up beneath the gums. The resulting immune response can gradually destroy the tissue and bone that anchor teeth. Deep cleaning and antibiotics can reduce the number of bacteria, but they may leave existing damage unrepaired and do not always stop the disease from progressing.

“We aimed to create a multifunctional DNA nanoplatform capable of delivering both curcumin and the antimicrobial peptide defensin directly to periodontal tissues, with the goal of overcoming the limitations of existing therapies,” Zhang said.

The team loaded both compounds into tiny, four-sided DNA structures called tetrahedral framework nucleic acids (tFNAs). The researchers tested the resulting complex, called Cur-de-tFNA, against bacteria linked to periodontitis and used a bacterial molecule to trigger inflammation in human periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) in the lab.

“The DNA carrier enhanced the stability and cellular uptake of curcumin while protecting defensin from degradation, allowing both therapeutic agents to function more effectively,” Lin said.

In lab tests, the combined treatment outperformed its individual components across several experiments. It acted against bacteria linked to gum disease, reduced oxidative stress, and dampened an inflammatory signaling pathway called TLR4/NF-κB. It also increased levels of proteins involved in bone formation.

In rats with periodontitis, the treatment reduced bone loss and inflammatory damage around the teeth. The researchers observed less activity from cells that break down bone, along with more collagen deposition and new bone formation. “As a result, the nanoplatform delivered stronger antimicrobial activity, better control of inflammation, and improved preservation of periodontal tissues in preclinical models,” Cai said.

The biodegradable DNA carrier was well tolerated in preclinical tests. Human trials are needed to determine whether the treatment is safe and can help preserve or rebuild the tissues that support teeth.

Reference: “DNA framework-based nanomedicine platform: a triple-function strategy for treating periodontitis via antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and osteogenesis-promoting activities” by Geru Zhang, Weitong Cui, Haoyan Wu, Xiaowei Liu, Liwei Huang, Yunfeng Lin and Xiaoxiao Cai, 29 April 2026, International Journal of Oral Science. DOI: 10.1038/s41368-026-00439-2
