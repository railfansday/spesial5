Populism Isn’t Built for Setbacks

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The competitive advantage of conservatism over populism is that we are prepared for the inevitability of defeats.

President Donald Trump departs the White House, July 15, 2025, and supporters gather for a midterm campaign rally in San Antonio, Texas, October 7, 2026.

NR Illustration; Jonathan Ernst, Callaghan O’Hare/Reuters

Populism, for all of its faults, can be a powerful force in politics when it is rising. While populist agendas can come in many different varieties, they are all propelled by a broad sense that the people — or more properly, The People — are being held back by some elite force, whether that be Big Business or an insular political establishment or something darker. If only we can overcome the enemy, we are told, we will sweep to victory on the broad, sunny uplands.

As I’ve noted before, the very nature of populism is that it has to redefine The People to include something less than all the people when it inevitably encounters the reality that its premises are not universally shared. And so it goes from there, because populism always sooner or later must decide to cling to its narratives when forced to choose between them and empirical reality:
