As Dubai’s Hotels Struggle, Branded Homes Keep Launching

Dubai’s hotels are under pressure, but developers are still signing deals for branded residences. The main bet is on buyers, with brands delivering a healthy premium over the wider market.

Developers in the UAE are still launching branded residences, betting on long-term demand as the U.S.-Iran war that began in late February weighs on Dubai’s hotel market.

Branded residences are residential properties developed in partnership with an established brand, such as a luxury hotel operator.

Avenew Development has launched The St. Regis Residences at Dubai Islands with Marriott International. Dubai South has signed UK-based Cheval Collection for Cheval South Bay. And Al Seeb Real Estate Development has started main construction on Chedi Private Residences in Dubai.

The launches come against a weakened hotel market. Dubai occupancy fell to 68.4% in October, down 16.5 points from 84.9% a year earlier, according to CoStar’s STR. Average daily rate dropped 28.1% to $181 and RevPAR fell 42% to $124. International visitors in the first 10 months of the year fell 38% to 9.7 million, according to Dubai’s Department of Economy and Tourism.

UAE developers are still launching branded residences even as Dubai's hotel market weakens under the U.S.-Iran war that began in late February. Avenew Development has launched The St. Regis Residences at Dubai Islands with Marriott, Dubai South has signed Cheval Collection for Cheval South Bay, and Al Seeb Real Estate Development has begun main construction on Chedi Private Residences. The trading backdrop is sharply worse: Dubai occupancy fell to 68.4% in October from 84.9% a year earlier, ADR dropped 28.1% to $181, RevPAR fell 42% to $124, and international visitors in the first 10 months fell 38% to 9.7 million. Ferras Hafez of Cavendish Maxwell said the branded residential sector is showing a degree of caution, with some developers reassessing timelines, and that it would be premature to suggest operators are pursuing more of these deals to offset weaker hotel performance. The clearest pressure point is rental income, since many hotel-linked residences pool units and pay owners a share of revenue tied to the same falling occupancy and rate figures. Chedi's 117 freehold homes carry no rental program at all. Most of the new supply lands in 2029 and 2030. Buyers continue to pay roughly a third more for a branded home in Dubai than for a comparable unbranded one nearby, and Savills projects the Middle East and Africa branded residences market will grow 51% year over year by the end of 2026.
