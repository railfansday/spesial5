England captain Harry Kane has praised Tottenham Hotspur academy graduate Will Lankshear and revealed that he has spoken to one of the striker’s former coaches about his goalscoring ability.

Speaking at England’s pre-match press conference ahead of Thursday’s UEFA Nations League encounter with Croatia, Kane was asked by GIVEMESPORT reporter Sam Cohen whether Lankshear could eventually succeed him as England’s first-choice centre-forward.

Lankshear has attracted attention following his goalscoring performances for Middlesbrough, with the former Tottenham youngster emerging as a potential candidate for future senior international recognition.

Kane, who also developed through Tottenham’s youth system before establishing himself as one of the world’s leading strikers, admitted he had been impressed by the youngster’s progress.

“I wouldn’t say I’ve followed his whole career. Obviously, this season it’s been hard not to hear about him, and he’s done extremely well at Middlesbrough,” Kane explained.

“I’ve spoken to a friend of mine who coached him here at the younger age groups and said that he’s a goalscorer. He knows how to score, he’s in the right place at the right time, he’s a great finisher.

We talk about a striker sometimes as having that goalscoring instinct, and I think he’s one of those players for sure.

Despite his praise, Kane was keen to stress the importance of allowing Lankshear to continue developing without unnecessary expectations.

“He’s still early in his development. I think what he’s doing right now is perfect for him, playing loads of games, scoring goals and getting confident.

“Whether he comes into the senior side, it’s down to Thomas. If he did, I’m sure he would learn loads here, surrounded by the attackers that we have.”

Will Lankshear for Middlesbrough this season

Kane, who was named the 2026 Ballon d’Or winner, also acknowledged the ongoing discussion surrounding who might eventually replace him as England’s number nine, but warned against placing that responsibility on a young player too early.

“It’s not a time to put too much pressure on him. I know there’s a lot of talk about who’s going to replace me when I’m not here anymore, but just let him do what he’s doing. I think it’s great for his development.”

The England captain revealed that he has crossed paths with Lankshear on a couple of occasions during their respective Tottenham careers, although the pair have not spent significant time together.

“I’ve come across him a couple of times, when he first signed here at Tottenham and then again when we played him in pre-season. I haven’t really spent much time with him.”

Kane concluded by expressing his willingness to help the youngster should he eventually receive an England call-up.

“As time goes on, if he continues, I’m sure he’ll be on the right path to get his chance.

“If he does make it into the senior team, it’ll be great to work with him and try and help him develop as a player.”

Kane’s comments provide an encouraging assessment of Lankshear’s potential, while making clear that the striker’s immediate priority should remain regular football and continued development at club level.
