Knicks 97, Trail Blazers 96: “We found a way to get a win”

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

If you wanted to explain to a new sports fan what the term "tale of two halves" means, just show them the tape from last night. The game started with a classic Jalen Brunson takeover that saw him go for 19 points, four rebounds, and two assists while shooting 6-7 from the field and 5-5 from three. And the Knicks did an amazing job playing off of his confidence and gravity en route to 40 first-quarter points. New York was playing with pace; the ball movement and player movement were constant and crisp, and everybody got in on the action. For a moment or two, it was reminiscent of the Knicks' Mother's Day massacre of the 76ers in the playoffs just months ago.

New York ended the first quarter with nine three-pointers while missing just two. Aside from Brunson's aforementioned five made threes, Mikal Bridges, Andre Drummond, Shamet, and McBride all added one each. And besides the nine threes, the Knicks had two dunks and one mid-range jump shot by Brunson. It was one of the most analytically beautiful shot charts anyone has ever seen.

The Trail Blazers, on the other hand, managed to score just 23 points in the first quarter. Deni Avdija did get off to a nice start with eight points, and Damian Lillard chipped in six of his own that accounted for more than half of their team's points. Toumani Camara, who was Trail Blazers head coach Micah Nori's answer to trying to stop Brunson, started off the game cold; Cidy Sissoko went scoreless, and starting center Donovan Clingan added just three points.

The second quarter didn't produce the same kind of exhilarating offense as the Knicks scored "just" 26 points. But even with the shooting seeing some expected negative regression, the Knicks still won the quarter by six as the Trail Blazers' offense limped its way to a 20-point quarter. The Knicks did struggle a bit with Brunson out as a Kolek-led second unit got some quality looks but just could not create advantages on a consistent basis. But as Knicks fans have said plenty of times over the last couple of years, thank god for Shamet. The veteran guard came up huge throughout the entire game as Brunson's much-needed sidekick. Shamet scored 13 of his 20 points in the second quarter and helped the Knicks take a 23-point lead into halftime.

But as noted earlier, the second half looked like a completely different ball game. The Knicks played one of their worst offensive quarters of the season, managing to score just 15 total points. Some of that was the Trail Blazers amping up the defense and coming out playing much more energized and more physical. But as head coach Mike Brown alluded to after the game, the Knicks looked like they were stuck in mud. There was significantly less movement overall. The ball was no longer zipping around the perimeter. Players were stagnant. And they no longer had the shooting touch to mask their lackluster offensive execution and subpar process. The Trail Blazers offense scored 29 points in the period and just like that, the lead was cut down to just nine points.

The Knicks often like to keep their fans on the edge of their seats, and Saturday night was no exception. Instead of dominating the third quarter with the same type of execution and intensity that they started the game with and taking the Trail Blazers out of the game, it was now down to one quarter. And you could tell early on in the final period that this wasn't going to be an easy win for the Knicks to pull out.

The Trail Blazers now had the confidence and the momentum. Lillard had struggled for much of the game, but he hit some key shots. Clingan was now dominating the paint and was on his way to a 22-point, 13-rebound double-double. Avdija, despite the Knicks generally playing good defense on him, was getting going. And that was happening all while the Knicks' offensive struggles continued. The Trail Blazers continued their upped intensity and were doing a much better job sticking with Shamet. Brunson, who was starting to look fatigued, was missing shots he usually makes. And both of those things were made much worse by the fact that Josh Hart, who had done a lot of nice non-scoring things in the first half, wasn't looking to shoot at all.

But there's a reason everyone calls the NBA a make-or-miss league. With the Trail Blazers taking a two-point lead in the final seconds thanks to a clutch three by Lillard, Bridges, who has slowly but surely turned the page on what was a disastrous first few weeks of the season, made a game-winning three against the Trail Blazers for the second time in three seasons.

Had he missed that shot, Saturday night's loss would have turned into the worst loss of the season. Yes, Karl-Anthony Towns and OG Anunoby were sidelined. But to lose that game at home after how the first half started would've been a gut-wrenching loss. Instead, Bridges makes the shot and the Knicks win by one, and it's the Trail Blazers who are now on the wrong side of what was objectively a fun NBA game. Portland finds themselves regretting how they came out in the first half, and Avdija has to be thinking about the three free throws he missed in the fourth quarter.

The Knicks' Saturday night win over the Trail Blazers was emblematic of the Knicks as a whole. Sometimes, they look like one of the best offenses of all time. Sometimes, they can't concoct anything better than a stagnant possession that leads to a Brunson isolation with seconds left. But as Brown said during his postgame presser, they "found a way to get a win".

As this team has proven time and time again, regardless of who is playing and who is out, regardless of who the opponent is, this team will find ways to stay in the game, and more often than not, they'll come out on top. It isn't always easy. It isn't always pretty. But a win is a win.
