Saints at Bears Q&A: Dennis Allen Revenge Game Part 2

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Bears are back from their bye week and ready to welcome the New Orleans Saints to Soldier Field. The Bears are sitting at 7-2 and in prime position to make the playoffs. The Saints sit at 3-6, but fortunately for them, they are only a game and a half out of first in the weak NFC South.

To help get you set for this matchup, we sat down with Nic Jennings of our sister site, Canal Street Chronicles (SB Nation's New Orleans Saints site), to get you set for their opponent.

1. The Saints are sitting at 3-6, but from my outsider view, I feel like this team is close to clicking, potentially even the back half of this season and start winning some football games. What's the vibe around the Saints for the fan base?

The vibe around the fanbase isn't great right now. I don't think fans have given up on the players, but rather they've given up on Kellen Moore, Mickey Loomis, and Gayle Benson. The Saints are somehow only one game out of first place in the division, so there's still hope that they can turn things around and make a push. But it is frustrating to watch a roster with this much talent continue to fail to put everything together. At this point, I think changes need to be made within the regime, and until that happens, I expect the vibes around the fanbase to remain largely the same, win or lose.

2. I'll be honest, when Kellen Moore was hired, I didn't think much of him as a head coaching candidate, but I've come around on him a little. What did Saints fans think of the hire last year, ar and where are they at with him midway through 2026?

The initial reaction to Moore's hiring was that the Saints finally had another offensive-minded head coach in the building who could bring a refreshing change to the way things had been done. However, that optimism has long since faded. Moore is running out of excuses, and quite frankly, I don't think there is any way he can win the fanbase back at this point. Through his first 26 games, the Saints have trailed in every single one of those games, while failing to score in the first quarter 18 times. Two consistent issues have plagued Moore's teams across both seasons: slow starts that force the Saints to claw their way back into games and an offense that continues to look dysfunctional, with a putrid run game forcing Tyler Shough to carry the team on his back. At this point, the frustration has gone beyond results, and fans are rightfully questioning whether Moore is the guy to lead this team moving forward.

3. I've never been a fan of how Mickey Loomis has handled the cap and the rebuild/reload (whatever you want to call it) of this roster. Are Saints fans still behind Loomis or do they want to see a change?

Yes, the fanbase still wants a change in the front office, but it's not necessarily because of the financial side of things. The Saints are expected to finally be out of "cap hell" next season and back to operating with more financial flexibility. However, Mickey Loomis' draft-day decisions and head coaching hires since Sean Payton's departure have taken their toll on the fanbase. For a team that has been in a rebuilding phase, it's difficult to get out of that cycle without consistently drafting impactful players, and that's been one of the biggest issues. The tricky part is that Loomis is the NFL's longest-tenured general manager at 25 years and has won a Super Bowl with the franchise. Given his history and relationship with the organization, I don't see Gayle Benson moving on from him unless he decides to step down himself. That's what makes the situation so complicated.

4. Tyler Shough certainly looks like he's taking strides this year, but he's also 27 years old. Do you think Shough is a legit NFL QB that can lead this Saints offense for the next 5 to 7 years?

Absolutely. Shough is the real deal, and his teammates rally behind him. He's been one of the few consistent bright spots for the Saints this season, along with Chris Olave, Chase Young, and Carl Granderson.

Shough showed a lot of promise in his limited starts as a rookie, then attacked the offseason and blew expectations out of the water. He's carried that same momentum into this season. His ball placement, ability to stretch the field with his arm, poise in the pocket, and ability to extend plays with his legs give the Saints a dimension offensively that they haven't really had. And most importantly, he has that clutch gene. He's shown he can make plays when the Saints need them most.

I do think there are still things he can improve on, and he's had a bit of a rough stretch over the last two weeks. Like I previously mentioned, the offense has developed a habit of starting slowly, and while I'd put more of that on Kellen Moore, Shough still plays a role in getting the offense into a rhythm. Though,gh to Shough's credit, the Saints have a nonexistent rushing attack, which hasn't eased the pressure on Shough and the passing game one bit.

But overall, I've seen more than enough to believe he can be the long-term answer in New Orleans.

As for his age, to put things into perspective, Drew Brees joined the Saints at 27 years old, and it felt like he was in New Orleans for a lifetime. Even as a 26-year-old rookie, Shough was visibly more prepared than his fellow rookies. I view his age as a benefit rather than a drawback and believe he has a long career ahead of him.

5. What is one player on the Saints offense and one player on the Saints defense that may not be a headliner that you think can make an impact on Sunday? Do you have a prediction for the game?

After the Saints invested a fourth-round pick in him last year to pry him away from Denver, Vele has come in and become the contested-catch slot receiver the Saints have been searching for since Michael Thomas.

He's quickly become one of Tyler Shough's favorite targets and has established himself as the go-to guy in third-and-long situations.

With Jordyn Tyson back in the mix alongside Chris Olave, Vele's production has dwindled a bit over the past two games. That said, I believe his opportunities will come, and he's proven he can take advantage of them.

For a six-foot-five receiver to move the way he does and possess that type of body control is truly remarkable.

On defense, keep an eye on second-year defensive back, Jonas Sanker. He consistently finds his way to the football and has been impressive in his first year starting in the slot. He returned a fumble for a touchdown against the Vikings and had another return for a touchdown in Week 1 against the Lions that was called back after Jared Goff apparently "intended" to throw the football forward.

With Justin Reid now out of the picture in New Orleans, perhaps we see Sanker move back to his natural position at safety, where he spent his rookie season.
