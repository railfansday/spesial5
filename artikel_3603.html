Kathy Ireland Reveals Her Husband Greg Escaped a Burning Boat Amid Explosion

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Former swimsuit model Kathy Ireland has revealed her husband, Greg Olsen, narrowly escaped danger after his boat burst into flames due to an engine exploding.

“Engine exploded and a fire quickly melted all navigation equipment and survival raft! My heart is both heavy and full. Heavy because I know the love, sweat and life you poured into this vessel,” Ireland, 63, wrote via Instagram on Sunday, November 15. “Full because of the outpouring of love and support. I’m so thankful God gifted you with a brilliant mind to navigate this disaster so seamlessly.”

Ireland added that Olsen, a former physician who retired to pursue commercial fishing, was “out alone” amid her being “on the road for business.”

Footage accompanying her message showed the boat, named “the 4th Watch,” rocking out at sea as a blazing fire and thick black smoke gushed from atop.

A subsequent Instagram post shared by Ireland also revealed what her husband found in the aftermath of the incident. “Greg went to the harbor to assess the damage. The boat and all its contents are no more. Besides our commercial fishing licenses, the only thing on the boat that survived is his Bible,” she captioned three photos from Olsen’s return to the scene. “Completely unscorched! My heart is overflowing full!”

The former Sports Illustrated Swimsuit star and Olsen married in August 1988 and share three children — son Erik, 32, and daughters Lily, 27, and Chloe, 23.

Ireland evolved her modelling career into a lifestyle brand, launching clothing lines, operating events spaces and writing children’s books over the years. In February, 2012, she was named by Celebrity New Worth as the richest supermodel in the world. Forbes magazine also reported at the time that she had sold “an astounding $2 billion worth of licensed products” at retail to make her as big as “Martha Stewart.”

Us reported in March that Ireland and Olsen were suing their former business managers amid allegations of negligence, theft, intentional representation, constructive fraud and more.

The lawsuit, obtained by Us at the time, stated, “This case is about trust betrayed on a staggering and unconscionable scale. For over 35 years, defendants Jason Winters and Erik Sterling held themselves out to Kathy and Greg, not only as their managers, but as their family. … But in fact, they concealed and misrepresented information from Kathy and Greg to hide their predatory misdeeds.”

Ireland had claimed the defendants were liable for damages “in the tens of millions of dollars, if not exceeding $100 million.”

After the lawsuit attracted headlines, kathy ireland Worldwide (kiWW) chair and chief executive Officer Brittany Duncan shared a statement via Linkedin, slamming the suit. “Kathy Ireland may be spiritually broken, but she is not financially ‘broke,’” Duncan wrote at the time. “I have known this woman my entire life. This situation is deeply sad. … What is being done is beneath the dignity of this firm.”

Us reached out to Ireland’s attorney for comment on the LinkedIn post but did not hear back.
