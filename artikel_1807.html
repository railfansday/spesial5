Tottenham new boys score in shoot-out win over Sydney FC

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Tottenham Hotspur fans enjoyed their first glimpse of summer signings Andy Robertson and Sandro Tonali in the shirt, with both players scoring penalties in the shoot-out that followed a 1-1 draw with Sydney FC.

Spurs took the lead through a first-half Mathys Tel free-kick, before Roberto De Zerbi’s youthful defence was undone by Takahiro Sekine, who tucked away one of the Australian side’s only chances of the game.

Tonali was brought on as a half-time substitute, before Robertson took the captain’s armband from Archie Gray after he was introduced in the 65th minute.

The former Liverpool left-back delivered three dangerous crosses which went unconverted, something of a theme for a Spurs team who failed to make the most of long spells of dominance.

James Maddison and Mateus Fernandes were not in the match-day squad, while Pedro Porro, Marcos Senesi, Cristian Romero, Micky van de Ven and Jean Paul van Hecke have yet to join the group after the World Cup.

There was another impressive showing from 17-year-old prospect Luca Williams-Barnett, who is pushing for first-team opportunities this upcoming season.

Penalties followed the final whistle, with Spurs scoring all four of their spot kicks. Goalkeeper Martin Dubravka, signed on a free transfer this summer, saved one and Sydney FC’s Josh Lacey hit the post to give Spurs the spoils 4-2.

Spurs fans took their first look at summer signing Mateus Fernandes in a 2-0 victory over Auckland, and their pre-season tour continues today against Sydney FC.

Fernandes, an £85m purchase from West Ham United, also scored a stunning volley in a behind-closed-doors game against MK Dons.

Sandro Tonali is part of the travelling squad for these friendlies, as is new centre-back Jean Paul van Hecke. Marcos Senesi, Cristian Romero and Pedro Porro have been given extra time off after their respective nations reached the World Cup final, won by Porro and Spain.

Van Hecke and Senesi ranked very highly among defenders for progressive passing, which offers an insight into the qualities Roberto De Zerbi is prioritising this summer.

The Italian likes his teams to bait opponents into pressing them high up the pitch, before moving the ball through central areas using third-man combinations and wall passes. The addition of two ball-playing centre-backs and two new central midfielders should mean Spurs can play like a recognisable De Zerbi team.

While Dane Scarlett and Richarlison scored against Auckland, Spurs’s squad still lacks high-class options in forward areas, with a move for Bournemouth’s Eli Junior Kroupi mooted earlier in the summer.

Spurs fans will be excited to watch 17-year-old Luca Williams-Barnett, the midfielder who is one of the outstanding players in his age group in the country.

“He’s a talent,” Ledley King said. “There’s a lot of hard work ahead of him, but there’s no doubt that he’s someone with great ability.

“He’s seen players like Harry Kane come through the academy and, through sheer hard work and determination, put themselves in a position to kick on. He couldn’t have a better role model.

“There’s no doubt we’re excited by his talent and the next part will be up to him.”
