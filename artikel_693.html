Mindy Kaling Will Be Honored With The Hollywood Reporter’s Equity in Entertainment Award at Women in Entertainment Gala

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Maitreyi Ramakrishnan is set to present Kaling with the award at the Los Angeles event, which is presented by founding sponsor Lifetime and title sponsor Lilly. Mary Beth Barone will open the program.

The Hollywood Reporter announced Wednesday that award-winning writer, director, actor and producer Mindy Kaling will receive this year’s Equity in Entertainment Award during its annual Women in Entertainment breakfast gala in Los Angeles.

Actor Maitreyi Ramakrishnan, who starred in Kaling’s hit Netflix series Never Have I Ever, will present Kaling with the award. Kaling joins Taylor Swift, who The Hollywood Reporter previously announced will receive the Sherry Lansing Leadership Award, as an honoree at this year’s gala. Additionally, actress and comedian Mary Beth Barone is set to open the program, which is presented by founding sponsor Lifetime and title sponsor Lilly. Lina Polimeni, chief marketing officer — consumer at Eli Lilly and Company, will share remarks on the important intersection of health and entertainment.

The Equity in Entertainment Award recognizes individuals who amplify the voices of underrepresented communities in the entertainment industry. The award was created in 2016, and previous honorees include Jennifer Lopez, Selena Gomez, Kerry Washington, Issa Rae, Ryan Murphy, Amy Pascal and Nina Jacobson.

“Mindy Kaling is one of the most influential creative voices of her generation,” said Maer Roshan, editor-in-chief of The Hollywood Reporter. “Through her sharp humor, emotional honesty and inclusive storytelling, Kaling creates opportunities for new perspectives and stories to take center stage. We’re thrilled to recognize her with this year’s Equity in Entertainment Award, celebrating her enduring contributions to the industry and her commitment to making entertainment more inclusive and reflective of the world we live in.”

Kaling is an Emmy-nominated writer, Tony Award-winning producer, New York Times best-selling author, acclaimed director and actor. At age 24, Kaling joined the writing staff of NBC’s The Office, becoming the only woman on the team. She penned 23 episodes over eight seasons, which was more than any other writer. Kaling also earned an Emmy nomination, marking the first time a woman of color had been recognized in any writing category.

Her achievements have continued to garner national recognition. In 2023, Kaling was awarded the National Medal of Arts by the White House, the highest honor bestowed upon artists by the U.S. government, and received the Producers Guild of America’s Norman Lear Achievement Award in Television. She has been named to Time’s 100 Most Influential People in the World list (2012), and in 2022, her company, Kaling International, was named one of Time’s 100 Most Influential Companies.

Kaling is the creator, star and executive producer of the hit series The Mindy Project, for which she wrote 24 of the show’s 117 episodes over six celebrated seasons. Since then, she has solidified her reputation as one of Hollywood’s most prolific and powerful storytellers, particularly in shaping the future of comedy, teen drama and women-led television.

In 2020, she created the Netflix teen dramedy Never Have I Ever, which reached 40 million households in its first four weeks and concluded in 2023 to widespread acclaim. She co-created and executive produced The Sex Lives of College Girls, which became the most watched comedy premiere on Max in 2021. In 2024, she produced the Oscar-nominated short film Anuja, a powerful narrative about sisterhood, exploitation and hope.

In 2025, Kaling co-created and executive produced Running Point for Netflix, which debuted as the No. 1 TV show in the U.S., surpassing a billion minutes viewed in its first week. The series was quickly renewed for a second season, which recently premiered on Netflix. She is also the creator and executive producer of the 2026 Hulu series Not Suitable for Work, a workplace dramedy following a group of ambitious young professionals in Manhattan.

Through her company, Kaling International, she has cultivated a distinctive brand rooted in sharp humor, emotional honesty and inclusive storytelling. Her leadership has helped launch the careers of diverse new writers, showrunners and creatives, particularly women and people of color who have gone on to shape major projects across the industry.

Beyond television and film, Kaling is a best-selling author. Her comedic memoirs Is Everyone Hanging Out Without Me? (And Other Concerns) and Why Not Me? became New York Times best-sellers, followed by her personal essay collection Nothing Like I Imagined. She went on to launch Mindy’s Book Studio in partnership with Amazon Publishing, an imprint dedicated to championing new and underrepresented literary voices. In January 2027, Kaling will release I Did Everything Right, Right?, a collection of six personal essays from Amazon Original Stories exploring motherhood, ambition, identity and the unexpected realities of adulthood with her signature wit and candor.

Polimeni will speak about the need for more accurate depictions of breast cancer onscreen. She will cite research, funded by Lilly and conducted with Dr. Stacy Smith and the USC Annenberg Inclusion Initiative, finding that just two female characters had breast cancer across a full year of Hollywood’s most popular films and TV shows, out of more than 8,600 speaking characters. Polimeni will urge the industry to bring more stories to screen that reflect the experiences of women living with breast cancer. Her remarks come as Lilly brings free mobile mammograms to Koreatown in Los Angeles from Dec. 4-6, a neighborhood with one of the densest concentrations of late-stage breast cancer diagnoses in L.A. County, so more women can get screened close to home.

Ramakrishnan first captured the attention of international audiences as the charmingly relatable Devi Vishwakumar in Netflix’s hit series Never Have I Ever and has shifted into high gear ever since. She leads Netflix’s comedy dance film Best of the Best, now streaming. Other recent projects include scene-stealing as Lindsay Lohan’s endearingly neurotic pop star client Ella in Disney’s Freakier Friday and in Amy Wang’s horror-comedy Slanted. She has also guest starred in Prime Video’s critically acclaimed series The Boys as Countess Crow and has voiced characters in witty coming-of-age stories including Netflix’s Big Mouth and Disney’s Turning Red. Up next, she has roles in The Angry Birds Movie 3, in theaters Dec. 23, and season two of Prime Video’s Legally Blonde prequel series Elle, expected to release next year.

Barone is a comedian, writer, actor and producer. She recently wrapped production on the independent film Still We Met, which Barone wrote and stars in opposite Joe Alwyn. Barone’s original series Hard Feelings, which she created, wrote and will lead, was recently acquired by Hulu and A24. She also stars in and writes for the hit Amazon and A24 comedy series Overcompensating, which will return for a second season Feb. 17, 2027. Barone recently wrapped production on the Amazon MGM Studios romantic comedy Love Love, in which she will appear opposite Tyriq Withers and Isabel May. Her hourlong comedy special, Galaxy Brain, was released on Netflix in July 2026 and made the Netflix Top 10 the week of its release. Galaxy Brain was also released as a comedy album on Aug. 19 and as a limited edition vinyl this fall via Universal Music’s MCA. Barone is the co-host of the popular podcast Ride with Benito Skinner.

Attended by 600 industry leaders and VIPs, THR’s Women in Entertainment breakfast coincides with the release of The Hollywood Reporter’s Women in Entertainment issue, featuring the highly anticipated Power 100, a list of the most powerful female executives and talent in entertainment.

Approximately 40 high school juniors and seniors currently participating in THR’s Women in Entertainment Mentorship Program will attend the event. The program pairs some of the brightest young women from underserved high schools across Los Angeles with some of the most powerful female executives in film and TV. The WIE Mentorship Program, now in its 17th year, is a joint venture with Big Brothers Big Sisters of Greater Los Angeles and the Entertainment Industry Foundation. Over 300 students have taken part in the program, with mentees having received more than $16 million in scholarships over the past 17 years. A highlight of the annual breakfast, this year’s celebration will feature the presentation of more than $500,000 in university scholarships to high school seniors in the program, generously provided by The Chuck Lorre Family Foundation and Lifetime. Earlier this month, The Hollywood Reporter announced Target as the new leading partner of its Women in Entertainment Mentorship Program.

Presented by founding sponsor Lifetime and title sponsor Lilly, THR’s Women in Entertainment event is also sponsored by Delta Air Lines, Target, Waymo and WME and in partnership with Big Brothers Big Sisters of Greater Los Angeles, Entertainment Industry Foundation, Chapman University, Loyola Marymount University, Golden Goose, UTA Foundation and Warner Bros.

The news comes on the heels of THR’s Daytime Emmys win last month. THR’s acclaimed video roundtable series, Off Script With The Hollywood Reporter, won the award for best arts and popular culture program.

Also in 2026, THR took home a record 20 SoCal Journalism Awards, including best magazine, best website and best podcast — more wins than any other media outlet. It earned a record 96 nominations at the National Arts & Entertainment Journalism Awards, outpacing any other press organization, after winning 50 top honors in 2025. Other accolades include an American Society of Magazine Editors award for best cover and the 2026 Webby People’s Voice Award for best entertainment website.
