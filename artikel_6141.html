Bilt is changing how its credit cards earn rewards, and cardholders who want to keep earning 4% Bilt Cash on everyday spending need to make a choice. New applicants must apply, be approved and accept their offer by December 31. I’ve already opted to keep the benefit—it’s a big reason my Bilt Palladium gets most of my spending.

Frequent Miler‘s latest ‘Ask Us Anything’ was a strong endorsement of Bilt. Their three Bilt Palladium Card (See rates and fees) cardholders plan to renew, and even the group’s Bilt skeptic plans to apply. They all want to keep earning 4% Bilt Cash.

If you don’t have a Bilt card, I’d get one now. You need to apply, get approved and accept the card offer by December 31, 2026 to preserve the choice of earning 4% Bilt Cash. Existing cardmembers also need to actively choose to keep it before their first card anniversary in 2027. Doing nothing means moving into the new rewards structure.

Nick Reyes calls renewing Palladium “pretty much a no-brainer.” It’s his “daily driver,” the card he uses most often. He’s happy earning two or three Bilt Points per dollar because they’re “very valuable,” with “plenty of great transfer partners” and continued good transfer bonuses. With a points accelerator active, he’ll often use it even for spending that another card would bonus. He’s approaching Platinum status (thanks to his card spend), but says the card’s earning makes sense even before transfer bonuses and status benefits.

Tim Steinke says he expects all three Palladium holders—Greg Davis-Kean, Nick and himself—to renew and retain the current rewards structure. Stephen plans to get the card before the old earning option closes, despite having no intended use for Bilt housing payments. He calls the points “absolutely fantastic.”

That’s also how I use the Bilt Palladium Card: it gets the majority of my spending. I pay my mortgage through Bilt, but the card would be valuable to me even without that.

I’ve written that Palladium looks complicated, but it’s the best everyday rewards card.

Most cardmembers already choose the second option. Palladium is still a great card that way. But for most people putting significant spending on the card, especially when they’re generating more Bilt Cash than their housing payments would consume, I think the 4% option is better. Once you’ve reached the highest housing multiplier, spending more doesn’t increase that multiplier. With Flexible Bilt Cash, more spending keeps producing more Bilt Cash to use.

There are exceptions. I’ve explained how the housing option can produce an effective 4 points per dollar on everyday spending. At the 25% spending threshold, $1,000 of everyday spending and $4,000 of eligible housing payments earn 2,000 points from the purchases and 2,000 from housing. That’s 4,000 points against $1,000 of everyday spending. The relationship between your spending and housing payments determines which choice works best.

For me, the accelerators alone are a reason to preserve Bilt Cash earning. Spend $200 in Bilt Cash to activate one and you earn an extra point per dollar on the next $5,000 in eligible spending. Palladium’s 2X becomes 3X. That $5,000 also generates another $200 Bilt Cash, which can fund the next accelerator. Each eligible cardholder can activate five per calendar year.

My wife and I have both maxed out our accelerators this year: 3 points per dollar on $50,000 of combined spending. We’ve earned points on housing payments as well.

She has an authorized user card on my Palladium account. It costs $95 annually and comes with her own Priority Pass membership. She doesn’t receive a second set of my annual hotel and Bilt Cash credits, but she can earn rewards in her own Bilt account (or I can switch it to mine).

I’ve laid out how my wife and I use this. I directed the points and Bilt Cash from her spending into her account instead of mine. She earns her own status, funds her own accelerators, and can take advantage of transfer bonuses separately. When an offer bonuses transfers of up to 100,000 Bilt Points per member, two accounts give us room to bonus 200,000 points, with each person’s bonus determined by their own status.

An authorized user’s spending doesn’t automatically get the primary cardholder’s active accelerator. They need to activate their own, using their own Bilt Cash. Bilt also gives the primary cardholder separate card numbers and controls over authorized user spending, including transaction and monthly limits. You can add an authorized user starting at age 13. But for my wife and me, the separate rewards accounts are the reason this has been so useful.

The new structure still allows Palladium and Obsidian cardmembers to use accelerators. Palladium’s $400 annual hotel credit and $200 annual Bilt Cash become a $600 annual Bilt Cash deposit at the card anniversary. That can fund three accelerators. Members can also earn Bilt Cash from other activity, including $50 for each 25,000 qualifying points. But they won’t replenish it at 4% of everyday spending.

Keeping the existing structure also preserves my separate $200 hotel credits, which I can stack with Bilt Cash hotel redemptions. The Bilt Cash hotel redemption allowance moves from monthly to twice yearly starting January 1 even for those keeping 4% earning, but I can still get the two $300 discounts this way.

Plus, Bilt Cash now expires at the end of the month 12 months after it’s earned. Cash earned in February 2026 lasts through February 28, 2027. The old rule would have wiped out everything over $100 at year-end. Having more time to use what I earn makes the 4% option better for me.

If you don’t have a card, I’d apply now and complete approval and acceptance before December 31. My choice is Bilt Palladium. The ability to preserve 4% Bilt Cash also applies to the Bilt Obsidian Card (See rates and fees) and no annual fee Bilt Blue Card (See rates and fees).

And if you already have a card, don’t assume your current selection of Flexible Bilt Cash protects you from the change. You need to proactively choose to keep it. I’ve already done this:
