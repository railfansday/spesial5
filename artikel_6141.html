Trump Says His Private Pilot Used To Use Foreign Air Traffic Control Because The American System Is So Bad

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Donald Trump says his private pilot used another country’s air traffic control because the American system was so bad. He wouldn’t identify the country, as though his pilot had discovered a better provider for approaches into Teterboro and didn’t want everyone else signing up. Pilots don’t get that choice. And this isn’t just an ambiguous reference to flying overseas. Trump has made this claim before and described landing in New York, Florida and Tennessee. At his Friday press conference, Trump said his “pilot would use the air traffic control from a totally different country.” This was supposedly before his first term, 11 or 12 years ago. Then he declared, “I know a lot about air traffic control.” Watch Trump describe his pilot using another country’s air traffic control — Aaron Rupar (@atrupar), November 20, 2026 Air traffic control assigns clearances and separates aircraft sharing the same airspace. The controller responsible for your flight changes as you move between sectors and facilities. Your pilot can request a different route or altitude. They can reject an unsafe instruction. They can’t decide to use a different country’s air traffic control service because they like it better. And there’s a clear division of responsibilities. Pilots fly the aircraft and request changes to clearances when needed. Controllers coordinate the traffic. Could There Be A Real Story Behind This? I’ve tried to come up with something plausible, to create the most generous interpretation of these remarks. The best I can do is that Trump is confusing navigation equipment with air traffic control. Perhaps his pilot told him about a preferred navigation system, approach procedure or a piece of equipment he liked from overseas, and that became a story about choosing another country’s controllers. There are also real circumstances where an American flight uses foreign air traffic services: Crossing the North Atlantic. A flight to Scotland could pass through airspace handled by Nav Canada’s Gander operation and Britain’s Shanwick operation. Taking a route through Canada. A pilot could request a route that avoids congested U.S. sectors and spends more time in Canadian airspace (but it doesn’t give Canadian controllers responsibility for a landing in Tennessee). Flying in U.S. airspace assigned to Canadian controllers. “American airspace means American controllers” isn’t always true. Under a 1963 agreement, the United States delegates control of certain portions of its airspace within 50 nautical miles of the Canadian border. But the government assigned that responsibility, the pilot doesn’t choose their preferred controller. None of this would reconcile his comments with what he claimed on May 8, 2025. So, if I’m landing in New York or Florida or Tennessee, they’re using a system from another country. He repeated the story on June 27, 2025, too. So this is a recurring explanation of how he thinks flying works. Training qualified controllers takes years. In fact, I supported the direction of Trump’s 2017 air traffic control proposal.
