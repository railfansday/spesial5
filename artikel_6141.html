3,400+ Miles: Air Canada's Longest Ever Boeing 737 MAX Flights Launch In 4 Weeks

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Air Canada is preparing to launch its longest-ever Boeing 737 MAX 8 route in less than four weeks. Beginning December 13, the Canadian flag carrier will connect Vancouver International Airport (YVR) with Liberia International Airport (LIR) in Costa Rica, covering 3,425 miles (5,512 km). The new service will surpass Montreal-Nantes as the airline's longest MAX route by distance, while the return journey will have a scheduled block time of up to eight hours and 55 minutes.

The route will be operated by Air Canada Rouge, the airline's leisure subsidiary, which is undergoing a significant transformation. Air Canada is transferring its 737 MAX fleet to Rouge, while moving older Airbus narrowbodies to mainline operations or retiring them. The result will be a substantially larger Rouge fleet, with nearly 70% more installed seats than at the end of 2025.

Air Canada's new Vancouver-Liberia service will launch on December 13, operating twice-weekly until April 12, 2027, with three weekly departures during selected periods. Flights will use Rouge's 177-seat 737 MAX 8, configured with 12 business-class, 18 preferred economy and 147 standard economy seats. The route is part of Air Canada's expanded winter sun network.

The route will provide another nonstop option for Canadians visiting Costa Rica's Guanacaste region, known for its Pacific beaches and resorts. Air Canada will compete directly with WestJet, which already operates Vancouver-Liberia flights using the MAX 8. The additional service also strengthens Vancouver's position as a departure point for winter leisure travel to Central America.

The new route follows the opening of Rouge's Vancouver crew base in March this year, supporting the airline's Western Canada expansion amidst the ongoing fleet transformation. The updated Rouge cabin includes seatback entertainment, reclining seats, and complimentary Wi-Fi for Aeroplan members, making it better suited for longer leisure flights.

Air Canada's COO Mark Nasr emphasized the importance of a consistent passenger experience across the airline's network.

“When customers step onto our aircraft, they should instantly feel a sense of comfort, care, and pride. This renewal program is about delivering that feeling consistently, across every flight. Every update is designed with our customers in mind, as we introduce an entirely new Air Canada Rouge product.”

Air Canada's 737 MAX 8 has operated some remarkably long routes, including transatlantic flights to Ireland, France, Belgium, and the United Kingdom, as well as services to Hawaii and the Caribbean. However, the new Vancouver-Liberia route will take the top spot at 3,425 miles (5,512 km), exceeding Montreal-Nantes by 98 miles (158 km).

The ranking illustrates how Air Canada has used the MAX to support thinner transatlantic markets. Halifax to London Heathrow Airport (LHR) is particularly significant, recording nearly 2,000 departures since the variant was introduced, while Montreal–Nantes and Toronto–Shannon demonstrate the aircraft's ability to serve secondary European destinations. Several of these routes are now transitioning to the longer-range Airbus A321XLR.

Beyond Europe, the MAX has also supported long leisure routes from Western Canada, including Calgary-Honolulu and Calgary-Kahului. But the aircraft's future is increasingly tied to Rouge's leisure strategy, with Edmonton-Montego Bay joining Vancouver-Liberia in December 2026. These additions illustrate how Air Canada is redirecting its MAX fleet toward vacation destinations while deploying the A321XLR on selected longer international routes.

The list is dominated by leisure destinations across Europe and the Caribbean.

Rouge's transformation involves more than replacing aging Airbus aircraft. At the end of 2025, the subsidiary operated 33 aircraft, comprising 15 A319s, five A320s and 13 A321s. Air Canada's fleet plans envisage an eventual all-MAX fleet of 52 aircraft, representing a 58% increase. The A320s and A321s are being transferred to mainline following refurbishment, while the A319s are being retired.

The capacity implications are substantial. Rouge's fleet will grow from 33 to 52 aircraft, but with the average aircraft capacity also increasing from 164 to 177 seats, the installed fleet seats will increase from 5,428 to 9,204, a gain of 3,776 seats, or 70%. Standardizing around the MAX should simplify training, maintenance and scheduling while providing newer aircraft with improved fuel efficiency.

The larger fleet also gives Air Canada more flexibility to expand leisure flying from Western Canada, supported by Rouge's new Vancouver base. New services to Liberia, Mazatlán and Puerto Escondido illustrate that strategy, while mainline receives refurbished Airbus aircraft and introduces the A321XLR for longer international operations. Vancouver-Liberia is therefore more than a distance record: it represents the emerging role of a substantially larger Rouge operation within Air Canada's network.
