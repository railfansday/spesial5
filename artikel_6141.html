The right travel credit card is important for any trip, as it can earn points or miles and provide valuable travel protections. But when you travel for months at a time, other factors — including no foreign transaction fees, international acceptance and benefits you can use outside the U.S. — become especially important.

Living and working on the road without a permanent home base puts these priorities to the test. The best card benefits solve practical problems and remain useful long after a typical vacation would have ended.

With that in mind, these are some of the best cards for long-term travelers and digital nomads.

Now, let's dive into what each of these cards has to offer.

All of the welcome bonus valuations in this guide are based on TPG's November 2026 valuations.

The is best for long-term travelers who want broad travel protections, premium lounge access and flexible points in one card.

Welcome bonus: Earn 100,000 bonus points after spending $6,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening.

The Chase Sapphire Reserve is an excellent card for long-term travelers because it earns at least 4 points per dollar spent on flights and hotels booked directly and 3 points per dollar spent on dining purchases.

The card's up-to-$300 annual travel credit is straightforward to use, while Chase Sapphire and Priority Pass lounge access can provide a place to work, eat and recharge between flights. The card also charges no foreign transaction fees.

Its travel protections are another reason why it's a great choice for digital nomads. Baggage delay coverage, for example, can help cover essentials when your checked bag doesn't arrive on time. Trip delay and trip cancellation/interruption coverage can also soften the financial hit when plans unravel.

Plus, Chase Sapphire Reserve cardholders can transfer Chase Ultimate Rewards points to hotel and airline partners, including fan favorites like United MileagePlus and World of Hyatt.

However, the card carries a $795 annual fee, and several of its statement credits are tied to particular merchants.

Cardholders can get up to $500 in statement credits each calendar year for qualifying prepaid stays of at least two nights through The Edit by Chase Travel℠, with a maximum of $250 per transaction. It can be a fantastic perk when a long trip calls for a comfortable hotel reset between apartment stays or after a long-haul flight.

If you spend most of the year outside the U.S., weigh the $795 annual fee against the benefits you’ll use regularly.

To learn more, read our full review of the Chase Sapphire Reserve.

The is best for digital nomads who can use its extensive lounge network, hotel elite status and premium perks.

Welcome bonus: Find out your offer and see if you are eligible for as high as 175,000 Membership Rewards Points after spending $12,000 on purchases within the first six months of card membership. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer.

The Amex Platinum comes offers one of the largest airport lounge networks available. The American Express Global Lounge Collection includes access to Centurion Lounges and participating Priority Pass lounges (enrollment required for Priority Pass Select).

That pairs nicely with a high earning rate on flights booked directly with the airline as well as up to a $200 airline fee statement credit every calendar year on your selected qualifying airline (enrollment required; terms apply).

Cardmembers can transfer their Membership Rewards points to Amex's airline and hotel partners, opening up award flights and hotel stays worldwide.

Several other card benefits lend themselves nicely to travelers who stay in hotels, rather than long-term rentals. Hotel elite status, including Hilton Honors Gold Elite, Leaders Club Sterling and Marriott Bonvoy Gold Elite, can make your stays more comfortable (enrollment required).

The up-to-$600 annual lodging statement credit adds more value to the card and can give you an excuse to add luxe stays to your itinerary. It is split into two up to $300 semiannual credits and applies to qualifying prepaid Airbnb Luxe, Fine Hotels + Resorts and The Hotel Collection bookings through American Express Travel®. (Airbnb Luxe and THC require a minimum two-night stay; FHR will also require a minimum two-night stay beginning Jan. 1, 2027; terms apply).

It's worth noting that you likely won't be able to use every statement credit. Someone spending a lot of time outside the U.S. may have trouble using several U.S.-focused perks, including Uber Cash (for U.S. Uber rides and Uber Eats orders) and Walmart+.

To learn more, read our full review of the Amex Platinum.

The Venture X is best for digital nomads who want premium travel perks and simple rewards on everyday spending.

Welcome bonus: Earn 75,000 bonus miles after spending $4,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening.

For digital nomads, the Venture X works well because of how little strategy it requires, at home or abroad. It earns 2 miles per dollar spent on purchases outside bonus categories. These flat-rate, hassle-free rewards are useful when your spending habits change from one country to the next.

The card allows you to transfer your miles to 15-plus airline and hotel partners. That's even more flexibility when your travels take you all over the world.

The card's $300 annual Capital One travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles (worth $100 toward bookings made through the Capital One Travel portal) are very easy ways to offset the annual fee.

Primary cardholders also receive complimentary access to Capital One Lounges, Capital One Landings and participating Priority Pass lounges.

The card offers cellphone protection when you pay your monthly wireless bill with it (up to $800 per eligible claim, with a $50 deductible and a maximum of two claims per 12-month period). That's useful when your phone is your boarding pass, map and connection to work.

To learn more, read our full review of the Capital One Venture X.

The offers a useful middle ground for digital nomads who want strong everyday rewards without paying a hefty annual fee.

Welcome bonus: Earn 60,000 bonus points after spending $4,000 on purchases in the first three months.

At $95 a year, the Strata Premier is the easiest card on this list to justify. Its protections are particularly practical when you live out of a suitcase. The card's 24-month extended warranty can be especially useful when living on the road, as nomadic life can take a toll on electronics and other gear.

Cardholders can transfer ThankYou points to more than 20 airline and hotel partners. There's also an annual $100 hotel benefit per calendar year for a single hotel stay (of $500 or more; excluding taxes and fees) booked through CitiTravel.com or by calling Citi. That's a fast way to offset the card's $95 annual fee.

The Strata Premier offers fewer statement credits than pricier cards. Its $95 annual fee makes it a good fit if you’re traveling on a budget or want an affordable second card.

To learn more, read our full review of the Citi Strata Premier.

The is best for everyday spending abroad, thanks to its earning rate on eligible foreign transactions.

Welcome bonus: Earn 80,000 bonus points and a 25,000-point Global Companion Award after spending $4,000 on purchases in the first 90 days from account opening.

For digital nomads, the card's value comes from a benefit that applies well beyond Alaska Airlines or Hawaiian Airlines flights. Cardholders earn 3 Atmos Rewards points per dollar spent on foreign transactions, whether that's a hotel stay, meal, pharmacy visit or souvenir.

You'll also get a 10% rewards bonus on points earned from card purchases if you have an eligible Bank of America account.

Atmos Rewards points can be redeemed for flights on Oneworld carriers and other airline partners, in addition to Alaska and Hawaiian.

The annual Global Companion Award provides a 25,000-point discount when two travelers book award travel on the same itinerary. Based on our valuations, this benefit alone is worth $400. That's enough to justify the annual fee, even if you never touch the other Alaska or Hawaiian-specific benefits.

Cardholders who spend $60,000 in a card anniversary year can earn an additional Global Companion Award worth up to 100,000 points. Since these awards can be used in any class of service, the larger award could be particularly valuable for a premium-cabin or long-haul partner redemption.

That same $60,000 in spending earns 30,000 status points. Combined with the 10,000 status points awarded after each account anniversary, you'd have enough for Atmos Gold status (mapped to Oneworld Sapphire). On eligible itineraries, that's good for business-class lounge access — even when you're flying economy — priority check-in and boarding, preferred seating and extra baggage.

To learn more, read our full review of the Atmos Summit card.

One business card that's particularly well-suited to this lifestyle is the (see rates and fees).

This card earns 3 points per dollar spent on travel, shipping, social media and search engine advertising, and cable, internet and phone services (on the first $150,000 in combined purchases each account anniversary year, then 1 point per dollar spent).

The Ink Business Preferred also offers cellphone insurance, primary rental car insurance on business-related rentals and no foreign transaction fees.

To learn more, read our full review of the Ink Business Preferred.

When you live out of a suitcase, the best card benefits aren't always flashy. What matters is what follows you: protections when your laptop dies or your flight gets canceled, solid rewards on everyday spending in any currency and lounges in the airports you actually pass through.

A long list of statement credits might only be great if you'll be back home in a few days. For extended travel, it's more important to pick the card that works wherever you wake up tomorrow.
