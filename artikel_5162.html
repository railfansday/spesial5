2026 hasn’t been a great year as far as security is concerned. Whether it is Bitget or Binance, every single episode this year has simply highlighted how important it is.

It hasn’t been a great time for chipmakers around the world either. The latest news associated with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company or TSMC is a case in point.

TSMC is in the news today after hackers reportedly offered up private information linked to one of the company’s employees on an underground forum. According to the hacker, they have access to over 46,000 records right now. These records will be released publicly if the hackers aren’t paid 3 BTC or $214.7K within one week.

Here, it’s worth pointing out that the hacker in question isn’t exactly holding the information to ransom. At the time of writing, there hadn’t been any demands or efforts to blackmail. Instead, the hacker is looking for a buyer, any buyer, for the information in question.

For its part, TSMC acknowledged that “data of some current and former employees was disclosed without authorization.” However, the company was also quick to stress that there is no real evidence that any of its security systems were compromised. Its preliminary investigative report suggested that,

… this data matches the data in the system service of a certain cloud service provider.

TSMC also clarified that the data in question wasn’t associated or linked with any clients or production.

Right now, the popular chipmaker is working with cloud service providers and cybersecurity agencies to better understand what happened. It is also taking measures to make sure this doesn’t happen again.

Here, it is worth clarifying that the present case isn’t exactly a traditional ransomware attack. Instead, it’s a simple case of data breach since no malware was used and TSMC hasn’t been locked out of its system.

Still, this is an industry that isn’t exactly new to ransomware episodes. Back in 2023, for example, TSMC was targeted by the LockBit group who demanded $70M.

According to Black Kite’s ransomware report, for instance, there has been a 24% YoY increase in ransomware disclosures compared to the previous disclosure period. It also found,

The threat actor ecosystem grew right alongside the victim count, reaching 127 active groups by the close of the period and 146 by June 2026.

Finally, the report in question also revealed that the manufacturing sector accounted for around 22% of all ransomware attacks. Professional, scientific, and medical services accounted for another 18% of the total share. The ransomware breach that targeted DaVita Inc. back in 2025 and affected over 2.7M patients is perhaps the most famous example of the same.

The timing is interesting. Especially since news of this incident coincided with Nvidia hogging the headlines after CEO Jensen Huang resigned following an extramarital scandal. In fact, one can argue that the news has taken the spotlight off TSMC for now.

As expected, this has had a corresponding effect on the sector, related industries, and their performances. For instance, the likes of IREN, Core Scientific, and TeraWulf all fell on the charts in the hours following the aforementioned resignation.
