'Too Big A Risk' - Game Developer Sparks Debate After Delaying Playtest Due To AI Concerns

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Will it push us towards a more cloud-focused future?

As AI continues to adopt a bigger influence in the gaming industry, there have been concerns in some circles about how people can use AI to reverse-engineer games, including for upcoming titles that haven't even been released yet.

This became a particular talking point a couple of weeks ago when Moonlighter developer Digital Sun Games decided to delay a planned (PC) playtest for their upcoming game ReVamp, worrying that the code might be reverse-engineered before they actually had a chance to release the full game.

Here's what they had to say in a statement at the time:

This ended up sparking a whole bunch of discussion regarding developers that might choose to adopt the same strategy, potentially opening us up to a future where demos and trials for upcoming games will become less prominent.

Some even took it further, suggesting that reverse-engineering will push us to a cloud-focused future:

A few people pushed back in the comments, suggesting that games have still been successful despite the existence of piracy and other elements that have threatened their potential over the years, while others highlighted that stronger protections in the future may be able to limit the damage from reverse-engineering situations.

For what it's worth, it's been reported in recent days that Claude has begun blocking attempts to clone and reverse-engineer games, and it was confirmed by an Anthropic dev that the idea is to "steer it away" from recreating other developers' titles because infringing IP is against the company's usage policy.

Ultimately though, it's too early to know what the future holds, but it's an interesting topic of discussion at the very least. Don't be surprised if instances like this still crop up, and hopefully it's something that Xbox is actively thinking about.
