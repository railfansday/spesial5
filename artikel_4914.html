Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Bitcoin Wallet Maker Trezor Says Customer Data Was Exposed Again

The “days since last third-party breach” counter at Trezor has reset to zero once again.

This time, hackers accessed shipping partner Skladon's systems and likely exported information tied to approximately 27,000 hardware-wallet orders, according to a company disclosure published Monday.

The intrusion occurred November 7, but Skladon did not notify Trezor until November 15. The exposed records cover orders placed between August 9 and November 7, including customers' full names, phone numbers, delivery and email addresses, plus the products they bought and their prices.

Trezor said its own systems were not compromised, and no wallet backups, PINs or passphrases were involved. The breach does not, by itself, let an attacker move a buyer's coins, but it gives them a potentially dangerous combination: a home address and evidence that someone bought a device designed to store crypto.

The latest incident follows an August shipping partner hack and a September email provider breach, making three separate third-party security incidents in a little over three months.

Trezor said some customers have already reported fraudulent phone calls. Criminals could cite the exact model and price of a wallet to make an unsolicited call, text or letter seem legitimate, or use a home address to identify someone who may hold cryptocurrency.

In August, logistics provider ShipMonk suffered a breach that initially appeared to affect fewer than 14,000 Trezor customers. An updated investigation uncovered records for another 67,000 U.S. buyers from 2019 to 2021 that ShipMonk had retained despite assurances they had been deleted, bringing the total to 80,689 people.

September brought another incident, this time at marketing email provider Brevo. Hackers used Trezor's legitimate email channel to distribute a fake security warning urging recipients to install an app that requested their wallet backups. Trezor later said 347,149 email addresses had been exported.

These weren't Trezor's first brushes with the problem. A Mailchimp insider attack targeted Trezor users in 2022, and a 2024 breach of its external support portal put the contact details of up to 66,000 people at risk.

The fallout can go beyond inbox spam. In February, fake letters were sent to hardware-wallet owners, complete with Trezor branding, holograms and QR codes leading to phishing sites.

Asked why an order from Trezor's official store involved an outside company, Trezor responded that it’s basically a logistics question, not a tech issue. “Companies of our size and global reach can’t run efficiently without third-party service providers,” the company replied to a comment on X.

Hi, companies of our size and global reach can’t run efficiently without third-party service providers. Handling as much as possible internally is the goal, but doing every part of the business ourselves isn’t realistic. Skladon is one of the partners that delivers Trezor orders,…

It said Skladon held only the information needed to deliver parcels, and handling every stage of global distribution in-house was not realistic.

Trezor says Skladon does not ship to the United States, so American buyers were not affected by this incident. Affected customers have been notified where possible, though some people who ordered between August 9 and 14 may not receive an email because Trezor had already anonymized their records.

The company has introduced anonymous pickup at parcel lockers or collection points in 18 European countries, allowing buyers to avoid supplying a home address. It is also working to shorten retention of order data from 90 to 45 days and says shipping partners will have to follow the same policy.

Trezor advises customers to ignore unexpected calls and messages about their orders, check any concerns through its official support site and never share a wallet backup. It has notified a data protection authority, but has not yet disclosed how attackers got into Skladon's systems.
