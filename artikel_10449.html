KFC store closures: See the full list of doomed restaurant locations as a large franchisee files for bankruptcy

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Bad news for fried chicken fans.

Dozens of KFC restaurants have closed or will close their doors soon after their owner sought Chapter 11 protection this week—part of a wave of recent bankruptcies involving leveraged fast-food franchisees.

The franchisee, Tasty Chick’n LLC, operated 75 KFC restaurants and another nine cobranded KFC/Taco Bell locations across 10 states at the time of its bankruptcy petition.

But it also disclosed a list of more than 70 restaurants whose operations it did not deem worthy of continuing.

Those restaurants—many of which appear to have closed before the bankruptcy petition—are located in Alabama, Arkansas, Florida, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Missouri, Mississippi, Tennessee, and Virginia.

The Memphis area appears especially hard hit, with more than a dozen of the city’s KFC locations on the closure list.

Why did the franchisee file for bankruptcy?

Texas-based Tasty Chick’n and its affiliates say higher operating costs and declining revenue have eaten into their bottom line, with restaurant-level profitability declining since 2023. 

At the same time, the franchisee says costly demands for store upgrades from Yum Brands—parent company of KFC and Taco Bell—have led to $15.6 million in unpaid royalties owed to the restaurant giant.

The situation became so precarious that Yum Brands in September sent Tasty Chick’n a “notice of intent” to terminate the company’s franchise agreements, court filings say. The franchisee turned to the Chapter 11 process with the hope of selling its assets while it explores “other stand-alone restructuring alternatives.”

Reached for comment by Fast Company, a spokesperson for KFC said the brand works closely with its franchisees as they navigate difficult operating environments like this one.

“We’ve been actively engaged with Tasty Chick’n as it works through its challenges and remain focused on positioning our franchisees for long-term success,” KFC said in an emailed statement.

Tasty Chick’n did not immediately reply for a request for comment.

Which KFC restaurants have closed or are closing?

In court papers filed on Tuesday, Tasty Chick’n listed dozens of its restaurants that it says are closing soon, in the process of closing, or have already closed.

A cursory search of the addresses reveals that most of the stores are already marked as closed on Google, with some of the closures happening recently.

The company and its affiliates are now seeking court permission to get out of their lease obligations for the locations and abandon the properties.

It’s not clear if additional stores could close or how many jobs may be impacted by these closures.

Tasty Chick’n is managed by Tasty Restaurant Group (TRG), which oversees a sprawling empire of fast food restaurants across the South, Midwest, and Northeast—a fleet that includes Burger King, Pizza Hut, and Dunkin’ locations, in addition to KFC and Taco Bell. TRG is not identified as a debtor in the Chapter 11 proceedings.

Why does this sound familiar? 

Tasty Chick’n is not the first large fast-food restaurant operator to seek Chapter 11 protection this year.

In September, the owner of more than 300 Wendy’s restaurants filed for bankruptcy after years of sales struggles and a contentious relationship with the chain’s parent company. Franchisees for Denny’s, Subway, Moe’s Southwest Grill, and others have made similar moves.

Collectively, these franchisees have closed hundreds of restaurants in 2026.

Is KFC in trouble?

It’s not having the best year. Yum Brands recently reported a third-quarter decline of 6% for KFC’s U.S. sales. The picture is rosier elsewhere in the world, particularly in markets like the Middle East and Asia, where KFC’s Q3 sales were up by double digits.

The brand has faced increased competition in the market, particularly among younger consumers who have gravitated toward trendier chains such as Dave’s Hot Chicken and Raising Cane’s.

As of this writing, shares of Yum Brands Inc (NYSE: YUM) were up roughly 6.31% year to date, slightly underperforming the S&P 500, which was up 7.25% over the same period.
