America's largest IPO ever carries potentially seismic implications for political spending ahead of 2028

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

FOX Business breaks down Anthropic's historic $81.34 billion IPO, the largest public offering on record, surpassing SpaceX and valuing the Claude AI maker at roughly $1.5 trillion. The blockbuster Wall Street debut could deliver massive payouts to Anthropic's founders, employees and early investors, including Ashton Kutcher.

Anthropic’s Thursday stock market debut, valued at around $1.5 trillion, is the largest in American history and, given promises made by its founders, is likely the preface to a similarly historic flood of cash into the political system.

Bloomberg projects that each of Anthropic’s seven co-founders will be worth approximately $24.5 billion now that their artificial intelligence firm has gone public. Each of the seven has also pledged to give away 80% of their equity in the firm to philanthropic causes — a promise that has raised eyebrows given Anthropic’s links to a controversial moral philosophy and its history of spending big to influence elections.

Effective altruism, which some of Anthropic’s early funders and employees have voiced support for, generally treats massive political and philanthropic giving as a moral obligation to prevent AI "doomsday" scenarios, such as a rogue agent working to exterminate the human race. Though the company's founders have distanced themselves from the movement, some early employees and other figures associated with Anthropic have maintained connections to its ideas, particularly its emphasis on preventing potentially catastrophic risks from artificial intelligence.

Politically, this has manifested as Anthropic's leaders throwing large sums of money behind efforts to regulate AI development. This approach has often put Anthropic at odds with the White House and its Republican allies, who are pursuing unchained innovation to spur economic growth, a dynamic that could become even more tense now that the firm’s leaders have billions of dollars at their disposal.

A Fox News Digital analysis of campaign finance filings found that Anthropic employees donated about $7.1 million to Democrats and Democratic-aligned committees, compared to just around $3.7 million to Republicans and Republican-aligned committees during the 2026 electoral cycle.

Such a split is noteworthy given that Anthropic offers to match shares donated by its employees by contributing additional shares to the organizations they opt to support. If the firm’s employees decide to give large chunks of equity to left-of-center organizations, for instance, it would result in a significant Anthropic-funded financial windfall for the left.

"I think there’s a lot of overlap between certain left-of-center causes," Nathan Leamer, a consultant who works on tech policy, told Fox News Digital when asked about the political orientation of Anthropic’s employees and effective altruists more broadly. "They've benefited from capitalism, but their worldviews do not always reflect free market values."

While Anthropic's rank-and-file employees appear to favor Democrats in their political donations, some of the company's most prominent financial contributions have gone toward a political network seeking to influence both parties around AI regulation, rather than exclusively supporting one partisan side.

Anthropic’s founders and longtime employees have comparatively little in the way of direct partisan donations, with much of their money being directed toward Public First, a super PAC network that supports candidates in both parties who favor stronger AI safeguards.

Defending Our Values PAC, on the right, and the Jobs and Democracy PAC, on the left, spent millions of dollars influencing Republican and Democratic primary elections during the 2026 cycle to boost candidates seeking to advance safety-related AI regulations. The Democratic PAC spent far more, around $20 million, than its GOP-focused counterpart, which paid out just under $8 million.

As 2028 approaches, the seven co-founders' newly acquired fortunes could give them substantially greater financial capacity to support causes and organizations they believe in — and presidential primaries will be a natural target for their newly acquired war chests.

In the general election, whichever candidate takes a position more aligned with Anthropic’s preferred regulatory stance may be able to count on large sums to back their bid for the White House. The new funds also enable Anthropic and its allies to focus on a much broader range of down-ballot races to win more allies in Congress and state legislatures.

Explicitly political organizations aren’t the only ones likely to see their coffers swell from the deluge of cash.

A cottage industry of think tanks and advocacy organizations has sprung up in D.C. and San Francisco to conduct research and make arguments aimed at convincing those with power of the merits of robust AI safety regulation. Many nonprofit advocacy organizations, including 501(c)(4) groups such as Public First Action, are not required to publicly disclose their donors, making it difficult to identify the full network of individuals and organizations financing the push for AI regulation. Anthropic, however, made its initial $40 million donation to Public First Action to support such education and policy work.

"I can tell you among civil society — think tanks and foundations and other organizations in the nonprofit world — this is absolutely making an impact," Leamer told Fox News Digital when asked about this dynamic. "I was in a conversation last night with a conservative organization who was telling me they're being offered hundreds of thousands of dollars to engage in a more [effective altruist]-aligned worldview, even though it's not necessarily where they are philosophically … If that’s happening among people I’m talking to, I can’t imagine how many more they’re talking to."

An individual close to the think tank world, requesting to remain anonymous, told Fox News Digital that donors aligned with Anthropic and effective altruism are already attempting to throw money around with the aim of influencing conservative thought leaders. In their telling, prospective funders will approach right-of-center organizations and offer them funding to research topics they are already interested in, such as child safety, then use that grant funding as leverage to influence their messaging.

Much of this messaging has been oriented toward pushing regulation to slow AI development so that engineers can devise mechanisms to stop the technology from being used for evil, or from inadvertently hurting people.

Anthropic’s leaders maintain that such regulations are necessary to prevent AI from harming humanity, whereas its detractors argue that such guardrails will kneecap America’s ability to compete with China. Some critics have argued that Anthropic is pushing for safety laws as a way to create rules that it can then exploit to give itself a leg up over rivals.

WHO IS DARIO AMODEI, THE ANTHROPIC CEO PUSHING INDEPENDENT AI OVERSIGHT?

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei speaks on the day Anthropic holds its initial public offering at the Nasdaq MarketSite in New York City, on Nov. 19, 2026. (Brendan McDermid/Reuters)

Some have traced Anthropic’s caution toward AI development to the effective altruist movement, which itself has segments who predict that unregulated advancement in the field may bring about the end of humanity.

Critics of effective altruism, which holds that individuals should seek out the avenue through which they can do the most good for each dollar spent, argue that its emphasis on speculative threats, including catastrophic artificial intelligence, can divert resources from immediate problems such as poverty.

In its extreme manifestation, some effective altruists have argued that one of the most efficient ways to engage in charitable giving is by improving the welfare of shrimp, as hundreds of billions of shrimp are farmed annually, so even modest improvements in how they are treated could reduce enormous amounts of suffering at relatively little cost. Mainstream effective altruists, however, generally argue that funds should be directed toward researching AI safety given the near-infinite negative downside of the technology destroying humanity.

That Anthropic’s leaders will actually open their pocketbooks to the extent promised, or even to these causes in particular, is not guaranteed. The IPO’s prospectus calls the commitments "voluntary, revocable expressions" of philanthropic intent that "do not constitute legally binding obligations."

Their ability to spend will also be dependent on Anthropic’s stock holding value amid a turbulent political and economic environment.

Anthropic and Public First Action did not respond to requests for comment when reached by Fox News Digital on Thursday.
