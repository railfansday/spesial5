Ryanair Cancels All Flights To 5 Destinations: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Ryanair plays a critical role in Europe. In November 2026, OAG data shows that the ultra-low-cost carrier has 14% of the continent's short-haul flights. In other words, the carrier provides one in every seven services.

Due to only having 189-seat Boeing 737-800s and 197-seat 737 MAX 8-200s, Ryanair has 17% of Europe's short-haul seats for sale. More than one in every six seats is provided by the budget operator. This dominance does not mean it is immune to routes and airports being cut. Far from it, with five airports removed in October alone.

It'd be easy to conclude that the following places have simply stopped being served because October is when Northern Hemisphere airlines switch from summer to winter schedules based on IATA slot seasons. This occurred on Sunday, October 25, with the airports all last seeing Ryanair just before that date.

The cuts are not seasonal, and the markets are not coming back in 2027. They have been removed from the ULCC's network, just like others were earlier in 2026, and another will be in early 2027. Some may return in the future. Ryanair had previously ditched two of them only to return later. In Volos' case, only one route (Brussels Charleroi) existed when the carrier resumed operations.

Notice the two Turkish vacation hotspots of Bodrum and Dalaman. By no longer serving them, Ryanair has entirely pulled out of Turkey. Unsurprisingly, this was enveloped in the discussion of fees and charges and additional support to aid the carrier's commercial success there. Turizm Ekonomi suggested that marketing support did not materialize to the agreed extent.

Turkey was never a notable part of the ULCC's map. In the all-important August 2026, for example, only 10 weekly departures existed: six to Dalaman and four to Bodrum. It was the least-served nation in Ryanair's entire network. Only Western Sahara had fewer flights (two), but it is not usually recognized as an independent country.

Turkey was always a special case for Ryanair because the country does not have an open skies agreement with the EU and the UK. As such, the ULCC's growth was limited. This is why it only flew from Dublin (using its Irish air operator's certificate, with the FR IATA code) and the UK (using Ryanair UK; RK code). The Slovak capital of Bratislava was a special case and was served using a bilateral loophole.

Think Serbia, think Air Serbia and Wizz Air, not Ryanair. In the peak summer of 2026, Ryanair only had 11 departures to the country, which meant that it was the airline's second least-served nation after Turkey. It had only ever flown to Niš, which is the third most populous administrative area in the country.

Given the ten-year existence, Niš must have performed sufficiently well overall. According to EX-YU Aviation News, the removal of Nis was due to refueling and fuel regulatory problems. The same source indicates that Ryanair has ruled out flying to Belgrade due to its high costs, although this could change. This is the main reason why it does not fly to the capitals of various other countries in the Balkans.

Ryanair appears reasonably keen to fly to Belgrade, subject to the right deal. In 2024, the carrier stated that "Belgrade Airport is too expensive and is not an option for us at the moment. We continue to track the [Serbian] market. It is interesting to us. If we can turn potential into a profit, we will." That was before the carrier pulled out of Serbia completely.
