High-density polyethylene (HDPE), a popular food-grade material, is highly durable, corrosion-resistant, and non-leaching. HDPE containers are also lightweight, making them easy to transport, and they can withstand a wide range of temperatures without degrading.Stainless steel is another excellent material for water storage containers. It’s durable, non-corrosive, and does not react with water, helping maintain the quality of the liquid inside. Stainless-steel containers are also resistant to bacterial growth, making them suitable for storing water for extended periods.

Glass containers are also considered very good for water storage because they don't leach chemicals into the water and are easy to clean. However, glass is more fragile, and glass containers tend to be heavier than plastic or metal options, which may limit portability in some situations. Only consider glass if you don't expect to ever bring your container from place to place.

Water storage containers come in various sizes and capacities to accommodate different needs and scenarios. Small containers, typically 1-5 gallons, suit short-term emergencies or portable use. They’re convenient for storing water in vehicles or bug-out bags, or for one person to use during outdoor activities. Small containers and portable water filters are also ideal for short-term water-supply disruptions.

Medium-size containers, ranging from 5 to 20 gallons, balance portability and capacity. They are suitable for supplying water for small families or individuals for a few days to a week during emergencies. And they’re often stackable, making them space-efficient for storage.

Large containers can hold 30 gallons or more and are your best bet for long-term emergency water storage at home. Large containers may take the form of barrels, tanks, or cisterns and require more storage space. Many individuals also use these in areas without running water for non-drinking needs such as quick showers, washing dishes, or cleaning.

Bulk storage solutions are also available for communities or organizations preparing for large-scale emergencies. Water tanks or reservoirs with capacities ranging from hundreds to thousands of gallons are typical for these situations. They are suitable for storing water for communal use and may require specialized infrastructure for installation and maintenance. Because of that investment and because we’re focusing this article on water storage for individuals and families, we don’t include any here.

Yes, water storage containers can impact the taste of water. Different materials, such as plastic, metal, glass, or ceramics, can leach subtle flavors or odors into the water over time, especially if you don’t clean the container properly.

For example, plastic containers may release chemicals like BPA into the water, while metal containers can impart a metallic taste. Additionally, stagnant water in poorly maintained containers can develop a stale or off-putting taste. Choosing a high-quality, food-grade container and cleaning it regularly can minimize negative effects on the taste of stored water.

The best materials for water storage containers are typically food-grade and inert, so they don’t impart taste or odor to the water. Glass containers are excellent for small quantities because they’re non-porous, non-reactive, and won't leach chemicals into the water. Stainless steel is another popular choice because it’s durable, non-corrosive, and won’t impart a metallic taste.

Food-grade plastic containers (HDPE or PET) are also suitable, but make sure they are BPA-free to avoid potential health concerns. Ceramic containers, especially those with a food-safe glaze, also work for water storage.

That depends on its quality, storage conditions, and the container type. Generally, commercially bottled water has a recommended shelf life of around 1-2 years if you store it in a cool, dark place away from direct sunlight and chemicals.

Tap or purified water can last much longer—potentially indefinitely—if stored in clean, airtight containers made of food-grade materials such as glass or BPA-free plastic. Regularly rotate stored water and periodically check for signs of contamination or changes in taste or odor.
